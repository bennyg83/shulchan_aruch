[14] He is called a day hire, etc. — and so wrote Beit Yosef in the name of Shibulei Leket; and he wrote on it: it does not appear so from the words of the poskim; and it is possible he did not see the words of the Ran in ch. 5 of Ketubot, who wrote the reason: if they dismiss him in the middle of the week he need not pay him for the whole week, only for the days he worked — therefore he is called Shabbat wages — until here; and he proves thus from Hagahot Maimoniot; and the same law applies when one rents a room to his fellow (Magen Avraham). And in my humble opinion, to compare this law to one who says twelve dinars a year, a dinar each month, in Choshen Mishpat siman 312 seif 15 — we are in doubt whether we follow the first wording regarding an intercalated month — the same law applies here; and the Ran there deals with a rebellious wife, that they add in truth for each day; but this law does not deal with that. And now it is settled: in responsa R' Eliezer Ashkenazi siman 73 he was in doubt about this law — see there. Also it appears to me that he cannot dismiss him in the middle of the week, since he hired him for a month and did not tell him except to pay each day such-and-such, and not at the end — and still requires study. And further Magen Avraham wrote: it appears to me the same law applies to lenders at interest: they must lend on terms that if he repays in the middle of the week he pays for the whole week or does not repay at all, and it is not counted by days — as in a rebellious wife whom they penalized to give such-and-such per day; even so it is forbidden to give from Shabbat wages; and the same law applies to every penalty like this; but if they reduce his debt each day such-and-such, it is not called Shabbat wages — until here. And to me it appears regarding lenders at interest: certainly, since they did not say from the outset to pay each day, only for the week — even if afterward he does not pay him for the whole week, there is waiver in collection. And the essence of this law of Shabbat wages — Rashi explained in the chapter "Even though," a decree because of commerce; and Beit Yosef challenged in siman 495 that commerce itself is rabbinic — and it is a decree upon a decree.
