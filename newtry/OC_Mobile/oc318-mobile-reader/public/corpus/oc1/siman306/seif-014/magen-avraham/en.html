It is a mitzvah to assess, etc. Although we do not tell a person to sin so that his fellow should gain merit, as stated in siman 254, seif 6, there the case is different, since he was negligent by placing the bread in the oven, as Tosafot explain. Here, however, that concern does not apply.
Beyond three parsahs. Even according to the view that going beyond the techum is according to the Torah, it is permitted here, for in relation to the concern that she may abandon observance and desecrate Shabbos all her life, this is considered a minor prohibition. It is better to desecrate one Shabbos so that she will observe many Shabbosos, as the Beit Yosef writes. Accordingly, if they wish to force her into another transgression, even idolatry one time, one does not desecrate Shabbos for her through a melacha according to the Torah, for one who desecrates Shabbos is like one who worships idols, as stated in Eruvin 69a and Chullin 5a. Rashi there explains that Shabbos is more severe than idolatry, and this requires study as to its source. This is what the Rama wrote above in siman 328, seif 10. The Bach wrote that here it speaks where we are concerned that they will frighten her until she abandons observance, whereas there it speaks where they want to force her, and that is not so severe a transgression, since the Torah exempts one who is coerced. Therefore Shabbos is not desecrated for her sake, for even a rabbinic enactment is not uprooted in the face of coercion, as stated at the beginning of Kesubos. This does not appear correct to me, as I wrote, for on the contrary, when they coerce him, we tell a person to sin on his behalf, as I wrote above in the name of Tosafot, and it is not comparable to the passage in Kesubos; see there. The matter is clear as I have written. See the end of siman 329 in the gloss, where it is stated explicitly that here too the case involves coercion. However, if the daughter is a minor, it requires study whether an adult should sin for the sake of a minor, since we are not commanded to separate him from transgression; see siman 343. Or perhaps this is like a case of danger to life, where we desecrate Shabbos for a minor and say, desecrate one Shabbos for him so that he will observe many Shabbosos, and here too the same applies. This requires study. In the glosses to the Rif, chapter 14 of Shabbos, he distinguishes between a case where the rescuer also gains a mitzvah; therefore the Rashba wrote that even an individual should not commit a prohibition to save the public. But all the poskim disagree with him. See the responsum of Beit Yosef, siman 2 in the laws of kesubos, and as written in siman 120. To go beyond the techum on Shabbos in order to avenge one's father is permitted, as stated in Nimukei HaRamah, laws of embarrassment, siman 21. This requires study, for it is no better than a mitzvah need, which is forbidden.
