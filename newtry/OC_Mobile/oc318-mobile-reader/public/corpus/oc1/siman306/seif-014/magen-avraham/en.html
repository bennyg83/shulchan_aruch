A mitzvah to set, etc. — and even though impossible for a person to sin so his fellow gains merit, as written siman 334 seif 6 — this case is different, for he was negligent in what he attached the loaf [Tosafot]; unlike here.

Beyond three parasangs — even according to those who hold it is d'oraisa permitted, for regarding "lest you turn and do all your days" desecrating Shabbat, this is a small prohibition, and better to desecrate one Shabbat so she will observe many Shabbatot (Beit Yosef); and if so, if they want to coerce him to another sin, even idolatry once, they do not desecrate Shabbat with d'oraisa melacha, for desecrating Shabbat is like idolatry, as in Eruvin 69a and Chullin 5a — Rashi explained Shabbat is more severe than idolatry; and it requires study whence this; and this is what Rama wrote plainly in siman 328 seif 10; and Beit Yosef wrote: here it deals when they fear she will convert, and there when they want to coerce her, and this is not so great a sin, for coercion the Merciful One exempts; therefore they do not desecrate Shabbat for it, for even a rabbinic remedy they do not uproot before coercion, as in beginning of Ketubot — end of his words; and it appears to me as written, that on the contrary when they coerce him they say to a person "sin for him," as above in the name of Tosafot; and there is an opinion for what we say in Ketubot on erev Shabbat, and the matter is clear as written; and it deals with 329 in the gloss where it is found explicitly that here too it deals with coercion; but if the daughter is minor — it requires study whether the adult will sin for her, for we hold the court is not commanded to separate her; or perhaps like pikuach nefesh they desecrate Shabbat for the minor, saying "desecrate one Shabbat for him so he will observe many Shabbatot" — likewise here; and it requires study; and in Hagahot Rif ch. 14 of Shabbat he distinguished between where he too benefits from the mitzvah; therefore Rashba wrote even an individual should not do a prohibition to save the community; but all poskim dispute him; and see in responsa Beit Yosef siman 2 on laws of Ketubot, and as written siman 120 [so it is]: to go beyond techum on Shabbat to avenge one's father from murderers is permitted (Nimukei Rama on laws of humiliation siman 21); and it requires study, for it is no better than other mitzvah need, that forbidden to go beyond techum.
