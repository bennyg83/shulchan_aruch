Seif 14 one to whom they sent etc. Tosafot Shabbat 4a s.v. vechi etc.; and that which was taught etc. this case is different etc.; and one can say etc.; and like both resolutions of Tosafot — and it is permitted only without negligence and a great mitzvah; therefore he said specifically that they took her out, and specifically in such a case — unlike siman 328 seif 10 where even though it is a great transgression nevertheless they do not desecrate Shabbat for her, for there is no mitzvah greater than keeping Shabbat — unlike this case to bring her into the Jewish people. Behold Rashba in a responsum forbids and wrote they permit only where the prohibition was done through him, as in ch. 3 of Eruvin — and these are the beginning of Tosafot Shabbat's words; but Tosafot conclude there that for a great mitzvah it is permitted as above; and in the second version they wrote where there was no negligence it is permitted. Rashba follows his view that he wrote in his novellae there, first version of Tosafot that a great mitzvah is different, and wrote "it appears to me" etc. half-freed slave has no concern of "you shall serve" because of the aspect of freedom in him; and that of a maidservant half-freed through whom a prohibition was done — there since many stumble it is certainly a public mitzvah, different as stated explicitly in "The sender" and ch. 3 "they ate." Behold the Mechaber ruled like Tosafot as above; see siman 328 seif 10 in the gloss where he forbids there — this works as above; and Avodah Zarah and Taz and Magen Avraham elaborated on this.
