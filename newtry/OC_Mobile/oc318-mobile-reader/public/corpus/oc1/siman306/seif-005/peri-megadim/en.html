Nevertheless, Taz and Bach, and likewise Magen Avraham letter 29: here too it deals with coercion; and she would remain apostate forever — they save in doubt of pikuach nefesh; and below, only the first time they coerce. And behold, three distinctions in the matter for Taz and Magen Avraham: the distinction when she would remain apostate forever — even initially under coercion they desecrate Shabbat in doubt (and it implies to my humble opinion: his daughter or relative — the same law applies to another person of Israel, even a minor, except that we are harsh upon his daughter); and in siman 328 seif 1, for idolatry they do not desecrate; and Bach distinguished between coercion — they do not desecrate, and willingly — they desecrate; and in the Levush he wrote: for three sins one dies and does not transgress — they desecrate Shabbat upon him even the first time, to save a Jewish life; unlike other sins — transgress and do not die; and in siman 328 it deals with other sins; and it implies that for three sins, even if they know they will not deliver themselves to death — nevertheless the law is thus; see there.
