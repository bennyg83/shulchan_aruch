(s.k. 7) It is called, etc. — and possible he did not see the words of the Ran ch. 5 of Ketubot, for they taught there: a wife who rebels against her husband from intimacy — they reduce her ketubah each week seven dinars, a dinar per day; and when the husband rebels against her they add to her ketubah each week three dinars, for the distress of a man when she rebels against him is double the distress of a woman when he rebels against her; therefore when he rebels they did not penalize him except half from the woman, that is, half a dinar per day; and they challenged: when he rebels they penalize him only half of what they penalize her when she rebels — nevertheless when she rebels they reduce for her seven dinars, a dinar per day — thus they reduce for her also one dinar because of Shabbat day; and thus for the husband, when they penalize half a dinar per day, he should add three and a half dinars per week, that is, half a dinar because of Shabbat day; and they answered: what is the reason they do not add half a dinar because of Shabbat day — because it is Shabbat wage; unlike what they penalize her and reduce for her a dinar also because of Shabbat day, for there it is not Shabbat wage. And the Ran asked: why did they call the addition on Shabbat when the husband rebels Shabbat wage — behold Shabbat wage swallowed is permitted, and here it is swallowed, for they add three and a half dinars for the entire week; and he answered: this is not swallowed, for initially the Sages fixed the penalty for the entire week together, that is, three and a half dinars; nevertheless when he reconciles with her in the middle of the week they do not add to her against the entire week, only against the days he was rebellious — thus it is day wage and not swallowed; and this is the words of Shulchan Aruch and Rama.

And so in the Gemara, etc. — that is, what was written above that what they reduce from her ketubah is not Shabbat wage.
