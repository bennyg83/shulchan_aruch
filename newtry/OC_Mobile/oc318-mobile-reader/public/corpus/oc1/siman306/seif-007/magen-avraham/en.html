Such as to measure. The same applies to measuring a hole to determine whether it has an opening of a handbreadth and impurity enters there. Although one could leave the house, nevertheless, since he is measuring Torah measurements, it is not considered weekday activity. Tosafot at the end of chapter 17 wrote that specifically measuring is permitted for a mitzvah need, because it is not much of a prohibition and is only forbidden because it resembles weekday activity; but other matters are not permitted. The Tur in Choshen Mishpat siman 73 wrote in the name of the Rosh that if Reuven swore to pay Shimon one hundred zuz on the seventh of Nissan, and if he did not pay him, and that day occurred on Shabbos, he must give him collateral worth one hundred zuz on Shabbos in order to fulfill his oath, for an item worth money is like money, and it is a matter of Heaven that he fulfill his oath. The Beit Yosef wrote that it is possible that even the Rosh said he fulfills his oath by giving collateral only when they assessed it and gave it as an assessed payment; therefore he needed to seek permission because it is a matter of Heaven. For if he does not give it as payment, this is an explicit Mishnah: if he does not trust him, he leaves his tallis, etc. But in the responsa of Mahari N. Y., part 1, he wrote that even if he swore to pay him in money and not with collateral, he should give him silver collateral, for an item worth money is like money. These are the words of the Beit Yosef. Based on this the Shulchan Aruch ruled there to permit the assessment, and the Sema permits even the measuring. This requires study, for the Rosh brings proof from the fact that one may arrange a match for a child and teach him a trade, saying that certainly it is permitted to pay on Shabbos in order to fulfill his oath. If so, it is enough for the derived case to be like the source, and there it is forbidden to mention the sum, as written in seif 6; also in seif 3 it is forbidden to mention an amount. The Maggid Mishneh wrote that the reason is that fixing a price is not itself a mitzvah, but only preserving his money. If so, here too he should give him movable items that he certainly knows are worth more than the debt, and he will not need assessment or measuring. The Rosh also wrote afterward that they again said that he had sworn to give money and not collateral, which implies that at first he said he should give it as collateral. The reason he needed to say that it is a matter of Heaven is because one may not pay debts on Shabbos, and even giving collateral is forbidden unless it is for a Shabbos need, as written in seif 6 and siman 307, seif 11; see there. Therefore it appears obvious to me that assessment and measuring are forbidden. See Yoreh Deah siman 239, seif 6. It appears to me that if he has money, he should give it to him through a non-Jew, as written in siman 307, seif 5; and if he has no money, he should tell a non-Jew to assess it, as stated in Sanhedrin 14a, or he should do as written in siman 500, saying, ‘This utensil is worth like this one,’ and tomorrow they will assess it.
