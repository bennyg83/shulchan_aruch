(א) Cooking. Even though there is no cooking after cooking — nevertheless there is cooking after roasting; and likewise there is roasting after cooking; therefore forbidden to place something that was cooked near the fire without gravy — Piskei Rekanati; but according to all there is no roasting after roasting; and something roasted is permitted to place near the fire even if it cooled; and the same applies to baked; and something cooked is permitted to warm in hot water, as is written seif 4 — Magen Avraham. The drinkers of the Land of Israel who heat water and mix coffee into it, and sometimes they do not put in all that is needed while it is still boiling on the fire, and when they put it in cups they add and put on the cup ground coffee to flavor and fix it — if one does so on Shabbat it is forbidden to add and to put from the coffee in the cups; and one who does so — when the Temple is rebuilt he is liable to bring a chatas — from responsum of R' Eliezer HaLevi in Ginat Veradim part 1 general 3 siman 2; and there until siman 9 what the sages of the Land of Israel expanded on this; and they wrote that one who is lenient has upon whom to rely, and one who is stringent — praiseworthy; and see Sefer Zera Avraham part 1 siman 1; and see responsum Penei Me'orot siman 84; and Shevut Yaakov part 1 laws of Shabbat:

(ב) Even in a kli shelishi. The reason in Beit Yosef: perhaps soft bread cooks even in a kli shelishi; and some permit — this is Tur's view, who holds we do not find cooking in a kli shelishi except for salt according to one version in a kli shelishi, and nothing else; and Magen Avraham wrote that according to what I wrote in seif 4 in the name of Bach one should be stringent in a kli shelishi, for it appears like cooking. (But in Sefer Eliyah Rabbah he brings in the name of a responsum of Mishna Berurah that it is permitted in a kli sheini):

(ג) In a kli rishon. Since it is dry there is no cooking after its baking:

(ד) That there is yad soledes bo. Meaning they do not put bread in gravy as long as there is yad soledes bo; and it appears to me that those who cut onions very fine and put them in gravy should not put them until there is no longer yad soledes bo; for with bread there is a leniency because it was already baked, and nevertheless they are careful — all the more so with onions; and even though it is possible that onions do not cook in a kli shelishi — nevertheless one can say that perhaps it is like salt for one version that cooks, as is written in seif 9 there, for we are not expert in what is called soft and forbidden — and this is all as long as yad soledes bo in the gravy — Taz:
