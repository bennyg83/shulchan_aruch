(א) If he cooked it afterward, etc. This Tur wrote in the name of R' Eliezer of Metz: even though there is no cooking after cooking in a dry thing — nevertheless there is cooking after roasting; and his proof from the case in the chapter "A kli" daf 41 regarding the Paschal offering — if they roasted it and afterward cooked it he is liable; this is according to R' Yosi, who said they go out in a wafer soaked in water on Pesach but not in cooked, for we do not call it bread even though it was already baked in an oven, since he returned and cooked it — it lost the status of bread; so too there is cooking after baking for Shabbat; and Ravyah disagreed, for in the chapter "A kli" they bring that case of R' Yosi for the blessing — boiled vegetables we bless shehakol, not borei pri ha'adamah as at first like matzah for R' Yosi; and he concludes there that R' Yosi only said this because we require taste of matzah and there is none; and Beit Yosef praised Ravyah, for his challenge is strong, for we establish that his blessing is ha'motzi, and therefore the rejection that we require taste of matzah stands per the halacha; and so Tosafos there, in the chapter "Every hour" that one can reject due to requiring taste of matzah on Pesach as in the chapter "A kli" for the blessing. And I am not fit to decide between great mountains; nevertheless as appears to me, to resolve R' Eliezer of Metz's words properly in this way — he holds like Rabbenu Yonah and R' Yosi above in seif 1 regarding contracts and is spoiled, that there is no cooking after it was cooked completely; and we already explained his reason nearby — in contracts and is good for him there is concern of cooking since he benefits from Shabbat labor in a new taste; unlike contracts and is spoiled; so too we may say here this reasoning, that even if you wish to reject and explain that case of R' Yosi due to requiring taste of matzah — this helps to reject the case of Pesach where they roasted and then cooked, for there the Torah decree is that we require roasted and not taste — the matter does not depend on taste; therefore we can say roasting was not nullified there; but with matzah we require taste of matzah — poor man's bread we require; so too we can reject the Pesach case according to Tosafos; but here for Shabbat plainly this rejection does not help, as stated, for melacha requires intent, as is stated in many places; and a fortiori we should say it is forbidden rabbinically due to change of taste; and what is written here forbidden — according to this view it is correct:

(ב) Even in a kli shelishi. The reason in Beit Yosef: perhaps soft bread cooks even in a kli shelishi; and some permit — this is Tur's view, who holds we do not find cooking in a kli shelishi except for salt according to one version in a kli shelishi, and nothing else:

(ג) And they practice to be careful l'chatchila. Meaning they do not put bread in gravy of the stew; and it appears to me that those who cut onions very fine and put them in gravy should not put them until there is no longer yad soledes bo; for with bread there is a leniency because it was already baked, and nevertheless they are careful — all the more so with onions; and even though it is possible onions do not cook in a kli shelishi — nevertheless one can say perhaps it is like salt for one version that cooks, as written in seif 9 there, for we are not expert in what is called soft and forbidden — and this is all as long as yad soledes bo in the gravy:
