We are not concerned that he will instruct another Jew to cook it, because a person does not sin without personal benefit. Magen Avraham, paragraph 2; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1. Nehar Shalom, at the end of paragraph 2, however, writes that it is proper to forbid it also to the person for whom it was cooked; see there. All this applies when someone cooked incidentally. If a Jewish innkeeper regularly cooks every Shabbos, it is certainly forbidden to a person for whom he cooked with that person's knowledge and consent. Moreover, even one for whom it was not cooked at all may not purchase it from him, because of placing a stumbling block before the blind and assisting transgressors. Responsa Ksav Sofer, Orach Chaim, siman 50; see there.
