Whether the illness began that day. And it is not forbidden due to muktzeh; and Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Rosh that we rule like R' Shimon, who said there is no muktzeh except a lamp lit on Shabbat [it appears to me there is a textual error and it should read: burning on Shabbat, as is written siman 279] and dried figs and raisins that he set aside with his hands and removed his mind from them, etc., end of his words; and afterward he wrote in the name of Rambam and Maggid Mishneh — not like Ra'avad and Ran, who did not permit for one who became ill on Shabbat due to muktzeh, even according to R' Shimon; and there is a difficulty from what Beit Yosef wrote siman 310 that R' Shimon concedes in something not fit for permitted use; and here there was certainly prohibition on the animal that was not slaughtered — nevertheless they permit due to muktzeh according to R' Shimon here; and therefore the reason is that afterward on that Shabbat it became permitted and fit; and so Beit Yosef wrote there that only in this does R' Shimon permit — namely when the prohibition departed; and on this there is a great difficulty: behold we do not rule like R' Shimon, for we forbid a lamp lit on it that Shabbat, and if extinguished — for R' Shimon permitted and we rule to forbid, as is written siman 279; if so, why did he rule here to permit an animal slaughtered on Shabbat for one who became ill on Shabbat? And one may answer that Beit Yosef's words in siman 310 are according to Ra'avad and Ran, that here where Beit Yosef brings that one may slaughter for a sick person — permitted for a healthy person — this is when the illness began erev Shabbat; but on Shabbat it is muktzeh, and even for R' Shimon this is not so, as is clear; for per the halacha, so too Beit Yosef in siman 310 came to resolve that the halacha should not contradict the halacha; and Beit Yosef wondered at Ran how he did not notice that we rule like R' Shimon that there is no muktzeh; and according to what I wrote one should wonder the opposite on those who permit; and it appears to me that the reason we do not rule like R' Shimon in a permitted matter that comes because the prohibition departed — this is that we say: since it was set aside for bein hashemashos, it was set aside for the whole day; and this is in a matter where permission came on Shabbat due to a change — the prohibition changed from what it was; namely a lamp lit on that Shabbat — even if extinguished, forbidden to move — we say: since it was burning at bein hashemashos, it is as if still burning and we do not regard the change in the lamp; and so a sukkah whose walls fell — we say since they were hanging on the sukkah at bein hashemashos they are as if still hanging; and so money on a hide — we see it as if still placed; unlike slaughter on Shabbat for one who became ill on Shabbat — the animal as it is and as it was at bein hashemashos alive, so it still is, only a new factor arose that a person became ill and the Torah permitted it due to pikuach nefesh; and if that sick person had been at bein hashemashos he would also have had permission; therefore there was no muktzeh name on it from prohibition at all — we do not say "since it was set aside," etc., but because the person set his mind away from it for the whole Shabbat and did not consider it for this — we do not rule like R' Shimon that muktzeh is permitted, as stated — correct and clear, even though this reasoning and distinction is not found explicitly; and Ra'avad and Ran do not hold thus; nevertheless those who permit — namely Rambam and Rosh — hold well thus, for it is a good reasoning, as appears to me:
