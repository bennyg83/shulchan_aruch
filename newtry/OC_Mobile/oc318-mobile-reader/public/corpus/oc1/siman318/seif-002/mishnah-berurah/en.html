(א) Became ill that day — and we do not say the animal is muktzeh because of prohibition, for it was set aside at bein hashemashos because of slaughter prohibition since at that time it was not yet ill that there would be intent to slaughter it; for we rule like R' Shimon who has no muktzeh except where he set it aside with his hands, such as a lamp one lit on it that Shabbat — for he set it aside with his hands not to use it that Shabbat since he lit in it; but regarding an animal he did not set it aside with his hands. See further in Beit Yosef:

(ב) A healthy person is permitted, etc. — the reason: since the essence of slaughter is for the sick person's need, for it is impossible to have an olive's bulk of meat without slaughter, and "lest he increase for him" does not apply here:

(ג) Alive on Shabbat — and without salting, for salting is forbidden on Shabbat; and rinsing is required because of visible blood — unlike blood absorbed in it there is no prohibition. All this he did not explain:

(ד) For a sick person forbidden — but at Motzei Shabbos permitted immediately, and we do not require for this "until it is done." And the reasoning: if the dish is good for the sick person it is permitted also on Shabbat:

(ה) Where there is no danger — for he is like a healthy person for this matter that it is forbidden from the Torah to cook for him through an Israelite, and we fear that if they permit eating from this perhaps they will increase for him:

(ו) Lest he increase for him — to put meat into the pot; and this is d'oraisa prohibition when he increases for him, even if he increases once before placing the pot on the fire. And some say it is d'rabbanan since it is at one time; see Beit Yosef [Magen Avraham; see in Ahavat Chesed ch. 7 where we clarified proof from the Gemara that the essence is like the first view]:

(ז) Forbidden on Shabbat — for a healthy person to benefit from it; but at Motzei Shabbos permitted immediately. The reason: since a non-Jew cooked it permissibly, we do not require waiting until it is done; and they also did not decree here because of non-Jewish cooking [Gra]:

(ח) From erev — meaning even if he intended from erev to cut the fruit on Shabbat — nevertheless forbidden, because preparation does not help at all for something attached, similar to this that grows on Shabbat; but fruit that finished ripening and no longer grows — preparation helps even though attached; but an animal that became ill today is forbidden in every case for a healthy person because of muktzeh of something attached:

(ט) That grows — see in Magen Avraham who asks that the growths should be nullified in the main substance which is the majority of the permitted, and it should be permitted for a healthy person when it became ill from erev; and in Biur HaGra he also leans thus for halacha:
