(א) He became ill that day. And even though the animal was muktzeh machmas issur shechitah — nevertheless permitted, for we rule like Rashi that there is no muktzeh machmas issur except when they set it aside with their hands, such as a candle that one lit upon it [Maggid Mishneh ch. 2]. And there is difficulty: for if so muktzeh machubar should also be permitted when fruits fell — and one can say that there the reason is lest he climb and detach, as is stated beginning of Beitza; and see there another reason in Beit Yosef:

(ב) Alive on Shabbat. And without salting [Tur]; and see Yoreh De'ah siman 67 seif 2 that rinsing in any case is required, and see siman 325 seif 11:

(ג) Lest he increase. And this is a d'oraisa prohibition when he increases for his sake even before he places the pot on the fire; and some say it is d'rabbanan, a weekday act:

(ד) And even if he cooked, etc. But from erev Shabbat permitted immediately [Shach], and his reason: since the non-Jew cooked with permission, he need not wait bekedei sheye'aseh:

(ה) Because it grows and continues. It deals with fruits whose cooking was not completed [Beit Yosef]; but when cooking was completed we do not say it grows and continues, for otherwise why would we need in first chapter of Beitza to forbid machubar lest he climb and detach or due to muktzeh — and see Tosafos who were forced there, and it should have said due to grows and continues; rather perforce when cooking was completed it no longer grows; and if so hachanah helps, and so Eliyah Chayim infers on Yom Tov — see Tosafos Bava Kamma daf 69 that what grew is nullified in the majority, and see Shach 62; and in Beitza Tosafos daf 37b it appears this is not muktzeh even for R' Yehuda, for an animal too grew and went and it is permitted to slaughter on Yom Tov — see there in Tosafos:
