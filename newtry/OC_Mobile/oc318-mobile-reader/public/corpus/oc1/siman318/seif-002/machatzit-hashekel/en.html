(ג) And see Yoreh De'ah end of siman 102 that if that item became mixed with others it is nullified (namely, if it was cooked on Shabbat intentionally and remains forbidden to him forever; but if he cooked unintentionally and it is permitted on Motzei Shabbat even to the one who cooked — it is obvious that if it became mixed on Shabbat it is davar sheyeish lo matirim) and is not davar sheyeish lo matirim similar to terumah, for we rule it rises in one in a hundred and is not davar sheyeish lo matirim, even though for a kohen it is permitted without mixture — nevertheless, since for an Israelite it is forbidden and for an Israelite it is not davar sheyeish lo matirim, likewise for a kohen it is not davar sheyeish lo matirim since it has no permission for the entire world. And the language "similar to terumah" is unnecessary. And behold Beit Yosef there brought this law in name of Rabbenu Yerucham, who so wrote in name of Ra'am, and wrote that one can say a proof from chapter of Orlah — and it is stated there daf 82: a piece of tamei chatas that became mixed in one hundred pieces of tahor chatas — it is nullified; but pieces of tahor chatas that became mixed in one hundred pieces of non-sacred meat — it is not nullified. And it challenges: what is different in the first clause and what is different in the last clause? And it answers: R' Eliezer — the last clause is because it is davar sheyeish lo matirim. And Rashi explained: permitted to a kohen. And we conclude: this of R' Eliezer is fiction — for whom? If for a kohen — when permitted, permitted (and Rashi explained: even now; and that which we rule davar sheyeish lo matirim is not nullified, such as when it will become permitted tomorrow; and for whom it is forbidden now, such as an egg born on Yom Tov). If for an Israelite — forever forbidden. And if so, his proof from a piece of tahor chatas mixed in non-sacred meat — we conclude it is not davar sheyeish lo matirim; except Tosafos there s.v. "because," etc. wrote: but it is difficult for R' Eliezer's view — why does terumah rise in one in a hundred? end of his words:

(ד) And there is difficulty, etc., regarding terumah (and likewise one can say so regarding the pieces of chatas mentioned). And Chiddushei Turei Zahav asked: if so, according to Magen Avraham's words, that in such a case as here it is davar sheyeish lo matirim — if so, why did it say R' Eliezer's view is fiction? One can establish it, for example, when tahor chatas became mixed for a tamei kohen or one who immersed that day, who now is forbidden to him and in the evening was permitted. In truth Maharshal in Yesh Seder in Yevamos already preceded him (but I did not understand Maharshal's explanation). And in my opinion, according to his words it is difficult — but what else? Rather, since it has no permission for the entire world — for to an Israelite it is forbidden — it is not davar sheyeish lo matirim; if so, what the Gemara said "if for a kohen when permitted, permitted" is superfluous language — it must teach in any case, since for an Israelite forever forbidden it is not davar sheyeish lo matirim. Also what it said "if for a kohen when permitted" — this is not a difficulty, for it told him it deals with a tamei kohen as stated. And in truth I saw in Turei Zahav that he concluded why the Gemara cited "if for a kohen when permitted" — for clarity of the matter we cite it. And in my opinion it is forced: even if it were an exclusion, "if for a kohen when permitted" would be a complete exclusion even though it is not needed for this, and it would suffice to say "if for an Israelite forever forbidden" — then one could explain through the forced explanation of "for clarity we cite it." But now that "if for a kohen when permitted" is not an exclusion at all, for it told him it deals with a tamei kohen as stated — "for clarity" does not apply; rather one can say Magen Avraham's words are correct. And what he challenged — that it should have said it deals with a tamei kohen — one can say: in any case the Gemara properly said R' Eliezer's view is fiction, for R' Eliezer stated his words generally and said the last clause is because it is davar sheyeish lo matirim, and did not say it deals with a tamei kohen — it appears in every case it is davar sheyeish lo matirim even if there is no tamei kohen, and this matter is fiction as the Gemara said "if for a kohen when permitted," etc. And do not challenge him that R' Eliezer's words are fiction — nevertheless the Gemara for itself could establish such a case with a tamei kohen. This is the difficulty: one can say for the Gemara other explanations are more plausible than establishing it became mixed for a tamei kohen. But in Turei Zahav he first challenged Magen Avraham's words, who holds in our case that since for others it is davar sheyeish lo matirim, also for himself — even though he has no permission — it is called davar sheyeish lo matirim and is not nullified; if so, why did the Gemara say R' Eliezer's view is fiction — are there no tamei kohanim and those who immersed that day in the entire tribe, who today are forbidden to them and in the evening will be permitted, and for them it is davar sheyeish lo matirim? And if so, according to Magen Avraham it is davar sheyeish lo matirim for the entire world. And in my opinion one can say Magen Avraham also holds: when there is permission for a private person or certain individuals — therefore it is not called davar sheyeish lo matirim relative to others; rather Magen Avraham spoke only in our case, where it is davar sheyeish lo matirim for the entire world except a private person. Such a case is davar sheyeish lo matirim, and likewise there with chatas — even though certainly tamei kohanim and those who immersed that day will be found, for whom it is davar sheyeish lo matirim — nevertheless because of individuals it is not davar sheyeish lo matirim; and if it were davar sheyeish lo matirim for all kohanim, it would be called davar sheyeish lo matirim for the entire world, since they are the priestly family alone, and we say kohanim are a people unto themselves — see Talmud:
