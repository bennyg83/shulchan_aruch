(s.k. 19) They permit, etc. — hold leniently like the second explanation of Tosafos. Tosafos on daf 39 challenged Rabbeinu Tam who holds iruy cooks to the extent of a peel — if so, that which we learned there "and whatever did not come into hot water before Shabbat — one rinses it in hot water on Shabbat" except old salted food and colias of Aspamia, whose rinsing is gmar melachto — for Rabbeinu Tam, that which teaches "one rinses it in hot water" perforce means from kli sheni, for from kli rishon unnecessary, for iruy from kli rishon cooks at least to the extent of a peel; and since it says "one rinses" — meaning from kli sheni — why specifically rinsing? Even warming should be permitted, for kli shelishi does not cook. And one can answer: nevertheless warming is forbidden, for it appears like cooking. Or: likewise soaking is permitted, and that which it taught "one rinses" is to inform us that old salted food and colias — even rinsing is forbidden. And so Shulchan Aruch's view from that it taught "and forbidden to place bread," etc. — it appears specifically bread that is soft; but other things do not cook and are permitted. And for the first explanation of Tosafos — nevertheless it should be forbidden, for it appears like cooking; even so it holds like the second explanation of Tosafos; and on this he wrote one should be stringent — namely the first explanation of Tosafos. And even though in any case Magen Avraham in s.k. 18 wrote that even in other things, not only bread, one should be stringent — nevertheless one can say in s.k. 18 he forbids only in other soft things, and here he wrote one should be stringent even in a hard thing, for it appears like cooking:
