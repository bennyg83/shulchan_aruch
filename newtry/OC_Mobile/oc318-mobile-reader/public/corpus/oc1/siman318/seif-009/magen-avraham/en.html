(א) As long as it was yad soledes bo. And it appears to me that if there is no yad soledes bo it is permitted even though some forbid, as written in Tosafos Chayyim general rule 57 — namely due to absorption and emission; and also some forbid even in kli shelishi. But according to all it does not cook; however in the Yerushalmi it is stated they made a distance for kli sheni even when there is no yad soledes bo, and so in Teshuvot Tashbetz, and see Yoreh De'ah siman 94 in Taz in name of Rashal:

(ב) Of an ox. Granted the meat does not cook — the moisture in it cooks (Tur):

(ג) Blood in it. And it deals, for example, when it was rinsed after salting — for otherwise still forbidden due to blood in the salt; and not as Bach wrote. Nevertheless the essential view of Beit Yosef appears that specifically when it is still salty then the salt assists cooking, as Bach wrote in name of Rabbenu Yerucham, and see siman 68; and Bach decided like Rama, and to conclusion that Rosh wrote even if salted anew, etc. — he retracted his earlier words [Bach], and so in hints and in Tur; and see in Tosafos Chayyim general rule 1 and general rule 2:

(ד) That the salt is nullified in sixty. And even though something done for taste is not nullified — here it is zeh v'zeh gorem, for presumably it was also salted on erev Shabbat. And even though davar sheyeish lo matirim is not nullified — since it was nullified before it became forbidden it is nullified, for it dissolves immediately and is not cooked immediately, for it requires cooking like ox meat (there). And it must be said that the salt he now placed did not have enough to give taste in the pot except the salt from yesterday — for if it had enough to give taste it is forbidden, as written Yoreh De'ah siman 87; and so brought in Mishnah chapter 2 of Orlah and in Beitza ch. 5.
