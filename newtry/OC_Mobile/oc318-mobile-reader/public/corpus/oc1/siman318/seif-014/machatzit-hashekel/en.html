(א) (s.k. 14) Permitted, etc. — not like Bach who is stringent, for Bach brought in name of Hagahot Mordechai that he was uncertain in the matter whether permitted even in kli rishon or not — therefore Bach is stringent from doubt. But Magen Avraham holds we do not abandon what is clear to Tosafos, Mordechai, and Ran to permit because of Hagahot Mordechai's doubt:

(ב) And it appears from Shulchan Aruch's language, etc. — only if it fell into hot water forbidden, etc. — namely even though Beit Yosef in his long book wrote it appears from Rambam's language that since it fell from erev Shabbat into boiling water, even though it was not cooked from erev Shabbat, nevertheless it is considered cooked from erev Shabbat for this matter that it is permitted to warm it in hot water on Shabbat — and he inferred from the Mishnah: "whatever came into hot water from erev Shabbat — one soaks it in hot water on Shabbat," and it did not teach "whatever was cooked from erev Shabbat," etc. — it appears only when it fell into hot water from erev Shabbat; and so Magen Avraham: from Shulchan Aruch's language it does not appear so, but we require specifically that it was cooked from erev Shabbat; therefore he wrote good to be stringent as Shulchan Aruch implies — not like Rambam:
