[9] To insulate it in sand, etc. Even rolling food upon sand is prohibited (Rosh; Rabbenu Yonah). On a roof containing earth, even fully roasted food may not be placed there to preserve its heat, even before Shabbos, just as with sand and dust. If the roof contains no earth, Maharshal in Responsa siman 61 permits it. Yesh Seder LaMishnah and Magen Avraham prohibit, while Nachalat Tzvi and Lechem Mishneh, chapter Kirah, rule like Maharshal. Ran writes that food may not be placed above the hot springs of Tiberias on Shabbos, but may be placed there before Shabbos; placing it inside the water is prohibited as stated in siman 326, seif 3. Rambam, chapter 9, writes that one who places meat on coals is liable once an amount the size of a dried fig is roasted. According to Raavad, this applies even at half cooking; according to Rambam, when only part of the meat cooks one is not liable until the entire piece reaches half cooking (Magen Avraham). This requires study, because Raavad appears to agree with Rambam and merely explains that half cooking means the degree of ma'achal ben Drusai, contrary to Rashi, who defines it as one third cooked. Rambam further writes that melting any metal, heating metal until glowing, melting wax, fat, pitch, or sulfur, or firing earthenware until it becomes pottery, creates liability as a derivative of cooking. The general rule is that whether one softens a hard substance or hardens a soft substance by heat, one is liable.
