(א) That it is forbidden to cook. One who cooks in toldos ha'or is liable:

(ב) To insulate it in sand. Even from erev Yom Tov — see siman 257 seif 3; and even to roll it on sand so it will roast is also forbidden [so too Rosh]. And there is difficulty why Tur omitted it, for we rule the halacha is like Rabah [Bach], and it appears Rosh wrote according to R' Yosi — see there; but we rule like the rabbis, and if so it is obvious it is forbidden, for we decree toldos chamah due to toldos ha'or, and so Ran — see there. Nevertheless Rashal wrote in a responsum that one may roast on a roof heated by the sun since it is not exchanged for toldos ha'or — see there, and his words are not compelled; and also Taz disagrees with him; and in the Yerushalmi it is stated explicitly it is forbidden. Rambam wrote one who cooks in the hot springs of Tiberias is exempt, for it is like toldos chamah, and so in the Gemara, and so in Mishnah chapter 9 of Negaim; and in Sanhedrin they say some remained from the flood — and it is possible nevertheless it has the law of a toldah of chamah. Ran wrote it is forbidden to place food on the hot springs of Tiberias, but from erev Shabbat permitted, and to leave it inside forbidden, as written siman 326 seif 3. Rambam wrote chapter 9: If one placed meat on coals — if it was roasted in it like dried figs he is liable even at half-cooking according to Raavad; and for Rambam he is not liable at half-cooking until partially cooked until all the meat is half-cooked. One who melts a type of metal or heats metals until they become coal, and likewise one who softens wax or milk or tar and pitch, or one who cooks earthenware until it becomes pottery — liable. The general principle of the matter: whether he burned a hard body or hardened a soft body — liable.
