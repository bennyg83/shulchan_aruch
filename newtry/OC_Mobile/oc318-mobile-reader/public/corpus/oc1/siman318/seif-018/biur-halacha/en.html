(א) That one finds it stirred, etc. — See Mishna Berurah that all the more so with actual stirring; and see in Acharonim siman 253 seif 4 who wrote that b'dieved if he stirred without it being cooked completely — the dish is forbidden like one who cooked on Shabbat; and it is not clear, for after the view of Ramban, Rashba, Ran, and Rosh that after it was cooked as food for a dog there is no concern of cooking or stirring even l'chatchila — and all the more no stirring concern; and although we rule in seif 4 like Rambam that without complete cooking there is concern of cooking — nevertheless b'dieved to forbid one may rely on the aforementioned Rishonim not to forbid; and even if we say that even according to them there is concern of cooking — at least rabbinically, and like some Acharonim who hold thus in seif 4 — behold Gra wrote above in seif 1 that even for rabbinic, according to Shulchan Aruch the halacha is like R' Meir that b'shogeg he may eat; and otherwise Gra's view above in seif 1 that even in Torah law we rule b'shogeg he may eat and say permitted is also shogeg; and according to his view if he stirred in the dish even without cooking as food for a dog — likewise b'shogeg he may eat; and all the more if cooked as food for a dog — certainly one should not be stringent b'dieved to forbid the dish even with the pot standing on the fire — clear as stated:

(ב) Even in a pot, etc. — See Mishna Berurah what he wrote in the name of the Acharonim — namely Taz, Acharonim, Chayei Adam, and Gra [and it is astonishing that I saw in Peri Megadim he wrote that actual stirring in fully cooked food has rabbinic prohibition; one who examines Taz will see explicitly there is no prohibition, as explained below at the end of siman 321 — only extra distance Rama wrote here; but there is no concern of prohibition; and so I saw in many Acharonim they ruled thus]; and it is implied that all types are equal to be lenient in this; and in Magen Avraham it is implied that in other types besides legumes, where one can pour easily from the pot to a bowl — he should pour and not remove with a spoon l'chatchila; but the world does not practice thus:
