(א) A dish that was cooked, etc. This is Rashi's view, who explained in the first chapter of Shabbat daf 18 what we say: they place wool in a pot near Shabbat and we are concerned lest he stir it — he explained: lest he turn the pot, and in stirring there is cooking; from this we learn there is cooking after cooking; and in the chapter "The barrel" (Shabbos daf 145) we learned: anything that entered hot water — meaning it was cooked erev Shabbat — they soak it on Shabbat in hot water so it will melt — behold there is no cooking after cooking; and one must distinguish: that case in "The barrel" deals with a dry thing, as they say there regarding a chicken — R' Abba explained that he cooked it and soaked it for days in hot water to soften by itself and ate it for healing; but in a dish with gravy there is cooking after cooking; and Rosh wrote, and it appears to me to bring proof from what we learned in the chapter "One who brings" — one brings the fire and one the wood, etc. — all are liable, even though the one who put water in was already liable for cooking — the one who stirs is also liable for cooking; behold there is cooking after cooking even if it did not cool; and one may reject: until it was not cooked as food for a dog, whoever advances its cooking is liable; and after it was cooked as food for a dog, perhaps there is no cooking after cooking; and it is reasonable that until it reaches food for a dog, whoever advances its cooking is liable; and after it was cooked as food for a dog, if it cooled and he reboiled it he is liable, as Rashi explained in the first chapter of Shabbat regarding "lest he boil" — lest he boil a pot that cooled when he wants to insulate it and boils it in fire and he ends up cooking; and that which they challenge "and we are concerned lest he stir" — this is due to dyeing; it is implied from Rosh's conclusion in this that he wrote "and it is reasonable," etc., that after it reached food for a dog there is liability only if it cooled and he reboiled it; and it is implied that in complete cooking there is no cooking after it even if it cooled; but Tur wrote in this that even while still boiling there is cooking; and if it cooled and he reboiled — even if cooked completely — and this is like Rashi's view; and why does Tur bring the distinction between gravy and a dry thing? For according to Rosh one need not this, for he holds in complete cooking there is no cooking after cooking — what is the difficulty from "anything that entered hot water," which deals with fully cooked? Rather certainly Tur holds like Rashi; and so here in Shulchan Aruch; and it appears Rosh also holds thus, and he mentioned liability when cooled only for Torah law, that whatever cooled is liable by Torah law to a chatas, even if already in complete cooking, since the liability is due to cooling — there is no distinction; and he did not mention "as food for a dog" except because of the reversal of the first section — that before reaching this there is liability even without cooling; and Tur deals with the side of prohibition, that there is a distinction between "as food for a dog" and complete cooking; and explicitly Maggid Mishneh wrote in chapter 9: and know the view of Rashba and some commentators that whatever was cooked as food for a dog has no Torah concern of cooking; nevertheless near actual fire it is forbidden; and this is learned from the law of something that contracts and is spoiled in chapter 3, end of his words; therefore Tur wrote that while still boiling, by Torah law exempt, nevertheless rabbinically forbidden since there is additional cooking; but if cooled — forbidden even in complete cooking, and then liable to a chatas; and Beit Yosef did not want to explain thus, because we would ask: if there is prohibition, there is also liability; and let us establish that Maggid Mishneh explicitly wrote as stated; and further he challenged that Rosh in his reasoning wrote "and after it reached food for a dog, even if it cooled and he reboiled he is liable, as Rashi explained regarding lest he boil" — behold he did not distinguish between fully cooked and reaching food for a dog; and whence for our teacher to distinguish between them? This too is no difficulty, for certainly the truth is thus: when cooled there is no distinction even according to Tur, since there is Torah prohibition; only while still boiling is there a distinction — when it reached food for a dog exempt but forbidden; and when fully complete — permitted even l'chatchila; and this is near the bonfire; but directly on the fire — obviously forbidden in every case, even if it contracts and is spoiled; and we reconciled this in seif 15:
