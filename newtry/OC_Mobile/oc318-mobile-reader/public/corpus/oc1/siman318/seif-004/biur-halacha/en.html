(א) There is concern of cooking, etc. — Behold this law — even though the Mechaber ruled anonymously — nevertheless there is a dispute among the Rabbis: Rambam, Rashba, and Ran hold that afterward there is no concern of cooking even if it cooled completely; but the Mechaber ruled anonymously to be stringent like Rashi, Rabbenu Yonah, Rosh, and Tur; and see siman 253 in Biur HaGra that he did not decide between the views; and all this is regarding placing it opposite the bonfire; but to place it on the fire itself or on an oven that is not cleared — according to all forbidden in any case rabbinically, even while actually boiling:

(ב) If it cooled — See Mishna Berurah that this means yad soledes bo no longer applies; and if he poured into a kli sheini — even if yad soledes bo still applied, one can say its law is like cooled and now there is concern of cooking [from Peri Megadim]:

(ג) Cooking applies to it — And even though he could eat the dish through exertion at first and he is liable for the measure like food for a dog due to cooking — as Rambam wrote chapter 9 law 5 — nevertheless one who completes its full cooking is also called cooking by Torah law; and therefore if he removed the pot from the stove on Shabbat and is unsure whether it already cooked completely — forbidden to return it even though still in his hand and the stove is cleared, as long as there is heat in the stove by which it can cook more through standing there:

(ד) Even while still boiling — This is Rambam's and Tur's view [and so it is implied in Tosafos 39 in the passage "Everything," etc.; but in Tosafos beginning of the chapter Kirah in the passage "Hot food and dish" it is implied that whatever was cooked as food for a dog — permitted to return]. And it was astonishing to me at first regarding the Mechaber's ruling that he ruled anonymously like Rambam, for many early authorities disagree — Ramban in his Chiddushim, Rabbenu Yonah, Rashba, Rosh, Ran, and Me'iri — all rule that once it was cooked as food for a dog there is no concern of cooking and permitted to return on Shabbat to a cleared oven from the coals even though it is hot and can finish cooking thereby; however afterward I found that Rambam's view is also not a lone view, for many early authorities hold like him — Or Zarua greatly tends thus there, in the melacha of baking; and he also brings thus at the end of his words in the name of Riva's commentary; and further I found many early authorities who stand in one view that until its cooking is fully complete there is concern of cooking — namely Sefer HaTerumah, Semag, Semak, and Hagahot Maimoniyot, who wrote one should be careful not to add covering after dark on a pot whose cooking is not fully complete, for thereby it hastens its cooking; and Tur copied them above siman 257 seif 4; and therefore it appears to them there is Torah prohibition, for if there were no Torah prohibition it would be completely permitted, as Beit Yosef wrote in this siman; and so it is implied from Gra's explanation who wrote the reason from "lest he stir" — behold he holds there is Torah prohibition here [but there is a difficulty on Rosh in the chapter "With what may we insulate" at the beginning, who also copied the law not to add covering even though he himself holds return is permitted once cooked as food for a dog; and one may resolve his words refer only to stoves, not to the pot itself; but on the other early authorities mentioned we cannot resolve thus, for they wrote explicitly on the pot — there]; and so according to Rashi — therefore he also holds there is concern of cooking; see Rosh chapter Kirah siman 10 and Kuntres Acharon there. The conclusion of our words: Rambam, Rashi, Terumah, Semag, Semak, Hagahot Maimoniyot, Or Zarua, Riva, and Tur — all hold there is concern of cooking; and therefore one should not depart in practice from Shulchan Aruch's ruling, especially since it is d'oraisa; and nevertheless it appears that b'dieved if a Jew returned the pot to a hot place — namely to a cleared oven [for otherwise forbidden due to chazarah according to all, as in siman 253] — one should not forbid the dish b'dieved, as Peri Megadim wrote s.k. 10 that wherever there is doubt in the dispute whether it is cooking, one does not forbid this b'dieved:

(ה) Except, etc. — See Mishna Berurah what he wrote in the name of Taz; and even though one can say Taz's intent to forbid in hot water is specifically if very salty that it cannot be eaten without rinsing in hot water, since he relied on old salted food that cannot be eaten without rinsing in hot water as explained in Rashi's words — nevertheless one should not be lenient, for from the end of his words he is supported by tuna that Beit Yosef brought, which is permitted to rinse in cold; and there it is not fit to eat even without rinsing in hot water as explained there; and nevertheless it is not permitted except in cold according to Beit Yosef's understanding of Pardes's words [and see Machatzit HaShekel who explains Taz thus]; and in truth it is very difficult to be lenient regarding salted fish in our place [called herring] — first, sometimes it is from the previous year and perhaps it is in the category of old salted food mentioned in the Mishnah; and even if known to be from this year — it is not known that it was a fish called tuna in their language, as written in Sefer Shulchan Atzei Shatim [see there that Taz was unaware of this]; and if so, even if we explain Pardes's view on tuna to be lenient to rinse even in hot water, as Acharonim explained in the name of Perishah and Tosafos Shantz and Colias HaIspanin that the Mishnah forbade rinsing in hot water — a different type of fish — nevertheless for halacha we have no power to decide thus, for many early authorities explain that Colias is tuna — first for all of Aruch under the entry Aspanin in the name of R' Yehuda; and Rashi in the chapter Kirah (Shabbos daf 39), and Ran there, and so in his Chiddushim — all wrote explicitly it is tuna [and even though Rashi himself in the chapter "The barrel" wrote it is a fish name they eat due to its salt through rinsing in hot water — implying without rinsing in hot water it would not be eaten; if so this is not tuna — for regarding dried fish they said on salted fish that they eat raw due to its salt, Rashi wrote in Berachos daf 44 that it is tuna; therefore we are compelled to say Rashi himself is doubtful here, for it is an old dispute; and when there are two views, etc.]; and Rambam chapter 6 of Masechet Machshirin explained this differently — see there; if so it appears this is an ancient dispute among the Rishonim in explaining Colias — and who can decide and permit regarding liability for a chatas? And what Pardes challenged — behold tuna is fit to eat even without rinsing in hot water, so why obligate for rinsing due to cooking? One may answer: it is like other things eaten as they are raw, and nevertheless liable for cooking them — as we will write below; and the reason is they are improved more through cooking, and Chazal established that with slight rinsing in hot water their cooking is completed and they are improved for eating like complete cooking and one is liable to a chatas; and therefore one should be very careful in this not to rinse in hot water, even through iruy from a kli sheini:

(ו) And Colias HaIspanin, etc. — And not specifically these; the same applies to everything similar — very thin and soft [Rambam chapter 9]:

(ז) And their rinsing, etc. — See Mishna Berurah that in cold it is permitted — so it is implied in Beit Yosef; and so in Acharonim in the name of Perishah and Tosafos Shantz and Sefer Ma'aseh Rokeach and other Acharonim; and I saw in Peri Megadim that he wants to innovate that a thing that cannot be eaten at all without rinsing — its liability is due to makeh b'patish; and for this reason he challenges Beit Yosef and Pardes — why permitted in cold and the difficulty remains; and behold in Levush I also saw he wrote regarding softening a hard thing that its liability is due to makeh b'patish; but in truth this matter is not clear at all; and already Acharonim caught this — in Rashi and Rambam it is explained explicitly that its liability is due to cooking; and they mean: why did they not obligate there due to makeh b'patish — or that in these matters makeh b'patish does not apply; and therefore in cold, where cooking does not apply, it is permitted; and as Rambam's and Rashi's words prove explicitly on daf 39a in the Gemara, where Rav Yosef said: a salted egg — liable to a chatas; and Mar brei d'Ravina said: we too learned: "Anything that entered," etc., except Colias HaIspanin — whose rinsing is the completion of its melacha — behold here too the liability is due to cooking; and therefore specifically in hot water; and so it is stated explicitly in Sefer Yereim for Ra'avad, and Rabbenu Yerucham, and Or Zarua, and Kol Bo, and Me'iri, and Ran — from all of them it is implied explicitly that liability is specifically in hot water and due to cooking; and also according to Levush and Peri Megadim that whatever thing whose repair for eating is completed through some action — even if we cannot obligate due to cooking we obligate due to makeh b'patish — if so, why did the Gemara say there regarding an egg roasted on a weekday and in road dust it is only a rabbinic prohibition that it is not a toldah of fire — obligate it due to makeh b'patish; and further why did they say there on daf 155, brought below siman 324, "we value foods" — obligate it due to makeh b'patish since it is the equivalent of this food? Or in food matters makeh b'patish does not apply; and behold Peri Megadim relies on Sefer Pardes and says this is his view that a thing that cannot be eaten without rinsing — its liability is due to makeh b'patish — yet Pardes himself writes that in cold it is permitted; and there is also length in explaining Pardes's words; and afterward I found in Sefer Nishmat Adam that he also wants to tend that a thing that cannot be eaten without rinsing — its liability is due to makeh b'patish; and he himself challenged what I challenged from daf 39 "we too learned," etc.; and he rejected that we require old salted food and Colias HaIspanin could be eaten at first through exertion — therefore we cannot obligate afterward due to makeh b'patish; but this is wrong, for also old salted food at first is not eaten at all, and through rinsing it is eaten through exertion, as is stated in Chiddushei Ran and so in Avodah Zarah that old salted food at first is not eaten at all; if so, just as there liability is only due to cooking and not makeh b'patish — so too for everything that cannot be eaten due to its salt or softening a hard thing — liability is also due to cooking and not makeh b'patish; and therefore specifically in hot water; and so it is implied in Biur HaGra that one reason applies to both — there. The conclusion: the matter of makeh b'patish in food is something new not found in poskim; and on the contrary there are several contradictions to this, as we wrote above; and therefore one should not be stringent with rinsing in cold in any manner, as appears to me there's help:

(ח) This is the completion of its melacha — At first glance it appears this too is specifically when yad soledes bo applies, parallel to "they rinse" at the beginning where certainly permitted even in hot water where yad soledes bo applies; and on this it says in the Mishnah: except old salted food and Colias HaIspanin — whose rinsing is the completion of their melacha — implying it deals with one manner; and Taz and Shach did not mention permission — only cold — and a reason is needed why; and requires study for practical law:

(ט) And the same applies to every hard thing — See Mishna Berurah who challenged why he needed to conclude that it is completion of melacha — see there; or one can say Hagahot Mordechai holds like Mordechai who explained in siman 82 that this "they rinse" is even in a kli rishon; and therefore forbidden — he permitted in a kli sheini even if permitted; and therefore he says regarding a hard thing whose soaking softens it to be fit to eat — forbidden to soak in every manner; and there is no difference between these two resolutions except in explaining his words; and from Biur HaGra it is implied more like the first explanation:

(י) For it is completion of melacha — See Mishna Berurah what he wrote about cooking tea; and see in Seder HaDorot what we wrote that one should put cold anise into the cup he drinks from after pouring hot water into it; and see Sefer Beit Meir who wrote that nevertheless one should not protest against others who put cold anise beforehand and afterward pour from a kli rishon even though yad soledes bo applies, since it is cooked; however every person of spirit should be concerned for himself not to pour from a kli rishon as long as yad soledes bo applies — there; and therefore advise him to put the hot water first:
