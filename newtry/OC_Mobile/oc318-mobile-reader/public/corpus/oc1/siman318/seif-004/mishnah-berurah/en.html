(ב) Cooking — and therefore forbidden from the Torah to place this dish on Shabbat in a hot place where yad soledes bo, and to mix it into boiling food in a kli sheini there is room to be lenient [later authorities]:

(ג) If it cooled — meaning yad soledes bo is not in it even though it is somewhat warm still; but if yad soledes bo is in it, even if it cooled from its boiling, cooking no longer applies, for it is included in "boiling" and there is no cooking after cooking [and specifically regarding returning it to the oven when swept of coals or they are dim; but to an oven when not swept, and all the more on the fire itself, forbidden for all to return even fully boiling, as above in 253]. All this is Mechaber's view; but Rama below in seif 15 in the gloss wrote that as long as it did not cool completely, the custom is to be lenient that there is no cooking concern — see there:

(ד) And improves for him — but if it shrinks and harms him, permitted to leave it near the fire even where yad soledes bo; see Bach who ruled like the first view that there is no distinction in this, and so implied in Biur HaGra:

(ה) Like cooked food — and the same applies more until its cooking finishes — cooking from the Torah applies:

(ו) Even while still boiling — and obvious that even through a non-Jew it is forbidden to finish the cooking; and it appears b'dieved do not forbid the dish, for one may rely on poskim who hold that since it was cooked like cooked food, there is no longer cooking concern:

(ז) And these matters, etc. — meaning what we said that when it cooled, cooking applies in every case:

(ח) Broth — for in something moist, since its heat goes and it cools, its original cooking name ceases:

(ט) But something, etc. — meaning when it was already fully cooked:

(י) That was already cooked — specifically that it was cooked; but if it was only poured erev into a kli rishon where yad soledes bo, forbidden to soak it in hot water where yad soledes bo on Shabbat, for although there is no additional cooking, nevertheless since it was not truly cooked but was in a kli rishon, a bit of cooking is added when placing it a second time in hot water. Some are lenient in the matter; Peri Megadim's view is the lenient ones refer to something whose way and nature is easy to finish cooking even in a kli rishon removed from the fire; but something whose way is not to cook fully in a kli rishon — for all forbidden; and good to be stringent in every case — see there. All this is regarding soaking in kli rishon; but to soak in kli sheini even where yad soledes bo there is room to be lenient in every case, since erev it was poured into hot water where yad soledes bo:

(כ) And it is dry — meaning he poured off the broth from it:

(ל) Permitted to soak it — even in a boiling kli rishon so it will soften there and become something moist, for there is no cooking after cooking; but do not place it in a kli rishon standing on the fire:

(מ) That in hot water — where yad soledes bo; and even if he will not leave it there until it softens, and even in kli sheini also forbidden to soak, for it appears like cooking; and it is not similar to spices in seif 9 where they forbade only in kli rishon, for spices are to sweeten the pot and do not appear like cooking:

(נ) They rinse it — meaning they pour from kli sheini upon it, for pouring from kli rishon is forbidden to pour upon it, for pouring is like kli rishon and cooks to the depth of a peel as written seif 10; and the same that permitted pouring from kli sheini onto something cold and moist [later authorities]:

(ס) Except old salted fish — salted fish of a year past, and "colias" is a fish species they eat because of its salt through rinsing in hot water; therefore even rinsing is forbidden, for this completes its melacha and is cooking; and it is stated in the Gemara that if he rinsed them in hot water he is liable to chatas. Many later authorities wrote that the nature of these things is they cannot be eaten at all without rinsing in hot water; therefore their rinsing is considered cooking that makes it food; and the same applies to other salty things like this that cannot be eaten at all without rinsing. But things that can be eaten through rinsing in cold water — no prohibition to rinse them in hot water from kli sheini; and accordingly salted fish in our place called herring that can be eaten through cold rinsing and sometimes even without rinsing — permitted to rinse even in hot water. From Taz it is implied one should be careful not to rinse it in hot water; and so wrote in Sefer Shulchan Atzei Shittim and Chayei Adam; and so correct to practice; see Biur Halacha:

(ע) And their rinsing, etc. — meaning in hot water; but in cold permitted to rinse and even to soak; and even in something that through soaking in cold becomes fit to eat — everything not food is permitted to make food on Shabbat as written siman 324:

(פ) And the same applies to every hard thing — meaning something fit to eat raw without cooking but dry and very hard until not fit to eat at all because of this without soaking in hot water — forbidden to soak and it appears why Rama needed to conclude this is completing melacha; let it be derived that every dry thing not cooked from erev is forbidden to soak in hot water on Shabbat, as above. One may say he wants to inform us that in this, which is completing its melacha, there is d'oraisa prohibition in soaking like rinsing old salted fish; see Biur Halacha:

(צ) That forbidden to soak it — even in kli sheini; and the same if it will become fit to eat through rinsing in hot water alone — rinsing is also forbidden, as above regarding salted fish. Now we will explain the law of cooking tea leaves relevant in several matters in this seif. Tea on Shabbat — obvious in poskim that there is cooking concern; in mezid there is stoning prohibition, and in shogeg chatas liability; therefore one must be very careful, and unfortunately many stumble on leniencies without substance; therefore I must explain modes of permission and prohibition there's help. Pouring from kli rishon onto tea leaves — certainly primary melacha concern, for we rule pouring cooks to the depth of a peel as below seif 10; and all the more if he then stands it on the oven or in coals until yad soledes bo — certainly all will come to stoning prohibition thereby. Even if he wishes to place the tea leaves into the vessel after pouring the hot water so it will be kli sheini — also forbidden as we rule in this seif that something not in hot water before Shabbat may not be soaked on Shabbat even in kli sheini; and all the more according to what is explained in this seif that there are soft things easy to cook even through rinsing from kli sheini — possible there is d'oraisa prohibition concern in submerging even this way; therefore later authorities agreed one should pour erev boiling from kli rishon so through this the tea will be called partially cooked, for pouring cooks to the depth of a peel and at the time of pouring will turn the tea well inside the boiling from top to bottom [better that it be truly cooked through standing in a cooking place]. Then pour the infusion into another vessel so the tea leaves remain dry and it will be permitted to pour hot water from kli rishon on them on Shabbat since they were already cooked erev, as we rule in this seif for dry things there is no cooking after cooking even if cooled [and afterward permitted to return also this cold infusion water into this vessel itself, as we wrote above that something moist that cooled may be placed in boiling kli sheini], for if the infusion liquid remains, we rule that in moist things there is cooking after cooking if cooled, unless the cold infusion waters are abundant so they will not heat through the water poured tomorrow. The same law for coffee — be careful not to pour hot water from kli rishon unless he poured yesterday and poured off the water from upon them, for the reason as all above [see responsa Chatam Sofer siman 74 and Pithei Teshuvah on Orach Chayim]. Know that even though we permitted pouring hot water on tea leaves and coffee if he placed water from kli rishon yesterday — nevertheless be careful not to stand the tea and coffee on the oven or in coals after pouring water, for pouring only cooks to the depth of a peel and therefore it was not fully cooked yesterday but only to a peel, and now through standing in the oven it will cook fully. This mode we explained — even though one should not protest those who practice it — nevertheless later authorities wrote better advice: prepare the infusion completely erev so he need not pour more boiling on Shabbat; tomorrow when he needs to drink he places cold infusion into the cup he drinks from after they poured hot water into it and it becomes kli sheini; and the same permitted to place in this kli sheini cup milk that cooled; but forbidden to pour from kli rishon on them, as below in this seif.
