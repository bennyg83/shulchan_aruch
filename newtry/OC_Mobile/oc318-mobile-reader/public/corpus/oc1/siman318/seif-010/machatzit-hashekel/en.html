(א) (s.k. 10) To insulate, etc. And there is difficulty why Tur omitted it, for we rule the halacha is like Rabah [Bach]. Know that we learned in Shabbat 42b: one may not crack an egg in a cloth heated by the sun so it will roast — for we decree toldos chamah due to toldos ha'or; and R' Yosi permits, for he holds we do not decree, for it is not exchanged. And we learned at the end: one may not insulate in sand, in road dust — in this R' Yosi does not disagree, it appears he concedes to the rabbis. And the Gemara asked: what is R' Yosi's reason that he concedes? And Rabah said: the reason is sand and road dust change in rainwater and Rabah also decreed on R' Yosi lest he insulate in rainwater; unlike a cloth, which is not similar to rainwater and is not exchanged. And R' Yosef said: the reason he concedes is the decree lest one move dust from its place. And it asked: what is the difference between Rabah and R' Yosef? And it concludes: in loose dust one can decree lest he insulate, but lest he move — no. And it raised a refutation: one may roll an egg on a boiling roof — granted for the one who says lest he insulate, fine — for why did he roll on the roof? Since there is no hatmanah, there is no decree lest he insulate in rainwater; but for the one who says lest he move — let us decree? And it answers: an ordinary roof has no dust and is not needed. And Rosh wrote, and this is his language: for Rabah who said due to the decree lest he insulate in rainwater — forbidden even from erev Yom Tov for Shabbat need, as proven later, etc. Afterward Rosh brought the Gemara's difficulty on R' Yosef from the baraita of rolling on a boiling roof, and wrote, and this is his language: from that it challenges from this baraita "one may roll" from the Mishnah, etc. — it appears there is no distinction between rolling on sand and insulating in sand, and even to roll on sand, etc. — forbidden, end of Rosh's words. From now Bach's words are difficult, and Magen Avraham understood that Bach's difficulty was not that Tur should have brought Rosh's first law that even from erev Yom Tov it is forbidden — therefore Magen Avraham hinted at the beginning of his words "even from erev Yom Tov," see siman 257 seif 3 — namely there this law is brought, that one may not insulate in something that adds steam even from erev Yom Tov, and in seif 3 counted things that add steam and considers sand with them, wet and dry (and see what is written there) — behold it is clear to him that forbidden to insulate in sand even from erev Yom Tov. And therefore Bach's difficulty was only to bring Rosh's second law that even to roll on sand is forbidden. And according to this his words are astounding — what is Bach's difficulty? Behold we rule like Rabah; on the contrary, this supports it — that for this reason Tur did not bring it, for Rosh so too for R' Yosef that the reason is lest he move; but for us who rule like Rabah that the reason is lest he insulate — on a roof permitted, as the questioner said "granted for Rabah" fine that there is no need on the roof lest he insulate; therefore Magen Avraham understood Bach holds like Rashal in responsum 61 who brought Rosh's words and challenged: what difference? Rosh wrote that for R' Yosi even on a roof forbidden — behold we rule like Rabah. And he answered: for Rosh there was a difficulty from Tosafos' question there, that the Gemara asked what difference between them — a roof that has dust: for Rabah permitted and for R' Yosi forbidden — perforce the Gemara knew Rabah also said lest he move, except he added the reason lest he insulate; therefore a roof without dust — even for Rabah forbidden. And what the Gemara said "granted for the one who says lest he insulate" fine — even though he also holds lest he move, the Gemara's intent is for the reason lest he insulate fine; if so, why did Rosh say that for the reason lest he move even on a roof forbidden — there is a difference also for us who rule like Rabah, for Rabah concedes he said lest he move — and so Bach holds. If so, Bach's difficulty on Tur is correct that he should have brought that even on a roof forbidden, even though we rule like Rabah — behold Rabah concedes as stated. And what Bach wrote "behold we rule like Rabah" — still needs explanation; on the contrary, if we ruled like R' Yosef it would be more difficult that Tur should have brought that even on a roof forbidden. For this one can say Bach's intent is like Rashal: behold we rule like Rabah, and if so for what purpose did Rosh write this law? Even so Rosh holds Rabah also said lest he move, and for Rabah forbidden on a roof, and Tur should have brought it even though we rule like Rabah. And with this Bach's words are reconciled. And what he wrote — and it appears to me Rosh wrote according to R' Yosef, etc. — perforce it must be as Rashal wrote that Rabah said lest he move; nevertheless for us Rosh did not need to bring that for us it is obvious that even on a roof forbidden, for perforce Rabah and R' Yosi did not need their reasons except according to R' Yosi who does not decree toldos chamah due to toldos ha'or; but for us who rule like the rabbis who decree toldos chamah due to toldos ha'or — in any case forbidden to insulate in sand, for we decree toldos chamah due to toldos ha'or; and for this reason it is obvious that even on a roof forbidden, like we forbid cracking on a heated cloth where there is no hatmanah. And if so, even according to Rashal's words Rosh did not write his words for halachic ruling but to clarify the sugya; and from now Rashal's words are unnecessary that Rosh holds Rabah also said lest he move — in any case Tur did not need to bring it, for although forbidden for us who rule like the rabbis, it is an obvious matter:

(ב) Nevertheless Rashal wrote, etc., that he wrote it is forced that the entire sugya and Rosh's words are stated according to R' Yosef whom we do not rule like; rather perforce in sand, road dust, and roof the rabbis agree there is no toldos ha'or, and also for the rabbis Rabah and R' Yosef's reasons were needed; and according to this Machatzit HaShekel's explanation goes: still, even though we rule like the rabbis, Tur should have brought that on a roof too is forbidden, for it is not an obvious matter even for the rabbis, for in such a case there is no toldos ha'or:
