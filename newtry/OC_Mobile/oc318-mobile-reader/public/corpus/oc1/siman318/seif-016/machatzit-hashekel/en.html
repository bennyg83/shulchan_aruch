(s.k. 16) Thus he challenged, etc. — at first glance it is difficult: what did Magen Avraham add? Behold Mechaber so stated explicitly except for old salted food; and it is an explicit Mishnah. Rather it must be Machatzit HaShekel came to explain and be lenient: what Mechaber forbade — old salted food — its law is like a hard item that Rama forbade, that it is forbidden only when it is not fit to eat at all without warming; likewise old salted food that Mechaber forbids — only when not fit to eat without warming. And Machatzit HaShekel learned this from Beit Yosef's words on the Perush that brought old salted food and colias of Aspamia are tarit, and Sefer HaPerush wrote thus: old salted food means not eaten unless rinsed in hot water — therefore rinsing is gmar melachto; but tarit, since sailors eat it raw due to its salt, if so rinsing is not gmar melachto since it can be eaten without rinsing — therefore permitted to rinse tarit in cold water on Shabbat, similar to washing cups and bowls — so are Perush's words. And Beit Yosef wrote on this: his words are confused — for if so, even in hot water permitted to rinse tarit; why was only cold water permitted? end of Beit Yosef. And Machatzit HaShekel holds for the law Beit Yosef rules like his challenge on Perush, that even in hot water permitted to rinse tarit; and what Perush said cold water is without precision, as Bach wrote that his words are confused; and if so, perforce what he forbade as old salted food means where it is impossible to eat at all without rinsing; and so in Turei Zahav. And according to this, permitted to rinse herring even in hot water, since it can be eaten without rinsing; but Taz in s.k. 5 did not write to permit herring but only rinsing in cold water — it appears he holds Beit Yosef challenged Perush that it should have permitted even in hot water; nevertheless for the law we rule like Perush, specifically cold water and not hot:
