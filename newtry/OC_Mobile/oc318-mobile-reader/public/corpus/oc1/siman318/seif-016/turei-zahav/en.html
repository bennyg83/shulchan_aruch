(א) In a place where there is yad soledes bo. Beit Yosef brought that Mordechai wrote like Terumat HaDeshen — namely those who are stringent here; and Rabbenu Yechiel of Paris brought proof to permit from the chapter Kirah: a woman smeared her hand and warmed opposite the bonfire and smeared white of egg — olive oil is hard — and nevertheless permitted; and nevertheless good to be careful not to place so close to fire but far so there will not be yad soledes bo, end of his words. And Beit Yosef wrote: it appears that by Torah law it is forbidden only where there is yad soledes bo, and there is a textual error [in Beit Yosef it should read as here]; and further it is implied from Rabbenu Yechiel that permitted to warm olive oil even where there is yad soledes bo — and we concluded in the chapter Kirah that oil has concern of cooking and warming it is its cooking, end of his words; and he was not precise in these words, for what he first wrote that it appears from Mordechai's words that by Torah law forbidden specifically with yad soledes bo — Mordechai holds according to Beit Yosef and there is a textual error that by Torah law stringent even without yad soledes bo due to nolad; only Mordechai's conclusion itself is to distinguish for practical law l'chatchila to be stringent with yad soledes bo; and what he further wrote that in the chapter Kirah they said oil has concern of cooking, etc. — I wondered at this great man who saw the tip and not the whole, for otherwise there is a difficulty from what Rabbenu Gamliel said: a woman smeared her hand, etc. — behold there is concern of cooking, for she at least warms her hand; and even from warming — behold even in warming there is cooking; rather plainly since it was through change it is permitted and forbidden only when the oil is distinct by itself, as Rashi wrote there that it is permitted due to change; and so too here with fat on a pasty — permitted to warm even where there is yad soledes bo; what is written about pasty is a measure regarding oil, and "place where there is yad soledes bo" deals at least where it cannot cook there, for otherwise it would be forbidden due to cooking, not due to nolad, as is written in seif 14 in a place where it can cook — forbidden; and he did not mention there "where there is yad soledes bo," for certainly there is a place with yad soledes bo where it still cannot cook completely; and the same with oil — we do not find permission even through change except where it cannot come to complete cooking; therefore they did not forbid by Torah law here except due to nolad; therefore Rabbenu Yechiel brought proof well regarding nolad that it is permitted here like oil where there was change, as stated; and therefore he wrote here to permit even with yad soledes bo, for he retracted from what he wrote in Beit Yosef to dispute Mordechai; but according to those who are lenient — Rabbenu Yechiel, Ramban, and Shulchan Aruch — who disagree with the baraita, permitted even with yad soledes bo; and in Levush he wrote: it appears to me there is an error in Shulchan Aruch and it should read "in a place where there is no yad soledes bo" — he follows Beit Yosef's challenge that we mentioned; but the truth is Beit Yosef retracted in Shulchan Aruch and there is no error in Shulchan Aruch:

(ב) And even though the fat, etc. This is Semag's view, that we do not forbid due to nolad and it is comparable to not crushing snow or hail, as is written siman 320 seif 11 — here he does nothing with his hands, rather it melts by itself; and regarding hail too we learned: but he may put into a cup and need not be concerned even though it melts, since it is by itself; and accordingly permitted also in heat; and Ramban is even more lenient and holds that fat, since it is always edible, even to crush and squeeze is permitted; and those who are stringent — Terumat HaDeshen and Ran hold: as long as it did not harden it flowed and was clear and its way is thus; even when hardened it is like fruits standing for liquids; and accordingly forbidden also in heat; and Beit Yosef brought in the name of Terumat HaDeshen that if he poured out the water and the fat in it — permitted to warm the pasty on Shabbat; and wrote on this: and it appears from here that even for one who is stringent it is permitted to warm fatty meat slices on Shabbat even though part flows — since a small amount that separates is not significant and permitted, end of his words:
