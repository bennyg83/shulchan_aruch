(א) In a place where there was yad soledes bo. Levush wrote there is a textual error and it must read in a place where there was no yad soledes bo, for in a place where there was yad soledes bo it is forbidden due to cooking, as Beit Yosef wrote regarding oil. And it is not necessary, for here, since it was already cooked and is dry, there is no issue of cooking, as written seif 15 and see siman 253 seif 5, where it is stated explicitly thus; and so in Mordechai, that for those who permit it is permitted even in a place where there was yad soledes bo. But he wrote it is good to be careful not to place in a place where there was yad soledes bo, and this refers to pastida — not as Beit Yosef understood, that it refers to oil, and he challenged him; and so too Kolbo explicitly in name of R' Meir — see there; and so proven in Gemara and Rashi who wrote daf 145: they soak it in hot water so it will melt — it appears that even after it melts it has the law of a dry item, and likewise fat; except Mordechai and Kolbo were concerned for Rabbenu Peretz's words, and therefore wrote not to place in a place where there was yad soledes bo so it not melt so much that it flows out, but place from afar; and even if it melts inside it does not need it, as Beit Yosef wrote; and so in Sefer Mechaneh — see there; and so implied in Hagahot Semak, that roasting and pastida are the same:

(ב) And all the more so a pot that has, etc. — permitted. But nevertheless forbidden due to cooking for those who forbid, as written seif 15:

(ג) And some are stringent. Because the fat floats above and is b'ein and is nolad — similar to liquids that flowed out; unlike snow, which is permitted, as written siman 320 seif 9, that when it melted in water it is not recognizable. And it appears that even b'dieved forbidden to eat the fat, for it is nolad; nevertheless the pastida itself is permitted, for in the fat absorbed in it there is no prohibition, as it is like a piece of meat that is permitted to warm, as Beit Yosef wrote. And it is possible that since the fat b'ein became forbidden, it returns and is absorbed in the pastida and becomes forbidden, as written Yoreh De'ah siman 113 seif 3; nevertheless one may be lenient b'dieved, as Rama wrote. And it appears to me permitted to place the pastida before the oven was lit, as written siman 253, that even one who places a pot before it was lit is exempt but forbidden; and all the more so here, where some permit placing even after it was lit — at least one may permit before it was lit. Nevertheless one should not be lenient before an am ha'aretz, and one should do it through a non-Jew. Rambam wrote chapter 9: One places the fire, and one the wood, and one the pot, and one the water, and one the meat, and one stirs — all are liable due to mevashel, for whoever does something needed for cooking — behold he cooks. But if one placed the pot, and one the water, and one the fire, and one the wood, and one stirred — the last two are liable (but the one who places the fire is not liable, since the fire would go out and the food would not cook if not for the second placing the wood). But at the beginning it deals when they placed both fire and wood together [so is in name of R' Yochanan bar Rav]. And it must be said it deals when he did not kindle the fire but brought it from elsewhere and placed it here — for if he kindled it he is liable due to meva'ir. And Rashi explained that when he carries a coal from place to place it reignites from the wind of his walking and he is liable due to meva'ir — see R' Levi Chaviv siman 21. And Maggid Mishneh wrote: some say liability for stirring is only at the first stirring, but after stirring once, that without stirring it cooks — exempt; and so Ramban and Rashba, of blessed memory [and so too Maharam of Padua in his glosses]. And certainly according to this view, wherever there is no liability in its cooking, such as in something already cooked — there is no liability in stirring it, end of his words.
