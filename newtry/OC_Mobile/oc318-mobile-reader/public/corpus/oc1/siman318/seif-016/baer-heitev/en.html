(א) That yad soledes bo. Levush wrote there is a textual error and it should read: in a place where there is no yad soledes bo; for where there is yad soledes bo it is forbidden due to cooking; and it appears to me not so, for here since it already cooked and is dry there is no concern of cooking, as is written in seif 15; and see siman 253 seif 5, where it is stated explicitly thus — Taz and Magen Avraham:

(ב) And it melts. For he does nothing with his hands; rather it melts by itself:

(ג) That it is permitted. Nevertheless forbidden due to cooking for those who forbid, as is written in seif 15:

(ד) And some are stringent. Because the fat floats above and is distinct, and it is nolad — comparable to liquids that flowed; unlike snow, which is permitted, as is written siman 320 seif 9, for when it melts in water it is not recognizable; and it is implied that even b'dieved it is forbidden to eat the fat, for it is nolad; and nevertheless the pasty itself is permitted, for in fat absorbed in it there is no prohibition, as Rabbenu Yosef wrote and as I will write nearby; and it is possible that since the fat in its distinct form became forbidden, it returns and is absorbed in the pasty and the pasty becomes forbidden, as is written Yoreh De'ah siman 113 seif 3; and nevertheless there is room to be lenient b'dieved, as Rama wrote; and it appears to me permitted to place the pasty before the oven was lit, as is written at the end of siman 253 that even one who places a pot before it was lit is exempt — but forbidden; and all the more so here, where some permit placing even after it was lit; at minimum one may permit before it was lit; and nevertheless one should not be lenient before an am ha'aretz, and one should do it through a non-Jew. Beit Yosef wrote: it appears that even for one who is stringent it is permitted to warm on Shabbat fatty meat slices even though part of it flows, since it is a small amount that separates — it is not significant and permitted according to all, end of his words; and Or Zarua wrote: permitted to place a cup of wine that has ice on the oven to dissolve the ice. Rambam chapter 9: if one placed the fire, and one the wood, and one the pot, and one the water, and one the meat, and one stirs — all are liable due to cooking, for whoever does something needed for cooking is cooking; but if one placed the pot, and one the water, and one the fire, and one the wood, and one stirs — the last two are liable. But one who places the fire is not liable, since the fire would go out and the dish would not cook if the second had not placed the wood; but in the first case it deals when both brought fire and wood together, as is written in the name of R' Yosef Bei Rav. And one must say it deals when he did not kindle the fire but brought it from elsewhere and placed it there, for if he kindled he is liable due to kindling; and Rashi explained that when one carries a coal from place to place it ignites from the wind of his walking and he is liable due to kindling; and see R' Levi ben Chaviv siman 21. Maggid Mishneh wrote: and some say there is liability for stirring only the first stirring; but after he stirred once, without stirring it cooks and he is exempt — and so Ramban and Rashba. And certainly according to this view, anything that has no cooking liability — such as fully cooked — has no stirring liability, end of his words.
