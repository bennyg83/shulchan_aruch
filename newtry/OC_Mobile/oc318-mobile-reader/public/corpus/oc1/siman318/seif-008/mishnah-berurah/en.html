(א) To leave a cold thing — this seif and seif 17 are one matter; and these two views in this seif are themselves those in seif 17, except there it deals with something moist where if it cooled there is cooking concern, therefore it wrote a vessel with something hot inside; here it deals with something dry where even if cooled there is no more cooking concern, as above in this seif:
(ב) And everything permitted to leave, etc. — meaning everything explained in seif 4 that has no cooking concern, such as in dry even if cooled and permitted to soak in kli rishon — likewise permitted to leave opposite the fire somewhat distant, for we do not fear stoking, as explained in seif 15:
(ג) A kettle on the fire — and even though even on a swept stove from the fire it is not permitted in siman 253 except returning; but to leave l'chatchila forbidden as explained there seif 5 in the gloss — if so here, even though the lower kettle interposes between the vessel and the fire, what of it? One must say this view holds interposition of the kettle even when not swept is better than a swept stove, for there the pot stands on a stove which is a place where cooking is always done and appears like cooking; but here it stands on a pot where cooking is not customary, therefore permitted even to leave l'chatchila:
(ד) And the first view is primary — and so ruled Avnei Nezer and Gra; and it applies also to what is in seif 17 that it is one matter, as we wrote above:
(ה) Gravy — for since there is gravy in it, Mechaber already decided above in this seif that when cooled there is cooking after cooking; automatically forbidden to place on a hot kettle even if not on the fire, as above in seif 6:
(ו) And shrinks and improves for him — know that all the language of this seif is from Rabbeinu Yerucham and follows his view mentioned above in this seif in the gloss that specifically when it shrinks and improves is there cooking after cooking; and for practical law we already wrote above in this seif halacha is like other poskim who do not distinguish in this; and even Mechaber possibly holds thus as he stipulated there, only he uses Rabbeinu Yerucham's language:
