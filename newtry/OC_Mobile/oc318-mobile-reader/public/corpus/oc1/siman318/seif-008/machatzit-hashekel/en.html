See in Tosafos Bava Kamma, etc., regarding what grew — the modest ones who had vineyards that were fourth-year vineyard forbidden to eat outside Jerusalem without redemption would know there are people who are not scrupulous and would eat from the modest ones' vineyards by theft; and to save them from theft they forgave this — but to save them also from eating fourth-year vineyard without redemption they would place money and say whatever is gathered from this shall be redeemed on this money. And Tosafos challenged: what did their enactment help — for after redemption the fruit again grew and this is impossible for them to redeem, for it is davar she'bilti mitoch? (ב) And see siman 320. He wanted to reconcile Tosafos' difficulty there, who still challenged: what did it help — for what grew afterward is not nullified, for it is davar sheyeish lo matirim through redemption after it grew. And one can explain: in any case it saved them from a Torah prohibition — namely it is not nullified, this is d'rabbanan, for by Torah law even davar sheyeish lo matirim is nullified; and for this Magen Avraham hinted to see siman 320, and Tosafos' difficulty is resolved — for we rule there that if the prohibition was never recognizable b'ein, even though it is davar sheyeish lo matirim, nevertheless it is nullified. And if so, there is no longer Tosafos' difficulty that what grew after redemption never stood b'ein — according to this, Rama's ruling that fruit from machubar is muktzeh because it grew on Shabbat — one can say it is nullified in the majority, and even though it is davar sheyeish lo matirim, since the prohibition never stood b'ein we rule even davar sheyeish lo matirim nevertheless it is nullified:

(ג) And in Beitza, etc., it is stated there that two who bought an animal in partnership from erev Yom Tov — one made an eruv to the east and one to the west; and we establish that an animal, utensils, and food are like the feet of the owners, and another person may not carry them more than the owners may walk. And the partners divided the animal on Yom Tov. Rav said: each of the partners cannot carry his portion except to a place where his fellow partner may also go, because they suckle each other's techumim — namely, when the animal was alive each partner's portion would suckle from his fellow partner's portion, and it is as if mixed in his portion and his fellow's portion. R' Kahana and R' Assi said to Rav: you were concerned for techumim prohibition and not for muktzeh prohibition. And Tosafos explained: according to you who hold like R' Yehuda in muktzeh — if so, how is it possible to slaughter the animal? Behold the animal's portions suckle and it grew also on Yom Tov and it is muktzeh. And Rav was silent.
