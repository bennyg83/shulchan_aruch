One must wait the time needed to perform the labor only when a non-Jew performed it for a Jew, because the matter appears lenient to him and he might arrange for it again. When a Jew performs an act directly, there is certainly no concern that he will openly cook again. Beis Yosef; Taz, paragraph 1; Magen Avraham there. If a non-Jew heated water on Shabbos and it subsequently cooled, so that one derives no benefit from the Shabbos labor, one can say that after Shabbos no waiting period is required. Even according to the reason that he might tell the non-Jew to do it, he is now drinking cold water. That same day, however, all opinions forbid it because a prohibition was performed. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 2. Nevertheless, in a place where people drink well water only after boiling it to prevent harm and then cooling it, one must wait after Shabbos the time required to perform the labor, because the non-Jew's act provided benefit.
