For we do not require "until it is done" except for melacha done by a gentile for an Israelite, because it is light in his eyes and he will come to do so another time; but a thing an Israelite does with his hands — certainly no concern he will come to cook openly. Beit Yosef; Taz s.k. 1; Magen Avraham there; however if a gentile cooked water on Shabbat and it cooled — he did not benefit at all from Shabbat melacha — one may say he need not wait until it is done, for even per the reason lest he tell him — behold he drinks cold; but on that day forbidden for all, for a prohibition was done (R' Akiva Eiger or 2); nevertheless it appears where they drink well water and heat it so it not harm and afterward drink cold — he must wait at Motzei Shabbos until it is done, for they benefited from the gentile's act:
