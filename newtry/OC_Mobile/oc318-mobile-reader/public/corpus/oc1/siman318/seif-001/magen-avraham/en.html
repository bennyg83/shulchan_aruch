(ב) That from erev Shabbat immediately. For specifically when a non-Jew does it we require bekedei sheye'aseh, for we are concerned lest he tell him to do it; but an Israelite will not listen to him even according to Rashi who wrote the reason is so he not benefit from Shabbat labor, as written siman 415 — nevertheless here it is permitted, for in an uncommon matter they did not decree [Tosafos Chullin daf 95] — not like Bach: if it was from erev Yom Tov it is forbidden, for Shabbat does not prepare for Yom Tov [Lekutei Mahariv siman 2]. And see Yoreh De'ah siman 102 that if that item became mixed with others it is nullified and is not davar sheyeish lo matirim, similar to terumah — see there; and there is difficulty, for our case is not comparable to the proof of terumah, for terumah is not with any person davar sheyeish lo matirim — for if for a kohen even today it is permitted, and if for an Israelite forever forbidden; but here, since the designation davar sheyeish lo matirim fell upon it for others, even for himself it is not nullified, as is stated chapter 3 of Bikkurim that if bikkurim became mixed they are forbidden even to zarim, even though for them it would be davar she'ein lo matirim, since for kohanim it is davar sheyeish lo matirim that they can eat in Jerusalem — even for zarim forbidden; and so Raavad and Rambam in explanation of the Mishnah and in his work chapter 4 of Bikkurim. However Rash wrote it is not davar sheyeish lo matirim since forbidden to zarim; nevertheless one should be stringent. And what is written Yoreh De'ah siman 102 — perhaps it deals when it became mixed after Shabbat, for then it is truly similar to terumah, and so is essential. And even though in Rambam's explanation it appears specifically when mixed for a kohen then forbidden even to an Israelite — nevertheless in his work it does not appear so, and also his words in the explanation can be explained thus. It appears to me that for one for whom it was cooked, its law is also like himself, as written Yoreh De'ah siman 99 seif 5 — for likewise the reason is due to penalty; however from Beit Yosef's words there it appears specifically there we are concerned lest he tell a non-Jew to nullify it, but here in any case he must wait bekedei sheye'aseh when a non-Jew cooked it, and for an Israelite we are not concerned "a person does not sin unless it benefits him" — see siman 253 seif 1:
