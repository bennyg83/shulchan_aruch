[1] It is prohibited for him, etc. Even the pot itself is prohibited (Rashba, siman 175). One for whom the food was cooked has the same status as another person if the food does not belong to him, because we do not fear he will tell another Jew to cook for him: a person does not sin for someone else's benefit (Magen Avraham).
[2] [Levush] From the Torah, etc. That is Rabbi Yochanan HaSandlar's reason. But since we remain uncertain whether the law follows Rabbi Yehudah, it appears that even deliberate cooking is prohibited only rabbinically (Malbushei Yom Tov). Rosh in chapter Merubah and Ran in chapter Kirah make clear that even according to Rabbi Yochanan HaSandlar the prohibition is rabbinic, since Rav Acha and Ravina dispute its details according to his position, and the halachah follows the lenient one.
[3] [Levush] And according to what I wrote, it is permitted, etc. If the following day is Yom Tov, it is prohibited because Shabbos may not prepare for Yom Tov (Mahari Weil, siman 2). If the prohibited item became mixed into permitted food on Shabbos, for other people it is a davar sheyesh lo matirin and is not nullified. According to what I wrote, however, it is nullified and permitted even to him (Magen Avraham). See my work on Yoreh Deah at the end of siman 102 and seif katan 11 there, where I reconcile Levush's statements there with those here.
[4] [Levush] For they did not say, etc. This requires study, because Tosafot, Rosh, and Ran imply that even regarding a rabbinic prohibition we do not presume a Jew will violate it, and the waiting period of bichdei sheya'asu is required only when the act was done by a non-Jew. Perhaps Levush independently decided otherwise because Beit Yosef cites many explanations that always require waiting long enough for the act to be done after Shabbos, so that one not benefit from work done on Shabbos. Yam Shel Shlomo, Chullin 1:25, Bedek HaBayit of Ra'ah, p. 18, Riaz in Shiltei Gibborim on chapter Kirah, and Bach likewise imply this. Magen Avraham objects from Tosafot in Chullin that the Sages did not decree concerning an uncommon occurrence. There is no difficulty, because Chiddushei Rashba there makes clear that Rashi does not accept that principle, and Yam Shel Shlomo, chapter Elu Tereifot, siman 5, writes that even when uncommon there remains degradation in benefiting from melachah performed on Shabbos.
[5] And inadvertently, etc. Forgetfulness is considered inadvertent (Maharam Mintz, siman 28). Acting according to the ruling of a scholar is likewise considered inadvertent (Radach). Coercion or unavoidable circumstances should not automatically be equated with inadvertence (Binyamin Ze'ev, siman 130).
