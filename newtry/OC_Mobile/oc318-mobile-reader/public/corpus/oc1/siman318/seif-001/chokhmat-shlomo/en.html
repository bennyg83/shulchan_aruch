Seif 1, one who cooks on Shabbos. It appears: behold the Rif wrote that since in the dispute of R' Eliezer and Ravina the law follows the lenient view, automatically the law is not like R' Yochanan the sandal-maker; and Rosh in chapter 7 of Bava Kamma in the sugya of slaughtering via another wondered at him — what does this matter have to do with that? And behold the pious gaon R' Eliyahu of Vilna may he rest in Eden resolves the words of the Rif that his intent is: for what reason is it fit to say the law is like R' Yochanan the sandal-maker — that is, by force that the rabbis hold like him as the Talmud establishes there; and since we rule like the lenient view that is only d'rabbanan — if so it must be resolved in this baraita otherwise; if so there is no proof that the rabbis hold thus; and since the rabbis do not hold thus — automatically the law is not like the words of R' Yochanan the sandal-maker, at present. And after forgiveness his words are astounding, for per the view of the Rif it is impossible to explain thus, for behold the Rif cited this establishment of slaughtering via another on the mishnah of one who stole and slaughtered on Yom Kippur — that deals with slaughtering via another; if so it is difficult — if so one who stole and slaughtered on Shabbos too; and why does it teach in the conclusion that he is exempt? And it must be as Rosh resolves that the mishnah comes like R' Yochanan the sandal-maker; if so it is difficult: if the mishnah comes for us like R' Yochanan the sandal-maker, it should rule like R' Yochanan the sandal-maker by force that the rabbis hold like him — all the more so it should be law like him on account that an unattributed mishnah is like him, and the law follows an unattributed mishnah; and it must be that one does not lean on the words of the Rif except as Rosh said, that therefore in the conclusion he is exempt like R' Yochanan the sandal-maker. And that which was asked: since the Rif and Rosh ruled that it is d'rabbanan, still even for R' Yochanan the sandal-maker slaughter is fit, as explained in the Talmud — this is a difficulty in its own right on the Rif and Rosh, and in my novellae I expanded on this. However in any case the Rif and Rosh must hold that the mishnah is like R' Yochanan the sandal-maker, as Rosh explains explicitly; if so Rosh well wondered that it is impossible to explain the intent of the Rif like the explanation of the gaon cited may he rest in Eden, for if so still the mishnah would be like R' Yochanan the sandal-maker and the law would be like him; and it must be that this is not the intent of the Rif. And in the body of the words of the Rif see in my novellae there at length, and investigate. And behold regarding the law whether chullin is forbidden on Shabbos like in eating — see in Maggid Mishneh what he wrote on this, and the view of Chacham Tzvi is the reverse, and we already wrote of this in several places; and now it appears to me support to say that chullin is permitted on Shabbos, and it is from what we learn in the first chapter of Berachos daf 11a: R' Eliezer says — in the evening you slaughter and at sunset you eat, the season of your going out from Egypt you burn; R' Akiva says, etc., and when do you eat until the season of your going out, etc. And behold the matter is astounding — how did he first say the season of your going out from Egypt you burn, behold one does not burn kodashim on Yom Tov; and Rashi already noted this and strained to explain that the intent is that the time of burning arrived, but he does not burn it until Motzei Yom Tov — and this is strained, for the language of the Talmud "you burn" implies actual burning; therefore it appears properly that behold the obligation of the mitzvah of burning notar is with a k'zayit, similar to its eating, as we say in the first chapter of Beitzah regarding chametz that we derive removal from eating; and for Beit Shammai who do not derive removal from eating, the measure of removal is more than a k'zayit, and so is proven in chapter 3 of Pesachim regarding sacred meat "until how much does it return," it appears from there that the measure from the Torah for the obligation of burning is [for less than the measure one is not obligated to burn it (in a positive commandment there is no half-measure, as I wrote in Sefer Or Chadash to be uncertain thus, and it would be proven from this that in a positive commandment half-measure does not apply, as I wrote in my composition Sefer HaChayyim in its reason, see there, and investigate)]. Accordingly, if he has notar of the measure he is obligated to burn, certainly forbidden to burn on Yom Tov, for either way to burn together is fixing, for behold he performs a mitzvah; and to burn less less than the measure — either way if it is not a mitzvah, behold he nullifies the mitzvah of burning that requires a measure, even if only less than the measure remained; if so, since there is no obligation in the mitzvah of burning, he can burn on Yom Tov, for behold burning is in its measure in any case, nevertheless he spoils and is exempt, only that for the need of an important mitzvah it is fixing; therefore one does not burn kodashim on Yom Tov. If so it works where it is a mitzvah; but where it is not a mitzvah, again it is spoiling and permitted to burn. Therefore he well said at sunset you burn — that is, you are permitted to burn it — that is, less than the measure, such as if only less than the measure remained; therefore he did not write burning explicitly, for then it would imply he is obligated to burn, and if he were obligated again it would be forbidden to burn, for it is fixing and he is liable for kindling even in any case; therefore burning was not written explicitly to hint it is not an obligation, only permission. And with this I understood the reason for their dispute, for one who examines in the Talmud Shabbos daf 107a — for R' Yehuda who is liable for melacha she'einah tzricha legufah exempts for spoiling in burning, and he is only liable in fixing or for the need of a mitzvah it is fixing, as Tosafos wrote there; and therefore R' Eliezer holds spoiling in burning is exempt and he is only liable in fixing; therefore he explains at sunset you burn. And even though one does not burn kodashim on Yom Tov, you find it on Shabbos and there is no fixing; but R' Yehuda holds like R' Shimon, and especially R' Akiva who is the teacher of R' Shimon, and we say in several places that R' Shimon in the view of his teacher R' Akiva said it; if so here too since R' Shimon holds melacha she'einah tzricha legufah exempt upon it, R' Akiva his teacher also holds thus; if so likewise spoiling in tying is liable; if so even half a measure is forbidden to burn on Yom Tov, since burning is in any case, as Rambam wrote in the laws of Shabbos. Therefore he explains that at sunset the season of your going out — the intent is until when do you eat until the season of your going out, etc. And with this it was resolved for me further that which was difficult to me in Rashi who explains that the season of your going out — the intent is that then it becomes (notar) and it is the time of burning; and it was difficult: since according to my view its eating time is only until midnight — if so immediately after midnight it becomes notar, and how did he say in the morning, etc., and even though at night one does not burn kodashim, behold on Yom Tov too one does not burn kodashim — why does he say that in the morning it becomes notar and not immediately at night after midnight? However according to what I said it works well: one can say that certainly in all prohibitions half a measure is forbidden from the Torah; but in melacha of Shabbos and Yom Tov, since the Torah only forbade melacha machsheves, therefore half a measure is permitted. Therefore it works well that at night, since the Torah forbade burning kodashim at night, even half a measure is forbidden to burn, for it is fit to combine; but in the prohibition of Yom Tov half a measure is permitted from the Torah; therefore in the morning he can burn it if only half a measure remained, and it works well, and investigate. And we find an example for our reasoning between Shabbos and other prohibitions in Yoreh De'ah siman 238: one who swore generally not to eat — even though he is only liable with a k'zayit as the measure of eating, nevertheless half a measure is forbidden from the Torah; but one who swore explicitly not to eat a k'zayit — half a measure is permitted, even though in the end if he eats half a measure and another half a measure they combine; nevertheless as long as he only ate half a measure it is permitted, since he specified only a k'zayit; so too on Shabbos, since the Torah only forbade melacha machsheves — if so it is like there where he specified a k'zayit and half a measure is permitted in it; but other prohibitions where the Torah did not specify melacha machsheves — even though we require a measure, nevertheless half a measure is forbidden from the Torah like in eating, and investigate; and our words work well there's help.
