(א) A bath of the bathhouse, etc. In Tur he wrote: but a bowl of hot water, etc. — and this is very astonishing, for he ruled against all the halacha mentioned in the Talmud — namely that they say in the explanation of Abbaye: Abbaye brought a baraita: a bowl is not like a bath — for regarding a bath they say there that permitted in hot into cold and not the reverse; but in a bowl permitted in every manner; afterward they say Rav does not care about water — meaning in a bowl whether hot to cold or the reverse or the opposite — permitted; and everywhere we rule like Rav, and all the more here; and Rif and Shulchan Aruch hold like Rav — how did Tur rule not like Rav? Beit Yosef explained two explanations and his honor is in its place; these two verses do not teach. One: because we do not know the form of these vessels, one should forbid in any second vessel made for washing. Therefore our teacher wrote to forbid even in a bowl; and this cannot be written, for then he should have written the Talmud's law and the distinction between bowl and bath and afterward written "and now we are not expert" — forbid every vessel; and why did our teacher rule practically what is completely false to the Talmud's law because of lack of expertise? At minimum he should not have mentioned bowl at all, but write generally any kli sheini that has hot water for washing, etc. And further, what expertise applies between bowl and bath? The second explanation: Tur explains bowl is a third vessel and this is against the whole sugya, for they say Rav does not care about water in a bowl — and presumably it refers to a bowl that is a kli sheini. And Maharshal chose another path to explain the sugya according to Tur, that he rules not like Rav, and made a dispute between the baraitot — and all these paths our fathers did not devise; and in my humble opinion it is fit to emend further Tur's words, for he first wrote "a copper pot one may not put hot water into it" — singular; and so in cup he wrote afterward he puts cold into it and did not mention plural "into them" — into the waters; and when he wrote the bowl law he wrote "forbidden to put into them," etc., but to pour from them — plural; and afterward he returned and wrote singular "and so the law," etc., "to pour from it." And further: why did he not mention the bath law at all since it is in the Gemara, and there is room to err and think that in a bath even hot into cold is forbidden, as Tosafos wrote — for if not for two baraitot of R' Chiya I would have said so regarding a bowl; and if R' Chiya needed to be careful from this — how much more should Tur have been careful regarding a bath; and further he wrote afterward "and so the law in a kli rishon," etc., for two uses — implying that before this it was kli rishon for washing and not found; therefore it appears fit and correct to emend our teacher Tur's words, and thus it should read: but a bowl that has hot water for washing, and it is a kli rishon, or a bath, etc., even if it is a kli sheini — and his words will align with Tosafos who wrote in the passage "But in a bath," etc.: and this bowl is certainly a kli sheini — why is it not like a bath? — end of their words; behold it is clear that bowl kli rishon equals bath in kli sheini; and therefore Tur wrote properly that bowl kli rishon for washing and likewise bath even kli sheini, etc.; and now Tur's plural "to pour from them" is correct — meaning from the bowl and bath; and also what he wrote afterward "and so the law in a kli rishon for drinking" — meaning as the law in kli rishon for washing that he already mentioned by bowl, per our emendation; and let it not be difficult for you to emend, for Beit Yosef also emends in this matter in the words of the Remazim and it is reconciled for our teacher to attribute a textual error in the transmission so the halacha agrees with other poskim, rather than attribute to him a unique view that does not appear and is not found — clear as the sun, in my humble opinion:

(ב) Of the bathhouse. Shulchan Aruch's language in this seif with the next seif is Rambam's language in chapter 22; and we see in the first glance it is astonishing in the explanation Beit Yosef explained there — therefore we will explain the sugya in order. It is stated in the chapter Kirah: the Mishnah — a pot that was emptied — one may not put cold into it so it will be heated; but he may put into it or into a cup to cool them. What does it say? R' Ada bar Masna said: the Mishnah means — a pot from which he emptied hot water may not put a small amount of water so they will be heated; but he may put a large amount to cool them; and does it not combine? He holds a davar she'eino mitkaven is permitted; Abbaye challenged immediately: does it teach "a pot from which water was emptied"? It teaches "a pot that was emptied" — implying he emptied it from the fire itself; rather Abbaye said: a pot that was emptied and has hot water in it — he may not put a small amount so they will be heated; but he may put a large amount to cool them; and a pot from which he emptied water — he may not put water at all due to combining; and R' Yehuda holds davar she'eino mitkaven is forbidden. Maggid Mishneh wrote: Rav Ada's resolution remains established for practical law even though rejected, and there is no concern of combining here, end of his words — meaning Abbaye's view also holds for practical law like R' Yehuda both in pot emptied and pot emptied from fire, except Abbaye did not want to explain the Mishnah as emptied from the side of the law that said "emptied"; therefore he must explain "emptied" and from this it follows the Tanna holds in "emptied" forbidden even in large amounts due to combining, and R' Yehuda — and if so, since we rule like R' Shimon, permitted also in emptied in large amounts; and if so there is no law distinction between Rav Ada and Abbaye. With this we will explain Rambam's words in order. First he wrote "bath of the bathhouse" — meaning kli sheini specifically; only in this they said permitted hot into cold and not the reverse; but if it were kli rishon — forbidden even hot into cold; and Rama emended here "even if it is a kli sheini" — implying also in kli rishon the law is so — following Tur as written nearby; but for Rambam it is not so, as stated — rather the Mishnah deals only with kli sheini; and it teaches there is stringency in this kli sheini because it is of the bathhouse; but it was not exactly like a kli rishon, which is forbidden even hot into cold. This is the bath law in the Gemara; afterward he wrote the pot law — this is for drinking; first the law that if he emptied water from it, the pot is still kli rishon and forbidden even hot into cold; nevertheless there is permission when they are large and the heat has power only to cool — from this we also know the law of pot that was emptied, which is also kli rishon — permitted in large amounts; only it teaches in emptied from it water that we do not forbid due to combining for the halacha we rule like R' Shimon — both for Rav Ada and Abbaye, as stated nearby; afterward he wrote the cup law for drinking, which is kli sheini — its law is permission hot to cold and cold to hot, for kli sheini does not cook; and on this he said "provided it is not in a kli rishon" — meaning if in kli rishon forbidden even hot into cold unless they are large, as stated; and Beit Yosef and Maggid Mishneh went another way to say there is a dispute between Abbaye and Rav Ada in emptied — forbidden for Rav Ada even in large amounts, and they explain Rambam's words like Rav Ada and explain what Rambam wrote "of the bathhouse" not literally but kli rishon, and its law like every kli rishon. What Rambam wrote that in kli rishon it is forbidden — this is in one manner, namely cold into hot; but hot into cold permitted even in kli rishon — all this is astonishing: how can Rambam rule like Rav Ada since it did not remain in the Gemara thus from Abbaye's challenge, and he wants to reject Abbaye's challenge — no posek has such power; also other strained points in his explanation; and according to what I wrote everything in Beit Yosef's challenges on Maggid Mishneh is reconciled. One dispute remains between Rambam and Tur: for Rambam forbidden in kli rishon even hot into cold unless the cold is large; and for Tur, who wrote even in kli sheini — it is implied that also in kli rishon the law is so that there is a distinction between hot into cold — permitted — and the reverse forbidden, for he holds the lower overcomes, as stated explicitly there; but in stringency of bathhouse kli sheini Tur also holds like Rambam that cold into hot is forbidden, unlike other drinking kli sheini. And in what Tur wrote the reason that the lower overcomes — fit to resolve his words: behold Rashi explained this reason and Tosafos in the name of Rabbeinu Tam rejected it — for if so there is a difficulty for the one who says the upper overcomes from here; and Tosafos resolved that the reason is because there is more water in the lower — therefore we follow the lower; and how did Tur write not like Tosafos, for it is not his way to dispute Tosafos and he does not bring their words at all? And it appears we can establish: for the one who says the lower overcomes the reason remains as plainly stated; only for the one who says the upper overcomes one must add — and for the one who deals when the lower is greater than the upper; and since we rule the lower overcomes, he ruled here properly even without the lower being greater — thus it appears very correct, there's help:
