And likewise implements of food preparation that it was not possible, etc. And the reason is because it is written, "but only that which every person is to eat, that alone may be done for you," and they expounded in the Gemara that "for you" comes to include all your needs, namely the implements for the food; and it is permitted even without a change, like food preparation itself. And behold, from the Mechaber it appears that food preparation is permitted to do on Yom Tov even if it was possible to do it beforehand when it would not lose flavor at all, and even without a change; that is, if it is something that is not the way to do for many days. And the view of "some are stringent" is that even in this one needs a change, since it does not lose flavor at all if he would do it from erev Yom Tov; but for something that is the way to do for many days, even according to the Mechaber one needs a change. And all this is when it would not lose flavor at all if he would do it from erev Yom Tov; but if it would lose flavor, it is permitted even in this without a change [all this from the Biur HaGra]. And behold, according to this we are forced to say that what the Mechaber forbids in siman 506, se'if 8, to make sourdough on Yom Tov except by means of a change, is because the way is to make them for many days.
However, if he did not do it, etc. By means of a change; and Semag and Ran are marked on this: it is a scribal error, for according to the Ran it is permitted even without a change, as the Gra wrote, and this is the view of the Mechaber, who did not mention at all a stringency regarding food preparation itself, implying that in every case it is permitted. And the words of Darkei Moshe on this matter require further study on two counts: one, that it appears from him that according to the stringent view even by means of a change it does not help, and Semag is among the lenient, and here it appears that Semag is the stringent one, while according to the first view even a change is not required; also it appears from him there ostensibly that Semag and Ran stand in one view, and in truth this is not so, as above. And it appears to me to resolve: that in truth, according to the view of Maharil and Or Zarua, Darkei Moshe holds that they hold that even a change does not help [and regarding salt, perhaps the Sages were lenient because it is impossible to eat a cooked dish without salt]; and according to the Ran, which is the view of the Mechaber, and as the Gra wrote, even a change is not required; and that is what he wrote at first, "And some are stringent," namely the view of Or Zarua and Maharil that even a change does not help; and what he wrote afterward, "However, if he did not do it" etc., that is to say, after the fact one may rely on the view of Semag that a change helps [and what he wrote in Darkei Moshe, "and so wrote the Ran," means that in this both are equal, that one should be lenient at least by means of a change, and not like the view of the stringent ones]. And according to this one should not be lenient ideally to leave it for Yom Tov and do it by means of a change; and in my humble opinion this is the intent of Magen Avraham, who attached the words of Maharil regarding what he was at first lenient about leaving it for Yom Tov and doing it by means of a change: for according to Maharil one should not do so, as is proven from Darkei Moshe.
