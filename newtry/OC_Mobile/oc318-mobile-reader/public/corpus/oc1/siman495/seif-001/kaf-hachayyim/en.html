1) [Seif 1] Every labor prohibited on Shabbos is prohibited on Yom Tov. The reason, rooted in the mitzvah, is so that the Jewish people will remember the great miracles that the Holy One, blessed is He, performed for them and for their ancestors, speak about them, and make them known to their children and grandchildren. By resting from worldly occupations, they will be free to engage in this. Were labor permitted, even light labor, each person would turn to his occupation, and the honor of the festival would be forgotten by children and adults alike. There are also many other benefits to resting: all the people assemble in synagogues and batei midrash to hear words of Torah, and the leaders of the people guide them and teach them knowledge. This resembles the Sages’ statement that Moshe instituted for the Jewish people that they expound the laws of Pesach on Pesach and the laws of Atzeres on Atzeres. Sefer HaChinuch, mitzvah 315; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1.
2) There. Every labor prohibited on Shabbos, etc. Even an act prohibited on Shabbos as a shevus is prohibited on Yom Tov. Likewise, anything that a Jew is prohibited from doing may not be requested of a non-Jew, just as on Shabbos. Beis Yosef; Levush; Magen Avraham at the beginning of the siman; Ateres Zekeinim; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 10; Mishna Berurah, paragraph 1. See below at the beginning of siman 543 and our comments there, with the help of Heaven.
3) Nevertheless. For the needs of an ill person who is not dangerously ill, although a Jew is prohibited from performing the act, he may instruct a non-Jew to perform it, even on Shabbos, as stated in siman 328; see there. Beis Yosef.
4) It is prohibited to tell Karaites to perform labor on Yom Tov. Mishpetei Shmuel, siman 134; Binyamin Ze’ev, simanim 410 and 407, in his own name and in the name of Rabbi Avraham Yerushalmi, of blessed memory. This is also evident from the words of the author of Tumas Yesharim, of blessed memory, siman 166. Knesses HaGedolah in Hagahos HaTur.
