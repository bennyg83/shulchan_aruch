{1} Forbidden on Shabbos: whether its prohibition is according to the Torah or because of shevus; and likewise whatever it is forbidden to do oneself, it is forbidden to tell a non-Jew to do as well, as on Shabbos.
{2} Except for the melacha of food preparation: as it is written in the verse, "but only that which every person is to eat, that alone may be done for you."
{3} And except for carrying out and kindling: and the details of their laws will be explained below in siman 511 and 518.
{4} And likewise implements of food preparation, etc.: and for the details of this law, see below siman 508 and 509.
{5} That it was not possible: that it spoiled on Yom Tov, or that he did not have enough time; and it appears that regarding food preparation itself, according to the Mechaber it is permitted even when it was possible to do it from the day before. However, for something that is ordinarily done at one time for many days, even according to the Mechaber it is forbidden when the thing is better from the day before and its flavor will not be lost, as below in siman 510, se'if 3; see there. And by means of a change, it appears from the Gra that it is permitted.
{6} From yesterday: but if it is possible to do it from yesterday, behold it is like other melachos and is forbidden according to the Torah.
{7} And some are stringent, etc.: by rabbinic [law], so that he not spend the whole day in melachos and refrain from the joy of Yom Tov.
{8} Anything that does not lose [flavor]: for kneading and baking and slaughtering and cooking, if one did them on erev Yom Tov there is a deficiency of taste in them: bread kneaded and baked today is not like bread kneaded and baked from yesterday, nor a dish cooked today like a dish cooked from yesterday, nor meat slaughtered today like meat slaughtered from yesterday; therefore they did not decree regarding them. But something that has no spoiling of taste and no loss if done from yesterday: why should he do them on Yom Tov? And the later authorities wrote that we hold in practice like this view. And they further wrote that according to this view it is forbidden to knead on Yom Tov vermicelli (which they call lokshen), for possible that those kneaded from before [Yom Tov] are better; and likewise it is forbidden to cook dried fruits on Yom Tov, for it is known that those cooked from erev Yom Tov are better for eating afterward on Yom Tov. However, if he did not previously have time to cook, and all the more so if he did not previously have the fruits, certainly it is permitted to cook them on Yom Tov; and likewise regarding the kneading mentioned above.
{9} Flavor at all: it appears that if it loses flavor even somewhat, it is permitted according to all opinions. And in Maharil it is written that it is forbidden to crush almonds on Yom Tov to extract milk from them, since he could have crushed them from yesterday, even though it loses a little flavor; and the later authorities wrote that for this reason he was stringent here, for it somewhat resembles squeezing fruits, which is forbidden. Nevertheless, by means of a change it appears that one should permit.
{10} It is permitted to do it by means of a change: and if he did not do it from erev Yom Tov because of some constraint, it is permitted even without a change. And Magen Avraham wrote that even ideally it is permitted to wait to do it by means of a change; and in the book Bigdei Yesha he is doubtful about this; however, if he has no time before Yom Tov, certainly one may rely on this.
