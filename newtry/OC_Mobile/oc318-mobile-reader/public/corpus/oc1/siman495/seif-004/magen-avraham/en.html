According to their words. This wording is not precise, for there are those who permit on Yom Tov [even] muktzeh and nolad; rather the Rama holds that in any case those who practice leniency regarding muktzeh should not practice leniency regarding nolad; if so, one should not carry bones that became detached [from the meat] on Yom Tov.
A storehouse of fruit. And the same applies to fruits that stand for commerce.
It suffices when he says. And according to those who permit muktzeh, one needs a verbal statement only regarding dried figs and raisins, as is written in siman 310, se'if 5 (Beit Yosef, siman 518).
And he need not mark. See in Beit Yosef, siman 518, that the Rambam disagrees and rules like the Sages that he must mark; and he wrote in Darkei Moshe there: and from the words of Beit Yosef it appears that he holds like R' Eliezer that he need not mark, for we hold that in rabbinic matters there is bereirah; end quote. And therefore he ruled here that he need not mark. And to me this requires further study: for Tosafos wrote that it is stated in the Yerushalmi that the Sages hold like Beit Hillel, and Beit Hillel hold that there is bereirah; and furthermore the Rambam also ruled that in rabbinic matters there is bereirah, and nevertheless ruled that he must mark; rather it must be that the reason is not because of bereirah, but as Tosafos wrote: since he designated it by hand, it is more severe and requires full preparation; and so Tosafos wrote at the end of chapter [the relevant chapter], that the halacha is not like R' Eliezer, for he is of Beit Shammai; and so Yam shel Shlomo ruled like the Sages. And further it appears to me that fruits that are [partly] fit and [partly] unfit, according to all opinions, he must mark and say, "From here until here I will take tomorrow," for there is concern that perhaps he will leave the bad and take the good and carry something unfit, as is written in siman 497, se'if 10; see there; and so it appears in Beit Yosef, siman 518, in the name of Kol Bo.
