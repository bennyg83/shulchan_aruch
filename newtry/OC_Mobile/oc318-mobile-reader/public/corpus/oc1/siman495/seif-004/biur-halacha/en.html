(ב) And he need not mark — see Magen Avraham who wrote that for fit and not fit, according to all opinions he must mark, for we are concerned etc.; and see in Sha'ar HaTziyun that we wrote that from Tosafos and Me'iri it does not appear so; also from the Mechaber's words above in siman 310 seif 5 it appears that even for fruits fit and not fit, a plain designation suffices [for he did not mention marking at all there]; and from the words of the Gemara Beitza 20b that mentioned there that designation helps — it is not so strong a proof, for perhaps they were debating there whether designation helps, but the manner of designation depends on the dispute of R' Eliezer and the Sages; but Shulchan Aruch should have explained, and so noted in Beit Me'ir, and it appears the Mechaber's view is also to rule like R' Eliezer, and the reason is that he holds like Rashi and Ran that their dispute is about bereirah, and since we rule that there is bereirah in rabbinic matters, automatically the halacha is like R' Eliezer. However I found in the novellae of Ra'ah who gave another reason for their dispute, and this is his language there: the dispute of R' Eliezer and the Sages is whether we require an act or not, and the Sages require an act by hand — even for Beit Hillel who permitted above in "this and this I will take," there with pigeons the bodies are divided, but here with fruits that are mixed we require marking etc. — end of his words; and automatically the halacha must be like the Sages, and so Me'iri extended on this on daf 26b and daf 34 and his view tends to rule like the Sages, and this is his language there: and the Sages say until he marks — meaning he shall make a sign on them and say from here until here I will take, for he holds there is no bereirah; and even though we rule generally that in rabbinic matters there is bereirah, here is different, for since they are not entirely fit, lest he come to reconsider and not resolve in his mind to take from them, and in such a case even in rabbinic matters there is no bereirah; and "this and this I will take" too without marking does not help, for only thus was stated regarding pigeons where two or three suffice and he has visual recognition of them, but fruits without marking he will not remember until which place he prepared [and similarly Ra'ah explained above]; however if he is confident in his memory he is also permitted to do so and it is like marking, and it does not come out except in "from here I take" and he did not clarify which of them etc.; and for this reason I say the halacha is like the Sages, as we say individual and many — the halacha is like the many — end of his words; and for this reason we ruled plainly for fruits fit and not fit to be stringent like the latter view, and so is the view of Magen Avraham and Peri Chadash; however for fruits standing for commerce, where Me'iri's reason does not apply, and likewise Tosafos' reason of ak'tziyya b'yadayim does not apply so much here, and also Magen Avraham's reason does not apply here [except Ra'ah's reason above], I ruled plainly to be lenient; and in Beit Me'ir he tends to rely to be lenient in all cases since it is only a rabbinic doubt, and per the plain meaning of the Mechaber's ruling in siman 310 seif 5. And know further that the plain meaning of the matter appears that the marking mentioned here is like "this and this I will take" regarding pigeons, except that regarding fruits "this and this" does not apply because one eats much [or because they are mixed and not recognizable as Ra'ah explained above] — therefore one must mark the place; and so appears from Beit Yosef's words in siman 518 — see there; and so appears from Me'iri's words above; and the view of Eshel Avraham appears not so — see there well and requires further study; and see what the Gra wrote on this in Ketzot HaChoshen.
