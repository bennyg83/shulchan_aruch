It suffices when he says, "From here," etc. Chayei Adam wrote: it appears that shopkeepers whose way is always to sell, behold it is as if he said [so].
And he need not mark. See Magen Avraham, who wrote that for [fruit that are] fit and not fit, according to all opinions he must mark, for we are concerned etc.; and see in Sha'ar HaTziyun that we wrote that from Tosafos and the Meiri it does not appear so; also from the plain implication of the Mechaber's words above in siman 310, se'if 5, it appears that even for fruits that are fit and not fit, a plain designation helps [for he did not mention there the matter of marking at all]. And from the words of the Gemara Beitza 20b, which mentioned there that designation helps, there is not so much proof, for perhaps they were discussing there whether designation helps, but the manner of designation depends on the dispute of R' Eliezer and the Sages; but Shulchan Aruch should have explained; and so a note was made on this in Beit Meir; and it appears that the Mechaber's view is also to rule like R' Eliezer, and the reason must be that he holds like Rashi and the Ran that their dispute is about bereirah, and since we hold that there is bereirah in rabbinic matters, automatically the law is like R' Eliezer. However, I found in the novellae of the Ra'ah who gave another reason for their dispute, and this is his language there: the dispute of R' Eliezer and the Sages is whether we require an act or not; and the Sages who require an act by hand [hold so] even for Beit Hillel, who permitted above in "this and this I will take": there regarding pigeons the bodies are separate, but here regarding fruits that are mixed we require marking etc.; end of his words. And automatically the law must be like the Sages; and so the Meiri lengthened on this on folio 26b and on folio 34, and his view tends to rule like the Sages; and this is his language there: and the Sages say until he marks, that is to say that he make a sign on them and say, "From here until here I will take," for they hold there is no bereirah; and even though we hold in general that in rabbinic matters there is bereirah, here is different, for since they are not entirely fit, lest he come to reconsider and not resolve in his mind to take from them, and in such a case even in rabbinic matters there is no bereirah; and "this and this I will take" too without marking does not help, for specifically thus was said regarding pigeons, where two or three suffice for him and he has recognition of them by eye, but fruits without marking he will not remember until which place he prepared [and similarly the Ra'ah explained above]; however if he is sure of his memory he is also permitted to do so, and it is like marking, and it comes out only as "From here I will take" where he did not clarify which of them etc.; and for this reason I say the law is like the Sages, as we say: an individual and many, the law is like the many; end of his words. And for this reason we ruled plainly regarding fruits that are fit and not fit to be stringent like the latter view; and so is the view of Magen Avraham and Peri Chadash. However, regarding fruits that stand for commerce, where the Meiri's reason does not apply, and likewise Tosafos' reason of designating by hand does not apply so much here, and also Magen Avraham's reason does not apply here [except according to the Ra'ah's reason above], I ruled plainly to be lenient; and in Beit Meir he tends to rely to be lenient in every case, since it is only a rabbinic matter, and according to the plain implication of the Mechaber's ruling in siman 310, se'if 5. And know further that the plain meaning of the words appears that the marking said here is like "this and this I will take" regarding pigeons, except that regarding fruits "this and this" does not apply because one eats much [or because they are mixed and not recognizable, as the Ra'ah explained above]; therefore one must mark the place; and so it appears from the words of Beit Yosef in siman 518; see there; and so it appears from the words of the Meiri above; and the view of Eshel Avraham appears not so; see there well, and this requires further study; and see what Shulchan Aruch HaRav wrote on this in Kuntres Acharon.
And it is permitted to prepare, etc. See Mishnah Berurah, what he wrote in explaining this law; and this law is from the words of Kol Bo, who wrote that this question was asked [and apparently it is from a responsum of the Geonim], and they answered: regarding muktzeh where there is room to decree lest he carry and leave, such as a hen standing to raise eggs, in this it certainly appears forbidden, as we see regarding pigeons of a dovecote that it does not suffice for them with "From here I will take tomorrow" until he says "this and this I will take"; but regarding other muktzeh where this decree does not apply, such as wood and fruits of a storehouse, it certainly appears that preparation helps for them, as we say "in any case"; end quote. It is clear in his words that regarding pigeons, where one must say "this and this," it is forbidden to say so on Yom Tov; and so the Taz followed his words, writing in s.k. 5: even though it is forbidden to prepare from one Yom Tov for its fellow, here is different, for one needs only speech alone regarding something that is not a living creature; end quote. And ostensibly the matter is puzzling, for it is proven in the sugya according to Rashi's explanation and other commentators that when he says "this and this" he stands from afar and does not touch the nest at all, and even so we are not concerned lest when he comes to take he will carry and leave; for since he did not care on erev Yom Tov to handle the pigeons, certainly he will not select even on Yom Tov which of them is better; and if so, why is it forbidden to prepare also regarding a living creature from one Yom Tov for its fellow, since he needs only mere speech? [And perhaps since he wants to prepare on Yom Tov itself, and in this one cannot prove from the fact that he did not care on erev Yom Tov to handle etc., for behold it is muktzeh, and therefore we are concerned that perhaps tomorrow he will carry and leave.] And perhaps further: since he needs to estimate how much he needs for tomorrow, and he also needs to mark by eye-recognition which they are so that he remember well, it is like weekday activity, and it is forbidden to do this on Yom Tov for the sake of tomorrow; and see Shulchan Aruch HaRav, siman 497, se'if 18. And it is further possible that this responsum explained the words of the Gemara there in a different manner from Rashi's explanation; for in truth the language of the Gemara is very puzzling, as the Meiri noted; and it is possible that according to this responsum, regarding pigeons where in preparation one must say "this and this I will take," one must also handle them [to see] if they appear fit in his eyes for eating, for otherwise we are concerned that when he takes them tomorrow he will carry and leave; and therefore they forbade doing so on Yom Tov; and it is possible with difficulty to explain the words of the Gemara also in this manner; and it is possible that they had a different reading on this; and this requires further study.
