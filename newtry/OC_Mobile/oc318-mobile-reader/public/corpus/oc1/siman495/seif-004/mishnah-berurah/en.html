{15} Muktzeh, even though it is permitted, etc.: meaning that above in siman 310 it is explained that there are several kinds of muktzeh that are permitted on Shabbos, such as muktzeh because it stands for commerce, or where he put it into a storehouse and his intent is not to take from it until after some time, or muktzeh because of disgust, and several other kinds; but all this they were lenient specifically on Shabbos, which is severe in people's [eyes], and we are not concerned that if we are lenient with him to carry a muktzeh item he will come to be lenient in other Shabbos prohibitions; but on Yom Tov, which is light for him, for many melachos are permitted to him for food-preparation need, if we are lenient with him regarding muktzeh he will come to be lenient also in other Yom Tov prohibitions; therefore the Sages forbade him to carry it and to eat it. And according to this view, an animal standing for its milk, and a hen for its eggs, and an ox for plowing, it is forbidden to slaughter them on Yom Tov until he prepares them for that from erev Yom Tov; and likewise anything of this sort requires preparation from beforehand; and something that is muktzeh because of nolad is obviously forbidden according to the Mechaber on Yom Tov.
{16} And some permit, etc.: for it is no more severe than Shabbos; and Magen Avraham wrote that such is also the view of Maharshal, and so Peri Chadash also ruled.
{17} According to their words, etc.: and even though there are poskim who permit even regarding muktzeh of nolad, the Rama did not wish to grasp their view in this, but only like those poskim who are stringent at least regarding muktzeh of nolad; and the later authorities wrote that even though on Shabbos we hold as the main [rule] to permit even regarding muktzeh of nolad, unlike the holders of this view, nevertheless on Yom Tov one should not permit, at least regarding muktzeh of nolad; and according to this one should not carry bones that became detached from the meat on Yom Tov, even though they are fit for dogs, because yesterday when they were attached to the meat they were also fit for a person, and it is nolad.
{18} And every muktzeh, etc.: this refers to the first view, that muktzeh is forbidden; for according to those who permit muktzeh, one needs a verbal statement only regarding dried figs and raisins, because presumably he actively pushed them away by hand when he put them on the roof to dry them, and even on Shabbos it is forbidden, as above in siman 310, se'if 2.
{19} That is not a living creature: for with a living creature it does not help that he say "From here I will take," but rather he must specify "this and this I will take," as is explained below in siman 497, se'if 10.
{20} Such as a storehouse, etc.: that is to say, and his intent from the beginning was only to take from it after some time; and the same applies to fruits that stand for commerce, or to muktzeh of dried figs and raisins that have not dried completely.
{21} It suffices when he says, etc.: for those that he will take tomorrow, behold they are as if clarified retroactively, that it was for them that he intended in his preparation; for in a rabbinic matter we hold that there is bereirah.
{22} And he need not mark, etc.: and know that there are several poskim who disagree with this, and their view is that it does not suffice that he say "From here" etc.; rather he must mark with a sign the place that he intends to eat; and their reasoning is that since they are muktzeh because of an act he did with them to designate them, such as dried figs and raisins that he put on the roof to dry them, or even fruits that he put into a storehouse, behold he designated them by hand for sale; a mere preparation does not suffice to remove them from muktzeh until he clarifies well the fruits that he intends to eat and puts a sign on them. And for practical law one should be stringent like the latter view, at least regarding dried figs and raisins that he put on the roof; and regarding fruits that one puts into a storehouse, it is possible to rely on the first view; and see Biur Halacha.
{23} And it is permitted to prepare, etc.: that is to say, in such a case of fruits and a storehouse where one need not do an act, and by mere speech there is preparation, it is permitted to prepare even on Yom Tov itself from one day for its fellow; for in any case: if the second day is holy, behold today is weekday and it is permitted to prepare; and if today is holy and tomorrow is weekday, behold one needs no preparation at all, and it turns out that he fixes nothing by his preparation so that it should be forbidden for him. And where do we forbid preparation from one Yom Tov for its fellow? Where he does an act, for we are concerned lest tomorrow be weekday, and it turns out that he did an act on Yom Tov for weekday need, like that of siman 503; see there. [And likewise regarding muktzeh of a living creature, such as a hen standing to raise eggs: he cannot designate from one Yom Tov for its fellow unless he designated from erev Yom Tov.] And according to this, per what we wrote to be stringent regarding muktzeh that he designated by hand, such as dried figs and raisins that require marking: if he forgot to mark on erev Yom Tov and remembered on Yom Tov, it is forbidden to make any mark to prepare fruits for the second day of Yom Tov, lest tomorrow be weekday and it turns out that he made a mark on Yom Tov for weekday need.
