Se'if 4: Muktzeh, etc. As is written at the beginning of Yom Tov [Beitza]: "Now then, who set down anonymously" etc.; they said etc. And likewise where it challenges there: "With what are we dealing? Let us say" etc.; rather etc.: "What is written? For Beit Shammai it is muktzeh"; hence it is plain to them that muktzeh is forbidden. And likewise there on folio 4a: "rather, regarding a hen" etc.; "she and her mother are forbidden" (Rif at the end of Yom Tov); and see siman 510, se'if 10.
And some permit, etc. From what is written at the end of Shabbos: "And even Levi held" etc., like that of Levi when he would bring a [case of an animal found to be] tereifah; and he brings a proof from Yom Tov to Shabbos. And there: "And even R' Yehudah held" etc.; "And did R' Yehudah say so? But R' Yehudah said the law is like an anonymous Mishnah, and we learned: 'One may not'" etc. R' Yehudah [taught] anonymously etc., and he does not distinguish between Shabbos and Yom Tov; rather Rav Nachman alone distinguishes, to uphold his [own] resolution of the sugya; and we hold like the other resolutions there. And further, from the challenge in the chapter of the stove [Shabbos]: "And does Rabbi hold like R' Shimon" etc.; and if it were so, then Rabbi ruled anonymously on Shabbos like R' Shimon and on Yom Tov like R' Yehudah; rather we do not hold so.
But nolad, etc. This too is not like Rav Nachman, who said: "Whoever holds" etc.; and [it is] like Rava in the chapter "HaMeivi," regarding a beam that broke etc.; and at the end of [the chapter] "Noteil," Shmuel would carry etc. Even though at the end of Shabbos we say that Shmuel held like R' Shimon, the anonymous Gemara at the end of the chapter "Noteil" holds that Persian [breads left unfinished] are forbidden. And see the Rosh there, who rejected all the proofs from Rava: there is no proof, for Rava holds even regarding [ordinary] muktzeh like R' Yehudah, as is written at the end of the chapter "Noteil": "Does this mean" etc. And from Shmuel there is no proof, for he holds like R' Shimon even regarding nolad, as is written at the end of the first chapter of Shabbos; rather as Tosafos wrote there, that he was stringent upon himself.
And every muktzeh, etc. Like R' Eliezer at the end of "HaMeivi," in the Mishnah on folio 34; and as Rashi wrote there, that they disagree about bereirah; and we hold that in rabbinic matters there is bereirah; and as is written in chapter 5 there, folio 38a, that Mar Zutra expounded that the law is like R' Oshaya; and as the Rif wrote there. And they say in the Yerushalmi at the end of "HaMeivi," and Tosafos brought it there: the method of R' Eliezer seems swapped: for we learned there that Beit Shammai say one may not take unless he shook it from before [Shabbos]; and R' Eliezer is not of Beit Shammai; rather it is a stringency in something that has the spirit of life; and the same applies for us, who hold there like Beit Hillel: one must say the distinction above, for there even according to Beit Hillel it does not suffice with "From here I" etc., as is written there on folio 10a; and that is what is written: "that I am not" etc.
And it is permitted to prepare, etc. See Magen Avraham and Taz; and as is written at the end of chapter 3 of Eruvin, folio 38a: "And furthermore R' Yehudah said: A person may stipulate" etc.; and see Rambam at the end of chapter 1, law 24: "Therefore" etc.
