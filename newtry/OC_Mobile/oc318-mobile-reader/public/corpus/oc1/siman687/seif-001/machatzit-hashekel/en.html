Magen Avraham wrote. He wrote in Hagahos Maimoniyos: a convert who converted, etc.; "and it implies that another who did not read it, etc." It is explained above, siman 58: if one was prevented by circumstances and did not recite the nighttime Shema, he recites it after dawn before sunrise and fulfills, for we consider it night. However, if he is constrained and must set out on the road to a place of wild animals and bandits, and it is impossible to recite Shema on the road, he is permitted to recite the morning Shema after dawn even before sunrise. But one may not do two contradictory things, to recite before sunrise after dawn both the nighttime Shema that he forgot and also the daytime Shema because he wants to go out to a place of wild animals and bandits; see there. The same applies here regarding a convert who converted after dawn before sunrise. If we say that he should then read the nighttime megillah, perforce we consider it night. But in Yoreh Deah, siman 268, section 3, there is a disagreement among the poskim whether conversion at night is effective. If so, according to the one who says that if he converted at night he is not a convert, this would be two contradictory things: if you consider him a convert and obligate him in reading the megillah, perforce you say that after dawn it is day; if so, how can he fulfill the nighttime megillah reading? He also was not obligated in it at all. And if you say that nevertheless it is proper to read the nighttime megillah, and he fulfills according to the poskim who hold that a convert who converted at night is a convert, and we consider before sunrise as night, about this he wrote that nevertheless he is exempt, since its main mitzvah is by day. That is to say, he holds the main view is like the poskim who say that a convert who converted at night is not a convert; and since the main megillah is by day, we are not so concerned for the nighttime megillah to fulfill according to all opinions, even according to the one who says that a convert who converted at night is a convert. It implies that another person who did not read at night has compensation before sunrise, even though dawn has risen.
