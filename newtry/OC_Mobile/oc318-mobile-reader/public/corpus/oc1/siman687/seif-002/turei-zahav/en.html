(1) Except for a dead mitzvah etc. In Rambam it is written in this language that whoever encounters him buries him first and afterward reads. And Mizrachi was precise that "and afterward he reads" is superfluous language, rather it teaches us: if there is time to read afterward — yes; if not — no. And it requires study, for plainly in the gemara dead mitzvah is preferable to Megillah, for the master said: great is human dignity that postpones a Torah prohibition, and the postponement is that the negative commandment is completely set aside, meaning as Rashi explained that it is written "you cannot ignore" and it is written "and you ignored" — times that you ignore, such as an elder not according to his honor; if so, in Megillah reading too human dignity should completely postpone the Megillah, and it is a fortiori, for Megillah is only from rabbinic enactment; and the extra language Mizrachi was precise on does not appear to come on that intent, rather per what Tosafos wrote "and you asked — let them do their service immediately and afterward read Megillah alone," and one can say it is better to read with the congregation because there is greater publicizing of the miracle — end; and for that intent Rambam wrote nevertheless: whoever has both before him and both have ample time — a Torah prohibition, because of publicizing the miracle in congregation, Megillah precedes — it teaches us that in this we are not concerned for greater publicity, rather he reads it individually afterward; but if there are no hours afterward, Rambam is not dealing with this, and the law is they completely cancel Megillah because human dignity postpones it; and know this: would one who finds a dead mitzvah in a field without a Megillah with him throw away the corpse and go to the city to read Megillah, and perhaps thereby dogs will eat it — and is it said he should do so? Surely it is void to say so; and apart from this there is no substance to Shulchan Aruch here whose words contradict each other — at first Rama wrote but if it is impossible to do both etc., implying other mitzvot precede Megillah; and at end wrote specifically when possible to read afterward, implying when impossible to read afterward Megillah precedes nevertheless, all the more other mitzvot. Regarding Megillah and Temple service that we say in gemara Megillah is preferable and we derive it from what is written family and family to bring priestly families and Levitical families who cancel their service and come to hear Megillah reading — Ran wrote; however specifically when they can afterward complete the service — if not so, surely they do not cancel d'oraisa service because of d'rabbanan Megillah; and it requires study on this, for we say Megillah is preferable to Torah study, and there Torah study is completely postponed because of it — meaning the study he has now an obligation in this hour cannot be made up in compensation, for what he will learn later is a mitzvah in itself, and nevertheless it is postponed because of Megillah — the same also for service; and it is a fortiori, for we say in gemara Torah study is preferable to service. Also Rambam's language he copied here instructs so, that he wrote a fortiori for other mitzvot that all are postponed because of it, and including this surely they too are completely postponed; and this is certain that you will find mitzvot dependent on that time, and nevertheless when they are postponed they are completely postponed; and what Ran wrote that service is d'oraisa and Megillah d'rabbanan — one can say it is divrei kabbalah, for we rule divrei kabbalah like d'oraisa as Beit Yosef wrote siman 554 in name of Rabbenu Tam regarding tzitzit; and therefore we properly say service is postponed; and Beit Yosef wrote Tosafos's view is like Ran who wrote: priests cancel their service to hear Megillah, and one asks why they cancel — is there not much time for service after the reading? And one can say since day has brightened it is time for service and they set it aside for the reading, it is called cancellation — end; and I do not see proof from this to agree with Ran — Tosafos refers to Talmud language who said they cancel their service, meaning at the time of their service every day when day has brightened, and on this they challenge "but there is time afterward all day"; but if it happens service was not done until a little before its time ends and Megillah reading is before him — so too Megillah completely postpones service since impossible to fulfill both — publicizing the miracle is preferable, as appeared to me.
