Magen Avraham, seif katan 3. "Nevertheless, it appears to me that there is no difficulty at all, for in any case, etc.; but many does not apply here." This is how the text should read. That is to say, what we say, that there is nothing that is not pushed aside, etc., can be said only regarding an individual, who must interrupt his study and go hear it publicly. But regarding many, since in the end they must read the megillah, it is not called pushing aside. One cannot say that if many are studying, as in the incident of Yehoshua, they need not interrupt at all for reading the megillah; for if so, even with individuals where each studies on his own, we should also say this, since they too are called many, as each one interrupts. Rather, perforce this is not so: even many who are studying must interrupt their study and read the megillah, and if so this is not called pushing aside.
