Megillah. Publicly; Beis Yosef and Bach.
As needed. It implies that he holds that although it has buriers, since they are not enough for its needs it is called an unattended corpse. So it also appears in the gloss at the end of siman 696, section 7, where he holds that its burial comes first; see there. Magen Avraham disagrees and wrote that this is specifically with a corpse that has no buriers. So wrote Shiltei Giborim, specifically when it is in a field and lying in disgrace, but in a city, megillah comes first. So wrote the Bach, and so it appears from Darkei Moshe in the name of Or Zarua, who wrote: the burial of a Torah scholar precedes megillah; end quote. His reason is that we say the honor of an individual Torah scholar is more stringent, implying that for every other person, megillah comes first when he is lying in a city. So is the main view; end quote.
Where it is possible. But if it is close to dark, he should read the megillah, for the unattended corpse can be buried at night. But if it is in a situation where it will be impossible to bury him afterward because of bandits, reading the megillah is certainly pushed aside. See Magen Avraham and Taz. Regarding circumcision and reading the megillah: if there is time for both, reading the megillah comes first because of publicizing the miracle; and if there is not time for both, circumcision in its proper time, which is Torah-level, comes first over reading the megillah, which is rabbinic, Pri Chadash. But Terumas HaDeshen, siman 262 and 263, inclined to say the opposite, that when there is time he should advance circumcision, and when there is no time he should advance megillah. It appears to me that the reason is that he can circumcise on the ninth day. Keneses HaGedolah; see there.
