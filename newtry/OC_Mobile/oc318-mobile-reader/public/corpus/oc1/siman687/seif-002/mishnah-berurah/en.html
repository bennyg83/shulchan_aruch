7) We interrupt Torah study, etc. That is to say, although Torah study is weighed against all the mitzvos, and even if it was Torah study of a large group of one hundred people studying in some house, nevertheless they must interrupt and go read it publicly because "in the multitude of the people is the King's glory." Chayei Adam wrote: nevertheless, one who prays all year on a fixed basis in his special minyan where he prays need not go to the synagogue.
8) As needed. From this language it is proven that even if he is in a city and has buriers, so that he is no longer lying in disgrace, and the haste to bury him first is only additional honor for him, nevertheless one may advance him before reading the megillah. So it also appears in the gloss at the end of siman 696, for he holds that the burial of his dead comes first. But Magen Avraham brought in the name of several poskim that they hold this is specifically an unattended corpse, meaning one found in a field, lying in disgrace, who has no buriers at all. But in a city, where there are people who care for him, reading the megillah comes first, unless he is a Torah scholar, whose burial precedes reading the megillah. So the Gra agreed in Biur HaGra as the halachah. However, he wrote that if they already began taking him out, they do not interrupt in the middle, and then reading the megillah is entirely pushed aside even if it will be impossible to read it afterward. Pri Chadash also wrote similarly; see there.
9) Where there is time, etc. The same applies regarding circumcision and megillah: if there is time to do both, megillah comes first because of publicizing the miracle, Pri Chadash and Peri Megadim. The world practices to circumcise before the megillah, as below at the end of siman 693, Chayei Adam.
10) But if it is impossible, etc. That is to say, the Torah mitzvah will be completely nullified if he does not perform it on that day. What he wrote later, that if it is impossible for him to read it afterward he should read it first, there the mitzvah of burial will not be entirely nullified, for it will be possible for him to bury him at night. Indeed, if it will be impossible for him to bury him afterward, for example because of bandits and the like, certainly the unattended corpse comes first.
11) No Torah mitzvah is pushed aside, etc. Because reading the megillah is only rabbinic. See Taz, who brought in the name of Maharshal and Bach that reading the megillah always comes first because of publicizing the miracle. The Gra in his Biur also holds this way; see there.
12) Where it is possible for him to read it. But if it is close to dark, he should read the megillah, for the unattended corpse can be buried at night. Rama copied this from Mahara Mizrachi, but several later authorities disagree with this and hold that even in a manner where the megillah will be pushed aside entirely, the unattended corpse still comes first because of human dignity. However, we have already explained above that this is specifically with a true unattended corpse, but not with other dead. Even if we rule there that regarding other dead too it is a mitzvah to advance their burial before reading the megillah, in any case, where through this the reading of the megillah will be entirely pushed aside, certainly megillah comes first and he buries his dead at night.
