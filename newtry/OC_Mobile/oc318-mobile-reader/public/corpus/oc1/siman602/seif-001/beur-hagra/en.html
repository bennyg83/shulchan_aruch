And they fast, etc. In the first chapter of Rosh Hashanah, 18b.
Seif 1, on all the days, etc. As written in Rosh Hashanah there, 17b, if he repented in between, etc.; and there 18a, But is it not written, Seek Hashem, and in chapter 12 of Yevamos.
And they say, etc. Because Rabbi Akiva was answered through it, as written in the second chapter of Taanis, and they established it from then onward for generations, as written in Kol Bo.
Except on Shabbos. As written in Yerushalmi Shabbos, at the end of chapter Ve'elu Kesharim: They taught that it is forbidden to request one's needs on Shabbos. Rabbi Zeira asked Rabbi Chiya bar Ba: What about saying, Shepherd us and sustain us? He said to him: such is the fixed form of the blessings.
And even if, etc. For even on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur, when Tachanun is forbidden, they say it.
And they say every day, etc. As written in Yoma 87a; and see Rashi, s.v. Chatasi, etc., and s.v. Ana, etc.
Except, etc. Because they are destined to say it twice, at Minchah and Maariv, and it is also Yom Tov.
And one does not, etc., and one does not, etc. As written at the beginning of chapter HaDayanim: here punishment is exacted from him and from the entire world. So too, the punishment of a cherem is as severe as the punishment of an oath, as written in Tanna Devei Eliyahu: to teach you that cherem is an oath and an oath is a cherem. The proof is from what is written concerning Achan: There is cherem in your midst, Israel, etc.; and all Israel was punished because of him.
We do not recite Kiddush Levanah, etc. As written in Maseches Sofrim, that this is specifically when he is fragrant and in joy. And in the Midrash, on Motzaei Yom Kippur a heavenly voice goes out and says, Go, eat with joy, etc. Hagahos Maimoniyos brings this. See above, siman 624, seif 5, in the gloss: and they eat and rejoice, etc. The main view is like those who say that it is good to recite Kiddush Levanah before Yom Kippur so that this mitzvah will tip the scale toward merit.
And the Shabbos between, etc. Not like Maharil, who wrote that one should not say it because the verse says, And Your judgments are the great deep, while we say, Do not enter into judgment with us. See above, where he wrote that, on the contrary, one should say it, as written in Arachin 8b: What is the plain meaning of the verse written about, etc.
