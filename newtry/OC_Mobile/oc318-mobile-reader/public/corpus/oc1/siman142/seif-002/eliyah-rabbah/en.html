[2] Or in the vowels, etc. This implies that when the meaning changes, even through the vowels, such as ya’aseh with a patach instead of ye’aseh with a tzeirei, or bachalev with a chataf instead of bacheilev with a tzeirei, we make him repeat. Responsa Beis Yaakov, siman 76, expresses astonishment at Rama, who derived this essential distinction, whether the meaning changed, from Piskei Terumas HaDeshen, siman 181, whereas it is explicit there that even when the meaning changes we do not make him repeat, for he writes that one who read Haran instead of Aharon has fulfilled his obligation. This is no difficulty, for Beit Yosef, citing Mahari Ibn Chaviv, writes that Aharon and Haran do not constitute a change in meaning. We must therefore say that Rama derived this distinction from Beit Yosef, who states it explicitly. His citation also to Piskei Terumas HaDeshen refers only to the ruling that one rebukes him even where the meaning did not change. I have seen, however, that Bach likewise rules that for any error we do not make him repeat. I therefore say that one should not protest against someone who does not repeat.
