Seif 1: If he read, etc. Yerushalmi, chapter 4 of Megillah: If he erred from one word to another, we make him repeat, even if he erred between im and ve’im. It states there that even if he erred in the Targum, we make him repeat, as the Rosh writes in chapter 3 of Megillah, siman 6. All the more so regarding precise reading, since all of these laws are derived from one verse, as stated in the first chapter: “They read from the book,” etc. Hagahos Maimoniyos. There is further proof from the Yerushalmi in Megillah, chapter 2: “We are not exacting,” etc., “one read Yehudim,” etc. This implies that in Torah reading, even such an error is corrected. See siman 690, seif 14.
Even in the precise reading of a letter. This is derived from the Yerushalmi cited above concerning Yehudim, which implies that with a Sefer Torah even such an error requires repetition, and all the more so a complete error. Even regarding Megillah, one must repeat, as is evident from the objection there: “Do we know how to pronounce ha’achashteranim,” etc.? Likewise, if even one letter is missing from a Sefer Torah, then even if he read it correctly, the reading is of no effect. It is only regarding Megillah that we say that if the scribe omitted letters, etc. Proof may be brought from chapter 3 of Menachos: Even one letter invalidates it. The Gemara asks: Is this not obvious? It answers that the ruling is needed regarding even the point of a yud; and there, concerning the leg of the hei at its opening, etc. We also learned in the first chapter of Megillah: The only distinction between sacred books and tefillin and mezuzos is, etc. Ramban, cited by Ran in chapter 2 of Megillah. Although Ran rejects his proof, his words are not compelling. He writes that the passage was stated regarding the obligation upon each person to write a Sefer Torah for himself, and that the Mishnah means there is no distinction, etc., because if it is not like tefillin, he has not fulfilled the mitzvah of writing a Sefer Torah. His words are astonishing, for this is not mentioned in the Mishnah at all. Furthermore, the books of Nevi’im and Kesuvim are not mentioned even in the Gemara, while the Gemara explains the Mishnah as referring to other sacred books. It may, however, be said that the Mishnah was taught regarding the law of rendering the hands impure. This is what the Gemara challenges there: “But is it not taught concerning Scripture,” etc.? The same implication appears in several places regarding rendering the hands impure. Nevertheless, since it does not render the hands impure, it is merely like a chumash, and one may not read from it. See the beginning of chapter 16 of Shabbos. It further appears to me that if a defect in a Sefer Torah did not invalidate it, the Gemara would not have challenged there regarding tefillin and mezuzos, “Is this not obvious?”
And likewise, etc. Lest one say that the reason is because of the blessing, yet no blessing is recited over the Targum. Moreover, in their times only the first and last readers recited blessings.
And specifically, etc. As stated in the Yerushalmi in Megillah. See above, siman 690, seif 14. This requires study, for it implies that specifically with Megillah we are not exacting in such a case.
