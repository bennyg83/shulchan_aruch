[1] Even in dikduk, etc. Hagahot Maimoniyot wrote: one must not leave a moving sheva unpronounced, nor move a silent sheva; he must not soften the strong nor strengthen the soft, end quote. Maharam Mintz, siman 81, wrote: the chazzan must learn the parashah so that it is fluent in his mouth, all the dikduk details, the metegim, upper and lower accents, right and left; and he should read very slowly and not quickly at all, lest he swallow some letter or word, end quote. On Chag HaShavuot one should read the Ten Commandments with the upper cantillation, which teaches the place of the Ten Commandments, and one is not concerned with the beginnings of the verses or the place where a verse ends. The first commandment begins "Anochi" and ends "leshomerei mitzvotai," etc., all one commandment; and "Anochi" and "lo yihyeh lekha" were heard from the mouth of the Almighty. The second verse is "lo tissa," and since it is one verse it has only one tune; and one reads "panai" with a patach under the nun, for it is not the end of a verse. The tav of "tirtzach," "tinaf," and "tignov" is dageshed, with a kamatz under the tavs, and the first "mitachat" in the verse "lo tissa" has a patach under the nun. But on Shabbat Parashat Yitro and Va'etchanan one reads with the lower cantillation, which teaches the beginnings and ends of the verses; "panai" has a kamatz under the nun, and the tavs mentioned above are soft, with a patach under the tavs, and the first "mitachat" in the verse "lo tissa" has a kamatz under the tav. So I extracted from the essence of the intent of Responsa Masat Binyamin siman 6, and he discusses it at great length. See Chizkuni, Parashat Yitro, Or Torah, and Magen Avraham siman 494. Responsa Chut HaShani siman 42 wrote: an incident came before me with one who recited the berachah, and before he began to read, an error was found in the Sefer Torah; they took out another, and I ruled that he should recite another berachah, end quote. His main proof is from what the Beit Yosef and Levush wrote at the end of siman 142: after they took out two Sifrei Torah together, they are considered as lying before him, implying that whenever they have not yet taken it out, he must recite a berachah. In this he contradicts the words of the Taz, who wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 279 that he does not need to recite another berachah, for we say that at the time of the berachah he intended that if this one is invalid, the berachah should apply to the second; see there at length. In my humble opinion, there is no contradiction here at all, for the Beit Yosef and Levush speak of two parshiyot, whereas here it speaks of one parashah, since he intended this parashah; what difference is there whether they read it in this Sefer Torah or in another? Presumably his mind is to read it in whatever way possible. Even the one who is lenient in the case of taking a lupine and it falling from his hand may possibly agree, for there, when he recited the berachah and it fell from his hand, he could choose not to eat another; but here, it is a mitzvah upon him to read it, and certainly his mind is that if it becomes invalid, he will read from another. Furthermore, with lupines, one is not like another, and perhaps this one is what he wants to eat and that one is not what he wants to eat; but here, what difference is there between this Sefer Torah and another? With this all his words are rejected; see there.
