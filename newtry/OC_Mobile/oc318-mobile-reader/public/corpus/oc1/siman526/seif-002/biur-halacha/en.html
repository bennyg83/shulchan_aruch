It is forbidden to leave him overnight until the second day of Yom Tov - See Mishnah Berurah for what he wrote in the name of the Acharonim: that if they delayed him so as not to bury him through a Jew, that is in Responsa Menahem Azariah, siman 16, and Eliyah Rabbah also cited it; and Eliyah Rabbah concludes on this: and if there are no non-Jews, it requires further study whether to bury him through a Jew. And in my humble opinion it seems simple in this that certainly one does not delay him until after Yom Tov, and they bury him through a Jew: for what sin did the dead commit, that they delayed him? Rather, even the main ruling of Menahem Azariah also does not seem [correct], for according to several early authorities we hold like Mar Zutra on the first day of Yom Tov, that even Rav Ashi concedes to him; and if so, according to them by law one must delay him until the second day of Yom Tov. And granted that we do not follow those posekim, and on the contrary it is better to bury him on his day: nevertheless we have not found that if someone delayed him we should penalize the dead by not burying him through a Jew, in a case where according to several posekim one needed to do so ideally. And furthermore, even the main ruling of the Mechaber is not agreed upon; and the Raavad in fact ruled in practice to delay a Torah scholar until the second day of Yom Tov so that he be buried through a Jew; only that Orchos Chaim wrote that they disputed him; see there in Beis Yosef. However, the Meiri also cited that of the Raavad and those who disputed him, and he agreed with the Raavad; and so ruled the Shittah Mekubetzes [only that in the Shittah Mekubetzes it is clear that this is specifically regarding a proper and honored person for whom it is a disgrace to bury him through non-Jews; and the Meiri, even though he cited that of the Raavad which deals with a Torah scholar, nevertheless from the end of his words (and this is his language: "and since they said the Rabbis made it like a weekday, nothing remains of it for any matter of the dead"; end quote) it is implied that everywhere it is permitted to delay them]. And if so, even though one should not be lenient ideally even regarding a Torah scholar against the plain ruling of the Mechaber, still: to penalize him, from where? Especially since the penalty is upon the dead, and it is not fitting to penalize him, for he committed no offense. And see Shaar HaTziyun for what we brought in the name of Olas Shabbos and the Gra, from whose words it appears that they explained that they touched upon the prohibition of leaving the dead overnight; and according to them it certainly does not apply at all to penalize him and disgrace him further by not burying him at all, or to bury him specifically through a non-Jew. And so I found in Yaavetz according to their reasoning, and he also distinguished regarding the main ruling of the Mechaber; and this is his language: it appears that it deals with a dead person whom it is forbidden to leave overnight, excluding a dead person whom they leave overnight for his honor to bring him a coffin and shrouds, for certainly it is permitted, for even on a weekday it is a mitzvah to leave him overnight; and the same applies regarding a father and mother, since thrusting them aside is disgraceful: if so, presumably this is his honor even on a weekday, all the more so on Yom Tov, where there is in any case a reason that if he has not lingered one must delay him to bury him on the second [day] through a Jew and not on the first through a non-Jew. And even though there is a contrary reason that the honor of Yom Tov is to hasten to remove him: nevertheless, who tells us to push aside one before the other? All the more so if the mourners want thus, that there is honor of the living and of the dead: it appears that in such a case the Raavad's reasoning is preferable; end quote. What emerges from all these words is that even though it is hard to dispute the Mechaber according to the reasoning of Olas Shabbos, and also not to distinguish in it according to the reasoning of Mor UKetzi'ah, who apparently stood only on the reason of the prohibition of leaving the dead overnight and did not stand on the reason we brought for the prohibition of delaying: nevertheless, where one transgressed and delayed, it appears that one should not penalize at all; for even according to our reason, if he is a sinner, what sin did the dead commit? And according to the Meiri and Shittah Mekubetzes [even though they descended to the reason of a shevus that involves an act], according to the conclusion that the second day of Yom Tov is like a weekday, etc., it is permitted ideally where his honor is greater through Jews: if so, at least after the fact one may rely and bury him through a Jew, and especially if he is a Torah scholar, for whom it is degrading to bury him through non-Jews. So it appears in my humble opinion. And all this is according to the Mechaber's reasoning; for according to the Rama, in any case they bury through non-Jews even on the second day of Yom Tov.
