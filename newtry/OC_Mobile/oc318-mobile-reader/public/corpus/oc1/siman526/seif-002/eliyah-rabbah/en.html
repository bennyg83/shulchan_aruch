[2] Even if they are able, etc. In a place where there is no established cemetery, one may not bury at all on the first day of Yom Tov, unless the body has remained since the preceding Shabbos or will otherwise remain until after Shabbos, or it is evident that it cannot be kept because of heat and swelling. Yam Shel Shlomo, siman 6. Tosafos, Moed Katan 8a, s.v. “One may not,” write that this applies specifically nowadays, when graves are not prepared during a person's lifetime, so that if a grave already exists one may not dig a new grave. It appears that they refer to a grave in which the deceased is actually placed. If it is merely an empty cavity in which the deceased is not placed, they would agree that it is permitted, as the Rosh writes. See there, for Tosafos and the Rosh disagree, and his view inclines toward Tosafos. I have written what appears correct in my humble opinion. Even according to his understanding that they disagree, the Rosh's view appears primary, and so rule Rambam, Rif, Ran, and Rabbeinu Yerucham.
