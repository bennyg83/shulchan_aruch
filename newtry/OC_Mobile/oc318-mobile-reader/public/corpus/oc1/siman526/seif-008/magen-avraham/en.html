In order to hasten his burial. That is to say, although he wrote in seif 4 that if he does not wish to bury him that same day it is forbidden to do anything for him, there he can do it after Yom Tov. Here, however, he needs to go with them to hasten the burial, since he is lying in disgrace in the field. The Rivash, siman 516, wrote that it is forbidden to go there, for even on Chol HaMoed it is forbidden to make boards in another courtyard, as written in siman 547, seif 10; all the more so here, where one who sees him will say that he is going for his own need. So wrote the Kenesses HaGedolah in the name of several great authorities, and so wrote the Yam Shel Shlomo: since he does not want to bury him that same day, it is forbidden to go there. Although there is concern that dogs will eat him, because of such a concern it is not proper to be lenient, especially since one must be concerned that the dogs will eat him before they arrive there, in which case he will have desecrated Yom Tov for nothing. But in a place where they see that the dogs will certainly eat him, they may rescue him and perform labor, even if he is not fit to be buried today; nevertheless, since they are doing the labor today in order to bury him tomorrow, it is permitted, end quote. It seems to me to be lenient, for in siman 547 he wrote that if the matter is publicized, it is permitted. Also, there is no concern that Jews will encounter them on the way, for on Yom Tov Jewish travelers are not common. However, if he is not lying in a field but only in a house, one should be stringent.
And it is permitted to bring. Even if he is lying in a city, if there are no graves there, it is permitted to bring him to a city that has graves. This is no worse than cutting myrtle for him, which is for his honor. If there are graves there and he instructed that they bring him to the graves of his ancestors, although it is a mitzvah to fulfill the words of the dead, and especially in such a case, where it is his honor to be buried among his ancestors, nevertheless it is not his honor to desecrate Yom Tov for nothing. For if he had not instructed so, it would be forbidden to remove him from one grave to another, as the Or Zarua wrote in the first chapter of Moed Katan. Therefore his own actions and instruction caused this for him. Thus, where there is concern that he may putrefy, they bury him in his place; and if there is no concern that he may putrefy, they leave him overnight and bury him after Yom Tov in the graves of his ancestors, end quote of Yam Shel Shlomo. See Beis Yosef, Yoreh Deah, siman 363, seif 2.
