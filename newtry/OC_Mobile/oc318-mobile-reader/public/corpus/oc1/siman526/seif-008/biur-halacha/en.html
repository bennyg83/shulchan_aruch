It is permitted to go on a journey, etc. — and in the gloss: and it is permitted to bring a corpse, etc. See Magen Avraham s.k. 17, who brought the view of Rivash who disagrees on this; and because one does not make a coffin except with the dead in the courtyard, and all the more here where the viewer will say he goes for his need. Magen Avraham answered on this that it is explained there in a well-known matter it is permitted (and Machatzit HaShekel explained: since it was announced to them from the mouths of other maggidim, the matter is well-known). Also one need not fear Jews will harm them on the road, for on Yom Tov Jewish travelers are not commonly found. However, if the corpse is not laid in the field but in a house, one should be stringent, end of his words. Magen Avraham was very brief and also contradicts his own words in s.k. 17 that walking outside the techum is compared to cutting cedars, which is forbidden even for a well-known corpse. Peri Megadim in Eshel Avraham questioned this section on Magen Avraham's words in s.k. 17 and answered: since the corpse is laid in disgrace in the field, they were lenient further. It is wondrous, for this section in Beit Yosef appears to derive from Teshuvot HaTashbetz who challenged what was ruled in Rivash's responsum to be stringent (see there in Chayei Adam siman 22). In Rivash's responsum the act was that gentiles shut the dead in a cave with his clothes; nevertheless Shulchan Aruch ruled to be lenient. When I examined Knesset HaGedolah in all places and in Rivash and Tashbetz, I saw the law of s.k. 17 — walking outside the techum compared to cutting cedars — derives from responsum Rivash siman 116; and his words in s.k. 17 are per Tashbetz's view disagreeing with Rivash's case; and although Magen Avraham did not see the responsum, he intended it in part. a) We are few in our places and our law is like a well-known corpse as Tosafot wrote. b) They forbade only in public where possible in private, or to delay one day that is not so degrading because of mourning; but such a matter even in public may be permitted, similar to Rav Yosef in Kashuri who said the privacy of these days — as Rashi explains there, since it is impossible; and here too the hour presses and one cannot wait more because of concern for stench, dogs, or fire — we are not concerned for publicity. c) Going outside the techum is not called public (see Moed Katan 12 regarding accepting illness). d) One should not fear publicity except for making a coffin and grave, which are Torah labors. Tashbetz also wrote: since we find no prohibition in their words, from where shall we be stringent; all the more here touching human dignity, especially when no one will attend — it is a met mitzvah that even so defiles; and on chol certainly we are obligated to bury; and so on the second day of Yom Tov which is like chol; and rather such a death is graver than if in the city. The claim that otherwise there will not be time to bury is weak, for as long as he delays he increases degradation; all the more if there is concern for dogs or burning. See Tashbetz at length. In brief: Rivash forbids even in his case; and certainly a corpse in an Israelite house when one cannot bury there — one may not go outside the techum to bring it, for walking abroad is compared to cutting cedars. Per Tashbetz, even a corpse in one's house outside the techum, if laid in a nearby place where one can reach to bury on Yom Tov — one may lean to permit, for it is not like cutting cedars, which is complete labor; and going abroad is not called publicity. And so a corpse laid here needing to go outside the techum for shrouds, coffin, and the like — also permitted. The Mechaber and Rama appear to hold Tashbetz's view. Rama copied Rashba in the gloss where the corpse was far and they went to bring it — implying leniency to go abroad and bring a corpse. Knesset HaGedolah discussed this; Mahari Titatzak permitted riding horses; Magen Avraham was concerned. In a place where the corpse already delayed, one may go abroad and bring; and likewise here to bring burial needs the same day — permitted per Tashbetz; especially as Mechaber and Rama incline so. Know all this is on the second day of Yom Tov, not the first, for met mitzvah does not override Shabbat and Yom Tov; but through a gentile one may permit [Eliyah Rabbah]:
