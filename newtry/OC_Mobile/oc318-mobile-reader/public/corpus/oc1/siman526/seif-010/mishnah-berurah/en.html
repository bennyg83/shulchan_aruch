{48} Nefalim, etc.: it appears that he is not speaking of definite nefalim, for those it is forbidden to bury on Yom Tov, and if so, independently it is not relevant to circumcise them, for they circumcise them upon their grave, as the Mechaber wrote in Yoreh Deah, siman 263, se'if 5; rather he refers to the nefalim of se'if 9, whom we treat, regarding burial, with the presumption of viable children, and we occupy ourselves with their burial on Yom Tov; and even so, to fulfill the custom to circumcise them one should not permit, for this is not among the needs of burial.
{49} And it is forbidden to bury him: according to what we explained of the Mechaber's words, the Rama's words are not so precise, for regarding that of se'if 9 the Rama does not dispute, unless because the Mechaber stated plainly "nefalim," and lest one err that specifically the orlah they do not remove, it teaches us that one who is in the presumption of a nefel is also forbidden to bury. And one may further say that even regarding a doubtful nefel such as that of se'if 9, the Rama's view is that it is better to leave his burial until after Yom Tov so that he remove his orlah before the burial; and they bury a doubtful nefel only after he has been circumcised, or a female nefel; and so wrote some Acharonim. However, regarding a child whom they did not circumcise because of illness and the like, and he died after thirty [days], certainly they bury him on Yom Tov and then also remove his orlah. And know that some Acharonim dispute the main laws of nefalim, and according to them burial of nefalim is also a mitzvah; and if so, it is possible that one should at least permit on the second day of Yom Tov through a non-Jew; and see Chochmas Adam, klal 170, se'if 7.
