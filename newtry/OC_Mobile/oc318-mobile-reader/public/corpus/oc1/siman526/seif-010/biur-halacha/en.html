And it is forbidden to bury him on Yom Tov — see Meiri Beitzah 6, who also brought the view of the poskim that one does not bury a definite or doubtful stillborn within thirty days, because it is like a stone, and there is no kevod habriyot here — neither of the living nor of the dead; and it is forbidden even on the second day of Yom Tov, even through gentiles, for it is like a carcass and no better than an eight-day-old live child. He concludes: however, if there is great need, or perhaps they will delay — we move it by the law of graf shel rei; and I do not know if its burial was permitted; the matter is uncertain in my hands, except that in our composition we cited it as permitted — end of his words. It appears his intent is that since in any case it may be moved, it is also permitted to bury it — requires study. See above siman 311 and in Chayei Adam klal 137 seif 7.
