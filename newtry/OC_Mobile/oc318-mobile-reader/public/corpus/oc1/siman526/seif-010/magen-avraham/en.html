It is forbidden even, etc. For there is no mitzvah to bury a stillborn, and therefore Yom Tov is not overridden for the needs of his burial. Furthermore, what they customarily do to remove it is not a Torah custom, but merely an ordinary custom because of the rectification of sinners, for they say in Bereishis Rabbah that the Holy One, blessed is He, removes the foreskin from the children and places it upon the sinners of Israel, as in Hagahos Maimoniyos. In Bedek HaBayis he wrote that this speaks where we know with certainty that his months were not complete, for otherwise we bury him on Yom Tov, as written in seif 9. This implies that he holds that we bury him but do not circumcise him. In Darchei Moshe he wrote that in any event one should wait until tomorrow and circumcise him, even if his hair and nails were fully formed. It implies that if it is after the circumcision, or if the infant was female, the Rama agrees that we bury him on Yom Tov, as written in seif 9. What is written in Yoreh Deah at the end of siman 263, that one does not bury stillborn infants on Yom Tov, refers to a definite stillborn. In Kenesses HaGedolah he wrote in the name of Rabbi Shlomo HaLevi, siman 14, that if he is thirty days old or more and they mourn for him, they remove his foreskin on the second day of Yom Tov of the diaspora. In Tosafot Shantz they permit burying a complete stillborn on Yom Tov, end quote. To me, what Hagahos Maimoniyos wrote requires study, that there is no mitzvah to bury a stillborn, and he brings proof from what we say in the Gemara, that a maidservant cast a stillborn into a pit, and we say: a pit into which stillborn infants are cast. This requires study, for nevertheless it is burial, as the Rosh wrote in chapter 14, mishnah 3 of Ohalos. One can also answer that it speaks of a stillborn that is merely a shapeless mass, as stated elsewhere. Furthermore, we say in Niddah, folio 57, that the Cutheans would not bury stillborn infants but would cast them away. What did they expound? Whoever has inheritance has a boundary. This implies that we do not rule like them. Furthermore, since a verse was needed in Toras Kohanim, parshas Emor, that a kohen does not become impure for his stillborn son or daughter, this shows that it is a mitzvah to bury them, for a kohen becomes impure only for the need of burial, as written in Yoreh Deah, siman 373, seif 5. Furthermore, at the end of chapter 7 of Bava Basra it states that they would make niches for stillborn infants, and so too in Sanhedrin, chapter 6. This requires study, for at first glance it implies in the above folio in Niddah that the prohibition of leaving the dead overnight also applies to stillborn infants.
