Beit Yosef cites Rashba as saying, “all the more so to Jewish graves, and all the more so to his ancestral graves,” but this refers to a place where there are no graves at all. This is also implied by Darkei Moshe, who did not copy the concluding phrase, “all the more so to his ancestral grave,” contrary to Olas Shabbos's understanding. Responsa Menahem Azaryah, siman 15, and Maharshal, siman 13, rule likewise. If the deceased instructed that he be taken to his ancestral grave, we do not obey him; rather, we bury him that day in his present place. If the body will not decay, however, we wait until the next day and transport him. Later authorities and Maharshal. Know that Olas Shabbos appears to understand Hagahos Ashri as permitting transport from outside Eretz Yisrael to Eretz Yisrael. This requires study, for Hagahos Ashri discusses an ordinary weekday, as Beit Yosef explains regarding the ruling that a corpse should not be transported from a city that has graves to another city. Regarding that weekday case he says that transport from outside Eretz Yisrael to Eretz Yisrael is permitted. On Yom Tov, however, it appears forbidden.
