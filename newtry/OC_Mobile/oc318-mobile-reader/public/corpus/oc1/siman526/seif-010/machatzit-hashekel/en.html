(Seif katan 10) To cover it with earth. The Taz writes: “Meaning, to make the earth into a mound.” From the Taz in Yoreh Deah 399, Magen Avraham appears to have understood that Taz forbids this even on the second day of Yom Tov. This is incorrect, for Taz explicitly writes there that it is forbidden on the first day, implying that it is permitted on the second day. He writes the same here in seif katan 2.
It is possible that they maintain, etc. Meaning, those who permit it, as cited by the Levush, maintain this.
It is forbidden to clear away, etc. The same is stated above in siman 312, seif 9.
Except for members of the burial society, for whatever can be done, etc. Meaning, members of the burial society ordinarily perform the principal needs of the deceased and desecrate Yom Tov for him. Why should we expand the desecration by allowing people who are not members to do so as well? Members of the society are all equal and may desecrate Yom Tov. Even if four or five members would suffice, were we to permit only that number, who would determine which ones? Each would say that his fellow should desecrate Yom Tov rather than he, and the matter might be neglected. Therefore it was permitted to all of them.
Regarding circumcision, etc. Although one mohel could perform both the cutting and the uncovering, why should two be permitted to circumcise, thereby causing the second also to desecrate Shabbos? We likewise find regarding offerings that many kohanim performed the service of the daily and additional offerings on Shabbos. All the more so here. Regarding milah, although Shabbos is only overridden and not fully permitted, it may nevertheless be performed by two. Here, regarding a corpse, Yom Tov was fully treated as a weekday by the Sages, so all the more may even people who are not members of the burial society participate. Although Rama concludes regarding milah that it is proper to be stringent and not use two mohalim, here, where Yom Tov was treated as a weekday, Rama would agree that one need not be stringent. See what Shach writes there, and observe the common practice that two mohalim perform a circumcision on Shabbos.
