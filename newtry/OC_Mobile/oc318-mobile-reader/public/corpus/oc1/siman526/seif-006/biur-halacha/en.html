And they return to their place that same day - See what I wrote in Mishnah Berurah regarding if there is a Jewish settlement there; and supporting our words are the words of Radbaz, part 4, siman 107, who wrote in explaining the words of Kol Bo, folio 46, that the sages of Beziers permitted regarding a dead person who was in the city of Aqde to escort him to Beziers: two of the relatives, and to ride on the way; but they did not return that day to their place, but remained in the city of Beziers that day; end quote. And Radbaz wrote on this: therefore they did not permit them to return, since they were in an inhabited place and better than their place; what need have they to return? And here there is no reason of "so that it not be a stumbling-block for them in the future." But in our case, where the cemetery is in a desolate place and wilderness, and even if there is nowhere to shelter from the sun, and there is neither food nor drink: certainly there is the reason of "so that it not be a stumbling-block for them"; end quote. And know further that one should be in doubt in places where the cemetery is very far, such that no person ever escorts as far as the cemetery, but there are people of the Chevrah Kaddisha who are constantly appointed to carry the dead there and come for their wage: whether regarding them they permitted to return, and whether the reason "so that you not cause them to stumble" applies, for they are hired and go for their wage; and if because the mourners would have to pay more for this, it requires further study whether we permit them for this to return several miles. However, four thousand amos they can certainly return, for in any case they are no worse than all who go out with permission, who have four thousand with absorption, as above in siman 405; but beyond four thousand, which we permit only because of the reason "so that it not be a stumbling-block for them," it requires further study whether this applies. And I further doubt whether regarding the mourners too we say that they return to their place; for apparently regarding them "he will refrain" does not apply, for certainly they will not refrain from escorting their father because of this, and it applies only to ordinary people. And perhaps this is the reason of the incident cited in Kol Bo above, that they did not permit them to return because they were relatives of the dead and would not refrain from escorting. And from the plain wording of the posekim it is implied that one should not distinguish in this; and it requires further study.
