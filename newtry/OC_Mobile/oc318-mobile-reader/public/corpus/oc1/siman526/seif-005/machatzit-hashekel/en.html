(Seif katan 5) To the point of squeezing, etc. This rejects Olas Shabbos, who asked why the reason “lest he squeeze” is needed, since it is forbidden even if no squeezing will occur. Before washing the deceased, the sheet must first be soaked in clean water, and we rule in siman 302, seifim 9 and 10, that soaking a garment in water is its laundering and is forbidden because of whitening.
Even according to the opinion that a manner of soiling is permitted. Soaking the sheet in clean water for washing the deceased is not a manner of soiling. Magen Avraham therefore explains that the prohibition was stated because of squeezing, rather than relying upon laundering, since some maintain in siman 302, seif 9, that a clean sheet is not subject to laundering, and the sheet used for washing the deceased is presumably clean. Nevertheless, it remains forbidden because of squeezing. One must explain that although we rule that where there is no laundering and the extracted liquid is discarded we do not decree lest one squeeze, for there is no reason to squeeze either for cleansing or for use of the liquid, Magen Avraham nevertheless writes in siman 320, seif katan 23, in the name of Ran that squeezing in such a case is still prohibited D'Rabbanan. Accordingly, “lest he come to squeeze” here does not mean a decree lest he squeeze, which would be a decree upon a decree. Rather, he must take care not to reach a situation in which squeezing is certain or nearly certain, comparable to a psik reisha. Although he does not desire it, we do not rule like the Aruch, who permits such a case, as explained in siman 320. Magen Avraham writes similarly in siman 523, seif katan 2.
