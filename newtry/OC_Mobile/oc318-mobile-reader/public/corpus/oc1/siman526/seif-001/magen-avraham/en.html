Even if it will putrefy. If he must bring him to another city farther than a day's journey, and the non-Jews will not bring him tomorrow, and it is also impossible to bury him tomorrow through Jews, they should bury him through non-Jews in his home, and after Shabbos they should take him out and bury him; or they should place him in a coffin and seal it with mortar and pitch through non-Jews, so that it will not putrefy, as in the Rivash, Beis Yosef, and Yam Shel Shlomo.
Non-Jews should attend to him. One may not bury through Karaites, because of 'before the blind you shall not place a stumbling block.' See Tashbetz, siman 166; Mahari Weil, siman 134; and Rashbash, part 8, Responsa of Shlomo ben Shimon Duran, siman 16, and so it is.
To purify him. The practice is to purify him completely without the assistance of non-Jews, unlike the Radbaz, and so it is.
And to take him out. Since there is a mitzvah need involved, it is permitted; see siman 518. The Yam Shel Shlomo wrote that in a place where there is no fixed custom, it is proper not to bury him on the first day of Yom Tov through non-Jews unless he has remained unburied, or it will be necessary to leave him unburied, or he is swollen; and some great authorities wrote likewise. A small child, even after thirty days, may be left overnight in a cellar, and it also cannot be said regarding him that he is disgraced among the living as with an adult corpse. Therefore it is better not to bury him on the first day of Yom Tov, end quote. The Bach wrote that specifically stillborn infants are not buried; see the end of the siman.
To squeezing. This means even according to those who permit in siman 302, seif 9 in the gloss, unlike the Olas Shabbos.
