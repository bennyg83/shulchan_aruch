1) [Se'if 1] If it is the first day of Yom Tov, etc. Since its prohibition is under Torah law, it is not set aside for the honor of the deceased. Olas Shabbos, subsection 1. The positive commandment of burial does not override the prohibition and positive commandment of Yom Tov. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 1. See below, subsection 22.
2) There, in the gloss. Jews should not attend to him, etc. The same applies through members of the non-Jewish group discussed by the authorities, because of the verse, 'You shall not place a stumbling block before the blind.' Tumas Yesharim, siman 166. This is also evident from Mishpetei Shmuel, siman 134. Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur. See there. Magen Avraham, subsection 2; Chemed Moshe, subsection 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 5. It is likewise forbidden to do this through a Jewish apostate because of 'before the blind, etc.' Chochmas Adam, rule 140, subsection 1; Chemed Moshe there; Mishnah Berurah there. See above, siman 304, subsection 8.
3) There, in the gloss. Jews should not attend to him, etc. This applies even if the person died on the first day of Yom Tov when it fell on Friday and burial by non-Jews is impossible. For example, there is no cemetery in their location, the non-Jews will not agree to transport the body if burial is delayed until Sunday because that is their festival day, and if burial is delayed until Monday the body will decompose. There is then no remedy except to have non-Jews bury him in his house that day, after which he should be removed following Shabbos and buried with his ancestors. Alternatively, non-Jews should prepare a shroud and coffin that day, place him in it, and seal it with clay and pitch so that no odor escapes, and he should be buried after Shabbos. Beis Yosef in the name of Responsa Rivash, siman 487; Yam Shel Shlomo, first chapter of Beitzah, siman 10; Magen Avraham, subsection 1; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1; Chemed Moshe, subsection 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 4; Chochmas Adam there; Mikraei Kodesh, siman 28, subsection 3; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 3.
4) There, in the gloss. Even if the body will decompose, etc. For example, the person died before Yom Tov, and if the body remains until evening it will decompose. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 2. The same applies when the person died during the hot season. Mishnah Berurah there.
5) There, in the gloss. Even if the body will decompose, etc. A person died on the first day of Yom Tov, and the authorities did not permit his burial on Yom Tov because of their festival day. Burial would therefore be delayed until after Shabbos, the third day after his death, because Yom Tov fell on Thursday and Friday. Because of concern that the body would decompose, it was permitted to perform taharah on the second day of Yom Tov and place him in a coffin. Although burial could not yet take place, placing him in the coffin constitutes partial burial. Shevus Yaakov, volume 2, siman 26; Shaarei Teshuvah, subsection 2; Mikraei Kodesh there, Likutei Rima, subsection 6.
6) There, in the gloss. See above, siman 304. This refers to a Jewish servant who has not undergone circumcision and immersion, and whether one may instruct him on the first day of Yom Tov to attend to the needs of the deceased. See there and our words there, with Heaven's help.
7) There. Even if they can leave the body until later, etc. The Sages did not forbid instructing a non-Jew regarding a deceased person, because of the deceased's honor. Although the body will not decompose, it is nevertheless an honor not to leave it overnight, for one who hastens the bier is praiseworthy. This is also stated in Yoreh Deah, siman 357. Furthermore, delay causes additional distress to the relatives. Beis Yosef in the name of several halachic authorities; Levush. Nevertheless, see Beis Yosef, who cites an opinion that a body is not buried by non-Jews on the first day of Yom Tov if it can be preserved until the next day. See there. Yam Shel Shlomo, first chapter of Beitzah, siman 10, writes that in a place without an established custom, it is proper not to bury through non-Jews on the first day of Yom Tov unless the body has already remained for some time, will need to be delayed further, or has become swollen. Accordingly, he writes that a young child, even after thirty days, can be left overnight in a cellar. Nor does the concern that the deceased will be disgraced among the living apply to a child as it does to an adult. It is therefore preferable not to bury the child on the first day of Yom Tov. See there. Magen Avraham, subsection 4; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 2; Chemed Moshe, subsection 1, cite this. Beis Meir, however, disputes the aforementioned words of Yam Shel Shlomo and writes that according to Shulchan Aruch in se'if 2, greater concern should be given to the prohibition against leaving the deceased overnight. Beis Yosef may be relied upon even in a place without an established custom. See there. Shulchan Aruch HaRav also omits the ruling of Yam Shel Shlomo, implying that the halachah does not follow it. Chochmas Adam, rule 170, subsection 1, writes that it requires further study. Mishnah Berurah in Biur Halachah likewise writes that in practical halachah one certainly should not take Yam Shel Shlomo's ruling into account, even in a place without an established custom. See there. Regarding a child and a nonviable infant, see below, se'ifim 9 and 10.
8) There. All this concerns making a grave and coffin, etc. These are full melachos forbidden under Torah law, and the principle of 'since they were permitted, etc.' does not apply to them. They are therefore forbidden when performed by a Jew. Levush; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 6.
9) There. All this concerns making a grave, etc. Likewise, covering the deceased with earth must be done by non-Jews, because filling a hole resembles building. Taz, subsection 1; Sheyarei Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef, subsection 2; Shulchan Gavoah, subsection 2; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 3; Maamar Mordechai, subsection 4; Chemed Moshe, subsection 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 3; Chochmas Adam, rule 109, subsection 1; Mikraei Kodesh, siman 28, subsection 2; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 12. Forming a mound over the grave is likewise a Torah melachah and is forbidden on Yom Tov. It appears that the same applies to preparing the small grave markers called nepelach, which is a Torah melachah. Chochmas Adam there; Mikraei Kodesh there. See below, subsections 17 and 56.
10) Accordingly, on a weekday the son of the deceased or another close relative customarily places earth over the eyes of the deceased, and each person accompanying the deceased to the cemetery places a clod of earth into the grave. On the first day of Yom Tov, doing so is forbidden under Torah law because it resembles building. Shulchan Gavoah there; Zechor LeAvraham, volume 1, Yoreh Deah, Hilchos Aveilus, subsection 10; Mikraei Kodesh there, Likutei Rima, subsection 3.
11) There. All this concerns making a grave and coffin. Making a coffin includes even merely sawing the boards to the length of the deceased, which is a Torah melachah of cutting to size. It appears that it is likewise forbidden to cut linen cloth, whether measuring and cutting it from a large piece or cutting it into small pieces to sew them together in the manner used to make a garment, because one is liable for cutting to size. Chochmas Adam there; Likutei Rima there; Mishnah Berurah, subsections 1 and 6.
12) There. All this concerns making a grave, etc. Even if there is a certain excavation in the cemetery in which the deceased could be placed until after Yom Tov, or even a complete grave that is not his and from which he would later have to be removed, non-Jews may dig a grave for him on Yom Tov. Moving his bones and removing him from the temporary grave would not honor the deceased. Rosh, first chapter of Moed Katan. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 2, agrees, unlike Olas Shabbos. Mishnah Berurah there rules likewise. See below, subsection 34.
