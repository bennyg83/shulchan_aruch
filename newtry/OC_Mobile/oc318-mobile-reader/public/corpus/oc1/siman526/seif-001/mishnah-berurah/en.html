{1} Jews should not attend to him - For the mitzvah of burial does not override the positive commandment and negative commandment of Yom Tov. Even through Karaites, and even if one became a gentile, it is forbidden, for they are Jews and are commanded, and there is a prohibition of "do not place a stumbling block before the blind."
{2} Even if it will putrefy - For example, if it has been lying since before Yom Tov, or it is the days of heat; and even if the first day of Yom Tov was Friday, so that until Sunday it will be impossible to bury him, and in a place where the gentiles do not allow work to be done on their festival day, so that he must lie until the second day, it is also forbidden through Jews.
{3} But gentiles should attend to him - If the first day of Yom Tov falls on Friday and the cemetery is farther from the city than a day's journey, and the gentiles will not arrive there until tomorrow, on Shabbat, it is forbidden, as below in seif 3. Rather, they should bury him through gentiles in his city, and after Shabbat they should take him out and bring him to the cemetery; or they should place him in a coffin and seal it with mortar and pitch through gentiles so that his odor not spread, and after Shabbat they should take him out and bury him.
{4} And see above siman 304 - Regarding slaves, whether one attends to the dead through them.
{5} And even if they can keep him, etc. - See Biur Halacha.
{6} And all this is with making the grave, etc. - For these are complete Torah-level melachot. The same applies that it is forbidden for him himself to cut a linen garment, whether what they cut by measure from the large piece or what they cut into small pieces in order to sew them afterward together, as is done with a garment, for all this is included in cutting [Chayei Adam]. The poskim wrote that even if there is some excavation in the cemetery where it is possible to place him until after Yom Tov, or even a complete grave that is not his and they will need to move him, it is permitted to dig on Yom Tov through gentiles, because it is not honor for the dead to move his bones and transfer him.
{7} And shrouds - Nevertheless, if it is possible to take prepared shrouds from someone and they are approximately his size, one certainly does not make them even through gentiles.
{8} But to dress him - For this is merely moving.
{9} And to heat, etc. - For none of this contains a Torah prohibition, since once kindling was permitted for food needs, it was also permitted not for such a need, and the same applies to carrying. Even according to the one who says that something entirely unnecessary is forbidden by Torah law, in a case like this, where there is some need and a mitzvah, and it would be degrading for gentiles to attend to his purification and the like, it is permitted because of the honor of the dead.
{10} And to take him out - Meaning to bury him. But to move him from the bed to the ground, as is practiced immediately after death, is permitted only through a loaf or child, as on Shabbat, for this moving is not a burial need.
{11} And to place him in the grave - But to cover him afterward with earth is permitted according to everyone only through gentiles, for filling a pit is like building.
{12} Permitted through a Jew - Nevertheless, to escort beyond the techum is forbidden, although techumin are also only rabbinic, as below in seif 6, and there it will be explained. Know that although the Mechaber copied this seif without dispute, several Rishonim disagree and say that the Sages did not permit a Jew in this matter for anything, since ultimately we need gentiles for the essential matter of burial, so they should do the other matters as well. This is also the view of Radbaz in a responsum, and the view of the Gra in his commentary also inclines this way. Nevertheless, one who wants to practice according to the Shulchan Aruch certainly should not be protested, for several poskim follow this approach.
{13} With straw, etc. - Meaning to purify him upon leather or a board and not upon garments; and with what should they wash? With straw, and the same applies with his hand, but not with sheets.
{14} Lest he come to wringing - Although from the moment he soaks it in water there is a concern of laundering, as above in siman 302, and he should not have mentioned only the concern of later wringing, one may say that ordinarily they wash the dead with clean and white sheets, and according to many poskim in this we do not say that soaking is laundering, as above in siman 302 seif 9. But there is a concern of wringing here, especially because after washing the sheet is soiled from the sweat of the dead and the like, and he may come to wring it, which is full laundering.
