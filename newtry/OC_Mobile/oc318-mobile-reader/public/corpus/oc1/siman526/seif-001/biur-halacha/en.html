And even if they can delay him until tomorrow — R' Yeshayah diShani wrote that where there is no custom one should be stringent and be concerned for the many authorities who do not permit on the first day of Yom Tov even through gentiles, unless there is great need, or he must be delayed, or because of heat and swelling; and this is from Or Zaru'a. He also wrote that according to this reasoning, for a dead infant, even with great need, one should not bury him on the first day through gentiles, for an infant's flesh does not spoil so quickly; also one may place him in a cold cellar; also there is not so much concern for disgrace among the living as with an adult — see there; and they cited this in Magen Avraham; and in Avodat HaKodesh he cited the beginning of his words. It is clear that for us, who are lenient regarding an adult even without great need, likewise for a minor one buries him on the first day through gentiles, even without great need; and so it is explained there explicitly in his wording — see Yeshayah diShani there. For practical law, certainly one need not be concerned for the main point of Yeshayah diShani in this, even where there is no custom, for according to his words one must wait until the second day and bury through Jews, like Rav Ashi's conclusion on the second day even without great need; but we hold the opposite — that it is forbidden to wait until the second day in order to bury through Jews, as explained in seif 2 in the Mechaber, and so all the later authorities cited. [In truth, in Yeshayah diShani this law was omitted entirely because it has no place according to his approach.] Thus it is clear that for us, on the contrary, there is shevut with a serious act involved; and it is astonishing that Magen Avraham brought this again. I also saw Beit Meir who challenged Yeshayah diShani on this and hinted to what is in seif 2; and perhaps his intent is what we challenged there — that his wording is abbreviated here; and in Shulchan Aruch the Gra omitted Yeshayah diShani as well.
