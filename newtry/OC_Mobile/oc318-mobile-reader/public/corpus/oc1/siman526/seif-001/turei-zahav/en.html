I saw fit to mention what is found in a new edition of Maharil — one hagahah: in the year 5324 a woman died here in Frankfurt on the first night of Sukkot and on the second day, etc., and they did not practice aninut on Yom Tov; it was Shabbat on the sixth day, and on the night of the sixth at Shabbat entry the scholars required the mourners in mourning — head wrapping and changing place, etc. — up to here. Those scholars ruled incorrectly, for head wrapping is not appropriate on Shabbat publicly as Rokeach brought in Yoreh De'ah siman 386: a mourner must wrap like now pulling the hat before his eyes all seven, and on Shabbat privately not publicly. Morenu Chayim z"l wrote when the chazan begins Barchu he raises the hat so it not be public; and so Shulchan Aruch siman 64: on Shabbat he removes his wrapping — how did they rule to begin wrapping on Shabbat? Likewise changing place: Shulchan Aruch siman 393 ruled on Shabbat they do not change place, but Rama brought there 11 that on Shabbat too they change — such is custom; meaning before Barchu begins he sits in changed place while still weekday, and since he began on weekday he sits there afterward too. It is simple: if the mourner comes to synagogue after Barchu he should not change place, for aninut begins publicly on Shabbat; all the more what the shamash announces "go out toward the mourner" as practiced before Barchu; all the more requiring mourning on Shabbat — none of this is correct, for what would be added for him on Shabbat more than chol hamoed where aninut is practiced privately; therefore this new hagahah was printed improperly and one should change nothing on that Shabbat from what was on chol hamoed.
