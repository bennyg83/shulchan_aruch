I saw fit to mention what was found in a new printing of Sefer Maharil, one gloss as follows: In the year 5264, here in the holy community of Frankfurt, a woman died on the first night of Sukkot, and on the second day, etc. They did not observe aninut on Yom Tov. Simchat Torah was on Friday, and on Friday night at the entry of Shabbat the scholars required the mourners to observe mourning with head covering and change of place, etc.; end quote. These scholars ruled improperly, for head covering is not appropriate publicly on Shabbat, as the Rokeach wrote, which I brought in Yoreh Deah siman 386: a mourner is obligated in head covering, as nowadays they draw the hat before his eyes all seven days, but on Shabbat privately and not publicly. My father-in-law z"l wrote that when the chazzan begins Barekhu he should raise the hat so it not be public. So too in Shulchan Aruch siman 64 it says that on Shabbat he removes his covering. How, then, did they rule that on Shabbat he should begin covering? The same applies to changing place: Shulchan Aruch siman 393 rules that on Shabbat they should not change their place, though the Rama brings there an opinion that even on Shabbat they change, and so is the custom. That means that before Barekhu begins he sits in the changed place while it is still weekday, and since he began during weekday he remains there afterward too. It is obvious that if the mourner came to synagogue after Barekhu, he should not change place, for that begins mourning publicly on Shabbat. All the more so where the shamash announces "go out to greet the mourner," as is customary before Barekhu. All the more so what they wrote, that they required him to mourn on Shabbat; all this is incorrect. What new thing happened to him on Shabbat more than he had on Chol HaMoed, when he also observes the law of aninut privately? Therefore this new gloss was printed improperly, and nothing should be changed on that Shabbat from what was done on Chol HaMoed.
