(א) (sk 11) Alas, etc. And although it appears from Rashi, etc., nevertheless it appears to me it is a textual error, etc. — so wrote the Rosh, first chapter of Moed Katan 8, on the Mishnah that one does not dig pits and graves on the moed (I brought this above sk 8): Rashi explained that he has not yet died but so that they may bury in them after the moed — forbidden because it is extra exertion. That which we say generally that even on the second day of Yom Tov Jews may deal with him — this refers to moving or another matter without exertion, such as washing him or shearing his hair; but digging — no. Rashi's words are astonishing, for he explained initially "one does not dig" for the need of those who will die — what relevance to bring from this "if one died on the second day of Yom Tov Jews may deal with him" — meaning to bury him that day? Also what he wrote "but digging — no" does not imply one may not dig a grave on the second day of Yom Tov — and that is not so, for the second day of Yom Tov is like weekday, etc. It appears to me to resolve his words: it was difficult for him — why is the second day of Yom Tov different? If one died on the second day of Yom Tov close to evening so there is no time to bury him that day, and nevertheless Jews deal with him that day for weekday burial needs — likewise on chol hamoed it should be permitted to dig graves for weekday burial needs; and explain that in such a case they permitted only moving and matters without exertion, but digging — no, since there is no time to bury them that day, etc.; and likewise forbidden to weave him a garment, etc.; and likewise on chol hamoed, etc. From his words we learn that if one died on the second day of Yom Tov and they do not want to bury him that day — they do no forbidden melacha act that day; and it appears forbidden even through a gentile, etc. — end of the Rosh. Turei Zahav siman 82 wrote: since the Rosh did not cite permission in such a case when they do not want to bury him that day, only permission of moving — it implies even carrying to karmelit is forbidden. And therefore Turei Zahav: and although it appears from Rashi, etc. He distinguished between a matter that is melacha, i.e., carrying out; and shearing hair which is not melacha. Maharshal disagrees that shearing hair is more melacha than carrying to karmelit; rather the distinction between shearing hair and other melachot is that any matter with exertion is forbidden since it is heavy melacha; unlike shearing hair — although it is d'oraisa melacha, since there is not much exertion and it is not heavy melacha, it is permitted; and all the more so carrying to karmelit is permitted per the Rosh. Afterward Maharshal wrote: nevertheless it appears to me it is a textual error, etc., and disagrees with the Rosh and wrote: how could he say like the Rosh — Rashi's difficulties from what he wrote "second day of Yom Tov like weekday" that he holds even if one died close to evening and cannot bury him today, nevertheless permitted to deal with him for tomorrow's burial — whence for Rashi that perhaps in such a case Jews do not deal with him at all? For necessarily, even according to Rashi's answer, he concludes: even to weave him a garment and shear his hair — it deals only when they want to bury him today and there is time for this; if so, likewise the beginning that said they made it like weekday also deals specifically when they will bury him this day; but if they will not bury him today, Jews do not deal with him at all for tomorrow's needs; therefore it is a textual error in the beginning. And so we must say in the beginning "one does not dig" — meaning to bury in them after the moed; or it deals with the dead who already died. That which we say generally that even on the second day of Yom Tov they deal, etc. — accordingly Rashi's difficulties are only per the second explanation that "one does not dig" deals with a dead person who already died; but per the first explanation that it deals for those who will die — and so we hold for practical law — if so, there is no proof between explanation 1 and 2 to permit anything when they do not want to bury him today, whether shearing hair or carrying. For moving, see above siman 310 seif 1 where he elaborated.

Rather in my humble opinion Magen Avraham's intent regarding shearing hair is that there is no melacha at all, even for R' Yehudah who holds melacha she'einah legra is liable; the reason as he wrote at the end: for if one circumcises an eight-day-old on Shabbat he does nothing, etc.; and although cutting skin and flesh of a living person is liable for detaching — as Taz wrote siman 336: and further one should beware not to detach from his skin, etc., for it is like attached to the ground, etc. — nevertheless for an eight-day-old, since he is considered dead, one who cuts his flesh is like cutting flesh in general and there is no prohibition; and what difference whether he circumcises and tears the skin or cuts hair — therefore for a dead person there is no issue of shearing and detaching.
