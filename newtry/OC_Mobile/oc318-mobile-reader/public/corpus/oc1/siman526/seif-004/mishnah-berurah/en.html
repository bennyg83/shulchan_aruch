{18} Even to cut myrtle for him: in places where they practice placing myrtle on the bier of the dead; and even though this is not among the primary needs of burial, but only for honor.
{19} And to make shrouds for him: to cut and to sew, even though it is possible to wrap the dead in a sheet without sewing. Nevertheless, if one can find ready-made shrouds of his measure, one should not make new ones, as we wrote above in se'if 1; and if they are soiled, it is permitted to wash them.
{20} And a coffin: if he has no boards ready, it is permitted to saw on Yom Tov; and see siman 547.
{21} And to hew a grave for him: some wrote that nevertheless, whatever one can minimize of desecration one should minimize; and therefore they wrote that where there are members of a society who are accustomed to dig, sew, and occupy themselves [with burial], others besides the members of the society may not occupy themselves with burying the dead, unless it is eve of Shabbos near nightfall, for then whoever is diligent to assist so that they not come to desecration of Shabbos is praiseworthy. And several Acharonim are lenient in this, because the second day of Yom Tov regarding the dead is like a weekday.
{22} And if there is none in that city, etc.: but if there is a cemetery, only that he commanded before his death to take him to the graves of his ancestors, one should not desecrate Yom Tov for this; and since in any case there is a mitzvah to fulfill the words of the dead, they leave him overnight until after Yom Tov, and afterward take him to the place of his ancestors. And if there is concern that he will putrefy, they bury him on Yom Tov in his city.
{23} And they hire a boat for him, etc.: or a wagon to take him to the place of burial; and it is also permitted to take him on a Jew's animal. And specifically the burial attendants are permitted to enter the boat; but other people, in order to escort him, are forbidden; and more will be explained below in se'if 7.
{24} The Rabbis made it like a weekday: nevertheless it is not exactly like a weekday, and whatever is forbidden on Chol HaMoed is forbidden on the second day of Yom Tov all the more so; for example, to hew stones for a grave, and likewise to cut trees from a forest in order to make a coffin from them, which is forbidden on Chol HaMoed, as explained in siman 547, and the same law applies here. And it is also explained there that to saw trees into boards to make a coffin from them, and to cut myrtle, and to sew shrouds, and the like, is permitted only in the courtyard of the dead and the like, so that all see that he is doing it for the dead; and regarding a well-known person who died, it is permitted to do even in the marketplace, for all know it is for him. And it is further explained there that in places where not many Jews live in the city, even regarding an ordinary person it is permitted to do all the melachos not in the place of the dead, for since Jews are few, when one dies all know. And whether it is permitted to go outside the techum to bring a dead person from there to bury him, or to bring shrouds and a coffin for a dead person, will be explained below in se'if 8.
{25} Rather, wherever it is possible through non-Jews: and specifically when there are non-Jews before us; but if one must wait many hours until non-Jews come, and thereby the dead will come to disgrace by swelling and the like, one need not wait, and Jews should occupy themselves with him.
{26} As on the first day of Yom Tov: nevertheless the Acharonim wrote that it is permitted to purify him with a sheet in the usual way, and we are not concerned lest he come to wringing, only that he be careful not to wring by hand; and it appears to me that they should be exacting that the sheets be clean, for if not, soaking them is their washing, as above in s.k. 14. And it is permitted to escort him outside the techum, as below in se'if 6, even though on the first day of Yom Tov it is forbidden; for nevertheless it is not a melachah.
{27} Except for the dome, etc.: that is, their practice was to build a structure, and this is forbidden, since he has already been buried; why should we desecrate Yom Tov?
{28} But it is permitted to cover him with earth: that is, even as they practice heaping earth on the grave until it becomes like a mound, for that is the completion of burial. And it is implied from the plain wording of the Rama that even according to our custom that we are stringent regarding full melachos to do them through a non-Jew, nevertheless regarding this they did not practice to be stringent.
{29} But if they do not wish: for example, if they leave him overnight for his honor. And the same law applies if it was near evening and there is no time to bury him; and see below in se'if 8 that sometimes it is permitted to begin occupying oneself for the needs of the dead on Yom Tov in order to hasten his burial on a weekday, and there it will be explained.
{30} But carrying is permitted: even through a Jew; and carrying out is not included in this, and even to a karmelis it is forbidden [Acharonim]. And the conclusion of most Acharonim is that even carrying is not permitted for the needs of the living, for example to clear him from the house so that kohanim may enter the house, or because of anguish of soul, unless it is a need of the dead, such as from sun to shade and the like; and even regarding this they are stringent that it is permitted only by means of a loaf and a child, and according to what is explained above in siman 311.
