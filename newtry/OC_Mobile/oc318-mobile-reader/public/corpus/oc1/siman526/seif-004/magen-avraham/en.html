On the second day of Yom Tov, etc. In Kenesses HaGedolah it implies that in the lands of Togarmah, where many Jews live, one should not permit burying him on the second day of Yom Tov through Jews except for a distinguished person, as Rabbeinu Yerucham wrote. Even if the matter became known in the city, that does not help. Going outside the techum is comparable to cutting cedars, which is forbidden even for a distinguished person, as in Tashbetz, siman 114.
Like a weekday, etc. But the second day of Yom Tov of the first days is like Chol HaMoed, as in Tashbetz, siman 404; Tosafot wrote that one should not distinguish, and so it is.
As on the first day of Yom Tov. In Yam Shel Shlomo he wrote that it is permitted to purify him with a sheet on the second day of Yom Tov, since it is only a decree lest one squeeze; one must only be careful not to squeeze actively.
To cover it with earth. For this is the completion of burial, as in the Beis Yosef. At first glance, according to our practice this is forbidden, for Terumas HaDeshen wrote that our practice is not to perform labor according to the Torah, and the Beis Yosef wrote that closing the grave is labor according to the Torah. In Sefer Raavan he wrote regarding the first day of Yom Tov that non-Jews should cover him. In the Taz in Yoreh Deah he wrote: I saw some places where Jews return the earth into the grave, and in my eyes this is a complete prohibition, etc.; see there. In Levush he wrote that on the second day of Yom Tov some forbid and some permit, and one may be lenient, end quote. Perhaps he holds that it is not labor according to the Torah, for we say in Shabbos, folio 81, that one may not relieve himself in a plowed field on Shabbos, for perhaps he will take a clod from above and throw it below; one who fills a hole in a field is liable because of plowing, and in a house because of building. This implies that in a field that is not plowed he is not liable at all. However, perhaps this is comparable to a house, since he repairs it thereby so that the dead will not be exposed. Therefore it seems to me that it is forbidden. In Sefer Masas Binyamin he was very stringent and wrote that in a place where the custom is to bury through Jews, no other men should dig except the members of the society accustomed to digging, and all the more so other people should not throw earth to close the grave, for whatever can be minimized in desecration, we minimize, end quote. Responsa of Shlomo ben Shimon Duran, siman 16, permits everyone; see there. This appears correct to me, for the Sages treated it like a weekday, and they said something even greater regarding circumcision on Shabbos, that two mohalim may circumcise, as written in Yoreh Deah; see siman 266. All the more so here.
But moving is permitted. But it is forbidden to take him out even to a karmelis. Although it implies from Rashi and the Rosh that it is permitted to wash him and cut his hair, nevertheless carrying out is more of a labor, for cutting hair on a corpse is not a labor at all, as in Terumas HaDeshen. In Yam Shel Shlomo he objected: the Rambam wrote in chapter 9 that one who shears, whether from a living being or from a corpse, is liable, and so it is in the Yerushalmi, chapter Klal Gadol. Rather, the view of the Rosh according to Rashi must be that a weighty labor is forbidden but carrying is permitted. Nevertheless, it seems to me that there is a textual error in Rashi and one must emend it; see there. Accordingly, it is forbidden to do anything for the need of tomorrow, end quote; see seif 8. It does not seem so to me, for what Terumas HaDeshen wrote, that cutting hair is not a labor at all, means because he has no need of the hair at all, whereas the Rambam speaks where he needs the hair; see what I wrote in siman 340. Although carrying out is also a labor not needed for its own sake, nevertheless the name carrying applies to it. But here it is not a labor at all, for we say in Shabbos that if one circumcised an eight-month infant on Shabbos, he has done nothing, for it is merely cutting flesh. All the more so hair. Nevertheless, it is good to be stringent, and so it appears from Terumas HaDeshen and the Shulchan Aruch.
