"On the second day of Yom Tov," etc. Knesset HaGedolah implies that in the lands of Turkey, where many Jews reside, one should not permit burial on the second day of Yom Tov through Jews except for a prominent person, as Rabbeinu Yerucham writes. Even if the death became publicly known throughout the city, this does not suffice. Going beyond the techum is comparable to cutting cedar trees and is forbidden even for a prominent person. "Like a weekday," etc. Some write that the second day of the first days of Yom Tov has the status of Chol HaMoed for this purpose, while Tosafos writes that no distinction should be made. "Like the first day of Yom Tov." Melacha that is not necessary for the burial remains forbidden. The authorities discuss acts performed for the dignity of the deceased but not essential to burial, including cutting hair and similar preparations. Terumat HaDeshen writes that cutting the hair of a corpse is not a melacha at all where one has no use for the hair. Rambam's discussion of cutting hair refers to a case where the hair itself is needed. Although carrying is likewise ordinarily a melacha not needed for its own sake, the act is still halachically called carrying; cutting flesh or hair from a body where nothing is accomplished through the removed material can be more lenient. The Gemara similarly says that one who cuts the flesh of a nonviable 8-month infant on Shabbos has done nothing in the halachic sense, because it is merely cutting flesh. All the more so hair. Nevertheless, it is proper to be stringent, as appears from Terumat HaDeshen and the Shulchan Aruch. The general principle is that although the Sages treated the second day of Yom Tov leniently for burial needs, one should minimize melacha as far as possible and not perform acts that are merely for later convenience or that can be omitted without impairing the burial. Where Jewish participation itself is subject to dispute, the local custom and the distinctions concerning a prominent deceased person, the techum, and acts requiring a full melacha must all be observed.
