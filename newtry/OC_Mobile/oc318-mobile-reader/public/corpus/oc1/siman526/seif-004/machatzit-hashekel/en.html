(2) Yeshuot Shani wrote, etc. — for it says in Beitzah 6a: Rava said: if one died on the first day of Yom Tov, gentiles may attend to him; if on the second day of Yom Tov, Jews may attend to him, even on the two days of Rosh Hashana. Mar Zutra said: we only said this when it remained over; but if it did not remain, we do not delay him. R' Eliezer said: even if it did not remain we do not delay him. Why? The second day of Yom Tov regarding the dead the Sages made like weekday. Rishonim disputed this sugya: some say Mar Zutra addresses the entire matter — both first Yom Tov for gentiles and second for Jews — requiring that it remained; R' Eliezer only disagreed on second Yom Tov, but on first Yom Tov he agrees if it did not remain we delay and burial is not permitted even through gentiles. Some say Mar Zutra himself agrees on first Yom Tov even if it did not remain they bury through gentiles, and he only addresses second Yom Tov for Jews — here we require specifically that it remained; R' Eliezer disagrees that also on second Yom Tov even if it did not remain Jews may attend. We rule like some disputants and like R' Eliezer; if so, both on first Yom Tov through gentiles and on second through Jews it is permitted even if it did not remain. So too Ran — such is Jewish custom. On this Yeshuot Shani wrote: specifically where they are accustomed thus; but where there is no custom one should be stringent per the first view that Mar Zutra addresses both first and second and R' Eliezer only disagreed on second; but on first Yom Tov he agrees we require specifically that it remained; therefore practice on first Yom Tov: not permitted through gentiles except when it remained — when the first day of Yom Tov falls after Shabbat on Sunday and he died on Shabbat; or it requires waiting — when first Yom Tov is erev Shabbat and he died on erev Shabbat, then permitted through gentiles; but otherwise not permitted through a gentile and they must delay him:
