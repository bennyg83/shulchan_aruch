Se'if 4. And to hew a grave for him and to heat water for him: and it is somewhat difficult, for this is permitted even on the first day of Yom Tov through a Jew.
Magen Avraham, s.k. 14. And if so, it is permitted to move them so they not be stolen; and likewise also "so they not be stolen" is how it must be read: so it is in the Ramban. That is, even if you say that it is not an item whose melachah is for a permitted [use], for with an item whose melachah is for a forbidden [use] it is forbidden to move it except for need of its body and its place: nevertheless here, where there is concern that they be stolen, it is permitted because of "their end because of their beginning," so that he not refrain, fearing they may be stolen. And the Ramban concluded: and even a house next to the wall that is secured for them, it is permitted to return them to whatever house they wish, for an item whose melachah is for a permitted [use] is permitted even from sun to shade; end quote. This implies that this is the permit because it is called an item whose melachah is for a permitted [use]; but if it were called an item whose melachah is for a forbidden [use], he would need to leave [them] in the nearby secured house, for "their end because of their beginning" does not apply, since he will not refrain given that they are secured. And it is difficult for me: does not Magen Avraham write above, siman 266, se'if 19, that if muktzeh is already in his hand he may move it to whatever place he wishes? If so, since they permit him, because of his beginning, to take it and place it in the nearby house, automatically it is permitted to return it to his house, since the muktzeh is in his hand. And even according to the view of Even HaOzer there: nevertheless he holds that regarding a vessel whose melachah is for a forbidden [use], since he has a permit for need of its body and its place, they are also lenient if it is in his hand, that moving is permitted; if so here, even if burial tools are called items whose melachah is for a forbidden [use], it should be permitted to return them to his house.
