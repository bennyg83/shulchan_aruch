Although the body will not decompose, it is nevertheless an honor not to leave it overnight, for one who hastens the bier is praiseworthy. This is also stated in Yoreh Deah, siman 357. Furthermore, delay causes additional distress to the relatives. Beis Yosef in the name of several halachic authorities; Levush. Nevertheless, see Beis Yosef, who cites an opinion that a body is not buried by non-Jews on the first day of Yom Tov if it can be preserved until the next day. See there. Yam Shel Shlomo, first chapter of Beitzah, siman 10, writes that in a place without an established custom, it is proper not to bury through non-Jews on the first day of Yom Tov unless the body has already remained for some time, will need to be delayed further, or has become swollen. Accordingly, he writes that a young child, even after thirty days, can be left overnight in a cellar. Nor does the concern that the deceased will be disgraced among the living apply to a child as it does to an adult. It is therefore preferable not to bury the child on the first day of Yom Tov. See there. Magen Avraham, subsection 4; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 2; Chemed Moshe, subsection 1, cite this. Beis Meir, however, disputes the aforementioned words of Yam Shel Shlomo and writes that according to Shulchan Aruch in se'if 2, greater concern should be given to the prohibition against leaving the deceased overnight. Beis Yosef may be relied upon even in a place without an established custom. See there. Shulchan Aruch HaRav also omits the ruling of Yam Shel Shlomo, implying that the halachah does not follow it. Chochmas Adam, rule 170, subsection 1, writes that it requires further study. Mishnah Berurah in Biur Halachah likewise writes that in practical halachah one certainly should not take Yam Shel Shlomo's ruling into account, even in a place without an established custom. See there. Regarding a child and a nonviable infant, see below, se'ifim 9 and 10.
