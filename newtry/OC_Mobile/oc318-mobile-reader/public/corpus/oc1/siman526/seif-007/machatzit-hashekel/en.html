(Seif katan 7) On Yom Tov, etc. Where many Jews reside, etc. The Mishnah in Moed Katan 9b states, “and a coffin with the deceased in the courtyard.” Meaning, on Chol HaMoed one may make a coffin only in the courtyard where the deceased lies, but not in another courtyard, where people would not know that someone died and would suspect that it was being made for another purpose. For this reason the Tur writes in the name of Ri that work performed for a corpse on the second day of Yom Tov should be done discreetly, since the second day is certainly no more lenient than Chol HaMoed. The Yerushalmi, however, states that if a prominent person died, the coffin may be made even in another courtyard, because his death is publicized and people know that it is being made for him. Therefore, nowadays, when only a small number of Jews live in each city, every person has the status of a prominent person, since his death becomes known to all. This is how we rule below in siman 547, seif 10. Knesses HaGedolah therefore writes that in Turkey, where many Jews live in one city, the law reverts to that of the Gemara and to Ri's ruling. Even if the death happened to become publicized, this does not help if the deceased was not a prominent person, because the Sages did not distinguish.
For cutting cedars, etc. Because the matter becomes highly publicized, as written below in siman 547 regarding Chol HaMoed.
