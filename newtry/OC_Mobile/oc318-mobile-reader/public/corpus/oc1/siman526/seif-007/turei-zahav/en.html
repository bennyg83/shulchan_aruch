If impossible they permit them, etc. — second Yom Tov for his needs in every impossible case they made like weekday; so Beit Yosef in the name of Ramban; it requires study on Shulchan Aruch, for Ramban also holds where gentiles are common it is permitted through Jews, as in seif 4; and further Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Rashba: we do not heed techumim for the dead at all on second Yom Tov; Rama likewise in the following seif — even if burial is possible in his place; here too riding prohibition is derabbanan — why did Ramban write here they only permitted when impossible? It appears this "impossible" does not mean utterly impossible, but not easily except through some effort and pain — somewhat is also permitted; nevertheless it requires study what he wrote from above; for practical law it appears as written: where there is some pain or effort it is permitted, even per Rama who is stringent where possible through gentiles — meaning d'oraisa melacha; not so here in a derabbanan matter, as it appears clear to me:
