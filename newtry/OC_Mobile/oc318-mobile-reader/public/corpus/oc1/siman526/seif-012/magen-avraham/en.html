Before prayer. In order that the onenim will be able to pray. In our times, when everything is done through non-Jews, they should not bury them until after eating, as in Mateh Binyamin at the end of the book. It implies that his view is that since they bury through non-Jews, the onenim are permitted to pray. This requires study, for it is stated in siman 71 that they are exempt from prayer. Perhaps he holds that since they do not want to bury him until after eating, they are obligated to pray, as at night, as written in siman 548. In Yam Shel Shlomo he wrote: I have seen the practice that they bury before eating. Although Kol Bo wrote before prayer, in our country it is impossible to do so early. But what they wrote, to bury him after eating, does not seem correct to me. If they say this because of the joy of Yom Tov, this is not so, for if a meis mitzvah precedes reading the Megillah, all the more so does he precede the joy of Yom Tov. In our times everyone is considered a meis mitzvah. Furthermore, it is stated in the Midrash that it is forbidden to eat a fixed meal before the dead is buried. Therefore, if he is an important person, they bury him immediately after leaving the synagogue; and if not, they gather a minyan and bury him while the shaliach tzibbur is saying piyyutim, end quote. Nowadays the practice is to bury him in the morning, and for the most part it extends into the time of prayer, and one must be careful about this; see siman 72, seif 2. It seems to me that if he is an important person whom many people must escort, they should take him out to the cemetery after leaving the synagogue, eat, and afterward bury him properly, as written in siman 443.
