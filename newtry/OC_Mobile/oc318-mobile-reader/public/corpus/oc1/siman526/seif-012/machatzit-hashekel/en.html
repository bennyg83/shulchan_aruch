(Seif katan 12) It is permitted, etc. As in the law of chalitzah. Yevamos 106b, and so we rule in Even HaEzer 169:50: if a yevamah falls before a yavam who is unsuitable for her and he refuses to perform chalitzah unless she gives him two hundred zuz, he may be deceived by promising payment after the chalitzah, and afterward she need not pay him anything. The same is done here, and it is permitted for the sake of a mitzvah. Beit Yosef explains there that Rashi gives the reason that Shabbos wages are forbidden because they resemble commerce. Commerce itself is prohibited only D'Rabbanan, so Shabbos wages resemble a decree upon a decree. The prohibition is therefore only an added stringency, and was permitted for a mitzvah. Taz, however, writes below in siman 585 that those who permit payment for a mitzvah must reconcile the opposing view with the baraisa stating that one who hires a worker to guard the red heifer or grain for the Omer may not pay him for Shabbos, although this is for a mitzvah. He therefore concludes that even according to the permissive opinion, it is allowed only for a mitzvah that is an obligation of that day, such as shofar blowing on Rosh Hashanah. The same must apply here: the deceased lies before him and must be buried that day so as not to violate the prohibition against leaving a corpse overnight, and it is therefore a mitzvah that is the obligation of the day.
