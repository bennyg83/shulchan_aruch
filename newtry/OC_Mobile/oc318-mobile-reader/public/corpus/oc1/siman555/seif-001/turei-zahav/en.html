It is written in Tur: Rabbeinu Hai — a mourner whose seventh day fell on Tisha B'Av: what is forbidden on Tisha B'Av, such as bathing, anointing, wearing shoes, marital relations — remains in its prohibition the entire day; but things permitted on it and forbidden for a mourner, such as wrapping the head and turning over the bed and removal of tefillin — the choice is in his hands: if he wishes to wait until evening he waits, and if he wishes to remove mourning customs immediately he does so. And I do not understand what he wrote "things permitted on it and forbidden for a mourner" such as removal of tefillin, for behold even a mourner is permitted from day one and on, end of his words. And Beit Yosef strained to answer that Rabbeinu Hai holds like R' Yehoshua who said in chapter Elu Meguyyadot: a mourner — the first two days it is forbidden to lay tefillin; from the second day and the second day included it is permitted to lay tefillin; and if new faces came he removes them; and it is implied for Rabbeinu Hai that all days of mourning if new faces came he removes; and now he says that if his seventh fell on Tisha B'Av and new faces came before the comforters stood up from beside him and he removed tefillin — if he wishes to wait to lay until evening he waits, and if he wishes to lay them after the comforters stood from beside him he lays, end of his words. And I wondered at the explanation in what he wrote "if he wished to wait" — that is, to lay tefillin again after removal — what difference does it make to us when he lays them again, and what relevance is this to mourning or Tisha B'Av, for behold on the second time it is permitted to return and lay them again; and furthermore Rabbeinu Hai said it is forbidden due to mourning, and this second laying is permitted even during mourning. And it appears to me to explain that certainly Beit Yosef wrote well here that it deals with removing tefillin after new faces came; only in what Beit Yosef wrote that he holds like R' Yehuda — this is impossible, for Ula said explicitly the halacha is like R' Eliezer regarding removal, meaning when new faces came he does not remove; and Maharachatz z"l challenged this as well; rather it appears to me that R' Eliezer and R' Yehuda disagree regarding removal whether he is obligated to remove — R' Eliezer holds he is not obligated to remove and R' Yehuda holds he is obligated to remove; and if so, one who wishes to be stringent on himself and remove may do so, for thus it is explicit there on daf 21 in several matters; and perhaps he does an extra matter in several matters; and if so the words of Rabbeinu Hai are explained in this way: we already rule that on the seventh day part of it is like all of it; and in matters forbidden on Tisha B'Av certainly forbidden the entire day until night; and therefore Rabbeinu Hai said that regarding what is forbidden due to Tisha B'Av and due to mourning — forbidden the entire day. And our dispute here "part of it like all of it" since from the side of Tisha B'Av it is forbidden the entire day — and this itself teaches that on Tisha B'Av forbidden the entire day what is forbidden on it; but in matters permitted due to Tisha B'Av and forbidden due to mourning, such as wrapping the head and turning over the bed and removal of tefillin — per R' Yehuda; and our dispute regarding one who wishes to do an extra matter as is customary to remove tefillin all days of mourning; and if so in this matter there are two views: one — since from the side of mourning it is permitted immediately, meaning after the comforters stood — likewise permitted on this seventh day which is Tisha B'Av; and there is another view: since on this day there is prohibition in the prohibitions of Tisha B'Av until night, it extends also to prohibitions due to mourning, even though at another time it would be "part of it like all of it"; nevertheless on this day the prohibition extends the entire day; in this Rabbeinu Hai said the choice is in his hands: if he wishes to wait — meaning the matter of his prohibition that he wishes to extend until evening he waits, and there is reason: since the prohibition of Tisha B'Av extends until night, likewise mourning prohibition extends on this day; and if he wishes to remove mourning customs immediately — meaning after the comforters stood he may do so, even though the prohibition of Tisha B'Av extends until night — nevertheless it does not drag with it the mourning prohibition; this appears very correct in explanation of Rabbeinu Hai's words.
