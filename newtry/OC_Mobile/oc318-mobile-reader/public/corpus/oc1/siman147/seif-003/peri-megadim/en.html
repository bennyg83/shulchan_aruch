Meaning: when the scroll — Ateret Zekenim, explicitly. Beit Yosef elaborated in the explanations, and Taz explains: when one person does everything — hagbahah and showing to the people — and afterward gives the Torah scroll into another's hand to hold, and he rolls: he turns the writing toward himself and begins to fasten the cloth (which is called a mappah) specifically on the side outside relative to him, i.e., behind the Torah scroll, which is opposite the one holding the Torah scroll; and from there he begins to fasten the cloth and afterward brings it toward him, opposite his face. And the reason is like Chanukah candles in siman 676, letter 16: whatever has corners — beginning and end — one begins from the right corner toward the left; and like the Chanukah candle, his intent is to begin with the rightmost candle at the start, and afterward turn to the left; here too he begins on his right side, and it is from outside, etc. And what Rama wrote, etc. — I did not understand now.
