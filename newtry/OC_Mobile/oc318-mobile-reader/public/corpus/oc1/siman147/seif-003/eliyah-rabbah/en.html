This requires study, for on the contrary, the incense altar was covered with gold on the outside. Sefer Chassidim, siman 535, compares it to the boards of the Mishkan, which were covered with gold on the inside. Magen Avraham writes that there appears to be a scribal error in Mordechai, and it should read, “like the Ark.” Agudah at the end of Megillah likewise writes that with respect to the mantles, meaning the wrapping cloth, the silk should face the Sefer Torah, while on the outer mantle the silk should face outward, like the Ark, which was gold inside and outside with wood in the middle. This is strained. I am also astonished that he altered the wording of Agudah, which states: “When they cover the Sefer Torah with a cloth, and there are two or three cloths, the lower cloth should be turned toward the Sefer Torah, and the beautiful upper cloth should face outward.” This discusses a cloth whose two sides are alike, not one that itself has different materials on its two sides. In the latter case, perhaps the outer mantle does not help. I would explain Mordechai as referring to the golden altar being inside and the copper altar outside. Still, this is not a complete proof concerning a cloth whose silk is wrapped around the Sefer Torah as well. Perhaps this is why Rama writes that the practice is not to be stringent. Magen Avraham nevertheless writes that one should follow this rule. In any event, a scrupulous person should make the cloth identical on both sides, even if he places linen in the middle. Agudah writes there that one should be careful not to reverse the cloths so that the sides normally facing inward face outward, just as the boards of the Mishkan were marked to preserve their positions.
