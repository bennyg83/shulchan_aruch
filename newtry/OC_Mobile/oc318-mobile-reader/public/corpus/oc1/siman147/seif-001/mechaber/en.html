The Laws of Rolling the Torah Scroll. Containing 8 S'ifim: It is forbidden to grasp a Torah scroll bare [handed], without a hand-cloth. The greatest among those who read the Torah [i.e. received an aliyah] rolls [the scroll closed], and it is typical to buy it [i.e. this honor] with a large sum of money to foster love of the mitzvah [i.e. the rolling].
It is forbidden to grasp a Torah scroll bare [handed], without a hand-cloth. The greatest among those who read the Torah [i.e. received an aliyah] rolls [the scroll closed], and it is typical to buy it [i.e. this honor] with a large sum of money to foster love of the mitzvah [i.e. the rolling].Gloss: And there are those who say that the same applies to the rest of the sacred writings (Agur and Tosefot - first chapter of Tractate Shabbat), but we do not practice this way; and it is good to be stringent if one has not washed his hands; but regarding a Torah scroll, even in this case [where one has washed] it is forbidden. (His own opinion and the Agudah) Gloss:) There are those who say that if the [Torah] cover is on one side [made] of linen and on the other side, silk, it is necessary to turn the silk towards the side of the scroll and to roll [it around the scroll that way] (Mordechai - end of [Tractate] Megillah), but we do not practice such. (Beit Yosef - siman 123). The Torah scroll should not be rolled using a torn material if one has another [un-torn one that can be used]. (Mahari"l). And we do not make a Torah scroll covers from old things which were [initially] made for a ordinary use; see below in siman 153.
