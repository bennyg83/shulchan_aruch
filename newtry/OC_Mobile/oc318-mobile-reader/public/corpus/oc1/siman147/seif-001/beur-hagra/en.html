Seif 1: Some say, etc. Tosafos, Shabbos 14a, in the passage beginning “one who holds, etc.” Bach and Magen Avraham write that even holding the wooden rollers is forbidden because they render the hands impure, as Rash writes at the end of chapter 3 of Yadayim in the name of the Tosefta concerning the poles, straps, case, etc. This is not a proof. Tefillin prove otherwise, for they render the hands impure, as stated in Yadayim, chapter 2, mishnah 2, yet Tosafos, Chagigah 24b, in the passage beginning “as we learned,” explain differently, and Ri answers, etc. Rash likewise writes this in mishnah 2. Nevertheless, the unqualified word “scroll” implies all sacred writings.
It is proper, etc. Mordechai writes that the prohibition applies only if one did not wash his hands. At first glance this is also implied by the Gemara’s question concerning which decree was enacted first. Tosafos there, however, in the passage beginning “since, etc.,” hold that the prohibition applies even after washing. The Mechaber therefore rules here in accordance with his own conclusion.
The greatest, etc. Rashi, Ran, and Baal HaMaor, in his first explanation, understand “the greatest” to mean the greatest among those present in the synagogue. In Baal HaMaor’s alternate explanation it means the greatest among those who were called to the Torah. The ten are the seven who read from the Torah, the translator, the chazzan, and the maftir. The first explanation is primary.
They are accustomed, etc. See the end of chapter 3 of Sukkah concerning “even for one thousand zuz, etc.”
Some say, etc. This is based upon the verse, “Solomon overlaid the interior of the House, etc.”
This is not the custom. This is also the correct ruling. No proof can be brought from that verse because the covering remained visible. A proof may be brought from behind the doors, which were not plated with gold, as stated in chapter 4 of Middos. Another proof comes from tefillin and mezuzos. Tefillin do not require ruled lines, whereas the conclusion is that a mezuzah does require ruled lines because it is examined twice in seven years, unlike tefillin, which are never examined.
One should not roll, etc. This resembles the law of priestly garments in chapter 2 of Zevachim, where torn garments are invalid. See Rashi there.
One should not make, etc. As stated at the end of Zevachim 116b: just as the altar must not, etc. The Tosefta, chapter 2 of Megillah, likewise states this, and Magen Avraham cites it concerning utensils and stones, etc.
