[1] A bare Sefer Torah, etc. It is proper for the reader, the one who raises it, and the one who rolls it to be careful to hold even the poles of the Sefer Torah through the corner of a tallis. Bach. So too Responsa Maharit, siman 136. Taz is lenient and writes that even meticulous people follow the lenient practice. Magen Avraham, however, brings proof from the fact that the poles impart impurity to the hands like the parchment sheets. In my humble opinion, this may be because the Sages did not distinguish, and it is comparable to tefillin, which impart impurity to the hands although they are touched bare. The same applies even to sacred writings according to Rama. Tosafos in Megillah 7a are also only uncertain whether the prohibition applies to sacred writings. Magen Avraham writes that it applies specifically when they are written in Ashuris script, on parchment, and with ink, like Megillas Esther, but our printed sefarim are permitted. This implies even before washing one’s hands. Rama’s statement that the custom is not to be stringent regarding sacred writings may be understood as referring to a Megillah, since other printed sefarim are always permitted. This applies, however, only to ordinary hands. If they are known to be dirty, one should be stringent.
