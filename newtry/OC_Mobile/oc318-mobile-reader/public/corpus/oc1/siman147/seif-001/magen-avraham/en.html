Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Mordekhai: if he washed his hands and did not distract his attention, it is permitted to hold [the scroll] naked; and on this the Ashkenazim rely, that they hold the Torah scroll naked at the time they raise it — until here Beit Yosef. And he wrote: Darkei Moshe — and we are children of their children, and I did not see this custom; but rather the opposite — they are careful not to touch the Torah scroll naked. But in holy writings I have not seen in my days anyone who was concerned about this — until here. And it is possible that anything clean suffices — siman 4; and Bach explains that even on the atzei chayim it is forbidden to hold if not by means of a cloth or gloves, etc.; and so Ri'az, siman 136 [thus]. And with this the Ashkenazim are lenient, and one should be stringent — until here. And so Rambam wrote, chapter 9 of Laws of Impurity of Hands, and these are his words: the handles and straps that one sewed onto a book — even though one is not permitted to maintain them, as long as they are attached to the book they impart impurity to the hands; a case of a book, a box of a book, and cloths of books — while they are sewn impart impurity to the hands; the blessings, even though they have letters of the Name, do not impart impurity — until here. And so Ran in the name of Tosefta. If so, all the more so on the atzei chayim; and on Shabbat daf 19 it says that the main reason hands become impure is so that one not hold them naked; and if so, it is proven that it is forbidden to hold them naked, even on the atzei chayim. And from what Rambam wrote that one is not permitted to maintain them, we learn that it is forbidden to sew anything onto the body of the book if it is not needed for its preservation, such as the case and the like. Holy scriptures: so too Tosafot, and they proved thus from impurity of hands; if so, also the straps of tefillin, and all the more so the tefillin themselves, are forbidden to hold naked, for behold they impart impurity to the hands. However, in Chagigah daf 24 Tosafot wrote: the straps of tefillin with the tefillin — the law of R' Pinchas does not apply, etc.; and so Rash in tractate Yadayim. If so, it is implied that it is permitted to hold them naked. However, holy scriptures specifically — when they are written in Assyrian script on hide with ink, as stated in the Mishnah; and so Rambam. If so, Rama wrote well that he did not see in his days anyone concerned about this, for our books are not written in this manner. And what he wrote here "and it is good to be stringent" is an extra matter; however, for a megillah that is written properly, one must be careful, for Rambam there does not hold like Shmuel, who said Esther does not impart impurity to hands.
Those who read. And some say: the greatest in the synagogue rolls, even though he did not read in the Torah.
They are accustomed to buy it. This requires study: since the greatest one rolls, what is the matter of buying it? And one must say: nevertheless, one who contributes more money has precedence over the greatest, in order to endear the mitzvah.
The silk toward the book. It is similar to the altar of incense (Mordekhai); and this requires study, for on the contrary, the altar was coated with gold on the outside; and in Agudah at the end of Megillah he wrote that the lower coat — i.e., the cloth — the silk should be toward the Torah scroll; and the upper coat should have the silk toward the outside, similar to the Ark, which was gold inside and outside with wood in the middle — until here. And it appears to me that there is a scribal error in Mordekchai, and it should read: similar to the Ark; and so one should practice; and so in Sofer Chayim siman 532, and he brings proof from the beams that were coated with gold on the inside and the curtains on the outside.
Old materials. It is written in Taz, Yoreh Deah siman 283: an implement of an implement is permitted — until here. However, in the responsum of Maharil siman 121 he wrote that even a curtain is forbidden to make from old materials; and our curtain is an implement of an implement, as written siman 154, s.k. 6. And in Tosafot chapter 2 of Megillah it says: vessels that were made from the outset for a commoner — one does not make them for holy use; stones and beams that were hewn from the outset for a commoner — one does not build with them on the Temple Mount. And Rambam wrote, chapter 1 of Laws of the Chosen House, that it implies that even if they are new, whatever was made for a commoner is forbidden. However, the world is accustomed to make even from old materials, as Maharil wrote; and it appears to me that he holds that the Beit HaMikdash is different. And further it appears to me: specifically as they are, it is forbidden to use them; but if one changed their form and made from them another implement, it is permitted — for behold the basin was made from the mirrors of the midwives. And even though on the altar wood is required that a commoner not have used it, one may say that the altar is different; and so in the Gemara. And see Yalkut on Megillah; and further, behold offerings are brought from animals that a commoner used.
