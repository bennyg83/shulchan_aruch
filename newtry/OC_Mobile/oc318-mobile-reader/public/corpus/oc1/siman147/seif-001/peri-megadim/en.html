Forbidden — Taz. It is lenient to hold the poles of a Torah scroll without interruption from here, and proof from Rashi Megillah 32a: "naked without a cloth" — my grandfather in a Torah book — and it is not enough "without a cloth" but to exclude the poles; and Mahari Trani part 1 siman 136 holds: when the Torah scroll is in a cloth one may touch the poles thus; but when the Torah scroll is naked — even on the poles it is forbidden to touch, lest he come to touch the Torah scroll itself; and so he deduced from Rambam chapter 10 of Laws of Torah Scroll (6): "and one should not hold a Torah scroll when it is naked" — and the word "when he" is superfluous, for "naked" refers not to a naked person, as is clear from Shabbat 14a "hands from the book" — meaning when the book is naked it is forbidden even on the poles, and when it is covered it is permitted on the poles; and the reason is lest he touch the Torah scroll itself — see there; and if so there is no proof from Rashi, as stated. And therefore Beit Yosef wrote well that he saw Ashkenazim who are not careful when they lift and hold "naked" — meaning the poles, as Bach wrote; and what they are not careful at the time of the blessing — one may say they cover with a cloth, even though one should not cover as explained in siman 139 seif 4 — see there. And Tosafot Shabbat 34a "the one who holds" — somewhat implies not a Torah scroll; and one may say Rav Parnok — Torah scroll naked even poles they decreed in Torah scroll for it is stringent; and in other books specifically on the mantles, not on the poles; and so Rashi Shabbat "Torah scroll naked" — only by cloth, by way of exclusion — for there is no remedy even for poles except by cloth; and in Megillah 32a he explained "without cloth" — and it is not clear why he changed from Shabbat; and per my opinion it is well explained: for in Megillah he specified "naked without cloth" around the Torah scroll — he fixed thereby the word "naked" that it is forbidden to hold even on poles when the book is naked, and excluded "when he" when the person is naked, as Maharit wrote — and this suffices; not so in Shabbat: we learned "hands from the book" from all books of Scripture — and how did he say "one who holds Torah scroll naked" — what is the reason? Certainly so is holy writings, as Tosafot there "the one who holds Torah scroll" wrote; and therefore he fixed and specified on the words "Torah scroll naked" — only by cloth — meaning in Torah scroll they decreed "one who holds naked" — even on poles forbidden lest he touch the mantles, as Maharit wrote; not so in other holy writings they did not decree so much; and how would there be holding that the roller must hold it and show the writing to the people? Therefore he said "only by cloth" — he may hold the poles since he holds naked; but when it is in a cloth he can hold it on the poles thus — so it appears from Rashi Megillah, as Maharit wrote — and see Pri Chadash here; and it will be explained further in Magen Avraham:
