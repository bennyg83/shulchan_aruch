And it is proper to be stringent if he did not wash his hands, and regarding a Sefer Torah even, etc. The reason is that Mordechai, in the name of Raavyah, is lenient even regarding a Sefer Torah, permitting one to hold it bare if he washed his hands and did not divert his attention from guarding them. This is also implied by Rashba, Shabbos 14. Tosafos there on 14b, however, prohibits it, and this is also the view of the Agudah. Ritva likewise writes explicitly in Megillah 7 that even other sacred writings may not be held bare, and they render the hands impure even after washing. Therefore, regarding sacred writings, where it is in any event not clear that one must be stringent, since Tosafos there, s.v. ha-ocher, writes only that it is somewhat implied, and Responsa Radbaz, part 2, siman 771, states that sacred writings are not included in the law of a Sefer Torah in this respect, Rama ruled that after washing one may rely upon Mordechai and those who agree with him to be lenient.
