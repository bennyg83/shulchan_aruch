1) [Seif 1] It is forbidden to hold a bare Sefer Torah, etc. For so it is [stated] at the end of Megillah and in Shabbos, folio 14a: Rabbi Parnach said in the name of Rabbi Yochanan: anyone who holds a bare Sefer Torah is buried bare, without that mitzvah. For the Tosafos wrote there: some explain 'the mitzvah of holding' [is lost], and it is difficult -- what is the novelty? It is obvious that he has no reward, since he did not perform the mitzvah properly. Therefore the Riva explained, 'without that mitzvah' that he performed at that time: that if he held a bare Sefer Torah and read from it, he has no reward from the reading; and likewise if he held it to roll it or to correct it. End of his language. And so the Tosafos wrote in Shabbos there; and so wrote the Rosh; and the Beis Yosef cited it. And it is implied from the words of the Gemara and the explanation of the aforementioned Riva that this [prohibition] that it is forbidden to hold a bare Sefer Torah is specifically if there is a mitzvah in that holding, such as at the time of hagbahah or the reading or the gelilah after the reading, or if he needs to correct something in it during the reading; for it says 'he is buried bare without that mitzvah.' But if he does not hold it for [the purpose of] a mitzvah, such as when the Sefer Torah is taken to place it in another place, or when he rolls it before the reading to the place of the reading, or when he holds the sheets of the Sefer Torah not at the time of the reading in order to correct them, and the like -- there is no prohibition to hold [it] without a covering, since there is no mitzvah in that holding. And so wrote the Rashash, of blessed memory, in Shaar HaKavanos, folio 49b, that this obligation is only at the time of the reading of the Sefer Torah in public, when supernal lights are revealed as stated; but not at the time of the reading, such as when one needs to repair it or for another matter, there is nothing to it. End of his language. And it is reasonable that he means specifically at the time of the reading, when there is a mitzvah and also when the lights are revealed; but if not at the time of the reading, or when there is no mitzvah in that holding, there is nothing to it, as is implied from the words of the Gemara. And see the Ben Ish Chai, parashas Toldos, ot 18, who wrote that if, at the time that he opens the Sefer Torah to show the writing to the congregation, he sees that it is not open at the parashah that they read, and he needs to roll two or three columns, he must hold [it] with a covering even according to the words of the Rashash, of blessed memory, since it is explained in Shaar HaKavanos there that at the time that he opens the Sefer Torah in the Heichal in order to read, the illumination of the Torah written within it is revealed outward to the entire congregation; and he wrote that the shaliach tzibbur must likewise take care, at the time that he reads in public and a column is completed such that he needs to draw the sheet, that he hold [it] by means of a covering; see there. However, according to what was stated, it appears that there is no proof for this from the words of the Rashash, of blessed memory; and also from the words of the Gemara and the explanation of the Riva, of blessed memory, that we brought above, it appears that there is no prohibition except specifically if there is a mitzvah in that holding, and in this matter there is no mitzvah; nevertheless one should be stringent. And see our words above in siman 134, ot 10.
