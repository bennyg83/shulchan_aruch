1) [Seif 1] It is forbidden to hold a bare Sefer Torah, etc. For so it is [stated] at the end of Megillah and in Shabbos, folio 14a: Rabbi Parnach said in the name of Rabbi Yochanan: anyone who holds a bare Sefer Torah is buried bare, without that mitzvah. For the Tosafos wrote there: some explain 'the mitzvah of holding' [is lost], and it is difficult -- what is the novelty? It is obvious that he has no reward, since he did not perform the mitzvah properly. Therefore the Riva explained, 'without that mitzvah' that he performed at that time: that if he held a bare Sefer Torah and read from it, he has no reward from the reading; and likewise if he held it to roll it or to correct it. End of his language. And so the Tosafos wrote in Shabbos there; and so wrote the Rosh; and the Beis Yosef cited it. And it is implied from the words of the Gemara and the explanation of the aforementioned Riva that this [prohibition] that it is forbidden to hold a bare Sefer Torah is specifically if there is a mitzvah in that holding, such as at the time of hagbahah or the reading or the gelilah after the reading, or if he needs to correct something in it during the reading; for it says 'he is buried bare without that mitzvah.' But if he does not hold it for [the purpose of] a mitzvah, such as when the Sefer Torah is taken to place it in another place, or when he rolls it before the reading to the place of the reading, or when he holds the sheets of the Sefer Torah not at the time of the reading in order to correct them, and the like -- there is no prohibition to hold [it] without a covering, since there is no mitzvah in that holding. And so wrote the Rashash, of blessed memory, in Shaar HaKavanos, folio 49b, that this obligation is only at the time of the reading of the Sefer Torah in public, when supernal lights are revealed as stated; but not at the time of the reading, such as when one needs to repair it or for another matter, there is nothing to it. End of his language. And it is reasonable that he means specifically at the time of the reading, when there is a mitzvah and also when the lights are revealed; but if not at the time of the reading, or when there is no mitzvah in that holding, there is nothing to it, as is implied from the words of the Gemara. And see the Ben Ish Chai, parashas Toldos, ot 18, who wrote that if, at the time that he opens the Sefer Torah to show the writing to the congregation, he sees that it is not open at the parashah that they read, and he needs to roll two or three columns, he must hold [it] with a covering even according to the words of the Rashash, of blessed memory, since it is explained in Shaar HaKavanos there that at the time that he opens the Sefer Torah in the Heichal in order to read, the illumination of the Torah written within it is revealed outward to the entire congregation; and he wrote that the shaliach tzibbur must likewise take care, at the time that he reads in public and a column is completed such that he needs to draw the sheet, that he hold [it] by means of a covering; see there. However, according to what was stated, it appears that there is no proof for this from the words of the Rashash, of blessed memory; and also from the words of the Gemara and the explanation of the Riva, of blessed memory, that we brought above, it appears that there is no prohibition except specifically if there is a mitzvah in that holding, and in this matter there is no mitzvah; nevertheless one should be stringent. And see our words above in siman 134, ot 10.
2) There: It is forbidden to hold a bare Sefer Torah, etc. The Beis Yosef wrote that the Mordechai wrote that this applies to [holding with] bare hands generally, but if he took [it] and did not divert his attention, we are not particular. Until here. And the Beis Yosef wrote: and it is possible that upon this the Ashkenazim rely, who hold a bare Sefer Torah at the time that they raise it to show the writing to the people; however, I found written in the name of the Agudah that even close to the taking it is said [to be forbidden]. End of his language. However, the Rama, of blessed memory, in the Darkei Moshe, ot 2, wrote: I am of their descendants and I have not seen this custom among them; rather, on the contrary, they are careful not to touch a bare Sefer Torah; but with other holy writings they are not careful at all, and I have never seen anyone who was concerned about this with other sefarim. End of his language. And the Bach wrote on this 'it is forbidden to hold [it] bare,' etc.: the explanation is that the reader from the Torah must hold the pillars of the Torah at the time of the blessing and reading, as [stated] above at the end of siman 139, and it is forbidden for him to hold the pillars of the scroll without a covering; and our custom that they are not careful in this relies on what the Mordechai wrote in the name of the Ra'avyah at the end of Megillah, that this applies to [holding with] bare hands generally, but if he took [it] and did not divert his attention we are not particular, etc.; and he concluded that every G-d-fearing person should not hold the pillars of a bare Sefer Torah without a covering. And likewise I have seen among the meticulous that both at the time that they read and at the time that they raise [it], they hold the pillars of the Sefer Torah by means of the corner of the tallis; and I have heard that so they practice in the land of Ashkenaz, and it is proper. End of his language. And so is the view of the Magen Avraham at the beginning of the siman, that even the pillars it is forbidden to hold bare; see there. And the Pri Chadash wrote, ot 1, that from the words of the Bach it appears that even [when the scroll is] wrapped in a covering it is forbidden for him to hold the pillars of the scroll without a covering or by means of the corner of the tallis; and this is not [so], for although when it is bare it is forbidden to hold its pillars, as the Mahari"t, part 1, siman 136, inferred from the words of the Rambam, and so it appears from the words of the Beis Yosef -- but when it is wrapped in a covering, it is obvious that it is permitted to hold the pillars of the scroll even without a covering. End of his language. And so wrote the Yad Aharon in the Hagahos Beis Yosef. However, the Nehar Shalom, ot 1, wrote that if the Sefer Torah is wrapped in a covering, according to all opinions it is permitted to hold the pillars without a covering; and that which the Beis Yosef wrote, that the Ashkenazim hold a bare Sefer Torah at the time that they raise [it], etc., means that they would hold it without a covering around it; see there. And so wrote the Maamar Mordechai, ot 1, that if the Sefer Torah is wrapped in a covering it is permitted to hold the pillars without a covering; see there. And so wrote the Mishbetzos Zahav, ot 1, and the Eshel Avraham at the beginning of the siman; see there. And see the responsum of the Noda BiYehudah, Orach Chaim, siman 7, who wrote that regarding the pillars it is certainly [right] to permit after the taking, and the Ashkenazim practiced well; see there. However, that which appears from the words of the Nehar Shalom there, that also what the Taz permitted, s.k. 1, to hold the pillars without a covering, is [only] if the Sefer Torah is wrapped in a covering -- this is not [so]; rather it appears that the Taz permits even if the Sefer Torah is not wrapped in a covering, as is understood by one who examines [it]; and so wrote the Maamar Mordechai there. And so is implied in the responsum Shav Yaakov, siman 11, that regarding the pillars it is permitted to hold [them] without a covering; see there, and the Pischei Teshuvah and the [author of] Orach Chaim cited it, ot 2; and such appears the view of the Eliyah Rabbah, ot 1, like the view of the Taz. It turns out that there are three [views] in the matter: according to the view of the Bach, per the understanding of the Pri Chadash, even if the Sefer Torah is wrapped in a covering it is forbidden to hold the pillars without a covering; according to the view of the Pri Chadash and those who agree with him, if the Sefer Torah is wrapped in a covering it is permitted to hold the pillars without a covering; and according to the view of the Taz and those who agree with him, even if the Sefer Torah is not wrapped in a covering it is permitted to hold the pillars without a covering. And see the Noda BiYehudah there, siman 8, who wrote that one who wishes to be stringent not to touch the pillar of a Sefer Torah except with the interposition of a tallis is permitted [to do so], provided that it be in private; see there, and the Machazik Berachah cited him, ot 3, and in his sefer Ledovid Emes, siman 3, ot 10, and the rest of the later authorities. However, from the aforementioned words of the Bach it appears that since there is a dispute in this, one who wishes to be stringent -- there is no [concern of] haughtiness in it, and on the contrary it is proper to do so; and so wrote [the author] above in siman 146, ot 22, in the name of the Pesach HaDvir; see there. And the custom of the Sefardim is to make a case for the Sefer Torah made of wood and coated with silk or with silver, and when they raise the Sefer Torah to show the writing to the people they hold the case and raise [it], and at the time of the reading they hold the covering, as stated above in siman 139, ot 24, in the name of the Arizal, of blessed memory; see there.
3) There: Without a covering. The explanation is that washing the hands does not suffice. Olas Tamid, ot 2; and it is from what the Beis Yosef wrote in the name of the Agudah, whose words we brought in the previous ot, that even close to the washing it is said [to be forbidden] to hold a bare Sefer Torah; and so is the view of the Rama, of blessed memory, in the gloss, who wrote 'and with a Sefer Torah even in such a case it is forbidden' -- meaning that washing the hands is of no avail. And so wrote the Levush. And the view of Maran, of blessed memory, is that even to hold the pillars, washing the hands does not suffice, as he wrote in the Beis Yosef, and we brought his words in the previous ot, where he wrote on the words of the Mordechai, 'and it is possible that upon this the Ashkenazim rely, who hold a bare Sefer Torah at the time that they raise [it],' etc.; and the raising of the Ashkenazim is by the pillars, because they do not have the custom to make a wooden case for the Sefer Torah as the Sefardim do; and since he said 'upon this the Ashkenazim rely,' it is implied that he himself does not hold so. And so is the view of the Levush, that also to hold the pillars, washing the hands does not suffice, only if the Sefer Torah is wrapped in a covering.
4) There: Bare, without a covering. And I have heard that there are those who explain that this 'bare' does not refer to the Sefer Torah but to the person holding the Sefer Torah, that if he is bare of a fringed garment he is buried bare. Mahar"t Mildola, of blessed memory, in the sefer Mayim Rabbim, Yoreh De'ah, siman 53; Yad Aharon [in the] Mishnah Berurah, in the Hagahos HaTur, ot 1. And further see the Maamar Mordechai, ot 2, who wrote that there are those who explained 'bare' as referring to the person, that a person who is bare should not hold the Sefer Torah, and they rejected their words, and he wrote that all the poskim wrote like the words of Maran, of blessed memory, that 'bare' refers to the Sefer Torah without a covering; see there. And the practical difference is that it is proper to be stringent also according to the 'some explain,' even though according to the law we rule like the words of Maran, of blessed memory, and like the agreement of all the poskim, that 'bare' refers to the Sefer Torah. And so wrote the Yafeh LaLev, ot 1, that this matter provides support for what the Rav Ma'aseh Avraham wrote, Orach Chaim, siman 36, that one should not ascend and read from the Sefer Torah, nor perform any mitzvah connected to the Sefer Torah, without being adorned in his garment, with a tallis on his head, etc.; however, he wrote there that one should not hold back the mitzvah on account of this; see there.
5) There, gloss: And some say that the same applies to other holy writings. So wrote the Tosafos in Shabbos, folio 14a, that it is somewhat implied that it is not specifically a Sefer Torah, for all the holy writings we learned in Maseches Yadayim (chapter 3) that they render the hands impure. End of its language. And the Magen Avraham wrote, s.k. 1: and if so, also the straps of the tefillin, and all the more so the tefillin themselves, it is forbidden to hold bare, for they render the hands impure. However, in Chagigah, folio 24b, the Tosafos wrote that the straps of the tefillin together with the tefillin -- the [law] of Rabbi Parnach does not apply, etc.; and so wrote the Rosh in Maseches Yadayim; and if so, it is implied that it is permitted to hold them bare. However, [regarding] holy writings, [this is] specifically when they are written in Ashuris on leather and with ink, as stated in the Mishnah (there, chapter 4); and so wrote the Rambam (chapter 9 of Hilchos Avos HaTumah). However, [regarding] a Megillah that is written according to its law, one must be careful, for from the fact that the Rambam [stated it] without qualification, we learn that we do not rule like Shmuel (Megillah 7a), who said that Esther does not render the hands impure. End of his words. And so wrote the Mor U'Ketziah in siman 691, that the Megillah of Esther requires a covering, as stated in 'Chelek,' folio 100a; and the Machazik Berachah cited him there, ot 3. And so it is written in the sefer HaEshkol, laws of Talmud Torah, siman 12, that it is forbidden to hold the Megillah bare; see there. However, the Radbaz, part 2, siman 11, wrote that this is according to the one who explains that 'bare' refers to the person, but according to the one who explains that it refers to the Sefer Torah, the other holy writings are permitted to hold when they are bare; and the Knesses HaGedolah cited him in the Hagahos Beis Yosef and wrote 'and so the people practice.' And likewise in the responsum Shav Yaakov, Orach Chaim, siman 11, he elaborated on this matter, and concluded at the end that the other holy writings are permitted to hold when they are bare, like the words of the Radbaz, of blessed memory; see there, and the Yad Aharon in the Mishnah Berurah in the Hagahos Beis Yosef cited him, ot 1; Birkei Yosef, ot 1. And so wrote the Eliyah Rabbah, ot 1, that what the Rama said, that they did not have the custom to be stringent with holy writings, can be explained as referring to a Megillah, for with our other sefarim it is always permitted. And so it is written in the responsum Panim Me'iros, part 1, siman 76, that the world is not careful to hold a Megillah bare, and they have that upon which to rely; and the Machazik Berachah cited him, ot 4; see there. Zechor L'Avraham, ot 40. And see below, ot 7.
6) And the Radbaz wrote further there that according to all the explanations, the Oral Torah [sefarim] are permitted to hold, whether when he [the person] is bare or when they are bare; and the Knesses HaGedolah cited him there. And see the Eliyah Rabbah there, who wrote that one should be stringent even with our sefarim when it is known that his hands are soiled; see there. And according to this, also with sefarim of the Oral Torah one should be stringent not to hold them when his hands are soiled.
7) There, in the gloss: And they did not practice so. The explanation is that even with those that are written in Ashuris on leather and with ink, such as a Megillah, [they did not practice to be stringent], as is implied from the plain [reading] of the words of the Rama, of blessed memory; and so wrote the Eliyah Rabbah, ot 1, and we brought his words above in ot 5; and so it is written in the responsum Panim Me'iros, part 1, siman 76; and so it is written in the Biur HaGra; and not like the Magen Avraham, s.k. 1. And the reason that they did not practice so, one can say, is because the Tosafos wrote 'it is somewhat implied that it is not specifically a Sefer Torah,' as stated above in ot 5, and the Gemara in the chapter 'Chelek' (Sanhedrin, folio 100a) can also be explained in another way. And nonetheless, we have already written above in ot 5 that there is a dispute in this, and therefore it is proper to be stringent to hold the Megillah at the time of the reading with a garment or with its pillar, or at least not to divert his attention from the washing [of the hands], as the Rama, of blessed memory, wrote; and specifically regarding the other holy writings one may be lenient [with just] not diverting his attention from the washing, but for holding a Sefer Torah without a covering one should not be lenient [with just] washing, as the Rama, of blessed memory, wrote, and as [the author] wrote above in ot 3; see there.
8) There, in the gloss: And it is good to be stringent if he did not wash his hands. It appears to me [that this refers] to hands generally [unwashed], for we are concerned lest he touched a soiled place; and the reason that washing the hands is of avail for the other holy writings is because the Mordechai is lenient even with a Sefer Torah, as stated above in ot 2, and the Tosafos wrote 'it is somewhat implied that it is not specifically a Sefer Torah,' as [stated] in the previous ot; and therefore the Rama, of blessed memory, wrote by way of compromise that for holy writings washing the hands suffices, but not for a Sefer Torah; and examine it well.
