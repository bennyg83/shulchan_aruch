In Yerushalmi it says one should not take the second from the ark until the first is returned to its place; and so in Mateh Moshe chapter 11; and our custom is not thus — rather we take out both together and do not remove, etc. — end of his words; Darkei Moshe in the name of Rabbi Zechariah — and this requires further study: from where to act not like the Yerushalmi? And furthermore in our Gemara Yoma 56 it says: one descended before Rava and said he took the blood of the bull and placed the blood of the goat; and he said to him — say he placed the blood of the goat and took the blood of the bull — if so, behold you have that he must place the first before taking the second. And in Hagahot Maimoniyot he wrote one does not remove, etc., and brings proof from chapter 8 of Pesachim that we say "receive the full and return the empty," etc.; and he wrote that so is the Rosh chapter 2 of Shabbat in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah — and I searched in the Rosh and found nothing; and also I did not understand the proof — for we say in the Gemara "receive the full and return the empty" but not the reverse — for when one hands it to him immediately he must receive it; it supports Rashi who said one does not pass over mitzvot once it has come to his hand — end of his words; it implies specifically because he hands it to him he must receive — otherwise one need not receive; for if the reason is so he should not be idle — if so from where to support Rashi? And it is possible to say they are all one reason — therefore one does not pass over mitzvot so he should not be idle from mitzvot — and it is forced. And that of Yoma it is possible to say that if he took the blood of the bull he must take with the left, and therefore it is better to place the blood of the goat first so they take with the right; and that of Pesachim must be that the empty one holds in his left and the full he receives in his right — not so in Yoma where both are full; and this requires further study. One to whom they give to lift the Torah scroll and to roll — and he cannot do both — should lift and give to another to roll, for rolling is the main matter as written in siman 139; but when they honor him to hand him the mantles — rolling is preferable for it is the essence of the mitzvah, as we say: one who merited the incense says to his friend "you merited the coal pan with me" — see Yoma 70. When one sees a mitzvah he may leave it and go see another mitzvah, even though there is a mitzvah to see the performance of the mitzvah because "in the multitude of the people is the king's glory" — nevertheless he may pass from it (Rashi); and see Tosafot Yoma 33b — where we only do one mitzvah one is not obligated to do what came to his hand first — see there; and it is not necessarily so; and see Menachot 64b. And in Sotah 8 we say one does not give two sotot to drink together, and does not purify two metzora'im, and does not pierce two slaves, and does not behead two heifers together — for one does not do mitzvot in bundles, bundles; and Tosafot wrote it is forbidden to stand both in the azarah at once even though they do not drink together — and it is astonishing: would he forbid in such a case to stand two sacrifices together in the azarah and slaughter one and then the second, etc.? — see there. And the Gemara concludes with one kohen one does not give to drink, and with two kohanim one gives — and this requires further study on the Rambam who ruled plainly without distinguishing; therefore it appears to me that he explains the reverse — with one kohen he must give them one after another; therefore even if they stand at once in the azarah that is fine; but with two kohanim giving to drink at once — this one one and that one one — it is forbidden. And with this the difficulty of Tosafot who asked from two metzora'im is resolved — see there. And behold for the Rambam it is permitted to bring two infants at once and circumcise this one and then that one; but always at once with two mohalim is forbidden; and for Tosafot it is forbidden to bring them at once to circumcise them one after another, but to circumcise them at once with two mohalim is permitted; and if so with two Torah scrolls we practice like the Rambam that we are not particular about the bringing; and for Tosafot in wedding blessings one should not bring two cups before him at once but after Birkat HaMazon take one cup and bless seven blessings — so is Tur and Shulchan Aruch in Orach Chayim siman 62. And what Tosafot asked from two sacrifices — one may say specifically with two obligatory mitzvot it is bundles; and similarly with two obligatory korbanot it is forbidden to stand them together — so is Yoma chapter 5: we learned "they brought him the goat" — implying one does not bring bull and goat together — see there.
