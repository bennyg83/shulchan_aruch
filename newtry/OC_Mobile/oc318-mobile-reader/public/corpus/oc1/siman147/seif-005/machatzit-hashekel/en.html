(s.k. 5) From old materials, etc. — however, in the responsum of Maharil he wrote, etc., that he wrote at first that one should not make implements of a Torah scroll from a garment that a commoner used, and concluded; and even though the world is accustomed to make cloths and a curtain from old garments — "not the son of Ravina signed on it" — until here; behold, that on a curtain too he wrote "not the son of Ravina signed on it," which implies that on a curtain too his view is to forbid from the perspective of law.
However, the world practices, etc., as Maharil wrote; but Maharil did not agree with the custom, as should be read.
And it appears to me that he holds, etc. — he wanted to settle the custom so that it would not be against Tosafot.
For the basin, etc. — in Sefer Or Zarua he questioned that one should bring proof from a spoon, ring, and pomander; and per my humble opinion, since they were melted, there is no proof, since they were melted; if so, certainly "new faces came here," unlike making from old garments — even though now it is a different implement, nevertheless the form of the garment is still upon it, i.e., the initial shape; one cannot learn permission from spoon and ring that were melted in fire, even though nevertheless gold remained as at first — nevertheless through melting by fire there was a complete change; therefore he brought proof from the mirrors of the midwives, which were not melted first in fire, but from those mirrors themselves a [different] implement was made — the basin; and they were not melted first in fire, but an other implement similar [was made] from them — fine [analogy] to old garments from which one makes holy implements; and so I found in responsum Chavat Yair.
And even though on the altar wood is required, etc. — this is the proof of those who forbid, from what we say in Menachot daf 22b: it is written "upon the wood that is on the fire that is on the altar" — just as the altar [belongs to] the congregation, so too wood and fire [belong to] the congregation and not to an individual — the words of R' Eliezer in the name of R' Shimon; R' Eliezer ben Shimon says: just as the altar was not used by a commoner, so too wood and fire that were not used by a commoner; and Rambam ruled in Laws of Forbidden Offerings like R' Eliezer ben Shimon — see Berakhat HaZevach the reason he ruled like him; and from this they learned that the same applies to implements of a Torah scroll: one requires something that was not used by a commoner.
And so in the Gemara — meaning that the derivation was needed for wood and fire to the altar to forbid wood and fire that a commoner used; it implies that if not for the derivation, one would not derive by binyan av wood and fire from the altar, which requires that a commoner not have used it; and if so, now that there is a derivation for wood and fire, one does not derive from it implements of a Torah scroll — for what [comparison] to wood and fire, which are more similar to the altar, for both are matters of the Beit HaMikdash? And nevertheless, without the derivation one would not derive from the altar; all the more so one does not derive by binyan av implements of a Torah scroll, which are not similar to implements of the Beit HaMikdash; rather we say: what was revealed, was revealed, and no more.
