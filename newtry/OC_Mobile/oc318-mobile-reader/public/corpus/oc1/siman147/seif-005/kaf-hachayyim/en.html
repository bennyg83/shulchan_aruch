5) There, gloss: And some say that the same applies to other holy writings. So wrote the Tosafos in Shabbos, folio 14a, that it is somewhat implied that it is not specifically a Sefer Torah, for all the holy writings we learned in Maseches Yadayim (chapter 3) that they render the hands impure. End of its language. And the Magen Avraham wrote, s.k. 1: and if so, also the straps of the tefillin, and all the more so the tefillin themselves, it is forbidden to hold bare, for they render the hands impure. However, in Chagigah, folio 24b, the Tosafos wrote that the straps of the tefillin together with the tefillin -- the [law] of Rabbi Parnach does not apply, etc.; and so wrote the Rosh in Maseches Yadayim; and if so, it is implied that it is permitted to hold them bare. However, [regarding] holy writings, [this is] specifically when they are written in Ashuris on leather and with ink, as stated in the Mishnah (there, chapter 4); and so wrote the Rambam (chapter 9 of Hilchos Avos HaTumah). However, [regarding] a Megillah that is written according to its law, one must be careful, for from the fact that the Rambam [stated it] without qualification, we learn that we do not rule like Shmuel (Megillah 7a), who said that Esther does not render the hands impure. End of his words. And so wrote the Mor U'Ketziah in siman 691, that the Megillah of Esther requires a covering, as stated in 'Chelek,' folio 100a; and the Machazik Berachah cited him there, ot 3. And so it is written in the sefer HaEshkol, laws of Talmud Torah, siman 12, that it is forbidden to hold the Megillah bare; see there. However, the Radbaz, part 2, siman 11, wrote that this is according to the one who explains that 'bare' refers to the person, but according to the one who explains that it refers to the Sefer Torah, the other holy writings are permitted to hold when they are bare; and the Knesses HaGedolah cited him in the Hagahos Beis Yosef and wrote 'and so the people practice.' And likewise in the responsum Shav Yaakov, Orach Chaim, siman 11, he elaborated on this matter, and concluded at the end that the other holy writings are permitted to hold when they are bare, like the words of the Radbaz, of blessed memory; see there, and the Yad Aharon in the Mishnah Berurah in the Hagahos Beis Yosef cited him, ot 1; Birkei Yosef, ot 1. And so wrote the Eliyah Rabbah, ot 1, that what the Rama said, that they did not have the custom to be stringent with holy writings, can be explained as referring to a Megillah, for with our other sefarim it is always permitted. And so it is written in the responsum Panim Me'iros, part 1, siman 76, that the world is not careful to hold a Megillah bare, and they have that upon which to rely; and the Machazik Berachah cited him, ot 4; see there.
