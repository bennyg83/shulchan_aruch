Until here. And the Beis Yosef wrote: and it is possible that upon this the Ashkenazim rely, who hold a bare Sefer Torah at the time that they raise it to show the writing to the people; however, I found written in the name of the Agudah that even close to the taking it is said [to be forbidden]. End of his language. However, the Rama, of blessed memory, in the Darkei Moshe, ot 2, wrote: I am of their descendants and I have not seen this custom among them; rather, on the contrary, they are careful not to touch a bare Sefer Torah; but with other holy writings they are not careful at all, and I have never seen anyone who was concerned about this with other sefarim. End of his language. And the Bach wrote on this 'it is forbidden to hold [it] bare,' etc.: the explanation is that the reader from the Torah must hold the pillars of the Torah at the time of the blessing and reading, as [stated] above at the end of siman 139, and it is forbidden for him to hold the pillars of the scroll without a covering; and our custom that they are not careful in this relies on what the Mordechai wrote in the name of the Ra'avyah at the end of Megillah, that this applies to [holding with] bare hands generally, but if he took [it] and did not divert his attention we are not particular, etc.; and he concluded that every God-fearing person should not hold the pillars of a bare Sefer Torah without a covering. And likewise I have seen among the meticulous that both at the time that they read and at the time that they raise [it], they hold the pillars of the Sefer Torah by means of the corner of the tallis; and I have heard that so they practice in the land of Ashkenaz, and it is proper. End of his language. And so is the view of the Magen Avraham at the beginning of the siman, that even the pillars it is forbidden to hold bare; see there. And the Pri Chadash wrote, ot 1, that from the words of the Bach it appears that even [when the scroll is] wrapped in a covering it is forbidden for him to hold the pillars of the scroll without a covering or by means of the corner of the tallis; and this is not [so], for although when it is bare it is forbidden to hold its pillars, as the Mahari"t, part 1, siman 136, inferred from the words of the Rambam, and so it appears from the words of the Beis Yosef -- but when it is wrapped in a covering, it is obvious that it is permitted to hold the pillars of the scroll even without a covering. End of his language. And so wrote the Yad Aharon in the Hagahos Beis Yosef. However, the Nehar Shalom, ot 1, wrote that if the Sefer Torah is wrapped in a covering, according to all opinions it is permitted to hold the pillars without a covering; and that which the Beis Yosef wrote, that the Ashkenazim hold a bare Sefer Torah at the time that they raise [it], etc., means that they would hold it without a covering around it; see there. And so wrote the Maamar Mordechai, ot 1, that if the Sefer Torah is wrapped in a covering it is permitted to hold the pillars without a covering; see there. And so wrote the Mishbetzos Zahav, ot 1, and the Eshel Avraham at the beginning of the siman; see there. And see the responsum of the Noda BiYehudah, Orach Chaim, siman 7, who wrote that regarding the pillars it is certainly [right] to permit after the taking, and the Ashkenazim practiced well; see there. However, that which appears from the words of the Nehar Shalom there, that also what the Taz permitted, s.k. 1, to hold the pillars without a covering, is [only] if the Sefer Torah is wrapped in a covering -- this is not [so]; rather it appears that the Taz permits even if the Sefer Torah is not wrapped in a covering, as is understood by one who examines [it]; and so wrote the Maamar Mordechai there. And so is implied in the responsum Shav Yaakov, siman 11, that regarding the pillars it is permitted to hold [them] without a covering; see there, and the Pischei Teshuvah and the [author of] Orach Chaim cited it, ot 2; and such appears the view of the Eliyah Rabbah, ot 1, like the view of the Taz. It turns out that there are three [views] in the matter: according to the view of the Bach, per the understanding of the Pri Chadash, even if the Sefer Torah is wrapped in a covering it is forbidden to hold the pillars without a covering; according to the view of the Pri Chadash and those who agree with him, if the Sefer Torah is wrapped in a covering it is permitted to hold the pillars without a covering; and according to the view of the Taz and those who agree with him, even if the Sefer Torah is not wrapped in a covering it is permitted to hold the pillars without a covering. And see the Noda BiYehudah there, siman 8, who wrote that one who wishes to be stringent not to touch the pillar of a Sefer Torah except with the interposition of a tallis is permitted [to do so], provided that it be in private; see there, and the Machazik Berachah cited him, ot 3, and in his sefer Ledovid Emes, siman 3, ot 10, and the rest of the later authorities. However, from the aforementioned words of the Bach it appears that since there is a dispute in this, one who wishes to be stringent -- there is no [concern of] haughtiness in it, and on the contrary it is proper to do so; and so wrote [the author] above in siman 146, ot 22, in the name of the Pesach HaDvir; see there. And the custom of the Sefardim is to make a case for the Sefer Torah made of wood and coated with silk or with silver, and when they raise the Sefer Torah to show the writing to the people they hold the case and raise [it], and at the time of the reading they hold the covering, as stated above in siman 139, ot 24, in the name of the Arizal, of blessed memory; see there.
