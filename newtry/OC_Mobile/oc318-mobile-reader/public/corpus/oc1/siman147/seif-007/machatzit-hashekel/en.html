(s.k. 7) And he shall begin, etc. — he says specifically, etc., explicitly. Meaning: in Beit Yosef he brought two explanations — whether the intent in what he wrote "and he shall begin from outside" is specifically, or meaning relative to what he wrote that one must fasten from inside — that is, "and he shall begin" even from outside. And since the plain meaning of the law implies that he begins specifically from outside, similar to what he wrote "and he shall fasten from inside," which is specifically and not from outside — and as Rama wrote the reason: the same applies to "he shall begin from outside," meaning specifically. And it is impossible that the Rav Beit Yosef's explanation is the main one — that "he shall begin" means he is permitted to begin, but if he wishes he may begin from inside; Beit Yosef should have explained thus in Shulchan Aruch, since in his book Beit Yosef he is uncertain in this law whether it implies specifically, or whether his view in Shulchan Aruch is truly that he begins specifically from outside and is not permitted to begin from inside.

One must be concerned that it will fall, etc. — in Shulchan Aruch it speaks when one person does the raising and rolling; certainly one may say that perhaps it will fall when he begins from inside, unlike when he begins from outside, for it is impossible that it fall outward, for he holds it with the cloth with which he begins to roll around it. And even afterward, when he rolls and the cloth reaches the side of the inside, nevertheless one may say that perhaps it will fall outward — meaning, since it is already somewhat wrapped in the cloth and he holds the cloth in his hand, it cannot fall outward so quickly; and when he sees that it began to fall, he holds it in his hands. But per our custom, where one raises and the second rolls — there is no need for such concern that it will fall; for since the one rolling stands before the Torah scroll, it cannot fall quickly and he will not notice. It is possible that Magen Avraham agrees that if he wishes he may begin to roll it from the side of the inside, since the owner of the rolling stands before the Torah scroll.
