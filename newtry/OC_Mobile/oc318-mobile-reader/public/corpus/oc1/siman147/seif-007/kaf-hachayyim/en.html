7) There, in the gloss: And they did not practice so. The explanation is that even with those that are written in Ashuris on leather and with ink, such as a Megillah, [they did not practice to be stringent], as is implied from the plain [reading] of the words of the Rama, of blessed memory; and so wrote the Eliyah Rabbah, ot 1, and we brought his words above in ot 5; and so it is written in the responsum Panim Me'iros, part 1, siman 76; and so it is written in the Biur HaGra; and not like the Magen Avraham, s.k. 1. And the reason that they did not practice so, one can say, is because the Tosafos wrote 'it is somewhat implied that it is not specifically a Sefer Torah,' as stated above in ot 5, and the Gemara in the chapter 'Chelek' (Sanhedrin, folio 100a) can also be explained in another way. And nonetheless, we have already written above in ot 5 that there is a dispute in this, and therefore it is proper to be stringent to hold the Megillah at the time of the reading with a garment or with its pillar, or at least not to divert his attention from the washing [of the hands], as the Rama, of blessed memory, wrote; and specifically regarding the other holy writings one may be lenient [with just] not diverting his attention from the washing, but for holding a Sefer Torah without a covering one should not be lenient [with just] washing, as the Rama, of blessed memory, wrote, and as [the author] wrote above in ot 3; see there.
