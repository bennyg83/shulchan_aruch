(16) Meaning: when the scroll, etc. — meaning that one should not err to interpret what they said, "he rolls it from outside," [as meaning that] when he holds the atzei chayim of the Torah scroll to show the writing to the people, he compresses his hold in a manner that its back is toward him; and automatically, when he wants to roll, the rolling too will be only behind the scroll, for the writing is not facing his face. And therefore it teaches us that it is not so: even though it is a mitzvah to show the writing to the people, as stated in Massekhet Soferim, nevertheless the writing must be facing the face of the one holding the Torah scroll — so at the time of hagbahah, and so at the time of rolling; for since he is engaged with the Torah scroll, the honor of the Torah is that the writing be facing his face. And what they said, "he rolls it from outside," means that the beginning of the rolling should be from outside, as we will explain in the s.k. after this.

(17) And he shall begin to roll — meaning: the beginning of placing the cloth on the Torah scroll to roll it should be from outside; and see Machatzit HaShekel, who tends to say that this is specifically per what Rama concludes, that it speaks when the one who raises also does the rolling; and therefore we are concerned that if the rolling begins from the side of the inside, one must be concerned, Heaven forbid, that the Torah scroll fall toward the outside [unlike when he begins from outside, for it is impossible that it fall outward, for he holds it with the cloth with which he begins to roll around it]. But per our custom, where one raises and the second rolls, there is no need to be so concerned that it will fall; for since the one rolling stands before the Torah scroll, it cannot fall quickly and he will not notice. It is possible that if he wishes he may begin to roll it from the side of the inside; and such is our custom — we are not particular about this.
