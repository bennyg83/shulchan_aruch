The one who rolls the Torah scroll rolls it from outside; and when one fastens it, one should fasten it from the inside. {Rama: Gloss: meaning: when the [Torah] scroll is standing before one, the writing should be opposite one's face, and one should start to roll from the outside. And after one finishes the rolling, one should fasten the end of the cloth on the inside so that when they come to read from it [the next time], they will find the fastening on the inside and they won't need to flip over the Torah scroll [to unfasten it]. (Tur and the Rosh - end of chapter "B'nei Ha-ir"). And it seems that all of this is when one person does the entire rolling [process]. But nowadays when the custom is that one person raises [the Torah] and another person rolls it, the writing should be facing the one raising it. And that is the custom, since he (ie. the one raising it) is the main "roller" and he is the one holding the Torah.}
