[4] From old things, etc. This is specifically without changing them, Olat Tamid at the end of siman 153. So too Magen Avraham wrote that if he changed their form and made another vessel from them, it is permitted, for the laver was made from the mirrors of the women who assembled, end quote. This is difficult, for there the reason is as Rashi explained at the end of Vayakhel, that the mirrors were more beloved than all else, because through them the women established many hosts, etc. Furthermore, Ramban, Gur Aryeh, and Divrei David explained that it was polished copper, and even when it became a laver its appearance did not change; see there. It would have been better for Magen Avraham to bring proof from the nose ring and earring written earlier. Shiyarei Kenesset HaGedolah wrote that this applies specifically to implements of sanctity, but implements of mitzvah, such as a bag for a tallit, are permitted; and in the book Beit Hillel in siman 139 he wrote that even the tallit itself is permitted, and so it is implied in Responsa Bach siman 17. The Taz in Yoreh De'ah siman 282 wrote: Responsa Maharil wrote that one should not make implements of a Sefer Torah from a garment used by an ordinary person, and he learns it from what we expound from the altar, etc.; those who practice so should be protested, end quote. But I examined the responsum itself, siman 122, and he did not write that one should protest. Rather, this is his language: although people practice making them from old garments, Mar son of Ravina did not sign upon them; and when people ask, I tell them to buy new ones for this. Therefore new is preferable, end quote. This implies that one should not protest, but when people ask, we tell them not to buy them; and if one bought them, perhaps they are permitted. This is why he concluded, "new is preferable," implying that there is no prohibition in old ones. It appears to me that for this reason the Levush included it in the first clause together with a torn cloth, where there is no prohibition in the matter. He also was precise and wrote, "they supported it on a verse," and did not write in Maharil's language, "he learned it," etc., because it is only an asmachta; and it will be explained further at the end of siman 153. The Taz there wrote that this applies specifically to implements of sanctity themselves, but an implement of an implement mentioned in siman 154 is permitted from old things. Magen Avraham challenged him: our parokhet is an implement of an implement, and nevertheless Maharil forbids; end quote. Perhaps in the days of Maharil they sometimes placed the Sefer Torah on the parokhet, as Shiyarei Kenesset HaGedolah wrote, and it was itself serving. But what the Mordechai wrote in the explanation of Ri is difficult: granted, regarding a tallit we would not forbid if an ordinary person had used it, etc.; this implies that for sanctity even an implement of an implement is forbidden. Perhaps he mentioned a tallit because of the novelty in the latter clause, that one of gentiles is forbidden. If an implement of sanctity was already made from it, it is forbidden afterward to make from it an implement of an implement; for although we are stringent not to make from it an implement of sanctity, nevertheless we do not say leniently that sanctity did not take hold in it. See Responsa Bach there. The Taz there, seif katan 8, wrote: it appears that if one says, "this money is for an implement of a Sefer Torah," there is no rule of speech to the Most High, for he can sell this; only regarding charity do we say so, end quote. So too I found in Responsa Maharil there, that even if he bought a garment for a cloth, he may retract, even though at the outset he had vowed a cloth for a Sefer Torah; nevertheless he must fulfill his vow and buy another mantle.
