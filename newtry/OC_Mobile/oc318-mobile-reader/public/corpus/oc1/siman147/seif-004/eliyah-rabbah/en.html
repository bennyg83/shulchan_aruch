[5] The writing facing the one who raises it, etc. When I roll the Sefer Torah, I customarily draw the poles toward me so that the scroll remains level as it had been lying until it is completely rolled. The writing then faces me and the one who raised it. I found support for this in Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah. It is obvious that if one is offered both raising and rolling, he should choose raising, and if he is offered rolling and handing over the mantles, he should choose rolling. See Yoma 70a, that it is a mitzvah to observe the performance of mitzvos because “In a multitude of people is the King’s glory.” Nevertheless, one may leave and go to observe another mitzvah once he is no longer engaged in the first.
[6] He should not roll, etc. Because this is degrading to the Torah. For the same reason, it appears proper to be stringent not to hold the straps of tefillin and allow the boxes to roll around the straps. Rather, one should hold the tefillin in his hand and wind the strap around them. Although the straps possess sanctity, the boxes possess greater sanctity, as explained above in siman 32.
