In Shulchan Aruch seif 1 'And likewise one may visit,' etc., and in Tosafot in Mishna Berurah — in Daat Chachmah he wrote to arouse about this, and how good is the custom of Jerusalem, may it be rebuilt speedily, if the visit is in honor of a groom or the father of the son — it is like a moment of entry and exit only. And he also wrote in the name of the moralists that one who has means should not act properly by visiting a sick person on Shabbat; rather he should go on weekdays to visit poor sick people, see the poor and their needs, and have mercy on them as his hand reaches — and this is the mitzvah of visiting the sick as enacted, etc., see there. And it appears: one who on weekdays is busy with heavenly or worldly matters and on Shabbat when he has leisure goes to a sick person he loves, knowing the sick person derives satisfaction from his visit — he performs a mitzvah and one should not prevent him from going on Shabbat and Yom Tov. And one who is soft-hearted and suffers over the sick person's pain should not go on Shabbat to visit, for Shabbat was given for pleasure and not for sorrow:
