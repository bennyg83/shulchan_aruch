And His mercies are abundant, etc. In the Gemara the Tanna Kamma holds one need only say 'It is Shabbat,' etc., and all the more so 'and His mercies,' etc.; and Shevna holds one may also say 'and His mercies,' etc.; and the Tur rules like Shevna, and likewise in Yoreh De'ah siman 335 because Beit Yosef wrote that Shevna did not come to disagree but to explain the words of the Tanna Kamma — meaning to add to his words — only that Rambam did not mention 'and His mercies,' etc.; and likewise in Shulchan Aruch there in Yoreh De'ah regarding visiting the sick. And it requires study why he ruled here that one must say 'and His mercies,' etc. And the main wording of consolation here is very puzzling: since the deceased has already died, what is there left to cry out and pray, as King David said at the death of his child? Also the word 'healing' is not applicable here, and in the Gemara this wording is not stated except regarding visiting the sick. Rather it is possible this refers to the judgment that rules at that time as if a sword is placed on his neck, as explained in Laws of Mourning; and we find there the word 'healing' — one says when a son was born to him the whole family was healed. And it is pleasing to me in what Maharal, may his memory be blessed, wrote in the name of Rashal in this wording: 'It is Shabbat, not a time to comfort; consolation will soon come, and His mercies are abundant, and rest in peace' — this wording applies well at that time.
