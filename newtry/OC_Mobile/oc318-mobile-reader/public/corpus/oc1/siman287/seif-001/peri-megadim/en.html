And His mercies are abundant — Taz, Shabbos 12 side 2: on weekdays the halachah follows R' Yosei who includes him among the other sick of Israel; and on Shabbat the halachah follows the Tanna Kamma who says one only says 'It is Shabbat, not a time to cry out; healing will soon come.' And the Tur holds that Shevna explains the words of the Tanna Kamma that he also concludes 'and His mercies are abundant,' and Bach here and Eliyahu Rabbah letter 3 explained that Rama holds one need not say 'and His mercies are abundant' — there is no prohibition and it is optional to say as one wishes; but in Levush it implies it is forbidden to say 'and His mercies are abundant' because it is language of supplication. And Taz asks: granted one need not say it and the Tanna Kamma has permission — if he wants he says and if not he does not — nevertheless why here did he rule that one must say it? See siman 526 in Taz regarding the law of a mourner, and siman 696 regarding Purim regarding calling up a mourner, and Yoreh De'ah siman 393 in Shach letter 7 — it implies if the mourner came to the synagogue after Barchu, nevertheless he changes his place; and in Taz 526 it does not imply so. And if the mourner came to the synagogue courtyard after Mizmor Shir leYom haShabbat — according to us they no longer call out 'Go out against the mourner,' for one should not mention mourning in public; nevertheless he may go himself to say 'It is Shabbat, not a time to comfort,' etc., and he is permitted.
