It requires study whether, when a person forgot to say “Hashem, open my lips,” this is considered a deficiency in the prayer itself, since the Sages incorporated it into the prayer, or not. No proof can be brought from the ruling here that the chazzan should say it quietly, for then the listeners lack this verse and nevertheless fulfill their obligation of prayer. Here is different, because the verse “Hashem, open my lips,” does not apply to them at all, since they are not praying themselves, unlike an ordinary case. It is more reasonable that the Sages said it is considered an extended prayer only with regard to preventing an interruption between redemption and prayer, but not that it is literally part of the prayer such that one must repeat the prayer on its account. Proof may be brought from Hashkiveinu, which is likewise considered an extension of redemption, yet we rule that the blessings do not prevent fulfillment of one another, and if one omitted Hashkiveinu he fulfills his obligation with the blessing Emes VeEmunah, as stated in Berachos 9a. It follows that this characterization was stated only to teach that it is not an interruption between redemption and prayer, and the same applies here.
