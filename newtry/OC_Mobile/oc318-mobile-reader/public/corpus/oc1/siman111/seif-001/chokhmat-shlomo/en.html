Seif 1: One must adjoin Geulah to prayer, etc. NB: The reason is explained in the Yerushalmi brought in the Tur — a parable to the king's beloved who knocked on his door; the king went out to ask him to know what he seeks, and found that he had gone to excess; he too went to excess. And behold, based on this one can say: as our Sages expounded on the verse "and the good in Your eyes I did" — what is "and the good in Your eyes I did" that adjoined Geulah to prayer? Many already wondered from where specifically this [and I have already spoken at length on this in some place]; and now it appears thus: it is difficult for him from "the good in Your eyes," which implies but not in my eyes — in the manner that our Sages expounded on the verse "and the matter was good in his eyes" and Rashi explained "and not in the eyes of the Omnipresent." Likewise here the intention is the reverse — "and the good in Your eyes" and not in the eyes of man; and Scripture was careful about this and said in Parashat Shoftim several times "that you do the straight and the good in the eyes of Heaven," doubling the language "good and straight" or "straight and good"; and there is in Midrash Yalkut "good in the eyes of Heaven and straight in the eyes of man," and one homiletically expounds the reverse — see there the dispute of R' Yehuda and R' Akiva. In any case Scripture was careful to explain that it is also correct in the eyes of man, lest it appear chas v'shalom that it is not good in the eyes of man; and if so, here too it should have said "the good and the straight in Your eyes I did" — why mention the good alone? Therefore he explains that the intention is on adjoining Geulah to prayer; for behold the need for this is from the reason in the Yerushalmi, as a parable to the king's beloved who knocked on the king's door, the king went out to know what he seeks, etc. If so, this applies only to one who truly loves him and is important in his eyes, and immediately when he knocks the king goes out toward him himself — the reason applies. But to one who does not love him, the king does not go out toward him immediately when he knocks; he only waits until he cries out and begs; and even if he goes out he does not go out himself but sends an agent, etc. And likewise is with Him, may He be blessed: behold in truth one who is like R' Chiyah and his sons — then immediately when they said "Who causes the wind to blow" they merited as their prayer, as our Sages expounded regarding Shmuel the Little — so it is in praising the congregation he said "Who causes the wind to blow" and we answered, etc. If so, exceptional individuals of the sons of the upper realm merit that while they still mention the praise He, may He be blessed, goes out Himself toward them. But the multitude of people do not merit that He, may He be blessed, goes out immediately before the prayer toward them; and also afterward, would that their prayer be accepted through an angel! If so, for these the reason of needing Geulah to prayer does not apply. And therefore even in great tzaddikim, since they need extra humility — and Moshe Rabbeinu, peace be upon him, proves — how will they make themselves beloved to Him, may He be blessed, to appreciate themselves so highly; and how will they not fear yuhara chas v'shalom to adjoin Geulah to prayer? Therefore, were it not that He, may He be blessed, desires this, they would not be permitted to do so intentionally; only that He, may He be blessed, desires this and He considers all Israel as sons of kings and His beloved — He sought that they adjoin Geulah to prayer; therefore he said "and the good in Your eyes I did," that in Your eyes it is good and not as in my eyes, for I know I have not reached this level, only since in Your eyes it is good I did so — therefore necessarily the intention is on adjoining Geulah to prayer. And it is clear, and this requires study.
