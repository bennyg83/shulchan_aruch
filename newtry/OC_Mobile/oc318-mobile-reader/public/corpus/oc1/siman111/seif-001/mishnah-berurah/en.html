(א) One must juxtapose [redemption to prayer]—but in Musaf and Mincha it is permitted to say verses before "Heaven, open my lips," but not afterward, for this verse is part of the prayer itself; for from this reason we may say it between redemption and prayer, since the Sages established it in the prayer it has the status of prayer; therefore one may not interrupt between it and the Amidah.**** END BLOCK ****

(ב) Between them—even for a mere pause longer than the time of speech one should be careful ab initio [the time of speech is the time it takes for a student to ask his teacher].**** END BLOCK ****

(ג) Even with Amen, etc.—and similarly for Kaddish and Kedushah one may not answer, as above in siman 66 seif 9; and he mentioned Amen because there the topic of the blessings of Shema concludes, and one might think one could answer Amen even after oneself, as below in siman 215 seif 1; he informs us that it is forbidden because of interruption, and even after the shatz one may not answer.**** END BLOCK ****

(ד) And not with any verse—and even at Maariv before the Amidah one should likewise be careful about this.**** END BLOCK ****

(ה) Amen—but for "Who has redeemed" and Kedushah it is not an interruption, for Amen relates to the blessing and is not an interruption, unlike with those.**** END BLOCK ****

(ו) And so we practice—meaning after the chazan and not after oneself; and see above in siman 66 in the gloss and in Mishna Berurah there.**** END BLOCK ****

(ז) And Shabbat, etc.—and Yom Tov, since this day is considered like the other weekdays—a day of distress—the verse "May Heaven answer you," etc., after the verse "May the words of my mouth be acceptable," which speaks of redemption, also applies to it.**** END BLOCK ****

(h) Is not a time of distress — and the Beit Yosef disagrees on this and holds that that verse is merely juxtaposed in the text, and the main requirement to juxtapose redemption to prayer is from the words of the Sages, who compared the eighteen blessings after the blessing of redemption to the beloved of a king who came and knocked on the king's door and the king went out to greet him; and if the king sees that he went far away and left, he too goes far away and no longer draws near to him when he returns and knocks again — so it is for one who interrupts between redemption and prayer; therefore the Rama wrote that it is good to be stringent.

(i) That needs this — meaning that it is permitted to respond to Kaddish and Kedusha and Barchu between redemption and prayer on Shabbat, even in the morning service; so too in Eliyah Rabbah and Peri Megadim and Shelomei Tzibbur; see in Biur Halacha.
