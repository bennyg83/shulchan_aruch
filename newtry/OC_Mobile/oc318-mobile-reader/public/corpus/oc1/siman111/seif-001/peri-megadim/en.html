To place immediately before. See Taz, who discusses this at length, and see Bach and Maharshal. The verse “May Hashem answer you” is a promise. Why is a verse needed, since every mitzvah carries a reward? This indicates that on Shabbos there is no need to join redemption to prayer, etc. There is therefore a practical difference between the Tur’s explanation, according to which one does join them on Shabbos, and Maharshal’s explanation, according to which one does not. In the gloss to Shulchan Aruch, the Tur is cited, and perhaps this was the intended meaning. Nevertheless, it is Rama’s own ruling in Darkei Moshe. Regarding the Taz’s question as to what proof the verse provides for joining redemption to prayer, according to what was written in the name of Maharshal the matter is clear, since the end of the verse was cited. The practical difference between the two explanations is that on Shabbos it is unnecessary, whereas on weekdays it is required.
Now. In our novellae to Berachos 42a, regarding “immediately after redemption,” we wrote twenty-two cubits, and I have now seen the same in Peri Chadash. Nevertheless, siman 66, seif 8, implies that one may not interrupt, not only with a single utterance such as Amen, but even silently to put on a tallis. See what I wrote there. As for the phrase “immediately after washing the hands,” each case follows its own law.
