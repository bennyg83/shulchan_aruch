[1] To adjoin, etc. — that whoever adjoins is not harmed all that day (Levush). Nachalas Tzvi wrote the Levush was not precise when he wrote thus unconditionally, for in Tosafos first chapter of Berachos they wrote specifically one who recites like the vatikin is not harmed — up to here. And one may wonder on him that he did not see the Levush copied from the words of the Tur; and if so, it appears to me the Tur does not hold like Tosafos and holds like the Levush as the Tur. But it is difficult, for the Rosh wrote like Tosafos, and so it is implied above in siman 58 in the Tur itself. Therefore it appears to me he wrote thus to learn from it that in any case adjoining Geulah to prayer is a great mitzvah even if one does not recite like the vatikin, for even one who recites like the vatikin needs specifically to adjoin; and so it is implied in Tosafos themselves, as Ma'adanei Melech wrote page 5. Further it appears to me the Tur wrote for proof, for we are precise: why did the Tur need to write "and he should not interrupt at all"? Rather one must say: I would have thought that even though one must adjoin, nevertheless one may interrupt a little; and if so, from where do we derive that one truly should not interrupt at all? Therefore it appears to me for this he brings proof "whoever adjoins," etc. — for there perforce he does not interrupt at all, since he begins immediately at sunrise as above siman 58 — behold the language of adjoining Geulah to prayer is that he not interrupt at all, as mentioned above; and see above siman 66.
