[1] To place, etc. Whoever places redemption immediately before prayer will not be harmed throughout that day, according to the Levush. Nachalas Tzvi wrote that the Levush was imprecise in stating this without qualification, since Tosafos in the first chapter of Berachos writes that only one who recites Krias Shema at the ideal time of the vasikin will not be harmed. This criticism is surprising, because he did not notice that the Levush copied the words of the Tur. It would therefore appear that the Tur disagrees with Tosafos, and the Levush follows the Tur. This is difficult, however, because the Rosh writes like Tosafos, and the Tur itself also implies this above in siman 58. It therefore appears that the Tur wrote this to teach that, in any event, placing redemption immediately before prayer is a great mitzvah even when one does not recite Krias Shema at the time of the vasikin, because even one who does recite it at that time must specifically place redemption immediately before prayer. Tosafos itself likewise implies this, as Maadanei Melech writes on page 6. It further appears that the Tur brought this as a proof. Why did the Tur need to write that one should not interrupt at all? One might otherwise have thought that although redemption must be joined to prayer, a brief interruption is permitted. From where, then, do we know that no interruption at all is permitted? The Tur therefore proves it from the statement that whoever joins them will not be harmed, because there he necessarily makes no interruption at all, as he begins the Amidah immediately at sunrise, as explained above in siman 58. Thus, the phrase “placing redemption immediately before prayer” means making no interruption at all, as stated above. See also siman 66.
