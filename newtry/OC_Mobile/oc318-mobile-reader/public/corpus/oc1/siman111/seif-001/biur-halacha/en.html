"It is good to be stringent if not in a place that needs to do so" — see in Mishna Berurah that I concluded to be lenient regarding responding Amen, etc.; and even though Sha'arei Teshuvah wrote that according to what the Sha'agat Aryeh concludes, the halacha follows Beit Yosef that one should not distinguish regarding adjoining Geulah to prayer between Shabbat and weekday — if so, likewise one should not be lenient in this; nevertheless, in my humble opinion one should not be stringent in this, after even on weekdays there are those who are lenient regarding responding Amen between Geulah and prayer [see in Sefer Even Ha'ozer]; one may rely at least on this regarding Shabbat, and so ruled Derech HaChaim. And see in the novellae of Rabbi Akiva Eiger that he is uncertain regarding Shabbat: if he found a congregation that recited Kerias Shema and are standing to pray, whether it is permitted to begin praying with them; and it is somewhat astonishing that he did not bring that Eliyah Rabba and Rabbi Akiva Eiger wrote explicitly to be lenient in this as well per the words of Rama. And it is implied from him that he holds that even according to Rama, perhaps adjoining Geulah to prayer on Shabbat morning is stricter than weekday Maariv; and according to what the Sha'agat Aryeh concludes that the halacha follows Beit Yosef, certainly one should not be lenient in this law. And the words of Sha'arei Teshuvah are true and established regarding this; and also the custom is not to be lenient in this.
