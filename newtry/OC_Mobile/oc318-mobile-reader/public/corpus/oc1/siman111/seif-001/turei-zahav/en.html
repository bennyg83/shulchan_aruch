To juxtapose redemption to prayer. And in the Yerushalmi it states in this language: immediately after redemption, prayer — for it is written "May the words of my mouth be acceptable" etc., what is written after it? "May Heaven answer you" etc. And the Tur wrote: "May the words of my mouth be acceptable" — this is redemption, and "May Heaven answer you" — this is prayer. And he brought from Morenu Our Teacher the Holy One blessed be He the words of Maharshal who questioned the Tur: let it teach us — in this verse both are written "May the words of my mouth be acceptable — they say — this is prayer" as stated in the first chapter page 9, that the Sages enacted to say "May the words of my mouth be acceptable" at the end of the eighteen blessings, since David said after eighteen sections "and Heaven my rock and my redeemer" — that is redemption; and therefore Maharshal explained: if so, from here they learn it, and he did not rely on the verse "May Heaven answer you" except that one will not be harmed all that day, as the testimony of R' Yosei ben Eilikum because of the divine presence in Jerusalem. And the explanation of the Yerushalmi "immediately after redemption, prayer" — for it is written "May the words of my mouth be acceptable" etc., that redemption and prayer are written in it; what is written after it? That his reward shall be — meaning that he shall not be harmed all that day; and this is "May Heaven answer you on a day of distress." And the meaning is: since he did not say in language of prayer "Answer me, Heaven, on a day of distress, raise me up, Heaven of Jacob" but rather refers to the above that he said "May the words of my mouth be acceptable" etc., David said: take heed that you do so and juxtapose redemption to prayer, for I promise you that Heaven will answer you on a day of distress — end quote. And it does not imply thus as the Yerushalmi explains, that he brings three "immediately"s and brings a proof for each one, and for this "immediately" of redemption he brings proof from the verse "May the words of my mouth be acceptable" and what is written after it; and this follows the Tur's explanation — for per Maharshal's explanation prayer would precede redemption, and we require redemption before prayer. And the Tur's explanation is the correct one, for this is what it says: "May the words of my mouth be acceptable — they say" — meaning that David informs us here when "May the words of my mouth be acceptable — they say" shall be — at the time when you say "my rock and my redeemer" which is redemption, and afterwards he said "May Heaven answer you" — since he said "May Heaven answer you" we learn that you are praying before this, for otherwise how would "answer you" be applicable? Therefore he said this is prayer — meaning from this you learn that it deals with prayer.
