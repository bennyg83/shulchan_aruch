These are the words of Mahariv: a non-Jew, a deaf-mute, a deranged person, or a minor should not assist, neither in kneading nor in rolling nor in baking; end quote. It implies specifically these three labors that are particular to bread, as it is in the Mishnah in chapter 3 of Pesachim; but regarding piercing, that they pierce the matzos, there is no insistence; but in Maharil it is written: and likewise all preparations are forbidden, for we require guarding for the sake of the mitzvah; end quote. And nevertheless one may say that piercing too is permitted, and it is not compelling. And these are the words of the Rashba, siman 26: they need guarding for the sake of the mitzvah; end quote; whether the guarding of kneading or the guarding from the time of harvesting; and therefore thanksgiving-loaves one does not fulfill with them; and therefore it is invalid with a non-Jew; end quote. And the Bach ruled that even after the fact one does not fulfill one's obligation with a non-Jew. And nevertheless, where it is impossible otherwise, he should let the non-Jew knead before him. And Beis Yosef wrote in the name of the Rashba that even a Jew standing over him does not avail; but in siman 11, se'if 2, and siman 39, se'if 9, the Rosh holds that it does avail when [a Jew] stands over him; and therefore it seems to me as the Bach wrote, that if it is impossible otherwise, it is permitted through a non-Jew when a Jew stands over him and tells him to do it for the sake of Pesach, and it is good that he assist a bit; and nevertheless it is better to do it through a minor than through a non-Jew; and see Yevamos, folio 104b. The Rosh, etc. For we say at the beginning of chapter 2 of Kiddushin: it is a greater mitzvah to do it himself than through his agent; see siman 250.
