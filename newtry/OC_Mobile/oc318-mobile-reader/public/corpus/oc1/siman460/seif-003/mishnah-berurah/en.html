(8) After the time of the prohibition of chametz: for before the time of prohibition, even if it was on the fourteenth day when one already searched other chametz, nevertheless one need not say [the formula] at the time of kneading, and it suffices that one nullify close to the time of its prohibition; and besides, one says "all chametz," and these crumbs too are included; but after the time of its prohibition, once it became chametz, it is not in his power to nullify, and there is concern that perhaps he will not remove them from the world immediately and will transgress bal yeraeh and bal yimatzeh; and therefore one must nullify before it leavens. And even if one is not standing there, one can nullify them in his house [Peri Megadim].
(9) One should say at the time of kneading, etc.: and nevertheless after baking one should sweep the house, and likewise the table on which they rolled the matzos, even though one nullified; and what one finds of the dough one should burn. And some wrote that for this reason it is good that one tread the crumbs with his feet in the earth immediately when they fell, before they come to leavening, for if they come to leavening one must burn them.
(10) That which sticks to the vessels: see above in siman 459, se'if 4, in the gloss, regarding if one kneaded on Yom Tov what one should do with the vessels afterward; and here it deals with one who kneads on erev Pesach after the time of its prohibition, that one can scrape the chametz off them afterward and rinse them (and so one must indeed do afterward even though one nullified them); but we are concerned that perhaps one will delay a bit and transgress bal yeraeh.
(11) I nullify them: or "I declare them ownerless"; but one should not say "crumbs are ownerless," for it is not a clear formulation that one is declaring them ownerless, for one could say that the implication of the language is that of themselves they are ownerless, and that is not so. And the later authorities wrote that formulations of bitul and hefker one must say specifically in a language one understands.
(12) That one nullifies them before their leavening: and consequently, even if they leaven afterward, one does not transgress bal yeraeh, for it is not his.
