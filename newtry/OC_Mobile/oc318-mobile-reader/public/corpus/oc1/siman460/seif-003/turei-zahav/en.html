I nullify them. The world practices to say in the language "crumbs are ownerless," and in truth this language does not avail, for it implies that they are already ownerless of themselves, whereas he needs specifically to nullify them now by his saying; rather one must say "let them be ownerless," following the formulation written here, "I nullify them." And a proof for the matter is regarding annulment of a get: it is stated in the chapter "The one who sends," that if one says "this get is void," it is nothing and the get is valid; rather specifically when one says "let it be void"; and the same exactly applies here; and this I am accustomed to preach in public. And that it wrote here specifically after the time of its prohibition: the reason is that before the time of its prohibition they are included in the bitul of "all chametz," which is not so after the time of its prohibition, when he has no power to nullify it if they became leavened. And it is written in Maharil that even though we say in the first chapter of Pesachim that (crumbs) of themselves are nullified, that means bread crumbs that cannot stick together; but dough can stick together into a kezayit and one would transgress bal yeraeh and bal yimatzeh. And he further wrote: after baking one should sweep the house even though he nullified, and what he finds of the dough he should burn.
