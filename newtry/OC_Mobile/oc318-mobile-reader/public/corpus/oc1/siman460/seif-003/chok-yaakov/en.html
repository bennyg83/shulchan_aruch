I nullify them. The world practices to say "crumbs are ownerless," and it is an error in their hands, for it implies that of themselves they are already ownerless, and it does not avail unless one says "let them be ownerless" (Darchei Moshe from Mahariv, Mateh Moshe, Shelah). And in the Rokeach, siman 273, he wrote to say "let them be ownerless"; and so is the consensus of the later authorities from what is stated in Even HaEzer, siman 141, that if one said "this get is void," it is nothing until one says "let it be void." And see Magen Avraham, who toiled and pressed to resolve the custom; and to my humble opinion, the custom of Israel is Torah without any doubt at all: for even though regarding a get one must say a "void" formulation that implies for the future, nevertheless regarding hefker it is from the law of a neder (as the Rambam wrote in the laws of nedarim, and it is explained in Choshen Mishpat, siman 273), and regarding a neder, if one says "this loaf is konam" or "hekdesh" and the like, it is plain that it avails even though one did not say "let it be"; and that which is implied in "The one who sends," folio 32b, from what Rav Acha said to Rav Ashi, "what is the difference from 'behold it is hekdesh' and 'behold it is ownerless,'" that annulment of a get is similar to hekdesh and hefker: nevertheless it is explained there from the Rosh's words explicitly as I wrote, and these are his words: even though regarding the language, behold he compares it to hekdesh, it is not entirely similar to hekdesh, for regarding hekdesh, if one said "this loaf is hekdesh," it avails; end quote. Behold, explicitly as I wrote; and see there in Tosafos. (Also according to the Rambam's view that even regarding a get, if one said "void" with a tzeirei under the tet it avails, for it is for the future; and see Beis Shmuel, siman 141, s.k. 76; see there; if so the same applies here, that it avails if one said "ownerless" with a tzeirei under the kuf.) And all the more so according to what we hold regarding chametz, that mere revelation of intent avails, as explained above in siman 448: it is plain that such a formulation avails. And I wonder at the great later authorities who toiled for nothing in this to nullify the custom of the early ones; on the contrary, it seems to me that one should not change from this custom, for even though the language "let it be" is in any case correct, nevertheless one should not say it at all, for the reason the Rosh wrote regarding "al biur chametz," as I wrote above in siman 432, s.k. 3.
Before their leavening. But after they became leavened it is not in his power to nullify, since it is after the time of its prohibition; and the like is explained above at the end of siman 454. But before the time of its prohibition, then one can nullify even after they became leavened; therefore the Mechaber wrote "one who kneads after the time of the prohibition of chametz": even though the Tur wrote it without specification, nevertheless from his writing "before it leavens" it follows that he refers to after the time of its prohibition; and this is the intent of the Mordechai and Hagahot Maimoniot; therefore he wrote adjacent to this law what he wrote in Or Zarua, that after the sixth hour one cannot nullify what already became leavened; and so it is explained explicitly in Shibolei HaLeket and Maharil in his derashos; and not like the Bach, who challenged the Mechaber from the Tur's language and wrote that it deals even before the time of its prohibition; and this is certainly not so, for before the time of prohibition there is no insistence, and one can nullify even after they became leavened; and the Tur's words too can be explained as I wrote; and it is plain.
