One should not put. For moisture comes out of them and impedes its baking; and even a cooked egg is close to forbid, for it impedes its baking. And some say that the same applies, that it is forbidden to knead the dough with seeds (Beis Yosef, Orchos Chaim). And it is implied from the language of the Shulchan Aruch, which wrote "one should not put eggs, etc.," that l'chatchila it is forbidden and after the fact it is permitted; and so it is implied from se'if 6, which wrote "one should not be lenient, etc.," implying that after the fact it is permitted. And panadish means ones filled with meat, and even so after the fact it is permitted, and we do not say that it impedes its baking. However, according to our custom that we forbid a folded matzah because it impedes its baking, it seems to me that one should also forbid when one put whole eggs or meat inside it; and furthermore, fruit juice with water hastens leavening, as written in siman 462. But if one put into it seeds that are not sharp, even if they are not crushed, after the fact it is permitted, for a small matter like this does not impede its baking, similar to coals and small stones that sometimes come into the matzah; and so it is implied at the end of siman 455. Even though one could reject [this], that there it deals with crushed spices, nevertheless what I wrote seems to me the main [view]; and also in the Gemara there it establishes the case where one put many spices in one place; and see Tosafos.
