Taz writes: “For this reason I prohibited a matzah in which a legume was found after baking, because it impeded the baking.” Magen Avraham disputes this ruling, arguing that something so small does not impede baking, just as small coals and stones that occasionally enter a matzah do not impede it. Although this proof can seemingly be rejected, because objects that enter the matzah only in the oven, even a large stone, may assist the baking like the oven itself once they are heated, whereas a legume entering during kneading may impede baking, nevertheless it appears that one should not be stringent after the fact. Some support for this appears from siman 467 concerning a grain of wheat found inside a baked matzah. Many authorities permit it in every case, and even those who prohibit do so because of concern that the wheat itself became chametz, not because such a small object impeded the baking.
