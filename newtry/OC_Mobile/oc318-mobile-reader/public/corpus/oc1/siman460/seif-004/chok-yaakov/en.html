One does not make. Meaning: l'chatchila; and after the fact one fulfills with them.
Whatever they make. Meaning: even though it is like a decoration it is permitted. Magen Avraham wrote: and some practiced on Chol HaMoed to make decorations, and it is not proper; and also by means of a mold it is forbidden; end quote.
With a comb. See Yoreh Deah, siman 120, se'if 5, in the gloss: the Rema ruled that the irons with which they prepare the matzos need no tevilah; end quote. And the Taz wrote there that one should immerse them without a blessing; end quote. However, in Issur veHetter, klal 58, din 82, he wrote: and these are his words: the irons called in the vernacular "Stift-Eisen," and likewise the blade with which one cuts dough, need no tevilah even without a blessing, lest one come to recite a blessing over them and it be like a blessing in vain; end quote. And Peri Chadash wrote there in the name of the Rokeach that they need tevilah; therefore one should immerse them with other vessels; end quote. And that is proper.
Wafers. And since they make wafers, therefore they practiced to be lenient to make decorations (Beis Yosef); but the Bach ruled that even like this they practiced to forbid. And Kol Bo wrote in the name of a derashah of the Raavad, of blessed memory: and these are his words: it is proper to make matzas mitzvah small wafers, not thick and large, for that is not bread of poverty; end quote. And see Beis Hillel in Yoreh Deah, siman 96, who wrote that the custom is to make the matzah the thickness of a finger.
