(s.k. 4) Thick, etc. For according to the conclusion, etc.: for we say on folio 37: Our Rabbis taught: One does not bake thick bread on Yom Tov of Pesach; the words of Beis Shammai. And Beis Hillel permit. And how much is thick bread? Said Rav Huna: a tefach, for so we find with the lechem hapanim, which needed to be matzah a tefach thick. R' Yosef challenged this: if they said [this] regarding the meticulous, will they say [it] regarding those who are not meticulous, etc.? And it concludes: rather, "thick bread" means a large [quantity of] bread, and because of toil on Yom Tov; see there. If so, according to the conclusion, the law of thick bread was not mentioned at all. And therefore one must say that even thick bread too can be guarded from becoming chametz. For if you would think that with thick [bread] it is impossible to guard it, then we would not know how much the measure of its thickness is; for from the lechem hapanim one cannot learn, and perhaps even the thickness of a finger is also called thick, and the Sages would have had to explain the measure of its thickness. Rather, in every respect it is permitted, and even thicker than a tefach; and the lechem hapanim was not made thicker than a tefach not because of concern for chametz, but because its mitzvah is so; and Peri Chadash disagrees. And see Magen Avraham at the end of siman 456: it needs a bit of resolution.
