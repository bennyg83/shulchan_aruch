[4] One should say, etc. I wonder why one needs to nullify them, for we hold that crumbs are ownerless and nullified of themselves (Olas Shabbos); and Maharil escaped his notice, who raises this difficulty and answers that we are concerned that after baking they sweep the house and [the crumbs] will gather together and be fit to be reckoned significant; and Mahari of Ronkel answered that specifically bread crumbs are nullified and not dough crumbs; and so wrote Mateh Moshe, and he concluded: many err in this, saying simply "crumbs are ownerless," which implies that crumbs are presumably ownerless, and this is a complete error, as I have written; rather one must say "let crumbs be ownerless"; end of his words. The implication of his words is that in truth crumbs are ownerless, but there is a distinction, and presumably we explain the speaker's intent so, and he did not descend to the distinction mentioned above. And with this is resolved what Chok Yaakov challenged: how is it that saying "this loaf is hekdesh" avails even if one does not say "let it be"; see there; for here it is worse, as we said; so it seemed to me.
