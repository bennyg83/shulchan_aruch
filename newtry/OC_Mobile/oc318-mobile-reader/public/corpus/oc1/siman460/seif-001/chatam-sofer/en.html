In Magen Avraham at the beginning of the siman: a non-Jew, a deaf-mute, a deranged person, or a minor should not assist. Note: at first glance one must examine [this] according to the Rambam, who requires guarding from the time of harvesting, and according to the Rambam it does not avail if a Jew stands over him; if so, how did we fulfill the obligation of guarding from the time of harvesting, when the harvesters are non-Jews, and even the milling is sometimes done by a non-Jew with a Jew standing over him? And it seems to me that at first glance this requires further study regarding all the poskim who require kneading and baking by a Jew: is it that kneading and baking are written [as the mitzvah], when "guarding" is what is written; and here, since a Jew stands and guards for its sake, what difference does it make who kneads and who bakes? And one must say that the one who kneads and the one who bakes must himself be the one who guards from any leavening, for the dough is under his hands, and it is impossible for another to guard this; and since we require guarding for its sake, it is impossible that a non-Jew, a deaf-mute, a deranged person, or a minor knead. However, that is specifically regarding kneading and baking; but the guarding of harvesting is nothing but supervision that water not fall upon the wheat from elsewhere, and this the Jewish guard can guard with intent for its sake, while the harvesters and millers perform ape-like labor; and it works out well. And it is explained well in my responsa; see there, Chatam Sofer, Orach Chaim, siman 128. [The questioner inquired whether, if the harvesters have guarded wheat in their hands, there is in them a concern of middlemen, which is forbidden because of theft, or whether we say that because he acquires through a change (wheat made into flour), and whether it is permitted to recite a blessing over them. And at the beginning of his responsum the author, of blessed memory, wrote what had always been puzzling to him: why do we rely on harvesting by a non-Jew, and what appeared to him to resolve our custom. And afterward he wrote on the questioner's words that at first glance it would seem as he said, that the chasidim who harvest themselves according to the secrets of the Torah would lose according to the revealed [law] out of concern for middlemen; nevertheless one can learn a merit, according to what Magen Avraham wrote in siman 473, s.k. 14: Mahariv wrote, etc.]
