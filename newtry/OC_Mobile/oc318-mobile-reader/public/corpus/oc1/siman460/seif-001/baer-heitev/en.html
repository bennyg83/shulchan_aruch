A non-Jew. The main [view] is that as a measure of piety one should guard from the time of harvesting for matzas mitzvah; but since we do not have fields now, guarding from the time of milling suffices; namely, that the Jew stand over him at the milling is enough, and even one who mills with a hand mill: standing over him suffices, since he is not occupying himself with the flour itself but only doing his labor through a vessel; which is not so of baking and kneading, where his labor and occupation are with the dough and the matzah itself: standing over him does not avail. (And regarding piercing, that they pierce the matzah, there is no insistence; but from Maharil's language one should be stringent.) And so wrote the Bach in the name of Maharshal. Halachah lemaaseh regarding one who was imprisoned and did not wish to eat on the first night of Pesach from matzah that they made before him. And nevertheless the consensus of the later authorities is that if it is impossible otherwise (so as not to nullify an affirmative commandment according to the Torah; not like the Rashba who was stringent; and see Beis Yosef), it is permitted when [a Jew] stands over him, and he should let the non-Jew knead before him and tell him to do it for the sake of Pesach; and it is better to do like this through a deaf-mute, deranged person, or minor than through a non-Jew, if possible; see Chok Yaakov.
A deaf-mute. That is, one who does not hear and does not speak. And a deranged person who loses what they give him. And a minor means one who has no understanding of what is said to him, that he should do it for the sake of the mitzvah that Hashem commanded. Taz. And in Maharil he wrote until he is bar mitzvah, and so ruled Chok Yaakov. And after the fact there is room to permit through a minor. Taz. I have seen those who practice to be lenient even l'chatchila to make the piercings through a minor and the like, since adults also stand over him; and so ruled Maharil; and it is more proper in order that there be many helpers to hurry the matter of making them; and nevertheless if it is possible to be careful regarding matzas mitzvah, one should be careful as much as possible. Chok Yaakov.
