One does not knead, etc. It seems to my humble opinion that specifically kneading and baking are forbidden through a non-Jew, for we require guarding for the sake of the mitzvah, and a non-Jew acts only for his own intent, even if a Jew stands over him and warns him to intend for the sake of the mitzvah, as Beis Yosef wrote in the name of the responsa of the Rashba; but milling the wheat is not forbidden through a non-Jew if a Jew stands over him. And even though the Tur wrote in siman 463 in the name of the She'iltos that it must be guarded from leavening from when water falls upon it, and if a non-Jew, a deaf-mute, a deranged person, or a minor guarded the wheat, one does not fulfill one's obligation with it: that is because of leavening; consequently, if a Jew stands over it and guards from leavening, there is no prohibition; which is not so regarding kneading and baking, where it is not because of leavening but that one intend for the sake of matzah: in this specifically it is forbidden with a non-Jew and with a deaf-mute, deranged person, or minor. And this is proven, for these two laws, namely the one here and the one in siman 453, are [both] of the She'iltos; and above it mentioned guarding from chametz, and here it mentioned guarding for the sake of the mitzvah; rather, certainly as I explained. And the reason is that the essence of making the matzah is kneading and baking, and this needs [to be] for the sake of the mitzvah. And if so, what the She'iltos forbid here regarding kneading matzos through a non-Jew means even with a Jew standing over him. And my teacher and father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote here: it appears that it deals with wheat that was milled by a Jew, for if [milled] by a non-Jew, a deaf-mute, a deranged person, or a minor, even if a competent person kneaded and baked, one does not fulfill one's obligation, as above in siman 453; end quote. Implying even with a Jew standing over him, similar to the prohibitions of a non-Jew; and in truth according to my words it is plainly permitted. And so they practice in all places where there are no water mills, such as in most of the lands of Lithuania, where they mill with hand mills through a non-Jew, and there is no concern in this if a Jew stands over him. And all this deals with matzas mitzvah; but with other matzos of Pesach that a non-Jew baked before a Jew, it is permitted, like milling that we mentioned, for here there is concern only because of leavening; and this is plain, for they explicitly permitted even doughs of a non-Jew in siman 454, even according to Rabbeinu Chananel's explanation that Beis Yosef brings there, that it deals with where a non-Jew kneaded before him: in any case this is permitted, only that one does not fulfill one's obligation with it. The upshot of this is that matzas mitzvah is forbidden through a non-Jew and through a deaf-mute, deranged person, or minor, even if a Jew stands over him; and therefore one should be careful not to give matzas mitzvah to a minor even to pierce the matzah. And a deaf-mute means one who does not hear and does not speak; and a deranged person means one who loses what they give him, as written in Yoreh Deah, siman 1; and a minor here means one who is not of understanding, for it is explained in the responsum of the Rashba that Beis Yosef brings that the matter depends on understanding, namely that he has comprehension of what is said to him, that he should do it for the sake of the mitzvah that Hashem commanded. And in Maharil it is written until he is bar mitzvah, and it appears that that is an excessive stringency; and after the fact it appears that it is permitted in any case with a deaf-mute, deranged person, or minor, for Beis Yosef brings in the name of Orchos Chaim in the name of the Rosh and the Rif and Rambam that one need not be concerned who the baker is; and Rabbeinu Hai permits matzah baked by a non-Jew before a Jew; and if so, one should permit at least after the fact in a pressing time. And from here there is wonder at what Maharshal wrote in one gloss that my teacher and father-in-law, of blessed memory, copied: and these are its words: and an elderly or sick person who fears coming to leavening and has no helpers: it is better that he pay a non-Jew to do it before his eyes; and there was a case of one who was imprisoned and no Jew was found there, and the non-Jews wished to prepare [matzah] and he did not wish to eat what non-Jews prepared for him on the first night, and on the rest of Pesach he ate what they made before him, and he did not know [how to recite] the Haggadah, and he had a Chumash and said all the passages of the Exodus from Egypt; end of his words. And in truth he should have eaten even on the first night in order to fulfill the affirmative commandment according to the Torah; and so my teacher and father-in-law, of blessed memory, challenged him; also in the gloss itself he brings a case that contradicts; therefore one should not heed that case for halachah.
