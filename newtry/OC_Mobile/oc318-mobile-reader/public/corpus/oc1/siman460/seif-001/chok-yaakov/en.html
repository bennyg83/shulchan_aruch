Matzas mitzvah. Which is not so of other matzos: they need no [special] guarding; but Jews are holy and practiced to do guarding for all the matzos, as written above in siman 453, s.k. 15.
Through a non-Jew. See Beis Yosef in siman 453 and in this siman: it is explained that there are many differing views among early and later poskim; and what emerges for law, it seems to me the main [view] is that as a measure of piety one should guard from the time of harvesting for matzas mitzvah; but since we do not have fields now, guarding from the time of milling suffices; namely, that the Jew stand over him at the milling is enough, and even one who mills with a hand mill through them: standing over him suffices, since he is not occupying himself with the flour itself but only doing his labor through a vessel; which is not so of baking and kneading, where his labor and occupation are with the dough and the matzah itself: standing over him does not avail; and so it is implied in the Shelah; see there. And so wrote the Bach in the name of a gloss of Maharshal, as halachah lemaaseh regarding one who was imprisoned and did not wish to eat on the first night of Pesach from matzah that a non-Jew made before him; end quote. (And even guarding for the sake of matzah is only a mere asmachta of rabbinic [status], as written in the Rambam, chapter 5 of the laws of chametz; and how did he nullify an affirmative commandment according to the Torah? One may say that passive refraining is different; and that is the reason of lulav and shofar, that they nullify because of Rabbah's decree; and with this the wonderings of the Bach and Taz are removed; and see Yevamos, folio 90.) And nevertheless the consensus of the later authorities is that if it is impossible otherwise, it is permitted when [a Jew] stands over him; and it is better to do like this through a deaf-mute, deranged person, or minor, who are not capable of guarding, than through a non-Jew, if possible; and so wrote in Sefer Tanya that one should be careful of them in all matters of Pesach in whatever one can; and so wrote Maggid Mishneh: and whoever is stringent, this one is praiseworthy; end quote.
And not through a deaf-mute, a deranged person, or a minor. That is, one who does not hear and does not speak. And a deranged person who loses what they give him, as explained in Yoreh Deah, siman 1. And a minor means one who has not reached thirteen years and one day; and so wrote Maharil; and the Taz challenged him, and his words are not compelling; on the contrary, the implication of the poskim is that they compare this law to gittin and chalitzah and shechitah, and there we specifically require a full adult, as it is in the first chapter of Chullin: a minor has no intent; and see there. And after the fact he ruled that there is room to permit through a minor. And I have seen those who practice to be lenient even l'chatchila to make the piercings through a minor and the like, since adults also stand over him; and so ruled Maharil; and it is more proper in order that there be many helpers to hurry the matter of making them; and nevertheless if it is possible to be careful regarding matzas mitzvah, one should be careful as much as possible.
