And the responsa of the Rashba, of blessed memory, siman 26 and [siman] 493, wrote: one must know that matzah requires guarding: according to one authority from the time of harvesting, and according to one from kneading; and he brought this regarding a deaf-mute, deranged person, or minor; and he also wrote explicitly guarding for the sake of matzah, whether guarding of harvesting or of kneading, according to one and according to one; behold, explicitly that there is no distinction. And according to the authority that [requires guarding] from the time of harvesting, every making specifically needs [to be] for its sake; and in Pesachim 40a Rava said: overturn [the grain] for the sake of the mitzvah, implying that we require all guarding for its sake. And see in the Rif: "make," that is to say, be careful that water not come upon them, implying like the Taz, that even according to the authority that guarding is from the time of harvesting, nevertheless one need not make specifically for the sake of matzas mitzvah except from kneading onward; but before that, a non-Jew with a Jew watching him that it not come to leavening suffices; and from kneading onward, when one occupies oneself with the body of the mitzvah, we require for its sake; and this distinction needs a bit of further study. And see Eliyah Rabbah, letter 1: certainly if possible, Jews should harvest for its sake, and every making, whether milling and the like, of those careful with shemurah matzah. And what he wrote regarding a minor of understanding: see Eliyah Rabbah and Chok Yaakov, implying that we specifically require thirteen years and two hairs; see there; and it is implied that even piercing l'chatchila should be by adults, specifically Beis Yosef. And whether guarding for the sake of matzah is according to the Torah or rabbinic: in the Bach it is implied that it is a rabbinic prohibition; and see Rambam, chapter 5 of the laws of chametz u'matzah, halachah 8-9, which Chok Yaakov brought in letter 2; and Peri Chadash wrote that guarding for the sake of matzah is from the Torah; and so it is implied in the Rashba, of blessed memory, simanim 26 and 493, and Levush, siman 454; see there. And in the Rambam, of blessed memory, there is no decisive [proof], for he so wrote regarding grain that did not crack, that from the Torah one forbids, for there is no leavening, and the Sages forbade because we require complete guarding; and God willing in [siman] 467 it will be explained further. And accordingly one should not rely l'chatchila to say that presumably they have thirteen years and two hairs until they are adults in years; see siman 55 in Magen Avraham, letter 7, and [siman] 199, letter 7. And what Chok Yaakov wrote, that for that reason he did not eat, for they upheld their words: it is difficult; there is here no prohibition of concern for leavening, only that one does not fulfill one's obligation of eating matzah according to one view; if so, why did he not eat, and [why] not recite a blessing, for blessings do not hold up [the mitzvah]; and the second night is equal to the first night, for all its laws are equal; and with doughs of a non-Jew one fills his belly and eats a kezayit of matzah at the end; see Levush 454 when they were kneaded before a Jew and it is recognizable that there is no concern of leavening, and even a Jew standing from afar: all the more so in a case like this. And this "for its sake": it appears that speaking [it] with the mouth suffices; see Even HaEzer; however it appears that after the fact even thinking alone suffices, and the unspecified status of matzah is for its sake; which is not so of milling and harvesting, whose unspecified status is not for its sake; and this requires further study. And bread of a non-Jew: see Yoreh Deah 112 regarding this.
And one who has only a scant kezayit on the first night from which challah was not taken from the dough: how should he act? If he eats it as is, he does not fulfill his obligation, even regarding tevel of rabbinic [status]; and it is not [like the] case of one who eats and afterward separates [challah], from Magen Avraham; see Yoreh Deah that so he wrote: he should separate challah and nullify this kezayit in the majority and fulfill the affirmative commandment according to the Torah; see Shach there, letter 14. And when kneaded from before Yom Tov, for it is forbidden to separate on Yom Tov: see siman 506; one may say that they upheld their words; see siman 446; and this requires further study. And specifically when kneaded on Yom Tov like this; and God willing in [siman] 475 it will be explained further.
And behold, if we say that from the Torah we require guarding from kneading, and a non-Jew and a deaf-mute, deranged person, or minor with a Jew standing over him: after the fact one may rely on Rabbeinu Hai and the Rosh and the Rif like this; and that of doughs in Pesachim 40b, only that one eat a kezayit of matzah at the end, deals with where one does not warn him, etc.; if so, when one has no other, one may rely on this; and it is possible that one recite a blessing, for it is a double doubt: perhaps the law is like Rabbeinu Hai, and perhaps guarding is only rabbinic; and all rabbinic disqualifications in a pressing time they set upon the Torah law; see siman 629 in Magen Avraham, letter 22; and in the introduction to the laws of blessings we wrote about this, whether for a rabbinic disqualification in a pressing time one recites a blessing, and whether with a double doubt one recites a blessing. And if a non-Jew and a deaf-mute, deranged person, or minor kneaded, and there is no Jew standing over him but only from afar: according to the Bach that it is rabbinic, one should eat on the first night, and it appears that one eat without a blessing; and even where these kneaded and [a Jew] stands over him, it requires further study whether one recites a blessing; and as we said, see what I wrote in [siman] 644 regarding sleeping in a sukkah, from Chag HaMatzos according to Rabbah and R' Yochanan regarding thanksgiving-loaves, and in the Yerushalmi according to his method; see there; and understand.
