Magen Avraham wrote: and these are the words of Mahariv, etc. It implies specifically these three labors, etc. It implies somewhat that milling too, even with a hand mill, or the law regarding milling, is permitted through a non-Jew; and so is the view of the Taz, that even though in any case from the time of milling one needs guarding, as written in siman 453, that means guarding that it not leaven; but for the sake of the mitzvah, guarding of these three labors suffices; not like the Bach; see there. And see Chok Yaakov, who is lenient with a hand mill even without this, since it is done through a vessel: what difference does it make to him?
And these are the words of the Rashba, etc. Guarding from the time of harvesting, etc.; and that is like the Rif's view above in siman 453, se'if 4; and see there in Peri Chadash that even as an absolute requirement, according to the Rif, matzas mitzvah requires guarding for the sake of the mitzvah from the time of harvesting. In the name of the Rashba: even if the Jew stands over him. And that is to say: if he tells him to do it for the sake of Pesach, it does not avail, for a non-Jew acts for his own intent.
But in siman 32, se'if 9: where we require that the parchment be for the need of writing tefillin, we require processing for its sake. And the Rema wrote there that they practice like the Rosh, that it avails through a non-Jew if a Jew stands over him. And so it is in siman 11, se'if 2, regarding spinning of tzitzis, for which we also require for its sake; see there. And siman 39, se'if 2: see there in Magen Avraham, s.k. 16, that that means processing the straps. But regarding writing, Magen Avraham wrote there in s.k. 2 that the Rosh agrees that it is invalid through a non-Jew. And it is good that he assist, etc. And even though in siman 32 he wrote "if a Jew stands over him and assisted a bit in the work," implying that assistance is required by law, and so he wrote in siman 11: even so Magen Avraham wrote the language "and it is good, etc.," implying that it is only a stringency; that is as the Bach and Shach wrote in Yoreh Deah, siman 271, s.k. 5, that it is only l'chatchila on the side of the more desirable, not like the Beis Yosef; see there.
And nevertheless it is better, etc. Through a minor. And in Chok Yaakov he wrote: it is good to do through a deaf-mute, deranged person, or minor, etc., implying that a deaf-mute and a deranged person too are preferable to a non-Jew. And to my humble opinion: a deranged person, a deaf-mute, and a minor: a deaf-mute and a minor are preferable; for a deranged person has no understanding at all. But regarding a deaf-mute and a minor, it seems to my humble opinion that a deaf-mute is preferable; for behold R' Elazar holds in Yevamos, folio 113a, that a deaf-mute who separated terumah: his terumah is a matter of doubt, for he is unsure whether we say his mind is clear or weak, or he is unsure whether at times he is healthy and at times deranged, as explained there; and there are Amoraim there who hold like him. And regarding a minor there is no authority who says that his terumah is terumah even as a doubt. And although we do not hold in this like R' Elazar, nevertheless even according to us one may say that he is preferable to a minor. And even though above in siman 266, se'if 5, a deaf-mute and a minor are equal: there there is another reason, as explained there in Shabbos, folio 153.
And a deaf-mute means one who does not hear and does not speak; and a deranged person means one who loses what they give him, as written in Yoreh Deah, siman 1; so wrote the Taz. And it is further explained there the signs of a deranged person: he goes out alone at night, or tears his clothing, or lodges in a cemetery; and with one of these four things he is called deranged. And specifically when that is his way; but because of one time, even if he acted in a deranged manner, he is not called deranged. And even if that is his way, specifically when he does it in a deranged manner; for otherwise, even if he did all of them, he is not called deranged; so it is there in Yoreh Deah. And a minor: Maharil's view is that until he is bar mitzvah he is called a minor; and the Taz holds that once he has understanding he is no longer called a minor; and see the later authorities.
