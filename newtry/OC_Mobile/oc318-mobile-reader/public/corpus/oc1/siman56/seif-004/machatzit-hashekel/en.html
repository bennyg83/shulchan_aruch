(Se'if 4) And one should stand, etc. However in my view what he wrote that the Jerusalem Talmud is mistaken, etc. — meaning per the simple understanding of the Ari, that the Jerusalem Talmud in Jerusalem can be understood that this law is not reasonable to him, or he had a tradition that the law in the Jerusalem Talmud is mistaken; and one cannot dispute the Ari without reason. Therefore Magen Avraham: in my view his reason for what he wrote that the Jerusalem Talmud's law is mistaken is because the proof it brings from the Jerusalem Talmud from Judges chapter 3, that Ehud said to Eglon king of Moab "arise, for God has spoken to you" and he arose from the throne — from which they derive in any case for Kedushah one stands (even though for mentioning the Name one does not stand, for words of prophecy are different) — and since it is not written in Scripture that the prophet commanded him to stand, the Jerusalem Talmud's proof does not apply; therefore its law also does not apply, and the entire law is mistaken; and since the Ari's reason is known, we have in our hands to resolve his difficulty and uphold the Jerusalem Talmud's law, as Magen Avraham wrote.

That he commanded him to stand — Yoma 11 is not the simple meaning; but in my humble opinion in Sanhedrin page 60a it does not imply thus, for we taught there: when the judgment of the blasphemer was finished, the greatest of the witnesses must say before the Sanhedrin the Name that he blasphemed; and we taught the judges stand then on their feet; and he brings proof from the aforementioned verse "and he arose from the throne" — are these not a fortiori: what Eglon king of Moab, who was a gentile, stood — Israel all the more so, etc. It implies Ehud did not command him, since he did not bring proof from Ehud's words. And it is possible our Gemara disputes the Jerusalem Talmud.
