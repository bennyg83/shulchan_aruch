(Seif katan 4) One should stand, etc. Nevertheless, it appears to me that the statement that this is a scribal error means as follows. The plain understanding is that the Arizal said the Yerushalmi contains a scribal error, which could be understood to mean that he considered the law itself unreasonable or had a tradition that the Yerushalmi’s ruling was erroneous. One cannot disagree with the Arizal without explanation. Therefore the Magen Avraham writes that, in his view, the Arizal’s reason for saying that the Yerushalmi’s ruling was a scribal error is that the Yerushalmi adduces proof from Judges, chapter 3, where Ehud said to Eglon king of Moav, ‘Arise, for I have a word of God for you,’ and he arose from the throne. Since Ehud commanded him to stand, the Yerushalmi derives that at least for a matter of holiness one must stand, even though one does not stand merely upon mention of Hashem’s Name, because words of prophecy are different. Yet Scripture does not state that the prophet commanded him to stand. Therefore the Yerushalmi’s proof is absent, and consequently its ruling too is absent, with the whole passage being a scribal error. Once the Arizal’s reason is known, we can answer his difficulty and uphold the Yerushalmi’s ruling, as the Magen Avraham writes.
This proves that he commanded him to stand. Sefer Yereim is not available to me. Yet in my humble opinion Sanhedrin 60a does not imply this. The Mishnah there teaches that when the blasphemer’s trial is concluded, the greatest of the witnesses must state before the Sanhedrin the Name that he blasphemed, and the judges then stand. The Gemara adduces proof from the verse, ‘He arose from the throne,’ and derives an a fortiori argument: if Eglon king of Moav, who was a non-Jew, stood, then Jews certainly must stand. This implies that Ehud did not command him, for otherwise the proof should have been brought from Ehud’s words. It is possible that on this point the Bavli disagrees with the Yerushalmi.
