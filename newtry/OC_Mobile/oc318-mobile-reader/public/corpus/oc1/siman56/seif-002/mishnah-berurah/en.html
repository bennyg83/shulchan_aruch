(12) After yitbarakh—but after Yitgadal ve'yitkadash sh'meih rabbah the custom is to answer Amen [and it is surprising that the Shulchan Aruch did not cite this Amen, which is clear in the Rambam and was cited in Beit Yosef].

(13) After b'rikh Hu—some err by, when the shatz says b'rikh Hu, also answering those words "b'rikh Hu l'eila," which is certainly blasphemy, implying He is blessed only above and not below, God forbid; rather they should also say "mikol birkhata" or say only b'rikh Hu as is our custom.

(14) And do not interrupt—see Sha'agat Aryeh, who brought from Sefer HaKavanot proof to the contrary, that the word b'rikh Hu is attached to d'kudsha, and afterward there is a slight pause like an etnachta, and then one begins again l'eila, etc.—meaning that His name be blessed and praised above, etc.; and the Gra agreed in his commentary.
