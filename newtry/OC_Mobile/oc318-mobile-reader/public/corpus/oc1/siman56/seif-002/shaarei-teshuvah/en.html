Yitbarakh—Be'er Heitev. As above in Kavanot: if the responder finishes through "ba'olma" before the shatz, he must answer Amen after sh'meih d'kudsha b'rikh Hu, as above; and from this it is clear that b'rikh Hu must be attached to the words d'kudsha, and not as Darkei Moshe wrote in the name of Or Zarua that Magen Avraham brought, and likewise in Hagahah; rather the pause slightly like an etnachta is after the word b'rikh Hu, and then one begins again l'eila, etc.—meaning that His name be blessed and praised above, etc.; and see below siman 66 that where interruption is forbidden one should say only yitbarakh, as above.
