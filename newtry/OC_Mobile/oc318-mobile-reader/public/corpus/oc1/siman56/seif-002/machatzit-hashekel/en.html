Both this and that. Meaning: earlier Rava said a person should not say y'hei sh'meih rabbah and then m'varakh, but rather "y'hei sh'meih rabbah m'varakh" together. Rav Safra said: both this and that — resolving the matter; we have no ruling — meaning both regarding "barukh haba" and regarding y'hei sh'meih rabbah.

And thus the Gra wrote, etc. — from him flow the words of Rama.

If so, even between "rabbah" and "m'varakh." Meaning according to Ri one may not interrupt between "sh'meih" and "rabbah," nor between "rabbah" and "m'varakh," for it is all one word (not as appears from the commentators in Hagahot).

In both one may interrupt. Meaning according to Rav Safra it is resolving the matter. According to Ri, between "sh'meih" and "rabbah" and between "rabbah" and "m'varakh" are equal.

And it seems to me he holds, etc. — what the Shakh wrote in the margin; see Tosafot there. Meaning: do not say we do not hold like Rav Safra but are stringent like Rava — for this is not so, for Tosafot wrote there "amar Rava" and thus: you might say at the end of mitzvat chalitzah Abbaye said regarding one who calls the yevamah when she performs chalitzah that she must say the verse "lo avah yavami" — the yevam must say "lo chafatzti lakachat" — he should not call to her "lo" alone or "avah yavami" alone, for "avah yavami" alone implies "avah yavami"; rather "lo avah yavami" in one breath. And he should not call to her "lo" alone or "chafatzti lakachat" alone, for it implies "chafatzti lakachat"; rather "lo chafatzti lakachat" in one breath. Rava said: resolving the matter — we have no ruling on this. One could say after he heard here from Rav Safra, until here — behold Rava himself retracted. See Tosafot Yevamot, who wrote another explanation that Rava did not retract.

For in any case one could say in one breath "lo avah yavami" and "lo chafatzti lakachat" — for even though by law one breath is not required, as Rava resolved above, nevertheless we are stringent; and Rama holds the same regarding y'hei sh'meih rabbah m'varakh. And even though in chalitzah they are stringent that it must be in one breath, and here it teaches one may not interrupt — it must be that Rama holds this is lighter than chalitzah and we descend by degrees. And for "l'olam" with vav: some wish to say "l'olam" without vav so there are not seven words from "y'hei" until "l'olam" but twenty-eight letters. Therefore "whoever answers y'hei sh'meih rabbah with all his strength — they tear his decree," etc. — meaning twenty-eight letters; and therefore Rabbeinu Bechayei wrote one should not change from the coin the Sages stamped as we received from our ancestors — to say "l'olam" with vav because of some derashah. Rabbeinu Bechayei also wrote: therefore some say "sh'meih" without yod so there are twenty-eight letters; and for those who say until "d'amiran b'alma" they are twenty-eight words.
