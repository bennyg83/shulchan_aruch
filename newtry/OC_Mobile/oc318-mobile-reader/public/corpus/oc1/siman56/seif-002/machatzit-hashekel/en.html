(Seif katan 2) ‘And not,’ etc. This is stated in the chapter Lulav HaGazul, 38b.
Both this and that. Meaning, earlier Rava said that a person should not say, when reciting Hallel, ‘Baruch haba.’ He then said that a person should not say ‘Yehei Shemei Rabbah,’ etc. Rav Safra said regarding this, ‘Both this and that,’ meaning both regarding ‘Baruch haba’ and regarding ‘Yehei Shemei Rabbah.’
This is the wording of the Hagahos Ashri, etc. The Rama’s ruling derives from there.
If so, even between rabbah, etc. Meaning, according to Rabbeinu Yitzchak’s explanation, one may not interrupt between ‘shemei’ and ‘rabbah,’ nor between ‘rabbah’ and ‘mevorach,’ because it is all one phrase, contrary to what appears from the commentators on the gloss.
For in both of them one may interrupt. Meaning, according to Rav Safra, concluding the phrase is unnecessary. According to Rabbeinu Yitzchak’s explanation, the interval between ‘shemei’ and ‘rabbah’ and the interval between ‘rabbah’ and ‘mevorach’ are equivalent.
It appears to me that he holds, etc. The Shach wrote in the margin, ‘See Tosafos there.’ His meaning is that one should not say that we do not rule like Rav Safra, but rather are stringent like Rava. That is incorrect, for Tosafos there, s.v. Amar Rava, write: ‘This is difficult, for at the end of Mitzvas Chalitzah Abaye said that one who prompts the yevamah, when she performs chalitzah and must recite “lo avah yevami,” and the yavam must say “lo chafatzti lekachtah,” should not prompt her with “lo” separately and “avah yevami” separately, for that would imply “my brother-in-law desired”; rather, he must prompt her with “lo avah yevami” together. Likewise, he should not prompt him with “lo” separately and “chafatzti lekachtah” separately, for that would imply “I desired to take her”; rather, “lo chafatzti lekachtah” together. Rava said that completing the phrase is unnecessary. One may answer that Rava said this after hearing Rav Safra’s teaching here.’ Thus Rava himself retracted. See Tosafos in Yevamos, who offer another answer according to which Rava did not retract.
Nevertheless, ‘lo avah yevami’ and ‘lo chafatzti lekachtah’ should be said in one breath. Although by strict law one breath is unnecessary, as Rava concludes, nevertheless we are stringent. The Rama holds that the same stringency applies to ‘Yehei Shemei Rabbah mevorach.’ Although regarding chalitzah we are stringent that it be recited in one breath, whereas here the teaching says only that one may not interrupt, implying that breathing between the words is at least permitted, one must say that the Rama holds this case to be more lenient than chalitzah and therefore lowers the requirement by one degree. One should say ‘ule’almei’ with a vav. Some wish to say ‘le’almei’ without a vav so that from ‘yehei’ through ‘almaya’ there will be exactly twenty-eight letters. Concerning this it is said that whoever answers ‘Yehei Shemei Rabbah’ with all his strength has his harsh decree torn up, meaning with the twenty-eight letters. The Beit Yosef therefore writes that one should not alter the formula established by the Sages, as received from our predecessors, by omitting the vav because of any homiletical interpretation. The Beit Yosef also writes that for this reason some say ‘shemei’ without a yud so that there will be twenty-eight letters, while according to those who recite through ‘da’amiran be’alma’ there are twenty-eight words.
