Almaya, yisbarach. Beit Yosef implies that one must say the entire formula from “yisbarach” through “da’amiran b’alma,” totaling twenty eight words. The Shelah writes likewise. It appears to me, however, that the practice of the early authorities is primary, for they would answer only through “yisbarach.” Beit Yosef writes at the beginning of siman 125 in the name of the Rosh that one must remain silent and concentrate while the chazzan recites Kaddish, and should not recite it together with him, because one who hears is considered as though he answered. In particular, when the chazzan recites it quickly, the individual would have to hurry even more in order to finish before the chazzan, as written at the beginning of siman 51, and haste prevents concentration. It is therefore preferable to listen to the chazzan. The Shelah writes that “veyisbarach” is said with a vav, but our custom is to say it without a vav, and this is also implied by Beit Yosef and the Writings.
