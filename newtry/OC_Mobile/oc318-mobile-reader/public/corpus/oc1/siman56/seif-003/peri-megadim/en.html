And not Taz and Magen Avraham note het—that they do so to indicate there is no interruption between b'rikh Hu and l'eila; and Taz wrote that on the contrary their enactment ruined it, and one must also say l'eila from mikol birkhata, and not pause between b'rikh Hu and l'eila from mikol until mikol birkhata. Or Zarua: the shatz should say in one breath at keri'at Shema "v'imru amen," for the congregation does not answer Amen until the shatz says Amen. Similarly it is not proper at the end of a blessing not to finish the ending properly while the congregation answers hurried Amen; see 124 where more is explained. I looked in Daniel 2:20 "lehevei sh'mah di tilei m'varakh" with mappik hei without yod; therefore Alkabetz wrote sh'meih thus in his siddur, as in Targum. Difficult on Taz here, and Darkei Moshe and Bach. This is by way of remez not peshat, for sh'mah with two mems tzere indicates power of yod of name Y-H, hint to din as books say "yisser yissereni Yah..." in Bahyei similarly, and Elohim Elohim vemi yipared—in exile as Peri Chadash in name of Saba on Mishpatim—in exile separated mi and ascended shamayim and buildings fell, as above. It appears we say doubly Yitgadal Yitkadash sh'meih rabbah—name Y-H rabbah—and say Amen yehei shmei rabbah once on the essence—why stand afar? Name Y-H afar min vav, and second pray for Elohim separated from mi, and thus "al eleh ani bokhiah." Zohar on Isaiah placed mem-yod-alef-lamed-hei... Therefore remez sh'meh name Y-H di Elohim... Daniel saw din is mercy in exile to purge sin, therefore blessed leih—understand.
