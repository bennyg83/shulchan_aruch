See Ateres Zekeinim and Magen Avraham, letter 8, who explain that some do this to indicate that one should not pause between “berich Hu” and “le’eila.” Taz writes that, to the contrary, their attempted correction causes damage. One must also say “le’eila min kol birchasa,” and should not pause from “berich Hu” through “le’eila mikol birchasa.” See Eliyah Rabbah, who writes that the chazzan should say “bizman kariv ve’imru Amen” in one breath, because the congregation does not answer Amen until the chazzan says “Amen.” Similarly, those who do not fully complete the end of a blessing and allow the congregation to answer Amen do not act properly, for this is like a snatched Amen. See siman 124, where, with Hashem’s help, this will be explained further. I examined Daniel 2:20, “Lehevei shemei di Elaha mevarach,” where “shemei” has a pronounced final hei but no yud. According to this, Rabbi Hanau wrote correctly in his prayer book that “shemei” should be written in this manner, because this is its form in Aramaic. This presents a difficulty for Taz, Darchei Moshe, and Bach. By way of allusion, but Heaven forbid not as the plain meaning, one may say that because the second mem of “shemei” has a tzerei, the power of the yud of the Name Yah is present within it, alluding to judgment. The works of Kabbalah write similarly concerning “Yah has chastened me severely” and “Yah is my strength and song,” apparently in the name of Rabbeinu Bachya. The words “Elohim,” “Eleh,” and “Mi” became separated, due to our many sins, in exile. Peri Chadash writes this in the name of the elder cited in the Zohar to Mishpatim: in exile, “Mi” separated and ascended to Heaven, while the structures fell; see there. It therefore appears that we say “Yisgadal veyiskadash shemei rabba” twice in effect. “The Name Yah is great,” and we say one “Amen, yehei shemei rabba” regarding the Essential Name, as in “Why, Hashem, do You stand afar,” for the Name Yah is distant from the letters vav and hei. The second prayer concerns the word “Eleh,” which became separated from “Mi.” This is the meaning of “For these I weep.” The Zohar writes that this is the meaning of the verse in Yeshayahu, “Who are these that fly like a cloud, and like doves to their windows,” joining “Mi” and “Eleh.” We wrote about this in Sefer HaMaggid on parashas Ki Savo, and this is not the place for it. Accordingly, “shemei” alludes to the Name Yah, and “di Elaha” alludes to Eleh. Daniel thus perceived that judgment itself is mercy in exile, cleansing sin, and therefore blessed Him for it. Understand this.
