(15) Almaya, yisbarach. The Beit Yosef implies that one should recite the entire formula from ‘Yisbarach’ through ‘da’amiran be’alma,’ twenty-eight words. See Magen Avraham, who concludes that the ancient custom is primary, namely, to say only through ‘Yisbarach’ and then remain silent, concentrating on what the chazzan says, for hearing is equivalent to answering. Biur HaGra writes that one should say only through the word ‘almaya,’ because that is the conclusion of the praise ‘Amen, Yehei Shemei Rabbah,’ while from ‘Yisbarach’ onward a new section begins and the two should not be combined. It appears that even according to the Gra, if one says them in two breaths it is permitted. In practice, customs differ by locality. In any event, when standing in a place where interruption is forbidden, one should be careful to say only through ‘almaya,’ as stated by Chayei Adam.
