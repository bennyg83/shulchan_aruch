Those who answer, etc.—because, etc. In the midrash, R' Eliezer b'R' Yosi chapter 1: I was walking on the road and found Eliyahu z"l with four thousand camels laden. I said to him, "What are these laden with?" He said, "Wrath and fury to take vengeance with wrath and fury on one who counts between Kaddish and Barekhu, between blessing and blessing, between section and section, between "Amen yishtabach rabba" and "yitbarakh"—and likewise Ra'avad: one may rely on the secret. But all the poskim wrote to answer only until "l'almai almaya," which has twenty-eight letters—and likewise the Geonim, and likewise Rambam in Seder haTefillot there. From the midrash there is proof to the contrary, as they said—for he counts it among the sections and among between Kaddish and Barekhu, between blessing and blessing—and he only forbade not to speak between them, because the individual answers until "Sh'meih" and hears from there and is silent; and common people are accustomed to speak—and likewise between Kaddish, etc. Therefore he did not say "from one who counts" before "l'almai" and in the middle of the blessing; and likewise what Ra'avad wrote per the secret—it seems to us it is not better than between sections, for per the secret they are adjacent as known to one who understands that they are intertwined, and you have no greater cutting than "Sh'meih"—and he also forbade in the midrash cited. Nevertheless, one may be lenient together, and it is easier than in the middle of a section, as written in the Mishnah; except that from the order of R' Eliezer and Rambam, who wrote to answer "Amen" after "yitbarakh," it implies that there the matter ends; therefore the Tur wrote "yishtabach" without a vav. Nevertheless Rambam and the Tur wrote to answer only until "l'almai almaya"—and that is the main point.
