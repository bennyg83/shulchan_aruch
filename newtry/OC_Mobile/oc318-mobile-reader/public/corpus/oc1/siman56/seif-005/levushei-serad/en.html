(Se'if 5, siman 124) There at the end of the siman in Hagahat Maharshal: if one hears most of the congregation answering amen and he did not hear the blessing at all but knows which blessing it refers to — he answers amen with them; the same here when he hears amen from most of the congregation and knows this amen is on Kaddish — he also says amen. This is what Magen Avraham wrote: if he can direct his mind to know, etc. — meaning he gave heart and knew the shaliach tzibbur is saying Kaddish even though he did not hear the words of the shaliach tzibbur — he says amen with most of the congregation. Not so if at the time they said amen he did not know what it referred to — even if afterward he hears y'hei sh'meih rabbah and understands it was said on Kaddish, he does not say amen but only y'hei sh'meih rabbah; even though it is not like an orphaned amen, since he knows it was said on Kaddish — nevertheless the amen from most of the congregation has already finished, for they are already saying y'hei sh'meih rabbah; and it is forbidden to say amen, for we require that one say amen adjacent to the blessing or Kaddish, or at the time most of the congregation answers, as explained there in se'if 4. Alternatively one could say there is also [a problem] of orphaned amen, since at the time the congregation said amen he did not know what it referred to — therefore even if afterward he hears y'hei sh'meih rabbah it is an orphaned amen; understand.
