Tur gives a further explanation: the words are literal, and "Shemei rabba" is the Aramaic translation of "His great Name." Accordingly, one must say "Shemei rabba mevarach" in one breath, without pausing. Rema, Lechem Chamudos on folio 24, and Bach rule likewise. Bach adds that, to satisfy also the interpretation that "Shemei" means the Name Yah, one should clearly pronounce its final hei. Shelah writes that one should pause slightly between Amen and "yehei Shemei rabba," because Amen refers to "Yisgadal." Similarly, one should pause between Amen and Modim because Amen refers to Retzei. He further writes that "mevarach" should be vocalized with a kamatz, because it is Aramaic, whereas "mevorach" is Hebrew. I also received the tradition that "le'alam" is vocalized with a sheva under the first lamed, a kamatz under the ayin, and a patach under the final lamed, like the Aramaic word in Daniel. Kitzur Shelah writes that one should say "di vera" as two words. It appears to me, however, that "devara" can also bear this meaning. We similarly find in the Targum to Chukas that "asher ein" is rendered "deleit," though the Hebrew consists of two words, and there are many similar cases, including "asher ta'aseh" and "avodeihem te'abedun." This is also explained in Beis Yosef, Even HaEzer, siman 126, regarding wording in a get such as "deyasva, detehevein," and "detzivein." Because of the stringency of a get, some adopted the stricter practice there, but that concern does not apply here. I found in Sefer HaManhig that Michael, Gavriel, and Raphael, who are appointed over Israel, understand Aramaic. See below, siman 101. Sefer Tanya explains that the enemies in their time decreed that Kaddish not be recited, so it was instituted in Aramaic in order that they not recognize it. Even after the decree was annulled, the earlier practice was not restored, so that the miracles and wonders would not be forgotten. Kol Bo explains that it is so the angels will not prosecute Israel for praying for the Redemption, when the righteous will be greater than the angels. Angels by nature can do only good and therefore deserve no particular credit for not sinning, whereas the righteous overcome their evil inclination. Abudraham writes that one should not question why "yisbarach, veyishtabach, veyispa'er," and the following praises were instituted in Hebrew, for these praises could not be rendered into Aramaic. See there also regarding the Kaddish after burial. Knesses HaGedolah cites Rabbeinu Yonah: if one must choose between answering "Amen, yehei Shemei rabba" and Kedushah, he should answer "Amen, yehei Shemei rabba." Nevertheless, if two synagogues are before him, one approaching Kedushah and the other the Kaddish after Shemoneh Esrei, he should go answer Kedushah, because there he will also hear Kaddish after the prayer. Magen Avraham.
