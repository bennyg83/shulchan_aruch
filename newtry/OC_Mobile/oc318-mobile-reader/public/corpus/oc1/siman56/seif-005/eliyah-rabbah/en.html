[5] [Levush] And the explanation that we, etc. I have already written another reason. Another explanation the Tur wrote: that on the plain reading it is a translation of "His great name" — "y'hei sh'meih rabbah." According to this one must say "y'hei sh'meih rabbah m'varakh" all in one breath, and one may not interrupt between them; so ruled Rama and Lechem Chamudot page 24, and so ruled Beit Chadash. But he wrote that to satisfy also the explanation that "sh'meih" is the name Y-H, meaning "sh'meih" with a mappiq hei. The Shlah wrote one must pause slightly between "amen" and "y'hei sh'meih rabbah," for "amen" refers to "yitgadal"; and likewise between "amen" and "modim," for "amen" refers to "ratzeh." He further wrote one must say "m'varakh" with kamatz, for it is Aramaic; but "m'vorakh" is Hebrew. I also received the word "l'olam" pointed with sh'va under the lamed and kamatz under the ayin and patach under the lamed, for the pointing of "l'olam" is like the translation in Daniel. The abridged Shlah wrote one must say "di v'ra" as two words — see there. In my view also that "v'ra" implies thus; we find in Targum Chukat "asher ein" — "d'leit asher ein" — and its meaning is in two words; and likewise in many places "asher ta'asem"; and we find "avudeihem ta'avd"; and it is explained in Beit Yosef to Even HaEzer siman 126 regarding a get "d'yasva d'tihavein d'titz'vayin," and because of the stringency of a get they were somewhat stringent — see there what is not so here. In Sefer HaManhig I found that Michael, Gabriel, and Raphael, who are appointed over Israel, recognize Aramaic; see below siman 101. In Sefer Tanya the Tur wrote the reason: in their days their enemies decreed not to say Kaddish, and they instituted saying it in Aramaic so they would not recognize it; and even though the decree was annulled they did not wish to return the matter to its former state so the miracles and wonders would not be forgotten. Kol Bo wrote the reason: so the angels would not prosecute for praying about redemption, for in the future the righteous will be greater than angels; and the nature of angels is only to do good, and therefore one cannot hold good against them unless they sin; but the righteous overcome their inclination. Avudraham wrote: let it not be difficult in your eyes that they enacted saying "yitbarakh," "yishtabach," "yitpa'ar," etc. in Hebrew, for praise could not be changed to the language of translation. See there regarding Kaddish after burial of the dead. Kenesset HaGedolah wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah: one who happens to answer "amen, y'hei sh'meih rabbah" and Kedushah and cannot answer both — answers "amen, y'hei sh'meih rabbah," until here. Nevertheless, when there are two synagogues before him, in one they reach Kedushah and in one the Kaddish after the eighteen blessings — he goes and answers Kedushah, for there he will also hear Kaddish after the prayer; so wrote Magen Avraham.
