(1) [Se'if 1] One should have concentration when answering Kaddish, etc., and answer it in a loud voice, etc. For it is stated in tractate Shabbat, chapter Kol Kitvei, 119b: Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi said — whoever answers "amen, y'hei sh'meih rabbah m'varakh" with all his strength — they tear up his decree, etc. Rabbenu Yonah wrote in the chapter Mi sheMeto: meaning with all the power of his intention; and because there are people whose intention is not aroused except through force, it says "with all his strength" — but one need not raise a very loud voice so people mock him, until here. So too Rashi explained there, in Kol Kitvei: "with all his strength" — with all his intention. Tosafot wrote there, in Ri's name: it is stated in Pesikta in the story of Rabbi Yishmael ben Elisha, who said there: when Israel enter synagogues and say "y'hei sh'meih rabbah m'varakh" in a loud voice, harsh decrees are annulled, until here. So too in Zohar, parashat Terumah 129b: "and to answer with strong might 'amen, y'hei sh'meih rabbah m'varakh,'" etc. — all this Rabbeinu Bechayei brought and wrote it appears one must answer in a loud voice. Thus the Zohar there: this holiness is not like other holinesses that we triple; but this holiness ascends on all sides above and below and on all sides of faith, and breaks iron locks and seals (meaning: stamps) and evil shells, to raise the glory of the Holy One, blessed be He, over all; and we must say it in the language of the Other Side and answer with strong might "amen, y'hei sh'meih rabbah m'varakh," so the might of the Other Side is broken and the glory of the Holy One, blessed be He, ascends over all. When it is broken in this holiness, the might of the Other Side — the Holy One, blessed be He, ascends in His glory and remembers His children and remembers His name. And because the Holy One, blessed be He, ascends in His glory in this holiness, it is only with ten; and in this language the Other Side is subdued against its will and its might is broken and the glory of the Holy One, blessed be He, ascends, and iron locks and seals and strong chains and evil shells are broken, and the Holy One, blessed be He, remembers His name and His children. Fortunate are the holy people to whom the Holy One, blessed be He, gave the holy Torah to merit in the world to come, until here — Beit Yosef brought this. He wrote that from here one may reflect how much concentration is required in Kaddish and how much effort to answer it, until here. Therefore Maran ruled in Shulchan Aruch that one must do both — concentrate when answering Kaddish and also answer in a loud voice. Further in Zohar, parashat Bereishit 38b: thus — within this hall all those who sanctify with all the might of their Master's name stand and answer "amen, y'hei sh'meih rabbah m'varakh" with all might, and these stand within this hall; and that light comprising all colors shines for them; and from that light they exist and see other lights that unite and do not unite within them; and above them is Mashiach, who enters and stands over them and descends to them — he takes from this hall and enters, until here. Now you have been shown how great is the merit of answering Kaddish with intention and a loud voice; nevertheless one need not raise a very loud voice so people mock him, but raise his voice slightly with all his intention. See Ma'amar Mordechai letter gimel; Mishnah Berurah siman 2 letter lamed dalet; Siddur Beit Yosef on laws of answering Kaddish letter gimel; and below letter tet vav.
