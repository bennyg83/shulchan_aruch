[1] One should have concentration, etc. The chazzan should be careful to emphasize the gimel of "yisgadal," lest it sound like "yiskadal," for kadal is the Aramaic word for the back of the neck. He should not emphasize the beis of "yisbarach," lest it sound like "yisparach," meaning that He should crumble. He should also articulate the hei of "yis'halal," lest, God forbid, it sound like "veyischalal," meaning that He should be profaned. Shelah, folio 252. I say that one should protest against a chazzan who pauses between "ba'agala uvizman kariv" and "ve'imru amen." Rather, he should say them in one breath, for otherwise the congregation begins "Amen, yehei Shemei rabba" before the chazzan says "ve'imru amen," which is improper. I likewise found in Sefer HaManhig: The shaliach tzibbur says "ve'imru amen," and the congregation then responds "Amen, yehei Shemei rabba," as it is stated, "Declare the greatness of Hashem with me." It is therefore improper to precede the chazzan. Responsa HaRosh, rule 4, states that one should not recite Kaddish together with the chazzan, because Kaddish is recited only with ten; rather, he must listen to the chazzan and then answer "Amen, yehei Shemei rabba." Shibbolei HaLeket writes that the expressions "yisgadal veyiskadash Shemei rabba, yisbarach, veyishtabach, veyispa'er, veyisromam, veyisnasei, veyis'hadar, veyis'aleh, veyis'halal" correspond to the ten utterances through which the world was created; this is why we say "be'alma di vera chir'usei." Since the Ten Commandments are included within the ten utterances, and there was a division between the first two commandments, which were spoken by the Almighty, and the other eight, which were spoken by Moshe, Kaddish similarly separates the two praises "yisgadal veyiskadash" from the final eight. Kol Bo writes that the eight forms of praise beginning with "yisbarach" correspond to the seven heavens, with the eighth corresponding to the firmament above the heads of the Chayos. One should prolong and draw out the praises so as to enumerate them. There is another explanation of "Shemei rabba": reversed, the word bara alludes to the Name through which Hashem fashioned the worlds. We say "in your lifetimes and in your days" because only one from a city and two from a family will remain; Israel is therefore blessed that it occur in their lifetimes. Recanati explains this as a prayer for the leaders of Israel, that they not die because of others. Kol Bo writes that one must draw out "rabba" with all his strength, so that he may think in his heart and, while drawing it out, have all the intentions described above. I found in Ra'avan, siman 12: I was asked why fifteen praises were instituted in Kaddish; from "yisgadal" and "yiskadash" through "veyis'halal, shirasa, tushbechasa, venechemasa." It appears to me that they correspond to the fifteen Songs of Ascents. Yishtabach likewise contains fifteen praises, and Emes Veyatziv contains fifteen vavs through "tov veyafeh"; "emes" is not counted, because it belongs to the preceding words, "Hashem Elokeichem emes." It appears that these correspond to the Name formed by yud and hei. Kol Bo writes that "kedushasa" is spelled with a vav because kodesh has the numerical value 410, the number of years the First Temple stood. In it the Name was blessed, and the Kohanim pronounced it as written; now we therefore require consolation above. "Tushbechasa venechemasa" complete four forms of praise corresponding to the four letters of the Name. You can now understand the expression "nechemasa." See Tur.
