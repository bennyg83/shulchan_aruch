(1) One should have kavanah, etc. The chazzan should be careful to emphasize the dalet of "yitgadal" so it not be heard as "yitkadel"—a translation of "oref kadal" (stiff neck); and likewise he should not emphasize the bet of "yitbarakh" so it not be heard as "yitparekh"; and he should be careful to cut the heh of "yit'hallel" so one not hear, heaven forbid, "v'yitchalel"—so wrote Shlah page 252. I say one should protest a chazzan who pauses between "b'agala uvizman kariv" and "v'imru Amen"—rather he should say them in one breath, for the congregation begins "Amen y'hei sh'meih rabbah" before the chazzan says "v'imru Amen," which is not according to law. And likewise I found in Sefer haManhig—his language: the public emissary says "v'imru Amen" and the congregation answers after him "Amen y'hei sh'meih rabbah," for it is written "Gadlu laHashem iti" (Psalms 34:4); therefore it is not proper to precede the chazzan—until here. In responsa of the Rosh, klal 4: one should not say Kaddish together with the chazzan, for Kaddish is said only with ten; rather one must listen to the chazzan and answer "Amen y'hei sh'meih rabbah" after him. Shibolei haLeket wrote: "Yitgadal v'yitkadash sh'mei rabbah, yitbarakh v'yishtabach v'yitpa'ar v'yitromam v'yitnasei v'yit'hadar v'yit'aleh v'yit'hallel"—both correspond to the ten utterances by which the world was created; therefore they say "d'amei ber'a k're'utei." And because the Ten Commandments are included in ten utterances, and there was a break between the first two words and the eight—for two words were spoken from the mouth of the Almighty and eight from Moses—likewise they made a break in Kaddish between the first two praises "yitgadal v'yitkadash" and the last eight—until here. And in Kol Bo he wrote: "Yitbarakh," etc.—eight kinds of praise against seven heavens, and the eighth against there above the heads of the living creatures; and one must extend and draw out the praises to count the praise. There is also a reason for "sh'mei rabbah bara" in reverse of the word—meaning: with the name Y-H, Rock of the worlds. "B'chayechon uv'yomeichon"—because none will remain except one from a city and two from a family; therefore Israel is blessed "b'chayechon," and in Birkat Kohanim on the providers of Israel that they not die because of others. Kol Bo wrote: a person must draw out "Rabbah" with all his strength so that in his heart and in drawing out "Rabbah" he intend all those kavanot we wrote—until here. I found in Ra'avan siman 12: I was asked why they instituted in Kaddish fifteen praises such as yitgadal, yitkadash, yimloch, yitbarakh, etc., until "v'yit'hallel shirata v'shebachata v'nechamata"—behold fifteen. And it seems to me corresponding to the fifteen Shir haMa'alot psalms; and likewise in Yishtabach there are fifteen praises; and likewise in Amen and Emes there are fifteen vavs until "tov v'yafeh"—and "Emes" is not counted, for it is considered above "Elokeichem Emes"—until here. And it appears they correspond to the name Y-H. Kol Bo wrote: kedushah with a vav—for "kodesh" equals 410, for the First Temple stood 410 years and in it was "baruch" this Name, and the priests would pronounce it as written; and now we need comfort "l'eila." But "shebachata v'nechamata"—four kinds of praise corresponding to the four letters of the Name; and now understand the language of "nechamata"—and see the Tur.
