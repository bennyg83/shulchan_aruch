One should not interrupt between "Y'hei Sh'meih Rabbah" and "m'varakh," etc. Some say one should not interrupt between the word "Sh'meih" and "Rabbah," but between "Rabbah" and "m'varakh" we have no concern (Hagahot Asheri in the name of Or Zarua). The main point is that one may not interrupt even between "Rabbah" and "m'varakh"; rather one should say "Y'hei Sh'meih Rabbah m'varakh" in one breath. And one should also pronounce "Sh'meih" with a mappik heh—and thus one satisfies all the explanations (my master in Beit Yosef).

When he begins "Yitgadal," one should say first "Ve'attah yigdal na" (Numbers 14:17). Some say also "Bereshit bara," etc., "Vaydaber Elohim et kol hadevarim ha'eleh lemor," "Koach ma'asav higid le'amo," "the Omnipresent b'chochmah yasad aretz," etc. In each of these there are twenty-eight letters, and among all of them twenty-one words together with "Ve'attah yigdal na," etc., and "Amen y'hei," etc. However, in the verse "Koach ma'asav" and in "the Omnipresent b'chochmah" each has one more that is not counted in the number. One may say these verses even in the Yotzer Or prayer, and one need not worry that he makes an interruption between Yishtabach and Yotzer Or—for they only forbade interrupting with idle words; but words of praise related to the occasion are obviously permitted. But in the evening prayer, where we require adjacency of redemption to prayer, one should not say anything (Maharshal siman 64). Some are lenient in the evening prayer because it is optional (Rashba).
