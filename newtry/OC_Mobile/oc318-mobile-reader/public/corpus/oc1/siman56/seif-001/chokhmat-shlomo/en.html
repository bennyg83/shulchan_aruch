Se'if 1: One should concentrate when answering Kaddish, etc. See Taz, se'if katan 1, citing Tur that Kaddish was instituted in Aramaic so the ministering angels would not become jealous of us, etc. This appears alluded to in the verse, 'He redeemed my soul in peace, for the many were with me.' Some matters accomplished in Heaven occur peacefully, while others do not. When the ministering angels present no accusation, the matter occurs peacefully. When they accuse, even if their words are not accepted, the matter is accomplished through dispute rather than peace. Thus, although a prayer recited in Hebrew is accepted, it is not accepted peacefully but amid dispute. A prayer in Aramaic, which the ministering angels do not understand and therefore cannot oppose, arrives peacefully. Prayer in another language, however, is effective only with a congregation, not for an individual, as stated below in siman 101. An individual therefore cannot have both qualities together; a prayer that is accepted and that arrives peacefully. If he prays in Hebrew, the ministering angels can accuse and it does not arrive peacefully; if he prays in Aramaic, his prayer is not heard at all. With a congregation, both qualities are possible when they pray in Aramaic. Thus David said, 'He redeemed my soul in peace'; without any accusation by the ministering angels; 'for the many were with me': I prayed with the congregation in Aramaic, there was no accusation, and my prayer was peacefully accepted. This explains the verse. May it be His will that the same occur soon, and may the prayers of the entire House of Israel be accepted. Amen.
