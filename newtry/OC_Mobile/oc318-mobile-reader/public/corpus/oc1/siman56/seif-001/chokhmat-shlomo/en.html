Seif 1: One should have kavanah when answering Kaddish, etc. NB: see in Taz note 1 what he wrote in the name of the Tur—that therefore Kaddish was instituted in the vernacular so the ministering angels would not be jealous of us, etc. And behold this appears alluded to in scripture, as the poet said: "He redeems my soul in peace from battles against me, for many were with me" (Psalms 55:19)—for there is a matter done above in a way of peace, and there is a matter that is not a way of peace. Regarding a matter in which there is no prosecution from the ministering angels, it is a way of peace; but what the ministering angels prosecute—since in any case their words are not heard—it is not done in peace but in dispute. Therefore prayer that comes in the holy tongue—if the prayer is accepted, nevertheless it is not a way of peace but in dispute; but prayer that comes in the vernacular, which the ministering angels do not understand and there is no prosecution from them—this comes in a way of peace. But behold, in other languages it helps only in public and not for an individual, as written below siman 101. Therefore prayer of an individual cannot be both together—a way of peace and that the prayer be accepted; necessarily, if he prays in the holy tongue there is prosecution from the ministering angels and it is not in peace; and if he prays in the vernacular the prayer is not heard at all. But in public you find both—that it will be in peace and the prayer will avail, such as praying in the vernacular. And this is what he said: "He redeems my soul in peace"—without prosecution from the ministering angels at all—and that is by virtue of "for many were with me": I stood and prayed in the vernacular and there was no prosecution and my prayer was accepted in peace—and it fits well; understand. May it be His will that it be so in the exile, and may their prayer of all the house of Israel be accepted, Amen.
