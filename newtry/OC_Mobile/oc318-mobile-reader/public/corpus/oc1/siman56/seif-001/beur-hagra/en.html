And one should not interrupt, etc. This is the language of Hagahot Maimoniyot in chapter 3 of Berakhot: one should not interrupt between "Sh'meih" and "Rabbah," but between "Rabbah" and "m'varakh" we have no concern. In Dvar Malchut he explains: per the Tur's explanation, which is the explanation of Machzor Vitry that Tosafot on Berakhot 3a brought regarding "and they answer," etc. But per Tosafot there—and the Tur also brought it—the opposite is true; and Tosafot proved there from the baraita in Sotah 41b: "Y'hei sh'mo hagadol m'vorakh"—and furthermore they wrote there: also what the world says, etc., means that per the explanation because of amen that they do not understand, therefore not like Machzor Vitry—for there "Y-H" is the Holy Name. Granted, regarding what the world says we are not concerned, since they do not understand the rest; and likewise in the Tur. Therefore the main point is not to interrupt between "Rabbah" and "m'varakh"—and likewise Beit Yosef, that "Sh'meih" lacks a yod and is per Tosafot. But this law is difficult, for it is against the Gemara in Sukkah 39a—and Magen Avraham, and his answer is not correct, for in Dvar Malchut he learned from the words of Hagahot Maimoniyot, and there he wrote like the Gemara cited. Also the law of Hagahot Maimoniyot is difficult, since we hold the matter is resolved; if so, the same applies between "Sh'meih" and "Rabbah."

And one should stand, etc. Yerushalmi: "The word of God came to me"—from here R' Eliezer said: when answering "yishtabach rabba," and likewise regarding kedushah one must stand on one's feet.

And one who comes, etc.—for praise by itself is sufficient; and also the matter of Kaddish is not yet finished, and we do not speak of answering the orphan "Amen" (Magen Avraham).

And when he begins, etc.—see there "Yitgadal," and it contains twenty-eight letters and twenty-eight words, as Mahari wrote, and Shach; and he wrote that one says it with all one's strength—and likewise in the Zohar.

"Zachor," etc.—for he remembers His mercies at that hour, as written in Berakhot 3a (Tur).
