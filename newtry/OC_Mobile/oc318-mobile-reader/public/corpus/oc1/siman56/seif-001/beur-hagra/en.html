Seif 1: He should not pause, etc. This is the language of Hagahos Ashiri in chapter 3 of Berachos: one should not pause between “shemei” and “rabba,” but there is no concern about pausing between “rabba” and “mevarach.” The Darchei Moshe writes that this follows the interpretation of the “some explain” cited by the Tur, namely the interpretation of Machzor Vitri brought by Tosafos to Berachos 3a, s.v. “and they answer.” According to Tosafos there, however, whose interpretation is also cited by the Tur, the reverse is true. Tosafos prove this from Shabbos 21b, “May His great name be blessed.” Tosafos further write there, “and also what people say,” etc. Their meaning is that, according to the explanation based on the unlearned who do not understand, it necessarily cannot follow Machzor Vitri, because the Divine Name Yah is Hebrew. By contrast, according to what people commonly say, this presents no difficulty, since they do not understand the remainder either. This is also the Tur's formulation. Accordingly, the principal rule is not to pause between “rabba” and “mevarach.” Beit Yosef likewise writes that “shemei” is written without a yud, following Tosafos' interpretation. This ruling, however, requires study, because it appears contrary to the Gemara in Sukkah 39a. See Magen Avraham. His resolution is not correct, because the Darchei Moshe derived the ruling from Hagahos Ashiri, and Hagahos Ashiri states the same as the Gemara cited above. Moreover, the ruling of Hagahos Ashiri itself requires study. Since we maintain that this completes the phrase, the same should apply between “shemei” and “rabba.”
One should stand, etc. The Yerushalmi expounds the verse, “God has a matter for me to tell you.” From here Rabbi Elazar said: when answering “Yehei shemei rabba” and every sacred response, one must rise to his feet.
One who arrives, etc. It is an independent praise, and the subject of Kaddish has not yet been completed. It is therefore unlike answering an orphaned Amen. See Magen Avraham.
When he begins, etc. This refers to “Yisgadal.” It contains twenty eight letters and twenty eight words, as Maharai and the other authorities write. They explain that this is the meaning of “with all his strength.” The Zohar states likewise.
Remember, etc. For Hashem remembers His mercy at that moment, as stated in Berachos 3a and in the Tur.
