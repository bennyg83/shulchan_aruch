(א) One should have proper intention—for the Sages said: whoever answers Amen, Yehei sh'meih rabbah with all his might, his harsh decree is torn up. The early authorities explained that "with all his might" means with all his intention and all his limbs—that is, he should say it with heart and soul, not merely as one moving his lips while his heart is not with him. One should also intend to hear Kaddish from the mouth of the shaliach tzibbur so that he knows what he is answering when he says Amen, Yehei sh'meih rabbah, and the Amen after v'imru amen. And all the more so one must be very careful not to converse in the middle of Kaddish or Kedushah, as stated in tractate Derech Eretz: Rabbi Chama encountered Elijah with many thousands of camels laden with wrath and weapons to punish those, and whoever speaks in those places—the verse says about him, "Yet you have not called upon Me, O Jacob" [brought in Beit Yosef siman 125]. It is written in Sefer Chasidim: a story of one chasid who saw another chasid after death, his face glowing; he asked, why is your face glowing? He said, because I would speak when the shatz was saying Vayechulu and Berachat Magen Avot and Yitgadal. In Mattenat Moshe siman 411 he brings in the name of a midrash: a certain sage appeared to his student in a dream; the student saw a mark on his forehead and asked, why did this happen to you? He said, because I was not careful to refrain from speaking when the chazzan was saying Kaddish. Even to think words of Torah is forbidden while the chazzan says Kaddish, because one must have much concentration when answering Kaddish [Pri Chadash]. There is no difference between Kaddish after Scripture, aggadah, or mishnayot [Peri Megadim].

(ב) Kaddish—the formula of Kaddish Yitgadal veyitkadash was established from the verse "I will be exalted and I will be sanctified" (Ezekiel 38:23) regarding the war of Gog and Magog, when the Omnipresent's name will be magnified, as it is written "on that day the the Omnipresent will be One and His name One." One says the poor man's dagesh on yitgadal and yitkadash with tzerei because it is Hebrew, not Aramaic translation [see Beit Yosef]. Not with double sheva as if the tav and gimel had sheva, but the gimel with patach. One should sharpen the gimel of yitgadal so yitkadash is not heard as yitkadal—the word for nape; Aramaic for nape is kadal. One should not over-emphasize the bet of yitbarakh. One should cut well the heh of v'yithallel—"(vi) di b'ra" are two words. After the words k'ra'usayh he should pause slightly, for these words refer back to yitgadal above. At b'zman kariv v'imru amen it is proper that the shatz say them at once without pausing between them, because the custom is that the congregation does not answer amen until the shatz says amen. If the shatz lengthens greatly the melody of v'imru amen, they need not wait for him when answering amen. When the responder says Amen, Yehei sh'meih rabbah he should pause slightly between amen and Y'hei sh'meih rabbah, and similarly between amen and Modim, for the word amen refers to the shatz's [previous line], while Y'hei sh'meih rabbah is a statement on its own. Y'hei sh'meih rabbah, etc., is the translation of "May His great name be blessed forever and to all eternity" [Beit Yosef and Ri's explanation]. Sh'mei without yod, also without mappik on the heh [Taz]; from Peri Megadim's words it seems better to say with mappik on the heh. M'varakh with kamatz under bet and patach under resh. Le'olam with kamatz under ayin. Le'olmei with vav. The shatz must also say quietly Y'hei sh'meih rabbah, etc., and not again say amen—only begin Y'hei sh'meih, etc. Afterward he begins yitbarakh, etc., in a loud voice; it contains eight expressions of praise corresponding to the seven heavens and there above the heads of the hayos; at the start he says yitgadal and yitkadash, two expressions of praise that are in the sum of one hundred—ten expressions of praise corresponding to the Ten Commandments. Min kol birkhata is three words. On days when they double the Kadishes, for le'eila ule'eila one should not say only mikol birkhata so that the sum not be only twenty-eight words.

(ג) And do not interrupt—see Peri Megadim and Machatzit HaShekel, meaning he should not pause in silence between them, but one need not say them in a single breath.

(ד) [Regarding] m'varakh—see Magen Avraham; according to his words one should also not interrupt between sh'mei and rabbah.

(ה) In a loud voice—for through this intention is aroused, and through this voice harsh decrees are annulled; nevertheless one should not raise overly loud voices so that people mock him and thereby cause them sin.

(ו) To hear Kaddish—for answering Amen, Yehei sh'meih rabbah is a very great mitzvah. Amen, Yehei sh'meih rabbah is preferable to Kedushah and Modim; therefore in large congregations in Ezra where there are several minyanim, when one hears both together as one he should answer Amen, Yehei sh'meih rabbah. Nevertheless if before him are two synagogues, in one they reach the Kaddish after Shemoneh Esrei and in one Kedushah, he should go and answer Kedushah, for there he will also hear the Kaddish after Shemoneh Esrei. It is forbidden to interrupt in the middle of Amen, Yehei sh'meih rabbah when hearing Kedushah, and the reverse is uncertain [Chayei Adam].

(ז) And one should stand—until after one finishes Amen, Yehei sh'meih rabbah; and some say one should stand until the amen after yitbarakh, etc., v'imru amen.

(8) "Matters, etc."—Some say one need not stand [for every Kaddish], but rather that for any Kaddish one begins while standing—such as after Hallel—one should not sit until one has answered Amen to Y'hei sh'meih rabbah. One should heed the strict view. We may learn a fortiori from Eglon king of Moab, who was a gentile yet rose of his own accord from his throne to speak of God—how much more so we, His people. In Kavanot it is reported that the Ari would remain standing for all Kaddishes after the standing [prayers] of Shacharit, Minchah, and Maariv; for Titkabel and the Torah-reading Kaddish he would answer and then sit. For the Shabbat Maariv Kaddish of those who say Kaddish before Barchu, some also say one should stand.

(9) He joins in with them—even if he arrives when they are finishing answering Amen to Y'hei sh'meih rabbah, i.e., when they are saying "m'varakh" and the like, he should still say Y'hei sh'meih rabbah, etc., and should not say Amen, for Amen refers to the chazzan's Yitgadal. But Y'hei sh'meih rabbah is praise in its own right. Similarly regarding Kedushah: if he arrives before they have finished, he may join with them; if he arrives when most of the congregation is saying Y'hei sh'meih rabbah, he should also say Y'hei sh'meih rabbah with them if he can, and then focus his mind on the Amen so he reflects on what he is answering. [This appears to be the Magen Avraham's intent in se'if kaf-heh; see Levushei Serad.]

(10) When he begins—i.e., before he has begun: when the chazzan says Yitgadal one must listen and be silent, as explained at the beginning of siman 125, that one must heed and understand what the shatz says, and also know afterward what one is answering Amen to.

(11) "Ve'attah yigdal" (Num. 14:17)—the Ari would not have people say it; he would nearly stammer and say it is not among the tikkunim. Therefore one should not say it between Yishtabach and Yotzer, nor in Maariv Kaddish between geulah and tefillah, nor wherever interruption is forbidden.
