(1) One must have intention. The Sages said that whoever answers ‘Amen, Yehei Shemei Rabbah mevorach’ with all his strength has his harsh decree torn up. The early authorities explain that ‘with all his strength’ means with full concentration and with all his limbs, namely, that he should say it with heart and soul, not merely moving his lips while his heart is elsewhere. He should also intend to hear Kaddish from the chazzan so that he knows to what he is responding with ‘Yehei Shemei Rabbah’ and with the amen after ‘da’amiran be’alma.’ All the more must one be exceedingly careful not to converse during Kaddish or Kedushah. Maseches Derech Eretz relates that Rabbi Chama found Eliyahu accompanied by many thousands of camels laden with wrath and fury to punish those who speak in these places, concerning whom Scripture says, ‘But you did not call Me, Yaakov.’ This is cited by the Beit Yosef in siman 125. Sefer Chassidim records that one pious man saw another pious man after his death, with his face green. He asked why his face was green, and the deceased replied that it was because he had spoken while the chazzan recited Vayechulu, Bircas Magen Avos, and Yisgadal. Mateh Moshe, siman 411, cites a Midrash in which a sage appeared to his student in a dream. The student saw a stain on his forehead and asked how this had happened. He replied that it was because he had not been careful to refrain from speaking while the chazzan recited Kaddish. It is even forbidden to think about Torah matters while the chazzan recites Kaddish, because great concentration is required when answering Kaddish, as stated by the Peri Chadash. There is no distinction between Kaddish following Tanach, aggadah, or Mishnayos, as stated by the Peri Megadim.
(2) Kaddish. The formula ‘Yisgadal veyiskadash’ was instituted on the basis of the verse ‘I will be magnified and sanctified,’ stated in Yechezkel 38:23 concerning the war of Gog and Magog, when Hashem’s Name will be magnified, as it is written, ‘On that day Hashem will be One and His Name one.’ The dalet of ‘yisgadal’ and ‘yiskadash’ should be pronounced with a tzeirei, because the words are Hebrew, not Aramaic, as explained in the Beit Yosef. They should not be pronounced with two shevas, as though both the tav and gimel had a sheva; rather, the gimel has a patach. The gimel of ‘yisgadal’ should be emphasized so it not sound like ‘yiskadal,’ an expression of the nape, for the Aramaic word for nape is ‘kadal.’ One should not overemphasize the beis of ‘yisbarach.’ The hei of ‘veyishalal’ should be clearly articulated. ‘Di vera’ consists of two words. One should pause slightly after ‘chirusei,’ because those words refer back to ‘yisgadal.’ It is proper for the chazzan to say ‘ba’agala uvizman kariv ve’imru amen’ continuously without pausing, because the custom is that the congregation does not answer amen until the chazzan says ‘amen.’ If the chazzan prolongs the melody of ‘ve’imru amen’ excessively, the congregation need not wait for him before answering amen. When answering, one should pause slightly between ‘amen’ and ‘Yehei Shemei Rabbah,’ and similarly between ‘amen’ and ‘Modim,’ because amen responds to the chazzan, whereas ‘Yehei Shemei Rabbah’ is a separate declaration. ‘Yehei Shemei Rabbah,’ etc., is the Aramaic translation of ‘May His great Name be blessed forever and for all eternity,’ as explained by the Beit Yosef in accordance with Rabbeinu Yitzchak. One should say ‘shemei’ without a yud and also without pronouncing the final hei, according to the Taz. The Peri Megadim implies that it is preferable to pronounce the final hei. ‘Mevorach’ is pronounced with a kamatz under the beis and a patach under the reish. ‘Le’alam’ has a kamatz under the ayin. One should say ‘ule’almei’ with a vav. The chazzan must also recite quietly ‘Yehei Shemei Rabbah,’ etc. He should not say amen again, but begin with ‘Yehei Shemei,’ etc. He then begins ‘Yisbarach,’ etc., aloud. This passage contains eight expressions of praise corresponding to the seven heavens and the firmament above the heads of the living creatures. At the beginning one says ‘Yisgadal’ and ‘Yiskadash,’ two additional expressions of praise, making ten expressions corresponding to the Ten Commandments. ‘Min kol birchasa’ consists of three words. On days when ‘le’eila ule’eila’ is doubled in Kaddish, one should say only ‘mikol birchasa,’ so that the total remains twenty-eight words.
(3) He should not interrupt. See Peri Megadim and Machatzis HaShekel. This means that he should not pause silently between the words, but they need not be said in one breath.
(4) Mevorach. See Magen Avraham. According to his words, one should also not interrupt between ‘Yehei Shemei’ and ‘Rabbah.’
(5) Aloud. This arouses concentration, and through this voice harsh decrees are nullified. Nevertheless, one should not shout so loudly that people ridicule him, thereby causing them to sin.
(6) To hear Kaddish. Answering ‘Yehei Shemei Rabbah’ is an exceedingly great mitzvah. ‘Yehei Shemei Rabbah’ takes precedence over Kedushah and Modim. Therefore, in large congregations where several minyanim are held in the women’s section or adjoining area, if one hears both simultaneously he should answer ‘Yehei Shemei Rabbah.’ Nevertheless, if two synagogues are before him, one reaching Kaddish after the Amidah and the other reaching Kedushah, he should go and answer Kedushah, because there he will also hear the Kaddish after the Amidah. It is forbidden to interrupt in the middle of ‘Yehei Shemei Rabbah’ upon hearing Kedushah, and the reverse requires study, as stated by Chayei Adam.
(7) One should stand. He should remain standing until after completing ‘Yehei Shemei Rabbah.’ Some say he should remain standing until the amen after ‘Yisbarach,’ etc., and ‘ve’imru amen.’
(8) A matter, etc. Some say one need not stand, except that any Kaddish which finds him already standing, such as after Hallel, he should not sit until answering ‘Amen, Yehei Shemei Rabbah.’ One should be concerned for the stringent view. This may be derived a fortiori from Eglon king of Moav, who was a non-Jew and rose voluntarily from his throne for a word of Hashem, all the more so we, His people. The writings concerning the Arizal state that for every Kaddish following the Amidah of Shacharis, Minchah, and Maariv he remained standing. For Kaddish Tiskabel and the Kaddish following the return of the Sefer Torah, he answered and then sat. Regarding the Friday night Maariv Kaddish, according to those who recite Kaddish before Barechu, some likewise say one should stand.
(9) He answers with them. Even if he arrives when they are concluding ‘Amen, Yehei Shemei Rabbah,’ such as when they are saying ‘mevorach’ and the like, he should nevertheless say ‘Yehei Shemei Rabbah,’ etc., but should not say amen, because the amen refers to the chazzan’s ‘Yisgadal,’ whereas ‘Yehei Shemei Rabbah’ is an independent praise. Similarly, regarding Kedushah, if he arrives before they have finished, he may recite it with them. If he arrives while most of the congregation is saying ‘Yehei Shemei Rabbah,’ he should also say it with them, provided he can then concentrate on the amen and understand to what he is responding. This appears to me to be the intent of the Magen Avraham, seif katan 5. See Levushei Serad.
(10) And when he begins. This means before he begins, for while the chazzan says ‘Yisgadal’ one must listen and remain silent, as explained at the beginning of siman 125. One must attend to and understand what the chazzan says, and also know afterward to what one is answering amen.
(11) And now, may it be magnified. The Arizal did not wish this verse to be said and almost stammered, saying that it is not part of the established formulations. Therefore it should not be said between Yishtabach and Yotzer, during the Kaddish of Maariv between redemption and prayer, or anywhere interruption is forbidden.
