On answering Kaddish. In Tur he brings two reasons for saying Kaddish in the translation tongue: one, so the angels not be jealous of us for saying such great praise—for sh'meih rabbah indicates the name Y-H rabbah, that the name Y-H will be complete in the future. Another reason, that the amei ha'aretz understand it—in Babylon it was enacted and their language was Aramaic. And he wrote: therefore sh'meih rabbah should be according to its plain sense a translation of "His great name," etc.—meaning that under the first explanation one cannot say it according to plain sense, for then there would not be so much jealousy. It is written in Agudah chapter 7 of Berakhot: "Y'hei sh'mo hagadol m'varakh"—from here it implies one may say Y'hei sh'meih without mappik hei, which would mean the name Y-H, etc. Therefore those chazzanim who say it with mappik hei hold the first explanation, i.e., translation of the name Y-H; and those who say without mappik hei hold the second explanation, translation of "His great name." And this is primary, for Beit Yosef concludes that what they wrote—that one needs in Amen yehei shmei rabbah 28 letters—means the word sh'meih lacks yod, as in Daniel "lehevei sh'mah di Eloha," etc.

And one should stand when answering Kaddish—I already wrote of this in the beginning of siman 53 that some did not write so.
