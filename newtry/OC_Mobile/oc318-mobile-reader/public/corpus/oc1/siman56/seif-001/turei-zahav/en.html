When answering Kaddish. The Tur gives two reasons why Kaddish is recited in Aramaic. One is so that the angels will not be jealous of us for reciting such a great praise, because ‘Shemei Rabbah’ alludes to the Name formed by yud and hei, which will be complete in the future. The second reason is so that the unlearned people would understand it, since Kaddish was instituted in Babylonia, where Aramaic was their language. The Tur writes: ‘According to this, its meaning is literal, for “Shemei Rabbah” is the Aramaic for “His great Name.”’ His meaning is that according to the first explanation it cannot be interpreted literally, for if so the jealousy would not be so great. The Agudah writes in the first chapter of Berachos: ‘May His great Name be blessed. From here it appears that one should say “Yehei Shemei” without pronouncing the final hei, for pronouncing it would imply the Divine Name formed by yud and hei.’ Accordingly, chazzanim who pronounce the final hei follow the first explanation, namely, that it is the Aramaic form of the Name formed by yud and hei, while those who do not pronounce the final hei follow the second explanation, that it is the Aramaic for ‘His great Name.’ The latter is primary, for the Beit Yosef concludes that what was written concerning the need for twenty-eight letters in ‘Yehei Shemei Rabbah’ requires that the word ‘shemei’ lack a yud, as it appears in Daniel: ‘May the Name of God be,’ etc.
One should stand when answering Kaddish. I have already written concerning this at the beginning of siman 53, where I noted that some authorities did not write this requirement.
