“Kaddish.” Sefer Chasidim relates that one pious man saw another pious man after his death, and the deceased man's face had turned green. He asked him, “Why has your face turned green?” He replied, “Because I used to speak while the prayer leader recited Vayechulu, during the blessing Magen Avot, and during Yitgadal.” Matah Moshe, siman 411, likewise cites a Midrash concerning a sage who appeared to his student in a dream. The student saw a stain on the sage's forehead and asked, “Why did this happen to you?” He replied, “Because I was not careful to refrain from speaking while the cantor recited Kaddish.”
“Mevarach.” In the word mevarach, the letter bet takes a kamatz and the letter resh a patach. In le'alam, the ayin takes a kamatz. Shelah. The word ule'almei is written with a vav; see Beit Yosef. The Ba'er Heitev printed before this one writes le'almei without a vav in the name of the Kavanot of the Arizal, but that is incorrect; see there. Yitbarach is written without a vav. Di vera consists of two words. Concerning the phrase min kol birchata, see the Kavanot and Yad Aharon.
“Kaddish.” Answering Amen Yehei Shmei Rabbah takes precedence over responding to Kedushah or Modim. Therefore, if one hears Kaddish together with Kedushah or Modim and cannot answer both, he should answer Amen Yehei Shmei Rabbah. Be'er Sheva. Responsa Shai LaMora, siman 9, rules likewise. Magen Avraham writes: It appears to me that if two synagogues are before him in a city, one congregation is reaching Kedushah and the other is reaching the Kaddish after the Amidah, he should go and answer Kedushah, because there he will also hear Kaddish after the prayer. See siman 109. If he has begun answering Kedushah, he should not interrupt it to answer Amen Yehei Shmei Rabbah. Pri Chadash, siman 128.
“Kaddish.” Darkei Moshe writes that Maharil would not stand for Kaddish. If a Kaddish began while he was already standing, however, he would remain standing until after Amen Yehei Shmei Rabbah. Knesset HaGedolah writes likewise: I too follow Maharil's practice; see siman 58. Sefer HaKavanot of the Arizal also states that the apparent implication of the Yerushalmi (that one must rise to answer Amen Yehei Shmei Rabbah) is mistaken, because the statement is not actually from the Yerushalmi; rather, one sage composed it. See what Magen Avraham writes. A gloss in the Kavanot states: My teacher of blessed memory practiced as follows. For every Kaddish following the standing Amidah of Shacharit, Minchah, or Maariv, he remained standing. For the Kaddish Titkabel and the Kaddish recited when the Torah scroll was returned, he answered and then sat down; see there. See also Sefer Nagid U'Metzaveh. Mahari Saruk writes that one should stand for the Kaddish at Maariv on Shabbat.
“With strength.” The Arizal did not wish to say it loudly; he would almost stammer, saying that this is not among the proper tikkunim. Magen Avraham writes that one therefore should not say it between Yishtabach and Yotzer, between redemption and prayer, or anywhere else that interruption is forbidden. Shelah and Bach. The cantor repeats and says Amen Yehei Shmei Rabbah even though he already said “and say: Amen.” Responsa Darkei Noam, Orach Chayim, siman 18. See also Responsa Ne'eman Shmuel, siman 1. In the addenda to Knesset HaGedolah, however, he writes that this is improper. Rather, the prayer leader should repeat Yehei Shmei Rabbah without saying Amen Yehei Shmei Rabbah, because otherwise he would be answering Amen twice. See siman 61, se'if 12, in the gloss and in what I wrote there. See also Halachot Ketanot, part 1, siman 16.
