The Kaddish. Sefer Chasidim wrote of an incident with one pious man who saw another pious man after his death, and his face was green. He said to him: why is your face green? He said to him: because I would speak while the shaliach tzibbur said Vayechulu, the blessing Magen Avot, and Yitgadal. In Mateh Moshe siman 411 he brought in the name of a Midrash that one sage appeared to his student in a dream, and the student saw that he had a mark on his forehead. He said to him: why did this happen to you? He said to him: because I was not careful not to speak when the chazzan said Kaddish.
To the blessed One. Mevarakh has a kamatz under the bet and a patach under the resh. Le'alam has a kamatz under the ayin. Shelah. Regarding ule'almei with a vav, see Beit Yosef. The Ba'er Heitev before me wrote le'almei without a vav in the name of the intentions of the Ari z"l, but this is not so; see there. Yitbarakh is without a vav. Di vera are two words. Min kol birkhata: see the writings on kavvanot and Yad Aharon.
Kaddish. Amen yehei shemei rabba is preferable to Kedushah and Modim. Therefore, if one hears Kaddish and Kedushah or Modim and cannot answer both, he should answer Amen yehei shemei rabba. Be'er Sheva; so too responsa Shay LaMora siman 9. Magen Avraham wrote: it appears to me that if there are two synagogues before him in the city, in one they are reaching Kedushah and in the other Kaddish after the Amidah, he should go and answer Kedushah, for there he will also hear Kaddish after the prayer; see siman 109. If he began answering Kedushah, he should not interrupt it to answer Amen yehei shemei rabba. Pri Chadash siman 128.
Kaddish. In Darkei Moshe he wrote that Maharil would not stand for Kaddish, but any Kaddish that caught him standing, he would remain standing until after Amen yehei shemei rabba. So wrote Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah: I too practice like Maharil; see siman 58. So too in the book of the Ari's kavvanot it is written there: what appears from the Yerushalmi, that one must rise when answering Amen yehei shemei rabba, is a mistake, for it is not actually from the Yerushalmi, but one sage made it. See what Magen Avraham wrote. In the kavvanot there is a gloss as follows: the practice of my master z"l was that in all the Kaddishes after the Amidah of Shacharit, Minchah, and Ma'ariv he would remain standing. For Titkabel and for returning the Sefer Torah, he would answer and afterward sit; see there. See the book Nagid UMitzvah. Mahari Sarug z"l wrote that for the Kaddish of Shabbat evening one should stand.
Strength. The Ari z"l did not want to say it, and he almost mumbled, saying that it is not from the Tikkunim. Magen Avraham wrote: therefore one should not say it between Yishtabach and Yotzer, or between redemption and prayer, or in any place where it is forbidden to interrupt. Shelah and Bach. The chazzan returns and says Amen yehei shemei rabba even though he already said "and say Amen"; responsa Darkei Noam, Orach Chayim part 1 siman 18. See responsa Ne'eman Shmuel siman 1. But in Shiyurei Knesset HaGedolah he wrote that this is not proper; rather, the shaliach tzibbur returns to answer yehei shemei rabba, but not Amen yehei shemei rabba, for that is like answering Amen twice. See siman 61 seif 12 in the gloss and what is written there. See Halachot Ketanot part 1 siman 16.
