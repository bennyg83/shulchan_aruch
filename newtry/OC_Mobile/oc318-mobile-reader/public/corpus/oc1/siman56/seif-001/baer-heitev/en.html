Kaddish. Sefer Chassidim relates: a certain pious man saw another pious man after his death, and his face was ruddy. He asked him, "Why is your face ruddy?" He answered, "Because I would speak while the shaliach tzibbur was saying Vayechulu, and during the blessing Magen Avot, and during Yitgadal." In Sefer Matteh Moshe siman 411 he brings in the name of a midrash: a certain sage appeared to his student in a dream, and the student saw that he had a stain on his forehead. He asked him, "Why did this happen to you?" He answered, "Because I was not careful to refrain from speaking while the chazzan was saying Kaddish."

"M'varakh." With kamatz under the bet and patach under the resh. "L'olam" with kamatz under the ayin (Shlah). "V'l'olmei" with a vav—see Beit Yosef; and Baer Heitev before me wrote "l'olmei" without a vav in the name of the kavanot of the Arizal z"l, but it is not so per Shinui. "Yitbarakh" without a vav. "Di bara"—two words. "Min kol birkhata"—see the kavanah and Yad Acharon.

Kaddish. "Yishtabach rabba" is preferable to kedushah and modim; therefore if one hears Kaddish and kedushah or modim and cannot answer both, one answers "yishtabach rabba." Be'er Sheva; and likewise in responsa She'eilat Ya'avetz siman 9. Magen Avraham: it seems to me that if there are two synagogues before him in the city—in one they reach kedushah and in one Kaddish—after "Amen y'hei" one should learn and answer kedushah, for there too one will hear Kaddish after the prayer; see siman 109. And if he began to answer kedushah, he does not interrupt it to answer "yishtabach rabba" (Peri Chadash siman 128).

Kaddish. In Dvar Malchut he wrote that Maharil would not stand for Kaddish; yet any Kaddish he caught while already standing, he remained standing until after "yishtabach rabba"—and likewise Shakhnehag: I too practice like Maharil; see siman 58. And likewise in Sefer Kavanot of the Arizal: what appears from the Yerushalmi that one must stand when answering "yishtabach rabba" is an error, for it is not actually from the Yerushalmi but one sage made it up; and see what Magen Avraham wrote. In Kavanot there is a hagahah: what my teacher z"l practiced was that in all the Kaddishim after the standing of Shacharit, Minchah, and Ma'ariv he remained standing; and for "Titkabel" and the Kaddish of returning the sefer Torah he would answer and then sit—see there. And see Sefer Nagid uMitzvah. Mahari Soruk z"l wrote that for the Ma'ariv Kaddish of Shabbat one should stand.

"Koach." The Arizal z"l did not want to say it, and he would almost stammer and say it is not from the tikkunim. Magen Avraham wrote: therefore one should not say it between Yishtabach and Yotzer Or, or between geulah and tefillah, and wherever interruption is forbidden (Shlah, Bach). The chazzan repeats "Amen yishtabach rabba" even if he already said "Amen" (responsa Darchei Noam chelek 1 siman 18; and see responsa Ne'eman Shmuel siman 1). But in Shiyurei Kenesset HaGedolah he wrote it is not proper; rather the shaliach tzibbur should return to answer "yishtabach rabba" and not "Amen yishtabach rabba," for it is like answering "Amen" twice. See siman 61 seif 12 in the hagahah and Mashash. And see Hilchot Ketanot chelek 1 siman 16.
