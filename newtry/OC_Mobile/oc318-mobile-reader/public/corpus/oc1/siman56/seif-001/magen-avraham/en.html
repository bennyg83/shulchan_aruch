One should concentrate. Beit Yosef wrote: since they asked in the Gemara what if he interrupts to say y'hei sh'meih rabbah, and for modim and Kedushah it is obvious to them that one does not interrupt — learn from this there is greater mitzvah in answering y'hei sh'meih rabbah than Kedushah and modim; so too Maharik Yonah siman 9. Nevertheless it seems to me if there are two synagogues before him, in one they reach Kedushah and in one the Kaddish after the eighteen blessings — he goes and answers Kedushah, for there he will also hear Kaddish after the prayer.

And one does not interrupt. The Shakh wrote in the margin and thus: so too in Darkei Moshe and Beit Chadash; uncertain, for in the chapter Lulav HaGazul it is explicit "we have no ruling," and see Tosafot there, until here. Thus the Gemara: Rava said a person should not say y'hei sh'meih rabbah and then m'varakh, but rather y'hei sh'meih rabbah m'varakh together. Rav Safra said: both this and that — resolving the matter; we have no ruling, until here. Thus the Gra, chapter 3 of Berakhot: one does not interrupt between "sh'meih" and the word "rabbah," but between "rabbah" and "m'varakh" we have no ruling, until here. Rama holds the Gra holds like the first explanation — that we pray the great Name that now the Name is half, meaning Y-H, and in the future His word will be filled; and "m'varakh" is a separate utterance; from this Rama learned that according to Ri, who holds the meaning is "y'hei sh'mo hagadol m'vorakh," etc. — if so, even between "rabbah" and "m'varakh" one may not interrupt. Uncertain, for then it contradicts the Gemara. For the Gra one could say Rav Safra said "we have no ruling" specifically between "rabbah" and "m'varakh, since in any case it is not one utterance; but between "sh'meih" and "rabbah" it is forbidden to interrupt. But according to Ri there is no difference between interrupting between "sh'meih" and "rabbah" or between "rabbah" and "m'varakh" — in both one may interrupt. It seems Rama holds: since we hold in Orach Chaim siman 169, se'if 29, that in any case lekhatchilah one should read in one breath, etc., and explicitly in the order of chalitzah se'if 66 — therefore also one should not interrupt lekhatchilah; and it suffices that one need not say it in one breath, as above, to reconcile Rama's view. According to Rashi one says "sh'meih" without mappiq hei [Darkei Moshe, Chiddushei Agudah, Beit Chadash]; "m'varakh" with kamatz under the bet and patach under the resh; "l'olam" with kamatz under the ayin [Shlah]; and "l'olami" with vav [Beit Yosef].

In a loud voice — but one should not raise a very loud voice so people mock him and cause them to sin (Rabbenu Yonah).

And one should stand. So too Hagahot Maimoniyot in the name of the Jerusalem Talmud; and in Darkei Moshe he wrote Maharil would not stand, but every Kaddish he caught while standing — such as after Hallel — he remained standing until after y'hei sh'meih rabbah; so too in Kavanot and writings that thus practiced the Ari, and he said the Jerusalem Talmud is mistaken, until here. Nevertheless it seems what he wrote the Jerusalem Talmud is mistaken is because it brings "arise, for the word of Hashem is to you" — but in truth it is not written thus, only "and he arose from the throne," implying he arose of himself; and this was already resolved in Yonat Elem, siman 89, from the verse "kum" implying he was commanded to stand, etc. And further, even if he arose of himself — we have a fortiori: what of him who was a gentile, he stood for the word of Hashem — how much more we, His people; and similarly in the Midrash. Therefore one should not be lenient.

He answers with them. Meaning: when they begin "y'hei sh'meih," etc. — for it is praise on its own [there, in Hagahot]. It seems if he can direct his mind to know to answer on the Kaddish — he also says amen, as written siman 124.

And when he begins. Meaning before he began — for when the chazzan says "yitgadal" one must hear and be silent so he knows what he is saying, as written siman 125; and also so he knows afterward what he answers amen to.

And now let power be magnified. The Ari did not want them to say it and almost stammered, saying it is not from the tikkunim (Kavanot); therefore one does not say it between Yishtabach and Yotzer, or between redemption and prayer, and everywhere interruption is forbidden [Shlah and Beit Chadash siman 236 in the name of Rashal]. In a responsum Rashal wrote they say it between Yishtabach and Yotzer.
