One should concentrate. Beis Shmuel writes that since the Gemara asked whether one may interrupt to say “yehei shemei rabba,” whereas regarding Modim and Kedushah it was obvious that one may not interrupt, it follows that answering “Amen, yehei shemei rabba” is a greater mitzvah than answering Kedushah or Modim. Mahari Yonah writes likewise in siman 9. Nevertheless, it appears to me that if two synagogues stand before him, and in one they are approaching Kedushah while in the other they are approaching the Kaddish after Shemoneh Esrei, he should go and answer Kedushah, because there he will also hear the Kaddish after the prayer.
He should not pause. Shach writes in a marginal gloss as follows: “This is also written in Darchei Moshe and Bach, but it requires study, for in the chapter Lulav HaGazul it is stated explicitly that there is no concern. See Tosafos there.” The Gemara states: Rava said that a person should not say “yehei shemei rabba,” and afterward “mevarach,” but should say “yehei shemei rabba mevarach” together. Rav Safra said: both forms complete the phrase, and there is no concern. Hagahos Ashiri in chapter 3 of Berachos states: one should not pause between “shemei” and “rabba,” but there is no concern about pausing between “rabba” and “mevarach.” Rama understands that Hagahos Ashiri follows the first interpretation, namely that we pray that the great Name, which is presently only half complete, namely Yah, will in the future be restored to its full form, while “mevarach” begins a separate phrase. From this Rama inferred that according to Rabbeinu Yonah, who explains the words as “may His great Name be blessed,” one may not pause even between “rabba” and “mevarach.” This requires study, because if so it contradicts the Gemara. Regarding Hagahos Ashiri, one may answer that only between “rabba” and “mevarach” did Rav Safra say there is no concern, since at least these are not one phrase. Between “shemei” and “rabba,” however, it is forbidden to pause. According to Rabbeinu Yonah, however, there is no distinction between pausing between “shemei” and “rabba” and pausing between “rabba” and “mevarach”; one may pause in either place. It appears to me that Rama holds that since in Even HaEzer, siman 169, seif 29, we rule that at least initially the words should be read in one breath, as explained there and in the explanation of the chalitzah procedure, seif 66, the same applies here. One therefore should not pause initially, although it is sufficient that the words need not literally be said in one breath. This is the forced explanation of Rama’s view. According to Rashi’s interpretation, “shemei” should be pronounced without sounding the final hei. This is stated by Darchei Moshe, Chiddushei Agudah, and Bach. “Mevarach” is vocalized with a kamatz under the beis and a patach under the reish. “Lealam” has a kamatz under the ayin, as stated by the Shelah, and “ulealmei” is said with a vav, as stated by Beit Yosef.
Aloud. He should not, however, make excessively loud sounds that will cause people to mock him and thereby cause them to sin. This is from Rabbeinu Yonah.
One should stand. Hagahos Minhagim writes this in the name of the Yerushalmi. Darchei Moshe writes that Maharil would not rise, but whenever a Kaddish found him already standing, such as after Hallel, he would remain standing until after “yehei shemei rabba.” The Kabbalistic Intentions and the Writings likewise state that this was the Arizal’s practice, and that the passage in the Yerushalmi contains a scribal error. Nevertheless, it appears to me that when they say the Yerushalmi contains a scribal error, they refer to its citation of the verse, “Rise, for Hashem has a matter for you,” when in truth the verse does not say this, but rather, “He rose from the throne,” implying that he rose on his own. Yonat Elem, chapter 99, already resolves this: since he rose, it follows that he had been commanded to stand; see there. Moreover, even if he rose on his own, we may learn by a kal vachomer. If he, who was a non-Jew, rose for the word of Hashem, all the more so should we, His people, rise. It appears that the Midrash states this. One therefore should not be lenient.
He answers with them. This means that he begins “yehei shemei,” etc., because it is an independent praise. This is stated there in the glosses. It appears to me that if he can direct his attention so that he knows that he is answering Kaddish, he should also say Amen, as written in siman 124.
When he begins. This means before he begins, because while the chazzan says “Yisgadal,” one must listen and remain silent in order to know what he is saying, as written at the beginning of siman 125, and also so that he will afterward know to what he is answering Amen.
And now, may the strength. The Arizal did not wish this to be said, and almost stammered while saying that it is not from the words of the Tikkunim. This is stated in the Kabbalistic Intentions. It should therefore not be said between Yishtabach and Yotzer, between redemption and the Amidah, or anywhere that an interruption is forbidden. This is stated by the Shelah and Bach in siman 236 in the name of Maharshal. In a responsum, however, Maharshal writes that it is said between Yishtabach and Yotzer.
