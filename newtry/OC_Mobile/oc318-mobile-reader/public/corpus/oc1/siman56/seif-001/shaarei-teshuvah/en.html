Kaddish—Be'er Heitev. And so in Be'er Mayim Chayim in the name of Mahari Molcho in responsa manuscript: just as it is forbidden to pass before one praying, so it is forbidden to pass before one saying Kaddish.

M'varakh—Be'er Heitev. And see Birkei Yosef, for according to what Magen Avraham wrote one should not interrupt neither between sh'meih and rabbah nor between rabbah and m'varakh; the text should read "one should not interrupt between sh'meih rabbah m'varakh," and so in Lashon Chazal. But in Darkei Moshe he mentioned only between rabbah and m'varakh, as above. And what Be'er Heitev wrote "mikol birkhata," and on days when they double Kaddishes l'eila ul'eila, say only "mikol" so there not be only 28 words, as above; and therefore they hinted that one answering Amen with all one's might. And what he wrote that one not in kavanot say "almaya" without vav—the careful reader there will find it is so; and the shatz likewise says "almaya" without vav; and see Beit Yosef that this was Mahari Abuhav's view.

Kaddish—Be'er Heitev. And he wrote in Be'er Mayim Chayim in the name of Mahari Molcho: when praying in a large synagogue in two or three places, and while one says "bechayechon" another says Yitgadal and the like, one cannot answer two or three Kaddishes—one answers only on the first, as above. One should protest against those who say Kaddish with the shatz while he chants; and thus the Rosh in responsa klal 4 siman 19: it is a simple matter that one should not say Kaddish with the chazzan, as above; and they brought it in Shulchan Aruch.

Be'er Heitev—and what he wrote that for Shabbat Maariv Kaddish one should stand: see Shulchan Aruch, who wrote that Maharach wrote in his siddur one should stand for Kaddish Barchu of Shabbat Maariv (and he means those who say Kaddish before Barchu of kabbalat Shabbat) (and not as Be'er Heitev seems to imply, that he speaks of the Kaddish after Maariv tefillah—for that is like other Kaddishes). And see Pa'aneach Raza in Hagahot Mahari Tzemach, that since the Kaddish after kabbalat Shabbat, when standing, one must remain standing (and according to what Shulchan Aruch wrote we do not rely on Be'er Heitev for this), for there is kabbalat ruach, as above; therefore in Ufros Aleinu, for there is kabbalat neshamah of Shabbat, as above; and possibly he means that the Kaddish after Ufros should also be standing for this reason.

Koach—Be'er Heitev. And in Be'er Mayim Chayim he wrote in the name of Maharatz Chajes, who wrote in the name of Tzror HaChayim: some people say "ve'attah yigdal," etc.; and I heard from my teacher the Rashba, who heard from our teacher the Ramban, that he said no such thing, but would say "Zokhor rachamekha..." etc., because they said in Berakhot that when saying Amen yehei shmei rabbah, etc., as above; and it appears that this verse too one should not say when standing in a place where interruption is forbidden.
