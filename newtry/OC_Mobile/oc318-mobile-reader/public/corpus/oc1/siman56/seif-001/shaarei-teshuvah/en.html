The Kaddish. See Ba'er Heitev. Birkei Yosef writes in the name of Mahari Molcho, in manuscript responsa, that just as it is prohibited to pass in front of a person who is praying, so is it prohibited to pass in front of a person reciting Kaddish.
To bless. See Ba'er Heitev. See Birkei Yosef, who writes that according to Magen Avraham one should interrupt neither between Shemei and Rabba nor between Rabba and Mevarach. The text should therefore read, 'He should not interrupt between Shemei Rabba Mevarach,' and this is also the reading in Lechem Chamudot. Darkei Moshe, however, mentions only the interruption between Rabba and Mevarach; see there. Regarding what Ba'er Heitev writes concerning the words min kol birchata: on days when le'eila le'eila is doubled in Kaddish, one should not say min, but mikol, so that the text will contain only 28 words; see there. This is alluded to by the teaching that one who answers Amen does so 'with all his strength,' the numerical value of kocho being 28. Regarding what he writes that the Kavanot instruct one to say almayya without a vav, one who examines that work will find that this is indeed so. Shalmei Tzibur likewise writes that almayya should be said without a vav. See Beit Yosef, who states that this was also the view of Mahari Abuhav.
Kaddish. See Ba'er Heitev. Birkei Yosef writes in the name of Mahari Molcho that when groups are praying in two or three places in the synagogue's vestibule, and while one person is saying bechayyechon another is saying yitgadal, or similar cases, so that it is impossible to answer two or three Kaddish recitations, one should answer specifically to the first; see there. One should protest against those who customarily recite Kaddish together with the prayer leader while he is chanting. The Rosh writes in Responsa, klal 4, siman 19: 'It is obvious that one should not recite Kaddish together with the cantor'; see there. Shalmei Tzibur cites this.
See Ba'er Heitev. Regarding what he writes that one should stand for the Kaddish of Friday-night Maariv, see Shalmei Tzibur, who writes that Mahar Chaim Vital stated in his siddur that one should rise for the Kaddish and Barechu of Friday-night Maariv. This refers to those who customarily say Kaddish before Barechu following Kabbalat Shabbos. It does not accord with the implication of Ba'er Heitev, who appears to refer to the Kaddish after the Maariv prayer; that is not so, for the latter has the same law as other Kaddish recitations. See Pri Etz Chaim in the glosses of Mahari Tzemach: Since one is standing for the Kaddish after Kabbalat Shabbos, one must remain standing. According to what Shalmei Tzibur writes, however, this is not the basis of the ruling. The reception of the ruach occurs there; see there. Therefore, at Ufros Aleinu, where the reception of the Shabbos neshamah occurs, see there, it is possible that he means that the Kaddish following Ufros should likewise be recited while standing for this reason.
Strength. See Ba'er Heitev. Birkei Yosef writes in the name of his grandfather, who wrote in the name of Tzeror HaChaim: Some people say, 'And now, may it be magnified, etc.' I heard from my teacher Rashba, who heard from our teacher Ramban, that Ramban did not say any such text. He customarily said, 'Remember Your mercies, Hashem, etc.,' because of what the Sages said in Berakhot concerning the time when people answer Yehei Shemei Rabba, etc.; see there. It appears that even this verse should not be said when one is at a point where one may not interrupt.
