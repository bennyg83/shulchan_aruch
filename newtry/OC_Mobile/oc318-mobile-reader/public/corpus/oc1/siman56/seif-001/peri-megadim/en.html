On answering Kaddish—the Mechaber wrote two explanations for "all who answer Amen yehei shmei rabbah with all their might" (Shabbat 119): with full intent or with a loud voice; therefore both apply. And answer note (bet): do not raise one's voice above the one saying m'varakh (siman 124, se'if 12). On Shabbat 119, Amen yehei shmei rabbah has two explanations for "with all their might," but they did not explain Amen with all one's might, for one may say that regarding Amen of praise one does not raise [voice] more than the blesser, as Berakhot 45 says "Gadlu..." together—i.e., in praise—unlike a petition blessing; and answering Amen yehei shmei rabbah, which is faith, one may perhaps raise louder. Amen on a petition is that it be fulfilled speedily in our days; nevertheless they said Amen generally louder than m'varakh. Saying Kaddish Yitgadal and Yitkadash is on the verse (Ezekiel 38:23) "I will be magnified and sanctified"; in Aramaic for two reasons the Taz brought [from Tur], and Beit Yosef wrote further in Zohar Terumah that intent is because they proclaim all should go aside for the King the Lord of Hosts is coming, and must proclaim in their tongue that the Holy One blessed be He is holy and pure; Aramaic has attachment for the sitra achra, so they proclaim in their tongue.

What the Taz wrote: according to explanation one, say sh'meih with mappik hei; not according to explanation two; explanation two is primary, for Y'hei sh'meih rabbah through almaya is 28 letters and Amen yehei shmei rabbah is in all 28 (Amen is not counted, for it refers to what the shatz said Yitgadal...); and sh'meih lacks yod—thus according to explanation two, for the first sh'meih with yod is the name Y-H; nevertheless Bach wrote that even for explanation two there is no problem when saying sh'meih with mappik. Therefore one says Y'hei sh'meih rabbah m'varakh three words in one breath with mappik, yielding eighteen for two explanations. Here are three [occurrences of] sh'meih, and one may also say sh'meih d'kudsha with mappik without concern. I saw in the siddur of the great grammarian R. Shlomo Alkabetz that the third sh'meih is with mappik without yod—which seemingly contradicts; as appears from Taz here, also in yitgadal and yitkadash see the tzerei, for vav is Hebrew on the name; and the dalet of yitgadal is with tzerei, and in Aramaic with patach—not as some say with two sheva na, as if tav and gimmel had sheva, but rather gimmel with patach.

Emphasize gimmel of yitgadal so it not sound yitkadel (back of neck); do not over-emphasize beit of yitbarakh; cut the hei of v'yit'hallal well. Sha'ar HaKavanot and Rokeach. Pause between Amen and Y'hei sh'meih rabbah, and likewise Amen and Modim. Sha'ar HaKavanot, Rokeach, Rabbeinu Yerucham; and truly Beit Yosef ruled Y'hei shmei rabbah... 28 letters and Amen refers to the shatz, as above. And Maharal Tzintz wrote in my small pamphlet two Amens—perhaps they are prayer and Yitgadal will certainly be so forever, only the prayer that it be in your lives speedily; therefore do not say Amen after sh'meih rabbah; and in Magen Avraham I wrote from this; and elsewhere in Magen Avraham I explain further, see note gimel here.
