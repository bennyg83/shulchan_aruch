In answering. Regarding Kaddish, the Mechaber records two interpretations of the statement in Shabbos 119 that whoever answers “Amen, yehei shemei rabba” with all his strength is rewarded: either with full concentration or in a loud voice. One should therefore fulfill both interpretations. See Eliyah Rabbah, letter 2, who writes that one should not raise his voice above that of the person reciting the blessing, as in siman 124, seif 12. In Shabbos 119, two interpretations are given concerning “Amen, yehei shemei rabba with all his strength,” whereas regarding Amen alone the Sages did not explain “with all his strength.” One might distinguish between an Amen that affirms praise, which should not be louder than the person reciting the blessing, as Berachos 45 states, “Exalt Hashem with me, and let us exalt His Name together,” and an Amen to a supplication. In answering “Amen, yehei shemei rabba,” the Amen affirms the request that this should occur immediately in our days, and perhaps one may raise his voice above the reciter. Nevertheless, the authorities state generally that Amen should not be louder than the person reciting the blessing. The wording of Kaddish, “Yisgadal veyiskadash,” is based on the verse in Yechezkel 38:23, “I will be magnified and sanctified.” Two reasons are given for its recital in Aramaic; see Beit Yosef. Taz cites them and also writes that Beit Yosef, in the name of the Zohar to Terumah, explains the underlying intention. In summary, a proclamation is made that all should move aside, because the King, Hashem of Hosts, is coming. The proclamation must be made in their language, because Hebrew is holy and pure, whereas the Other Side has some grasp of Aramaic, and therefore the proclamation is made in its language.
What the Taz wrote. According to the first interpretation, one should pronounce the final hei of “shemei,” but not according to the second interpretation. The second interpretation is primary, because the phrase from “yehei shemei rabba” through “almaya” contains twenty eight letters, corresponding to answering with all one’s strength. Amen is not included in the count, because it responds to what the chazzan said, “Yisgadal,” etc. Furthermore, “shemei” is written without a yud. This therefore follows the second interpretation, whereas according to the first interpretation, “shemei” would contain a yud because it denotes the Name Yah. Bach nevertheless writes that even according to the second interpretation there is no concern if one pronounces the final hei. According to this, one should say the three words “yehei shemei rabba mevarach” in one breath and pronounce the final hei, thereby satisfying both interpretations. There are three forms of “shemei,” and one may say that even “shemei deKudsha berich Hu” may be pronounced with its final hei, without concern. I saw in the prayer book of the great grammarian Rabbi Shlomo Zalman Hanau that the word “shemei” is written with a pronounced final hei but without a yud. At first glance these features are contradictory. Apparently, as is indicated by the Taz here, the same applies to “Yisgadal veyiskadash.” See there. The dalet of “Yisgadal” should be vocalized with a tzerei when treated as Hebrew, but with a patach in Aramaic. It should not be pronounced with two shevas, as though the tav and gimel both carried a sheva. Rather, the gimel has a patach.
He should emphasize. He should emphasize the gimel of “Yisgadal,” so that it not sound like “yiskadel,” meaning a neck. He should not, however, overemphasize it. In “yisbarach,” the beis, and in “veyis’halal,” the hei, should be articulated distinctly. This is stated by the Shelah, Rokeach, Beis Yosef, and Eliyah Rabbah. One should pause between Amen and “yehei shemei rabba,” and likewise between Amen and Modim, as stated by the Shelah and Eliyah Rabbah, letter 5. This is correct, for Beit Yosef rules that “yehei shemei rabba,” etc., contains twenty eight letters, while Amen is the response to the chazzan; see there. For this reason I wrote in my small treatise that I do not know the function of the two Amens. Certainly they are prayers, whereas “Yisgadal” will surely occur in the future. The prayer is only that it occur “in your lifetime, speedily.” Therefore, one should not say Amen after “shemei rabba.” I discussed this in Magen Avraham, and with Hashem’s help I will explain it further elsewhere. See what I wrote in letter 3 on this matter.
