[4] Bareheaded, etc. Even indoors; saintly conduct even less than four cubos — so Bach wrote. Taz wrote: sitting is permitted; riding an animal or sitting in a wagon is like walking — until here. This is somewhat hard against what Taz himself wrote siman 94 s.k. 4 that one may stand in a wagon; see below siman 91 for mentioning God’s name or synagogue; Olas Tamid omitted Shulchan Aruch’s words there. Shelah 198 wrote saintliness is covering the head at night like by day — until here. Maharshal responsa 72 brought proof there is no prohibition of bareheadedness from Midrash Rabba: God tells Israel regarding Shema “first matters for Me” — I did not trouble you to read neither standing nor bareheaded, etc. — until here. It seems to him there is no prohibition parallel to “standing and reading.” Then the world’s question from Midrash “I did not trouble you to bring an animal offering,” etc. — that is forbidden to bring — is settled; examine; see Taz beginning siman 61 who did not adopt this. Still for practical law he concludes to forbid bareheadedness. Magen Avraham wrote one should be careful to cover children’s heads.
