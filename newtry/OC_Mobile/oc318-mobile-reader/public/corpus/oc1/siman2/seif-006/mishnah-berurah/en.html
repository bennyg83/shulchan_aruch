(ט) With an erect posture — “as if forcing the feet of the Divine Presence,” so later authorities wrote it is forbidden even less than four cubos; obviously even standing in place without walking at all.
(י) And he may not walk [four cubits bareheaded] — even in a roofed house, and how much more so under the open sky everyone must be careful.
(יא) Four cubos — and saintly conduct even less than four cubos, even when sleeping at night. Some lean to say even four cubos is not strict prohibition by law only for the exceptionally pious in conduct; but Taz wrote below siman 8 that in our times bareheadedness is full prohibition by law even sitting at home, see there the reason; Mahari Bruna responsa wrote similarly; Magen Avraham wrote even children should be accustomed to cover the head so awe of Heaven may be upon them as Shabbat 156 says “cover your head so awe of Heaven may be upon you.” Know also: for bareheadedness covering the head with the hand suffices, and likewise if he bows his head it is permitted to be “bareheaded.” See Peri Megadim who wrote: to walk four cubos under the open sky hand on head does not suffice.
(יב) Bareheaded — how much more so forbidden to bless or study bareheaded; hand-covering does not help, for hand and head are one body and the body cannot cover itself. Some are lenient in pressing need, e.g. at night wishing to drink with no hat — covering the head with the hand suffices; better is common practice: extend the garment sleeve over the hand and cover the head with it — then it is proper covering for all. Peri Megadim wrote: be careful when laying head-tefillin not to recite the blessing bareheaded; a wig of hair, even sewn under a garment, some forbid because of marit ayin that people will say it is hair, and some are lenient.
(יג) And inspect his openings — so that afterward his body be clean at the time of accepting Heaven’s kingship in Shema and prayer; checking alone suffices, and if he checked himself and does not need to relieve himself now, that is legally a clean body; Heaven forbid afterward again at Shema time or even only communal prayer.
(יד) Barefoot — our sages said one should sell all he has and buy shoes for his feet. In Arab lands where going barefoot is customary it is permitted. Some add in the name of Shelah: if done for repentance for sins it is permitted; King David peace be upon him walked barefoot [so].
