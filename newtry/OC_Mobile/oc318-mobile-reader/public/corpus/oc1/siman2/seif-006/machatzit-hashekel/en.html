(S.k. 6) Four cubos and saintly conduct even less than four cubos. This does not mean to explain Shulchan Aruch’s “four cubos” as: four cubos forbidden by law but less permitted by law yet saintliness would still require stringency — that is untrue, for Magen Avraham wrote below siman 91 s.k. 3 on Shulchan Aruch there not to mention God’s name or enter synagogue bareheaded — Magen Avraham said what siman 2 calls not walking four cubos bareheaded is saintliness alone — hence even four cubos is only saintliness. Rather Magen Avraham brought Bach who holds even less than four cubos has saintliness. Perhaps Maharshal did not mean that — only saintliness forbids four cubos not less. Or Shulchan Aruch agrees with Bach that even less has saintliness, as Bach said: since the reason is God’s glory fills the earth and for the Divine Presence’s honor one covers the head, what difference four cubos or less? Why Shulchan Aruch said “four cubos” — Gemara also says four cubos yet Bach said it is not precise, see there — same may apply to Shulchan Aruch; this seems Maharshal’s main view, cited Taz below siman 8: no prohibition in bareheadedness, only saintliness; also because the masses treat it lightly and licentiously one should be strict; still if he wishes to bless and has nothing to cover the head, e.g. at night, covering with the hand suffices. Although for “his heart sees nakedness” hand on chest does not help (siman 74 s.b.), hand on head where that is not normal usage does help. Taz wrote prohibition by law because of “their practices” — then hand on head shows he is not following their ways; still for blessing hand does not help — same siman 74; Magen Avraham siman 91 s.k. 4 also disagrees with Maharshal that hand does not suffice for blessing. Nedarim 31a implies children: one who vows from “black heads” is forbidden in men — women cover heads, children bare them. Ran: women always cover — if he meant women he would say “covered heads”; children always bare — if he meant them he would say “uncovered heads”; therefore he meant men who sometimes bare and sometimes cover — hence “black heads.” Shabbat 156b; siman 282 s.k. 3 Magen Avraham s.k. 8 — not from here [alone].
