Maharshal wrote that the prohibition of walking with an erect posture and of walking bareheaded applies specifically under the open sky, but not indoors, for there one is less visibly conscious of standing in awe before the Holy One blessed be He than under the open sky. Maharsha wrote that a straw or reed hat counts as a covering. Elya Rabba wrote the same applies to hair called in the vernacular a “peruke” (wig) — it counts as a covering; yet because of marit ayin one should be careful when possible.
It is forbidden to mention God’s name bareheaded (Mahari); placing one’s hands on the head does not suffice. But Maharshal wrote: if one bathes in a bathhouse and wishes to drink, or at night when one has no hat, covering the head with the hand is enough. Sefer Hakavanos wrote one should be careful not to wear two garments together, for that leads to forgetfulness; one should hold both sides of the tallit or garment in the right hand, then give the left side to the left hand, and always intend that everything is subsumed under “right,” then from right to left (Lechem Shamayim).
