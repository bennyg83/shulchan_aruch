Erect posture. Even less than four cubos — Bach, Taz, Magen Avraham. Yad Aharon wrote: since from the Gemara it appears that within four cubos it is permitted, how could our author be strict even within four? Taz s.k. 4 wrote our author did not have the text “four cubos” in the Gemara, see there — but this is difficult, for our author wrote in his hints to Kiddushin ch. 1 that it is forbidden to walk four cubos with an erect posture or bareheaded; hence he did have “four cubos.” See his hints to Berachos ch. Keitzad Mevarchin, where he did not write “four cubos” — clarify. See what later authorities wrote: bareheaded conduct is a pious stringency and not a strict prohibition, see there — this needs study, for in his hints he grouped and taught “erect posture” and “bareheaded” and in all of them taught “forbidden.” Yad Aharon.

Four cubos. As an extra standard of piety, even less than four cubos — even children — it is proper to cover their heads so that awe of Heaven may be upon them. Magen Avraham.

Bareheaded. Taz in this siman s.k. 5 wrote the same applies when riding an animal or sitting in a wagon — it is considered like walking; in siman 8 s.k. 3 he wrote even sitting is forbidden because of “you shall not follow their practices,” see there. Maharshal wrote that the prohibition of walking with an erect posture and bareheaded applies specifically under the open sky but not indoors, where one is less visibly conscious of standing in awe before the Holy One, blessed be He, than under the open sky, see there. Elya Rabba wrote one may rely on this in time of need; a straw or reed hat counts as a covering, as explained below siman 91 s.k. 4. Elya Rabba wrote the same applies to hair called a “peruke” (wig) — it counts as a covering; yet because of marit ayin one should be careful when possible. Entering a synagogue bareheaded is explained below siman 91 s.k. 3 — forbidden. It is forbidden to mention the Name bareheaded; placing the hands on the head does not suffice, there siman 91 s.k. 3. But Maharshal wrote: if one bathes in a bathhouse and wishes to drink, or at night when one has no hat, covering the head with the hand is enough. Taz siman 8 and siman 74 ruled that covering the head with the hand suffices so that one does not sit or walk bareheaded, but for blessing or uttering sacred words hand-covering does not suffice, see there — and Magen Avraham ruled similarly in siman 91 s.k. 4. If another places his hand on one’s head it counts as a covering even for blessing, there siman 91.

Barefoot. It appears in Shabbos 128a and Pesachim 112 that one should sell all he has and buy shoes for his feet. {Rama: (Sefer Eliyahu Rabba cites in the name of the Shelah that if one does so for repentance for his sins it is permitted; King David peace be upon him likewise went barefoot.)}
