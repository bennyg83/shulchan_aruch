His robe while seated. Then he necessarily exposes his body. It appears in ch. Kol Kitvei: Rabbi Yochanan said “my house beams never saw my cloak” — Tur explains: the edge of his cloak. Rashi: when removing his robe as he wore it, through the head, he first covers himself for modesty with a sheet underneath and sits on his bed — end of his words’ explanation: when removing bottom-to-top one cannot avoid showing flesh because whenever he lifts part of the robe he covers that part with the sheet; not so if he removed inside-out — impossible to cover with the sheet what he exposes, as reason dictates; from this Tur learned that when donning the robe too he should act so as not to expose his body. That Rabbi Yochanan prided himself regarding removing his robe and not donning suggests donning is easier to guard — one does so while still lying, as Tur and Shulchan Aruch say — not so when removing, which requires sitting and less sheet-covering is possible; therefore Rashi added “and sits on his bed” to teach even then he was careful.
