(1) While seated — for then he necessarily exposes his body, and a person must conduct himself with modesty and shame before the Holy One blessed be He, even at night and in inner chambers, for is not “the whole earth full of His glory,” and darkness and light are alike before Him, may He be blessed. So too one must always be careful, because of this reason, not to uncover his flesh where it is not necessary — even a little — in anything that is normally always covered by clothing; but he may uncover his hand up to his elbow and his neck up to his chest; therefore stockings he should put on or remove under the sheet so as not to uncover his feet, which are normally always covered in these countries where people do not go barefoot even in summer, and likewise anything similar unless it is impossible otherwise. In a bathhouse, where people’s custom is to go there naked and it cannot be otherwise, there is no immodesty in this; likewise when bathing in a river the law is the same — only he should be careful to undress and dress close to the river as much as possible so as not to walk with bodily exposure that is not necessary; even his nakedness need not be covered when descending into the river; moreover, covering looks as if he is ashamed of the matter and as if denying the covenant of Avraham our father; but when ascending from the river, if his face is toward people, he should bow or place his hand opposite his nakedness to cover it, provided he does not touch it, as will be explained in Even HaEzer siman 3. It is stated in the Talmud: the robe of a talmid chacham is one from which his flesh is not visible beneath it. Today people are not careful about this because everyone goes about in stockings and the flesh is not visible.

(2) When he rises — meaning when he rises and leaves the sheet covering under which he lay naked when he removed his robe, he should now at once be covered, for the robe will fall over his whole body by itself; see below siman 239 s.b. regarding removing the robe. One who places his garments at his head forgets his learning; perhaps if he places something separating his head from the garments there is no concern. One should also beware of wearing two garments together at once, for that leads to forgetfulness. One who reads an inscription on a grave or gazes at a dead person’s face forgets his learning; see also Peri Megadim on things that restore learning.
