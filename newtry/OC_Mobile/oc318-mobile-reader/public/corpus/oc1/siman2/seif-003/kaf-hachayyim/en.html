4) [Seif 3] Be careful with his robe, etc. Not only his robe; the same for other garments; the stringency is that even his robe, the undergarment, when worn inside-out he troubles to remove all garments and wear properly; Perishah letter 3, Eshel Avraham letter 1.
5) There; not to turn, etc. Taz s.k. 2 wrote there is a distinction between a scholar and others: for a scholar if he wore the robe inside-out he must reverse it; ordinary people should from the outset avoid wearing it inside-out, see there; so Perishah letter 3. A scholar’s reason: every scholar must be dignified for his Torah’s honor (Perishah there. “For this matter every person can make himself a scholar”) Eshel Avraham letter 1; but Rabbi Shmelke Chadashos 112b asks on this and writes even everyone if he wore it inside-out must reverse it at least for prayer; Birkei Yosef letter 1; Mateh Yehudah letter 3 in name of Pri Tzadik: no distinction between scholar and everyone.
6) Beware not to wear two garments together, for that leads to forgetfulness; Shaar HaKavanos in Drush Bereshis, cited Magen Avraham s.k. 3; the reason is explained in Shaar HaKavanos: each garment has an aspect of “light,” and one who wears two together does not give room for the light to enter between the two garments and surround each garment, hence shells are not driven away; forgetfulness comes from shells, see there. It seems the same if wearing two hats one inside the other; concern for forgetfulness; therefore it is not good for those who wear the small cap on the head that is red (called fez) inside the large bonnet etc. by custom to wear one inside the other (that too causes forgetfulness and is obvious) Ruach Chayim letter 3.
