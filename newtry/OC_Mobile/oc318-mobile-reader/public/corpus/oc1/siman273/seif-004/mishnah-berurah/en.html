(16) To recite Kiddush for others. He may likewise recite for children without eating with them to train them in mitzvos, provided they cannot recite it themselves, as below concerning an adult.
(17) With them. Even if he already fulfilled his own obligation.
(18) With them. For blessings over benefit, one cannot discharge another unless he also benefits then. Only obligations such as Kiddush allow him to discharge another, as explained in siman 167:19 and 205.
(19) And even, etc. Although daytime Kiddush consists only of Borei Pri HaGefen, it is a mitzvah instituted principally as obligation rather than benefit, so he may discharge others without benefiting, like nighttime Kiddush. This differs from Hamotzi at the three Shabbos meals: although eating is obligatory, the obligation is for Shabbos enjoyment, not an intrinsic eating act (one who enjoys fasting need not eat, as in siman 288) so he cannot discharge others without eating. If he recites Kiddush over bread, he also discharges Hamotzi, which then resembles HaGefen for wine Kiddush.
(20) Provided they do not know. If they can recite Kiddush themselves, one who is not presently fulfilling his own obligation cannot discharge them. Pri Chadash in siman 585 disagrees and permits every case. Artzos HaChaim, siman 8, concludes that by law he may always discharge them, though initially a knowledgeable person should recite it himself when the other is not presently fulfilling his own obligation. A widow unable to recite Kiddush should not enter another house to hear it if she will not eat there; someone should enter her home and recite for her, which works even if he is not presently fulfilling his own obligation.
