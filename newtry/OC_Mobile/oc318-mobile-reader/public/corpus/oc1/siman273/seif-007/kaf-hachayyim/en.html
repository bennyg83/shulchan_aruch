7) If one recited Kiddush and ate there food whose Torah prohibition is uncertain, Mishbetzos Zahav writes in Yoreh De'ah, siman 119, at the end of paragraph 1, that according to the opinion that such food is forbidden by Torah law, he did not fulfill the obligation of Kiddush if this was all he ate there. According to the opinion that its prohibition is rabbinic, it can be said that he need not repeat Kiddush; see there. Petach HaDevir, paragraph 5, however, writes that the pleasure of his throat suffices. If, when he ate the food of uncertain Torah prohibition, he did not know of the uncertainty, and he learned of it only afterward, once the food had descended into his stomach but before digestion, he need not repeat Kiddush. At the time he ate and his throat benefited, he believed he was eating permitted food, and at that moment he experienced delight through the pleasure of his throat; see there.
