(Seif katan 2) And from a house, etc. Even if his intention had not been, etc., such as where he thought rain was falling and made kiddush in the house, and before he began eating they told him that rain was not falling, and he entered the sukkah to eat. The same applies in the opposite case, where he made kiddush in the sukkah and before he began eating rain fell, so he went to eat in the house. But if the partitions of the house, etc., such as where it is built in the courtyard or street. The same applies, in my humble opinion, if he has a sukkah built from year to year, even inside his room: since all year he uses that area like another room, except that on Sukkot he opens the roof above and sits there for the mitzvah of sukkah, it is a complete partition that interrupts.
And so it is implied in Tosafot, Perek Arvei Pesachim, 100b, s.v. yedei kiddush, etc. They brought the Yerushalmi: Shmuel holds that there is no kiddush except in the place of the meal. Rav said that one whose sukkah is pleasant to him and who wishes to eat in the sukkah also on the eighth day, but cannot because of bal tosif as written in siman 666, makes kiddush on Yom Tov night in the house and ascends and eats in his sukkah. This implies that Rav holds there is kiddush not in the place of the meal, as stated in our Talmud. R. Bun said that they do not disagree: Shmuel speaks where he did not intend to eat elsewhere, and Rav speaks where at the time of kiddush he intended to eat in the sukkah. This is the opinion of the final "some say" brought by the Rav Beit Yosef in this seif. But why did Rav need to intend to eat in the sukkah? Let him derive that from house to sukkah is like moving from corner to corner. Rather, perforce one must say that where the partitions of the house interrupt, it is like moving from room to room, and there the partitions of the house interrupted, for it teaches "he ascends and eats in his sukkah," meaning from the ground to the roof.
And the Olat Shabbat did not examine the Mordechai. Meaning, Olat Shabbat wrote the reason: since he is obligated to eat in the sukkah, this is better than one who makes kiddush in one place intending to eat in another. The same applies in the opposite case, where he made kiddush in the sukkah and rain fell and he goes to the house to eat, because he must go to the house when rain falls, for otherwise he is called a commoner; this is better than if he had intended to go elsewhere; see there. According to this, even where the partitions of the house interrupt it is called the place of the meal. But in the Mordechai it is clear that this is not so.
