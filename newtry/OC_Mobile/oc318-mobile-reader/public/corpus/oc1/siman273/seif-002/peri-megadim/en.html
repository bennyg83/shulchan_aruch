(א) And if — notation: in Beit Yosef he wrote the end of this siman, and the Taz brought from Pesachim 101a, and see in Rashbam s.v. ki hava mekadesh — we want to exempt from there and go to our houses, see there — it implies after Kiddush they wanted to leave from there; and Rabbah has in Rosh Hashanah 28b that mitzvot do not require intent, as appears from the Rosh there — Rabbah's text reads Rabbah and Rava, etc., and therefore he said to them: stay, taste something lest, etc., and they fulfilled Kiddush here; and see Ran there — there is not such a decisive ruling whether the text reads Rava or Rabbah; and in Turei Zahav and Choshen Mishpat they were angry at our master the Taz, and for what is written — it would seem.

(ב) And know that what Rama wrote in Semag, letter 3 — rabbinic laws do not require intent, and in Ra'avan, letter 1 — one fulfills in prayer and Kiddush on the cup, rabbinic laws in any case do not require — this is not that of Rava and Abbaye; and see Rosh in Rosh Hashanah there, and in Ran it appears mitzvot require intent to fulfill — it is not clear, but from doubt we go stringently, so too the Rosh, and so Ran — taking the stringent is preferable; therefore it requires study: one who heard Kiddush from another and did not intend to fulfill — only incidentally — if he may return and sanctify, like two householders in one room that is a place of a meal for them, and all the more per the one who says rabbinic laws do not require intent; and see siman 239 from there there is room to reject. Therefore it is good that he intend explicitly not to fulfill in this hearing; but if not so, if he heard only incidentally, he may not sanctify again lest it be a blessing in vain for Kiddush — only hear from another and intend. And if not so in the opening — general opening and opening for blessings will be explained further. And in the words of Kiddush of the Levush it is written: if his intent was not to eat there but elsewhere, he fulfills nevertheless, for his intent was to eat, see there; and so in Beit Yosef — another place; and in the gloss he omitted it — one might say even if he did not intend to eat at all and reconsidered, he fulfills, for intent does not harm here (but nevertheless since he intends to fulfill in Kiddush, or rabbinic laws do not require intent) — and he himself would say he learned thus from that of Abbaye, etc., and there his intent was in any case to eat immediately in the place — and if not so in Magen Avraham it will be explained.
