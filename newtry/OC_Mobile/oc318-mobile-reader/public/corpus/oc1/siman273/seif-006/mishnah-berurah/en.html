(28) If he recited Kiddush. He may even initially recite at home so a neighbor in another house hears [Magen Avraham]. If the neighbor cannot recite it, his Kiddush works even if he does not eat, as above in se'if 4 and paragraph 20. Magen Avraham adds that when reciting over bread, he may bless Hamotzi for them.
(29) And his table is set. Not literal; intending to set the table and eat immediately likewise helps, if intended at Kiddush, as above in se'if 3, gloss.
(30) Such as when he intended, etc. This is not limited to that case; whenever reciting Kiddush for others, the rule applies. Nowadays, if the shaliach tzibbur recites Kiddush in the study hall, a listener does not fulfill it even if intending to and planning to eat there, unless the shaliach tzibbur intended to discharge him.
