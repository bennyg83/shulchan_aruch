1) [Seif 1] Kiddush is valid only in the place of a meal. This applies both to the night Kiddush and to the daytime Kiddush, as stated below at the beginning of siman 289. The reason is the verse, 'You shall call Shabbos a delight': where the calling occurs, there must be delight. Beis Yosef; Taz, paragraph 1. According to this reason, even one who recites Kiddush over bread must do so in the place of a meal. The person reciting Kiddush must also eat in that place, as stated in seif 4. Although he recites Kiddush for himself and others, if they eat there and he does not, they fulfill their obligation but he does not. Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 1. This implies that the reverse also applies: if he recites Kiddush for himself and others and he eats there but they do not, he fulfills his obligation but they do not. See below, paragraph 4.
2) There: Kiddush is valid only in the place of a meal. If one recited Kiddush in one place, ate nothing there, and went elsewhere to eat, he may not eat until he recites Kiddush again. Bach. This applies when, at the time of Kiddush, he did not intend to eat elsewhere. If he had such an intention, see below, where some say he fulfilled his obligation.
3) There: Kiddush is valid only in the place of a meal. This is Shmuel's opinion. Although Rav and Rabbi Yochanan disagree with him, we maintain Shmuel's view, as explained in Tur and Beis Yosef; see there. Ginas Veradim, klal 3, siman 12, writes that since the daytime Kiddush is quite lenient, one who relies upon Rav and Rabbi Yochanan has not acted improperly. We do not mean that one should rely upon this ab initio. Only in pressing circumstances and for the honor of a large gathering may one rely upon this rationale to be lenient, and one should not protest against the public when they act this way. Birkei Yosef, paragraph 1, cites this; see there.
4) There: Kiddush is valid only in the place of a meal. Where there is no meal, there is no obligation of Kiddush. For this reason Kiddush is not recited on Yom Kippur, even when it occurs on Shabbos. Birkei Yosef, siman 272, Shiyurei Berachah, in the name of Sefer HaMachria and Sefer HaBatim; see there. Responsa of the Geonim at the end of Neharos Damesek, siman 105, similarly states that if a person has no opportunity to eat on Shabbos night, or cannot eat, he should not recite Kiddush until the next day at his meal; see there. In siman 106 there, it states that he should nevertheless recite Vayechulu even without a meal, because the two angels who accompany a person hear this testimony from his mouth, place their hands upon his head, and say, 'Your iniquity has departed,' etc. Yafeh LaLev, paragraph 1, cites this and writes that were it not for this reason, it would be proper for every person to recite it at home when Yom Kippur occurs on Shabbos; see there. Although Yafeh LaLev writes that he did not observe this custom, each person should nevertheless adopt it and recite Vayechulu at home, because nothing is lost through merely reciting verses. See above, siman 268, paragraph 3, and note this well.
5) Birkei Yosef, paragraph 2, writes: The aforementioned words of Ginas Veradim demonstrate that even under pressing circumstances, regarding the daytime Kiddush and for the honor of a large gathering, he permitted only that one not protest against them. Certainly, however, a person whose heart has been touched by the fear of God should not taste anything in the following situation: on Shabbos day, when people enter to honor a bridegroom, the father of a newborn son, or the like, Kiddush is recited and afterward sweets and similar items are served, but there is no bread or pas haba'ah bekisnin, only delicacies and coffee. It does not suffice that the person who recited Kiddush drinks the entire cup, which the Geonim regard as Kiddush in the place of a meal. That helps only the person who drinks the cup himself, for whom it is considered as though Kiddush was recited in the place of a meal. For the others it is not the place of a meal, and they may not taste anything. This is explicit and clear in the Gemara and halachic authorities. I wrote it only to correct those whom I have seen err in this matter. These are his words. Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 4, similarly writes that the person reciting Kiddush drinking a revi'is does not help the others, contrary to what Me'am Loez, parashas Yisro, writes; see there. We wrote the same above in paragraph 1; see there. Chesed LaAlafim writes there that even if one drank a revi'is of wine or ate an olive-sized portion of pas haba'ah bekisnin in the morning, it is proper to recite Kiddush again at his table so that Kiddush will be in the place of the principal meal. The practice of scrupulous people is that immediately upon leaving the synagogue, they act promptly and recite Kiddush.
6) There: Kiddush is valid only in the place of a meal. If a person recited Kiddush and ate a proper meal in accordance with halachah, then arose and immediately vomited it, may he eat elsewhere without Kiddush? See Petach HaDevir, paragraph 1, who discusses this matter at length and concludes that he should not repeat Kiddush at the second meal, especially since there is a possibility that doing so would constitute a blessing in vain. If possible, however, it is proper to hear Kiddush from another person who is reciting it for himself, with both the listener and speaker intending that the listener fulfill his obligation; see there.
7) If one recited Kiddush and ate there food whose Torah prohibition is uncertain, Mishbetzos Zahav writes in Yoreh De'ah, siman 119, at the end of paragraph 1, that according to the opinion that such food is forbidden by Torah law, he did not fulfill the obligation of Kiddush if this was all he ate there. According to the opinion that its prohibition is rabbinic, it can be said that he need not repeat Kiddush; see there. Petach HaDevir, paragraph 5, however, writes that the pleasure of his throat suffices. If, when he ate the food of uncertain Torah prohibition, he did not know of the uncertainty, and he learned of it only afterward, once the food had descended into his stomach but before digestion, he need not repeat Kiddush. At the time he ate and his throat benefited, he believed he was eating permitted food, and at that moment he experienced delight through the pleasure of his throat; see there.
