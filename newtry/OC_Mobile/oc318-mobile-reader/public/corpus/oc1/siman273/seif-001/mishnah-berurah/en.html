(1) In the place of the meal. The verse says, 'You shall call Shabbos a delight': where the delight (the meal) is held, there Kiddush must be proclaimed.
(2) To eat in this corner, etc. Certainly if he recited Kiddush intending to eat in another corner, it is valid.
(3) And changed his mind to eat, etc. Initially it is best not to leave the place of Kiddush, since some are stringent even from corner to corner, unless at Kiddush he intended to eat in the other corner; then there is no reason for stringency.
(4) In another corner. If he decided to eat in another room, even in the same house, he must repeat Kiddush, since he did not intend this at Kiddush.
(5) And from the house to the sukkah, etc. If rain forced him to recite Kiddush indoors and then stopped, he may eat in the sukkah without repeating; likewise if he recited in the sukkah and rain forced him indoors. This concerns a sukkah inside the house beneath a removed roof, with no partition besides the sukkah wall. Since that wall serves the mitzvah, not ordinary use, moving is like corner to corner without a partition and requires no repetition even absent prior intent. If the sukkah is outside and the house walls intervene, it is like room to room: absent initial intent, Kiddush must be repeated unless he can see the first place, as below. Some poskim dispute this entire analysis, as explained in Biur Halachah. Therefore, one planning to relocate after Kiddush should first drink a revi'is of wine there, making it a meal-place as below; everyone then agrees no new Kiddush is needed.
(6) Whenever he can see his place. If from the meal-place he can see the Kiddush-place, even through a window and even only part of it, all is considered one place.
(7) Even from house to courtyard. The same applies house to house if he sees his place and no private path used regularly in summer and winter separates them. Rely on this opinion only after the fact under pressure when he cannot eat at the Kiddush-place. Otherwise, avoid relying on visibility between houses, since some later authorities are stringent even after the fact.
(8) In one house. Meaning beneath one roof, even without seeing his place.
