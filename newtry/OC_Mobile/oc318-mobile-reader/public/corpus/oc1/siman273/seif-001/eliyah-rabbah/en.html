(א) And afterward he reconsidered, etc. It implies when his intent was to eat elsewhere it is fine; however one may say the same law applies when his intent was for this — it does not help; and that he wrote reconsidered is because of the latter clause — from corner to corner even when reconsidering it is permitted; but it appears from what he wrote in seif 5, and his intent was to eat, and we already said in seif 2 that in such a case it is permitted, etc. — his intent here is specifically when reconsidering it is forbidden, but with intent even in another house it is permitted; and so appears from the beginning of siman 278. And it requires study, for in Shulchan Aruch and Rama they ruled specifically from room to room or from level to level — two places in one house — that intent helps, but from house to house it does not help; and so in Tur and Beit Yosef no decisor who disagrees is mentioned. I looked again — there is wonder in Tur, who wrote that Tosafot explained R' Nissim Gaon's words specifically when two places are in one house, etc.; and in Tosafot daf 100 in chapter Arvei Pesachim he did not mention the Ran's words mentioned above, but explained on his own thus, and so the Rosh; and furthermore in Mordechai, chapter Arvei Pesachim, and Kolbo daf 35, they wrote in the name of the Ran mentioned above that Kiddush done in the synagogue is a place of a meal since his intent is to eat at home — behold the Gaon's view is that even from house to house intent helps, and so is explained in Shibolei HaLeket, siman 19. And what Tosafot challenged — from challenging Shmuel: why should I sanctify in the synagogue? — it appears to me the Ran answers as the Rosh wrote: he challenges lest one err that there is Kiddush [that is] not in a place of a meal even without intent, and it answers they should not err and think because of guests; and if so, since the original enactment was not annulled now even though there are no guests, since there is no prohibition by law. And I saw in the explanations of Rashal to Semag, siman 29, who answered: they permitted only where eating applies, unlike the synagogue, which is fit only as a guest house — until here. And one may wonder he did not see Mordechai and Kolbo mentioned above, that the Ran himself held it is permitted in the synagogue for this reason, and so in Tosafot, Rosh, and Ran there it is clear there is no distinction; and Maharsha wrote on Tosafot's words there — furthermore proof, etc., until here his words. And incidentally he was not precise, for Rashi, Rashbam, and Tosafot daf 101 explain the lamp was uprooted and you will not eat — if so, what does his intent help since he does not eat where he thought to eat? And so is explained in Shibolei HaLeket there; and Mordechai who brought this proof follows his approach, who explained one may eat without a lamp, only Kiddush is forbidden without a lamp, not like Rashi and Tosafot; and if so he brings proof well — so it appears to me clearly the intent of Tosafot, examine well; and in any event for practical law, since Tosafot, Rosh, Mordechai, and Ran in chapter Arvei Pesachim forbade in another house, one should not be lenient.
