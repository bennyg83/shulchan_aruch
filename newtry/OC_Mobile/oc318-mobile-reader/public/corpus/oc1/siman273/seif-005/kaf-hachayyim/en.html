5) Birkei Yosef, paragraph 2, writes: The aforementioned words of Ginas Veradim demonstrate that even under pressing circumstances, regarding the daytime Kiddush and for the honor of a large gathering, he permitted only that one not protest against them. Certainly, however, a person whose heart has been touched by the fear of God should not taste anything in the following situation: on Shabbos day, when people enter to honor a bridegroom, the father of a newborn son, or the like, Kiddush is recited and afterward sweets and similar items are served, but there is no bread or pas haba'ah bekisnin, only delicacies and coffee. It does not suffice that the person who recited Kiddush drinks the entire cup, which the Geonim regard as Kiddush in the place of a meal. That helps only the person who drinks the cup himself, for whom it is considered as though Kiddush was recited in the place of a meal. For the others it is not the place of a meal, and they may not taste anything. This is explicit and clear in the Gemara and halachic authorities. I wrote it only to correct those whom I have seen err in this matter. These are his words. Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 4, similarly writes that the person reciting Kiddush drinking a revi'is does not help the others, contrary to what Me'am Loez, parashas Yisro, writes; see there. We wrote the same above in paragraph 1; see there. Chesed LaAlafim writes there that even if one drank a revi'is of wine or ate an olive-sized portion of pas haba'ah bekisnin in the morning, it is proper to recite Kiddush again at his table so that Kiddush will be in the place of the principal meal. The practice of scrupulous people is that immediately upon leaving the synagogue, they act promptly and recite Kiddush.
