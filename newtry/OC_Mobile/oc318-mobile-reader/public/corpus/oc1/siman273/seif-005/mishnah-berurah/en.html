(21) A small amount. At least a kezayis, since 'for which he incurs a blessing' refers to both items.
(22) A blessing. A revi'is, which incurs an after-blessing. The Mechaber follows those requiring a revi'is for the after-blessing [Chiddushei Rabbi Akiva Eiger above, siman 190:3]. Pri Megadim inclines that even if ordinarily less suffices, it is not considered a meal-place with less than a revi'is of wine.
(23) And completes his meal. Above, siman 178:2, gloss, says initially not to leave without Birkas HaMazon. After the fact, if he left, he need not bless before continuing; he blesses after completing the meal.
(24) Elsewhere. There he need not bless again over the cup before eating, since Kiddush was already fulfilled.
(25) Or drank wine. Certainly grain-based pastries suffice, being more meal-like than wine. Beer and other beverages, even chamar medinah, do not, because they do not sustain the heart like wine. Rabbi Akiva Eiger and Tosefes Shabbos prove that several Rishonim do not accept even a cup of wine as Kiddush in the meal-place. Therefore be lenient only under pressure.
(26) Fruit does not. The five grains are called food and wine sustains the heart; other foods, however abundant, are not a meal. Thus, one visiting a groom's celebration or bris where after Kiddush only delicacies, not pastries, are served should taste nothing. It does not suffice that the one reciting Kiddush drinks a full revi'is; that helps only him, not others [later authorities]. Shiltei HaGibborim says fruit suffices because every Shabbos meal is fixed, but Tur and Shulchan Aruch are primary [Magen Avraham]. If somewhat weak and without grain food after the cup, some later authorities allow reliance on Shiltei HaGibborim by day, never at night, since the other poskim disagree.
(27) The measure. A revi'is. Some say even a revi'is does not make Kiddush occur in the meal-place; he must drink an additional revi'is besides the Kiddush cup.
