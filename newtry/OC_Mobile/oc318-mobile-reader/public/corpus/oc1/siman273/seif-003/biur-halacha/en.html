(ב) Immediately — see in Mishna Berurah what is written in the name of Shakhna and his words are also brought in Sefer Yad Malachi; and it is simple to the author of Shakhna that this is so, until he wrote the matter should be silenced and the opposite was not stated. And behold Magen Avraham, note 5, at its end wrote that if he left his place in the meantime he must sanctify again as stated, and behold Shulchan Aruch HaRav and Darkei Chayim and Chayei Adam imply they explain it according to its plain meaning — if he left his place, even if he returned, he must sanctify again — and so it is explained more explicitly in Hagahot HaGra [but in Turei Zahav and Mateh Yehuda it appears they explain Magen Avraham's words when he did not return to his place but ate there, and to teach us that even though it was under constraint that he had no candle in the first place, and regarding considering it a place of a meal, since l'chatchila he intended not to remain, as written there earlier — nevertheless regarding change of place it makes no difference]. And so that they should not disagree with each other, it appears Magen Avraham refers only to where he relied earlier that it deals with one who delayed due to constraint, that he need not return and sanctify because he did not intend this from the start; and on this he concluded that if he left his place in the meantime he must return and sanctify — namely because otherwise he already delayed; but for the reason of leaving outside alone, he would also hold like Shakhna that it is not considered a change, since in the end he ate in the place of Kiddush, and he relied on this from what we hold that matters requiring a blessing afterward in their place — if one left and returned he need not return and bless, because of his first establishment he returns, and behold there even if he blesses in the place he went he also fulfills the mitzvah in Birkat Hamazon, and even so, since l'chatchila the mitzvah is to return to his place, we say he did not remove his attention from his first place and need not return and bless — all the more so here he does not fulfill Kiddush unless he returns and eats in the place of Kiddush; and also if he does not return it is revealed he drank initially improperly, for there is no Kiddush except in a place of a meal — it is simple he did not remove his attention from the first place [and even though per Tosafot Pesachim 101 s.v. aval, since we compare Kiddush to blessing regarding change of place — that is only regarding room to room, for regarding blessing a change of place is also a change of place for Kiddush, and corner to corner is considered one place; but in what we hold regarding blessing that he loses his blessing because of leaving outside alone even if he returned — the reason is only because of distraction — in Kiddush it is different, for certainly he did not distract his attention, and it is like matters requiring blessing afterward in their place, as stated]. And so all the great ones mentioned who copied Magen Avraham's words for practical law relied the law of leaving outside on the case of one who delayed due to constraint, and it implies they were stringent b'dieved only in this manner and not for leaving alone; and afterward I found in Petaḥ Devarim who expanded greatly on this matter and sided with the law not like Shakhna, and brought in the name of Sefer Nachalat Tzvi, student of the Bach, that his view explicitly is that when one left outside and returned home it is also within change of place for Kiddush; nevertheless it appears one should not be stringent b'dieved against Shakhna and Yad Malachi, for whom it is simple, and for them it would be a blessing in vain to sanctify again; and also all the great ones mentioned who copied Magen Avraham's words also appear they hold like Shakhna when there was no delay, as written above, and especially since the essence of Kiddush in a place of a meal is only rabbinic, and certainly in hardship one may rely on them not to return and sanctify again; but l'chatchila one should certainly be careful in this.
