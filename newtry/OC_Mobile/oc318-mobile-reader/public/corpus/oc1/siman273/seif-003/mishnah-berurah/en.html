(11) Even the obligation of Kiddush, etc. This teaches that Kiddush not in the meal-place fails entirely, not merely to permit eating elsewhere; even if he will not eat afterward at all, he has not fulfilled Kiddush.
(12) Immediately. He should not interrupt even briefly. Knesses HaGedolah says that if some reason made him rise, go outside, and return, no repetition is needed because the meal ultimately occurred at the Kiddush-place. Some disagree, so initially take great care. After the fact (especially if he needed the privy) one should certainly not require a new Kiddush, since this resembles meal needs.
(13) Or that he should. The word 'or' shows the first case is unstated intent, not explicit intent to eat immediately; immediate eating nevertheless suffices. It then teaches that explicit intent at Kiddush suffices even if circumstances delay eating. If he also left the place meanwhile, several later authorities require repeating Kiddush. Likewise, in a large hall, moving corner to corner; or reciting in one room intending immediate eating in another under the same roof, or from ground floor to upper floor (even if walking itself caused delay) requires no repetition.
(14) But otherwise, etc. If he did not intend immediate eating but intended an intervening activity, then interruption (even if ultimately forced) means he did not fulfill the obligation despite later eating at the Kiddush-place. An interruption for meal needs does not appear to count.
(15) There immediately. Initially, at Kiddush, he intended neither to eat until later nor in this house but another. If he then changed his mind and ate immediately at the Kiddush-place, no repetition is needed.
