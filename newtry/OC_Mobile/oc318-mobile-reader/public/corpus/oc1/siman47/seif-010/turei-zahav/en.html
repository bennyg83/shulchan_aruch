Since his intent, etc. — apparently this means that if he has no intent to return, such as when he has a fixed time and the rest of the day goes to his affairs, and it happened to him on a day when he cannot attend to his affairs and therefore returns to his study — he must bless again, as if he changed his mind. But the truth is not so, for we already explained above the reason that interruption causes no loss: because the obligation of study always rests upon him, only that distraction exempts him, and whenever he is not distracted the obligation applies to him; therefore he need not return and bless. And the matter does not depend on intent; moreover, we say that presumably his intent is that if it happens that he can study, even at an hour when he is not accustomed to study, he will study. And so teaches the wording of Shulchan Aruch, who says "since his intent is to return" — he did not say "if his intent is to return," but we say presumably his intent is so.
