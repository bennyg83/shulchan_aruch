Since he intends, etc. At first glance, this implies that one who does not intend to return to learning, such as one with a fixed study period who spends the rest of the day in business, would need a new blessing if circumstances unexpectedly allow him to return to study. This is not correct. As explained above, an interruption does not invalidate the blessing because the obligation to learn constantly rests upon him, and only practical burdens temporarily exempt him. Whenever he is no longer occupied, the obligation returns. He therefore need not recite another blessing, and the matter does not depend upon his actual intention. Moreover, we presume that if an opportunity to study arises even during a time when he does not ordinarily learn, he intends to take advantage of it. This is also indicated by Shulchan Aruch's wording: “Since he intends to return,” not, “If he intends to return.” The presumption is that this is his intention.
