(10) [Seif 5] The blessing on the Torah: "who chose us from all nations and gave us His Torah"—on words of Torah. The Ashkenazi version: "to engage in words of Torah." Tur and Bach. Magen Avraham, note 3, and Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Radak that it is more correct to say "on words of Torah," because it includes both study and performance of mitzvos—see there. And Beit David, Magen Avraham there. And so Peri Chadash, letter 5. And see there what he wrote on the words of the Bach and what Peri Megadim resolved in Eshel Avraham, letter 3; and further see there in Peri Chadash that the main version is "on words of Torah," and they testified about Maharai that he would say thus, end quote—and he is the Ari, may his memory be blessed, as Yafeh Lelev wrote, letter 4, for so testified Maharach Vital in Shaar HaKavanos in the text of prayer, folio 50, amud dalet, and thus: I also saw him answer Amen in the middle of two blessings of "who sanctified us" on words of Torah and between the blessing and "Ha-arav na, Hashem our God"—apparently they are two blessings, and as Baal HaTurim wrote (it seems it should read: and not as Baal HaTurim wrote—see below letter 13), for "veHa-arav na, Hashem our God" is a second blessing on its own and is not the conclusion of blessing one, end quote. And what the Taz wrote, note 4, and Mahari Luria wrote that one may answer Amen after the blessing to engage in words of Torah, etc.—it does not mean that the Ari would say this version "to engage," the opposite of the testimony of Maharach Vital, but he did not come to say except regarding answering Amen, that the Ari would answer Amen after the blessing "on words of Torah." And the Taz adopted the Ashkenazi version, as Yafeh Lelev wrote there. And so Rabbi Menachem Aharon, may his memory be blessed, 22, amud gimel, that the Ari's version is "on words of Torah"—see there. And they brought the words of Menachem in Hagahos Tashbetz, letter 3.
