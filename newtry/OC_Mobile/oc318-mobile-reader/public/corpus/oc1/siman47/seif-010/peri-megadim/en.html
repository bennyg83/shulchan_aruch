Women. See Taz. The phrase LaAsok BeDivrei Torah may imply Oral Torah, yet the text of the blessing was not differentiated. Furthermore, women are obligated to study the laws that apply to them. See Beit Yosef, Nachalas Tzvi, and Magen Avraham, letter 14.
See below, siman 139, seif 9. One who was called to the Torah before reciting Birchas HaTorah fulfills the blessing Asher Bachar Banu. Bach, Maharshal, and other later authorities write that he should still say LaAsok BeDivrei Torah and VeHaarev Na. Levush there appears to say that he is fully exempt from Birchas HaTorah. One might explain Levush according to his view here that LaAsok and VeHaarev relate to yesterday's Torah, while Asher Bachar Banu relates to today's Torah. He therefore means that one is exempt from today's Birchas HaTorah. This requires study. If so, one should similarly say that Ahavah Rabbah exempts only today's Asher Bachar Banu, while LaAsok and VeHaarev relating to yesterday would still be required. Perhaps this is indeed true. We, however, rule that all the blessings relate to today's obligation, because blessings are not recited after mitzvos, as explained in Niddah 51b. In any event, if he has time before public Torah reading, he should first recite LaAsok and VeHaarev, and afterward recite Asher Bachar Banu over the Torah in the order instituted by the Sages. Why do we ordinarily recite LaAsok BeDivrei Torah rather than Asher Bachar Banu, which was also instituted for public Torah reading? The principal Torah study is Oral Torah, where one develops new Torah insights and labors in Torah. See Bach. This is why LaAsok is central.
One further point. Know that some hold Birchas HaTorah is D'Oraisa and others hold it is D'Rabbanan. I initially thought that the blessing during public Torah reading is D'Rabbanan according to everyone, instituted for the honor of the congregation, because the reader already recited Birchas HaTorah in the morning. Eliyah Rabbah, siman 139, end of letter 7, somewhat implies that even the public Torah blessing may contain one D'Oraisa blessing. I do not know why. Even if that were so, one who hears Asher Bachar Banu from the person called to the Torah fulfills his own obligation in Asher Bachar Banu, because one who already fulfilled a blessing can discharge another. A minor, however, cannot discharge an adult. In letter 1 we wrote that a minor may sometimes discharge an adult in this public setting, because the Torah-level Birchas HaTorah applies only in the morning. Public Torah reading and its blessing were instituted by Moshe Rabbeinu and Ezra for the honor of the congregation and are D'Rabbanan. I later saw in Peri Chadash, siman 139, seif 8, that the blessing over public Torah reading is D'Rabbanan according to everyone, unlike Bach. This accords with my view.
