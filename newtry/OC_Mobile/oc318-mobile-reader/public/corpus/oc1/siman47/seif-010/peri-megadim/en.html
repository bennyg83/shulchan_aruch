Women—see Taz: "to engage" implies Torah she-be'al peh—they did not differentiate in the text of the blessing. And furthermore they are obligated to learn laws relevant to them—in Yevamos. And see Magen Avraham letter 14.

See below 139, seif 9: one whom they called to the Torah before "who chose us"—he is exempt from "who chose us," but "to engage" and veHa'arev na he says—as the Bach and Maharshal and other later authorities wrote. But in Levush it implies there that he is exempt from Birchot HaTorah entirely. And to say according to my view here—the blessing "to engage" and veHa'arev na is for yesterday's, and "who chose" is for today's—and properly he said from today's Birchot HaTorah. And nevertheless requires investigation—for if so, in Ahavah Rabbah too we should say he is exempt from today's "who chose us," but "to engage" and veHa'arev na for yesterday's—here too he blesses. However we hold that all of them are for today, for one does not bless on mitzvos after them, as explained in Niddah 51b, and there is no need to elaborate. And nevertheless if he has leisure he should say "to engage" and veHa'arev na, and afterward bless "who chose us" on the Torah, in the order of blessings the Sages enacted. And that which we bless "to engage in words of Torah" and not "who chose us"—it was also enacted for reading the Torah—one could say because the essence is that we study Torah she-be'al peh and innovate innovations of Torah and labor in Torah, Ab'ach—and there is no need to elaborate.

Furthermore, momentarily I will speak: know that Birchot HaTorah—some say de'Oraita and some say derabbanan. I would have thought to say Beir Heitev in public—to all it is derabbanan out of respect for the congregation, since he already blessed at dawn on words of Torah; except in Eliyah Rabbah 139 end of letter 7 it somewhat implies that Birchot HaTorah in public too are one blessing de'Oraita—and I do not know why. And nevertheless if it were so: if he heard "who chose us" from one who blessed on the Torah, he fulfills "who chose us"—all the blessings—even though he fulfilled through discharge. But minor and adult—a minor does not discharge the adult; and in letter 1 we wrote that a minor also discharges in such a case—for necessarily they say Beir Heitev words of Torah in the morning alone; unlike regarding its reading in public—Moshe our teacher and Ezra enacted the reading and blessing out of respect for the congregation, and it is derabbanan. Again I saw in Pri Chadash 139, seif 8, Beir Heitev in public to all derabbanan, unlike Bach—see there—and this is as I said.
