(22) "Since he intends to return"—it implies that one whose way is not to study, but who changed his mind to study, must bless again; so too Magen Avraham. And Taz wrote the opposite—for since the Shulchan Aruch did not write "if he intends to return," it implies that he holds that we presume one's intent: if an opportunity arises to study, even at a time when he is not accustomed to study, he will study; and one may be lenient in a doubt regarding blessings [Eliyah Rabbah; and Peri Megadim left it unresolved; and the Gra in his commentary—although he explains the Shulchan Aruch like Magen Avraham—nevertheless for practical law he agrees at the end of seif 8 like Taz, and that is like the first explanation in the Rosh, who holds that even for one not accustomed to study one need not bless again].

(23) "For sleep"—meaning a casual nap, namely that he dozed while seated, leaning on his elbows—and even at night.

(24) "And the bathhouse and outhouse"—for there too he has not yet distracted his mind from studying afterward; and furthermore, there too one must be careful in their laws, such as uncovering a handbreadth by a handbreadth in the outhouse, and the like in the bathhouse regarding greeting with peace—therefore he has not yet distracted his mind from the Torah. And see the Gra's commentary.
