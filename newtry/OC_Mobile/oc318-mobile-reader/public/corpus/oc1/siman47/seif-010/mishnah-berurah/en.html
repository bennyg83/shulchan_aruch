(22) Since his intention. This implies that a person who does not ordinarily study, but then decides to study, must repeat the blessing. Magen Avraham writes likewise. Taz rules the opposite. Since Shulchan Aruch does not say, “If his intention is to return,” but rather, “Since his intention is to return,” it implies that we presume a person's intention includes any opportunity that may arise to study, even during a time when he does not ordinarily learn. Because this is a doubt concerning blessings, one should be lenient. Eliyah Rabbah. Peri Megadim also leaves the matter unresolved. Although Biur HaGra explains Shulchan Aruch like Magen Avraham, he ultimately agrees in practical halachah with Taz, following the first interpretation in Rosh, that even one who does not ordinarily study need not repeat the blessing.
(23) Sleep. Meaning an incidental sleep, such as dozing while seated with his head upon his arms, even at night.
(24) And a bathhouse and lavatory. Even there, he still does not divert his intention from studying afterward. Moreover, he must be mindful there of the laws applying to those locations, such as how much of the body may be uncovered in a lavatory and similar laws concerning a bathhouse, and therefore his attention has not entirely departed from Torah. See Biur HaGra.
