And there is room for doubt. See Taz and what I wrote in Magen Avraham, letter 6. At first I understood Levush in seifim 7 and 8 like Peri Chadash's citation of Raavad and Rashba: Ahavah Rabbah exempts only the study performed immediately, not later study. For this reason Levush omitted the phrase “there is room for doubt,” because it would make no practical difference. I now see that this is not correct. In seif 10 Levush writes the opposite: if one began learning, interrupted, and returned, he need not recite a new blessing. He says that this applies even where Ahavah Rabbah was the blessing. One could distinguish that ordinary Birchas HaTorah continues even if he had no intention at all to return and later changed his mind, whereas Ahavah Rabbah continues only if he intended to return. This would explain the wording in seifim 7 and 8. Nevertheless, Levush should not have omitted the phrase “there is room for doubt,” because the practical difference is considerable, as explained. See what I wrote in Magen Avraham, letter 6, and with Hashem's help in letter 8.
