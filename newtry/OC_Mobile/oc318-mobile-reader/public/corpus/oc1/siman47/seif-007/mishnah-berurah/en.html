(13) Ahavas Olam — he used Ahavas Olam per what he ruled later in siman 59, seif 61; but for Rama there they say Ahavah Rabbah in Shacharis. And it is obvious that the same applies to Ahavas Olam in Ma'ariv — it exempts, for one follows the reason. A practical difference: if he slept fixed sleep on his bed beforehand — per the Mechaber later in seif 11, fixed sleep is an interruption; and even per the custom we practice like Yesh Omrim there to be lenient — nevertheless it is better to intend in Ahavas Olam to exempt oneself to satisfy the majority of poskim who are strict in this, as explained later in Mishnah Berurah, see there. And see Biur Halacha for another practical difference.

(14) It exempts — for it contains something of the Blessing of the Torah: "and place in our hearts to study, to teach, to observe, to perform, to fulfill" etc.; and apparently from his silence it implies that even if he did not intend at the time of the blessing to exempt the Blessing of the Torah thereby, it suffices. And see Biur Halacha.

(15) If he studied — for Ahavah Rabbah, since the main formulation was adjusted for Kerias Shema, does not appear to be for the name of the Blessing of the Torah unless he studies immediately; and the Levush wrote that even if he prayed and finished prayer, this is not called an interruption since he studied immediately afterward.

(16) Immediately — even a little for the sake of the mitzvah of study; and then he need not bless again all that day, even if he interrupted, stood, and returned to his study, as will be explained later regarding the Blessing of the Torah.
