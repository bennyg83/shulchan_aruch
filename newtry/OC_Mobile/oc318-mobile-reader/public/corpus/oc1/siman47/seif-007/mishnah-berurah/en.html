(13) Ahavas Olam. The Mechaber uses the phrase Ahavas Olam in accordance with his ruling below in siman 60, seif 1. According to Rama there, one says Ahavah Rabbah at Shacharis. The same law plainly applies to Ahavas Olam at Maariv, which likewise exempts Birchas HaTorah. A practical difference arises where one passed through a fixed sleep upon his bed beforehand. According to the Mechaber below in seif 11, a fixed sleep is an interruption. Even according to our custom to be lenient like the alternative view there, it is still preferable to intend in Ahavas Olam to fulfill Birchas HaTorah, thereby satisfying the majority of poskim who are stringent. See Mishnah Berurah below. See Biur Halachah for another practical difference.
(14) Exempts. It contains the substance of Birchas HaTorah: “Place in our hearts to learn and teach, observe, perform, and fulfill,” etc. At first glance, the Mechaber's unqualified language implies that Ahavas Olam is sufficient even if one did not intend during the blessing to fulfill Birchas HaTorah. See Biur Halachah.
(15) If he studied. Since Ahavah Rabbah was instituted principally for Krias Shema rather than Birchas HaTorah, it appears to serve as Birchas HaTorah only if one studies immediately. Levush writes that even if he prayed and completed the prayer, this is not considered an interruption, provided he studies immediately afterward.
(16) Immediately. Even a small amount of study for the sake of the mitzvah suffices. He then need not recite Birchas HaTorah again for the entire day, even if he interrupts, stops, and later returns to study, in accordance with the rule explained below regarding Birchas HaTorah.
