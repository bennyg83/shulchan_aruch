"The blessing Ahavas Olam," etc. — see Mishnah Berurah; and another point for one not accustomed to study who wants to study immediately after Maariv — he need not bless even per the Mechaber below in seif 10, according to what Magen Avraham wrote there, that he is stringent about interruption for one not accustomed to return to his study.

"It exempts" — see Mishnah Berurah. And from Terumat HaDeshen's words there it seems that if he knows for certain he did not intend, or such as if he remembered he did not say the blessing on the Torah until after he said Ahavah Rabah — Ahavah Rabah does not exempt. But from the Rosh's words there, according to how he explained the Yerushalmi's view that one must repeat on the spot — it seems there is no distinction in this. But see: I later found this in Peri Megadim in siman 52 — if he wishes he may intend explicitly in the blessing Ahavah Rabah not to exempt the blessing on the Torah; in this all agree he does not fulfill even per the view that mitzvos do not require intent, see there. And it is astonishing that Peri Megadim did not mention Terumat HaDeshen's words above at all.

"If he studied immediately" — see Mishnah Berurah in the name of Levush; afterward I found more than this in Or Zarua HaGadol — specifically if he was excessive in his affairs between the blessing Ahavah Rabah and study; however, all matters of prayer, and also going after their books to bring them to the beit midrash, is not an interruption, so far. And it appears specifically if he did not interrupt in speech; but if he interrupted in speech not about the matter, he should return and bless according to Or Zarua as well.
