The blessing Ahavas Olam, etc. See Mishnah Berurah. A further practical difference concerns one who does not ordinarily study and wishes to study immediately after Maariv. He need not recite Birchas HaTorah, even according to the Mechaber below in seif 10, in light of Magen Avraham's ruling there that an interruption is significant for one who does not ordinarily return to his study.
Exempts. See Mishnah Berurah. Rabbeinu Yonah's words there appear to imply that if one knows with certainty that he had no such intention, or remembered only after saying Ahavah Rabbah that he had not recited Birchas HaTorah, then Ahavah Rabbah does not exempt him. According to Rosh, however, based upon his explanation of the Yerushalmi's requirement to study immediately, there appears to be no distinction in this regard. I later found in Peri Megadim, siman 52, that one may expressly intend in Ahavah Rabbah not to exempt Birchas HaTorah. In that case, everyone agrees that he does not fulfill the obligation, even according to the view that mitzvos do not require intention. See there. It is surprising that Peri Megadim did not mention Rabbeinu Yonah's words.
If he studied immediately. See Mishnah Berurah in the name of Levush. I later found an even broader statement in Or Zarua. Only if one became deeply occupied in his affairs between Ahavah Rabbah and study is there an interruption. All matters of prayer, and even going to retrieve one's books and bring them to the beis midrash, are not considered an interruption. It appears that this applies only when he did not interrupt with unrelated speech. If he spoke about an unrelated matter, then even according to Or Zarua he must recite Birchas HaTorah again.
