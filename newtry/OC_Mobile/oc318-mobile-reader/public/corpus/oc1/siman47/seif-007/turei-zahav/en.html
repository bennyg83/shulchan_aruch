If he studied — so wrote Tosafos, the Rosh, and Mordechai; and thus in the Yerushalmi: "and he who repeated in place" — meaning immediately he studied in that place; it implies that if he interrupted in the middle after Kriat Shema he must bless birkat haTorah. Rabbi Yehudah ruled that specifically Ahavah Rabbah — for it is not the main blessing for birkat haTorah but was enacted for Kriat Shema; therefore it requires that he repeated in place, and if he does not study immediately it does not exempt. But the blessings "who chose us" and "to engage in Torah," which were enacted as the main [blessings] for birkat haTorah, exempt for the whole day — but when one arises to engage in Torah he must bless. On this we rely, that we do not study immediately after Ahavah Rabbah because we are busy and sometimes go without study until midday or more, etc. And one asked: why from sukkah, that one must bless every time one eats to sit in the sukkah? One could say: I am Torah — one does not despair, for at every hour a person is obligated to study, as it is written "and you shall meditate in it day and night," and it is as if he sits all day and studies; but regarding sukkah there is a fixed time for eating — thus far. It is clear from their words that regarding Beir Heitev one need not study immediately after the blessing; and this is not comparable to other performance of a mitzvah, where one must fulfill the mitzvah immediately after the blessing — for here its time is all day, only that distraction exempts him; and as these poskim wrote regarding interruption in the middle that it does not cause loss, here too literally regarding interruption between the blessing and its beginning — it is one reason; and so Beit Yosef wrote on this. I wonder why the poskim needed to seek permission for us that we do not study immediately after Ahavah Rabbah because there is a distinction between birkat haTorah and Ahavah Rabbah — for even without this distinction we have permission, for we truly study immediately after Beir Heitev when we say Parashat HaTamid, the mishnah of Eizehu Mekoman, and the braita of Rabbi Yishmael's thirteen attributes, which are Torah study. And one could say that Parashat HaTamid and mishnayot of sacrifices are not considered Torah except in place of sacrifice — one could say they are certainly Torah, for the Tur and Shulchan Aruch wrote in siman 50 that this is in place of study; and all the more so the braita of Rabbi Yishmael, which is in place of Talmud, as written below. If so, it is literally like "repeated in place" that we mentioned — that even after Ahavah Rabbah there is no loss if one interrupts afterward, all the more so with birkat haTorah. And this is simple; otherwise it is difficult for those who do not study at all during the day — can one think they bless a blessing in vain, Heaven forbid, in the morning? Rather it is simple that they fulfill through reading Parashat HaTamid, as written; and I can say nothing more on this except that they wrote so for amplification of the matter — that even where one cannot [read] Parashat HaTamid in the morning, there would also be permission even if one interrupts between study and blessing, as above.
