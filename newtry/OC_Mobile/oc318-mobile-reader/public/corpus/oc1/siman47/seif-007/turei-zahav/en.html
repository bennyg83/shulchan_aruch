Since it was not instituted primarily as a blessing over Torah but as a blessing of Krias Shema, it exempts Torah study only if one studies immediately. The blessings Asher Bachar Banu and LaAsok BaTorah were instituted specifically as blessings over Torah and exempt the entire day. The reason ordinary interruptions do not require a new blessing is that a person never abandons his intention toward Torah. Every hour he remains obligated to learn, as the verse states, “You shall contemplate it day and night.” It is therefore as though he studies all day. This differs from sukkah, where eating occurs at fixed times and a new blessing is required for each meal. These words show that after the ordinary blessing over Torah, one need not begin learning immediately. This differs from other mitzvah blessings, where the mitzvah must follow at once. Torah study extends throughout the day, and only practical burdens temporarily exempt him. The same reasoning that prevents an interruption during the day from canceling the blessing also prevents a delay between the blessing and the beginning of study from doing so. I am surprised why the poskim needed to justify our failure to study immediately after Ahavah Rabbah by distinguishing that blessing from the regular Torah blessings. We actually do study immediately after the morning blessings, for we recite the passage of the daily offering, the Mishnah Eizehu Mekoman, and the baraisa of Rabbi Yishmael's thirteen principles. These are Torah study. One cannot say that the passage of the daily offering and the sacrificial Mishnayos count only as substitutes for offerings and not as Torah. Tur and Shulchan Aruch in siman 50 state that these passages are recited in place of study, and the baraisa of Rabbi Yishmael is certainly Gemara. This is exactly immediate study. Even after Ahavah Rabbah, once one studies immediately, a later interruption does not matter. All the more so after the regular blessing over Torah. Otherwise, what of those who do no other learning during the day? Could their morning blessing be in vain, Heaven forbid? Clearly, they fulfill their learning through reciting the passage of the daily offering. The poskim apparently wrote their distinction only for added clarity, to show that even where the passage of the daily offering cannot be recited immediately, the later interruption still does not invalidate the regular blessing over Torah.
