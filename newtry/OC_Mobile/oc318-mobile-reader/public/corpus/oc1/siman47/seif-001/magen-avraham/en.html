The Bach wrote to say "and our offspring and the offspring of our offspring"—for they say: whoever he is, and his son, and his son's son—a Torah scholar—Torah does not cease from his seed forever—end of his words. And it seems to us that "our offspring" means the offspring of our loins, and so too the Targum on Job 21 on "and his offspring"—sons and sons of sons; therefore one should not change the formula. The writer. It appears specifically regarding one who writes books for himself as his manner of study and understands what he writes; but a scribe who copies and does not seek to understand what he writes need not bless, for this is not study. And even regarding one who writes as his manner of study—requires investigation, for at minimum it is contemplation. And in Agudah he concluded and wrote: provided that he not write letters of permission. And so too Teshuvot Tashbetz. And one must say that the matter is: even though he writes verses in letters of permission, he need not bless.
