Bach writes that one should say "and our descendants and the descendants of our descendants," because the Sages say that whenever a person, his son, and his son's son are Torah scholars, Torah will never cease from his offspring. It appears to me, however, that the word "our descendants" already means all who issue from our loins, and the Targum on Job 21 renders "and his descendants" as "sons and sons of sons." Therefore the established text should not be changed. The writer. It appears that this applies specifically to one who writes sacred texts for himself as part of his learning and understands what he writes. A copyist who merely copies and does not seek to understand what he writes need not recite Birchot HaTorah, because this is not Torah study. Even regarding one who writes as part of his study the matter requires consideration, because at minimum writing is comparable to contemplation. Agudah concludes that this applies provided he is not writing ordinary letters, and Tashbetz writes likewise. The meaning must be that even if he happens to write Scriptural verses in ordinary correspondence, he need not recite the blessing.
