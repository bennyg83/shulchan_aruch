Birchas HaTorah. See Taz. Regarding the word “very,” see Bach. Torah scholars are accustomed to studying and rising before morning, and they may forget Birchas HaTorah. Therefore, exceptional care is required, especially since the punishment is severe: the land was lost, and one does not merit a son who is a Torah scholar. This is alluded to by the verse, “They shall not depart from your mouth or from the mouth of your descendants,” meaning Torah learned for its own sake, and likewise by “our offspring,” etc. From Bach's explanation I understood the teaching concerning Eretz Yisrael, which is called “the land of the deer.” Just as a deer's hide does not contain its flesh once removed, so too Eretz Yisrael expands beyond its apparent dimensions. At present, the land can be traversed in one day and measures six days in length and width, as Rama writes concerning the Prophets. In truth, Eretz Yisrael is physically small. When holiness rests upon it, however, it expands, as the verse says, “Like a city joined together,” and as demonstrated by the tribes ascending there and by the people standing crowded but bowing with ample space. So long as they studied Torah for its own sake, and Torah is composed of the Names of the Holy One, blessed is He, the Shechinah rested in Eretz Yisrael and it expanded. Now that Torah is studied not for its own sake but for material purposes, the spiritual presence has departed, and only the limited physical measure remains. This is literally what is meant by “the land was lost.”
Peri Chadash writes that Birchas HaTorah is D'Oraisa, as appears at the beginning of the chapter Three Who Ate and from the opening chapters Keitzad Mevarchin and Mi SheMeiso. A practical difference is that when one is uncertain, he must repeat all three blessings. See there. We already discussed this in the general introduction. Another practical difference is that a minor who has reached the age of education cannot discharge an adult. A woman, however, can discharge a man. See siman 186, seif 2, and Magen Avraham, seif katan 3. The blessing recited during public Torah reading, however, was instituted by Moshe Rabbeinu and Ezra. On Simchas Torah, when many people are called, a minor may recite the blessing and discharge an adult.
