1) [Se'if 1] One must be exceedingly careful with Birchos HaTorah. Nedarim 81a asks: Why is it uncommon for talmidei chachamim to have sons who are talmidei chachamim? Ravina said: Because they do not first recite a berachah over the Torah, etc. The commentator there explains that they do not recite a berachah when they rise early to study Torah. Because talmidei chachamim are diligent in Torah study and accustomed to it, they are not careful to recite the berachah when they begin, and the blessing 'May we and our descendants be students of Torah' is not fulfilled. See Tur and Beis Yosef. Levush writes: A person must be exceedingly careful with this berachah, thereby demonstrating that Torah is precious in his eyes and that he derives pleasure from it, just as he recites a berachah over all his pleasures. If he does not recite a berachah over Torah, he does not merit a son who is a talmid chacham. This punishment is measure for measure: since he does not bless over it, he demonstrates that he does not study it for its own sake, does not rejoice in it, and derives no pleasure from it, but regards it as an ordinary trade. He therefore does not merit the continuing chain promised to one who studies it for its own sake, as it is written, 'They shall not depart from your mouth,' etc. The principal care required is to understand the meaning of the words and recite the berachah slowly, word by word, omitting no word or letter, as stated above in siman 5:2. One must also say it with intense desire and take to heart that he is blessing over the Torah, which is our life and the length of our days in this world and in the World to Come.
2) There: Birchos HaTorah require, etc. There is a dispute whether Birchos HaTorah are a Torah obligation or a rabbinic obligation. The practical difference is that, if they are a Torah obligation, one who is uncertain whether he recited them must recite them; if they are rabbinic, he need not do so, as Maran writes in siman 209:3; see there. The poskim dispute this matter. Shaagas Aryeh 24 holds that the obligation is from the Torah, and one who is uncertain whether he recited the berachah must recite it; see there. Mor U'Ketziah rules likewise, and Pri Chadash agrees in his comments to this se'if. Yad Aharon cites this in Hagahos HaTur. This is also the view of Rabbi Aharon HaLevi in Zekan Aharon 60; see there. Pnei Moshe, Responsa 1:1, however, writes that Rambam and Tur state the law without saying it is a Torah obligation, implying that they consider it rabbinic; see there. This is also implied by Maran below, siman 209:3: 'Regarding all berachos, if one is uncertain whether he recited the berachah, he does not recite it, etc., except Birkas HaMazon, which is from the Torah.' By singling out Birkas HaMazon, he implies that Birchos HaTorah are included among the rabbinic berachos. This is also implied by Agur, cited by Beis Yosef in this siman. Levush 1 writes that the Sages attached it to a verse, and in se'if 12 that omission of the berachah does not prevent fulfillment, implying that he considers it rabbinic. Olas Tamid likewise writes in se'if 9 that if one interrupted between the berachah and learning, he does not repeat the berachah because berachos are rabbinic. Thus, he states explicitly that Birchos HaTorah are rabbinic. Mahari Ayash agrees in Mateh Yehudah 1, writing that one who is uncertain whether he recited Birchos HaTorah should not recite them; see there and his comments on Pri Chadash. See also Chida, Birkei Yosef 8, who rejects Pri Chadash's proof, and Machazik Berachah 1-2, where he agrees that one should not recite the berachah in a case of doubt, because the great principle is that doubt concerning berachos is resolved leniently. The glossator to Shalmei Tzibbur 56a cites him. Kesher Gudal 5:25 and Siddur Beis Yaakov, laws of Birchos HaShachar 22, rule likewise. Maamar Mordechai 1 also agrees, but writes that one who is uncertain should intend that Ahavas Olam exempt the obligation; see there. This is also the view of Kisei Eliyahu and Yafeh LaLev 1, who write that this is unlike Be'er Heitev in the names of Pri Chadash and Yad Aharon; see there. Nevertheless, the proper course for one who is uncertain whether he recited Birchos HaTorah is to endeavor to hear the berachah from someone who has not yet fulfilled his obligation. He should intend to fulfill his obligation, and tell the person reciting it to intend to discharge it for him, as is known. Alternatively, he may recite it without G-d's Name and kingship; and when he reaches Ahavas Olam, he should intend that it also discharge Birchos HaTorah, thereby satisfying the stringent opinions. See what was stated above in siman 46:52.
2) Know that our statement that one who is uncertain whether he recited Birchos HaTorah does not repeat them applies whether he is uncertain about all of them or only some of them; he does not recite whichever berachah is in doubt. Pnei Moshe writes, as Pri Chadash cites there, that even according to those who consider Birchos HaTorah a Torah obligation, only one berachah is required by the Torah. Shaagas Aryeh 25 likewise writes that one berachah (whichever it may be) is a Torah obligation, though he inclines toward reciting 'Who chose us'; see there. Accordingly, for us, who rule in accordance with those who consider the obligation rabbinic because doubt concerning berachos is resolved leniently, he does not repeat any berachah about which he is uncertain, whether all the berachos or only some. Pri Chadash also holds that the berachah recited over the public Torah reading is rabbinic, as he writes in siman 139:8; see there. Birkei Yosef 8 in this siman writes likewise.
