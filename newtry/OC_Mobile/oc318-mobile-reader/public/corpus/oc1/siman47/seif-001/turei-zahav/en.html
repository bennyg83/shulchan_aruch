Birkat haTorah — one must be careful, etc. The reason in Beit Yosef in the name of the Ran, because of Rabbi Yehudah: through the blessing it is clarified that one engages in Torah for its sake — see there. With good reason on the statement "Who is the wise man," etc.; and incidentally we will mention our share, there's help — thus in the Gemara: Rabbi Yehudah said in the name of Rav, "Who is the wise man who will understand this, and to whom the mouth of the Omnipresent has spoken that he may declare it — why was the land lost?" This matter was asked of the sages and prophets and the members of the Great Assembly, and they did not explain it until the Holy One, blessed be He, explained it Himself, as it is said: "And the Omnipresent said: Because they forsook My Torah that I set before them, and they did not heed My voice and did not walk in it" — "and they did not heed My voice" means "and they did not walk in it." Rav said: because they did not bless over the Torah first. One could say much on this statement; for love of brevity we did not record it. It appears that He, blessed be He, asked here two questions and hinted in the question itself at the answer belonging to the question, by way of a hint; and thereby He mentioned that nevertheless they did not know how to state the answer — and therefore He said, "Who is the wise man who will understand this," namely, on the core of the question. Second: "and to whom the mouth of the Omnipresent has spoken" — meaning that the mouth of the Omnipresent spoke the answer, and nevertheless it appears whether he will declare it. Behold, the two questions were: (1) "Why was the land lost" — meaning specifically this land now; had they not already transgressed idolatry, forbidden relations, and bloodshed — what changed now? (2) That Torah would leave Israel and be lost from them — and therefore He said, "It is parched like a desert, without a passerby," that Torah was emptied from Israel like a desert emptied of every passerby. Behold, the answer is hinted in the difficulty, as stated afterward, except they did not know how to interpret the matter until the Holy One, blessed be He, explained it Himself. What He said first — namely, on why it was not destroyed before, because Torah was protecting them, as stated in Sotah: "Torah protects and saves from sin" — but after they forsook My Torah they were punished for what they already did not heed My voice in the transgression of idolatry, forbidden relations, and bloodshed that they committed; and this is "for their forsaking My Torah and not heeding My voice." And on the second — namely, the removal of Torah, because they did not walk in it: Torah is sustained only in one who kills himself over it, namely, who engages in pilpul and give-and-take of Torah, as it is written "in My statutes you shall walk" — on condition that you toil in Torah; unlike those who study words of Torah from pleasure and do not toil in it — Torah is not sustained with them; therefore He said "and they did not walk in it," meaning they did not engage in the laws of Torah through give-and-take of pilpul that relates to "walking," as written. And this is "they did not bless over the Torah first," for the blessing is to engage in words of Torah specifically through toil — and this answer is hinted in what the Holy One, blessed be He, said in the question: "without a passerby" — this "without a passerby" is the answer to the difficulty "it is parched like a desert," why Torah was parched like a desert — because it was "without a passerby," meaning they did not engage in it through negotiation, as written. It is found that in the difficulty the answer was mentioned and no one knew it until the Holy One, blessed be He, made an exposition on His own words; and therefore the author of the statement said "and they did not explain it" — not in language and not in exposition, but "they did not explain it" — no one explained the things that came from Him, blessed be He. This is the correct way in this statement, and it follows that the main excellence of those who engage in Torah is specifically through toil and exertion — and to this they intended in the blessing to engage in words of Torah. I brought these matters here even though they are not the topic of this work; nevertheless it seems to me a correct explanation, close to the plain sense of the statement that the Tur brought.
