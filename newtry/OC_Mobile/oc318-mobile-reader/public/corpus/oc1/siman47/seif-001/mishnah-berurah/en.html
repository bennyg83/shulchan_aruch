(1) The Blessing of the Torah—see Sha'agas Aryeh, siman 24, who concludes that the Blessing of the Torah is from the Torah; therefore if one is unsure whether he blessed the Blessing of the Torah he should bless again. Nevertheless, since he is in doubt he should not bless except "who chose us," which is the choicest of the blessings, as stated in the Gemara; and see Sha'arei Teshuvah, who cites in the name of some later authorities that in doubt one should not bless again—and it is truly very difficult to rely on them, since many early authorities [namely the Ramban, Chinuch, and Rashba in chapter 3 of Berachos] hold that the Blessing of the Torah is from the Torah. And it is known that the punishment for one who does not bless on the Torah is very great. However, if he remembered this after prayer, having already blessed Ahavah Rabbah, one may be lenient even if he did not study immediately after prayer [Peri Megadim in the introduction]. And if he can ask another to include him in the Blessing of the Torah, or intend ab initio in Ahavah Rabbah to exempt himself and study immediately after prayer—that is best. And in particular, according to what I later found in Maamar Mordechai, citing the Levush and Eliyah Rabbah, that it is rabbinic—certainly one must be careful ab initio to do so. And the Blessing of the Torah in public is, according to all, rabbinic, for the honor of the congregation, since he already blessed at dawn [later authorities].

(2) "Very much"—not to study until he blesses, and to bless it with great joy. For we find that they asked the Sages, "For what did the land perish?" and God said, "For their abandoning My Torah"; and they asked the prophets, "For what did the land perish?"—Israel were engaged in Torah! And we find that whenever they were engaged in Torah the Holy One, blessed be He, forgave their sins; therefore they did not know why it perished. But the Holy One, blessed be He, who examines hearts, knew that although they were engaged in Torah they were not engaged for the sake of Torah study but as one learns other wisdoms; therefore they did not bless the Blessing of the Torah, for the Torah was not precious in their eyes—and therefore it did not protect them. Therefore one must be very careful and give thanks for choosing us and giving us the vessel of His desire. They also said that one will not, Heaven forbid, merit a Torah scholar son if he is not careful with the Blessing of the Torah.
