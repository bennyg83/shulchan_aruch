(1) Birchas HaTorah. See Shaagas Aryeh, siman 24, who concludes that Birchas HaTorah is D'Oraisa. Therefore, if one is uncertain whether he recited Birchas HaTorah, he must recite it again. Nevertheless, because the matter is doubtful, he should recite only the blessing Asher Bachar Banu, which is the superior of the blessings, as stated in the Gemara. Shaarei Teshuvah cites some later authorities who hold that one should not repeat the blessing in a case of doubt. In truth, it is very difficult to rely upon them, since many Rishonim, including Ramban, Sefer HaChinuch, and Rashba in chapter Three Who Ate, hold that Birchas HaTorah is D'Oraisa, and it is known that the punishment of one who does not bless over Torah is very severe. If he remembers only after prayer, when he has already recited Ahavah Rabbah, there may be room to be lenient even if he did not study immediately after prayer. Peri Megadim, in his introduction. If possible, it is best for him to ask another person to discharge his obligation in Birchas HaTorah, or to intend initially in Ahavah Rabbah to fulfill the obligation and then study immediately after prayer. This is especially proper in light of what I later found in Maamar Mordechai, citing Levush and Olas Tamid, that Birchas HaTorah is D'Rabbanan. The blessing over public Torah reading is D'Rabbanan according to everyone, instituted for the honor of the congregation, since one already recited Birchas HaTorah in the morning. Later authorities.
(2) Very. One must be careful not to study before reciting the blessing, and should recite it with great joy. The Sages explain the verse, “Why was the land lost? Hashem said: Because they abandoned My Torah.” This matter was asked of the prophets because Israel had in fact been engaged in Torah study. So long as they studied Torah, the Holy One, blessed is He, overlooked their sins, and therefore no one understood why the land was lost. The Holy One, blessed is He, Who examines the heart, knew that although they studied Torah, they did not study for the sake of Torah itself, but as one studies other disciplines. Therefore, they did not recite Birchas HaTorah, because Torah was not precious in their eyes, and it did not protect them. One must therefore be extremely careful and give thanks that Hashem chose us and gave us His cherished instrument. The Sages also say that one who is not careful with Birchas HaTorah does not merit, Heaven forbid, to have a son who is a Torah scholar.
