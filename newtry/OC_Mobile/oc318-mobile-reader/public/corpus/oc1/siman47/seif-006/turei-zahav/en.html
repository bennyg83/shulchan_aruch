And veHa'arev na with a vav — for he holds that every short formula followed by a blessing is not like other blessings adjacent to their companion that must open with "Blessed," but certainly it is one blessing with what precedes it. And Mahariv Luria wrote that one could answer Amen after the blessing to engage in words of Torah, before veHa'arev na.

But it is better, etc. — for thereby we fulfill all views. But if you say it without a vav, veHa'arev na is a blessing by itself and constitutes an interruption for the view that holds it is one blessing. Rashba wrote: the reason we do not bless on Torah after it is because it is a mitzvah, and one does not bless on mitzvos after them — as written in chapter Ba, siman 46; and although in public they bless after them, there it is an enactment of Moshe and Ezra, and on enactments one blesses, as we find in Hallel and Megillah — thus far. And that which we say the blessing "who chose us" opens with "Blessed" — Rabbi Yehudah, because the custom is to say it by itself when blessing on Torah in public. In Rif's wording the blessing appears: "We are all knowers of Your name and students of Your Torah for its sake"; and in the Gemara and Asheri and Tur it is not — and it appears that nevertheless one should intend when saying veHa'arev na that Torah be sweet to us, that we engage in it for its sake and not for another intent, Heaven forbid. And the Tur wrote that one should intend in his blessing on standing at Mount Sinai, who chose us from all nations and brought us near before the Holy One, blessed be He, and made us hear His words from the fire and gave us His holy Torah, the instrument of His delight with which He would occupy Himself every day — thus far. And it seems to me further that they enacted to conclude "who gives the Torah," which has the sense of present tense and not "gave" in past tense; rather the intent is that He, blessed be He, always gives us His Torah every day — namely, that we engage in it and He, blessed be He, provides us new explanations in it, as it is written "on her paths they are renewed at all times" — "what is this child," etc.; and in siman 110 I wrote one formula that one should pray before study.
