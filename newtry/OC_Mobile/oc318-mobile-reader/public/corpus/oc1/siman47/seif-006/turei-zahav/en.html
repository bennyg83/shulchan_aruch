VeHaarev Na with a vav. This view holds that every short blessing followed by another blessing is unlike an ordinary adjacent blessing and should begin with Baruch. Since Haarev does not begin with Baruch, it must be one blessing together with the preceding formula. Mahari Luria, however, writes that one should answer amen after LaAsok BeDivrei Torah before VeHaarev.
But it is better, etc. In this way one satisfies all opinions. If one says Haarev Na without a vav, it appears to be a separate blessing and creates an interruption according to the view that it is one blessing. Rashba writes that no blessing is recited after Torah study because Torah study is a mitzvah, and no blessing is recited after mitzvos. Although the congregation recites a blessing after public Torah reading, that is an enactment of Moshe and Ezra, and blessings are recited over enactments, as with Hallel and Megillah. Ri explains that Asher Bachar Banu begins with Baruch because it is often recited independently when blessing over public Torah reading. Rif's text includes, “All of us who know Your Name and study Your Torah for its own sake.” This does not appear in the Gemara, Rosh, or Tur. Nevertheless, when saying Haarev Na one should intend that Torah be sweet to us so that we engage in it for its own sake and not for any ulterior purpose, Heaven forbid. Tur writes that in this blessing one should direct his mind toward the revelation at Sinai: Hashem chose us from all nations, drew us near before Mount Sinai, caused us to hear His words from the fire, and gave us His holy Torah, His cherished instrument with which He delights every day. The concluding formula is “Who gives the Torah,” in the present tense, rather than “Who gave.” Hashem continually gives us His Torah each day as we engage in it and grants us new explanations. This is like the teaching on “May her breasts satisfy you at all times,” comparing Torah to an infant who constantly finds new nourishment.
