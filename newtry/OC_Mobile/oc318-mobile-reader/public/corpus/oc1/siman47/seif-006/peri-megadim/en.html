If he studied — see Taz: he agreed with Beit Yosef and Shulchan Aruch, but the Bach's view is not so. In brief: the Yerushalmi says that "repeated in place" — if there is a distinction between Eliyah Rabbah and Beir Heitev, Tosafos in Eruvin say that Eliyah Rabbah says so; and if we say there is no distinction, one can give four resolutions: (1) "repeated" comes to exclude Kriat Shema — no. (2) "in place" — and prayer — there was no interruption, as the Levush wrote. (3) For Eliyah Rabbah, only that study which was repeated in place, but study afterward — no (and as Pri Chadash wrote in the name of the Ra'avad and Chokhmat Shlomo, and apparently so is the Levush's view, as Magen Avraham wrote in letter 6 — but in letter 7 I will explain that this is not the Levush's view), and Beir Heitev exempts for the whole day. The fourth is the Bach's view, and Pri Chadash agreed with him that it is certainly forbidden to interrupt, as with other mitzvos, and if one interrupted one may return and bless — except for Eliyah Rabbah: "repeated in place" reveals his intent, that Eliyah Rabbah's intent was also on the study; if he did not repeat in place, one could say Eliyah Rabbah's intent was only for Kriat Shema; but Beir Heitev, whose main point is Torah — even if he did not repeat in place but immediately said Parashat HaTamid for words of Torah, for his intent was only for Parashat HaTamid and not for the whole day (and for Rashi, Parashat HaTamid counts as communal reading in Yevamos) — nevertheless it exempts for the whole day. Accordingly, our French rabbis who also say "These are the matters," the mishnah of Eizehu Mekoman, and the braita of Rabbi Yishmael — one could say all of this is because they hold that "repeated in place" differs between Eliyah Rabbah and Beir Heitev, and this is our custom — understand.
