(Note 6) If he studied—see siman 46—came to resolve Rabbi Yisrael's difficulty: why is there doubt whether it suffices to read Kriat Shema adjacent to it, as is written in the section thereafter? One must therefore say that Kriat Shema is not considered study for this matter, and its law here is like prayer and supplications—for otherwise, why did they stipulate in the Yerushalmi that Ahavah Rabbah exempts only when he studied immediately, as is explained here in Shulchan Aruch? Learn from this that each time one must read Kriat Shema after the blessing of Ahavah Rabbah—and see what he wrote. And to this Magen Avraham alludes—see ahead siman 60, where he wrote there: if one read Kriat Shema without blessings he must afterward say the blessings; and from the law one need not read Kriat Shema again—see there. And therefore you find such a case: that he said afterward Ahavah Rabbah and did not then read Kriat Shema—such is what the Yerushalmi said. And specifically when he studied immediately; but always one could say that Kriat Shema is considered words of Torah, and then it exempts study for the whole day—that is, there is practical difference brought in Rabbi Yisrael regarding the condition "if he studied"—that in the Yerushalmi it means it exempts only what he studies immediately, not what he studied afterward if he interrupted between the first study and second study; we learn from this that it is not so. And the law of Ahavah Rabbah if he studied immediately is exactly like the law of Birchot HaTorah.
