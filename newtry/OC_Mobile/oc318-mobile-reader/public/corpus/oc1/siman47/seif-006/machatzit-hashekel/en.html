(Seif katan 6) If he studied, see siman 60. Magen Avraham comes to resolve Beit Yosef's question: what doubt is there whether reciting Krias Shema immediately afterward is sufficient, as stated in the next seif? One must apparently say that Krias Shema does not count as Torah study for this purpose, but has the status of prayer and supplication. Otherwise, why does the Yerushalmi require one to study immediately after Ahavah Rabbah? One always recites Krias Shema after Ahavah Rabbah. Magen Avraham alludes to what he wrote in siman 60: one who recited Krias Shema without its blessings must afterward recite the blessings, but by strict law need not repeat Krias Shema. See there. Thus, one can have a case in which he afterward recites Ahavah Rabbah without then reciting Krias Shema. The Yerushalmi says that in such a case he must study immediately. It therefore remains possible that Krias Shema itself counts as Torah study and, when recited then, exempts the day's later study. The explanation cited by Beit Yosef, that “if he studied” means Ahavah Rabbah exempts only the study performed immediately and not later study after an interruption, is rejected. Rather, once he studied immediately, Ahavah Rabbah has the same law as the regular blessing over Torah.
