(11) With a vav. This is one blessing. If VeHaarev were a separate blessing, it would begin with Baruch. Although it is adjacent to another blessing, since these are short blessings it should still begin with Baruch, like the blessings of Havdalah, which begin with Baruch even though they are adjacent. The opposing view holds that the blessings of Havdalah are different because they are not inherently adjacent. One may recite each independently, such as the blessing over fire or spices.
(12) It is better, etc. In this manner one satisfies all views. If one says Haarev Na without a vav, it appears to be a separate blessing and creates an interruption according to the view that it is one blessing. The present custom is therefore to say VeHaarev with a vav. Regarding answering amen after LaAsok BeDivrei Torah, the later authorities disagree. Most incline that one should not answer amen. It is therefore proper for the person reciting this blessing to say it quietly.
