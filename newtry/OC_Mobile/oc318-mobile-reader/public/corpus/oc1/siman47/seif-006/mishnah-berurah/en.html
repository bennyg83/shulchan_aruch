(11) The vav — for it is one blessing; for if it were a blessing on its own it would open with Baruch, and even though it is adjacent to its fellow, nevertheless since they are short blessings it should open with Baruch — as with Havdalah blessings, which open with Baruch although they are adjacent, since they are short; and Yesh Omrim hold that Havdalah blessings are different, for they are not adjacent, since one may say each on its own over the candle and spices.

(12) "Better" etc. — for thereby we satisfy all views; but if you recite without the vav, "Ha'arev na" is a separate blessing and constitutes an interruption for the opinion that holds it is one blessing; and it is customary today to say "Veha'arev" with a vav. Regarding answering Amen after "to engage in divrei Torah," there are views among the later authorities; and most later authorities lean not to answer Amen, and it is proper for the one blessing to say this blessing quietly.
