Levush writes that the two morning blessings are arranged so that one blessing relates to the previous day's Torah and one to the present day. See there at length. One should consider the statement in Niddah 51b that blessings are not recited after mitzvos, except according to the custom of the people of the West. One may answer that this blessing is not a blessing after the previous study, but a blessing for the future, asking to continue guarding the mitzvah. See Tosafos there. The general rule is that whenever one remains permitted to continue the mitzvah, no concluding blessing is recited. This resembles tzitzis removed at night and likewise kilayim, where removal is not because continued performance of the mitzvah became prohibited. Rambam, cited by Beit Yosef above in siman 29, implies this. It still requires study what Rashba means when he says that blessings are recited after enactments. If he means Rabbinic mitzvos, why then would one not recite an after-blessing over lulav on the remaining days? It should not be superior to the first day. Chanukah lights likewise show that no after-blessing is recited. Perhaps there is a distinction between Rabbinic mitzvos generally and formal enactments. Regarding Torah study, although one fulfills the obligation through Krias Shema, as explained in Yoreh Deah, siman 246, and recites blessings afterward, this itself was their question: if an after-blessing is appropriate for Rabbinic enactments, why not here? The matter remains unresolved.
