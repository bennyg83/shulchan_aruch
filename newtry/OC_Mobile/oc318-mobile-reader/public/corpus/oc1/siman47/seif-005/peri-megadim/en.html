But — see Taz. What he wrote in the name of the Rashba — see Beit Yosef, that one is obligated to study the daily offering; and the Levush wrote two blessings in the morning, one for yesterday's [study] and one for today's — see there, where he elaborated. One should consider what they said in chapter Ba, siman (Niddah 51b): on mitzvos one does not bless after them, and the sons of the West, etc. (and one could say that they do not bless after them only for the future, to keep, etc.) — see Tosafos there. The principle is: anything that one is not forbidden to do again, one does not bless after — as with tzitzit when one removes it at night; and likewise kilayim, for nevertheless it is not on account of the prohibition of performing the mitzvah that one removes it — as the Rambam wrote; Beit Yosef brought this above in siman 29. Nevertheless one should consider: what is this that he wrote that on enactments one blesses after them — if to say that on rabbinic [mitzvos] one blesses and not on Torah [mitzvos]? And the lulav on the other days — it is not fit to bless, lest it be greater than the first day; nevertheless the Chanukah candle proves that one does not bless after them unless rabbinic mitzvos alone and enactments alone. And on Torah study — even though one fulfills with Kriat Shema, as explained in Yoreh De'ah siman 246 — and one blesses after it? On the contrary, this is their difficulty: on rabbinic [matters] it would be fit to bless, except that an enactment is different; and it still requires investigation.
