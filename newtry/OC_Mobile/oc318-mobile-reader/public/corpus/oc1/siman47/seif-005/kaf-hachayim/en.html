(ה) [Seif 3] One who writes words of Torah, even if he does not read aloud, must bless. See Taz note 2, who challenged this law: why is it different from thought, which he ruled in the following seif that one does not bless on thought, because it is not like speech, as Beit Yosef wrote at the end of this siman; and in writing too there is only thought, etc.; and he concluded: it seems to me that "one who writes words of Torah" means one who writes or copies and intends to learn—then he properly blesses; but a scribe who copies a book and his intent is to do work to earn money need not bless first—see there. And so Magen Avraham note 1, except that he concluded that even one who writes as his method of study requires further study, for in any case it is thought—see there. And so end of letter 2; Yad Aharon in Hagah Beit Yosef. However, Levush in seif 4 wrote the reason that one who writes blesses even if he does not read, because he does an act through writing; and so Peri Megadim in Mishbetzos Zahav letter 2—see there. And according to this it is implied that even if he does not intend to learn what he writes, he is obligated to bless because he does an act through writing; and so Mateh Yehuda letter 2, that the implication of Shulchan Aruch is that there is no distinction between one who writes for study and one who copies to earn wages—like speech—and must bless—see there. And Chayei Adam, klal 9, letter 11, wrote the reason: because it is the way of the writer to utter from his mouth what he writes, lest he utter from his mouth unintentionally; and he wrote that all the more so one who writes a Torah scroll, tefillin, and mezuzos must utter from his mouth and must bless. And so in rule 1 letter 2 it is forbidden to write a Torah scroll until he blesses the blessing on the Torah, because he must utter from his mouth what he writes—see there. And for practical halacha, to escape all doubts—whether a scribe writing a Torah scroll or a scribe copying a book as his study method, or one who writes some words of Torah—he should bless first the blessing on the Torah and then recite the portion of the priestly blessing as is customary, so that his main blessing be on the portion of the priestly blessing and afterwards write what he wishes of words of Torah. And so one who hears words of Torah from others should bless the blessing on the Torah and recite the portion of the priests and then listen, because there is a dispute in this—see Machazik Berachah letter 4 and Orach Mishpat letter 2; but if he writes some words from verses in a letter of greetings to speak wittily, it is not necessary, as Taz and Magen Avraham wrote there.
