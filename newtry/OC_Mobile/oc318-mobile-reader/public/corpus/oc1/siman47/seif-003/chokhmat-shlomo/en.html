(Siman 3) One who writes in matters of Torah, etc., must bless, etc. Note: behold the Mechaber's words are astounding, for here it is proved that he holds writing is preferable to meditation; and in the laws of Megillah [siman 690] regarding "he was writing it" it is proved there that one fulfills only if he read aloud — and it is proved that by writing one does not fulfill; and so it is proved explicitly in the Talmud there in Megillah [18a] that one does not fulfill by writing, only by articulation. And why — behold without study, "remember" implies "remember," and it would suffice even by remembering in the heart; but since it is written "you shall not forget," the heart's frequency is meant — what then do I fulfill "remember" with the mouth? And if so, one may again say that at least writing — since writing is preferable to meditation — if it were written only "you shall not forget," it would suffice by meditation of the heart; if so, the verse of "remember" would be revealed that it does not help in the heart alone. Still, perhaps by writing — since writing is preferable to meditation — it is also difficult on the Talmud: whence do we know the intent of "he was writing it" — that he read it aloud? Perhaps the intent is that by writing itself one fulfills; and perforce it is proved like the words of Taz and Magen Avraham that writing is only like meditation. And apparently this is astonishing; and what seems to me to reconcile: since the Talmud says that according to all it must be placed entirely before him — if so, per the view that one fulfills by meditation, behold it is not entirely before him; we would have thought it should be placed before him and he would meditate on it. And what is derived from "remember" "remember" — that he read aloud — he did not fulfill, and we do not say behold it is not entirely before him; and we were certain that per the view that one fulfills by reading aloud we do not require that it be placed before him — only for us, who already derive from "remember" "remember" that we require [it] like a book, from this reason we require that it be entirely before him. And if so, it comes out well: if he reads aloud and afterward writes — then the reading with the mouth is from the scroll placed before him and it is complete, and he fulfills with it; but if he does not read aloud, only that he would fulfill by the act of writing — behold at the time of writing he does not see the scroll placed before him, only what he writes now, for it is impossible to set his eyes elsewhere and write; and perforce his eye is on what he writes. And since he does not look at what is placed before him, again it is impossible that he fulfill by writing, for what he writes is not placed entirely before him. And one may always say writing is preferable to meditation — so it appears in my humble opinion; and it comes out well; examine well.
