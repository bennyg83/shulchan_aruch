One who writes words of Torah. I did not know the source of this — from where did the Radak learn it, whom Beit Yosef brings? Did he not rule in the following seif that one does not bless in contemplation, because it is not speech, as Beit Yosef wrote at the end of this siman? And in writing there is only contemplation; and although Rabbi Yehudah at the beginning of "He was reading" [Shabbat 12a] [on] forbidden relations — that the scribe's way is sometimes to utter words from his mouth while writing — nevertheless for this one should not bless, since it is not certain. And further, it is difficult: for regarding testimony we find "from their mouths and not from their writing"; and since Scripture revealed to us that writing is not like speech, how can we bless on it? It requires investigation regarding practice — it is fitting for every writer of words of Torah to utter from his mouth with certainty some words, to escape a blessing in vain. But if not so, one could say: since he says "may He bless you" after birkat haTorah, he is like one who studies words of Torah; and it seems to me that "one who writes words of Torah" means one who writes or copies and intends to learn — then he blesses properly. But a scribe who copies a book and his intent is to perform work to earn money — he should not bless beforehand; and so one should learn from Abudarham's own words, who concluded in this language only that he should not write mundane matters — the implication is that even if he writes some verses in a letter of greetings and the like, he should not bless; and if so, one who copies alone is like mundane matters, as above.
