[3] On "my words," etc. Our formula is "to engage in words of Torah," because the main promise that He, may He be blessed, gave us was only regarding engagement in Torah—it is labor and toil, as Rashi explained on "If you walk in My statutes" that you will be laborers in Torah; so too wrote the Bach. And it seems to me to intend by this the conclusion of the blessing "Ha-arav na," etc., for it is stated in Sanhedrin 99b: "A soul that labors—labors in it, for I bent his mouth upon it"—he labors in one place and his Torah labors for him in another place. Rashi explained: one who reviews the Torah frequently—it becomes arranged in his mouth, and Torah labors for him, returning to him and asking its Creator to convey to him the reasons of Torah, because he bent his mouth on words of Torah, up to here. And Hagahos on Aggados explained that mysteries of Torah are revealed to him; therefore for this they pray "Ha-arav na," etc., "in our mouth," etc.—meaning that it should be sweet in our mouth and not be toil for us but arranged in our mouth. "All of us, knowers of Your name" means that You should convey to us the reasons and secrets of Torah, for the entire Torah is the names of the Holy One, blessed be He, as is known; and in this I explained the words of the Zohar in Parashat Pinchas: "Whoever strives in Torah, the obligation of stars and constellations is nullified from him," up to here—for on Shabbat we say "children, life, and sustenance do not depend on merit but on mazal," and Tosafos explained that great merit does help. And it seems to me that when one learns simple parts of Torah we say it does not depend on merit; but when one labors and engages and strives in Torah it becomes great merit—and this is the intent of the Zohar "whoever strives," etc. And it seems to me that this is difficult for Rashi on the verse "If you walk in My statutes," etc., which concludes "and I will give your rains in their season"—for it is difficult: behold, children, life, and sustenance do not depend on merit; therefore he resolves: on condition that you be laborers in Torah, and it becomes great merit. And one can also intend "and we shall be knowers of Your name"—meaning and not the name of stars and constellations—for by our engaging, which means laboring in Torah, the obligation of stars and constellations is nullified; so it seems to me. The Bach wrote it is correct to say "and our offspring and our offspring's offspring and the offspring of all," etc.—for whoever sees him and his son's son engaging in Torah, the Torah no longer ceases from his seed, up to here. Magen Avraham challenged him from the Targum on Proverbs 21, the text, up to here. And if so, it is surprising that I found in Abudraham in the name of Even Yerachi as the words of the Bach—I looked again and saw that in Proverbs it does not say "and his offspring" at all, only in Job 21 and 27; but the Targum did not explain there except "sons of sons" and not sons—see there. It also seems to me that he explained thus because "his sons" is mentioned explicitly in the verse, therefore they must interpret "and our offspring" as sons of sons; but when they mention "and his offspring" alone, it means his sons alone. Lechem Chamudos, folio 9, wrote that he should say "and learners of Your Torah for its sake," and so wrote the Bach and R' David Abudraham—and this is to exclude one who reads to become rabbi or judge, but rather does things for their sake and honor comes at the end.
