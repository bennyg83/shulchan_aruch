To bless — Ba'er Heitev. And below it is explained that regarding birkat haTorah one need not study immediately; and it is obvious that contemplation of words of Torah is in any case not called an interruption. And it is possible his intent is that if one does not utter words of Torah from the mouth immediately after the blessing, one should be concerned — for even though now he sits and contemplates, perhaps some matter will occur that will require him to interrupt in speech; therefore he should utter some words of Torah from the mouth immediately after the blessing. And see Halakhot Ketanot volume 2 siman 159, who wrote that one who hears words of Torah must bless, since hearing is like answering, which is better than contemplation; and also that the one who blesses has not lost, for the obligation of study applies to him all day, and for birkat haTorah one need not study immediately — see there. And see Machatzit HaShekel, who wrote to reconcile that [case] in siman 141, that one who goes up to the Torah should read calmly with the prayer leader so that his blessing not be in vain — see there; and Taz, and Pri Chadash, and She'elot Ya'avetz part 1 siman 75, and responsa Zaken Aharon siman 60; and it seems proper to be stringent that even one who does not wish to study himself but only hears another who studies and is silent — he should bless birkat haTorah first, and it is not comparable to contemplation alone.
