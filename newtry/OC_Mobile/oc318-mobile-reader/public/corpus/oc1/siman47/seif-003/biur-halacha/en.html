One who writes words of Torah. See Mishnah Berurah, where I wrote concerning the practical ruling, etc. Taz, Magen Avraham, and Biur HaGra all agree that writing is equivalent to thought. Although Peri Megadim inclines to require a blessing, that is because in siman 62 he inclines to rule that, on a Torah level, thought is equivalent to speech, unlike Peri Chadash. According to what we brought there in siman 62, however, nearly all the Rishonim and Acharonim hold that thought is not equivalent to speech on a Torah level. The ruling therefore certainly follows Taz and Magen Avraham.
Even though he does not read. If he writes and copies only in order to earn money, it is possible that even if he reads the words aloud he need not recite the blessing, because this is not considered Torah study. See Magen Avraham in siman 38, seif katan 8. Accordingly, even a hired scribe writing sifrei Torah, tefillin, and mezuzos, who by law must articulate the words he writes, may perhaps not need to recite Birchas HaTorah. Taz writes that even according to the Mechaber, who treats writing like speech, if one's activity is solely for monetary gain it is not considered study. This proof may be rejected. There, because his intention is only to earn money, the act of writing is not considered speech. Actual verbalization, however, may still retain the status of words of Torah in every case. This requires study. In practice, one who rises early in the morning to write sifrei Torah, tefillin, or mezuzos should first recite Birchas HaTorah and afterward say the customary verses.
