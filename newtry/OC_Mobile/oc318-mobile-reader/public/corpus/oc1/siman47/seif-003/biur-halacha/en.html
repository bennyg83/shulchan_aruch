"One who writes in matters of Torah" — see Mishnah Berurah in what I wrote; and regarding practice, etc. — did not Taz, Magen Avraham, and Gra all agree that it is like meditation? And although Peri Megadim inclines that one must bless — this is because he inclines in siman 62 that by law meditation is like speech from the Torah, and not like Pri Chadash. But according to what we brought there in siman 62 — that almost all early and later authorities hold that meditation is not like speech from the Torah — certainly the law is with Taz and Magen Avraham.

"Even though he does not read" — and if he writes and copies and his intention is only to earn money, it is possible that even when reading the words aloud he need not bless, for this is not in the category of study; see siman 38 in Magen Avraham, seif kaf ches. And therefore even if he is hired to write a sefer Torah — the law is that he must read aloud the words he writes — it is possible he need not bless on this the blessing on the Torah; see Taz, who wrote that even per the Mechaber, writing is like speech — if his action is only to earn money, this is not in the category of study. One may reject this: there, since his intent is only to earn, writing is not considered speech; not so with actual speech — it is possible it is not excluded from matters of Torah in all respects; and this requires study. And regarding practice one should be stringent: if he rose in the morning to write a sefer Torah, he should bless the blessing on the Torah at the outset and afterward say the customary verses.
