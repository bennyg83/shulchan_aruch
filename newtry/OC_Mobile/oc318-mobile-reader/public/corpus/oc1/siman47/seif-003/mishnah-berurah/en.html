(4) One who writes. The Mechaber holds that writing is superior to thought. Some explain that this is because writing involves an action. Others explain that a person ordinarily articulates words while writing. This applies where one writes books for himself as part of his study and understands what he writes. A scribe who merely copies and does not seek to understand need not recite the blessing, because this is not study. All the more so, one who writes a verse in an ordinary letter for stylistic purposes need not recite the blessing, since he does not intend to study. In practice, the later authorities agree that one should not rely upon the Mechaber's view and recite the blessing for writing alone in every case. Writing still amounts only to thinking words of Torah. Therefore, anyone writing words of Torah should articulate at least several words, to avoid a possible blessing in vain, unless he recites the verses of Bircas Kohanim or the baraisa Eilu Devarim after the blessing, as is customary.
