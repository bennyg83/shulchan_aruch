(ט) In the gloss there: and likewise one may rule on a halacha, etc. Rabbi Menachem Aharon, may his memory be blessed, klal 5, letter 34, decided that the ruling on halacha that Moram wrote means instruction of prohibition and permission specifically, and not monetary laws—as Moram himself wrote explicitly in Darkei Moshe in siman 85 from the Ran, and he cited it in Darkei Moshe in this siman; and the reason is clear why in monetary laws, whether at the beginning of judgment or at its end, it is forbidden to rule before the blessing on the Torah, even without giving a reason, because that is Torah; and from this he disagreed with the Rav Mateh Yehuda letter 4, who understood according to Moram that it means monetary laws. And he wrote there that it is forbidden to rule on monetary laws between litigants; and this instruction of prohibition and permission is simple instruction that need not analyze in a book. However, if a rabbi needs to learn something through analysis in a book, this is not mere thought and requires blessing—see there, Petach HaDeah letter 3.
