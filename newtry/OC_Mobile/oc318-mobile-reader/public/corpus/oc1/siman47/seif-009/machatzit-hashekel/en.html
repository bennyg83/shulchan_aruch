(Note 9) "Since," etc.—it implies, etc. And the Taz wrote: one who has a fixed time every day for Torah and the rest of the day is engaged in his affairs—and one day, after his fixed time for Torah passed, he went to his affairs and no business befell him and he returned to his study—need not bless again, for the obligation is upon him at all times and there is no distraction of mind; only that the disturbance of his affairs exempts him. And since no business befell him, automatically the first obligation applies to him, and automatically he is exempt with the first blessing, as he extended longer. And with this it is also possible that Magen Avraham agrees—and Magen Avraham deals with a person who does not study at all on a fixed basis and once reconsidered to study; for this was not his intent to exempt the study, therefore he must bless. And perhaps the Taz also agrees to this.
