9) There, in the gloss: The same applies in that one may issue a ruling, etc. Minchas Aharon 5:34 concludes that Rama's reference to issuing a ruling means specifically a ruling concerning what is forbidden or permitted, and not monetary law. Rama himself states this explicitly in Darkei Moshe, siman 85, in the name of Ran, and refers to it in Darkei Moshe to this siman. He explains why, in monetary law, whether at the beginning or conclusion of a case, one may not issue a ruling before Birchos HaTorah even without giving a reason: this itself is Torah. On this basis, he disagrees with Mateh Yehudah 4, who understood Rama to refer to monetary law. He writes that it is forbidden to adjudicate monetary law between litigants. Moreover, the license concerning forbidden and permitted matters applies specifically to a simple ruling that requires no textual research. If the matter requires substantial study through consulting a text, it is not mere thought and requires a berachah. Pischei HaDevir 3. See Yafeh LaLev 3, who writes that both types of ruling are permitted without stating a reason; see there. It appears that, wherever possible, one should be stringent in accordance with Minchas Aharon.
