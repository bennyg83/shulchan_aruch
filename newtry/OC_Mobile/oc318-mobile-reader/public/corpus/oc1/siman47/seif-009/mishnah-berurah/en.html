(19) "If he interrupted" — their reason: specifically with Ahavah Rabbah, which is not the main blessing for the Blessing of the Torah — then it does not exempt unless he studied immediately without interruption; but the Blessing of the Torah exempts all day even if he did not study after Shacharis until midday, and it is not comparable to all blessings where one may not interrupt between the blessing and the mitzvah, as written in siman 206 — for here it is different, since one must meditate on Torah day and night, and thus there is never distraction of mind. But most later authorities disagree with the Mechaber, agreeing with the view of Yesh Omrim, that the Mechaber wrote only "and the correct view" etc., and they hold that the law of the Blessing of the Torah is like every blessing on mitzvos or benefits — one must bless again if he interrupted immediately after the blessing, even if he knows clearly he did not distract his mind, for since he has not yet begun the mitzvah it cannot apply afterward; and the Pri Chadash and Gra agreed, and Chayei Adam ruled thus. Nevertheless it appears to me that since there are poskim who hold one need not bless again, it is better if he interrupted after the Blessing of the Torah that he intend afterward in Ahavah Rabbah to fulfill the Blessing of the Torah thereby and study a little immediately after finishing his prayer. And see Biur Halacha.

(20) And so they practiced — the reason for the three verses they wished to arrange like a Torah reader, choosing these because they contain blessing; and one may say them even before morning light, unlike those who are strict to forbid — for we say them for study, not for nesias kapayim, which is not done at night.

(21) Birkas Kohanim — and they practiced to say also "these are things that have no measure" — which is a Mishnah and baraisa of "these are things a person..." — to fulfill Mikra, Mishnah, and Gemara.
