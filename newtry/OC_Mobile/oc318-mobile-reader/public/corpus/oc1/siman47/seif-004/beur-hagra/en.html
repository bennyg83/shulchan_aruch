Seif 4 — "One who meditates," etc. — it is established that it is not like speech, as Tosafos wrote in Berachos 20b, s.v. veRav Chisda, and as written in Shabbos 150a; and this contradicts what was written in seif 3 regarding one who writes, etc. The Taz raised this difficulty as well.

The same applies, etc. — from what was written in Avodah Zarah 44b: "A tanna taught: when he went out," etc.; see Tosafos there, s.v. tanna, etc., the explanation of Rashbam, etc., and Or Zarua, etc.; and the Ran likewise wrote in the name of Yesh Mefashim — except that he wrote not because of enmity, but that in the middle row meditation is permitted, and whoever does not articulate the matter is considered as meditating; therefore wherever meditation is permitted, this too is permitted, as written below in siman 84 in the Rama's gloss and in siman 85 in the Rama's gloss. But all this requires study, for here one blesses on the mitzvah — is there no mitzvah in meditation? Was it not said, "And you shall meditate on it," etc. — meaning, in the heart, as written, "Meditation of my heart"; and all the more so to rule on a matter, etc. Does one not need to bless for Scripture? So too when reading Torah in public.
