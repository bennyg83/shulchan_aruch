(5) He need not. Thought is not equivalent to speech. Accordingly, those who study analytically from a book should take care to articulate some words of Torah after the blessing, unless they recite Bircas Kohanim or other words of Torah after the blessing, as is customary.
(6) Because he may, etc. This appears to apply specifically to issuing a ruling between litigants, which is not performed as a mode of study. One who studies a law from a book, even without its reasons, should recite the blessing, because this is no less than writing. Mateh Yehudah in this siman.
(7) To rule. The reasoning, which is the essence of the ruling, remains only in his mind. Biur HaGra disagrees and holds that issuing a ruling is no less than reading Scripture alone, which requires a blessing. Gra is even stringent regarding thought alone, and does not permit thinking words of Torah before Birchas HaTorah. Everyone agrees, however, that one may perform a mitzvah before Birchas HaTorah, even though he certainly thinks about its law while performing it. Since he does not intend Torah study, no blessing is required. Gra below in seif 10.
