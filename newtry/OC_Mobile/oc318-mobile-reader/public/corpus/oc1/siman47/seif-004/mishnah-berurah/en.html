(5) He need not — for reflection is not like speech; therefore those who study in depth from a book should take care to pronounce some divrei Torah aloud after the blessing if they do not say the verses of Birkas Kohanim or other divrei Torah after the blessing, as is customary.

(6) "That he may" etc. — it appears specifically for ruling a case between litigants, which is not a method of study; but to study a halachah from a book without articulating the reasoning — one blesses, for it is no worse than writing [Mateh Yehuda in this siman].

(7) To rule — for the reasoning, which is the essence of the halachah, is only reflection in his heart; and the Gra in his commentary disagrees and holds that this is no worse than one who reads Scripture alone, who must bless. And even for reflection alone he is strict not to reflect so much without having blessed the Blessing of the Torah. But according to all, one may do some mitzvah act before the Blessing of the Torah, even though while performing the act he certainly reflects on this law — nevertheless it is permitted, for whenever one does not intend study he need not bless [Gra later in this siman].
