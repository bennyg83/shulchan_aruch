"One who meditates in matters of Torah," etc. — see Biur HaGra, who raises a difficulty: is not meditation in Torah also a mitzvah, as it is written, "And you shall meditate on it day and night"? And in Neshamas Adam one wants to reject the difficulty; see Binyan Olam, who reaches the above and agrees with Gra. However, what seems from there from Gra — that meditation in Torah is literally like speech, and because of this he wrote there what Rama wrote in siman 68 — that one who studies by meditation, etc., has no prohibition, etc. — he follows his view here, that he agrees in the gloss with the Mechaber that even for Torah meditation is not like speech; not so according to Gra's view — there would be prohibition even by meditation, for interruption is considered in the blessings of Kerias Shema. In my humble opinion this is not well reasoned, for Gra too admits that even for Torah meditation is not like speech; rather he holds that meditation too is a mitzvah, as written, "And you shall meditate on it day and night," as his language is clear for one who examines well. And another clear proof to my words: otherwise, what does the Gemara ask in Shabbos 150a — did Rabbi Yehuda not teach that meditation is not like speech? Yet Rabbi Yochanan [taught that] in every place one may meditate on matters of Torah except in the bathroom, etc. There too it is written, "and indecent matter shall not be seen on you," etc. — is it not that for matters of Torah all agree meditation is like speech? If so, even if "and indecent matter" refers to this, meditation would be forbidden. Rather, certainly as we wrote; examine well.
