One who thinks words of Torah, etc. See Biur HaGra, who challenges this ruling. Thought in Torah is itself a mitzvah, as the verse states, “You shall contemplate it day and night.” Nishmas Adam attempts to reject the challenge. See Binyan Olam, who disputes Nishmas Adam and agrees with Gra. Binyan Olam appears to understand Gra as holding that thought in Torah is fully equivalent to speech. On that basis he writes that Rama's ruling in siman 68, that one who studies by thought does not create a prohibited interruption, follows Rama's view here that thought is not equivalent to speech even in Torah. According to Gra, however, even thought would constitute an interruption in the blessings of Krias Shema. In my humble opinion, this is not correct. Gra agrees that even in Torah, thought is not equivalent to speech. He merely holds that thought itself is also a mitzvah, as the verse says, “You shall contemplate it day and night.” This is also apparent from Gra's wording to one who studies it carefully. There is a further clear proof. In Shabbos 150a the Gemara asks whether Rabbi Yochanan holds that thought is not equivalent to speech, even though Rabbi Yochanan says that one may think words of Torah everywhere except in a bathhouse and lavatory. The Gemara answers that there too the verse says, “No naked matter shall be seen among you.” If, regarding Torah study, everyone agreed that thought is equivalent to speech, then even if the verse “No naked matter shall be seen” applied to this matter, thought would obviously be forbidden there. The proof is therefore exactly as we wrote. Examine this carefully.
