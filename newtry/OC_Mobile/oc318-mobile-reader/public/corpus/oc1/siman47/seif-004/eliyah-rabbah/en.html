[4] With a vav, etc. For it is one blessing with "to engage in words of Torah," and it seems to me that it is not required that perhaps it does not open with Baruch because it is a blessing adjacent to its companion (Levush). One could object: behold, Beit Yosef wrote the reason that wherever blessings are short, even when adjacent they open with Baruch; and if we say that Levush holds like Rambam and Baal HaMaor that even with short blessings when they are adjacent they do not open with Baruch, Levush should have explained. And furthermore their dispute is explicit in Beit Yosef and what depends on his opinion; and more one could object that in siman 46, seif 4, regarding the morning blessings, Levush himself wrote the reason of Rabbeinu Tam because they are short. And it is difficult for me that even in Beit Yosef he does not force that it is one law, for he brought the words of Abudraham who agreed with Rambam and Tosafos and his supporters and that the practice is to say without a vav; and Beit Yosef wrote on this: and for practical halacha it seems to say with a vav, for according to those who say it is one blessing, if the vav is missing it is an interruption in the middle of a blessing; and according to those who say it is a blessing on its own, even though they say with a vav there is nothing in it, up to here—behold he holds more like Rambam and his supporters, only that for convenience of speech they say with a vav so there not be an interruption according to those who say it is one blessing. The opposite is reasonable: even according to those who say it is one blessing, if he said without a vav it is not considered an interruption at all. And some evidence for this is from what the Bach wrote below in siman 207 regarding the blessing "who creates many souls"—this is his language: thus one should explain without a vav as with a vav, up to here. Furthermore, "evening" is language of request, and the blessing to engage in Torah matters is acknowledgment that we are commanded to engage in it, as Sefer Tzeda Laderech wrote, folio 19. Also in several blessings we find this—for example the blessing "You are He" until "before You were not created," etc., and the conclusion of the blessing "sanctify Your name," etc., without a vav. And furthermore, according to those who say they are two blessings, if one says with a vav it appears like the conclusion of the first blessing and he lacks one blessing. It also seems to me evidence from the words of the Rosh, and all the poskim who brought the language of the Gemara: Rabbi Yochanan said "Ha-arav na," etc.; and even according to our version [Berachos] 11a, Rabbi Yochanan concludes with it "and Ha-arav na," etc.—greater evidence, for from that he says he concludes with it, behold this is the conclusion of the blessing to engage, and even so he says "Ha-arav" without a vav; and so it is in Rashi's language. And Lechem Chamudos, folio 9, brought Rashi's words in error—see there. It also seems to me that even for Rabbeinu Tam, who used to say "veHa-arav" with a vav, this was because it was obvious to him that it is one blessing, only that he saw several poskim who hold they are two blessings; there is also implication for this in the language of the blessing, as R' David Abudraham wrote that the language changes and speaks in future tense, and if so they will err and answer Amen before "Ha-arav na" not according to law; therefore he would say "veHa-arav" with a vav so as not to answer Amen by mistake. And so appears his reason in "Yehi ratzon" in siman 46, where Rabbeinu Tam also wrote to say with a vav, for in its meaning it is another blessing since there is not in their conclusion similar to the opening, as Tosafos wrote Berachos 46a; and so holds the Rokeach. Therefore he used to say with a vav so as not to err in answering Amen; but if not for the reason of answering Amen he would also say without a vav for the reasons mentioned above, even though it was obvious to him that it is one blessing. Furthermore, apart from this law, I saw in Sefer Tanya in the name of Rav Neturanoi Gaon and in Sefer Shibulei HaLeket and Rokeach siman 382 and Sefer Tzeda Laderech folio 19 that they ruled like Rambam and his supporters that they are two blessings. And furthermore, in Abudraham he wrote the reason of three blessings corresponding to Scripture, Mishnah, and Gemara that they say after them; and Mahari Luria wrote in Shulchan Aruch that he practiced thus. Therefore it seems to me for practical halacha to say without a vav; and in any case regarding answering Amen one should not be lenient so much. The Rashba asked why we do not bless after it "who chose us," etc., as we bless on the Torah in the synagogue; and he resolved: Torah is a mitzvah and we do not bless on mitzvos after them, but reading the Torah in the synagogue is a separate enactment and we bless. And Beit Yosef resolved that the mitzvah of reviewing Torah is day and night—it is not relevant to bless after it, up to here; and so I found in Shibulei HaLeket siman 2. And see in Maadanei Melech who challenged in Berachos 21a that it says: we learned the blessing of Torah after it by kal vachomer—and what food that does not require before requires blessing after, Torah that requires before should require after. One can challenge: what about food, for one enjoys—and he did not challenge thus [there] 48a that Rabbi Yishmael made kal vachomer from temporary life to eternal life—not all the more so; and his resolution is strained, therefore I did not bring it. And in my humble opinion, one cannot challenge from food that one enjoys, for certainly eternal life is superior; but on 21a, where he learns kal vachomer from that it does not require before, etc., and did not learn kal vachomer from food of temporary life, etc.—on this the challenge is proper. And my master my father, may his light shine, resolved that thus he challenges: what about food, for one enjoys, and the Sages expounded it is forbidden for a person to enjoy without a blessing, as it is written "to the Omnipresent is the earth"; and if so, it also requires blessing before, and it is explicit in Scripture; and if so, there is no kal vachomer. And Levush wrote that the two blessings of Torah in the morning: one is for the concluding blessing of the previous day's study. And Nachalat Tzvi disagreed and wrote the reason of two blessings corresponding to written Torah and oral Torah, up to here; and so I saw in Sefer Berachas Avraham. But one could object on him, for Levush explained that the blessing to engage was the first blessing and "who chose us" is the concluding blessing on study learned yesterday—behold Levush explained the opposite, and as explained explicitly at the end of the gloss, and according to this what Nachalat Tzvi challenged on Levush, why they did not first explain the concluding blessing, etc., is surprising—see there; and I shortened because it does not emerge for practical halacha. Also a puzzle on Rashi in Bava Metzia 85b—this is his language: the blessing "who gave us Torah of truth" that was before "words of Torah," up to here—and it is against the Gemara Berachos 11a and all poskim who bless before it "who chose us." And my master my father, may his light shine, wanted to resolve that it is Rashi's own language that he adopted, that one blesses on the Torah before him on "who gave us Torah of truth," but he did not mean that "who gave" is the blessing before.
