Siman 8 — "and there is room to question," etc. — see Beit Yosef, who extended in explaining the words of the Rosh and Tur; but their explanation is as follows: one may interpret two explanations in the words of the Yerushalmi. (1) One is exempt only for what he repeated on the spot, but afterward one is not exempt even if he repeated immediately, because of the interruption — and the same applies to the blessing on the Torah. (2) One is not exempt for the whole day unless he repeated on the spot, and then he is exempt for the whole day. On the second explanation it is difficult: why need "that he repeated" — he recites Kerias Shema! And since it says "that he repeated" and does not say "that he read," it implies specifically repetition, as Rashba wrote. On the first explanation it is difficult: why did he say Ahavah Rabah and not say it regarding every blessing on the Torah, as the Rosh asked — he should have stated it generally, etc.; therefore he agreed to the second explanation. The above difficulty is resolved: specifically if he repeated on the spot, then the blessing is seen as for its purpose, etc.; not so with Kerias Shema alone. He wrote, "and then no," etc., and wrote, "and likewise one who blesses," etc. — to exclude the first explanation because of the above difficulty, as he wrote: otherwise, etc.; and it says, "one who blesses on the Torah and studies" — specifically if he studied immediately, then he is exempt for the whole day; but otherwise he must return and bless. And it says, "and even if you say," etc. — meaning, even if I establish the first explanation, and because of the above difficulty one may say that in truth there is no distinction and he cited one of them; also according to this it turns out, etc. But between the blessing on the Torah and study there is no interruption that helps per the Rosh — one must return and bless; for here it does not help if one is not distracted, as in the blessing on bread there, 40, and so in all blessings on mitzvos or benefits: specifically in the middle of the mitzvah or meal an interruption does not invalidate and it helps that one is not distracted — not so between a blessing and a mitzvah, since the mitzvah has not yet taken effect, as the poskim wrote; so too the Tur. Specifically if, etc., or he blessed the blessing on the Torah and began to study, etc. — specifically if he studied immediately, then it exempts; but he cited the conclusion of the Rosh, who wrote "and even if you say," etc., because in responsa, general principle 4:62, he wrote thus — except that in Tosafos there, s.v. shekvar, etc., it implies that one need not study immediately; and Mordechai likewise copied Tosafos' words as they stand. But Maharshal already wrote that this is not so, and so it is written in Semag in the name of Tosafos, as the Rosh wrote: specifically if he studied immediately after the blessing on the Torah. And otherwise, the words of Tosafos who wrote "and the French," etc., imply that they would exempt with this for the whole day — for it is impossible that they would bless every time they sat to study; it should have said "more," and "the French bless every time," etc., and they conclude because of the Yerushalmi. And per the Yerushalmi's explanation, what one repeated immediately does not help for the whole day; and the words of the Rosh and Semag are primary, and so too Rambam and the other poskim. Therefore what he wrote in seif 9, "and the correct," etc., is the law: if he interrupted he must return and bless. And what is written here, "and there is room to question," etc. — there is no doubt that Kerias Shema does not help, for the whole structure of the Rosh is on this; and so it is implied from the words of Tosafos that they wrote; and more, even per the Yerushalmi, etc., see there; and so it is implied in Hagahos Maimoniyos and Mordechai and Shmuel, and as Rashba wrote regarding the girsa "that he repeated on the spot," and Tosafos and Rosh and Shmuel [wrote] the girsa "that he repeated." And even per the second explanation that the Rosh concludes — one is exempt only for what he read and repeated immediately, and one is not exempt for the whole day at all, except for a person whose mind is on his study all day — he is exempt by logic; and if so, there is no proof from the Yerushalmi. Nevertheless the view of the Rosh and Tosafos is correct, that in Kerias Shema it is not recognizable, etc.; and the first explanation is also reasonable, except that for spreading the matter and as a general stringency he wrote the second explanation, as Ri wrote.
