One who slept — Ba'er Heitev; and see Magen Avraham siman 494. He wrote in Birkei Yosef, as Mahari Dayan in his responsa Sha'arei Yeshuah, manuscript: one who rose at the watch and studied and returned to sleep on his bed — fixed sleep; when he rises he blesses again all the blessings, including birkat haTorah; but casual sleep is not an interruption — see there; and so Pri Chadash. But he wrote that if one rose with intent not to sleep and sleep overcame him in the middle of his study, he need not return and bless — see there. And see Shulchan Tzedeck: in Mateh Yehuda he wrote on this that Rashi means even if he slept again in fixed sleep on his bed, since his intent was not so from the beginning it is not considered except as casual sleep — see there. And he wrote further from the words of the kabbalists, Maharash Mizrachi, who wrote regarding blessings that even if one wishes to return and sleep it does not matter, and it is as if he slept by day — it appears from his words that when he returns to sleep he need not bless on rising birkat haTorah a second time — see there. And it appears his reason is that presumably his intent was to exempt himself for the whole day, even though his intent is to sleep again. And I saw that in Peri Megadim he also left Pri Chadash's words on this as requiring investigation. For practical law it appears that the matter depends on intent: if his intent was only to exempt this study until the time of sleeping again, it is obvious he must return and bless when he rises again; unlike in the general case, even though his intent is to return and sleep — nevertheless, so as not to multiply blessings, his intent is to exempt for the whole day; therefore he need not return and bless.
