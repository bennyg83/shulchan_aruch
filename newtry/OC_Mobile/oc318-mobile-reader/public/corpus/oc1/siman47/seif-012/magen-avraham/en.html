So long as he did not sleep. This implies that one who remained awake all night need not recite the blessing in the morning. This requires study. According to Tur, who holds that the matter depends upon sleep, if one did not sleep he need not recite a blessing. But according to our custom not to recite a new blessing after daytime sleep, the morning blessing must be because the Sages instituted this blessing each day like the other morning blessings. This can be proved from Rabbeinu Tam. According to him, one who rises before dawn need not recite the blessing, but once day begins he must recite it. Thus, the new day itself creates a new obligation. Accordingly, for us as well, even if one did not sleep, once day begins he should recite Birchas HaTorah, because his original intention was to exempt only one day in accordance with the Sages' enactment. If he slept and rose before dawn, he should recite it immediately, just as one may recite the other morning blessings before day. See what I wrote at the end of siman 639.
