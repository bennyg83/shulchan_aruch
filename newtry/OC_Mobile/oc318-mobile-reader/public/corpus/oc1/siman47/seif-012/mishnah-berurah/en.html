(26) At night. Meaning even if he did not study at all during the day.
(27) That passed. So long as he remains awake, the obligation to study rests upon him whenever he has time. An interruption or lapse of attention is therefore not considered an interruption with respect to Birchas HaTorah.
(28) He did not sleep. Meaning that he did not have a fixed sleep. If he had a fixed sleep upon his bed, even at the beginning of the night, it constitutes an interruption. Even the alternative view in seif 11 agrees. If one remained awake all night, some say he need not recite Birchas HaTorah in the morning, while others say he must recite it because the Sages instituted this blessing every day like the other morning blessings. Because of doubt concerning blessings, one should be lenient. If possible, however, he should hear Birchas HaTorah from another person. He should ask that person to intend to discharge his obligation, intend to fulfill the obligation himself, answer amen, and afterward recite several verses so that it counts as study. Alternatively, he may intend to fulfill Birchas HaTorah through Ahavah Rabbah and study a little immediately after completing prayer. If he slept a fixed daytime sleep upon his bed and remained awake the entire following night, Rabbi Akiva Eiger rules that according to everyone he must recite Birchas HaTorah in the morning. Ahavas Olam at Maariv does not exempt him unless he studied immediately after prayer.
