(26) At night — meaning even if he did not study at all during the day.

(27) "That time passed" — for whenever he is awake he is obligated to study, as long as he has leisure; and interruption and distraction are not considered an interruption regarding the Blessing of the Torah.

(28) "That he should not sleep" — meaning fixed sleep on his bed; but if he slept fixed sleep on his bed, even at the beginning of the night, it is considered an interruption, and even those who say in seif 11 admit this. And if he was awake all night, some say he need not bless in the morning, and some say he must bless, for the Sages established this blessing every day, similar to the other morning blessings; and doubtful blessings are treated leniently — but if possible he should have another include him in the Blessing of the Torah and tell him to intend to discharge him in his blessings, and he should intend to fulfill his obligation and answer Amen, and then say some verses so it counts as study for him; or he may intend to fulfill his obligation through Ahavah Rabbah and study a little immediately after finishing his prayer. And if he slept fixed sleep during the day on his bed, and on the following night he was awake all night, Rabbi Akiva Eiger ruled that in this case, according to all, he must bless the Blessing of the Torah in the morning, and the Ahavas Olam of Ma'ariv does not exempt him unless he studied immediately after prayer.
