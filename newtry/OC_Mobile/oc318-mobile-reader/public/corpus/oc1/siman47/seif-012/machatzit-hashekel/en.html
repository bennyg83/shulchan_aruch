(Note 12) "As long as," etc.—per the Tur this is the first opinion in seif 11.

You will know, for according to Rabbeinu Tam, etc.—that Beit Yosef brought in the name of Agur, who wrote that his father practiced when he slept during the day a fixed sleep—not to bless, since it is a dispute among the poskim whether one must bless; and Rav Beit Yosef challenged that we do not find a dispute in this, and according to all one must bless. And he wrote: and perhaps his reason is because according to Rabbeinu Tam, even sleep for the entire night is not an interruption; and even though we do not rule like him—in night sleep; but in daytime sleep one should be concerned for his words—end of his words. And so is the second opinion brought in Shulchan Aruch seif 11. And therefore Magen Avraham wrote: for according to Rabbeinu Tam, who holds that even night sleep is not an interruption—therefore what we bless every day the blessing of the Torah is that the Sages established this blessing every day, like all the morning blessings that one blesses every day. And therefore according to him, even if one arose before dawn he need not bless until dawn. One could therefore say that per the view of the father of Agur—and this is the second opinion in seif 11—that they were concerned for Rabbeinu Tam's view, he holds like Rabbeinu Tam that sleep is not an interruption whether by day or by night; only that the Sages enacted the blessing of the Torah every day, like all morning blessings—only that in this we disagree with Rabbeinu Tam: when one arose before dawn one must bless immediately, as is written in seif 13, that he holds it does not exempt except for one day. And since he arose—even though dawn has not come—for this matter it is called another day, that he must bless. And therefore automatically, if one was awake all night, he must in any case bless when dawn comes, for it did not exempt except for one day—like the other morning blessings. Even though one could say he does not hold like Rabbeinu Tam in this; rather he holds two requirements: that daytime sleep, even fixed, is not an interruption; and also at night there is no interruption—only sleep at night then is an interruption; and therefore when one was awake all night he need not bless. On this Magen Avraham wrote: "You will know, for according to Rabbeinu Tam," etc. And since according to Rabbeinu Tam the law is so, logically we should distance the dispute between us and Rabbeinu Tam—it is better to adopt the first side, that he holds like all of Rabbeinu Tam's view, only that in this we disagree when one arose before dawn, as above; and therefore when awake all night he must bless. And in siman 494, Magen Avraham himself ruled that when one was awake all night he should bless when day breaks. And if there is a person who sleeps at night, he should hear from that person the blessing of the Torah and intend to fulfill the obligation for the listener, and he should intend to fulfill. And so too in Eliyah Rabbah. And in siman 46 note 12, Eliyah Rabbah wrote, and these are his words: he wrote in the gloss of Magen Avraham—if he did not sleep all night he should not bless "My God, the soul," etc., and "who removes sleep"—end of his words.
