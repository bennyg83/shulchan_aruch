(29) He blesses the Blessing of the Torah — even if he returned and slept afterward fixed sleep before daybreak or during the day, he need not bless again, for presumably a person's intent is to be exempted with this blessing until the night's sleep that follows; and so too according to Yesh Omrim in seif 11. But according to what we wrote there, that even with fixed sleep during the day one who blessed does not lose, all the more so here, where he slept fixed sleep before daybreak — one who blessed does not lose.

(30) Before daybreak — and even one who rises after midnight for some matter, and intends to return and sleep again afterward fixed sleep — nevertheless he may bless all these blessings and need not bless them again when he rises in the morning, except the Blessing of Elokai Neshamah, which he says without the closing formula, and HaMa'avir Shenah, which he says without mentioning the Name; and then when he rises in the morning he says them with proper mention of Name and kingship. And see Biur Halacha.

(31) "Binah" — and the later authorities agreed that he may bless this blessing too even before day breaks; but some say that ab initio one should be careful not to bless it before day breaks unless he heard a rooster crow, and post facto he fulfills even if he did not hear a rooster — provided he blesses from midnight onward; but before midnight, even post facto he should bless again, even if he heard a rooster crow. And see Biur Halacha.

(32) And the parashah of the Tamid — and all the korbanos Mishnayos that follow it, for they correspond to their offering, and their offering is only by day.

(33) To study and to bless — and he wipes his hands with anything that cleans.
