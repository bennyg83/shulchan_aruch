(29) He recites Birchas HaTorah. Even if he later returns to a fixed sleep before dawn or during the day, he need not recite it again, because a person ordinarily intends this blessing to exempt him until the fixed sleep of the following night. This follows the alternative view in seif 11. According to what we wrote there, however, that even after a fixed daytime sleep one who recites the blessing has not lost, all the more so in this case, where he slept a fixed sleep before dawn, one who repeats the blessing has not lost.
(30) Before daylight. Even one who rises after midnight for some purpose and intends to return afterward to another fixed sleep may recite all these blessings. He will not need to repeat them when he rises in the morning, except that he should recite Elokai Neshamah the first time without its concluding formula, and HaMaavir Sheinah without mentioning Hashem's Name. When he rises in the morning he should recite them properly with Hashem's Name and sovereignty. See Biur Halachah.
(31) Binah. The later authorities agree that this blessing may also be recited before daylight. Some say that initially one should be careful not to recite it before daylight unless he heard a rooster. After the fact, he has fulfilled the obligation even if he did not hear a rooster, provided he recited it after midnight. If he recited it before midnight, he must repeat it even after the fact, even if he heard a rooster. See Biur Halachah.
(32) And the passage of the daily offering. The same applies to all the sacrificial Mishnayos that follow it, because they substitute for the actual offerings, and offerings may be brought only by day.
(33) To study and recite the blessing. He should clean his hands with anything that cleanses.
