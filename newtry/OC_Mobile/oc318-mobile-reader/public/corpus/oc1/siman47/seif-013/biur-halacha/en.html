"One who rises early," etc., "blesses the blessing on the Torah" — see Mishnah Berurah in what he wrote: if he again slept, etc. This follows from the Chayei Adam, who wrote that if he again slept afterward, whether by day or by night, he need not return and bless; and his intent here was even if he again slept a substantial sleep before dawn, as he himself concludes — to exclude Pri Chadash, who requires returning and blessing for this. But his intent is not to exempt here even if he again slept a substantial sleep at the beginning of the night after that, as it seems from the language of Peri Megadim here at the end of letter 12 in Orach Chayyim — for he concludes the Chayei Adam until the sleep of the night that follows. It is more reasonable to say that Peri Megadim's view is likewise; and what Peri Megadim wrote — that perhaps his intent is to exempt from the morning prayer after it — his intent is only to exclude Pri Chadash's view, that his view is only until he rises from sleep in the morning; for this he says it is not so. But plainly he too admits that a substantial sleep, even at the beginning of the night, is an interruption, as is also implied from the language of Beit Yosef at the end, s.v. vekatav od haAgur, see there. And furthermore, we do not abandon all these plain rulings of many great authorities who rule that one must return and bless, because of a doubt in Peri Megadim — for Beit Yosef himself, and Pri Chadash, and Gra, and Olas Reiyah require returning and blessing even for a substantial sleep by day; and although there we are stringent according to a lone view, at least for a substantial sleep on the night after this one must surely bless.

"One who rises before daylight," etc. — see Mishnah Berurah in seif kaf lamed; and bedieved, if he also blessed Elokei Neshamah and HaMaavir Sheinah properly the first time — Pri Chadash's view is that he returns and blesses these two blessings when he rises a second time from his bed; Chayei Adam agreed with him regarding the blessing HaMaavir Sheinah. And from what is written in siman 6 it implies that he should not return and bless; so too it is implied from the Bach, and safeq berachos lehakel. And if sleep has overtaken him, all agree he need not return and bless them [Pri Chadash in siman 46].

"He blesses the entire order of blessings except," etc. — see Mishnah Berurah in what he wrote; "and the later authorities," etc. — namely Magen Avraham in the name of the Zohar, and Gra, and Pri Chadash, and Chayei Adam; and what I wrote, "but there are those who say," etc., is from Chayei Adam. And in Shaarei Teshuvah it is found that some are lenient in this as well; and according to Baer Heitev and Chayei Adam one should be stringent lechatchilah even without hearing a rooster's crow, and I did not want to conceal this like them, because Pri Chadash and Gra acted to permit, as Pri Chadash wrote and in Sefer Maaseh Rav. And moreover, from Peri Megadim's words it is implied that before daybreak one may bless even without hearing a rooster — for only after midnight is hearing a rooster required; but Chayei Adam's words seem to contradict Magen Avraham from what he concluded at the end of seif kaf gimmel. Nevertheless it is good to be careful lechatchilah; and from this follow the words of Chayei Adam. However, when we examine well, it appears that he explains Magen Avraham's words according to their plain sense — what Magen Avraham wrote: see siman 46, seif 8 — meaning, there Rama was lenient even without hearing a rooster, for the Gemara's intent is per the world's custom; and if so, one may say that the Zohar's intent too in what it said, "when a man calls," is like this; and on this Magen Avraham concluded. Nevertheless it is good to be careful lechatchilah — meaning, to be stringent when he did not hear, but when he heard one may be lenient even lechatchilah.
