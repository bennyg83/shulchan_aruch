(10) The order of the blessings, etc. This continues through the passage of the daily offering. Shelah, pages 124 and 129, writes at length that one should not interrupt from Al Netilas Yadayim until completing the entire morning-blessing order, except for HaNosen LaSechvi. One who interrupts with learning or another activity renders the blessing over washing questionable. He further writes that it is good to recite the Akedah, the passage of the manna, the Ten Commandments, and other verses of mercy, supplication, and requests, and only afterward begin learning. See above, siman 1. Mateh Moshe writes that he found in an ancient book that before a person begins learning he should recite eight times the eighth verse of each section of Tehillim 119, beginning with “Hashem is my portion; I said,” through “Teach me Your statutes.” He should then pray that Hashem open his heart in Torah and command the appointed angels associated with the transmission of wisdom to teach him Torah, so that his heart be open like the entrance to the Ulam, the words of Torah be arranged in his mouth like a prepared table and a flowing spring, his learning remain preserved in his heart without forgetfulness, and he be like a plastered cistern that loses no drop. He should pray to arise early and remain late over Torah, as it is written, “This book of Torah shall not depart from your mouth; you shall contemplate it day and night, so that you may observe everything written in it, for then you shall make your ways successful and then you shall act wisely.” Afterward he should recite the blessing over Torah. See the end of siman 110 concerning the prayer upon entering and leaving the beis midrash. Every halachic decisor should say the entrance prayer in the morning and the departure prayer in the evening, even when learning alone.
