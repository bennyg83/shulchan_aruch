(13) [Seif 6] One says "veHa-arav" with a vav. Gloss: some say without a vav, etc. Behold the reason of those who say "veHa-arav" with a vav: because they hold it is one blessing with "who sanctified us," and one does not answer Amen after "on words of Torah"—and this is the view of Tosafos, the Rosh, and Tur. And the reason of those who say "Ha-arav" without a vav: they hold it is a blessing on its own and one answers Amen after "on words of Torah"—and this is the view of Rambam and Rashba in the name of Baal HaMaor and the Radak, as all this is written in Tur and Beit Yosef—see there. And Beit Yosef wrote: and for practical halacha it seems to say "veHa-arav" with a vav, for according to those who say it is one blessing, if the vav is missing it is an interruption in the middle of the blessing; and according to those who say it is a blessing on its own, even though they say with a vav there is nothing in it, end quote. And behold Maran did not reveal his opinion except to say "veHa-arav" with a vav; but regarding answering Amen after "on words of Torah" he did not reveal his opinion. However, Maharach Vital in Shaar HaKavanos testified about the Ari that he would answer Amen in the middle of the two blessings of "who sanctified us" on words of Torah and between the blessing and "Ha-arav na," as written above letter 10. And it is implied that he would say "veHa-arav" with a vav according to Maran, and also that he would answer Amen after "on words of Torah," for he holds they are two blessings according to Rambam and Rashba. And so in Sefer Peri Chadash, gate of blessings chapter 1. And so Mateh Tzvi. Naggid U'Mitzvah 11, 1a. Mateh Chadash on tractate Chatzit chapter 3, letter 11. And so the Taz note 4 and Magen Avraham note 1, that the Ari practiced to answer Amen in the middle—see there. And so in Hagahos Tashbetz. And he wrote: not like Peri Chadash who wrote that Maharai would say without a vav—which is not so.
