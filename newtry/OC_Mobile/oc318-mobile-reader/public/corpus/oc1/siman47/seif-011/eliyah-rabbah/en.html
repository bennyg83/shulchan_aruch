(9) And this is the custom, etc. Lechem Chamudos writes: “In my opinion, one who recites the blessing should be blessed,” and reports that his teacher Mahari Ginzburg followed this practice. I likewise found written in the margin of a Shulchan Aruch that Maharshal in Chochmas Shlomo, Berachos, chapter 1, cites Mahari in his writings as ruling like Rosh that a fixed daytime sleep constitutes an interruption. Shibolei HaLeket, Rabbeinu Yerucham, and Sefer Zikaron by the gaon Rabbi Yosef HaKohen also rule this way. Although Responsa Re'em, siman 6, does not rule so, his reasoning and proof do not appear correct. I have abbreviated the discussion. If one remained awake the entire night, it appears that everyone agrees he must recite the blessing over Torah in the morning. This is even clearer than the morning blessings, because the blessing was certainly not intended to extend beyond one day and night. This is unlike Magen Avraham. See below, simanim 494 and 639.
