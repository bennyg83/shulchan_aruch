One who writes. See Taz. Levush and Olas Tamid write that writing involves an action, while Levush says that no blessing is recited over thought. According to what I wrote in siman 62, when one is prevented he should think the words and thereby fulfill the obligation because thought may be equivalent to speech. In my novellae to Berachos 6b, Tosafos s.v. “and Rav Chisda,” I wrote that it is not clear whether thought is equivalent to speech. With Hashem's help, we shall discuss this in siman 62. Here, no blessing is recited over thought, just as no blessing is recited over nullifying chametz, even though before it is removed there is a Torah-level positive commandment, because one does not recite blessings over matters performed only in the heart. See siman 431. When one writes, however, he performs an act and therefore recites a blessing. See siman 85. In a place where thought is permitted, such as the middle room of a bathhouse or a place not entirely clean, see siman 84, it requires study whether writing words of Torah is permitted there. Perhaps writing is equivalent to actual study, or perhaps it is not. Understand this. Regarding a scribe who merely copies, Magen Avraham, letter 1, writes likewise.
