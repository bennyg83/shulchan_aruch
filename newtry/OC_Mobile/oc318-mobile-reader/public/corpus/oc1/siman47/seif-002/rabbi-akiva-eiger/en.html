Magen Avraham, note 12 — and it requires investigation; granted, according to his view it would seem that Magen Avraham holds that Beit Yosef explicitly explains the root of the custom that they practiced not to bless when sleeping: its source is from the Agur, who wrote that his father instituted this, and it is fitting to act thus, for one who is lenient with blessings does not lose. Beit Yosef explained his words: according to Rabbeinu Tam, even a night's sleep is not an interruption; and although the halachah is not like him, since all the poskim disagree — that is, regarding a night's sleep — but regarding sleep by day one should be concerned for his words; see there. Thus, out of concern for a blessing in vain they practiced not to bless when sleeping by day, and to be concerned for Rabbeinu Tam's view. But if one was awake all night, one should not bless — for the main [halachah] is not like Rabbeinu Tam, except when one slept at night one must bless when day breaks, by the rule of a mitzvah performed at night and day breaks — for Rabbeinu Tam, day causes one to bless; and for the Rosh, sleep causes one to bless. And likewise one who slept by day and was awake all the following night — he blesses in the morning by that rule: for Rabbeinu Tam he must bless, for he was exempted only from the immediate [study]; and for the Rosh, sleep by day is also distraction of mind. And one could say that in such a case he need not bless, for he was exempted by the blessing Ahavat Olam before Kriat Shema of Maariv, for it contains an aspect of birkat haTorah, and it is comparable to Ahavah Rabbah of Shacharit. Nevertheless it seems to me for the main point that he must bless, since according to Rabbeinu Tam he certainly must bless; and also as the Ra'avad holds, and in my humble view he must bless, for they hold that Ahavah Rabbah exempts only what one studies immediately in place — and Pri Chadash agreed to this. And also as the other poskim hold, in the plain sense of the matter Kriat Shema is not called study, as appears from the author's wording, and one should be uncertain whether reading Kriat Shema suffices, etc.; therefore one should be careful to bless birkat haTorah beforehand. And he did not write an exaggeration that if he did not bless beforehand he may not bless birkat haTorah afterward — the implication is that he holds for the main point that Kriat Shema is not called study as the plain sense of the Yerushalmi, but out of a way of caution one blesses first. And so from the Rama's wording in the gloss in siman 52: "nevertheless one says all the blessings as explained in siman 46 and 47" — thus far. From what he hinted at siman 47 it is explained that he holds one blesses birkat haTorah after prayer, for he holds for the main point that Kriat Shema is not study; if so, without studying immediately after the evening prayer he was not exempted by Ahavat Olam. Also at the foundation of Magen Avraham's words on what Shulchan Aruch wrote — it implies that if one was awake all night he need not bless in the morning. I am astonished, for on the contrary, night follows day — it implies that birkat haTorah exempted only day one; although I am not fit to dispute Magen Avraham, nevertheless since he wrote himself in siman 494 for the main point that if one was awake all night he should bless, it is fitting to combine our words that Shulchan Aruch's view is also so; and according to all, if one was awake all night he must bless. At any rate, one who slept by day and was awake all the following night — he certainly should bless by the above rule of mitzvah performed at night and day breaks, and need not be concerned that he was exempted by Ahavat Olam — for according to many poskim it exempts only what one studies immediately, and according to many poskim Kriat Shema is not called study, as is my humble view.
