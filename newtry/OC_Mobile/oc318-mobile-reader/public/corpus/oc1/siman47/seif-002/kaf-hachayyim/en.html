3) [Se'if 2] One must recite the berachah whether studying Scripture or Mishnah, etc. For all of them are called Torah. Levush. The same is true according to the Zohar and Kabbalah, and it is obvious. The kabbalists write that these four categories (Scripture, Mishnah, Gemara, and Kabbalah) correspond to the four worlds of Atzilus, Beriah, Yetzirah, and Asiyah. See the newly printed Nehar Shalom, page 37b. It is known that through the berachah one draws down an encompassing light, and through performance of the mitzvah an inner light, as explained in Etz Chaim, gate 40, derush 2; Shaar HaKavanos, derushim 11-12 concerning Sefiras HaOmer; and many other places. It is also known that all the sparks clarified and mochin drawn down through a berachah and performance of a mitzvah pertain specifically to that berachah and mitzvah. Since Scripture, Mishnah, Gemara, and Kabbalah correspond to four distinct aspects, and one recites the morning berachah over everything he will study that day, possibly including all four, he must therefore intend at the time of the berachah to include them all. The clarifications and the drawing down of mochin appropriate to each will thereby occur. Understand this well.
4) There, in the gloss: Whether Midrash. Maran did not mention Midrash because it is included within Gemara, as Maran writes below in siman 50. Maamar Mordechai 2 writes likewise; see there for his comments on Pri Chadash concerning this se'if.
