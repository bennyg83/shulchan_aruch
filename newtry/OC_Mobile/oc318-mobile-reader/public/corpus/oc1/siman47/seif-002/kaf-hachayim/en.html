(א) There. The blessing on the Torah—whether it is from the Torah or rabbinic, and the practical difference: if it is from the Torah, if he is in doubt whether he blessed or not he must bless; and if it is rabbinic, he need not bless, as Maran wrote in siman 209, seif 3—see there. Behold on this matter the poskim disagreed: the Rav Shaagat Aryeh, siman 24, holds it is from the Torah, and in doubt whether he blessed or not he returns and blesses—see there. And so holds the Maharam of Kutna. And Peri Chadash agreed on this seif. And Beit David, Yad Aharon in Hagahos Tashbetz; and so the view of Maharal HaLevi in Sefer Zaken Aharon siman 60—see there. However, Rav Pnei Moshe in responsa part 1, siman 1, wrote that from the words of Rambam and Tur, who were silent for us with silence and did not write that it is from the Torah, it is implied that he holds it is rabbinic—see there. And so is implied from the words of Maran below siman 209, seif 3, who wrote: in all blessings, if he is in doubt whether he blessed or not he does not bless, etc., except Birkas HaMazon which is from the Torah, end quote—from that he says except Birkas HaMazon it is implied that the blessing on the Torah is in the category of rabbinic blessings. And so from the words of the Agur that Beit Yosef brought in this siman—see there. And so from the words of Levush seif 1, who wrote "and they relied on a verse," etc., and seif 12 who wrote that the blessing does not hinder—see there, it is implied that he holds it is rabbinic. And so holds the Orach Tzedek who wrote in seif 9 that if he interrupted between blessing and study he should not return and bless, for blessings are rabbinic—behold explicitly that he holds the blessing on the Torah is rabbinic. And Mahari Ayash agreed in Sefer Mateh Yehuda letter 1, who wrote that if he is in doubt whether he blessed the blessing on the Torah he should not bless—see there. And see there what he wrote on the words of Peri Chadash; and further see the Chida in Berkei Yosef letter 8 who rejected the proof of Peri Chadash. And further there in Machazik Berachah letters 1 and 2 he agreed that one does not bless out of doubt, because the great rule is: doubt in blessings—be lenient—see there. And Beit David, the Rav HaMagiah in Shatz Dinei Niddah 1a; and so ruled in Kaf HaChaim siman 5, letter 25; Siddur Beit Yaakov in laws of morning blessings letter 22. And so the view of Maamar letter 1, except that he wrote if he is in doubt he can intend to exempt it with Ahavat Olam—see there; and so Kitzur Shulchan Aruch and Yafeh Lelev letter 1, and he wrote: and not as written in Erech HaTalmud in the name of Peri Chadash and Yad Aharon—see there. Nevertheless, the correct practice for one who is in doubt whether he blessed the blessing on the Torah or not: he should try to hear the blessing from another while he has not yet fulfilled his obligation and intend to fulfill; and also tell the one blessing to intend to discharge him, as is known; or he blesses without Name and kingship, and when he reaches the blessing Ahavat Olam he intends to fulfill with it also the blessing on the Torah, in order to fulfill the view of the stringent—see what was written siman 46, letter 52:

(ב) And know that what we said—that if he is in doubt about the blessing on the Torah he does not return and bless—whether regarding all of them or regarding part of what he is in doubt about, he does not bless—for Rav Pnei Moshe wrote there and Peri Chadash brought there: even according to those who hold the blessing on the Torah is from the Torah, it is only one blessing; and so the Rav Shaagat Aryeh in siman 25, that one blessing is from the Torah, whatever it may be, except that he inclined his mind to bless "who chose us," etc.—see there.
