He should rise early in the morning. It is preferable to purchase on Friday rather than on Thursday, and he should say concerning each item he purchases, “This is in honor of Shabbos” (Writings of the Arizal). See siman 572:1, which implies that one should purchase on Thursday; see Seder HaYom. Even if he prepared early in the morning, it is a mitzvah to add further preparations at twilight, as it is stated, “And it shall be double,” etc. (Shiltei HaGibborim; Beit Yosef). The Darkei Moshe writes in the name of the Or Zarua that it is a mitzvah to prepare for Shabbos from Sunday onward, in accordance with Shammai. Although they said that Hillel possessed a different trait, the meaning is that although all his deeds were for the sake of Heaven, he possessed this particular trait; nevertheless, Hillel too concedes that the trait of Beis Shammai is preferable. This is also evident from the incident involving the butcher, which teaches that one should act in accordance with Shammai. The Ramban, however, writes in his commentary on the Torah that the halachah does not follow Shammai. Siman 242 implies agreement with the Or Zarua. The Bach likewise rules so at the end of siman 242, and Rashi writes similarly in his commentary on “Remember,” etc. It is a mitzvah to taste the cooked foods, and one must taste each dish on Friday (Writings of the Arizal).
