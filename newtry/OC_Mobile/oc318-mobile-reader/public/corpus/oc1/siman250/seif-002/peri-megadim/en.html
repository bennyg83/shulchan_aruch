In the phrase “they shall prepare that which they bring,” the word “that which” is understood like the word in “plowshares and pruning hooks,” Isaiah 2:4; see there, and see Michlol under the root alef-tav. The Sifrei expounds the verse “You shall know that your tent is at peace” as referring to sharpening the knife; see Beit Yosef. The Taz adopts the reason given by Sefer Chayei Olam, cited by the Beit Yosef. The Mechaber’s words, which are the wording of the Tur, “for this is his honor,” satisfactorily answer the question raised in Responsa Chavos Yair, siman 205: how could the Sages treat their own honor lightly, when the honor of a great scholar is compared to the honor of Heaven? The answer is that engaging in a mitzvah is itself their honor, when it is evident that they act for the honor of God, as David said, “I will make myself even more lightly esteemed than this.” This differs from returning a lost object by an elder for whom it is beneath his dignity, and similar cases, where the purpose is not evident. See Chavos Yair, Berachos 19b and 20a, and Magen Avraham, paragraph 2; God willing, the matter will be explained there.
