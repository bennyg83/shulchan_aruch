Personally. See Baer Heitev in the name of the teachings of the Arizal: the perspiration a person produces through exertion on Fridays is propitious for erasing sins, like tears; therefore, one should exert himself greatly in honor of Shabbos. Similar statements were written regarding performing the mitzvos on the eve of Pesach. Tosafos Shabbos writes that the principle that it is a greater mitzvah to perform an act personally than through an agent should also be observed at a circumcision feast and a wedding. In Kiddushin we find the incident involving Rabbi Eliezer and Rabbi Yehoshua, who were reclining at the feast of Rabban Gamliel’s son while Rabban Gamliel the Prince stood and served them drink. They said that one greater than he had served others. Chavos Yair, siman 205, however, writes that only degrading service is forbidden, unlike service performed to honor others. He discusses at length the incident in which Rav Huna chopped wood, though this would ordinarily be beneath his dignity. He also cites the dispute between the Rambam and the Rosh concerning returning a lost object: whether a conscientious person may act stringently and return it even where doing so is beneath his dignity. The Sages were not particular about their honor and personally occupied themselves with building a sukkah, drawing water through the public domain for matzah used for a mitzvah, and attending to all the needs of a burial society. He also cites the incident of Rabbi Yehudah bar Ilai dancing with myrtle branches. See there. See also Be’er Yaakov on Choshen Mishpat, which cites the passage in Kiddushin where Rabbi Yehudah found Rav Nachman constructing a parapet, as well as the incident in which they went to the butcher market of Emmaus to purchase an animal for the feast of Rabban Gamliel’s son. Machazik Berachah discusses this at length and cites the Maharit’s commentary to Kiddushin, who writes that Rav Nachman wished to construct the parapet personally because it is a positive commandment upon which one must recite a blessing, and we say that it is a greater mitzvah to perform it personally. See there. See also Berachos regarding Rabbi Ami and Rabbi Assi tying the wedding canopy for Rabbi Elazar, and Kesubos, chapter Metzi’as HaIshah, regarding Hillel, who once found no servant to run before the poor man. See there.
