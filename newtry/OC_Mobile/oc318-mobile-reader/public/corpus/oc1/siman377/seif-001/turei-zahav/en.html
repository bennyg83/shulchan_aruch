She made a depression in the courtyard, etc. In this seif Tur's wording was brought; I greatly struggled to settle this and could not — in the mishnah: two dyot opposite one another, part made a depression and part did not — the one that made is permitted and the one that did not is forbidden; Rava said: only when they did not join; but if they joined, permitted; and if they did not join why forbidden? Rav Ashi said: a decree lest they bring utensils from houses there; Tosafos questioned: if they did not join, both should be forbidden; answer: they do not pour directly into the depression in the courtyard but pour onto the dyot and water descends to the adjacent depression; even though the courtyard is unjoined, sages did not forbid here, etc.; those who did not make a depression are forbidden lest they bring water in house utensils here because they do not want water to fall and ruin the courtyard — a fine distinction between the upper story that made the depression and the opposite one without: the other, being far from the depression, will not pour from the upper story lest water flow to the depression and ruin the courtyard — unlike the one that made the depression adjacent to it who need not pour to the courtyard at all. Tur follows Tosafos and added words without context — in the case they did not join he distinguished upper stories regarding joining for themselves — if one joined for itself and the second did not join at all — what eruv is needed here? Since there is no courtyard eruv together, what eruv does the upper story make for itself without the courtyard? Who requested this? What did he fix? No hint in Gemara and poskim; Tur's own wording is mixed — he began with making the depression forbidding the other for lack of depression, ended with prohibition for not joining for itself; if it needs separate eruv and did not join — what of the depression? Mahariv tried to explain there is a balcony — readers find no answer; he concluded many err with strange explanations; I have seen no explanation; perhaps textual error or omission in Tur; wonder that Beit Yosef did not notice and brought it in Shulchan Aruch — leave it to one with calm heart. Levush was asked and answered: reason from depression near one and far from other is untrue — it depends only on depression and eruv as Tosafos — main distinction between upper stories besides no explanation for "and likewise if residents of the second," etc. — why doesn't one's depression help the other? He wrote without precision forgivingly; Mechaber there properly raises difficulty and he does not answer. Beit Yosef wrote: deals with courtyard less than four amot — if more, do not pour directly unless joined; if not joined each pours in its domain and water goes by force to unjoined courtyard — we do not fear carrying utensils since courtyard measure exists — end; Rabbi thought fear only in small courtyard — wonder: with depression adjacent to other upper story why ruin small courtyard — water flows to depression; what is different in large courtyard — same fear; certainly one fear in large courtyard without joint eruv with depression near one upper story — far one will not pour lest soil be dirtied and lower house utensils — applies also to four amot with depression; Rashi on mishnah: courtyard before them less than four amot because depression law applies to such courtyard; Beit Yosef and Rama: if four amot they do not regularly pour into depression on weekdays but sometimes to courtyard itself — on Shabbat no fear if pouring on upper story descending to ground — Rambam wrote this law chapter 4; Maggid Mishneh: deals with courtyard four by four — doubt.
