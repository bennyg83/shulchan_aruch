1) [Se'if 1] And one made a pit in its courtyard, etc. Rashi's explanation has already been presented in siman 357. A courtyard adjacent to a public domain that does not measure four amos is not capable of absorbing the two se'ah of water a person ordinarily uses each day. One must therefore make a pit in the courtyard into which the water will descend. Then, even if it flows outside, the person is not pleased by that result, and it is not considered as though the person poured directly into a public domain, as stated there. Therefore, if one courtyard made a pit and the residents of both courtyards established an eruv together, both may pour. Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 1.
