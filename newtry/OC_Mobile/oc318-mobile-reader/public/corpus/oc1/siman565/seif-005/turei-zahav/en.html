An individual may not say. The Tur wrote thus in the name of R' Nasan, and the Tur concluded: and I do not know what concern there is in this matter, for it is only like one reading in the Torah, since the Sages said the requirement of ten only concerning any matter of holiness, such as Kaddish and Kedushah and Barechu, end quote. And it is obvious that the Tur's intent is that the individual should intend that his reading not be by way of request, but rather like one reading in the Torah. And so the Beis Yosef brings in the name of the Rashba, who wrote: but if he comes to say them merely by way of reading, he may say them; and the Beis Yosef concluded that the world has not practiced thus. And it appears that the reason is that presumably, since he mentions them, his intent is by way of request, as the congregation does. And in Terumas HaDeshen, siman 8, he concludes that an individual may properly say them with melody and trop, as if reading them in the Torah, and then there is no longer an objection, since they are verses of the Torah; and as he concludes here. And regarding what the Rama wrote, that Selichos too an individual should not say, which is from the words of the Or Zarua, first chapter of Taanis: I do not know the reason, for they contain only petitions alone, and is an individual forbidden to say whatever petitions he wishes? And my master, my father-in-law of blessed memory, permits saying Selichos without "Vayaavor"; and one must say that the prohibition is because one mentions in the Selichos "and remember for us today the covenant of the Thirteen," and similarly in many Selichos one finds that he mentions the merit of the Thirteen Attributes, and those one should not say even without "Vayaavor"; but those Selichos in which the Thirteen Attributes are not mentioned, certainly an individual may say them, and there is no concern in this, as it appears to me.
