In the fast of our affliction. Bach wrote one should practice as Agudah chapter 2 of Shabbat says: to say Aneinu on the day of the fast of my affliction — how can he say “our fast” when they are not fasting? But Rashba did not hold so, for he concludes and wrote: but if he comes to add words of appeasement such as “Accept my repentance and my blood,” even though he says in singular “my,” since it is not the fixed wording of the berachah, etc.; it is difficult — the reason should be how can he say “our milk and our blood” since they are not fasting? Rather there is nothing in this, for it is impossible that there should not be one person in the world fasting that day; so we say in siman 110 regarding one who sets out on a journey — even alone he says “lead us in peace,” because it is impossible there should not be one person at the end of the world also traveling.
