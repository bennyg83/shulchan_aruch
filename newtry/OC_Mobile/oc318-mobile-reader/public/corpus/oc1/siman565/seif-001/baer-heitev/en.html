Our fasting. And the Taz raised a difficulty: how can he say "the fast of our fasting," which is a falsehood, since the many are not fasting today; therefore he should say "Aneinu on the day of the fast of my fasting." And so wrote the Bach. And the Magen Avraham in the name of the Rashba did not write so; rather, even an individual who is fasting may say "our fasting," and it does not appear as a falsehood, for it is impossible that there not be someone at the end of the world who is fasting today; see there. And Yad Aharon agreed with the Bach and Taz. One who is fasting a private fast and was the shaliach tzibbur and prayed the repetition should not say Aneinu in Shomea Tefillah, for when the congregation is not fasting, how can he say Aneinu in the repetition of the prayer? But when he comes to the synagogue and finds that the congregation has already prayed quietly, and he must pass before the ark immediately, he goes down before the ark and prays aloud for the congregation, and then he may say Aneinu, since he is not repeating and praying quietly; Kenesses HaGedolah, see there; and see siman 566, se'if 5.
