1) [Seif 1] An individual recites Aneinu, etc. I found in Kol Bo that Aneinu contains 63 words, corresponding to the 63 letters in the verse, "Yaakov was greatly afraid, etc." In Agudah it is written that they correspond to the 63 letters in the names of the three Patriarchs and the 12 tribes. Eliyah Rabbah, os 1. One can further say that they correspond to the Divine Name of numerical value 63, which relates to the aspect of Binah, and Binah is associated with Gevurah, as it is written, "I am Binah, Gevurah is mine." Therefore, 63 words corresponding to it are recited in the prayer of Aneinu on a fast day, in order to sweeten the severities that are within the aspect of Binah. However, the text in the Sephardic siddur does not contain 63 words. Nevertheless, one who wishes to add two words relevant to the blessing so that there will be 63 may do so, as explained in the responsa of the Rashba, siman 25. The Taz, s.k. 1, cited his words; see there. See above, siman 119:
2) Ibid. An individual recites Aneinu in Shomea Tefillah. Meaning, he does not establish it as an independent blessing between Goel Yisrael and Refa'einu, as does the shaliach tzibbur, but includes it within Shomea Tefillah, as stated below in the Rama's gloss. If he erred and established it as an independent blessing, reciting it between Goel Yisrael and Refa'einu, he certainly should not repeat it in Shomea Tefillah, since he has already fulfilled his obligation. Nevertheless, he should not do so initially, because this independent blessing was instituted only for the shaliach tzibbur:
3) Ibid. An individual recites Aneinu in Shomea Tefillah. If he forgot until after he completed Shomea Tefillah, then even if he has not yet begun Retzei, he should not recite Aneinu there. Rather, he includes it in Elokai Netzor, as written above in siman 119, os 25; see there. See below, seif 2:
4) Ibid. Whether an individual praying with the congregation, etc. Even an individual who prays together with the shaliach tzibbur and recites Kedushah and "LeDor VaDor" with him should nevertheless not recite Aneinu together with him between Goel Yisrael and Refa'einu, but in Shomea Tefillah. Mateh Moshe, siman 753. Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur, os 1. Siddur Beit Yaakov, os 14. Mishna Berurah in Biur Halacha:
5) Ibid. Whether an individual praying with the congregation, etc. One who fasted a private fast and served as shaliach tzibbur should not recite Aneinu in Shomea Tefillah during the repetition, because when the congregation is not fasting, how can he recite Aneinu? If, however, he came to the synagogue and found that the congregation had already prayed silently, and he must immediately go before the lectern, he descends before the lectern and prays aloud for the congregation. He then recites Aneinu, since he is not first praying silently and then repeating the prayer. Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahos Beit Yosef, os 2. Shalmei Tzibbur, 175a. Yafeh LaLev, volume 2, os 1. Yafeh LaLev wrote there that if he does not wish to publicize his fast, see seif 6, he should recite it privately after completing his prayer, in Elokai Netzor, without a concluding blessing, in accordance with the law of one who forgot, written in seif 2, and the law of one who fasts a dream fast on Shabbos, written above in siman 288, seif 6; see there. His father, the author of Ruach Chaim, os 1, cited his words; see there. However, Chayei Adam, klal 132, os 33, wrote that he should recite Aneinu only after completing his prayer; see there. In any event, some authorities prohibit reciting the repetition without first praying silently, as written above in siman 124, os 8; see there:
6) Ibid. Whether an individual praying with the congregation, etc. The Bach wrote that an individual who is not fasting on a communal fast and prays with the congregation may recite Aneinu in Shomea Tefillah, saying, "on this communal fast day"; see there. Ateres Zekeinim cited his words. So too wrote Chayei Adam there. However, Maamar Mordechai, os 1, wrote that although he does not say "on my fast day," it nevertheless appears false, since the prayer he is reciting is on his own behalf. He expressed astonishment at the aforementioned words of the Bach; see there. The Mishna Berurah cited his words in Biur Halacha and wrote that his words are correct, for an individual who is not fasting should not recite Aneinu under any circumstances:
