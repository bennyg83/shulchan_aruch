He should say Aneinu on the day of our fast, etc. I saw here in responsa of Rashba siman 25: a question — an individual who accepted a fast says Aneinu on the day of this fast of mine, as we say “a person includes himself with the congregation,” or he should say in singular. Answer: he says Aneinu, and it is good always to include himself with the congregation, as we do every day; if he wishes to add supplications like his need in singular and like “Accept my repentance and my blood,” and the like, even though he says in singular “my,” etc., end of his words — behold they asked only about the wording Aneinu which is plural, and he answered it is good to include with the congregation, but regarding “on the day of the fast of our affliction” they did not ask and he did not answer — for certainly he should not say it, for it is false since the many are not fasting that day. Rashba's version is correct: “the fast of affliction” — but the prayer for the many that He answer them — even though he prays alone there is no falsehood that he prays also for the many. Maharshal brought in the name of Agudah in chapter Bava Metzia that one should say on the day of the fast of my affliction — so is primary. I wondered why he did not challenge Rama as he wrote, and likewise an individual may say Aneinu on the day of this fast of mine — so is from Tur in siman 566: if the shaliach tzibur is not fasting even though the congregation fasts he says on this day of fasting, and all the more if an individual fasts alone he should not lie to say “our fast.”
