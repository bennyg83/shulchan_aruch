In the Tur it is written here: and some say that since he already finished all eighteen blessings, he may say it with a closing, and there is no concern here of "an individual does not establish a blessing for himself"; and this is not correct in my opinion, end quote. And in siman 220, regarding a Torah scholar who fasts on Shabbos, the Tur brought as follows: and the Bahag wrote that he should say Aneinu in Elokai Netzor. And my master, my father of blessed memory, wrote: even though he establishes a blessing for himself, there is no concern, since he finished the blessings; or he includes it in Elokai Netzor, end quote. And a reason is needed why he did not write there as well "and this is not correct in my opinion," as he wrote here. And in siman 568 the Tur also brought regarding Aneinu on Shabbos that it seems better to include it in Elokai Netzor, but he did not disagree with the first view that holds he establishes a blessing for himself. And it appears to me that there on Shabbos there is no fixed place for Aneinu among the blessings that belong to Shabbos, and therefore we may say that he may properly say it with a closing, for there that is its place on Shabbos; which is not so here, where it has a fixed place in Shomea Tefillah, except that he forgot to say it in its place; therefore he should not say it with a closing, for the closing needs to be in its place. And it is written in the responsum of Maharil, siman 12, that if he uprooted his feet, so that they do not have him repeat: it is good that he intend to follow the shaliach tzibbur word for word and thus fulfill his obligation, as the Bahag and the Geonim explained, end quote. And in siman 124 he ruled that even for matters for which they have him repeat, if he uprooted his feet it helps that he intends to follow the shaliach tzibbur from beginning to end.
