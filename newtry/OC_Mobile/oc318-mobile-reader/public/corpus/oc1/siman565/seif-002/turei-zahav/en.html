He says it without a closing. Tur wrote here: some say that since he already finished all eighteen berachot he may say it with a closing, and there is no concern here of “an individual may not establish a berachah for himself.” In my humble opinion, end of his words. In siman 210 regarding one who fasts on Shabbat as a Torah scholar, Tur brought: Hagahot wrote he should say Aneinu in Elokei Netzor; R' Yitzchak Abuhab wrote: even though he establishes a berachah for himself there is no concern since he finished the berachot, or he includes it in Elokei Netzor, end of his words. A reason is needed why he did not write there too “in my humble opinion” as here; in siman 568 Tur also brought regarding Aneinu on Shabbat that it seems better to include it in Elokei Netzor, but he did not argue with the first view that holds he establishes a berachah for himself. It appears to me that there on Shabbat there is no fixed place for Aneinu among berachot related to Shabbat, therefore we say he may say it with a closing because its place on Shabbat is there; unlike here where it has a fixed place in the [Musaf] prayer of a fast — if he forgot to say it in its place he should not say it in the closing, for the closing must be in its place. Maharil wrote in responsum 12: if he stumbled so they do not return him — it is good that the shaliach tzibur intend word by word and it comes out as Hagahot and Geonim explained, end of his words. In siman 124 he ruled that even for matters for which they return him, if he stumbled it helps if the shaliach tzibur intended from beginning to end.
