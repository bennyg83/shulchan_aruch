After his prayer. It appears to me that he means specifically after his prayer he may say it; but if he remembered before he began Retzeh, he should not say it, for it is an interruption in the prayer. And to this he alluded when he wrote "and see siman 294"; and so wrote the Beis Yosef at the end of siman 114 in the name of the Remazim; and so wrote the Bach there, not as he himself wrote at the end of siman 119. And if you ask: why did he write in the Shulchan Aruch at the end of siman 119 that if the chazzan finished "Refa'einu" he should not go back, because he would need to say Aneinu and afterward "Refa'einu," and it would be a blessing in vain; let him derive it from the fact that it would be an interruption? One may answer that for a shaliach tzibbur who says it as a blessing on its own, it is not applicable to say that it is an interruption; but for an individual it is an interruption.
