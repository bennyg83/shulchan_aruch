And the Rosh raised a difficulty on this: it is astonishing; if so, how can a person lend his fast and repay, for then he is found to be a liar in his prayer? And further, Rav said to R' Yehoshua son of R' Idi, "Let the master lend the fast and repay"; let him say to him, "I have already prayed the prayer of a fast." Rather, certainly, since at the time he prayed his intent was to fast, he is not called a liar if he ate afterward, end quote. The meaning of the Rosh's difficulty is that from the fact that there is a permission to lend the fast even though he already prayed Aneinu at Shacharis, it is clear that he is not called a liar if he ate afterward; and likewise R' Yehoshua should have had to say, "I cannot lend the fast, since I have already prayed Aneinu and I would be a liar." And the Beis Yosef answered that R' Yehoshua had not yet prayed the prayer of a fast; and so it is in Tosafos in the first chapter of Taanis. And in the Semak he added a further difficulty against the Geonim: if rain fell before midday they eat, even though they already prayed Aneinu, and we are not concerned about being a liar. And it appears to me, according to my humble opinion, to answer all the difficulties: here he would be a liar because he conditions the answering [of his prayer] on Hashem answering him on account of the fast, which implies that if not for the fast He would not answer him; and how can he do so, lest bulmus seize him and he lose the answering, or be a liar in asking that He answer him even at that time? Which is not so regarding rain, for he will no longer need answering, since his request has already been fulfilled. I have a further answer: it is stated in siman 569 that regarding rain, specifically for a congregation we say they need not complete the fast, for the heart of the beis din stipulates for them when they will fast or not; which is not so for an individual, for matters of the heart do not avail him; therefore we are concerned about bulmus here for an individual, lest he be found a liar; which is not so there for a congregation. And from R' Yehoshua too there is no difficulty against the Geonim, who hold that he should not pray Aneinu until Minchah: R' Yehoshua certainly also feared bulmus and did not pray at Shacharis; not like the Ran that the Beis Yosef brings, which implies that the early authorities were not concerned about this. And therefore they properly said to lend the fast and repay, since he had not yet prayed Aneinu. And it appears that if he wishes to pray Aneinu at Shacharis and skip "on the fast of our fasting," he has permission to do so, for then there is no concern that he will be found a liar. And so the wording in the Rif implies, who wrote as follows: but if he remembered before he uprooted his feet, let him say thus: "Aneinu, Hashem, aneinu, for we are in great distress," etc. And so it is fitting to do, since the Ran concludes as follows: but if he is confident that he will fast, such as the earlier generations, he mentions [it], end quote; if so, we too should mention it, since he skips and we are not concerned about being a liar, as it appears to me according to my humble opinion.
