[3] When he reaches “in everything,” etc. There is room to question the intent of Rama and Levush whether they mean he should not say at any time of trouble and distress at all, or that he should say it and afterward say “for You hear,” etc.; the wording of Maharil's laws of fast seems somewhat so, and so is printed in some siddurim; but it requires study in Tosafot Taanit 13, from which Rama brought this — their wording: one says “for You, the Omnipresent, answer in time of trouble and save,” and afterward “for You hear prayer,” until here — implying he should say only until “redeems and saves.” Also according to this calculation one intends 63 words with “for You hear,” etc., without the word “and saves”; in truth the word “and saves” is not found in the versions. It appears to me Rama's view is that Tosafot cited the abbreviation of “redeems and saves in every time of trouble and distress,” meaning that the Omnipresent saves; in truth one must say until “and distress” and afterward “for You hear”; but in Hagahot Minhagim between Pesach and Shavuot he wrote: one says until “and I will hear,” and afterward “for You hear,” and so is printed in many siddurim — which is puzzling against all poskim. I again found so in Rambam at the end of Sefer Ahavah, and it requires study.
