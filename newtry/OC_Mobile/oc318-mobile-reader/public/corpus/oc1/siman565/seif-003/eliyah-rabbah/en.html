When he reaches "in every," etc. There is room to doubt regarding the intent of the Rama and Levush: whether their intent is that he should not say "in every time of trouble and distress" at all, or that he should say it and afterward say "for You hear," etc. The wording of the Maharil, Laws of Fasting, implies somewhat the latter, and so it is printed in some siddurim. But this requires study in Tosafos, Taanis folio 13, from which the Rama derived this. They wrote as follows: he should say "for You, Hashem, answer in a time of trouble and save," and afterward "for You hear prayer," end quote. This implies that he should say only until "Who redeems and saves." Also according to this reckoning, the count of 63 words fits with "for You hear," etc., without the word "and save"; and in truth the word "and save" is not found in the versions. It appears to me that according to the Rama, what Tosafos wrote "and save" is their abbreviated citation of "Who redeems and saves in every time of trouble and distress," whose meaning is that the Holy One, blessed be He, saves; and in truth one must say until "and distress," and afterward "for You hear." But in Hagahos Minhagim regarding the period between Pesach and Shavuos he wrote as follows: he says until "and I will hear," and afterward "for You hear"; and so it is printed in several siddurim; and this is puzzling, for it is against all the poskim. I later found likewise in the Rambam at the end of Sefer Ahavah, and this requires further study.
