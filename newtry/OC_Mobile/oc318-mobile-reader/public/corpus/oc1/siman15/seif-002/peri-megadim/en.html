Not. See Taz. The permission to remove them without placing them upon another garment is derived from Tosafos on Shabbos 22a, s.v. “Rav said.” Tosafos write this according to both resolutions there. It is also compelled by the fact that the Gemara did not state the greater novelty that Shmuel permits simple removal from a garment, even though permissive rulings ordinarily state the greater novelty. Furthermore, the Gemara concludes, “one may remove from one garment to another garment,” rather than simply saying, “one may remove,” parallel to “one may light”; see there. This indicates that removal without placing them upon another garment is forbidden. According to Rashi on Menachos 41b, s.v. “Perhaps,” the reason is that it degrades the tallis. There is no degradation of the tzitzis themselves, because they are being placed upon another garment. According to Tosafos there, s.v. “Rav,” the reason is that he removes the tallis from the mitzvah, comparable to removing a mezuzah from a house, which is forbidden. A house is not subject to degradation in this sense. According to Rashi, one may perhaps explain the mezuzah case through the second answer in Shabbos, namely because of harmful forces. A practical difference between the reasons arises where one wishes to place other, more beautiful tzitzis upon this tallis. According to Tosafos it is permitted, because the tallis does not leave the mitzvah. According to Rashi one might say that the original tzitzis are degraded. Nevertheless, it is permitted even according to Rashi, because there is no degradation where he intends to replace them with more beautiful tzitzis. Levush writes the same because of “This is my God, and I will beautify Him.” However, from Taz's wording, “one who removes his tzitzis,” it appears that destroying or tearing them is forbidden, because he ruins a mitzvah object that is fit for another garment. If they are so short that they are unfit for another garment and tying them would create an entirely new form, it is permitted. See siman 12, Taz, seif katan 3. Levush, however, writes that if he wishes to make more beautiful ones and cast these away, it is permitted. This implies that one may invalidate them. I later saw that Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 1, cites Olas Tamid that one may not destroy them, which accords with Taz.
It appears to me. The ruling that, according to Shmuel, one may remove tzitzis from one garment to another, and that the halachah follows him, applies specifically when tzitzis are not readily available in the city. If tzitzis are readily available, one should not remove them from one garment to another, since other tzitzis can easily be purchased at low cost. I derive this as follows. Shmuel maintains that garments stored in a box are obligated in tzitzis. Tosafos on Menachos 41b, s.v. “Rav said,” therefore explain that where a person has only enough tzitzis for one tallis, it is better to place them upon the garment with which he actually covers himself. We may derive permission only for a case in which tzitzis are unavailable. Where they are available in the city, one should not gratuitously remove a tallis from its mitzvah, especially since some authorities maintain that tzitzis is an obligation upon the garment. Although we do not rule that way, one should not remove a tallis from the mitzvah unless no tzitzis are available, as I have written. One cannot object according to Shmuel that he is leaving a tallis without tzitzis. This may be answered according to Mordechai in siman 13: there is no prohibition inherent in possessing a tallis without tzitzis; the obligation is to attach tzitzis. Where a person lacks suitable strings, comparable to Shabbos, he is not in violation. See siman 13, Magen Avraham, seif katan 8. By way of association, regarding mezuzah, Yoreh Deah 291 rules that one may not remove it from its place and does not distinguish whether he intends to affix it in another house. This implies that removal is forbidden in every case. See Darkei Moshe there, who follows Tosafos' second answer on Shabbos, because of harmful forces. Meaning, where people continue to reside there, as Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe write in siman 291, removal causes harm to those who live there. If the house will be closed and no person will live there, one might say that removal is permitted. Nevertheless, if he possesses only one mezuzah and no others are available in the city, he may certainly remove it from one place in order to affix it where he himself intends to reside. According to this, however, why do Tosafos challenge Shmuel from mezuzah? In the tzitzis case it must be that no other tzitzis are available, for otherwise, according to Shmuel's view that garments stored in a box are obligated, removing them would violate a positive commandment. I shall further clarify. Here the Mechaber rules like Shmuel that one may remove tzitzis from one garment to another, as Beer HaGolah writes. In siman 674 the Mechaber likewise rules like Shmuel; see Taz there. Beer HaGolah there, however, writes that the ruling follows Rav and the authority who gives the reason of degradation of a mitzvah. If so, why does he rule here like Shmuel, when the general rule is that the halachah follows Rav in prohibitory matters, were it not for Abaye's statement there on Shabbos 22a, “in all matters, etc.”? The same should apply to Chanukah lights. In my novellae to Shabbos I discussed this at length. Here is not the place to elaborate. With Hashem's help I shall explain it in siman 674. One may say that there the Mechaber ruled like Rav because of the Gemara's question whether lighting accomplishes the mitzvah, etc.
