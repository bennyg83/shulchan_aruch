(Subsection 2) No — and it implies in Tosafot, etc. That this law forbidding placing them on another garment derives from Tosafot Shabbat 22a: Rav and Shmuel dispute — Rav holds one may not transfer tzitzit garment to garment; Shmuel permits; we rule like Shmuel. Tosafot wrote for Rav: even if he holds tzitzit is man's obligation not tallit's, nevertheless why did he say "one may not permit"? As we find regarding mezuzah — one is punished for removing it (Bava Metzia 102a), although it is a house obligation. Later Tosafot for Shmuel: although Shmuel holds tallit obligation, nevertheless he permits since he does it for another garment's need. From this they wrote in Shulchan Arukh: even for us who rule like Shmuel, it was permitted only if placed on another tallit. Though no proof — Tosafot wrote so for Shmuel who holds tallit obligation; but for us who hold man's obligation (as below siman 104), one could say permitted even without another tallit. Nevertheless, since Tosafot concluded for Shmuel: "regarding mezuzah he was punished — perhaps he did not intend to place it in another house" — end quote; mezuzah is house obligation yet punished for not intending another house — so too tzitzit for us: forbidden to untie if he does not intend another tallit. Though Tosafot did not write Shmuel's reason, Magen Avraham holds the reason Tosafot wrote for Rav to forbid even with knot-intent for another tallit is the same reason for Shmuel. Truly on Shabbat Tosafot wrote no reason for Rav; but Menachot 41b: "one should not nullify the mitzvah of tzitzit" — meaning: although no obligation to place tzitzit on a tallit for us (man's obligation), once placed, one should not remove the tallit mitzvah. So Gemara Menachot 43: Tannaim — one who buys a fringed tallit from a non-Jew or merchant is valid; although they said one may not sell a fringed tallit to a non-Jew until he unties his tzitzit. Reason: from merchant valid — if he says he bought from Israel and they were made valid, trusted, for a merchant does not harm his trade and certainly does not lie; therefore the baraita said although they said one does not sell, etc. — if so, this one lies against presumption that one does not harm trade. They say: truly Israel acted improperly and sold to him. Explained below siman 20. From baraita "one may not, etc., until he unties" — not "until he unties and places on another tallit"; surely since sold to non-Jew not subject to obligation, permitted even without another tallit (and apparently one could bring proof from permitting tallit of the dead even without another tallit).

And he wrote in She'iltot, etc. But Tosafot forbid regarding mezuzah — that is, in tractate Shabbat they wrote another answer for Shmuel who permits, and he was punished for removing the mezuzah: "Or: mezuzot are different, made to save from harm" — end quote (meaning: it is like harming another with one's hands). It implies for this reason even if he intended another house it is forbidden. As in responsa of the Rosh general rule 2, brought in Beit Yosef siman 3: "The world did not check tzitzit every hour one wraps in a tallit, for we establish it in presumption of validity; and further tzitzit are not easily invalidated, and people are accustomed to fix them before invalidity" — end quote. Thus custom is to fix others even if the first were not invalidated but only severed somewhat and still valid; see Magen Avraham above siman 8 subsection 11.
