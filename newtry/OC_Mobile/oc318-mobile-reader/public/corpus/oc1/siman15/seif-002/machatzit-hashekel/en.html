(Seif katan 2) Not, and Tosafos implies, etc. This ruling, that it is forbidden to remove them without placing them immediately upon another garment, is derived from Tosafos on Shabbos 22a. Rav and Shmuel disagree there: Rav maintains that one may not remove tzitzis from one garment for another, while Shmuel maintains that one may remove them, and the halachah follows Shmuel in this matter. Tosafos write that according to Rav, even if he maintains that tzitzis is an obligation upon the person rather than upon the garment, his ruling that one may not remove them is nevertheless understandable, just as we find regarding mezuzah that one who removed it was punished, in Bava Metzia 101b, even though mezuzah is an obligation upon the resident. Tosafos then write according to Shmuel, who permits removal, that although Shmuel maintains that tzitzis is an obligation upon the garment, he nevertheless permits removal because the person does so for the sake of another garment. From this the Shulchan Aruch writes that even according to our ruling like Shmuel, removal was permitted only when one places the tzitzis upon another tallis. Admittedly, this passage alone is not conclusive, because Tosafos wrote it according to Shmuel, who maintains that tzitzis is an obligation upon the garment. According to our ruling that it is an obligation upon the person, as explained below in siman 19, one might say that removal is permitted even if he does not place them upon another tallis. Nevertheless, Tosafos conclude there according to Shmuel: “Regarding the mezuzah for whose removal he was punished, perhaps he did not intend to place it in another house.” Although mezuzah is an obligation upon the resident, they write that he was punished because he did not intend to place it in another house. The same therefore applies to tzitzis according to our ruling that it is an obligation upon the person: it is forbidden to remove them if he does not intend to place them upon another tallis. Although Tosafos do not explicitly state Shmuel's rationale, Magen Avraham understands that the reason Tosafos give according to Rav for forbidding removal, even where he intends to place them upon another garment, applies in this respect according to Shmuel as well. In truth, Tosafos on Shabbos do not state Rav's reason either. Tosafos on Menachos 41b, however, write: “One may not nullify the mitzvah of tzitzis.” Meaning, although according to our ruling there is no obligation to attach tzitzis to a garment that is not being worn, once tzitzis have already been attached, it is improper to remove the mitzvah from the tallis. The Gemara on Menachos 43a likewise implies this. It teaches: “One who purchases a tallis with tzitzis, etc., from a non-Jewish merchant, it is valid,” even though the Sages said that one may not sell a tallis with tzitzis to a non-Jew until he removes its tzitzis. The reason a garment purchased from a merchant is valid is that when he says he bought it in that state from a Jew, he is believed that it was properly made, because a merchant does not damage his professional reputation and certainly does not lie. The baraisa then says, “Although they said that one may not sell, etc.” One might think that his statement must therefore be false. This is not so, because the presumption that a merchant does not damage his reputation is stronger, and we assume that a Jew in fact acted improperly and sold it to him. This is also explained below in siman 20. The baraisa says that one may not sell it until he removes its tzitzis. It does not say that he must remove the tzitzis and place them upon another tallis. This shows that because he is selling it to a non-Jew, who is not obligated in the mitzvah, it is permitted to remove them even without placing them upon another tallis. One could seemingly have brought the same proof from the permission to remove tzitzis from the garment of a deceased person, even though they are not placed upon another tallis.
She'iltos writes, etc. But Tosafos forbid regarding a mezuzah. Meaning, Tosafos on Shabbos there offer a second resolution according to Shmuel, who permits removing tzitzis. They write: “Alternatively, mezuzah is different, because it is made to protect from harmful forces.” Meaning, removing it is like directly causing damage to another person. This implies that according to this answer, even if he intends to place the mezuzah in another house, it is forbidden. This accords with responsa Rosh, rule 2, cited by Beit Yosef in siman 8: “The common practice is not to inspect the tzitzis every time one wraps himself in a tallis, because we preserve their prior presumption of validity, etc. Moreover, tzitzis do not easily become invalid, and people ordinarily repair them even before they become invalid.” This shows that the practice is to replace them even though the original strings are not yet invalid, but are merely somewhat severed and remain valid.
