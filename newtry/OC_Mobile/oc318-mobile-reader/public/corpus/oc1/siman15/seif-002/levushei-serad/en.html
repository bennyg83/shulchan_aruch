Magen Avraham, seif katan 3. “This implies that the principal point.” Magen Avraham follows Beit Yosef and disagrees with Taz in two respects. First, Rosh's reason alone does not invalidate the tzitzis, and there is no first category of something already made here, because the tzitzis were attached through an act. Even if he takes the corner and attaches it to another garment, that is not inherently problematic. The second garment is merely an addition to the original corner. Surely it is permitted to sew a patch onto a tallis. Although no act was performed with the tzitzis relative to that patch, the patch is treated as an addition to the tallis. The same applies here. According to Taz, it is not treated as an addition because the piece itself bears tzitzis, as he writes at the end of seif katan 3. According to Magen Avraham, if the corner itself contains the minimum garment size, it may be attached even to another tallis, because its tzitzis were never invalid. The baraisa speaks of a corner measuring one cubit by one cubit, which lacks the minimum garment size. Its tzitzis therefore became invalid, and sewing it onto another garment creates the second category of something already made. Why, then, did Rosh need his reason? Without it, one could ask why a tear outside three fingerbreadths may be sewn, since the tzitzis were initially made validly and the invalidity did not affect them. Rosh answers that where their original making was upon another garment, their present placement on this garment is considered an initial making in an invalid state. It is therefore invalid under the second category. If, however, the corner contains the minimum garment size, it remains valid even when taken from another garment, because it was never invalid. This is Magen Avraham's first disagreement with Taz. Second, if the corner was entirely severed from this very garment outside three fingerbreadths, one may sew it back. Although the tzitzis became invalid, they were initially made validly. Magen Avraham does not accept Taz's reasoning that once the piece was severed it is treated as though it came from another garment. One might ask why the Mechaber writes in seif 3 that if each half contains enough material for wrapping, one may rejoin the pieces. According to Magen Avraham's explanation, this is no novelty at all. There is no problem of something already made because the tzitzis never became invalid, and Rosh's reason alone does not invalidate. Moreover, since this piece came from the same garment, Rosh's reason does not apply at all. Even if it lacked the minimum garment size it should be permitted, just like a tear outside three fingerbreadths that may be sewn even after complete severance. It appears that the minimum garment size is itself the novelty. One might have thought that because the piece has the status of an independent garment, its tzitzis are treated as though they came from another garment, and Rosh's reason would apply, as Taz maintains. This would make it worse than a strip torn outside three fingerbreadths, which retains only the status of a corner, not of an independent garment. The Mechaber therefore teaches that it is nevertheless valid. Rosh's reason alone does not invalidate. It serves only to create a case of something already made when combined with actual invalidity. Here, because the piece retained the minimum garment size, there was no invalidity. This is why Magen Avraham concludes, “and the same appears from seif 3.” At first glance the statement is unclear. He brings proof for his explanation that Rosh's reason alone does not invalidate, but only when combined with something already made. If Rosh's reason alone sufficed, then in seif 3, where each half has the minimum garment size, the tzitzis would be as though they came from another garment, and Rosh's reason would apply. This differs from the case of a tear outside three fingerbreadths, where the torn strip never acquired the status of an independent garment. Since seif 3 nevertheless validates it, Rosh's reason alone cannot invalidate. It invalidates only when combined with something already made. The practical result of Magen Avraham's approach is as follows. A piece containing the minimum garment size may be attached even if it came from another garment, because there is no invalidity of something already made. A piece from this same garment, even if smaller than the minimum garment size, may be reattached so long as it is at least three fingerbreadths wide. In seif 3, the ruling concerning a tallis divided into two is not exact, because even pieces from two different talleisim may be joined. Consider this carefully.
