{Rama: Magen Avraham subsection 3} It implies that the main ruling follows the Beit Yosef's method, and he disputes the Taz in two matters. First: the Rosh's reason alone does not disqualify at all, and the first part of "you shall make and not from what is already made" does not apply here, since the tzitzit were made through an act; and even if one takes the corner and joins it to another garment — we have no concern, for the other garment is like an addition to the corner of the first garment. Is it forbidden to attach a patch to a tallit because that patch has no tzitzit in the act? For the patch it would be "from what is already made"; nevertheless the patch is an addition to the tallit (and for the Taz it is not an addition here since it has tzitzit — see his words end of subsection 3). So too here: if the corner has measure to wrap oneself, it is permitted to join it to another tallit, and the Rosh's reason alone does not disqualify. This deals with a corner — a cubit by a cubit — and the baraita lacks tallit measure; the tzitzit were disqualified and it is "you shall make and not from what is already made" of the second part. Why did the Rosh need his reason? So that "outside three he may sew" should not be difficult, since their making began valid; here too their making on this corner began valid. The Rosh's reason: since their making was on another garment, now on this garment their making begins invalid — invalid because of the second part of "you shall make and not from what is already made." But a corner with tallit measure is valid even from another garment, since it was never disqualified. One dispute with the Taz (that is, regarding a corner with tallit measure, valid even from another garment). Second: if torn entirely from this garment outside three, he may sew — for although the tzitzit were disqualified, their making began valid, and the Taz's reasoning that once torn from the garment it is as on another garment is not accepted. Why did the author write in section 3 that with wrap measure one may join the parts (as Magen Avraham explained in subsection 4)? In truth this is no novelty, for there is no "you shall make and not from what is already made" since they were not disqualified, and the Rosh's reason alone does not disqualify; and since it was from this garment, the Rosh's reason does not apply. Even with less than wrap measure it is permitted — that is the law of "outside three he may sew" even if torn entirely without wrap measure. Wrap measure is a stringency: since there is tallit measure, the tzitzit in it are as on another garment and the Rosh's reason applies — per the Taz, who holds the Rosh's reason alone disqualifies. This is stricter than "outside three he may sew," where the Rosh's reason does not apply since the strip was severed — it has only the name of corner, not garment; unlike where there is tallit measure, where the Rosh's reason applies whether it was entirely on another garment or torn here into two garments (Taz subsection 3 teaches nevertheless valid): the Rosh's reason alone does not disqualify; only the Rosh's reason combined with "you shall make and not from what is already made." Here there was tallit measure, so no "you shall make and not from what is already made"; therefore in any case valid. If there was tallit measure, no "you shall make and not from what is already made"; if not, their making on this garment began valid — understand well. Magen Avraham concluded thus and wrote "so too in section 3" — seemingly unclear, but he brings proof for his view that the Rosh's reason alone disqualifies only with "you shall make and not from what is already made." If the Rosh's reason alone could disqualify, then in section 3 with tallit measure it would be as if the tzitzit were on another garment and the Rosh's reason would apply — unlike "outside three he may sew," where no garment name remains on the tear. Thus the Rosh's reason alone disqualifies only with "you shall make and not from what is already made"; therefore even with tallit measure, where the Rosh's reason exists, it is valid, for there is no "you shall make and not from what is already made." Practical law from Magen Avraham's method: with tallit measure one may join even from another garment, since there is no "you shall make and not from what is already made" from this garment; even with less than measure, whatever is three [fingerbreadths] wide may be joined. In section 3, "tallit that they divided" — not specifically; even from two tallits one may join. Analyze well.
