(5) He may not. See Magen Avraham, seif katan 3. His words imply that the restriction applies specifically to a corner that does not contain enough material for wrapping. Although the Gemara forbids even a piece measuring one cubit by one cubit, that too does not contain enough material for wrapping according to his view. If the detached piece did contain enough material for wrapping, it may be joined to another garment even with its tzitzis attached. Taz, however, disagrees in seif katan 3 and forbids it. Levush and Derech HaChayim likewise imply stringency.
(6) With the tzitzis. Without the tzitzis, it is permitted to attach the piece if it measures at least three by three fingerbreadths, and afterward tzitzis may be attached to it. None of the tzitzis on the rest of the garment need be removed. This applies only if, before the corner was attached, the garment was still obligated in tzitzis, such as where the corner was not torn off in a rounded form. If it was torn in a rounded form and the garment became three-cornered and exempt from tzitzis, all the tzitzis must be removed and reattached after the garment again becomes four-cornered. Otherwise it is “you shall make,” and not something already made.
(7) Upon another garment. If he wishes to reattach the corner to the same garment from which it was torn, then even if it was completely severed and no connection remained at all, he need not remove its tzitzis. They are not invalid because of “you shall make,” and not something already made, since they were originally made validly upon this garment. This applies only if the garment did not become three-cornered through the tear, as explained in seif katan 6, and specifically if the corner measures at least three by three fingerbreadths, as explained in seif 4. All this follows Shulchan Aruch and Magen Avraham, seif katan 3. Taz, however, rules in seif katan 3 that there is no distinction between joining it to another garment and rejoining it to this same garment; both involve “you shall make,” and not something already made. One should be stringent. Derech HaChayim likewise rules this way.
