And if it tore and some remained, etc. — That is to say, if the tear was before affixing the tzitzit; therefore he must sew it first so that there is the measure of a thumb-knot at the time of affixing the tzitzit, and we need to know the laws of sewing; for this reason he concludes "if it is of wool, etc." But if the tzitzit were affixed before the tear, he does not set out the laws of sewing, for there it is not necessary to sew. And the wording "and he shall sew it" is not precise, for he is now engaged in the laws of sewing — how to act — as he concludes afterward: "he should not sew it," according to Rashi. And it is possible that this is what he means: if it is of wool, he did not transgress any prohibition, even according to Rashi; but if it is of other kinds, he transgressed a prohibition according to Rashi. He did not wish to say that it is invalid, because it is not decided for him in this matter, as he concludes afterward: "there is room to doubt," as the Gra wrote. He also did not wish to say here as well "there is room to doubt," because he truly holds the main law per the view of Rabbi Akiva, as he wrote in the Beit Yosef; therefore he does not conclude his main doubt, only on the law that he wrote afterward, where the doubt applies also per the view of Rabbi Akiva, as I wrote in the Mishnah Berurah; see there.

There is room to doubt — See in the Mishnah Berurah, and see in the Gra, who wrote that the author's intent is that even according to Rashi there is also room to doubt, even if he sews with threads of the same kind, for perhaps according to Rashi it is forbidden only at the outset and not to invalidate after the fact.
