(18) From the hole. This concerns a case where he did not sew the tear. The ruling teaches that although less than the distance of a thumb joint remained, it is valid, as explained above in siman 11, seif 10, because that measurement is required only at the time the tzitzis are made. If he wishes to sew the tear and the garment is wool, he should sew it with thread of other materials. He need not remove the tzitzis from the corner and reattach them after sewing, because the corner never became invalid. If the garment is made of other materials, according to Rashi he may not sew it with thread of the same material as the garment, lest some thread remain and be used for tzitzis. This concern applies even though tzitzis are already present, because the Sages did not distinguish. He should use thread of another material. It is preferable not to sew it with white thread, but rather with colored thread, because nowadays the custom is to use only white tzitzis, so there is no concern that colored sewing thread will be used for tzitzis.
(19) It is valid according to all opinions. According to Rashi, since the garment is wool, there is no concern that one will use sewing thread of another material for tzitzis. According to Rav Amram, because even a small amount of the fabric remained connected, it did not lose the status of a garment, as explained in the preceding seif. Certainly, according to the alternative opinion in the preceding seif, it is valid here because the tzitzis were attached after the sewing.
(20) He should not sew it. Meaning, not with thread of the same material. He may be lenient with thread of another material, and it is preferable to use colored thread, as explained in seif katan 18.
(21) If it tore, etc. Meaning, it tore completely from the hole to the edge. There is therefore room for doubt. Even if the garment is wool and he sews it with thread of other materials, which is certainly permitted according to Rashi, perhaps it is nevertheless invalid according to Rav Amram. Since no connection at all remained, the preceding seif explains that according to the first opinion it is treated as completely severed, and sewing may not help even if he attaches tzitzis afterward. Perhaps this case is better, because the corner remains whole on both sides and only the middle was split. This is the doubt of Shulchan Aruch. Taz disagrees and rules that there is no doubt: all agree that if he attaches tzitzis after sewing, it is valid. Nevertheless, it is preferable before sewing to place beneath the corner a patch measuring three by three fingerbreadths, thereby satisfying all opinions. If this is impossible, one may be lenient in accordance with Taz. In any event, we wrote in seif katan 15 that the later authorities maintain that sewing creates a connection and helps even when the corner was entirely torn from its place.
