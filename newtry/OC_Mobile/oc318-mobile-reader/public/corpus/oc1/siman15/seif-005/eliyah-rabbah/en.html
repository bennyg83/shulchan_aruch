The Bach wrote: it seems specifically when he wraps himself in each part by itself; but if he joins to each part another piece, even though the other has no tzitzit at all, the original tzitzit remain valid because of "you shall make and not from what is already made," for when he began to sew the others at one corner, that corner was nullified and there remain only three corners, and it becomes "you shall make and not from what is already made" — so far. Olat Tamid wrote that his words are correct. It seems from his words that if it had five corners and he made other corners on it so that it should have four, it is certainly valid — so far. It further seems that if he sews the middle of the garment together from the outset and afterward the corners, the Bach concedes that it is valid in every respect; however, the Taz challenged the Bach and validates the original tzitzit forever, and so it seems from the responsa of the Rashba siman 217, and so it seems more reasonable to say that when he begins to sew others at one corner and they both become one garment, and it becomes five corners, it is valid. it seems to me, per the Bach's view, since the questioner asked the question in the responsum of Maharam Mintz siman 13 — see there and analyze. And one may wonder at the later authorities who did not mention it; nevertheless, from the answer that Maharam answered there, it seems to permit.
