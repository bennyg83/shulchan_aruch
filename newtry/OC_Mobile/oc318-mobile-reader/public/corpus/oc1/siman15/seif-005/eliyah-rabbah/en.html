[5] It does not involve, etc. He may complete the tzitzis of each half. Bach writes that this applies specifically when he wraps himself in each half independently. If he joins another piece of fabric to either half, then even if the added piece has no tzitzis, the original tzitzis are invalid because of “you shall make,” and not something already made. When he begins sewing the additional piece onto one corner, that corner becomes nullified and the garment has only three corners. When he completes the sewing, it again becomes four-cornered, and the original tzitzis are then already made. Olas Tamid writes that Bach's words are correct. It appears from his words that if the garment had five corners and one corner was removed so that it became four-cornered, it is certainly valid. It further appears that if he first sews together the middle of the garment and only afterward sews the corners, Bach would agree that it is valid in every case. Taz, however, disputes Bach and always validates the original tzitzis, and writes that this is implied by responsa Rashba, siman 217. It also appears more reasonable to say that when he begins sewing the other piece at one corner, the two pieces become one garment with five corners, which is valid. In my humble opinion, the questioner in responsa Maharam Mintz, siman 13, intended Bach's reasoning; see there carefully. I am surprised that the later authorities did not mention it. Nevertheless, Maharam's response there appears to permit it.
