(Seif katan 5) Sewing thread that was not spun for the sake of the mitzvah. See siman 14, seif 2. Rashi indeed gives the reason of “you shall make,” and not something already made. Tosafos on Menachos 40b, s.v. “lest it tear,” object to Rashi's explanation: “He should have said that either spinning or attaching must be for the sake of the mitzvah. But there is no issue of something already made, according to what we explain below concerning strings made from loose tufts.” Magen Avraham therefore writes the reason that spinning must be for the sake of the mitzvah. Although Tosafos also write that attaching must be for the sake of the mitzvah, Magen Avraham omits that reason. He alludes to this by writing, “See siman 14, seif 2,” where two views are cited whether attaching must be for the sake of the mitzvah. He therefore prefers the reason based upon spinning, regarding which no authority disputes that intention is required. Tosafos rejected Rashi's explanation of something already made concerning strings made from loose tufts on Menachos 42b because the authority who states that such strings are invalid is Rav, and Rav does not accept the disqualification of something already made in this context. Tosafos write there: “Rashi explains that it is because of something already made. This is difficult, because the later act is the making itself, as Rav himself says: if one attached them and afterward cut the ends of the strings, it is valid, because that is their making. Rather, the reason is that attaching must be for the sake of the mitzvah.” This applies specifically according to Rav. According to our ruling, we do not follow Rav in this matter. On the contrary, we rule that if he attached them and afterward cut them, they are invalid, as stated above in siman 11, seif 13. Nevertheless, it is explained there that they are invalid because of something already made only if he already made at least one winding and one knot before cutting. Here, however, he has not yet made any winding or knot. Tosafos' objection from Rav is therefore the stronger objection. The words of Tosafos on 40b, s.v. “lest it tear,” likewise show that they raised the objection according to all opinions, as explained.
