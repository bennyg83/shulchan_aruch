6) If one string was severed, even if enough remains to tie a bow and it would have been valid had it remained on the corner, Maran writes in Beis Yosef that it is invalid for placement on another garment; see there. Birkei Yosef, paragraph 3, writes: Although Maran omitted this law from Shulchan Aruch, which somewhat implies that he was uncertain, the law is true and Maran's explanation is firmly correct. This is especially so now that Rabbi Betzalel cites it in the name of a marginal gloss from an early rabbi. The words of Rashi, the Rosh, and Rabbeinu Yerucham, who require the strings to be whole, likewise prove it, and Rif cites it. Birchas HaZevach also writes that the law is true, and Taz, Magen Avraham, and Eliyah Rabbah record it. It appears that Maran's statement that it is invalid for placement on another garment refers only to the initial practice, as implied by Rabbi Betzalel's wording cited by Birkei Yosef; Birkei Yosef equates Rabbi Betzalel's wording with Maran's view. The same follows from Taz to siman 12, paragraph 3. Magen Avraham to this siman, paragraph 1, explicitly writes that placing it on another garment is forbidden only initially. Olas Tamid, paragraph 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 2; Pri Megadim, Mishbetzos Zahav to siman 12, paragraph 3; Shaliach Tzibbur, folio 109b. Regarding Shaliach Tzibbur's statement that Magen Avraham validates it after the fact if less than enough to tie a bow remains, see Zechor LeAvraham, volume 3, paragraph 90. Siddur Beis Oved, paragraph 2; Shesilei Zeitim to siman 12, paragraph 7. This is also implied by Chayei Adam, rule 11, paragraph 20, unlike Machatzis HaShekel.
