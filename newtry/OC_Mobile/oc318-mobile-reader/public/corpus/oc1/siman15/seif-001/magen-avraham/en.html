“Permitted to untie.” If one string snapped, even if a kezayit-sized piece remains, ab initio it is forbidden to attach it to another garment (Beis Yosef end of responsum). It implies that if he wishes to tie onto it string until it reaches twelve thumbbreadths he is permitted, because we had to write regarding tefillin “and you shall bind them” that the binding must be whole—so that if it snaps he may not tie, as stated at the end of seif 33; from which in general tying is a superior connection; likewise in Mishnah Negaim chapter 8, mishnah 11, tying counts as one string for negaim, and the halacha follows the anonymous Mishnah—see there; likewise in this siman, seif 4, it wrote “he adds seven strings,” etc., which is impossible except by tying; and though in some books it says six strings, in most books it says seven strings, and so in Beis Yosef—hence it is proved that tying is permitted. That Beis Yosef responsum 11 in the Rambam’s name wrote one may spin one string partly tekheles and partly white, etc.—that is ab initio; but if one string snapped, tying it is permitted ab initio, as above.

“No.” Tosafos implies the reason is that it is forbidden to nullify the mitzvah of this tallis. It appears to me that if he wishes to make from the tallis a garment that will not have four corners he is permitted to remove them; likewise in the Gemara, page 43, that he may remove them if he sells the tallis to a gentile, since then he is no longer obligated—see there. It is written in Sheiltos, parashas Shelach Lecha, that when one sells a tallis to a friend it is forbidden to remove the tzitzis unless he wishes to attach them to another tallis; the same applies to a mezuzah: if he wishes to affix it on another door he may remove it—end quote. But Tosafos forbid removing a mezuzah. The Levush wrote that if he wishes to make nicer tzitzis he may remove them; it appears to me the same applies if they snapped from the first set even though still valid—he may untie them and make complete ones—likewise in the Rosh’s responsa, klal 2.
