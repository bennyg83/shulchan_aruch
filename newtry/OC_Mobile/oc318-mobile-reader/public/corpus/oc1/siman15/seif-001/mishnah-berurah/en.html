(1) And place them, etc. This is permitted even from one new tallis to another new tallis, and certainly from an old tallis to a new tallis that he wishes to wear and use for the mitzvah. It is likewise permitted to transfer them from a tallis gadol to a tallis katan, because a tallis gadol and tallis katan have equal status. One may not, however, remove tzitzis from an adult's tallis and place them upon a minor's tallis, whose obligation is only D'Rabbanan. Peri Megadim remains uncertain whether one may transfer tzitzis from a wool tallis to a garment of other materials according to the authorities cited above in siman 9, seif 1, who maintain that other materials are obligated only D'Rabbanan. Artzos HaChayim is lenient.
(2) Upon another tallis. See Peri Megadim, who writes that if other tzitzis are readily available, it is proper to be stringent and not remove these tzitzis, except in the manner explained in seif katan 4.
(3) Not upon another garment. This is forbidden because he needlessly degrades a tallis used for a mitzvah. If the tallis became worn out, he may remove its tzitzis in every case. The same applies if he wishes to sell it to a non-Jew, or convert it into a garment that will not have four corners. If he wishes to sell the tallis to a Jew, however, he may not remove its tzitzis, even though the purchaser could attach tzitzis himself. It is permitted to remove tzitzis and replace them with more beautiful ones because of beautifying the mitzvah, or to replace old strings with new ones. Likewise, if one string was severed and only enough remained to tie a bow, although the tzitzis are still valid, he may remove them and replace them with complete strings. Where the strings are complete and fit to be used upon another garment, unlike strings that retain only enough to tie a bow, some later authorities write that one should carefully untie their knots and windings rather than cut or tear them, so that they are not destroyed. The Sages said that one should not spill out water from his cistern when others need it. Chayei Adam writes that where untying them is excessively burdensome, one may cut them. This does not violate bal tashchis because he is not acting destructively.
(4) One who is obligated. Even one who is obligated only D'Rabbanan.
