(1) And to place them, etc. — even from a new tallis to a new tallis, and all the more so from an old tallis — one may untie and place them on a new tallis one wishes to wear and fulfill the mitzvah with. Also even from a large tallis to a tallit katan — tallit gadol and tallit katan are equal; but one may not untie tzitzis from a large person's tallis and place them on a child's tallis, for the child's obligation is only rabbinic; and in Peri Megadim they were uncertain whether one may transfer tzitzis from a wool tallis to a tallis of other materials — for those who hold above in siman 9 seif 1 that other materials are only rabbinically obligated; and Aruchat Chaim is lenient about this.

(2) On another tallis — see Peri Megadim who wrote that if he has other tzitzis available to obtain, it is proper to be strict not to untie his tzitzis unless in the manner explained in siman katan 4.

(3) On another garment, no — because there is a prohibition of disgracing a tallis of a mitzvah for nothing. If the tallis is not worn out, then one may untie its tzitzis in every case; or if he wishes to sell it to a gentile, or to make from the tallis a garment that will not have four corners — one may remove them. But if he wishes to sell the tallis to a Jew, it is forbidden to take its tzitzis, even though the second Jew could tie tzitzis himself — nevertheless it is forbidden. One may remove the tzitzis and put nicer ones underneath because of hidur mitzvah, or when they are old and he removes them in order to renew them, or when one string snapped and only enough for a knot remains — even though the tzitzis is still valid, nevertheless one may remove and put complete ones underneath. And know further that where the tzitzis are complete and fit to be placed on another garment (excluding if only enough for a knot), some later authorities wrote that one should be careful to untie their knots and windings and not cut and tear them so as not to destroy them; and our sages already said: a person should not empty his cistern while others need [the water]. Chayei Adam wrote that where the trouble of untying is burdensome in his eyes, he may detach them and there is no bal tashchit here, since he is not doing destructive action — see there.

(4) Bar chiyuva — even if he is only obligated rabbinically.
