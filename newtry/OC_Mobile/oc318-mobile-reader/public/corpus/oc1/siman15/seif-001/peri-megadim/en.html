And place them. See Taz. This is the wording of Rashi on Menachos 41a, s.v. “And all agree,” that one may bring techeiles, meaning remove it from another garment, etc. Taz was dissatisfied with this explanation, because what novelty is taught by permitting removal from one garment to another? Even according to Shmuel there, neither the issue of “you shall make,” and not something already made, mentioned there, nor Rashi's interpretation of “upon the corners of their garments” appears relevant here. Taz therefore explains that “you shall make,” and not something already made, does not apply here at all. The requirement of “upon the corners of their garments,” however, as the Mechaber writes in seif 2, and as Rashi explains there, is somewhat relevant. One might have thought that the verse requires the tzitzis to be made specifically upon the corner of this garment, and once they were made upon the corner of another garment they can no longer be placed upon a different garment. The ruling therefore teaches that they are valid.
Perishah writes. He introduces a novel ruling. If one suspended long strings between two corners and tied only one set of tzitzis with a knot and winding, while the second set remained merely inserted, even though such insertion is valid under the circumstances described in siman 11, seif 13, here it is invalid. Since one set of tzitzis was already completed with a knot and winding, it invalidates the mere insertion in the second corner. This ruling appears novel to me. The later authorities do not mention it, and Beit Yosef likewise does not accept it, as is evident from his comments on Tur. For this reason it was omitted from Shulchan Aruch.
