Shulchan Aruch, seif 2. “And this corner was not there.” Know that there are two categories within “you shall make,” and not something already made. The first, which is the primary meaning of the verse, is that the tzitzis must come into being upon the garment through an act. One may not take already completed tzitzis and simply hang them upon the garment, because that is something already made. The second category is what the Sages meant by “and not something made through an invalid state.” Even though he attached them to the garment through his own act, if they were invalid at that time, the act is treated as nonexistent. When they later become valid, they are something already made. This second category applies specifically where their initial making was invalid. If they were initially made validly, later became invalid, and then returned to validity, they remain valid, unless the invalidity affected the tzitzis themselves. In that case the original act is nullified, and it is as though they were never made validly. This is explained in Magen Avraham, siman 23, and in our words above in siman 12, letter 4. The distinctions do not apply to the first category, because no act was performed upon them at all. I shall now explain the general laws of this siman according to Taz. Seif 2 is based upon the statement in the chapter HaTecheiles that all agree one may not bring a piece measuring one cubit by one cubit from elsewhere with techeiles attached and hang it upon the garment. Rosh gives the reason, cited by Taz in seif katan 4, from the verse “upon the corners,” as the Mechaber writes. Taz maintains that this invalidity is not based upon the second category, namely that the corner lacked the minimum garment size and its tzitzis became invalid. That cannot be the reason, because they were initially made validly and the invalidity did not affect the tzitzis themselves. Rather, when the corner is attached to another garment it is invalid because of Rosh's reason: relative to the second garment, the tzitzis are something already made. Therefore Taz rules in seif katan 4 that even if the corner was entirely severed from this tallis outside three fingerbreadths, it may not be sewn back, unlike Beit Yosef, because of “you shall make,” and not something already made. Rosh's additional reason is needed because this is the first category. Although the tzitzis were attached to the original garment through an act, they came to this garment without an act. Once this is so, even if the piece measuring one cubit by one cubit contained enough material for wrapping, or many cubits, so that the tzitzis never became invalid, they would still be invalid when attached to the other garment. This is not the second category, because they were initially made validly. It is the first category, according to Rosh. Taz maintains that even though the tzitzis were originally made upon this very garment, once the corner was completely severed it is as though it came from another garment. This is what Taz writes in seif katan 3, where he invalidates even when the tallis was divided into two and each half retained the minimum garment size. The tzitzis on each half are treated, relative to the other half, as though they had been upon a different garment at the time of their making. According to Taz, Rosh's phrase “at the time of making” does not mean only the moment when the tzitzis were first attached. It means that while the tzitzis remain attached, they must continuously remain upon this garment. Once the piece is completely severed, it is treated as another garment, and sewing it back creates the first category of something already made. The baraisa's phrase “from elsewhere” is therefore not exact. Even a piece from this garment has the same law once it was completely severed. The practical conclusion according to Taz is that whenever a piece was entirely severed, even if the piece itself contains the minimum garment size, sewing it back creates something already made, because of Rosh's reason and the first category. Some portion must remain attached. The required attached portion is specifically three fingerbreadths, as explained by Taz in seif katan 4. According to this, seif 2 is entirely superfluous as practical law, because even if the corner remains attached to this very garment by less than three fingerbreadths, it may not be sewn. The baraisa says that all agree only to teach that even according to the opinion that a remaining connection of less than three fingerbreadths suffices, if the corner came from another garment, or was entirely severed from this corner, it may not be sewn. Taz explains in seif katan 4 that the baraisa is needed only according to the view that three fingerbreadths are unnecessary. According to the practical ruling that three fingerbreadths are required, seif 2 adds nothing. Beit Yosef disputes Taz throughout and follows the approach of Magen Avraham, which I shall explain in my comments to Magen Avraham, seif katan 3. Consider this carefully.
