{Rama: Shulchan Arukh section 2:} And this corner was not. Know that there are two parts in "you shall make and not from what is already made." Part one — the main meaning of the verse "you shall make" — is that tzitzit must be formed on the garment through an act, not that one takes ready-made tzitzit and hangs them on the garment, which would be "from what is already made." Part two is their statement that invalid tzitzit are "not from what is already made": although he hung them on the garment through his act, if they were invalid his act is as if it were not, and when validated later it becomes "from what is already made." This second part applies only if their initial making was invalid; but if initially valid, then invalidated and revalidated, they are valid — unless the invalidity is in the tzitzit themselves, when the first act is nullified as if never made valid (Magen Avraham siman 13; see our words siman 12 letter 4). In the first part these distinctions do not apply, since he performed no act in them. Now I explain the general laws of this siman per the Taz. This section follows what we say in the chapter on tekhelet: "And they agree that one should not bring a cubit by a cubit from elsewhere with tekhelet and hang it." The Rosh gave the reason [brought by] Taz subsection 4: because of "on the corners of their garments," as the author wrote. The Taz holds this disqualification is not from the second part — that since the corner lacks tallit measure (explained in the next siman) the tzitzit are disqualified and it is "you shall make and not from what is already made" of the second type, for their making began valid and the invalidity is not in the tzitzit themselves; therefore joining to another garment is invalid because of the Rosh's reason, for regarding another garment it is "from what is already made." Therefore the Taz ruled in subsection 4 that even outside three [fingerbreadths], if torn entirely from the tallit, it is forbidden to sew (unlike the Beit Yosef), because of "you shall make and not from what is already made" (for the Rosh needed his own reason — the first part: although he hung them on the first garment with an act, for this garment they came without act; and even if a cubit-by-cubit measure for wrapping remained, or several cubits so the tzitzit were not disqualified at all, still if…). It is not from the second part, for its making began valid, but from the first part — the Rosh's reason. The Taz holds that although tzitzit were initially made on this garment, once torn entirely from the garment it is as if from another garment (Taz subsection 3), and the Magen Avraham disqualifies there even if the tallit is divided in two and each part has tallit measure — forbidden to join, because tzitzit in each part regarding the other part are as from another garment at the time of making. The Taz holds "time of making" means not when he hung the tzitzit but while they hang they must remain on this garment; once torn it is as from another garment, and when resewn it is "you shall make and not from what is already made" of the first part. The baraita's "not bring a cubit on a cubit from elsewhere" — the Taz explains: not specifically elsewhere; even from this garment the law is the same once torn entirely, as stated. Practical law for the Taz: whatever is torn entirely — even if the garment still has tallit measure — when resewn it is "you shall make and not from what is already made" because of the Rosh, as if on another garment (first part), unless something remains attached; then it may be sewn. The measure that must remain attached is specifically three fingerbreadths (Taz subsection 4). Thus section 2 is entirely unnecessary for practical law: even if the corner remains on this garment, if the attachment is less than three it is forbidden to sew. The baraita taught "and they agree" only to inform us that even for the view that less than three suffices, if the corner is from another garment or torn entirely from this corner, sewing is forbidden — as the Taz explains in subsection 4. The "and they agree" is needed only for the view that less than three suffices; but practically, since three is required, section 2 is unnecessary. The Taz gave no explanation until this section because the Beit Yosef disputes all this with the Taz, as the Magen Avraham does — as I will explain in Magen Avraham subsection 3; analyze.
