(Subsection 1) Permitted, etc. Forbidden at the outset, etc. — Beit Yosef, Gemara in Menachot 41a: "And they agree that one brings tzitzit from elsewhere and hangs them" (and Rashi permits tzitzit from another tallit and hangs them, for there is no "you shall make and not from what is already made," since one must return to wind and fix them as at first). The baraita concludes: provided they are not interrupted. Rashi: all threads must be whole. The rabbi in Beit Yosef wondered: obvious — what does it teach? Therefore Rabbeinu Yosef explained: even if they have enough for a knot — on the first tallit they were valid — nevertheless on the second tallit where he places and fixes them at first they are invalid. If so, what Magen Avraham wrote, "forbidden at the outset," is not precise that even after the fact they are invalid, as in Rabbeinu Yosef; rather he cited the reason that Magen Avraham disqualifies because one does not fix at the outset.

And it implies that if he wishes to tie, etc. — Not on the above thread that remained with enough for a knot, but on the general case: if one may tie a thread shorter than twelve thumbbreadths, it is permitted to tie a thread to extend it to twelve thumbbreadths.

So too in Mishnah 8 chapter 1 of Nega'im: warp or woof defiles in nega'im, specifically when the threads of warp or woof suffice to weave from them a garment three by three fingerbreadths; if the threads were severed they do not combine, but if he tied them again — per the first Tanna they combine, and Rabbi Yehudah holds they do not combine; Rabbenu Gershom ruled like the first Tanna, and Rambam in his composition copied the first Tanna's wording. Thus tying is considered connection.

And he adds seven threads to it. Thus one must be concerned lest he tie one thread. And it is impossible to forbid generally from the Torah out of concern lest he err and take the sewing thread for tzitzit, which is invalid. He may also err that it is permitted to tie — one must spin one thread partly tekhelet, etc.; for we hold that when tekhelet exists the mitzvah is to take two tekhelet threads (four heads) and two white (also four heads). But Rambam holds three white (six heads) and the fourth half tekhelet half white. How he did it: he took half — one white head — and half a thread — one tekhelet head; he joined the two half-threads in the middle, therefore he spun the two half-threads together in their middle. If tying were valid, why spin together — he should tie together. Therefore Magen Avraham: at the outset — when possible, spinning is preferable to tying.
