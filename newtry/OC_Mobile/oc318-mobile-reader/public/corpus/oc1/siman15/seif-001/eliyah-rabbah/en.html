[1] But not, etc. This is because of degradation of a mitzvah, unless he wishes to replace them with tzitzis more beautiful than the original ones. Bach cites the wording of She'iltos in parashas Shelach: one may not remove tzitzis from a tallis in order to sell it to a Jew, because he thereby nullifies the mitzvah, but one may transfer them from one tallis to another. Olas Tamid labored greatly to explain this. In my humble opinion there is a scribal error, and the text should read, “in order to sell it to a non-Jew.” If it said “to a Jew,” what reason would there be to remove them merely in order to sell it to a Jew? According to my emendation, however, it is understandable: he removes them because it is forbidden to sell a garment with tzitzis to a non-Jew, as stated below in siman 20, and She'iltos teaches that even then it is forbidden to remove them. If he will place them upon another tallis, however, it is permitted to sell the garment to a non-Jew even though the tzitzis holes remain. This appears to me to be the practical halachah. I later saw that Magen Avraham explains the passage as follows: if he wishes to convert the tallis into a garment that will not have four corners, he may remove the tzitzis. The Gemara on Menachos 43a likewise implies that one may remove them when selling the tallis to a non-Jew. This is no proof, because the Gemara may concern a case where he transfers them to another tallis, as I explained. The wording of She'iltos itself requires study when, with Hashem's help, it comes into my possession. Olas Tamid writes that the same applies when the tzitzis are severed but still valid according to the strict law, or when he finds tzitzis more beautiful than the original ones because of “This is my God, and I will beautify Him.” He may remove them. He should, however, untie the knots and windings rather than destroy the strings, so that they remain fit for another garment. Levush writes that the prohibition applies specifically where he removes them and does not wish to place them upon another garment. This implies that if he wishes to attach other tzitzis to this same garment, the removed strings need not be used on another garment. Perhaps Levush agrees that they must at least remain fit for another garment. His phrase “and cast them away,” however, does not imply this.
