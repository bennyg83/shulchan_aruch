[1] But not, etc. Because of degradation of a mitzvah if one does not wish to make beautiful tzitzit from the outset — here the Bach brought the wording of the She'iltot on the portion "Send for yourself," that one may not untie tzitzit from a tallit in order to sell it to a Jew, because he nullifies the mitzvah for it; but from tallit to tallit it is permitted — so far his wording. And Olat Tamid strained greatly in his explanation. it seems to me there is a scribal error, and it must read: in order to sell it to Kutim, etc. For if to a Jew — what is "in order to sell it to a Jew," which implies that he permits it in order to sell it to a Jew, and what reason is there in that? But as I emended, to Kutim it is settled that he permits it, because it is forbidden to sell a garment with tzitzit, as below in siman 20, and he says that it is forbidden to untie them unless he makes it into another tallit; then it is permitted to sell to Kutim even though the holes of the tzitzit remain. So it seems to me for practical law. Again I saw in Magen Avraham that he wrote: he meant to say that if he wishes to make from the tallit a garment that will not have four corners, it is permitted to remove them; and so it implies in the Talmud [Menachot] 43a, that it is permitted to remove them if he sells the tallit to a Kutite — so far. But this is not proof from the Talmud, for one can say that it deals with when they place them on another tallit, as I explained. I need to examine the body of the She'iltot when it comes to my hand, if God wills. Olat Tamid wrote: it seems that the same law applies, that one may untie the tzitzit when they are cut off even though they are kosher per the law; and likewise if he found tzitzit more beautiful than the first ones, because of "This is my God and I will glorify Him" — nevertheless he unties the knots and windings so that he does not wear out the tzitzit, that they should be fit for another garment — so far. But Malbush wrote: it seems to me specifically when they untie, etc., and he also does not wish to make, etc. — implying that if he wishes to make other tzitzit on this garment, he need not make these tzitzit on another garment. And it is possible that he concedes that in any case we require that they be fit for another garment; but from his writing "and to cast them away, etc." it does not imply so.
