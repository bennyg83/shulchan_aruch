(10) Within three fingerbreadths. Above three fingerbreadths it may be sewn according to all opinions. According to Rashi, that area is not the place for tzitzis, as explained above in siman 11, and there is no concern that one may use the sewing thread for tzitzis. According to Rav Amram Gaon, the torn piece retains the status of a garment. Even if that corner, together with its tzitzis, was completely severed from the garment, one may nevertheless reattach it and the tzitzis remain valid. Taz disagrees and rules that the tzitzis must be removed from that corner and reattached after the sewing. One should be stringent, as stated above in seif katan 7.
(11) With wool thread. Rather, he should sew with thread of other materials. Everyone knows that other materials do not exempt a wool garment, so he will not use that thread as a tzitzis string. The same leniency applies to garments of other materials if they are sewn with thread of a different material.
(12) Because it tore, etc. The same applies if, when initially making the garment, he sewed a piece of fabric measuring less than three fingerbreadths onto the edge of the garment, whether lengthwise or widthwise, and attached tzitzis to that piece. According to this opinion, it is invalid because it is treated as severed even after being sewn to the garment. If he attaches the tzitzis above the sewn piece, they will be more than three fingerbreadths from the edge and invalid. He should therefore sew beneath the corner a piece measuring three by three fingerbreadths. See seif katan 16 below.
(13) And if he did, etc. Meaning, even if he attached new tzitzis to that corner piece after sewing it to the tallis, they do not exempt the tallis, because sewing is not considered a connection in this case and the piece is still treated as severed if the corner was initially torn completely from the garment.
(14) Any amount is valid. Meaning, even the tzitzis that were present when it tore remain valid. Some are stringent and maintain that the original tzitzis became invalid and must be reattached after the sewing. See Biur Halachah.
(15) Some say that according to Rav Amram, etc. They maintain that sewing is considered a connection, and only the tzitzis that were present while it was being sewn are invalid because of “you shall make,” and not something already made. See Taz, who disagrees with Shulchan Aruch and writes that all authorities agree that if he attached the tzitzis after sewing, it is valid. Bach, Shiurei Knesses HaGedolah, and the Gra write the same, and Artzos HaChayim concurs. Nevertheless, to satisfy the first opinion, he should sew beneath the corner a patch measuring three by three fingerbreadths.
(16) He satisfies all opinions. Therefore, the custom is to sew onto each corner of the tallis a complete piece of fabric measuring three by three fingerbreadths. Many garments, even when new, commonly have stitching within three fingerbreadths, which would be invalid according to Rav Amram under the first opinion, as explained in seif katan 12.
(17) Where possible. Where this is impossible, we adopt Rav Amram's view as primary and likewise rule that sewing is considered a connection.
