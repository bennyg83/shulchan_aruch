He cannot take the corner even if it were an amah by an amah (chapter on tekhelet, Menachot 41b), and specifically when bringing it from another place; but if it tore above three fingerbreadths and is still attached to the garment, it is permitted (Rosh in Laws of Tzitzit). But if one of its corners was torn off completely, even if he wishes to go back and sew it onto the same garment itself, he must untie the tzitzit from the beginning, as if he had sewn it onto one garment (R. Yisrael Mintz, siman 3; Magen Avraham, siman 232, in explanation of Rambam, chapter 1 of Laws of Tzitzit; Bach per the view of Rav Amram, and this is the view of Tosafot and Mordechai), unless three whole fingerbreadths remained that were not torn, for then it is still called a connection. And some say: if the tallit tore outside three fingerbreadths from the edge of the corner—even if that entire strip was torn, whether in its length or its width, into two pieces—when he returns and sews it, even with the tzitzit, it returns to validity, for the corner is from this garment. But if it tore within three fingerbreadths, it is forbidden to return and sew it with the tzitzit; but if he wishes to put tzitzit on it after he sewed it, even if it tore completely, it is valid (Beit Yosef; Nimukei Yosef).
