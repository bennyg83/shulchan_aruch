He may not take the corner, even if it measures one cubit by one cubit. Menachos 41b. This applies specifically when bringing a corner from elsewhere. If the garment tore more than three fingerbreadths from the edge and is still attached to the garment, it is permitted. Rosh, Hilchos Tzitzis. If one of its corners was completely torn off, then even if he wishes to sew it back onto the same garment, he must first remove the tzitzis, just as if he were sewing it onto a different garment. Mahari Mintz, siman 3; Magen Avraham, siman 232, in his explanation of Rambam, chapter 1 of Hilchos Tzitzis; Bach according to Rav Amram's approach, which is also the approach of Tosafos and Mordechai. This does not apply if three complete fingerbreadths remained unsevered, for then it is still considered attached. Some maintain that if the tallis tore outside the area of three fingerbreadths from the edge of the corner, even if the entire strip was torn in two, whether lengthwise or widthwise, once he sews it back together, even with the tzitzis still attached, it returns to validity, because the corner is part of this very garment. If, however, it tore within three fingerbreadths, it is forbidden to sew it back together while the tzitzis remain attached. If he intends to attach the tzitzis only after sewing it, it is valid even if the corner was completely severed. Beit Yosef; Nimukei Yosef.
