Section 4, "If it tore, etc." This follows Rabbi Meir, for Rabbi Yehudah holds like him. The Nimukei Yosef has the opposite reading, and this is correct, for there is support from the Rabbis, and the discussion there on 40b also follows this view.
"And some say that according to Rabbi Akiva, etc." The first opinion in the name of Rabbi Akiva is the Rosh, and the Shulchan Arukh understands that they disagree. But this is not so, for even according to the Rosh it must be because of the rule of "you shall make," and not from what is already made, as is stated there, 40b. Furthermore, sewing is certainly considered a connection, as is stated there, 41a: "And they agree that if he folded it, etc.," and there, 37b: "One who sewed it, etc.; if it were so, etc." Otherwise he acted properly. The Taz wrote likewise; see there. There is no difficulty from Rashi, for he explained that the sewing thread is invalid because of "you shall make," and not from what is already made. He likewise explained there, 42b, "from the remnants, etc.," also because of that rule, meaning that he did not cut them off but left them in the garment. Rashi holds that according to Rav we do not require that the tzitzit be made for their own sake at all; therefore in Sukkah 9 the Gemara challenges from Shmuel and not from Rav. He also explains that Rava there on 40b holds like Rav, following his own view, for he explained the dispute of Abaye and Rava in Sanhedrin 48 as Rava following the Rabbis; and we say here, 42b, "It is a dispute of Tannaim, etc." But Tosafot do not accept Rashi's explanation, as is clear there, 40b, in the comment beginning "Perhaps, etc." Rather, they explain like Rabbi Akiva, and likewise there, 42b, in the comment beginning "The thorns." They follow their own view, for they explain there in the comment beginning "Until" that Rava follows Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel. We rule there according to Tosafot's explanation; therefore the explanation of Tosafot and Rabbi Akiva is primary. The Beit Yosef also wrote that the primary explanation is that of Rabbi Akiva. However, Tosafot explain "it tore outside three" to mean that three thumbbreadths remained along the length of the garment that did not tear, while the Nimukei Yosef in the name of Rabbi Akiva explains that the tear was outside three across the width of the garment. The words of the Rosh can also be explained this way, unlike the Beit Yosef. The Beit Yosef's words are not understandable, for in the Beit Yosef and Shulchan Arukh he wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Yerucham that according to the Rosh's explanation in the name of Rabbi Akiva, if even any amount remained, it is valid; and afterward he wrote that the words of the Rosh and Rabbeinu Yerucham are like the explanation of Tosafot. But according to this explanation it is settled.
