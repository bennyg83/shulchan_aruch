Seif 4, "If it tore, etc.": Like Rabbi Meir, for Rabbi Yehudah holds like him. Nimukei Yosef has the opposite reading, and this is correct, for there is support from the Rabbis, and the sugya there on 40b is likewise.

And some say that according to Rabbi Akiva, etc.: The first opinion in the name of Rabbi Akiva is the Rosh, and the Shulchan Aruch understands that they disagree. But this is not so, for even per the Rosh it must be because of ta'aseh velo min ha'asui, as is stated there, 40b. Furthermore, sewing is certainly considered a connection, as is stated there, 41a: "And they agree that if he folded it, etc.," and there, 37b: "One who sewed it, etc.; if it were so, etc."—otherwise he acted properly. Taz wrote likewise; see there. There is no difficulty from Rashi, for he explained that the sewing thread is invalid because of ta'aseh velo min ha'asui. He likewise explained there, 42b, "from the remnants, etc.," also because of that, meaning that he did not cut them off but left them in the garment. Rashi holds that according to Rav we do not require leshmah at all; therefore in Sukkah 9 the Gemara challenges from Shmuel and not from Rav. He also explains that Rava there on 40b holds like Rav, following his own view, for he explained the dispute of Abaye and Rava in Sanhedrin 48 as Rava following the Rabbis; and we say here, 42b, "It is a dispute of Tannaim, etc." But Tosafot do not accept Rashi's explanation, as is clear there, 40b, s.v. "Perhaps, etc." Rather, they explain like Rabbi Akiva, and likewise there, 42b, s.v. "The thorns." They follow their own view, for they explain there, s.v. "Until," that Rava follows Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel. We rule there according to Tosafot's explanation; therefore the explanation of Tosafot and Rabbi Akiva is primary. Beit Yosef also wrote that the primary explanation is that of Rabbi Akiva. However, Tosafot explain "it tore outside three" to mean that three fingerbreadths remained along the length of the garment that did not tear, while Nimukei Yosef in the name of Rabbi Akiva explains that the tear was outside three across the width of the garment. The words of the Rosh can also be explained this way, unlike Beit Yosef. Beit Yosef's words are not understandable, for in Beit Yosef and Shulchan Aruch he wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Yerucham that per the Rosh's explanation in the name of Rabbi Akiva, if any amount remained, it is valid; and afterward he wrote that the words of the Rosh and Rabbeinu Yerucham are like the explanation of Tosafot. But according to this explanation it is settled.
