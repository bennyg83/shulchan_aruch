(Seif katan 4) There is no. Bach writes that it is invalid when he sews it, etc. Meaning, Bach too agrees with the ruling stated here in the Shulchan Aruch, which is from responsa Rashba. That ruling concerns a case where one wraps himself in each half of the tallis independently and adds two new tzitzis to each half; the two original tzitzis remain valid. Bach invalidates the original tzitzis only when one sews another piece of fabric onto one half of the tallis.
It appears to me that it is valid, etc. What difference is there whether a piece was torn, etc.? Beit Yosef likewise writes in the name of Rashba, comparing a tallis divided into two with a garment from which a piece was torn. See Taz, who cites Rashba's wording.
The reason is that when he begins sewing, the two become one garment. His intent is straightforward. Even if you say that this corner was nullified, the garment nevertheless becomes a six-cornered garment and remains obligated in tzitzis, as explained above at the beginning of siman 10.
Even though he sewed the corners, etc. This implies that sewing does not nullify the corner, although there the reason is that he ultimately intends to undo the sewing. Rashi writes there that if he did not need the corners, he should have cut them off; since he did not, he evidently intends to undo the sewing. Beit Yosef writes the same there and here. Our case is different, because he does not intend to undo the sewing. Yet this itself is Magen Avraham's proof. In siman 10, although he sewed the corners, the corner is not nullified because he ultimately intends to undo them. Thus the present status is judged according to the intended final result. The same applies here: we judge according to the intended final result, and he intends to sew the entire garment so that it will become one four-cornered garment. The corner was therefore never nullified. Know that Taz, seif katan 3, discusses sewing together the two halves of a tallis that was previously divided. Although he agrees with Magen Avraham against Bach, Taz nevertheless writes that when sewing the two halves back together as originally, one must first remove the tzitzis from one half; see there for his reason. I do not understand his words. Chemed Moshe likewise disputes him; see there.
