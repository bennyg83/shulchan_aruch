(Subsection 4) There is not in it. The Bach wrote: invalid when he sews, etc. — that is, the Bach also concedes to the law explained here in Shulchan Arukh. It is from the Rashba's responsum: when one wraps in each part of the tallit alone and makes two new tzitzit, the two old tzitzit are also valid. But when he sews another piece to part one's tallit, the Bach disqualifies the original tzitzit.

And it seems to me it is valid, etc. What difference if a piece tore, etc. — so Beit Yosef in the name of the Rashba: like dividing into two is like a piece torn; therefore the Beit Yosef brought the Rashba's wording.

And the reason: when he begins to sew they become one garment. His intent is plain: although this corner was nullified, nevertheless it became six-cornered and nevertheless remained obligated, as above section 10.

Even though he sewed the corners [back on] — it implies that sewing does not nullify the corner, even though there the reason is that he will ultimately untie the sewing; and as Rashi writes there: it is impossible that he does not need them — he would have to cut them off, or [the reason is] that he will ultimately untie them; likewise Rabbeinu Yonah there and here. Not so here, where he does not intend to untie the sewing but rather the opposite — it is what gives [validity] — and this is Magen Avraham's proof in siman 10: even though he sewed the corners, nevertheless the corner was not nullified, since he will ultimately untie them — behold, we judge by its end; likewise here one should judge by its end, and his intention is to sew it all together so it becomes one garment with four corners, and thus the corner was never nullified. And know that what Taz wrote in seif katan 3 regarding sewing two parts of a tallis that were first divided — even though he agrees with what Magen Avraham wrote, not like the Bach, nevertheless Taz wrote that when sewing two parts of the tallis together as at first one must untie the tzitzis of part one — see there for the reason. And his words are not clear to me; also in Chemdas Moshe he disagreed with him — see there.
