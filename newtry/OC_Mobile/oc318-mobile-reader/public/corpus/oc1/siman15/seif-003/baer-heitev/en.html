And not something already made. The tzitzis that remain have no invalidity. He may therefore attach two new tzitzis to the newly formed corners. Alternatively, he may join another piece of fabric to that half and attach tzitzis to it. He may not, however, rejoin the second piece that was cut from it to the first piece so that the garment returns to its original form, because the tzitzis that were on the severed second piece became nullified, creating a problem of “you shall make,” and not something already made. Rather, he must remove the tzitzis from the second piece, sew the pieces together, and afterward make new tzitzis on the second piece, which is then like another new garment. Taz. Bach forbids joining even another new piece of fabric to that half. He permits only making the two tzitzis on another side of the same half, meaning along the place where it was cut. Taz disagrees; see there. Sefer Peri HaAretz permits even sewing the two pieces back together as they originally were and maintains that this does not involve “you shall make,” and not something already made. See there and Yad Aharon.
