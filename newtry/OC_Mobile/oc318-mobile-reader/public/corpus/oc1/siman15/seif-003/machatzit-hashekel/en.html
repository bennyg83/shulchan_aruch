(Subsection 3) It was not from Beit Yosef's words, etc. Rabbeinu Yosef in the chapter on tekhelet: "And they agree that one should not even bring a cubit on a cubit from another tallit with tzitzit and place on this tallit." Rashi and Rosh: reason — "on the corners of their garments" is written; this corner, etc. Rabbeinu Yosef: "I did not understand our great rabbi Mahari Abohav: 'But one should not take the corner, etc.' — the Rosh explained this reason because of 'you shall make and not from what is already made'; it seems to me it was already made for the mitzvah, and the Rosh's words are plain — not because of 'you shall make and not from what is already made' he disqualified it, but because we require 'corners of their garments,' etc." Mordechai: specifically to bring from elsewhere; but torn above three, etc., permitted to sew — plainly we read well "corners of their garments." Nimukei Yosef: reason because of "you shall make and not from what is already made" — "And they agree even for the view 'outside three he may sew,' since the corner was not nullified here, he admits one should not bring, etc., for it is 'you shall make and not from what is already made'" — end Rabbeinu Yosef. Magen Avraham holds: since Rabbeinu Yosef did not write "but Nimukei Yosef wrote, etc." or "in my view wrote, etc." — then it implies he brings Nimukei Yosef's words disputing Rashi and Rosh. From "but Nimukei Yosef wrote" it implies it refers above, wondering at Mahari Abohav who understood the Rosh's reason as "you shall make and not from what is already made," while the Rosh gave another reason; therefore "but Nimukei Yosef wrote" explaining the reason as "you shall make and not from what is already made." So as not to renew dispute, fit to explain the Rosh also intended "you shall make and not from what is already made," and then the law is with Mahari Abohav. Although the Rosh wrote because of "on the corners of their garments," the intent is not that it is a Torah decree, but as Magen Avraham wrote here.
