(Seif katan 3) It was not. Beit Yosef's words imply, etc. Beit Yosef cites the baraisa in the chapter HaTecheiles: all agree that one may not bring even a piece measuring one cubit by one cubit from another tallis, with tzitzis attached, and sew it onto this tallis. Rashi and Rosh explain that the reason is the verse, “upon the corners of their garments,” and this corner was not a corner of this garment. Beit Yosef writes: “I did not understand what our great teacher Mahari Abuhav wrote: ‘But he may not take the corner, etc. Rosh explained the reason as “you shall make,” and not something already made.’ It appears to me that since it was already made for the sake of the mitzvah, this is not so. Rosh's words are straightforward. He does not invalidate it because of something already made, but because the Torah requires ‘the corners of their garments,’ and that condition is absent.” Mordechai writes that this applies specifically when bringing a piece from elsewhere. If the garment tore more than three fingerbreadths from the edge, it may be sewn, because it is still properly called “the corners of their garments.” Nimukei Yosef, however, explains that the reason is “you shall make,” and not something already made. He writes: “All agree, even the authority who permits sewing a tear outside three fingerbreadths, because the corner was not nullified, that here one may not bring, etc., because it is something already made.” Magen Avraham understands that because Beit Yosef did not write, “But Nimukei Yosef writes,” or, “And Nimukei Yosef writes,” which would indicate that he cites a dissenting explanation, but instead wrote, “Nevertheless, Nimukei Yosef writes,” he refers back to Beit Yosef's objection to Mahari Abuhav. Beit Yosef had said that Mahari Abuhav understood Rosh's reason as something already made, while Rosh gives a different reason. Beit Yosef then adds that Nimukei Yosef explicitly gives the reason of something already made. Therefore, in order not to create an unnecessary dispute, one should explain that Rosh too intended something already made. Mahari Abuhav's understanding is therefore correct. Although Rosh writes that the reason is the verse, “upon the corners of their garments,” his intent is as Magen Avraham explains here.
