There is no. See Taz and Beit Yosef concerning responsa Rashba. The questioner asked about a tallis torn into two, whether the remaining tzitzis become invalid because two of the original tzitzis were removed and only two remain, while four are required. This is a mistaken approach. If that were so, it would be impossible to make tzitzis on any tallis, since all four cannot be attached simultaneously. Rather, the question concerns the following. Once the garment is torn, for example to within less than three fingerbreadths, that corner becomes nullified, because the corner in the middle of the garment lies more than three fingerbreadths away, while the other corner remains connected and contains three fingerbreadths. If one attaches tzitzis while the garment has only three valid corners and later completes it, it is invalid under siman 10, seif 5, because of “you shall make,” and not something already made. Rashba answers that this is not “you shall make,” and not something already made. Otherwise, the baraisa on Menachos 41a, which states that a tallis torn outside three fingerbreadths may be sewn, etc., and that all agree one may bring techeiles and suspend it, should require removal of the first three tzitzis from the three corners that remain attached. Yet it does not. Even though one corner became nullified when part of the garment tore, the other tzitzis remain valid. It is possible that Rashba also understands the case as one in which the corner was entirely torn away and nothing remained attached, or where it was torn in a rounded shape so that only three corners remained. Nevertheless, the original tzitzis remain valid. Since they were valid when first made, and only afterward one corner tore away, once the corner is resewn and a new tzitzis is attached, the earlier tzitzis regain validity. Their bodies were not made through an invalid act. This resembles siman 10, seif 6, where one attached tzitzis over existing tzitzis and it is valid if he later cut away one set. Since both were initially made with the intention of tzitzis, the cutting of one set validates the other. The same applies here. Taz understands Rashba's proof also from the case of a piece measuring one cubit by one cubit with techeiles attached. If it has no techeiles, it may be sewn on, and the original tzitzis of the tallis do not become invalid, even though during partial sewing one corner is temporarily nullified. This likewise proves that such a case remains valid.
I later saw. Responsa Rashba is best understood according to its plain meaning, and Levush likewise understood that the questioner asked whether, because two tzitzis had been cut away, the remaining two became invalid, so that attaching two more would render the first two “you shall make,” and not something already made. The two sides of the question were drawn from the case of a piece measuring one cubit by one cubit. The questioner assumed that this piece contained the minimum size of a tallis, yet its existing tzitzis are invalid when it is attached to another garment because part of the original garment was torn away. Perhaps the same applies here. Alternatively, perhaps the reason there is the straightforward meaning of Rashi's phrase, “upon the corners of their garments”: that corner was not a corner of this garment when the tzitzis were made. Here, by contrast, these tzitzis were made upon this very garment. Rashba answered that the remaining tzitzis do not become invalid. His proof is that where a garment tears and the detached part contains no techeiles, it may be sewn back, and the three tzitzis that remained are valid even though one corner was torn away. A similar proof may also be brought from the case of a tear outside three fingerbreadths that may be sewn according to Nimukei Yosef in the name of Rav Amram. Levush concludes that this is no worse than a new tallis upon which one attaches one or two tzitzis, pauses for a long time, and later completes the rest. According to this interpretation, the question submitted to Rashba could have concerned a single severed tzitzis while the tallis itself remained whole. It appears, however, that the question arose specifically because the tallis was torn and two new corners came into existence that had not existed when the first tzitzis were made.
Now. Darkei Moshe in siman 14 writes, in the separate Fürth edition, for I did not find it in Darkei Moshe printed alongside Tur in the Berlin edition: “Rashba wrote in responsum 217 that a tallis with four tzitzis that was divided into two may be rejoined and sewn together, and this is not ‘you shall make,’ and not something already made.” In Taz, the two words “rejoined and sewn together” were omitted. This is the responsum of Rashba cited by Beit Yosef here. Darkei Moshe found it difficult to understand why the tzitzis should become invalid merely because two tzitzis were removed, since that would make it impossible to make tzitzis on any tallis, as explained above. He therefore understood the question differently. The questioner asked whether the two tzitzis on one half, which by itself has four corners, become displaced from the other half, and vice versa. His proof was the case of a piece measuring one cubit by one cubit with tzitzis attached. The questioner assumed that this piece contains the minimum tallis size, yet it may not be attached to another tallis because the tzitzis on it were displaced from that garment, which has its own four corners. Alternatively, perhaps there the reason is “upon the corners of their garments,” for that corner was not part of this garment when the tzitzis were made. Here, however, when he reattaches and sews together the very same tallis, the reason does not apply. Rashba answered that it is permitted. According to this, Rama's silence here regarding the Mechaber's ruling in seif 3 indicates that he understands it to mean that there is no issue of “you shall make,” and not something already made even when the two halves are rejoined. Taz objects: why should this be valid? While the pieces are separate, the two tzitzis on each half are not fit for the other half. Rejoining them should therefore be no better than attaching a piece measuring one cubit by one cubit with tzitzis already upon it. Taz assumes that such a piece contains the minimum tallis size. See what I wrote in Magen Avraham and in Tzon Kodashim on Menachos 41a. Taz also treats the requirement of “upon the corners of their garments” as essentially a form of “you shall make,” and not something already made. The tzitzis on the one-cubit piece were not fit for the torn tallis at the time they were made, and therefore become something already made. The same applies here when the two halves are rejoined. Taz's statement that the tzitzis on the piece removed from the garment became nullified means that, when a one-cubit piece bearing tzitzis was cut away, those tzitzis ceased to relate to their original tallis. They likewise do not relate to the torn tallis to which one now wishes to attach them. All the more so, in a tallis divided into two, one should not be permitted to rejoin the halves while retaining the tzitzis on both. According to Taz, Rashba intended only that each half may remain independently and that the two tzitzis upon each half do not become invalid. His proof is from the one-cubit case: if the piece lacks tzitzis, it may be sewn onto the tallis and the three tzitzis remaining on the tallis do not become invalid. In any event, this does not accord with Bach's ruling that one may join a new piece or the two halves after removing the tzitzis from one half, and that this alone suffices.
