(8) And each portion, etc. If neither portion contains enough material for wrapping, as commonly occurs with our talleisim ketanim when divided in two, the mitzvah of tzitzis immediately ceases from both pieces. If he later wishes to rejoin one piece to the other or to another piece of fabric, he must first remove all the tzitzis. If one piece contains enough material for wrapping and the other does not, he need remove only the tzitzis from the piece that lacks the required size, and afterward reattach them once the pieces have been joined.
(9) It does not involve, etc. The tzitzis remaining on each piece are not invalid, because each piece contains enough material for wrapping. He may therefore join another piece of fabric to either half and attach new tzitzis to the newly formed corners. Certainly, he may make that half into a complete tallis by attaching two new tzitzis to the new corners. If he wishes to sew the two divided portions back together, some maintain that he must first remove the tzitzis from one portion, sew the pieces together, and afterward reattach tzitzis to that portion. Otherwise it constitutes “you shall make,” and not something already made. Most later authorities are lenient and maintain that this is not considered “you shall make,” and not something already made. Nevertheless, it is preferable initially to be stringent.
