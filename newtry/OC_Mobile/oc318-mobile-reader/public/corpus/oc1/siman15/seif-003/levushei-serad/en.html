Seif katan 4. “When he begins to sew.” Meaning, when he has sewn less than three fingerbreadths, the pieces are not yet considered joined, as explained in seif 4. Nevertheless, the corner at the place of sewing is nullified and the garment has only three corners. Although the tzitzis were initially made validly and the invalidity does not affect the tzitzis themselves, here, because the piece contains enough material for wrapping, it is treated as another garment. The second category of something already made therefore applies together with Rosh's reason, as explained in the preceding letter. Magen Avraham refutes this from the law that a tear outside three fingerbreadths may be sewn. Although we explained that a piece containing the minimum garment size is worse than a tear outside three fingerbreadths, the proof remains because the statement “outside three fingerbreadths” implies any distance beyond three fingerbreadths, whether small or large, even enough to contain the minimum garment size. It is nevertheless valid. The same should apply to a tallis divided into two, which may be rejoined. He further refutes Bach from siman 10, seif 3. There, although the garment was sewn, its corners are not nullified because the sewing will certainly later be undone, and we follow its eventual state even now. All the more so here: when he begins sewing, he does so with the intention of sewing the entire garment. It is therefore considered as though the sewing were already complete, because we follow the ultimate result. Consider this carefully.
