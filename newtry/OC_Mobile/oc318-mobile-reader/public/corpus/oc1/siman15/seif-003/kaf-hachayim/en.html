3) It is permitted to untie tzitzit, etc., and specifically to untie the tzitzit from this garment and return to tie and wind them on another garment; but to cut the garment and remove the tzitzit whole as they are tied and wound and place them on another garment by hanging them with a thread in a hole and the like, or inserting the ends of the threads where four are folded to eight inside the hole and returning to insert the branch and thick strand among them and hanging them by a loop — is forbidden, unlike Rabbeinu Yeshayah the First in Hilchot Tzitzit in Sefer Semachim page 14b, who permitted this, for he is alone in the matter; rather, like Rashi [Menachot 41a end of 41a] and his colleagues, who hold that it is "you shall make and not from what is already made," and so agreed the later rabbis; so too Lev Chaim siman 96, and see there that he wondered at Birkei Yosef, who brought the words of Rabbeinu Yeshayah the First in Yoreh De'ah in general and did not mention that they are against all the decisors — see there. So too from the words of Tosafot Menachot 42a, in the comment beginning "And I will send him" — it is clear, not like Rabbeinu Yeshayah the First, who wrote: "It seems that he did not pass [the threads] through the hole of the patch, but seven threads at most, and the eighth he leaves and ties in it; for otherwise he could have passed the tzitzit from the tallit through the patch after making them, and you do not find kilayim in tzitzit" — end quote. It implies that whenever one can pass the tzitzit from the tallit after making them through the patch, this tying on the tallit is not considered tying, because we require tying that has a prohibition of kilayim, and this is not like Rabbeinu Yeshayah the First. See HaRav Pa'alim, Choshen Mishpat part 3 siman 1, what he wrote on this; and it appears he did not see the words of Tosafot cited. Also what he wrote there, that the windings and knots of the tzitzit are similar to writing a mezuzah — Birkei Yosef siman 19 letter 2 did not write so, but rather spinning the tzitzit and its braiding are similar to writing a mezuzah, and fixing the threads of the tzitzit in the garment is like fixing the mezuzah in the house — see there. Thus this law does not emerge from a dispute, and one must be concerned for the prohibition of wearing a four-cornered garment without tzitzit; for according to Tosafot this is not considered tying on the garment, since it is not similar to kilayim. And further one must be concerned lest there is also "you shall make and not from what is already made," per the plain sense of Rashi and his colleagues, as Lev Chaim wrote. Also per the Taz and Maamar Mordechai subsection 1, that each one explained a different explanation in Rashi's words — one can say that there is in Rashi's words their explanation and also "you shall make and not from what is already made" — see there. Thus we have no permission to permit prohibitions through strained explanations; and therefore, etc.
