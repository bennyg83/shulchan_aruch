3) It is permitted to remove tzitzis, etc. This applies specifically when one unties the tzitzis from this garment and then ties and winds them again on another garment. It is forbidden, however, to cut the garment and remove the tzitziyos intact, as they are tied and wound, and attach them to another garment by suspending them with a thread through the hole or the like. It is likewise forbidden to insert the ends at the place where the four strings are doubled into eight through the hole, then pass the loose strings and wound section through them and suspend them by means of a loop. This is unlike the first Rabbeinu Yeshayah, in Hilchos Tzitzis in Sefer Sam Chaim, folio 14b, who permits this, for he stands alone in this matter. Rather, the law follows Rashi, Menachos 41a, and those who agree with him, who hold that this constitutes ta'aseh velo min ha'asuy. This is also the consensus of the later rabbis. Lev Chaim, siman 96, writes this and expresses surprise that Birkei Yosef cites the first Rabbeinu Yeshayah without qualification in Shiyurei Berachah and does not mention that his view opposes all the poskim. The same follows from Tosafos, Menachos 42a, s.v. "ve'avik lehu meivak," unlike the first Rabbeinu Yeshayah. Tosafos writes: It appears to me that he would pass at most seven strings through the loop's opening, leave the eighth outside, and tie with it. Otherwise one could transfer completed tzitzis from a tallis through the loop, and shaatnez in tzitzis would never be possible. This implies that whenever completed tzitzis can be transferred from the tallis through the loop, their attachment is not considered a binding to the tallis, because the required binding must be one in which the prohibition of shaatnez is possible. This is unlike the first Rabbeinu Yeshayah. See Rav Pe'alim, volume 3, siman 1. It appears that he did not see this Tosafos. His comparison there of the tzitzis windings and knots to writing a mezuzah also differs from Birkei Yosef, siman 19, paragraph 2. Birkei Yosef compares spinning and twisting the tzitzis to writing the mezuzah, while fixing the tzitzis strings to the garment corresponds to affixing the mezuzah to the house. This law therefore remains subject to dispute, and one must be concerned about wearing a four-cornered garment without tzitzis. According to Tosafos, this is not considered a binding to the garment because it does not resemble shaatnez; one must further be concerned that it constitutes ta'aseh velo min ha'asuy, according to the plain meaning of Rashi and those who agree with him, as Lev Chaim writes. Even according to Taz and Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 1, each of whom interprets Rashi differently, one can say that Rashi's words contain both their interpretations and the disqualification of ta'aseh velo min ha'asuy. We should not permit prohibitions on the basis of strained interpretations. Understand this.
