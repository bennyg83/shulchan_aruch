And a matter that results from it is destruction, such as shofar blowing whose sound is heard by many and they will come to blow on Shabbat — the court is commanded to separate him (Ran chapter 3 of Rosh Hashana). Bach wrote that regarding impurity they are commanded to separate him even if he has not reached education; but Rama's view in Yoreh De'ah siman 373 is they are not commanded to separate him, and so hold most poskim and the plain sense of the Gemara (Magen Avraham and Taz — see there):

(ב) His father. But his mother is not commanded to separate him — likewise in Nazir daf 29, and it implies there the father is not obligated to educate his daughter — see there; and in Tosafos there they challenged what is written that in the Temple one is obligated to educate his daughter — see there; and it is possible that all mitzvot are compared to Temple service and one must educate them — likewise in Yalkut Emor daf 178 (Magen Avraham):

(ג) D'oraisa. And in truth even for a rabbinic prohibition the father is obligated to educate him, as stated in siman 106 (seif 2) and in many places — only that if they did not separate him the court does not rebuke him; but for a d'oraisa prohibition the court rebukes the father to separate him (Beit Yosef — see there; and see Taz). And Or Zaru'a wrote: specifically a child with understanding, but not one without understanding we have no concern, for a nursing infant even from a non-kosher animal — end of his words; and see Yoreh De'ah siman 81 s.k. 7 that nevertheless it harms the child; and Shach wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 372 in the name of Rokeach that a priest's pregnant wife may enter a tent with a corpse, for it is a doubt whether it is female or whether it is a miscarriage — see there; and it requires study, for otherwise it is permitted because impurity in the throat does not impart impurity — it requires study (Magen Avraham — see there):

(ד) By hand. And it is stated in the end of chapter 9 of Shabbat that it is forbidden to give a child a live impure locust to play with lest it die and he eat it; and even a matter with no prohibition except "you shall not abhor" — forbidden to place in his hand; all the more so an impure thing that appears edible to him; and likewise in the gloss there — the reason is it is as one who feeds him by hand; but it is permitted to give an impure bird to play with, for even if it dies it does not appear edible — as stated in Bava Batra daf 20; a minor who has not reached education — drinking water harms him and there is no Jewish wine — permitted for an adult to give him ordinary wine on condition he dilute with six parts water and put the water first and afterward the wine (Raanach part 1 siman 112); and it appears to me that if the wine is not strong that it be lost by this — he should command a non-Jew to give him (Magen Avraham). R' Yehuda of Paris wrote in part 1 that one should not rebuke non-Jews who feed them prohibition, but it is forbidden to tell a non-Jew to feed them prohibition — end of his words. And if the child needs it, such as if he is somewhat ill — permitted to tell a non-Jew to feed them; and so is customary on Pesach to command a non-Jew to carry the child to his house and give him chametz to drink; and if the child is ill and it is impossible to carry him outside the house — ask a non-Jew to give the child chametz; and the non-Jew brings chametz and places it in the house — it is the non-Jew's chametz; then even if the non-Jew goes away at times one can command the minor to give him to drink; but he should not touch it lest he eat or drink from it; but if the child is not so ill — carry him outside and do not bring chametz into the house at all; and see siman 450 s.k. 11; and see Magen Avraham:
