Magen Avraham wrote "and a matter," etc.; Bach wrote that regarding impurity they are commanded to separate him — Bach wrote thus to resolve the challenge of the rabbi on Tur who ruled like Rabbi Pedas in Yevamos 114 that a minor who eats nevelot — the court is not commanded to separate him; and in Yoreh De'ah siman 373 Tur wrote regarding kohen impurity that the court must separate him — on this Bach answered: there in Yevamos they challenge Rabbi Pedas from what the mishnah teaches: since it is written regarding kohen impurity "speak to the kohanim" and "say to them" to warn adults regarding minors — is it not that we tell them "you shall not become impure" — behold the court is commanded to separate them; they answer: no — they need only not impart impurity by hand; afterward they challenge from creeping things "you shall not eat" to warn adults regarding minors — is it not to separate them; they answer: likewise not to feed by hand; afterward they challenge regarding blood "any soul among you shall not eat blood" to warn minors — they answer: no, only not to feed by hand; from this emerges the rabbi's challenge: behold tumah of kohanim is equal in this to other prohibitions — the court is not commanded to separate them; Bach answered: afterward the Gemara makes needs-parsing that three verses regarding tumah, vermin, and blood are needed — one cannot learn each from the other; if only tumah of kohanim were written it would be impossible to derive, for kohanim are different with extra commandments; if we heard vermin because its prohibition is severe with an olive-bulk; if we heard blood because it has karet — see there; therefore per the conclusion we need not say in the braita of tumah of kohanim "to warn" means not to impart by hand, but they must be separated, although we hold for other prohibitions the court is not commanded — tumah of kohanim is different with extra commandments; Tur holds that by the conclusion of needs-parsing we do not take the braita of tumah from its plain sense but that the court must separate them, although for vermin and blood Tur would hold the court is not commanded — one could say the braita's "to warn" means to separate them although generally the court is not commanded — vermin and blood have a stricter side — nevertheless we do not say so except for tumah of kohanim where "speak and say" implies separation, unlike vermin and blood where the plain sense does not imply separation — until now Bach:
