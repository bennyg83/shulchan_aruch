(א) From d'oraisa prohibition. He wrote this per Beit Yosef's explanation in Rambam — that Rambam wrote for rabbinic prohibition the court is not commanded to separate him, and if the father left him the court does not rebuke; Tur challenged that the Gemara implies even Torah prohibition; Beit Yosef answered in his father-in-law's name: what Rambam specified rabbinic prohibition is because of the latter part that the court does not rebuke the father — for Torah prohibition the court rebukes the father; and on this Rama wrote here "specifically from d'oraisa prohibition." In my humble opinion: specifically regarding needing the court to rebuke the father Beit Yosef distinguishes thus; but regarding the father himself whom Shulchan Aruch mentioned — he certainly must rebuke even for rabbinic prohibition, as Beit Yosef wrote shortly: "and likewise forbidden to accustom him to desecration of Shabbat and Yom Tov even in shevut matters" — end of his words. Beit Yosef brought here what Tur wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 373 regarding corpse impurity that the father must separate him; Beit Yosef wrote the reason: although in chapter "A deaf person" it implies only regarding not imparting impurity by hand we say adults must warn minors — before we knew the three derivations of tumah, vermin, and blood — but after they were revealed we have two verses that come as one and do not teach each other; if only tumah of kohanim were written we could not derive from it, for kohanim are different with extra commandments; if we heard vermin because its prohibition is severe — suffices with an olive-bulk; if we heard blood because it has karet — see there; therefore per the conclusion we need not say in the braita of tumah of kohanim "to warn" means not to impart by hand, but they must be separated, although we hold for other prohibitions the court is not commanded to separate — tumah of kohanim is different with extra commandments; Tur holds that by the conclusion of the needs-parsing we do not take the braita of tumah of kohanim from its plain sense but that the court must separate them, although for vermin and blood Tur would hold the court is not commanded — one could also say the braita's "to warn" means to separate them, although generally the court is not commanded — vermin and blood are stricter sides — nevertheless we do not say so except for tumah of kohanim where the verse's plain sense "speak" and "say" implies they must be separated, unlike vermin and blood where the plain sense does not imply separation — until now Bach. In my humble opinion the needs-parsing is certainly precise and there is a challenge on "from what side" — we challenge any challenge; here it was a strong challenge even though not as strong as the challenge we challenge in the Gemara; nevertheless it is clear this needs-parsing is precise and there is no "two verses that come as one" at all — otherwise the Talmud should have answered thus; in my humble opinion to explain Tur's words: just as the kohen is warned not to become impure, so he is warned regarding minors not to become impure; Rambam wrote adults are warned only not to impart by hand, but if the minor comes to become impure himself the court is not commanded to separate him — only the father is commanded to educate in holiness; it appears they must be separated — laws derived from "speak" and "say" to warn adults regarding minors imply they must be warned not to become impure; but after the Gemara explained "not to impart by hand" specifically — that warning was on the court, which the questioner understood refers to the court; but on the father there was no Torah warning, only from education one should educate as he concluded "educate in holiness" — holiness is not from Torah obligation but rabbinic; this is Rambam's view and Tur disagrees and says that in any case this "speak and say" implies he must be separated; what the Gemara said "not to place in his hand" specifically is on the court per the questioner's understanding that it refers to the court; but in truth the essence of the verse mentioning warning is on the father who is obligated from the Torah — deduced from Tur's wording that he began with kohanim who are warned — the father on his son in his domain — he did not mention the court; therefore he concludes "they must be warned" — singular — on his father to fulfill "speak and say" to warn him and separate him; first he said "it appears they must be separated" — plural — on all kohanim that they warn their sons as he began with kohanim; but ultimately Tur also holds that for tumah the court is not obligated to separate them; proof: Beit Yosef brings Nissi'im who agree with Tur, and Nissi'im speak only of the father; in my humble opinion their words agree with Rambam and we need not distinguish tumah from other transgressions, since the Talmud equates them explicitly:

(ב) Nevertheless it is good for him to accept upon himself, etc. — accordingly I explain Rashi Bava Kamma 98 regarding one who burned his fellow's document: an incident occurred and Rabbah coerced R' Ashi and collected from him as a loan to an idol — R' Ashi burned his fellow's document in his youth; one can question: certainly R' Ashi is exempt by law — a minor's damage is bad, for injury he is exempt, all the more his property; and Avnei Nezer learned from here a minor is liable when he matures; he brings proof from Bava Kamma daf 39 and Hagahot Maimoniyot chapter "One who injures" in the name of Avnei Nezer; but Maharshal wrote we do not hold thus, for regarding an ox it is a rabbinic enactment for societal repair as Tosafos wrote chapter 4 and 5 there; in my humble opinion R' Ashi is certainly exempt by law — Rabbah coerced him with words to act beyond the letter of the law as we find at the end of "Craftsmen" where Rav ruled on Rabbah bar Chanah that he pay workers' wages for what they carried for him and broke the barrel through negligence, and Rabbah said the law is thus, Rav said to him: "that you may walk in the way of the good and keep the paths of the righteous" — Rabbah did so to R' Ashi; Heaven forbid R' Ashi needed coercion — Rabbah coerced him with words as we find in "The Nazirite" with coercion in words; Rabbah's intent was he act beyond the letter of the law — end of my words:
