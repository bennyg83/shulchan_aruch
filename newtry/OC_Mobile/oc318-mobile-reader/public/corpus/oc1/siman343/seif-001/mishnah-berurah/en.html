(א) Nevelot — and likewise all other prohibitions, and likewise desecration of Shabbat if he does so intentionally; but if he does so for an adult one must rebuke him, as above siman 334 s.k. 25 — see there in Mishna Berurah:
(ב) But his father, etc. — for even to educate his sons and daughters in mitzvot is incumbent upon him, as written "educate the youth according to his way" — all the more to separate them from prohibition incumbent on the father; and some Acharonim hold the mitzvah of education is also on the mother:
(ג) From a d'oraisa prohibition — in truth even from a rabbinic prohibition he must separate, as he must educate in rabbinic mitzvot, as above s.k. 2; only the difference is that for rabbinic prohibition if the father did not separate him the court does not rebuke him; but for d'oraisa the court rebukes the father to separate him; and Gra explained therefore he specified "from d'oraisa prohibition" — for from rabbinic prohibition sometimes one need not separate him and even to give him l'chatchila is permitted — when needed, such as washing and anointing on Yom Kippur; and it is obvious that if he hears his young son and daughter speaking lashon hara he must rebuke them and separate them; and likewise from dispute, falsehood, curses, and blasphemy — many stumble in this by letting their children speak lashon hara, gossip, curse, and they become accustomed so that even when they mature and hear there is a great prohibition it is hard to separate from what they were accustomed to for many years and the matter was in their presumption of permission; and know that the measure of education in positive commandments is for each child according to his sharpness and knowledge in each matter according to its subject — such as one who knows about Shabbat is obligated to accustom him to hear kiddush and havdalah; one who knows to wrap himself properly is obligated in tzitzit — likewise siman 17 and all similar, whether positive commandments of Torah or of rabbinic law; but education in negative commandments, whether of Torah or of their words, is for every child with understanding who understands when told this is forbidden to do or eat; but a child without any understanding — his father is not commanded to prevent him per se from eating forbidden foods or desecrating Shabbat even in Torah prohibition, since he does not understand the matter he is prevented for; and likewise if he is a kohen he need not remove him from a house with impurity inside unless he has understanding — then the father is commanded to remove him to separate him from prohibition on account of the mitzvah of education; and to bring him into a house with impurity inside and to feed him other prohibitions is forbidden even for a child not yet with understanding, as will be written below; nevertheless a pregnant priest's wife close to birth may enter a tent with a corpse l'chatchila, for although it appears to us she will certainly give birth there and perhaps a male will be born and become impure there — nevertheless it is permitted, for it is a doubt whether female or miscarriage:
(ד) Forbidden — for every person, and even if the child has no understanding at all; and this is forbidden from the Torah — we derive it from what is written regarding creeping things "you shall not eat them" — an extra verse, and the Sages received that it means "you shall not feed them to minors"; and likewise regarding blood "any soul among you shall not eat blood" — the Sages received it means "you shall not feed minors"; and likewise regarding kohen impurity "speak" and "say" — they received "speak" to adults that they say to minors. And behold from these three mitzvot we learn for the entire Torah that all Torah prohibitions — forbidden to feed them or command them to transgress; therefore forbidden to give a child forbidden food even to play with lest he eat it — it is as feeding by hand [Magen Avraham]:
(ה) Even, etc. — and to tell a non-Jew to give forbidden matter to the child is also forbidden, like all prohibitions where telling a non-Jew is forbidden — all the more to tell the child himself to eat, for it is forbidden [Acharonim]; and if the child needs it, such as if he is somewhat ill — permitted to tell a non-Jew to feed them even in something forbidden by Torah; and so is customary on Pesach to command a non-Jew to carry the child to his house and give him chametz [and likewise if he needs to drink ordinary wine and water harms him — command the non-Jew to give him]; and if the child is ill and impossible to carry him outside — ask a non-Jew to give the child chametz; and the non-Jew brings chametz and places it in the house — it is the non-Jew's chametz in a Jewish house where he does not transgress "it shall not be seen" — then even if the non-Jew goes away at times one can command the minor to give this one to drink; but he should not touch it lest he eat from it, as written siman 450; but in all this beware not to give him a dinar beforehand whereby the chametz becomes acquired to him [Magen Avraham and Shulchan Aruch HaRav]:
(ו) To accustom him to desecration of Shabbat, etc. — such as telling him "bring me the key," and even via a karmelit:
(ז) They must separate him — they hold that since he reached education, it is incumbent on every person to separate him from his prohibition like on his father; and what we hold the court is not commanded to separate when he has not reached education — see in Chayei Adam that for d'oraisa prohibition one should be stringent per this view; therefore if a non-Jew wants to feed an Israelite child who reached education something forbidden by Torah — others are also obligated to rebuke him; but for rabbinic prohibition only the father is obligated:
(ח) And some say, etc. — this is not a new view but the Mechaber's view above:
(ט) It is good for him — a minor who stole or damaged — the court should strike him so he not become accustomed [and likewise injury, shame, and all interpersonal matters — the court must separate him lest harm occur through him]; but he need not pay if the theft is not in evidence — all this is the law; but beyond the letter of the law, whether he injured his body or damaged his property he must pay [Taz and Chayei Adam, and so implies Gra]:
