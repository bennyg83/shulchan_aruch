(א) Not so, etc. — that a person does not squander himself — meaning it is not fit that a person should apportion honor to himself.

(ב) So that he not ascend by himself — and this is because in his place the custom was as Rabbenu Yonah wrote, that the chazan calls whomever he wishes; but for Rama's custom that the sgan calls whomever he wishes — no; and likewise what was needed to give a reason that there is no flaw here applies specifically to the custom of Rabbenu Yonah's place.

(ג) And uncertain lineage, etc. — it should read: such as when one woman came before he was gathered from the market, etc. — meaning: granted, for uncertain lineage of his father it is unknown but his mother is known — one can say in the name of his mother's father; but a foundling gathered from the market — his mother too is unknown — how can one call him in the name of his mother's father? Therefore he wrote it deals when one woman came before he was gathered, etc., for in such a case she is believed that she is his mother, as written in Orach Chayim there. But still requires study: perhaps it deals even if she said after he was gathered from the market that she is his mother, and even though in such a case she is believed as written there — nevertheless what do we lose if we believe her to call him to the Torah by his father's name? And it is forced to say Rama's wording is difficult for him, who wrote "in the name of his mother's father" — it implies we know who his mother's father is, and therefore he wrote she said before he was gathered that she is believed and therefore we know for certain; but it is more plausible he spoke of the present situation in exile, for in most places Israel dwell among gentiles, and in such a case there are also gentiles — even if Israelites are the majority, nevertheless we hold the foundling there is in presumption of doubtful gentile status — and if so, how is it permitted to ascend to the Torah? Rather, that she said before he was gathered that she is believed, and we establish him in presumption of Israel; and see Taz who asked we should fear he will divorce his wife in the name of his mother's father — meaning in the name of his father's father there is no such concern that he will divorce in the name of his father's father, and one should not do so, as Rama wrote in Orach Chayim siman 129 seif 10; nevertheless there Beit Shmuel note 19 in the name of Maharshal wrote that because of concern for agunah it is valid when written "Joseph ben Samuel," and in truth his father was called Shimon, but his father's father was called Samuel — and if so, no ruin; unlike if he writes in the name of his mother's father, which is certainly invalid; and Magen Avraham on this in Sefer Avi Ezri.
