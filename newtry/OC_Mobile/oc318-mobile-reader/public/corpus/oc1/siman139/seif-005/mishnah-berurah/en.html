(20) The custom is to cover it. Although strictly speaking it should be rolled closed, as stated in seif 4, the custom is to leave it open and, instead of rolling it, cover it with a cloth before the concluding blessing.
(21) The custom is that it be rolled closed. Covering it alone is insufficient. Taz writes that according to this custom it no longer needs to be covered, since covering it is an unnecessary burden. Only while Kaddish is recited after the reading should it be covered, because at that point they have withdrawn from the reading. The same applies whenever there is a lengthy delay, such as when they sing for a groom or recite an extended Mi Shebeirach; then it is covered with a cloth. Later authorities.
(22) Between one person and the next. The reason is that it is disrespectful to the Torah for it to remain open and wait until the next person arrives.
