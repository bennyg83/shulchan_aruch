Seif 10, "after," etc. This is analogous to Birkat HaMazon, where one returns to the place at which he stopped. Rif and the other authorities explain that he returns and says, "Blessed is the One from Whose food we have eaten," etc. "In order to include," etc. Although he says "HaMevorach," the law differs from Birkat HaMazon, where the leader says "Nevarech" in order to include himself and then repeats "Baruch." The reason is that "Barechu" is an expression of praise rather than a request, unlike expressions such as "Give greatness" or "Say to God," as appears from the Yerushalmi and Bereishit Rabbah. The blessings over the Torah are treated like the blessings over other mitzvot. The Yerushalmi, Berachos chapter 7, discusses the enactment that each reader recites a blessing. Before that enactment, the first reader would bless before the reading and the last after it. The same principle appears in the Tosefta concerning a synagogue in which no one is able to read, and see the end of siman 143. See also Tosafos, Rosh Hashana 33a. Seif 9, "that it is no worse," etc., follows the principle stated in the first chapter of Berachos. Seif 10, "the final blessing," is based on the Mishnah and Gemara at the beginning of the third chapter of Megillah, Berachos 21a, Masekhet Soferim 13:8, and the Yerushalmi, Berachos chapter 7. The Yerushalmi asks how the blessing over Torah reading applies when an individual reads alone, since the source from zimun concerns a group. It answers that the Sages treated Torah reading like the other mitzvot of the Torah, each of which has its blessing. This is also the basis of the ruling in siman 692, seif 1, in the Rama. "This is the Written Torah," as it is written, "and that which was written was upright, words of truth." "And life," etc., is connected to the conclusion of the verse concerning "the words of the wise." In the first chapter of Chagigah they expounded that just as a goad directs the animal, words of Torah direct a person and impart life. The continuation, "like nails firmly planted," refers to the Oral Torah, as indicated by "the words of the wise" and "masters of assemblies"; the Sages expounded that these declare one matter pure and those declare it impure, yet all are words of Torah.
