Seif 10, after, etc. As with Birkat Hamazon, where we say there that he returns to the place where he stopped; and Rif and the other poskim explained that he returns and says, "Blessed is He of whose food we have eaten," etc.
In order to include, etc. Although he said "the blessed One," for similarly in Birkat Hamazon he says "let us bless" in order to include himself, and nevertheless he returns and says "Blessed," etc. And as the Yerushalmi says in Berakhot chapter 7: Shmuel said, he does not remove himself from the group. They challenged: But in the blessing over Torah he says "Bless." Rabbi Avin said: Since he says "the blessed One," he too does not remove himself from the group.
Seif 8, even if he blessed, etc., for because, etc. Like a kohen who reads in place of a levi, who returns and blesses, and as the Yerushalmi says in Berakhot chapter 7: Rabbi Zeira stood and called a kohen in place of a levi, and the levi blessed; they wanted to silence him, and Rav Chiya bar Ashi said to them: leave him, for so they practice among them. That is, they enacted that each one blesses; before the enactment he would only stand and sit, as written in the Tosefta regarding a synagogue where there is no one to read, etc. See below and the end of siman 143, and see Tosafot Rosh Hashanah 33a s.v. ha, and Rabbeinu Tam says that one does not, etc.
Seif 9, that it is no worse, etc. As written in the first chapter of Berakhot.
Seif 10, the final blessing. This is from the Mishnah and Gemara at the beginning of chapter 3 of Megillah, Berakhot 21a, Masekhet Soferim 13:8, and the Yerushalmi, Berakhot chapter 7: Rabbi Zeira asked, these three readings, etc.; they learned the Torah blessing from zimmun only for a group. If so, when one is alone should he not bless? Meaning, even before it, etc. It answers: they made it like the other mitzvot of the Torah; just as the others, etc. This is what is written in siman 692 seif 1 in the gloss: "and one does not," etc.
This is the Written Torah. As it is written, "and written uprightly, words of truth."
And life, etc. This is the end of the verse, "the words of the wise," etc.; and they expounded in the first chapter of Chagigah: just as the goad, etc., to bring life to the world. The end of the verse, "and like nails planted," refers to the Oral Torah, as it is written, "the words of the wise," etc., and "masters of assemblies"; and they expounded: these declare pure, etc.
