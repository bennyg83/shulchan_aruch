And immediately they called him, etc. — this matter is written in the Tur that his brother R' Yehuda was uncertain, and these are his words: I was uncertain whether he must bless "who chose us" — if we say there is no distinction since they enacted this blessing on its public reading, he must bless even though he already blessed; or we say certainly one of them is in vain — how can he bless one blessing twice without interruption in between? At that time I asked my uncle the Rosh and he said he must bless, for the Sages did not distinguish — end of his words. It implies that if there is interruption in between there is no doubt that certainly it was enacted for public reading and a blessing he blesses on his private study does not help for what he will read in public; but if he did not interrupt at all in between he was uncertain whether the Sages' enactment applies also to this — and certainly he could have resolved this doubt if he had recited one verse in between on his way to read; but he was uncertain whether this is needed or it suffices that he say the blessing again without interruption — and on this the Rosh answered that in truth there is no distinction and no interruption is needed in between; and Beit Yosef wanted to resolve this question from Tosafot end of Rosh Hashana but was not precise, for there it deals with interrupting in between — and this was not a doubt for R' Yechiel at all.
