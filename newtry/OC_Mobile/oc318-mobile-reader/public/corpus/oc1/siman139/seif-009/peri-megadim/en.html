He must. See Taz. See Nechalas Tzvi, who gives two explanations. One may say that this applies only during the blessing, and one may say that it applies only during the reading, based on the proof from the end of the chapter Lulav HaGazul: “He reads from the Torah and places it on the ground,” etc. A third explanation may be offered: from the blessing until the end of the reading, he holds the Sefer Torah in his hand. See siman 206, beginning “He must take in his right hand that over which he recites the blessing.” Perhaps Mordechai, cited by Beit Yosef here, alludes to this when he brings proof from Megillah 32a: “He opens, looks, and recites the blessing.” This accords with Maharshal, cited by Taz in seif katan 5, that since he recites the blessing over the Torah, it is proper that it be open. Consequently, he also holds the Sefer Torah during the blessing, and all the more so during the reading, as proved from Sukkah: “He reads and places them on the ground.” See Mordechai, Sukkah.
