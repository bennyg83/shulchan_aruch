[9] They should not answer with him, etc. This is somewhat difficult, for above, siman 124, seif 5, it appears that they do answer. Perhaps one may distinguish that there the congregation answers, whereas here only the chazzan answers. According to this, Olas Tamid’s inference from Rama’s words, “so that they should hear,” etc., namely that even if ten heard, he must repeat the blessing for the sake of the others, is difficult, for they could answer when they hear those who heard responding. It is strained to distinguish between ten and a congregation. I later saw that Magen Avraham in siman 57 writes that if the congregation hears others answer “Baruch,” etc., they answer amen after them, as explained in siman 139, seif 6. He did not notice all of this. In my humble opinion, if one hears the congregation, he too answers “Baruch,” etc. I also find it difficult why Levush omitted the Shulchan Aruch’s ruling that some say if he recited the blessing silently, he must repeat it aloud. It appears that Levush holds that since Shulchan Aruch writes in seif 8 regarding one who arrived late, etc., that he repeats because of the honor of the Torah, it must be that fundamentally he need not repeat it aloud and that this is not indispensable after the fact. This is also implied by Levush, and Beit Yosef similarly infers in the name of Mahari Abuhav from the Tur. In truth, I am astonished by them, for the Tur mentions only that Bar’chu should be said aloud so the congregation can answer “Baruch Hashem,” etc. It is possible that the Tur agrees that this is indispensable. Seif 8, however, discusses the blessing Asher Bachar Banu after he already recited it, and therefore the reason of honoring the Torah is necessary. This is also implied by Abudraham. Rama’s gloss, “so that the people should hear Baruch,” etc., likewise implies that only Bar’chu must be repeated aloud, but not Asher Bachar Banu. I likewise showed grounds for this above in siman 49. This also raises a difficulty against Bach and Olas Tamid, seif katan 16. Mahari Abuhav writes that he found in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah that one must repeat the blessings aloud. It appears to me that this citation is imprecise, for I examined Kol Bo, page 10, which writes these very words in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah, but says only that they must be recited aloud. Mateh Moshe and Shelah cite it likewise. The reasons they give may also be understood as applying only to Bar’chu. I am also astonished that Mahari Abuhav concludes, “Therefore it is good to say it aloud,” which implies that all the reasons apply only initially, but after the fact it is not indispensable and he need not repeat the blessing. This is likewise implied in Kol Bo there and somewhat at the beginning of the next siman. Perhaps for this reason Levush omitted it. This requires study. In any event, initially one must be careful to recite all the blessings aloud. See Sefer Chassidim, siman 254. They move the Sefer Torah when saying, “Who gave us His Torah,” and likewise when saying, “the Torah of truth.” Mateh Moshe.
