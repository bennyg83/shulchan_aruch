(9) They do not answer with him, etc. — it is somewhat difficult, for above siman 124 seif 5 implies they answer; and one can distinguish that there the congregation answers, which is not so here where only the chazan answers. Per this, what Olat Tamid wrote — that it is implied from what Rama wrote "so that they hear," etc., that even if ten heard, he must repeat and bless because of those remaining, for they can answer when they hear those who heard answering — is forced to distinguish between ten and the congregation. Again I saw in Magen Avraham siman 57 that he wrote: if the congregation heard answering "Blessed," etc., he says after them Amen, as explained siman 139 seif 6; end of his words. And he did not notice all this, for it appears to my humble opinion that if he heard from the congregation answering also "Blessed," etc. It is also difficult for me why Levush omitted what Shulchan Aruch wrote: some say that if he blessed quietly he must repeat and bless aloud. And it appears he holds that since Shulchan Aruch wrote in seif 8 "if he delayed coming," etc., because of honor of the Torah, etc., one could derive he must repeat and bless aloud; but he holds for the essence that it is not required and does not invalidate b'dieved, and so is implied in Levush's words; and so deduced Beit Yosef in the name of Mahari Abuhav from the Tur's words. And I truly wonder at them, for the Tur only mentioned that he should say Barechu aloud so the congregation answers "Blessed is Heaven," etc., and through this it is possible the Tur concedes it invalidates; but in seif 8 it deals with the blessing "Who chose us" which he already blessed — therefore they need the reason of honor of the Torah; and so is implied in Avudraham; and so is implied from Rama's gloss "so that the people hear Blessed," etc. — it implies specifically Barechu requires repeating and blessing aloud, not "Who chose us"; and so above siman 49 I gave a face to this. Also on Bach and Olat Tamid note 16 it is difficult from this. Also what Mahari Abuhav wrote that he found in the name of Rabbeinu Yona that one must repeat the blessings aloud — it appears to me not precisely, for I examined Kol Bo page 10, which wrote these things exactly in the name of Rabbeinu Yona, and wrote only "must be said aloud," and so brought Mateh Moshe and Shelah. Also the reasons they wrote can be interpreted specifically on Barechu, for it is wondrous to me that Mahari Abuhav ended with this language: "therefore it is good to say it aloud" — it implies all the reasons he wrote are only l'chatchila, but b'dieved it does not invalidate and one need not repeat and bless; and so is implied in Kol Bo there, and somewhat at the beginning of the next siman; and perhaps therefore Levush omitted it — requires study. In any event l'chatchila one must be careful to say all blessings aloud. And see Sefer Chasidim siman 254; and they sway the Torah scroll when they say "and He gave us His Torah," and likewise when they say "Torah of truth" (Mateh Moshe).
