And afterward. See Taz, seif katan 3, regarding his question, and see what he answers in siman 428, seif katan (6) [5]. At first glance, one may ask from Megillah 22a: “This too is reasonable, for Rav read as a kohen. For if you should think he read as an Israelite, why did he recite the blessing beforehand,” etc. See there. According to the Taz’s explanation, what proof is this? Perhaps he indeed read as an Israelite and wished to recite a blessing. Examine this carefully. Tosafos Yom Tov answers that the Mishnah discusses fast days, when the blessings and curses are read. See there. See also Etz HaChaim and Yad Aharon regarding what they challenge against him. Regarding the principal question, one may answer from Megillah 29b, which states that in the West they completed the Torah in three years. Accordingly, perhaps on Shabbos they read only twenty-one verses for seven people. It would then emerge that the final reader recited the concluding blessing over the curses, and for this reason they said that one may not interrupt in the curses. See the derashos of Maharit and Etz HaChaim on the formulations of Rambam, composed by Rabbi Chaim Abulafia. Responsa Penei Moshe writes that according to all opinions the concluding blessing is D’Rabbanan. The opening blessing is D’Oraisa according to Ramban, Rashba, and Sefer HaChinuch, while according to Rif, Rambam, and Rosh it is D’Rabbanan.
And he recites the blessing. The Sefer Torah should remain closed until the person comes and opens it for his reading. Maharshal, Magen Avraham, Taz, and Olas Tamid.
His face. Taz writes that turning one’s face away is improper, for it appears as though he is not reciting the blessing over what he will read, but over something else, since he makes it appear that he is withdrawing from it. Bach likewise writes that it is proper for the Sefer Torah to remain fully open during the blessing and that he should not turn his face away at all, for this is the opinion of the well-known poskim and Geonim; “and this is my practice.” Those who bow during Bircas HaTorah are mistaken, for one may bow only where Chazal instituted bowing. Lechem Chamudos. Shach, in the name of Rokeach, writes that the ancient custom is to bow and prostrate during Bircas HaTorah in honor of the Torah, unlike those who do not follow this practice. Maamar Mordechai writes likewise.
