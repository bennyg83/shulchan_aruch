[4] By the name of his maternal grandfather, etc. Taz disagrees, because perhaps he will later divorce his wife and write that name in the get, thereby invalidating it. Therefore, he should be called “son of Avraham,” which certainly does not invalidate it, since everyone is a child of Avraham. This is no difficulty, for the rabbi must investigate the father’s name, and the investigation will reveal the truth. Proof is that the son of one who converted to idolatry is called by the paternal grandfather’s name. However, it appears to me that in that case Rama holds like Beis Shmuel in Even HaEzer, siman 129, seif katan 19, in the name of Maharshal, that a get is valid with the paternal grandfather’s name because of the concern of agunah. Even so, this is difficult, since by strict law it is invalid, and Rosh also invalidates it, as does Rama there in his responsum at the end of the matter. Moreover, “son of Avraham” is invalid in a get, as Beis Shmuel writes there in seif 20, unless he wrote “Avinu.”
