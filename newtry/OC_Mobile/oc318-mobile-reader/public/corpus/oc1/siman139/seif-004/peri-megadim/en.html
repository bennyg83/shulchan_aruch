And he sees — Taz Megillah 32a; and in Tosafot s.v. they rolled, etc., Avi Ezri explains what is written "all in it they rolled" — three views: view 1 — R' Yehudah himself l'chatchila concedes to R' Meir, and argues only b'dieved, meaning one should not rebuke him and there is no prohibition if he blesses while still open; view 2 — they hold specifically open, that on it he blesses (or possibly as siman 206 seif 63: between blessing and action there should not be interruption more than kedei dibbur, even in silence); view 3 — doubtful whether like view 1 or the second — and how should he act? Therefore he opens and turns his face; understand well; and Taz explained another explanation; and in my chiddushim I asked on R' Yehudah: he asks from what is written regarding the reader who did not help the translator — if it were that l'chatchila he concedes to R' Meir and only b'dieved there is no prohibition, what is the difficulty? Also per explanation 2 it is bother, and therefore the final blessing they roll first — if so, the translator is required; also regarding blessings there is nothing to err — it implies; and Rashi: all know it is not written, etc. (and per the hint there: let us say the halacha is like R' Yehudah because they reversed them; and Rashi: R' Meir to R' Yehudah — do not err, for there are those who turn their faces, and therefore he noted at the beginning this, and afterward there is nothing to err). Magen Avraham, if not here, in the next letter.
