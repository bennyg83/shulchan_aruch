(1) All who read bless — in the Gemara chapter HaKorei Megillah: the tanna holds the opener blesses before and the closer after; nowadays all bless before and after — the Sages enacted this because of those entering and leaving, lest they not hear the first reader's blessing and say Torah needs no blessing. Difficulty from Bnei HaIr — we do not interrupt in curses and read one — R' Levi: one cannot bless on calamity; rather when beginning he begins the portion before them and ends the portion after them; Midrash Rabbah brought by Tosafot — one reads all because "I said I am with him in trouble" — they should not bless him on their troubles. This seems to contradict that each reader's blessing is from enactment, whereas by law only beginning and end — one can answer this derashah is mere asmakhta, and in mishna days this enactment did not yet exist until amoraic days as "nowadays" states. It appears if two read, even without each blessing, it counts as if each blessed — the second relies on the first; and if two read curses it is as if each blessed and the blessing is on the curses; not so if one reads all — he can begin before curses and end after, and the blessing does not cover only what is unlike the curse — as explained.

(2) And he sees the verse, etc. — braita: open, see, roll, bless per R' Meir; R' Yehuda: open, see, bless, read — Ulla's reason for R' Meir lest they say blessings are written in the Torah; R' Yehuda holds blessings cannot be mistaken and all know blessings are not written in Torah. Halachah: open, see, bless; Tosafot: l'chatchila roll and close before blessing lest they say blessings are written; b'dieved no concern for common people; but after reading in it certainly must roll before blessing per R' Yehuda. One can question why Tosafot concern themselves with R' Meir when halachah is not like him — Tosafot hold R' Yehuda's reason applies when generation were Torah scholars, but now generations deteriorated and ignoramuses exist — concern like R' Meir l'chatchila; b'dieved if already taught, do not cancel; poskim bless l'chatchila while open — Beit Yosef in name of Kol Bo: roll and bless; some say do not roll but open see bless — Rambam; some turn face so blessings not seem written — Rama brought this. If so like Tosafot — but unreasonable: why must R' Meir roll before blessing if could leave open and turn face? Turning face here is wrong — shows he does not bless on what he will read but on something else, as if removing face from Torah; Shulchan Aruch HaRav: proper fully open at blessing, not turn face — opinion of poskim and famous geonim; so I practice.

(3) And after reading he rolls — so too in name of Tosafot; what he wrote "for R' Yehuda" means even R' Yehuda agrees here to R' Meir — as Beit Yosef: reason at beginning R' Meir requires roll bless then open is extra trouble, therefore not needed for R' Yehuda; but at end when no more trouble, roll then bless. So Beit Yosef in name of Mordechai deduced from "open and see" that it was rolled between readers; so Maharshal: final blessing for early authorities — for reading it should be open to see what he will read; but after he read why open — this is ignorant custom not to say immediately after roll and bless he reopens, but stay closed until next reader opens for his reading.
