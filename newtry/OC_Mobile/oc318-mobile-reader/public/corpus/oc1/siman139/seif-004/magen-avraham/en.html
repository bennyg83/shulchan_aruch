(א) They bless before it — Beit Shmuel wrote page 98 that the blessing before is d'oraisa; understand well; and nevertheless if he blessed on the Torah in the morning he fulfills his obligation; and in public it was enacted because of honor of the congregation, as Shulchan Aruch HaRav wrote. He wrote further: if he erred in the first and said "Who gave," they rebuked him, and he finished "Who chose us" — he fulfilled, not like Maharil; and see Rabeinu Tam: and if he blessed "Who gave," he finishes after it "Who chose us," end of his words.

(ב) And he opens, etc. — and in Tosafot it is written that he should close before he blesses, for even R' Yehudah concedes in this that it is preferable to roll so they not say blessings written in the Torah; and so in writings that one should practice thus; and Bach wrote that he practiced that the Torah scroll should be open, as the great poskim wrote, and also he should not turn his face; Levush chapter 5 of Berachot wrote: those who bow in the blessing of the Torah err, for one should not chat except in what the Sages enacted, as written siman 481, end of his words; and Shulchan Aruch HaRav wrote, and this is its language: Rokeach implies the early custom was to bow and prostrate in the blessing of the Torah, unlike those who do not practice; and so Maaseh Rav siman 156, end of his words — and he is Rokeach siman 319; and the reason is our intent is not to chat in the blessing but to honor the Torah; and so is implied in Levush siman 57.
