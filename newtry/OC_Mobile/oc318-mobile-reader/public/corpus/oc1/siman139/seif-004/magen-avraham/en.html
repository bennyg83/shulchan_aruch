They recite the opening blessing. Beis Shmuel, page 98, writes that the opening blessing is D’Oraisa. See there. It appears to me that nevertheless, if one recited Bircas HaTorah in the morning, he fulfilled his obligation, and the communal blessing was instituted because of the honor of the congregation, as stated in Sefer Chassidim. He further writes: If the first reader erred and recited Asher Nasan, they rebuked him and he concluded Asher Bachar, he fulfilled his obligation, unlike Maharil. See siman 209. If he completed the blessing Asher Nasan, he should recite Asher Bachar afterward.
And he opens, etc. Tosafos writes that he should close it before reciting the blessing, for even Rabbi Yehudah agrees that this is preferable, so people should not say the blessings are written in the Torah. The writings likewise state that this is the proper practice. Bach writes that his practice was for the Sefer Torah to remain open, as held by the leading poskim, and that he also did not turn his face away. Lechem Chamudos, at the end of the fifth chapter of Berachos, writes that those who bow during Bircas HaTorah are mistaken, for one may bow only where Chazal instituted bowing, as stated in siman 281. Shach writes: “Rokeach implies that the ancient custom was to bow and prostrate during Bircas HaTorah, unlike those who do not practice this.” Mateh Moshe, siman 156, writes likewise. This is in Rokeach, siman 319. The reason is that our intention is not to bow because of the blessing, but in honor of the Torah. This is also implied by Levush, siman 150.
