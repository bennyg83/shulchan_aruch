One must hold the sefer Torah at the time of blessing — in the Tur it is written, and these are his words: and he relied on this custom from that in Sukkah — such was the custom of the people of Jerusalem: a man goes out from the door of his house with his lulav in his hand, etc., until he was reading in the Torah and raising his hands — he places it on the ground — meaning one must take the book in his hand at the time of reading — end of his words. And one can ask: why did he write at the time of blessing? And one must say that he speaks even at the time of blessing, and all the more so at the time of reading. And one can ask: from where do we know at the time of blessing? For we say there "he was reading in the Torah," etc. One can say he derives it from what is stated above "he opens and sees and blesses" — from this one learns he must hold it in his hand when he blesses, as the Mordechai wrote — this is the precise reading.
