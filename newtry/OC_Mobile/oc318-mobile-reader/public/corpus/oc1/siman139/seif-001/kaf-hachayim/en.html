(1) [Seif 1] In a place where they practice that the oleh himself reads, etc. — like that incident of R' Akiva, whom they called to the Torah and he did not wish to ascend because he had not prepared it first; and the verse teaches: "then he saw and declared it," etc. — Midrash Tanchuma parashat Yitro and Midrash Rabba parashat Ki Tisa; Tur and Beit Yosef. And Beit Yosef wrote that this is for them, where the oleh read aloud; but for us, where the oleh does not read aloud, even if he did not prepare it we have no concern; and so Bach. However he wrote: nevertheless it is a mitzvah from the preferred way that the oleh too prepare it first two or three times; and therefore it is fit that each person prepare it first, lest they call him tomorrow on Shabbat to ascend, end of his words; and they brought this in Keneset HaGedolah in Hagahot Beit Yosef note 2, and wrote: and it appears to me that in reading the portion "twice Scripture and once translation" one fulfills the preparation. And so Olat Tamid note 1; Rabbi Akiva Eiger note 1; Avnei Nezer on the law of ascending to the Torah note 52; Ledavid note 52; and so Peri Chadash note 1, except he wrote there Peri Chadash that one must return and prepare it again another time so it will be three times; see there; and they brought Rabbi Akiva Eiger note 1; and what Yafeh LaLev wrote at the end of note 2, that from the words of the Zohar that Shulchan Aruch HaRav brought below siman 141 note 1, it appears sufficient even once is not required — see there; and if so, why should we divide the equals where there is no decisive ruling; and see our words below at the beginning of siman 142.
