1) [Seif 1] In a place where they have the custom that the oleh himself reads, etc. Like that [incident] of Rabbi Akiva, whom they called to the Torah and he did not wish to ascend because he had not first arranged [reviewed] it, and he expounds it from the verse, 'then He saw it and declared it,' etc. Midrash Tanchuma, parashas Yisro, and Midrash Rabbah, parashas Ki Sisa. Tur and Beis Yosef. And the Beis Yosef wrote that this is for them, where the oleh would read aloud; but for us, where the oleh does not read aloud, even though he did not arrange it, it is of no concern to us. And so wrote the Bach; only that he wrote: however, it is the choicest [manner of] the mitzvah that the oleh too arrange it first, two and three times; and therefore it is fitting that each one arrange it first, for perhaps they will call him to ascend the next day on Shabbos. End of his words. And the Knesses HaGedolah cited him in the Hagahos Beis Yosef, ot 2, and wrote: and it appears to me that with the reading of the parashah twice Scripture and once Targum one fulfills the obligation of arranging it. And so wrote the Olas Tamid, ot 1; Eliyah Rabbah, ot 1; Emes LeYaakov in the law of ascent to the Sefer Torah, ot 52; Ledovid Emes, siman 5, ot 52. And so wrote the Pri Chadash, ot 1; only that the Pri Chadash wrote there that one must return and arrange it another time, so that it be three times; see there, and the Eshel Avraham cited him, ot 1. And as for what the Yafeh LaLev wrote at the end of ot 2, that from the words of the Zohar cited by the Soles Belulah below in siman 141, s.k. 1, it appears that even once suffices -- this is not compelling, as seen there; and if so, why should we distinguish [between] the equal [views] in a place where there is no decisive [proof]? And see our words below at the beginning of siman 142.
