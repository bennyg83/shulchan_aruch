[7] He turns his face, etc. — Bach concludes it is proper not to turn his face at all but leave it fully open — we are not concerned they will say blessings are written in the Torah; such was the practice, brought by later authorities; so I found in Matteh Moshe siman 227; Kenesset HaGedolah wrote he practices not to look at the writing but to look outside. Penei Raz wrote "and he put a mask on his face" — from when they come from the ark and Torah reading they cover the face. Shelah 254d — I heard from Maharshal that those who go up to Torah and bow in the Torah blessing should be taught not to bow; but there was a custom when going up and seeing the portion he would read, he bowed — not for the blessing's bow but in honor of the Torah scroll, then said blessings — until here. In Lechem Chamudot 43 I found: I say all who bow at Torah blessing beginning and end err — until here; see above siman 51. But below end siman 150 Levush explains one bows; and I saw in Rokeach end siman 319 they say yehalelu when returning Torah to its place because in Torah blessing they bow for honor of Torah — yehalelu to inform it is not for divinity in Torah that the Shechinah rests on him, nor because he too is a god — His name alone is exalted — until here; brought at length in Matteh Moshe siman 256; he concludes from his words ancient custom is to bow in Torah blessing, not as those who say it is not custom. It implies he bows in the blessing itself, for difficult to explain before beginning; nevertheless seems he should not bow at end of blessing lest it appear for the blessing as siman 113. Panim Me'irot teshuvah — final blessing for all d'rabbanan, first for Ramban and Rashba and Sefer HaChinuch d'oraisa, for Rif and Rambam and Rosh d'rabbanan.
