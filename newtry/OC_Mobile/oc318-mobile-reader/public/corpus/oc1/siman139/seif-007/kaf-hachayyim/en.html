7) There, gloss: And in these countries they do not practice so, etc. The Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah wrote in the Hagahos Beis Yosef, ot 4, and this is his language: it appears to me that the customs do not conflict; for that which they practice in our places, that the shaliach tzibbur ascends on his own without being called, is on days when the segan does not call [the olim], but rather the olim ascend on their own, such as Monday and Thursday, Shabbos Minchah, Rosh Chodesh, Chanukah, Purim, and public fasts; and that which they practice in Ashkenaz, that the chazan does not ascend unless the segan calls him, is on days when the segan calls; and so it is the widespread custom in these places, that the chazan does not ascend on his own unless the segan calls him. And by this distinction, that which the world practices on the aforementioned days, ascending on their own, is settled: for since they have the custom on these days that the segan does not call, it is like granting permission, as though the segan said, 'whoever wishes to ascend and read, let him ascend.' And this is also the reason for that which they practice in Constantinople, that when there is a celebration in the congregation, after they call up a Kohen and a Levi and three [more], whoever wishes to ascend, ascends: since they have the custom not to call by name except a Kohen and a Levi and 'samuch' and 'mashlim,' and the rest, whoever wishes to ascend to honor the celebrant, ascends -- it is as though they called them. And in Turya, may God protect it, I saw that when there is a celebration in the congregation, after the celebrant calls up one and two [olim] and does not wish to call any more by name, the shamash stands and announces, 'whoever wishes to honor the celebrant, let him ascend and read.' And they practice so because in these places most call up all the olim at their celebration, and no oleh ascends on his own; therefore the shamash announces, 'whoever wishes to ascend, ascends,' which is a granting of permission. Also that which they practice in some places, that they do not call the Sefer Torah by name, but rather they ascend according to [where they are] seated, that one Shabbos begins from one [end] until seven, and on another Shabbos ascends the one who left off at his place for another Shabbos -- this too is like granting permission. And the same applies also to Simchas Torah, when the entire congregation ascends without being called except for a Kohen and a Levi and three and 'samuch' -- it is like granting permission. But indeed, those who ascend without being called on a day when the segan calls do not act properly; and perhaps there is no objection to the ascent itself, for even though he ascends on his own, as long as the chazan tells him to read, it suffices. And this is also the reason [that permits] the sage of the congregation or the head of the congregation, when they call him for the Shirah or for the Ten Commandments or for the Tochachah, to read himself without being granted permission: for since the custom is for the sage to read them, it is like granting permission. However, if they called a person who is not accustomed to read them, he does not read until they tell him to read, etc. End of his language. However, one who observes will see that this distinction that he wrote is only according to the custom of his place; but there are places that have the custom that even at Shabbos Minchah and Monday and Thursday and Rosh Chodesh and Chol HaMoed and at a celebration and the like, they do not ascend without being called; and so, 'each river follows its own course.' And in a place where they have the custom that at all times they do not ascend without being called, the shaliach tzibbur too does not ascend without being called, for so the custom has spread in most places, like the Rama, of blessed memory, as the Ledovid Emes wrote, siman 5, ot 44, and the Yafeh LaLev, ot 3; and so is the custom here in the holy city of Yerushalayim, may it be rebuilt and established.
