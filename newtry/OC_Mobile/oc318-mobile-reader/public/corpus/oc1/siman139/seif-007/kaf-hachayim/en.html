(7) There, Rama gloss: and in these countries they do not practice thus, etc. — Keneset HaGedolah wrote in Hagahot Beit Yosef note 4, and this is its language: it appears to me the customs do not disagree; for what they practice in our places, that the chazan ascends by himself without being called — that is on days when the sgan does not call but the olehim ascend by themselves, such as Monday and Thursday, Minchah of Shabbat, Rosh Chodesh, Chanukah, Purim, and public fast days; and what they practice in Ashkenaz not to have the chazan ascend unless the sgan calls him is on days when the sgan calls; and so is the simple custom in these places not to have the chazan ascend by himself unless the sgan calls him. And through this division is settled what the public practices on those days ascending by themselves — for since they practice on those days not to have the sgan call, behold it is like granting permission, as if the sgan said: whoever wishes to ascend and read, let him ascend. And this reason too for what they practiced in Constantinople: when there is rejoicing in the congregation, after Cohen and Levi and three read, whoever wishes ascends — for since they practice not to call by name but Cohen, Levi, the next, and the one who concludes, and the rest whoever wishes to ascend to honor the host of the simchah ascends — behold it is as if they called him. And in Turei Zahav I saw: when there is simchah in the congregation, after the host of the simchah reads for one and two and does not wish to read more by name, the shamash stands and announces: whoever wishes to honor the host of the simchah, let him ascend and read; and they practiced thus because in these places most call at their simchah for all the olehim, and there is no one ascending by himself — therefore the shamash announces whoever wishes to ascend, for it is granting permission. Also what they practiced in some places not to call the Torah scroll by name but to ascend according to those seated — Shabbat one begins from one through seven, and the next Shabbat whoever stopped in his place for the next Shabbat — this too is granting permission; and likewise Simchat Torah when all the congregation ascends without being called except Cohen, Levi, and three and the next — it is granting permission. However, those who ascend without being called on a day when the sgan calls do not act properly; and perhaps there is no stringency on the ascent itself, for even if he ascends by himself, as long as the chazan tells him "read," it suffices. And this is also the reason for the sage of the congregation or head of the congregation: when they call him for Shirah or the Ten Commandments or rebukes, he reads by himself without granting permission, for wherever the custom is for the sage to read them, behold it is like granting permission. However, if they called a person not accustomed to read them, he does not read until they tell him to read, etc.; end of his words. However, the observer will see this division he wrote is only per the custom of his place; but there are places that practice that even on Shabbat Minchah, Monday and Thursday, Rosh Chodesh, Purim, and simchah and the like, they do not ascend without being called — and if so, each place and its custom; and in a place where at all times they do not ascend without being called, the chazan too does not ascend without being called, for so spread the custom in most places, as Mor Uktzia wrote, as Ledavid siman 5 note 44, and Yafeh LaLev note 3; and so is the custom here in the holy city Jerusalem.
