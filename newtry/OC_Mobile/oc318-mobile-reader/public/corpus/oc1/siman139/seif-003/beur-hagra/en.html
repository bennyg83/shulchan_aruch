Seif 3: Even the head, etc. This is stated in the Tosefta cited by the Rosh in the third chapter of Megillah, siman 11.
And in these countries, etc. Because in these countries the chazzan does not call people up on his own authority, but at the direction of the gabbai. See Darchei Moshe.
And one whose father, etc. As in the verses, “Do you know Lavan son of Nachor?” and “Mefivoshes son of Shaul,” etc., there. Likewise, “They came to Reuel their father,” as stated in the Mechilta and Rashi there. See Yevamos 62b.
But not, etc. Specifically, etc., so that, etc. As stated, human dignity is so great that it overrides a negative commandment of the Torah, all the more so in such a case. Chazal were exceedingly stringent regarding public humiliation, as stated, “It is preferable for a person to cast himself into a fiery furnace,” etc., and as we find concerning Tamar and the incident of Mar Ukva.
And likewise if, etc. For they permitted assisting a non-Jewish woman in childbirth, and even on Shabbos they considered permitting it because of enmity, were it not possible to answer, “Our own,” etc. Likewise, Rabbi Yehudah Nesiah considered accepting a gift from that heretic on his festival, except that they devised a stratagem. See Josippon, which relates that they named kohanim after Alexander of Macedon because of the Kohen Gadol, and see Mordechai there. The same is true in many cases, and certainly here, where there is no prohibition at all. The statement, “The name of the wicked shall rot,” means that one should not name others after them, and likewise that one should curse them. But attributing a son to his father is permitted, whether the father was wicked, as in “Achav son of Omri” and many similar examples, or righteous, as in “King Chizkiyahu son of Achaz” and the like.
And an asufi. See Magen Avraham.
By the name of his maternal grandfather. As above, because the children of daughters are also regarded as children, as stated in Yevamos there.
And if it is unknown, etc. As stated in the Yerushalmi, first chapter of Bikkurim, cited by Rash there: It was taught in the name of Rabbi Yehudah that a convert himself brings and recites. What is the reason? “For I have made you the father of a multitude of nations.” Formerly you were the father of Aram; from now on, you are the father of all nations. See above, siman 53, seif 19.
A blind person, etc. As stated in the third chapter of Megillah: A blind person may lead the responsive recitation of Shema and translate. This implies that he does not read from the Torah, since it says that he translates. See Tosafos, Sotah 39a, s.v. keivan, etc.
Because, etc. Gittin 60b; Temurah 14b.
Mahari Weil wrote. That he recites the blessing over the chazzan’s reading, as stated in the Tosefta, Megillah chapter 2, and in the Yerushalmi there, that one person reads and another recites the blessing, and as stated below, siman 692, seif 1, in the gloss. Only actual reading from the Torah is forbidden to a blind person. As for the proof of the Rosh in the third chapter of Megillah, siman 10, “and this is proof,” etc., I do not understand it. In their time, only the first and last readers recited blessings, as the Rosh himself writes there. Why, then, would they call the others up? His words require study.
