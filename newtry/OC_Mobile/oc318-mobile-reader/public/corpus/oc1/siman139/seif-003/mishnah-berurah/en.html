(6) Even the head of the congregation, etc. All the more so, an ordinary person may not call himself. The reason is that it is improper for a person to confer honor upon himself. Moreover, this prevents disputes, with each person seeking to precede the others.
(7) Because it is, etc. The meaning is that it is as though they authorized him to do this from the time they appointed him as chazzan. Beit Yosef explains it more fully: Since the present practice is that the chazzan reads in place of those receiving aliyos, by authorizing him to serve as chazzan they authorized him to read from the Torah. Presumably, they do not object if he also recites the blessing, since in any event he is the reader.
(8) He is not called by name. This is because he is standing there.
(9) So as not to embarrass him publicly. Nevertheless, the caller should not recite titles for his father, such as Chaver or Moreinu, because one may not speak falsely. Rather, he should say the name quickly so that the people do not notice. This applies specifically in that city, as Rama writes. If he comes to another city and the gabbai asks how he should be called to the Torah, he should give the name of his maternal grandfather. Derech HaChayim and Shaarei Ephraim.
(10) By the name of his maternal grandfather. This refers to a case where a woman came before he was taken in from the marketplace and said that she was his mother. Magen Avraham. See Taz, who holds that he should be called so-and-so ben Avraham. Chayei Adam also writes that this is more correct.
(11) If it is not known. If the name of his maternal grandfather is unknown, he is called so-and-so ben Avraham, based on the verse, “For I have made you the father of a multitude of nations.”
(12) Nowadays a blind person may be called. The reason is that since our practice is for the chazzan to read, and he reads from the written text, we are no longer particular about the person receiving the aliyah, because hearing is equivalent to responding.
(13) As we, etc. Meaning, our custom is to be lenient even when the person cannot read word for word with the chazzan from the written text, based on the reason stated above. The same applies to a blind person. As a practical ruling, the later authorities have already written that the custom is to be lenient in accordance with Maharil. Nevertheless, for Parashas Parah and Parashas Zachor it is proper not to call them initially.
