Even the head of the congregation or the chazzan should not read from the Torah until he is told, 'Read.' The custom, however, is that when the prayer leader wishes, he recites the blessing and reads without first obtaining permission, because it is regarded as though, when he was appointed prayer leader, permission for this was already granted to him. {Rama: In these lands this is not the custom, and the chazzan goes up only when the gabbai tells him to go up. He is not called by name like other people receiving an aliyah, who are called 'so-and-so son of so-and-so.' One whose father became an apostate to idolatry is called by his grandfather's name, and not by his own name alone, so as not to shame him publicly. [Terumat HaDeshen, simanim 21 and 68] This applies when he had not previously been accustomed to be called by his father's name. But if he is an adult and was already accustomed in that city to be called by his father's name, and afterward his father became an apostate, he is called by his father's name as before, so as not to shame him publicly. The same applies where there is concern for hostility from the apostate. [Maharam Padua, siman 87] An asufi and a shetuki are called by the name of their maternal grandfather. If even that is unknown, they are called 'son of Avraham,' like a convert. [Rama's own ruling] Maharil wrote that nowadays a blind person may be called to the Torah just as an am ha'aretz may be called.} A blind person is forbidden to read from the Torah, because one may not read even one letter by heart.
