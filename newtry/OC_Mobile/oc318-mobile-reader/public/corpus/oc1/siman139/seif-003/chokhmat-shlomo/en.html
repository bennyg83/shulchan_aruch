Seif 3. In the gloss: “One whose father is an apostate to idolatry is called by the name of his paternal grandfather, but not by his own name alone, so as not to embarrass him publicly.” Note: See Taz, seif katan 1, and see Eliyah Rabbah, who rejects his words on the ground that in a get the rabbi must investigate the father’s name. See there. His answer is ineffective. First, the rabbi need investigate only where the person’s name and his father’s name have not been established in the city. If they have been established in the city, no further investigation is required. Here too, if he becomes established as being called to the Torah by the name Avraham, testimony has thereby been established that he is known as being called by that father’s name, and no further inquiry is required. Second, is a rabbi specifically required for a get? If he divorced her on his own before witnesses, wrote the get himself in his own handwriting, had witnesses sign it, and gave it to her, is she not divorced after the fact? There is therefore concern that he may write her a get independently, have witnesses sign it, divorce her, write the name Avraham, and she may then marry another man. The Taz therefore wrote correctly. See my novellae to Even HaEzer, siman 129, regarding what I wrote concerning these words of the Taz. See there and examine carefully.
