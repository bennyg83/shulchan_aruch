And the blessings in a loud voice, etc. — Some say one must return and bless in a loud voice. From this wording it appears it refers also to the blessings; see Avraham Zuckerman who wondered, for from all proofs Beit Yosef brought in name of Mahari Abuhav it is not compelled — only Barchu is explicit there, not blessings; and Rama gave reason for Barchu only. From Hagahot Maimoniyot chapter 7 laws of prayer orach 80 in name of R' Eliezer of Metz it appears l'chatchila blessings too must be aloud, but b'dieved it does not invalidate; Shulchan Aruch HaRav siman 254 — saying blessings quietly steals the mitzvot — implies prohibition but need not return for this. Even if Beit Yosef's source can be rejected as Avraham Zuckerman wrote, from essence of law there is room to question b'dieved, since these blessings were enacted only for honor of Torah in public — what honor when said between himself and himself and congregation does not hear? [See Sotah 39 — reader may not read until Amen from congregation — implies loud blessing; not explicit it delays b'dieved.] It is not explicit in the Gemara that this delays b'dieved. I also found in Meiri a great support for the first opinion he brings in Shulchan Aruch — because of those entering between first and second to hear Torah reading and they hear without blessing before; and because of those leaving before the last reads and do not hear blessing after. On this explanation they practiced in France to bless in a medium voice to make all blessings heard. From all this: even though b'dieved it seems one need not return and bless — doubt in blessings is lenient — nevertheless we learn the great obligation l'chatchila.
