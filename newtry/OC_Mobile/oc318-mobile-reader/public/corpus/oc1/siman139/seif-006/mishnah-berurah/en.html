(23) Barchu, etc. The reason is as Rama concludes. The blessings too should be said aloud so the entire congregation can answer amen.
(24) He errs. For to whom is he saying Barchu when he says it quietly? Anything not heard by ten people is considered quiet, because a davar shebikdushah may not be recited with fewer than ten listeners. See Magen Avraham, who writes that one should likewise be careful not to say Kaddish quietly for this reason.
(25) So that the people hear and answer. Rama's words imply that this opinion applies only to Barchu, but regarding the blessings it does not invalidate the act after the fact if he recited them quietly. See Biur Halachah for what I wrote concerning this. Initially, however, it is certainly a mitzvah to recite them aloud, as stated above.
(26) The person reciting the blessing. Meaning, when he said Barchu.
(27) Even though they heard the chazzan, etc. See above, siman 57, Mishnah Berurah seif katan 2 and Biur Halachah, where we discussed this.
