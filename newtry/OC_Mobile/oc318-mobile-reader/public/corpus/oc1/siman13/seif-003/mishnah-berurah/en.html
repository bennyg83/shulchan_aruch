(8) On Shabbos — and if he knew from yesterday and forgot to affix tzitzis, it seems we do not penalize him. Proof from below siman 355:3 in Rama where they forgot and did not make an eruv, see there [Chiddushei R. Akiva Eiger].

(9) When in karmelis — a place where carrying is forbidden only rabbinically; therefore he need not remove it because kevod habriyos overrides rabbinic prohibition. Even though tzitzis is a biblical positive commandment, and we do not set aside biblical law for kevod habriyos, nevertheless it is permitted because the Torah did not say "do not wear a garment without tzitzis" but only a positive command to affix tzitzis; and since he cannot affix on Shabbos he does not violate the positive commandment and has only rabbinic prohibition — therefore permitted for kevod habriyos and need not remove the tallis. But in reshus harabim which is Torah law he must remove it, whether tallis gadol or tallis katan, even if he remains naked, for kevod habriyos does not override such Torah prohibition. Below in laws of Shabbos, with God's help, we will explain whether nowadays we have reshus harabim. It appears that if the gedil was intact and invalidation was only in the anaf we can join the view of Rashi above who validates and need not remove immediately but hurry home.

(10) Invalid — whether a corner of the garment was severed leaving three, or one of his tzitzis alone was severed. Even if already invalid when leaving home, since when dressing he did not know we do not penalize him for kevod habriyos is great. Even our tallis gadol which we wear only at prayer for the mitzvah and removing it involves no disgrace — also need not remove, for the Sages did not distinguish tallis from tallis. R. Akiva Eiger questions this in his chiddushim.

(11) Under his garments — it would not be obvious afterward if he walks without tallis katan; nevertheless removing it is greater embarrassment.

(12) Of the tzitzis — whether he came to synagogue and wants to wear his tallis and before donning found the tzitzis invalid and is ashamed to sit in public without a tallis — permitted to wear without a blessing; or if while wearing he sees the tzitzis severed — also need not remove [Birchei Yosef, Orach Chayyim, and other acharonim]. See acharonim who agreed it all depends on the wearer — if he would be ashamed he may wear without a blessing; if not ashamed, no permission of kevod habriyos applies.

(13) That he may wear it — specifically if he learned today; but if he knew from erev Shabbos that the tzitzis were invalid, forbidden to wear on Shabbos for he should have fixed yesterday; if he forgot, one may be lenient [Chiddushei R. Akiva Eiger].

(14) On account of kevod habriyos — specifically if he cannot obtain a borrowed tallis in synagogue.

(15) Specifically on Shabbos — since on Shabbos there is only rabbinic prohibition as above; but on a weekday he violates Torah prohibition every moment of kum va'aseh to make tzitzis, and Torah prohibition — even only shev v'al ta'aseh such as this where he prevents himself from the mitzvah — is not set aside for kevod habriyos unless great embarrassment. Therefore forbidden to wear tallis gadol when he sees it lacks tzitzis, for to sit without tallis Rama holds is only minor embarrassment; likewise if known in reshus harabim while wearing tallis gadol that one tzitzis severed — must remove immediately, for nowadays removing tallis gadol in the market is also only minor embarrassment. However if in synagogue after already wearing the tallis one tzitzis became invalid, many acharonim are lenient not to remove immediately, for since he is among many in synagogue removal is greater embarrassment — only hurry home or the room before the synagogue and remove there. Chayei Adam is stringent and holds removing tallis gadol is only minor embarrassment, for otherwise people regularly remove tallis in public in synagogue. Afterward I found Pri Megadim in introduction to laws of tzitzis also holds like Chayei Adam; therefore proper to follow Orach Chayyim's advice to declare it ownerless, then exempt from making tzitzis, and when home reclaim it.

(16) Forbidden — see Magen Avraham who concludes Rama's weekday stringency does not refer to what he wrote initially "even tallis katan," for regarding tallis katan if tzitzis became known invalid and great embarrassment to remove from under garments — whether in synagogue or in public — need not remove, for great embarrassment overrides kevod habriyos even Torah shev v'al ta'aseh; but must hurry home or room before synagogue and remove there even if engaged in mitzvah in synagogue, since Torah prohibition. On Shabbos need not hurry from synagogue while engaged in mitzvah. One who sees friend's tzitzis severed should not tell him until home — meaning call him to come home and there tell him to remove. Magen Avraham further wrote: if no tzitzis in the city, weekday law like Shabbos; same for tallis obligated only rabbinically, e.g. borrowed after thirty days — weekday law like Shabbos.
