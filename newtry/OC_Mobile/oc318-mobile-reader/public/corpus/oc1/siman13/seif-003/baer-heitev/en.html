The tzitzis. In the marginal notes of Knesset HaGedolah he raised specifically when coming to the synagogue and before donning it he finds the tallis invalid—then it is permitted to wear it without a blessing, for if he will not be wrapped in a tallis like others he will be embarrassed that they will say he has no tallis with tzitzis according to law and is not careful about mitzvos. But if at the time of donning it was valid and he wrapped himself in it and afterward one of the tzitzis snapped, he may remove it from himself, for there is no issue of human dignity since they already saw he had a tallis with tzitzis according to law—see there. And Biur Halacha disagrees and writes that from Rama's words it seems even if he wore it previously and afterward they snapped—see there. And Yad Aharon upheld the words of Knesset HaGedolah and wrote that the meaning of Rama's words implies his words—that he may wear it without a blessing implies that before donning the tallis it snapped, and therefore he writes that he may wear it—see there. He further raised there that everything depends on the will of the wearer: if he is embarrassed he may wear it without a blessing, and if he is not embarrassed there is no issue of human dignity—see there. And see Bnei Chayyim and in Magen Avraham s.k. 1 what he says in the name of Beit Yosef.

And specifically on Shabbos. Magen Avraham raised that if tzitzis snapped on Yom Tov on a tallis katan he need not remove it even on a weekday, for removal is great disgrace; and that which it specifies Shabbos is to include that even though there is also a Shabbos prohibition, nevertheless he need not remove it, and on Shabbos he need not hurry to leave the synagogue; but on a weekday he must hurry to go home to remove it. One who sees his friend's tzitzis snapped should not tell him until he comes home—that is, he should call him to come home and there tell him to remove it. If he knew from yesterday that it was invalid, it is forbidden to wear it on Shabbos, for this is fixing from yesterday. It appears that on a weekday if tzitzis are not available in the city his law is like on Shabbos—Magen Avraham. And one might ask: why do we permit wearing on Shabbos because of human dignity—is there not a remedy to give it to another as a gift and borrow it back, and then he is exempt from tzitzis? One can say it is forbidden to give a gift on Shabbos except for Shabbos need—see Magen Avraham.
