Human dignity. Human dignity overrides a D'Rabbanan prohibition. Carrying in a karmelis is likewise D'Rabbanan, and nowadays we do not have a true public domain, as explained in siman 303. See Knesses HaGedolah.
That is beneath his garments. Although it is not noticeable if he walks without a tallis katan, removing it is nevertheless humiliating for him.
The tzitzis. Shiurei Knesses HaGedolah concludes that this applies specifically when one arrives at the synagogue and, before putting on the tallis, discovers that it is invalid. He may then wear it without a blessing, because he would be embarrassed to sit without a tallis while everyone else is wrapped in one, and people would say that he does not own a properly fringed tallis. If, however, he discovered the defect while already wearing it, he need not remove it until he returns home. Magen Avraham.
Specifically on Shabbos. Magen Avraham concludes that if a tzitzis becomes severed in a public domain while one is wearing a tallis katan, he need not remove it even on a weekday, because removing it would be extremely humiliating. The reference to Shabbos teaches a greater novelty: even though a Shabbos prohibition is also involved, he nevertheless need not remove it. On Shabbos, however, he should place the garment upon himself in a manner that reduces the prohibition, as explained by Magen Avraham.
