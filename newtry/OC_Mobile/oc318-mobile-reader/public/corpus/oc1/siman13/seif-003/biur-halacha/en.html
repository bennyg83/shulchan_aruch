And specifically on Shabbos, etc.—see in Mishnah Berurah what he wrote in the name of Chayei Adam and Peri Megadim regarding if one of his tzitzis snapped in the synagogue on a weekday that he must remove it immediately; see in Peri Megadim in his introduction where he was in doubt if only two strings snapped and two strings remained intact whether he must remove immediately—perhaps because of human dignity we rely on the view that holds we require only the strings for the white—see there. It appears if they are invalid only due to a doubt—for example he lacked only two of all eight strings, and the like, and as above siman 12—certainly one may be lenient that he need not remove it in the synagogue even on a weekday.

But on a weekday it is forbidden—written in the siddur of Derech HaChayyim in the name of Peri Megadim that on a weekday if the corner of the tallis snapped in the synagogue and the gedil remains, he need not hurry to go home, for one may rely on R. Yitzchak who validates what remains sufficient for a knot even from the gedil, and immediately when he comes home he removes it and ties tzitzis on it. In truth this matter is astonishing, for behold it is a Torah doubt—perhaps the law follows Rashi. I looked in Peri Megadim there who explained his words: specifically where tzitzis are not available to him at all—for there it is only a rabbinic prohibition like on Shabbos, as Magen Avraham wrote; not so in the case of Derech HaChayyim where he has tzitzis at home—certainly he must at least hurry to go home, for behold it is a Torah doubt.
