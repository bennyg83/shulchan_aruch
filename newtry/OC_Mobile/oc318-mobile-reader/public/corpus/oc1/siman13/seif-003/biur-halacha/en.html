Specifically on Shabbos, etc. See Mishnah Berurah, which cites Chayei Adam and Peri Megadim regarding one whose tzitzis became severed in the synagogue during the week, requiring him to remove the garment immediately. Peri Megadim, in his introduction, remains uncertain where only two strings were severed and two strings remained whole, whether immediate removal is required. Perhaps, because of human dignity, one may rely upon the view that only the strings assigned to the white portion are required; see there. It appears that where the tzitzis are invalid only because of doubt, such as where only two of the eight ends are missing, or a similar case, as discussed above in siman 12, one may certainly be lenient and need not remove the garment in the synagogue even during the week.
But during the week it is forbidden. Siddur Derech HaChayim writes in the name of Peri Megadim that if the loose portion of a tzitzis string was severed in the synagogue during the week but the wound section remained, one need not hurry home. He may rely upon Ri, who validates the tzitzis when enough remains to tie a bow even if that amount is present only within the wound section. Upon reaching home, he should immediately remove the garment and attach valid tzitzis. This ruling is surprising, because the matter is a doubt concerning a Torah obligation, since perhaps the halachah follows Rashi. I examined Peri Megadim there and found that he limits his ruling to a place where no replacement tzitzis are available at all. In that case the prohibition is only D’Rabbanan, comparable to Shabbos, as Magen Avraham writes. This does not apply to the case discussed by Derech HaChayim, where valid tzitzis are available at home. There he must at least hurry home, since the matter is a doubt concerning a Torah obligation.
