Seif 2. Whether a tallis katan or a tallis gadol. See the language of Rosh cited by Beit Yosef: regarding our talleisim, even if they are not properly fringed, one may go out wearing them in a public domain. Either the attached strings constitute valid tzitzis, or, if not, they become subordinate to the garment because he does not intend to complete them. See there. This requires study in light of the ruling above in siman 10, seif 7, that garments left open and therefore obligated in tzitzis only because of stringency may not be worn on Shabbos. Why do we not apply the same either-or reasoning? If they are not obligated in tzitzis, the strings should nevertheless become subordinate to the garment because he does not intend to complete them. See responsa Shav Yaakov, end of siman 5. It appears to me that the strings remain significant, and his intention to complete them means that he intends to open the garment so that it will certainly become obligated in tzitzis. He will then untie the strings, set them aside, and attach them again to avoid the problem of “you shall make,” and not something made through an invalid state.
Magen Avraham, seif katan 5. He need not inspect it. According to what I wrote above in my gloss to siman 8, on Taz, seif katan 8, there is no proof for this. One may say that inspection was not required because of carrying on Shabbos, since that would require him to inspect the tzitzis continuously whenever walking in a public domain. The concern of an invalid blessing, by contrast, has a defined point: each time he recites a blessing, he must first inspect them.
