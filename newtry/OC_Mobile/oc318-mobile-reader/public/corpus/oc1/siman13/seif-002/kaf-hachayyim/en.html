2) [Se'if 2.] If it was properly fitted with tzitzit, it is permitted to go out wearing it into the public domain, etc. Rif, of blessed memory, writes in a responsum that it is forbidden to go out on Friday night wearing a tallit fitted with tzitzit, because night is not a time of obligation for tzitzit. Rabbi David ben Mesir Leon cites this in a manuscript responsum; Knesset HaGedolah brings it in Hagahot Beit Yosef. Rabbeinu Yeshayah the Elder writes likewise in his manuscript rulings to the chapter Tolin; Birkei Yosef, ot 2, cites him. Beit Yosef, however, writes in siman 18 that it is permitted to go out even at night, although night is not a time of obligation for tzitzit, because the tzitzit are not a burden but an adornment and ornament of the garment. Knesset HaGedolah there likewise writes in the name of Radbaz, part 1, siman 244, that he is among those who permit it: although night is not a time of obligation for tzitzit, a properly fringed tallit is nevertheless apparel for every Jew. These are his words. Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah, ot 4, also cites him and writes: “Although the author, in his abridged work, omitted Rambam's phrase ‘whether by day or by night,’ this is not because he holds that it is forbidden at night. He simply writes that it is permitted to go out wearing it, which implies both by day and by night. I ruled likewise in a responsum, and this is the common practice.” Olat Tamid, ot 3 and ot 4; Magen Avraham, se'if katan 1; Erekh HaShulchan, ot 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ot 2; Ikkarei HaDat, siman 2, ot 70; and Shatilei Zeitim, ot 3, rule likewise. See siman 18, ot 1, where we wrote further on this. Even garments made of materials whose tzitzit obligation is only rabbinic may be worn into the public domain. Olat Tamid there, ot 3.
