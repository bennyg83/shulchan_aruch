It is permitted to go out. Even at night, which is not the time for the mitzvah of tzitzis, because the tzitzis adorn the garment. Beit Yosef, siman 18.
A small garment. Responsa Rama, siman 110, discusses whether one may go out wearing a small four-cornered garment that lacks the minimum size stated in siman 16; see there.
Techeiles. For we rule that the absence of techeiles does not prevent fulfillment with the white strings.
Upon his shoulders. Meaning, not in the manner in which he ordinarily wears it during the week. See siman 301, seif 30.
Its established presumption. One might ask: the halachah requires inspection when reciting the blessing and does not permit reliance upon the garment’s prior valid status, as stated in siman 8, seif 9. The answer is that there, if one did not inspect the tzitzis when reciting the blessing, he might never inspect them, and one cannot rely indefinitely upon their prior status because strings are liable to break. Here, however, he inspected them when reciting the blessing and therefore need not inspect them every time he goes out. From this one may derive that if a person left the synagogue and removed his tallis, even if he diverted his attention and must recite a new blessing when putting it on again, he nevertheless need not inspect it again.
