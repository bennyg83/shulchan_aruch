The ruling teaches that one should not think a tallis used for a mitzvah is considered ordinarily worn even when draped in a manner that is not normal clothing, and is therefore permitted. Rather, it is forbidden. Alternatively, one might have thought that the tallis itself is a burden, but that it is permitted on Shabbos because the positive commandment of tzitzis overrides the prohibition of carrying. See Rambam, chapter 19, halachah 20, and Maggid Mishneh there. One might then conclude that it was entirely permitted even when merely placed upon his shoulders. The ruling teaches that this is forbidden. First, the tzitzis cannot be worn in any other manner, whereas carrying the tallis itself was never permitted. Second, tzitzis does not override Shabbos. The reason one may go out wearing valid tzitzis in a public domain is that they are ornaments and appurtenances of the garment, as Rambam writes there. For this reason they are permitted even at night. See Magen Avraham, seif katan 1; with Hashem’s help the matter will be explained there.
