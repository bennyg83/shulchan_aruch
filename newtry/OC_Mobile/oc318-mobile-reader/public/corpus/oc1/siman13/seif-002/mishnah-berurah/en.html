(4) It is permitted to go out. Even at night, when the mitzvah of tzitzis does not apply, because they adorn the garment. This applies even to talleisim made from other materials, according to the authorities who maintain that their obligation is only D’Rabbanan, because they are nevertheless certainly obligated and the tzitzis adorn the garment. This differs from a garment exactly half open and half closed, regarding which the Shulchan Aruch rules in siman 10, seif 7, that one may not go out wearing it on Shabbos, because its obligation is only doubtful. So write Peri Megadim and Artzos HaChayim, unlike Shav Yaakov; Olas Tamid likewise rules this way plainly.
(5) Techeiles. We rule that the absence of techeiles does not prevent fulfillment with the white strings, nor does the absence of white strings prevent fulfillment with techeiles, for the verse says, “You shall see it,” implying each one independently.
(6) Upon his shoulders. It is not an adornment but a burden, because this is not the ordinary manner of wearing it during the week. If, however, it covers most of his body, that is sufficient. See below, siman 301, seif 30.
(7) Before going out with it. Since he inspected it in the morning when reciting the blessing and found it intact, it is unlikely to break within such a short time, even if he removed it in the interim. This implies that tzitzis must also be inspected on Shabbos, as Bach writes explicitly in siman 8. The common practice is not careful about this; see Biur Halachah.
