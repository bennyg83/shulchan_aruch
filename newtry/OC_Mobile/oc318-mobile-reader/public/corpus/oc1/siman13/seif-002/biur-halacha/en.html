Before going out with it. See Mishnah Berurah regarding inspection on Shabbos. Perhaps the reason for the common practice is that inspection would serve no purpose, since even if the tzitzis were found invalid, it would still be permitted to wear the tallis in the synagogue on Shabbos, in accordance with Rama’s ruling in seif 3. This explanation works only according to Magen Avraham, who maintains that the purpose of inspection is to avoid walking in a garment without valid tzitzis. According to Taz, however, the purpose is to avoid the severe prohibition of uttering Hashem’s Name in a blessing recited in vain. He should therefore inspect them, and if they are invalid, wear the tallis without a blessing. Even according to Magen Avraham, the rationale applies only in the synagogue. If one puts on the tallis at home, such as in a city without an eruv where the custom is to put it on at home, inspection is certainly required for two reasons: first, so that he not walk in a garment without tzitzis; second, because he intends to carry it into a karmelis or public domain. The proper practice is to inspect the tallis on Erev Shabbos when placing it in its case, so that it will not require inspection on Shabbos, as Magen Avraham rules above in siman 8; see there.
