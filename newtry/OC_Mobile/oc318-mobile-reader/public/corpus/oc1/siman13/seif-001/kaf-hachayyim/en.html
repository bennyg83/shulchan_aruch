1) [Se'if 1.] The four tzitzit are mutually indispensable, etc. One who goes out wearing such a garment into the public domain on Shabbat is liable to a sin-offering. The remaining strings constitute a burden. One cannot say that they are nullified to the garment, because they are important to him, since he intends to complete them. Beit Yosef writes this in the name of Tosafos and Rambam.
1) According to this, if all four sets of tzitzit were severed and none retained kedei anivah, so that he will certainly replace them with others, the remnants are nullified to the garment and it is permitted to go out wearing it on Shabbat. Turei Zahav, se'if katan 1. Olat Tamid, ot 2, agrees. Levush, however, writes in siman 301, se'if 38: “If it has no tzitzit at all, it is permitted to go out wearing it,” etc. This implies that if any remnants remain, even without kedei anivah, it is forbidden to go out wearing it. Eliyahu Rabbah, se'if katan 1, writes likewise. Eliyahu Rabbah adds: “It appears that even according to Turei Zahav it is permitted only with respect to the Shabbat prohibition. It remains forbidden because of neglecting the mitzvah of tzitzit, unless the case is one where it is permitted because of human dignity, as explained below. The same is implied by Levush there.” Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ot 1, and Shatilei Zeitim, ot 2, write likewise. See Ma'amar Mordechai, ot 1, and Mishbetzot Zahav, ot 1. See also Pitchei Olam, ot 1, who cites Biur HaRambam as calling the aforementioned words of Turei Zahav surprising: since he intends to cut off the remnants, it cannot be said that they are nullified to the garment. These are his words. One should therefore be stringent in accordance with Levush and Eliyahu Rabbah.
