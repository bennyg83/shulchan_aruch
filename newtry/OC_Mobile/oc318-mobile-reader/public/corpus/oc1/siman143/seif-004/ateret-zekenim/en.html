If an error is found in the maftir reading, there is no need to take out another Sefer Torah, because it is not part of the day's obligation. Beit Yosef, siman 282, in the name of Rashba. If a Sefer Torah was found to be missing one word when the seventh aliyah was being read, and there was no other Sefer Torah available, while three sections still remained before completion of the sidrah because the custom in that city was to call ten men, then since seven men had already been called and the day's obligation had been fulfilled, no additional men should be called to the Sefer Torah with blessings before and after. Rather, the chazzan should continue and complete the sidrah in that Sefer Torah together with the man called for the seventh aliyah, and the seventh should recite the concluding blessing. If, however, the omission is found during the reading for the fifth or sixth aliyah, so that seven men, who constitute the day's obligation, have not yet been called, one may rely on the great authorities, Kol Bo and Abudraham, who permit completing the entire sidrah with seven men, since this is the day's obligation and it is a pressing circumstance. At Minchah they should not take out a Sefer Torah, because the Minchah reading is not such a full obligation, for Ezra instituted it only on account of those who sit at street corners, as stated at the end of the chapter Merubah. Maharam of Lublin, siman 84. If the word rechavah is found in the Sefer Torah in place of rechavo, Maharam there considers it a change of wording. Nevertheless, it appears that the meaning is not actually altered, because rechavah may be read with a cholam, like rechavo, just as ahalah is read with a cholam under the lamed as though it were written ahalo. So it appears to me. If the yud was omitted from the Divine name Elokeinu in a Sefer Torah, Maharam of Lublin writes in siman 112 that one should erase the nun and vav and afterward write the Name correctly in their place. This appears correct, unlike what is written in Mishnas Binyamin, that the yud should be suspended above the line. That is entirely untenable, for the yud is an essential part of the Name, and part of the Name may not be suspended. Likewise, if the word HaAdon is found written with a full vav, or in a similar case, it is obvious that the vav should be erased and the dalet extended toward the nun. This does not constitute erasing the Divine Name, because that vav was never sanctified. Since this Name properly consists of only four letters, the vav is like any other letter that is not part of the Name and certainly has no sanctity. This appears obvious.
