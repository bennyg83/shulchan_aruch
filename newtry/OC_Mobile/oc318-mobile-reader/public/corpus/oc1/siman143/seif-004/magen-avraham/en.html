If an error is found. And Bach wrote, and these are his words: our custom according to Mordekhai — if he has not yet read three verses, he reads the error from memory until he reads three verses and stops there; and if it is less than three verses adjacent to the parasha, he completes the parasha; and so practiced the great ones of the world, the Ashkenazim before us. And Shulchan Aruch ruled this as a new ruling, and I did not know from where this ruling came — until here. And so too Maharal of Prague in Sefer Ner Yisrael daf 66: one word that has no meaning at all unless it is joined to another word is permitted to say from memory; and in truth the words of Shulchan Aruch have no basis except Mahari Bei Rav ruled thus, and as Rav Yaakov Bei Rav in Yoreh Deah siman 279 — the reason: since Rambam permits reading in an invalid Torah scroll, although we do not hold like him, nevertheless one may rely on him b'dieved that his reading is valid and he need not bless again. And his words require study, for even in a valid Torah scroll, if they take out another, one must bless — as is explicit in Yoma chapter 7, and so in siman 144, and so is implied by all the poskim (and see siman 215). If so, why does he not bless again? And Taz in Yoreh Deah strained to settle this — and therefore it appears to me that in a place where there is no custom, one should practice as Bach wrote, and in Massekhet Soferim chapter 11 halakhah 2 implies it is forbidden to read from memory; therefore they read in the invalid Torah scroll three verses and read the error from memory — siman 144. And in a place where there is a custom, it is difficult to change the custom; and in any case one should practice that if he began only the first word of the third verse, he should complete it and stop there, even if it is two verses adjacent to the parasha — he should read until the parasha, for it is better to push off this than to enter ourselves into doubt of a blessing mentioned explicitly in the Talmud, especially in a matter where Bach testified that the great ones practiced so. And in Levush on Laws of Torah Scroll, section 8, he wrote in the name of the Gaon Maharal of Prague that one should not take out another at all, as Beit Yosef wrote in Yoreh Deah siman 279 in the name of some poskim; therefore one may rely on them at least to complete the parasha with that in which the error was found. Rama wrote in Yoreh Deah siman 279, and these are his words: and there is no distinction in this between whether the error was found in the first Torah scroll or whether the error was found in the second that they read for the day's obligation for the maftir — until here. And his words are taken from what Beit Yosef wrote in siman 282 in the name of Rashbatz, and these are his words: a second Torah scroll in which an error was found — one must take out another Torah scroll, for the day's obligation is the maftir; and on every Shabbat, if a Torah scroll became invalid after they read seven — for example it was torn — it is possible, since the maftir reading is only on account of honor of the Torah, that therefore it suffices to read three verses in an invalid book without a blessing, so as not to burden the congregation; and one may question the matter — until here. And therefore Rama inferred and wrote in the second Torah scroll that they read for the day's obligation for the maftir, and he did not write generically "maftir" because it is doubtful to him; and if so it is clear one may rely on the great ones that I wrote above that one should not take out another; and not only that, but even if the error is found in the seventh [aliyah], he completes his reading in this Torah scroll and blesses after it, and he is the maftir — siman 282 s.k. 5, that we should not enter ourselves into dispute needlessly on account of an addition. And in this, siman 282, he inferred from the words of Rashbatz that if it occurred in the seventh after he read three verses, one should not take out another — because all his words are rejected, for it is clear one must read the entire order; for although there are places that practice to complete the order in another verse, nevertheless they read it on the coming Shabbat; if so, in any case they are obligated to read them; and so from what is written siman 137 s.k. 3, that even if he skipped one verse he returns — unlike the maftir, who reads only what they already read. And Taz in Yoreh Deah there wrote that if it is found in the seventh, he completes his reading and they take out another Torah scroll for the maftir at the time of saying Kaddish; and if the error is found in the maftir, he reads the error from memory — until here. And to my humble opinion, as I wrote: and it is clear he blesses after it; and see siman 215.

From the place where the error was found. Even if they did not know of the error until after they read three or four verses after the error — nevertheless they do not begin except from the place where they stopped, for b'dieved they fulfilled what they read in the invalid scroll.

And it is possible to stop — meaning that it is three verses before the parasha.

Missing and extra [letters]. Such as avotenu missing a vav, and the like; but an error where the reading of the word changed, such as kesev written as keves when it is known to be an error, even though it has one meaning — they take out another (Shlah). And in the responsum of R' Moshe of Lublin it is written: if it is written rachava instead of rachavo, it is considered a change of language, even though it is possible to read rachava with a kamatz like ohelah; nevertheless there too there is reason to discuss why it is written with a hei, as Rashi wrote there — unlike here — until here. And all this was unnecessary, for since it is a complete error they take out another; specifically for missing and extra letters we are not experts, as is stated in Kiddushin chapter 3; and if so, perhaps the other scroll is also not precise in missing and extra letters; but a complete error is clearly pasul, as stated above. Tzitz Eliezer wrote: if he erred and wrote misgerosam instead of misgereshem, he need not take out another, since we find sometimes thus, as we find in Ruth; and if a letter stuck to a letter, one may rely on Rashba who validates it and need not take out another (Bach siman 32); however Rashba validated only when the letter was written correctly and stuck at the end, but when it stuck in the middle, such as artzecha where the hei stuck in the middle — all the more so if it stuck at its head — Mode holds it is pasul, as written in teshuva siman 291.

And in a pressing circumstance. R' Moshe of Lublin ruled: if an error is found in a Torah scroll and there is no other, one completes the seven readers in this scroll and does not add to them; and for Minhah one does not remove it, for it is not the day's obligation, but only on account of the inhabitants of Keren HaRez — until here siman 84. And it appears to me that even in a place where it is an obligation, one should not remove it l'chatchila unless one chumash is complete, as Rama wrote here.

Errors in others. And so too Chayei Adam in Gittin daf 59 — the reason one does not read from chumashim is on account of honor of the congregation; and here it is permitted, since all are together, but there is an error in one.
