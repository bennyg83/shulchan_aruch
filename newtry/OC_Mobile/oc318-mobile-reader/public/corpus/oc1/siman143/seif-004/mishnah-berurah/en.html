(13) If an error is found, etc. — in order to understand the words of this seif, I will precede it with a short introduction. Behold, the view of most poskim is that a Torah scroll missing one word or one letter, or in which an error is found, is pasul to read from; and according to them, if an error is found in it, even after they already read seven readers, one must return to the beginning in a valid Torah scroll and bless upon it, for that first reading is as if it never existed. And the view of some poskim is that even if several verses are missing — and all the more when an error is found in it in general — although it is not called a Torah scroll for fulfilling in it the positive mitzvah of writing a Torah scroll, nevertheless it is permitted to read from it if one has no other. And behold, the author in seif 3 ruled plainly that l'chatchila one should not take out an invalid Torah scroll to bless upon it, even in a place where one has no other, after the majority of poskim hold it is forbidden. However, if the error was found after they already read the sedra, Mahari Bei Rav ruled that for b'dieved one relies on the view of some of the aforementioned poskim who validate reading in it, and their reading is valid, and they need not take out another Torah scroll; and the acharonim agreed with him in this. And the same is true if the error was found between one man and another, and they have not yet completed the seven readers: regarding what they already read, one relies on the view of those who are lenient that they fulfilled b'dieved; but to read further in it is forbidden, and they take out another and begin from the place where the reading stopped, and complete the readers with those first ones who read in the erroneous scroll — meaning that they too join the seven readers, since it is b'dieved, as stated [and the acharonim wrote that nevertheless, if possible, it is better that they read seven readers in the Torah scroll they took out]. And all this if he already recited the closing blessing on the invalid scroll. But if in the middle of his reading they found an error, even if they already read many verses [and all the more when they have not yet read three verses], and even if he finished his reading entirely — as long as he has not yet recited the closing blessing, one does not recite the closing blessing on the invalid scroll, for this is l'chatchila; rather they take out another and read there three verses, and bless the closing blessing there alone. This is the method of Mahari Bei Rav that the author brought in this seif. And the view of Mordekhai is that if they already read three verses and it is possible to stop there — not being two verses adjacent to the parasha — they stop there and bless the closing blessing, and take out another for the remaining readers to ascend from there. And if he has not yet read three verses, or he read three verses but only two verses remain adjacent to the parasha where it is impossible to stop — he reads the error from memory and completes his reading in the invalid scroll and blesses the closing blessing, and afterward takes out another [and likewise if it is Rosh Chodesh and the like, where they must complete the parasha for the day's obligation and cannot add readers — if so, when the error is in the fourth reading, it is impossible to stop there to bless the closing blessing, for then they would need to add to the count of readers; and since it is impossible to stop there, he completes the reading in the invalid book and blesses the closing blessing]. And Rama in the gloss made a compromise between them: if he has not yet read only two verses, or even if he read three verses but cannot stop there — such as when it is two verses adjacent to the parasha and the like — he does not bless the closing blessing, but takes out another and completes his reading there and blesses the closing blessing, as the author's view; but if he already read three verses and is in a place where it is possible to stop there, his view is like Mordekhai: he blesses the closing blessing on the invalid scroll, and afterward takes out another. And now we come to explain the words of Shulchan Aruch in detail.

(14) At the time of reading — meaning they have not yet read except some of those called up; and when the order of the parasha is finished and afterward the error is found, they do not take out another, for b'dieved we rely on the poskim who validate reading in an invalid Torah scroll. And the author speaks when the error was found between one man and another [aliyah]; and therefore all the men who already read are counted for the number of seven, and even the last one, which is complete b'dieved.

(15) From the place where the error was found — it speaks when at the place where the oleh stopped, there the error was found; and therefore he wrote that they begin from this place. And the same is true even if they did not know of the error until after they read three or four verses after the error — likewise they do not begin except from the place where they stopped, for b'dieved they fulfilled what they read in the invalid scroll [Magen Avraham].

(16) Over those, etc. — meaning that also those who already read join the count of seven; and the same on Monday and Thursday for the count of three. And nevertheless, if it is possible to read seven readers from the place where they stopped until the end of the sedra, it is certainly fitting and proper to do so l'chatchila [acharonim].

(17) In the middle of the reading, etc. — meaning even if they already read several verses, as long as he has not yet recited the closing blessing, he cannot exempt himself with what he already read in the invalid Torah scroll, for this is l'chatchila, since he can read three verses in a valid scroll; and therefore he finishes, etc.

(18) He finishes his reading — meaning that he reads there three verses.

(19) And he does not go back to bless, etc. — for he holds that the first blessing with which the oleh blessed at the beginning of his reading in the first scroll applies also to this one, for his intent was from the start on this parasha — what difference to me whether this Torah scroll or another.

(20) Before it — and the same when the error is found between the blessing and the reading: they roll up this Torah scroll and take out another to read in it; and the oleh who already blessed need not bless again unless he interrupted with speech in the meantime. And in a place where they practiced that the oleh also goes back and blesses, one should not protest them, for they have what to rely on.

(21) And if they already, etc. — this opinion disputes the author, as we wrote above in the introduction; and it should have been written in the language of "some say," except that such is his way in many places.

(22) And it is possible to stop — meaning that it is three verses adjacent to the parasha; for if it were only two verses adjacent to the parasha, he cannot stop there — he completes his reading in the valid scroll until the parasha, as stated above.

(23) And he blesses after it — for he holds that since they read three verses before the error became known, he fulfilled his reading by this; and therefore he must bless after it, and he need not again finish his reading in the valid scroll. But when the error was found after they read only two verses, where he cannot stop there, he agrees with the author that he finishes his reading in the valid scroll and blesses after it. And see Biur Halacha, where we clarified in the name of several acharonim that one should practice thus per the ruling of Shulchan Aruch and as Rama decided — unless in a place where there is a fixed custom in the city, per Magen Avraham who adopted the method of Mordekhai that I brought in the introduction; they should not change their custom. The practice is not to take out another Torah scroll on account of the maftir — meaning, if an error is found after they already read the entire sedra before they said Kaddish, they do not take out another Torah scroll on account of the maftir; and b'dieved we rely on the one who read last, who also ascends for maftir (and he does not read again in the Torah), as is decided in siman 282 that the maftir counts for the number seven; and Kaddish is said after the closing blessings of the haftara. And if the error was not known until after they said Kaddish, the maftir reads in this scroll again without a blessing, and they do not say Kaddish again after the haftara. And if the error is found in the middle of the reading of the seventh [aliyah], Magen Avraham's view is that the reader completes until the end of the sedra in this scroll, and this reading counts for him also for maftir, as is decided in siman 282 and as above; and he blesses after it, and afterward he reads the haftara from the Prophets, and afterward he says Kaddish. And many acharonim dispute this and hold there is no distinction between an error found in the seventh and an error found in the other readers; and therefore, if he has not yet read three verses, he finishes his reading in the valid scroll; and if he already read three verses in the seventh — and the same in the last [aliyah] — he stops and blesses, and reads afterward in a valid Torah scroll from where he stopped onward. And it is implied from Magen Gibborim that one should practice thus, unless in a place where they practiced as Magen Avraham. And if the error is found after they read seven and he already blessed at the last [aliyah], but they have not yet read the entire sedra — per all opinions they must take out another, for perforce they must complete the entire sedra; and they read in the second with a blessing, and they may add [aliyot] as on other Shabbatot. If an error is found in a Torah scroll at the time of the maftir's reading on Shabbat, he completes his reading and does not bless after it; and all this is regarding the Shabbat maftir. But in the haftara of Yom Tov or of the four parshiyot and the like, where the maftir's reading in the second scroll is the day's obligation — if an error is found in it, one takes out another, as if there were an error in reading the Shabbat sedra.

(24) And they complete the count — and the same, that one may add from there; and see above s.k. 16.

(25) A complete error — such as a word or letter was missing or extra, or it was read as kri instead of ketiv, or open instead of closed or the reverse; and likewise if he wrote open or closed where it was not needed, or it was missing — in all these one must take out another. And the same if two words were not spaced properly until a child who is neither wise nor foolish reads them as one writing, or the reverse — that he spaced in the middle of the word until it appears to such a child as two words; or as in "pedah-tzur eli-tzur ammi-ndav," and the like, which is accepted among us as one word — if it is found in two lines, one must take out another. Letters and words that were erased somewhat — if their outline is visible so that a child who is neither wise nor foolish can read them, one need not take out another [and even l'chatchila it is permitted]. But if the black ink was removed and a reddish appearance remains, this is a change of appearance and it is pasul — so in Darkei Chayim in the name of Acharonim; and see what we wrote above in siman 32, Mishna Berurah s.k. 288, in the name of Chatam Sofer; and especially regarding taking out another, certainly one should not be stringent about this. If the stitches between one sheet and another were torn — even if most was torn but it is still connected by five or six enduring stitches, it is kosher; but with less than that, one should take out another (however, what was found torn in one chumash of the chumashim in this Torah scroll does not invalidate the other chumashim to read from them, as will be explained at the end of the gloss). If at the time of reading one letter was found stuck to its fellow — if the adhesion is along the entire length of the letter, or the form of the letter was changed by the adhesion, or he erred and wrote a dalet instead of a reish, or a bet instead of a kaf — in all these their law is like a complete error that requires taking out another; and even if through this adhesion the root letter was not corrupted but only a servile letter, such as vav or yud used as a vowel — if there is no adhesion along the length of the letter, only a little, and the form of the letters was not changed and they are well recognizable — l'chatchila one should not take out this Torah scroll to read from it as long as they have not scraped off the glue. And if it was not known and they took out the scroll and the error is found at the time of reading — if the adhesion is at the end of the letter, so that if they scrape off the glue alone it will return to its validity, then on a weekday when it is possible to scrape and it is easy to do, even at the time of reading one may scrape and read onward. And if there is no one who can scrape easily without loss and damage, and there is burdening of the congregation to wait for one who will scrape slowly, or if it occurred on Shabbat when scraping is impossible — it is permitted to read in it thus. And if the adhesion is at the beginning of writing the letter or in its middle, so that from the place of adhesion onward it was written invalid — one should take out another, even though the form of the letter was not changed thereby; and some are lenient that one need not take out another even in this manner, since the form of the letter was not changed thereby (and even though scraping is required — we hold that whatever is fit for soaking is not impeded by lack of soaking, and its law is as if it were already scraped; and even on Shabbat when scraping is impossible on account of Shabbat prohibition — nevertheless, since it is not on account of the scroll but a Shabbat prohibition, one need not take out another). And in a pressing circumstance, where there is no other scroll and time is needed to bring a valid Torah scroll from elsewhere, one may rely on this to complete the reading in this scroll. If wax dripped on some letter, see above siman 32 in Biur Halacha what I wrote in the name of Darkei Chayim, and siman 340, Mishna Berurah s.k. 10; and see the view of Shaarei Ephraim on this in section 5, letter 7. And there are many more cases where one must take out another Torah scroll on their account; but the scroll is too short to contain them — the investigator will find them in Peri Megadim and in Sefer Shaarei Ephraim sections 5 and 6, and in Derekh HaChayim. But take this rule in your hand: wherever it is mentioned in the laws of a Torah scroll that it is certainly pasul, one must take out another; and a matter that is disputed among the poskim, one may be lenient regarding Torah reading not to take out another (since there are some poskim who are lenient to read in an invalid Torah scroll).

(26) On account of missing and extra [letters], etc. — such as vavs or yuds that are full or missing, where the meaning was not changed and the pronunciation — such as where it should have been written avotenu full with a vav and it was found missing, or the reverse; and likewise where it should have been written full with a servile yud and it was found missing, or the reverse. But an error where the pronunciation changed, even though the meaning did not change — such as keves that was written instead of kesev, or Shlomo that is a complete word — one must take out another. And the same if he wrote misgereshem instead of misgerosam, for there is a change of language; and if he wrote rachava instead of rachavo, one must take out another, even though we can read it with a kamatz like ohelah; and likewise in the verse "ve-hineh tomim be-vitnah," if he wrote tomim full with an aleph as te'omim, even though the meaning is one — one must take out another, for the pronunciation is stirred; and likewise in the word venachnu mah, if he wrote ve-anachnu mah; and the same if the meaning changed thereby even though the pronunciation did not change — such as in the word venimtza damo, if he wrote venimtze damo; or in the word ma'an yevami, which is the root of refusal, if he wrote me'ayin with a yud; and all similar cases require taking out another. And see Darkei Chayim and Shaarei Ephraim, who elaborated on these details.

(27) One does not take out another — and the same for large and small letters transmitted in the mesorah, or inverted ayin, or bent pei, and the like transmitted in the mesorah — this is not a change of pronunciation to take out another, for they are no better than the aforementioned missing and extra letters. And likewise if the nekudot that are sometimes in a Torah scroll are missing — such as in "ei Sarah ishtekha" that should have nekudot per the mesorah — if the nekudot are missing, one need not take out another.

(28) One must compromise thus — meaning, this is the way of resolution: the view of Agur is that even in a complete error one does not take out another, for one does not gain, since the other Torah scroll too may be pasul through missing and extra letters; and the view of Beit Yosef is that even for missing and extra letters one takes out another; and therefore Rama personally decided this distinction as halakha.

(29) And some invalidate — this is like the author's view above in s.k. 3; and so the acharonim ruled plainly that l'chatchila they do not take out another, even for the day's obligation. However, if they began to read and afterward an error was found in it, and there is no other Torah scroll besides it — some say they complete seven readers and bless before and after it; only the maftir should be the seventh, and they say Kaddish after the haftara. And Dagul MeRevavah wrote that one should not multiply blessings in this manner; and so Shaarei Ephraim wrote, section 6, letter 61 — rather, he completes with this oleh in whose reading the error was found his parasha, and he does not bless the closing blessing; he only stands there, and the prayer leader calls the other olelim until seven olelim are completed, and they do not bless at all; only the prayer leader reads for each one his parasha until the sedra is finished. And this oleh in whose reading the error was found, standing there, does not distract his attention and reads in a whisper with them; and at the end of the sedra he blesses the closing blessing, and he also says the haftara from the Prophets with its blessings. And there is one who wrote that he says the haftara without blessings — until here (and see in Pitchei She'arim, who wrote that Dagul MeRevavah also agrees to all this — see there). And if the error is found in the third [aliyah] or in the rest of the olelim after they finished their reading and blessed the closing blessing, and there is no other Torah scroll besides it — the reader reads the rest of the parshiyot until the sedra is finished without a blessing.

(30) One chumash, etc. — meaning that the entire Torah scroll is in one scroll, but it is not complete without error except for one chumash; but if there is only one chumash there, per all opinions one does not read from it, as above in seif 2.

(31) One may be lenient — meaning, even per the view of "some invalidate" who forbid even in a pressing hour — all this is when the disqualification is in that chumash from which he wants to read; but in the complete chumashim one need not be stringent in a pressing hour, since in any case in this portion in which he reads it is complete; and it is not comparable to one chumash that is pasul per all opinions to read from it — and as above in seif 2, for there it is different, since it is not honor of the congregation to read from one chumash, unlike here where all five chumashim are together.

(32) To read — meaning, with a blessing; and see Biur Halacha.
