Seif 4. In a Sefer Torah during the reading. Meaning, some of the aliyos completed their reading and the concluding blessing had already been recited, and only afterward was the error found. This is fully after the fact, so we rely on those who permit reading from an invalid Sefer Torah. Those who already read therefore count toward the seven, and the remainder read from a valid Sefer Torah. If, however, the concluding blessing has not yet been recited, even if the section assigned to that aliyah has already been completed, this falls within the latter clause, where an error is found in the middle of the Torah reading. The Shulchan Aruch holds that even if three verses have already been read, another Sefer Torah is taken out, as Taz explains in seif katan 1. There is certainly no distinction between three verses and many verses, or between completing and not completing the section, so long as the concluding blessing has not yet been recited. The reason is that, until the blessing is recited, the case is still considered initial, since he can read three verses from a valid scroll and then recite the blessing. Mordechai, cited by Rama, holds that once three verses have been read and it is possible to stop there, the case is considered after the fact and we rely on the lenient authorities. If three verses have not yet been read, Rama also holds that another scroll is taken out, because the case is still initial. It follows that if three verses have been read but it is impossible to stop, such as where only two verses remain before the end of the section, Rama likewise holds that another scroll is taken out in order to read the remaining two verses, for Rama disagrees only where it is possible to stop. This is clear. Taz's ruling in seif katan 1, in a case where it is impossible to stop, disagrees with Rama. This is clear.
