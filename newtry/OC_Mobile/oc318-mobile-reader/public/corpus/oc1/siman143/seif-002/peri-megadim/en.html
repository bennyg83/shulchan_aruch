Missing [letters] — Ateret Zekenim: hidden nekudot whose reading does not change on account of this — one does not take out another; and for a missing letter it appears one takes out another (to harmonize the views of the poskim, and thereby is resolved what Agur wrote in the name of Rosh that one does not take out another — this is the opposite of what Rosh wrote explicitly, except that missing and extra letters are what is discussed — see Perishah). And he brought the responsum of Maharam Lublin siman 84 (Magen Avraham, letter 8): when there is no other Torah scroll and they already began to read and found an error, they complete the count of readers in this scroll and bless before and after it; and it is implied there that even l'chatchila they take out and read; and we practice like Rama in this: as long as they have not begun, they do not take out, even on Monday morning, if they invalidate it — unless one chumash is complete; and if they began, they complete the count of readers if there is no other Torah scroll. And for Minhah, which is only on account of the inhabitants of Keren HaRez — nevertheless it is possible they complete all that they began, for it is nevertheless an enactment like Monday and Thursday; and what difference to me whether Minhah or Monday and Thursday? And Magen Avraham, letter 8. And even though Taz implies that crack is not visible to the eye, it implies that a missing word — one does not rule thus; behold, in the responsum of Mordekhai he ruled thus, and so Magen Avraham, letter 8 — only l'chatchila one does not take out.

And know: whenever there is a dispute among the poskim whether this is an error or not, one does not take out another, for it is a double doubt: perhaps the halakha is like Rambam in his responsum that they read in an invalid scroll — on this we rely b'dieved; and perhaps the halakha is like those who hold that in such an invalid scroll it is not pasul. And even though we wrote in another place that in doubt of a blessing one is stringent — one may say that a double doubt does not help; nevertheless, one who examines siman 489, in doubt whether he counted, should bless — Beer Mayim Chayim, see there, s.k. 8, that it is a double doubt; if so, it is implied that a double doubt helps to bless; and therefore in siman 185 this will be explained.

If a letter stuck to a letter, even on Shabbat one need not take out another, for one may rely on Rashba in this — Taz, Bach siman 32, letter 18, and Magen Avraham, letter 7; and from the conclusion of Bach in siman 32 and Taz it is implied that even if it stuck in the middle, one may say one does not take out another. And regarding wax that dripped and covered thickly so one cannot read the word at all — siman 340, Taz, and one vav and one hei — one may say: whatever is fit for soaking, like for conversion, one relies on Rashba; however, because it is forbidden to read from memory, one may say he reads from a chumash and they do not take out another. But when wax dripped on one place, one may say they rely certainly as I wrote; and see responsum Ma'il Tzedakah siman 25; see what is written in Kamma, Magen Avraham, letter 9, as they write and do not read Parah from memory — see there.

He wrote in responsum Ma'il Tzedakah to the Gaon Maharar Yona, siman 29, on the parasha of Noach: "and it was all the days of Noach" (Genesis 9:29) — so it should be; and some [manuscripts have] "and they were," but the essence is "and it was" without a vav; and nevertheless on Shabbat one does not take out another — see there. Here, whenever there is a doubt, one does not take out another. But I learned from his words that we say: missing and full [letters] — one does not take out another; the vav of Noach is hidden in the middle of the word, so the reading does not change at all — unlike if it is written veyihu instead of vayehi, which is pasul, even though the vav of Noach is hidden, for the reading was changed on account of the writing — Magen Avraham, letter 7, rachava/rachavo, and there it will be explained below. "The fire of religion: one word and they read two" — and if in two lines it is possible [to read as] pasul, like ketiv and not kri, a change invalidates them — until here.

And these are his words — Rambam, chapter 10 of Laws of a Torah Scroll, halakhah 1: twenty things invalidate a Torah scroll. And here: he made open closed or the reverse — pasul, and they take out another. But if he did not leave nine letters in the section — as in ashekh, siman 14, siman 475 — some say three letters suffice b'dieved; if so, when there are three letters one does not take out another, in doubt of a dispute, as stated above, for it is a double doubt. And likewise if the Name went outside the column, or part of the Name was suspended — some say it is kosher d'oraita — Magen Avraham siman 32, letter 47: any doubt — one does not take out another; and see Rosh Akiva, letter 7, and Taz, letter 4, and Rambam chapter 1 of Laws of Tefillin: if a vav suspended part of the Name it is pasul; but in chapter 10 of Laws of a Torah Scroll, halakhah 1, he did not count this among the twenty — possibly because "they do not read publicly" applies only to those twenty; part of the Name is perhaps a doubt, and one may rely on it in any case, for it is a double doubt. And therefore any doubt of a dispute whether it is pasul or not — they may read from it l'chatchila, from the doubt itself, which is a double doubt.

If the head of a lamed entered one of the letters (or the nekudot of nun, tzadi, pei within letters under tet, shin, and the like) — see siman 32, s.k. 288, and Taz siman 32, and Magen Avraham, letter 40, that some validate it; if so, it is clear one does not take out ateret; and it is possible even to read l'chatchila with a blessing if there is no other, for perhaps the halakha is like Rambam in his responsum that they read in an invalid Torah scroll when there is no other — as Rama wrote in the gloss: some validate in such a case, and perhaps it is kosher — Magen Avraham from this will explain below. And do not answer me from what Bach wrote in siman 32, seif 13 at the end, that in adhesion of letter to letter one does not take out l'chatchila — one may say there is an opinion that it is kosher, or that we hold not like Rashba, unlike a complete doubt. And if a letter reaches without margin from the beginning of the parchment — likewise one does not take out another, for we rely on Rashba — Magen Avraham siman 32, letter 27, see there.

Yoreh Deah siman 274, s.k. 7: zein, she'atnez, gatz — one must be careful; and with vowels or tropes it is pasul. And above siman 36, Magen Avraham in the name of Bach, invalidates if it is not zein, she'atnez, gatz. And here Rambam validates this in the explanation of chapter 7 of Laws of a Torah Scroll, halakhah 10, and "some" validate with vowels and tropes, since in chapter 10 he did not count among the twenty-one items explicitly — if so, in these invalidations Rama holds one does not take out another, even to read l'chatchila when there is no other Torah scroll; one may say they read, and this requires study.

And know: in Yoreh Deah 481 he counted the invalidations for writing; mamzer — some invalidate [a scroll] found in the hand of a gentile — it is a doubt; and I thought to say that also in such a case it is a dispute-doubt and a double doubt. Again I saw: a Torah scroll that is missing or has an error — one may say it is like chumashim: if not for honor of the congregation they would read from them; and in error there is no honor of the congregation, as the viewer will see in Pri Chadash here, where he brought Rambam's responsum and many [cases] in chapter 10, see there. And if so, only in such a case; but whatever was entirely written in invalidity — there is no Torah scroll upon it at all; and even those who read — b'dieved their reading did not count, and they must read from the beginning of a valid Torah scroll if they have one (as the view of Rosh in Yoreh Deah 279, and all agree to this; and even though Rambam, chapter 10, halakhah 1, lists twenty-one things, and there is one worked not for its sake, and an apikores, and the like — all in invalidity; and since he included them together that one does not read from them, it implies missing letters too, even b'dieved; and it is impossible to say one reads from memory at all — it must be that they are not compared to each other in all matters, as stated).

We already wrote: if it occurred in the parasha of Parah or Zachor that there was an error and they read part of the parasha — one must take out a valid scroll and read from the beginning, as the view of Rosh in Yoreh Deah 279; for these are among those that are d'oraisa, as in siman 146, s.k. 2. And one who examines Yoreh Deah 279 — all the leniency is because reading in public is an enactment of the prophets, and they were not stringent about it; and there is no difference between a Torah scroll and tefillin regarding the mitzvah of writing d'oraisa; if so, in these too it is d'oraisa, and one returns from the beginning without a blessing, for it does not impede. And also from Mordekhai, Darkei Moshe brought in Beit Yosef Yoreh Deah 279, proves that even on the last [aliyah] and maftir, if one can stop there, they take out another, as Magen Avraham wrote, letter 16. And now I saw in Eshel Avraham, letter 16, he noticed this — see there. And see Levush Yoreh Deah 279; and here too the reason is since it is an enactment of the prophets.

And the rest of the matters will be explained below in Magen Avraham, letter 7, section 9; and here one need not elaborate further.

Another point I will speak: Eshel Avraham wrote here that the letter hei was found as a complete word doubled — they take out another, for all extra [letters] are considered as removed. And to me this requires study; for perhaps in such a case one may say: whatever is fit for soaking, like a letter stuck to a letter — so here; and even if one holds that extra is considered removed — in treifot they rule thus completely, unlike here — and this requires study.

The lay scribe said: All that I heard from my rabbis, and some of what was taught to me from Heaven, I wrote down — surely five Torah scrolls, long and wide as the sea; and, with God's help, the corruption of the times [has damaged them]. And every God-fearing man should be careful about this, especially scribes of STaM; go and see what Tzitz Eliezer wrote in siman 119: after he ruled, he feared perhaps he had not aimed correctly according to halakha, and he was great in his generation — and what will they answer for us today? Therefore let every man fear for his soul before ruling a halakhic matter, and the like.
