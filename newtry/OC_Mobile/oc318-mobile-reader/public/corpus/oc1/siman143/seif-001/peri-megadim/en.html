And if [an error is found] — Ateret Zekenim. Here it implies that there are three views. (1) The view of Mahari Bei Rav, the teacher of Beit Yosef, that whenever an error is found — if he finished the reading and blessed after it, and afterward the error was found — b'dieved the reading in the invalid Torah scroll is valid, and they complete until the count of seven on Shabbat, or six on Yom Tov and Yom Kippur, and four and three on Rosh Chodesh and weekdays, in a valid scroll (and so in Beit Yosef Yoreh Deah 279, and Levush there, and so he wrote here "and they complete," and they need not read seven anew), for b'dieved we rely on Rambam in his responsum that reading in an invalid scroll is valid; and that missing one letter did not fulfill the mitzvah of writing a Torah scroll — see Beit Yosef Yoreh Deah 279; and Ran added that Torah reading is an enactment of the prophets and does not impede b'dieved, for the difference between a Torah scroll and tefillin and mezuzah is only regarding missing one letter not fulfilling the mitzvah of writing, but for reading it is an enactment of the prophets — see there; and so Levush Yoreh Deah 279, seif 2, see there. And therefore Parah Adumah and Zachor — some say they are d'oraisa — require study in this, that perhaps in an invalid Torah scroll it does not help even b'dieved if they read the entire parasha and afterward found an error on this column and the like — siman 146, s.k. 2, Parah and Zachor, which require ten d'oraisa, and Magen Avraham siman 488, and as we need to say below. And all this if he already blessed the closing blessing on the invalid scroll; but if in the middle of his reading they found an error, even if they read much — one does not bless the closing blessing on the invalid scroll, for this is l'chatchila; rather they take out another and read there (it implies three verses in a valid scroll) and bless the closing blessing there; and all the more if before three verses — they read in the second and bless the closing blessing, and not the first. And Mordekhai holds: if they read three verses, he blesses the closing blessing there b'dieved; and even if they did not read three verses, he says the error from memory where it is impossible, and completes three verses and blesses, and afterward they take out a valid one. And Rama in the gloss explained between the views: whoever did not read three verses reads in a valid scroll (and if possible three verses; and if the sedra is finished, he finishes thus) and blesses after it and not before it — and this is Mahari Bei Rav; and when he read three verses, he ruled like Mordekhai that he blesses the closing blessing on the invalid scroll; and if he cannot stop, being two verses before the parasha, then he reads the error from memory like Mordekhai. And therefore it should have been written "some say"; and in Levush he wrote here "some say" — if he read three verses he blesses on the invalid scroll; and in Yoreh Deah 279 he did not write "some say," which implies his view is like view (1) plainly: that in the middle of his parasha, even if he read much, he finishes in a valid scroll and blesses the closing blessing there — unlike his teacher here. But one who examines Beit Yosef here and Yoreh Deah may say that Mahari Bei Rav too, in the middle of reading when he did not read three verses — if he read three verses, he admits one blesses the closing blessing on the invalid scroll, as appears from the flow of Shach's words in Yoreh Deah 279, s.k. 3; and so too from Taz there in what he answered from Kiddushin on Yom Kippur: therefore one does not bless the opening blessing here on the second, for he has not yet fulfilled any mitzvah since he did not read three verses — if he read three verses, all agree he blesses the closing blessing on the first Torah scroll; and this teaches as I wrote, and there are only two views. And Rama in the author ruled completely like Mahari Bei Rav. And from what is written in Yoreh Deah "he raised," etc. — you will see that Rama in the gloss here may be that he ruled completely like Mahari Bei Rav, and one does not read an error from memory in any case, unlike Mordekhai; and if so, when he read three verses and cannot stop, there being two verses to the parasha, they take out another and finish there — for otherwise it is difficult from Kiddushin on Yom Kippur: whenever he read three verses the mitzvah is done and it is fitting to bless the opening blessing on the second, as Yoreh Deah writes; and therefore in such a case he reads from memory; and also the last [aliyah] likewise reads from memory on account of burdening the congregation — see there; and for the maftir they take out another, since they say Kaddish there is no burden — see there; and for the maftir likewise he reads the error from memory, like the last — such is his view there. And for practical law, Magen Avraham, letter 4, will explain below. And on the last [aliyah], Taz [holds that] even if only one verse, he completes in the invalid scroll.
