Eating. See Taz, who concludes that when one established himself for eating, even after saying, “Bring us the cup and let us recite Bircas HaMazon,” he may continue eating without a new blessing. Consequently, if he wishes to drink while continuing to eat, he need not recite a new blessing over the drink, because drinking follows eating. The disciples of Rav in Pesachim 106, whom Rav told, “You may no longer drink,” wished only to drink and not to eat. Taz further writes that even drinking alone may not be ended by the declaration when the beverage is commonly drunk for pleasure rather than thirst. There appears to be a textual error, and it should read that the rule was not stated regarding a beverage customarily drunk for pleasure. Nevertheless, where the gathering was established only for drinking and not eating, perhaps there is no fixed meal status for drinking, and any conclusion ends it even regarding pleasurable beverages. This requires study. The Mechaber appears to rule like Rabbeinu Yonah and Ran. See Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 4. It appears that establishment for eating is not limited to bread; the same applies to other nourishing foods. According to Rabbeinu Yonah and Ran, saying the declaration does not end the after-blessing for such eating, and if one wishes to eat more he may do so without a new blessing.
