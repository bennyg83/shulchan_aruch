Even if he said, “Come, let us,” etc. - See Mishnah Berurah. Peri Megadim in Eshel Avraham, seif katan 3, implies that if one washed his hands, we assume that he certainly withdrew his attention in every case. Rabbi Akiva Eiger writes similarly in the name of Responsa Perach Shoshan. Mateh Yehudah disagrees, as does Minchas Shmuel.
It is not a diversion of attention - See Mishnah Berurah; this wording is taken from Magen Avraham. It might appear that if the host does not eat or drink any more, and the guest changes his original intention and wishes to eat or drink, he may not do so, since he said, “Come, let us recite Bircas HaMazon,” and thereby withdrew his attention. In truth, however, one may say that as long as the host has not said, “Come, let us recite Bircas HaMazon,” the guest’s own statement does not constitute a diversion of attention at all. He certainly did not definitively withdraw his attention so long as the host had not yet said it. This is indicated by the wording of the Shulchan Aruch and Levush. I later found that Minchas Shmuel writes likewise.
Until the host says it - The same applies to the most distinguished person at the table, as Eliyah Rabbah writes in the name of Tzror HaChaim. The Mechaber’s phrase, “at another person’s table,” implies at first glance that a person subordinates his intention specifically to that of the host, but not in another situation. Peri Megadim also implies this somewhat. This requires practical study.
