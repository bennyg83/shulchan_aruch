6) There: According to Rabbeinu Yonah and Ran, eating is different, etc. Beis Yosef writes in the name of Ran that the distinction between drinking and eating is that drinking has no fixed framework. Therefore, when they say, 'Let us recite the blessing,' he is considered to have formed a new intention and must recite another blessing. Eating, however, has a fixed framework, for sometimes a person sits for a small meal and it extends into a large meal. Consequently, even if he finished and even if he removed the food, he may eat until he washes his hands and need not recite a blessing; see there. Taz, paragraph 2, cites this and writes that if he returns only to drink, he may not drink without a blessing. If he returns to eat and this leads him also to drink, he certainly need not recite a blessing over the drink, for in this case it properly follows the eating; see there. Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 3, and Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 3, write the same. Shulchan Aruch HaRav writes there, however, that if he is thirsty because of his original eating, he must recite a blessing; see there.
