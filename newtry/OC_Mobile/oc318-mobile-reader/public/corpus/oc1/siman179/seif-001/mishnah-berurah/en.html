(1) To eat. The same applies that initially one should be careful not to interrupt even briefly with speech, because Bircas HaMazon should immediately follow mayim acharonim. For this reason one should not delay for a prolonged period after washing, and certainly should not occupy himself with another matter. If he must interrupt for an urgent need, or if he interrupted after the fact, he should wash again so that Bircas HaMazon immediately follows mayim acharonim.
(2) Until he recites Bircas HaMazon. This differs from saying, “Bring us the cup and let us recite Bircas HaMazon,” after which one may eat if he first recites HaMotzi, as explained below. Once he washed, however, he may not interrupt. Several poskim hold that even after mayim acharonim there is no prohibition against eating, provided he first recites HaMotzi. The later authorities rule leniently, provided that after eating he washes again so that mayim acharonim immediately precedes Bircas HaMazon. Nevertheless, Bach and Magen Avraham hold that initially one should be careful not to do this. See Biur Halachah for further discussion. All this concerns eating. But if he does not wish to eat or drink, all agree that he may not interrupt with speech or occupy himself with another matter.
(3) “Bring us the cup and let us recite Bircas HaMazon.” This means, bring us a cup over which to recite Bircas HaMazon. Where Bircas HaMazon is recited without a cup, saying, “Let us recite Bircas HaMazon,” has the same law and constitutes diversion of attention. Likewise, if one explicitly resolved in his mind not to drink further and then wishes to drink, he must first recite a new blessing over the beverage.
(4) To drink. If the group was assembled only for drinking and they said, “Let us recite the after-blessing,” the same rule applies. This constitutes diversion of attention, and they may not drink further without a new blessing.
(5) Eating has the same law as drinking. The Gemara mentions the rule explicitly only regarding drinking, and this opinion holds that the same applies to eating.
(6) He withdrew his hand from eating. Meaning, even if he resolved not to eat further.
(7) Even if they cleared the table. This is Rabbeinu Yonah’s wording. Although he holds that clearing the table is a stronger diversion of attention than saying, “Bring us the cup and let us recite Bircas HaMazon,” it nevertheless does not end the meal regarding eating. See Biur Halachah for the view of the Rosh.
(8) To resume eating, etc. Taz writes that if, by resuming eating, his desire is also drawn toward drinking, then according to this opinion he need not recite a new blessing over the drink either, because drinking follows eating. Shulchan Aruch’s earlier ruling that all agree a new blessing is required over drinking applies only where he did not resume eating.
(9) He has not withdrawn. Since it is common to be drawn from a small amount of food to a larger amount, it is not considered complete diversion of attention unless he washed mayim acharonim. Drinking is different: even a slight diversion of attention ends the drinking, and if he changes his mind he must recite a new blessing. Biur Halachah cites many poskim who follow the Rosh, that saying, “Bring us the cup and let us recite Bircas HaMazon,” constitutes completion of the meal and even eating is forbidden without a new blessing. Shulchan Aruch likewise rules without qualification below in siman 197:1. Therefore, initially one must certainly be careful not to eat after making that declaration, and Derech HaChaim rules accordingly. Know also that one who requires a new blessing in the middle of a meal must wash again, because having diverted his attention, he did not guard his hands. He does not, however, recite a blessing over the washing.
