Until he recites Bircas HaMazon. Even if he wishes to recite a blessing first, he may not eat. Beit Yosef derives this from the Rosh in the chapter Kisui HaDam. The Rosh writes that Bircas HaMazon must immediately follow mayim acharonim, and that saying, “Bring us the cup and let us recite Bircas HaMazon,” is equivalent to mayim acharonim according to Rashi, etc. Beit Yosef infers that after mayim acharonim all agree one may no longer eat. This requires study, for in his abridged rulings the Rosh explicitly writes that after mayim acharonim one may eat if he first recites a blessing, as the Tur writes. How could the abridgment contradict the full work? Rabbeinu Yerucham likewise implies that there is no distinction between the declaration and mayim acharonim, and he was a disciple of the Rosh. Moreover, the Rosh himself writes explicitly in Arvei Pesachim that although they washed their hands and became forbidden to eat, we maintain that after saying, “Bring us the cup and let us recite Bircas HaMazon,” one need not first recite Bircas HaMazon, and HaMotzi suffices. He writes similarly in siman 10, that once one said the declaration he may not drink without Borei Peri HaGafen, all the more so after taking the cup for Bircas HaMazon. That passage discusses one who had washed, as is also implied by Beit Yosef in siman 477. It is difficult to say that a mitzvah need is different, for the Rosh should have stated this, especially since he compares it to the declaration. It therefore appears that according to the Rosh, even after mayim acharonim one may resume eating if he first recites a blessing, and may wash again immediately before Bircas HaMazon. The Rosh’s statement in Kisui HaDam means that Bircas HaMazon must immediately follow mayim acharonim, so one may not interrupt with any activity. According to Rashi, saying the declaration is equivalent to mayim acharonim, because even if he wishes to recite a blessing first he may not eat, and must proceed immediately to Bircas HaMazon. Therefore he may not interrupt with another activity. According to the Rosh, however, the declaration is merely diversion of attention, and one may interrupt with another activity. The statement that some distinguish means that they distinguish further between mayim acharonim and the declaration, namely between eating and drinking. This is the clear meaning of the Rosh, and Beit Yosef abbreviated the quotation and therefore explained it differently. Bach also gives another explanation, and the reader may choose. In practical law, however, Bach agrees that mayim acharonim and the declaration are equivalent, though he writes that a conscientious person should be stringent and recite Bircas HaMazon immediately after mayim acharonim. Rabbeinu Yonah writes likewise. See what I wrote in siman 197. Tosafos in Berachos 42 and Pesachim 101, the Tur in siman 477, and the Rosh in chapter 7 of Chullin imply that whenever a new blessing is required because of diversion of attention, washing is also required, because one who diverted his mind from eating presumably did not guard his hands.
