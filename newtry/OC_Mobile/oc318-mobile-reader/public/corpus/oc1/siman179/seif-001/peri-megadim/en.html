And he washed. See Taz and Magen Avraham. Perishah and the Tur understand another approach: according to the Rosh and Tur, mayim acharonim is equivalent to saying, “Bring us the cup and let us recite Bircas HaMazon,” in that if one wishes to eat, he recites a new blessing before eating and need not first recite Bircas HaMazon. There are three reasons regarding mayim acharonim: first, diversion of attention; second, Bircas HaMazon must immediately follow washing; third, once he prepared himself to recite Bircas HaMazon, it is as though he began it. Beit Yosef rules in Shulchan Aruch according to the third reason. Taz rules according to the second reason, and therefore if he washes again before Bircas HaMazon, it is acceptable. Levush cites both opinions. See siman 271:6, Magen Avraham seif katan 18 and Taz seif katan 9. It appears there that even after washing, according to the Rosh one does not first recite Bircas HaMazon, which contradicts the ruling here stated without dissent. Perhaps one can distinguish that there it is forbidden to taste from the cup of Bircas HaMazon. See Beit Yosef in siman 271 in the name of the Rosh citing Rabbi Yeshayah of Trani. This somewhat resolves the question from Arvei Pesachim 102 in light of what Magen Avraham writes in siman 197, seif katan 3, that where a mitzvah is involved, even after washing one may eat after reciting HaMotzi, without first reciting Bircas HaMazon. Taz here cites proof from Rama there, who disagrees with the Mechaber here and holds that mayim acharonim is equivalent to the declaration. Since the declaration is explicit diversion of attention and does not require Bircas HaMazon first, all the more so mayim acharonim. See Bach. Magen Avraham in siman 197 was not satisfied with this, for Rama’s silence here indicates agreement, whereas there a mitzvah is involved. With Hashem’s help, this will be explained further there.
