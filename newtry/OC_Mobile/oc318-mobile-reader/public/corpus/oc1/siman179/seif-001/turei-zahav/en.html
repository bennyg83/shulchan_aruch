And took, etc. Shulchan Aruch's view divides netilat yadayim and let us bless—for let us bless suffices hamotzi blessing need not birkat hamazon; with netilat yadayim needs birkat hamazon first—follows Beit Yosef; challenged Tur equating netilat yadayim to let us bless from Rosh ch. Kol Mevaser: and if said let us bless like netilat yadayim per Rashi Rashbam who explained since you said let us bless forbidden drink until bless birkat hamazon; some distinguish eating drinking—not reasonable—end quote; seems from his words netilat yadayim forbidden eat until birkat hamazon. I find wondrous—for reason Tosafot and Rosh for let us bless applies literally to netilat yadayim—for there reason no interruption between act and final blessing, final blessing on all already ate as Beit Yosef wrote before—applies to netilat yadayim; further Tosafot Kol Mevaser wrote therefore since took hands last forbidden eat until bless, same if said let us bless even without netilah—end quote—they equated them—this is Rosh following Tosafot; Rosh's intent to teach netilat yadayim is like let us bless, on both said afterward per Rashi Rashbam need specifically birkat hamazon—not intending to rule like Rashi Rashbam relying on blood cover they do not hold like Rashi Rashbam requiring birkat hamazon with let us bless but hamotzi; here said only per Rashi Rashbam these two equal; poskim with let us bless need only first blessing—consequently netilat yadayim too, Rosh's view did not arise to distinguish; as Tur explicitly wrote, words align; Beit Yosef in name R' Yitzchak after took may not drink even with blessing—for took hands must bless immediately after netilat yadayim—certainly distinguishes netilat yadayim and let us bless from closeness reason not interruption reason, as said later Beit Yosef; nevertheless if wants eat or drink after took hands and take hands again afterward—permission in his hand for fulfills immediately after netilah blessing, need not birkat hamazon first—for first netilah considered plain water not mayim acharonim, no diversion concern as Beit Yosef only closeness; Rabbenu Yonah not discussing this—here Rabbenu Yonah agrees and Rosh per force as Beit Yosef explains he holds Rabbenu Yonah nevertheless agrees here. Tur also seems this view—netilat yadayim need not birkat hamazon meaning take hands again after. Beit Yosef wrote since took hands to be ready to bless—as if began blessing, forbidden interrupt eating drinking entirely—very wondrous—even prepared himself no diversion why cannot retract—for only closeness concern not vain blessing mention; Rama siman 197 netilat yadayim like let us bless. For halacha should not be stricter than take hands again—saw ch. Ayin Peh beraita group Rosh wrote: even though washed hands forbidden eat—we hold let us bless need not birkat hamazon—explicit as we said; Beit Yosef omitted these Rosh words; truth netilat yadayim not stricter than let us bless; both need only hamotzi not birkat hamazon—cannot be stringent here for vain blessing concern.

Eating is different. So Beit Yosef in name Ran and R' Yitzchak—for eating not removed by small removal like let us bless custom continue meal to meal—only netilat yadayim; not so drinking removed by any removal. Beit Yosef wrote on this difficulty students of Rav sitting at meal said let us bless—removal—learn also for eating; answered when established for eating let us bless is removal for drinking forbidden drink without blessing, permitted eat (only question) since not require blessing for eating not logical require for drinking in that meal since one meal drinking follows eating, etc. Two words (only question) I added to emend Beit Yosef; so Moharchal z"l else no meaning; Levush copied as is—error. On Beit Yosef's question I say if came only to drink alone forbidden without blessing; if came to eat and dragged to drink—certainly no blessing to drink—for properly we say drinking follows eating. Further for this they said so only drinking beverage custom drink for pleasure even without thirst—certainly more follows from eating; reminded what wine much follows as I find.
