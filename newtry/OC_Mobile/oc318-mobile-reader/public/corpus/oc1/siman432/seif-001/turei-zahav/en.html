(א) On biur chametz. Although he does not burn, nevertheless now biur begins through checking to burn and annulling chametz unknown to him — automatically he burns also now; and it is akin to tomorrow's biur. Beit Yosef wrote one does not bless on annulment of chametz, for it is a matter of the heart, and one does not bless on thought. And one does not bless on bedikat chametz since this is not the end of the mitzva:

(ב) All the time he did not finish his checking. For all the time he did not finish he is still called passing to its performance; and if he already finished checking he does not bless, although the finish of the mitzva is still before him in biur — nevertheless blessing is not applicable if he is not doing an act of mitzva. And it appears to me — if he did not bless until finishing checking, he blesses at the fifth hour when engaging in burning chametz, for then he does an act in the essence of the mitzva; and apparently the opposite is implied in Rosh first chapter, who wrote: and what that one does not bless on biur at the fifth hour when removing chametz from the house, which is the essence of the mitzva from the Torah as written on the first day you shall eliminate, etc.; and I say — removing chametz before its time of prohibition does not derive from tashbitu, for tashbitu means burning, and Scripture did not command burning except after its prohibition is like notar; but while it is still permitted to eat one does not burn it; and what they remove from the house is so one not transgress on it it shall not be seen — end of his words. This is simple — Rosh did not question why he should not bless again on what he fulfills as a d'oraisa mitzva, for at first he blessed only at time of d'rabbanan mitzva; since he already blessed on biur chametz, how can he bless one blessing twice on one mitzva? Rather he questioned the essence of Sages' enactment — they should have enacted the blessing at time of fulfilling d'oraisa mitzva and not at checking which is d'rabbanan; for this he answered that then there is no d'oraisa, since it is still permitted, but d'rabbanan because of concern afterward; if so he should bless on the beginning which is also d'rabbanan; and if so it is proven as stated — if he did not bless on the beginning which is d'rabbanan, he blesses at the fifth hour which is also d'rabbanan, as I explained. Tur wrote in the name of Rosh that one need not bless Shehecheyanu at bedikat chametz, for checking is for the need of the festival — we rely on the time of the festival. Maharshal questioned: say — chametz prohibition precedes the festival from the time of slaughtering the Pesach, for it is an ordinary weekday and forbidden d'oraisa; if so why not bless Shehecheyanu? And it is not comparable to making sukkah and lulav, for their mitzva did not precede the festival — end of his words. And one may answer: that time of prohibition relates to the festival, for Scripture called it first — that is, first of the seven days of the festival; therefore it is better to bless on the essence of the festival and exempt what is secondary to it:

(ג) And he should beware not to speak, etc. This is Rosh's conclusion in Tur; and explanation is needed in what he wrote first — Yesh Omrim that the checker should not speak until he finishes all checking; and some add that if he conversed in matters not related to checking, etc.; and all this is not equal to me, etc.; and it is difficult — since he himself agrees afterward that one should beware so until end of checking, if so this is the beginning of those Yesh Omrim who also hold so as stated; and so Maharshal questioned and answered: certainly from what he wrote one should not speak and did not distinguish between blessing and beginning of checking and after he began to check — apparently he lumps them in one category; and this is not so, for between blessing and beginning of checking the reason is interruption, and in checking itself the reason is distraction of attention; and on this he wrote all this is not equal to me, etc. — end of his words. And it is difficult — for certainly in any case there is a distinction between one who has not yet begun, who must return and bless, and one who began — although he should beware, nevertheless he need not return and bless; apparently he does not lump them in one category. And it appears to me to answer Maharshal's difficulty: in Rosh's words in the conclusion he mentions there it is good to beware not to engage in idle conversation, etc. — that is, in what he has no need for, even for the sake of checking; rather he has permission to speak of necessities even not for the sake of checking, since there is no interruption here but only that he set his heart to check well, and it suffices if he bewares of idle conversation entirely; but for Yesh Omrim above there is prohibition from interruption; therefore idle conversation is not mentioned there, but that the checker should not speak — implying even his necessities, only that they are not for the sake of checking there is prohibition — so I reconcile his words; but my soul is divided — it appears to me Yesh Omrim's words are correct and there is interruption when he speaks before finishing checking. One may say for sukkah and meal that Rosh brings proof one may interrupt in the middle — there if he wishes he finishes there and eats no more in sukkah and meal, unlike checking where it does not suffice unless he finishes checking; and automatically all that he did not finish, that portion is still called beginning for him, for if he did not bless initially he still blesses at that time as written in this siman seif 1; and although regarding if he conversed he need not return and bless since he already began the matter, nevertheless he must beware in fulfilling the mitzva not to interrupt in its fulfillment in all his necessities, and it does not suffice otherwise; and similar to this I wrote siman 592 regarding not interrupting in conversation between tekiyot sitting and standing — see there; and so in the middle of checking it appears it is literally like in the middle of tekiyot sitting themselves — certainly forbidden to interrupt between them per all:
