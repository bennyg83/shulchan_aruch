Seif katan 2, "throughout the entire time," etc., because he recites the blessing "al biur chametz." This means that the blessing is not on the search itself, which has already passed, but on the destruction, and the obligation of destruction still applies to him. In addition, he recites the nighttime annulment, as stated in siman 434, seif 2. "And all the more so in such a case," meaning we do not rule like Maharik; rather, where he forgot to recite the blessing at the time of the search, he recites it at the time of burning. "Any mitzvah that continues for a period of time," etc. This exclusion applies specifically to brit milah or slaughter, for once he performed the procedure or slaughtered, the mitzvah has passed and he can no longer recite the blessing. A mitzvah that continues, however, such as tefillin or tzitzit, ideally has its blessing recited immediately when one puts on the tefillin or wraps in the tallis, but since he is still engaged in that mitzvah and the tefillin or tzitzit remain upon him, he may recite the blessing if he omitted it initially. The same applies to bedikat chametz, whose mitzvah continues until the next day when the chametz is burned, so he may recite the blessing at the burning. See also the end of siman 214, where Rambam's wording is cited. In Yoreh De'ah, siman 19, Shach cites Sefer Beit Avraham as holding that even in slaughter, if one failed to recite the blessing at the time of slaughter he may recite it afterward. Shach disagrees. For a mitzvah that continues for a period of time, however, Shach also agrees.
