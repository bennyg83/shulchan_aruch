In Magen Avraham seif katan 6: "It is not comparable to what is written in siman 585," etc.; if another circumcised him, that other blesses, and see Tur siman 641; so it must read. The intent of his words appears to me as follows: certainly where the obligated person performs the mitzvah himself, he properly fulfills his obligation through another's blessing, as explained in siman 8 seif katan 8 in the name of Hagahot Ashri. Therefore regarding shofar, the blower properly blesses, since the person obligated to hear the blowing hears and performs the mitzvah himself, and the blower discharges him with his blessing. What the Magen Avraham wrote, "the hearer," means the person obligated to hear the shofar sound, who hears it from this person and hears the blessing and fulfills through it. Here, however, the obligated person does no mitzvah at all; another performs the mitzvah, and he should not bless since the mitzvah is not incumbent on him. This is not like separating terumot and maaserot, where if one appoints an agent to separate, the agent blesses, as implied regarding challah, for there the separating agent also has relevance to it, since he may not eat from this dough before separation, unlike here. Nevertheless, perhaps if the homeowner stands there at the time of the blessing, the Magen Avraham agrees that the agent may bless properly: since the homeowner, who is obligated to search, fulfills the obligation of searching through this agent, the agent is like him and as though he searches himself; the homeowner has an obligation to bless on this search and fulfills it through the blessing of the searching agent. But the Magen Avraham inferred from the phrase "if the homeowner wants," etc., that only in such a case they rely on the homeowner's intent; otherwise they themselves bless. This is difficult: if the homeowner is present when this one wants to search, it is certainly better for the homeowner to bless and the agent search through the homeowner's blessing, since the homeowner is the obligated party. Therefore it must mean even if the homeowner does not want to fulfill through this blessing, nevertheless the other who searches blesses. On this he asks why he should bless, since the homeowner is not there to fulfill through the blessing and the agent is not obligated to search. He then wrote that it is like circumcision, where the other blesses, implying even if the father is absent, because in any case the mohel performs a mitzvah. So too the searcher performs a mitzvah, although it is not incumbent on him. This is somewhat difficult, for circumcision is different: if the father does not circumcise him, the court is obligated to circumcise him, so it is a mitzvah in which the agent too has relevance, unlike here, where if this person does not want to search his house, no obligation rests on another to search that person's house. I found that Dagul Merevavah also left this in need of study. The Magen Avraham then wrote: see Tur siman 641, meaning there it is explained that one who performs a mitzvah for others also blesses. The Magen Avraham brought these words of the Tur also in siman 366 seif katan 20, and there he brought the Rambam that one who makes a mezuzah for others blesses, and distinguished where the one blessing performs an act. See Bach siman 676, who wrote in manuscript to distinguish: where the mitzvah rests on the person's body, such as lighting Chanukkah candles, one who lights for another does not bless; but where it is a matter requiring a blessing, such as separating terumot and maaserot, he blesses. The distinction appears based on Tosafot Rid in Kiddushin, who explains that agency does not help if one tells another, "put on tefillin for me" or "sit in the sukkah for me," because the mitzvah rests on the person's body. Accordingly, even with Chanukkah candles, when another lights for him, this is not by the law of agency; rather, the mitzvah incumbent on the person is that a Chanukkah lamp be lit in his house, whether by himself or another. The one lighting is lighting that which the obligated person must have done, like saying to another "put tefillin on me." Since the lighter does not have the status of agent, he does not bless, because this mitzvah is not incumbent upon him. But where the mitzvah rests on the object, such as separating terumot and maaserot, the one made agent to separate is legally like the homeowner, and he properly blesses. The main distinction between resting on the person and on the object is that where there is no constant obligation, such as terumah, if he has no produce or does not want to eat there is no obligation on him at all; unlike tefillin or Chanukkah lighting, where the obligation rests on the person to perform the mitzvah. Regarding bedikat chametz, it requires study whether it resembles Chanukkah lighting, where the mitzvah rests on the person to check his domain from chametz, for every person has a place where he keeps chametz all year and the Sages enacted checking on the fourteenth, but still another's checking for him works as with Chanukkah lighting and not through agency; or perhaps, since the main mitzvah is that his domain be checked from chametz, it is object-based like terumah separation and agency works. In any event, when the homeowner stands by him, either way the agent properly blesses. So too is explained in siman 670 in the Bach's above words, brought by the Magen Avraham there. Regarding circumcision, my grandfather the Gaon, author of Tevuot Shor, siman 28, wrote that we say a person's agent is like himself. There is room to examine his words, but this is not the place.
What he wrote, "and the custom is to place chametz nuksheh," means hard chametz, and refers to what the Rama wrote, "and the custom is to place," etc. Perhaps it is a separate marker on the words of the Rama.
