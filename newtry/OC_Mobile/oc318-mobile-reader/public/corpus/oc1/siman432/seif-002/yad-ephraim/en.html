In Magen Avraham s.k. 6 — and it is not comparable to what is written siman 585, etc., if another circumcised him the other blesses; and see Tur siman 491 as should be — and the intent of his words appears to me: certainly where the obligated one performs the mitzva himself he fulfills well through another's blessing, as explained siman 8 s.k. 8 in the name of Hagahot Maimoniyot; therefore regarding tekiah the blower blesses well, for the one obligated to hear tekiah is listening and performing the mitzva himself, and the blower fulfills him through his blessing; and this that Magen Avraham wrote the listener — meaning that one obligated to hear shofar sound and hears it from this one's mouth — he hears the blessing and fulfills through it; unlike here where the obligated one does not perform mitzva at all, only the other performs the mitzva — he has no [reason] to bless since the mitzva is not incumbent on him. One may say for separation of terumot and tithes — even if he appoints an agent to separate, the agent blesses as implied regarding challah, for it is different there since also the agent who separates is included — forbidden for him to eat from this dough before separation — unlike here; and nevertheless it is possible if the head of household stands there at the blessing Magen Avraham concedes the agent blesses well, since the head of household who is obligated to check fulfills through this agent the obligation of checking, and it is as himself and as if he checks himself — obligation of blessing on this checking applies to head of household and he fulfills through the checking agent's blessing; except Magen Avraham implies from what he said and if the head of household wishes, etc. — it implies specifically in such a case they may rely on the head of household's view; if not, they bless themselves. If so it is difficult — if the head of household is there when this one wishes to check, it is obvious it is preferable the head of household bless and the agent check through head of household's blessing, since the head of household is obligated in the matter; and therefore he means even if the head of household does not wish to fulfill through this blessing, nevertheless the other checker blesses; and on this he questioned why he should bless, since the head of household is not there to fulfill through this blessing and the agent is not incumbent to check; and on this he wrote it is comparable to milah — the other blesses; and it implies even if the father of the son is not there the law is the same, because nevertheless the circumciser performs a mitzva; and so the checker performs mitzva although it is a mitzva not incumbent on him. And slightly requires study — milah is different, for if the father did not circumcise, the court is obligated to circumcise him — it is like a mitzva where the agent is also included; unlike here, if this one does not wish to check his house, no obligation is incumbent on another to check this one's house. And I found in Dagul Merevavah who also left it requiring study. And again Magen Avraham wrote see Tur siman 491 — meaning there it is explained that one who performs mitzva for others also blesses; and Magen Avraham brought Tur's words also in siman 366 s.k. 20, and there he also brought Rambam's words that one who makes mezuzah for others blesses; and he wrote to distinguish between where the blesser does the act — see there; and see Bach siman 476 who wrote to distinguish between where the mitzva is incumbent on the person's body, such as lighting Chanukah candle — the lighter for another does not bless, unlike where it is a matter requiring blessing such as separation of terumot and tithes; and it appears the distinction is per Tosafot Rid in Kiddushin — therefore agency does not help when one says to his friend put on tefillin for me or sit in sukkah for me, because the mitzva is incumbent on the person's body — see there. And per this, also in Chanukah lighting what a friend lights for him is not from the law of agency, only that the essence of mitzva incumbent on the person is that Chanukah candle be lit in his house, and there is no distinction whether by himself or by another; and this lighter is lighting for the obligated one in the mitzva, and he is as one who says to his friend put on tefillin for me through you; if so, since the lighter is not an agent appointed by him, he again has no [reason] to bless since this mitzva is not incumbent on him; unlike when the mitzva is incumbent on the body of the matter, such as separation of terumot and tithes — this one becomes agent to separate, and one's agent is as himself, and he is as head of household himself, and he blesses well; and the essence of distinction between incumbent on the person or on the matter is that wherever the obligation is not constant — such as for separation of terumah, if he has no grain or does not wish to eat, no obligation is upon him at all — unlike placing tefillin or Chanukah lighting where the obligation is on the person's head that he perform the mitzva. And here regarding checking requires study whether it is comparable to Chanukah where mitzva is incumbent on the person's body to check his domain from chametz — for there is no person who has no place where he places chametz all year, and Sages enacted to check on the 14th; nevertheless checking of another who checks on his behalf helps as written regarding Chanukah, and not from the law of agency. Or perhaps here, since essence of mitzva is that his domain be checked from chametz as incumbent on a matter, it is like separation of terumah and agency helps in it; and nevertheless when head of household stands with him, either way he blesses well, and so explained in siman 491 in Bach's words brought there, and Magen Avraham brought it there; and regarding milah Eshel Avraham the Gaon author wrote responsum siman 28 that we say one's agent is as himself, and there is room for investigation in his words, and this is not the place.
