"He should appoint members of his household." In any event, he himself should also search, because it is a greater mitzvah to perform it personally than through an agent, as stated at the beginning of the second chapter of Kiddushin. See what I wrote in siman 431 and siman 470. The Gemara implies that in some places the custom was to hire a person to perform the search. "Relying upon the blessing." The agents may rely upon the homeowner's blessing when they hear it and intend to fulfill through him, and each can then search a different place. If the homeowner is not present to discharge them, the person who actually performs the search recites the blessing, because he is performing the mitzvah. This resembles other mitzvot in which the person performing the act for another can recite the blessing, such as one who performs a brit milah for another's child. The matter differs from shofar, where the obligated person himself performs the mitzvah through hearing and merely fulfills the blessing through the blower. Here the agent performs the actual search. Nevertheless, when the homeowner is present it is preferable that he recite the blessing, because the obligation rests primarily upon him and the agents act on his behalf.
