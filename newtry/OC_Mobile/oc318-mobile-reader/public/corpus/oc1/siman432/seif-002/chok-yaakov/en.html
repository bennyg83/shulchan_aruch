He may inspect several houses. Beit Hillel writes on Yoreh Deah, siman 284, seif katan 1, that walking from one house to another is not an interruption. Since even speech would not require a new blessing, walking certainly does not. He should appoint members of his household. This states the common case. The same applies to someone who is not a member of his household, as implied at the end of the siman, where some places hire a person to perform the inspection. In any event, the homeowner should assist, because it is preferable to perform a mitzvah oneself rather than through an agent. Initially, it is proper to appoint adult men. After the fact, women and minors are also valid, because everyone is trusted regarding bedikas chametz, even servants. Maharil. Responsa Radbaz, siman 289, writes that nowadays one should not rely upon our non-Jewish maidservants. See Tosafos, Eruvin 31b, under the heading “Here,” who write that although a minor is trusted regarding bedikas chametz, this refers to inspection in his own house. Regarding agency, perhaps he is not relied upon. This conflicts with the implication of the later authorities here. See the end of siman 437. Members of his household with him. They should answer amen to his blessing. If they are not standing with him when he recites the blessing, the implication of the poskim and Tosafos is that he cannot send them to inspect. Olas Shabbos wonders why. If the homeowner has already blessed, why can he not appoint an agent, since a person’s agent is legally equivalent to himself? This is an error. Although the homeowner may appoint an agent, the agent himself cannot perform the mitzvah without a blessing. Even HaEzer, siman 35, states that when betrothal is performed through an agent, the agent recites the blessing. One might suggest that the agent should bless here as well. However, since the homeowner has already blessed and this is one mitzvah, one cannot recite two blessings over it. The agent should therefore stand with him and fulfill his obligation through the homeowner’s blessing. Tur, Yoreh Deah, similarly states that one who listens without speaking may perform shechitah if he heard the blessing from another. Without hearing it, he may not slaughter, even as an agent. If he transgressed and slaughtered without hearing the blessing, the shechitah is valid, because blessings do not prevent fulfillment. After the homeowner recited the blessing. Maharil writes in the name of Maharshal that when the homeowner wishes to discharge those assisting him, he should recite “concerning the mitzvah of removal.” This is not the accepted custom. The later authorities. Pieces. These should be hard pieces of chametz that do not crumble. The later authorities. If he did not place them, this does not prevent fulfillment. Raavad in Tamim Deim, siman 29, writes that this custom is merely an act of women. Taz likewise writes that initially it is proper not to place anything. Mahariv, siman 193, explains that the pieces are placed so that one will not forget to nullify the chametz the next day. When he still has chametz, he remembers at the time of removal to nullify it. In my opinion, the reason for the custom is that if one inspects for some time without finding anything, he may become lax, despair of finding chametz, and cease inspecting carefully. Pieces are therefore placed so that when he finds chametz he will pay closer attention and inspect carefully, perhaps finding more. One should not abolish an established Jewish custom.
