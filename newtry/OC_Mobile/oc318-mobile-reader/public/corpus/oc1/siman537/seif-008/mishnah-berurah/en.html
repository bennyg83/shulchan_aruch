(29) So that water enter it, etc. For it is like fertilizing a field on the moed, as above.
(30) In order to eat them on the moed. See Beis Meir, whose view is that it is forbidden only if his intent is to leave all the fish for after the moed; but if his intent is to eat of them, even if he does not intend to eat all of them, it is fine. However, from Beur HaGra, who compares this to that which was taught on folio 13b, "crushed [grain, they mill] privately for the need of the moed," and there the Mechaber wrote above in siman 533, se'if 1, that one should not use a pretext to mill more intentionally [indeed, the law of pretext itself is not a clear matter for prohibition; see there]; and see Peri Megadim, who also is in doubt about this.
(31) For example, he opens a place above, etc. For even though his intent is for the fish, we require that his actions be evident to that [purpose]; therefore he should open two openings. But one opening: even though his intent is for the fish, it is forbidden, since his actions are not evident and it looks as if he intends to water it. And Ritva wrote in his novellae that all this is only regarding a prohibition ab initio; but after the fact we do not penalize him if he says that his intent was for what is permitted. And the same law applies regarding se'if 9, 10, and 11.
(32) And below a place where they exit. That is, he makes a small hole there so that the water flow out from there and the fish remain trapped of themselves; and even though the field incidentally drinks from the water, it is permitted, for it is a matter he does not intend.
