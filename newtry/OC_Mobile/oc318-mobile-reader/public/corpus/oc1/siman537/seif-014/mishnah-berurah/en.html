(41) Sheep into a pen. That is, they bring animals into the field and they lie there at night, and all night the animals move from place to place until the entire field is fertilized.
(42) To fertilize the field. Even though it is not by hand, because it looks as if he is fertilizing himself; and fertilizing is a toldah of plowing, because he improves the ground to sow in it.
(43) Even if he shows him gratitude. That is to say, even on Shabbos itself.
(44) And on Yom Tov he may, etc. That is to say, after Yom Tov; for food [as payment] does not look like an actual wage. (And nevertheless this is specifically in this [case], which is not a complete melacha; but a non-Jew who does a complete melacha on Yom Tov for a Jew: it is forbidden to give him the wage of his food, for he shows that he was pleased with the work he did for him.) And likewise he may give him food on Yom Tov itself.
(45) Provided that he not give him other wages. For when he gives him [other wages], they will know that he acted with the Jew's knowledge, and they will conclude that he hired him to do melacha for days.
(46) Provided that he not mention it to him. That is to say, that he not say to the non-Jew, "Do [it] and I will give you wages after the moed"; for wherever one may not do [it himself], one may not tell a non-Jew to do it, as below in siman 543, se'if 1.
(47) And he should not hand over a guard to him, etc. Because it looks as if he hired the non-Jew from the outset for this purpose (Levush).
(48) On Chol HaMoed. But on Shabbos and Yom Tov it is forbidden even with a year-hire; and likewise to hand a guard over to him is forbidden on Shabbos and Yom Tov.
(49) And they hand over to him, etc. But to hire a guard for him on the moed is forbidden even in a case like this.
