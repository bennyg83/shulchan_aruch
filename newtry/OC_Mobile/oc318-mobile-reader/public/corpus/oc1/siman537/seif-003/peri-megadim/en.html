In order. See Taz and see Magen Avraham; and with Hashem's help there it will be explained what is "drawing" and what is "drawing with a bucket." And the rule of a davar ha'aved is that it is permitted only without excessive bother; but if [done] through [much] toil it is forbidden even for loss, as with eggs in siman 536 in the Taz at the end of letter 1, and see Magen Avraham there 3, and in the Levush there and here; and see Maggid Mishneh, chapter 7 of Hilchos Yom Tov, law 2; and so it is in the Gemara Moed Katan 2a and 4a. Except that Maggid Mishneh explains it, or reads it thus, as "excessive bother"; and it turns out that the matter is so: whatever is for the need of the moed, even great toil is permitted; and whatever is not for the need of the moed, if it is [something that would be] lost, is permitted even if there is some toil, but great toil is forbidden even in a place of loss. Like that of siman 536: a hen that fled after three days to rebelliousness, even if she sat three days, such that [the eggs] are completely lost, it is forbidden to return her, for there is great toil; and as Rashi explained, Pesachim 55b, that she is not easy to return. And it implies that even if there is a doubt that perhaps she will warm the eggs and chicks will emerge for the need of the moed, it is forbidden, even though a doubtful davar ha'aved is permitted; see Magen Avraham on this. Likewise one might say that a doubtful need of the moed should also be permitted, and say that Rashi follows his own view at the beginning of Moed Katan, where it implies that Chol HaMoed is [forbidden] according to the Torah and [the details] were handed to the Sages; therefore one may say a doubtful davar ha'aved is forbidden, and likewise a doubtful need of the moed, for perhaps chicks will not emerge at all. But that is not so, for seating and returning are entirely rabbinic, and one may say [leniency] somewhat. However, even without this, it seems to fit: for a chick is forbidden until its wings grow; and this requires further study in Yoreh De'ah siman 15; and see Taz there, Rif and Rosh, in the dispute of R' Eliezer ben Yaakov and the Rabbis; and to say that it depends on whether Chol HaMoed is forbidden according to the Torah, they are stringent in a doubt: that is not so. Also, opening of the eyes: is it not conceivable in three or four days? And this requires further study. And according to this, a davar ha'aved of principal is permitted with toil, but not with great toil; and forgoing profit, even through some toil, is forbidden; but with no toil at all it is permitted, even forgoing profit: one may say it is [treated as] loss. And that is where there is no complete melacha, only a rabbinic prohibition, such as buying and selling and the like; but a complete melacha, one may say even with no toil at all, forgoing profit is forbidden. And see Maggid Mishneh, chapter 7 of Hilchos Yom Tov, law 22; and see siman 533 in Magen Avraham 6, and 539:6; and with Hashem's help there it will be explained further.
