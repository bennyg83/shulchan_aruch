15) [Se'if 3] Provided that they do not cease flowing. Some explain this as referring to ponds that do not dry up because they have an abundant supply of water. Others explain it as referring to rivers, meaning that the river flowing from the pond does not cease. If it does cease, then although the river presently contains so much water that there will be no need to draw during the festival, it is nevertheless forbidden to water from it. We decree lest one think that the waters are so abundant when perhaps they are not, causing him to draw water and engage in excessive exertion, as explained in Beis Yosef. See there. Shulchan Gavoah, subsection 9, writes that it appears proper to be stringent according to all the explanations, since the phrase 'they do not cease' can bear both meanings. Therefore, whenever the pond water or the rivers flowing from it cease, watering from them is forbidden lest one come to finish watering with his pitcher. These are his words. Mishnah Berurah, subsection 9, writes likewise.
16) There. When an irrigation channel, etc. If a channel from a flowing spring constantly passes among them, one may water from it during the festival because its waters do not cease. Nimukei Yosef on Rif. There is no distinction between a channel that enters the trenches without flowing out beyond them and one that flows out the other side. In every case, if its waters ordinarily cease, watering is forbidden; if they do not cease, it is permitted. This is unlike Beis Yosef, who makes this distinction. Darkei Moshe, subsection 3; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 5.
