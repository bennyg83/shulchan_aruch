(ב) there. Davar ha-aved is permitted, etc., even doubtful davar ha-aved is permitted. Shach Orach Chayyim siman 113. Knesset HaGedolah in Hagahot Tur Orach Chayyim letter 1, Magen Avraham beginning of the siman, Eliyah Rabbah letter 1; and the reason Ateret Zekeinim wrote is because chol ha-moed is rabbinic and doubt is lenient. And so Machatzit HaShekel and Levush. And so it implies that per the view it is from the Torah one should be stringent. However in Beit Yosef siman 539 s.v. and what Rosh wrote, he wrote: since it is not certainly davar ha-aved it is forbidden, etc., see there. And this is what he wrote there per Rosh's view, who is among those who hold melacha on chol ha-moed is rabbinic prohibition as Rosh wrote at the beginning of Moed Katan and brought in Beit Yosef siman 530, see there. And therefore from this it implies that also per the view it is rabbinic, doubtful davar ha-aved is forbidden. Nevertheless it would seem there is a contradiction to this from mishnah Moed Katan 18b "and these write on the festival," etc., and Tosafot wrote there it appears all these are davar ha-aved lest the giver die, etc., and so Ritva's chiddushim there; and from this it implies we are lenient from doubt; and so from Rashba's responsum brought in Beit Yosef siman 545 who wrote the reason the Tosefta permits writing his accounts on the festival lest he forget and lose his money — end of his words; and Rashba is among those who hold melacha on chol ha-moed is forbidden from the Torah as written in Beit Yosef siman 530, and nevertheless holds doubtful davar ha-aved is permitted. And so Maggid Mishneh chapter 7 Laws of Yom Tov law 3 in the name of Ramban that what they required bringing produce in discreetly (see below siman 538 seif 2) is because they are not certain loss but doubt, etc., see there — behold he permits doubtful davar ha-aved; and he is also among those who hold from the Torah as written in Beit Yosef siman 530, see there. And therefore it appears this does not depend on Torah or rabbinic, but all that it is close to certainty that loss will come — per appearance one should permit even in doubt; and all that the concern is distant one should not permit from doubt. And see Chayei Adam general rule 106 letter 5 who wrote doubtful davar ha-aved should be done through a gentile, see there. And therefore if possible one should be stringent and do through a gentile; and so with a distant concern, if loss will come, do through a gentile.
