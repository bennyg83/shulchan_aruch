(א) Davar ha-aved, etc. — see Magen Avraham that doubtful davar ha-aved is also permitted; and see Peri Megadim and Machatzit HaShekel that this law would seem to depend whether melacha on chol ha-moed for something that is not davar ha-aved is forbidden from the Torah or rabbinically; and see Nekudat HaKesef who proved that Beit Yosef's view in siman 539 is to be stringent on doubtful davar ha-aved, meaning even per those who hold the essence of melacha prohibition on chol ha-moed is rabbinic, since Beit Yosef wrote there this is per Rosh's view and Rosh is among those who hold at the beginning of Moed Katan it is rabbinic; nevertheless it would seem there is a contradiction to all this from the mishnah on daf 18b "and these write," and Tosafot wrote there it appears all these are davar ha-aved lest the giver die or the witnesses or the court or they go to a distant land; and similarly in Ritva's chiddushim on the entire mishnah — behold we are lenient from doubt; and so from Rashba brought in Beit Yosef siman 545 that therefore the Tosefta permits writing his accounts on chol ha-moed lest he not remember them until after the festival — behold they permit from doubt; and more than this, for Rashba is among those who hold melacha on chol ha-moed is essentially from the Torah as I brought in siman 530 in Biur Halacha, see there. And therefore it appears to my humble opinion this essentially depends on whether the concern that there will be davar ha-aved if not done on chol ha-moed is common — we are lenient even per those who hold melacha on chol ha-moed is essentially from the Torah, and therefore the mishnah is lenient to write in all those matters it mentioned; see Nekudat Yosef and Ritva (and what Tosafot wrote "lest the giver die or the witnesses," etc. — meaning through all these together it is certainly common). And similarly one must answer what Magen Avraham wrote in siman 544 s.k. 2 in the name of Bedek HaBayit, see there. And if the loss is not common one should be stringent even per those who hold melacha prohibition on chol ha-moed is rabbinic; and therefore the matter in siman 539 that Chayei Adam brought from Beit Yosef's words — there the loss is not so common, for it could be that next week also it will be expensive [and in any case there is no proof from Rosh's words there, for there it deals only with passing profit that would be called davar ha-aved, unlike concern for actual loss; and Beit Yosef himself at the beginning of the siman wrote only Rashbam was somewhat concerned to be stringent on doubt, see there well]; and likewise what Beit Yosef wrote in siman 545 on everyone's words in it is also because Beit Yosef holds the concern that perhaps he will not have leisure after Yom Tov to edit is not common; and therefore what Magen Avraham wrote that doubtful davar ha-aved is permitted is if that concern is common, as it appears to me. Afterwards I found in Rav HaMaggid chapter 7 Laws of Yom Tov law 3 in the name of Ramban who also permits doubtful davar ha-aved, but they must be done discreetly. And see in siman 538 seif 2 that if it cannot be done discreetly it is permitted publicly.
