1) [Se'if 1] A matter subject to loss, etc. This means that something which will incur a loss if not done now is called a matter subject to loss.
2) There. A matter subject to loss is permitted, etc. Even a doubtful matter subject to loss is permitted. Maharshach, volume 1, siman 113; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur; Olas Shabbos, subsection 1; Magen Avraham, at the beginning of the siman; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1. Eshel Avraham explains that the reason is that the laws of Chol HaMoed are rabbinic, so a doubt is treated leniently. Machatzis HaShekel and Levushei Serad write likewise. This implies that according to the opinion that the prohibition is under Torah law, one should be stringent. Nevertheless, Beis Yosef, siman 539, s.v. u'mah shekasav haRosh, writes: 'Since it is not certainly subject to loss, it is forbidden, etc.' See there. He writes this according to Rosh, who is among those who maintain that melachah on Chol HaMoed is prohibited rabbinically, as Rosh writes at the beginning of Moed Katan and Beis Yosef cites in siman 530. See there. This implies that even according to the rabbinic opinion, a doubtful matter subject to loss is forbidden. At first glance, however, this appears to contradict the mishnah in Moed Katan 18b, 'The following documents may be written during the festival, etc.' Tosafos there write that all these are matters subject to loss, lest the giver die, etc. Ritva writes likewise there in his novellae. This implies that we rule leniently in a doubtful case. The same appears from Responsa Rashba cited by Beis Yosef in siman 545. Rashba explains that the Tosefta permits one to write his accounts during the festival lest he forget and lose his money. Rashba is among those who maintain that melachah on Chol HaMoed is forbidden under Torah law, as Beis Yosef writes in siman 530, yet he permits a doubtful matter subject to loss. Maggid Mishneh, chapter 7 of Hilchos Yom Tov, law 3, likewise writes in the name of Ramban that produce must be brought in privately because the loss is uncertain rather than definite. See below, siman 538, se'if 2. This shows that he permits a doubtful matter subject to loss, and Ramban too is among those who hold that the prohibition is under Torah law, as Beis Yosef writes in siman 530. See there. It therefore appears that this does not depend upon whether the prohibition is under Torah or rabbinic law. Whenever it appears nearly certain that a loss will result, one may permit the work despite some doubt. When the concern is remote, one may not permit it because of the doubt. Chayei Adam, rule 106, subsection 5, writes that a doubtful matter subject to loss should be performed through a non-Jew. See there. Therefore, when possible, one should be stringent and have a non-Jew perform it. Likewise, when the concern is remote, if a loss may result, it should be performed through a non-Jew.
3) There. A matter subject to loss is permitted, etc. Printing books on Chol HaMoed is not considered a matter subject to loss, even if one employs workers whose wages were fixed monthly, whether or not work is available, and therefore loses the wages paid to them. The printing itself is not subject to loss. It is also forbidden even if he hires workers who lack food, because a person may not perform melachah on Chol HaMoed merely to provide wages to a worker who lacks food. Maharam of Provence, handwritten responsum, siman 58. Birkei Yosef, subsection 1, cites this and comments: 'See below, siman 542, and what the later authorities write, and examine the matter carefully.' He means that siman 542, se'if 2, states that one may give work to a laborer who lacks food, so how can Maharam forbid it? Birkei Yosef there, subsection 2, likewise writes that Maharam's words require resolution. Shaarei Teshuvah in this siman cites this and resolves Maharam's ruling by saying that permission was granted only when the melachah provides some benefit for Yom Tov, unlike printing. See there. In my humble opinion, this distinction is strained. Permission was stated without qualification for a laborer who lacks food, implying any melachah, even one not needed for the festival. It appears to me that the resolution is as follows. Permission to give melachah to such a worker applies specifically when he takes the work into his own domain and performs it there. Anyone who sees him will know that he does so because he lacks food. He may not, however, perform the melachah in the employer's domain, because observers will suspect the employer of having his work performed during the festival. Printing must be performed in the printing house, which is the employer's domain, and therefore arouses suspicion. Maharam consequently forbids it. This will be explained further below, siman 542, se'if 2, and siman 545, se'if 4; take the ruling from there.
4) A Jewish congregation that vowed not to conduct business on Chol HaMoed, even in commerce subject to loss, not to permit even one who lacks food to do so, and not to open any shop at all, should be blessed from Heaven for forbidding and accepting this upon themselves as a protective measure. Mahari ibn Migash, handwritten responsum, siman 145; Birkei Yosef, subsection 2. See above, siman 530, subsection 7.
5) There. Therefore, an irrigated field, etc. This is a field that must be watered continually and will be ruined if not continually watered. Tur; Levush.
6) There. It may be watered. If one does not water it, he will suffer a great loss. The Sages permitted exertion on Chol HaMoed for a matter involving loss. Rashi and Tosafos at the beginning of Moed Katan.
7) There. Since he began to water it, etc. If he did not water it beforehand, it is forbidden to water it. Tur. This applies although it will be ruined if left until after the festival. Levush. This follows Rambam's view as presented by Beis Yosef. In the name of Raavad, however, Beis Yosef writes that if circumstances beyond one's control before the festival prevented watering, the field may be watered during the festival. It is forbidden only when one forgot or was negligent. See there. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 2, writes that Shulchan Aruch somewhat implies that it rules according to Raavad: if circumstances beyond one's control prevented watering before the festival, the field may be watered during the festival when it will otherwise be ruined. This distinction applies when the produce is not needed for the festival. If it is needed for the festival, such as vegetables to be eaten during the festival, all authorities permit watering, as written below, se'if 4. See there.
8) There: But a ba'al field. Rain from the heavens is sufficient for it. Tur; Levush; Be'er Heitev.
9) A field that has not been planted but is continually saturated with water in preparation for planting may not be saturated during the festival. Although one loses the benefit of the saturation performed before the festival, since there are no seeds in the field, this is not considered a loss. After the festival he can saturate it again as much as he wishes. He merely delays his produce and forgoes the profit and improvement he could have obtained. Riaz. Shiltei HaGibborim on Rif, first chapter of Moed Katan, cites him.
