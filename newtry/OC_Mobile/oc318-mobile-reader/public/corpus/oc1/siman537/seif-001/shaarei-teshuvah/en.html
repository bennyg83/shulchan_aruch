(*) In Shulchan Aruch seif 1: a davar ha-aved is permitted. Birkei Yosef writes in the name of Maharam Provençal in manuscript responsum that printing books on Chol HaMoed, even if he has workers with fixed wages whether or not he gives them work, is not called a davar ha-aved because he loses the workers' wages, since the printing work itself is not a davar ha-aved. Even if he takes workers who have nothing to eat, it is forbidden, for a person may not do work on Chol HaMoed in order to give wages to a worker who lacks food. Birkei Yosef notes to see below siman 542 and the later authorities, meaning that there it sounds one may give work to a worker who has nothing to eat. In siman 542 he writes that Maharam's words require explanation. In my humble opinion, Maharam holds this is only where the work itself has some Yom Tov relevance, unlike printing, which is merely for profit. See Noda BiYehudah cited in siman 542, and below siman 545 what I wrote in the name of Mor U'Ketziah.
