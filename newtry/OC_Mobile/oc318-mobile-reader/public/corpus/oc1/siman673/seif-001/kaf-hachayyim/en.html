1) [Seif 1] All oils and wicks are valid, etc. There are oils that are not drawn after the wick, and there are likewise wicks on which the flame does not take hold properly. On Shabbos it is forbidden to kindle with them, lest one tilt the lamp, as written above in siman 264. The ruling teaches us that they are valid for the Chanukah lamp. The Tur wrote that the reason is that we are not concerned that the lamp may go out and one will kindle it again, because even on a weekday, if it went out before its prescribed time had elapsed, one need not kindle it again. Nor are we concerned that one might tilt it because its light is poor, since it is forbidden to make use of its light.
2) Ibid. They are valid for the Chanukah lamp. However, the lamp used to kindle the other lamps, which is called the shamash, and whose light may be used, may not be made with oils and wicks that are forbidden for Shabbos. Bach; Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahos Beit Yosef, os 2; Magen Avraham, s.k. 1; Eliyah Rabbah, os 4. This applies to those whose practice is to set aside as the additional lamp the shamash with which they kindle, as written below in the gloss. Those whose practice is to set out another lamp as the additional lamp, rather than the shamash with which they kindle, may not make that other lamp with oils and wicks forbidden for Shabbos. So too wrote Knesset HaGedolah there. This applies specifically on Shabbos, when we are concerned that one might tilt it, since its light may be used. On a weekday, however, even the additional lamp may be made with oils and wicks forbidden for Shabbos, since there is no prohibition against tilting. So too wrote the Dagul Merevavah and Machatzis HaShekel in their explanation of the words of Magen Avraham, s.k. 1. So too wrote Mishna Berurah, os 3. However, the words of Levushei Serad imply that even on a weekday one should take care not to make the additional lamp with oils and wicks forbidden for Shabbos; see there.
3) Ibid. They are valid for the Chanukah lamp. Nevertheless, the preferred manner of performing the mitzvah is to use a wick of cotton or strands of flax. Chayei Adam, rule 154, os 8; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, siman 139, os 4; Mishna Berurah, os 2.
4) Ibid. They are valid for the Chanukah lamp. They are valid even initially, because if the lamp goes out one is not required to attend to it, and it is possible that it will not go out. Regarding the quantity of oil, however, we are particular initially, because if the required quantity is not present, it will certainly go out. Re'em; see there. Erekh HaShulchan, os 1. However, see above in siman 672, os 15, where we wrote that some maintain that even after the fact one has not fulfilled the mitzvah if he did not place the required measure of oil; see there.
