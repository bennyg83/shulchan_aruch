Oils and wicks that are fit for Chanukah, containing four se'ifim. All oils and wicks are fit for the Chanukah lamp, even if the oils are not drawn after the wick and the flame does not hold well to those wicks. Gloss: Nevertheless, olive oil is the preferred mitzvah. If olive oil is not available, the mitzvah is with oils whose light is clear and clean. In these countries the custom is to light with wax candles, for their light is clear like oil. Even on the night of Shabbos during Chanukah, it is permitted to light the Chanukah lamp with the oils and wicks with which it is forbidden to light the Shabbos lamp. Gloss: This is if he puts in the lamp only the amount required for its mitzvah. This is because it is forbidden to use the Chanukah lamp, whether on Shabbos or on a weekday. Even to examine coins or count them by its light is forbidden, and even a use of sanctity, such as learning by its light, is forbidden. There is one who permits use for a matter of sanctity. The custom is to light an additional lamp so that if one uses the light, it will be by the additional lamp, which is the one lit last; and he should place it a little away from the other mitzvah lamps. Gloss: In these countries the custom is not to add, but only to place beside them the shamash with which he lights the lamps, and this is preferable. One should make it longer than the other lamps, so that if he comes to use the light, he will use that lamp. If a Chanukah lamp that is forbidden for benefit became mixed with other lamps, even one in a thousand, it is not nullified, because it is an item that is counted. Rather, he should light from the mixture so many that certainly a permitted lamp is burning together with the forbidden lamp, and then it is permitted to use their light.
