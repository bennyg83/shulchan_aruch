(S.k. 1) Fit for the Chanukah lamp. But not for the shamash; this is how it should be read. That is to say, since it is permitted to use the light of the shamash, therefore on Shabbos the shamash must be a good lamp and good oil, for otherwise one could say that perhaps he will tilt it. But for the Chanukah lamp itself, all oils and wicks are fit, even where there is a shamash, for nevertheless, even where there is a shamash, it is forbidden to use the light of the Chanukah lamp, as the Magen Avraham wrote in s.k. 4 in the name of the Milchamos. According to what the Magen Avraham wrote above in siman 264, s.k. 4, that where there is one valid lamp, even beside an invalid lamp, it is permitted to use their light when they stand next to one another, and it implies that even ab initio it is permitted to light an invalid lamp if a valid lamp stands beside it, and so wrote Sefer Tosefes Shabbos there, then for us, who light a shamash, since the shamash must have valid oil and wick, the Chanukah lamp may be from invalid oils and wicks even if it were permitted to use their light.
One should take from wax, etc. Regarding wax that comes from houses of worship of non-Jews, see above, siman 154, se'if 11, that it is forbidden. In Sefer Ateres Zekeinim he wrote in the name of Maharal of Prague to forbid wax candles, for they are like a torch, but the custom is not so.
