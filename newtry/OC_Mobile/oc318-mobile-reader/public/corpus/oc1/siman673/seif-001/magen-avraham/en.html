Fit for the Chanukah lamp. But not for use; Bach. One should take from the wax that drips in the synagogue, since one mitzvah has already been performed with it, etc.; Mateh Moshe and Bach.
To examine coins. For this requires careful examination and his hands are close to the lamp. But a casual use that does not require his hands to be near the lamp is permitted; Beis Yosef, Darkei Moshe, and Maharshal in a responsum. The Bach ruled stringently, and so wrote the Semak and Sefer HaTerumah, that even to eat near it is forbidden. So too it is implied in siman 678, for otherwise he should buy a Chanukah lamp and kindle it in his house, and there would also be peace in the home. Rather, it must be that all use is forbidden, and this is the custom.
And even a use, etc. So too the Maharshal ruled.
So that if one uses. Nevertheless, it is forbidden to use near them, for one who sees it says that he lit all of them for his own need, since sometimes a person lights several lamps; Milchamos. In the Maor it implies that even if there is a shamash beside the lamps, a lamp is needed on the table so that people should not say that he lit them for his own need. Rabbeinu Yerucham wrote that if he lit them in a place where people do not ordinarily light a lamp, he does not need another lamp in order to use its light. End quote. Nevertheless, he must place a shamash beside them, lest he use their light.
He places beside them. Even if many people are lighting, each and every one must place a shamash beside his lamps, even if there is a lamp on the table; Rama in Responsa, siman 81. So too it is implied in the Bach. The Maharshal is lenient; see there.
It is more preferable. For then it is explicitly evident that it is not part of the count of the lamps.
Longer. The same law applies if he sets it higher than the other lamps; this is fine. Bach, Maharil, and Mitzvas Chanukah.
That is forbidden for benefit. This means after it was lit and went out, for before then it did not become forbidden, since designation is not a legally effective matter, as written in siman 638, end of se'if 1. So too the Olas Shabbos wrote in the name of Mahari Bruna.
Something counted. Even in a place where candles are sold by weight, nevertheless mitzvah candles are all counted every night; Terumas HaDeshen. The Maharshal ruled that one is nullified in two; see Yoreh Deah, siman 110.
So many that certainly. This means that if one Chanukah lamp became mixed in, he should light two, and if two became mixed in, he should light three. There are those who have the reading 'two, two,' and there is no need to emend it.
It is permitted to use near them. For the lighting of the shamash is in order that he use their light; this proves that if they are together, it is permitted to use near them; Darkei Moshe. The Bach disagrees and holds that it is permitted only with a shamash that stands higher than all the lamps, for then he uses the light of the shamash, which is not the case here. End quote.
