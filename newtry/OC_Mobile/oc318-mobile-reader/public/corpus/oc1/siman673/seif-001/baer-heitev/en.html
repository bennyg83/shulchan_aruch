Fit. See Responsa Shaar Ephraim, siman 38, regarding whether it is permitted to kindle a Chanukah lamp with butter that was cooked in a ben yomo meat pot and became forbidden because there was not sixty times as much, and he ruled that it is permitted. He was further asked there whether it is permitted to kindle it in order to illuminate the house, and the reason is that it is forbidden for benefit, and he forbids it; see there. One who was attaching wax candles to the wall in order to light them for the mitzvah of the Chanukah lamp, and while he was occupied with this they brought him olive oil, should light with the wax candles, even though the preferred mitzvah is with olive oil; see Shevus Yaakov, part 1, siman 36. In Responsa Chacham Tzvi, siman 45, he rejected his words and his proofs, and brought many proofs that he should withdraw from the wax candles and light with olive oil; see there. See Responsa Yad Eliyahu, siman 42. Afterwards, the author of Shevus Yaakov again took up the matter a second time in part 2, siman 30, and maintained his view; see there. One should not kindle Chanukah lamps partly from olive oil and partly from wax or other oils, for since it is for publicizing the miracle, people will say that some of them are not from the mehadrin practice, for one lamp is sufficient, and he is not from the mehadrin min hamehadrin, because they will say that two people lit them; Responsa Shaar Ephraim, siman 39, see there. See Shevus Yaakov, part 2, siman 31, who wrote that in a place where olive oil is expensive and other oils are cheap, on the first night and on all the added nights he should light with olive oil, and the remaining lamps with the other oils; see there.
Chanukah. This is not specifically the lamp that one first lights, which comes for the mitzvah of the Chanukah lamp; rather, even the one that the mehadrin add each night, it is forbidden to use its light; Mahara Halevi in Sefer Gan HaMelech, siman 42.
Coins. For this requires careful examination and one's hands are near the lamp. But a casual use that does not require his hands to be near the lamp is permitted; Beis Yosef and Darkei Moshe. The Maharshal and Bach ruled stringently, and so wrote the Semak and Sefer HaTerumah, that even to eat near it is forbidden. This is also implied in siman 678, for otherwise he should buy a Chanukah lamp and kindle it in his house, and he would also have peace in his home; rather, it must be that all use is forbidden. And this is the custom; Magen Avraham in siman 678, s.k. 2, from what he wrote there. See Taz and Responsa Shevus Yaakov, part 2, siman 31.
Of sanctity. See the Taz, who discusses this at length and concludes that the one who permits use for a matter of sanctity means specifically in a casual manner, but not to learn in a fixed manner. And here another person being with him does not help, for even on a weekday it is forbidden, because there would be no recognition that it was made for the sake of Chanukah. End quote.
The shamash. Even if many people are lighting, one must place a shamash next to each and every one, even if there is a lamp on the table; Rama in Responsa, siman 81. So too it is implied in the Bach, while the Maharshal is lenient. It is implied in the Maor that even though the shamash is next to the lamps, there must be a lamp on the table; see Magen Avraham, s.k. 4.
Longer. The same law applies if he sets it higher than the other lamps; this is fine. Bach, Maharil, and Mitzvas Chanukah.
Use. It is forbidden to use it for a degrading use, and all the more so, Heaven forbid, to play cards near it; I found this written.
For benefit. This means after it was lit and went out, for before then it did not become forbidden, since designation is not a legally effective matter, as written in siman 638, end of se'if 1; Magen Avraham.
Something counted. Even in a place where candles are sold by weight, nevertheless mitzvah candles are all counted each night; Terumas HaDeshen and Magen Avraham, see there. See also Taz, Responsa Panim Meiros, siman 98, Responsa Shevus Yaakov, part 2, siman 32, and see Yad Aharon.
So many. This means that if one became mixed in, he should light two, and if two became mixed in, he should light three. The Maharshal ruled that one is nullified in two, and this is also the view of the Taz; see there.
