Section 1. The oils and wicks are valid for the Chanukah lamp, etc. Note: we read in the Talmud: Rav Huna said, all oils, etc., may not be used for Chanukah, whether on a weekday or on Shabbos. He holds that if it went out he is required to tend to it, and it is permitted to use its light. Tosafos asked: from where do we know that he holds it is permitted to use its light? Perhaps the reason is that if it went out he is required to tend to it, and on Shabbos he cannot kindle it. They answered that if so, he should have said plainly, they may not be used for Chanukah. Since he said whether on Shabbos, it proves that on Shabbos there is another reason to forbid it, because perhaps he will tilt it, etc. At first glance this is not understood, for in the end, why does he need to teach this? Since because of if it went out he is required to tend to it alone it is also forbidden on Shabbos, why did he need to mention whether on Shabbos? To say that he teaches this so that we will know that it is permitted to use its light is difficult. Besides being forced, for he should have stated explicitly that it is permitted to use its light, there is a stronger difficulty: Tosafos themselves wrote that Rav said one may kindle whether on a weekday or on Shabbos, and did not say plainly, because if he had spoken plainly I would have thought only on a weekday and not on Shabbos, for the initial assumption would have been that no amora would hold that it is forbidden to use its light. If external reasoning says it is permitted to use it, to the point that Rav needed to teach us that it is forbidden, then the one who says it is permitted to use it would not need to teach this, since reasoning already says so, for no person would say that it is forbidden to use its light. Rather, the intent seems as follows. Since he said plainly, they may not be used for Chanukah, this proves that even mehadrin min hamehadrin may not use them, even for the additional lamps. At first glance this is difficult. Since we hold in several places that where after the fact it is valid without this, ab initio we are not concerned lest one forget, then granted, with one lamp, since he holds that if it went out he is required to tend to it, then even after the fact he has not fulfilled the Chanukah lamp, and therefore one must be concerned lest he forget to kindle it afterward. But regarding the mehadrin lamp, since after the fact even if he forgets there is no concern, it should be permitted to kindle the additional lamps of the mehadrin with these oils. Why did he say plainly that they may not be used for Chanukah? Perforce, he holds that it is permitted to use its light. If so, on Shabbos it is automatically forbidden to kindle even the additional lamps, because since it is permitted to use its light, perhaps he will tilt it. Therefore even on a weekday it is forbidden to kindle with these oils even for the additional lamps, because if it were permitted to kindle the additional lamps with them, people would say that the reason one may not kindle with them on Shabbos is also only because if it went out he must tend to it. They would then mistakenly kindle the additional lamps with these oils on Shabbos just as on a weekday, while in truth there is a concern that he may tilt. Therefore even on a weekday one may not kindle with them at all, even for the additional lamps, as a decree because of Shabbos. This proves that he holds it is permitted to use its light, for without this, even on Shabbos it would be permitted to use these oils for the additional lamps, and he would not have said plainly that they may not be used for Chanukah. Thus it proves that he holds it is permitted to use its light, and the question of Tosafos is resolved; examine it. There in the same section in the gloss. And the practice in these countries is to kindle with wax candles, because their light is clear like oil. Note: regarding the law of one who was affixing wax Chanukah candles to the wall and then olive oil was brought to him, what should he do? The great authorities of the world disagreed. The view of Shevus Yaakov is that he should finish with the wax candles, while the view of Chacham Tzvi in responsum 44 is the opposite. Since this is Torah and I need to learn, I too, the poor one, will answer according to my share in this matter. First, it seems possible to bring proof from the sugya in chapter 2 of Shabbos, from the words of Tosafos on the question whether kindling performs the mitzvah or placement performs the mitzvah. Tosafos wrote there that the practical difference is if a deaf-mute, mentally incompetent person, or minor kindled it. Now it is not difficult that they should have said the practical difference is if it went out between kindling and placement: if kindling performs the mitzvah, he already performed the mitzvah, and we hold that if it went out he is not required to tend to it; but if placement performs the mitzvah, he has not yet performed the mitzvah and must go back and kindle. This can be answered by saying that they wanted to state a practical difference according to everyone, even according to the one who says that if it went out he is required to tend to it. One may also say that before placement, according to all, if it went out he is required to tend to it, even if kindling performs the mitzvah, for at any rate we require a recognizable sign that it is for Chanukah, and we hold that one who was holding a Chanukah lamp and standing has done nothing, as explicit in the Talmud, because one who sees says that he kindled it for his own need. If so, as long as he has not placed it, according to all he is required to tend to it. But this is difficult: they should have said that the practical difference is if he was kindling wax candles, and after the kindling olive oil became available. If kindling performs the mitzvah, then he already kindled and it is not relevant to kindle a second time. But if placement performs the mitzvah, he has not yet performed the mitzvah, and he is obligated to withdraw his hand and kindle olive oil. Since they did not write this, it proves like Shevus Yaakov. Therefore, even if placement performs the mitzvah, since he has at least begun their performance, he is no longer obligated to withdraw from them, and therefore they could not state this practical difference; examine it. Now let us turn to the words of Chacham Tzvi. Although he rejected the proofs of Shevus Yaakov well, nevertheless there is no proof in the opposite direction. What he brought as proof from what Rabbi Nachman said, I agree where there are leftovers for eating, proves that where there are no leftovers for eating one does not say to bring another, is no proof. There it is speaking where delay is needed until he brings another, and that we certainly do not say, as explained in Yevamos 39: we do not delay any mitzvah, even where there will be a preferred mitzvah, as it says there that chalitzah by the older brother is preferable; see there. But Shevus Yaakov speaks of a case where delay is not needed. Then one may say that he must withdraw his hand in order to perform the preferred mitzvah. The proof he brought from the end of chapter Hotzi'u Lo, from the fact that the mitzvah is with the first, he himself already saw that this proof can be rejected, for one may say that there the first was already fit. What he brought as proof from what is brought there, that if the second is more choice than it he should bring it, and from what is written in Shevuos 11, if he found another nicer than it, and from untying from garment to garment, etc., all of this seems to be rejectable with one rejection. Certainly where the beautification is recognizable in the object itself, he is obligated to beautify and pursue beautification, even if he already began dealing with another, for what does the body of the mitzvah lose by that? Any time the beautification is recognizable in the body of the mitzvah, this is honor for the mitzvah, and this we learn from This is my God and I will beautify Him. In that case he is certainly obligated to do so even if he already began with another. But where the beautification is not in the body of the mitzvah, only from the perspective of the owners, upon whom it is incumbent to do a mitzvah in the preferred manner, such as in our case, where from the viewer's perspective there is no difference between olive oil and a wax candle, and the advantage of olive oil is only because the miracle occurred with oil and because it is similar to the Menorah, then the preferred mitzvah is not because of beautification in the body of the mitzvah but because of the performer. This is not learned at all from This is my God and I will beautify Him. In such a case one may well say that once he already began with another item, he is not obligated to withdraw and go back to do it another way, even if it would be a mitzvah in the preferred manner. This is correct in my humble opinion, for Chacham Tzvi compared separate matters. Therefore as law it would appear like Shevus Yaakov. But there is one point to raise from Yevamos 39, where it says: come and hear, if he made the matter depend on a minor until he grows up, we do not listen to him. If intercourse by the minor when he grows up is preferable, let us wait, perhaps he will grow up and perform yibbum, etc. What is the question? Let us say that since now they are dealing with the adult, they should not stop from him and go after others. Even according to the answer that we do not delay a mitzvah, this implies that only because there is delay is it so, but if there were no delay it would be proper to wait for the adult or minor, even though now they are dealing with those who are here. This proves that because of beautification of the mitzvah one stops from one matter to another. One cannot say that there it speaks where they had not begun dealing with him at all, for still, what is the question? Let us say that the Mishnah speaks where they already began dealing with yibbum or chalitzah with this one who is here, and afterward he made it depend on the adult or minor, and therefore we do not listen to him. However, one may say that there it proves perforce that it speaks where they had not begun dealing with him at all; for if they had begun, why mention specifically that the adult is overseas or the minor is still truly a minor? Even if he were nearby and fit for yibbum and chalitzah, they would not stop from the one with whom they began in order to perform the preferred mitzvah. Now at first glance it seems that this is also the dispute between Rashi and Tosafos there, regarding the Talmud's question: similarly regarding brothers, if they did not want either to perform chalitzah or yibbum, why do we return to the oldest to force him? It answers: since the mitzvah rests on him, we force him. Rashi explained there that if you say that all cases of did not want and wanted refer to yibbum, this is why we return to the oldest, because since all of them want chalitzah and he wants chalitzah, he comes first. But if you say that he does not even want chalitzah, etc. This implies that even according to the initial assumption, before it knew that the mitzvah rests upon him, nevertheless since all of them wanted chalitzah, he comes first. Why? Since the younger brothers want to perform chalitzah and they are before the court, why should the court return to the oldest? Perforce, Rashi holds that because of beautification of the mitzvah too, we go from the matter being dealt with to the matter not being dealt with, to perform the preferred mitzvah. But Tosafos there wrote that even if they did not want yibbum but only chalitzah, it could have asked. This implies that they hold like Shevus Yaakov. Did the questioner not know what is explicit in the verse and Mishnah, that the mitzvah of yibbum is with the oldest, and that this is beautification of the mitzvah? Even so, it proves that because of beautification of the mitzvah one does not stop from what is before us to return to what is not before us. The Talmud answers that the mitzvah is with the oldest, meaning that yibbum is different, because the verse states, and the firstborn whom she bears. Therefore whenever the oldest is possible and before us, it is as though the mitzvah did not take effect at all on the younger brother, and this is not merely beautification of the mitzvah but considered the body of the mitzvah. But generally, where one fulfills with another item and the second is only beautification of the mitzvah, one should not stop from what he began to go back after another item. There too the beautification is not recognizable in the body of the mitzvah, exactly like the Chanukah lamp and olive oil. Therefore Tosafos follow their own view, and for this reason in Shabbos too they did not write this practical difference. Examine it. Therefore the main law is like Shevus Yaakov, especially in a rabbinic matter; examine it. There, in the same section. And there is one who permits sacred use, etc. Note: this view is very difficult for me from the Yerushalmi brought by the Tur in siman 281, that one may not recite Havdalah over a Chanukah lamp because it is forbidden to use its light. According to this view, at any rate one benefits from its light for mitzvah use. Why then do we recite Havdalah over a synagogue lamp, which is also a mitzvah lamp and is forbidden for weekday use, yet we recite Havdalah over it? All the more is this difficult according to the view brought in the later authorities that light and temporary use is permitted. If so, one benefits from its light in this way. From where do we know that for Havdalah we require benefit from its light for regular use? Perhaps temporary use is enough. This requires examination. Perhaps the Yerushalmi means that although temporary use is permitted, since its essence was not made to provide light for use, one does not bless over it. It is comparable to a lamp of the dead: although it is permitted to benefit from it, since it was not made for light, one does not bless over it. So too here, since the Chanukah lamp was not made to use its light, one does not bless over it. With this one can reject the proof of Knesses HaGedolah in siman 681, that one should kindle the Chanukah lamp before Havdalah, from the Yerushalmi brought by the Tur above. See what I wrote there about this. According to what I wrote here, this proof is also rejected, for one may say that there the Yerushalmi is speaking where he kindled the lamp for the sake of the Chanukah lamp, had not yet blessed over it, and wants to recite Havdalah over it and afterward bless over it for the Chanukah lamp. It teaches that even in this case it is forbidden, because at any rate he did not kindle it in order to use it, only to be a Chanukah lamp; it is like a lamp of the dead, over which one does not bless. This is clear and correct in my humble opinion; examine it. Now regarding the prohibition to use its light, Ran wrote that the reason is that it is like the Menorah, and so wrote the other authorities; see there. At first glance this astonishes me, for there is an explicit Mishnah that a woman would sort wheat by the light of the water-drawing celebration. If so, it is proven that even sacred light was permitted. At first glance I saw in Tosafos, chapter 2 of Pesachim and the beginning of chapter 2 of Shabbos, that they sensed this, though not regarding our issue, but regarding what we say in Pesachim, that sound, appearance, and scent have no me'ilah, but there is still a prohibition. They asked from what is written in the first chapter of Shabbos, that they would light from the worn trousers of the kohanim, etc. It is difficult: why did they address this and not mention our matter, where they said it is forbidden to use its light because it is like the Menorah, proving that there is a prohibition? Also, what they asked from the Yerushalmi, which implies that she was actually sorting, what is the question? In our Talmud too it is a dispute of amoraim, for the one who says it is permitted to use its light holds like the Yerushalmi, that with the Menorah too it was permitted and she actually sorted; while the one who says it is forbidden to use its light holds that with the Menorah too it was forbidden. Why did they bring the Yerushalmi? This requires examination; examine it.
