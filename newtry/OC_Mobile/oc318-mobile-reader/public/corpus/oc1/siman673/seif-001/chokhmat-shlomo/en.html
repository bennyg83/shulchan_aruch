Seif 1: Oils and wicks are valid for Hanukkah light, etc. NB: We have the text in Shas: all oils, etc. — one does not kindle with them on Hanukkah, whether weekday or Shabbat — he holds if it goes out one is concerned, and permitted to use its light. Tosafot questioned: from where does he hold permitted to use its light? Perhaps because if it goes out one is concerned; on Shabbat one cannot kindle — they answered: if so he should have said anonymously one does not kindle with them on Hanukkah; from "whether on Shabbat" it is proven on Shabbat there is another reason to forbid lest one tilt, etc. It appears unexplained why the Gemara teaches this, since for "if it goes out one is concerned" alone it is forbidden on Shabbat — why mention "whether on Shabbat"? One could say it teaches we may use its light — besides being strained to infer in the explanation "permitted to use its light," it is more difficult: Tosafot themselves wrote therefore Rav said one kindles whether weekday or Shabbat, not anonymously — for if he said anonymously one might think only weekday not Shabbat, as one might think no amora would hold forbidden to use its light; if the reasoning externally is permitted to use, until Rav had to inform us forbidden — if the view permitting use would not need to inform us, since reasoning is so no one would say forbidden to use its light. However the intent appears: from "one does not kindle with them on Hanukkah" anonymously it is proven even mehaderin do not kindle even in additional lights; it is difficult: since we hold in many places where b'dieved valid without this, l'chatchila no concern lest one forget — granted for one light, since he holds if it goes out one is concerned, b'dieved he does not fulfill Hanukkah, therefore concern lest he forget to relight; but for mehaderin's light, b'dieved if he forgets there is no stringency — it should be permitted to kindle additional lights by mehaderin with these oils — why say anonymously one does not kindle with them on Hanukkah? However necessarily he holds permitted to use its light — therefore on Shabbat forbidden to kindle even additional, since permitted to use its light lest one tilt; therefore even weekday forbidden with these oils even in additional — if in additional permitted, they would say what on Shabbat we do not kindle from them is only because if it goes out one is concerned, and they would err to kindle additional on Shabbat like weekday; there is concern lest one tilt — therefore even weekday one does not kindle with them at all even in additional, lest Shabbat; and it is proven he holds permitted to use its light — otherwise on Shabbat additional with these oils would be permitted, and he would not say anonymously one does not kindle on Hanukkah — it is proven he holds permitted to use its light; Tosafot's question is settled — examine. First to Zion: proof from sugya chapter 2 Shabbat on kindling versus placement — Tosafot wrote there practical difference if kindled accidentally. This is not difficult — one could say difference if it went out between kindling and placement; if kindling accomplishes mitzvah he already did mitzvah and we hold if it went out not concerned; if placement accomplishes mitzvah he has not yet done mitzvah and must relight. One could say they meant difference even for all, even for view if it goes out concerned. Also before placement for all if it goes out concerned even if kindling accomplishes mitzvah, for we need recognition it is for Hanukkah — we hold he held and stood with Hanukkah light and did nothing, as Gemara explains the viewer says for his need he kindled; therefore all the time he did not place, for all he is concerned. However difficult — one could say difference if he was kindling wax and afterward received olive oil: if kindling accomplishes mitzvah he already kindled and cannot kindle again; if placement accomplishes mitzvah he has not done mitzvah and must withdraw hand and kindle olive oil. From not writing thus it is proven like Shevut Yaakov. Therefore if placement accomplishes mitzvah, since he already began doing them he is not obligated to continue — therefore cannot say this difference — examine. Now Chacham Tzvi's words: what he rejected of Shevut Yaakov's proofs he rejected well; but for the reverse too there is no proof. From R' Nissim of Modena where there are remnants for eating — it is proven where there are no remnants he does not say bring another — this is not proof, for there it speaks needing delay until bringing another — we certainly do not say that, as explained Yevamot 39: any mitzvah we do not delay even if more ideal mitzvah — see there. However Shevut Yaakov speaks where no delay needed — one could say he must withdraw to do more ideal mitzvah. From end chapter Hotsi'u Lo — since he began with first offering, etc. — he himself saw that proof can be rejected — perhaps there the first was already valid. From what he brought Shevuot 11 and if he found another nicer, and from permitting garment to garment — all this can be rejected in one rejection: certainly where hidur is recognizable from the thing itself, one is obligated to beautify and seek better hidur even though he already began with another — the hidur is recognizable in the mitzvah itself; this we learn from "and I shall glorify Him" — certainly obligated even if already engaged with another not as nice, even where hidur is not in the mitzvah itself only from owners' obligation for ideal mitzvah — e.g. here no difference to viewer between olive oil and wax, only because miracle was with oil and similar to menorah — here hidur is not from mitzvah itself only from doer; nothing is learned from "and I shall glorify Him" — one who began with another need not leave to do separately even if more ideal — Chacham Tzvi's words are correct as separate matters. Therefore it appears for law like Shevut Yaakov, especially rabbinic matter — examine. (Same seif) And some permit use of sacred utensils, etc. NB: This view is very difficult from Yerushalmi Tur siman 681 brought: one may not make havdalah on Hanukkah light, forbidden to use its light; that view — they nevertheless benefit from its light for mitzvah use — why make havdalah on synagogue light which is also mitzvah light, forbidden weekday use, yet havdalah on it? All the more difficult for view of Acharonim that temporary sacred use is permitted — they would suit its light for this; from where require permanent suit? Perhaps Yerushalmi means permitted temporary use since its essence is not for illumination — we do not bless on it, similar to dead person's light: permitted to benefit since not made for light, no blessing — likewise Hanukkah not made to use its light, no havdalah. For what is written here, this proof is also rejected — perhaps there he kindled for Hanukkah need, not yet blessed, wants havdalah on it then bless on Hanukkah — teaches even then forbidden since he did not kindle to use, only for Hanukkah — like dead person's light no blessing — such is correct — examine.
