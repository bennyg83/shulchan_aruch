In Turei Zahav, paragraph 3. And so it implies in the hints, for he did not mention degradation, etc. Nevertheless, this resolves only his question of what degradation there is in counting coins. Still, it remains difficult: why did Rosh need to say because they are near the lamp? He should have answered simply that Rav Ashi teaches that even temporary use is forbidden. Therefore he wrote, and since the Tur did not write against, etc. Meaning, the Tur truly does not hold that one should distinguish whether they are near the lamp or not, and therefore he wrote only the word use, so that one should not mistakenly infer from it as Rosh did from Rav Ashi's wording. Therefore the Tur used the phrase by its light, to say that when Rav Ashi said opposite the Chanukah lamp, he used ordinary wording, not to infer from it that it must be near the Chanukah lamp. This is the opposite of Beis Yosef, who asked against the Tur that he should have written like Rosh. About this Turei Zahav said at the beginning of his words: to me, there is difficulty in the words of Rosh, etc. Meaning, on the contrary, the difficulty is on Rosh, why he wrote this way, while the Tur truly did not write so; Rosh should have answered simply that Rav Ashi teaches that all temporary use is forbidden.
