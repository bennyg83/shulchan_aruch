Turei Zahav, paragraph 1. It is forbidden because perhaps it will go out, etc. Rashi explained in Shabbos 21 that the reason is lest he tilt it, for when the lamp is good he will not tilt it during ordinary use, and use that requires examination is truly forbidden, as explained in siman 275: one may not examine garments and one may not read. But with a bad lamp, he may tilt it even during ordinary use. So wrote Magen Avraham at the beginning of siman 264. Alternatively, if another person is with him, it is permitted even to learn by a good lamp, but by a bad lamp, even in such a case we are concerned lest he tilt it, as Turei Zahav wrote here, paragraph 4. However, in the Tur here it implies that there is another reason for a Shabbos lamp, namely lest it go out and he go back and kindle it. Therefore Turei Zahav wrote this reason here, to say that this reason would apply to Chanukah on a weekday were it not for the rule that if it went out he is not required to tend to it. Afterward he also wrote Rashi's reason, which applies on Shabbos Chanukah, were it not for the fact that it is forbidden to use its light even for use that does not require examination.
