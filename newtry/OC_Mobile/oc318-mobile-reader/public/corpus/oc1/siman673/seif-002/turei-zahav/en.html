Lighting, etc., therefore if it went out, etc. I do not know how this depends on that, for even if you say that placement performs the mitzvah, the same law would apply. Furthermore, in the Gemara he brought many proofs that we do not hold that placement performs the mitzvah; why did he not prove it from what we hold, that if it went out he is not bound to it? Rather, it appears that here he did not come to exclude the opinion that placement performs the mitzvah. Rather, this is what he means: since he lit it, he immediately fulfilled the mitzvah; therefore, if it went out, he is not bound to it.
And even if it went out on erev Shabbos, etc. I have room to investigate these words, which are in Terumas HaDeshen, siman 103, and I will copy them. This is his language: It appears that he is not bound to it even in such a case. Although the main mitzvah is from nightfall, as the Mordechai wrote, and therefore we could have said that if it went out before the mitzvah began to be fulfilled he is bound to it, nevertheless, from the fact that we recite the blessing while it is still day, 'to light the Chanukah lamp,' even though the mitzvah is not yet present, since it is impossible in another way it is considered preparation for a mitzvah, as Tosafos explained at the beginning of Yevamos regarding preparation for the mitzvah of honoring father and mother, etc. Therefore one recites a blessing over it. Here too, if it went out, he is not bound to it, for the mitzvah has already begun through its preparation. And there is proof from the fact that they permitted on Chanukah that falls on Shabbos the oils that are forbidden for a Shabbos lamp, the reason being that it is forbidden to use its light, as above. Now, if you would say in our case that he is bound to it, why is it permitted? Let us be concerned that perhaps it will go out after accepting Shabbos, for that acceptance must be before bein hashmashos, and throughout the entire duration of bein hashmashos there is still no fulfillment of the mitzvah, as written above in the name of the Mordechai, and he will therefore be prevented from the mitzvah; there is reason to decree in this case, since he cannot light. End of his words. These words do not settle for me. According to what appears, certainly what they said, that if it went out he is not bound to it, means if it burned at least during the time of the obligation, namely from sunset. But if it burned a little before this and went out during that little time, he is certainly bound to it. Only on Shabbos, when it is impossible to wait until sunset to light and one must begin earlier, that time is called the time of beginning, just as on a weekday from the end of sunset; for so the Sages enacted, that he should light at the time when he is permitted to light. This is not called preparation for a mitzvah, but is an actual mitzvah, since that time is like after sunset. Therefore it is permitted to light with invalid oils on Shabbos, and we are not concerned that perhaps it will go out after accepting Shabbos, for then it is truly like Shabbos itself. But if he lights with them before accepting Shabbos, indeed he must light again, and even with all oils the law is so. This is unlike before accepting Shabbos, when he still has permission to light; who tells us that that time is like afterward? Certainly we do not derive what is possible from what is impossible. Although the Sages gave him permission to recite a blessing before the time of accepting Shabbos, nevertheless it is not then actual Shabbos. This matter is comparable to the case of Rav, who would pray the Shabbos prayer on erev Shabbos, and certainly it did not become actual Shabbos to obligate him if he performed labor at that time that he had advanced, as though he had performed it on Shabbos itself. Even though in prayer he had already made it Shabbos, it is really so. In the Beis Yosef at the end of siman 672 he brings in the name of Mahari Abuhav that from plag haminchah one may advance the lighting, comparable to Rav praying the Shabbos prayer on erev Shabbos. Furthermore, even regarding lighting the Shabbos lamp, it appears above in siman 263 that there is permission to perform labor until Barchu, even though he already recited the blessing to light the Shabbos lamp. So too here, regarding the Chanukah lamp, should we really say that he fulfilled the mitzvah by its burning during that time? For this reason, what the Terumas HaDeshen wrote from the case of 'slaughter for me, cook for me,' that it is considered fulfillment of a mitzvah, is also rejected. There too, since he wants to feed his father, he must first slaughter for him, and this is like the time after accepting Shabbos here. I then saw that the Maharshal also wrote in his brief laws that he is bound to light a second time on erev Shabbos, not like Mahari Abuhav. End quote. In any event, it appears to me that he should not recite a second blessing when he lights if it went out before accepting Shabbos, meaning so long as there is still permission because of the halachic time of accepting Shabbos; rather, he should go back and light without a blessing, for he already recited a blessing permissibly. This appears clear to me. Although the Rama wrote this regarding one who wants to be stringent, etc., it appears to me that according to the law one should rule this way.
And if he wants to be stringent, etc. The Maharshal wrote: One who wants to merit the complete mitzvah should go back and light, for it is no worse than the mehadrin. End quote. He wrote further: It is a mitzvah to buy superior wax from what dripped from the candles of the synagogue, since one mitzvah has already been performed with them. He should be careful not to buy wax from the candles of their houses of worship, for although they have been nullified, nevertheless they are repulsive for mitzvah uses. End quote. So too the Mordechai wrote in chapter Rabbi Yishmael in the name of Raavyah, also regarding Shabbos candles or synagogue candles. It is written in the gloss to the Rif: If he lit it in the presence of wind and it went out, he should go back and light it in a place where wind is not common, for it is like putting in less oil than the required measure. It appears to me that he need not recite a second blessing, for perhaps it is like oils that are not drawn after the wick.
