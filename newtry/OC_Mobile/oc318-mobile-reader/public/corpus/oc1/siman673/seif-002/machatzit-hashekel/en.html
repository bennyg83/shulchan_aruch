(S.k. 2) To examine, etc. And his hands are close. The Rosh wrote that through this it is a degrading use; see the Taz.
For even to eat, where one's hands do not need to be near the lamp, is forbidden. So too it is implied in siman 678.
For otherwise he should buy a Chanukah lamp, etc. That is to say, we say there: a Shabbos lamp and a Chanukah lamp, the Shabbos lamp takes precedence because of peace in the home. This means that if he has only one lamp, he should take it for the Shabbos lamp and not light the Chanukah lamp. This is the statement of Rava on folio 23. If it were possible that it is permitted to eat near the Chanukah lamp, he should light it for the sake of the Chanukah lamp and eat near it, for then both would be present: he would fulfill the mitzvah of the Chanukah lamp and there would also be peace in the home. And although in the time of the Talmud they would light outside at the entrance of the house or courtyard, so there would be no peace in the home if he lit it for the sake of the Chanukah lamp, for us, who light inside, the Magen Avraham indeed wrote in siman 678 that perhaps one should do so. It must be that his proof is from the words of the Talmud. Although in their days they lit outside, nevertheless, in order not to be prevented from the mitzvah of the Chanukah lamp, he should have lit it inside, for at least if he lit inside even in the time of the Talmud, he would fulfill the obligation when it is not possible otherwise, since in a time of danger we say in the Talmud that he places it on his table, as written above in siman 671, se'if 5. And although in a time of danger, where he places it on his table, we say that another lamp is needed for using its light, as also written above in siman 671, that is because of recognition, so that it should be recognizable that it was lit for the sake of the mitzvah, as the Ran wrote there. Nevertheless, if he did not place another lamp near it, it implies that he still fulfilled the mitzvah of the Chanukah lamp; only where possible do we require another lamp ab initio for recognition. Therefore, where he has only one lamp, in order not to be prevented from the mitzvah of the Chanukah lamp, it would have been proper for him to light it on his table. It must therefore be that it is forbidden to eat near it. As for what the Magen Avraham wrote below in siman 677, that nowadays, when we light inside, even though it is forbidden to eat near it, nevertheless he should light it for the sake of the Chanukah lamp and eat near it, since it is not possible otherwise, for in a time of danger he places it on his table even though perforce he uses it: that is specifically for us, since in any event we light inside, so he should light it for the sake of the Chanukah lamp and place it on his table as in a time of danger. Although in a time of danger another lamp is needed, that is where possible, for recognition. But where it is not possible, the Magen Avraham holds that it is permitted even when there is no other lamp, since it is only for recognition, as above. But in the time of the Talmud, when they lit outside, granted that if it were permitted to eat near the Chanukah lamp the Magen Avraham would hold that they should have permitted lighting it inside on his table, even where there is no other lamp, as above. Nevertheless, since it is forbidden to eat near the Chanukah lamp, they did not want to be lenient and permit lighting it inside, since the main mitzvah is outside, and also eating near it. Although in a time of danger it was permitted, that is where he had another lamp for recognition; but where he did not have another lamp, to be lenient both to light inside and to eat near it, while he would have no other lamp, to that extent they were not lenient.
