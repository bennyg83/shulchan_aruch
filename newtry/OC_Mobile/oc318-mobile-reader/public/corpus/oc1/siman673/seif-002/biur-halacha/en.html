Lighting performs the mitzvah. In Responsa Mahari Bruna, siman 39, he wrote that one should not remove his hand after the blessing until he lights most of the wick that extends from the oil; Pitchei Teshuvah.
If it went out, etc., he is not bound to it. However, at the time of lighting, there must be enough oil in it that it can burn for the required measure, as stated above in siman 672, se'if 2. It appears to me that similarly, at the time of lighting, all the lamps must be present according to the count of the days, and then it is considered mehadrin min hamehadrin. Therefore, if one of them went out before he completed the lighting, he must go back and light it. Know further that what they said, 'he is not bound to it,' means even if all of them went out, for publicizing the miracle is required only at the time of lighting.
