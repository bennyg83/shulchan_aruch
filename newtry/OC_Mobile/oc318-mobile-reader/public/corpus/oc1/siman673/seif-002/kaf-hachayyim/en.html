However, the lamp used to kindle the other lamps, which is called the shamash, and whose light may be used, may not be made with oils and wicks that are forbidden for Shabbos. Bach; Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahos Beit Yosef, os 2; Magen Avraham, s.k. 1; Eliyah Rabbah, os 4. This applies to those whose practice is to set aside as the additional lamp the shamash with which they kindle, as written below in the gloss. Those whose practice is to set out another lamp as the additional lamp, rather than the shamash with which they kindle, may not make that other lamp with oils and wicks forbidden for Shabbos. So too wrote Knesset HaGedolah there. This applies specifically on Shabbos, when we are concerned that one might tilt it, since its light may be used. On a weekday, however, even the additional lamp may be made with oils and wicks forbidden for Shabbos, since there is no prohibition against tilting. So too wrote the Dagul Merevavah and Machatzis HaShekel in their explanation of the words of Magen Avraham, s.k. 1. So too wrote Mishna Berurah, os 3. However, the words of Levushei Serad imply that even on a weekday one should take care not to make the additional lamp with oils and wicks forbidden for Shabbos; see there.
