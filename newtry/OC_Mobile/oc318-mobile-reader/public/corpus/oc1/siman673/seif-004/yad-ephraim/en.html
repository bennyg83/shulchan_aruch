Turei Zahav, paragraph 4. This is not so, for if so, why did they disqualify oils, etc.? This requires examination. Certainly regarding lighting on all Shabbosos, they properly spoke of oils that are not drawn after the wick, for he lights those lamps in order to use them for all uses that he will need. If so, it is obvious that one cannot permit lighting them, for would he always go find another person to be with him? This is not so regarding the Chanukah lamp on Shabbos night, where it is permitted, for it is forbidden to use its light except for learning; and since in any event one may learn only if another is with him, there is no longer concern even with disqualified oils. If one lit with disqualified oils on Shabbos, where it is stated above in siman 275 that it is forbidden to use their light, perhaps if another is with him it is truly permitted. Or perhaps even in such a case, when he transgressed by lighting, it is forbidden even with another person present because of a penalty, since he violated the decree of the Sages. But here that does not apply, and it is proper to light the Chanukah lamp with the disqualified oils even on Shabbos. And if the concern is because of learning, since there is another person there is no concern.
