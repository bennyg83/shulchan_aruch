Such as the sukkah of gentiles, etc. - This does not refer back to the adjacent phrase, as if it must be made for shade "such as," etc., and as if saying that these were certainly made for shade; that is not so. Rather, it refers to the main law he wrote, that although the sukkah was not made for the sake of the mitzvah, it is valid on condition that it was made for shade. About this he says: such as ganbach and rakbash sukkot, etc., that although they were not made for the mitzvah of sukkah, they are valid under the stated condition [Ma'amar Mordechai], and this is simple. Know that Rashi explained regarding the sukkah of gentiles that they are made to live in during the hot season. This implies that only when one lives in it all year is it called made for dwelling, and not in this manner. Know further that with one made for dwelling, storage, or privacy, any small renewal does not help to validate the whole sukkah through it [Bach and other authorities].
Animal - If foul refuse is lying there, and all the more so if it is sometimes made for a person to relieve himself there, that place should not be validated for a sukkah. See above in siman 83 in Biur Halachah, s.v. "and he should see."
But a sukkah that was made on its own is invalid because it was not made for shade, etc. - At first glance this language is very forced, for even if his intention at the time of making the stack was to hollow it out afterward and have the upper roofing remain for shade, it is still invalid, for in any event he did not touch the roofing, but only hollowed out the walls, and the roofing was made on its own. This is as Rashi explained on page 11: What is "make it and not from what is already made"? Such as one who hollows out a stack to make himself a sukkah, for the roofing existed by itself and he does no act in it at all, but only removes one of its walls, enters into its depth, takes the sheaves and throws them out, and the sukkah is made by itself, etc. So too it appears in the Levush, who wrote: but if it was made only for privacy and not for its shade, or it was made for shade but he did not do an act in the roofing, only made the walls and the roofing was made by itself, it is invalid. At first glance one might say that the Rambam, whose language the Mechaber copied, holds like the Ba'al HaMaor, who wrote in the sugya on page 12 that if he originally brought up the sheaves into the stack for the sake of a sukkah, intending to hollow it out in the stack, it is valid. But this is not implied by all the decisors; even if he made it for shade, we specifically require that he make an empty space of a handbreadth over seven handbreadths. This is also somewhat proven from what he concludes: therefore, etc., an empty space of a handbreadth over seven for the sake of sukkah. Therefore, in my humble opinion, one can resolve it somewhat: his intent is only to exclude a case where he first made an empty space of a handbreadth over seven for the sake of sukkah, for then its roofing was made for shade, as he concludes afterward.
And he hollowed it out afterward and completed it, etc. - At first glance it is simple that he does not need to do this for the sake of sukkah, for the name sukkah takes effect upon it through this handbreadth, as the decisors wrote that the name tent takes effect on a handbreadth. Likewise in the Rambam and all the decisors, they mentioned that it must be made for shade only regarding the first handbreadth, for through this the name sukkah takes effect upon it. So too it is explicit in the Rosh. But what shall we do, for in the Rif it says: and he hollowed it out for the sake of sukkah. This requires great examination.
Valid - Even according to those who are stringent when rain cannot fall, this is permitted, for they prohibited rabbinically when rain cannot fall only where it resembles a house. This is unlike a stack, which does not resemble a house at all [Taz]. The Eliyah Rabbah's view is that it speaks here where he removed part of it in such a manner that rain can fall.
And if he hollowed out four cubits from two sides, etc. - Regarding invalid roofing, we do not require four cubits on two sides; even on one side it invalidates, as above in siman 632, seif 1. Nor can we say that his intent is that two cubits from this side and two cubits from that side combine, as above there. So too it is proven in the rulings of the Rosh, who wrote plainly: if there are four cubits from the continuation of the first seven handbreadths until the wall, even the first seven are invalid. So too it appears in the Levush. In truth, however, one can answer simply: why does four cubits on one side suffice to invalidate? Because through this, the sukkah that originally had three walls is left with only two walls. Therefore in our case one can sometimes find that hollowing on two sides is needed to invalidate it, such as where, when he hollowed the stack from one side four cubits and made an empty space, he stood on that side and cleared the sheaves from the stack in the place where there had been an empty handbreadth, and made it ten handbreadths high over an area with three walls around it. In this manner, the four cubits certainly cannot invalidate it, for even if with four cubits we cannot say bent wall and one wall is lacking, in any event three walls still remain. Therefore the Mechaber depicted the case as one where he hollowed four cubits from two sides, for then only two walls will remain for the sukkah.
