Such as - See Taz, Sukkah 8, and see Bach. Even with two weaknesses, namely that they are not fixed and that gentiles made them, who are not subject to the obligation, nevertheless it is valid. What he wrote regarding Rashi too, etc., see Magen Avraham. What he wrote regarding Rabbeinu Tam, who explained that it should not be thickened so that rain cannot fall through it, etc., see Bach. The Taz answered that the tanna taught the common case: these sukkot are ordinarily made thickened against rain, and it teaches us that in such a case they are invalid as a sukkah when rain cannot fall there. See letter 20. Thickened so that rain does not fall: it is rabbinically invalid. In the Bach it implies that it is Torah-invalid because he made it for dwelling; therefore if he thinned part of the roofing it would not help, because of "make it, and not from what is already made." But this is not compelling. By contrast, if it is rabbinic, or if by Torah law a sukkah requires that rain be able to fall through it, see Tosafot at the beginning of Sukkah, it becomes valid when he thins part of the roofing, as in siman 631, Magen Avraham letter 2; see there. A sukkah made by a minor can be said to be valid, for they have action; see Chullin regarding action with thought. See siman 636 regarding renewing something in it; this requires examination.
