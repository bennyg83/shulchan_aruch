(1) [Seif 1] It is forbidden to leave and abandon a Torah scroll, etc. — we learn in the first chapter of Berachot 8a: "and those who abandon the the Master will be consumed" — this refers to one who rolls the Torah scroll closed and leaves; Tur, Beit Yosef, and Levush brought it; further in the gemara Rav Papa asked: between one verse and another what is the law — unresolved; Beit Yosef explained: since he is not reading in the Torah — between verses the translator was translating and there is no "abandoning the the Master," even though it is open; or perhaps since the Torah scroll is open it is forbidden; since we concluded unresolved we go stringently — Tur likewise stringently; Peri Chadash section 1 likewise; Matteh Yehuda section 1: what Maran did not write in Shulchan Aruch because we do not now practice translating as in previous siman, therefore certainly forbidden to leave during reading; Matteh Yehuda likewise; however Magen Avraham note 1 brought this law forbidding leaving between verses, and wrote there is a difference on Shavuot when they say Akdamot — meaning some Ashkenaz places on Shavuot the kohen blesses on the Torah and reads first verse of the day's obligation, then they say Akdamot, then finish reading; on this Magen Avraham wrote forbidden to leave during Akdamot like between verses during translation; but Avodat HaGershuni section 1 wrote it appears from Levush permitted to leave during Akdamot since they roll the Torah scroll during Akdamot, unlike between verses — even in their times when they translated it implies they did not roll, for if they rolled why may the reader not assist the translator, as in siman 139 seif 4 — end of his words; however per above siman 145 seif 4 in name of Peri Chadash even if they cover the writing during translation the chazan may not assist the translator — see there; therefore no proof from Avodat HaGershuni; one can say they covered the writing during translation and nevertheless forbidden to leave, and the same for Akdamot as Magen Avraham; however Taz siman 494 wondered at this custom interrupting for Akdamot, for even to speak words of Torah is forbidden; he wrote that recently eminent rabbis instituted saying Akdamot before the kohen begins the blessing of Torah reading, and wrote fit to practice in all communities; also Yatziv Pitgam on second day of Yom Tov after first verse of haftarah — see there; and so is widespread custom among Sephardim when saying piyyutim during Torah reading such as Shavuot or chatan, etc., they say them before beginning Torah blessing so there is no interruption in Torah reading — proper custom.

(1) There — forbidden to leave, etc. — and if they call a person in middle of Torah reading to go up in the adjacent synagogue, it is permitted without any doubt; and so was custom in Jerusalem; however one who is not reading the Shma portion should not go to another synagogue, for in this reading hearing from the chazan he fulfills his obligation, and by going to ascend in another synagogue he passes the reading they read while he is there — for the law section 8 note 25 in Yoreh Deah section 8.
