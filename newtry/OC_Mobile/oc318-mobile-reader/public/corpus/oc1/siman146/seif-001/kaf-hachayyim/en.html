1) [Seif 1] It is forbidden to leave and abandon the Sefer Torah, etc. For we learn in the first chapter of Berachos, folio 8a: 'and those who forsake Hashem shall be consumed' -- this is one who abandons the Sefer Torah when it is open and leaves. And the Tur and Beis Yosef and Levush cited it. And further it is stated there in the Gemara: Rav Pappa asked, between one verse and another, what [is the law]? [It remains] unresolved. And the Bach explained: since he is not [then] reading from the Torah, for between one verse and another the translator would translate, there is no [violation] of 'those who forsake Hashem shall be consumed,' even though it is open; or perhaps, since the Sefer Torah is open, it is forbidden; and since it concludes as unresolved, we act stringently. End of his language. And so the Tur ruled stringently. And so wrote the Pri Chadash, ot 1; Mateh Yehudah, ot 1. And the reason Maran, of blessed memory, did not write it in the Shulchan Aruch is because we do not now have the practice to translate, as stated in the previous siman; and if so, it is certainly forbidden [in any case] to leave at the time of the reading. And so wrote the Mateh Yehudah there. However, the Magen Avraham, s.k. 1, brought this law that it is forbidden to leave between one verse and another, and wrote that there is a practical difference on Shavuos, when they say Akdamus. End of his words. And he means that there are places in Ashkenaz that have the custom on the day of Shavuos that a Kohen blesses on the Torah and reads the first verse of the day's obligation, and afterward they say Akdamus, and afterward he completes the reading; and on this the Magen Avraham wrote that it is forbidden to leave during the recitation of Akdamus, just as it is forbidden to leave between one verse and another when they translate. But the Eliyah Rabbah, ot 1, wrote that it is implied from the Levush that it is permitted to leave during Akdamus, for they roll the Sefer Torah [closed] during Akdamus, which is not the case between one verse and another; [and] even in their times, when they would translate, it is implied that they would not roll [it closed], for if they would roll it, why is the reader not permitted to assist the translator? And so is implied in siman 139, seif 4 and 5. End of his language. However, according to what was stated above in siman 145, ot 4, in the name of the Pri Chadash, that even if they cover the writing during the translation it is forbidden for the chazan to assist the translator, as seen there -- if so, there is no proof from the words of the Eliyah Rabbah, and one can say that they would cover the writing during the translation, and even so it is forbidden to leave, and the same applies to Akdamus, as the Magen Avraham wrote. However, the Taz in siman 494 was puzzled by this custom of interrupting with Akdamus, for even to converse in words of Torah is forbidden; and he wrote that recently distinguished rabbis instituted to sing Akdamus before the Kohen begins the blessing of the reading of the Torah, and [wrote that] it is fitting to practice so in all communities; and also regarding 'Yetziv Pisgam,' which they say on the second day [of Shavuos] after the first verse of the Haftarah, it is fitting to practice so; see there. And so is the widespread custom among the Sefardim, that when they say piyutim during the reading of the Sefer Torah, such as on the day of Shavuos, or for a bridegroom and the like, they say them before he begins the blessing on the Torah, so that there be no interruption in the reading of the Torah; and it is a proper custom.
1) There: It is forbidden to leave, etc. And if they call a person during the middle of the reading of the Torah to ascend in the adjacent synagogue, it is permitted and there is no objection whatsoever; and so was the custom in Yerushalayim, may it be rebuilt and established. However, one who does not [need to] read the parashah -- [that] of Simchas Torah -- should not ascend to another synagogue, for by this reading that he hears from the mouth of the shaliach tzibbur he fulfills the obligation, and through his going to ascend in another synagogue he loses the reading that they read while he is there. Ledovid Emes, siman 8, ot 25; Beis Menuchah, ot 8.
2) There: It is forbidden to leave and abandon the Sefer Torah, etc. Even though ten remain in the synagogue. Perishah and Derishah, ot 1; Erech HaShulchan, ot 1; Mishnah Berurah, ot 1. However, a woman is permitted to leave outside during the reading of the Torah. Sha'ar Shalom; Pesach Einayim, ot 1. And it appears to me [right] to be stringent if there is no need to leave.
3) There: But between one man and another it is well [permitted]. And this is specifically out of great pressure or complete duress; other than this, he should not leave. Seder HaYom; Knesses HaGedolah in the Hagahos HaTur; Magen Avraham, s.k. 2; Eliyah Rabbah, ot 2; Soles Belulah, ot 2; Ledovid Emes, siman 8, ot 25. And this is when he has already heard the reading of the Torah, or when he intends to come [back] immediately. And I am in doubt: if a person came to the synagogue in the middle of the reading of the Sefer Torah to search for a person and the like, whether he is permitted to leave in the middle of the reading; and see the Magen Avraham, siman 138, s.k. 1, [where] it is implied that in such a case too it is forbidden, and it requires study. Eshel Avraham, ot 2. And the Ruach Chaim cited him, ot 1, and wrote: in my opinion this is obvious, that he should not leave in the middle of the reading, for he is called one who forsakes Hashem, who forsakes the Torah and leaves; and even if he already heard the reading of the Torah in another synagogue and fulfilled the obligation, it is nonetheless forbidden; see there. However, it appears that one who has already heard and fulfilled the obligation, or who intends to hear in another synagogue in the place where he prays, is permitted to leave between one man and another even without pressure; and so the people practice.
4) There: But between one man and another it is well. The Pri Chadash wrote, ot 1: it is reasonable that since the reading has been completed, even though he has not yet blessed 'asher nasan lanu,' he may leave. End of his language. But the Mateh Yehudah, ot 2, disagreed with him and wrote: since this blessing belongs to the reading, it is considered like it, and it is included in 'those who forsake Hashem shall be consumed'; and all the more so according to the one who holds that the blessing of the Torah before and after it is from the Torah. Therefore it appears that one should not leave between one man and another until he hears the blessing from the mouth of the oleh and answers Amen; see there. And so wrote the Maamar Mordechai, ot 2. And what the Erech HaShulchan wrote, ot 2, can be refuted.
