When it is open. This wording implies that even if they have not yet begun reading, it is forbidden to leave once the Sefer Torah has been opened. Although in seif 2 the Mechaber rules without qualification like Rambam, that the prohibition applies specifically once the reading has begun, leaving is more stringent. Although between one aliyah and the next is treated more stringently there than here, since here leaving is permitted then but there speaking is forbidden, with respect to leaving while the Sefer Torah is open, this case is more stringent than that one. However, Rambam’s wording implies that here too it is forbidden only once the reading has begun. Yet according to the position considered by Magen Avraham, which also appears to be the Gra’s view, there too the prohibition begins immediately when the Sefer Torah is opened. Accordingly, in our case one certainly should not be lenient.
But between one aliyah and the next. See Tashbetz, part 3, siman 98, who writes that this applies only when it happens occasionally. If people do so regularly, however, it appears to cast off the yoke of Torah, and I almost say that the verse, “Those who forsake Hashem shall perish,” applies to them, Heaven forbid. See there.
It is permitted. See Peri Chadash, who inclines to say that once the reading has ended, even if the concluding blessing of Asher Nasan has not yet been recited, one may leave. Mateh Yehudah disagrees, because the blessing pertains to the reading and is considered part of it. See there.
