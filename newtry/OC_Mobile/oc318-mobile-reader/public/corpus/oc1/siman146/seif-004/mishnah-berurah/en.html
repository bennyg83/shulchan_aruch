(17) One need not stand, etc. — and even those standing on the bimah need not stand before the Torah scroll, except when a person carries it; but when it rests in its place, one need not [see at the beginning of siman 141 that specifically the reader and the one called up must stand]; and likewise even when one holds it in his hand, such as when they read the haftarah — since he sits in his place, he need not stand before it, even if they are within its four amot on the bimah. And know that when they carry it, one is obligated to stand before it until it is covered from his eyes, or until the one bringing it reaches its place; and even leaning on some thing is forbidden, for leaning is like sitting.
(18) At the time they read from the Torah — and at the time of Birkat Kohanim and answering "Baruch Heaven HaMevorach le'olam va'ed" — all need to stand, for it is a matter of holiness.
(19) And there are those who are stringent and stand — Peri Chadash and the Gra wrote that the essential law is like the first view, and so they practiced; and Bach in siman 141 wrote that Maharam too admits that by law it is permitted, only he holds it proper to be stringent and stand, for a person must elevate his mind when he hears the reading from the mouth of the reader, as if he received it at that hour from Mount Sinai; and at Mount Sinai all Israel stood, as it is written "I stand between the the Master and you," etc. And one who is a weak person for whom standing is difficult, and thereby his mind is not settled to concentrate well on the reader's voice — he may sit [Shulchan Aruch HaRav].
"And he did," he said, "and he did not die."
