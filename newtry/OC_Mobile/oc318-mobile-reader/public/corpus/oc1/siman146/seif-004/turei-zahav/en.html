One need not stand, etc. In the Gemara we say in chapter "Ein Omrim": Rabba bar Rav Huna said — since a Torah scroll has been opened it is forbidden to speak even in a matter of halacha, as it is said "and when he opened it all the people stood" — and standing means only silence, as it is said "they stood and did not answer anymore." And for this reason the Tur wrote in the name of Rav Sherira Shalom that one need not stand during Torah reading — for the standing written in the verse means silence. And the Maharam, who was strict and stood during the reading, as the Rama wrote nearby — so too the Mordechai from him; and he wrote there that also during circumcision he would stand, as it is said "and all the people stood at the covenant," and the Rama brought this in Yoreh Deah in Laws of Circumcision and wrote regarding this that so is the practice; and if so, here too it is fitting to practice thus — for in one place they combined them — the Maharam there. And at first glance one may ask on why the Maharam stood — for the Gemara explains that standing means only silence; and I in my humble opinion did not understand the words of Rav Sherira Shalom — for certainly the standing was literally, but that standing must be in silence; for otherwise one may ask on what we say in chapter Shevuot HaEdut — at the time of concluding judgment the litigants must be standing, as it is written "and the two men shall stand" — shall we also say one need not be literally standing but only in silence? And in Levush it is written another proof — for if one would think literal standing — behold it is already written "and you, stand here with me" — which implies specifically the shatz who is the intermediary; and this is a fabrication, for we do not find this in the Gemara; rather certainly in any case standing is needed literally, but at the time of standing silence is required; and therefore the Maharam's practice of standing is well explained. And even though we do not have the power to decide, nevertheless the Maharam's words appear correct and so it is fitting for us to practice. And again — I found in a teshuva to R' Ovadia Sforno siman 90 that he ruled thus that one must stand; and at the time of saying Barchu it is obvious one must stand; and it is said in Yerushalmi: every matter of sanctity requires one to stand on one's feet, etc.; and for this reason it appears that when the congregation wishes to sit after the shatz says Barchu they should not sit until they first say "Blessed is Heaven, blessed forever and ever."
