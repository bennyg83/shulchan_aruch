[3] Once the reader began, etc. This implies that the prohibition does not begin immediately upon opening the Sefer Torah. Lechem Chamudos, folio 44, likewise rules this way. Malbushei Yom Tov writes: “I do not know what Levush saw that led him to disagree. Although he inferred from the verse, ‘When he opened it,’ that the prohibition begins as soon as it is opened, one may say that there was no interruption between the opening and the reading. If there is an interruption, however, it is permitted until the reading begins.” I am astonished, for Rashi in Sotah 39a explains “when he opened it” to mean “when he began,” so the word “opened” denotes beginning. This also appears to be the view of Rambam cited above.
