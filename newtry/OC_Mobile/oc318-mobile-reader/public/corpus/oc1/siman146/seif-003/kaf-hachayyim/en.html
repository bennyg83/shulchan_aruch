3) There: But between one man and another it is well [permitted]. And this is specifically out of great pressure or complete duress; other than this, he should not leave. Seder HaYom; Knesses HaGedolah in the Hagahos HaTur; Magen Avraham, s.k. 2; Eliyah Rabbah, ot 2; Soles Belulah, ot 2; Ledovid Emes, siman 8, ot 25. And this is when he has already heard the reading of the Torah, or when he intends to come [back] immediately. And I am in doubt: if a person came to the synagogue in the middle of the reading of the Sefer Torah to search for a person and the like, whether he is permitted to leave in the middle of the reading; and see the Magen Avraham, siman 138, s.k. 1, [where] it is implied that in such a case too it is forbidden, and it requires study. Eshel Avraham, ot 2. And the Ruach Chaim cited him, ot 1, and wrote: in my opinion this is obvious, that he should not leave in the middle of the reading, for he is called one who forsakes Hashem, who forsakes the Torah and leaves; and even if he already heard the reading of the Torah in another synagogue and fulfilled the obligation, it is nonetheless forbidden; see there. However, it appears that one who has already heard and fulfilled the obligation, or who intends to hear in another synagogue in the place where he prays, is permitted to leave between one man and another even without pressure; and so the people practice.
