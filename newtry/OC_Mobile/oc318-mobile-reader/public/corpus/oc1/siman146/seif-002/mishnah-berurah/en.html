(4) Once he began. Magen Avraham inclines to say that from the moment the Sefer Torah is opened, even if the reader has not yet begun to read, it is already forbidden to speak. This also appears to be the Gra’s view in his commentary, that from the moment they open the Sefer Torah in order to recite the blessing, speaking is forbidden.
(5) Even words of Torah. Likewise, it is forbidden to explain a Torah matter or issue a ruling to someone who asks. To prevent a person from committing a prohibition, however, one may say something briefly if it is impossible to stop him by a gesture.
(6) Even between one aliyah and the next. The concern is that the study may continue into the reading. Bach is lenient regarding study between one aliyah and the next. Eliyah Rabbah inclines to say that one should not be lenient when studying with others, lest the discussion continue. If one studies privately, however, one should not protest against a person who is lenient. As for issuing a practical ruling at that moment, one need not be stringent, since it is uncommon for this to continue.
(7) If he completed, etc. Meaning, he completed reading the verse twice and once in translation. See Peri Megadim, who inclines to say that even if he has already heard Krias HaTorah with a minyan, it is still forbidden. This is also implied by the Gra’s commentary.
(8) And some permit, etc. This refers to a case where ten people besides him are listening to the Sefer Torah. Even then, according to this opinion he may not study aloud, so as not to disturb the other listeners.
(9) One whose Torah study is his occupation. Meaning, even where ten people are listening, one may study during the reading only if Torah study is his occupation and he does not neglect it for any period. See Eliyah Rabbah, who cites several early authorities that nowadays we have no one whose Torah study is his occupation for this purpose.
(10) One who began beforehand, etc. The reason for this view is that since he had already begun the mitzvah of study beforehand, and the people in the synagogue saw that he had established himself in his learning, he is not required to stop. If he had not begun beforehand, however, no person, even one whose Torah study is his occupation, may study in any manner. Students of Rabbeinu Yonah.
(11) It is permitted. This is according to all the opinions mentioned above, since he is engaged in the same subject that the chazzan is reading, even though he is not reading the exact verse the chazzan is reading. Nevertheless, this too is permitted only quietly, so that he does not disturb the listeners. Peri Megadim.
(12) All this does not relate. Meaning, all the leniencies mentioned in this siman regarding listening to the reading.
(13) And Parashas Parah. See the Gra’s commentary, Peri Chadash, and other authorities, who write that this is not the primary view and that Parashas Parah is D’Rabbanan. Nevertheless, regarding this matter it is reasonable that even according to them we do not apply all the leniencies mentioned above.
(14) And the proper practice regarding all the portions, etc. Meaning, even reading the weekly portion twice and once in translation during Krias HaTorah is not the preferred practice. Rather, one should be careful to listen to the reader word for word.
(15) It is proper for one who is meticulous in his conduct. Peri Chadash writes that as a matter of strict law, it is forbidden to engage in anything, even reading the verse twice and once in translation, during Krias HaTorah, even where ten people besides him are listening. Magen Avraham likewise cites Shelah, who is stringent and requires one to remain silent and listen to the reading from the chazzan. This is also the Gra’s view in Maaseh Rav. Nevertheless, even according to this opinion, one may read the verse twice and once in translation, or with Rashi’s commentary, between one aliyah and the next. Magen Avraham cites Mateh Moshe that it is proper to read quietly, word for word, together with the chazzan, for otherwise it is impossible to concentrate and listen. Several later authorities write likewise.
