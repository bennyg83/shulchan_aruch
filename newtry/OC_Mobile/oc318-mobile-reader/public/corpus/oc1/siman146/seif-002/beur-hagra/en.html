Seif 2: Once he began, etc. Rashi and Rambam explain that this does not mean specifically when the Sefer Torah was opened, for the word “when he opened” in the verse means the beginning of the reading. However, the Yerushalmi and Maseches Soferim explain that one opens it and afterward recites the blessing, as it is stated, “When he opened it,” and afterward, “Ezra blessed,” etc., which implies actual opening.
Even between, etc. For it does not say, “as long as it is open,” but rather, “once it has been opened.” Students of Rabbeinu Yonah.
And even if, etc. So that he should not separate himself from the congregation, as written below.
And there are, etc. All this is intended to resolve what is stated in the first chapter of Berachos concerning Rav Sheshes, who turned his face away, etc. The first three explanations are written in Tosafos there in Berachos.
And some explain that one who began beforehand, etc. Since he had already begun the mitzvah, he need not interrupt, and the people in the synagogue saw that he had already established himself in his learning. This is what is meant by “he turned his face away,” etc. According to the first explanations, they were forced to explain that the Gemara stated this as a greater novelty.
And to read, etc. That itself is prohibited, as stated in the third chapter there, so that the congregation should not be occupied, etc., with something in which the congregation is engaged. This case is different.
