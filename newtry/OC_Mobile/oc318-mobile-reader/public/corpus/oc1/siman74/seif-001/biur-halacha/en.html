(א) We shall copy here the introduction from Peri Megadim to simanim 74 and 75, for it is very necessary— A. Know that the law of the prohibition of ervah explained in this siman and in siman 75 is divided into five parts, as will be explained, with Heaven's help: (1) His eye sees his nakedness. (2) His heart sees his nakedness — e.g. when he walks in a shirt without pants and without a belt, or when he was lying naked under a cover and stuck his head outside while his heart is with the nakedness under the cover. (3) Uncovering of nakedness — e.g. when he walks naked in his house even if he stuck his head outside to the house and his heart is also covered with clothing; for there is no issue here of his eye and heart seeing the nakedness — nevertheless there is a prohibition because of uncovering of the nakedness. (4) His eyes see another's nakedness — and there is no difference between the nakedness of a Jew or a non-Jew, and even a minor boy of nine and a minor girl of three, and all the more so a woman, whether married or unmarried (see in Chacham Tzvi). (5) His heart sees another's nakedness. And behold, for (1) his eyes see his nakedness or (4) seeing another's nakedness — it is forbidden by Torah law, as it is written "and He shall not see in you any naked matter" — to recite Shema or engage in words of Torah and holy matters. If so, one who recites Shema and sees ervah transgresses a Torah prohibition; the same applies to every holy matter. [But hirhur is permitted regarding ervah; only "hearing as if answering" is forbidden regarding ervah, for that is speech.] And see in Peri Megadim there, who further suggests that even though one is not flogged for this prohibition since it has no action, nevertheless one is liable for rabbinically mandated lashes and is disqualified from testimony rabbinically. And in doubt whether he saw ervah, he must return and recite, for a doubt in Torah law is stringent; therefore he should return and recite out of doubt. Regarding whether he must return and recite with its blessings — one could say it depends on whether we say the halachah follows Rava in Temurah, that anything the Merciful One said "do not do," if one did it it is ineffective — it would be a Torah doubt in Shema, so he should recite with its blessings. But if we say he returns and recites only rabbinically, one could say a rabbinic doubt is lenient. There is a difference between seeing his own nakedness and another's: for his own, there are opinions in siman 75 that squeezing his eyes shut does not help, and turning away his face cannot be imagined, for he must also turn away his body (as the Taz there). But for another's nakedness, all agree that turning away the face together with the body helps. Also a difference between his eyes seeing ervah or uncovering of ervah: although both are from the Torah, nevertheless there is a difference — for "his eyes see," covering by water does not help, like ervah by lamplight; see inside in Shulchan Aruch. But for uncovering of ervah, covering by water helps; see inside in Shulchan Aruch. B. The law of "his heart sees ervah" — whether his own or another's — the prohibition is only rabbinic. C. All this is when he is dressed in a shirt so the ervah is covered; but for uncovering of ervah, even if his heart is covered and his eyes do not see — e.g. he squeezes them shut or stuck his head outside a window — nevertheless it is forbidden to recite and engage in Torah; not like the Bach in Yoreh De'ah siman 200 who holds there is no prohibition of uncovering ervah at all, only because his heart sees ervah — see there; only uncovering ervah is a Torah prohibition, as the Rashba wrote (Berachos 24), from what is written "and He shall not see in you any naked matter" — it does not write "you shall not see" but "He shall not see" — i.e. others shall not see; and needless to say if another stands opposite him it is Torah forbidden; but even if no other stands opposite, it is forbidden, as Rashi wrote on the Chumash that "He shall not see in you" refers to the Holy One, blessed be He. And if he recited while his nakedness is uncovered, he must return and recite. The prohibition of "his heart sees ervah" is only rabbinic; therefore if his heart is outside the water, or ervah by lamplight and his heart sees it — it is permitted to recite Shema and engage in words of Torah (unlike "his eyes see," which is Torah forbidden in such a case). And a difference between his heart seeing his own ervah or another's: for his own, interruption by his arms helps, and all the more so with a belt; for another's it does not help, for in the end his heart sees another's ervah, and specifically covering with a garment over the heart is required; covering with the hand over the heart does not help even for another's ervah, for one body does not cover another body. If he walks in a shirt alone, then the prohibition is because his heart sees the ervah; and as one who does not walk with a belt — in such a case there is remedy by embracing his arms, or even with his hand it helps — e.g. covering with the shirt over the heart. If the shirt is very thin and the ervah is visible from there, its law is like the law of water explained in this siman; and for his eyes it does not help, like ervah by lamplight. Thus is the content of Peri Megadim's words, in brief explanation.

He was sleeping naked etc. — i.e. even if he stuck his head outside his tallit so that he would not be with his nakedness under one cover, nevertheless it is forbidden because his heart sees ervah until he presses the tallit on his heart to create an interruption; then even if he does not stick his head outside the tallit it is permitted thereby. Thus it is proven in the baraita in Berachos 23b; see there.

He must press — see in Mishna Berurah; and the Lechem Chamudos and Eliyah Rabbah wrote that if he covers his heart with a garment by itself it suffices even if he does not press it against his heart.

Because of his heart — see in Mishna Berurah regarding the law of a canopy; and if he stuck his head and heart outside the canopy it is forbidden, for it is as if he is entirely outside. And if he stands naked in a wooden tower and sticks his head outside, the Yerushalmi is uncertain whether his law is like a house and tent and forbidden, or like a vessel and is like a garment — and Rabbeinu Yeruham rules leniently on this, provided his heart does not see the ervah; and likewise regarding a canopy — all this is explained from the words of Magen Avraham; and therefore it plainly implies that if one stands naked inside a vessel, the vessel is considered like a garment and it helps when he looks with his eyes outwardly; and likewise the Derishah in Yoreh De'ah siman 200, and the Taz is stringent there; and Peri Megadim wondered at him — nevertheless one must create an interruption between his heart and the ervah, for it is no better than a shirt.
