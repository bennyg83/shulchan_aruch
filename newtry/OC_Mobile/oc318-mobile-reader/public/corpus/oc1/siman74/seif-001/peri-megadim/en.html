What the author wrote in seif 1: if his heart sees nakedness it is forbidden; and the Rema glossed the same for another's nakedness. In Beit Yosef he did not explain its source; in Bach he wrote from siman 75 that even another's nakedness is forbidden to see. Now his eyes see from the Torah and his heart sees rabbinically, as we want to say with God's help in letter 1, and there is no proof. And Taz wrote from siman 73: why do I need turning away the face — see there. And one can say that if so, when one turns one and recites it is nothing, and why turn both? And moreover, necessarily he separates with a tallit on his heart so his heart not see his own nakedness — automatically his heart will not see another's nakedness.

(ב) As to Taz — here one will need introduction 1, and this is its description.

(ג) Know that the law divides into five: (1) his eye sees his nakedness; (2) his heart sees his nakedness; (3) exposed nakedness even though his eyes and heart do not see; (4) his eyes see another's nakedness; (5) his heart sees another's nakedness. Now his eyes seeing his or a non-Jew's nakedness is forbidden by Torah from the verse (Deuteronomy 23:15) "there shall not be seen among you any naked thing," and as appears from Berachos 25 and Shabbos 150:1 that it is forbidden, and a complete drasha "that one is needed," and so from the conclusion of all the poskim; and in Eliyah Rabbah on Berachos see there — in such a way that it is not from the Torah that one who sees nakedness and recites Shema or engages in words of Torah and a sacred matter; however the verse "your camp shall be holy" does not refer to nakedness but only to feces (as explained regarding nakedness in turning away the face in siman 75 seif 6, and regarding feces there is a dispute of Rashba and Rosh whether "he shall not see" applies also to feces or only to nakedness; and Shabbos 150 answers "that one" according to R' Yehuda — so too one may say regarding feces in another domain as in siman 79 seif 2, and there improper thoughts are permitted). And it will be explained with Hashem's help in seif 8. If so, one who recites Shema and sees nakedness violates a negative command; and the same for any sacred matter; but improper thoughts are permitted as is evident in Shabbos 150 and in Rashi there; and see Eliyah Rabbah 75:7 and 14, siman 1 in Taz and Shach 19 and 37, and in Peri Megadim there that improper thoughts are permitted regarding nakedness, but hearing as reciting is forbidden regarding nakedness because it is like speech. And the translator there "offense of speech" means an offense in speech — reciting Shema in an unfitting place (in Sefer HaMagid we expanded that it is also disrespect and great is his sin, like disrespect — one who brings words of Torah into a place of impurity, Heaven forbid).

(ד) One must consider: if he recited Shema and his eyes see nakedness — does this disqualify him for testimony? Nevertheless certainly not, for there is no lashes in it, since speech that does not produce an action is not an action; unlike here where he does not produce an action. And one could say that one who fulfills Shema fulfills this positive command — this is an error, for a non-action is not called fulfillment unless an action was done in practice, such as stopping with the voice so the animal stopped eating and speaking (see Pischei Teshuvah on hiring and Pischei Teshuvah on vows). And moreover that blessing too applies — for one fulfills Shema as in siman 83 seif 5; and even Pri Chadash in Orach Chayyim 67 admits here as explained. But even rabbinically I need to consider — one could say that what disqualifies is rabbinically, parallel to Torah lashes for which one is liable for rebellious lashes, as the viewer will see in Pischei Teshuvah and Yoreh De'ah 5 on testimony that wrote it was an offense for which one is liable by their words, etc.; unlike here where by speech it is possible he would not receive rebellious lashes, for their words should not be stricter than Torah, and a negative command for which one is not forewarned — one is not lashed. Nevertheless in Shoshanas HaAmakim in the category of rebellious lashes we explained that even negative and positive commands that have no action at all — one is lashed with rebellious lashes by their words, as Pri Chadash wrote in Orach Chayyim 496 letter 1; and proof from one who utters Hashem's name in vain in Rema end of chapter 5 Niddah and Virgin — see there and it will be explained with Hashem's help; and here there is no need to expand. And so in Even HaEzer 21:61 regarding voice heard nevertheless.

(ה) In such a way: his eyes seeing his or another's nakedness — nevertheless he violates a negative command and is lashed nevertheless by their words. But his heart seeing his or another's nakedness — he sees rabbinically; in Scripture we do not find it, and all this when he is dressed in a shirt so the nakedness is covered. But in exposed nakedness, even though his heart is covered and his eyes do not see — squinting (for the author in siman 75 seif 6, and Taz letter 2), or he put his head outside the window (Magen Avraham here) — nevertheless it is forbidden to recite and engage in Torah. Now in Yoreh De'ah 200, Taz and Shach expanded, and Shach rejected Bach's words who said that exposed nakedness has no prohibition at all except because his heart sees nakedness — see there; and truly so it is, and all the more for the author in siman 75 that squinting helps; if so, go out and support nakedness — he will not tithe; squinting his eyes and stopping with his arms as the author wrote here seif 3. But nevertheless in the reason of the matter, from the drasha there it implies rabbinically that there is no honor of the Divine Presence that one be in exposed nakedness and engage in Torah. But from Taz there and here and Shach there it implies that nevertheless it applies, and this is as Rashba wrote in his novellae on Berachos 24:25 — "he shall not see any naked thing" is not written "you shall not see" but "he shall not see" — for others; and in my novellae I expanded, and one shall surely see: if another stands opposite him it is forbidden nevertheless; and if no other — rabbinically because of honor of the Divine Presence in Perishah. But from Rashi on Chumash "the Holy One, blessed be He, shall not see" (see R' Eliyahu Mizrachi — "he shall see" must read thus, for otherwise "you shall not see" in second person; one could say the vav of "and he shall not see" has no bearing here; and in Shach "he shall see" in tzerei — perhaps so it is). If so, we learned in explanation of exposed nakedness: even though his eyes and heart do not see, it is forbidden nevertheless, as said here.

(ו) Say now: his eyes seeing his nakedness, or exposed nakedness — Torah law forbids even if no other is with him; and doubt is forbidden and he returns and recites from doubt — except that here one could say: if we say the halacha follows Rava in Temurah 4 that if one did it it does not help, it is a Torah doubt in Shema as in siman 67 with its blessings (for Magen Avraham there — not for separation, for because of the presumption the law will not change; understand Magen Avraham there). Unlike if we say rabbinically he returns and recites — one could say rabbinic doubt to be lenient. And there is a difference between his eyes seeing, for water covering does not help like nakedness in a lantern; but for exposed nakedness water covering helps, as Taz and Shach and Magen Avraham wrote, as the viewer will see in their words in Yoreh De'ah 200 and here. They are truly the reason — I do not know why, since "he shall not see" applies to his eyes and exposed nakedness — what is this from that; and in our novellae we were puzzled on this, and now I saw Pri Chadash and he sensed this, and Shach siman 14 raised it and did not explain — see there. And for Perishah in Yoreh De'ah it is rabbinic — it would be that water covering helps; but from Rashi on Chumash it does not appear so, as I wrote; and see R' Chaim in Semag there and understand. And see Magen Avraham letter 5.

And his heart seeing nakedness is rabbinic — one could say well: all whose heart is outside the water they were lenient; and the braisa in Berachos 25:2 "clear water — he sits up to his neck and recites" (and there would be a novelty, and so they dispute — some, and Av Beit Din) supports the law: all who are outside the water — the heart; and some agree. And do not challenge what is written regarding nakedness in a lantern, that his eyes are Torah and his heart rabbinic — all who are covered in water, and the same in a lantern even though his heart sees — Shach and Pri Chadash; and what some say that his eyes — water covering does not help, as Magen Avraham that squinting (and from this the author proved in siman 75 seif 6 his law, and there is no difference between another's nakedness or his own — understand this); and see what Magen Avraham wrote letter 5 on this. And his eyes seeing another's nakedness — nevertheless it applies, and his heart is rabbinic. And there is a difference: for his own nakedness, according to siman 75 squinting does not help — turning away the face cannot be conceived, Taz letter 2 there; but for another's nakedness, all agree turning away the face helps. And his heart seeing another's nakedness is also forbidden. We find an answer: his heart seeing another's nakedness is more severe — for his own nakedness, stopping with his arms helps; but for another, in the end his heart sees; and covering with the hand on the heart — even for another's nakedness it does not help, for one body does not cover a body, as we want to say below with God's help.

And if the shirt is very thin and the nakedness is visible from there — its law is like the law of water; Magen Avraham 75 regarding a woman in such a case; and for his eyes it does not help, like a lantern — and with God's help it will be explained further.
