Rabbeinu Yonah wrote in siman 76 in the name of the Rosh, chapter HaRo'eh — Yerushalmi: R. Yirmiyah asked: If one was sitting inside a wooden tower, what if he makes it like a garment, puts his head out through a window, and recites [Shema]? He was uncertain whether, if his heart sees the nakedness, it is forbidden or permitted; and above we ruled that it is forbidden, until here the Rosh. It is wondrous in my eyes regarding the Me'or HaGadol how he explained thus — for if so, why was the question needed regarding a tower, which is an uncommon term? He should have asked when wearing a talit or a robe! And it is simple that the Gemara in Sukkah 10b was omitted: they say, if one was sleeping in a canopy, he may put his head out beyond the canopy and recite; and if it was ten handbreadths high it is forbidden. Rashi explained: a tent is not considered an interposition, like one standing in a house who puts his head out through a window — we follow the head after the majority of the body; and if it is not ten handbreadths high, it is as one who puts his head out beyond his cloak, until here. If so, it is clear that the Yerushalmi's question is whether a wooden tower is considered a tent or should be like a vessel — and like a cloak or talit. And this is Rabbeinu Yeruham's language: If one was sitting inside a wooden tower and made it like a garment, puts his head out through a window and recites — if his heart sees the nakedness it is forbidden — thus in the Yerushalmi, until here. Behold, the question is resolved leniently; but it is asked why the codifiers omitted the case of Sukkah. Therefore I hold that the Yerushalmi and the Gemara in Sukkah hold that if one's heart sees the nakedness it is permitted; but according to us, who hold it forbidden — if so, necessarily, even if you say the tent is considered an interposition, nevertheless one's heart sees the nakedness. And if he also puts his heart out through the window — then his head and majority are outside and it is as if he is entirely outside. It is possible that Rabbeinu Yeruham establishes it that his heart is covered with a garment — then his heart does not see nakedness; but in order that there be an interposition between his head and the nakedness, we require that he put his head out through the window; but in a house such as this it is not considered an interposition. And I hold that the same applies if there is feces in the house and he put his head out through a window — it is forbidden to recite, for we follow the head after the majority; and if he brought his head into a room where there is feces, it is also forbidden to recite, for in any case the feces is before him. The nakedness of one's fellow: he explains that there is no interposition between one's heart and one's fellow's nakedness.
