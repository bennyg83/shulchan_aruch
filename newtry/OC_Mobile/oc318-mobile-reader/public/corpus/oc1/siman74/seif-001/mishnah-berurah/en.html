(1) Was sleeping naked — the author adopted the Gemara's wording [Berachos 23b], for there that expression applies; but in our context the same applies when he was sleeping in his shirt without trousers — the law is likewise that it is forbidden in words of Torah or to recite any blessing until he presses his shirt with his hands at the place of the heart or below the heart, so that there be an interposition between his heart and the nakedness.

(2) Must press — meaning, he must press the talit against his heart so that there be an interposition between his heart and the nakedness; but without this it is forbidden, for although his heart is covered by the talit that is over his whole body, nevertheless his heart and nakedness are still together and there is nothing between them.

(3) On his heart — and the same applies if he interposes with his talit below his heart so that there be an interposition between his heart and the nakedness, even if his heart is entirely exposed, as below in section 6.

(4) Because one's heart, etc. — for nakedness depends on sight, as it is written (there shall not be seen in you any nakedness of a thing), etc.; and we find the language of sight regarding the heart, as it is written (and my heart has seen much wisdom) — therefore the Sages were stringent in this. And know that even if one's heart is covered with a garment to interpose between it and the nakedness, one's nakedness must also be covered; and if it is exposed, many Acharonim agreed that it is forbidden by Torah law to recite, even if covered with a talit over one's head and eyes so that one cannot see the nakedness — for it is not said (you shall not see nakedness of a thing), but (there shall not be seen in you) — meaning that it shall be covered in a manner that it cannot be shown even to others. Therefore one standing naked in a house who put his head out through a window so that his nakedness not be seen — even though he interposed with a garment on his heart — he may not recite, since his nakedness is exposed and visible in the house. But one sleeping naked in a canopy that is not ten handbreadths high who put his head outside it and also interposed with something between his heart and nakedness — even though his nakedness is exposed in the canopy, this is as covered in a talit; for since the canopy is not ten handbreadths high, there is covering upon it like a garment, and it is not like a house, which is a separate domain for itself, and we cannot call it covering there — therefore it is forbidden. And likewise if there is feces in the house within four cubits and he put his head out through a window, it is forbidden to recite Shema, for we follow the head after the majority [Magen Avraham; and Ralbag that it is forbidden even according to those who permit below in siman 76, section 4 — see there, and see Peri Megadim]. And if he brought his head into a room where there is feces, it is forbidden according to all to recite, for in any case the feces is before his eyes; and according to Rashba brought in siman 79, section 2, it is forbidden even if he stands outside opposite the opening, since the feces is opposite him — see there [Peri Megadim].

(5) Sees, etc. — see Biur Halacha: even after the fact, if he recited Shema and prayed, he repeats; and this is proven from Peri Megadim in siman 71, section 1, letter 5. It is further proven there that Shema returns with its blessings if he recited them while his heart saw the nakedness — for thus the Sages enacted. But regarding other blessings there is room to be uncertain after the fact whether he should repeat; and doubt regarding blessings is resolved leniently. And in Chayei Adam, general rule 3, law 33, and in Nishmas Adam, general rule 4, it implies that one may be lenient after the fact also regarding prayer in a matter where the prohibition is only rabbinic; and see Biur Halacha siman 185. And it is possible to adopt his view in this matter, and we combine the view of Rashi and his disciple R. Shimon brought in Tosafos [Berachos 25b].

(6) Sees a fellow's nakedness — meaning there is no interposition between his heart and a fellow's nakedness; such as if both lie naked and cover themselves with one talit and are distant from one another — meaning their flesh does not touch, only their faces are opposite one another — and he himself has an interposition between his heart and his nakedness; nevertheless it is forbidden because his heart sees a fellow's nakedness. Or such as if both stand in water and his heart does not see his own nakedness because it is covered with a garment from below up to his heart, but he sees a fellow's nakedness that is not covered — it is forbidden even though his eyes are outside the water, for in any case his heart sees.
