(1) If he was sleeping naked. The Mechaber adopts the wording of the Gemara in Berachos 23b, where that expression is appropriate. For our law, the same applies if he slept in a shirt without trousers. He may not speak words of Torah or recite any blessing until he presses the shirt with his hands at the heart, or below the heart, so that there is an interruption between his heart and the ervah.
(2) He must press. Meaning, he must press the tallis against his heart so that it forms an interruption between his heart and the ervah. Otherwise it is forbidden. Although his heart is covered by the tallis covering his whole body, his heart and ervah are still together with nothing separating them.
(3) Against his heart. The same applies if he forms an interruption with his tallis below his heart, so that there is a separation between his heart and the ervah, even if his heart is entirely exposed, as explained below in seif 6.
(4) Because his heart, etc. The Torah makes the prohibition of ervah dependent upon sight, as it is written, “No nakedness shall be seen among you,” and we find the language of sight applied to the heart, as it is written, “My heart saw much wisdom.” The Sages therefore ruled stringently in this matter. Know that even if his heart is covered with a garment separating it from the ervah, the ervah itself must also be covered. If it is exposed, many Acharonim agree that it is forbidden by Torah law to recite, even if the tallis also covers his head and eyes so that he cannot see the ervah. The verse does not say, “You shall not see nakedness,” but, “No nakedness shall be seen among you,” meaning that it must be covered so that it cannot be seen even by another person. Therefore, one who stands naked inside a house and puts his head outside the window so that he cannot see his ervah may not recite, even if a garment interrupts at his heart, because his ervah is exposed and visible inside the house. If, however, one sleeps naked beneath a canopy lower than ten handbreadths, puts his head outside it, and also places an interruption between his heart and the ervah, he is considered covered by a tallis even though his ervah is exposed within the canopy. Since the canopy is not ten handbreadths high, it has the status of a covering like clothing. It differs from a house, which is a separate domain and cannot be called a covering. Similarly, if excrement is in a house within four cubits and he puts his head outside the window, he may not recite Krias Shema, because we associate his head with the greater part of his body. The Magen Avraham means that it is forbidden even according to the authorities who permit the case discussed below in siman 76 seif 4. See there and Peri Megadim. If he puts his head into a room containing excrement, all agree that he may not recite, because the excrement is at least opposite his eyes. According to the Rashba cited in siman 79 seif 2, it is forbidden even if he stands outside opposite the doorway, since the excrement is before him. See there. This is from Peri Megadim.
(5) Sees the ervah, etc. See Derech HaChaim, who rules that even after the fact, if one recited Krias Shema or prayed in this condition, he must repeat it. This is also evident from Peri Megadim in siman 71, Eshel Avraham, letter 5. It is further evident there that if he recited Krias Shema and its blessings while his heart saw the ervah, he repeats Krias Shema together with its blessings, because the Sages instituted them in that manner. Regarding other blessings, however, it is uncertain whether he should repeat them after the fact, and a doubt concerning blessings is treated leniently. Chayei Adam, klal 3, law 33, and Nishmas Adam, klal 4, imply that after the fact one may be lenient even regarding prayer when the prohibition is only Rabbinic. See Biur Halachah to siman 185. It may be appropriate to follow his view here and combine it with the opinion of Rashi and his disciple Rabbi Shimon cited by Tosafos in Berachos 25b.
(6) Sees another person's ervah. For example, both are lying naked under one tallis and are far enough apart that their bodies do not touch, but they face one another. Although he has an interruption between his own heart and ervah, it is nevertheless forbidden because his heart sees the other person's ervah. Another example is where both stand in water. His heart does not see his own ervah because he is covered by a garment from below his heart, but he sees the other person's uncovered ervah. This is forbidden even though his eyes are above the water, because his heart nevertheless sees it.
