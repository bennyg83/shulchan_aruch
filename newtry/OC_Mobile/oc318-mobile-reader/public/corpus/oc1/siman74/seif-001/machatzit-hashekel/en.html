(א) Rabbeinu Yonah wrote etc. — it was uncertain to him etc. — these are the Rosh's words to explain the question of R' Yirmiyah; and the Rosh holds it is obvious that an ohel is considered an interruption — only that since he takes out only his head and the heart is still inside the tower, his heart still sees — if so. Therefore the Rosh concluded: and above we ruled forbidden — meaning in chapter Mi SheMeisu etc. the Rosh ruled that his heart seeing the ervah is forbidden; and thereby the question is resolved for us.

We place the head after the majority and it is as if entirely inside, and his eyes see the ervah.

And if it is not ten high etc. — outside the window. And this is specifically in a canopy that is not fixed; but a house, even if not ten high, since it is fixed it is forbidden, for we say in every case we place the head after the majority — as stated in the Gemara in Sukkah.

If the wooden tower is considered an ohel etc. — in our Talmud both sides are explained: if ten high it is not an interruption; if not ten high it is surely an interruption. And in the Yerushalmi it is uncertain, but not clear whether it deals with ten high and not high is obvious to him, or deals with not ten high and ten high is obvious to him, or the Yerushalmi is uncertain in every case between ten high and not high (and possibly a tower is more fixed than a canopy, as stated below). And this is Rabbeinu Yeruham's language: and if his heart sees the ervah — forbidden — so in the Yerushalmi; and therefore Rabbeinu Yeruham too explains the Yerushalmi as Magen Avraham wrote, and ruled the question leniently as Magen Avraham wrote; and if so, Rabbeinu Yeruham's words. But if Rabbeinu Yeruham explains the Yerushalmi like the Rosh, who is uncertain regarding his heart seeing the ervah — if so, why did he conclude "so in the Yerushalmi" if based on "his heart sees the ervah — forbidden" he took the question stringently? For this he would not need proof from the Yerushalmi's sufficiency, for there is a dispute of tannaim in our Talmud in chapter Mi SheMeisu; and if he intended the beginning where he wrote "stick his head outside the window" etc. — i.e. when his heart does not see the ervah, as Magen Avraham wrote afterward — behold this is not explained in the Yerushalmi for leniency; on the contrary, perhaps the Yerushalmi is uncertain in this, per Magen Avraham's view — truly this is the Yerushalmi's question; but perforce Rabbeinu Yeruham too explains the Yerushalmi as Magen Avraham wrote; and if so — and that Rabbeinu Yeruham needed to bring from the Yerushalmi and did not bring the sugya in Sukkah that Magen Avraham brought: it is obvious to our Talmud that it is permitted — one could say either because there is a latter wording in Sukkah that the law of a canopy for Shema was not stated at all, but rather an old sukkah sleeping inside a canopy in a sukkah — whether "sleeping" applies inside the sukkah, and the canopy is not considered interruption; and the latter wording is primary — if so, the law for Shema is not explained. Therefore he brought proof from the Yerushalmi; or possibly Rabbeinu Yeruham holds a wooden tower is more fixed than a canopy, and the Yerushalmi deals with not ten high and does not dispute our Talmud that even in a canopy less than ten there is interruption — nevertheless he was uncertain regarding a tower that is more fixed, that perhaps there is no interruption; therefore Rabbeinu Yeruham cited for abundance: even a wooden tower is interruption, and all the more a canopy. And the law of a tower is not explained in Sukkah; and that Rabbeinu Yeruham did not distinguish between less than ten and ten high — either because an ordinary tower is less than ten, or conversely Rabbeinu Yeruham permits in every case, since he holds the latter wording in Sukkah is primary — if so, the law of a canopy for Shema is not explained at all in our Talmud. Since the Yerushalmi is uncertain, an ordinary tower implies both ten high and not ten high, and he ruled the question leniently; and for that reason in every case there is interruption and it is permitted.

And if he also takes out his heart etc. — as if he is entirely outside; meaning: and it is forbidden in such a case.

And it is possible that R' Yirmiyah establishes it etc. — and the codifiers who omitted it seem to them forced to establish it thus — for otherwise our Talmud and Yerushalmi would need to explain that he covered his heart, or hold that his heart seeing the ervah is permitted. And likewise Tosafos explicitly in Sukkah: perforce that which permits sticking the head outside the canopy and reciting Shema — therefore he holds his heart seeing the ervah is permitted; and perforce it must be that it seemed forced to them to establish that he covered his heart. And since we hold his heart seeing the ervah is forbidden, therefore they omitted it; and although there would still be reason to cite if he covered his heart that the canopy is an interruption if he stuck his head out — this is not a difficulty on the codifiers to cite every legal innovation emerging from pilpul that is not explained explicitly in the Talmud; and likewise many times regarding Rambam — unlike Rabbeinu Yeruham, as Magen Avraham wrote, who establishes it that our Talmud and Yerushalmi deal thus, and therefore they cited it.

But in a house etc. — and this is even if it is not ten high, as stated.
