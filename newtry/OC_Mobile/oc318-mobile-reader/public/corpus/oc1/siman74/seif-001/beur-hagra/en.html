Seif 1: If one was, etc. This follows the first opinion, for the anonymous Gemara there, 25b, challenges: “But his heart sees, etc.” Furthermore, since Abaye and Rava disagree concerning whether his heel sees, etc., it follows that regarding his heart they both hold that it is prohibited. Although Shmuel in Sukkah 10b holds that it is permitted, the halachah follows Abaye and Rava. This is the Rosh's view. It appears to me that Rashi, who explains the passage according to the opinion that permits it, follows his own approach. There, s.v. “But his heel, etc.,” he explains: “The initial assumption was, etc.” Meaning, he was troubled that once the Gemara answers that he holds his heart, etc., why does it then challenge from his heel? Rashi therefore explains that one's heel is more severe than one's heart, since it is not its normal manner to see.
The same applies if his heart, etc. As stated there, 25b: “A naked gentile, etc.” The Gemara teaches us that with them, etc. All of these rulings derive from “there shall not be seen in you, etc.” It then asks: “Regarding a Jew, is this not obvious, etc.?”
