1) [Seif 1] If one was sleeping naked in his garment, he must press the garment against his heart, etc. This means that he should press the garment against his heart so that there will be a separation between his heart and the ervah. Without this, it is prohibited. Although his heart is covered by the garment that covers his entire body, his heart nevertheless sees the ervah and is directed opposite it. He must therefore cover his heart or interpose the garment beneath his heart so that there will be a separation between his heart and the ervah. Maamar Mordechai, ot 1.
2) There. He must press the garment against his heart, etc. If he covers his heart with a separate garment, that is sufficient even if he does not press it against his heart. Lechem Chamudot, chapter Mi SheMetu, ot 114; Eliyah Rabbah, ot 1.
3) One who is standing. If one is naked in a house and extends his head outside through a window so that he will not see his ervah, then even if he covers his heart with a garment, he may not recite, since his ervah is exposed and visible in the house. If, however, one sleeps naked in a canopy that is not ten handbreadths high, extends his head outside it, and also covers his heart with a garment, then although his ervah is exposed within the canopy, it is considered covered by a garment. Since the canopy is not ten handbreadths high, it has the status of a covering. It is unlike a house, which constitutes a separate domain; since the ervah is visible within the house, recitation is prohibited. Magen Avraham at the beginning of the siman and seif katan 8; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ot 2; Ben Ish Chai, parashat Bo, ot 1; Pithei Olam, ot 1. If he also extends his heart outside the window, his head and the majority of his body are outside, and it is as though his entire body were outside. The same applies if there is excrement in the house and he extends his head outside the window: he may not recite, because his head is assigned the status of the majority of his body. If he inserts his head into a room containing excrement, he likewise may not recite, since the excrement is in front of him. Magen Avraham there. Birkei Yosef, ot 2, agrees, unlike the opinion cited in Hagahot HaTur, who writes that this ruling is questionable; see there. Shaarei Teshuvah, ot 1.
4) If. If another person is naked with him in the house, while he is dressed and has extended his head outside the window, it appears that he may recite and recite blessings according to the view of Maran below, siman 75, seif 6. Birkei Yosef, ot 3. See what we wrote there, with the help of Heaven.
5) There. It is prohibited because his heart sees the ervah. There is no distinction between Torah study, an ordinary blessing, and Keriat Shema; in all of them they said that it is prohibited when his heart sees the ervah. Although we find an authority who is lenient in one of these cases, we do not rule that way. We rule that it is prohibited when his heart sees the ervah, and we maintain that there is no distinction between Keriat Shema and other sacred words. Machazik Berachah, ot 1, who writes that this is also implied by the halakhic authorities and by the words of Maran below, seif 4; see there. Zechor LeAvraham, volume 1, ot 100; Shaarei Teshuvah there. Ben Ish Chai there rules likewise. See further below, ot 7.
6) There, gloss. The same applies if his heart sees another person's ervah: it is prohibited. So write the Tur and Beit Yosef. Bach writes that this applies, for example, when two people are standing in water and the heart of one does not see his own ervah because it is covered by a garment, but it does see the uncovered ervah of the other; he may not recite. This is so even though his eyes are above the water, he is looking outward, and his eyes do not see the ervah. Eliyah Rabbah, ot 2, cites his words. Magen Avraham, seif katan 1, explains that the case is one in which there is no separation between his heart and the other person's ervah. Peri Megadim, Eshel Avraham, ot 1, writes that Magen Avraham means that they are sleeping beneath one garment, apart from one another and not touching, while his heart sees the other person's ervah, his own ervah is covered by a garment, and his head is outside the robe; meaning outside the garment. This analysis results from their following their respective views in siman 75, seif 6, and Magen Avraham, seif katan 9. According to the author there, however, closing one's eyes is sufficient and these scenarios are unnecessary; by remaining silent there, Rema conceded to him. Peri Megadim there.
