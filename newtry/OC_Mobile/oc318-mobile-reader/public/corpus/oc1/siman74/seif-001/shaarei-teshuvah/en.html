His heart — Be'er Heitev. And Magen Avraham abbreviated and did not copy much of what he wrote on the law "he was sitting inside a wooden tower," etc.; and see Yad Ephraim explaining Magen Avraham's words; and what Shulchan Aruch wrote to put outside the tallit on his heart — see Yad Ephraim; and see in Hagahot Maimoni who wrote that one must cover opposite his heart, etc.; and see Eliyah Rabbah in the name of Levush Yom Tov; and see Mishna Berurah who wrote in the name of an old booklet that one may engage in Torah and bless a blessing even though his heart sees the nakedness — and Mishna Berurah disputes him, for there is no difference between sanctity in Shema and other sacred matters; and see Pri Chadash and Beit Yosef siman 206; and see below regarding women in the blessing of immersion; and see there in Mishna Berurah who expanded on the law of Yerushalmi and Magen Avraham, and there brought Meiri's words who holds that when taking his head outside the tallit he need not put outside opposite his heart. And likewise one who wears a wide tallit and is not girded with a belt — permitted; and the great ones of the generation taught that even this requires pressing on the heart. And likewise one who wears a wide tallit must gird a belt to tighten it so his heart not see the nakedness, etc.; and see there that Rosh's view is like the custom of the groves for prohibition — and so we rule. And what Be'er Heitev wrote that in Yad Aharon he wavers on Magen Avraham's law; and in Be'er Heitev he agreed to Magen Avraham that feces in the house at his side and he put his head out — we do not call it "your camp shall be holy," for his head we judge after the majority, as Rashi wrote in Sukkah, etc. — see there.
