In Magen Avraham at the beginning of this siman: Yerushalmi, chapter HaRo'eh, wrote — but it is difficult why the poskim omitted that [braisa] of Sukkah, etc. It appears to explain his words that from Rosh it implies a simple matter: certainly the wooden tower is like clothing, and this is not like standing naked, forbidden to bless even though he does not see the nakedness at all; rather the doubt is whether here there is prohibition because his eyes and heart see the nakedness and it stops him — if taking out the head alone suffices, for then his eyes do not see the nakedness; but what his heart sees the nakedness — we do not care, or perhaps there is prohibition because his heart sees the nakedness; and on this Rosh wrote above: we ruled forbidden. If so, it is simple there is no difficulty on the poskim who did not bring Yerushalmi — since they say his heart sees the nakedness is forbidden, they no longer need to bring Yerushalmi, for certainly regarding not being considered naked the law is simple that the tower is a kind of clothing. And on this he wondered: if the main question is whether his heart sees the nakedness, it should be the same regarding shirt and tallit. Therefore he holds: on the contrary, the main question is whether it is considered a kind of clothing and is a stop, or not — as he divides in Sukkah between a tent and other things; but regarding his heart seeing it is not discussed at all, and it depends on the dispute: per the view that permits, taking out his head suffices; per the view that forbids, he must take out his heart too; and on this R' Yeruham brought, resolving the question leniently that it is well like clothing. And what R' Yeruham wrote "and if his heart sees the nakedness — forbidden" means: according to what he ruled forbidden, he must not have his heart inside either; and nevertheless one need not challenge the other poskim who did not bring Yerushalmi, for they hold the question stringently — the tower is certainly like a tent and not considered a stop; and since it is stringent, the default is like his explanation that whoever does not wear clothing is forbidden — what difference tent or tower? But this itself is difficult: why did they omit that of Sukkah, to inform us that a tent is not considered a stop and is not like clothing? For this he said the poskim hold Gemara and Yerushalmi like the one who permits; but per the view that forbids you will not find it, for even if he takes out his heart too it would be as if he is entirely outside — therefore they did not need to explain; and therefore one must say R' Yeruham who wrote this question disputes the poskim and holds that if he takes out his heart too it is fine and we do not judge him after the majority; and therefore he wrote initially "his head comes out" — meaning for Yerushalmi taking out the head suffices; "and if his heart," etc. — then forbidden until his heart too is outside; and in any case with this it suffices and he is not considered standing naked outside. From this he says it is possible Rabbeinu Yeruham explains it that his heart is covered — meaning he too admits to this reasoning that we judge after the majority, but holds it discusses a case where there is not, etc.; and in such a case we rule the tower is like clothing and not like standing naked; and since he takes his head outside and his eyes do not see the nakedness — permitted to recite; but a house is not a stop and is like standing naked, and what his head is outside to his cloak does not help, for certainly we judge him after the majority. A short path as explained above: before us are two paths. Path one: even though his eyes and heart do not see the nakedness, nevertheless if he is in exposed nakedness it is forbidden — such as in a tent that is not considered a stop, and it does not help that he does not see — nevertheless he recites in exposed nakedness. Path two: even though there is a stop and it is not called exposed nakedness, nevertheless if his heart sees the nakedness it is forbidden. And initially Magen Avraham thought the Sukkah braisa too holds his heart seeing is forbidden; and what permits in a kilah is that he takes out his heart too — per the view that forbids; and what teaches "he puts out his head" — because the dispute is not discussed in the braisa; and so too for the one who forbids he must take out his heart; and if it were ten [tefachim] high it would be forbidden — for a tent is not considered a stop; therefore whether taking out his head or his heart — forbidden because it is like reciting in exposed nakedness; therefore it is difficult why the poskim omitted this law; and therefore Magen Avraham wrote that truly the braisa holds it is permitted; and therefore properly he informs us regarding height ten that it is a tent: even when taking out his head, which is considered independently — we do not judge him after the majority; if so it is as if his head were elsewhere and he does not recite in exposed nakedness — he informs us. Unlike the poskim who hold forbidden — then there is no novelty except if he takes out his heart too; and in such a case there is no expansion at all; and it is simple we judge the minority after the majority and it is as if he is entirely outside, and there is [prohibition] because of exposed nakedness. And regarding this matter it is simple a tent does not stop, for we do not cast the minority after the majority — specifically regarding the head, which is considered independently; and it would be an error [to think] the braisa's words that even in a tent we do not judge after the majority — and he needed to inform us that not so; unlike if his head and majority are outside — there is no expansion at all, for it is simple the minority is dragged after the majority. And R' Yeruham who wrote the question leniently — and apparently you will not find it at all; it is possible to establish it when his heart is covered in clothing, etc.; and therefore one must answer why the poskim did not write the braisa — for there is relevance: that a tent does not stop, such as when his heart is covered, because they hold it is simple one cannot say a tent is also clothing; and if so, wherever nakedness is exposed it is simple even if he does not see it is forbidden, as Magen Avraham wrote to distinguish where he is covered in water in any case; and that braisa informs only if the kilah is not ten — even though his heart sees, permitted; and when there are ten upon it, tent law — then it is simple there is no relevance to what he puts outside the window; and this law of kilah ten having tent law is already explained in the laws of Shabbos. Unlike in my view who holds the braisa too is like the one who forbids — then necessarily in the beginning it discusses a case where there is no [issue] because his heart sees; and if so the main expansion of the braisa regarding height ten is not because taking out his head — for a tent does not stop; and if so the poskim should have informed us this law too; but necessarily they hold the braisa is like the one who permits; and that the poskim do not establish the braisa when covered in clothing and inform us that kilah ten is worse than a wooden tower — for although in a tower in such a case permitted when taking out his head, nevertheless in a kilah forbidden, for only the tower becomes a kind of clothing. And nevertheless Magen Avraham's words are difficult in my view in what he wrote that R' Yeruham ruled the question leniently — for on the contrary R' Yeruham follows Rosh's method, that this is clear to Yerushalmi that the tower is clothing for this matter, permitted to bless when taking out his head, and there is no prohibition because of exposed nakedness; and the main question is regarding his heart; and therefore when his heart does not see — permitted when covered — R' Shimon was sitting and making like clothing, etc.; "and if his heart sees," etc. And it is possible Magen Avraham infers from his writing thus in Yerushalmi; and in my view, on the contrary, according to Magen Avraham it should be difficult — for truly in Yerushalmi it is doubtful to him, and how did he write thus plainly? But according to what some say, in any case he learned from Yerushalmi like clothing; and what Yerushalmi wrote "what if he makes," etc. — that "what" refers to the end of the matter, where he concludes "and he will bless" — meaning if he is permitted to bless because his heart sees, etc.
