(5) Pressed to the ground etc. — for when she sits, then her face below is covered by the ground; but in any case her buttocks are exposed — unlike when her face is pressed to the ground, when the buttocks are also not visible at all. And it seems that even pressed to the ground is not required except to be fixed and stuck in the ground in a way that covers the folds between the buttocks; also from behind, even if not covered on the sides — for the sides of the buttocks are included in the thighs and not in the buttocks — so I found in Perishah siman 206. And I saw in Magen Avraham there that he extended to challenge the essence of this law of pressed to the ground, since the Rif and Rosh did not cite it — see there; and I wonder that they cited it in the laws of challah, and likewise the Tur ruled Yoreh De'ah siman 328 that we require pressed to the ground. And the reason seems to me because in Berachos folio 24 it says "she translated: her face is pressed" etc., and the term "she translated" implies it is halachah. Also what he cited from Bertinoro chapter 2 of Challah is wondrous — for he himself wrote there that we require a pressed face; and truly one could question Bertinoro: since he wrote that buttocks have no concern of ervah, why require a pressed face? And one who requires a pressed face holds that buttocks have concern of ervah. And I have no doubt that the words of [the Rosh] [Rash] that the Yerushalmi cited — that buttocks have no concern of ervah — did not look at Bertinoro; for the truth is they did not write [Rosh] [Rash] except because of the latter clause: "this you say for a blessing, but to look" etc.; but for halachah we hold that buttocks have concern of ervah, in our Talmud, which establishes it with a pressed face.
