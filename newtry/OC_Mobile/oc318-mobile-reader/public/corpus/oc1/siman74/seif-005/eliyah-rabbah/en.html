[5] Pressed against the ground, etc. When she sits, the front of her lower body is covered by the ground, although her buttocks remain exposed. This excludes a case where her front is pressed against the ground, when nothing of the buttocks is visible either. It appears that being pressed against the ground need only mean that she is firmly positioned in the ground so that the folds between the buttocks are covered, including from behind, even though the sides are not covered. The sides of the buttocks are included with the thighs and are not themselves considered the buttocks. I found this in the Derishah to siman 206. I saw that Magen Avraham there extensively challenges the basic ruling requiring her to be pressed against the ground, because Rif and Rosh did not cite it. This surprises me, because they did cite it in the laws of challah, and Tur in Yoreh Deah siman 328 likewise rules that she must be pressed against the ground. The reason appears to be that Berachos 24 says, “Explain it as referring to one whose front is pressed against the ground,” and the expression “explain it” indicates that this is the halachah. His proof from the Bartenura in the second chapter of Challah is also surprising, for Bartenura himself writes there that her front must be pressed against the ground. Indeed, Bartenura's words are difficult. Since he writes that the buttocks are not considered ervah, why must her front be pressed against the ground? The opinion requiring this clearly holds that the buttocks are considered ervah. I have no doubt that Bartenura did not carefully examine the words of the Rash that he cites from the Yerushalmi, that the buttocks are not considered ervah. The Rash wrote that only because of the concluding distinction, that this applies to a blessing but not to gazing. For practical halachah, however, we rule that the buttocks are considered ervah, in accordance with our Gemara, which establishes the case as one where her front is pressed against the ground.
