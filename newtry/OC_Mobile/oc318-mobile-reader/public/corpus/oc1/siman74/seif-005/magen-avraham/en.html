Whether with another person's ervah. The Beit Yosef writes that this is learned all the more so from the law of two people sleeping under one tallis, where it is prohibited even when their bodies touch one another, as written at the beginning of siman 73. His words end here. This is difficult, for if so, why was it necessary to state this ruling? It therefore appears to me that there the prohibition applies specifically when the two touch one another from their waists downward. If, however, one person's other limbs touch the other's waist, it is permitted. It was therefore necessary to state that if his limbs touch another person's ervah, it is prohibited, unlike the Beit Yosef's explanation. The Hagahos Ashri writes that with one's young children it is permitted to recite even if their ervah touches one another. It is astonishing to permit such a thing. The Beit Yosef writes that there is some basis to infer a prohibition from Rabbeinu Yonah's statement concerning a naked gentile. The Darchei Moshe writes that one who examines Rabbeinu Yonah carefully will see stronger proof to permit than to prohibit. In my opinion, it should be prohibited, just as seeing a minor's ervah is prohibited. See siman 75, seif 4.
