Whether regarding one's own nakedness or a fellow's. Rabbeinu Yonah wrote: this is learned from what is written from the law of two sleeping in one talit — that even if their flesh touches one another it is forbidden, as written in section 73, until here. It is difficult: if so, why was this law needed to be written? Therefore I hold that there, specifically when both touch one another from their loins downward; but if the rest of his limbs touch their loins it is permitted — therefore it was necessary to write: when his limbs touch a fellow's nakedness it is forbidden — unlike Beit Yosef. Eshel Avraham wrote regarding his small sons: even if their nakedness touches one another, it is permitted to recite; and it is astonishing to permit this. And there is somewhat to be stringent to forbid from what Terumat Yisrael wrote regarding a naked non-Jew, until here Beit Yosef. And it is written in Darkei Moshe: one who examines Terumat Yisrael's words will see there is more evidence to permit than to forbid, until here. And I hold to forbid, as in the case of seeing a minor's nakedness — engaged in section 75, section 4.
