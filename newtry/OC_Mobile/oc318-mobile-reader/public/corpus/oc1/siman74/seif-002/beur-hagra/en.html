Seif 2: Specifically, etc. This follows from the foregoing, and likewise from the Gemara there. The phrase “he should place it upon his heart” implies specifically with a garment, but not with his hands. This is the meaning of “the same applies, etc.” This ruling is learned from there. See Magen Avraham.
And if it is not, etc. The statement there that he should sit in the water up to his neck would not need to specify “up to his neck” except because of the first opinion. According to that opinion, specifically in such a case he muddies the water with his feet. This is the Gemara's challenge there: “But his heel, etc.” Meaning, even without that concern it would be prohibited according to the first opinion because of his heel. As for the Gemara's challenge to the Mishnah, “Let him cover, etc., but surely, etc.,” after answering that it follows the practice of the early pious men, it challenges because he must necessarily also pray there. The reason for their practice was to juxtapose redemption to prayer, and regarding prayer the Gemara states on the first side of the page that one must cover his heart.
