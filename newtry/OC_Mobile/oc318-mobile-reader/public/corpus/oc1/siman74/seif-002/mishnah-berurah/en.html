(7) He should cover with a garment, etc. Although the water is clear, its covering is effective so that he is not prohibited because of exposed ervah, provided that he puts his head outside the water and does not look at the ervah, since it can be seen through the clear water. A prohibition nevertheless remains because his heart sees the ervah, as his heart and ervah are together in the water. He must therefore cover himself with a garment below his heart so that there is an interruption between his heart and the ervah. The same applies if he places the garment against his heart, as explained above in seif 1.
(8) When he recites the blessing. The same applies to covering his head, so that he should not recite the blessing with an uncovered head.
(9) But with his hands, etc. This applies whether he covers his heart itself or the area between his heart and ervah, unless he embraces himself with his arms, as explained below. The reason is that one part of the body does not cover another part. This applies specifically to his own hands. If other people cover his heart with their hands, it is permitted.
(10) It is not considered, etc. Meaning, it is not considered a covering permitting him to recite a blessing or utter other sacred words. See above in siman 2 and below in siman 91 in Mishnah Berurah.
(11) Murky. If the water is clear, he should make it murky with his feet. This applies only when he stands on earth, but not in a vessel containing no soil or mud, as the Magen Avraham writes. Or Zarua, siman 128, writes, however, that this can apply even when one washes himself in a tub.
(12) Its odor is foul. See below, siman 86, and Mishnah Berurah there.
(13) Only above, etc. The water is considered an interruption between his heart and the ervah. He must also ensure that his eyes are outside the water and that he does not look at the ervah, as explained above. It is no better than ervah behind glass, discussed in siman 75 seif 5, which is forbidden.
