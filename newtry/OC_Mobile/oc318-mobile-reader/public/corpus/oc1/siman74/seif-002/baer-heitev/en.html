The nakedness. Although the water is clear, it nevertheless helps so that there is no prohibition arising from exposed nakedness. There still remains a prohibition because his heart sees the nakedness, since both his heart and his nakedness are in the water. Therefore, a garment specifically must cover his heart. His head, however, is permitted when it is above the water, as Magen Avraham writes.
A covering. This applies specifically when he covers his heart with his own hands. If others cover his heart with their hands, it is permitted. Peri Chadash.
With his hands. See above, siman 2, seif katan 6, and what is written there.
Cloudy. If the water is clear, he should make it cloudy with his feet. This applies specifically in a spring, but not in a vessel that contains no earth or mud. Magen Avraham.
It is permitted. This is because his eyes are outside the water and he is not looking at the nakedness. Rashba and Magen Avraham. It follows that if he looks at the nakedness, it is forbidden. Accordingly, we must say that the Sages were more stringent regarding what the eyes see than regarding what the heart sees. Thus, even if his eyes are above the water, he may not recite if he is looking at the nakedness. By contrast, if his heart is above the water, then even though the water is clear and his heart sees the nakedness, he may recite because his heart is above the water and the water separates between them. Olas Tamid.
