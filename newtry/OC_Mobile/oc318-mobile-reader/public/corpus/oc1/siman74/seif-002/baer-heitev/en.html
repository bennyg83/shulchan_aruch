The nakedness. He explains that water, even when clear, at any rate helps regarding that one should not be forbidden on account of revealed nakedness—but there is still a prohibition because his heart sees the nakedness, since the heart is with the nakedness in the water; therefore one specifically needs a garment covering on his heart. But his head, since it sees above the water, is permitted, as Magen Avraham wrote.

Covering. Specifically with his own hands; but if others cover his heart with their hands, it is permitted. Pri Chadash.

With his hands. See below siman 2, note 6, in the name of Shach.

Muddy. And if they are clear he muddies them with his feet—and this applies specifically in a spring, but not in a vessel that has no dust and mud. Magen Avraham.

Permitted. Since his eyes are outside the water and he does not gaze at the nakedness. Rashba—Magen Avraham implies that if he gazes at the nakedness it is forbidden. Therefore it must be said that they were stricter regarding what the eyes see than what the heart sees: for the eyes, even when they are above the water, if he gazes at the nakedness it is forbidden to recite; but for the heart, if it is above the water, even though the water is clear and the heart sees the nakedness—since it is above the water and the water interrupts—it is permitted to recite. See Tosafos.
