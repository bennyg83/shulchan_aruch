[6] Their hearts do not see, etc. Nachalas Tzvi and Olas Tamid extensively question the Rama and Levush. They had already written that even though the garments do not interrupt, their hearts do not see their ervah, which indicates that a woman's heart does not see her ervah at all even when she is standing. This is the substance of their objection. I wrote in Eliyah Zuta that they overlooked the words of Lechem Chamudos, page 26, who explains that the Rama and Levush hold that since a woman's ervah is positioned very low, wearing a shirt is considered an interruption. It appears to me that the concept of a woman's heart seeing ervah does apply, but because her ervah is low, wearing a shirt is considered an interruption. The Taz and Magen Avraham were subsequently printed, and I saw that they emend the Shulchan Aruch to read, “then their ervah is not visible,” and maintain that even when a woman is naked her heart does not see her ervah at all. Nevertheless, her ervah is visible, and she must therefore press herself against the ground. This reading is absent from every edition of Shulchan Aruch and from the Levush. Moreover, the Rama in Yoreh Deah siman 200 writes that she must cover herself with her garment. Why should this be necessary if her heart does not see the ervah? Exposure of ervah is not prohibited in water, as explained above in seif 2. The primary explanation is therefore as I wrote. The Taz's concluding statement that she should embrace herself or keep her heart outside the water also contradicts his own position. One must say that he intends to be stringent initially, as he writes in Taz to Yoreh Deah. I discussed this at length in Eliyah Rabbah there and resolved all the questions raised by Taz and Shach concerning the apparent contradiction between the Shulchan Aruch here and there. See there and examine it carefully. I cited part of this in Eliyah Zuta. I also explained the basis of the women's custom to recite the blessing over immersion while in the water. Generally, the mikveh is not deep enough for their hearts to be within the water, and this is permitted as explained in seif 2. If the heart is also within the water, however, it appears that she must cover herself or embrace herself with her arms.
