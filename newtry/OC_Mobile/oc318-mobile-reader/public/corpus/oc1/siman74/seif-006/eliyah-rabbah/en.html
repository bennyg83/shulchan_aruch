(6) There is no "his heart sees" etc. — Nachalas Tzvi and Olas Tamid extended to wonder at Rama and Levush, for they already wrote: even though they do not interrupt etc., and "there is no his heart sees" etc. — behold her heart does not see her nakedness at all even when standing, until here their words briefly. And I wrote in Eliyahu Zuta that the words of Lechem Chamudos folio 26 were omitted — that Rama and Levush hold: since their nakedness is very low, therefore wearing the shirt is considered an interruption, until here. And it seems to me that in women too "his heart sees ervah" applies — only that since their nakedness is low, wearing the shirt is considered an interruption. Again the books of Taz and Magen Avraham were printed, and I saw that they emend in Shulchan Aruch and one must say: then their nakedness is not visible etc., and it seems to them that truly even when naked her heart does not see ervah at all — only that in any case her nakedness is visible, therefore she needs pressed to the ground, until here. And in every Shulchan Aruch book it is not so, and likewise in Levush. And further, Rama in Yoreh De'ah siman 200 wrote that she should cover with her garment — why? Behold her heart does not see ervah; and because of uncovering ervah there is none in water, as above in section 2 — but the essence is as I wrote. Also the Taz's words concluding that she should embrace or her heart be outside the water contradict themselves; and one must say that ab initio his intent was to be stringent, as he wrote in Taz to Yoreh De'ah; and in this I extended in Eliyahu Rabbah there and settled all the difficulties Taz and Shach challenged there — that Shulchan Aruch's words here and there contradict; see there and understand. And I brought part of it in Eliyahu Zuta — see there. And I also raised the reason for women's custom to bless on immersion in water — because mostly the mikveh is not so deep that the heart is in water; and thereby it is permitted as explained in section 2; but if the heart too is visible in the water, she must cover or embrace with her arms.
