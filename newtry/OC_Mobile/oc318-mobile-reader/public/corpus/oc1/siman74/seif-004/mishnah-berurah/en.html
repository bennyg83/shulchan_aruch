(15) They may recite blessings, etc. The reason is that their ervah is positioned very low and their heart cannot see it. Nevertheless, if they stand naked, another prohibition applies, namely exposed ervah. This is not because of the heart, but because it is written, “No nakedness shall be seen among you.” A woman may therefore not recite a blessing unless she sits, as the Rama writes, because by sitting her lower front is covered and her ervah is not visible. The Rama's words, “then their hearts do not see their ervah,” are not precise, because their hearts do not see it in any case. He means that their ervah is then not visible. The text should read, “then their ervah is not visible.” This is the conclusion of the Acharonim.
(16) From the heart. The Bach disagrees and maintains that a woman also requires an interruption between her heart and ervah even when she is clothed. The other Acharonim, however, agree with the Shulchan Aruch. See Yoreh Deah siman 200, where Taz and Shach conclude that according to the Shulchan Aruch here, which holds that the law of one's heart seeing ervah does not apply to a woman, she may recite the blessing over immersion while standing in the water even though she is still naked. There is no prohibition of exposed ervah, because the water is considered a covering. It is better for her heart to be above the water, because this is permitted even for a man, as the Rama writes in the gloss to seif 2, or for her to embrace herself with her arms to interrupt between her heart and ervah. Chochmas Adam rules likewise. The same applies if she makes the water murky with her feet so that her limbs cannot be seen through it, which is effective according to all opinions. If other naked women are standing in the immersion room, she plainly must turn her face and body away from them while reciting the blessing. Even if we say that the mikveh area is a separate domain, this does not help, as is evident below in siman 79 seif 2. See there.
(17) Pressed. Meaning, attached to and covered by the ground. This differs from a man, whose testicles and organ protrude. See Magen Avraham below in siman 206, who holds that even if the buttocks are visible there is no concern, because buttocks are not considered ervah. This is also somewhat implied by Biur HaGra. Eliyah Rabbah, however, rules that buttocks are considered ervah.
