(15) May bless, etc. — the reason is that their nakedness is very low and a woman's heart cannot see it. Nevertheless, if they stand naked there is another prohibition — namely, prohibition of exposure of nakedness, not from the aspect of the heart but because it is said (there shall not be seen in you nakedness of a thing), and she is forbidden to bless unless she sits — as Rama wrote on this: through sitting her lower face is covered and her nakedness is not visible. And what Rama wrote, (then one's heart does not see their nakedness) — he did not mean literally the heart, for the heart in any case does not see their nakedness; rather he spoke so that there not be complete exposure of nakedness. And it is a textual error; it should read: and then their nakedness is not visible [Acharonim].

(16) From the heart — and Bach disputes and holds that also for a woman we require an interposition between heart and nakedness, even when she has a garment; but the rest of the Acharonim agreed to the Shulchan Aruch's ruling. And see Yoreh De'ah siman 200 — examine Taz and Shach there: according to what the Shulchan Aruch ruled here, that the concept of one's heart seeing nakedness does not apply to a woman, she may recite the blessing of immersion while standing in the water even though she is still naked — for there is no prohibition of exposure here, since water is considered like covering. And it is better that her heart be above the water, for this is permitted even for a man, as Rama wrote in the gloss in section 62; or that she embrace her arms to interpose between her heart and nakedness — and so ruled Chacham Tzvi. And the same if she muddies with her feet so that her limbs not be visible in them — this helps according to all. And if other naked women stand in the mikveh house, it is obvious that at the time of the blessing she must turn her face and body away from them; and even if we say the place of the mikveh is a separate domain for itself, it does not help, as proven below in siman 79, section 2 — see there.

(17) Pressed — meaning, attached and covered in the ground; unlike a man, for this does not apply, because the testicles and member protrude. And see Magen Avraham below, siman 76: his view is that even if the thighs are visible one need not be particular, for thighs have no aspect of nakedness; and so it seems somewhat from Gra's Biur. And in Aruch HaShulchan he ruled that thighs have an aspect of nakedness.
