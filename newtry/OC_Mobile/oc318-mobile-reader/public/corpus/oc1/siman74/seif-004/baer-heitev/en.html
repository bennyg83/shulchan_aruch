Below the heart. The reason is that their nakedness is below and her heart cannot see it; nevertheless, if they stand naked there is another prohibition—namely the prohibition of revealed nakedness, not on account of the heart but because it is said "that He not see in you anything indecent." Therefore if they stand naked she must sit, as Rama wrote there. However, that which Rama wrote "then one's heart does not see their nakedness" is not precise regarding "one's heart," for one's heart in any case does not see their nakedness; rather he speaks of not being in complete exposure of nakedness. And it is a printing error—it should read: "then their nakedness is not seen." Taz, Magen Avraham, see Tosafos, and the Bach disagree and hold that for a woman too we require an interruption between heart and nakedness even when she has garments; and Taz disagrees with him and ruled to permit as Shulchan Aruch wrote there. And see Yoreh De'ah siman 200. (And see in Eliyah Rabbah, who upholds the version in Shulchan Aruch "then one's heart does not see..." that for women too "one's heart" applies to seeing their nakedness—only that since her hair is below, wearing a robe is considered an interruption; and he raised in his book to Yoreh De'ah a reason for the women's custom to bless on immersion in water, because for most women the heart is outside the water; and indeed if the heart is in the water she must cover or clasp with her arms—see there.)
