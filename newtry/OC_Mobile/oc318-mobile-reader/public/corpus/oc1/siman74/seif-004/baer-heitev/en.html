From the heart. The reason is that a woman's nakedness is lower down and her heart cannot see it. Nevertheless, if women are standing naked, there is another prohibition, namely the prohibition of exposed nakedness. This prohibition is not because the heart sees it, but because the verse states, 'No naked matter shall be seen among you.' Therefore, when they are naked they must sit, as the Rama writes. However, the Rama's statement that then their hearts do not see their nakedness is not precise, because in any event their hearts do not see it. Rather, he means that their nakedness should not be entirely exposed. The text contains a scribal error and should read, 'Then their nakedness is not visible.' This is the view of Taz, Magen Avraham, and Olas Tamid. The Bach disagrees and maintains that a woman too requires a separation between her heart and her nakedness even when she is clothed. Taz disputes him and rules leniently, as stated by the Shulchan Aruch. See there, and see Yoreh De'ah siman 200. See Eliyah Rabbah, who upholds the reading in the Shulchan Aruch, 'Then their hearts do not see,' because the concept of a woman's heart seeing her nakedness also applies. Since her nakedness is lower down, however, wearing her shift is considered a separation. In his work on Yoreh De'ah he gives a reason for the women's practice of reciting the blessing on immersion while in the water, namely that in most cases their hearts are outside the water. If the heart is in the water, however, she must cover it or embrace herself with her arms. See there.
