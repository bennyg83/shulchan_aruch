Even though they do not interrupt below, etc. The reason is that a woman's ervah is positioned low and her heart cannot see it. Nevertheless, if women stand naked, another prohibition applies, namely exposed ervah. This is not because of the heart, but because the verse states, “No nakedness shall be seen among you.” We similarly find regarding a sotah that her front and back must be covered because her entire body is considered ervah. Rashi explains that her private area can be seen from both the front and the back. She must therefore sit and press herself against the ground, as the Rama writes. The Rama's concluding phrase, “so that their hearts do not see the ervah,” is not precise. Their hearts do not see their ervah in any case. Rather, he means that they should not remain entirely exposed. My father-in-law, the Bach, writes that this ruling is incorrect. He likewise writes in Yoreh Deah that a woman also requires an interruption between her heart and ervah even when she wears a garment, and argues that the Shulchan Aruch contradicts its own ruling later in siman 276 requiring a woman specifically to sit. His entire difficulty arose because he understood this opinion to maintain that there is no prohibition of exposed ervah for a standing woman. Since a standing woman is indeed prohibited, he concluded that this opinion is invalid. I already explained at length in Yoreh Deah siman 200 that this is inaccurate. This opinion agrees that a standing woman is prohibited, but not because her heart sees the ervah. Rather, it is because her ervah is exposed, as explained. It is therefore a clear and reliable ruling that a woman who wears a garment covering her front and back requires no interruption at all between her heart and ervah, and she may pray. Likewise, regarding immersion, if she stands even in clear water she may recite the blessing if her heart is outside the water, or even if it is within the water and she embraces her body with her arm beneath her heart. A man should do the same. See what I wrote, with Hashem's help, in Taz to Yoreh Deah siman 200.
