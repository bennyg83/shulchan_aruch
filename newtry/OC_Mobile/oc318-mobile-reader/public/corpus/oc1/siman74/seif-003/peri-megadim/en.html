In seif 2 the Mechaber writes, “below his heart.” Perishah, using an abbreviation, explains that “below” refers to being in the water, meaning that his heart is also in the water, in accordance with the Rama's second gloss. The distinction is that when he covers his heart directly with his hand, his action shows that he intends it as a covering, and even one part of the body can then cover another. When he places it below his heart while the heart is exposed, his action shows that he intends it as an interruption, and it is effective. Yet the Shulchan Aruch does not appear to support this, because the Rama did not attach his second gloss to the earlier phrase concerning being below the water. See there. Nachalas Tzvi, citing Lechem Chamudos, explains that placing one's hand on the heart is ineffective because people ordinarily do so, whereas embracing oneself with one's arms is unusual. According to this, the distinction is between the hands and the arms. Perhaps this is Perishah's intention, since the Shulchan Aruch does not distinguish between being directly on or below the heart, but only between hand and arm. One may further say that a hand does not form a complete interruption, whereas the arm, being thick, does. The law in seif 2 concerning covering with the hand presents no difficulty, because it may be explained like Lechem Chamudos or as we wrote. The question from siman 91, however, remains: covering the head with one's hand is also unusual, yet it is ineffective. Why does that differ from this case? The answer is that the head requires a covering, and one part of the body does not cover another. Regarding the heart, however, an interruption suffices, and therefore it is effective. Taz consequently cites siman 91. Although the Rama also cites it, Taz prefers to raise the contradiction within the Mechaber's own rulings.
With this you will understand that the Mechaber in seif 1 writes, “against his heart,” while in seif 2 he writes, “below his heart.” Perishah may explain “below” as referring to below within the water, thereby teaching the law of the Rama's gloss, that covering the heart with one's hand is ineffective while embracing oneself with the arms below the heart is effective. Levush in seif 1 writes, “against his heart or below it,” and that a hand is ineffective. Lechem Chamudos objects to this, as stated above. See Nachalas Tzvi and understand. You see that Levush's garments are white as snow, for the Shulchan Aruch implies unlike Perishah, since it distinguishes only between hand and arm, not between directly on and below the heart.
