We judge Taz — here in seif 2 the author translated "from below to the heart," and Perishah wrote in roshei teivos that the intent in the word "below in water" is to say his heart is also in the water; and as the Rema's gloss in the second gloss; and the distinction between covering the hand directly on the heart is really from the Torah law of covering — revealing one's thoughts; even one body covers a body — unlike below the heart where the heart is exposed, revealing one's thoughts, for by Torah law stopping helps (what is written in Shulchan Aruch does not imply so, since the Rema did not cite the second gloss above to "below in water" — see there). And Netsiv in the name of Levushei Serad gave a reason: on the heart it is customary to place the hand — it does not help; unlike embracing his arms — it is not customary — see there; and therefore the distinction between his hands and his arms (and perhaps this is Perishah's intent, that in Shulchan Aruch he did not mention the distinction between below the heart, only between hand and arm). And one could say further: with the hand there is not a complete stop, and with the arm it is substantial. Now from seif 2, hand covering for Shema — one could say Levushei Serad, or as written; unlike in prayer, covering on the head — there too it is not customary, it does not help, as written from here; and one could explain that the head in covering depends — one body does not cover a body, unlike the heart in stopping, for it helps; and therefore he mentioned prayer; and even though the Rema brought it, it is satisfactory for the author to challenge from his own to his own.

And with this you will understand that the author in seif 1 wrote on his heart, and in seif 2 from below to his heart; and one could say "below in water" as Perishah, and to teach as the Rema in the gloss — and hand covering on his heart does not help, and embracing his arms below his heart helps; and clothing in seif 1 on his heart or from below, and hand does not help; and on this Levushei Serad challenged as said; and see Netsiv and understand. And you see his clothing like white snow — that in Shulchan Aruch it implies not like Perishah, since he did not divide only between hand and arm and not between below and heart — understand.
