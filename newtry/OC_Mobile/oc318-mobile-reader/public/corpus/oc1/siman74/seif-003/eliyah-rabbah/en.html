By contrast, placing one's hand over one's heart is common and ordinary. Beit Yosef in siman 206 cites the Rosh in the chapter HaRo'eh: The Yerushalmi records Rabbi Yirmeyah's question whether one sitting inside a wooden cabinet may treat it like clothing, put his head outside the shirt, and recite a blessing. The doubt is whether it is forbidden because his heart sees the ervah, and above we ruled that it is forbidden. These are the Rosh's words. Magen Avraham writes that it is obvious that he overlooked the Gemara in Sukkah 10, which states that one sleeping in a canopy may put his head outside the canopy and recite Krias Shema, but if the canopy is ten tefachim high it is forbidden, because it is a tent like a house and his head is assigned after the majority of his body. Accordingly, the Yerushalmi's question is whether a cabinet is considered a tent or a utensil like a shirt or tallis. I examined the Yerushalmi itself and saw that it also asks concerning one sitting naked in a house whether he may put his head outside the window. It therefore necessarily follows that its question is not whether the structure is considered a tent. Indeed, it is a great wonder to me that all the great authorities did not notice that the Yerushalmi contradicts the explicit baraisa in Sukkah, which says that in a house he may not put his head outside. In my humble opinion, one may distinguish between Krias Shema and other blessings, but this requires study. Regarding Magen Avraham's question why the poskim omitted the passage in Sukkah, see the Rosh, though this also requires study. Tosafos in Sukkah there and the Agudah in the chapter HaRo'eh likewise require study.
