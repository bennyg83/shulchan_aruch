(א) (Section 3) With hands etc. — and he wants to stand naked — meaning one on whom the Name was written on his flesh and requires immersion; therefore he must remove his clothes; and since it is forbidden to stand before the Name naked — if he can cover the Name with clay he may cover and it is not an interruption that water enters it; and if he has no clay, the sages hold he should not cover with the hand lest he forget and remove the hand from the Name and he ends up standing naked before the Name — therefore he should not immerse; and R' Yosi holds immersion in its time is a mitzvah; therefore, not to delay immersion in its time, he holds they did not decree and he may cover with the hand — thus is the Gemara at present.

For it is one kind etc. — for the heart and hand are one body; unlike regarding covering the Name, where the hand does not come to cover the body but to cover the Name that is not of the body — thus Terumat HaDeshen siman 10; and meaning: written in ink or dye that is not of the body.

(ג) And furthermore in the Gemara as well — the wording of Magen Avraham seems to imply that he comes to add a reason according to Terumat HaDeshen; but one who examines Terumat HaDeshen will see that Magen Avraham disputes Terumat HaDeshen on this. For Terumat HaDeshen comes to prove that a straw hat counts as a covering, since we say regarding the Name that one covers with clay — and what difference is there to me whether clay or straw? At first he wanted to reject that one cannot learn from covering the Name; for thus regarding mentioning a berachah is stricter than standing before the Name naked — for regarding the Name, covering with the hand would have sufficed were it not for the decree lest he forget and remove it; and regarding a berachah Rabbeinu Tam wrote that it does not help. And that one places the hand on the heart so that one's heart not see — therefore specifically a garment is required; thus regarding mentioning a berachah is stricter. If so, also regarding a straw hat one cannot learn from there that it helps. And he concludes that always mentioning the Name and standing before the Name naked are equal — one can properly learn from it the law of a straw hat; and that covering with the hand helps there is without the decree lest he forget, unlike regarding mentioning a berachah. The reason is that min b'mino does not interpose, as stated — thus is Terumat HaDeshen's position at present. Behold the decree lest he forget is between the eyes of Terumat HaDeshen, yet he still challenges from it — therefore one must say that he holds regarding mentioning a berachah the decree lest he forget does not apply, and so I found in Peri Chadash. And the reason is that specifically immersion, which has duration, do we say lest he forget; unlike mentioning a berachah, which is a very short utterance — there is no need that within the short time from the start of the berachah, when he placed the hand on the heart, he would forget before finishing and remove the hand. Nevertheless, from the law itself placing the hand does not help for a berachah. Or one could say that even if he forgets, he will presumably immediately return and remember and cover; but specifically regarding the Name, immediately when he removes the hand he violated standing before the Name naked, and even if he again places his hand it does not fix what he spoiled — unlike a berachah, where whatever happens happens. Thus Terumat HaDeshen holds regarding a berachah that the decree lest he forget does not apply — here we are dealing with a berachah — and Magen Avraham holds that we decree lest he forget even for a berachah; therefore he disputes Terumat HaDeshen on this.
