With his hands, etc. “And he wishes to stand naked.” This refers to one who had Hashem's Name written on his skin and required immersion. He therefore had to remove his clothing. Since it is forbidden to stand naked before Hashem's Name, if he can cover the Name with a reed he should do so. It is not an intervening substance for immersion, because water enters around it. If he has no reed, the Sages maintain that he should not cover it with his hand, lest he forget and remove his hand from the Name, thereby standing naked before the Name. He should therefore not immerse. Rabbi Yose maintains that immersion at its proper time is a mitzvah. To avoid postponing immersion at its proper time, he holds that we do not issue this decree and that he should cover it with his hand. This is the substance of the Gemara's discussion.
Because they are of one kind, etc. The heart and hand are parts of one body. This differs from covering Hashem's Name, where the hand is not covering the body but the Name, which is not part of the body. These are the words of Terumas HaDeshen, siman 10. He means that the Name was written in ink or pigment, which is not part of the body.
Moreover, in the Gemara also, etc. The wording requires explanation, because it implies that the Magen Avraham adds another reason to the Terumas HaDeshen, while one who examines the Terumas HaDeshen sees that the Magen Avraham necessarily disagrees with him. Terumas HaDeshen proves that a straw hat is considered a covering from the Gemara's statement that one covers Hashem's Name with a reed, for what difference is there between a reed and straw? At first he sought to reject the proof, because mentioning a blessing may be more stringent than standing naked before Hashem's Name. Regarding the Name, covering it with one's hand would have sufficed were it not for the decree lest he forget and remove his hand. Regarding a blessing, however, Rabbeinu Tam writes that this does not help. Placing one's hand on the heart so that the heart should not see the ervah is ineffective, and a garment is specifically required. It would therefore follow that mentioning a blessing is more stringent, and the effectiveness of a straw hat cannot be derived from there. He concludes, however, that mentioning Hashem's Name and standing naked before the Name are actually equivalent, and the law of a straw hat may indeed be derived from there. The reason a hand would suffice there absent the decree lest he forget, while it does not suffice for a blessing, is that a substance of the same kind does not intervene, as stated above. This is the substance of Terumas HaDeshen's words. The decree lest he forget was therefore fully considered by Terumas HaDeshen, yet he still raised his proof. He must hold that this decree does not apply to mentioning a blessing, as Peri Chadash also writes. The reason is that immersion continues for some time, so there is concern that he may forget. Mentioning a blessing is very brief, and we need not fear that during the short interval from beginning the blessing, after placing his hand on his heart, he will forget before finishing it and remove his hand. Alternatively, even if he forgets, he will presumably remember immediately and cover himself again. Regarding Hashem's Name, the moment he removes his hand he has already violated the prohibition by standing naked before the Name, and replacing his hand does not correct what he did. Regarding a blessing, however, the matter is different. In any event, Terumas HaDeshen holds that the decree lest he forget does not apply to a blessing. Since the Magen Avraham holds that it does apply even to a blessing, he necessarily disagrees with Terumas HaDeshen on this point.
