Seif 1. One who prays, etc. NB: The source of this halacha is in tractate Berachos [30b]: How long must one wait between one prayer and another? Rabbah and Rava Chisda — one said: [long enough] that he may prepare his mind upon it; and one said: [long enough] that his mind may be aroused upon it. The one who says that he may prepare his mind upon it — as it is written "And I entreated Heaven"; and the one who says that his mind may be aroused upon it — as it is written "And Moshe waited." And in what do these two authorities disagree? And Rashi wrote that they disagree only in wording. And in my humble opinion it appears that they disagree in what they disagree above (29b) regarding one who makes his prayer fixed — what is "fixed"? R' Yaakov bar Idi said in the name of R' Ami: Anyone whose prayer is a burden upon him like a burden; and one said: Anyone who cannot say it in the language of supplication. And therefore in this dispute they also disagree here regarding what is the intention and preparation needed for prayer — for the one who says that he may prepare his mind upon it holds like the one who says that "fixed" is anyone who cannot say it in the language of supplication; therefore he holds that the waiting is so that he may prepare his mind upon it and he will be able to pray in the language of supplication. But the one who says that his mind may be aroused holds like the one who says anyone whose prayer is a burden upon him like a burden; and according to him the waiting is so that he may know that the prayer is not a burden upon him; and therefore he said that his mind may be aroused upon it and his mind will be clear that it is not a burden upon him. And they disagree in the halacha, and it works out well; understand well. And here is the source of this halacha that one who prays two prayers one after the other must wait between one and the other — in the Gemara chapter 4 of Berachos (30b): How long must one wait between prayer and prayer? Rabbah and Rava Chisda — one said that he may prepare his mind upon it and one said that his mind may be aroused upon it. The one who says that he may prepare his mind — as written "And I entreated the the Master"; and the one who says that his mind may be aroused — as written "And Moshe waited." And in what do these disagree — Rashi wrote they disagree only in wording. And in my humble opinion it appears they disagree in what they disagree above (29b) regarding one who makes his prayer fixed — what is fixed? R' Yaakov bar Idi said in the name of R' Ami: Anyone whose prayer is a burden upon him like a burden; and one said anyone who cannot say it in the language of supplication. And therefore in this dispute they also disagree here regarding what intention and preparation is needed for prayer — for the one who says that he may prepare his mind holds like the one who says fixed is anyone who cannot say it in the language of supplication; therefore he holds the waiting is so that he may prepare his mind and be able to pray in the language of supplication. But the one who says his mind may be aroused holds like the one who says anyone whose prayer is a burden; and according to him the waiting is so that he know the prayer is not a burden; and therefore he said his mind may be aroused and his mind will be clear that it is not a burden. And they disagree in the halacha; understand.
