Se'if 3. There is one who forbids. Tur. And he learned from the case of Ravina; and that is what he wrote, "And it appears to me," etc. For from there it is not proved, since there the repayment was in merchandise, as written in se'if 1. But Bach wrote that the Tur learned from there that any dealing in merchandise, whether at the beginning or at the end, is forbidden. Nevertheless this requires further study: why did he not bring the main law that at the end it is forbidden, as above in se'if 1? And one can say that it is a fortiori and unnecessary, since if he receives merchandise on the moed, it is obvious that it is forbidden.
And likewise it is permitted to lodge a complaint, etc. Yerushalmi, and according to the reading brought in the Mordechai: they accept [complaints] before the authority, etc.
