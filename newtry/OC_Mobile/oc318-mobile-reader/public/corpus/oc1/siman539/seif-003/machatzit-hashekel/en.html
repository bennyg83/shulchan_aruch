(סק ג) One garment, etc.—even with his belt it is permitted, even for us. Meaning in Moed Katan 14a R' Yochanan permits one who has only one garment to launder it on the festival; they challenge that the matter is not evident and there is concern for marit ayin. They answer: Mar bar Rav Ashi—his belt proves it; likewise in Chullin. Rashi in Chullin: the belt is always fixed to the garment; when he removes the garment and wears another he takes the belt from this and fixes it on that; and one who has no other garment launders with the belt, and the viewer seeing him launder with the belt knows he has no other garment. However for our garments where the belt is not fixed it is forbidden; rather we hold like Rashi in the first chapter on “his belt proves it”—when one has only one garment, when he removes it to launder he wraps himself in his cloak and girds the belt on the cloak; thereby everyone knows when he launders he has only one garment, for they see the belt girded on his cloak; therefore for us too it is permitted. But the Rabbenu Yerucham wrote that according to this explanation it would be forbidden for one person to launder even his wife's garment, for if another launders and one does not see the belt on the cloak there is marit ayin; also if one lends a garment only until after laundering he is not girding the belt on the cloak—also it should be forbidden; but since both are uncommon—for typically one with only one garment has no one to use it and must launder himself, and it is uncommon that one lends a garment only until after laundering and no more—it was permitted in all cases, even if another lends a garment after laundering or another launders and it is not he himself, since it is uncommon the rabbis were lenient.
