For all this is included in a davar ha'aved. From this wording it is implied that it refers to both laws; but according to what he himself stipulated regarding the first law, that specifically regarding debts that are permitted to claim, and of necessity it deals with claiming from a Jew, for it is not a davar ha'aved if he waits until after the moed (for from a gentile it is clarified in se'if 8 that it is permitted to receive even merchandise, for the reason that everything is included in a davar ha'aved); and if so, what he concludes, that all this is included in a davar ha'aved, is awkward, for regarding a davar ha'aved all debts are permitted. And the truth is that it is stock phrasing from the wording of Nimukei Yosef and Maggid Mishneh, and there the law of lodging a complaint in court is not mentioned at all (and in the long Darkei Moshe he did not copy the law of lodging a complaint in court except at the end of his words as a law unto itself); and there it refers to what he initially was uncertain about regarding the law of a loan to a gentile on interest, whether it is permitted on the moed or forbidden, for forgoing profit is not called a loss; and on this he wrote that collecting payment from him, and likewise rolling over his debt, is certainly included in a davar ha'aved and is permitted according to all views.
