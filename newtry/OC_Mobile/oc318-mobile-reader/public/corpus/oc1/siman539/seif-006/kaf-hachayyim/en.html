6) There. Whether buying or selling. See Responsa Ohel Yaakov, siman 63, regarding a communal enactment. Those who needed to earn money for Yom Tov had been selling privately inside their homes. The enactment was adopted as a protective measure lest commerce come to be conducted publicly by those who did not need the earnings. If the people can uphold the enactment, it is proper to do so. If they cannot, it should be released for those who need the earnings, while those who do not need them remain bound by the full force of the enactment. They may not be released from something already forbidden by law. See there. See Beis Yaakov, siman 40, who writes that a shop should be partly open and partly closed. One who has no shop and happens to acquire clothing or utensils may sell them publicly. See there. Shiyurei Minchas Tzvi cites this. See below, subsection 30.
