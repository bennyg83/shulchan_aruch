(S.k. 8) And a merchant, etc. And it is forced to say that there it deals with one selling the produce of his garden; that is to say, on the contrary, it is implied from the fact that se'if 10 says "sellers," and likewise in se'if 11 "sellers," etc., that it means specifically one whose craft is thus, as Magen Avraham inferred in s.k. 11.
And one can say that here specifically in his house, etc. And according to this, what Rama said, "And a merchant who buys from this," etc., means the same applies to any shopkeeper in such a case in his house.
And so Maharshal wrote, etc. Were I not afraid, I would say it is a printer's error, and this "And so Maharshal wrote" refers to what Beis Yosef wrote: But places that have a market day one day a week are not permitted, etc. On this Magen Avraham wrote, "And so Maharshal wrote" to forbid; for so it is in Responsa Maharshal, siman 79. But what he wrote, that he permitted when the shop is closed, etc., is not written thus for every market day that is once a week, but for the market day that in Poland they call Krasni Targ (and in this country it is called Guter Wochen Targ), which also comes at distant intervals; regarding this Maharshal permitted that the shop be closed, and when the buyer comes, etc. And even this he permitted only in order to spend from it for the joy of Yom Tov; see there; it is also brought in Taz, s.k. 3. And it is possible, if it is not an error, that Magen Avraham brings a proof to permit a shopkeeper in his house, and for the need of the moed it is permitted even in a shop; for even in Maharshal we find a distinction between selling in private, that is, Maharshal's private manner regarding Krasni Targ, that the shop should be closed, etc.; and from this, at any rate it is proper to permit when it is much more private, namely in his house. But this is a great stretch.
