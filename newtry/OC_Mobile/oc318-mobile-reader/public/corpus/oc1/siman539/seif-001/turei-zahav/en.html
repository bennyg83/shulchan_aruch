He cannot go and claim them. It appears to me that this is specifically when he must exert himself and go before courts and judges; and so it is implied in the Gemara, where we say: Ravina had money outstanding with the people of Akra deShanvasa; he came before Rav Ashi and said to him, "What about going after them today?" He said to him, "Since today you find them and on another day you will not find them, it is like lost merchandise and is permitted." Thus he said to him, "What about going after them," and he did not say, "What about claiming from them"; this teaches that it is forbidden to exert oneself in a claim by traveling if they are not in this city of the Bach's discussion, or if they are here but he needs exertion to claim in beis din. But if he need only claim for himself that the other repay him, we have not found a prohibition. And nowadays we see in all communities that they claim before the dayanim even for merchandise on Chol HaMoed; and I have something to say in defense: we see that the spoiling of debts is very common, and every day we are distressed and impoverished, and the yoke of the gentiles is hard upon us, as Tosafos wrote nearby in chapter "Ein Nezikin"; and see siman 551, se'if 2; and it is as Hagahos Ashri wrote in chapter "Mi sheAmar": But credit extended by a Jew to a gentile without a mashkon, Rashi permits receiving it on the moed, and Rashbam says, well done to the one who is stringent; end quote. And Beis Yosef in the name of Maggid Mishneh wrote plainly that to collect from a gentile is permitted according to all views, for it is included in davar ha'aved. And all this appears to apply even to claiming in court; so it appears to me in defense, that nowadays all debts have at least the status of a possible davar ha'aved, and the prohibition of claiming is not a great prohibition even in Talmudic times, for he is not doing melacha.
