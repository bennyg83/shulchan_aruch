Whether to buy, etc. And whatever is forbidden is so even through a gentile (Bach in siman 543, and it is brought in Peri Megadim).
In repayment of his debt. At first glance it is implied from this wording that specifically in such a case, where the merchandise was not yet acquired by him until now; but if it was already acquired by him previously, and now he wants to bring it to his house, there is no prohibition here, for he is not doing commerce on the moed. Nevertheless one should refrain from this for another reason clarified in the previous siman, se'if 3: it is forbidden to transport his money from city to city, and the same applies within the same city, as clarified there in Mishnah Berurah. However, if he fears he will come to a loss if it remains there, it is permitted.
To go and claim them. See Taz, who explains the intent of the Shulchan Aruch as specifically where he must go and claim them in another city, or where he must exert himself to go with them before courts and judges; but if he need not claim them in court and they are in one city, it is also permitted to claim them on the moed. And see the novellae of Rabbi Akiva Eiger, who wrote that from the words of the Rosh it is not so implied, but rather in every manner it is forbidden; and so Sefer Nehar Shalom wrote, that it is not so implied from the words of the poskim. And in truth, from the words of the Mechaber in se'if 3 it is also clearly implied that even if they are in one city it is forbidden; and likewise Sefer Shulchan Atzei Shittim does not agree with Taz on this, for the essence of the prohibition is that through receiving the merchandise the sale is completed now on the moed, and what difference does it make whether they are in one city or not? Therefore I did not copy Taz's words into our subject, only the end of his words where he wrote that nowadays debts spoil, which I copied in se'if 3; see there.
