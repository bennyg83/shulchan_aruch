1) [Se'if 1] All commerce is forbidden, etc. This is because of the exertion involved. Beis Yosef in the name of Rosh; Levush; Magen Avraham, subsection 1.
2) There. Even the smallest amount. This means that it is forbidden to sell during Chol HaMoed even an item worth only a perutah. Nimukei Yosef on Rif.
3) There. Even the smallest amount. It appears that selling is forbidden even in one's house, where it does not entail so much exertion. Olas Shabbos, subsection 1.
4) There. Even the smallest amount. If one conducted a purchase or sale on Chol HaMoed, the transaction is effective. Mabit, volume 1, siman 176; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur; Olas Shabbos, subsection 6.
5) There. Whether buying or selling. This is forbidden even through a non-Jew, as written below, siman 543, subsection 3. See there.
6) There. Whether buying or selling. See Responsa Ohel Yaakov, siman 63, regarding a communal enactment. Those who needed to earn money for Yom Tov had been selling privately inside their homes. The enactment was adopted as a protective measure lest commerce come to be conducted publicly by those who did not need the earnings. If the people can uphold the enactment, it is proper to do so. If they cannot, it should be released for those who need the earnings, while those who do not need them remain bound by the full force of the enactment. They may not be released from something already forbidden by law. See there. See Beis Yaakov, siman 40, who writes that a shop should be partly open and partly closed. One who has no shop and happens to acquire clothing or utensils may sell them publicly. See there. Shiyurei Minchas Tzvi cites this. See below, subsection 30.
7) There. On condition that they give him afterward, etc. This applies even if he did not stipulate that they give it to him during the festival. Mishnah Berurah, subsection 2.
8) There. In payment of his debt, etc. At first glance, this wording implies that the law applies specifically in this case, where the merchandise had not previously been acquired by him. If it was already his and he now wishes to bring it home, there is no prohibition of commerce, because he conducts no transaction during the festival. Nevertheless, this should be avoided for another reason explained in the preceding siman, se'if 3: it is forbidden to transport one's property from one city to another. The same applies within a single city, as explained there. If, however, he is concerned that leaving it there will cause a loss, it is permitted. Mishnah Berurah in Biur Halachah.
9) There. He cannot go and demand payment from them. It appears that this applies specifically when the debtors are not in the creditor's city, or when they are present but demanding payment requires the exertion of bringing a claim before beis din. If he need only demand personally that they repay him, we find no prohibition. Taz, subsection 1; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1; Yad Aharon, Hagahos HaTur; Mikraei Kodesh, siman 57, subsection 1. Chiddushei Rabbi Akiva Eiger, however, writes that Rosh's words do not imply this distinction, but that demanding payment is forbidden in every case. Nehar Shalom, subsection 1, likewise writes regarding the aforementioned ruling of Taz that the words of the halachic authorities do not imply it. See there. Shulchan Atzei Shitim also disagrees with Taz in this matter. See there. This is also the view of Mishnah Berurah in Biur Halachah. Nowadays, when every debt is presumed to be subject to loss, one may demand payment even through litigation. Taz there; Eliyah Rabbah there; Yad Aharon there; Mishbetzos Zahav, subsection 1; Mikraei Kodesh there, subsection 2; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 11. If one holds secure collateral, however, it is forbidden. Mishbetzos Zahav there. This applies specifically to a loan resembling commerce. A monetary loan, however, may be collected even when one holds collateral. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 6. See below, subsections 19 and 23.
10) There. Unless they are not readily available, etc. The same applies for any other reason that a loss might result if he does not demand payment now.
11) There. Unless they are not readily available, etc. Even in this case, permission applies specifically when he did not deliberately schedule the work for the festival. Magen Avraham, subsection 2; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 2; Eshel Avraham, subsection 2; Mikraei Kodesh there, subsection 1; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 4.
12) There, in the gloss. He may sell privately, etc. Likewise, he may buy privately. Levush; Magen Avraham, subsection 3; Mikraei Kodesh there, subsection 7; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 6. He must also spend for the joy of Yom Tov an amount from the profit that he estimates he will earn by purchasing that merchandise. Machatzis HaShekel. Know that wherever one is required to spend from the profit for the joy of Yom Tov, he need not spend the entire profit; part of it is sufficient. Machatzis HaShekel there. See below, subsection 29.
13) There, in the gloss. He may sell privately, etc. This does not resemble se'if 5, where public sale is permitted, because in that case the opportunity certainly is not always available. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 3; Eshel Avraham, subsection 3.
14) There, in the gloss. And he should spend for the joy of Yom Tov, etc. Beis Yosef cites a dispute. Some forbid the transaction even privately, while others permit it privately even if none of the profit will be spent for the joy of Yom Tov. He writes that as a matter of halachah, if the profit is substantial, the transaction may be conducted privately, and one should spend from it for the joy of Yom Tov more than he had intended to spend. See there. Rama rules accordingly here in the gloss, as do Levush and the other later authorities.
