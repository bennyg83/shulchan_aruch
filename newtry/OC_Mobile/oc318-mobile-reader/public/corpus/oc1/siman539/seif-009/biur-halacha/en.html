And it is permitted to buy. See Beis Yosef, that there are many poskim who are stringent regarding buying wine for weekday need; therefore it appears that one should practice as Hagahos Ashri wrote in the name of Or Zarua, that even according to those who permit, it is specifically in private; and so Sefer Shulchan Atzei Shittim wrote.
And if it is something uncommon, etc. The later authorities toiled to reconcile the words of Rama and the Mechaber so that they not contradict each other, and not that of se'if 5: since the Mechaber deals where he will not find after the moed as he finds now, which is exactly the case of Rama's words, why does he forbid more than the need of drinking for the year; for in se'if 5 he permits a case like this? And also from Beis Yosef himself it is proved that according to the law of the caravan clarified above one must permit even more than the need of drinking for the whole year (for what would be like one who buys merchandise in order to profit; even in that it is permitted)? And Maamar Mordechai distinguishes forcedly that regarding a caravan the matter is more recognizable that it is uncommon profit that is not always available, which is not so in this case; and see what I wrote in the name of Chayei Adam in Mishnah Berurah, and according to his words Rama does not contradict the Mechaber. And Bach holds that the permission of se'if 5 is specifically regarding great profit that arrives through the caravan and the like, and in this manner Rama permitted, and the Mechaber does not deal with this manner. And see Eliyah Rabbah, who explains Rama's words according to their plain sense, and his view is that since he intends by his purchase to profit, and such profit he will not find if he buys after the moed, for after the moed the wine will become dearer, certainly it is permitted like that of se'if 5; and the Mechaber who forbids more than the need of his drinking for the year does not deal with one who buys in order to profit, and therefore it is forbidden, for the extra effort is for nothing. And in Mishnah Berurah we copied the view of Chayei Adam and Bach; and it appears that in practice one may rely on Bach's view, and one who is lenient like Eliyah Rabbah, they also do not protest against him.
