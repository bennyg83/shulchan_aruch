It appears that this applies specifically when the debtors are not in the creditor's city, or when they are present but demanding payment requires the exertion of bringing a claim before beis din. If he need only demand personally that they repay him, we find no prohibition. Taz, subsection 1; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1; Yad Aharon, Hagahos HaTur; Mikraei Kodesh, siman 57, subsection 1. Chiddushei Rabbi Akiva Eiger, however, writes that Rosh's words do not imply this distinction, but that demanding payment is forbidden in every case. Nehar Shalom, subsection 1, likewise writes regarding the aforementioned ruling of Taz that the words of the halachic authorities do not imply it. See there. Shulchan Atzei Shitim also disagrees with Taz in this matter. See there. This is also the view of Mishnah Berurah in Biur Halachah. Nowadays, when every debt is presumed to be subject to loss, one may demand payment even through litigation. Taz there; Eliyah Rabbah there; Yad Aharon there; Mishbetzos Zahav, subsection 1; Mikraei Kodesh there, subsection 2; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 11. If one holds secure collateral, however, it is forbidden. Mishbetzos Zahav there. This applies specifically to a loan resembling commerce. A monetary loan, however, may be collected even when one holds collateral. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 6. See below, subsections 19 and 23.
