(18) If it is something uncommon, etc. Meaning: even though it was clarified in the previous se'if that it is not called a davar ha'aved unless, if he does not sell now, he will lose from the principal; but for profit alone it is not permitted unless he will thereby spend more for the joy of Yom Tov. All this is regarding common profit, where he can also sell his merchandise after the moed. But regarding uncommon profit that is not always available, as he explains, for this itself they also permitted him, for it is like a davar ha'aved.
(19) And the same applies to fixed fairs, etc. And likewise a market day called Krasni Targ, which is something uncommon and comes from time to time. And it is possible that the same applies when the week before their Pesach falls on Chol HaMoed, and it is known that then they buy a great deal, so it is reckoned like an uncommon market day (Chayei Adam).
(20) And even from city to city, etc. See Biur Halacha.
(21) It is not permitted to sell and buy, etc. Unless through the profit he will spend more for the joy of Yom Tov, as above in se'if 4.
(22) He should sell on the market day after the moed. And specifically when he estimates with certainty that at any rate he will then not lose from the principal.
(23) And a merchant who buys, etc. For if he sells today he will return and buy anew and sell on the second day; and if he refrains from buying and selling today he will certainly lose the profit. And see Magen Avraham, who concluded that nevertheless it is not permitted to sell except in his house, and not to sit in a shop publicly.
