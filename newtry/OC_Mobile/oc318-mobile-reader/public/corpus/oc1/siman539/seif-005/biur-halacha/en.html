And they sold cheaply. Specifically in this manner; but to buy from them at the equivalent market value, it appears that even if it is for the need of his use, so long as it is not for the need of the moed, it is forbidden (Maamar Mordechai).
And even from city to city, etc. Even though this is certainly great effort, and in siman 537 it is clarified that for profit it is forbidden to exert oneself for its sake, one must say that it deals with a person whose occupation and livelihood is always to travel to fairs; and since his life and livelihood depend on this, everything is included in a davar ha'aved and is permitted (see Machatzit HaShekel, s.k. 6, in his explanation of Magen Avraham).
It is not permitted, etc., on the market day. See Taz, who brought in the name of Maharshal, and these are his words: Other market days one should not permit at all sitting in a shop, unless the shop is closed and opened somewhat when someone comes to buy; and all this is in order to spend for the joy of Yom Tov; end quote. (And it appears he derived this from what is clarified in se'if 11 regarding fruits, clothing, and vessels: that even in private it is not permitted except for the need of the moed; and therefore he stipulated that he spend for the joy of Yom Tov, for in this there is the permission of the beginning of se'if 4.) And Machatzit HaShekel wrote that many of the later authorities cried out against the leniency practiced, for our many sins, regarding business dealing on Chol HaMoed, and he found no remedy for them (for even according to Magen Avraham, who equates the law of a shopkeeper to a merchant, it is not permitted except when the shop is closed and opened somewhat when the buyer comes to purchase); except that in Sefer Eliyah Rabbah he wrote, and these are his words: And it is possible they reckoned it a davar ha'aved, for if they do not sit so much time all the days of the festival in the shop, business dealing from Jews will diminish (and also since it is impossible to protest that everyone close their shops, and all the more so when there is a gentile merchant in the city; if so, if the God-fearing one alone closes his shop, his buyers will become accustomed to go to another merchant, and it is somewhat similar to a loan to those who regularly deal with him, which he permitted in se'if 12 for this reason). And Eliyah Rabbah concluded that at any rate one should not learn melacha from this, for it is not comparable to merchandise dealing, which has many sides of leniency in the poskim; end quote. If so, we still have not found a remedy, for sometimes he does complete melacha in the shop, for example when he sells some merchandise in the shop such as a piece of flax or other garment and cuts from it according to the measure needed for the buyer, for regarding this there is liability because of cutting (as Chayei Adam wrote in klal 36, and so it is proved from the Rambam in chapter 11 of Hilchos Shabbos, law 7); unless it is for a person who has creditors upon him with notes (vekslin) whose time of repayment has arrived, and if he does not redeem them he will certainly have a loss from this; and it is known that for a davar ha'aved it is also permitted to do melacha. However, according to what Magen Avraham wrote above at the end of siman 534, that one who has nothing to eat, even though it was permitted for him to do melacha, nevertheless he should not do so publicly, for who knows that he has nothing to eat; and likewise in our subject; or what is stated in siman 538, that a person may bring in his fruits because of thieves, and nevertheless he should bring them in privately, so that the matter not be so noisy; and likewise in our subject, there is no noisier matter than that the shops are open openly all day on Chol HaMoed, and in this there is degradation of the moed. Therefore it is certainly good to be careful that the shop be closed and opened somewhat when the buyer comes to purchase, and also that he spend some of the profit extra for the joy of Yom Tov, as Taz wrote in the name of Maharshal.
