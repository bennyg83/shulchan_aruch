(sk 5) Permitted, etc. — and he did not look in Mordechai, etc. in judgment of gentiles, etc. — meaning in Chol HaMoed it deals with judgment of Israel where the plaintiff is Israelite and the judges too are Israelites; and in Nisan and Tishrei, when they are busy with the bustle of the festival, they did not trouble him — neither the plaintiff nor the court — unlike Mordechai, which deals where the plaintiff is a gentile and the litigant in the case is also a gentile; and in Sefer Ateret Roshei he wrote: and he was not precise — Semak did not challenge here at all, only on the end of siman 545 that it deals with receiving in court of appeals — end quote; and in my answer I did not know why he wrote on him "and he was not precise" — for at the end of siman 545 Rabbeinu Yehudah wrote it is permitted to receive for his debts, etc., and the Rama did not emend on him — meaning the Mechaber did not write the word "in judgment," only "to receive"; and one could explain he means in court of appeals; nevertheless the Rama did not wish to challenge, since one could explain he means in judgment of gentiles — for Rabbeinu Yehudah's law below at the end of siman 545 also derives from this Mordechai that Rama's gloss here derives from it; and surely surely it is one. And from what Rabbeinu Yehudah wrote there earlier that they judge both monetary and capital cases, etc. — one could say it deals with the will of both plaintiff and court; and it teaches that although on Shabbat and Yom Tov they do not judge because of a decree lest one write — on Chol HaMoed it is permitted; and also in capital cases one finds the will of both — such as when the defendant is a murderer and imagines in his soul he will be acquitted in his judgment and desires that they hurry to bring his acquittal to light; also in any case one could say capital laws are different. And what he wrote "they excommunicate and strike one who does not accept the judgment" — the reason: since they already judged when permitted, either before Yom Tov or on Chol HaMoed.
