(S.k. 5) It is permitted, etc. And he did not look in the Mordechai, etc., regarding gentile court, etc. That is to say: on Chol HaMoed it deals with Jewish law, where the litigant is a Jew and the dayanim are also Jews; and in Nisan and Tishrei, when they are busy with the exertion of the moed, they did not trouble him, neither the litigant nor the beis din; which is not so regarding the Mordechai, which deals where the litigant is a gentile, and also the one of the court is a gentile. And in Sefer Eliyah Rabbah he wrote, and these are his words: And he was not precise, for the Sema did not challenge here at all, only the end of siman 545, which deals with lodging a complaint in the synagogue; end quote. And in my poverty I did not know why he wrote of him "and he was not precise"; for at the end of siman 545 Beis Yosef wrote it is permitted to lodge a complaint for his debts, etc., and Rama did not gloss upon him. Granted that the Mechaber did not write the word "in court," only "to lodge a complaint," and one could explain that he means in the synagogue; nevertheless Rama did not wish to challenge, since one can say he means in court and it deals with gentile court; for Beis Yosef's law below at the end of siman 545 also derives from the aforementioned Mordechai from which Rama's gloss here derives, and this and that are one. And from what Beis Yosef wrote there earlier, that they judge both monetary cases and capital cases, etc., one can say it deals with the consent of both litigants and the consent of the beis din; and it teaches us that even though on Shabbos and Yom Tov they do not judge because of a decree lest he write, on Chol HaMoed it is permitted; and also in capital cases one finds the consent of both, for example where the defendant is a murderer and the like, and imagines in his mind that he will emerge innocent in his judgment, and desires that they hurry to bring his innocence to light. Also even without this, one can say capital laws are different. And what he wrote, that they place in niddui and strike one who does not accept the judgment: the reason is that they already judged with permission, either before Yom Tov or on Chol HaMoed.
