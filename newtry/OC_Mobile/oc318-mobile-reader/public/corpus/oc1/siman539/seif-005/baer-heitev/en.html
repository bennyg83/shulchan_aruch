To fairs. Maharshal wrote in a responsum, siman 79: Know that I received as practical law to permit on Chol HaMoed if a fair arrives, even a market day called "Krasni Targ," which is something uncommon and comes at intervals, similar to caravans and ships; and we reckon loss of profit as loss of principal, etc. But on other market days one should not permit at all sitting in a shop, unless the shop is closed and opened somewhat when someone comes to buy; and all this is in order to spend for the joy of Yom Tov; end quote.
The moed. And specifically when he knows with certainty that he will not lose from the principal (Maharival; Magen Avraham).
Who buys. Magen Avraham concluded that specifically in his house it is permitted, but in a shop it is forbidden; and so Maharshal wrote, that the shop should be closed, and when the buyer comes to purchase he opens it somewhat; and at a fair it is permitted to sell, as is written in s.k. 7.
