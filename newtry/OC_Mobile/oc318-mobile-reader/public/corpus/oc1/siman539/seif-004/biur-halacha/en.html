He will lose from the principal. And even if it is uncertain whether he will lose, it is also permitted (Olas Shmuel in the name of Maharshal and Eliyah Rabbah). And it further appears that if he needs money to pay his debts whose time has arrived on Chol HaMoed, and if he does not pay he will suffer a loss, and for this reason he is forced to sell his merchandise on the moed, it is reckoned like a davar ha'aved.
And he will spend more for the joy of Yom Tov. The same applies to Chol HaMoed; so it is proved in the Yerushalmi that Beis Yosef brought as the source for this law.
But one who has money with ease, etc. Taz's view is that specifically if he is extraordinarily wealthy; but otherwise, even if he has money with ease, everything is reckoned as for a person's livelihood in our times, and they permit him to sell so that he spend more for the joy of Yom Tov. And Sefer Nehar Shalom doubts his words; see there.
