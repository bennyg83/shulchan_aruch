(34) Sellers of fruits, etc. For these are things that last after the moed, and it is not recognizable that it is for the need of the moed, and they come to suspect him that he is taking them for weekday need; therefore privacy is required. And the poskim wrote that specifically the fixed sellers whose craft is thus all year require privacy; but a householder who sells for the need of the moed does not require privacy.
(35) Or to an alleyway. That is to say, one that is not open through; for if it is open through, it is included in the public domain.
(36) And he locks one. And specifically in this case; but other things, even if he sits at the doorway of the shop and the shop is closed and the door is open, are forbidden.
(37) Of the festival of Sukkos. This is regarding decorating the market with fruits: it is needed only on the eve of the last Yom Tov of the festival, and because of the honor of Shemini Atzeres, which is a festival in its own right; which is not so on the eve of the last Yom Tov of Pesach, where there is no mitzvah to decorate. But regarding selling publicly, even on the eve of the last Yom Tov of Pesach it is permitted because of the honor of Yom Tov.
(38) And it is permitted, etc. So that we not say that since we are expert in the fixing of the months it is like a weekday, and they prepare from Chol HaMoed for weekday need, which is forbidden; it teaches us that it is permitted, so that they not treat it lightly.
(39) To buy, etc. See the novellae of Rabbi Akiva Eiger, that even doing complete melacha, such as cooking for the need of the second Yom Tov, is also permitted.
