(א) One does not buy, etc. — see Mishna Berurah what he wrote that from a gentile some say it is permitted to buy houses, fields, and vineyards, etc., because it is like saving from their hand; so Shach in the name of Tosefta, and Magen Avraham and Eliyah Rabbah brought it. And what he wrote the reason "because it is like saving from their hand" is not agreed upon by the poskim in my humble opinion, for in the Tosefta it concludes and writes and brings up in gentile courts because it is like saving from their hand — and literally in this language it is in Avodah Zarah daf 13 regarding going to a gentile fair, and Rashi explains it refers to courts that are permitted because it is like saving from their hand; and so explained in Maggid Mishneh chapter 9 Yom Tov, and Peri Megadim also brought this. And also in the Talmud on 13b that they weighed and discussed the reason implies this "like saving from their hand" does not refer to the essence of the permission to buy but only to the courts [however in Shach in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah it somewhat implies like his words here]. And therefore here too one must explain regarding writing and bringing up in courts that it is permitted on the festival because it is like saving from their hand. If so, another reason is needed for permission to buy from a gentile on Yom Tov. Indeed this very law in my humble opinion is not clear, for all the Rishonim did not copy this Tosefta at all, and since Rambam and all poskim omitted this Tosefta it appears they do not hold thus for halacha; and Rambam was silent and wrote the mishnah law that one does not buy houses, etc., only for festival need, and did not distinguish at all that from a gentile it is permitted to buy houses in any manner; and the reason one could say: since the mishnah was silent "one does not buy," etc., only for festival need and did not distinguish that from a gentile it is permitted, and so in the Talmud it did not bring this Tosefta to distinguish in this — evidently they do not hold thus for halacha; and more so since the mishnah specified only for festival need or for the seller's need — evidently we have no other manner of permission. And further one can say Rambam and Shulchan Aruch who did not write this permission explain the Tosefta with another explanation from Shach: the Tosefta did not come to innovate that from a gentile is more permitted than from an Israelite, but only that from a gentile is also permitted, and it also deals specifically with festival need and did not need to explain this since it refers to the mishnah and only added that from a gentile is also permitted; even though these matters need writing and bringing up in courts, nevertheless it is permitted, since buying is permitted for festival need, writing and courts are also permitted because it is like saving from their hand [and as explained from Rashi and Maggid Mishneh] [and were I not afraid I would also say the Tosefta does not refer at all to festival matters, for all the Tosefta's language was taught in Avodah Zarah daf 13 and Eruvin daf 47 regarding going to a gentile fair, and only these words "going to a gentile fair and buying," etc. are missing here and were taught here that the reason for permission is because it is like davar ha-aved that he will not find on another day [and in the preceding baraita it also deals with the festival regarding davar ha-aved], and as brought here in the Talmud daf 11 regarding davar ha-aved; and it is taught regarding idolatry similarly, see there and see in Avodah Zarah in Rosh and Ran in the name of Yerushalmi that all the permission is regarding idolatry because it is like davar ha-aved, for at the fair he finds opportunity to buy them — if so we have no Tosefta to permit here unless we say for this reason they also permitted on chol ha-moed to go to the fair and buy from them, for on another day perhaps he will not obtain them; but then Shach should have mentioned specifically fair]. Nevertheless regarding houses and fields in the Land of Israel perhaps one may be lenient because of settling the Land, but in the diaspora in my humble opinion this law is not clear at all.
