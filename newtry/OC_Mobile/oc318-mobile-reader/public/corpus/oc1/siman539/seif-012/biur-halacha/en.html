They do not buy, etc. See Mishnah Berurah, what he wrote: that from a gentile there are those who say it is permitted to buy houses, fields, and vineyards, etc., because it is like rescuing from their hand; so Shiltei Giborim wrote in the name of a Tosefta, and Magen Avraham and Eliyah Rabbah brought it. And what he wrote as the reason, that it is like rescuing from their hand, is not agreed upon in my humble opinion among the poskim; for in the Tosefta it concludes: and he writes and brings up before gentile courts because it is like rescuing from their hand; and in almost this very wording it is stated in Avodah Zarah, folio 13, regarding going to a gentile fair; and it is clarified there in Rashi that it refers to the courts, which are permitted because it is like rescuing from their hand; and so it is clarified in Maggid Mishneh, chapter 9, law 14, and he also brought these Rashi explanations. And also in the Gemara there, folio 13b, which discusses the reason, it is likewise implied that this "like rescuing from their hand" does not refer to the essence of the permission to buy, only to the courts (however, in Shiltei Giborim there in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah it is somewhat implied like his words here). And if so, here too one must explain regarding the writing and bringing up before courts, that it is permitted on the moed because it is like rescuing from their hand. If so, another reason is needed for the permission to buy from a gentile on Yom Tov. However, even the essence of this law, in my humble opinion, is not clear; for all the Rishonim did not copy this Tosefta at all; and since the Rambam and all the poskim omitted this Tosefta, it appears they do not hold so for the law. And the Rambam stated plainly and wrote the law of the Mishnah, that they do not buy houses, etc., only for the need of the moed, and he did not distinguish at all that from a gentile it is permitted to buy houses in every manner. And the reason one can say is: since the Mishnah says plainly "they do not buy," etc., except for the need of the moed, and did not distinguish that from a gentile it is permitted; and likewise in the Gemara it did not bring this Tosefta to distinguish in this; apparently they do not hold so for the law. And all the more so, since the Mishnah specified "except for the need of the moed or for the need of the seller," apparently we have no further manner of permission. And further one can say that the Rambam and other poskim who did not write this permission explain the Tosefta with a different explanation than Shiltei Giborim: that the Tosefta did not come to innovate that from a gentile it is more permitted than from a Jew, but only that from a gentile too it is permitted, and it also deals specifically for the need of the moed; and it did not need to clarify this, for it refers to the Mishnah; and it added only this, that from a gentile too it is permitted even though these things require writing and bringing up before courts; nevertheless it is permitted, since the purchase is permitted for the need of the moed, the writing and the courts are also permitted because it is like rescuing from their hand (as above from Rashi and Maggid Mishneh). (And were I not afraid, I would further say that the Tosefta does not refer at all to the moed; for all the wording of the Tosefta is taught in Avodah Zarah, folio 13, and in Eruvin, folio 47, regarding going to a gentile fair; and here are missing only these words: they go to a gentile fair and buy, etc. And it was taught here that the reason for permission is because it is like a davar ha'aved, that he will not find it on another day [and there in the preceding baraisa it also deals with the moed regarding a davar ha'aved]; and as the Gemara brought here on folio 11 regarding a davar ha'aved, and a similar baraisa is taught regarding idolatry; see there. And see in Avodah Zarah in the Rosh and Ran in the name of some who say that all the permission regarding idolatry is because it is like a davar ha'aved, for at a fair he finds to buy them; and if so, according to this we have no Tosefta to permit here, unless we say that for this reason they also permitted on Chol HaMoed to go to a fair and buy from them, for on another day he might not obtain them; but if so, Shiltei Giborim should have mentioned specifically a fair.) And nevertheless, regarding houses and fields in Eretz Yisrael it is possible there is reason to be lenient because of settling Eretz Yisrael; but elsewhere, in my humble opinion, this law is not clear at all.
Who has nothing to eat, or for the need of the hired worker, etc. See Magen Avraham, siman 542; and so he ruled above in se'if 4, that even if he has something to eat, but he wants so that he profit and spend more for the joy of Yom Tov than he is accustomed to spend, it is also permitted; and see above in se'if 4 what he wrote, "And it appears to me," etc.; and see below at the end of siman 542, that he must do so privately; see there in Mishnah Berurah.
