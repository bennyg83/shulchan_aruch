[Levush] Rather, for study, etc. Shach in Yoreh Deah siman 246 says this refers to long winter nights, when the main part of the night is for learning. During short summer nights the main need is sleep, though one should still learn a little each night. Perishah, Mateh Moshe, and Bach transmit the mnemonic "from lie down until arise, lie down; from arise until lie down, arise." That is, from Shavuot, where Megillat Ruth says "lie down until morning," until Tisha B'Av, where Eichah says "arise, cry out at night," one may lie down and need not rise early to learn. From Tisha B'Av, "arise," until Shavuot, "lie down," one should arise early in the morning to learn Torah. Magen Avraham cites Olat Shabbat that the period is from Tisha B'Av until the fifteenth of Iyar; some say the proper sleep time is eight hours. It appears to me that all depends on the individual. Mateh Moshe, gate 3, extols Torah study at night as though one were offering incense and sacrifices to Heaven; Hashem draws a thread of kindness over him and the Shechinah is opposite him, as it says, "Arise, cry out at night before the Presence of Hashem." Shelah, page 123, writes that it is good to learn before going to sleep even in summer, even a small amount, so that one lies down amid words of Torah.
