He need not remove it. Shiyurei Leket, klal 7, siman 62, concludes: The custom is to rebuke a non-Jew who enters a Jew’s domain with chametz, because crumbs may fall from his hand and the Jew may sometimes come to violate a prohibition through them.
Upon the table. Rashba writes that the matter is close to negligence when the Jew and non-Jew know one another. Just as the Jew is not particular that the non-Jew should not eat from his food, so the non-Jew will not be particular concerning the Jew. Even with a separating cloth it is forbidden, because chametz prohibits in any amount and it is unlikely that one of the non-Jew’s crumbs will not become mixed into the Jew’s food. The beginning of Rashba’s language implies that if they do not know one another, it is permitted for them to eat at the same table. We do not then suspect either sharing food or the mixing of crumbs. The concern exists because acquaintances may eat from one another’s food, causing the non-Jew’s crumbs to become mixed into the Jew’s food. My brother-in-law in Eliyah Zuta writes likewise, unlike Olas Shabbos, who did not quote Rashba accurately. Rashba’s wording also implies that if they are particular not to eat from one another, it is permitted even when they know each other. I wrote similarly in Minchas Yaakov, klal 76, in accordance with Maharshal. Taz’s challenge there is unfounded, and Pri Chadash followed Taz only because my work had not yet reached him. I resolved Maharshal’s words correctly there without difficulty. Nevertheless, one should not be lenient initially. Certainly regarding the severity of chametz, it is proper to be stringent even where they do not know one another and are particular not to share food.
