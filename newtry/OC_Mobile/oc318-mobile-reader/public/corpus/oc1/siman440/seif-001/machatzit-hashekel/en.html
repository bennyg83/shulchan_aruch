(א) In Shulchan Aruch seif 1 — non-Jew, etc. If he is liable for it — Kaf HaChayim infers he must accept liability explicitly (and this is what helps); otherwise there is no law of guardians for a gentile, as the verse "his fellow" excludes, as in Choshen Mishpat siman 301; and he wrote further that according to those who say even an unpaid guardian must destroy — see there.

(ב) And according to those who say in Choshen Mishpat that even when not liable by laws of guardianship he is liable for negligence — likewise here he must destroy at present; and it must be said that this view, who holds he is liable for negligence, is Rambam's view — he gave a reason: although exempt from all laws of guardianship, for negligence he is liable as a damager; thus what he is liable for in negligence is not because he became a guardian but because he damaged; therefore even if liable for negligence per this view he need not destroy since he did not become a guardian and it is not called his chametz nor is he liable for it. However, per Shach in siman 66 s.k. 126 to resolve Raavad's challenge on owners' negligence — Rambam does not mean actual damager but obligated by logic without a verse for negligence; therefore we do not exclude negligence from "his fellow" — see there at length; and thus in truth what he is liable for in negligence is by law of guardianship; and well one may say he must destroy per those who say an unpaid guardian must destroy.
