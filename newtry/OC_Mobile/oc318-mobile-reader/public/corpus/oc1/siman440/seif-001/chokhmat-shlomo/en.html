Seif 1 — a non-Jew who deposited his chametz, etc. — see in Magen Avraham what he was uncertain about: whether it is permitted to return the chametz to the non-Jew or forbidden lest he return and sell it to a Jew; and Kaf HaChayyim wrote plainly: since he is not selling to him but only returning what is his, there is no concern he will return and sell it to a Jew, etc.; and it appears in my humble opinion there is proof for Kaf HaChayyim's words from an explicit mishnah in Demai chapter 3 mishnah 2: one who buys vegetables from the market and decides to return them — he may not return until he tithes, for it is not lacking only quantity; and Rabbenu Ovadia explained there: since it was known so-and-so bundles per prutah and that is their value, therefore since through them it became his and he is obligated to tithe them, etc.; and behold this obligation to tithe is only so as not to remove from his hand something not fixed, lest others cook with it — as explained there explicitly in that chapter; and it is difficult: why discuss when he decided to return them — implying he initially bought clearly intending they be his, only afterward decided to return; it should follow even if he bought initially on condition to examine, he should tithe, for Shmuel said in Bava Batra 87: one who buys utensils from a artisan on condition to examine, and it was lost in his hand — he is liable; and not specifically when he left there, but even while still there he is liable for accidents, as proven there regarding the case of the leather worker — see there; and if so it is difficult: since it was a fixed value of vegetables and he took them to examine, he is already liable for accidents — how may he return something not fixed; and it should not say "and decided" but certainly he became obligated since he is not selling to him but returning what is his — there is no obligation to fix the defect, only when he bought initially intending to acquire clearly, then it became his and it is forbidden to remove a defect from his hand; but if it is not yet his, only in his responsibility, he need not fear the defect even though it is in his responsibility like accidents — all the more for chametz after Pesach he need not fear lest he sell to a Jew; however one may reject this proof, for there the prohibition does not extend from it, therefore if the item is not fully his they did not obligate him to fix it since he did not cause the defect and through him tithe was not obligated; but in our case, since without him the chametz would not have been forbidden after Pesach — for a non-Jew's chametz after Pesach is permitted and there is no concern of defect, only through him being in responsibility it became forbidden in benefit — the defect extends through him; therefore what difference whether the item itself is his or not — since the defect and prohibition came through him, he must fix so the defect through him should not reach a Jew; therefore Magen Avraham was properly uncertain; and being so, it appears to decide like Magen Avraham and not like Kaf HaChayyim, for what Kaf HaChayyim wrote that he is not obligated to buy the chametz from him is not necessary — for if the defect were not through him, certainly he would not be obligated to buy to fix lest he sell to a Jew; but since the defect is through him, behold the main concern lest he sell to a Jew — for all prohibitions are because it is fit that defect should not extend through him, and it is forbidden to remove from his hand something not fixed — therefore what difference whether the prohibition itself is his or not — since the defect and prohibition came through him, he must fix so this defect through him should not come to another Jew; so too here, since through being in responsibility and not making a fix before Pesach this prohibition extended on him to fix so this defect through him should not occur — therefore it appears correct like Magen Avraham; and do not ask; and see Kaf HaChayyim further that he raised we require explicit acceptance of responsibility by acquisition as the law that a non-Jew has no law of guarding; and see what I wrote in my novellae to Choshen Mishpat siman 301 in Maggid Mishneh that this is not a clear law; and one might say accepting from the non-Jew we do not require acquisition, explicit acceptance of responsibility suffices alone — see there; and in my responsum to Yoreh De'ah laws of bekhor siman 320 for community Kalish third edition I wrote further one might say that in Shulchan Aruch even without stipulation it is as if stipulated — and I settled thus the view of Ran here that Kaf HaChayyim brought here — see there; and examine. And behold people say in the name of a great man, the Gaon author of Kaf HaChayyim, that it is better to sell the room with the chametz than to rent, because if he rents it is like Jewish responsibility, for if the chametz is lost the non-Jew will not pay him chametz and it is like non-Jew's chametz in a Jew's house in Jewish responsibility — not so if he sells the chametz, he accepts responsibility for non-Jew's chametz in a non-Jew's house, etc. — and this is because he is concerned for the view of those who hold even in silence, if the non-Jew is silent he too is considered in responsibility, etc. — so I heard; and behold it is already explained in my work on Pesach laws that there is no room for concern here; and now I will explain further there's help: behold even those who hold that even in silence there is acceptance of responsibility — that is when he would have to pay money anew from his house if it is lost, like strong chametz of warriors — if it is lost he must pay money anew; but for what he need not pay anew, only loses his debt, this does not cause money, since loss of debt is not actual money, for from now and onward he collects and does not lose from his house actually; and know we do not find anywhere that it is forbidden to sell to a silent non-Jew the chametz lest since he is silent in any case he will not pay — the reason must be since it is only loss of debt it does not cause money, only like gorem legarmei; and the reason further: behold we establish gorem legarmei is not like money; and even if it were like money, when exempt from law and he has no obligation except from silence, certainly it is not like money; only in chametz the verse revealed "it shall not be found" — therefore you have only its novelty if from silence he would have to pay actual money from his house; but for only losing his debt, this is certainly not considered gorem legarmei, as I explained in my humble opinion — correct, and there is no distinction between sale and rental in this; examine, and it fits well. And behold Rosh wrote in the first chapter of Pesachim: since the depositor lent his house for guarding his property — [his house] of the depositor we call it; and it appeared to me to bring proof for his words from what is in Bava Kamma chapter 4 40b: a watchman was warned in a house and returned it to owners — owners pay half damages and the borrower is exempt entirely; end: permission changes and beginning permission does not change; R' Yehudah said: whoever taught this did not teach that; and I wondered: what is the challenge — behold this is explained, as they enacted acquisition in buyers, so too in watchmen, and without acquisition one does not become a watchman; and it is known every acquisition in error is not acquisition — if so the Gemara should challenge from the outset why the borrower owes half damages — let him say: the ox you lent me from you, the lion you did not lend me from you; and answers he knew it was a gorer — proven if he did not know he would be exempt entirely, for it is borrowing in error; if so for a forewarned ox it is as if the watchman did not acquire and it remains in the owner's domain, therefore permission does not change; but in the end, since it was tam and not borrowing in error, it is in the borrower's domain, and when he returns afterward to owners it is new permission, therefore permission changes properly; and even though one might say also in the beginning if we say permission changes, then it would cease being forewarned — if so it would again not be borrowing in error — how do we know perhaps we follow the opposite, since in truth it was forewarned we establish it in its original presumption and say since it was forewarned it is borrowing in error, therefore there is no new permission — nevertheless it is proven as Rosh's words, since the borrower lent his house to the lender — the lender's house we call it; and certainly the lender also holds thus for Rosh, for otherwise with a Jew borrowing chametz from his fellow he would be exempt from burning through the midrash of the mechilta — why did the poskim not distinguish; and why did Rosh need to establish the mechilta with a Jew who lent to a non-Jew — it should deal with borrower or renter where the object's presumption is for himself; and if so the Gemara should challenge properly if permission changes — whose domain is the place the ox actually stands; if so also in the beginning it stands in the watchman's domain and the house it stands in belongs to the watchman; then we do not follow the land it stands on actually, only after permission; also in the end, since he lent his house to the lender — the lender's house; and when he returns it is one permission; and also in the watchman's house it is as if the watchman accepted responsibility for the lender's ox that is in the lender's house — certainly responsibility alone does not count as permission changing; and it fits well; examine; and see what I wrote in my work to Choshen Mishpat Maggid Mishneh siman 291 — see there; and examine.
