(1) (a) If he, etc. - a dictation that light will not be found in your homes means that even the light is not entirely his own, it is forbidden by the Torah, who is it, ACP Meiri, who accepted responsibility for it, and Ashekia called it as his own, 16.

(ב) Liable for it, etc. — when he explicitly accepted liability and kinyan was made from his hand; otherwise there is no law of guardians for a non-Jew, as explained in Choshen Mishpat siman 301 seif 1 and seif 4. And if kingdom law makes him liable even without specification, from the moment he becomes a guardian it is in the Jew's domain even without stipulating liability for theft — as explained below.

(ג) Anywhere in his domain — even if he rented or lent him a place by itself for his chametz in his house or courtyard it does not help if he accepted liability, for we hold rental does not acquire; thus the chametz remains in the Jew's house. Nevertheless b'dieved if he kept the chametz with him until after Passover one may rely on Poskim who hold that where he designated a place for his chametz he does not transgress bal yera'eh even if he accepted liability, for we require chametz under the Jew's hand as an ordinary deposit under his domain to move and place anywhere; and if he designated a place by itself it is not called present and the chametz is permitted to eat and benefit. Designating a place is when he designates before him a house, room, or corner in his house by rental or loan; but if he received a deposit without designation and afterward designated a place without the non-Jew depositor's knowledge it does not help, lest the non-Jew does not want it placed except in the Jew's domain; and even making a partition does not help.

(4) (d) Obligor - when the time has come for clarification, if there is no akoum here, let them return it to him, and if it is fun to cancel and sell it, as in his chametz. That the chametz is placed in it for the ACM and the ACM will accept responsibility for theft and loss and the right thing to do is both, that is, the sale of the house and the sale of the chametz:

(ה) He must destroy it — at the time of biur if the non-Jew is not here to return it; and whether nullification and sale help like his own chametz — Acharonim disagree, many stringent since the body is not his; many lenient (see Sedei Chemed ma'arechet chametz umatzah siman 5 ot 33). Per Magen Avraham in s.k. 1 there is a remedy: return and deposit with another non-Jew or sell the house where the chametz rests to the non-Jew who accepts liability for theft and loss; best to do both — sell the house and sell the chametz.

(ו) And even if he returned and deposited, etc. — for it is as his own since he accepted liability; thus it is as a Jew depositing chametz with a non-Jew — forbidden, as in seif 4. And even if the other non-Jew accepted all liability that was on the Jew — nevertheless forbidden, for liability did not leave the Jew regarding the first non-Jew; some are lenient since the body is not his and not in his house; and per their view likewise when he sold the room where the chametz rests it is permitted if this non-Jew accepted liability for theft and loss — Peri Megadim.

(ז) (b) Another son, who is not of his father's father when he returned and entrusted with his husbands with his wife, D.C., as he was responsible for the occupier of his father in the house of his father:

(ח) And there are those who say, etc. — l'chatchila one should be concerned for this view; therefore if the non-Jew depositor is before him he must return to him; if the non-Jew is not here he should sell the chametz and also nullify (and although many Poskim hold sale and nullification do not help for chametz that is not his body, in such a case one may rely on the lenient); but b'dieved when the hour arrived that he cannot sell to the non-Jew as explained in siman 443, one may rely on the first reasoning and the Jew is not obligated to destroy from the world but may keep this chametz until the non-Jew owner comes to the city — even if he comes during Passover he returns his chametz immediately on arrival to remove Torah prohibition concern in this retention per the first view (only beware not to touch with his hands as in siman 444); and after Passover it is permitted to eat from it even if not returned to its owner.

(ט) And there are those who disagree — since the Jew did not accept liability and is exempt by Torah and kingdom law, the chametz is not considered his through compelling the non-Jew. Nevertheless the primary view is the first; see above s.k. 4; and if he transgressed and did not destroy and Passover passed, one need not be stringent even for eating — permitted.
