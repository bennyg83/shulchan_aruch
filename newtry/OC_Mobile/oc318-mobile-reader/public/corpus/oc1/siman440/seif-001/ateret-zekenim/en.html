(ב) And even if he returned and deposited it with another non-Jew, etc. — Hagahot Alfasi, beginning of Pesachim. And it appears to me that this is certainly obvious: if from the outset the non-Jew placed his chametz in the non-Jew's domain and there the Jew accepted liability, he need not destroy it, as Beit Yosef wrote that if he designated for him a corner, etc. — all the more so here, where from the outset he placed his chametz there even though the Jew accepted liability; and one may bring many proofs on this. And if he is not liable for it he is not obligated to destroy it, and he must make before him a partition ten handbreadths high; even if he designated a place for him one should be stringent and requires a partition ten handbreadths — Maharitz wrote, and this is his language in Machloket Michtav Michtav Kodesh: it appears to me one may not bring proof at all from there, for there the owner of the chametz is a Jew; if so, conversely there is room for concern for a weekday, etc. — if he does not say to the owners "here is yours before you" but the robber pays the owners in exchange for his chametz, then the owners of the chametz would have benefit from chametz that passed over Passover since they receive payment from the robber for his chametz; therefore we say he tells him "here is yours before you — go take your chametz," etc. — the robber would fulfill biur chametz and when burned he would pay as for lost property and there would be no concern that owners have benefit in the chametz through payment to them since the chametz is not in existence; the robber does not wish this and says conversely to the owners "here is the mitzvah of biur chametz before you and you fulfill it," etc. — not so here in our case, where if the Jew returns to the non-Jew he does not fulfill biur chametz and the Jew has benefit in returning the chametz to the non-Jew so he need not pay; and in truth there is no prohibition of benefit — understand well.
