Levush: And accepted, etc. This implies that he expressly accepted liability. If he did not explicitly accept it, it is not considered accepted. This also appears from Levush’s later words, “even if he did not accept,” etc. Tur, however, carefully writes that even if he is not legally liable, but the non-Jew is violent, etc. His meaning may be that the Jew accepted the deposit in the manner explained at the beginning of Choshen Mishpat, siman 291, by saying, “Place it before yourself,” in which case he is not liable. Alternatively, even if he explicitly said that he accepts no liability, this is ineffective because the non-Jew is violent. If nothing was stated, however, the Jew is liable. This is also implied by Shulchan Aruch, Beit Yosef, Bach, and Lechem Mishneh, chapter 4 of Hilchos Chametz. Although regarding a deposit belonging to a non-Jew we rule in Choshen Mishpat, end of siman 301, that the Jew is not liable without a formal acquisition, Magen Avraham there holds that he is liable for negligence even without an acquisition. Alternatively, regarding chametz his law is like that of a Jew, and he is no less stringent than a violent non-Jew.
