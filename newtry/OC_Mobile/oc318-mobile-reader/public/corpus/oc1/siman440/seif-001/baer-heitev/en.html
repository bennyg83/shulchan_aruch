He is obligated to remove it. If he transgressed and failed to remove it, Magen Avraham writes that if the non-Jew comes after Pesach, the Jew may return the chametz to him. Nevertheless, one might say that it is forbidden to return it for this reason, lest the non-Jew later sell it to a Jew, who would then stumble through chametz that remained over Pesach. Chok Yaakov disagrees and writes that the Jew may say to the non-Jew, “Your property is before you.” See there and Responsa Shevus Yaakov, part 2, siman 66.
Another non-Jew. If the second non-Jew accepted liability for theft and loss, Magen Avraham permits it. Chok Yaakov disagrees and rules stringently. See Pri Chadash. If the Jew deposited it with its owner, it is permitted. The same applies where the non-Jew placed his chametz in another non-Jew’s domain and the Jew accepted liability there. This is the consensus of the poskim.
And some disagree. After the fact one may rely upon the first opinion. This is also the conclusion of Chok Yaakov. If the Jew is liable under the law of the kingdom, he must remove the chametz according to all opinions. Chok Yaakov.
