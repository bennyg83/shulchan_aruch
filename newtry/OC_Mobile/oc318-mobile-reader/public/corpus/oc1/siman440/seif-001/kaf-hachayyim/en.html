1) [Se'if 1] If one is liable for it, etc. This is taught in Pesachim 5b: The verse states, 'Leaven shall not be seen by you.' You may not see your own, but you may see that of others or that which is consecrated. One might infer that one may hide it or accept deposits from a non-Jew; therefore, the verse states, 'It shall not be found in your homes.' The Gemara subsequently explains that the latter case is where one accepted liability for it, whereas the former case is where one did not accept liability for it. See there.
2) There. If one is liable for it, etc. For example, a Jew said to the non-Jew, 'If it is stolen or lost while in my possession, I will pay you,' and the non-Jew secured this undertaking through an act of symbolic acquisition so that the Jew could not retract. The same applies even without such an acquisition if the governing law requires the Jew to pay once the Jew explicitly made this undertaking when accepting the deposit. Chok Yaakov, subsection 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 9. Chok Yosef, subsection 1, however, writes that liability means liability under the governing law, because it is irrelevant whether the Jew would be liable under Jewish law; the non-Jew will certainly bring the claim under the non-Jew's law. See there. Birkei Yosef, subsection 1, writes likewise. It appears to me that everything depends upon the circumstances. If it is common for a non-Jew to bring such a claim before a Jewish court, and the Jew is liable under Jewish law, the Jew must eliminate the chametz. If it is not common for the non-Jew to bring such a claim except under the governing law, the Jew need eliminate it only when liable under the governing law.
3) There. For theft and loss. See Tosafos in the chapter HaUmanin and the chapter Shevuas HaDayanin, who write that according to Rashi one need not eliminate it unless one accepted liability even for unavoidable accidents. See Shach on Choshen Mishpat, siman 72, who writes that many authorities disagree with Rashi and labors to reconcile Rashi's words. See there. Chok Yaakov, subsection 2. The later authorities likewise agree with the ruling of the Shulchan Aruch.
4) There. Wherever it is within one's domain, etc. Regarding whether one must eliminate it when one designated a separate room for the non-Jew, see below, subsection 27.
