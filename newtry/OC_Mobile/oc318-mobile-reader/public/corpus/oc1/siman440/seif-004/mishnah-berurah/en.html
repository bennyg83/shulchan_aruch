(19) Although another accepted responsibility for it, etc. The substance of the property nevertheless belongs to the depositor. Above, in seif 1, acceptance of responsibility was treated as ownership only because responsibility creates a connection and legal interest in property that is not inherently his, and because of that interest the Torah obligates him to destroy it. The reverse is not logical: when the chametz itself belongs to him, another person’s acceptance of responsibility does not remove it from his domain.
(20) He transgresses regarding it. The text should read, “The depositor also transgresses regarding it.” The guardian also transgresses even though the chametz is not his, because he accepted responsibility and thereby acquired a legal interest in it, as explained in seif 1. What the guardian should do on erev Pesach when the depositor is absent is explained below in siman 443; see there.
