He need not remove it. During Pesach itself, however, he may not accept a deposit of chametz at all, even without accepting any liability. Rokeach, siman 167, writes likewise. If it was placed there without his permission, Rama explains how he should conduct himself. A partition ten handbreadths high. Magen Avraham writes that one should not make the partition from a sheet, because people pass beneath it. Covering it with an overturned utensil is insufficient here because the arrangement remains throughout Pesach, and the utensil may sometimes be removed for use. Rashi explains this. But if it is before Yom Tov. Before Yom Tov, when a partition can be made, one makes it. On Yom Tov, however, since it is only for one day and a partition may not be made, and since moving the chametz is also prohibited, covering it with an overturned utensil suffices. On Chol HaMoed he may move it and throw it out of the house through another object, or destroy it entirely if he is not obligated to compensate the non-Jew. There is no need to trouble oneself to make a partition on Chol HaMoed. Olas Shabbos writes similarly, and the matter is straightforward. This is unlike Magen Avraham, who writes that even on Chol HaMoed one should make a partition. His inference from Rashi has no basis. Rama’s wording also implies that a partition is made before Yom Tov, but not on Yom Tov or Chol HaMoed.
