So that he not forget and eat it. The unqualified language of the poskim implies that this is not limited to bread, which can be eaten immediately as it is when taken. It applies even to forms of chametz that cannot be eaten until after baking or cooking. We are concerned that he may forget, take it, and cook it, and a partition is therefore required. We do not say that during the time needed to prepare it he will remember that it is chametz. This is also somewhat implied by Rambam’s wording, that perhaps he will forget and make use of it. See there. Eshel Avraham of Butschatsch writes that one may be lenient in such a case where the flour is not definitely chametz but is only suspected of being chametz. See there, where he combines several leniencies.
Know further that even in a place where people refrain from non-Jewish bread, a partition is still required. We do not say that they are already accustomed to separating themselves from it. The Gemara said this only regarding consecrated chametz, because consecrated property is more severe, carries liability for misuse, and everyone is careful with it. Non-Jewish bread, however, is prohibited only as a customary stringency. So writes Rabbeinu Manoach. Maamar Mordechai agrees, unlike Re’em, who wished to be lenient. See there.
