The Magen Avraham wrote: And one who did not eat, etc. What is written in Shulchan Aruch, that on the first day one should say the berachah of sukkah and afterward Shehecheyanu, and on the second night one should say Shehecheyanu and afterward the berachah of sukkah, is the opinion of the Rosh. He wrote the reason: by law, it would have been proper to recite Shehecheyanu at the time of making the sukkah, for making the sukkah. But since on Yom Tov one must in any case say Shehecheyanu because of the festival, as on every Yom Tov, therefore we do not recite Shehecheyanu at the time of making it, and we rely on the Shehecheyanu that he will say on Yom Tov at the time of Kiddush; that Shehecheyanu blessing will count for two things, for making the sukkah and for the festival. So it is in the Gemara. Therefore on the first night he says, after Kiddush, the berachah of sukkah and afterward Shehecheyanu, so that the Shehecheyanu blessing will count for the festival and also for the sukkah. This is not so on the second night: although because of the doubt of the day he must say Shehecheyanu for the festival, since perhaps yesterday was weekday and he did not fulfill his obligation with the Shehecheyanu he said yesterday, nevertheless for the sukkah he need not recite Shehecheyanu, for even if yesterday was weekday, he fulfilled the obligation with the Shehecheyanu that he said yesterday. After all, it would have been proper to recite Shehecheyanu over the sukkah at the time of making it, which was certainly weekday, as above. Since on the second day the Shehecheyanu refers only to the festival alone, therefore one should first say Shehecheyanu and afterward the berachah of sukkah, in order to place Shehecheyanu next to Kiddush, which comes for the sake of the festival, and not interrupt between them, since there is no need for an interruption. Therefore the Magen Avraham wrote: one who did not eat in the sukkah on the first night. If so, although he recited Shehecheyanu yesterday, since he did not eat in the sukkah, the Shehecheyanu of yesterday did not exempt the Shehecheyanu of sukkah. If so, on the second night, when he eats in the sukkah, the Shehecheyanu blessing that he says will exempt both: the Shehecheyanu of the festival and the Shehecheyanu of sukkah. Therefore he has the law of the first night, and he should first recite sukkah and afterward Shehecheyanu, so that the Shehecheyanu blessing will count for both, for the festival and for the sukkah. The Bach wrote, etc. So too it is implied in the Gemara, for in Sukkah 56a Rav said: sukkah and afterward Shehecheyanu; and Rabbah bar bar Channah said: Shehecheyanu and afterward sukkah. We conclude there that Rav's reason is that for this reason it is proper to precede the berachah of sukkah, which is the obligation of the day, and is preferable to the berachah of Shehecheyanu. We rule like Rav. According to the reason stated in the Talmud, that the obligation of the day is preferable, there is no distinction between the first day and the second day; in all cases it is proper to precede sukkah to Shehecheyanu. In truth, the words of the Rosh require explanation, for he wrote a different reason than that written in the Talmud, but this is not the place. The Shelah wrote: one who keeps two days of Yom Kippur, etc. So it is in the Shelah, but he did not write the reason for the matter. The Rosh wrote in the name of R. Shimshon of Eivra that on the second night too one should recite sukkah and afterward Shehecheyanu, because the Shehecheyanu refers to both. Just as the Shehecheyanu of the festival must be said also on the second night because of the doubt of the day, perhaps yesterday was weekday and he did not fulfill the Shehecheyanu of the festival yesterday, so too one must recite it for the sukkah also on the second night because of the doubt of the day, perhaps yesterday was weekday and he did not fulfill the obligation with the Shehecheyanu that he said yesterday. Although it would have been proper to recite it at the time of making it, which is certainly weekday, the time of making is different, for in any case it is the time of making; this is not so on the first night, for if it was weekday, it was not the time of making it, and the time of fulfillment of the mitzvah is worse, and he did not fulfill the obligation. Since he must recite Shehecheyanu over both, over the festival and over the sukkah, one should precede sukkah before Shehecheyanu, as on the first day. The Rosh disagrees and wrote as follows: it is better when he recites Shehecheyanu while sitting in it for the sake of the mitzvah than if he recited it after making it, and he fulfilled the obligation with the Shehecheyanu that he said on the first night even if it was weekday. The Rosh, etc. The main dispute is regarding lulav, and the same applies regarding sukkah. The Shelah holds that specifically for us, who are expert in the fixing of the month and know that the first day is the primary day, and only because of the custom of our fathers do we observe two days, since we know that the first day is primary, we may well say the Rosh's reasoning that this is better than if he had recited it at the time of making it. But those who fast two days of Yom Kippur necessarily hold that although we are expert in the fixing of the month, we make ourselves as though we are not expert. For this reason we fast on Yom Kippur only one day, although every Yom Tov we observe two days: since we are expert in the fixing of the month, and we observe two days on every Yom Tov only because of the custom of our fathers, they did not wish to be stringent on Yom Kippur because of a mere custom and decree a harsh decree on the community to fast two days. They left the matter at the law, that one day is sufficient since we are expert. But those who observe two days of Yom Kippur and are stringent even to fast two days necessarily hold that by law one should make two days, as though we are not expert. Therefore the Shelah finds the reasoning of R. Shimshon of Eivra logical: the Shehecheyanu said on the first night does not help even for the sukkah if it was weekday, for it is worse than if he recited it at the time of making it. But in my humble opinion, the Shelah agrees that even those who observe two days of Yom Kippur should not say Shehecheyanu on the second day, as stated in siman 662, and for the reason explained there. Although according to them one may question that reason, nevertheless they should not recite, for this reason, an unnecessary berachah. This is not so with sukkah, where he does not add a berachah, for in any case he recites Shehecheyanu because of the festival; there is only the issue of precedence and interruption, and for that we are not concerned. So it appears.
