(Seif katan 2) "All, etc." And Rashi explained, etc.—that is, he explained that what was said, "enough to tie them all," means enough to tie the severed threads. And this measure is greater than those who say in the second version of the passage: "each one separately."

Therefore they ruled stringently—that is, according to Rashi's explanation, for this reason the decisors ruled that we require kedei aniva for all the severed threads together, for this is a greater stringency than those who say "each one separately."

(ג) But Mordechai, etc. This is Mordechai’s wording as brought in Beit Yosef, and for me, etc., he is uncertain about what the Gemara says in one version: “each one separately”—whether this means each of the tzitzis strings requires kedei aniva individually, or whether it means enough to tie one string alone, end quote. Rabbeinu Yerucham explained Mordechai’s uncertainty as: perhaps it suffices if he can tie that snapped string by itself alone, or we require enough to tie the snapped string together with another string—see there. Magen Avraham: this explanation does not seem correct, since Mordechai is a decisor and not a commentator; if so, for practical law what difference does this uncertainty make in how he explains “each one separately,” since we rule stringently on the stringent side of the doubt that we require enough to tie them all. Therefore Magen Avraham explained Mordechai’s uncertainty: if “each one separately” is explained as the simple understanding—that there is enough to tie the snapped string alone—and then it is a greater stringency than saying to tie them all together, and we rule thus stringently; or if “each one separately” means we require enough to tie the snapped string around each of the snapped strings, and this is a greater stringency than in the first instance where they said to tie them all together, and accordingly we rule to be stringent with kedei aniva for each one separately, meaning enough to tie around each of the snapped strings, each one by itself.
