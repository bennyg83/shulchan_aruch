(Seif katan 2) All, etc. Rashi explains, etc. Meaning, he interprets the phrase "enough to tie them all" as enough to tie together all the severed strings. This is a greater measure than the second possibility, namely that each severed string be sufficient by itself.
Therefore they ruled stringently. Meaning, according to Rashi's interpretation the authorities ruled that enough must remain to tie all the severed strings together, because this is more stringent than requiring each one individually.
But Mordechai, etc. Mordechai, as cited by Beit Yosef, writes: "I am uncertain whether the Gemara's alternative, 'each one individually,' means that every tzitzis string must itself retain enough to tie a bow, or that enough must remain to tie one string by itself." Beit Yosef explains Mordechai's doubt as whether it is enough to tie the severed string to itself, or whether there must be enough to tie the severed string together with another string. Magen Avraham finds this interpretation difficult. Since Mordechai is issuing a halachic ruling rather than merely explaining the Gemara, what practical difference does the doubt make? We rule stringently in accordance with the possibility requiring enough to tie all of them. Magen Avraham therefore explains Mordechai's doubt differently. Perhaps "each one individually" means, in its simple sense, that the severed string itself must be long enough to form a bow, in which case tying all of them together is the more stringent possibility and we follow it. Alternatively, it may mean that the severed string must be long enough to be tied around each of the other severed strings individually, which is more stringent than tying all of them together at once. According to this possibility, we must be stringent and require enough to tie the severed string around every other severed string separately.
