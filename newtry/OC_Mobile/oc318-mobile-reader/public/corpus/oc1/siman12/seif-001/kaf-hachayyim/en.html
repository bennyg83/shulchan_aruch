1) [Se'if 1.] If all the tzitzit strings on one corner were severed but enough remained to tie all the severed strings together, it is valid. At first glance, if all eight ends were severed, it would appear sufficient to be able to tie four severed strings together; it should not be necessary to tie all eight together, because Shulchan Aruch explains that we require only four strings with kedei anivah remaining. However, one may say that although four strings with kedei anivah suffice, the measure of that remnant is determined by the amount needed to tie eight strings together. See Levush. Zechor LeAvraham, part 3, ot 90, writes likewise: kedei anivah is enough to tie all eight strings together, which is more than the amount needed to tie each separately. Even if only one string was severed, the remnant is measured by whether it could tie all eight strings together. Magen Avraham, se'if katan 2, writes in the name of Mordechai that the severed string must be capable of being tied around each of the other severed strings separately. This is a longer measure than tying all the severed strings together, and he therefore writes that one should be stringent. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ot 1, writes likewise. This also appears to be the view of Yad Aharon in Hagahot Beit Yosef and Pri Megadim in Eshel Avraham, ot 2. Shulchan Aruch HaRav writes there that even if only seven ends were severed, the severed end belonging to one of the four long strings must retain three times the amount of kedei anivah around a string. If it retains that measure, it is valid. See Yoreh De'ah, siman 6, and Chesed LaAlafim, ot 1. Chayei Adam, klal 11, ot 20, writes that kedei anivah is four thumbbreadths. Although the Gemara and the halakhic authorities imply that it is less than four thumbbreadths; for according to Rabbeinu Yonah, kedei anivah is measured from the wound section, and that entire section is only four thumbbreadths; since we are not expert in the measure of kedei anivah, it appears to him that four thumbbreadths from the loose section suffice even according to Rashi. Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, siman 9, ot 13, writes likewise. In my humble opinion, however, kedei anivah is no more than two and two-thirds fingerbreadths. Rambam writes in chapter 1 of the laws of tzitzit, halakhah 6, as cited by the author above in siman 11, se'if 4, that the eight strings must be at least four fingerbreadths long. In halakhah 9 he writes: “The general rule is that one should intend the wound section to constitute one third and the loose section two thirds.” If the total string length is four fingerbreadths and one third is made into the wound section, two and two-thirds fingerbreadths remain in the loose section. Although the author cites an opinion there that the strings must be twelve thumbbreadths long, that applies specifically to their total length, not to the measure of kedei anivah; there is no reason to say they disagree about that measure, and this is clear. Therefore, even though we are not expert in the measure of kedei anivah, we need not estimate it as more than this. See Pitchei Olam, ot 3, who writes in the name of Beit Hillel that one should not be stringent beyond two thumbbreadths. See Beit Yosef, Ma'amar Mordechai, ot 1, and Yeshuot Yaakov, ot 1.
2) There: If kedei anivah did not remain even on one string that was completely severed, it is invalid. This means either that one string was severed at the point where it joins the others at the corner, leaving only three strings, or that both ends of one string were severed and neither retained kedei anivah. Beit Yosef. See Machazik Berachah, ot 1, and what we wrote above in siman 8, ot 37.
3) If both ends of one string were severed and neither retained kedei anivah, but the remnants of the two ends together amount to kedei anivah, Beit Yosef was uncertain about the law. He writes that one may say that whenever kedei anivah does not remain in one place, the remnant is not discernible and is of no significance; it is considered nonexistent. Levush likewise rules that it is invalid, as does Eliyahu Rabbah, se'if katan 2. Pri Megadim, commenting on Magen Avraham, se'if katan 3, writes that it is invalid because of the doubt. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ot 2; Shatilei Zeitim, ot 5, in the name of Or Zarua; and Zechor LeAvraham, part 3, ot 90, rule likewise. Eliyahu Rabbah writes there that even if a double doubt exists; perhaps the two severed ends do not belong to the same string, and perhaps their remnants combine; it is nevertheless invalid; see there. Pri Megadim writes there that this also appears to apply when each string separately retains kedei anivah. Pri Megadim explains that the authorities were so stringent because tzitzit are readily available. Shatilei Zeitim likewise writes there that we do not apply a double doubt in this case, contrary to Responsa Beit Yaakov. Eliyahu Rabbah also writes there that the words of Responsa Beit Yaakov are not conclusive.
