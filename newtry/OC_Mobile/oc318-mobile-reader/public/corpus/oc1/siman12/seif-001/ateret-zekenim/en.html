If two ends were severed on opposite sides, such that one did not retain enough to tie a bow while the other did retain enough to tie a bow, it is valid in any event. Beit Yosef; Shulchan Aruch.
According to our practice of carefully marking the four ends when making the tzitzis, etc., the same applies if all four ends on one side were severed and none retained enough to tie a bow: it is valid. So concludes Beit Yosef. One should not cut the ends of the strings with a knife, because it is stated, “You shall not raise iron upon them.” Rather, he should sever them with his teeth, which number thirty-two, and occupy himself with the strings, which likewise number thirty-two. Mateh Moshe and the glosses of Maharshal in the name of Rabbi David bar Menachem. Some have the custom to kiss the tzitzis whenever they see them. I likewise observed elevated individuals who cherished the mitzvos and kissed the sukkah when entering and leaving it, and similarly the four species of the lulav. All this is done to endear the mitzvos. They likewise kiss the matzos and maror on Pesach. Shelah. The word tzitzis is also an acronym for: “A righteous person should inspect his tzitzis constantly.”
