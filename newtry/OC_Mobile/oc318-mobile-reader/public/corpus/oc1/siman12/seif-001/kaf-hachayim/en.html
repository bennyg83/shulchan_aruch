1) [Seif 1] If all the strings of the corner were severed and enough remained in them to tie all the severed threads together—it is valid. Apparently it seems that if all eight ends were severed, it suffices to tie the four severed threads together, and we do not require kedei aniva of eight threads together, since we require only four threads that have kedei aniva, as explained in Shulchan Aruch. However, one may say that although four threads that have kedei aniva suffice, nevertheless the measure of tying is upon eight threads together—for Levush, see there; and likewise Zekanim LaChayim, part 3, letter 90, that the measure of tying is enough to tie all eight threads together, which is more than tying each one independently. And even when one thread was severed, one must estimate accordingly enough to be able to tie all eight threads together—so ends. And Magen Avraham, seif katan 2, wrote in the name of the Mordechai that one must tie the one severed thread around each of the severed threads independently—and this measure is longer than tying all the severed threads together; and he wrote that therefore one should be stringent. And likewise Rav Zalman, letter 1; and likewise appears the view of Yad Aharon in Hagahot Beit Yosef and Peri Megadim, Aishel Avraham, letter 2; and Rav Zalman wrote there that even if only seven threads were severed, there must be on the severed thread from the four long threads twice kedei aniva around a thread; and if it has this measure, it is valid—so ends. [And see Yoreh De'ah, siman 6, and Chesed LeAvraham, letter 1.] And Chayei Adam, general rule 11, letter 20, wrote that the measure of tying is four thumbbreadths; and although from the Gemara and decisors it appears that it is less than four thumbbreadths—for according to Ri, kedei aniva is from the gedil, and the entire gedil is only four thumbbreadths—since we are not expert in kedei aniva, it appears to me that two thumbbreadths from the anaf suffice even according to Rashi—so ends. And likewise Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, siman 9, letter 13. But in my opinion it appears that kedei aniva is no more than two fingerbreadths and two-thirds, for Rambam wrote in chapter 1 of Hilchot Tzitzis, halacha 6, and the Mechaber brought it above, siman 11, seif 4, that the length of the eight threads is no less than four fingerbreadths; and in halacha 9 he wrote: "The general rule is that one should intend the wrapped portion to be one-third and the anaf two-thirds"—so ends. And if so, since the entire length of the threads is four fingerbreadths, when one makes from them one-third gedil, the remainder of the anaf is two fingerbreadths and two-thirds. And although the Mechaber brought there in siman 11 that the threads must be twelve thumbbreadths—that is specifically regarding the length of the threads, but not regarding the measure of tying, for there is no reason they would differ on this, and it is obvious. And if so, even according to us, who are not expert in the measure of tying, one need not estimate more than this. And see Petchei Olam, letter 3, that he wrote in the name of Bach that one should not be more stringent than two thumbbreadths. And see Beit Yosef and Maamar Mordechai, letter 1, and Birkas Yitzchak, letter 1.
