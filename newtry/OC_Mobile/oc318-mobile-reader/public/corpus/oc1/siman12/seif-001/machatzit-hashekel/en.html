(Seif katan 1) Enough, etc. The principle is as follows. The first opinion in the Shulchan Aruch is that of Rosh, and the second is Rabbeinu Tam's view. Their dispute depends upon the statement of the sons of Rabbi Chiya that remnants of techeiles are valid, meaning that when the strings are severed, what remains is called the remnant. Shmuel states that the required measure is enough to tie a bow. Rosh maintains that both the techeiles strings and the white strings are referred to collectively as techeiles because techeiles is primary. Thus, when the sons of Rabbi Chiya said that remnants of techeiles are valid, they meant that even if all the strings, both techeiles and white, were severed, they remain valid provided that each string retains enough to tie a bow, as Shmuel stated. Rabbeinu Tam maintains that they spoke specifically of techeiles: if the two techeiles strings were severed but retained enough to tie a bow, it is valid because the two white strings remain entirely whole. If three strings were severed, however, even if each retained enough to tie a bow, it is invalid because only one whole string remains. This is also cited by Taz.
