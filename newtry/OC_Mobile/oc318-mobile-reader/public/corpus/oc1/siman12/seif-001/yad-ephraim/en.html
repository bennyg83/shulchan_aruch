In Taz, se'if katan 3: A question concerning a tzitzis string, etc.; this implies the reverse, etc. In my humble opinion, this does not compel us to follow the reverse case. If a string was too long and was shortened, and its excessive length disqualified it, the rule of ta'aseh v'lo min ha'asui applies: The tzitzis with which he now seeks to fulfill his obligation had already been made while invalid. No new act was performed upon the tzitzis themselves; he merely cut away what disqualified them. The same applies when tzitzis were placed on a three-cornered garment, or when the ends of the strings were cut after they had been tied. If, however, a string was too short and he lengthened it, this is not called min ha'asui. The tzitzis with which he now fulfills his obligation did not exist in their present form while invalid; only now, through a new act performed upon them; lengthening them; did they become valid. This is their required act of making: Through the new act performed upon the tzitzis themselves, lengthening them, they became fit for fulfillment of the mitzvah.
