The severed strings together. See Magen Avraham concerning the wording of Mordechai. He writes that one should be stringent and require enough length to tie a bow with each individual string. See seif 2.
It is valid. If he removed the tzitzis, it is forbidden to place them upon another tallis, because that would be considered an initial act. Taz.
That was entirely severed. Meaning, neither of its two ends retained enough to tie a bow, even by combining them; it is then certainly invalid. If the two ends together retain enough to tie a bow, Magen Avraham remains uncertain whether it is invalid. Taz writes that every God-fearing person should therefore inspect, when checking the tzitzis, the point where they first emerge from the hole and downward. If there is a tear there, the string is certainly considered entirely severed. Question: If a tzitzis string was severed and then retied, does the knot restore it as a complete string? Taz rules that if it was already too short and invalid when the tzitzis were made, tying it afterward does not help, because this is “you shall make,” and not something made through an invalid state. If, however, it was valid when made and afterward became severed and invalid, it becomes valid again if retied. The same applies if, before making the tzitzis, one string was severed and retied, and afterward used for the tzitzis; it is valid. See there. Magen Avraham writes the same in siman 15, seif katan 1.
Two ends. Meaning, neither retained enough to tie a bow. If the two ends together retain enough to tie a bow, I wrote in seif katan 3 that the matter remains doubtful. Although there is a double doubt, perhaps they are ends of two different strings, and even if they are from one string, perhaps they combine, this does not suffice. Bnei Chayei, Levush, and Olas Tamid. See responsa Beis Yaakov, siman 66.
If he was not careful. This is difficult, for the ruling should apply all the more when he was careful, as the gloss states afterward. The answer is that the wording “if he was not careful” was used because of the conclusion: “But if only two ends were cut, they are valid in every case.” That applies specifically when he did not keep track of them. If he did keep track, then when two ends on the same side were cut, it is valid even if they did not retain enough to tie a bow, because the other end of each string remains. If, however, two ends were severed on opposite sides, even if each retained enough to tie a bow they are invalid, because perhaps they are the two ends of one string. Magen Avraham.
