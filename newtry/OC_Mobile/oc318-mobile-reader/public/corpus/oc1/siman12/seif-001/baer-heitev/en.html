The severed [threads] together. Magen Avraham [on] what he wrote in the language of the Mordechai, that one should be stringent and require enough to tie on each thread independently; see seif 2.

Kosher. And if he untied the tzitzit, it is forbidden to place them on another tallit, because it would be ab initio [forbidden]. Taz.

Wholly severed. That is to say, that on two heads there did not remain enough to tie even through combining — then it is certainly invalid. And if on both of them there remained enough to tie through combining, there is room to doubt whether it is invalid; Magen Avraham. And the Taz wrote that therefore it is fitting for everyone who fears Heaven that at the time of inspection he also examine initially the origin of the tzitzit from the hole downward, for if there is a tear there, it is certainly wholly severed. Question: a tzitzit thread that was severed and then tied again — does it help that it became a complete thread through tying? The Taz ruled that if it was so at the time of making the tzitzit that it was short in a manner that rendered it invalid, what he later did and tied does not help, because it is ta'aseh v'lo min he'asuy. But if it was valid at the time of making and afterwards was severed and invalidated, it is valid if they tied it again; and likewise if before making the tzitzit one thread was severed and tied, and afterwards he made tzitzit from it — it is valid, see there. And so wrote Magen Avraham, siman 15, seif katan 1.

Two heads. That is to say, and there did not remain enough to tie; and if on both there remained enough to tie through combining, I wrote in seif katan 3 that there is room to doubt, even though there is a double doubt whether they are two threads or one, and even if you say one — perhaps they combine — nevertheless it does not help. Benei Chayya and Levush and Eliyah Rabbah; see responsa Beit Yaakov, siman 66.

If he did not mark [the four heads]. It is difficult, for all the more so if he marked, as stated in the gloss afterwards. And one can say that he took "if he did not mark" because of the conclusion: but if only two heads were cut, they are valid according to all opinions — that is specifically when he did not mark. But if he marked, if two heads were cut from one side it is valid even if not enough remains on both, for the other head remains. But if two were severed from both sides and not enough remains on both, they are invalid, lest they be one thread. Magen Avraham.
