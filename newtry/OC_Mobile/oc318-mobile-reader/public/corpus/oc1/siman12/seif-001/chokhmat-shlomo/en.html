Se'if 1: If all the strings of the corner were severed, etc. See Taz, who writes that all mitzvos were not compared to tefillin, etc. A scholar challenged me from Makos 11b, where the Gemara says that the entire Torah was compared to tefillin, and we follow that opinion there. I answered that this is no difficulty. 'The entire Torah' does not mean all mitzvos; it means that all passages of the Torah were compared to the passage of tefillin. Rashi's wording, s.v. 'So that Hashem's Torah shall be, etc.,' indicates this: 'The beginning of the verse discusses tefillin, and Torah is compared to tefillin.' Rashi does not say that all mitzvos were compared to tefillin, but that Torah was compared to tefillin; Torah literally. Even regarding a Torah scroll, this applies only to the beginning of its manufacture, such as sewing it with sinews, not to other details that arise afterward. Taz's intent is therefore correct. We learn in Keilim 28:7: If one patched a garment along one side, it is not joined; along two opposite sides, it is joined. If he made it like the Greek letter gamma, R' Akiva declares it susceptible to tumah and the Sages declare it pure. The halachah follows the Sages. See the commentaries there. It requires study why the poskim did not explain the law this way here regarding tzitzis, and likewise in Yoreh De'ah regarding kilayim: attachment along one side or in a gamma shape is not a joining, whereas attachment along two opposite sides is. Although two stitches constitute joining for kilayim, one may answer that there both fabrics are fully equal, and two stitches join them; where one is a large garment and a small patch is attached, it is joined only if sewn along two opposite sides. Alternatively, that case concerns wool directly joined to linen, for which Scripture decreed that two stitches constitute joining; that is the form of shaatnez the Torah prohibited. If, however, a patch containing wool on one side is joined to a garment containing linen on one side, it may be joined only when sewn along two opposite sides. This is certainly relevant to tzitzis: If one attaches a patch to one side of a garment and places tzitzis on it, does he fulfill his obligation? Apparently, since it is not deemed joined for tumah, whether leniently or stringently, it is likewise not joined for tzitzis. It requires study why the poskim left the matter unstated.
