(Seif 1) If all the strings of the corner were severed, etc. I saw: See in the Taz, who wrote that all mitzvos were not compared to tefillin, etc. And a certain scholar raised a difficulty to me on this from tractate Makos [folio 11b], where it says that the entire Torah was compared to tefillin, and we rule like this view there. And I answered him that by the law, when it says "the entire Torah" it does not mean all mitzvos; rather the intent is that all the parshiyot in the Torah were compared to the parshah of tefillin—and likewise the wording of Rashi, s.v. "so that the Torah of the Lord, etc.," teaches; and the beginning of the verse speaks of tefillin, and the Torah was compared to tefillin. And since Rashi did not write "and all mitzvos were compared to tefillin," but only "and the Torah was compared to tefillin"—this means Torah itself; and even Torah itself means only at the beginning of their making, such as sewing them with sinew, which constitutes the beginning of its making, and not for other details that were innovated afterwards. And the Taz's intent comes out well—delve and understand. And behold, we have the text in chapter 28 of Kelim (Mishnah 7): A patch on a garment from one side is not a connection; from two sides, one opposite the other, it is a connection. If one made it like a gamma—R' Akiva renders it impure and the Sages render it pure, and the halacha follows the Sages. And see in the commentaries there what they explained on this. And it requires further study why the decisors did not explain thus here regarding tzitzis, and likewise in Yoreh De'ah regarding kilayim—that from one side, or like a gamma, it is not a connection, and from two sides one opposite the other it is a connection. And although in kilayim even two stitches are a connection, one may say one reason—that there it deals with both being exactly equal, and in that case two stitches are a connection; but if one is a large garment and he attached a small piece to it, it is not a connection unless he sewed two sides one opposite the other. And one may say that there it deals with connecting wool and linen—for such is a decree of Scripture that with two stitches there is connection, as is the matter of shaatnez that the Torah stated. But in connecting a patch that has wool on one side to a garment that has linen on one side, one may say well that it is not a connection except from two sides one opposite the other; and all the more so in tzitzis, where it matters whether if he attached a patch to a garment on one side and placed tzitzis in it, whether he fulfills with it eighteen tzitzis or not. And according to what appears, since for impurity it is not a connection, whether for leniency or stringency, likewise for tzitzis it is not a connection. And it requires further study why the decisors concluded thus—delve and understand.
