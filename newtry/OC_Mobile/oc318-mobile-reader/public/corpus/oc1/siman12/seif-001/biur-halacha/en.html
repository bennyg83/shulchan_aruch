If they were severed, etc., and there remained in them, etc. See Mishnah Berurah. What I wrote, that there must remain enough to tie a bow around all the strings, follows the wording of Semag cited by Beit Yosef, although the Taz's wording is somewhat strained according to this. I did not cite Magen Avraham's view, which disputes the Mechaber and requires enough length to tie each of the strings individually. Even if we assume that Mordechai's meaning is as Magen Avraham explains, Rashi and Semag disagree with Mordechai, and Rosh also clearly explains like Rashi, namely that tying all the severed strings together requires the greater measure. Mordechai himself also appears uncertain regarding his interpretation. We should therefore not set aside the clear view of all these great authorities because of his doubt. See also Maharam Banet's commentary to Mordechai, which adduces proof for Beit Yosef's interpretation. It is therefore clear that one should not be more stringent than the ruling of the Shulchan Aruch. I later saw that Siddur Derech HaChayim likewise records the Shulchan Aruch's view as the halachah. Chayei Adam went too far in writing that the measure of enough to tie a bow is approximately four thumbbreadths. According to Rashi on Menachos 41b, the loose section of the tzitzis is four thumbbreadths. The Gemara states there that the minimum remnant of tzitzis is any amount, referring to the severed remnants, as Rashi explains. It also states on 39a that the required measure applies initially, but at the end any remnant suffices, provided that enough remains to tie a bow. It necessarily follows that this measure cannot be more than two thumbbreadths, for a majority of the original measure would not be called a remnant or any amount. Thus even according to Rashi, who is stringent that the bow must be tied with the loose section, the measure cannot exceed two thumbbreadths. This is all the more evident according to Rambam and the first interpretation of Rashi on Bechoros 39b, s.v. "One," who maintain that the combined measure of the wound and loose sections is four thumbbreadths, a view followed without qualification by the Mechaber above in siman 11, seif 4. According to them, the amount sufficient to tie a bow is certainly less than two thumbbreadths. It therefore appears that two thumbbreadths certainly suffice even according to Rashi, and we have found no other authorities who dispute him regarding this measure. Moreover, Beit Yosef, as cited by Eliyah Rabbah, writes that the halachah follows Rif and Rosh, according to whom it is enough to tie one string by itself, which is less than the measure stated in the Shulchan Aruch, and in a case of necessity we rely upon them. It therefore appears certain that one need never be stringent beyond two thumbbreadths. See there.
Even one string, etc. The same rule applies if the tzitzis string was severed within the wound section between the knots. If one string was severed, it is valid; if two strings were severed, it is invalid.
That was entirely severed. See Mishnah Berurah regarding combining the lengths. It appears that if together they contain enough to tie a bow, etc., one may rely upon this, because even without combining them there is fundamentally room to rely upon that measure alone, as I wrote above in the name of Beit Yosef and Eliyah Rabbah, especially where combining them produces a substantial measure.
Lest one string was severed. See Mishnah Berurah, seif katan 7, regarding the statement that tying helps after the tzitzis was made validly, meaning where one end was severed, etc. At first glance, even if that end was severed after completion of the first section, meaning after its windings and upper knot, tying the string should help. Since we rule that even one completed section is valid, the tzitzis has already been made validly and the severed remainder is included in the law of remnants. However, according to what I wrote above in siman 11, seif 4, Biur Halachah at the end of s.v. "Longer," so long as one is still engaged in making the tzitzis and intends to complete it, it may not yet be regarded as having valid remnants during its construction. The same uncertainty may therefore apply here. The practical ruling requires study.
According to Rabbeinu Tam we do not validate, etc. This applies only when each of the remaining ends is twelve thumbbreadths long. Above in siman 11, seif 4, the Mechaber rules without qualification like the first opinion. Here, however, he is discussing Rabbeinu Tam, who himself requires twelve thumbbreadths, as Beit Yosef writes there in the name of Tosafos. Furthermore, even the Mechaber certainly agrees that initially it is proper to take this opinion into account. Since the Mechaber cites Rabbeinu Tam here only as an initial stringency, as he himself writes at the end of the seif, he likewise presents Rabbeinu Tam's measure for this purpose.
The halachah follows the first opinion. Peri Megadim, Eshel Avraham, seif katan 7, indicates that one may recite a blessing over them. The same is evident from Magen Avraham, seif katan 7, contrary to Chayei Adam.
The custom follows Rabbeinu Tam. Know that according to Rabbeinu Tam, even if the three strings were not severed but merely became shortened so that they are no longer twelve thumbbreadths long, they are invalid in every case.
