"If they were severed, etc., and enough remained in them, etc." — See Mishna Berurah in what he wrote on making a knot for all the threads — this is from the language of Semag brought in Beit Yosef (and the Taz's wording is somewhat strained on this). I did not cite Magen Avraham's view, who disagrees with the Mechaber and holds that there must be enough on each thread to tie a knot on each thread independently, because in truth, even if we grant that the Mordechai's explanation is as he says, nevertheless Rashi and Semag disagree with the Mordechai, and in the Rosh it is proven that he explains like Rashi that all of them together are the [minimum] measure, which is larger. And in the Mordechai itself it appears that his explanation is not resolved; therefore we do not leave his doubt because of the clarity of all these many authorities. And see also in Biur Maharam Netiv on the Mordechai, who brings proof for Beit Yosef's explanation; therefore it is obvious that one should not be more stringent than the Shulchan Aruch's ruling. Afterwards I saw in Siddur Derech HaChayim that he also copies as law the Shulchan Aruch's view. And Chayei Adam went beyond measure in writing that kedei anivah is approximately four thumbbreadths; see there his reason. But in truth, according to what Rashi holds in Menachot 41b, that the measure of the branches of the tzitzit is four thumbbreadths, and he said there, "there is no tzitzit except from something and a stump," referring to what Rashi explained there; and he also said there on folio 39a, s.v. ... (that we require a measure for tzitzit and it refers to the branch for Rashi) at its beginning, but at its end, "the remainder of its stumps — any amount," only that we require that they have enough to tie a bow — then necessarily kedei anivah would be at most no more than two thumbbreadths, for this would not be called "remainder" or "any amount" since it is most of the measure. And thus it emerges that even according to Rashi, who is stringent that we require kedei anivah in the branch, as written in seif 3 according to him — nevertheless kedei anivah is at most no more than two thumbbreadths, and especially according to Rambam and the first law in Rashi in Bechorot 39b, s.v. "one," who holds that the measure of tzitzit — gedil and anaf — is in total about four thumbbreadths, and the Mechaber silently follows him above, siman 11 seif 4 — certainly kedei anivah is less than two thumbbreadths. Therefore it appears that at least two thumbbreadths suffice for Rashi, and we do not find an authority who disagrees with him on kedei anivah, especially as Beit Yosef wrote and Eliyah Rabbah brought that for law we follow Rif and Rosh that enough to tie on one thread independently suffices — and this is smaller than the measure stated in Shulchan Aruch; and when not possible we rely on them. Therefore apparently one certainly should not be more stringent than two thumbbreadths in any case. See.

"Even on one thread, etc." — The same applies if the tzitzit were severed at the place of the gedil between the knots: if one thread — valid; if two threads — invalid.

"Wholly severed." — See Mishna Berurah in what he wrote regarding combining; and it appears that if there is enough to tie, etc., because in any case from the outset of the law one may rely on this alone, as written above in the name of Beit Yosef and Eliyah Rabbah, especially if through combining there is a large measure.

"Lest one thread was severed." — See Mishna Berurah in seif katan 7 in what he wrote. And likewise after it has been made valid — namely, if one head was severed, etc. — apparently it seems that even if this head was severed after completing the first chulyah — namely, the wrapping and knot above it — the tying of the thread helps, since we rule that even if one wrapped one chulyah it is valid; automatically it is already considered made valid and is in the category of stumps. However, according to what I wrote above in siman 11 seif 4 in Biur Halacha at the end of s.v. "longer," see there — that as long as he is engaged in the mitzvah of making tzitzit and intends to complete it, perhaps we cannot call it at the time of making by the name of stumps; therefore perhaps here too it is such a case, and it requires further study for practical application.

"And Rabbeinu Tam does not validate, etc." — That each of the heads is twelve thumbbreadths long. See above siman 11 seif 4, that the Mechaber there follows the first view; but since here the discussion is about Rabbeinu Tam, and he indeed held above twelve thumbbreadths, as Beit Yosef wrote in that passage, in the name of Tosafot — and also that ab initio the Mechaber in that seif certainly agrees that it is good to be concerned for his view — and here the Mechaber also brings him only regarding being concerned ab initio, as he himself wrote at the end of the seif — therefore he also brings Rabbeinu Tam's view for this matter.

"And the halacha follows the first view." — See in Peri Megadim, Aishel Avraham, seif katan 7—it is evident from his words that it is permitted to recite a blessing upon it; and likewise it is evident from Magen Avraham, seif katan 7, not as written in Chayei Adam.

"And we practice like Rabbeinu Tam." — Know that according to Rabbeinu Tam, even if the three threads were not severed but only shortened such that their length is not twelve thumbbreadths, it is likewise invalid in all cases.
