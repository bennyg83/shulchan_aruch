If. See Taz, who cites Menachos 38b. In my commentary to the Tur I explained at length that there are four interpretations here, and this is not the place to elaborate. Taz asks about the Tur's statement that according to Rabbeinu Tam, if two ends were severed and each contains enough to tie a bow, it is valid. This implies that each end independently requires the full measure, because combining them does not help, as Beit Yosef explains. Yet if only one end was severed, enough to tie a bow should suffice in any event. Furthermore, what does the Tur mean by "According to this"? According to the first opinion it is obvious that even if all eight ends were severed, it is valid provided that each retained enough to tie a bow. Beit Yosef discusses this at length. Taz explains that the Tur is not returning to the lenient ruling, but means as follows: According to Rabbeinu Tam, who validates the tzitzis when two original strings remain whole at twelve thumbbreadths and two severed ends retain enough to tie a bow, only two severed ends are valid when at least one of them has enough to tie a bow. If three ends were severed, however, even if each retained enough to tie a bow, it is invalid, because perhaps the three ends come from three different strings, whereas Rabbeinu Tam requires two original strings to remain whole, each twelve thumbbreadths long.
