(א) And there remained in them—meaning on each string enough remained to make a loop, called a shleif, around all the snapped strings together. And if not all of them snapped, it suffices if enough remained to make a loop on the snapped ones alone and not on all the strings.

(ב) It is valid—and if he untied the tzitzis, it is forbidden to attach them to another tallit, for that is like from the outset; even if only one string snapped and it does not have a length of twelve thumbbreadths.

(ג) That it snapped entirely—meaning on both its ends there did not remain kedei aniva, it is invalid even though the other strings were whole. And if on both of them kedei aniva remained by combination, there is room to doubt whether it helps. It follows from Eliyah Rabbah and likewise from Derech HaChaim that one should be stringent in the matter; even if there is a doubt whether the two ends are from two strings or from one, so there is a double doubt—perhaps they are from two strings and perhaps they combine—nevertheless one should be stringent because tzitzis are readily available. Aruch HaShulchan is lenient in such a case. It appears that if on one of the ends there remains enough to tie one string alone, one may certainly rely on that.

(ד) It is invalid—see below in seif 3. And all agree that if one string snapped at its root, meaning where it attaches to the corner, it is invalid. Taz wrote: therefore it is fitting for every God-fearing person that he examine at the time of checking also at the beginning of where the tzitzis emerge, meaning where they attach to the corner, if there is a tear there.

(ה) Two ends—and on neither of them did kedei aniva remain; but if on one of them there is kedei aniva, it is valid.

(ו) Lest it snapped, etc.—meaning even if the two that snapped are from one side, nevertheless perhaps both are from one string, since he did not take care at the time of making.

(ז) Two ends, etc. — The same applies even if all the ends tore from one side, and of the four ends on the other side only enough for a loop remained on each — it is also valid; for according to this view it is invalid only if one string lacks a thread on both its ends. And what the Mechaber wrote, "for behold on the second end there is more than enough for a loop" — is not precise. A question: tzitzis strings that tore and he retied them — whether making them whole through tying helps. The later authorities conclude that the halacha is as follows: if initially, before he made the tzitzis from the strings, the strings tore and he tied them with a permanent knot and afterwards made tzitzis from them — it is valid, for the knot is a complete attachment. And similarly after they were made valid — meaning if one end tore after the tzitzis were placed on the garment — even if not enough for a loop remained, tying it now helps; and even if the second end of that same string tears afterwards it is valid, since at the time of tying the tzitzis were valid. But if the strings shortened in a way that invalidated the tzitzis thereby — for example both ends tore and not enough for a loop remained, or the string tore at the hole — ret tying does not help, for that falls under "you shall make it, and not from that which is already made" invalidly. And certainly if the string was shorter than its measure initially at the time of making — certainly ret tying after the winding and knot of the first section does not help, for that is literally "you shall make it, and not from that which is already made," as above in siman 11, seif 13 regarding cutting the ends of the strings.

(ח) And according to Rabbeinu Tam, etc. — His reason is that two strings which are four (when folded) we place in place of tekhelet, and two strings which are four we place in place of white — therefore at least one type must be complete.

(ט) If he was not careful — This is difficult, for it is all the more so if he was careful, as the Rama wrote in his gloss afterwards. And one can say he chose "if he was not careful" because of the end he concludes with; but if only two ends were severed, "we validate with enough for a loop" — that is specifically without being careful. But if he was careful, if two ends were severed from one side it is valid even if not enough for a loop remained, for on each the other end remains. But if two were severed from both sides and on each not enough for a loop remained — they are invalid, lest it is one string.

(י) "We validate with enough for a loop" — meaning even if the remainder of enough for a loop was only on one of them, as above in subsection 5.

(כ) And the halacha is, etc. — It is obvious one may recite a blessing on them, as we explained in Biur Halacha.
