(1) And there remained in them. Meaning, each severed string retained enough to make a bow, called a loop, around all the severed strings together. If not all the strings were severed, it is sufficient that enough remain to make a bow around the severed strings alone, rather than around all the strings.
(2) It is valid. If he removed the tzitzis, he may not place them upon another tallis, because doing so is considered an initial placement. This applies even if only one string was severed and it is now less than twelve thumbbreadths long.
(3) That was entirely severed. Meaning, neither of its two ends retained enough to tie a bow; it is then invalid even if all the other strings remain whole. If the two ends together contain enough to tie a bow, it is doubtful whether combining them helps. Eliyah Rabbah and Derech HaChayim imply that one should be stringent, even when it is uncertain whether the two ends come from two different strings or from one. Although there is then a double doubt, perhaps they are from two strings and perhaps their lengths combine, one should nevertheless be stringent because tzitzis are readily available. Artzos HaChayim is lenient in such a case. It appears that if one end alone contains enough to tie one string, one may certainly rely upon it.
(4) It is invalid. See below, seif 3. All agree that if one string was severed at its root, meaning at the point where it is attached to the corner, the tzitzis is invalid. Taz therefore writes that every God-fearing person should inspect, when checking his tzitzis, the point where the strings first emerge from the corner to see whether there is a tear there.
(5) Two ends. Meaning, neither retained enough to tie a bow. If one of them retained enough to tie a bow, it is valid.
(6) Lest one string was severed, etc. Meaning, even if both severed ends are on the same side, perhaps they are the two ends of one original string, since he did not keep track of them when making the tzitzis.
(7) Two ends, etc. The same applies even if all the ends on one side were severed, provided that each of the four ends on the other side retained enough to tie a bow. According to this opinion, the tzitzis is invalid only when one original string is deficient at both ends. The Mechaber's wording that the other end contains more than enough to tie a bow is not exact. Regarding tzitzis strings that were severed and retied, the later authorities conclude as follows. If the strings were severed before the tzitzis were made, and they were retied with a permanent knot before being used, the tzitzis is valid because tying creates a complete connection. The same applies after the tzitzis was initially made validly: if one end was severed after the tzitzis was attached to the garment, even if it did not retain enough to tie a bow, he may now retie it. If the other end of that same string is later severed, the tzitzis remains valid because it was valid at the time of the repair. If, however, the strings became shortened in a manner that invalidated the tzitzis, such as where both ends were severed without enough remaining to tie a bow, or where the string was severed at the hole, retieing it afterward does not help. This is included in ta'aseh velo min ha'asui, making the mitzvah through an already invalid object. All the more so, if a string was too short at the outset, tying additional length to it after the windings and knot of the first section were made certainly does not help. This is precisely ta'aseh velo min ha'asui, as explained above in siman 11, seif 13, regarding cutting the ends of the strings.
(8) According to Rabbeinu Tam, etc. His reason is that two original strings, which become four ends, correspond to techeiles, while the other two original strings correspond to the white strings. At least one of these two types must remain complete.
(9) If he was not careful. This appears difficult, because if he was careful the stringency should apply all the more, as the Rama writes afterward. The explanation is that the Mechaber says "if he was not careful" because of the conclusion of the seif: if only two ends were severed, they are valid when enough remains to tie a bow. This applies specifically where he did not keep track of the ends. If he did keep track, and two ends on the same side were severed, it is valid even if neither retained enough to tie a bow, because the other end of each original string remains. If two ends on opposite sides were severed and neither retained enough to tie a bow, it is invalid because perhaps they are the two ends of one string.
(10) We validate them when enough remains to tie a bow. Meaning, even if only one of the two ends contains the required measure, as explained above in seif katan 5.
(11) The halachah, etc. It is clear that one may recite a blessing over them, as explained in Biur Halachah.
