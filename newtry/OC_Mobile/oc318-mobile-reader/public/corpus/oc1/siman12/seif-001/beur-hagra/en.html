Section 1, "If they were severed, etc." This follows the explanation of the Rosh, that what the Gemara says, "if the stump was cut, etc.," means that one thread was completely severed. According to Rabbi, who holds that they are one mitzvah, the intact one can help its fellow; but according to the Rabbis, who hold that the elements do not invalidate one another, whether he has one element or does not have it, he must always have four threads if one was severed at its root. That is to say: according to Rabbi, the number of threads is not indispensable, only that there be blue and white; but according to the Rabbis, the blue does not invalidate, only the requirement that there be four threads. But if all four were severed, the remaining amount is valid according to everyone, by all opinions. The Gemara brings the case of the sons of Rabbi Chiyya only to show that their end need not be like their beginning. Tosafot write likewise, in the second answer in the comment beginning "Rather."
"All the threads." There it is an unresolved question, and therefore we rule stringently.
"The severed ones." So it appears in the Mordechai. Beit Yosef.
"And if not, etc., even." So the Beit Yosef explained what is stated there: "If a thread was severed, etc."
"And according to Rabbeinu Tam, etc." The Tosafot mentioned above explain that "if the blue thread was reduced to a stump, etc." means even if enough remained to tie a bow. This accords with their view that one places four blue threads and four white threads. See there, 38a, in the comment beginning "The blue," and 40a, in the comment beginning "Beit Shammai say four; what is the reason, etc." This is also the view of Rashi; see there in the comment beginning "They raised an objection: a garment does not, etc." So too it appears in Sifrei, Parashat Ki Tetze: "How many make a tassel? etc. And Beit Shammai say: four of blue and four of wool, etc.; and the law follows Beit Shammai." The Gemara there also rules like Beit Shammai. But the Raavad's view is that only two threads are blue and six are white, while the Rambam writes that only one thread is blue and seven are white. Their reason is from what is stated in Sifrei: "'They shall make for themselves tzitzit' - I might think that he should make each one by itself; therefore Scripture says 'a cord.' How many make a cord? No fewer than three, the words of Beit Hillel; and Beit Shammai say, etc.," as above. Tosafot cite this in the comment beginning "Beit Shammai say, etc." Since it says, "I might think, etc.," it implies that the blue thread is only one, and "cord" is written only regarding the white. The Raavad explains this as one complete thread, while the Rambam explains it as literally one thread. See Tosafot 38a, at the end of the comment beginning "The blue," as is proven in Sifrei, Parashat Shelach and Parashat Ki Tetze, etc.; and see there at the end of the comment beginning "Beit Shammai say." Therefore Tosafot were forced into a strained explanation of the number of blue threads, etc. They were also forced into a strained explanation of the count of eight threads, for the Gemara states only four, from "cord" and "cords," etc. They explain that "and twist them from within" means that he doubles them; see there in the comment mentioned above. But in Sifrei this still requires analysis, for Beit Hillel say three and Beit Shammai say eight, whereas they should disagree only about three and four. Rather, the matter is as follows: certainly "thread" implies one, and likewise the white "tzitzit" also implies one; nevertheless, the two are always equal. Beit Hillel explain "cord" as three threads, and the same applies to white, three. Beit Shammai explain "cords" as four, as the Gemara says, "cord" and "cords," because Beit Shammai hold that "cord" implies two, unlike Beit Hillel, who explain it as three and also do not expound the plural form; and the same applies to blue. What the Gemara says, "four" and "three," all refers to only one kind, either blue or white. This is also proven by the entire discussion. As for what Tosafot write at the end of the comment beginning "Beit Shammai say," "However, in Sifrei there are those who say, etc.": this is not in Sifrei. Rather, they derived it from the beginning of Sifrei, "'They shall make for themselves, etc.,'" as above, and as they wrote at the end of the comment beginning "The blue." It is possible that they explain Beit Hillel's statement that there are three to mean three white threads and one blue thread, and that Beit Shammai and Beit Hillel disagree only about the blue. But this is not so, for the Gemara proves that they disagree about the number of the tzitzit.
"Twelve thumbbreadths." This follows the view of the Tosafot mentioned above.
