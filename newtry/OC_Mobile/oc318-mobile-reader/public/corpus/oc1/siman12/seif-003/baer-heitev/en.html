One may rely on R' Yehudah. Above, siman 21, seif 1, he wrote that where it is possible, it is good to be concerned for Rabbeinu Tam's view. Explanation: where it is possible to find other tzitzit easily, but one need not go after him; and here he wrote "where it is not possible" — meaning in a place where no tzitzit at all are found, one may rely on R' Yehudah. Nevertheless, one should not recite a blessing over them, and it is forbidden to go out with it on Shabbos, per Rama; Magen Avraham. And in responsa Ginat Veradim, part Orach Chayyim, general rules 2, siman 1, he wrote that one may validate in a time of distress even when slightly severed from the tassel itself, see there. And see Halachot Ketuvot, vol. 2, siman (212) [218], who wrote that one whose tassel strings were torn and he has no other strings should untie three chulyot with the three upper knots and leave the lower knot with its chulyah so there will be ptil and gedil until he finds others (and it is obvious that when the tzitzit are invalidated one must remove them, and it does not help to stand in place and not go four amot, for the law of four amot applies only to walking without tzitzit).
