(א) That it will tear. At the end of Taz — and it follows his view in siman 8, letter 8, and Magen Avraham 11 there — for Taz the main inspection is in the corner strings, for there if one string tore from its root it would be invalid even per R' Yehuda in seif 3 here. And what he wrote "if he untied, it becomes lechatchila" — one wonders at Rambam and Tur who did not bring this, and see Bach — all agree lechatchila forbidden. And know that the Levush wrote: since this measure is rabbinic from Magen Avraham "anything suffices," the Sages did not trouble to permit always — therefore one may say where tzitzis are not available and he has tzitzis less than twelve thumbbreadths measure, one may say he may place them on his tallis for it is b'dieved. Therefore there is a difference between untying the evening strings in siman 11 seif 10 — if untied from evening strings it is valid, and Levush explained the reason the Torah was strict only from time of making — "and they made for themselves tzitzis on the corners of their garments" — and afterwards even under the corner less than thumb-knot binding valid; but if he untied tzitzis from this corner and made others — biblically invalid since their beginning was made biblically invalid for lack of thumb-knot binding — and this is simple.

(ב) And what he wrote. In the responsum — the question is from Gemara Menachot 40b: telluma invalid — one can say telluma biblically, or telluma rabbinically since length twelve thumbbreadths is rabbinic and all the more so as explained. And in Levush one can say telluma is not invalid; but from Mordechai proof long and shortened valid — no upper limit; but there is upper limit rabbinically also invalid telluma. And if made valid and afterwards the string tore from its root without enough for a loop remaining — one can say tying afterwards helps and that is their making: granted if initially he took one long string only ten thumbbreadths and wound and tied it is telluma; not so here where the beginning was valid and tore — tying to complete helps and that is their making. And analogous proof from siman 10 seif 6 — placed tzitzis on tzitzis that were invalid in both, remain, and by cutting one two became valid — he brought proof for leniency there that there is no invalidity in the body of the tzitzis, unlike here — therefore tying certainly helps to be a complete string valid before making, and the same after making and tearing with enough for a loop remaining. And if you ask why need tying — one can say practical difference if the second end was cut afterwards. And what is doubtful: strings require spinning and each string is doubled to two through spinning — if part of the string tore mid-spin, half spun, since part of the other remains but not enough for a loop from the spun portion — whether it validates thus; see Chullin regarding tendon junction and requires study and Magen Avraham. See siman 33 seif 65 — more than Torah measure, tying helps; and here even if torn at the corner it helps.
