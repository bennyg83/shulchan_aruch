According to Taz, the principal inspection is at the strings emerging from the corner, because if one string is severed at its root, it is invalid even according to Ri in seif 3 here. Regarding the statement that if he removed the tzitzis it is considered an initial act, see Beit Yosef in siman 15, who questions why Rambam and Tur did not cite this; see Bach. All agree that initially it is forbidden. Know that Levush writes that since this measure is D'Rabbanan, while according to the Torah any remaining amount suffices, the Sages did not trouble him to undo the tzitzis continually. According to this, where tzitzis are unavailable and he has strings shorter than twelve thumbbreadths, one might say that he may attach them to his tallis because this is treated as an after-the-fact situation. This creates a distinction from siman 11, seif 10, concerning warp threads that became detached. Levush explains there that they remain valid because the Torah was particular only at the time of making, as the verse states, "They shall make tzitzis upon the corners of their garments." Afterward, even if they lie below the corner at a distance of less than a thumb joint, they remain valid. If, however, he removed the tzitzis from that corner and attached other tzitzis there, they would be invalid according to the Torah, because they were initially made in a Torah-invalid position lacking the required distance of a thumb joint. This is clear.
What he writes in the responsum is a question. Menachos 40b states that ta'aseh velo min ha'asui invalidates. One might distinguish between ta'aseh velo min ha'asui according to the Torah and a D'Rabbanan form of that defect, because the requirement that the strings be twelve thumbbreadths long is D'Rabbanan, whereas according to the Torah any amount suffices. According to Levush, one might therefore say that ta'aseh velo min ha'asui does not invalidate here. Mordechai, however, provides evidence otherwise. He states that if the strings were initially long and were then shortened, they remain valid because there is no maximum measure. This implies that where a minimum measure exists, even D'Rabbanan, ta'aseh velo min ha'asui invalidates. If the tzitzis was made validly and a string was afterward severed at its root without enough remaining to tie a bow, one might say that tying it afterward helps and this itself constitutes its making. This differs from taking a string that was only ten thumbbreadths long initially and completing the windings and knots; that is ta'aseh velo min ha'asui. Here, the initial construction was valid, and after it became severed, tying can complete it and constitute a new act. A partial analogy appears in siman 10, seif 6, where one placed one set of tzitzis upon another. While both remained, both were invalid, but after cutting away one, the second became valid. One may reject the comparison, because there the defect was not in the body of the tzitzis, unlike here. Nevertheless, tying certainly helps to make it a whole string, both before the tzitzis is made and after it was made validly and then severed while enough remained to tie a bow. One might ask why tying is then needed. The practical difference is if the other end of that string is later severed. A further uncertainty concerns the requirement that the strings be spun together. Each string is doubled through twisting. If part of the string was severed so that only half of the twist remains, and the surviving twisted portion does not contain enough to tie a bow, is it valid because the other half remains? See Chullin concerning the junction of the sinews. The matter requires study; see Magen Avraham. See also siman 33, seif 5, where tying helps beyond the Torah-required measure. Here too, tying helps even when the string was severed at the corner.
