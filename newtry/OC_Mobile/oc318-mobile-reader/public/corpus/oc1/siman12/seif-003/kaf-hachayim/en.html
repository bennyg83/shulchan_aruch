3) And if two ends of the thread were severed and on one of them kedei aniva did not remain, but when the remainder of the two ends is combined there is kedei aniva—Beit Yosef was in doubt about this and wrote that one may say that whatever did not have kedei aniva remain in one place is not recognizable and not considered at all, as though it does not exist; and likewise the Levush that it is invalid. Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 2; and likewise Peri Megadim on the words of Magen Avraham, seif katan 3, that it is invalid out of doubt; and likewise Rav Zalman, letter 2; Shetil Zeisim, letter 5, in the name of Avraham Zvi; and likewise Zekanim LaChayim, part 3, letter 90. And Eliyah Rabbah wrote there that even if there is a double doubt—perhaps it is not one thread and perhaps they combine—it is likewise invalid; see there. And Peri Megadim wrote there, and it is also implied that when kedei aniva remains on each thread independently, it is likewise so; and the reason they were so stringent, Peri Megadim wrote there, is because tzitzis are common. And likewise Shetil Zeisim there that we do not say here a double doubt, and not like the responsum of Beit Yaakov; and likewise Eliyah Rabbah there on the responsum of Beit Yaakov that his words are not conclusive.
