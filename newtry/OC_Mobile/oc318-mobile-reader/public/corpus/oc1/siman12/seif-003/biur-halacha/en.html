One may rely upon Ri. Peri Megadim, Mishbetzos Zahav, siman 11, seif katan 3, writes: If all the strings were severed up to the wound section, which is invalid as explained in siman 12, seif 1, and one is traveling without other tzitzis, he should undo the wound section, which is four thumbbreadths or more, and fashion part as a wound section and part as loose strings. According to the Mechaber in siman 11, seif 4, he then fulfills the mitzvah initially and may recite a blessing. Even according to our view requiring twelve thumbbreadths, he may nevertheless wear it, though whether he may recite a blessing is doubtful. According to the Torah any amount suffices, so he does not violate the positive commandment of tzitzis. He must, however, undo the entire wound section, because otherwise it would be ta'aseh velo min ha'asui, since according to the Torah the tzitzis became invalid when it lacked both a wound and a loose section. This suggestion is intended to satisfy even Rashi. Peri Megadim and Rabbi Akiva Eiger in his novellae to siman 11 both agree that merely undoing part is also included in ta'aseh velo min ha'asui, contrary to Halachos Ketanos cited by Baer Heitev, who is lenient and does not require undoing the entire wound section even according to Rashi. I later found, however, that Peri Megadim himself writes at the end of siman 13, Mishbetzos Zahav, that this is not included in ta'aseh velo min ha'asui. It is remarkable that he contradicts himself. Know further that according to Rashi, if tzitzis strings were severed within the wound section between the knots, the severing of two strings invalidates them, for this is no better than two strings severed at the beginning of the loose section, which Rashi invalidates. If one string was severed, it appears valid, because Rashi disagrees with Ri only because both a wound and a loose section are required, as the Gra explains in the name of Mordechai, and here the other end of that same string still provides a loose section. This appears to apply only if the string was severed above the first section. Otherwise it is invalid because we rule that the upper knot, meaning the second of the five knots we make, is required according to the Torah, and when one string is severed it is considered as though the knot itself was severed, as is evident from Tosafos on Menachos 39b, s.v. "Enough to tie them." Indeed, according to Rashi one might infer from that passage that slightly more than the first section must remain, approximately enough to tie a bow, at least around one string according to Rif and Rambam as cited by Beit Yosef. Otherwise we assume that the knot will eventually come undone. There may, however, be some distinction from the Gemara's case, and the matter requires study.
