"One may rely on Ri." — And Peri Megadim, Mishbetzot Zahav, in siman 11, seif katan 3, wrote: Know that if all the threads were severed up to the gedil it is invalid, as explained in siman 12, seif 1. If he is on the road and has no other tzitzis, he may untie the gedil, which is four thumbbreadths or more, and make part gedil and part anaf; and for the Mechaber in siman 11, seif 4, he fulfills ab initio and may recite a blessing. And even according to us, who hold twelve thumbbreadths, nevertheless to wear and recite a blessing there is doubt; but from the Torah, any amount suffices and one does not violate the positive commandment of tzitzis. However, one must untie the entire gedil, for otherwise it is tolaimah—for they are invalidated by Torah law, since we require gedil and anaf—so ends there. And this advice of his is in order to fulfill even according to Rashi; and he and the Gra in his novellae on siman 11 both agreed on one matter—that this too is within the category of "you shall make" and not "from the pre-made," not like the author of Likutei Katz brought in Bach, who is lenient in this that one need not untie the entire gedil even according to Rashi. But afterwards I found in Peri Megadim himself at the end of siman 13, Mishbetzot Zahav, that he wrote this is not within "you shall make" and not "from the pre-made," and it is astonishing that he contradicts himself. And know further that according to Rashi, if the tzitzis threads were severed at the place of the gedil between the knots—if two threads, invalid, for it is no better than if two threads were severed at the beginning of the anaf, which is invalid according to Rashi; and if one thread was severed, it appears valid, for Rashi does not disagree with Ri except for the reason that we also require a thread, as explained in the Gra in the name of the Mordechai—and here is there not also a thread at the second end of that very thread itself? But it appears specifically if the thread was severed above the first chulyah, for otherwise it is invalidated because we rule that the upper knot (which is the second of the five knots that we make) is by Torah law, and in this case, when one thread snaps, it is considered as though the knot snapped, as is proven in Menachot 39b in the passage beginning "in order to tie them," see there. And more than this we can prove for our matter, apparently, from the sugya there according to Rashi—that there must also remain from the thread a little more above the first chulyah, namely approximately kedei aniva (at least on one thread according to Rif and Rambam, as Beit Yosef wrote), for otherwise we say that in the end the knot will certainly come undone; and perhaps one may distinguish somewhat from the sugya, and it requires further study.
