Beit Yaakov, siman 106, writes regarding the rule that the two sides combine to make kedei anivah according to all views: One may question whether this applies only when half is on one side and half on the other. If one side has a small amount and the other a larger amount, perhaps they do not combine even though together they equal kedei anivah; yet there is room to say they combine in this case too. See there. Leket HaKemach cites him as unequivocally ruling that they do not combine, but this is incorrect, for Beit Yaakov afterward writes that the Gemara in Chullin regarding a liver implies that half is not required from each side; see there. He further writes that combining kedei anivah from two sides depends on a dispute. According to Rashi, the measure is taken from the wound section and the portions do not combine, because the string is held by the tzitzis knots like the sewn case below. According to Ri and Rosh, the measure is taken from the loose branches, which have no knots; they are destined to unravel into the measure of kedei anivah and therefore combine. In my humble opinion this is unconvincing: There, when unraveled, the portions become one in his hand; here, one part of the string is on one side and the other on the other side, while the intervening knots prevent them from uniting. His comparison of Rashi's view to the sewn case is also unclear. The main ruling follows the later authorities, who invalidate it. Since there is no practical difference, I have been brief. Baer Heitev cites Taz that if they were tied, etc., it is valid if he retied them. With due respect, he did not understand Taz correctly. Although Taz discusses the issue, his conclusion does not validate a string after it has reached a disqualifying state. Retieing helps only when kedei anivah remains and it is still valid; the practical difference arises if the string is later severed on its other side as well. Mishbetzos Zahav writes likewise. See Machazik Berachah, who cites Eliyah Rabbah and rules unlike Baer Heitev.
