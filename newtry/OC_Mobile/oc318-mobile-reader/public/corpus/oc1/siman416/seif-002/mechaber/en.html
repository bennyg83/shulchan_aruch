One who makes an eruv for two days of Yom Tov in the diaspora, or for Shabbat and Yom Tov — even if it is one eruv in one direction for both days — the eruv must be present in its place during twilight of the first night and during twilight of the second night. How does he do this? He brings it to that place on Yom Tov eve or Shabbat eve and lets nightfall find him there, then takes it in his hand and returns. If the first night is Yom Tov he brings it to that place the next day and leaves it there until nightfall and eats it if the second night is Shabbat, or brings it back if the second night is Yom Tov — since they are two separate sanctities and not one day, so that we cannot say that by acquiring the eruv on the first night it takes effect for both days. If the eruv was eaten on the first day, it took effect for the first day and is not an eruv for the second. If he acquired Shabbat rest by foot on the first day, he must acquire it by foot on the second day — by going and standing at that place and resolving in his mind to acquire Shabbat rest there, saying nothing, since it is forbidden to make any preparation from Yom Tov for Shabbat or from Shabbat for Yom Tov even by speech. Certainly he cannot make an eruv with bread that was not already used on the first day, since he would need to declare it an eruv and would thereby be making a preparation. If he made the eruv on the first day with food and wishes to acquire by foot on the second day, it is valid. If he wishes to make the eruv with bread on the second day, he must use the same bread he used on the first day, needing to say nothing since the eruv was already declared — and thus he is making no preparation at all.
