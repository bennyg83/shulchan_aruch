Ordinary matzah. Since the oath takes effect upon optional matzah, it also takes effect upon the matzah of the mitzvah. It appears to me that one who failed to recite the Haggadah on Pesach night has no opportunity to compensate later. The verse states, “Because of this,” and the Sages explain that the obligation applies only when matzah and maror are placed before you. The verse further states, “In the evening you shall eat matzos,” from which point onward eating matzah is optional. This appears correct to me, unlike Olas Shabbos.
He receives lashes and eats, etc. This is a vain oath, because an oath does not take effect against a mitzvah. If, however, he says, “The eating of matzah is forbidden to me as a konam on Pesach night,” he is forbidden to eat it, because a vow does take effect upon a mitzvah. See Yoreh Deah, siman 239.
