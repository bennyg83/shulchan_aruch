It is forbidden to eat matzah. The reason is that then his oath takes effect through kollel of the other days, which are permitted matters, together with the forbidden matters, which are in the form of shev ve'al ta'aseh, even for a Torah prohibition. And see in Shevuos in Tosafos, folio 24, s.v. "ela," that Tosafos in the name of Riva distinguish in another manner between if he mentions Pesach in the oath, and then his oath does not take effect even through kollel, which is not so when he does not mention the name of Pesach. And the Raavad in Sefer Temim Deim, in his hassagot on the Baal HaMaor to Maseches Pesachim, in siman 245, further distinguishes in another manner between if his oath took effect before the obligation of matzah, so that the prohibition was already included with the permitted, [then] his oath takes effect; but if the prohibition had already taken effect, for example if he swore on Pesach itself, this is not kollel, but rather he begins with a prohibition, end quote. And see in Yoreh Deah, siman 236.
On the night of Pesach. For then there is here kollel of a permitted matter; and this is according to the view of Ri in Tosafos there, which is not so according to the view of Riva that I wrote, [that] even with kollel his oath does not take effect in such a case. And one should not raise a difficulty that even if he said "on the night of Pesach" there is likewise kollel of permitted things, namely matzah ashirah; and one can say that since he mentioned "the night of Pesach," his intent was specifically for that with which one fulfills the obligation of matzah on Pesach, as the Rivash wrote in siman 398, brought in Beis Yosef in Yoreh Deah siman 256, in the gloss of the Shulchan Aruch there at the end of siman 216 (and see in the Responsa Maharit, Yoreh Deah section, siman 47, who casts doubt upon the Rivash regarding this). But if he did not mention "the night of Pesach," it is possible that even matzah ashirah is included in his oath; and it is clarified in Yoreh Deah that ordinary nedarim are interpreted stringently, and their interpretations leniently. And see in Magen Avraham what he challenged here against the Olas Shabbos; and I already wrote above at the beginning of siman 473 that this is no challenge at all against the Olas Shabbos.
He is flogged and eats. And if he swore not to eat maror or the four cups, the consensus of the Acharonim is that his oath takes effect, since they are only rabbinic; but they compel him to have his oath annulled, as was explained above in siman 418 and siman 570. However, the Levush wrote there that there are those who say that the oath does not take effect without kollel, even regarding a rabbinic mitzvah that has a support from the Torah; his words have no root or foundation at all, as the Shach elaborated at length and challenged him in Yoreh Deah siman 215, s.k. 11; and see what I wrote above in siman 272, s.k. 19. And furthermore, it is possible that there is no Torah support here, as is written in Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah at length; and not like Maharash Halevi in a responsum, Yoreh Deah section, siman 12, who followed the Levush in this, see there.
