He is liable to lashes. This is a vain oath, because an oath does not take effect against a mitzvah. If he swore concerning maror or the four cups, however, the oath takes effect because they are Rabbinic obligations. Likewise, if he said, “The eating of matzah is forbidden to me as a konam on Pesach night,” he is forbidden to eat it, because a vow takes effect even regarding a mitzvah. See Yoreh Deah, siman 239. He is compelled to seek annulment of the oath or vow, as stated in seif katan 1. See Chok Yaakov. The author of Rosh Yosef expressed doubt where one said simply, “I swear that I will not eat matzah,” and afterward explained that his intention had been only Pesach night. What is the law? See Yad Aharon, who discusses this at length.
