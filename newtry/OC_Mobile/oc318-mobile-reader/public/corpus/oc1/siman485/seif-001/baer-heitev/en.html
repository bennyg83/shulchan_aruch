(א) Forbidden — since it applies to matzah of rabbinic origin, it also applies to matzah of a mitzvah. And we compel him to request annulment of his oath. Bach — and see Yoreh De'ah siman 236.

(ב) He receives lashes — for it is a vain oath, since an oath does not take effect on a matter of mitzvah. And if he swore regarding maror and the four cups, his oath takes effect since they are d'rabbanan. And likewise if he said "Konam be my eating matzah upon me on Passover night" — it is forbidden, for a vow takes effect on a matter of mitzvah; see Yoreh De'ah siman 239. And we compel him to request annulment of his oath or his vow, as stated in seif kaf alef and below. The Rav author of Rosh Yosef was uncertain: if one said an oath not to eat matzah without specification and afterward explained that his intent in what he swore was only on Passover night — what is the law? See Yad Ephraim at length there.
