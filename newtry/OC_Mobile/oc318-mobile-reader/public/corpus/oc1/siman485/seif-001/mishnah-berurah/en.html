(1) That I will not eat matzah, without qualification. Whether he made this oath before Pesach or on Pesach night itself, his oath takes effect. He did not state that his oath applied only to the matzah of the mitzvah, but included every kind of matzah, rather than only the matzah of that night. An oath can take effect through an inclusive formulation that joins permitted matters with a mitzvah whose neglect is passive. Nevertheless, the poskim write that it is a mitzvah for him to obtain release from his oath so that he can fulfill the positive commandment of eating matzah.
(2) On Pesach night. Since he specified Pesach night, his presumed intention is only the matzah of the mitzvah.
(3) He is liable to lashes, etc. This is because he took a vain oath, since an oath does not take effect against a mitzvah. Nevertheless, if he says, “The eating of matzah for the mitzvah is forbidden to me as a konam,” he may not eat matzah, because a neder takes effect even concerning a mitzvah. If he swears not to eat the maror of the mitzvah, he may not eat maror, because maror nowadays is D'Rabbanan, and an oath takes effect against a rabbinic mitzvah. The same applies if he swears not to drink the four cups. Nevertheless, he is compelled to seek release from his oath. Likewise, if he swears not to eat matzah on the second night, the oath takes effect and he may not eat it, since for us, who know the fixed calendar, observance of the second night is D'Rabbanan. In this case too, he should be compelled to seek release from his oath.
