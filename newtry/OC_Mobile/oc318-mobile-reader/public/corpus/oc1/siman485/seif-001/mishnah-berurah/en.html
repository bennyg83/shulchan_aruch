(א) That he will not eat matzah without specification — whether he said this before Passover or said on Passover night itself, since he did not say in his oath that he swears only on matzah of mitzvah but includes in his oath all matzah and not specifically matzah of this night alone, his oath takes effect, for an oath takes effect inclusively on permitted matters together with forbidden matters of sit and do not do; and nevertheless the poskim wrote it is a mitzvah to annul his oath so he can fulfill the mitzvah of eating matzah.

(ב) On Passover night — for since he said Passover night, presumably his intent is on matzah of mitzvah alone.

(ג) He receives lashes, etc. — because of a vain oath, for an oath does not take effect on a matter of mitzvah; and nevertheless if he said Konam be my eating matzah of mitzvah upon me, it is forbidden to eat matzah, for a vow takes effect on a matter of mitzvah. If he said an oath that he will not eat maror of mitzvah, it is forbidden to eat maror, for maror in our time is d'rabbanan and an oath takes effect on a d'rabbanan mitzvah; and the same if he said he will not drink the four cups. And nevertheless we compel him that he should request annulment of his oath; and the same if he said an oath that he will not eat matzah on the second night, which is also d'rabbanan for us who are expert in fixing the month — it also takes effect and is forbidden, only that here too one should compel him to request annulment of his oath.
