But in a positive commandment it does not take effect inclusively in Torah law, and even in d'rabbanan — see Yoreh De'ah 236 seif hei and Taz there letters yud alef and yud bet; and it implies even one who swore on Passover night itself — all Passover matzah is permission not to eat all days of Passover, then the oath takes effect; see there in Beit Yosef and Shabbat in the Gemara that one obligated in bread — not so siman 488 in Magen Avraham alef and siman 418 and 470 seif gimmel. And if he included rich matzah, it takes effect inclusively; but without specification rich matzah is not included since he mentioned Passover night — his intent is on what one fulfills on it; therefore he receives lashes and eats — see Yoreh De'ah 256 seif vav and Shach yud zayin. And that it is not inclusive of two kezayit — for without kezayit flour it is not matzah — see Eliyah Rabbah. And this requires further study: rich matzah without any water at all — if one placed matzah upon it, perhaps it is entirely not subject to leavening, and only with a little water one may say it is within matzah — see siman 471. And if it is difficult: one who swore by inclusion that takes effect and sits alone with no one to annul his oath — let the positive commandment of matzah come and push aside the oath; one may say it is a positive commandment one can fulfill through negligence — see Tosafot Eruvin 100 s.v. matan and Tosafot Kiddushin 38a s.v. de'ikriv; for where one swore on a kezayit of matzah and that he will not drink, one may say it is inclusive and there is no distinction between one kezayit and two kezayit, and all the more inclusion of rich matzah. And on the first night of Sukkot one who swore he will not eat bread — it is a positive commandment. And below seif alef and bet, and from the Mechaber's conclusion here and Yoreh De'ah it implies even if standing on Passover night and mentioning explicitly, whatever is inclusive takes effect. And if he has only half a kezayit of matzah he is not obligated to eat — responsa Shavut Yaakov part 2 siman 18, and it is simple; and if so the oath takes effect on it. And if he swore he will not eat a kezayit in the first meal, one may say it takes effect and he eats as usual; one may say we decree because of the first meal — see Tosafot Kiddushin there and Taz. Haggadah is a positive commandment, and even if he has no matzah and maror — see siman 454. And Rashi in Chumash (Shemot 13) explained "for this" — Pesach, matzah, and maror; and in Pesachim 116 and in Rambam chapter 6 law 1 and siman 488 and Eliyah Rabbah in Magen Avraham alef it is explained further.
