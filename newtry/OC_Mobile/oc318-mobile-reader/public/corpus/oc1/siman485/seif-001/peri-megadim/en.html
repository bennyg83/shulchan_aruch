An oath that conflicts with a positive Torah commandment does not take effect merely through a comprehensive formulation, and even regarding a D’Rabbanan obligation one should see Yoreh Deah, siman 236, seif 5, and Taz there, seifim katanim 11 and 12. It appears, however, that even if one swore on Pesach night itself not to eat ordinary matzah throughout Pesach, the oath may take effect regarding the remaining days, since the obligation to eat bread on Shabbos and Yom Tov is D’Rabbanan rather than Torah law. See simanim 188, 418, and 570. If he included matzah ashirah in his oath, the oath takes effect through inclusion. But in an unqualified oath made with reference to Pesach night, matzah ashirah is not included, because his intention is evidently the matzah with which one fulfills the mitzvah. He therefore receives lashes for violating the oath and nevertheless eats the required matzah. See Yoreh Deah, siman 215, seif 6, and Shach, seif katan 17. Why is the second kezayis not considered an additional permitted item included together with the obligatory kezayis? One may answer that a kezayis of matzah cannot be formed without a kezayis of flour. See Peri Chadash and Yoreh Deah, siman 218. It also requires study whether matzah ashirah made without any water at all bears the name matzah, since it can never become chametz. Perhaps only where a little water is present can it be included under the term matzah. See siman 471. One might ask: where an oath takes effect through inclusion and the person is alone with no one available to release the oath, why should the positive commandment of eating matzah not override the oath? The answer may be that this is a positive commandment whose conflict arose through his own negligence. See Tosafos, Eruvin 100b, and Tosafos, Kiddushin 38a. If he swore not to eat a kezayis of matzah and not to drink, the oath may take effect through inclusion. There is no material distinction between one kezayis and a second kezayis, and certainly not when matzah ashirah was included. The same issue applies on the first night of Sukkos to one who swore not to eat bread. The wording of the Mechaber here and in Yoreh Deah implies that whenever a permitted item is genuinely included, the oath takes effect even if made on Pesach night itself. If he possesses only half a kezayis of matzah, he is not obligated to eat it. So writes Responsa Shevus Yaakov, part 2, siman 18, and the matter is straightforward. The oath would therefore take effect upon that half kezayis. If he swore not to eat a kezayis at the first meal, one may argue that the oath takes effect and he should eat in the ordinary manner, though perhaps a decree applies because of the first meal. See Tosafos in Kiddushin and Taz. Relating the Exodus in the Haggadah is an independent positive commandment, even if one has no matzah or maror. See siman 473. Rashi on the Torah explains “because of this” as referring to the Korban Pesach, matzah, and maror. See Pesachim 116b, Rambam, chapter 7, halachah 1, siman 473, and Eliyah Rabbah.
