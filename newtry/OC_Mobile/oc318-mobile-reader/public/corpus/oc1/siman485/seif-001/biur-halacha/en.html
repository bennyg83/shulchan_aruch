An oath that I will not eat matzah on Pesach night. See Mor U’Ketziah, who writes that if one said, “I will not eat this matzah,” it is forbidden to him even if he has no other matzah available, and his oath must be annulled.
Know that if one said, “I will not eat matzah throughout the week of Pesach,” the oath takes effect through inclusion according to most poskim, even though he explicitly mentioned Pesach. Accordingly, according to those poskim who hold that the Torah obligation to eat matzah extends only until midnight, the following requires examination. When one says, “I will not eat matzah on Pesach night,” he includes within the oath both the period of exemption and the period of obligation. The oath should therefore take effect through inclusion and should not be a vain oath. One could answer only with difficulty that because he specified the matzah of Pesach night, his intent is limited to the time when people fulfill the positive mitzvah of eating matzah, and not to the later part of the night. This is strained, because according to that explanation he would be permitted to eat matzah after midnight. The matter requires study.
