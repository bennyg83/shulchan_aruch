Forbidden — Ba'er Hetev; and see in Shach Choshen Mishpat siman 73 seif kaf yud alef who wrote the reason that inclusion helps here to nullify the mitzvah, for in the middle of the year the obligation of matzah and sukkah has not yet applied to him at all — see there in Taz in Hagahat Chacham Tzvi; one may say he challenged Shach on this and wrote that if one said matzah without specification, even if standing on Passover nights it is forbidden to eat, etc. — see there; and see in the Acharonim there who wrote to reconcile Shach's words; and see in Kaf HaChayyim to Sefer Ketubot in Even HaEzer siman 218. (And I wrote there that initially it was difficult for me what Chacham Tzvi wrote from Tosafot Shevuot — Shach is not dealing with the matter of the essence of the oath taking effect, for regarding that there is no place for Shach's distinction, for now there is no mitzvah; nevertheless when we judge the oath's taking effect on Passover nights, then it is already a mitzvah; therefore Tosafot needed to divide on another matter, to say the oath takes effect only regarding whether one must fulfill the oath or must fulfill the mitzvah he is sworn and standing upon; and in truth the punishment of the oath depends on which preceded — Shach wrote regarding matzah that since there is no obligation now, the oath preceded when there was no obligation, and when the obligation arrives the oath's taking effect was already — therefore Shach did not mention from Tosafot Shevuot at all, for they deal with the essence of oath taking effect, and the matters are clear as I explained in its place, and here I shortened. And what Ketzot HaChoshen wrote — Ra'avad's words in Temim De'im — Kaf HaChayyim brought here, and this is not Shach's intent; also what he wrote that Rav R' Chasdai in responsa of Rivash is like Ra'avad — it is not so, as explained in responsa of Rivash, for it is explained there his reason is because even though it includes on what is not mitzvah, nevertheless the essence of the oath is on a matter of mitzvah, and he brings an oath not to eat matzah on Passover night — even though it includes entirely, nevertheless the essence of the oath is on matzah of mitzvah, and this Rivash rejected there — see there; and also the proof from an oath not to sleep, etc. is astonishing, for there since the oath he uttered cannot be fulfilled it is void entirely, unlike there where one swore not to sleep, etc.; and likewise in matzah, for we hold that on a matter that is not mitzvah it takes effect well — one may say that through inclusion it takes effect also on mitzvah, and in this Ran and Rivash deal, and I explained well in its place, with Heaven's help.)
