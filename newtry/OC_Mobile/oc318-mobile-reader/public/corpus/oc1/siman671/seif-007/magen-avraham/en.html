From the midpoint of the entrance. In Sefer HaTerumah the reading is: from the half toward the left of the entrance, meaning that he places it in the second half, not on the side of the mezuzah. Nevertheless, the custom is because he enters between two mitzvos; Darkei Moshe. It appears to me that if he has a window adjacent to the public domain, he should place it in the window, for in this there is no concern of danger, except in a place where harm is common.
From east to west. Some arrange them from north to south, as written by the Semag and the Rambam, and each river follows its course; Terumas HaDeshen. This is the language of the Maharshal: They should be placed from east to west, and do not pay attention to the custom. End quote. It appears to me that one should not move from the custom, for it has what to rely upon.
Before Minchah. So too the Maharshal ruled. In the Darkei Moshe it is written: We do not practice this way; rather, we light between Minchah and Maariv. It appears to me that if it is close to dark, and before a minyan comes to the synagogue the day will become sanctified, he should recite the blessing and light immediately, for nevertheless there is publicizing of the miracle when they later come to the synagogue. It is comparable to one who lights in the street at a time when no person is there, and afterward people come. So too it is in Maharil, that they lit before they came to the synagogue.
The shamash may, etc. But in his home, he himself must light all of them; Levush. However, the Maharshal wrote in a responsum that in his home as well he is permitted to do so. End quote. So it appears to me, for we find in Yoma, chapter 3: he cut most of it and another completed it through him, etc.; see there in Chullin, chapter 2.
