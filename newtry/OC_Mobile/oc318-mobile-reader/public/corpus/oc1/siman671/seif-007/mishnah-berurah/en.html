(31) Within the tefach adjacent, etc. - For if he distances it further from the entrance, it is not recognizable that the homeowner placed it there.
(32) To the entrance - Whether the placement is at the entrance of the house or at the entrance of the courtyard.
(33) So that it will be, etc. - So that he will be surrounded by mitzvot. Nevertheless, after the fact, if he placed the Chanukkah lamp on the right, he has fulfilled.
(34) And if there is none, etc., on the right - For in every mitzvah matter the right is preferable; furthermore, there is greater publicizing of the miracle, since everyone turns to the right.
(35) And if he placed it in the door itself, etc. - This refers to the beginning of the seif.
(36) From half of the entrance, etc. - Meaning the place of the door through which one enters the house should be divided in two, and in the half that leans to the left side, opposite the mezuzah, which is on the right, there he should place it, as the Magen Avraham writes. This implies that he may place it anywhere in that half. See the Taz, whose view is that in practice it is proper to be stringent and place the lamps on the left side at the far end near the wall.
(37) Nevertheless, the custom, etc. - So that he enters between two mitzvot.
(38) And one should not change - Nevertheless, if he has a window adjacent to the public domain, it is better to place it there so that it will be visible to people in the public domain and the miracle will be publicized, unless this is a place where danger is common because of it.
(39) And in the synagogue - Or in the study hall.
(40) On the southern wall - Or on the table standing near the southern wall. The reason for all this is as a remembrance of the Menorah, whose place in the Mikdash was in the south.
(41) Like the lamps of the Menorah - Or south of the Menorah. This is a scribal error, and it should read: like the lamps of the Menorah.
(42) And he arranges them from east, etc. - This too is like the Menorah, for the lamps in the Mikdash were arranged from east to west, and so he should arrange the Chanukkah lamps. Know that some Rishonim hold that although the Menorah stood in the south, its lamps were arranged from north to south; according to them, the Chanukkah lamps should also be arranged in this way. Some places have practiced so, and one should not protest, for they have what to rely on.
(43) To the west - The one lighting stands with his back to the south and his face to the north, and begins from his right, which is the lamp near the aron kodesh; then he adds progressively, beginning from the leftmost and turning to his right, as Responsa Chatam Sofer siman 186 writes. See below at the end of siman 676, where we will write about this.
(44) And they light and bless - Although in the synagogue it is only a custom, nevertheless they bless over it, just as they bless over Hallel of Rosh Chodesh, which is only a custom. If a mourner leads prayers on the evening of Chanukkah, another person should light the lamps because Shehecheyanu is recited then, and it is recognizable as an expression of joy for the congregation, so it is not fitting that the mourner light them. But at home he may recite Shehecheyanu. On the other evenings of Chanukkah, when Shehecheyanu is not recited, the mourner may light the Chanukkah lamp. A mourner leads Minchah and Maariv on Chanukkah. On the fifteenth of Av and Shevat, and on Lag BaOmer, when there is no Hallel, the mourner also leads Shacharit. On Chol HaMoed, which is like Yom Tov, I have sometimes prevented the mourner from leading Minchah and Maariv, as Peri Megadim writes.
(45) And he must go back and light - Even the shaliach tzibbur who blessed in the synagogue must go back and light in his home, for each person has an obligation to light in his home. Nevertheless, he should not repeat Shehecheyanu at home unless he is lighting to discharge the obligation of his wife and household.
(46) To light in the synagogue between Minchah and Maariv - Meaning that even those who practice to light every day in the synagogue at tzeit hakochavim light after Minchah, when the people are gathered. Also, after Maariv it is not proper to delay the people for this, since each person must hurry home to light the Chanukkah lamp.
(47) Before Minchah - So wrote Maharshal. But in Darkei Moshe he wrote that we do not practice so; rather, even on erev Shabbat we light in the synagogue between Minchah and Maariv, when the people begin to gather and there is publicizing of the miracle. However, if the people are delayed in coming, and before a minyan comes to the synagogue the day will become sanctified, he should not wait at all, but should bless and light immediately, even before Minchah, for in any event there is publicizing of the miracle when they later come to the synagogue and see the lamps. Regarding an individual, see below at the end of siman 679 in Mishna Berurah.
(48) If he wants to hurry, etc. - This is specifically in this manner; otherwise, it is better that he himself complete the mitzvah he began.
(49) The shamash may, etc. - For the blessing takes effect on the one lamp that he lit, which is the essential matter, and the rest are only an enhancement of the mitzvah. The same applies at home: if some urgent matter arises after he has lit one of them, another may complete the remaining lamps for him.
