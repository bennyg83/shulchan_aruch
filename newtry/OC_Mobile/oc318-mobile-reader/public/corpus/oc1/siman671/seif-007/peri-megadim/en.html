And he arranges them. See the Taz, Beis Yosef, and Terumas HaDeshen, siman 104, regarding the dispute of Rabbi Yehudah and Rabbi Elazar bar Shimon in chapter Shtei HaLechem. According to the Rambam, of blessed memory, they were placed north and south. See Pri Chadash. The Levush in Pozna arranged them north and south, and each river follows its course. According to everyone, the Menorah should be on the south side, closer to the south, meaning to the right of the prayer leader near the amud; there they should place the menorah as a remembrance of the Mikdash, where the Menorah was on the south side according to everyone. See siman 676, se'if 5, regarding how he begins lighting. Regarding what is written in the gloss, 'or to the south of the menorah,' the Be'er HaGolah emended it to 'or like the lamps of the Menorah,' meaning either he should place the Chanukah lamp on the southern wall, or in a menorah standing on the south side. For he ruled east to west; and even according to the custom of north and south, one can also say that he begins the first lamp on his right, siman 676, se'if 5. Nevertheless, here he wrote east and west.
