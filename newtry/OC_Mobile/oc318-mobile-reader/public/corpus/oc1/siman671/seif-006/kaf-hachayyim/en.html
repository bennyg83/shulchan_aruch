6) [Seif 2] How many lamps does he kindle, etc. The Gemara, Shabbos 21b, states: The basic mitzvah of Chanukah is one lamp for a man and his household; those who enhance the mitzvah kindle one lamp for each member of the household; and those who enhance the enhancement (Beis Hillel) say that on the first day one kindles one lamp and thereafter continually adds. Tosafos explain that this final practice builds upon ‘one lamp for a man and his household’: all members of the household together have only one lamp on the first night. Consequently, when two lamps are seen in the home on the second night, the increase corresponding to the miracle is recognizable. If those who kindle one lamp for each household member on the first night were to kindle two for each person on the second night, the increase corresponding to the miracle would not be recognizable, because observers might say that the household has many members and each has only one lamp. This is the view of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch. Rambam, however, explains that even those who kindle one lamp for each household member kindle two for each person on the second night, continuing until eight. This is the opinion cited by Rema in the gloss. It is unusual here that the Sephardic custom follows Tosafos, while the Ashkenazic custom follows Rambam; we do not find this elsewhere. Turei Zahav, s.k. 1. This is not unusual, however, for Rambam himself, in chapter 4 of Hilchos Chanukah, writes that this was the established custom, unlike the rule he had just stated as the law; see there. Thus this was an early custom. Chemed Moshe, paragraph 4; that is, a custom predating Rambam and Tosafos.
