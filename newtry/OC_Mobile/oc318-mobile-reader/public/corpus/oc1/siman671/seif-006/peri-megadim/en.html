From its midpoint. See the Taz and the Beis Yosef. As it appears, according to the Tur's version in the Semak, it speaks of a person standing inside the house with his face toward the door; the opening is the hollow of the door, the panel that closes the house. The person's right is toward the left of the door, the place of the hinge, and the person's left is toward the right of the door, toward the mezuzah. He made two conditions: first, that the door is divided into two. For example, if the door is four amos wide, we say that two amos belong to the right of the mezuzah and two amos to the left, where the hinge is. In those two amos toward the hinge he should place the Chanukah lamp toward the left, meaning at the end of the third amah from the mezuzah, which is the beginning of the fourth amah, so that the Chanukah lamp will be only one amah from the hinge of the door and the threshold of the house. But according to the second explanation, it speaks of a person standing outside with his face toward the door, with the person's right toward the right of the mezuzah and his left toward the hinge of the door. There is one condition: that he place the Chanukah lamp at the end of two amos from the mezuzah, and it is also permitted even if the Chanukah lamp is two amos away from the hinge. According to the Tur's explanation, he calls the part of the door close to the hinge 'entrance,' etc.; see Eliyah Rabbah there. It appears to me that one can say as we wrote, that even according to the first explanation, if he places it one amah close to the hinge it is enough, and he need not place it within a tefach adjacent to the threshold and hinge. See the Bach, who explained differently, and see Pri Chadash. I did not have time to examine it properly. With Hashem's help, in Magen Avraham, letter 8, it will be explained further; here is not the place to elaborate.
