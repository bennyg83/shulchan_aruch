But if he places it higher than twenty amos, he has not fulfilled the obligation, etc. Note: See the Tur and the Taz regarding the dispute whether, if the walls reach the ceiling, this helps. Rabbi Yehudah HaLevi holds that it helps, and the Tur disagrees and holds that it does not help; see there. In my humble opinion, one can bring proof for the Tur from the language of the Talmud, which is the source of this law in chapter 2 of Shabbos, where it says there: a Chanukah lamp that he placed higher than twenty amos is invalid, like a sukkah and like a mavoi. One should be precise about this lengthy language, for it should have said briefly, 'it is invalid,' or at least it would have been enough to mention one of them, to say like a sukkah or to say like a mavoi alone. Why did it take both, 'like a sukkah and like a mavoi'? The intent is that we hold like Rava, that the reason regarding sukkah is because we require a temporary dwelling. According to this, it is invalid even if the walls reach the s'chach, as we hold in Shulchan Aruch, siman 633. Therefore Rabbi Tanchum, from whom this law comes, holds like Rava, that with a sukkah it is invalid in every case, even if the walls reach the s'chach. Therefore he had to mention both. If he had mentioned only 'like a mavoi,' one might have thought that since the reason is that the eye does not notice it, if the walls reach the ceiling it is valid, and the same should apply to a Chanukah lamp. Therefore he teaches that it is invalid like a sukkah: just as with a sukkah it is invalid even if the walls reach the s'chach, so too here it is invalid even if the walls reach the ceiling. And if he had mentioned only sukkah, I would not know the reason it is invalid, since with sukkah, if the walls reach the s'chach the eye notices it, and it is invalid only for another reason, that we require a temporary dwelling. If so, with a Chanukah lamp, where that reason does not apply, I would not know why it should be invalid if the walls reach the ceiling. Therefore he said 'like a mavoi,' for there the reason is that the eye does not notice it. Thus he properly teaches that the precision is: it is invalid like a sukkah even where the walls reach the ceiling, and the reason in mavoi, that the eye does not notice it, applies even where the walls reach the ceiling, like the words of the Tur in this. This is great support for the view of the Tur; analyze carefully. Now, I was unsure if he placed the lamp partly below twenty and partly above twenty, such as with a wax candle, whether he has fulfilled the obligation or not. In sukkah, siman 633, and in mavoi, siman 363, se'if 26, we hold that it is valid, as the Talmud concludes in Eruvin 3, that the hollow space of a sukkah was taught and the hollow space of a mavoi was taught. What is the law here? It appears to resolve this doubt from the language of the Talmud, which says: a Chanukah lamp that he placed higher than twenty amos is invalid, like a sukkah and like a mavoi. This is difficult: why did it need this lengthy wording? It would have sufficed to say, 'a Chanukah lamp that he placed higher than twenty amos is invalid,' and stop. Perforce, it teaches that it is specifically like a sukkah and like a mavoi, meaning the hollow space of a sukkah and the hollow space of a mavoi; so too here. If we probe more deeply, why did it need both? It should have said only one: invalid like a sukkah, or invalid like a mavoi. One can say that it mentioned sukkah to exclude the view of Raavyah brought by the Tur, who holds that where the walls reach the ceiling, a Chanukah lamp is valid even above twenty, and the Tur disagrees. Therefore, for us in practice, where we hold that the invalidation of sukkah is because it is a permanent dwelling, so that a sukkah is invalid even where the walls reach the s'chach, as we hold in siman 633, he teaches that it is invalid like a sukkah, meaning that even where they reach the ceiling it is also invalid. What he mentioned regarding mavoi can be explained according to what we hold above in siman 633: if its s'chach was higher than twenty and branches descend within twenty, if, when you remove what is above, there remains more shade than sun, it is valid, and if not, it is invalid. The Taz there brought in the name of the Ran that what the Talmud says, that if part of its s'chach is higher than twenty and part within twenty it is valid, implies even where what is within twenty does not itself have more shade than sun, but that descending branches are worse; see there. But in my humble opinion this does not appear so. Rather, one can say that with sukkah, in every case we require that its shade be more than its sun. The fact that the Talmud did not explain this is because it relied on the law explained in the Talmud regarding descending branches, where this is explicit. But regarding a crossbeam, it appears well that since the Talmud did not distinguish whether that lower part could stand on its own or not, and it had nothing to rely on in this matter, it indeed implies that it validates even if it could not stand on its own were the upper part removed. The reason is that with a crossbeam, since intrinsically any amount is enough because it is only for recognition, and it would fall only physically, what difference does it make if it currently stands through some other support? Since it is not falling, its main validity is that it is below, and it becomes like iron rods. But with s'chach, whose main validity exists only through the joining of what is above, if the lower part itself does not have the required measure it does not help. This is clear in reasoning. Therefore one can say that with Chanukah, if part of the lamp is within twenty and part is above twenty, even if the lower part does not have the required measure except by joining the part above, it is nevertheless valid like a mavoi and not like a sukkah. Therefore he mentioned sukkah for the point that walls reaching the ceiling do not help, and he mentioned mavoi for the point that it is permitted if part of the lamp is within twenty and part above twenty. There is also room to distinguish further between mavoi and sukkah: with a sukkah, since the reason is temporary dwelling, what does it help that part is within twenty? Ultimately the upper part above twenty is invalid and therefore does not combine. But with a crossbeam, where the reason is recognition, if part is within twenty and he sees through it, then the sight controls that same item even above, so the part above is also valid. This is very clear. Therefore, with Chanukah as well, which is also for recognition, it is valid if part of the lamp is within twenty and part above, for this too is like a mavoi. Thus he mentioned sukkah regarding the point that walls reaching the ceiling do not help, for in this, even though the reason is recognition, it still does not help according to the Tur's reasoning. Therefore he mentioned sukkah, because for us it does not help even when the walls reach the s'chach. And he mentioned mavoi regarding the point that in this it is like a mavoi, for the reason is recognition, and it helps if part of the lamp is within twenty and part above, because through what is within twenty one sees all of it; analyze carefully. Now, while involved in this, I am very astonished at the poskim who copied this language of the Talmud, that a Chanukah lamp which he placed higher than twenty is invalid. This is surprising, for in Eruvin the Talmud concludes that the hollow space of a sukkah was taught and the hollow space of a mavoi was taught. If so, if the s'chach is higher than twenty, and likewise the crossbeam, it is valid if the hollow space is only twenty. There the Sages spoke of the body of the sukkah and the mavoi, that the sukkah itself is higher than twenty, and likewise the mavoi; in such a case it is invalid because the crossbeam and s'chach are then higher than twenty amos. But here with the Chanukah lamp, they stated the law about the body of the lamp, that if he placed it higher than twenty it is invalid. Why? If the hollow space is only twenty, it should be valid, for it says sukkah and mavoi, and with them too it is valid in such a case. One who says this would hold that with sukkah and mavoi it is also invalid even if the hollow space is only twenty. But for us, who hold that this is valid, the wording should not have been stated this way. It should have said: if the hollow space under the lamp is higher than twenty, it is invalid. Perhaps, since with Chanukah the main publicizing is the light, then granted, with a crossbeam and s'chach, where the purpose is recognition, if from the underside of the crossbeam or s'chach to the ground reaches twenty it is valid. But with a Chanukah lamp, since the lamp has some length and the main publicizing is the light, whenever the body of the lamp is higher than twenty, the light is above higher than twenty, and it is as if the body of the hollow space were more than twenty. Analyze carefully. Still, this matter requires investigation.
