There, seif 6. "But if he places it above twenty amot, he has not fulfilled, etc." NB: See the Tur and the Taz regarding the dispute whether it helps when the walls reach the ceiling: R. Yitzchak HaLevi holds that it helps, and the Tur disagrees and holds that it does not. See there. In my humble opinion one may bring proof for the Tur from the language of the Gemara, the source of this law in Shabbat chapter 2, where it says: a Chanukah lamp placed above twenty amot is invalid, like sukkah and like mavoi. One should examine the length of this wording. It could have said simply "it is invalid," or at least it would have sufficed to mention one of them, either sukkah or mavoi. Why did it mention both, "like sukkah and like mavoi"? The intent is that we hold like Rava, that the reason for sukkah is because we require a temporary dwelling. According to this, it is invalid even if the walls reach the s'chach, as we rule in Shulchan Aruch siman 633. Therefore R. Tanchum, the source of this law, holds like Rava, that sukkah is invalid in every case even when the walls reach the s'chach. He therefore had to mention both. If he had mentioned only mavoi, one might have thought that since the reason is that the eye does not control it, if the walls reach the ceiling it is valid even for a Chanukah lamp. Therefore he teaches that it is invalid like sukkah: just as sukkah is invalid even when the walls reach the s'chach, so too this is invalid even when the walls reach the ceiling. And if he had mentioned only sukkah, we would not have known the reason for invalidity, since in sukkah, when the walls reach the s'chach, the eye does control it, but it is invalid for another reason, because we require a temporary dwelling; for Chanukah lamp, where that reason does not apply, we would not know why it should be invalid if the walls reach the ceiling. Therefore he said like mavoi, where the reason is that the eye does not control it. He thus teaches well that the precision from sukkah is that it is invalid even when the walls reach the ceiling, and the reason from mavoi is that the eye does not control it, for even when the walls reach the ceiling the lack of visual control applies, as the Tur said. This is strong support for the Tur. Examine carefully. I was uncertain if one placed the lamp partly below twenty and partly above twenty, such as with a wax candle, whether he fulfilled the obligation or not. In sukkah, siman 633, and mavoi, siman 363 seif 26, we hold that it is valid, as the Gemara concludes in Eruvin 3, that we learned the open space of sukkah and the open space of mavoi. What is the law here? It seems possible to resolve this doubt from the Gemara's wording: "A Chanukah lamp that he placed above twenty amot is invalid, like sukkah and like mavoi." Why this length? It would have sufficed to say that a Chanukah lamp placed above twenty amot is invalid. Perforce it teaches that it is specifically like sukkah and mavoi, where the measure is the open space of the sukkah and the open space of the mavoi; so too here. More precisely, why were both needed? It could have said only "invalid like sukkah" or "invalid like mavoi." One may say that it mentioned sukkah to exclude the view of the Raavyah brought by the Tur, that if the walls reach the ceiling a Chanukah lamp above twenty is valid, while the Tur disagrees. For us, since the invalidity of sukkah is because of a permanent dwelling, sukkah is invalid even when the walls reach the s'chach, as we hold in siman 633. Therefore it teaches "invalid like sukkah," that even if the walls reach the ceiling it is invalid. It mentioned mavoi for another matter. In siman 633 we hold that if the s'chach was above twenty and branches descend within twenty, if, were the upper part removed, the lower part would have more shade than sun, it is valid; if not, it is invalid. The Taz there brought in the name of the Ran that when the Gemara says part of the s'chach is above twenty and part within twenty it is valid, this implies even if what is within twenty does not by itself have more shade than sun, and that descending branches are worse. In my humble opinion this does not appear correct. Rather, regarding sukkah one may say that in every case we require more shade than sun below. The Gemara did not explain this because it relied on the law stated regarding descending branches, where this is explicit. But with a korah it appears that since it did not distinguish whether the lower portion could stand by itself or not, and there was nothing else on which to rely, it clearly validates even if it could not stand by itself were the upper part removed. The reason is that for a korah, since intrinsically any amount suffices because it is a marker, only that otherwise it would fall, what difference does it make that it now stands through something else? Since it is not falling, its essential validity is that it is below, and it is made like iron rods. But with s'chach, whose validity exists only through combining with what is above, if the lower part does not have the required measure it does not help. This is clear in logic. Therefore one may say that with Chanukah, if part of the lamp is within twenty and part is above twenty, even if the lower part does not contain the required measure except by joining what is above, it is nevertheless valid like mavoi and not like sukkah. This is why the Gemara mentioned sukkah regarding the fact that walls reaching the ceiling do not help, and mavoi regarding the fact that part of the lamp within twenty and part above twenty is permitted. Another distinction between mavoi and sukkah can be made: with sukkah, since the reason is temporary dwelling, what does it help that part is within twenty? The upper portion above twenty is invalid and therefore does not combine. But with a korah, whose reason is visual recognition, once part is within twenty and is seen by it, the eye controls that item even above. This is very clear. Therefore with Chanukah too, since the reason is recognition, if part of the lamp is within twenty and part above twenty it is valid, like mavoi; by what is within twenty one sees the whole. Examine carefully. While on this matter, I find the poskim's copying of the Gemara's language, that if one placed a Chanukah lamp above twenty it is invalid, very astonishing. In Eruvin the Gemara concludes that we learned the open space of sukkah and the open space of mavoi, so if the s'chach or korah is above twenty it is valid when the open space is only twenty. There the Sages spoke of the body of the sukkah or mavoi being above twenty; in that case it is invalid, for then the korah and s'chach are above twenty amot. But here, regarding Chanukah lamp, they stated the law about the body of the lamp, that if he placed it above twenty it is invalid. Why? If the open space is only twenty it should be valid, as with sukkah and mavoi. One who says otherwise would also hold that sukkah and mavoi are invalid even where the open space is only twenty. But for us, who hold that such a case is valid, the wording should not have been copied this way; it should have said that if the open space beneath the lamp is above twenty, it is invalid. Perhaps because with Chanukah the main publicity is the light. With a korah and s'chach, they themselves are the marker, so if from the bottom of the korah or s'chach to the ground reaches only twenty, it is valid. But with a Chanukah lamp, since the lamp has some height and the main publicity is the light, whenever the body of the lamp is above twenty, the light is above more than twenty, and it is as though the open space itself is more than twenty. Examine carefully. This still requires study.
