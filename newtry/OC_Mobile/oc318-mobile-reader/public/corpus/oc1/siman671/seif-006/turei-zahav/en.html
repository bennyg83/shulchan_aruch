Higher than twenty. The Tur wrote in the name of Rabbi Yehudah HaLevi: specifically when he places it outside, but if he places it in the house, even higher than twenty it is valid, as we say regarding a sukkah that if the walls of the house reach the s'chach, even higher than twenty it is valid, because the eye notices it. It appears to me that the case is not comparable to the proof. There, we require that his eyes notice the roof, and since the walls reach the roof, through them his eyes notice the roof. But here, where his eyes must notice the lamps, since it is higher than twenty and his eye does not notice it, what difference does it make that the roof is above it? Because of the roof his eye does not notice it more. End quote. The Beis Yosef in the name of Mahari Abuhav answered that Rabbi Yehudah HaLevi's reasoning is that when there is empty air, the air damages the eyes and he cannot look upward as much as he wants; for this he brought proof from sukkah, that if the walls reach the s'chach, even higher than twenty it is valid, for the eye notices it. End quote. Although I am not worthy to decide, nevertheless the words of Mahari Abuhav are surprising to me. If, as he says, the main invalidation of a height above twenty is because there is air there that harms the eyes, why did they need in the Gemara, according to Rabbah, to find a permissive case specifically where the walls reach the s'chach? We could find a permissive case without this, namely if there is some wall near the s'chach in that measure that is above twenty, while the lower wall is a small wall. Since there is no air in that measure, the air below twenty should not harm him afterward; for if not, even s'chach below twenty should be invalid. But according to the Tur's reasoning, it is good: a person's sight does not go upward unless there are walls from below to above, and then sight goes from the walls to the s'chach. Therefore the walls must reach the s'chach from below to above without interruption, so that through them the eye's sight goes upward, and wherever there is an interruption, the sight drawn upward stops. It appears to me that on account of this the words of our teacher the Tur are primary, that walls do not help for a lamp that is higher than twenty, even in a house that has walls. Furthermore, it appears to me that it is not reasonable here to validate the lamp through the walls, because the main mitzvah of the Chanukah lamp is at tzeis hakochavim, as the Mordechai wrote, and whatever is higher than twenty amos, the eye does not go there through the walls, for the walls themselves are also not visible at night, and the light of the lamp from above does not control there. This appears correct to me.
