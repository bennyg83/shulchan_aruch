(S.k. 6) Lower than ten; it appears to me that if he lives, etc. For the reason that we require it to be higher than three is, as the Tur wrote, that below that it is like the ground itself, and it is not recognizable that it was lit intentionally, so there is no publicizing of the miracle. So too we require it to be lower than ten, because it is not the way of a lamp that one lights for his own need to place it lower than ten, since the main use is above; therefore there is publicity that it was lit for the mitzvah. According to this, the Beis Yosef wrote in the name of the Mordechai that for us, who light inside, it is permitted to place it higher than ten, since in any case there is recognition only for the members of the household, and they know that it was lit for the mitzvah. But the poskim wrote that nevertheless, even for us, one should not change from what they practiced when they lit outside. If so, when he lives in an upper story and places it in a window, even though it is higher than ten, it is nevertheless preferable to place it there, for at least there is some recognition for the people of the public domain, rather than placing it elsewhere below ten with no recognition at all for the people of the public domain. Also in the time of the Talmud, if he lived in an upper story, by law he could light only in the window and not elsewhere, and certainly even if the window was higher than ten it was fine. Nevertheless, certainly if he has two windows, one higher than ten and one lower than ten, he should certainly place it in the window lower than ten. The Pri Chadash wrote as follows: It is a clear matter that if the window is higher than twenty amos, he places it at the entrance to his house. End quote. Twenty amos are certainly measured from the ground of the public domain and not from the ground of the house, for the reason that if it is higher than twenty he has not fulfilled the obligation is that the eye does not notice it, and we require that the eye of the people of the public domain notice it. However, for us, where the main recognition is for the members of the household, it is enough if it is lower than twenty from the ground of the house. Nevertheless, perhaps even for us, where the window is higher than twenty from the ground of the public domain, it is preferable to place it nearby, within the tefach adjacent to the mezuzah, since higher than twenty there is no recognition for the people of the public domain, and for the members of the household their recognition is better near the entrance. As for the current custom to place it higher than ten even when it is not in a window, the Eliyah Rabbah wrote that we rely on the Mordechai mentioned above, that for us, who light inside, there is no concern. See there what he wrote in the name of Sefer Tzeidah LaDerech.
