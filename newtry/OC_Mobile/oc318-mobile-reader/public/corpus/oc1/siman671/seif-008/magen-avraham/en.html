Because of suspicion. They will say: since he did not light at this entrance, he did not light at the other either. But when they are on one side, it is visible to him, and we are not concerned for the suspicion of strangers who might think the house is divided inside and say they are two houses, for unknown people are not commonly found in the streets at night. The Beis Yosef challenged from siman 90, se'if 8, where we say that if a synagogue has another entrance it is permitted, etc., and we do not say: since he did not enter at this entrance, he did not enter at the other either. He answered that since there is monetary loss here, they suspect him more, unlike with a synagogue. End quote. It does not appear to me difficult at all, for there the reason is not that they suspect him of not entering the synagogue, since in truth many times a person is occupied with his affairs and need not go to the synagogue. Furthermore, when he walks in the marketplace at the time of prayer, we see that he does not enter the synagogue and nevertheless it is permitted. Rather, the reason is as Rashi wrote: when he walks near the synagogue, it appears as though he casts off the yoke and disgraces the synagogue, for although he does not enter it, he walks near it; he should have distanced himself from it and been embarrassed that he cannot enter it. But if there is another entrance, there is no disgrace to the synagogue. Examine Rashi in Berachos, folio 61, and you will find as I have said. Against the answer of the Beis Yosef there is difficulty: is there no monetary loss with a synagogue, since in the meantime he is idle from the marketplaces, as we say in Bava Metzia, chapter 2?
