A courtyard that has two entrances, etc. The Beis Yosef challenged: how is this different from that which appears in the first chapter of Berachos, that it is forbidden to pass behind a synagogue while the congregation is praying, and they said this only where there is no other entrance, but if there is another entrance there is no issue, for they say that he enters by the other entrance? Here too let us say that even though they see that he did not light at one entrance, they will not come to suspect him, for they will say that he lit at the other entrance. He answered that prayer is different, for if he does not pray he casts off the yoke, and therefore they do not suspect him of this. One can answer further that a Chanukah lamp is different, for it involves financial loss, so he is suspected more than with prayer. There are also other distinctions. In my humble opinion, it appears that certainly wherever there is suspicion, he must act so that he is clean before Hashem and before Yisrael. But if he has two entrances and many people also go through the other entrance, there is no suspicion at all, as appears in Avodah Zarah, folio 43, that regarding something done by many, we are not concerned for suspicion. One who passes behind a synagogue is certainly going on a path where many go to the synagogue through another entrance. That is, one whose place is near that entrance passes this way and does not enter through this entrance, because sometimes he would need to pass in front of those praying in the synagogue. For if not, what benefit does the other entrance give him, since there is no path there? Rather, certainly there is a path there, and therefore there is no suspicion at all.
And there is no recognition for the people of the public domain, etc. The Tur wrote this in the name of the Baal HaTerumah and wrote about it: It appears that one should not be lenient, for even for us there is concern, since we light at the entrance of the house and passersby see that he did not light, so there is suspicion. End quote. Therefore the Rama wrote to answer this, that nowadays we light literally inside, etc., excluding the Tur's difficulty in his time. They would light at the entrance of the house, and at least they made recognition for passersby, as he wrote at the end of siman 671; therefore there is suspicion wherever there is not proper recognition there for passersby. This is not so nowadays, when we light literally inside and we do not care at all to make recognition for the people of the public domain; therefore we do not care about their suspicion.
But only once, etc. This matter is explained explicitly this way in Responsa Rashba, siman 541.
