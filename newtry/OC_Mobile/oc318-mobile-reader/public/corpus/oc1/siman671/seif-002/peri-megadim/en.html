Which are called. Lampa. See the Taz. Our candelabras, made of polished copper and the like, are all round, and an middle lamp is not applicable. In Magen Avraham, s.k. 4, 'on the side of the middle one adjacent to the entrance' means that the main mitzvah is to begin with the lamp closest to the entrance. See siman 676, in the Taz, letter 6, in the name of the Maharshal, that he should place the lamps along the length of the tefach and light from left to right. If so, one who lights in candelabras should light the lamp closest to the entrance and turn rightward, from left to right; this still requires some investigation. What he wrote, that the meticulous do not light in lampas, applies even to our candelabras. The Pri Chadash brought glass lanterns and concluded that he saw people lighting in them, but I do not know the explanation: whether this means that they put a tallow or wax candle inside a glass partition, or oil in such a manner.
Said the ordinary writer: In the words of the Rav in the gloss I saw fit to explain somewhat what he wrote, 'and not in a circle.' In the Darkei Moshe he wrote: as they do in the synagogue, where they insert nails. I do not know if he means that they insert nails in the wall in a circle, or around one round board they insert nails in a circle. In any case, there is some distinction between a candelabrum and this; one can say so, and see Pri Chadash. You will see that the Rav, of blessed memory, wrote in the Darkei Moshe that wherever each one is separated from the other, it is not called a bonfire; but here he wrote that it must be greatly separated from the other. In Terumas HaDeshen, siman 105, he wrote that a candelabrum is not comparable to a bowl, because each branch is separated by a partition, and furthermore, according to the author of the Ittur, with a bowl, a distance of two fingerbreadths is permitted, while here they are very far apart; see there. The Mechaber forbids with a bowl even when they are very far apart, like the Tur, and from the Rav's silence he admitted to him. But with a candelabrum he was stringent that we require the branches to be separated and also much more distant than two fingerbreadths. Thus, if one of these is missing, meaning they are not separated by partitions, or they are separated but the branches are not two fingerbreadths apart from one another, it is forbidden, for it is like a bonfire. If so, it requires investigation when one attaches wax candles to the wall and they are not two fingerbreadths apart, for one can say it is forbidden. See Eliyah Rabbah: in the Tur, one fingerbreadth, and in Terumas HaDeshen, two fingerbreadths. With Hashem's help, in Magen Avraham, s.k. 3 and 4, this will be explained further.
