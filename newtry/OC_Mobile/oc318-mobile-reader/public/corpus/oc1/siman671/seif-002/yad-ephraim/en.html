(106) Many were perplexed, etc., and the side where the hinge is is the second side, etc. — meaning: when a person stands inside, his right faces the hinge and his left the mezuzah; when he stands outside it is reversed, his right toward the mezuzah and his left toward the hinge. When standing inside, the part of the hinge may be called the entrance side because it is closer to the inside of the house, and the part near the mezuzah is the outer side. Per the first explanation that the person is inside — if so the part close to the inside is called entrance, i.e. toward the hinge side, which is opposite the man's right; one could then explain "to the left relative to the man" as in their explanation — but this is not so, for then the lighting would be close to the mezuzah, since per this explanation when the man is inside his left is toward the mezuzah, and this is not reasonable. Therefore explain that this "left" is only left in the ordinary sense, i.e. toward the hinge side, as we say: Chanukah candle on the left and mezuzah on the right. If so, when he said half of the entrance he means that half on the hinge side — why then add the second condition that it be to the left? Rather it must mean that within that same part it should be to the left, i.e. at the far end near the wall — and this is the Taz's intent in what he wrote at the end. Not like the explanation that on the contrary, etc. And in Mishbetzos Zahav he wrote per the first explanation that in the entrance part one places it to the left, i.e. that side adjacent to the part near the mezuzah, which is the man's left side when standing inside — but this is not so, for the Taz concludes explicitly that it should be at the far end near the wall, etc. And it is clear as he wrote that per the first explanation there is no explanation of "left" as left relative to the man, for then it would be closer to the mezuzah side, which is not so; rather his explanation is "left" as the opening is called left everywhere, i.e. toward the hinge side of the door:
