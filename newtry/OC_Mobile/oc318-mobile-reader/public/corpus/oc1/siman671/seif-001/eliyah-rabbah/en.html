[1] He borrows, etc. But he is not obligated to hire himself out, as with the four cups in siman 472, because here he has recognition of the miracle when he sees lamps in his fellow's house, unlike the four cups; Olas Shabbos. This requires investigation, for the Beis Yosef wrote the main law here because he understood that the Rambam learned it from the four cups. If so, it is difficult: from where did he know to learn from there, since here is different because he sees in his fellow's house? It is further difficult, for the Maggid Mishneh at the end of Hilchos Chanukah wrote that the Rambam learned it from the four cups, that even a poor person, etc., because of publicizing the miracle, and all the more so with the Chanukah lamp, which is preferable to kiddush of the day. End quote. Thus the Chanukah lamp is preferable to the four cups. In truth, the Maggid Mishneh also requires investigation: what proof is there from the fact that it is preferable to kiddush, which has no publicizing of the miracle, to the four cups, which also have publicizing of the miracle? I then found in Lechem Chamudos that he too left it requiring investigation. What appears to me in my humble opinion is that he holds that the miracle of the Chanukah lamp is preferable to the four cups, because on Pesach there is another recognition, matzah and maror. What he wrote, that it is preferable to kiddush of the day, he wrote only to hint that it is publicizing the miracle, for that is why it is preferable to kiddush of the day. Nevertheless, the difficulty remains against the Olas Shabbos. It also requires investigation for me why he said 'he borrows' and did not say 'he takes a loan' as with the four cups. Perhaps it appears to me that the language 'he borrows' means that he asks at the doors, as above in siman 263, se'if 2, and 'is sustained from charity' refers to the charity fund. For this reason, perhaps the Shulchan Aruch relied on the fact that the same law applies that he should hire himself out, for they say in Bava Basra: a person should always hire himself for work that is foreign to him and not need, etc. This requires investigation. It appears to me that the obligation to ask is for one lamp, which is the law, but not for what is mehadrin in se'if 2. A proof is from siman 678 regarding a Chanukah lamp and kiddush.
