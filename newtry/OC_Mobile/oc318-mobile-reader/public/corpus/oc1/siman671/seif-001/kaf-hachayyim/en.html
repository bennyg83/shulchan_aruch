1) [Seif 1] One must be exceedingly careful, etc. The Gemara, Shabbos 23b, states: One who is accustomed to kindling a lamp will have sons who are Torah scholars. Rashi explains that the verse states, ‘For a mitzvah is a lamp and Torah is light’: through the mitzvah-lamp of Shabbos and Chanukah comes the light of Torah. Beis Yosef cites this. One cannot say that the person himself will merit Torah, because the passage speaks of a complete person, who has already attained his own perfection. Levush. Bach writes that if he is not himself capable of this attainment, he nevertheless merits sons who are Torah scholars.
2) There: Even a poor person supported by charity must borrow, etc. This is analogous to the four cups, and the reason is publicizing the miracle. Rambam, chapter 4 of Hilchos Chanukah, halachah 12; Beis Yosef; Levush.
3) There: He must borrow or sell, etc. His obligation to borrow applies only to one lamp (the basic legal requirement) but not to the enhanced practice described in seif 2. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1. Yad Aharon likewise writes in Hagahos HaTur that if he has enough to kindle one lamp on each night, he need not go from door to door or sell his garment, since he fulfills his legal obligation. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1; Chayei Adam, klal 154, paragraph 6; Olas Shabbos, paragraph 3.
4) There: He must borrow or sell, etc. It appears that the charity trustees are obligated to provide him with lamps for kindling, as they must provide the four cups. If they do not give them to him, however, he must act as stated in Shulchan Aruch. In any event, it appears that they need provide him only one lamp for each night (the lamp for a man and his household) not the enhanced number. Chemed Moshe, paragraph 1; Mishna Berurah in Biur Halachah.
5) There: He must borrow or sell, etc. He is not obligated, however, to hire himself out, as he is for the four cups above in siman 472, because here he still has recognition of the miracle when he sees the lamps in his fellow’s home. Olas Tamid, paragraph 1. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1, challenges this ruling and leaves it requiring examination; see there. Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 2, likewise writes that the words of Olas Tamid do not appear correct and that he is obligated to hire himself out just as for the four cups; see there. Erech HaShulchan, paragraph 1, writes similarly and proves from Responsa Rivash, siman 111, that no poor person is exempt; see there. Raviah rules likewise.
