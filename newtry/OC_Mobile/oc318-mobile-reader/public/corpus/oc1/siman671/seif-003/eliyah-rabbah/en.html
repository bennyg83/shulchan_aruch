If he has only a minimal amount of oil and his fellow has none at all, it is better that he light one lamp each night and also give to his fellow, for by law one needs only one; Magen Avraham. At first glance this is difficult, for his fellow too by law does not need more, since all members of his household light by law with one lamp. Perhaps it speaks of one who is not a member of his household. One can further say that in any event he must join in, and he wants to light by himself; see s.k. 9 and siman 677, se'if 3. However, Responsa Beis Yaakov, siman 117, requires investigation, for he ruled that a person should not lend a tallis to his fellow in a village or grant him ownership in his esrog, even though he can fulfill the obligation with his fellow's, because it is more pleasing to him, etc., to recite the blessing over his own. End quote. Here too, he wants to be from the mehadrin, and it is forced to distinguish. However, in siman 658, s.k. 12, I concluded unlike Responsa Beis Yaakov. Now, in Responsa Shaar Ephraim, siman 42, he asks why women do not have the custom to light when the husband is at home; how is she different from the other members of the household? See there. It appears to me that his wife is like his own self and is not included in mehadrin. So too it is implied in the Maharshal, siman 85, and Mateh Moshe, that specifically when she has no husband, or when the husband is not in the city, she lights. See siman 677, s.k. 4. So too it is implied in Raavan, siman 340.
