That one should not attach them together. This is the language of the Darkei Moshe: When one lights a Shabbos, Chanukah, or Yom Tov lamp, and the candles are attached close to one another until they heat each other and the wax drips because of the heat, and they also bend and fall, he has not fulfilled the mitzvah; Or Zarua. End quote. This implies that candles twisted together may be made, for on Shabbos and Yom Tov it is a mitzvah to make twisted candles, as written in siman 263. In Mahariv he wrote as follows: In Erfurt they take four or five Chanukah candles and attach them together, meaning that they are braided like a chain, and light them as one lamp; I forbade them, for it is like a bonfire. End quote. Perhaps two candles are permitted, but in Responsa Maharshal it implies that it is forbidden, and so it is implied in siman 298, s.k. 2, and siman 433, se'if 2. The Maharshal wrote in a responsum: One who lights in candelabras should hang it within the tefach adjacent to the entrance, begin with the middle lamp that is adjacent to the entrance, and turn toward the right. Those who are meticulous do not light in candelabras, because they have no beautification for the Chanukah lamp. End quote.
