He filled a bowl with oil and surrounded it with wicks. The same law applies when he surrounded candles in a circle in such a way that, through this, the flame can also join together.
And it is permitted to light in candelabras. See Mishnah Berurah. The later authorities wrote in the name of the Maharshal that although by law it is permitted, nevertheless this is not beautification for the Chanukah lamp.
Even with Shabbos candles, etc. From this language it implies that even on Shabbos and Yom Tov one must be careful not to do this, but this is not so, for on Shabbos and Yom Tov it is a mitzvah to make them twisted together, as above in siman 263. The Magen Avraham already raised this difficulty. See the end of his words, for according to what he concludes in the name of the Maharshal, that in our case it is forbidden even with two candles twisted together, there is consequently no explanation at all for the words of the Rama in what he concludes, that even with Shabbos and Yom Tov candles one must be careful. See the Pri Megadim, who was also very pressed in explaining the words of the Rama. In my humble opinion, it appears that one detail is missing here, which is mentioned in the Or Zarua, namely when they attach them after lighting until, through this, they heat up and fall. This is indeed forbidden both on Chanukah and on Shabbos and Yom Tov. This is the language of the Darkei Moshe: When they light a Shabbos lamp, or Chanukah, or Yom Tov lamp, and they attach the candles close to one another until they heat each other, the wax drips, and they also bend over and fall, they have not fulfilled the mitzvah; Or Zarua. End quote. A sign that the truth is as I say is that the Rama cited the Or Zarua, but the first law is not mentioned in the Or Zarua at all, only this law.
