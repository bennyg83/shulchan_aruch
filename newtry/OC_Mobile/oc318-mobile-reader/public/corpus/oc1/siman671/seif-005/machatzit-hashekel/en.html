(S.k. 5) And one needs, etc. The Ran explained, etc., and from this the custom emerged, etc., as the Rama wrote, etc. At first glance, on the contrary, in Responsa Rama it implies the opposite, for the questioner wanted to infer from the words of the Ran that if one is not lighting on the table, another lamp is not needed. But the Rama rejected this: that which the Ran's words imply, that specifically when lighting on the table another lamp is needed, is according to the custom in the days of the Talmud, when they would light outside at the entrance; then another lamp is not needed. But for us, who light inside, even if he lights near the entrance, nevertheless there is concern that perhaps he will use the light of the Chanukah lamp. Therefore our custom, that we place a shamash even when lighting near the entrance, works well even according to the Ran. These are the contents of his words. Nevertheless, it is clear from this that there is no proof from the words of the Ran for the custom. Rather, on the contrary, according to the custom one must explain the words of the Ran so that his words not contradict the custom. How then did the Magen Avraham write that the custom emerged from the words of the Ran? Perhaps one can say that this is specifically according to the questioner's version in the Ran, for he copied the words of the Ran as follows: Regarding that which Rava said about what we say, 'and in a time of danger he places it on his table, and another lamp is needed to use its light,' the Ran wrote: although Rava agrees that it is forbidden to use its light, and since so, it is obvious that here another lamp is needed, this is what he means: even though, etc., since he places it on his table, perforce he uses its light, therefore another lamp is needed to make a recognition. End quote. From the Ran's language, 'therefore,' etc., it somewhat implies like the view of the questioner, specifically when he places it on his table, since perforce he uses its light. The Rama too had to respond by distinguishing between lighting inside or outside. But according to the version that the Magen Avraham brought from the Ran's words, and this is the version in the Ran before us, 'perforce he uses its light; even so,' etc., from the fact that he wrote the language 'even so,' it implies that all the more so if he does not light it on his table, where it is possible that he will not use its light, it is obvious that another lamp is needed so that he not come to use its light. According to this, what the Magen Avraham concluded, 'as the Rama wrote in a responsum,' requires explanation, for in any event the responsum does not imply this.
