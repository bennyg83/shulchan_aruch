Higher than twenty. See the Taz, and so ruled the Bach. See Tosafos in Sukkah 2, s.v. 'and since,' that with the crossbeam of a mavoi, even though it rests on the walls of the mavoi, if it is a tefach, it is not recognizable; the same applies to the Chanukah lamp. See the Bach. This is what it means in Shabbos 22: a Chanukah lamp is invalid higher than twenty amos, like a sukkah and like a mavoi. This teaches: just as with a mavoi, even if the walls reach the crossbeam it is invalid, the same applies here. A sukkah too, according to Rava, whom the halachah follows, is invalid even if the walls reach the s'chach; see siman 633 and Darkei Moshe in the name of Mahara of Prague, of blessed memory. In se'if 5, he places it in the window adjacent to the public domain. See the Pri Chadash: if it is twenty amos high, it has no advantage at all. However, for us, who light inside, one may place it in the window, and nevertheless there is some recognition for the people of the public domain, so this is preferable. With Hashem's help, in Magen Avraham, letters 10 and 14, I will explain further.
And know that higher than twenty amos means even with long wax candles, that wherever the place of the flame is higher than twenty amos, it is invalid. This is all the more so according to what the Taz wrote, that at night the walls are not recognizable, nor the candles except in the place where they burn. See siman 633, se'if 1, regarding a space of twenty amos; this requires investigation here. See there.
