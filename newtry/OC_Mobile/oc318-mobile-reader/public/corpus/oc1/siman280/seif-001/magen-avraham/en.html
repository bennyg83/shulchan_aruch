It is a mitzvah to eat garlic on erev Shabbat or on Friday night (Yam Shel Shlomo, Bava Kama ch. 7; Semag), and the same applies to other things that increase seed. See the end of siman 608.
The Gaon wrote in Sefer Birkat HaZevach: R. Yaski, may his Rock protect him, Av Beit Din of the holy community of Wodzisław, asked me: We find that early sages ate their ordinary food in purity for sacred food; what did they eat on Shabbat? Perforce, after marital relations they were tevil yom, and forbidden to eat that which was prepared in purity for sacred food, for a tevil yom is a second degree. According to what I wrote, there is no difficulty, for we do not find a third degree impurity in ordinary food prepared in purity for sacred food, etc.; see there. I did not understand his words, for regarding ordinary food prepared in purity for terumah the Rambam agrees that it produces a third degree. If so, what did the kohanim do when they ate food prepared in purity for terumah? One cannot say they ate meat, for one is obligated to eat bread on Shabbat. Another difficulty: if they became impure through a corpse, what did they eat, since they were impure for seven days? One cannot say that they were careful to avoid corpse impurity, for what about the seven obligatory relatives? In my humble opinion there is no difficulty, for the main intent in eating ordinary food in purity for sacred food was to accustom oneself to eating consecrated food in purity, as stated in Chullin ch. 2. On the days of their impurity they ate ordinary food alone, for at that time they did not eat consecrated food. See Chullin 2b and Gittin 54. Alternatively, they ate bread that had not become susceptible to impurity and drank spring water, as Shnei Luchot HaBerit wrote to do during the Ten Days of Repentance.
