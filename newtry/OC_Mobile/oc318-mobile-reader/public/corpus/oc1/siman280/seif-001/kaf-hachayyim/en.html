1) [Seif 1] Marital relations are among the delights of Shabbos. It is a mitzvah to eat garlic on Friday or on Shabbos night. Yam Shel Shlomo, Bava Kamma, chapter 7; Sefer Mitzvos Gadol. The same applies to other foods that increase semen; see the end of siman 608. Magen Avraham. Specifically cooked garlic increases semen, whereas raw garlic actually diminishes desire. Sefer Chassidim, siman 390. See further there, where it states that all salty foods diminish semen, while unsalted legumes and lentils increase it; see there. It appears that this applies specifically to a married man. If he is unmarried, or his wife is not tahor, and the like, he should not eat foods that increase semen, lest he experience a nocturnal emission, G-d forbid. See Machazik Berachah, paragraph 1, who writes that Rambam omitted this enactment of eating garlic on Shabbos night because the customs of places and eras are not uniform. He writes, however, that some places still observe this enactment today; see there.
2) There: They are among the delights of Shabbos. Rosh writes at the end of the chapter Merubah that the mitzvah of marital relations on Shabbos night is alluded to by the verse, 'The Children of Israel shall observe Shabbos,' whose initial letters spell bi'ah, marital relations. These are his words. This means that 'shall observe' is understood like the verse, 'His father kept the matter in mind': he should refrain from relations with his wife until Shabbos night. The verse, 'So says Hashem to the eunuchs who observe My Shabbosos,' also refers to this, as stated there. Bach. We learn from here that women are likewise included in the command concerning marital relations on Shabbos, because they too are included in observing Shabbos. Hagahos Ashiri; Perishah, paragraph 1.
