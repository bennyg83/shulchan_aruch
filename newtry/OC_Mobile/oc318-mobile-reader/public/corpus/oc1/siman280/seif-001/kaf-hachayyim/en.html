1) [Seif 1] Marital relations are among the delights of Shabbos. It is a mitzvah to eat garlic on Friday or on Shabbos night. Yam Shel Shlomo, Bava Kamma, chapter 7; Sefer Mitzvos Gadol. The same applies to other foods that increase semen; see the end of siman 608. Magen Avraham. Specifically cooked garlic increases semen, whereas raw garlic actually diminishes desire. Sefer Chassidim, siman 390. See further there, where it states that all salty foods diminish semen, while unsalted legumes and lentils increase it; see there. It appears that this applies specifically to a married man. If he is unmarried, or his wife is not tahor, and the like, he should not eat foods that increase semen, lest he experience a nocturnal emission, God forbid. See Machazik Berachah, paragraph 1, who writes that Rambam omitted this enactment of eating garlic on Shabbos night because the customs of places and eras are not uniform. He writes, however, that some places still observe this enactment today; see there.
