It is a mitzvah to eat garlic on erev Shabbos or on Friday night. This is recorded by Yam Shel Shlomo and is among the enactments of Ezra. The primary enactment was to eat it on Friday night. They understood that garlic is digested and acts quickly to increase fertility, especially for those who conduct themselves with restraint and have marital relations only after midnight. Eliyah Rabbah cites Sefer Chassidim that unsalted legumes and lentils also increase fertility.
The cited source there explains matters that increase fertility.
The Gaon wrote this at the end of the cited work.
They necessarily remained tevulei yom. Even if they immersed in the morning, sunset had not yet occurred. A person who touched a corpse is a primary source of impurity, while one who immersed that day has the status of a second degree of impurity. In ordinary food, a first degree produces a second degree, but a second does not produce a third. With terumah, a second produces a third, while with sacred food a third produces a fourth. Since these people were tevulei yom on Shabbos, whatever terumah they touched would become a third degree of impurity.
We do not find a third degree, etc. The Gemara debates whether ordinary food prepared with the purity standards of sacred food has the status of sacred food. Rambam rules that it does not, because a person’s private decision to impose such an excessive stringency is disregarded. Therefore, the concern described here cannot be based on ordinary food alone. See citations 168, 274, 373, 603.
The Rambam concedes that it produces a third degree of impurity, and he likewise holds that even a tevul yom produces a third degree of impurity.
The kohanim who ate with the purity standards of terumah. He mentions kohanim because that is the ordinary case. Regarding food prepared with the purity standards of sacred food, even scrupulous Israelites would eat in that manner in order to accustom themselves to eating sacred foods in purity, since Israelites too ate sacred foods that are permitted to non-kohanim. Food prepared with the purity standards of terumah, however, was eaten only by kohanim, because the purpose was to accustom themselves to eating terumah in purity, and an Israelite cannot eat terumah.
And one cannot answer that they ate meat. Meaning, regarding those who ate with the purity standards of sacred food, the author of the cited work could not answer that they ate meat. What would that accomplish? They certainly ate meat with the purity standards of sacred food in order to accustom themselves to eating sacred meat in purity. But those who ate with the purity standards of terumah were careful only in order to accustom themselves to eating terumah in purity. Accordingly, there was no need for them to be careful with meat, because terumah does not apply to meat.
Regarding the seven relatives for whom there is a mitzvah to become impure, what can be said? He is obligated to become impure for them, even if he is a kohen, as written in Yoreh De'ah, siman 373.
One can further answer, etc., that they ate bread that had not become susceptible to impurity, meaning bread kneaded with fruit juice. Although according to this it would be pas haba'ah b'kisnin and would not be called bread, whereas on Shabbos one must eat bread, this is well understood according to the first answer written by Magen Avraham in siman 168, seif katan 16, that only bread kneaded with wine, oil, honey, or milk is pas haba'ah b'kisnin, but not bread kneaded with other fruit juices. Since we are saying that they ate bread that had not become susceptible to impurity, it necessarily was not kneaded with wine, oil, honey, or milk, for these are among the liquids that render food susceptible to impurity. Other fruit juices would therefore still produce ordinary bread. This is somewhat forced, however, because the words of Magen Avraham there imply that he considers his second answer primary, namely that there is no distinction and anything kneaded with fruit juice is pas haba'ah b'kisnin. We must therefore say as I wrote above in siman 274, also in the name of Tevuos Shor, that if one establishes a meal upon pas haba'ah b'kisnin, so that HaMotzi and Birkas HaMazon are required, it is considered bread. And they drank water from a spring, meaning that they bent down and drank directly with their mouths from the spring and did not touch the water with their hands.
As written in the Shelah. Magen Avraham cites this below in siman 603, but writes there: "This requires further study, for the water became impure in his mouth," etc. End of his words. It must be said that what is written here follows the view of the Shelah. See Eliyah Rabbah there. One can further say that specifically during the Ten Days of Repentance, when the purpose is to conduct oneself with holiness and purity, Magen Avraham's question is appropriate. Here, however, where one eats with the purity standards of sacred food merely to accustom oneself to eating sacred food and terumah in purity, one need be careful only regarding matters to which sacred food and terumah can apply. In this case, where one drinks directly from the spring with one's mouth, it is impossible for the water to possess inherent sanctity, and even if it becomes impure in his mouth there is no concern.
