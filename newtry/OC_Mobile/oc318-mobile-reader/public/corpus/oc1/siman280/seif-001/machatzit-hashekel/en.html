It is a mitzvah to eat garlic on erev Shabbos or on Friday night. This is recorded by Yam Shel Shlomo and is among the enactments of Ezra. The primary enactment was to eat it on Friday night. They understood that garlic is digested and acts quickly to increase fertility, especially for those who conduct themselves with restraint and have marital relations only after midnight. Eliyah Rabbah cites Sefer Chassidim that unsalted legumes and lentils also increase fertility.
They necessarily remained tevulei yom. Even if they immersed in the morning, sunset had not yet occurred. A person who touched a corpse is a primary source of impurity, while one who immersed that day has the status of a second degree of impurity. In ordinary food, a first degree produces a second degree, but a second does not produce a third. With terumah, a second produces a third, while with sacred food a third produces a fourth. Since these people were tevulei yom on Shabbos, whatever terumah they touched would become a third degree of impurity.
We do not find a third degree, etc. The Gemara debates whether ordinary food prepared with the purity standards of sacred food has the status of sacred food. Rambam rules that it does not, because a person’s private decision to impose such an excessive stringency is disregarded. Therefore, the concern described here cannot be based on ordinary food alone.
