1) [Se'if 1] Even permitted melachos, etc. This includes anything needed for the festival that requires an alteration; an unskilled person, who was permitted to sew in his ordinary manner; and anything else permitted through unskilled workmanship in the preceding simanim. An activity permitted through skilled workmanship without an alteration, however, such as the permitted work of barbers and launderers during the festival, or implements needed for food preparation, such as an oven, stove, and similar items, and certainly food preparation itself, such as grinding, sifting, baking, and the like, may be performed for payment if no one is available to do it without payment. This appears from the unqualified words of the halachic authorities. Such work has the same law as a matter subject to loss, and work involving a loss may be performed even for a fixed wage if it cannot be obtained without payment, even by a laborer who has food, as written below. Mishnah Berurah in Biur Halachah writes likewise. See there at length. See also below, siman 544, subsection 28.
2) There. But for payment it is forbidden. Receiving payment during the festival resembles a weekday activity. Beis Yosef; Levush; Taz, subsection 1. This means that all permitted melachos may not be performed for a non-Jew, even without payment. Accordingly, it is forbidden to cook for a non-Jew in order to send him a gift. Chayei Adam, rule 106, subsection 11; Mikraei Kodesh, siman 55, subsection 6; Pischei Olam, at the end of siman 543.
