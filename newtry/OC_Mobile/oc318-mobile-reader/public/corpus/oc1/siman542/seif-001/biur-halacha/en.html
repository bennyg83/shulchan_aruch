Even melachos that are permitted, etc. It appears to my humble opinion that regarding a matter that is ochel nefesh, it is permitted for a laborer to do [the work] even for wages; for otherwise, everything mentioned in siman 533 as permitted for the need of the festival, such as grinding, chopping wood from what is attached, or brewing beer, and the like, all matters of ochel nefesh, would need to be done entirely by oneself, for certainly one will not find people who are willing to do it for him for free. [And we can explain this well: just as they permitted, regarding davar ha'aved, to give wages, as is explained in the gloss, the same applies regarding ochel nefesh; and in general, ochel nefesh is preferable to davar ha'aved, for regarding davar ha'aved it is forbidden if he planned his work [for then], whereas regarding ochel nefesh we hold that even if he planned his work it is permitted, as is stated in siman 533.] However, it is surprising that the Acharonim did not comment on this. And according to what we brought above from the words of the Ritva, who permits everything that is for the need of the festival for wages if people willing to do it for him for free did not happen to be available: if so, it is possible that even a matter of ochel nefesh is equal to other matters that are for the need of the festival. And it implies even more from his language there that he holds that even regarding davar ha'aved the law is likewise: one may not permit giving wages to a Jew except when one cannot obtain other people who are non-Jews, or a Jew for free. The upshot of our words is that regarding ochel nefesh, if people willing to do it for him for free are not commonly available, one may be lenient to give them wages. Know further regarding barbers and launderers, in the manners in which they permitted them to cut hair and to launder, as explained above in simanim 531 and 534: it is also not clear that [it is permitted] specifically for free; for it is possible that since they permitted them to cut hair and to launder in their usual manner, even though in general regarding something that is for the need of the festival and is not ochel nefesh we hold that a change is required, as I wrote above in the Biur Halacha at the beginning of siman 531, perhaps because the Sages treated them as davar ha'aved, which does not require a change; and if so, it is reasonable that it is also permitted for wages, like davar ha'aved. And one should not bring proof from what they said in Pesachim folio 54: "the tailors, for so the tailor sews in his usual manner; the barbers and the launderers, for so," etc., and there on Erev Pesach they are certainly permitted to take wages, and presumably on Chol HaMoed they are also permitted to take wages; for one can dismiss [that proof]: the intent is that therefore they permitted them in the melacha because on Chol HaMoed as well they are sometimes permitted in this melacha; but regarding wages, it is not apt to compare them, for the Rosh wrote that the reason it is forbidden on Chol HaMoed to take wages is that it is like weekday labor; but on Erev Pesach, which is a weekday, this does not apply. Know further that in siman 544, in Magen Avraham, Maamar Mordechai, and Nehar Shalom, it is explained that all permitted melachos, if they are also for the need of a mitzvah, one is permitted to take wages; and see there in Peri Megadim, who wrote regarding accessories to ochel nefesh together with a mitzvah that it is permitted to take wages; it implies from his words that without a mitzvah it is forbidden to take wages; and to my humble opinion one may lean toward leniency in this, as above; and in any case, if [work] for free did not happen to be available, one may certainly rely on the Ritva to be lenient.
And davar ha'aved is permitted to do even, etc. Behold, the source of this ruling is from the Kol Bo [and see the Beur HaGra, who gave a bit of a source for this from the beginning of chapter 2, and it is like a slight support but not a compelling proof, as he wrote in Dimashek Eliezer; see there]. And to my humble opinion this ruling is not clear, for in the words of Rabbeinu Yerucham it is explained explicitly in the name of the Ramah that [it should be] for free, and it implies there that his opinion also leans that way; and so it implies somewhat in the novellae of the Ritva. However, if it is impossible for him to obtain a non-Jew for this, or a Jew for free, he may be lenient to rely on the opinion of the Ritva mentioned above, who permits even for wages in this.
