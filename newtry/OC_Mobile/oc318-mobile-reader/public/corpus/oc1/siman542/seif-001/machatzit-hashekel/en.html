(s.k. 1) Who has no, etc. It is permitted to write tefillin. Regarding what Rabbi Yose said, and we hold like him: he writes tefillin and sells [them] for his livelihood. The Rosh wrote, and these are his words: for his livelihood generously. For one who has nothing to eat is permitted to do any melacha. (That is to say: what superiority do tefillin have?) And Nimukei Yosef wrote, and these are his words: for the need of his livelihood: he does not mean for bare subsistence; rather, even though he has bread and water it is permitted so that he may make Yom Tov expenses more generously with meat and wine; but if he were wealthy, no; end quote. And from his writing "he does not mean for bare subsistence," necessarily his intent is as the Rosh wrote: for if so, every melacha would be permitted, and what is the novelty regarding tefillin? If so, according to what he wrote that the case is where he has bread and water, it implies that other melachos are forbidden in such a case if he has bread and water; and regarding tefillin, which is a mitzvah business, they were more lenient so that he may spend generously. And if so, according to this, on Chol HaMoed of Pesach one would not find [a case] to permit doing melacha because he has nothing to eat, for in any event every Jew has matzos for all the days of the festival.
In the name of the Maggid Mishneh. That is to say, that he was stringent even regarding tefillin like regarding other melacha.
And as for what is written in siman 559 in Beis Yosef, that according to the Rambam the case is, etc. That is to say: as for what the Rambam wrote in chapter 7 of Hilchos Yom Tov, law 27, and these are his words: Anything that is forbidden to do on the Moed: if he has nothing to eat, he may do [work] for his livelihood; and likewise he may engage in commerce for his livelihood. And Beis Yosef wrote in siman 539, and these are his words: It appears from his words that it is permitted only when he has nothing to eat. And it is possible that he too, of blessed memory, permits when he has [food] in scant measure and wants to sell in order to profit, so that he will have money generously, for this too is included in "for his livelihood"; end quote. But regarding melacha, he wrote explicitly in siman 545 that he has nothing at all to eat. That is to say: for he brought in the name of the Maggid Mishneh that even writing tefillin for others is not permitted unless he has nothing to eat, all the more so other optional melachos. But this is somewhat forced, for regarding the laws of commerce and melacha the Rambam wrote their permissions in one passage, and regarding both he wrote "for his livelihood"; and would they be distinct in their meanings, that regarding commerce he means generously, and regarding melacha he means specifically if he has nothing to eat? But nevertheless one can resolve it: for this reason the Rambam wrote regarding melacha, if he has nothing to eat he does for his livelihood; but regarding commerce he omitted "he has nothing to eat," and wrote only that he engages in commerce for his livelihood; that is to say, regarding commerce, even if he has something to eat, it is permitted generously.
And see the Taz, who permitted even wages much more than for his livelihood. Since it was permitted ab initio to begin doing it because he does not have enough for his livelihood, all the wages are permitted; similar to what is written: whoever has two hundred zuz may not take leket, shichechah, and peah; if he has two hundred lacking a dinar, even if they give him a thousand at once he is permitted to take. But afterward it is forbidden to do another melacha. He further wrote that if he has nothing to eat, even if he has household utensils to sell, we do not require him to sell; and the same law applies regarding charity.
