(ב) 'What he will eat' — it is known Magen Avraham's view and also several later authorities who copied his words, that specifically when he lacks even bread and water; and Eshel Avraham's view is that even if he has but lacks enough for his Yom Tov needs, it is also permitted. And behold in my opinion the Babylonian and Yerushalmi disagree in their explanations of our mishnah on this, for in Yerushalmi it states on that mishnah that one does not write documents on the festival, and if the borrower does not trust him or he has nothing to eat — he may write; R' Yirmiyah said: if the borrower does not trust the lender or he has nothing to eat, the scribe [may write] — and this is like the Babylonian explanation on daf 12; R' Yosi said to him: if you say so, you permit all crafts on the festival; rather only if the borrower does not trust the lender who passed by, or there is nothing to eat for this loan; and Ketzot HaChoshen explained: R' Yosi said to him — if the scribe has nothing to eat, he may do his work, you permit all labors on the festival, for most craftsmen work for their livelihood — end of his words. And if one explains per Magen Avraham that he has nothing to eat at all, even bread and water — there is no difficulty at all, for behold we see plainly that most craftsmen will not lack bread and water even if some day they have no work; nevertheless he explains he lacks enough for Yom Tov needs — i.e., meat and wine as customary — and therefore he challenges: if so, all workers are permitted to do their work on Chol HaMoed, for most artisans are limited; and therefore one must interpret another explanation in our mishnah and Gemara — he holds like R' Yirmiyah and is not concerned for R' Yosi's question, because he explains 'he has nothing to eat' literally, and there is no difficulty at all, and so it appears to me. And it appears to me further Yerushalmi's reason for not wanting to explain thus: because in mishnah daf 12 it taught there one does not buy houses etc. or he has nothing to eat etc. — and there necessarily it means he lacks enough for his needs, as Rosh and Mordechai wrote in the first chapter siman 23 from that Yerushalmi in chapter 2; and therefore Yerushalmi's view is also here that he lacks enough for his needs, and therefore he challenges properly, and so it appears. From all this: when he lacks enough for Yom Tov needs — per Yerushalmi one may not permit a worker to do labor for this, for otherwise all baalei melakha would be permitted to do labor, and we do not find the Babylonian explicitly distinguishes on this, as it appears to me:
