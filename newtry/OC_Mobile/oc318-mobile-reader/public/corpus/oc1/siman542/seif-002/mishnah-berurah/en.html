(6) Any melacha, etc. That is, even not for the need of the festival, and also no change is required, as above in siman 537, se'if 15; see there.
(7) Through a laborer who has nothing to eat. That is, who has nothing at all to eat; but if he has bread and water, it is forbidden for him to do melacha [Magen Avraham]. And some say that even if he has [food], just that he does not have Yom Tov needs, it is also permitted for him to do melacha. And see Magen Avraham, siman 534: that in any event he must do it privately, for the observer will not know that the laborer is so poor; and nevertheless, if it is impossible for him to do it privately, it is permitted for him to do it publicly, since he has nothing at all to eat.
(8) So that he may earn wages and profit. And even if he has household utensils to sell, it is permitted to do melacha, and he is not obligated to sell his household utensils; but if he has merchandise and can sell it, it is forbidden for him to do melacha [Acharonim]. They further wrote that melacha that he accepts at a time when he has nothing to eat: it is permitted to accept wages even much more than for his eating; but afterward it is forbidden for him to accept to do another melacha for others.
