And see above, siman 85: See Mishnah Berurah, where it cites the Bach. Seemingly, according to this, if one paused during Krias Shema for the time needed to complete all of it, and the pause was caused by duress, such as when he went to the privy to relieve himself, where we rule above in siman 65 that he returns to the beginning, only the time spent walking to the privy, when he was not fit to recite, and the time spent in the privy itself should be included in the measure. The time spent afterward walking from the privy to his home might not be included, for then he was not truly under duress. Had he wished, he could have cleaned his hands with earth or a pebble and completed Krias Shema, according to our ruling that washing the hands was instituted specifically in the morning, when one becomes a new creation, as stated above in siman 4 in Magen Avraham, seif katan 1. This requires study.
