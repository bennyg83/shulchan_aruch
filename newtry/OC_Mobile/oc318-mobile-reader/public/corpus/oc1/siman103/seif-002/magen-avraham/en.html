Backward. For we require that his face remain directed toward the place where he began to pray, so that when he turns his face back, it will appear as though he does not wish to return to his prayer.

And he says “Master.” In the midst of the prayer, since it is already interrupted and suspended by the gas, he can therefore also make a further interruption and say this in the middle. End of his words of Rashi. And it appears to me that specifically the gas is considered an interruption, but the walking is not considered as great an interruption as speech, as written in siman 104 seif 3.

Need not distance himself. And there were among the great authorities those who would compel themselves not to pass gas, for several elders perished from abstaining during Rav Huna’s lectures — they would hold back their bodily needs and transgress the prohibition of do not make yourself disgusting, out of embarrassment at leaving to attend to their needs before their master. For in a situation involving such disgrace we are not concerned about a d’rabbanan prohibition, and the same applies in our case. Nevertheless, it is a difficult matter to invent distinctions from our own reasoning (Terumat HaDeshen). And see what was written in siman 92 seif 2 in the name of the Rashba, that it is permitted — all the more so for merely passing gas it is permitted to hold back, and so it is implied from the language of the Rashba, as written above. And see siman 13, what was written.

And one should also not say, etc. For when he does not distance himself, his shame and disgrace are not apparent.
