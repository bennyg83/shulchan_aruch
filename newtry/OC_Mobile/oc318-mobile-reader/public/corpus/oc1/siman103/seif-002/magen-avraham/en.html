Behind him. His face must remain directed toward the place where he began praying, for if he turns his face away it will appear as though he does not wish to return to his prayer.
And he says, “Master.” He says it during the prayer, for the prayer is already interrupted because of the wind. He may therefore interrupt further and say this statement in the middle. These are Rashi’s words. It appears to me that specifically the wind constitutes an interruption, but walking is not considered as significant an interruption as speech, as stated in siman 104, seif 3.
He need not move away. Some great scholars would force themselves not to pass wind. Many elderly scholars were uprooted from Rav Huna’s study session because they restrained their bodily needs and violated “Do not make yourselves abhorrent,” due to embarrassment at going to relieve themselves before their teacher. In a circumstance involving such humiliation, we are not concerned for a Rabbinic prohibition. The same applies in our case. Nevertheless, it is difficult to devise distinctions from our own reasoning. [Terumas HaDeshen.] See what I wrote in siman 92, seif 2, in the name of Rashba, that it is permitted. All the more so may one restrain himself merely from passing wind. This also appears from Rashba’s wording, as stated above. See also what I wrote in siman 13.
And he also should not say, etc. For when he does not move away, his shame and embarrassment are not apparent.
