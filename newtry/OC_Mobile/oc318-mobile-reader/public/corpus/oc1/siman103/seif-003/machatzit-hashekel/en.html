(Seif katan 3) And he says, etc. The walking required to move four cubits behind him is not considered as great an interruption as speech. Therefore, although he already interrupted by walking, it would not have been appropriate to permit him to recite the prayer beginning “Master,” which is a greater interruption. Rashi therefore had to explain that the prayer is already interrupted and standing so because of the wind that escaped, and this interruption is equivalent to speech.
As stated in siman 104, seif 1, that if one can move aside from the road, he should move aside and should not interrupt by speaking. Thus, walking to move aside from the road is preferable and is not as significant an interruption as speech. Magen Avraham, seif katan 3 there, likewise writes that we do not find walking described as an interruption anywhere.
