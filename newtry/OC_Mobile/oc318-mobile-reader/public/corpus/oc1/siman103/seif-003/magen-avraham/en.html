One who sneezes. Rashi explained that this refers to one who passes gas audibly. And it appears to me that passing gas silently does not apply. And this requires further study regarding what he wrote. And in the Lublin printing it reads "passes gas silently," and it appears to me that this explains the passage that comes before it there, where the Talmud states: "The sneezing versus sneezing is problematic," and Rashi wrote: "There is no basis to distinguish between compulsion and free will, as sneezing only occurs under compulsion" — and the Talmud resolves it by saying: here from above, there from below. To this Rashi explained that "from below — passes gas silently," meaning it does not come under compulsion. And in the Rif’s text of Rashi’s commentary it reads only "passes gas," and this is the main view, for if there were a distinction, the Talmud should have distinguished and answered that this Sage passed gas silently. And if one says: but then how would they know? — one can say from the fact of the distancing that he distanced himself, which proves that even silently one must distance himself backward. For if not, why would the Terumat HaDeshen need to strain himself? — for certainly those sages passed gas silently so that others would not hear. Rather, there is no distinction, as stated above.
