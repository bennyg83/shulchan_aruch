(Seif katan 1) And he returns, etc. “Had he prayed immediately” means immediately after the odor ceased. Until the odor ceased, his interruption was caused by duress, but after the odor ceased, the time he remained paused voluntarily was not under duress. Consequently, the time he paused because of duress did not equal the time needed to complete the entire prayer.
Therefore, he need return only to the place where he stopped. This follows the view of the Rama above in siman 65, who writes that this is also the custom: there is no distinction between Krias Shema and prayer. Rather, the distinction is whether he paused for the time needed to complete all of it because of duress. If so, he must return to the beginning; if not caused by duress, he need return only to where he stopped. According to Beit Yosef there, however, who maintains that there is no distinction whether the pause was caused by duress, and that the distinction is instead between Krias Shema and prayer, since in Krias Shema he need never return to the beginning, whereas in prayer he must return to the beginning even when the pause was not caused by duress, the Magen Avraham’s statement here does not apply. According to Beit Yosef, if he paused for the time needed to complete all of it, he must return to the beginning, since the present discussion concerns prayer.
