1) [Seif 1] One who is fasting may taste, etc. He may taste the cooked dish in order to know whether it requires salt or seasoning. Rashi, Berachos 14a. It is possible that even if his intent is for his own enjoyment, it is permitted, as Yad Aharon emended, for he did not accept upon himself that he would derive no benefit, but only that he would not eat or drink, and this is not considered eating or drinking. Magen Avraham, s.k. 1; Eliyah Rabbah, os 1; Mishna Berurah, os 1.
2) Ibid. He may taste, etc. He accepted upon himself abstention from eating and drinking, but not from benefit. Therefore, one who is prohibited by a vow from a certain food is forbidden even to taste it. Taz, s.k. 1; Yad Aharon in Hagahos HaTur; Mishna Berurah, ibid. Even if he tastes and spits out any amount, it is forbidden, for he derives benefit. One who is prohibited by a vow from deriving benefit from a cooked dish is forbidden to taste it even in order to know whether it requires salt or seasoning. Mishbetzos Zahav, os 1.
3) Ibid. He may taste, etc. However, to eat even the smallest amount is forbidden. Eshel Avraham, os 1. So too is written in Mishbetzos Zahav, ibid., and so is implied at the end of seif 2.
