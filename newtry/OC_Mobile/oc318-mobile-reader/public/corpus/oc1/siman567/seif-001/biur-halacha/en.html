And on Yom Kippur and Tisha BeAv — the reason we wrote in Mishna Berurah is that they do not depend on acceptance, and it is not relevant to say regarding them that one accepted only from eating and drinking and not from other benefit (which is the reason for permitting tasting, as explained in the Gemara). It is different for the Mechaber regarding Yom Kippur and Tisha BeAv from other written communal fasts, even though they too do not require acceptance, because their obligation is not so great and depends on willingness — all this is clear from the words of Rabbeinu Yonah in the name of Rivash. In Eshkol I saw that he copied the words of R' Yosef of Barcelona, from whom this ruling flows to forbid tasting on Yom Kippur and Tisha BeAv: the reason is that we hold it forbidden to put one's hand to water on these days and every benefit is forbidden, end of his words. His intent is like regarding bathing — they did not forbid actual bathing but even putting one's hand to water; likewise regarding eating, not actual eating specifically but any benefit of eating. For this reason it is plain that only on Yom Kippur and Tisha BeAv is it forbidden. But Rama copied the view of Tosafot that on every fast that does not require acceptance it is forbidden.
