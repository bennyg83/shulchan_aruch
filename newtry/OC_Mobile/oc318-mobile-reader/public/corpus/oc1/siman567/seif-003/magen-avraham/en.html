It is not proper. This requires further study, for this is taken from the words of Terumat HaDeshen, who holds that on every communal fast it is forbidden to taste; but the Beis Yosef, who ruled above that specifically on Yom Kippur and Tisha B'Av tasting is forbidden: if so, why did he write here "it is not proper"? And one must say that he is dealing with more than a revi'is.
And even if there is in the water. Since he intends to rinse his mouth and does not intend to taste, he does not derive benefit even with more than a revi'is. And further: specifically from other liquids he derives benefit, but from water he does not derive benefit even with more than a revi'is (Terumat HaDeshen). And according to this reason it is forbidden to rinse with other liquids more than a revi'is. And it appears to me that with something that is not good for drinking it is permitted, for example vinegar, in siman 612, se'if 9, in the gloss.
And it is permitted to chew etc. Since he did not accept benefit upon himself (Mordechai), it implies that even other foods one may chew and spit out. It implies according to what the Rama wrote in se'if 1 that on every communal fast tasting is forbidden, then here too chewing is forbidden; and if so it is puzzling how the Rama wrote only regarding Yom Kippur that it is forbidden: he should have said that on every communal fast it is forbidden. Therefore it appears to me to forbid it on every communal fast. And also in Bedek HaBayis he wrote to be stringent, and he wrote that that which we say "one who chews peppercorns is exempt" is because they burn the throat and harm him, which is not so regarding cinnamon and other spices, end quote. The Agudah wrote in [its treatment of] Yoma that it is permitted to swallow saliva on Yom Kippur; and in the Bach, siman 612, it somewhat implies that according to us who hold that even foods unfit for eating are forbidden l'chatchilah, saliva too is forbidden to swallow. And it appears to me that saliva is permitted, since he has no intention at all of eating; and so it is in Sefer Chassidim; see what I wrote in siman 90, se'if 13.
