(19) In the presence of ervah. Scripture states, “For Hashem your God walks in the midst of your camp; your camp shall be holy, and He shall see no naked matter among you.” From here the Sages learned that wherever Hashem our God walks with us, meaning when we engage in Krias Shema, prayer, or Torah study, we must ensure that Hashem not see among us a naked matter. This means that no ervah may be opposite the face of the person reciting or praying within his field of vision, as is evident from siman 79, seif 2. It also means that he himself may not be naked, because his own ervah is thereby exposed. This too is included in the verse.
(20) Of a non-Jew. Although Scripture states concerning them, “Their flesh is the flesh of donkeys,” one might have thought that they are regarded merely like animals. The ruling teaches otherwise.
(21) The ervah of a minor. See Beit Yosef, who was concerned for the stringent opinion only initially. After the fact, if one recited Krias Shema, he does not repeat it.
(22) Some permit. A minor himself may recite Krias Shema while naked until the age of nine. In the presence of an adult man's ervah, however, he is permitted only until he reaches the age of education. See Magen Avraham and siman 343.
(23) Capable of relations. This means a boy up to nine years old and a girl up to three years old.
(24) This is the primary ruling. Magen Avraham writes that nevertheless the mohel should not hold the child's ervah in his hand while reciting the blessing. Eliyah Rabbah and Machatzis HaShekel are lenient. Peri Megadim writes that nowadays, when mohalim are not so expert, one must be concerned for danger and should hold it in his hand. Biur HaGra in Yoreh Deah, siman 265, likewise rules that one may not act in accordance with Magen Avraham.
