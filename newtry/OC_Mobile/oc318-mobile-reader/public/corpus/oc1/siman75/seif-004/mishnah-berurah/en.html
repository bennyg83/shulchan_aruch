(19) Opposite ervah — as it is written "for the Omnipresent your the Omnipresent walks in the midst of your camp... and your camp shall be holy, and He shall not see among you a matter of nakedness," etc.; from here the Sages learned that in every place where the Omnipresent our the Omnipresent walks with us — namely when we engage in Shema and prayer or words of Torah — one must be careful that the Omnipresent not see in us a matter of nakedness, namely that there not be a matter of ervah opposite the face of the man reciting or praying, as much as fills his eyes, as proven in siman 79 seif 2; and likewise that he not then be naked, whereby his ervah would be seen — also included in this verse.

(20) Of a non-Jew — even though it says of them "their flesh is the flesh of donkeys," I might say it is like an animal in general — it teaches us [otherwise].

(21) Nakedness of a minor — see in Beit Yosef who was not concerned for the opinion of the stringent only lechatchila; but bedieved if he recited he does not go back and recite.

(22) And some permit — a minor by himself may recite Kerias Shema when he is naked until age nine years; and opposite male genitals it is permitted only until he reaches the age of education. See Magen Avraham; see siman 343.

(23) For intercourse — that is, for a male minor until age nine years, and for a female minor until age three years.

(24) And thus is the main ruling — Magen Avraham wrote; nevertheless the mohel should not hold the organ in his hand at the time of the blessing. Eliyah Rabbah and Machatzit HaShekel are lenient. Peri Megadim wrote that now they are not so expert, one must be concerned for danger and should hold it in his hand. Also the Gra in Yoreh De'ah siman 265 ruled that it is forbidden to do as Magen Avraham.
