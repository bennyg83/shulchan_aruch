(5) One should be careful, etc.: so Shulchan Aruch wrote because Rosh wrote that "voice of a woman is ervah" means to hear and not regarding Keriat Shema; but Darkei Moshe wrote, and it seems to me that even without caution one should be concerned for prohibition since many forbid—until here. And Nachalat Tzvi wondered why Rama did not also write thus in Hagahot Shulchan Aruch. And in my humble opinion he hints in what Rama wrote, "but a voice to which he is accustomed is not ervah"—it implies regarding a voice to which he is not accustomed it is ervah, and the term ervah certainly implies absolute prohibition. And I saw in Be'er Sheva page 119 that he greatly wondered at those who forbid, who explained "voice of a woman is ervah" regarding Keriat Shema when she sings—for behold in the chapter "ten genealogies" Rabbi Nachman said to Rabbi Yehuda: let our master send him peace at night—"voice of a woman is ervah"; Rashi explained: if I ask of her welfare she will answer me—behold explicitly that "voice of a woman is ervah" speaks of hearing and not regarding Keriat Shema, and even when she is not singing—until here his words briefly; and he concluded: whoever has a mind broader than mine may resolve their words and receive blessing—until here. Therefore I cast myself down and examined, and found to reconcile their words that he holds: since in the gemara Berachos page 24 it says "voice of a woman is ervah" next to "a tefach on a woman is ervah," we say its explanation is regarding his wife and Keriat Shema, as the gemara explained on "a tefach on a woman is ervah" that regarding his wife and Keriat Shema we require specifically a tefach—and the same applies regarding another woman for Keriat Shema; but to gaze at another woman, even a small finger is forbidden as explained in the gemara. If so, the same applies: "voice of a woman is ervah" is regarding his wife for Keriat Shema and such as when she sings; but in ordinary speech such as returning peace, there is no prohibition for Keriat Shema, and the same law regarding another woman; but to hear from another woman, even returning peace, is forbidden—for "a tefach on a woman" was taken regarding gazing, that even a small finger is forbidden; the same judgment is that there is extra stringency regarding hearing a woman's voice, that even in returning peace it is forbidden—for if there were no prohibition for another woman to hear except when singing, we would not have been strict regarding Keriat Shema. And if so, Rabbi Yehuda in chapter "ten genealogies" properly said it is forbidden in returning peace—and examine. And in this manner one can explain what the gemara says there regarding "hair of a woman is ervah"—that regarding his wife and Keriat Shema it is forbidden only in a covered place, but outside her braid it is permitted for Keriat Shema even regarding another woman; but to gaze at another woman, even outside her braid, is forbidden as mentioned above. And I found great proof for my explanation in Rashba's novellae, only one who wants to delve deeply should examine there. Be'er Sheva wrote there: it is fitting and correct for every kosher God-fearing woman, whether married or unmarried, not [to make heard a voice of song in melodies nor in any matter at any time] whenever a man is with her—only her lips should move and her voice should not be heard at all in essence, lest the listening man come to arousal; and arousals of sin are harder than sin itself, for she must be careful that people not stumble through her, as Rabbi Yochanan said: we learn fear of sin from a virgin—and do not answer me from "and Deborah sang" and "Barak son of Avinoam," for I am exceptional in speech.
