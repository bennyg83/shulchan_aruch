Or — Taz: here, for the author, sight of nakedness is forbidden even in another domain; squinting is permitted even within four cubits in one domain, since "he shall not see." But "and your camp shall be holy" refers only to feces. And why in feces is hirhur forbidden while in nakedness it is permitted? Regarding feces the reverse applies: within four cubits it is forbidden even with squinting, and behind him too it is forbidden; "and your camp shall be holy" — all that is your holy camp we read. Sight, for Rosh, is permitted since "he shall not see" refers only to him, not to nakedness; therefore in another domain it is permitted, as explained in Even HaEzer 79:61. However, in one domain "before him as full as his eyes" — Rosh sided with us on this in our innovations, whether rabbinically or biblically, since all "before him as full as his eyes" is holy camp; if so, there is no question from "before him as full as his eyes" being forbidden at night or with squinting, as in Even HaEzer 79 where holy camp is required; only the language is difficult for Taz. What he wrote from Tosefta Terumah — the mishnah is in Terumah ch. 2; I did not understand what Beit Yosef wrote "naked shall turn his face" — who will tell us Beit Yosef was not particular about revealing nakedness? Also for Taz, even without the reason of revealing nakedness, one cannot rely on turning the face except opposite another nakedness, as he wrote later: turning the face specifically so that the nakedness is behind him; I already explained this in Orach Chayyim from what Bach, Taz, and Magen Avraham divided between water that is covered; here is not the place to elaborate. Also, nakedness visible through a lens — it seems even covered flesh through a lens squinting does not help; this is no worse than water cover, where squinting helps.

General rule: a matter forbidden due to hirhur — a handbreadth on a woman, hair, singing, and the like — if he does not see and does not hear, it is permitted, for a woman's body is not actual nakedness (Erev Megulash) but hirhur; and the evidence is that she sits and cuts her challah. Singing to an unmarried woman not at the time of Kerias Shema is permitted, as explained in Even HaEzer 21; an unmarried niddah is included in nakedness; also two prohibitions apply — see there. The voice of an unmarried idolatrous priest is certainly forbidden to hear; even an Israelite in public carries death and karet from Deuteronomy; included in arayos it is called thus in Even HaEzer 16 — not the place here. Regarding nakedness, the matter depends on sight; even in another domain it is forbidden, even for Rosh in Even HaEzer 79:62, for here "he shall not see" is written; if he squints his eyes here, one could say it is permitted — Magen Avraham 79:8, explained there. And likewise nakedness through a lens or in water is permitted when he squints his eyes — Bach and Magen Avraham too; even Taz could concede to this. But standing opposite nakedness, for the author squinting helps; according to them, specifically turning the face so the nakedness is behind him is permitted; squinting or turning the face alone does not help. For nakedness one need not require holy camp, for even within four cubits behind him is permitted, since holy camp applies only to feces — this all agree. Regarding feces, if "he shall not see" applies — this is the dispute of Rosh and Rashba in Even HaEzer 79, and Magen Avraham in Even HaEzer 76:61 regarding feces; in Even HaEzer 79 there it is explained.
