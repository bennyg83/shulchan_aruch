(9) Of a woman — and a man's hair, even ervah emerging through an opening in his garment, it is permitted to recite [Shema] opposite it; but if the pouch is visible, it is ervah.

(10) That it is her custom to cover it — and even if it is not her custom to cover it except in the market and not in the house and courtyard, nevertheless it is included in ervah according to all, even in the house, and it is forbidden there to recite [Shema] opposite it if some of them became uncovered. And know further that even if the custom of this woman and her friends in that place is to go with uncovered head in the market as the way of the licentious, it is forbidden; and likewise regarding uncovering her calf, which is forbidden in every manner, and as explained above in sk"b, since they need to cover hair by law [and there is in this a Torah prohibition from what is written "and expose the head of the woman" — from which [we learn] that it is covered]; and also all daughters of Israel who uphold the religion of Moshe are careful about this from the days of our fathers forever until now; it is included in ervah and forbidden to recite opposite them. And this does not come to reduce except virgins who are permitted to go with uncovered head, or such as hair emerging outside their braid — this depends on local custom: if the custom of daughters of Israel in this place is to be careful not to let even a little of a little emerge outside their tie, automatically it is included in ervah and forbidden to recite opposite them; but if permitted, since they are accustomed to them there is no lewd thought, as below.

(11) Virgins — and betrothed virgins are forbidden to go with uncovered head; and likewise virgins who were lain with must cover the head; nevertheless if she committed adultery and does not wish to go out with a scarf on her head as the way of women, they cannot compel her.

(12) That is their custom, etc. — see in Magen Avraham who wrote they should not go with uncovered head except if their hairs are braided and not disheveled; but Machatzit HaShekel and Magen Giborim are lenient in this. Chayei Adam wrote: non-Jewish women who are not warned to cover their hair — uncertain whether their law is like virgins in this.

(13) That are accustomed, etc. — meaning, and it is permitted to recite [Shema] in this even opposite another woman, since they are accustomed to them there is no lewd thought; but to intend to look at another woman even at hairs outside their braid is forbidden [Eliyah Rabbah].

(14) To go out, etc. — meaning, that besides a hat on her head she has a braid and it is a garment that constrains the hair so it does not go outside; and that little which cannot be constrained and goes out from the braid — on this the Rashba is lenient [Chatam Sofer siman 36, see there]. And in the Zohar, parashas Naso, he was very strict that no hair from a woman should be seen, for it causes danger to the house and causes to sons that they will not be taken into account in this generation and another side to serve in the house; and all the more if they go thus in the marketplace. Therefore a woman requests that even the beams of her house should not see a single hair from her head; and if she does so, what is written: "your children are like olive saplings" — what [is meant by] olive, etc.; [regarding] sons they will be removed in disgrace from the rest of the sons of the world; and not only that but her husband is blessed with all blessings above and blessings below with wealth in sons and sons of sons, etc. In brief; and Magen Avraham wrote it is fitting to conduct as the Zohar [requires]; and in Yoma it is stated in an incident where [protection] was merited through the excessive modesty that was in her that the beams of her house did not see [her hair] — they said: a garment came out from her [descendants who were] High Priests.

(15) Foreign hair [wig] — they call "foreign" the hair that was cut and is not attached to her flesh; and he holds that for this the Sages did not establish "a woman's hair is ervah," and also it is permitted to uncover it and there is no [violation] of uncovering the head; and some disagree and say that even with a foreign wig the concept of a woman's hair being ervah and prohibition of uncovering the head applies; and Peri Megadim wrote that in countries where women go out with foreign wig uncovered, they may rely on Shulchan Aruch, and it is implied from him there that even her own hair that was cut and afterward attached to her head — one may also be lenient; and in Magen Giborim he was strict in this, see there. And he wrote further there that if there is no local custom for women to go with foreign wig, certainly the law is with those who are strict in this because of maris ayin, see there.
