(2) Her custom is to cover it, etc.: even her thigh, but face, hands, and feet are permitted. And Bach's language in towns where they walk barefoot, that they are always accustomed to be uncovered—until here. And Lechem Chamudos wrote it appears her arms are not included in hands unless in a place where they are accustomed to uncover them—until here. And I saw in Rokeach siman 324: a tefach of her flesh or her arms uncovered is forbidden—until here. Levush wrote specifically for a man, but she herself—even entirely naked—is permitted when her face is pressed to the ground; and Malbushei Yom Tov wrote: why mention a man—even a woman too, for he only exempts regarding herself; and such is Rosh's language "specifically regarding another," etc. And perhaps he holds woman to woman is not forbidden, as Rashba wrote in Beit Yosef—only he found it difficult why he is lenient where Rosh is strict—until here his language. And in my humble opinion he holds what Rosh wrote, and "specifically regarding another" does not deal with woman to another woman but excludes regarding herself; or he took "another" to teach that also regarding another it is forbidden specifically at a tefach—and examine; or there is a scribal error in Rosh and it must read "specifically regarding others," and he meant for her husband or for another man—and I found this version in Sefer Zikaron of the Gaon R' Yosef HaKohen, and one who examines his book knows he followed Rosh, and the whole matter there he copied from Rosh's words—see there; and likewise he wrote in Hagahot Semak siman 64, and in Pesakim of Rosh it is explained thus, and likewise explained in words of Rabbeinu Yeruham, disciple of Rosh, page 25—see there and examine; and that is, Tur and all the great ones mentioned above did not write this innovation that woman to woman is forbidden, but rather according to all it is permitted. Further it appears to me proof for this from Beit Yosef, who wrote on Rosh's language—and Rashba likewise wrote—and it is difficult: behold there is a distinction between them, for according to Rashba woman to woman is permitted, only that also according to Rosh it is permitted; and from this is support for what I wrote above in Rama's intent—and examine. And I saw in Ma'adanei Melech page 26, who wrote on Rashba's words—this is his language: it appears from his words that a woman is not forbidden in seeing another's ervah; but Rosh's words appear, that just as for a man it is forbidden to see his friend's nakedness—and behold we find prohibition regarding women who stroll—until here his language. And I wonder, for certainly to see actual ervah, woman to woman is also forbidden, for it is not better than her own nakedness, as Levush implies; rather he speaks regarding other covered places that woman to woman is permitted because in this man to man is also permitted. And I already explained that Rosh too did not entertain forbidding woman to woman, and this is clear for practical law.
