2) There: In a place that she normally covers, etc. In a place that she does not normally cover, such as her face, hands, or feet, there is no concern. Beis Yosef; Bach; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2; Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 1. See Ben Ish Chai, Parashas Bo, paragraph 11, who writes that even according to the Zohar in Idra Rabba, which says that a woman's leg and a woman's hand are nakedness, the portion of the hand or foot that is normally exposed, such as the palm, sole, and digits, may be faced while reciting, because it is not termed nakedness. In a place where people walk with their feet covered, however, it is forbidden to recite while facing them when they are exposed. This is also explicit in Bach, who writes that in towns where women walk barefoot and their feet are always exposed, it is not forbidden to recite while facing them. Eliyah Rabbah there and Chayei Adam, rule 4, paragraph 2, cite this, implying that where feet are normally covered it is forbidden.
2) Lechem Chamudos writes in the chapter Mi Shemeiso, paragraph 116, that her arms are not included in "her hands," and it is reasonable that each locale follows its custom; if she normally does not cover them, they are not called nakedness. Rokeach, siman 324, however, writes that an exposed handbreadth of her flesh or arms is forbidden. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 3, cites him. This implies that her arms are like the rest of her flesh, without distinction whether or not she normally exposes them. Chayei Adam there likewise writes that her arms and lower leg are forbidden even if people habitually expose them in the manner of immodest women. Ben Ish Chai, Parashas Bo, paragraph 8, similarly writes that one may not recite while facing Arab women whose custom is always to expose their arms. See the following paragraph.
