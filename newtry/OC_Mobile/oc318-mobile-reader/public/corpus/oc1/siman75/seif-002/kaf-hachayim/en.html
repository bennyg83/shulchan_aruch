(2) There—in a place where her custom is to cover it, etc.; but in a place where her custom is not to cover it, such as her face or her hands and her feet, we have no concern—Beit Yosef, Bach. Eliyah Rabbah letter bet. Maamar Mordechai letter alef. And see Rabbeinu Ben Acharon z"l, Parshat Bo letter yud-alef, who wrote that even per the Zohar in Idra Raba, which said "leg of a woman is ervah, hand of a woman is ervah"—what is removed from the hand and from the leg that is customary to be uncovered, such as the palm and fingers, it is permitted to recite Keriat Shema opposite them, for the name ervah is not called on this—see there. However, in a place where they walk with covered feet, it is forbidden to recite opposite them when they are uncovered—and so explicitly Bach wrote in towns where they walk barefoot, that their feet are always uncovered, it is not forbidden to recite opposite them—so far; and Eliyah Rabbah brought it there, and Chayei Adam klal 4 letter bet implies that where they walk with covered feet it is forbidden.

(2) And he wrote—Lechem Chamudos in the chapter "one whose dead," letter kaf-yud-vav, that her forearms are not included in her hands, and it is reasonable that each place according to its custom, and if she is accustomed not to cover them it is not called ervah—see there. But Rokeach siman 324 wrote: a tefach of her flesh or her forearms uncovered is forbidden. And Be'er Heitev—Eliyah Rabbah letter gimmel: it implies her arms are like her flesh, and one may not distinguish whether her custom is to uncover or not; and likewise Chayei Adam there: her arms and thigh, even if they are accustomed thus as the way of the licentious, are forbidden. And likewise Ben Acharon, Parshat Bo letter chet: Arab women whose custom is to uncover their arms—even though their custom is always thus—they may not recite Keriat Shema opposite them. And see in the letter after it.
