A woman's hair. Hair of a man's ervah that protrudes through the garment is permitted (gemara page 24).

"Virgins whose custom, etc." He asked: in Even HaEzer siman 21 seif 1 it is written that daughters of Israel shall not walk with uncovered head in the market, whether unmarried women or a married woman; and so too Rambam chapter 21 from Issurei Bi'ah. And further that if she went out in the hinuma and her head was uncovered, this is a sign that she was a virgin; and it is strained to say that "unmarried women" that the mishnah teaches means a widow—for if so it should have explained "and." And one can say that "uncovered head" written in Even HaEzer means that they undo the fastening of their hair and go in the market, which is forbidden even for an unmarried woman; and likewise Rashi on Parshat Naso on "and the head of the woman shall be uncovered." Nevertheless it must be said that an unmarried woman is not forbidden mid'Oraisa, for the verse does not deal with an unmarried woman; if so, going with uncovered head should also be forbidden, for from here we derive in Kesubos chapter 7 that daughters of Israel shall not walk with uncovered head—but in any case the verse does not deal with an unmarried woman, only that it is a measure of modesty for virgins not to walk thus.

"That are accustomed to go outside." R' Moshe Alshakar wrote siman 35: those women who come from countries where their custom is not to uncover, to a place where their custom is to uncover—they are permitted to uncover if they do not intend to return (see siman 598). And in siman 115 in Even HaEzer it implies there that only in the market is it forbidden, but in a courtyard where people are not commonly found they are permitted to walk with uncovered head; and so too Tosafos in Kesubos. But in the Zohar Parshas Naso page 239 he was very strict that no hair from a woman should be seen, and such is fitting to conduct.

"And all the more so foreign hair, etc." As should read, and so in Shulchan Aruch seif 6, and so in Shulchan Gavoah that it is permitted lechatchila as stated in mishnah chapter 6 of Shabbos: a woman may go out with hair threads, etc., unlike Beit Shammai who argues with him—and he elaborated at the end of his book in rejected matters (see Shulchan Gavoah seif 4).
