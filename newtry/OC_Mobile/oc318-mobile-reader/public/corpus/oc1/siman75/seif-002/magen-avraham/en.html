A woman's hair. Hair from a man's private area that protrudes through his garment is permitted, as stated in the Gemara on page 24.
Unmarried women whose practice is, etc. This is difficult, for in Even HaEzer 21:1 it is written that Jewish women, whether unmarried or married, may not walk in the marketplace with uncovered hair, and the Rambam rules likewise in chapter 21 of Hilchos Issurei Biah. Moreover, at the beginning of the second chapter of Kesubos it states that if she went out in a bridal canopy with her hair uncovered, this was evidence that she had been a virgin. It is strained to say that the unmarried women mentioned there are widows, for if so the matter should have been stated explicitly. One may answer that the uncovered hair mentioned in Even HaEzer means that they undo the braids of their hair and walk in the marketplace, which is forbidden even for an unmarried woman. Rashi likewise explains the verse concerning the sotah, “and he shall uncover the woman's head,” in this manner. Nevertheless, one must say that an unmarried woman is not prohibited in this regard by Torah law. For were the verse also speaking of an unmarried woman, she would likewise be prohibited from walking with her head uncovered, since the Gemara in Kesubos 72 derives from this verse that Jewish women may not walk with uncovered heads. Rather, the verse addresses only a married woman, while for unmarried women refraining from doing so is a matter of modest conduct.
That they customarily allow to protrude. Rabbi Moshe Alashkar writes in responsum 35 that women who come from countries where the practice is not to expose such hair to a place where the practice is to expose it may do so when they do not intend to return. See siman 468. See also Even HaEzer 115, where it appears that exposure is prohibited specifically in the marketplace, but in a courtyard where men are not commonly present they may walk with uncovered hair. Tosafos in Kesubos writes likewise. The Zohar, Parashas Naso, page 239, however, is exceedingly stringent that no hair of a woman be visible, and it is proper to conduct oneself accordingly.
And certainly a wig, etc. This is the correct reading, and so it appears in the Venice edition of the Shulchan Aruch. The Shiltei Gibborim likewise writes that it is permitted initially, as stated in the Mishnah, chapter 6 of Shabbos, that a woman may go out with strands of hair. This is unlike the Beis Shmuel, who disputes him and elaborates at the end of his work with arguments that may be rejected. See siman 303:4.
