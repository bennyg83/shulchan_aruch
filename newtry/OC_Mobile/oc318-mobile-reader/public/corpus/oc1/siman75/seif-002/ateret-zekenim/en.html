And all the more so foreign peah, etc.—all this Rama wrote based on the gloss in Alfasi, chapter Bemah Ishah; he ruled accordingly in siman 503: Chazal did not say a woman's hair is ervah except specifically hairs attached to her flesh; but when cut, there is no issue of a woman's hair being ervah, nor of uncovered head. And some say even foreign peah is forbidden due to uncovered head and a woman's hair being ervah—for what we learned, that it is permitted to go out in foreign peah, throughout the Gemara refers only to when it is covered under the net (Be'er Sheva in the name of A.Z. Maharari Padua). Therefore it is forbidden to recite Keriat Shema opposite foreign peah. And even though the gloss authors of Maharama here spoke of foreign peah, nevertheless her own hair—even after it is cut and attached to her head so that she appears as a woman with hair—it is forbidden to recite Keriat Shema (Maharam of Rimini). And in Darchei Moshe, siman 503, Maharama wrote explicitly that it is permitted for a married woman to reveal her foreign peah, whether made from her own hair or from a friend's hair, etc. Therefore he wrote in the gloss anonymously—thus Maharam of Rimini disagrees with the gloss author on this.
