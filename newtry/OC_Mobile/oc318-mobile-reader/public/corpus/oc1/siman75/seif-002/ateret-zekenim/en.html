And certainly a wig, etc. The Rama wrote all of this based on the gloss to the Rif in the chapter Bameh Ishah. On this basis he ruled in siman 303 that Chazal said that a woman's hair is ervah only regarding hair actually attached to her flesh. Once it has been cut, it is not subject to the prohibition of a woman's hair as ervah, nor to that of uncovering the head. Some say that even a wig is prohibited because of uncovering the head and because a woman's hair is ervah. In their view, whenever the Mishnah permits a woman to go out wearing a wig, throughout the Gemara it refers specifically to a wig covered beneath the hairnet. Be'er Sheva in the name of Or Zarua and Mahari Padua. According to this view, it is prohibited to recite Krias Shema opposite a wig. Even according to the author of the glosses, Maharam Isserles, who permits a wig here, if it is her own hair that was cut and then attached to her head so that she appears to have hair, it is prohibited to recite Krias Shema opposite it. Maharam Yaffe. In Darchei Moshe, siman 303, however, Maharam Isserles explicitly writes that a married woman may reveal her wig, whether it is made from her own hair or from another woman's hair, etc. For this reason he wrote the gloss here without qualification. Maharam Yaffe therefore disagrees on this point with the author of the glosses.
