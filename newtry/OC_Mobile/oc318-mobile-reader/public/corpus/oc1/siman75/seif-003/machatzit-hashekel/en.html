Seif katan 3: “Unmarried women, etc.” Moreover, Kesubos states that a bridal canopy with her hair uncovered was evidence that she had been a virgin. This creates a difficulty for the Shulchan Aruch in Even HaEzer and for the Rambam, who write that unmarried women may not walk with uncovered heads. On the contrary, uncovered hair was the sign that she was a virgin.
And it is even more strained, etc. The Bach in Even HaEzer indeed explains the passage there as referring to a widow.
Rashi likewise explains in Parashas Naso, etc. Rashi writes: “He uncovers it,” meaning that he undoes the braiding of her hair in order to disgrace her. From here we learn that exposing the head is disgraceful for Jewish women. This implies that it is specifically undoing the braids that is disgraceful.
In Kesubos, chapter 7, page 72b. It is only a matter of modest conduct, etc. Accordingly, Rashi in his Torah commentary either refers specifically to a married woman, or he also refers to an unmarried woman but cites the verse merely as a support. In any event, even according to the Magen Avraham, an unmarried woman is at least prohibited from having her head uncovered in the sense that the braids of her hair are undone. The difficulty from the case of one who went out in a bridal canopy with her hair uncovered therefore remains, for Rashi explains at the beginning of the second chapter of Kesubos: “her hair was loose over her shoulders.”
That is, her braids were undone. This is indeed the customary practice.
