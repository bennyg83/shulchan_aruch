(Seif kaf-gimmel) Virgins, etc.: and further it says in Ketubot, etc.—and if so it is difficult on Shulchan Aruch, R' Ovadia, and Rambam, who wrote that unmarried women are forbidden to go with uncovered head; behold on the contrary, this is the siman where she was a virgin.

And it is more strained, etc.: and Bach in Even HaEzer explains thus, that there it deals with a married woman, a widow.

And likewise Rashi in Parshat Naso, etc., and this is his language: and Paro uncovered the fastening of her hair to disgrace her—from here for daughters of Israel that uncovering the head is disgraceful for them—so far; if so it implies specifically undoing the fastening is disgrace.

In Ketubot chapter 7, page 72b: only that the measure of modesty, etc.—and if so Rashi in the Chumash either deals specifically with a married woman or with an unmarried woman, and his words are merely support; in any case also according to Magen Avraham's view, in any case even a virgin is forbidden in uncovering the head—that is, undoing her hidden hair. If so, it is still difficult from that which "if she went out in the hinuma," etc.—for Rashi at the beginning of the chapter "a woman who was widowed" wrote: and her head uncovered, her hair on her shoulder—so far.

And that is her fastening undone—and such is the custom.
