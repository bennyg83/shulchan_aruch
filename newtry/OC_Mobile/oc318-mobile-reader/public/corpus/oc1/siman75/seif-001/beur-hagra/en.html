Seif 1, in place of, etc.: for there it says thigh, etc., and it appears unnecessary except because Rabbi Yehuda's case is under discussion, and it teaches that the thigh—even though it is not a place that men are accustomed to cover—nevertheless for a woman it is customary to cover and it is ervah, even regarding her husband—Rashba in the name of Raavad.

And some say, etc.: the Rosh's version there reads: no, this is another woman and this is his wife and Keriat Shema—and all the more so according to what is written in seif 6, that even if he is not gazing at ervah it is forbidden to recite Keriat Shema; there is no need to mention his wife.

And it appears, etc.: and there is a question from what is written above, siman 74 seif 1 in the gloss; also the Rosh's language is difficult, for he said specifically "another"—another woman, who mentioned her name? Rather it is a corruption, and it should read specifically "for others," meaning whether for her husband or for other men.
