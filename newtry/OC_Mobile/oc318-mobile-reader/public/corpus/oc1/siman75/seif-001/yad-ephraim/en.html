In Magen Avraham, se'if katan 3: In the marketplace, etc. At first glance there is no difficulty. Here, where she wishes to recite Krias Shema, the case is in a house or courtyard, where there is no prohibition, as he writes. One must say that Magen Avraham holds that if this were so, even a married woman should be permitted. Rather, since married women ordinarily cover their hair in the marketplace, it is considered ervah for Krias Shema even in a courtyard. Magen Avraham then asks from the virgin bride who goes out in a bridal veil. She walks this way from her father's house to her husband's house. If even an unmarried woman were prohibited, she could not walk from one courtyard to another through an alley, as stated in HaMadir. He answers that the prohibition there concerns undoing her braid. This is forbidden even for an unmarried woman because it violates modest conduct by exposing the head more fully. Sotah teaches regarding 'and he shall uncover the woman's head' that the kohen loosens her hair; Rashi explains that he increases its exposure by undoing her braid. Thus, even an unmarried woman may not do so. The bride goes out under a veil with her hair loose, but she does not undo the braid; rather, the length of her hair lies on her shoulders, as Rashi explains there. Magen Avraham further writes that the prohibition against an unmarried woman undoing her braid cannot be by Torah law, because the verse does not concern an unmarried woman. Otherwise, simply uncovering her hair would also be prohibited by the verse. We derive from 'he shall uncover' that a woman ordinarily does not go with her hair uncovered, since the verse implies it had previously been covered; even before the braid was undone. The plain meaning concerns removal of the covering; only an additional exposition teaches that he also loosens her hair. If that verse applied to an unmarried woman, mere uncovering would therefore be forbidden to her too. It follows that an unmarried woman's prohibition against undoing her braid does not derive from 'he shall uncover,' which does not discuss her, but from standards of modesty: An unmarried woman should not walk with her braid undone because this exposes her hair more fully.
