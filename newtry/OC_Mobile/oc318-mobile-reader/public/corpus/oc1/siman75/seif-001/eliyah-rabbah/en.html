(1) A tefach uncovered, etc.: it implies that regarding another woman there is no prohibition except for a tefach; and even though Rama wrote that some say even less than a tefach is ervah regarding another woman, behold Bach wrote that Rama also holds we do not rule thus—even though in the remainder of Knesset HaGedolah he challenged Bach. It appears he wrote thus because Rama concludes, and it appears from Rosh's words that "a tefach on a woman is ervah" even regarding another woman only regarding herself, etc.; and since he adjoined "a tefach on a woman is ervah" it implies less than a tefach is not ervah. And this is not correct precision, for since according to Rosh he specifically took "tefach," therefore it seems to me that is, that he understood what Rama wrote "even regarding another woman"—namely that he innovates a new law that even woman to another woman is ervah—and Olat Tamid understood likewise. But in my humble opinion they did not descend to the depth of Rama's words here, which stand above to divide against "some say" that regarding another woman even less than a tefach is ervah; and if so, the tefach mentioned in the gemara is specifically regarding his wife—for this he says it appears from Rosh's words that "a tefach on a woman is ervah" even regarding another woman, and he meant that "a tefach on a woman is ervah" is also said regarding another woman, that less than a tefach is not ervah. And further, that "some say" which Rama wrote wrote them—that he is Ba'al Halakhot Gedolot—and I say there is no proof from him, for he wrote in the chapter "one whose dead," that even his wife, if a tefach is uncovered it is forbidden to recite Keriat Shema opposite her—until here. And one can say that this is what he says: it is obvious regarding another woman that it is forbidden at a tefach, but even regarding his wife it is forbidden; but less than a tefach, also regarding another woman, there is no prohibition—and with this the difficulties of Olat Tamid are resolved well. Ba'al Halakhot Gedolot wrote there: "And if she wears thin clothing and appears like a gentile woman, it is forbidden"—until here—and this is learned from seif 5 of Ervah Ba'asirit. And it is plain there is no distinction between an unmarried woman and a married woman; and likewise Be'er Sheva page 119 wrote regarding voice.
