A tefach uncovered, etc.—see Shulchan Shelomo, who wrote that this measure applies even to a small girl from age three onward who is fit for intercourse; if she is his daughter, from eleven years and onward, as above in siman 73—see there.

In a place where her custom is to cover, etc.—see Mishna Berurah that we wrote that it is forbidden even when squeezing the eyes shut; likewise Chayei Adam, and so it appears from Biur HaGra in this seif in the gloss that he compares this matter to that in seif 6—see there. And what is written that some permit is from Bach and Peri Megadim in Mishbetzot Zahav letter 2—see there. And Chayei Adam proved in his Nishmat Adam that even for Rambam one may be lenient when squeezing the eyes shut [and it is astonishing that he did not bring what Rabbeinu Yonah wrote on this explicitly more than this, only that specifically when gazing it is forbidden—see there]. However, for practical law this matter is not clear to permit, for Rabbeinu Yonah's words—as Bach already challenged—were also challenged in Sefer HaEshkol, which writes there that our rabbis' view is not so. And Semag wrote as follows: "A tefach on a woman is ervah when uncovered, and one may not recite until he turns his face from her—and even if she is his wife," implying from the language that even if he is not looking at her at the time of reading it is also forbidden. Nevertheless, in pressed circumstances regarding another matter, one who is lenient and relies on Bach and Peri Megadim to permit squeezing the eyes shut—we do not restrain his hand.
