A handbreadth exposed, etc. See Shulchan Shlomo, which writes that this measure applies even to a minor girl from three years of age onward, since she is fit for relations. If she is his daughter, it applies from eleven years onward, as above in siman 73. See there.
In a place that she ordinarily, etc. See Mishnah Berurah, where we wrote that it is prohibited even if he closes his eyes. Chayei Adam writes this, and it is also implied by Biur HaGra on the gloss in this seif, who compares this matter to the ruling in seif 6. See there. What we wrote, that some permit it, is based on Bach and Peri Megadim in Mishbetzos Zahav, letter 2. See there. Chayei Adam proved in Nishmas Adam that according to Rambam as well one may be lenient when closing one's eyes. It is surprising that he did not cite Rabbeinu Yonah, who states this even more explicitly and prohibits only when one gazes. See there. Nevertheless, the matter is not sufficiently clear for a definitive lenient ruling. Sefer HaEshkol already challenged Rabbeinu Yonah's words, as Bach did, writing that this is not the view of our teachers. Semag writes: “A handbreadth of a woman is ervah when it is exposed, and he may not recite until he turns his face away from her, even if she is his wife.” This language implies that it is prohibited even if he is not looking at her while reciting. Nevertheless, when there is no other possibility, one who is lenient and relies on Bach and Peri Megadim to permit closing his eyes should not be protested.
