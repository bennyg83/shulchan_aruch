(1) In a place where it is her custom [to be covered] — because this brings a person to lewd thoughts when he looks at it; in general it is ervah, and it is forbidden to recite [Shema] or mention anything sacred opposite it, as opposite actual ervah. And according to what we explained below in seif 6 in the name of the later authorities, that opposite actual ervah it is forbidden even while squeezing his eyes shut until he turns his face away — the same applies here. And some permit in this if he is careful not to look at all. And when it is impossible in another matter, it seems one may rely on this.

(2) To cover it — but her face and hands, per the custom whereby it is customary to be uncovered in that place; and likewise with [exposure of] the feet up to the calf [which is up to the place called "knee" in a foreign language]; in a place where their custom is to go barefoot, it is permitted to recite [Shema] opposite it, since because he is accustomed to them he does not come to lewd thoughts. And in a place where their custom is to cover them, their measure is a handbreadth like the rest of a woman's body. But her arms and her calf — even if they are accustomed to go uncovered as the way of the licentious — it is forbidden.

(3) His wife — and therefore one must be careful at the time she is nursing and her breast is uncovered not to speak then any words of sanctity.

(4) It is forbidden to recite [Shema] — see in Peri Megadim who brought opinions regarding the matter of bedieved in this, whether one must go back and recite [Shema] as with the matter of full ervah; and in Biur Halacha it implies that even bedieved one must go back and recite; nevertheless it seems to me that when he did not intend to look, one need not be stringent bedieved to go back and recite, even [if it was] another woman.

(5) But, etc. — see in Nishmas Adam who wrote that according to all this is only rabbinic, and closing one's eyes is effective for this; and likewise with a woman's hair as explained in seif 2.

(6) With another woman — whether unmarried or married woman [= eshet ish =].

(7) Less than a handbreadth — and if the calf is uncovered, some say that even with one's wife and less than a handbreadth it is forbidden to recite [Shema] opposite her, because it is a place of greater lewd thought than other limbs. And all this is only discussed regarding the prohibition of Shema, for the prohibition for many poskim is to recite opposite what is uncovered even without intending to look; but regarding the prohibition of looking, according to all, one who looks at a woman even at a small finger — since he looks at it to enjoy — violates "do not follow after your eyes"; and they said that even if he has Torah and good deeds in his hand he will not be cleansed from judgment of Gehinnom; and a mere glance according to his ordinary [view], without enjoyment, is permitted if not from the aspect of modesty; and in Minchas Shmuel it was proven that an important person must be careful in every manner. And Peri Megadim wrote that in places where the custom is to be covered [such as her arms and the like, other places on the body], even a mere glance is forbidden; and the poskim wrote that our virgins are included in niddah from when they reach the time of vest and are included in arayos.

(8) From the words of the Rosh, etc. — but the Rashba disagrees on this, and his reason is that he holds that just as she herself may recite [Shema] when she is naked, evidently there is no [prohibition] in uncovering her entire body because of "let Him not find anything unseemly among you," but [only] because of men and because of lewd thought — so too another woman, it is permitted to recite and pray opposite her when she is naked; and the later authorities agree with the Rashba, and their opinion is that the Rosh also concedes to this. And know further that according to all it is not permitted to recite opposite her when she is naked except when she is sitting so that her face below is not visible; but when she is standing, her law is like opposite the ervah of a man, and even in another domain it is forbidden.
