If her garment is thin, etc.—as written in seif 65.

In Ervah Ba'asirit—that it is forbidden depends on sight, as it is written "and nothing obscene shall be seen in you"—and it is seen; and the same applies here.

(Seif kaf-alef) And some say, etc.: see Lechem Chamudos, and his words require study. Meaning: on page 24 R' Yitzchak said "a tefach on a woman is ervah," and the gemara concludes: for what halacha? If you say to gaze at her—and R' Sheshet said: whoever gazes at a small finger of a woman is as though he gazed at the place of the pudenda (and if so, less than a tefach too)—there is no difficulty: this is regarding another woman and this is regarding his wife and Keriat Shema (thus in Rosh; but in the gemara's version: only regarding his wife and Keriat Shema), and Rosh concludes "specifically regarding another." But she herself—behold above we said a woman sits and separates challah while naked—and Rama understood Rosh's words: it is plain to Rosh that since regarding his wife they also forbade even though his heart is bold toward her and there is no arousal concern, therefore the reason is "and nothing obscene shall be seen in you." If so, the same applies: even another woman may not recite or bless when her friend is naked opposite her from her body. And on this Rosh wrote "specifically regarding another," but she herself naked may recite. And behold from Shulchan Aruch's words that Rabbeinu Yosef wrote "a tefach uncovered," etc., "even if she is his wife," etc., it implies the same regarding another woman requires specifically a tefach; and so it appears from what Rama wrote "some say specifically regarding his wife," etc., "but," etc. (and Bach understood Tur's and Rambam's language, which is this language) that Rabbeinu Yosef holds that also regarding another woman requires specifically a tefach; and even though regarding another woman it is forbidden to gaze even at a small finger—that is gazing with intent to enjoy through his sight; but sight without intent is forbidden only at a tefach and Keriat Shema—so far Bach. And it must be said accordingly: that which it asks "if you say to gaze," and R' Sheshet, etc.—the questioner's intent is either way: if because of ervah, therefore it does not deal with one who sees casually and not to gaze to enjoy—for in that case even a tefach is permitted; therefore "to gaze" means with intent to enjoy, and if so behold R' Sheshet said even a small finger is forbidden to gaze. And it answers: regarding his wife and Keriat Shema, and it deals with one not intending enjoyment but mere sight—for in general it is permitted even regarding another woman and even a tefach; but for Keriat Shema it is forbidden even regarding his wife. And that is specifically a tefach, and even regarding another (and the reason for the measure of a tefach Bach wrote: the measure of the place of pudenda is a tefach)—in any case Keriat Shema is certainly stricter than not at the time of Keriat Shema. But Lechem Chamudos wrote on this that which Rosh wrote "specifically regarding another, but regarding herself," etc.—he holds according to Rosh that that which it answers "regarding his wife and Keriat Shema" is for amplification—it took his wife, and all the more so regarding another; and on this it says "specifically regarding another," etc., it implies he holds that specifically regarding another means for a man it is forbidden only opposite another, but she herself is permitted; and if so, Rosh's law of one woman opposite her friend is not explained. And if he holds it is permitted, why did he write "and she herself is permitted"—he should have written amplification that even regarding another woman it is permitted. And if he holds regarding another woman it is forbidden, why did he write "specifically regarding another"—according to Lechem Chamudos' explanation it means for a man it is forbidden opposite another woman—he should have written amplification that even regarding another woman it is forbidden. But this can be said somewhat. But afterwards Lechem Chamudos wrote: and from here we learn that Keriat Shema has a voice, etc., that even regarding another whom it is forbidden to gaze at even a small finger, they did not forbid in Keriat Shema except at a tefach; and the reason is lest he come to annul Keriat Shema—so far; and this is a great innovation, and it requires study—for it is lighter in Keriat Shema than in general, that something forbidden in general should be permitted in Keriat Shema.
