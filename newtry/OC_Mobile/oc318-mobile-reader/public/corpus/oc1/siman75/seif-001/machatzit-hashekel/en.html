Regarding ervah seen through glass, it is forbidden because the matter depends upon sight, as it is written, “and no nakedness shall be seen among you,” and here it is visible. The same applies in this case.
Seif katan 1: “And some say, etc.” See Lechem Chamudos, whose words require study. On Berachos 24a Rabbi Yitzchak states that a tefach of a woman is ervah. The Gemara asks what practical halachah this teaches. If it means that one may not gaze at it, Rabbi Sheshes has already said that anyone who gazes even at a woman's little finger is as though he gazed at the place of ervah, so even less than a tefach would be prohibited. The Gemara answers that one statement concerns another woman and the other concerns one's wife with respect to Krias Shema. This is the text of the Rosh, though our Gemara reads, “rather, concerning one's wife and Krias Shema.” The Rosh concludes that this applies specifically to another woman, but a woman herself may sit naked and separate challah, as stated earlier. The Rama understood the Rosh as follows: since even one's wife is prohibited, although he is accustomed to her and there is no concern of improper thought, the reason must be the verse “and no nakedness shall be seen among you.” The same would therefore apply to another woman, who may not recite Krias Shema or a blessing opposite her naked companion. The Rosh's qualification, “specifically another woman, but she herself,” then means that a woman herself may recite while naked. Yet the wording of the Shulchan Aruch, “a tefach exposed on a woman, even his wife,” implies that with another woman as well the prohibition requires a tefach. The Rama's wording, “some say specifically regarding his wife, but,” also indicates that Beit Yosef holds that even regarding another woman a tefach is required. The Bach understood the Tur and Rambam in this manner. He explains that looking at even a little finger of another woman is forbidden only when one intentionally gazes in order to derive pleasure, whereas mere unintentional sight is prohibited for Krias Shema only when a tefach is exposed. According to this, when the Gemara asks, “if for gazing,” it reasons as follows: if the prohibition is because of ervah, the case cannot be mere incidental sight, since even a tefach is permitted in that circumstance. It must refer to deliberate gazing for pleasure, but Rabbi Sheshes has already prohibited even a little finger. The Gemara answers that it concerns one's wife and Krias Shema, where there is no intent to derive pleasure, only ordinary incidental sight. Outside Krias Shema such sight is permitted even of another woman and even of a tefach, but during Krias Shema it is forbidden even regarding one's wife. This prohibition applies specifically to a tefach, even of another woman. The Bach writes that the measure of a tefach corresponds to the size of the place of ervah. In any event, Krias Shema is certainly more stringent than an ordinary circumstance. Lechem Chamudos, however, explains the Rosh's words “specifically another woman, but she herself” to mean that the Gemara mentioned one's wife only as an even greater novelty, and certainly the law applies to another woman. According to his reading, the Rosh means that only a man is prohibited opposite another woman, whereas a woman herself is permitted. The law of one woman opposite another is therefore not explicit in the Rosh. If he holds it permitted, why did he write only that she herself is permitted, rather than the greater novelty that even another woman is permitted? If he holds it prohibited, why did he write “specifically another woman”? According to Lechem Chamudos this means only that a man is prohibited opposite another woman, and he should instead have stated the greater novelty that even another woman is prohibited. This latter point can be resolved somewhat. Lechem Chamudos then writes that we learn from here a leniency in Krias Shema, namely that even regarding another woman, whom one may not gaze at even at a little finger, the Sages prohibited only a tefach during Krias Shema, so that people should not come to neglect Krias Shema. This is a major novelty and requires study, for it would make Krias Shema more lenient than an ordinary circumstance, permitting during Krias Shema something that is otherwise prohibited.
