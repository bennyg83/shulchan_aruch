On a woman: if her garment is thin and flesh is visible through it, it is forbidden. Gloss, Magen Avraham.

Opposite her: Rabbi Yehuda is lenient that it is forbidden only when he gazes at her, but if it is mere sight it is permitted—see Beit Yosef; this is not clear, rather it is forbidden in every respect. Bach, and such is the implication of the poskim. See Peri Megadim.

A handbreadth: Bach disagrees and holds that even regarding another woman there is no prohibition except specifically on a handbreadth, but less than a handbreadth is permitted—see above. According to his words, there is a question from what the Talmud says in Berachot 24: Rabbi Yehuda said, a handbreadth on a woman is ervah—for what purpose, etc., except for his wife and Keriat Shema—see above. Why does it not answer briefly here regarding Keriat Shema that it deals with all women, and why does it mention his wife and Keriat Shema? Rather we learn that there is a distinction—this requires study. If her thigh is uncovered, it is forbidden even regarding his wife when less than a handbreadth, for it is a place of greater arousal than other limbs. Taz, and Bach likewise. See Dvar Shmuel, siman 293.

Another woman: Rashba wrote that this applies only to men, for whom there is concern of arousal, but not to women. Nevertheless, page 26a ruled like the Rosh that even a woman is forbidden to see the nakedness of her friend. Shakh also challenged Rashba—see above. Pri Chadash wrote that Rashba's words are the primary view—see above.
