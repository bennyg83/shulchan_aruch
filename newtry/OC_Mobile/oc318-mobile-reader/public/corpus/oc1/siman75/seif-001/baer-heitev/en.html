With a woman. If she is wearing thin clothing and the flesh is visible through it, it is forbidden. This is the correct text, as in Magen Avraham.
Opposite her. Ri is lenient that it is forbidden only when one gazes at her, but if it is mere seeing it is permitted; see Beit Yosef. This is not clear; rather, in every manner it is forbidden, as the Bach writes, and so is the implication of the poskim. See Olat Tamid.
A tefach. The Bach disagrees and holds that even with another woman there is also no prohibition except specifically a tefach, but less than a tefach is permitted; see there. At first glance, according to his words it requires study why the Talmud in Berachot 24 says: Rabbi Yitzchak said, "a tefach in a woman is ervah" - for what purpose, etc.? Rather, with his wife and for Shema; see there. Why did it not answer briefly: here it is for Shema and concerns all women? Why did it specify his wife and Shema? Rather, it follows that there is a distinction, and it is clear. If her lower leg is exposed, it is forbidden even with his wife and even less than a tefach, for it is more a place of improper thought than other limbs. So wrote the Taz and the Bach. See Devar Shmuel siman 293.
Another. Rashba wrote that this applies specifically to men, where there is concern for improper thought, but not to women. Nevertheless, on daf 26a he ruled like the Rosh that even a woman is forbidden to see the ervah of her fellow. Kenesset HaGedolah also challenged the Rashba; see there. Peri Chadash wrote that the words of Rashba are primary; see there.
