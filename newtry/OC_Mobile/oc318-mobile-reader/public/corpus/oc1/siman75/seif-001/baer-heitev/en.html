With a woman. If she is wearing a thin garment and her flesh is visible through it, it is prohibited. Hagahot; Magen Avraham.
Opposite her. Rabbeinu Yonah is lenient, maintaining that it is prohibited only when one gazes at her, but mere seeing is permitted; see Beit Yosef. This does not appear correct; rather, it is prohibited in every circumstance. Bach. This is also the implication of the halakhic authorities. See Olat Tamid.
A handbreadth. Bach disagrees and holds that even regarding another woman the prohibition applies specifically to a handbreadth, but less than a handbreadth is permitted; see there. At first glance, his position requires examination from the Talmud's statement in Berakhot 24: Rav Yitzchak said, 'A handbreadth of a woman is ervah.' For what purpose, etc.? It answers: regarding his wife and Keriat Shema; see there. Why did it not answer simply that this concerns Keriat Shema and applies to all women? Why did it specifically say 'his wife and Keriat Shema'? Rather, this proves that there is a distinction, and this is readily understood. If her lower leg is exposed, it is prohibited even regarding his wife and even when less than a handbreadth is exposed, because it is a greater source of improper thoughts than other limbs. Taz, and Bach writes likewise. See Devar Shmuel, siman 293.
Another woman. Rashba writes that this applies specifically to men, for whom there is a concern of improper thoughts, but not to women. Mordechai, page 26a, nevertheless rules like the Rosh that even a woman may not look at another woman's nakedness. Shiyurei Kenesses HaGedolah also challenges Rashba; see there. Pri Chadash writes that Rashba's words are the primary view; see there.
