Some say, etc. — In Tur he wrote, etc. From this citation I think our master z"l had great intention. For in the Gemara (Berachos 24a) R' Yehuda said: a handbreadth on a woman is nakedness. R' Chiya said: the thigh on a woman is nakedness, as it says "Expose the thigh and your nakedness will be revealed." There are three explanations: (1) Beit HaRoeh (and Avnei Yashpeh) — to say the thigh is a modest place on a woman. (2) Bach wrote that the thigh on his wife, less than a handbreadth, is nakedness for Kerias Shema. (3) R' Chiya comes to say that on another woman too, less than a handbreadth, and he holds "thigh" — likewise other covered limbs — as Gemara Yevamos (and Rashi s.v. thigh on another woman proves Beit HaRoeh; and Bach wrote on this see below). If so, in the Gemara one can say per the third explanation; in Tur one cannot say so, for he ruled that on another woman too a handbreadth is required per the second explanation — that on his wife, thigh less than a handbreadth is forbidden for Kerias Shema. And this proves from the language he wrote: a handbreadth uncovered on a woman is forbidden to recite Kerias Shema even regarding his wife; and "and her thigh" to attach "her thigh" to his wife as stated. The author brought Tur's language and changed intentionally "a handbreadth uncovered — even his wife — forbidden," because he holds "and her thigh" like Beit Yosef; therefore he omitted "her thigh" here. Ra'avad will explain per the third explanation and brought it. Taz z"l wrote: why omit thigh? One could say thigh on his wife, less too, is a stringency, for both may be true — thigh on his wife less than a handbreadth, and Gemara Yevamos is forced from "handbreadth on his wife" and did not say only for Kerias Shema — it would be even on another woman for disqualifying Kerias Shema even if he returns and recites (see Magen Avraham there on Choshen Mishpat ch. 3 regarding Kerias Shema 16) — meaning on another woman less than a handbreadth is also forbidden for Kerias Shema and he returns and recites. Understand this and see Magen Avraham on this. Know that the language "thigh" is from the knee (per Rashi Vayikra 7:32 and Beit Yosef ch. 10 Chullin 44, and so Rambam z"l in Hilchos Issurei Bi'ah 9:5; thigh is sometimes called yerech) — so the whole leg until the knee, called shin there where they walk barefoot exposed, perhaps there is no concern; where they are accustomed to cover them — a handbreadth from the knee upward; less than a handbreadth too. Avnei Yashpeh wrote soiled thigh, etc. — meaning from labor; see there. See Netsiv's explanation of R' Chiya's words, so Chagigah 13a Rashi s.v. riding; see there.

I refined somewhat here: he said regarding his wife and Kerias Shema, and did not stipulate Kerias Shema — this proves like Gemara Yevamos; and Avnei Yashpeh, who questioned Tur how he wrote on another woman that a handbreadth is forbidden without Kerias Shema to look — meaning he holds one does not return and recite; and you could answer that looking without pleasure is permitted, as in Even HaEzer 21:61 Kerias Shema is forbidden; thus Gemara Yevamos and Ra'avad too deal with mere sight, and one need not return and recite as Peri Chadash wrote regarding hirhur. That he did not say Kerias Shema proves like Gemara Yevamos only from Rashi Berachos s.v. lest he look — if a married woman implies an unmarried woman is permitted; why not establish Kerias Shema generally on an unmarried woman — meaning an unmarried woman too, less than a handbreadth, is forbidden like Gemara Yevamos. It is true that in Even HaEzer 21:61 and 2 it is explained that a virgin is forbidden rabbinically; see Bach seif 2 — requires study now.

From the Levush I understood that he omitted his teacher's glosses because he holds he ruled like Rosh that on another woman too we require a handbreadth; therefore the Levush concluded specifically regarding another man's wife, not regarding herself; and likewise two women reciting thus — this proves Bach that Ra'avad agreed to the author; see Magen Avraham seif 1 from this. This is what we wanted to explain here.

In what Rashi Berachos 24a s.v. thigh and s.v. nakedness we wrote above — further explanation is needed: it is said regarding another woman that one who looks and enjoys is forbidden; however even uncovered, as the viewer sees in Even HaEzer 21:1 — but sight without enjoyment is permitted, as Bach wrote. It is obvious that regarding covered flesh even mere sight is forbidden; therefore Rashi on another woman, and this proves Beit HaRoeh. Understand this; see Beit Shmuel Even HaEzer 169:32 and Be'er HaGolah shin; in my book Chalitza I noted.

What the author wrote in this seif: forbidden to recite opposite the nakedness of a non-Jew; however opposite the nakedness of an animal is permitted — Peri Chadash; and it is explained in Berachos 25, what is donkey flesh — thus regarding an animal we have no concern. Hirhur is permitted opposite nakedness — Shabbos 150a, and Rashi there he brought in seif 4 from this; see there.
