6) There: It is forbidden to recite Krias Shema while facing it. Tur writes that if her lower leg is exposed, it is forbidden to recite while facing it. Bach asks why the Tur singled out the lower leg from all her other normally covered areas. He answers that even less than a handbreadth is considered nakedness there, as derived from the verse, "Bare the leg, cross the rivers," followed by "your nakedness shall be uncovered," implying that the entire lower leg is nakedness, even less than a handbreadth. Taz, paragraph 1, and Ateres Zekenim, paragraph 1, write likewise. Maran, however, in Beis Yosef, explains the Tur's statement differently: although the lower leg is not an intimate area in a man, it is nakedness in a woman. This is the reason Rashba gives in the name of Raavad; see Beis Yosef. According to Maran, the Tur did not single out the exposed lower leg to prohibit even less than a handbreadth. Rather, one might have thought that it is not nakedness at all because it is not an intimate area in a man, so the Tur teaches that it is nakedness. Its law nevertheless remains that of other normally covered areas, and recitation is forbidden only when a handbreadth is exposed. This also follows from the unqualified wording of Shulchan Aruch here, which does not distinguish the lower leg from other normally covered areas, implying that they are equal and only an exposed handbreadth is forbidden. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2, likewise writes after citing Rashba and Beis Yosef: "Taz, however, writes that even with his wife, less than a handbreadth of her lower leg is forbidden, because it is a more prohibited place than the other limbs." This is also implied by Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1; Chayei Adam, rule 4, paragraph 7; and Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 1. Nevertheless, initially one should take account of Bach, Taz, and Ateres Zekenim, since the later authorities cite and consider this view: Eliyah Rabbah there; Chayei Adam there; Shulchan Aruch HaRav there; Shesilei Zeitim, paragraph 1.
