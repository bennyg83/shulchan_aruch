Or if he squinted his eyes. So too Beit Yosef — and it is puzzling in my eyes. First, the Talmud's language does not imply this, for it states there the reason for what is written "and your nakedness of a matter shall not be seen in you" — it is visible and it does not say "and he sees" — meaning once it is visible opposite him, even though he does not see, it is forbidden. Further, regarding feces too it says the matter depends on sight — that is, "before him as full as his eyes," as stated in siman 69; nevertheless it is forbidden even blind and at night since it is possible to see. If so, the Talmud's language of sight where it says regarding feces "all that is not in another domain" should not be more severe than the scriptural language "and your nakedness of a matter shall not be seen in you" — yet both use the language of sight. Further, what we learned in Tosefta Terumah, and brought in gloss at the beginning of Chullin, that one who is naked may not separate terumah, and likewise regarding slaughter we rule at the beginning of Yoreh De'ah that one who is naked may not slaughter — according to Beit Yosef there would be permission if he squints his eyes at the time of blessing. And one could say the reason is because his heart sees the nakedness — if so it is difficult for one who says his heart seeing nakedness is permitted: how does he explain this Tosefta? And what Beit Yosef wrote as proof from Rambam who permits turning the face — there it is different, for when he turns his face the nakedness is not opposite him and not visible, for Scripture was particular only if the nakedness is opposite him, as written "forbidden to recite opposite it." And one could say regarding terumah and slaughter too he could turn his face and bless — nevertheless he himself is in revelation of nakedness and it is forbidden to bless because of "and your nakedness of a matter shall not be seen in you" — meaning there should not be in him nakedness of a matter; unlike Beit Yosef's explanation that the matter depends on sight and we do not care about visibility. And what Beit Yosef further wrote as proof from Rashba, whom Beit Yosef brought in siman 74 — why does the Talmud ask regarding one bathing in clear water "yet his heart sees the nakedness," but does not ask from his eyes, since his eyes are outside the water and he looks outside — he does not see the naked person? According to this, the rule we already stated applies: only nakedness opposite him is forbidden, not below him — if he stood at the edge of the pit and the nakedness was in the pit, there is no prohibition to recite Kerias Shema. And that he stands in water, even though clear, is called covered against people — only that he can look down at his nakedness; this is not called opposite him. Therefore it seems the law agrees with the Rokeach whom Beit Yosef brought, that it is forbidden even if he squints his eyes. And I also saw Maharshal z"l who wrote to be stringent; and this turning of the face that they permitted — that he turned his entire body and stands on another side — is similar to what is said in siman 73: if he slept with his wife he recites by turning his face, and there turning the face alone is not relevant since he lies down; and likewise here it is specifically only by turning his entire body.
