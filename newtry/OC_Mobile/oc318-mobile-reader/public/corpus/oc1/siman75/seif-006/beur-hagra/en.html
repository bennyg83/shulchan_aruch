Seif 6, "and he turned," etc.: Rambam—and as written above, 24:1—what is this turning, etc.; and even according to those who forbid because their backs touch.

Or a bone—thus Beit Yosef learned from the Rambam's words above regarding turning, and as written in the preceding seif that the Merciful One made it depend on sight, and as they wrote. And it would seem [difficult] from what is written in siman 79 seif 1 that there it does not depend on sight as they distinguished in the gemara, and as written in siman 76 seif 1. But Rokeach wrote that it does not help, and the later authorities agreed, and only turning his whole body helps, as in this case. Also regarding feces it helps regarding that he need not fill his eyes, as is clear from the Jerusalem Talmud cited above regarding feces, which says: since if it were daytime they would see what is before them—but in this case it is forbidden. And there, regarding filling his eyes, it is not because of the smell but because of sight—and this is what it says "since," etc., and nevertheless it is forbidden at night as well, and the same applies here. And Mordechai likewise learned from there regarding what is here in chapter 3 of Berachos, and he forbids as one forbids regarding feces at night in that Jerusalem Talmud.
