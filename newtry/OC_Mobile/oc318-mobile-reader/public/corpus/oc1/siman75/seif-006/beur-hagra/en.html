Seif 6, and he turned, etc. Rambam, as stated there on 24a: “What is the law if this one turns,” etc. This applies even according to the authority who prohibits it because their backs touch.
Or he closed, etc. Beit Yosef derived this from Rambam's ruling concerning turning away, and from what was written in the preceding seif, that the Torah made the matter dependent on seeing, as explained there. This is unlike siman 79, seif 1, because there the law does not depend on seeing, as the Gemara distinguishes and as written in siman 76, seif 1. Rokeach, however, writes that closing one's eyes does not help, and the later authorities agreed. Only turning one's entire body is effective, for in such a case it also helps regarding excrement insofar as one need not distance himself the full range of vision. This also appears from the Yerushalmi cited above regarding excrement, which says: Since, had it been daytime, they would have seen what was before them, in this situation it is prohibited. There, the requirement of the full range of vision is not because of odor but because of sight. This is the meaning of “Since,” etc. Nevertheless, it is prohibited even at night. The same applies here. Mordechai likewise derived the ruling here from that Yerushalmi in the third chapter of Berachos, and prohibits it just as the Yerushalmi prohibits excrement at night.
