(se'if kaf alef) A merchant, etc.—he is meticulous about his craft, such as pomegranate wine—as it should read.

For there is forgery even for idolaters: pomegranate wine they drink for healing, and if they mix vinegar into it, its remedy does not work.

Spun not for its sake. And regarding this I am troubled in my soul, for this does not depend on craftsmanship, and he can also sell it to idolaters—see in the responsa of the Chacham Tzvi, part 4, siman 39, what he wrote on this.

Therefore it seems to me, etc.—because it is not the way of idolaters, etc.; meaning, in conjunction with a merchant—for according to one view, craftsmanship: even though there is not so much meticulousness, as the Magen Avraham wrote above, nevertheless in conjunction with "it is not the way of idolaters," etc., even slight meticulousness is effective.

And that which he brought as proof from tzitzit to pomegranate wine—so it must be. Meaning, he brought proof (brought in Beit Yosef, Yoreh De'ah siman 114) that we rely on the tradesman's view—for according to one view, his craftsmanship in pomegranate wine, for we see in the Gemara that they buy a tallit with tzitzit from a merchant; and therefore, because he is a tradesman—for per the Magen Avraham's words, what proof does he bring from a tallit? A tallit is different, for there is also the view that it is not the way of idolaters, etc.

But to learn that we rely, etc.—for per the Magen Avraham, the view is excellent, and automatically we say from the view that since in a tallit slight meticulousness is effective—since one can combine the view that it is not the way of idolaters, etc.—if so, in pomegranate wine, where there is much meticulousness, the tradesman's view alone per the Magen Avraham suffices—we say in Avodah Zarah page 39.

Chavit is an acronym — the mnemonic is: tekhelet, wine, meat, fish (tav-tet-yod-kaf-dalet).

And according to one explanation it is Rashba; as he said, this is Rashi's opinion. And that Rav said tekhelet and did not state the novelty of even white — for his view that white does not require spinning lishmah.

Truly, white does not need a seal. And specifically tekhelet — for we are concerned about kela ilan, whose color resembles tekhelet and is invalid; therefore two seals are required.

“But according to us,” etc. This needs explanation: in Avodah Zarah we say that Shmuel said that the acronym “beit” requires two seals, namely meat, wine, and tekheles, and he omits a cut of fish, which is included in meat. If so, according to Shmuel, who holds that spinning lishmah is required, as in Sukkah 9 and Menachot 42, one should ask why he mentioned tekheles; he should have stated the greater novelty, even white strings. It is forced to say he mentioned it only because Rav did, for we see that he changed from Rav and omitted the fish-cut, since it is automatically learned from meat; all the more should he have changed from Rav and mentioned white strings, where there is practical halachic relevance. One could answer somewhat that the Magen Avraham could in any case have answered as the Beit Yosef wrote against Mahari’a: the rule not to buy a tallis from a non-Jew who is not a tradesman applies specifically in the time of tekheles, because one might say it is kela ilan; since tekheles is expensive, we fear the non-Jew may lie. But according to us, when we have only white, even from a non-Jew who is not a tradesman one may buy, since there is not such a price difference between spun lishmah and not lishmah. The Beit Yosef wrote the opposite: since tekheles can be checked, one should have said he will not falsify it, and nevertheless we are concerned; all the more with white, where one cannot check whether it was spun lishmah. If so, the Magen Avraham could likewise have answered his question: Rav mentioned tekheles as the greater novelty, that even tekheles, which can be checked, is still not enough with one seal, because we fear he may still trouble himself and falsify it; all the more white, which cannot be checked for lishmah, requires two seals. But the Magen Avraham’s true answer is that according to Rav, white does not need two seals, since he holds that spinning lishmah is not required. According to Shmuel, however, one may indeed say that tekheles was mentioned as the novelty, as above. And one can say, in the Eshel Avraham, that this is simple. If one can say that he benefits from the exchange, etc.: we are not concerned that a non-Jew will make a Jew stumble where the non-Jew derives no benefit, for what benefit does he get from the exchange if he does it only to anger the Jew? By the non-Jew’s mistaken view, the Jew does not receive punishment for the matter, since by the non-Jew’s belief there is no prohibition; he is only acting to anger the Jew by frustrating his will. Since the Jew does not know that he exchanged them, this is not so much “to anger.” So wrote the responsum of the Chacham Tzvi, siman 39.

And it is possible that if they are plied and cut — see below siman 11; meaning that he brought the dispute of the poskim: if one finds threads cut and plied, do we rely on them that they are certainly tzitzis, since a person would not trouble himself so much to make them for clothing purposes alone, or do we fear they were not spun lishmah with intent for the garment? Nevertheless, here one buys from a merchant cut and plied threads — it is possible that all permit, for one can combine the merchant’s reputation per the Magen Avraham, even though on that reason alone we do not rely, as the Magen Avraham wrote above, except when combined with the fact that gentiles do not usually put tzitzis on a garment. Still, with cut and plied threads one can combine another reason — that a person would not trouble himself so much with intent for the garment. Therefore, even for the view that when one finds cut and plied threads alone we do not rely on that reason alone, nevertheless with the addition that one buys from a merchant it is possible he would concede we rely, or it refers to that leniency — in such a case of plied threads it is permitted even with one seal.
