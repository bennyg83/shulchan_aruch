(Seif katan 1) A merchant, etc. "Particular about his trade," such as pomegranate wine; so it should read.
For it would be falsification even toward idolaters, since they drink pomegranate wine for healing, and if vinegar is mixed into it, it does not perform its healing effect.
That was spun not lishmah. He did not explain himself with this, for this does not depend on trade, and he can also sell it to idolaters. See the responsa of Chacham Tzvi, end of siman 39, what he wrote about this.
Therefore it appears to me, etc. Because it is not the way of idolaters, etc. Meaning, together with the merchant's concern not to ruin his trade; even though the concern is not so strong, as Magen Avraham wrote above, nevertheless when combined with the fact that it is not the way of idolaters, etc., even a small concern is effective.
And that which he brings, etc. It should read: from tzitzit to pomegranate wine. Meaning, he brought proof, cited in Beit Yosef, Yoreh De'ah siman 114, that we rely on the reasoning that a craftsman will not ruin his trade regarding pomegranate wine, for we see in the Talmud that one may buy a fringed tallit from a merchant, necessarily because a craftsman will not ruin his trade. According to Magen Avraham, what proof does he bring from a tallit? A tallit is different, for there is the additional reasoning that it is not the way of idolaters, etc.
Rather, to teach that we rely, etc. For the reasoning that a craftsman will not ruin his trade is, according to Magen Avraham, a strong reasoning. Consequently, we say by reasoning that since regarding a tallit a minor concern is effective because one can combine the reasoning that it is not the way of idolaters, etc., then regarding pomegranate wine, where there is a great concern, the reasoning that a craftsman will not ruin his trade alone is sufficient; as we say in Avodah Zarah 39.
Chavit is an acronym. A piece of fish, meat, wine, and tekhelet.
According to one explanation it is Rashba, who holds one seal is enough; that is the view of Rashi. And the reason Rav said tekhelet and did not state the greater novelty of even white strings is according to his own view, that he does not require spinning lishmah.
In truth, white does not need a seal. Specifically tekhelet requires concern for kala ilan, whose color resembles tekhelet and is invalid; therefore it needs two seals.
But according to us, etc. This requires explanation: we say there in Avodah Zarah that Shmuel said bayit requires two seals, namely meat, wine, and tekhelet. He omitted a piece of fish, which is included in meat. If so, according to Shmuel, to whom we require spinning lishmah, as stated in Sukkah 9 and Menachot 42, the difficulty remains: why did he mention tekhelet? He should have stated the novelty of even white. It is forced to say that he mentioned it because Rav did, for we see that he changed from Rav and omitted a piece of fish, since it is anyway inferred from meat; all the more he should have changed from Rav's wording and mentioned white, where there is a practical difference. One can answer somewhat: seemingly Magen Avraham could in any case have answered as Beit Yosef wrote regarding Mahari'a, who wrote that one may not buy a tallit from an idolatrous non-craftsman specifically in the era of tekhelet, for one can say perhaps it is kala ilan; and since tekhelet is expensive, we suspect perhaps the idolater lies. But according to us, when we have only white, one may buy even from an idolatrous non-craftsman, since whether it was spun lishmah or not lishmah there is not such great profit. Beit Yosef wrote the opposite: there is reason to the contrary, for since tekhelet can be checked, one might have said he would not falsify, and nevertheless we are concerned; all the more for making white, where one cannot check whether it was spun lishmah. Accordingly, Magen Avraham could likewise have answered his question: for this reason Rav mentioned tekhelet as a novelty, that even tekhelet, which can be determined by testing, nevertheless one seal is not enough because we still suspect he will trouble himself and falsify; all the more white, which cannot be checked whether it was spun lishmah, certainly requires two seals. But Magen Avraham answered truthfully that according to Rav, white does not need two seals, for he holds we do not require spinning lishmah. But according to Shmuel, in truth one can say that he mentioned tekhelet as this novelty, as stated. One can say in another way, but this is simple. If one can say that he benefits from the exchange, etc.: we are not concerned that an idolater would cause Israel to stumble where the idolater does not benefit, for what benefit does the exchange give him if he acts only to anger Israel? According to the idolater's mistaken view, Israel does not receive punishment for the matter, since according to the idolater's belief there is no prohibition in it; he only acts to anger Israel by ensuring his will is not fulfilled. Since the Israelite does not know he exchanged it, there is not so much provocation in this, as in the responsa of Chacham Tzvi, siman 39.
And perhaps if they are twisted, etc. See above siman 11. Meaning, there he brought the dispute among the poskim whether, if one found cut and twisted strings, we rely on this, for they are certainly tzitzit since a person would not trouble himself so much for the purpose of a garment, or whether we suspect they were not spun lishmah and he made them with a garment in mind. Nevertheless, here, to buy cut and twisted strings from a merchant, perhaps all agree it is permitted, for one can combine the reasoning that a craftsman will not ruin his trade. Although we do not rely on this reasoning alone, as Magen Avraham wrote above, but only together with the fact that it is not the way of an idolater to make tzitzit in a garment, nevertheless with cut and twisted strings there is another reasoning to combine: a person would not trouble himself so much with a garment in mind. Therefore even according to the view that, when one finds cut and twisted strings, we do not rely on that reasoning alone, nevertheless when combined with buying from a merchant, perhaps he agrees that we rely on it; or it refers to the adjacent case, that in this way, when they are twisted, it is permitted even with one seal.
