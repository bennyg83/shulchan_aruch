“From a merchant.” Even if he is a merchant in other merchandise he is believed, for if he is found a liar in one matter they will no longer believe him in another (Beis Yosef); and Yoreh Deah siman 114, seif 5, that for other prohibitions as well we rely on a merchant when there is strong concern in the matter. Still, this needs study: there it implies specifically something where there is concern for his craft—for example morays into which they mix vinegar, which is fraud even toward a gentile—but here there is concern that it was spun not for its sake, and that does not ruin his reputation—see: is one permitted to buy meat from a gentile merchant when he says it is kosher because it does not ruin his reputation? See Chullin chapter 95. Therefore it appears to me we are lenient specifically regarding a tallis because gentiles do not usually put tzitzis on a garment, and therefore the Gemara and poskim spoke of a fringed tallis; but it is forbidden to buy tzitzis from a gentile even if he is a merchant, and therefore the Gemara and poskim spoke of a fringed tallis. That which the Ran brings proof from tzitzis regarding morays—he brings it only to learn that we rely on a merchant when there is concern for reputation. We say in Avodah Zarah chapter 2: Rav Chiya taught: it is forbidden to send [anything] by a gentile’s hand except with two seals, and this is brought in Yoreh Deah siman 118; one of them is for tekheles, and according to one explanation specifically tekheles, whose cost is high—we are concerned for substitution; but for white tzitzis we are not as concerned for substitution, and one seal suffices. But in Shulchan Aruch and Taz the reason given is that for anything that is a biblical prohibition, even if its cost is not high, two seals are required—so for tzitzis as well two seals are needed, for biblically it requires spinning for its sake. That Rav specified tekheles follows his reasoning, for he holds we do not require spinning for its sake; but according to us two seals are needed. Still, specifically if there is concern he benefits from the exchange, as seif 2 says; and possibly if they are plied and broken threads it is permitted, as we say in Eruvin chapter 10—and see below siman 11.
