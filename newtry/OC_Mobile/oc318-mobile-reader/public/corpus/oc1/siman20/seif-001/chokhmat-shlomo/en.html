Seif 2. One may not sell a tallit with tzitzit to a non-Jew, etc. See note: See in Magen Avraham what he wrote in the name of Rashi, that the Talmud's intent in what we say "because of a prostitute" is that she will say a Jew gave it to her. And behold, at first glance I have a difficulty: why did Rashi not explain that the concern is lest he mislead the prostitute into saying that he is a Jew—since we establish that one suspected regarding a light matter is not suspected regarding a matter more severe than it; and if so, it is known that adultery with a non-Jew is more severe than with a Jew regarding an unmarried woman; and all the more for those who hold that "you shall not marry them" applies to Gentile women—for certainly there is a prohibition more severe than Torah law; and also for those who hold "you shall not marry them" applies to converts—nevertheless in the eyes of the world it is certainly more severe with a non-Jew than with a Jew. And if so, behold one suspected lightly in the eyes of the world is not suspected severely in the eyes of the world; and if so, it is fitting to make a regulation to reduce prohibition—for with a Jew, if she is suspect, who among all Israel is suspected to listen to her to commit adultery with her? But a non-Jew is certainly suspected regarding adultery, and he will come to mislead her and she will think he is a Jew and commit adultery with him. And behold, I again looked in Tractate Menachos [page 43] and saw that he indeed explained thus—that Scripture without a verse, and he explained that she transgresses because of "you shall not marry them," which proves he holds that "you shall not marry them" means Gentile women; and per his approach it follows, as Beit Shmuel wrote in his name in Even HaEzer [siman 4], and so is Rambam's view; and this is puzzling regarding the Magen Avraham who omitted Rashi's remark: And behold, through this I stood on Rambam's view, that he omitted this reason of the Talmud "because of a prostitute" and cited only R' Yehuda's reason "lest he accompany him"—the Beit Yosef sensed this and forced it because it is more common and more severe, but that is forced. But in my view it fits very well, for behold we have a Mishnah in chapter 3 of Demai [Mishnah 5]: One who gives tithes to an innkeeper—what he gives her, etc., R' Yosi says we are not responsible for Arameans, etc.; and it is explained in the Gemara Bava Kamma [page 69 side 2] the reason: feed him to the wicked and let him die; and so holds Rashbag in Tractate Maaser Sheni chapter 5 regarding the rest of the seven-year cycle: feed him to the wicked and let him die; and see Tosefes Yom Tov there that per Rashi in chapter 1 of Chullin, the Tanna Kamma also holds this view of feeding the wicked and letting him die—only that he holds an innkeeper intends for good; but from the Yerushalmi it is proven there that Tanna Kamma and R' Yosi dispute, for according to Tanna Kamma we do not say feed the wicked and let him die, and R' Yosi says we do—see there. Therefore Rambam holds like Rashi's first explanation, that the intent is "because of a prostitute"—that he will show her he is a Jew and she will commit adultery with him; and therefore it was difficult for me, for behold we are not responsible for Arameans; and therefore he holds this reason "because of a prostitute" holds like Tanna Kamma of Demai above, who holds we make regulations for Arameans, and therefore he holds the reason is "because of a prostitute"; and therefore from this reason itself R' Yehuda deduces another reason, "lest he accompany him," for it was difficult for him: it works out for the Rabbis, but for R' Yosi who holds we are not responsible for Arameans, for him it would be forbidden to sell him a tallit; therefore R' Yehuda innovates another reason, "lest he accompany him"; and since we hold like Rashbag that we are not responsible for Arameans, therefore he could not copy the reason "because of a prostitute," only the second reason. And what will sweeten this language of the Talmud in Menachos there, where it says: what is the reason here—translate it as "because of a prostitute," and what is the language "here translate it"? But per the above it fits well, for behold Rashi's view is that even for Tanna Kamma we are not responsible for Arameans, and the reason there was from the fact that she intends for good—only in the Yerushalmi Tosefes Yom Tov brought that per Tanna Kamma we are not responsible for deceivers, and therefore he said "here translate it" in Eretz Yisrael, who holds that for Tanna Kamma we make regulations for Arameans, and therefore he explains "because of a prostitute"; but our Talmud holds like Rashi that even for Tanna Kamma we are not responsible for Arameans—if so, one cannot say the reason at all stems from a prostitute, and therefore R' Yehuda, who was in Babylonia, needed another reason, "lest he accompany him," etc.; and it fits very well—understand well: And behold in our above words we can also reconcile what Peri Megadim critiqued in this siman on Rashi, that in adultery by chance there is certainly no Torah prohibition of "you shall not marry them"—see there; and per what I said, Rashi's intent would be thus: they made a regulation for this, lest when she knows he is a non-Jew she withdraws—and even with a non-Jew it is only rabbinic; and behold regarding rabbinic law she is suspect—in any case, in the eyes of the world adultery with a non-Jew is severe; and that is: in the eyes of the world it seems to them that "you shall not marry them" applies even by way of adultery, and as we say in general regarding "you shall not covet a man's wife without money"—it seems to them thus; so too are matters reversed—that it seems to them always Torah prohibitions even when not forbidden by Torah; and therefore here too, per the world's view, she would transgress "you shall not marry them" and would not have transgressed if she knew he was a non-Jew; but if she did not know he was a non-Jew, she would commit adultery with him; and since it is possible to withdraw from prohibition, we make a regulation—and it fits well. And per what was said, it also fits well what I wrote in my composition on Yoreh De'ah in the omissions, siman 118: if he is not wicked except regarding a rabbinic prohibition, we certainly make him a regulation so he should not come to Torah prohibition; and per the above explanation of Rashi, here there was Torah prohibition and the prostitute was unmarried—it was only rabbinic; if so, all agree it is fitting to make a regulation, and our words were concealed in this; but per the above it fits well that even with a non-Jew it was only rabbinic prohibition, only more severe in the eyes of the world; therefore it depends on the dispute of rabbinic authorities and R' Yosi; and Rambam's view fits well—understand well; and see in my Mahadura Tanina on Yoreh De'ah siman 118 what I wrote on these matters, and in my composition on Avodah Zarah page 44 in responsum to KK of Moshchisk in Avodah Zarah siman 109—see there and understand well.
