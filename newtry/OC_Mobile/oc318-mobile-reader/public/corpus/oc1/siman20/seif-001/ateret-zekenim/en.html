Or a non-Jewish merchant. {Rama: in Avodah Zarah} Even if he is not a merchant who sells tallitot specifically, but only a merchant of other goods, he is also trusted, for he does not ruin his soul—that if he is found to have lied in one matter, they will still not believe him (Beit Yosef, Nimmukei Yosef, and so it appears from Rashi's words). Here the Beit Yosef wrote, and these are his words: And the Nimmukei Yosef further wrote that from the fact that he is not a merchant it is invalid—if it is dyed, we are concerned lest it be his dyeing; and if the threads are white, lest he made it; and tzitzit that a non-Jew made are invalid, as R' Yitzchak also wrote. But our great master Mahari Abulafia challenged this, for concern for fraudulent dye applies specifically regarding tekhelet, because of kela ilan, since tekhelet is expensive and one would trouble himself and forge; but regarding white, because of the profit that it is a minor matter, he would not do it except through a Jew. And behold, per the view of the author of the Ittur, he disagrees with this, for he holds that a non-Jew can make tzitzit—for here it is not written "let them make" but "and they made," which implies others made them, end quote. And what they forbade in the Gemara regarding tzitzit bought from an ordinary person—Mahari Abulafia explained per the Ittur, that is, specifically regarding tekhelet because the reason is that we are concerned lest it be dyed; but regarding white, where this concern does not exist, it is permitted to buy them from an ordinary person, for even if a non-Jew made them they are permitted—for what should we be concerned? So understood the Rav Beit Yosef in Mahari Abulafia's words. And what the author of the Ittur wrote—that it is permitted to buy a tallit with white tzitzit from a non-Jew who is not a merchant because he permits making tzitzit through a non-Jew—does not seem correct at all, end quote, the Beit Yosef; and the rest of the matter, see in the books of the Ittur and you will find satisfaction, for the Rav elaborated very much.
