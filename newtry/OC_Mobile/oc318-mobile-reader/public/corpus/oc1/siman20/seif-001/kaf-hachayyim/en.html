1) [Se'if 1.] One who purchases a tallit fitted with tzitzit from a Jew, etc. The reason is that an ordinary Jew has a presumption of validity. If, however, he is a Jew suspected of violating this law, the tallit is invalid. Bach; Pitchei Olam, ot 1. See Yoreh De'ah, siman 119, and the following ot.
2) There: Or from a trustworthy non-Jewish merchant, etc. Specifically, a tallit already fitted with tzitzit may be purchased from a trustworthy non-Jewish merchant. Tzitzit strings themselves, however, may not be purchased from a non-Jew, even if he is a merchant. Turei Zahav, se'if katan 1; Magen Avraham, se'if katan 1; Yad Aharon in Hagahot HaTur; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ot 2; Pri Megadim in Mishbetzot Zahav, ot 1, and Eshel Avraham, ot 1; Nehar Shalom, ot 1; and Shatilei Zeitim, ot 2. Ramban writes in Milchamot, in the first chapter of Sukkah: “One who purchases tzitzit from a gentile merchant must be concerned about them; they may be purchased only from an expert chaver.” These are his words; see there. Erekh HaShulchan, ot 1, cites him. Nevertheless, in the preceding ot we already cited Bach, who writes in the name of Tur that an ordinary Jew has a presumption of validity. Shach writes likewise in Yoreh De'ah, siman 119, ot 1. Zivchei Tzedek there, ot 2, writes the same in the name of several halakhic authorities; see there, and ot 4, where he rules accordingly, contrary to the alternative opinion cited by Rama in the gloss there, se'if 1.
2) Regarding Turei Zahav's statement there that one may occasionally purchase tzitzit strings from a non-Jew who is not a merchant, just as one may purchase tefillin in siman 39 (a ruling cited by Eliyahu Rabbah, ot 4) Pri Megadim there, Ma'amar Mordechai, ot 1, and Nehar Shalom there disagree with him. Tefillin are different, because we say that it is not the practice of a non-Jew to write them, as explained by Beit Yosef; that reasoning does not apply here. They write that this distinction is also implied by Magen Avraham, se'if katan 1; see there.
