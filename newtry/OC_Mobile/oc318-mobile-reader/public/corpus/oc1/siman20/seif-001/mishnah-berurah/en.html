(1) From a Jew. Even if he does not know the seller to be reliable and observant, we nevertheless assume that an ordinary Jew has a presumption of validity and certainly spun, twisted, and attached the strings for the sake of the mitzvah, unless he is known to be suspect. For the general laws governing a suspected person, see Yoreh Deah, siman 119.
(2) Or from a non-Jewish merchant. Even if he is not known specifically as a merchant who purchases talleisim, but only as one who purchases other merchandise, he is nevertheless believed. If he is once found dishonest in any matter, people will no longer trust him in other matters.
(3) From a reliable Jew. Levush implies that the merchant need not say that he purchased it from a specifically reliable Jew. Purchase from an ordinary Jew suffices, just as where a Jew purchases from another Jew. The same is implied by the other later authorities. It is possible that Rama agrees, and that the word “reliable” refers to the merchant, meaning that he is believed upon his word. Rama added this to teach that we rely specifically upon his statement and believe him because he would not damage his own reputation by lying. In the absence of a statement, however, the consideration that he would not damage his reputation does not apply.
(4) Because he would not damage his reputation. See Magen Avraham, who writes that we rely upon this reasoning specifically regarding a tallis with its tzitzis attached, because a non-Jew does not ordinarily attach tzitzis to his garment. Purchasing tzitzis strings alone, however, is forbidden even from a merchant, because perhaps the spinning and twisting were not done for the sake of the mitzvah. Taz and Derech HaChayim rule the same. Some are lenient. See Artzos HaChayim and Yeshuos Yaakov, who agree that where the merchant's business is to purchase tzitzis from Jews and resell them, one may purchase them from him.
(5) It is invalid. Perhaps the non-Jew himself attached the tzitzis to the garment. All the more so, one may not purchase tzitzis strings from him, because perhaps they were not spun for the sake of the mitzvah. It is therefore obvious that even if one intends to remove them from the garment and retie them, it is forbidden. Magen Avraham and the other later authorities, unlike Taz. A fringed tallis found in the marketplace is valid. One may not send tzitzis, even already cut and twisted, through a non-Jew unless they are secured with two seals.
