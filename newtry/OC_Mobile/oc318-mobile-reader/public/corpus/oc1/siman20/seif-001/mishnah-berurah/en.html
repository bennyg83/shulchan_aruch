(1) From a Jew — even if one does not know him to be kosher and trustworthy, nevertheless we presume that an ordinary Jew is presumed kosher, and he certainly spun, plied, and attached them lishmah, unless he is suspect [Bach]; and the rules of a suspect person — see Yoreh Deah siman 119.

(2) Or from a gentile merchant — even if he is not established as one who buys tallitot, only established to buy other merchandise, nevertheless he is believed, for if he is found a liar in one matter they will no longer believe him in another.

(3) “From a Jew he is believed” — see the Levush, from whom it is evident that the merchant need not say he bought from a trustworthy person, for even from an ordinary Jew it is valid, as when a Jew buys from a Jew; and so imply other later authorities, and it is possible the Rema also holds thus. The word “believed” refers to the merchant, that he is believed in his statement; and the Rema added this to teach us that we rely and believe him specifically on his word, for he does not harm himself by lying, unlike in a case of silence where he does not harm himself thereby.

(4) “For he does not harm himself” — see the Magen Avraham, who wrote that we rely on this reasoning and believe him specifically regarding a tallis with its tzitzis, because it is not the way of a gentile to attach tzitzis to his garment; but to buy tzitzis is forbidden even from a merchant, lest the spinning and plying were not lishmah — and so ruled the Taz and Dagul MeRevavah; and some are lenient in this. See Biur Halacha and Yeshuot Yaakov, who agreed that where the merchant’s business is to buy tzitzis from a Jew and sell them, it is permitted to buy from the merchant.

(5) Invalid — lest he himself made the tzitzis on the garment; and all the more it is forbidden to buy tzitzis from him, lest they were not spun lishmah. If so, it is obvious that even if he wishes to remove them from the garment and tie them anew, it is forbidden [Magen Avraham and other later authorities, not like the Taz]. If one finds a fringed tallis in the market, it is valid. It is forbidden to send tzitzis, even cut and plied, by a gentile’s hand unless with two seals.
