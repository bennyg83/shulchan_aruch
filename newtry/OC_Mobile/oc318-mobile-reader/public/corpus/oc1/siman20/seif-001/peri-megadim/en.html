A tallis. See Taz. The conclusion of his words is that tzitzis strings without a tallis, when purchased from a merchant, are invalid if his entire merchandise and profit are in tzitzis. If, however, he only occasionally sells tzitzis strings and his main merchandise is clothing, it appears that the strings are valid even when purchased from a merchant. A tallis is certainly valid. This resolves Magen Avraham's question in letter 1 that the profit is small. Even a small profit is sufficient for a merchant when his principal merchandise consists of talleisim. With a non-Jew who is not a merchant, the law is the reverse: a tallis is invalid, while tzitzis strings alone are valid, like tefillin in siman 39, seif 6. The phrase “even from a non-Jewish merchant” means even though he is a merchant, but from one who is not a merchant the strings are valid. The phrase “also tzitzis strings alone” means that tzitzis, when sold independently, are comparable to tefillin in this respect, for we attribute them to the most common source and assume that he purchased them from a Jew. One may object that tzitzis strings are not truly comparable to tefillin. Below, at the beginning of siman 39, and in Semag, as well as in Magen Avraham to siman 11, seif katan 1, and here in letter 5, it is explained that a non-Jew cannot write tefillin. Tzitzis strings, by contrast, may have been spun, twisted, and cut by the non-Jew himself, and he may be skilled in doing so. See Magen Avraham.
Know. Bach and Perishah disagree regarding the prohibition against selling a fringed tallis to a non-Jew. Bach maintains that it may be sold to a merchant, while Perishah explicitly writes that even sale to a merchant is forbidden. Perishah writes that, nevertheless, perhaps a Jew transgressed and sold it to him. He then concludes that a merchant is not suspected of these concerns. This appears to be an additional reason, and perhaps he agrees with Bach. Magen Avraham writes in letter 2 that it is forbidden to sell even to a merchant, and that we merely assume a Jew transgressed and sold it to him. This will be explained there, with Hashem's help. In any event, the baraisa's wording, “a fringed tallis,” creates no difficulty. Either we say that a Jew transgressed and sold it, or that sale to a merchant is permitted. Eliyah Rabbah in letter 1 was already troubled by this. Taz may have spoken according to Bach's approach, understanding that the Shulchan Aruch's wording in seif 2, “one may not sell to a non-Jew,” excludes a non-Jewish merchant, to whom sale is permitted. If so, the Shulchan Aruch is difficult: why did he not state the greater novelty that tzitzis strings alone may be purchased from a merchant? Rambam likewise writes in chapter 2 of Hilchos Tzitzis, halachah 7, that one who purchases a fringed tallis from a non-Jewish merchant may use it. He deliberately omits the prohibition against selling there and records it only in chapter 3, halachah 9. If Eliyah Rabbah were correct, Rambam should have written here, “although, etc.” Bach writes that when purchased from a Jew, the article retains its presumption. If the Jew is ordinarily observant, the tzitzis are valid; if he is suspected, they are invalid. Derishah writes that the novelty is that with techeiles we do not suspect that it is merely indigo dye. Although one may purchase it only from an expert, as Menachos 42b states, most people found in this area are experts. See Tosafos there. If one finds a fringed tallis in the marketplace, it is valid. Olas Tamid and Eliyah Rabbah. This is obvious. See what is written in Magen Avraham concerning this.
The rule that one may not sell a tallis to a non-Jew until he removes its tzitzis. Were this not stated, one might think that it is entirely forbidden to sell the garment because doing so removes it from the mitzvah of tzitzis. The teaching informs us that once he removes the tzitzis, he may sell it. See what is written in Magen Avraham concerning this, that Rabbeinu Yerucham uses “unless” where the baraisa uses “until”; see there. Nevertheless, because the ruling is stated without qualification, it implies that even to a non-Jew who is not a merchant one may sell the garment after removing the tzitzis. This is so even according to Magen Avraham in letter 2. One should not say that the permission applies specifically to a merchant who will sell it to a Jew, and that the Jewish purchaser will then attach tzitzis.
