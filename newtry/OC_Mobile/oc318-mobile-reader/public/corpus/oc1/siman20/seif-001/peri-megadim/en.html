Tallis — Taz: and what emerges from his words is that tzitzis without a tallis from a merchant is invalid (if all his merchandise and profit are in tzitzis; unlike an occasional case of tzitzis alone, even from a merchant it would appear valid if his merchandise is only clothing — and tallitot are valid) (this resolves the Magen Avraham’s question in letter 1, that small profit helps for a merchant since his main merchandise is tallitot). With a gentile who is not a merchant the law is reversed: tallitot are invalid and tzitzis alone are valid, like tefillin in siman 39, seif 6. And from his words “even with a gentile merchant” — meaning even if he is a merchant; but without a merchant it is valid. And from his words “also tzitzis alone” like tefillin — meaning tzitzis are the same (if they are alone) as tefillin in this, that we rely on what is common and presumably they were taken from a Jew. Now one could say there is no comparison of tzitzis alone to tefillin — below in Shulchan Aruch, Semag, and siman 11 Magen Avraham letter 1, and here letter 5, that a gentile cannot write tefillin, unlike tzitzis. Eruvin 96 raises: perhaps the corner of a cloak in cut pieces; and if found in a gentile’s hand one could say he spun, plied, and cut them and knows this well — see Magen Avraham.

And know that the Bach and Prisha disagree on whether it is forbidden to sell a fringed tallis to a gentile: the Bach is lenient that to a merchant it is permitted, and the Prisha wrote explicitly that it is forbidden to a merchant — for he wrote that if so, perhaps a Jew transgressed and sold it to him — but he concluded that the reason a merchant is not in these suspicions appears that he is like “and more,” as the Bach. But the Magen Avraham wrote in letter 2 that it is forbidden to sell to a merchant; rather we say a Jew transgressed and sold to him — and that matter will be explained below. These show, however it may be, there is difficulty from the baraita that teaches “a fringed tallis,” or to say that even so we say a Jew transgressed and sold it, or that to a merchant it is permitted — and this was already questioned by the Eshel Avraham, letter 1. The Taz can possibly follow the Bach’s view, who said that wherever Shulchan Aruch, seif 2, says one may not sell to a gentile — to a gentile merchant it is permitted (and since it did not teach us the novelty of tzitzis alone permitted from a merchant, etc.). If so, on Shulchan Aruch it is difficult why it did not state the novelty that tzitzis alone are permitted from a merchant — and this is what the Rabbi Moshe (Peri Megadim) wrote, chapter 2 of Tzitzis: “One who buys a fringed tallis from a gentile merchant is valid,” and the law that one may not sell they omitted intentionally here, and wrote in chapter 3 of Tzitzis letter 9: “And if per the Eshel Avraham it should have said here ‘even though,’ etc.” The Bach wrote: from a Jew, the Rema — in its presumption as it is in the owner’s hand, if unspecified it is valid; and if the Jew is suspect, the tzitzis are invalid. And the Drisha wrote that it teaches us tekhelet — we are not concerned lest it is kela ilan, etc.; and even though one buys only from an expert (there Menachot 42b), most of those found are experts — see Tosafot there. If one finds a fringed tallis in the market it is valid — Eshel Avraham, and it is simple — see Magen Avraham from this.

This that we say one may not sell a tallis to a gentile until he removes its tzitzis — lest one say it is forbidden entirely to sell, lest the tallis be nullified from the mitzvah of tzitzis; it teaches that until he removes them and from then on he may sell. See Magen Avraham from this that Rabbeinu Yerucham holds only unless in a place of the word “until,” etc. — see there. However, since it states plainly, it implies even to a gentile who is not a merchant — if he removed the tzitzis he may sell. And even the Magen Avraham in letter 2, and the Taz — specifically a merchant who sells to a Jew and the purchasing Jew will attach tzitzis to it.
