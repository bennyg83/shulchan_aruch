[1] A tallit with tzitzit attached, etc. The Taz wrote that specifically a tallit with tzitzit attached, but tzitzit without a tallit are invalid—and it is not clear; also his reason is forced—see there. And so I found in Perishah that even one who buys tzitzit from a merchant is valid. And what the Taz deduced, that they did not list tzitzit without a tallit—I already challenged this in Eliyah Zuta against him, for in Menachos page 43 it says: buying from a merchant is valid, even though they said a person may not sell a tallit with tzitzit to Kutim, etc. And if so, it states a novelty regarding tzitzit—that obviously they are valid, for it is possible the merchant bought them from a Jew; but even a tallit with tzitzit, which is forbidden for a Jew to sell—I might have said that certainly the merchant made it—it teaches us that even so it is valid. One can also say that it teaches us that even though there are many concerns—spinning, plying, and making the tzitzit on the tallit, as we established above in siman 15 that it is invalid when Kutim made them—even so it is valid here. Again, the book Magen Avraham was printed and he too deduced like the Taz that tzitzit are invalid; and in what I wrote his words are rejected. And what he challenged—who permits buying from a Kutite merchant when he says it is kosher—can be reconciled.
