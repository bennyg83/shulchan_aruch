[1] A fringed tallis, etc. Taz writes that this applies specifically to a fringed tallis, but tzitzis strings without a tallis are invalid when purchased from a merchant. This is unconvincing, and his reason is strained; see there. I likewise found in Perishah that even tzitzis strings purchased from a merchant are valid. Taz infers his ruling from the fact that the sources did not state the law concerning tzitzis without a tallis. I already challenged this in Eliyah Zuta. Menachos 43a says: “If one purchased from a merchant, it is valid,” although the Sages said that one may not sell a fringed tallis to a non-Jew. The teaching therefore states the greater novelty. Tzitzis strings are plainly valid because the merchant may have purchased them from a Jew. Even a fringed tallis is valid, despite the prohibition against a Jew selling it to a non-Jew. One might otherwise have assumed that the merchant himself certainly made it; the teaching informs us that it is nevertheless valid. Alternatively, it teaches that the tallis is valid despite several concerns: the spinning, twisting, and attachment of the tzitzis to the garment, all of which are invalid when performed by a non-Jew, as ruled above in siman 15. Magen Avraham was later published, and he too inferred like Taz that tzitzis strings alone are invalid. What I have written refutes his inference. His question, how it is permitted to purchase from a non-Jewish merchant when he claims it is valid, can be resolved.
