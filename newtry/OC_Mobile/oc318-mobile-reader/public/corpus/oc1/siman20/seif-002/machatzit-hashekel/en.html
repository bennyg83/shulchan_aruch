Se’if katan 2: “One may not, etc.” The Bach writes, etc. Regarding “and Rashi explains,” etc.: according to the plain meaning of the baraita, one could explain as the Bach does; “one who buys, etc., from a merchant, it is valid, even though the merchant says, etc., that he obtained it from a non-Jew.” If so, however, the merchant would apparently be lying. This is not so: specifically one who is not a merchant may not sell it, but it is permitted to sell it to a merchant, for the merchant certainly purchases it in order to resell it and not to keep it for himself. One cannot answer that perhaps the non-Jew will kill him, rather than that this is prohibited because of a prostitute, as the Magen Avraham writes in the following se’if katan. Rashi’s commentary, however, proves that Rashi does not accept the Bach’s explanation.
