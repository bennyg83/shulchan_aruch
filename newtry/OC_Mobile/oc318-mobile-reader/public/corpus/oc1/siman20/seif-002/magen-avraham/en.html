“They do not sell.” The Bach wrote it is permitted to sell to a merchant. This needs study: in the Gemara we say one who buys a tallis from a merchant—it is valid even though they said one may not sell a fringed tallis to a gentile; and Rashi explained: even so we say regarding a Jewish merchant “he sold it to him”—if so, Rashi should have explained “even though,” etc., “even so it is permitted to sell to a merchant”; rather Rashi must hold it is forbidden to sell to a merchant as well, for he too is suspected of theft and dishonest trade; but if the merchant sells a tallis and says he bought it from a Jew he is believed, for he does not ruin his reputation.
“Lest he accompany him.” The Gemara gives another reason: because of a prostitute. Rashi explained: that he give it to a prostitute as her fee and people will say Jews gave it to her—and this is the Midrash Yalkut’s explanation: “to their generations, a generation innocent of theft, innocent of illicit relations, innocent of bloodshed.” It is astounding: what does that have to do here with tzitzis, to say they are innocent of these three sins? Rather it must mean this: that they should make tzitzis on the corners of their garments and not on gentiles’ garments. It is difficult: why, then, did [the Midrash] say “to their generations, a generation innocent of theft,” etc.—but gentiles are suspected on these, and there is concern lest one accompany him on the way and he kill him, or rob him, or because of a prostitute.
