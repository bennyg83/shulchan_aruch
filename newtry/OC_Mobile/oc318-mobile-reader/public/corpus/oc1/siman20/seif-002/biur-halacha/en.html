"Lest he accompany him"—See in the Mishna Berurah; and see in Biur HaGra, who wrote that therefore it is forbidden even to pledge with a non-Jew, because we cite stringently for this reason of "lest he accompany him," etc.; for the other reason, because of a prostitute, it would be permitted. And this means: how would he give it to a prostitute, since the tallit is not his? [And therefore, according to what Chavos Da'as wrote—that for us nowadays we are not concerned for the reason of "lest he accompany him," as the Mishna Berurah wrote in his name—from this it is automatically permitted to pledge; and this was concealed from Chavos Da'as—see there, who is stringent in this.] And see Nimmukei Yosef in haktuvah, which implies the opposite; and it is difficult regarding the Nimmukei Yosef's view in what he wrote "and therefore," etc.—see there. And perhaps his intent is that for a Gentile woman it is forbidden to hand over for safekeeping for this reason; but from the Beit Yosef's words that he brought, it implies that even to a Gentile man it is forbidden.
