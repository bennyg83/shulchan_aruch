Lest he accompany him. See Mishnah Berurah and Biur HaGra, who writes that for this reason it is forbidden even to give it as collateral to a non-Jew, because we rule stringently in accordance with the concern that the non-Jew may accompany him. According to the other reason, involving a prostitute, giving it as collateral would be permitted, for how could the non-Jew hand over as his own an item that does not belong to him? Accordingly, based upon Chayei Adam's statement that nowadays we no longer concern ourselves with the reason that he may accompany him, as cited in Mishnah Berurah, giving it as collateral should be permitted. Chayei Adam overlooked this, for he is stringent there; see there. See Nimukei Yosef in Hilchos Tzitzis, whose implication is the opposite. His reasoning in the passage beginning “Accordingly” requires great study. Perhaps he means that because of this concern one may not entrust it to a non-Jewish woman for safekeeping. Beit Yosef's quotation, however, implies that it is forbidden even to entrust it to a non-Jewish man.
