Or from a Gentile merchant—it is kosher, etc. And specifically one who buys a tallit with tzitzit from a Gentile merchant is kosher; but tzitzit are forbidden to buy from a Gentile, even if he is a merchant. Taz in Kuntres Acharon, Magen Avraham se'if kaf alef, Yad Aharon in Hagahot Taz, R' Zalman sign 2, Peri Megadim in Mishbetzot Zahav sign 1, and in Eshel Avraham sign 1, Nachalat Shiv'ah sign 1, Sh'tilei Zetim sign 2; and these are his words: the Ramban in Milchamot, chapter 1 of Sukkah—that one who buys tzitzit from a Gentile merchant is suspect, and they are not bought except from an expert colleague, end quote. See there. And Aruch HaShulchan brought this in sign 1; however, we already wrote in the previous sign in the name of the Bach, who wrote in the name of the Tur, that an ordinary Jew is presumed fit, and so writes the Shach in Yoreh De'ah siman 119 sign 1; and so in Sefer Zivchei Tzedek there sign 2 in the name of several poskim—see there; and in sign 4 he ruled thus as halacha, and not like the Gaon who brought the Mordechai in his gloss there, seif 1.

B) And what he wrote—the Taz there, that it is permitted to buy tzitzit from a Gentile who is not a merchant incidentally, as with tefillin in siman 38; and Eliyah Rabbah brought this in sign 4—they already disputed him: Peri Megadim there, Ma'amar Mordechai sign 1, and Nachalat Shiv'ah there, that tefillin are different, for we say it is not a Gentile's way to write—as one writes—see there in Beit Yosef; not so here. And they wrote that this is also implied from Magen Avraham se'if kaf alef—see there.
