To a non-Jew. It appears that this applies even to a non-Jewish merchant, since the baraisa did not specify a non-Jew who is not a merchant. Bach, however, writes that it applies specifically to a non-Jew who is not a merchant, but one may sell to a non-Jewish merchant. Magen Avraham challenges him from Rashi; see there. I say that there is no proof from Rashi, for who says that this is his interpretation? The Gemara may mean as follows: if it was purchased from a merchant, meaning from a non-Jew, it is valid. Although the Sages said that one may not sell a fringed tallis to a non-Jew, it is nevertheless permitted initially to sell a fringed tallis to a non-Jewish merchant, because as a merchant there is no concern. See Yad Aharon. Magen Avraham in siman 15, seif katan 2, writes that this proves one may convert a four-cornered tallis into another type of garment that does not have four corners, since the baraisa permits selling the tallis to a non-Jew after removing the tzitzis. If, however, he sells the tallis to another Jew, it is forbidden to remove the tzitzis unless he intends to attach them to another tallis; see there. Sending tzitzis through a non-Jew requires two seals, specifically where there is concern that he benefits from exchanging them. It is possible, however, that if the strings are already twisted and cut, it is permitted. Magen Avraham. If one finds a fringed tallis or tzitzis in the marketplace, they may be considered valid nowadays. Olas Tamid. See Beit Yosef. The Baer Heitev edition before me copied in the name of Olas Tamid that one should be stringent; see there. This is incorrect.
