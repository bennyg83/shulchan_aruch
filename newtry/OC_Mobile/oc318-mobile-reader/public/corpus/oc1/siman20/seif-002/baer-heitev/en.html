To a non-Jew. It appears that even to a non-Jewish merchant—from the fact that the baraita does not specify a non-Jew who is not a merchant. But the Bach wrote that specifically to a non-Jew who is not a merchant; but to a Gentile merchant they may sell. And the Magen Avraham challenged him from Rashi's explanation there. And I say that Rashi's explanation is no proof, for from where do we know his explanation? One could say that such is the Gemara's intent: from the merchant, from the Gentile—it is valid. And even though they said one may not sell a tallit with tzitzit to a Gentile, nevertheless to a Gentile merchant it is permitted to sell him a tallit with tzitzit ab initio, since he is a merchant there is nothing to fear. And see Yad Aharon. The Magen Avraham wrote in siman 15, se'if kaf bet, it is proven from here that it is permitted to make from the tallit, which is a four-cornered garment, another garment so that it should not have four corners—from the fact that it is permitted to sell the tallit to a non-Jew when he removes the tzitzit. But if he sells a tallit to his fellow, it is forbidden to remove the tzitzit unless he intends to attach them to another tallit—there. To send tzitzit through a non-Jew requires two seals. And specifically if there is concern that he benefits from the exchange; and it is possible that if they are plied and cut apart it is permitted—Magen Avraham. If one finds a tallit with tzitzit or tzitzit in the market, it may be validated in our time—Olah Tamid; and see Beit Yosef and the Be'er Heitev before me, who translated from there regarding tekhelet that one should be stringent—there—and it is not so.
