2) There: They recite an abbreviated Birkas HaMazon, etc. Sema writes in Choshen Mishpat, siman 337, that they likewise need not wait for the others until they make a Zimun; see there. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1, however, writes that they are obligated to wait for Zimun; see there. In any event, the present practice follows seif 2 of the Shulchan Aruch: they recite all four blessings and also recite the Harachaman passages through the conclusion, 'He Who makes peace in His heights,' etc. Consequently, they are also obligated to make a Zimun when there are three or ten. Anyone who hires laborers presumably hires them with the established custom in mind, including that they make a Zimun, recite all four blessings, and recite the Harachaman passages through the conclusion, 'He Who makes peace,' etc. This is the common practice.
