[Levush] It is not written, etc. Maadanei Melech, folio 16, writes that, with respect, the Levush did not need this explanation because we rule that the Sages have authority to uproot a Torah obligation by requiring passive nonperformance. Malbushei Yom Tov writes likewise. In my humble opinion the Levush's intent is to explain why the Sages had authority to omit Boneh Yerushalayim because of Birkat HaAretz rather than the reverse. This is why he concludes that they did not permit including Birkat HaAretz within Boneh Yerushalayim. Moreover, if they had intended actually to abolish a Torah blessing, they could have instituted only one blessing and included the other two within it. Note this well.
[Levush] And they did not permit, etc. Maadanei Melech explains that reason dictates including the later blessing in the earlier one. I think the Levush means that there was still no necessity to require specifically that it be included in Birkat HaAretz. They could have allowed Birkat HaAretz to be included in Boneh Yerushalayim, since Birkat HaAretz precedes Boneh Yerushalayim only because the gift of the land occurred earlier in time. Moreover, at present the need for Boneh Yerushalayim is even greater than the need for Birkat HaAretz.
[Levush] Because it burdens him, etc. Above in siman 63 the Levush gives the reason that Keriat Shema should be recited in a casual manner. One must say that regarding Keriat Shema the verse says 'and you shall speak of them,' meaning make them fixed, whereas in the synagogue the consideration is different. This requires study. Perhaps the two reasons are really one. Olat Tamid writes that this restriction appears to be initial only, but after the fact one fulfills the obligation, no worse than an intoxicated person discussed above. This creates some difficulty. If so, why did the Sages uproot the Torah blessing Boneh Yerushalayim for workers occupied with the employer's labor, as Tosafot on folio 16 explains? It would have been more appropriate for them to bless while working, since after the fact that is valid. Magen Avraham writes that even light work is forbidden during the blessing. The Taz writes that one should not engage in Torah study at that time. The same applies not only to Birkat HaMazon but also to all other blessings and prayer. Even while performing one mitzvah, it is improper to engage simultaneously in another mitzvah. This is the meaning of the verse, 'If you walk with Me casually,' namely in an incidental manner.
