It is forbidden to do melacha — Taz on Berachot daf 16a, and there they said in the second chapter: they engage in their work and recite kri'at shema; and so too Rambam z"l in chapter 2 of Laws of Kri'at Shema law 4, that even an am ha'aretz likewise; and the Mechaber repeated this in siman 63 seif 7, that even an am ha'aretz likewise; and it requires study. And even if we say the first paragraph of divrei Torah is separate — nevertheless, why should rabbinic mitzvot be less? And it is possible that Torah study is considered permitted, and one may engage in his work at the time of Torah study; and specifically when engaging in a mitzvah alone apart from divrei Torah it is forbidden to engage in melacha. And also when blessing birkat hamazon it is forbidden to engage and look in divrei Torah; and one engaging in this mitzvah, it is not proper to engage in another. And this is when there is time to do afterward — but if not, and one has ability to do both, one does both.

Levush wrote that the Sages were concerned: if they prolong birkat hamazon, the employer will be pressed and they will not have intent for it, etc. — it would appear that if not for nullification of the employer's work they would not have uprooted divrei Torah (for one who holds three berachot d'oraisa), if not that there is a boundary to divrei Torah; but from the wording of Tur and the Mechaber, "so as not to cancel the employer's work," it does not imply thus. And see Yevamot daf 90: "There is power in the hand of Chazal to uproot a matter from the Torah" in She'elot Uteshuvot — see there. But if so, what does the Yerushalmi prove, etc. — perhaps if they all bless, the employer will be pressed; and therefore it is good that they abbreviate; and it requires study. And in Magen Avraham, if Heaven wills, I will explain further regarding whether it is like a three-berachah summary of divrei Torah or not; also regarding the berachah before it; and in siman 194 in Magen Avraham note 3 from this; and here there is no need to elaborate. Regarding "walk with Me casually" in Sefer HaMagid — we wrote that it is said twice in kri'at shema: the tosefet yud in the kri, and "casual" of this world — they too make a mitzvah more serious than casual; understand this.
