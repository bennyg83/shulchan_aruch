(s.k. 1) They abbreviate, etc. And it appears to me the beginning of Tur's words there, that Tur there warns the worker not to cease even a moment from the employer's work. And he brought proof as follows: for the Sages were strict in zimun regarding the fourth berachah — meaning they exempted workers from the fourth berachah so they would not cease from the employer's work; if so, they too should warn not to cease. And Shach Me'ubad challenged the word "in zimun" that Tur wrote; therefore Shach Me'ubad holds Tur says two things: they exempted workers from zimun, for one need not wait for others until they invite him, and also exempted them from the fourth berachah. But Magen Avraham holds he says one thing: they exempted them from the fourth berachah, and what he took "in zimun" — even though in the baraita it is explicit in Berachot daf 16a "workers, etc., eat, etc., first berachah as established, etc.," as Shulchan Aruch wrote here — nevertheless one can reject, for since they are preoccupied with the employer's work, Chazal exempted them from the berachah of HaTov VeHaMetiv; but if they wished to be stringent and bless, the permission is in their hands. And therefore he brought proof from Abbaye on daf 45b, who answered Amen in a loud voice so that workers would stand — and if it were optional, why would Abbaye do so, whether they bless HaTov VeHaMetiv or not? And if to inform them they are exempt — nevertheless, since if they bless they do not act properly, Abbaye would not put himself at risk to answer in a loud voice.

Also the wording "so that workers stand" implies they need to rise from their work. And since Abbaye's case was in zimun, therefore Tur also took the wording "in zimun."

And necessarily he speaks where he was inviting them, for if they were blessing separately, what would Abbaye's answering Amen in a loud voice help, that they not bless HaTov VeHaMetiv — perhaps they had already blessed, or were standing in the middle of a berachah? Or that they fulfilled through Abbaye's berachah and heard birkat hamazon from his mouth — and therefore he answered in a loud voice when he reached HaTov VeHaMetiv, so they would not need more and would go to their work.

Tosafot wrote: even though they are d'oraisa — if so, why did they institute to combine the berachah of the land and Boneh Yerushalayim in one berachah? Behold from the Torah they are two berachot, as the Gemara derives from a verse on daf 48b.

For there is no count of berachot from the Torah, and one berachah suffices. And these verses on daf 48 are merely an asmachta.

And it appears to me, etc. — it is not meaning that one must conclude each separately, etc.; rather meaning that the three d'oraisa berachot that one must mention in birkat hamazon are these three matters: sustenance, land, and building Jerusalem — but each one does not require a separate berachah from the Torah, and it is permitted to combine them in one berachah.

Behold, Magen Avraham's version is reasonable. But what he understood thus from Rashi's words — I do not know how Rashi intended this, for even according to Tosafot or Rabbeinu Yonah one can explain Rashi's words.

And so too in the Gemara on daf 46 regarding all three d'oraisa berachot that it says there — until where is the berachah of zimun? Rav Nachman said until "Let us bless." And Rabbah bar bar Chanah said until "Who feeds." Let us say it is like the tannaim, for one baraita teaches in birkat hamazon two and three, and another baraita teaches three and four — they all hold HaTov VeHaMetiv is not d'oraisa (meaning, and it is not counted since it is not d'oraisa). What, do they not dispute about this? The one who says two and three holds until "Who feeds," and Tosafot explain in name of Rif that the baraita's intent is that sometimes birkat hamazon is blessed through three people — such as when each knows only one berachah; and since the berachah of zimun is until "Who feeds," there are only three berachot even when they bless the berachah of zimun. And therefore you do not find more than three people; but one berachah split between two people was not permitted. And sometimes with two — that is, when they know — and the same with four.

And the one who says three and four holds until "Let us bless." And if so, you find that four people bless birkat hamazon, since until "Let us bless" is the end of the berachah of zimun. And if so, when they invite, there are four berachot in birkat hamazon as the Gemara concludes now.

And one cannot say according to Rabbeinu Yonah that the three berachot are not d'oraisa — what deficiency is there in HaTov VeHaMetiv that it is not counted, because it is not d'oraisa? Behold, all of them are not d'oraisa; even though the three berachot have support from the Torah, unlike HaTov VeHaMetiv. Nevertheless, the Gemara's wording that says "they all hold HaTov VeHaMetiv is not d'oraisa" implies that this is the cause that it is not counted — because it is not d'oraisa, and not because it lacks support from the Torah.
