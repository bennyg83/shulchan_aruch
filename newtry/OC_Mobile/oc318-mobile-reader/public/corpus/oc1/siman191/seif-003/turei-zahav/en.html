It is forbidden to do melacha, etc. The Yerushalmi proves this from what it said that workers should bless only two on account of nullification of the employer's work — why should they not bless the last two while they engage in their work? Rabbi Muna said: this teaches that it is forbidden to do melacha until one finishes blessing. I copied this so you should not err to say specifically at the time of birkat hamazon they forbade doing melacha, and not in other berachot or tefillah; but certainly also in all matters of mitzvah one should not engage in other conversation. And they did not mention this here in birkat hamazon except to resolve a difficulty; but certainly in all mitzvot one should not perform them and engage in another matter, for this indicates he performs the mitzvah without intent but in a happenstance and casual manner; and this is included in the statement of our Torah "And if you walk with Me casually," meaning that even though you walk with Me, that is, perform the mitzvah, nevertheless it is in a happenstance and casual manner. And it appears to me that included in this prohibition is also not to engage in Torah at that hour, for this too causes birkat hamazon to be on the side of happenstance and chance; and even if one engages in this mitzvah and engages in another mitzvah with it, it is not proper, for one nullifies the other; so it appears to me.
