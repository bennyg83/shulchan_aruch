[16] By means of a loaf, etc. But not by means of a ring, because perhaps the ring is not worth as much as the purse. A child is different, because he is inherently significant even if he is not worth as much as the loaf. This is explicit in Sefer HaTerumot, the Bach, and Magen Avraham. Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah understood the cases as equal, but this is incorrect.
[17] From an eruv, etc. Sacred books may be rescued even into a courtyard without an eruv and into an alleyway. Rabbi Moshe Rivkash and the later authorities emended the Shulchan Aruch accordingly. The Levush appears to understand that 'provided that, etc.' refers back to the inference from the previous clause. This is easy to understand. The qualification applies even if he wishes to study from the books that same Shabbos.
