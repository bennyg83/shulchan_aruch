(א) (s.k. 14) And some are lenient, etc. — likewise utensils of use — meaning spoon, knife and bowl — things that are not garments:

(ב) And so in Semag — food and vessels, etc. — not as Shulchan Gavoah wrote and left it as a difficulty — in daf 122 we learned where does one save — to the mixed courtyard Ben Beteira says even to one that is not mixed; and to there he takes out all his utensils of use and wears all he can to wear, etc.; and Semag wrote food and vessels one saves only to the mixed courtyard; and afterward wrote garments one saves even to a courtyard that is not mixed; and Shulchan Gavoah understood vessels mentioned in the beginning means even garments — therefore he challenged three difficulties: one — in the beginning it teaches vessels meaning garments one saves only to mixed; and afterward wrote garments one saves even to not mixed; two — what reason to distinguish and be more lenient in garments and vessels to save even to not mixed more than food; three — from Mishnah language it implies no difference between food and vessels and garments — for after dispute of first Tanna and Ben Beteira it teaches "and to there he takes out all his utensils of use" — implies from "and to there," etc., implies for each as he said — for first Tanna specifically to mixed and Ben Beteira even to not mixed; and also on this "and he wears all he can," etc. — implies all things equal regarding saving — until here Shulchan Gavoah; and Magen Avraham came to settle the explanation is not thus — rather what Semag wrote in the beginning food and vessels one saves only to mixed — what he wrote vessels means utensils of use; and in the end it teaches explicitly garments and clothing they are permitted even to not mixed — and it turns out Semag's words do not contradict each other; and what was challenged to Shulchan Gavoah there is no reason to distinguish — Magen Avraham also wrote the reason since it is manner of wearing, etc.; unlike food and utensils of use; and what he further wrote Mishnah language does not imply thus — Magen Avraham also wrote thus is Mishnah's intent — "and to there" means for each as he said — for first Tanna specifically to mixed and Ben Beteira even to not mixed — takes out all utensils of use meaning such as knife and bowl; and afterward teaches "and he wears all he can," etc. — this is a matter on its own and does not refer to the beginning — for this wearing permitted for all even to not mixed since it is manner of wearing; and Mishnah closed what is obvious since it is manner of wearing permitted, etc., even to not mixed; and it is possible even according to Rabbenu Chananel:

(ג) And it appears to me not in the manner, etc. — not as Bach wrote regarding a ring, etc. — meaning Bach challenged Ba'al HaTerumah who wrote permitted to place tefillin into a purse with money in order to save the money via the tefillin on account of fire (and this is what is mentioned in Shulchan Aruch seif 15 — only Ba'al HaTerumah mentioned only on account of fire and Rav Beit Yosef in Shulchan Aruch added or on account of thieves and robbers) — but forbidden to place a ring into a purse with money to save on account of fire since perhaps the ring is nullified relative to money which is more important; and Bach challenged — since in the beginning required to save money to place tefillin with it — perforce deals with a courtyard that is not mixed (or in a house where the fire is according to Bach who brought Magen Avraham s.k. 1) — for if to mixed courtyard (and house where there is no fire) why need tefillin — for even money alone permitted to save on account of fire and coercion according to those who permit in seif 1; and Ba'al HaTerumah is from those who permit; and since the beginning perforce deals with a courtyard that is not mixed — if so the end too placing a ring to the money also deals thus to a courtyard that is not mixed; and furthermore if for mixed also in the end difficult — why need a ring — behold money itself permitted according to Ba'al HaTerumah as stated; and since it deals with not mixed — behold even the ring itself not permitted to save except to mixed; and why say perhaps the ring is nullified relative to money; and one can say forever both beginning and end deal with a courtyard that is not mixed — and nevertheless permitted to save there a ring since a ring is like a garment — permitted like law of garments permitted even to not mixed; and on this Magen Avraham distinguished — what does a ring help — it has law of a garment — nevertheless since he places it into the purse with money inside and does not place it on his finger — it is not in the manner of wearing and forbidden to not mixed; and what Asher HaTerumah did not see, etc. — meaning to resolve Bach's difficulty on Sefer HaTerumah — Magen Avraham also wrote and did not see, etc. — brought in Rabbenu Yehuda this section — beginning of Ba'al HaTerumah's words to permit saving money from coercion and afterward appears from his words to permit also saving money from fire; and he concluded even if also forbidden on account of fire to save his money without any holy thing with them in a pouch lest bandits come to extinguish — but on account of rulers and fear of robbers of money permitted since there is not so much for d'oraisa prohibition, etc.; and Rabbenu Yehuda wrote on this, and this is his language: it appears from his words that to save on account of fire it is not straightforward to him thus to permit moving muktzeh object — end of his words; and even though afterward Rabbenu Yehuda wrote from Ran parsha One For Whom It Became Dark who wrote in name of Tosafos and Ba'al HaTerumah permitted to move money on account of bandits or fire — he was not so precise — for in name of Ba'al HaTerumah he wrote first to permit on account of coercion; but on account of fire he wrote in name of Tosafos — see there; and according to this Ba'al HaTerumah's words here are not clear — for also a ring forever both in beginning and end deals with mixed courtyard; and that tefillin and ring were needed to save plain money on account of fire was not clear to him; and in order the permission be clear he gave an enactment via tefillin; but a ring — no; and what Shulchan Aruch wrote this section to permit moving money also on account of fire — this is not Ba'al HaTerumah's view but Tosafos' view;
