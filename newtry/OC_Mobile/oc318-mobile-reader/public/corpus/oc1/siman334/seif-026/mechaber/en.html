He may say before a non-Jew: whoever extinguishes does not lose, even if he is not present here — he may summon him to come even though he will certainly extinguish when he comes; and likewise all similar cases of sudden damage — such as if a wine barrel cracked, he may summon a non-Jew even though he will certainly repair it when he comes:And all the laws mentioned in the laws of fire — these matters are in their days; but in our time, those who dwell among non-Jews and there is concern of danger to life — the early and later authorities, may their memory be for a blessing, wrote that it is permitted to extinguish their fires on Shabbat because there is danger to life; and one who is zealous — behold he is praiseworthy; nevertheless everything is per the matter: if they were certain with certainty that there would be no danger in the matter — forbidden to extinguish; but with concern of possible danger — permitted to extinguish even a fire in a non-Jew's house, and so is the practice [Terumat HaDeshen siman 58 and Hagahot Ashiri chapter Mi sheHotziuhu in the name of Or Zarua]; and specifically to extinguish the fire, which is melacha she'einah tzerichah legufah and there is danger if he does not extinguish; but forbidden to desecrate Shabbat in order] to save [property]; and if he transgressed and desecrated — he must fast forty days on Monday and Thursday, and not drink wine and not eat meat, and give in place of a chatas 18 pshitim to charity; and if he wishes to redeem the fast he gives for each day 12 pshitim to charity [Piskei Maharai siman 9; and see Tur Yoreh De'ah Laws of Niddah siman 185]:]:
