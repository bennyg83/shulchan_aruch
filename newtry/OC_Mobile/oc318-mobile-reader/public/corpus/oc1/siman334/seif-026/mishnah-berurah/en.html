(א) He may say, etc. — since he does not tell him "extinguish"; and see in Yoreh De'ah siman 221 seif 8 that it implies there that to say in second person "if you extinguish you will not lose" — he is also not permitted, for this is also like telling him to extinguish; but in language of "whoever extinguishes" he speaks generally; and Shelatei Gibborim leans to be lenient in this regarding fire; and see what we wrote above in siman 307 seif 19:

(ב) Before a non-Jew — even if he is his hired servant for a set time; but it is forbidden to tell a non-Jew to extinguish for all, for since a person is agitated over his property we are concerned that if you permit him he will extinguish himself; except regarding holy writings it is permitted to tell a non-Jew to extinguish on account of disgrace to holy writings; and likewise in seif 18 regarding rescue via reshus harabim. Dead person and books — there is room to save both; dead person first. Healthy and dangerously ill — healthy first [Sefer Chasidim].

(ג) Whoever extinguishes, etc. — and they permitted saying this because since a person is agitated over his property, if they do not permit him even in this language he will extinguish himself; and likewise regarding other damage he will repair himself; but regarding other melachot of Shabbat it is forbidden to say "whoever does this melacha does not lose" so non-Jews hear and come [Gemara]:

(ד) Sudden damage — Beit Yosef wrote it is the way of the matter that he mentioned if he had known from the eve he would have repaired beforehand; but the same applies if he knew and delayed until Shabbat — it is also permitted to speak in this language; but the Acharonim did not cite his words, and it implies it is not clear to them:

(ה) He may read to a non-Jew, etc. — in Beit Yosef in name of Maharai Abuhav it is proven that the same applies: permitted to tell him "when you come, whoever repairs does not lose":

(ו) Because there is danger in it — when the fire intensifies they seize and plunder; and when a person places himself on his money they will come to kill him:

(ז) With concern of possible danger, etc. — and from this emerged the permission to extinguish fire in every place, since if he does not extinguish it perhaps there will be lacking in the city an elder or sick person who cannot flee and the fire will come upon him [Eliyah Rabbah in name of Sheyarei Knesset HaGedolah]; and see what we wrote above in s.k. 4:

(ח) Permitted to extinguish — and a mitzvah to inform this publicly that it is permitted to extinguish [Peri Megadim; and likewise in siman 328 seif 2]:

(ט) That it is melacha, etc. — explanation of this above in Eliyah Rabbah in Mishna Berurah s.k. 3:

(י) She'einah tzerichah legufah — meaning and there is one who says he is not liable upon it, as below in seif 27; and in truth regarding extinguishing he mentioned "in any case," for even if extinguishing were melacha tzerichah legufah it would also be permitted since he wrote there is danger if he does not extinguish; but he mentioned it because of the end, to inform us that regarding saving property it is forbidden to desecrate Shabbat even in melacha she'einah tzerichah legufah, for even in a rabbinic prohibition it is forbidden to desecrate Shabbat for saving property [however if he is in the field and there is concern non-Jews will seize him — there is room to lean lenient in this]; and what he wrote "and if he transgressed and desecrated he must," etc. — refers to other matters; but regarding extinguishing he need not repentance so they not refrain from extinguishing:

(כ) He must fast — even in rabbinic techumin [Magen Avraham]; and likewise regarding moving a lit lamp to save property — Dagul MeRevavah also leans one must fast; but it appears to be lenient in this with a three-day fast on a weekday; and perhaps also in techumin where he was stringent to fast forty days — it deals when there was not so much fear and great loss:

(ל) Forty days, etc. — and he can postpone them to winter days [Magen Avraham]; and all this is whether he transgressed a d'oraisa or d'rabbanan prohibition [Eliyah Rabbah]; and it deals when he transgressed inadvertently; and even when he transgressed intentionally it was because he dug a pit to bury money from bandits and the like; but one who transgresses willfully to fulfill his desires needs more and more repentance, for he is liable stoning or at least karet; and therefore he needs complete repentance [Chayei Adam]. One who desecrated Shabbat for pikuach nefesh needs no repentance at all; and what women who light a lamp for a woman in childbirth on Shabbat are accustomed to fast afterward — is folly and foolishness [Nezer Yisrael in name of Zichron Avraham]:

(מ) And he should not eat meat — meaning on the night after the fast [Acharonim]:

(נ) 18 pshitim — it is the calculation of 27 coins [and is approximately five gold coins], for each pshit is large, half a Polish gulden; and each gulden is a ma'ah; and the reason the poskim wrote this measure is because that was the least of sheep or goats at that time; and therefore one must give this measure to charity, for when the Temple stood one was liable to bring a chatas from these species if he did a melacha d'oraisa, and a sinner should not profit; and beware he not say he gives this in place of a chatas, but say that in place of a chatas he gives this to charity [Chayei Adam]; and the books wrote it is proper he recite the parashah of chatas and understand the manner of its offering; and Chazal already said whoever occupies himself with Torah of chatas is as if he offered a chatas:

(ס) 12 pshitim — which is 18 coins; and this is the minimum measure if possible; and the wealthy should give more — all according to his wealth, for the reason for redemption is weighing the pain of money against the pain of fasting [Acharonim]:
