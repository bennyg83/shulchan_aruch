Seif 15: they save the book pouch with the book. Also: behold the source of this law is in tractate Shabbat opening chapter Kol Kitvei (Shabbos daf 117) — see there, in the sugya until "he takes it with tongs," etc. And behold I was asked by one regarding what we learn in Menachos opening chapter "R' Yishmael says," etc., and they said there that R' Yochanan ben Nuri and R' Yishmael said one matter, etc.; and this is similar to this — behold here it was only shvut of taking with tongs, and there by R' Yishmael it was melacha d'oraisa. And I answered: perforce, since according to R' Yishmael from his view he needs to increase dancing more and it is also melacha d'oraisa, and since it is intentional — therefore in general he is liable for each dance and each dance; if so, what difference to me if he does one melacha many times or many melachos at once — they are equal from the Torah; only the distinction is that if he does one melacha many times it does not appear as much Shabbat desecration, but if he does many melachos more than needed at once it appears more as Shabbat desecration and is only mar'it ayin of greater desecration; and the distinction between this and that is only d'rabbanan and is also only shvut d'rabbanan — and they are properly similar to each other and it comes out well. But while I was on this I raised another difficulty: why does the Gemara challenge "but Abaye and Rava concede to R' Shimon in psik reisha," etc. — why does it challenge; behold it is melacha she'einah tzerichah legufah, and perhaps he holds like R' Yehuda who is exempt in melacha she'einah tzerichah legufah — see in Magen Avraham siman 278 in the name of Kesef Mishneh the distinction between melacha she'einah tzerichah legufah and davar she'eino mitkaven; and here behold he does not need to flay but only to interrupt — it is literally melacha she'einah tzerichah legufah, and requires study. Also difficult: why do they compare the book pouch with the book to flaying the hide, since at the time he saves the book he also saves the pouch and it is together — therefore we say "since it was permitted it was permitted," or "since it was pushed aside it was pushed aside" — see chapter 3 of Zevachim and in many places; but here behold at the time he flays, he already flayed until the chest first, and since he flayed until the chest he no longer needs the emurim — how is it permitted to desecrate Shabbat afterward since he no longer needs it for the mitzvah — requires study on this as well; examine well:
