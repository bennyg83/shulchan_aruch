[8] But into his own courtyard, etc. Sefer HaTerumot writes this. The Taz questions it from Shabbos 120, which seems to show that Rav Huna prohibits even the same courtyard, since otherwise his view would be identical to Rabbi Abba's, and we rule like Rav Huna. Yet the Beit Yosef explicitly cites Sefer HaTerumot this way, and Sefer HaTerumot itself, siman 200, is explicit. The Taz apparently did not examine the source, for Sefer HaTerumot rules like Rabbi Abba. This also explains why the Beit Yosef did not separately dispute Sefer HaTerumot concerning one's own courtyard: he already rules like Rav Huna. It appears to me that Sefer HaTerumot reads the Gemara as follows. When saving by one route, one may save even one hundred meals in one trip. When repeatedly folding and returning by several routes, one may save only three meals on each route even into the same courtyard. Rabbi Abba answers that into the same courtyard one may save even one hundred meals by several routes, while into another courtyard the rule depends on whether there is one route or several. Sefer HaTerumot follows Rabbi Abba because this is a rabbinic matter and his view is lenient. The Rif and Rosh follow Rav Huna because according to Rashi Rav Huna is the lenient opinion. Even according to Rashi, it appears that Rav Huna permits into the same courtyard, but he still differs from Rabbi Abba, who prohibits another courtyard even when saving by one route, as Tosafot writes. Examine carefully.
[9] [Levush] What difference does it make whether it is into the courtyard, etc. The Taz writes that in one's own courtyard there is less exertion, so there is less concern that he will become agitated and extinguish. This implies that he permits one's own courtyard as practical law. So too it appears in siman 335. The Shiltei Giborim writes that within one's own house, from room to room, one may save property.
