Ashurit on parchment, etc. This is from Mordekhai ch. 16 of Shabbat; and also in parsha HaKometz so too; and also Tosafos so too; and it is difficult — for behold Tosafos conclude it is a tannaitic dispute, and according to Rav Huna who said wherever they were not given to read in them one does not save — if so, also in books one does not save if they were written in ink and red paint, for according to him the question is not resolved; nevertheless Mordekhai's view is that Rav Huna agrees where they are written in ink and red paint one saves since in any case they are written in the holy language; and so in Mordekhai parsha HaKometz; and so Rambam ch. 23 and Tur; nevertheless requires further study — for behold nowadays it is permitted to write and read in them even written in any language and in ink and red paint, as is written seif 12; even though by law they are invalid to write in them; if so, likewise Megillah and wherever they were given to read in them — straightforward one saves, as the gemara says; and even though it is not fit to read in it in public — nevertheless permitted to learn from it; for behold a sefer Torah too is invalid to read in public unless written Ashurit on parchment and in ink; and nevertheless one saves it if written in any manner since it is permitted to learn from it; therefore it appears straightforward that Mordekhai agrees one saves Megillah even written in any language on paper because they were given to read in them on account of "a time to act for the Heaven," since not every person can write Ashurit and also does not understand the holy language and also has no means to buy parchment; but in ink and red paint they were not given to write even in our time, since one can easily buy ink like ink and red paint; therefore in other books even though written not properly and not given to read in them — one saves them on account of the Divine Names in them; but Megillah forbidden to save if written in ink and red paint since forbidden to read in them; and so brought explicitly in Mordekhai there — for one who examines there — it refers only to ink and red paint; only afterward he brought the language of the braita and its explanation that in Talmudic times it was forbidden to save it unless written properly since they were not given to write and read; but in our time permitted unless written not in ink; and Rabbenu Yehuda did not examine this sufficiently; and what is written in seif 12 "and one reads in them even written, etc., in ink and red paint" — meaning where written in any language and in ink and red paint; but where written in the holy language in ink and red paint — one does not read in them; nevertheless one saves them on account of fire; and all this in the twenty-four books; but other books — straightforward they are written even in ink and red paint:
