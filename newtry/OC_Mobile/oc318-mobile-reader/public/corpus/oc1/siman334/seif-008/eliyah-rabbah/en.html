[8] But to his courtyard, etc. So wrote Sefer HaTerumot; and Taz wondered at him from Shabbos 120b, where it is proven that Rav Huna forbids even in that courtyard — for if not, this would be R' Abba, and we rule like Rav Huna, end of his words. And I wonder why he needed to prove Rav Huna forbids even in that courtyard — behold this is clear in Beit Yosef in name of Sefer HaTerumot; and likewise Sefer HaTerumot wrote siman 200 explicitly; and in truth Taz did not look at the source of the law, for if he had looked he would not have wondered, for Sefer HaTerumot ruled like R' Abba. And with this the wonder is also resolved why Beit Yosef did not divide against Sefer HaTerumot also regarding his own courtyard, for he did not need to divide because he rules like Rav Huna; however it appears to me the reason for their dispute is that Sefer HaTerumot explained another plain meaning in the sugya here — to save when going one path one may save even one hundred meals at once; and here regarding folding, when going many paths one may not save in every manner except three meals, even to that courtyard; and R' Abba answers that to that courtyard one saves in every manner even one hundred meals; and to another courtyard he distinguishes between going one path and many paths. And it appears to me that he ruled like R' Abba because he is lenient, for in a rabbinic matter we go leniently; and Rif and Rosh ruled like Rav Huna because according to Rashi's explanation Rav Huna is lenient; and in truth according to Rashi's explanation it also appears to me that Rav Huna permits in that courtyard; and nevertheless he argues with R' Abba, for he forbids in another courtyard even to save as they wrote Tosafos — requires study:
