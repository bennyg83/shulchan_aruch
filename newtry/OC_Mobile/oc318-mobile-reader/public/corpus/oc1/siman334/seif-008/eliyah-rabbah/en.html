[5] And there is one who says, etc. Above, in siman 301, seif 44, the Shulchan Aruch rules simply like the first view, and this appears primary, for all the authorities I have seen rule so. The Rambam's view cited by the Beit Yosef is a lone opinion. Perhaps even the Rambam agrees. The Beit Yosef inferred otherwise from the Rambam's wording, 'garments that he can wear,' but the Semag and Semak use the same wording and nevertheless rule that one may remove and put them on repeatedly. Perhaps the Rambam speaks only of saving garments by hand, while when one wears them in the normal manner he agrees it is permitted. The Semag and Semak can likewise be understood this way. This also resolves the Shiltei Giborim's question that the Semag contradicts himself regarding a courtyard without an eruv: his prohibition concerns carrying by hand, while wearing is permitted. So too it appears from the Semak.
