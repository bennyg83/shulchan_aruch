Since he said "save for yourselves" — see in Mishna Berurah; and this is from the words of Magen Avraham and Bach; and behold this is obvious according to Taz; and so it is reasonable that if he did not say at all "save for yourselves" it is not hefker automatically, for it is not comparable to the surplus of the sea; and as Bach wrote, it is possible to save via a non-Jew or via Israelites who save and return to the owners; but if he said "come and save" and did not say "for yourselves" — why can they not acquire it? For certainly his intent was not that they save on his account and he will give them payment for their trouble — for this is forbidden, since he already saved food for three meals [and I did not merit to understand Bach's words on this matter]; therefore he said they should save for themselves; and perhaps one can say that in all cases where he does not say "for yourselves," even though they cannot acquire for themselves, nevertheless they do not become agents on his behalf:
