(א) Food for three meals. But more — forbidden (so wrote Shulchan Gavoah):

(ב) Since he said. But if he did not say — it is not hefker, since he can save via a non-Jew as is written siman 327 (Bach); and it appears to me that without statement he can say I would have found Israelite people who would have saved and returned to me:

(ג) And if they do not wish to acquire. Since they know that without his will they declared hefker — this is called yarei shamayim:

(ד) And it is not sachar Shabbat. For behold he did not stipulate, and from hefker they acquired (Rashi and Ran); and Rambam wrote in ch. 23: and this is not sachar Shabbat, for behold there is no melacha and no prohibition, for they did not take out except to a karmelis — end of his words; and Maggid Mishneh wrote: certainly sachar Shabbat is forbidden even in a permitted matter, as is written siman 307 seif 4; rather Rambam's intent is to say that nothing forbidden was permitted here on account of monetary loss so that one should not say it is permitted except to save for one who loses — end of his words; his intent is to what Rashi wrote that the rescuers should say from hefker they acquired the measure of their wages; and certainly if the Sages were permitting a forbidden matter on account of monetary loss it would be forbidden for them to save for themselves at all, as is written siman 266 seif 1; but now that he did no prohibition they are permitted to save on Shabbat on condition to take payment for their wages, and this is not sachar Shabbat, for up to the measure of their wages they acquired from hefker; but certainly if they acted and saved in violation they can also say from hefker we acquired for the measure of our wages; for one who lifted a purse on Shabbat in reshus harabim — even though he did a prohibition — he acquired it, as stated; it is in the gemara that a chasid should not take wages, for he should waive from his own in every matter that has a hint of transgression; and Shulchan Gavoah wrote that if they returned it to him and afterward they request wages — forbidden for them to take, for they can no longer say from hefker we acquired, as is written in Choshen Mishpat siman 183 — see there; and in siman 123 seif 6 in Semag; and even though on a weekday in such a case one must pay him, as is written in Choshen Mishpat siman 264 — here forbidden on account of sachar Shabbat even though they did not stipulate, since he did not acquire from hefker; and specifically when they saved in violation; but if they saved permissibly — even if they returned it to him — permitted to accept wages; so wrote Rambam; however it is possible one is not obligated against his will to give; and in Choshen Mishpat siman 299 and siman 369 — the law of the kingdom does not help with ye'ush; and it is possible that here it is different since he said save for yourselves:
