(א) Come and save for yourselves — but he cannot ask them to save on his account, for it was not permitted to him except his three meals needed for Shabbat; and Chayei Adam wrote: it is possible that on their own they can save on his account even more than three meals; and that which Shulchan Aruch did not permit them except three meals — specifically when they save for themselves:

(ב) And they may wear, etc. — and with this he can say "come and save with me" — meaning on my account; for regarding food, since only three meals are fit for him, therefore he had to say "save for yourselves"; unlike regarding garments, which are fit for him all day [Gemara]; and what he wrote "and if they wish," etc. — refers to food; and it is obvious that they too can remove and return and wear and take out like him:

(ג) Since he said "save for yourselves" — but if he did not say "save for yourselves" it is not hefker, for he could have said "I would have found Israelites who would save for themselves and return to me," even though he could save via a non-Jew, as is written s.k. 26:

(ד) And if they do not wish to acquire — because they know he did not declare hefker willingly:

(ה) And it is not Shabbat wages — for initially he did not have in mind payment for work, and they acquired from hefker. If they returned it to him and afterward request payment on a weekday — he must certainly pay; but on Shabbat there is reasoning to say it is Shabbat wages, after he already returned it to him on his own he can no longer say "from hefker I acquired for myself"; and when he takes Shabbat wages he takes — see Magen Avraham what he wrote on this in name of Shelatei Gibborim; and see in Eliyah Rabbah who doubts this. And behold regarding what Shulchan Aruch wrote that he acquires from hefker — Magen Avraham wrote see Choshen Mishpat siman 259 and siman 368 that dinah demalkhuta does not help for despair; and it is possible here it is different since he said "save for yourselves."
