19) [Seif 3] If he saved fine bread, etc. This is an obvious stratagem, since he certainly will not eat coarse bread while he has fine bread. The reverse is permitted, because he may say that in his panic he saved coarse bread but does not desire it and wants fine bread. Levush.
20) There: If he saved fine bread, etc. Even if members of his household ordinarily eat coarse bread, he may not save it. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 8. See there for his challenge to Olas Shabbos and his distinction between this case and siman 506, seif 7, regarding Yom Tov. Pischei HaDevir, paragraph 3, writes the same.
21) There: If he saved fine bread, etc. Even if he saved a large quantity of bread, each member of his household may save food for three meals. Magen Avraham, paragraph 4; Tosefes Shabbos there; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 3. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 4, explains that 'even if he saved much bread' means either that he transgressed and saved more than three meals, or that he may save three meals for every member of his household whom he ordinarily feeds. Tosefes Shabbos there explains that he saved much bread in one utensil, as stated in seif 6.
22) It appears that even one observing a dream fast on Shabbos may save food, because the Sages did not distinguish, just as they permitted saving it even when he already has food. Tosefes Shabbos there. See above, paragraph 2. Pischei HaDevir, paragraph 5, however, questions the law regarding one who fasts and inclines to be stringent.
23) He may save three meals of each type. If he saved meat for three meals, he may afterward save fish, because he can say that he desires fish. Darkei Moshe, paragraph 1; Magen Avraham, paragraph 5; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 4; Tosefes Shabbos there; Eshel Avraham, paragraph 4; Shulchan Aruch HaRav there.
24) There: But the reverse is permitted. Even initially, one may save coarse bread and afterward fine bread. Tosefes Shabbos there.
