(א) (s.k. 3) And some forbid — specifically one for whom it became dark, etc. — this is the view of those who permit as they permitted above siman 266 seif 6 regarding one for whom it became dark on the road with money and no non-Jew and no donkey and no companion — on account of loss they permitted him shevut to carry it less than four amot at a time until he reaches his house; and also permitted him prohibition of moving muktzeh on account of loss; and likewise here on account of concern of loss from coercion or fire — permitted to move muktzeh; and those who forbid hold here it is not comparable to that of siman 266 — for specifically in that of siman 266 they permitted him shevut because a person is agitated over his money and if we do not permit him shevut of moving less than four amot he will carry it directly four amot and more at once until he reaches his house and do a Torah prohibition — therefore they permitted him d'rabbanan prohibition so he not come to Torah prohibition; unlike here in coercion — why permit him d'rabbanan prohibition since there is no concern that if we do not permit him he will do Torah prohibition — from what shall we be concerned? If we are concerned he will take out to reshus harabim to escape the coercers — on the contrary he fears to take them out lest the coercers see the matter and rob him; and this reason suffices for concern of coercers; but concern of loss from fire — this does not suffice that one say he will take out to reshus harabim to escape the fire; and for this one must say like the second reason that he wrote — we have no reshus harabim, as below siman 345 seif 7; and if we permit him muktzeh moving lest he take them out to reshus harabim and come to two d'rabbanan prohibitions — muktzeh moving and carrying out to karmelis — it would be better to permit him one d'rabbanan prohibition — namely to move muktzeh for this; Rabbenu Yehuda wrote we are not permitted to permit d'rabbanan prohibition what we do not find explicitly in Shas; and in Shas we do not find they permitted d'rabbanan prohibition except so he not do Torah prohibition; but so he not do two d'rabbanan prohibitions — we did not find the Sages permitted d'rabbanan prohibition — see there; and if we are concerned in fire lest he extinguish — not according to Tur who holds melacha she'ein tzricha l'gufah exempt even if he extinguishes there is only d'rabbanan prohibition; rather Tur comes to reject the words of those who permit even if he holds like Rambam melacha she'ein tzricha l'gufah liable — nevertheless why concern lest he extinguish since it deals with a house adjacent to fire as Magen Avraham wrote s.k. 1 and there he is not so agitated that we concern lest he extinguish; and see Terumat HaShulchan what he wrote; and Bach ruled like those who permit and wrote also here whether in coercion or fire there is concern — if we do not permit him muktzeh moving he will do Torah prohibition — meaning he will dig in the ground there to escape fire and coercers:

(ב) Forbidden for all to move. Meaning even according to those who permit whom Bach agreed with; and in Shulchan Aruch he wrote only that some say even via a non-Jew forbidden in merchandise — not like those who permit via non-Jew; and as is written above siman 307 seif 19; but via Israelite — there is none who permits; and so siman 308 seif 37; and in the gemara, etc. — the law of siman 308 seif 37 derives from this gemara — and it is in Beitza daf 36b — R' Chiya of Abaye — rain fell on his millstones on Yom Tov and he was concerned they would spoil from rain — for in their days millstones were made of clay — and he asked Rabba what to do and they answered he should bring his bed to the place of the millstones and via the little rain that already fell on the millstones part of the millstones became mud and it would be repugnant in his eyes — and it would be like a graf shel re'i permitted to move on account of its repulsiveness — and he would bring the millstones to a place where rain did not fall; and Abaye challenged him — do we make a graf shel re'i l'chatchila? And Rabba did not answer him; and Tosafos challenged — what is Rabba's view? For Abaye challenged well; and they resolved — in a place of loss they make graf shel re'i l'chatchila; and so below siman 338 seif 8; and see above siman 308 seif 37; and on this Magen Avraham was challenged — since there is loss — why should he make graf shel re'i? He should move it directly; or that in a place of loss also forbidden for an Israelite to move even according to Bach who agreed with those who permit; however the reason is not clear why it was not permitted if rain falls on merchandise or regarding millstones; and what he wrote regarding coercion and fire — why in merchandise and millstones he is not so agitated — if so what does he bring from millstones; and likewise afterward from fit fruits — and who told Magen Avraham to equate agitations to each other — perhaps in millstones and fit fruits there is not so much agitation as in merchandise on which rain falls; and also no hint of this in Ran's words who wrote "and so somewhat in Ran"; rather Magen Avraham's intent is specifically in fire and bandits they permitted since he is agitated — tell him he will do Torah prohibition — for also in bandits tell him he will dig in the ground as Bach wrote; unlike the above merchandise where there is no concern he will do Torah prohibition; and likewise in millstones why moving was not permitted to him; and so explained in Rashba's responsum siman 484 — the questioner asked: merchandise on which rain falls and it is lost, etc. — what is the law to move it on Shabbat or tell a non-Jew to move it? For Rav in Asher HaLevi wrote permitted to hide money from the government on Shabbat; and Rashba answered Ba'al HaTerumah's words are not correct — in fire specifically they permitted one who extinguishes does not lose, etc.; and likewise from this that they compare to R' Chiya of Abaye where dew fell and they permitted only by bringing his bed, etc., and not comparable to one for whom it became dark on the road who gives his pouch to a non-Jew — there they permitted for if you do not permit him to move he will come to do Torah melacha; but here why permit moving? And even to tell a non-Jew they permitted only for the first matter so he not come to d'oraisa prohibition — but in general not, for whoever does not act does not tell a non-Jew to act — end of Rashba's words; it is clear from his words why forbidden in merchandise and millstones since there is no concern he will do Torah prohibition; and so Magen Avraham's intent; and likewise regarding fit fruits the reason is there is no concern he will do Torah prohibition:

(ג) And all Magen Avraham's proof follows this — wherever there is no concern he will do Torah prohibition — even according to those who permit — meaning Ba'al HaTerumah — forbidden for an Israelite to move; only Ba'al HaTerumah holds also in coercion tell him lest he do Torah prohibition as Bach wrote — not like Rashba and Tur; and so somewhat in Ran ch. 24 in tractate Shabbat who first clarified Ba'al HaTerumah's words who permitted as stated; and afterward what rejected Ramban's words and what Rashba challenged Ramban's rejection — only rejected Ba'al HaTerumah's words from another angle — they did not permit in one for whom it became dark on the road, etc., except so he not do Torah prohibition; but on account of coercion and bandits where there is no concern he will do Torah prohibition — we do not permit him, as is written in that responsum; and on this Ran wrote, and this is his language: and this responsum I do not recognize — for as we say here where there is no non-Jew with him and you permitted him, etc., so I do not move four amot at once, etc., here too tell one who bandits come upon him — if you do not permit him, etc., he will not listen to you and will move even four amot at once — and I see no difference between this and that at all — end of his words; it is clear from his words that also what is permitted in coercion is only so he not do Torah prohibition; only it is possible Ran holds in his own view that also in coercion tell him he will do Torah prohibition — therefore fitting to permit him moving; but if there were no concern he will do Torah prohibition — we would not permit moving as Rashba wrote; but Ba'al HaTerumah in his own view possibly holds even where there is no concern he will do Torah prohibition — nevertheless permitted on account of loss of moving; but it is implied from his language he comes to justify Ba'al HaTerumah's view thus — that Rashba's difficulties should not challenge him — it appears Ba'al HaTerumah also holds thus and agrees they did not permit moving except where tell him he will do Torah prohibition; therefore he wrote "and so somewhat" — it is not absolute necessity to say thus; and to exclude Pri Megadim's view who wrote in s.k. 3, and this is his language: and it appears according to Ran he holds to distinguish between a place where he is agitated and where he is not agitated — thus there is no need for this law of Rashba — for here too by lost merchandise he is also not so agitated and fitting to permit even to move by hand — end of his words; and his intent is to what Ran wrote in his conclusion to distinguish according to Ba'al HaTerumah from another angle, and this is his language: for this reason the Sages permitted here regarding one for whom it became dark, etc.; and in fire not permitted (except food for three meals) — because all loss falls suddenly, etc.; and one must hurry its saving — such as in fire like this it is relevant to say — from a person being agitated over his money — if you permit him he will come to extinguish — because the matter is very urgent and his heart is not on it — if you permit him something he too will not give his words to measures and will come to extinguish — for in all such cases there is more concern; and from a person being agitated (and he comes to extinguish) — from where is there concern? A person does not stand, etc. (and he desecrates Shabbat intentionally); but here (in one for whom it became dark) the hour is not so pressing that his heart be agitated over it — and if you permit him he too will save in the manner you permitted him alone; and if not you permit him something he too will not stand himself on his money and save as he needs, etc.; and regarding bandits the matters appear that a person's heart is agitated over them like fire since their saving is urgent, etc.; and if you permit him something he will come to d'oraisa prohibition — end of Ran's words; and from this Pri Megadim learned in the case where he is not so agitated like bandits — Ran agrees moving is permitted; if so in merchandise where he is also not so agitated — moving permitted even via Israelite; and this is not so — for Ran also agrees they do not permit moving except if we do not permit him one can say he will do Torah prohibition; only he holds in a place where he is urgent and greatly agitated it is good there is concern — for if we do not permit him he will desecrate Shabbat in Torah prohibition anyway; against this there is concern — if we permit him via a person being agitated he will forget it is Shabbat and desecrate inadvertently even in what we did not permit him; and there is more concern for this concern that via permitting him moving he will desecrate Shabbat more in Torah prohibition than we concern if we do not permit him he will transgress Torah prohibition (and Bach's view); unlike when he is not so agitated — there is no concern if we permit him moving he will transgress Torah prohibition since he is not agitated; and since there is no such concern — if so we concern if we do not permit him moving since impossible to stand oneself on one's money he will transgress Torah prohibition intentionally since there is no other concern against it; but Ran agrees in a place where there is no concern if we do not permit him moving he will do Torah prohibition — they do not permit moving; and since in merchandise it appears he will do Torah prohibition if we do not permit moving — moving forbidden for all; and as is implied from Ran's words; and so in beginning ch. 5 of Beitza, etc. — we learned one removes fruits (spread on the roof to dry and rain began to fall on them) via a skylight on Yom Tov; and one covers fruits with vessels on account of rain; and Ulla said even airspace of bricks (leftover from building) — even though they are muktzeh permitted to cover on account of rain (for he holds a vessel may be moved even for something that may not be moved); and he challenged — but we learned one covers fruits — it implies specifically fit fruits and not airspace of bricks; and they answered since the beginning taught one removes fruits — the end also teaches fruits; and on this Magen Avraham was challenged — the matter itself is difficult — it should have taught also in the beginning one removes airspace of bricks and covers airspace of bricks with vessels; rather perforce one must say the beginning that teaches one removes — meaning moving by hand forbidden in airspace of bricks that is muktzeh even though there is loss;
