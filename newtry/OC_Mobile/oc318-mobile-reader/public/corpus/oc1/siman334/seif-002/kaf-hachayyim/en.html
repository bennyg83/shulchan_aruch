11) [Seif 2] Some permit moving, etc. This is Sefer HaTerumah's view, cited by Tur, while the prohibiting opinion is Tur's disagreement. Bach writes that Sefer HaTerumah addresses Rabbeinu Tam's permission for nearby houses to save more than needed. Accordingly, they may even save money and muktzeh objects, because they are not so panicked since the fire is in another house; see there. Perishah, paragraph 1, writes the same, and this also appears to be Beis Yosef's view. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 2; Magen Avraham, paragraph 1; Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 2; Chayei Adam, klal 45, paragraph 6. Mahari Abuhav, however, infers from Ran in the chapter Mi Shehechshich that Sefer HaTerumah permits this even in the burning house itself. Tur objects only regarding other houses not burning, but raises no objection concerning the burning house because moving muktzeh may properly be permitted lest the owner extinguish the fire. Beis Yosef cites him, and Taz, paragraph 2, agrees; see there. Beis Yosef, however, questions Mahari Abuhav's words and offers a forced resolution; see there.
12) Issur VeHeter, klal 59, siman 41, writes: Today, when we have no public domain, though see below, siman 345, seif 7, where this is disputed, and there is no concern for a Torah prohibition, if a fire breaks out in one's house on Shabbos he may save anything so that he will not extinguish, because a person is distraught over his property. Certainly, if the fire began in another courtyard, he may save anything. Even money and other muktzeh items may be saved in a case of loss, and the prohibition of muktzeh need not be considered. This applies only to rabbinic prohibitions. One may not dig them into the ground or desecrate Shabbos through any Torah-prohibited primary or derivative labor for monetary property. These are his words. The gloss there writes that since the reason is that one is distraught over his property and might extinguish, this applies to the owner, his sons, and his wife, who need not account for the prohibitions of a karmelis, techum, or a courtyard without an eruv. A friend, for whom this reason does not apply, may certainly not carry the property out. Regarding muktzeh, however, which has no Torah root at all, it appears that anyone may help him move and carry it into a courtyard even without an eruv. The work there is uncertain whether another person may even help carry it into a karmelis; see there. Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur, paragraph 1; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1; Chayei Adam, klal 45, paragraph 8. Chayei Adam writes there, however, that all these words of Issur VeHeter require great examination, because they are found neither in Sefer HaTerumah nor in any halachic authority.
13) There: To save them from the fire, etc. This resembles the permission for one overtaken by night on the road to move his purse in increments of less than four amos. Tur in the name of Sefer HaTerumah. Those who forbid distinguish that there the permission prevents him from openly violating a Torah prohibition, whereas here there is no concern that he will commit a more severe prohibition, as Tur writes; see there.
14) There: Or from violent confiscators, etc. Maadanei Yom Tov writes that Maharash likewise permitted carrying property outside and removing it, because our alleys are not public domains; only a found object that never belonged to him was forbidden. Olas Shabbos writes that if a sack containing money is tied at its opening when enemies come to take it, one may cut it with a knife and break the knot to save it even on Shabbos, but may not untie the knot. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 2, writes that one may be lenient only regarding muktzeh, not carrying through a karmelis, and only in a house near the fire, not the burning house; see there. All this follows those who permit. According to those who forbid, even moving muktzeh is forbidden.
15) There: In a case of loss there is no concern, etc. Although one who forgot a purse in a courtyard may not move it even by means of a loaf or child, that case differs because he was negligent in forgetting it, as stated in siman 267, seif 8. Moreover, he can sit and guard it or entrust it to a non-Jewish guard. Magen Avraham, paragraph 2.
16) There: Some forbid, etc. Bach rules like those who permit. Magen Avraham, paragraph 3; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 7. See above, siman 13, paragraph 7, where we wrote that whenever Shulchan Aruch says 'some permit' and 'some forbid,' his intent is to rule like those who forbid, while in pressing circumstances one may rely upon those who permit; see there. Nevertheless, it appears to me that permission was given specifically because of robbers or fire, where panic exists and we are concerned that one may violate a Torah prohibition. If rain falls upon muktzeh merchandise, all opinions forbid a Jew from moving it. Magen Avraham there; Tosefes Shabbos there; Shulchan Aruch HaRav there.
17) There: One authority forbids. See above, siman 307, seif 5, where Shulchan Aruch unqualifiedly permits having a non-Jew perform a rabbinically prohibited act when there is a great need; see there and our words there in paragraph 41.
18) There, gloss: See above, siman 307, seif 19. There Shulchan Aruch unqualifiedly permits calling a non-Jew although one knows he will save the property. One may likewise say to non-Jews, 'Anyone who saves will not lose'; see there and our words there in paragraph 130.
