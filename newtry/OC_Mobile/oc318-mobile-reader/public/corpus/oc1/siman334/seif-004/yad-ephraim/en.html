And furthermore written in another script, etc. — meaning and on this he said as above per what he wrote: because not everyone is proficient to write Assyrian script — it only helps on account of et la'asot; and for them this does not apply; and on this Rama wrote in responsa, etc.; and per this view there is no need at all for that answer that not everyone is proficient, etc., for otherwise it would not apply that even without et la'asot it is permitted in another script or another language as written below; and automatically the words of Shulchan Gavoah do not apply that even though for them et la'asot does not apply, nevertheless initially writing was not forbidden at all except in Assyrian script specifically, and he did not permit writing matters of the Oral Law in Assyrian script except on account of et la'asot; but in another script that is not this one — permitted entirely, whether in the holy tongue or another language; and what Magen Avraham wrote "and see Yoreh De'ah siman 283," etc. — meaning from this there is support for Rama's view, for the reason not to write secular matters in Assyrian script is because it is a script designated for the Written Torah; therefore they were particular on Assyrian script specifically not to write matters of the Oral Law; and it is more reasonable to explain that per Beit Yosef one may say the reason they found a script from a sample to write secular matters — nevertheless to write matters of the Oral Law one may say forbidden even in a sample script; nevertheless requires study — meaning also per Rama's words the difficulty remains: in a language the people understand there was room to permit even from Talmudic law — why did he write "nowadays we save," etc.; and if we say per the first answer that they are not proficient in Assyrian script — then even from the language side there is no prohibition even from Talmudic law, nevertheless on account of the writing it was forbidden if not for not being proficient in Assyrian script — now in Assyrian script it was permitted even so on account of et la'asot (but requires study in Beit Yosef in the name of poskim that from what he said "nowadays," etc. it is from the language side); unlike if we say as Rama wrote — if so also from Talmudic law it is permitted, and even if we say here it deals even if written Assyrian script in another language — nevertheless it appears per Rama that just as matters of the Oral Law were forbidden only in Assyrian script because the Written Torah was given in that script — so too permitted to write matters of the Oral Law even in Assyrian script in another language, for the stringency applies only to the holy tongue in which the Torah was given; unlike another language where there is no impediment; if so what does he mean by "nowadays," for even from Talmudic law it is permitted; and on this he wrote see siman 439 seif 9 — meaning explained there one need not be concerned in which script it is written;
