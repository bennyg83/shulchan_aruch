There in Magen Avraham s.k. 13 — see in Shulchan Gavoah who did not write in Biur so much; only Magen Avraham explains his intent in what appears from him: if they saved permissibly he may take wages even after returning, unlike if he saved forbiddenly; therefore he wrote that when saving forbiddenly, since he cannot say "I acquire from hefker," even to acquire for his wages — per Rambam's reason because there is no prohibition here, as written earlier per Rambam; and therefore even though he says that since he intended to acquire from hefker we do not believe him since he says he did so and we do not leave him to take wages on account of his saying he intended to acquire from hefker and did a prohibition — a person does not put himself in harm's way; and if because on a weekday in such a case he must pay for benefit as written in Choshen Mishpat siman 264 — here forbidden on account of Shabbat wages; nevertheless if he has not yet returned he may properly say he intends to acquire and did a prohibition, since the items he saved are under his control we do not remove them from him on account of this reason that a person does not put himself in harm's way, as the poskim wrote regarding monetary claim he may properly claim even a matter he puts himself in harm's way over — see Choshen Mishpat siman 75 regarding a woman who claims she vowed to him as payment (but if he already returned and comes with a claim that he truly became wicked since one cannot clarify that his words are so — we do not believe him that he did a prohibition; and if he takes wages now it will be Shabbat wages — therefore bet din should prevent him from this), for it is a valid claim to make him swear on this; and all the more one should not remove from him, unlike if he saved permissibly — granted if he did not intend to acquire up to his wages it is forbidden to take wages on account of Shabbat wages; nevertheless if he comes to take wages we leave him, for even though he already returned he is believed to say he intended to acquire for his wages; and again Magen Avraham wrote it is possible they do not compel him — meaning that although if this one wishes to give we leave him to receive, nevertheless if this one claims "I do not believe you intended to acquire and on account of needing to pay for his benefit I have nothing to give you on account of Shabbat wages" — they do not compel him to give; and what Magen Avraham wrote "and so Rambam" — it appears this is a slip and must read "and so Rambam," meaning Shulchan Gavoah wrote thus per Rambam's reason that when saving forbiddenly forbidden to take wages, unlike according to Rashi and Ran who did not tie the matter to whether they saved permissibly — one may say even when saving forbiddenly they may properly take, for it does not depend on intent but automatically so: since they saved from hefker they acquire from hefker for their wages and therefore there is no Shabbat wages issue; if so there is no place to distinguish between forbidden and permitted here, as Rashi wrote that the rescuers say "we acquire from hefker" — not that they need to claim they intended thus, only they may say wages reach them from acquiring from hefker and not from Shabbat wages; but for Rambam who wrote the reason because there is no prohibition here, etc. — perforce one must say they must intend in this rescue to acquire from hefker for their wages; therefore specifically when saving permissibly — therefore also when returning the object there is a distinction between saving permissibly or forbiddenly for the reason I wrote; and possible what Magen Avraham wrote "and so Rambam" must read "and as Rambam said," meaning Rambam distinguishes between forbidden and permitted; and although not explained in Rambam regarding returning — nevertheless the distinction between forbidden and permitted is explained in Rambam; and possible that according to Rashi too it is so regarding returning — Rashi and Rambam agree on this; and what Rashi did not mention this reason is because without returning there is no distinction, and even when saving forbiddenly he may say he intends to acquire from hefker;
