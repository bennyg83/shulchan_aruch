[1] Three meals, etc. This applies even if he already has what to eat, as stated by the Beit Yosef. Issur VeHeter, rule 59, writes that nowadays, when we do not have a true public domain and there is therefore no concern of a decree leading to a Torah prohibition, if a fire breaks out even in one's own house he may save anything, including money, because in a case of loss we do not take muktzeh restrictions into account. The same applies because of thieves, a violent oppressor, fear of imprisonment, or siege. For this reason I have abbreviated most of the laws in this siman. See the gloss at the end of this siman. Issur VeHeter writes that specifically the owner himself, his wife, and his children need not be concerned for prohibitions involving a karmelit, techum, a courtyard without an eruv, and similar rabbinic restrictions. Regarding a friend, there is uncertainty. However, with muktzeh, which has no root in a Torah prohibition, anyone may assist him by moving and taking items into a courtyard even if it lacks an eruv. See there at length. All this applies only to rabbinic prohibitions. Digging under the ground, or desecrating Shabbos or Yom Tov through any Torah melachah for the sake of money, is entirely prohibited.
[2] Or because of violent oppressors, etc. Malbushei Yom Tov writes this, and Maharash likewise permitted in his derashah even moving property outside, because our alleyways are not a public domain. This applies to one's own property, but a found object that was never his is prohibited. He also writes that if enemies come to seize a sack of money whose mouth is tied, one may cut it with a knife and break the fastening to save it even on Shabbos, but may not untie the knot. The Bach likewise rules leniently, and so follows from Issur VeHeter cited above.
[3] [Levush] And for this reason they prohibited, etc. The Magen Avraham writes that everyone agrees to this prohibition, because here the person is not panicked, so we are not concerned that he will come to a Torah violation.
