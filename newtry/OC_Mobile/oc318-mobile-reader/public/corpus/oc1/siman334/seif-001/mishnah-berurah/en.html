(א) He may save, etc. — see below in seif 11 that to save from a house to his courtyard some say he may take out as much as he wishes:

(ב) And in the morning, food, etc. — Bach wrote that if it is after chatzot, even if he has not yet eaten, he saves only food for one meal; and in Peri Megadim he is uncertain about this:

(ג) That the fire broke out in it — meaning and therefore he is certainly agitated; and if you permit him he will come to extinguish. And behold here we forbid saving so it not be extinguished; and regarding a dead person they permit saving him so it not be extinguished, as Rama wrote siman 311; Tosafos wrote that regarding a dead person a person is more agitated over him and therefore they needed to permit so it not be extinguished intentionally; but here a person is not so agitated, and even if they do not permit him he will not come to extinguish intentionally; and if they permit him we are concerned that perhaps from his agitation he will forget and extinguish the fire:

(ד) The nearby ones — who are not so agitated [and see in Sefer Nezer Yisrael who brought in name of Shulchan Atzei Shetim who wrote that according to what is explained below in s.k. 26 in the gloss that in our time, when we dwell among non-Jews and there is concern of danger to life, it is permitted to extinguish automatically — they are permitted even in that house where the fire broke out to save food as much as they wish; and the same applies to muktzeh items, as is the law in a neighbor's house, since there is no reason to decree that from agitation he will come to extinguish, for behold in our time it is permitted to extinguish; and this is not necessary, for it is possible that from his agitation he will come to other melachot of taking out to reshus harabim and the like, as they said there, in the Gemara 117b regarding one whose barrel broke on his roof — see there; and nevertheless one should not protest against those who are lenient, for according to Taz brought in s.k. 2 it also implies that in saving money it is permitted according to Beit HaTerumot if he does not thereby transgress a d'oraisa prohibition; and likewise in Orach VeShalom, even though in Neshmat Adam he greatly doubts his words — see there] and Chayei Adam wrote: and it is possible that according to this reasoning others too, besides the homeowner and his wife and children from the house where the fire broke out — who are agitated — but other people who are not so agitated may save on his behalf, even money, for there is only a prohibition of muktzeh [and this is like the view of those who permit brought below]; but it is forbidden to move muktzeh on his behalf to karmelit, end of his words:
