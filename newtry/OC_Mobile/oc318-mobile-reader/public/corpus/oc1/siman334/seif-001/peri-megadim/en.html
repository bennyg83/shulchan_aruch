(א) On account of "et la'asot," etc. Tosafos brought Shabbos 44a s.v. "mitokh"; and in old Tosafos Yoma chapter 5 it implies that for property — whenever one does not save he is not troubled and will not forget and extinguish inadvertently; unlike a dead person — whenever one does not save he is troubled in his pain and agitated over his dead and will forget inadvertently and extinguish — they permitted saving the dead person with the light prohibition of moving muktzeh; here there is no trouble and not so much exertion in taking out the dead, and he will not forget — and Tosafos here possibly wrote this to reconcile R' Yehuda, for according to R' Shimon melacha she'einah tzerichah legufah is d'oraisa — why did they permit muktzeh prohibition explicitly on account of concern for inadvertence? Unlike for R' Shimon, for whom it is rabbinic — why did they permit? It would seem that here for a living person they did not permit lest he come to extinguish, for certainly he would extinguish intentionally, since it is a severe prohibition even though rabbinic, from the light moving of it they deal in siman 311 seif 1 in Taz and Magen Avraham; and what Taz wrote here "we forbid the permission" means permitted items in moving and to a joined courtyard; and for a dead person they permit moving muktzeh; and there is a missing word — "if not" permit; and see letter 2 and there it will be explained further, and in our chiddushim we wrote from this:
