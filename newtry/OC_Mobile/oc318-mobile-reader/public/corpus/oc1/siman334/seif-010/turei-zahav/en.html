All the saving we said, etc. This is Tur's view; and it is difficult for me in garments — what difference between mixed or not mixed, since he takes them out in the manner of wearing — even in reshus harabim there is no prohibition of carrying out; unlike food which is in prohibition of carrying out to one that is not mixed; and it appears this is Rabbenu Baruch's reason who wrote in Semag in his name that in garments permitted even to one that is not mixed; and in Mordekhai ch. 22 so too Rabbenu Baruch's words, and this is his language: we conclude one saves holy writings from fire, etc., and saves to his courtyard that needs no eiruv all his objects whether food or garments — all he has; but to his friend's courtyard where there is eiruv — specifically his utensils needed for that day and food for three meals for each in the manner he walks; and if he walks only one path he saves even one hundred meals together; and in garments to a courtyard that is not mixed — he wears and takes out and removes and returns and wears and saves much even to a courtyard that is not mixed; for since he is agitated over his money — if you do not permit him he will come to extinguish; but when the fire is in another house permitted to take out all he wishes — end of his words; and it appears what he wrote "and in garments to a courtyard that is not mixed" is a mistake since he wrote afterward "even to a courtyard that is not mixed"; rather it must read "and in garments he wears," etc.; and the reason in garments they were more lenient than food and vessels since he wears them — perforce permitted in them even to one that is not mixed; and what he wrote afterward "for since he is agitated," etc. — it appears likewise to resolve that not to forbid also in garments lest if you permit him he will be preoccupied he will forget Shabbat — here there is a decree to the contrary that if you do not permit him he will come to extinguish intentionally; unlike food; and so Shulchan Gavoah; and from these words that came from Rabbenu Baruch it implies in garments there is no difference between mixed or not; and in food there is a difference; if so what Rama wrote "some are lenient even to one that is not mixed" refers to garments alone; and in Beit Yosef he wrote Rabbenu Baruch ruled like Ben Beteira — if so even in food he is lenient; and this is only for the first Tanna who distinguishes as is written; and Maharach of blessed memory already wrote in this that Beit Yosef was not precise, for in Sefer HaTerumah Rabbenu Baruch wrote explicitly the halacha is like the first Tanna:
