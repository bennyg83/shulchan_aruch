Y) [Seif 4] But not from Shabbat to Yom Kippur, etc. For example when Yom Kippur falls on Sunday, for he does not eat until evening and in the evening he will trouble himself and bring. Rashi; and Tosafos wrote that for Yom Kippur itself to feed his children it is also forbidden to save; and even though Yom Kippur and Shabbat are not adjacent for us (as is written below siman 328) they taught this law to inform us that if Yom Kippur is on Thursday and he knows he will not find to buy on day six — they save. Magen Avraham s.k. 6; and the same applies conversely — it teaches us that if Yom Kippur falls on Monday and he knows that on day one he will not find to buy — nevertheless it was not permitted for him to save on Shabbat for Yom Kippur. Machatzit HaShekel; however Tosafos Shabbos note 9 wrote on Magen Avraham's words this is not so clear; and it is more reasonable there is a difference for those who observe two days of Yom Kippur, who are forbidden melacha on Yom Kippur, as is stated in siman 624 — see there; and further one can say since he needed to say "but not from Shabbat to Yom Tov" and not to the coming Shabbat, which applies in our time, he taught the entire language of the baraita — for this is the language of the baraita on Shabbos 117b.
