25) [Seif 4] But not from Shabbos for Yom Kippur, etc. For example, if Yom Kippur fell on Sunday, he would not eat until evening and could exert himself and bring food then. Rashi. Tosafos writes that even to feed his children on Yom Kippur, he may not save food. Although Yom Kippur and Shabbos cannot occur consecutively, as stated below at the beginning of siman 428, the ruling teaches that if Yom Kippur falls on Thursday and one knows food will not be available for purchase on Friday, food may be saved. Magen Avraham, paragraph 6. Conversely, if Yom Kippur falls on Monday and one knows food will not be available on Sunday, one still may not save food on Shabbos for Yom Kippur. Machatzis HaShekel. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 9, writes that Magen Avraham's explanation is not entirely clear. It appears more relevant to those who observe two days of Yom Kippur and are forbidden to perform labor on the additional day, as stated at the end of siman 624; see there. Alternatively, since the Baraisa wished to say 'but not from Shabbos for Yom Tov or the following Shabbos,' which applies today, it cited the entire language of the Baraisa in Shabbos 117b.
