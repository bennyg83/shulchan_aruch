[12] Assyrian script, etc. And Magen Avraham expanded to challenge from Mordechai that specifically in script in dye and red paint one may not save, since it is forbidden to read in them because ink is common; but in what is written in every language and on paper he permits even in Megillah, end of his words; and in small Mordechai on parchment I saw he did not mention dye and red paint except if not written as required; and so it appears in large Mordechai; however it appears to me if there is a difficulty, the difficulty is that Mordechai deals with the law of the Gemara, or that it teaches us other books are also invalid without ink — only regarding saving from fire does the Gemara deal, as it appears in Agudah; but for us, who permit reading in all books — one reads even in Megillah in dye and red paint, for we find no distinction for us among the poskim between language and language or script and script, except for reading in public — see previous seif; and so it appears in Shelatei Gibborim chapter Kol Kitvei; and further behold he wrote that for Rif and Rambam one reads even by law in Megillah in every language and likewise dye and red paint; and in truth I wonder — behold on daf 15b in the Gemara R' Ashi said as we learned in Megillah until it is written, etc.; however in Tosefta chapter 17 I found explained that even in Megillah in dye and red paint one saves; and perhaps Rambam explained what is written in the Gemara: if it is taught in a baraita, this is this Tosefta; and he did not read "what is taught — R' Ashi," etc.; and with this all Tosafos' difficulties there are resolved. And incidentally I note a difficulty in the Gemara: what does the Exilarch need, since he knows the baraita? And behold Rashi's explanation there: "behold R' Hamnuna taught," etc.; it appears to me we hold R' Hamnuna is an amora and not a tanna — if so how does it say "R' Hamnuna taught"?
