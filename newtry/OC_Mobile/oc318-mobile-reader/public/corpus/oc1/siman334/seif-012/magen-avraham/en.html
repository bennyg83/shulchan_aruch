(א) Holy writings. Written Torah takes precedence to save over Oral Torah (Sha'arei Chayim 661):

(ב) Nowadays. For the heart has diminished and they gave all books to be written so that they not be forgotten and require genizah:

(ג) Expert in it. But if the people do not understand that language — forbidden to write it and save it; and so in Ran; and Rambam wrote: if they were written in another script or another language — even on a weekday forbidden to read in them, rather one leaves them in an exposed place and they are lost on their own until they are written in Ashurit and the holy language — end of his words; and requires further study — for although they permitted to write in another language so the people understand — why permit in another script? And it is possible when it is another language it is permitted to write also in another script; but requires further study on what basis they relied to write Chumashim and the twenty-four books in a script that is not Ashurit; and it is strained to say that writing in meshita script is like Ashurit and permitted, for it is not in its proper form; and also in printing it would seem to forbid, for the writing is fine, as is written siman 484; and some say not everyone is expert to write Ashurit; and see Terumat HaDeshen and Shulchan Gavoah who wrote, and this is his language: one should ask about our books that are written in their languages — if they require genizah even on a weekday; and if there is concern for their Divine Names not to swear falsely by them and not to erase them — good to be stringent — end of his words; and requires further study — for behold it is not permitted to write in another language except on account of "it is a time to act for the Heaven"; and who permitted them? And furthermore they are written in another script; and Rama in responsum siman 34 wrote that it is possible the early ones prepared meshita script in order to write things of the Oral Torah; and one can say specifically in Ashurit script they were not given to write — see there; see siman 545; and in Pri Megadim siman 483 in Beit Yosef who wrote that it is not proper to write mundane matters in Ashurit script; nevertheless requires further study — for behold even in Talmudic times it was permitted to read a Gephtit for Gephtim; and see siman 691 seif 9; and in Beit Shmuel he wrote that it is permitted to learn with them Nevi'im and Ketuvim so they see the comfort of Israel — see there; and in Shelah he was very stringent in the matter — see there; and it is straightforward that other books are permitted to write in any script; and in Mordekhai first chapter of Megillah and in Hagahot Maimoniyot first chapter of Tefillin they wrote that Rabbeinu Yoel forbade a convert who did not know the holy language to copy a Chumash book in their language — see there; and it is possible he holds it is not "a time to act for the Heaven" except in a matter where all are equal:
