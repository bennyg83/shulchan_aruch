(א) All holy writings — meaning Tanakh and Gemara and all books; and Torah she'bichtav precedes saving Torah she'be'al peh; and even Torah she'be'al peh borrowed or rented to him — and if he does not save Torah she'be'al peh he will have to pay the owners — nevertheless Torah she'bichtav comes first [Eliyah Rabbah in name of Sefer Chasidim]. And saving a dead person precedes books [there]:

(ב) They save nowadays — that which he used the language "nowadays" is because he wished to conclude even what is written in every language, etc.; and the explanation of the matter: behold by law Torah she'bichtav is written only in Assyrian script [the script of a Torah scroll] and in the holy tongue; and Torah she'be'al peh [meaning Talmud and all books] may not be written but read by heart; and if one changed — whether Torah she'bichtav written not as required in its script or language, or if he wrote she'be'al peh — one does not read in them, and one also does not save them on Shabbat from fire, for it is forbidden to move them at all; but nowadays, when hearts are diminished and understanding and memory lessened, they permitted writing Torah she'be'al peh so it not be forgotten; and also in every script and language; and also Torah she'bichtav in every language and every script so all people understand the words of Torah, for not all are expert in the holy tongue; and they relied all this on what is written "it is a time to act for the Omnipresent — they have voided Your Torah"; and therefore one reads in them and saves them from fire on Shabbat. And our books written in the language of Cutheans found among them — there is room to doubt whether they require genizah, for it was not permitted to write in another language and another script except on account of "et la'asot laShem"; and who permitted them? And see in Magen Avraham who expanded further on this:

(ג) And even if written in dye, etc. — this refers to what is written in every language; but if the Tanakh were written in the holy tongue it was not permitted to read in them if written not in ink, for by law Tanakh must be written in the holy tongue and in ink; and although other languages were permitted on account of "et la'asot laShem," as stated — to change from ink was not permitted, for ink is easy to obtain [Magen Avraham in seif 13]; and all this is only regarding reading in them; but regarding saving from fire, and likewise regarding genizah — even if written in dye and red paint [Magen Avraham and Shulchan Aruch]:

(ד) And likewise a set of blessings — all this was permitted to write in our time and to bless and pray in them on account of "et la'asot laShem"; and therefore automatically one must save them from fire; and they also require genizah on a weekday so they not lie in a place of hefker:

(ה) Such as Yagar Sahaduta — mentioned in the Torah, meaning that with these words he completed the accounting of 85 letters, as below regarding these words alone one need not save them from fire even if he did not translate them to Hebrew [Gemara]:

(ו) That that people are proficient in it — but if the people do not understand that language it is forbidden to write and save it, for it was permitted only on account of "et la'asot laShem," as stated; and see in Biur Halacha that Ran's view in name of Ra'avad is that it was not permitted to write another language and read holy writings in it except when one is not expert in the holy tongue:

(ז) 85 letters — for through this measure there remains upon it some sanctity of a Torah scroll, as is found in the parashah of Vayehi B'nsoa:
