(ב) Proficient — see Mishna Berurah; and see in Ran who wrote that since we rule that they permitted a Gittite Megillah for Gittites, an Ilmite Megillah for Ilmites, etc., therefore the same applies regarding holy writings: for one to whom they were given to read in them — one saves them in their place from fire; but when you permitted a Gittite Megillah for Gittites — it does not seem reasonable that only for those not proficient in the holy tongue, but for those proficient — no, end of his words; and it appears to me that Ran refers only to Megillah; but in our matter, even one proficient in the holy tongue must also save it from fire, for it was given to read in that place for one not proficient in the holy tongue; and so is proven from the language of Shulchan Aruch who wrote "or in another language that that people are proficient in" — it implies that since the people are proficient in that language it suffices, even though many of them also know the holy tongue; nevertheless one saves, for it was given to read for one not proficient; and even though in Machatzit HaShekel it appears he explains that Ran refers to everything — in my humble opinion I wrote as above. However, regarding actual study of Torah she'bichtav in another language for one who can read it in the holy tongue — we too concede to Machatzit HaShekel that according to Ran in name of Ra'avad it is not proper to do so, as his language implies to one who examines it [and see siman 490 seif 10 in the first view, which is the anonymous ruling like Ra'avad]; and one should not challenge Ra'avad's words from Berachos 13, where the Gemara says to say that the Rabbis hold the entire Torah was stated in the holy tongue [meaning to read, as Rashi explains there] — for if one could say it was stated in every language, it would follow the Torah wrote "why" unnecessarily; and the Gemara answers: nevertheless it was stated in every language — see there; behold it is permitted to read in every language as regarding Kerias Shema; and regarding Kerias Shema no posek mentions a distinction whether one knows the holy tongue or not; one can say Ra'avad holds that since in that sugya it begins with the view of R' Yehuda saying the opposite, it is evident that for the authors of the Gemara this matter is not clear whether Torah she'bichtav to read was stated only in the holy tongue or in every language; and therefore he leans the law here is like Megillah — one who does not know the holy tongue may read it in every language; or one can say there it deals with reading the verses by heart [and similar to Kerias Shema which one reads by heart, and there is no concern of "matters written you may not say by heart," as likewise in siman 507 the poskim wrote the same regarding other verses if the public is proficient to say them by heart]; in another language — and therefore we say it was stated in every language to read; unlike in our matter, where we deal with one who wrote the Torah in another language and reads the language from the writing — there Ra'avad properly holds it was not permitted to write another language and read in it except when one is not proficient in the holy tongue:
