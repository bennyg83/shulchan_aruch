7) [Seif 2] Even when needed for its use, etc. The reason is that while it burns, it is obviously forbidden to move it in any manner. It therefore becomes a base for a forbidden item and is consequently set aside in this manner for the entire day. Beis Yosef at the end of the siman; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 3; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1. Taz, paragraph 2, writes that according to our practice, where we intend that the lamp remain upon the table only at night, it does not become a base, and after it is extinguished it may be moved when needed for its use or place; see there. See Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 4, who challenges this ruling and agrees that it is forbidden; see there. Mateh Yehudah, paragraph 3, likewise challenges it and writes that one should not depart from the Shulchan Aruch's ruling; see there. Taz himself similarly writes in siman 309, paragraph 1, that we cannot be lenient because Beis Yosef and the Shulchan Aruch rule that it is forbidden; see there. This is contrary to Maharikash in Responsa Ohalei Yaakov, siman 103. Where damage might result, however, all opinions permit moving it in any manner, as Tashbetz, volume 1, siman 137, and Maharikash there write. Erech HaShulchan, Kuntres Acharon, paragraph 1. See below, paragraph 19.
