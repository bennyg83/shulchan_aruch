However, accordingly it is difficult — behold here it deals with moving it for the vessel lest it be stolen — if so, what does it help that he conditioned? Nevertheless the vessel itself is a vessel whose melacha is for prohibition, as explained in Tosafot Shabbat 172, etc., s.v. deha — every candle, even if not lit, is called a vessel whose melacha is for prohibition; and for this purpose a condition does not apply. And perhaps it is proven in truth from this that we rule like the Rashba, who wrote in his novellae Shabbat 122 that a candle is not called whose melacha is for prohibition, for it does not serve a forbidden melacha but only became a base; and in responsa Tashbatz, part 1, siman 337, so too in the name of the Ramban in the name of the Raavad — perhaps here for moving through a gentile he relies on the Rashba's view, since the Jew himself may move it lest it be stolen; but regarding a Jew moving a candle on Shabbat lest it be stolen — perhaps they are not lenient like the Rashba in this, and as we say: since there are poskim that a condition helps, we rely on them for moving through a gentile; so too one may say for this matter one may rely through a gentile on the Rashba's approach.
