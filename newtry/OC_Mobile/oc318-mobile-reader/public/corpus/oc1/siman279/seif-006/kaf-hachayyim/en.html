6) There: It may not be moved or used during that Shabbos. This implies that after Shabbos one may use the remaining oil, just as sukkah wood and decorations may be used after the festival, as stated in siman 638. Orach Chaim asks why this differs from oil remaining from Chanukah lamps, which must be burned because it was set aside for its mitzvah, as stated in siman 677. Tosafos, Shabbos 44a, answer that the Chanukah lamp is not intended for one's benefit but to publicize the miracle. Because of affection for the miracle, one does not anticipate that it will be extinguished and sets its oil aside entirely for the mitzvah. The Shabbos lamp, by contrast, is intended for one's benefit, and one anticipates that it will be extinguished. Its remaining oil is therefore permitted. Ran writes the same at the beginning of the laws of Chanukah in the chapter Bameh Madlikin; see there. Mateh Yehudah, paragraph 2.
