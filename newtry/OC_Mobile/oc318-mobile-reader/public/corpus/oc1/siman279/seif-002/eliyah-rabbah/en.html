[Levush] He would be like a fool, etc. I am surprised, because the Gemara he cites says the halachah follows Rabbi Shimon except for muktzeh due to prohibition. This has no resemblance to the verse "a fool walks in darkness," which concerns contradictory conduct. Moreover, the question posed to the Levush is independently incorrect, because Shabbos 44 explicitly distinguishes according to Rabbi Shimon between a large lamp and a small lamp within muktzeh due to prohibition itself.
Even if it went out, etc. Shiltei Giborim writes that if there is fear of non-Jews it is permitted. Rashi, however, explains "pressing circumstances" as danger, implying that without danger it remains prohibited, as Magen Avraham notes from chapter Kirah, page 45. This requires study, because where there is actual danger it is permitted even while the lamp is still burning, just as extinguishing is permitted in the preceding siman and in the Mishnah Bameh Madlikin.
[Levush] For it became a base, etc. Taz explains that the case is where he did not definitely intend before Shabbos to remove the lamp from the table after it went out. Otherwise it would be permitted according to Sefer HaTerumot in siman 309 seif 4. This requires study. Since the lamp would ordinarily go out during the night, according to Sefer HaTerumot even the lamp itself should become permitted. It is even more difficult because below in siman 309 the Levush holds that Shulchan Aruch here follows the first, stricter opinion there. It also appears possible that here the case is an oil lamp, which cannot be moved at all during bein hashmashot even for need of its place, as above siman 277 seif 1. Even with a wax candle, perhaps Sefer HaTerumot agrees that a lamp is more thoroughly subordinated to what burns upon it than a bed or other object. Although Beit Yosef somewhat implies otherwise, Shulchan Aruch appears to hold this, which resolves Taz's question in siman 309. This still requires study.
Like a chamber pot, etc. Taz asks that if so, why seif 6 needs the separate rule that muktzeh due to repulsiveness is permitted; it should be permitted as a chamber pot. In my humble opinion there is no difficulty. Here the case is an unusually delicate person. Alternatively, seif 6 concerns something repulsive to use but not so repulsive merely to see standing before him, whereas here its presence itself is repulsive, as chapter Kirah implies. Another answer is that the rule permitting muktzeh due to repulsiveness teaches permission even in a courtyard where one does not live, where the chamber-pot rationale would not permit it, as below in siman 308 seif 34.
And one who is stringent, etc. Rama and Mordechai say "one who is stringent loses nothing." Bach, Taz, and Magen Avraham wonder that he certainly loses by failing to do what he desires, and they emend the wording. In my humble opinion there is no difficulty: it means he does not lose the mitzvah of oneg Shabbos even though the lamp is repulsive to him. Malbushei Yom Tov notes that Beit Yosef in siman 309 believes it is strictly prohibited. Bach and Magen Avraham, however, write that even one who is not unusually delicate may move it if it is repulsive to him.
