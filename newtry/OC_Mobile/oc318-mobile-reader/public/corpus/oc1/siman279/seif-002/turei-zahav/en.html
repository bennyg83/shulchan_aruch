(א) And his words do not appear. Beit Yosef gave a reason why here need of bodily use and its place does not help as with other vessels whose melacha is for prohibition, as written in siman 308 — and thus: the reason is that a vessel whose melacha is for prohibition was not set aside for bein hashemashot, for it was permitted to move for bodily need and its place; but a candle lit on bein hashemashot was forbidden to move entirely, even for bodily need and its place, for while lit it is obviously forbidden to move in every case, as we say in chapter Notel — one places a stone on a barrel mouth and coins on a pillow — one who places becomes a base for a forbidden object; therefore it was set aside for the whole day. If because of this there is no prohibition per the owner of terumot, siman 308, who holds it is not called one who places unless he intends it remain there the entire Shabbat — if so, there is permission for us, since we intend the candle not be on the table only at night and no more, and we are strict on this — it did not become a base for a forbidden object, as written in siman 277, seif 3 — behold its law is like one who forgot and was not set aside for the whole day, and it is permitted for bodily need and its place after extinguishing. And those poskim the Bach brings who appear to forbid even for bodily need and its place — it appears they speak where one places the candle in a place where he certainly does not intend to remove it after extinguishing. And based on this it appears to me there is no dispute in the matter, as Beit Yosef wrote — Mordechai and Agudah permit — they speak where he certainly intends to remove it after night and it did not become a base at all; and as written in siman 309 regarding this law.
