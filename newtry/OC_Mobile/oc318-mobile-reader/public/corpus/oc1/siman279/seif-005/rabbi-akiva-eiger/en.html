Magen Avraham, note 12. Specifically for his bodily need. In this I have room to argue — it appears from the Ran, chapter 3 of Beitzah daf 126, that their dispute between R' Yehudah and R' Shimon in muktzeh machmat mi'us is whether we say miggo it was set aside — R' Shimon holds that from the time he wishes to use this and is not concerned about its repulsiveness, its set-aside status is removed, and we do not say miggo it was set aside, see there; and if so, all the while he does not take it to use, its set-aside remains; and as R' Yehudah holds — an old candle forbidden even for its place, for it is unfit for anything — so too for R' Shimon they do not disagree except from the time he wishes to use it, for then its set-aside is removed; and therefore for its place it is forbidden all the while he does not wish to use it, for its set-aside is still upon it. And it appears that for its place it depends: all that he cannot use and is unfit for anything is forbidden for its place; but all that has a possibility of permission to use, it is permitted for its place; and see in Maor, chapter Mi SheHichshich — there was his animal; but for R' Shimon, even though he did not remove its set-aside, nevertheless since he can remove its set-aside and use it, it is permitted for its place — and so is proven from the Ran's words in what he wrote: but this spit is not so, etc., and therefore all that he wishes to use — you cannot forbid him unless you say, etc. — why must one say thus? Perhaps it is not unfit at all until he who wishes to use it nullifies his mind — perhaps in truth all muktzeh machmat mi'us, once he wishes to use it its set-aside is removed and permitted, also per R' Yehudah, since it was not set aside on bein hashemashot, and it is included in there is no muktzeh for half Shabbat; nevertheless the sugya there does not deal with one who wishes to use and its set-aside is still upon him — forbidden to move, and even per R' Shimon; or one must say the Rashbatz's words are so — that from the time of use its set-aside is removed and permitted by the law of there is no muktzeh for half Shabbat, and automatically it would be permitted for its place since if he wishes he can nullify its set-aside and use it — it is fit for him; and why need permission of casting it down due to public damage, similar to a thorn in the public domain — without this, since he needs its place for people to walk, it is permitted; thus from this it appears that in muktzeh machmat mi'us on Shabbat, where we hold like R' Shimon, from the time he wishes to use it his set-aside is nullified and it is permitted for its place even though he has not yet removed its set-aside — examine well.
