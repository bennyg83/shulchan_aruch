See there, where he proves that if it is repulsive, etc. The wording is difficult. He means that an earthenware lamp, which is repulsive, may be removed for anyone, like a chamber pot for anyone. He disputes Rema and proves it from Shabbos chapter Kol HaKelim: according to Rabbi Nechemiah, a utensil (even one used for permitted work) may be moved only for its designated use. How, then, could eating utensils be moved after a meal? The Gemara answers that they are like a chamber pot and may be moved because they are repulsive. An extinguished earthenware lamp is certainly more repulsive than used dishes: the authority who forbids a lamp due to repulsiveness nevertheless permits even soiled bowls, including where no one sits and they cannot literally be called a chamber pot. If used dishes are treated as a chamber pot, certainly an earthenware lamp is. Taz 2 explains Beis Yosef's rejection of permitting the lamp for use of its body or place: at bein hashemashos, while burning, it was a base for the flame and forbidden even for such needs, so it remains forbidden all Shabbos under siman 309:4. But according to the authorities who define deliberate placement only when intended for all Shabbos, while intent for part of Shabbos is like forgetting, a lamp intended to burn only temporarily has the law of something forgotten and may be moved for use of its body or place. Magen Avraham cites Bach in siman 276:8 that in a case of loss we rely on that view. Therefore, to prevent loss the lamp may be moved.
