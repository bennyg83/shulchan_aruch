On Shabbos it is forbidden. Since it was set aside at bein hashmashos.
By means of this bread. For the lamp becomes a base for something forbidden and something permitted, as written at the end of siman 310. If so, even while it is burning it would be permitted to move it. Nevertheless, the bread must be more significant than the oil. Furthermore, even if he placed something significant in it, this permission is still not clear, because the oil in the lamp is always more significant to him than the item he places in it, since he needs it now while it is burning. If so, even after it goes out it is forbidden. Rather, one may say that this is as though he made a condition, as written in seif 4, Beis Yosef in the name of the Rivash. This requires study, for the oil itself is forbidden only because it is a base for the flame. One must say that this is what he means: the flame is significant. This is the language of Rashi, folio 47: in such muktzeh, Rabbi Shimon concedes that the utensil is subordinate to the flame, end quote. See there in Tosafot. That is to say, it is known that the lamp is subordinate to the flame and is made for that purpose, and it is not made for the bread; therefore bread does not help. See what I wrote in siman 277, seif 3.
