1) [Seif 1] A lamp that was kindled, etc. If a person's eyes hurt, the pain intensifies, and the lamplight harms him, he may not move the lamp and bring it to another room. Admas Kodesh, volume 2, siman 1; Birkei Yosef, paragraph 1; Shaarei Teshuvah, paragraph 1. See, however, Responsa Sedeh HaAretz, volume 3, siman 15, which permits even the Jewish person himself to move it in an unusual manner; see there. Zechor LeAvraham, Hilchos Shabbos, paragraph 6, cites this.
2) There: Although it was extinguished, it may not be moved. This follows the principle that since it was set aside during twilight, it remains set aside for the entire day, even after it is extinguished. Although Rabbi Shimon permits it because he does not apply this principle even to muktzeh resulting from a prohibition, we do not follow Rabbi Shimon in this matter. Beis Yosef, who writes that Rif, Rosh, and Rambam rule this way; see there. Tur writes the same in siman 310.
3) There: It may not be moved. If there is fear of non-Jews, it may be moved after it is extinguished. Shiltei Gibborim. Rashi's commentary, however, implies that it is forbidden where there is no danger. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 1, writes that this does not mean literal mortal danger, because in that case it would be permitted even while the lamp is burning and even when the danger is uncertain, as stated in siman 278. Rather, the circumstances are somewhat close to danger; see there. Machatzis HaShekel writes the same. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1, writes that 'danger' does not specifically mean danger to life, but includes the prospect that a non-Jew will strike him, financial loss, and the like.
4) There: Likewise, the oil remaining in the lamp, etc. The reason is that while the lamp burns, one may not use its oil because doing so extinguishes the lamp. Since it was set aside during twilight, it remains set aside for the entire day. Beis Yosef in the name of Ran. Accordingly, on Yom Tov one may use the remnant of a wax candle, as stated in siman 514, seif 3, in Magen Avraham, paragraph 7. The Gemara, however, gives another reason: it was set aside for its mitzvah, as stated in siman 638 regarding sukkah decorations. Therefore, a mitzvah lamp kindled for Yom Tov and then extinguished may not be used for another purpose. For example, if it is made of tallow, one may not eat it. Magen Avraham, paragraph 2; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 2. It may, however, be moved. Machatzis HaShekel.
5) There: Likewise, the remaining oil, etc. The wick likewise becomes a base for the flame, and what remains of it may not be moved throughout that Shabbos. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 2.
6) There: It may not be moved or used during that Shabbos. This implies that after Shabbos one may use the remaining oil, just as sukkah wood and decorations may be used after the festival, as stated in siman 638. Orach Chaim asks why this differs from oil remaining from Chanukah lamps, which must be burned because it was set aside for its mitzvah, as stated in siman 677. Tosafos, Shabbos 44a, answer that the Chanukah lamp is not intended for one's benefit but to publicize the miracle. Because of affection for the miracle, one does not anticipate that it will be extinguished and sets its oil aside entirely for the mitzvah. The Shabbos lamp, by contrast, is intended for one's benefit, and one anticipates that it will be extinguished. Its remaining oil is therefore permitted. Ran writes the same at the beginning of the laws of Chanukah in the chapter Bameh Madlikin; see there. Mateh Yehudah, paragraph 2.
