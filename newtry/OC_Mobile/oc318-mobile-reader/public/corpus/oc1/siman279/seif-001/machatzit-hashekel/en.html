Paragraph 1: It is forbidden, etc. For at bein hashemashos it was forbidden, etc. When they were lit, the lamp, oil, and wick became a base for the flame, which is a forbidden object.
If there is fear, etc. Shiltei HaGibborim, chapter Kol HaKelim. Its source is Shabbos 45a, where the Gemara challenges the statement that Rav follows Rabbi Yehudah, who accepts muktzeh: Rav Kahana and Rav Ashi asked Rav whether a Chanukah lamp may be moved on Shabbos, after it went out, because of the Chavrei. The Chavrei nation decreed that no lamp be kept in any home except their idolatrous temple, or decreed religious persecution against lighting Chanukah lamps, and Rav permitted moving it. This apparently shows Rav follows Rabbi Shimon. The Gemara answers that he indeed follows Rabbi Yehudah, but duress is different. Shiltei HaGibborim proves from this that although we rule like Rabbi Yehudah that an extinguished lamp may not be moved, it may be moved under duress when gentiles are feared, relying then on Rabbi Shimon. Magen Avraham writes that Rashi disagrees, since Rashi explains duress as danger, implying ordinary duress is insufficient. Yet Rashi's 'time of danger' cannot mean actual mortal danger, since nothing stands before saving life; why would Rav Kahana and Rav Ashi have questioned it, when even extinguishing would be permitted? Rather, it means a situation close to danger. Tevuos Shor explains similarly; see there for his challenge to Magen Avraham's understanding of Rashi.
