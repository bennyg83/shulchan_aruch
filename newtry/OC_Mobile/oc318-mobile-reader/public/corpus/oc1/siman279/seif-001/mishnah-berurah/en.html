(א) Even though it was extinguished, etc. — meaning even if no oil remains at all; and the reason: since on bein hashemashot it was forbidden in moving, because the candle and oil and wick became a base for the flame, which is a forbidden object in moving, and from the fact that it was set aside for bein hashemashot it was set aside for the whole day — and this is called in the Gemara muktzeh machmat issur, for which we hold to forbid. If there is fear of a gentile upon him through the candle [as in the time of the Amoraim when there was one nation strict on their festival about Jews lighting a candle that day, and all such cases], it is permitted to move it after it is extinguished [Semag, and so agreed in Turei Zahav, unlike Magen Avraham and Peri Megadim — it appears that if he fears being struck or other monetary loss, for all it is permitted].
(ב) (b) The oil in the candle is permitted, and "the candle made of milk and groves that lighted it on the same Shabbat and the same flame."
(ג) To move it and use it, etc. — for since it was set aside at the beginning of Shabbat's entry — namely on bein hashemashot when it was lit it was forbidden to use it, for one who uses oil from the candle is liable for extinguishing, as above in siman 261, 265 — it was set aside for the whole day not to use it; and since it is forbidden to benefit from it all Shabbat day, it is also forbidden to move it, for it is unfit for anything. Besides this, in moving itself it was set aside on bein hashemashot when everything became a base for the flame, as above at the beginning of note 1.
