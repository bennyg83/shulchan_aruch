They say Retzei. On Taz: the Levush omitted the third explanation because of this difficulty — for included in "service" is everything: the service of the Levites, the Menorah, and the fire-offerings. And on the first explanation Taz explained: "and the fire-offerings of Israel" is prayer in its time as sacrifice and not in its time; and "and their prayer" — the Bach explained: "and their prayer" through voluntary offerings, additional offerings, and musafin. And Eliyah Rabbah wrote that it refers to other prayers — see there. And there is a difficulty: "willingly" applies to the prayer of the eighteen [blessings] in place of a sacrifice, but not to other prayers and a person's needs — it does not apply willingly. Furthermore Eliyah Rabbah wrote in the name of Matanot Kehunah that one should not say the word "mehera" per the middle explanation — the souls of the righteous — and they complete thirty-seven words (thirty-four should be said) with the word "et ha'avoda," and thus he upholds the reading of "mehera"; and therefore one should not say "et." See Tur: Retzei — there are those who omit it in Minchah. And per the reason of the Bach — that the kohanim knew when they ascend to the duchan, that is, in repetition it is impossible; but in silent prayer they said it, and we say it even in the repetition of the shaliach tzibur. And if one erred and began in Minchah with "and the fire-offerings of Israel," he returns as in siman 119, Magen Avraham ot 5. And it is implied in Beit Yosef that now in all places they say Retzei in Minchah; if so, this is a change from the coin that Chazal minted, and if intentionally he returns to the beginning of the prayer. And one can say that "and the fire-offerings of Israel" on a fast day are the fat and blood that are diminished on the fast day from natural heat, etc. — it appears to me.
