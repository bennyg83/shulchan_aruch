Retzeh, etc. 'And the fire offerings of Israel,' means the sacrifices. Although there is presently no Temple service, prayer stands in place of a sacrifice, as the Tur writes. The Beit Yosef explains that prayer is what stands in place of the fire offerings of Israel. It seems the difficulty is that, if so, why repeat 'the fire offerings of Israel and their prayer,' since the fire offerings of Israel would themselves mean their prayer? I would answer that 'their prayer' refers to other prayers that are not the Amidah, which are also called prayer, as I wrote above at the end of siman 110. The Tur further cites a Midrash that Michael, the great ministering angel, offers the souls of the righteous upon the heavenly altar, and that this is the basis for 'the fire offerings of Israel.' Others explain that 'the fire offerings of Israel' belongs to the preceding phrase, 'restore, etc.,' while 'and their prayer' begins a new clause. Mateh Moshe writes that some texts omit 'speedily,' as does Rabbi Yehudah of Worms, because if Michael offers the souls of the righteous one should not say 'speedily.' To complete the count of thirty seven words, corresponding to the thirty seven kohanim who brought the bull and the goat, with the officiating kohen corresponding to the chazzan, they instead say 'restore the service.' In my humble opinion our text with 'speedily' can be upheld. First, the Tur gives two additional interpretations, as stated, and Abudarham cites only those two and also reads 'speedily,' as does the Rokeach. Furthermore, Tosafot at the end of Menachot 110a reads it this way even though they cite that interpretation. Certainly the meaning is fixed that Michael offers them, and we pray only regarding acceptance, that the Holy One, blessed be He, accept them speedily. This is not at all a prayer, God forbid, for the death of the righteous. It is also reasonable that he offers souls of righteous people who died many years before. Moreover, Tosafot cite an interpretation that he offers lambs of fire.
