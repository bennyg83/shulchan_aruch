1) [Seif 1] One says Retzei, etc. And as for that which we say 'v'ishei Yisrael u'sefilasam tekabel b'ratzon' [and the fire-offerings of Israel and their prayer accept with favor], even though we have no korbanos now such that we should pray about them that they be accepted with favor, the Tur wrote that we pray about the tefillah, which is in place of the korban, that it be accepted with favor before Hashem, blessed be He. And the Beis Yosef wrote that this is its meaning: 'v'ishei Yisrael u'sefilasam' means the prayer of Israel, which is in place of the fire-offerings, which are the korbanos, accept with favor. End quote. And the Tosafos at the end of Menachos wrote, based on what is stated there in the Gemara on 'This is the eternal law for Israel': this refers to the built altar, and Michael, the great prince, stands and offers a korban upon it; and this is its language: The midrashim are divided: there is one who says the souls of the righteous, and there is one who says lambs of fire; and this is what we say in the Shemoneh Esreh, in the berachah of Avodah, 'v'ishei Yisrael,' etc. End quote. And the Tur brought only the first 'there is one who says,' and the Beis Yosef wrote that this is its meaning: 'v'ishei Yisrael' means, as it were, the people of Israel who are offered by Michael; or it may be from the language of 'isheh, a pleasing aroma,' meaning the korbanos of Israel, namely their souls, which are themselves offered by Michael; and also the prayer of Israel, all of it accept with favor. End quote. And the Tosafos there wrote yet another explanation, and the Tur cited it, namely that it connects to what precedes it: 'and restore the service to the Sanctuary of Your House, and the fire-offerings of Israel.' And the Beis Yosef wrote that this means: according to the first two explanations, 'v'ishei Yisrael' is joined with 'u'sefilasam' that comes after it; and according to this explanation, 'v'ishei Yisrael' is joined with 'and restore the service to the Sanctuary of Your House,' that is to say: restore the service and the fire-offerings of Israel, meaning their korbanos, to the Sanctuary of Your House; and afterward he begins and says: and their prayer, of Israel, speedily and with love accept with favor. End quote. And see the Taz, s.k. 1, who raised difficulties against the first and last explanations and wrote that the middle one is the choicest of them all; see there. However, the Pesach HaDvir, ot 2, wrote concerning the difficulty the Taz raised against the last explanation, namely: why need it say 'v'ishei Yisrael' at all, once it mentioned 'the service'; and if you will say that there is service without fire-offerings, at any rate it should not have interposed 'to the Sanctuary of Your House' between them, but should have said 'and restore the service and the fire-offerings of Israel to the Sanctuary of Your House': this is not a compelling inference, for we find in Scripture the like of it, in Tehillim 25, 'Remember Your mercies, Hashem, and Your kindnesses, for they are from eternity,' which is as though it were written 'Remember Your mercies and Your kindnesses, Hashem,' as is evident from the commentary of the Radak there; and here too it is similar, as though it said 'and restore the service and the fire-offerings of Israel to the Sanctuary of Your House.' End of his words. And so too the Gra wrote, that the last explanation is the essential one, and the Mishnah Berurah cited him at the end of ot 1.
