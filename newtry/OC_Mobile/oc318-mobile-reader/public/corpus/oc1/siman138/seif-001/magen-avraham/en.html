One should not leave [only two verses before a parashah]. Meaning: one should not stop reading two verses before a parashah in the Torah, because people will say that therefore he did not read until the parashah — that his intent is to read one more until the end of the parashah. And it appears to me that when there is a parashah in the middle of a verse — such as at the beginning of Pinchas — it is permitted to end even in the verse adjacent to it, for there is no reason to say that the second reader will read until the parashah, since it is known that one does not end in the middle of a verse even though there is a parashah there. And if one erred and ended two verses before the parashah, he should afterward begin one verse retroactively [Agudah]. And in Hagahot Maimoniyot he wrote: where the topic concludes — such as the parashah of the Festivals — and one erred on the fourth [aliyah] and left only two verses, then he reads with the fifth one verse retroactively, since it is impossible to read it more than that. Thus Tosafot imply in general that one reads two from this parashah and three from another parashah, as in the Gemara there, which answers that that case is different where it is possible; and one could say that the parashah is only five verses, and if he begins retroactively there will be two verses after the parashah — for the first Tanna is not concerned for this on Shabbat, but rather in any case we do not skip because of this. And the reason is that we are not concerned for those leaving who will say that the second reader will read only two verses, for it is forbidden to leave in the middle of the reading except between one reader and another. And what Tosafot wrote, s.v. iy ita, that we are concerned for those entering who will say it is permitted to begin two verses adjacent to a parashah — that is as the one who asks holds on Shabbat; and even according to Ran, who is concerned for this, that is because they did not want to fix lekatchila to read thus, as written in siman 523. But where an error occurred, they did not enact to skip because of this, as the Gemara concludes on Shabbat — the reason being that error is uncommon. And if so, likewise if one erred and finished two verses after the parashah, one does not begin retroactively — see end of siman 137.

One should not begin [two verses after a parashah]. Meaning: one should not finish leaving only one [reader with] two verses after the parashah, for then the second will begin two verses after the parashah and those entering will say that the first did not read except two verses — for otherwise why did he not finish when he reached the parashah. And the same applies if at the end of the order there is a parashah of five verses — the maftir should not begin two verses after the parashah but rather read five verses (Rokeach, Laws of Shabbat). And likewise in Tosafot Megillah at the beginning of daf 22 regarding vehikravtem. And even though all know that the maftir begins retroactively, nevertheless in the days of the Talmud the maftir began at the place where the first ended, as Rosh wrote, and it was forbidden to begin two verses after the parashah; even now the enactment was not nullified. And also there is concern that those entering did not know he was the maftir. It is written in Darkei Moshe in the name of Azriel: with the kohen it is permitted to begin — and so they practice to begin Vayechal, which is two verses adjacent to the parashah — end of his words; and likewise Agudah. And in Mordechai it is written: these parshiot are known and everyone knows that no man read before him, and there is no concern — end of his words. And this applies to say regarding vehikravtem, which is two verses after the parashah, that there is no concern; and likewise Vayechal. And in Tosafot Megillah daf 22 they wrote: these parshiot are different, for they are known forever and there is no error. And likewise the parashah of Rosh Chodesh that the third reads — and uvayom hashabbat — there is no concern that those entering will say the third did not read except two verses of uvayom hashabbat, for the matter is known forever; and also they have to think that otherwise he should not have stopped when he reached the parashah, for this too is a kind of parashah that he left in it — end of his words. And it requires study: if so, what the Gemara asks and discusses on Rosh Chodesh — how do they act? They should have fixed that on Rosh Chodesh they leave two verses adjacent to the parashah and the matter would be known forever. And it appears to me that only from those two reasons is it permitted. And further, I will add to wonder on Taz, who wrote that a parashah that is only two verses — it is permitted to stop, as the Shulchan Aruch writes here, and he brings proofs from uvayom hashabbat and from the words of Tosafot here — it implies that only uvayom hashabbat is permitted, since the matter is known forever; and it requires study. And it is possible to say that the intent of Tosafot is also thus — that the matter is known since there is a parashah in the Torah here, it is appropriate to end here; for when one finished in a place that is not a parashah, one comes to say why he did not finish at the parashah unless he started at the parashah and read two verses; but not so here, where he finishes at a parashah in the Torah — all know that in the way to end there. And further they have to think that otherwise, if it were permitted to read two verses, he should not have stopped with the second when he reached uvayom hashabbat, but should have read until uverashei chodsheichem and afterward read the third and fourth — unless it is forbidden to read two verses. And it appears to me that the intent of Tosafot is: from where would they come to say he did not read except two verses, since he has more to read — for behold the entire parasha and uverashei chodsheichem is still before him, which is from the topic of the day, and there is nothing to read there except the fourth; and from where would he stop there unless he had already read three verses, as stated. However, in our Torah scroll there is no parashah adjacent to Vayechal nor adjacent to vehikravtem — and likewise in Semag and the Nekudan in Hagahot Semag and in responsum of Maharil siman 105. And based on this one can resolve what is found in old siddurim to end with the kohen in the order V'atah tetzaveh one verse after the parashah — there is nothing to decree, for all know the kohen begins from the head of the order. And now new ones recently came and changed the parashah from its place and did not scrutinize well. And also in parashat Vayelech they end with b'ozneichem even though it is two verses adjacent to the parashah — for all know that after the third nothing is read on a weekday, unlike new siddurim that added two verses.

On a good thing. And Heaven’s Word is not a good word for Heaven’s Word, and Heaven’s Word is a blessing

And he should end [with something good]. And the same applies not to end with one who did an evil deed (Rambam end of Yadayim). One who is blind or lame — one should not call him to parashat Orat or Shebur; and likewise all similar cases so he will not be shamed. And likewise when the chazzan reads the blessings, he should not intend to bless his fellow (Shulchan Aruch siman 510).
