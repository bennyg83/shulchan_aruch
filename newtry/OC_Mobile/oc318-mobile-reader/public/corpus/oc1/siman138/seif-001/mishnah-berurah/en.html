(d) It is permitted to leave at the beginning — meaning to leave [verses] adjacent to the parashah, and one need not be concerned because of those leaving who will say that the reader after him will read only two verses; for whenever we do not see a change we are not concerned. Specifically, if he leaves two verses in the parashah at which this reader ends — that is a change and leads to suspicion; but not so when he completes the parashah at which he stands — there is no change here. And the same applies that it is permitted to read this parashah [together with what was read before it] and stop after it, and we are not concerned lest those entering afterward say he read only this parashah alone, which is two verses — for specifically in general when one stops in the middle of a parashah we are concerned, lest those entering say he presumably read only two verses — for otherwise why did he read below and not finish at the end of the parashah; but not so here, where in truth he finished at the end of the parashah — there is nothing to concern us. And there are those who are stringent in this that one should not do so lekatchila except in the Rosh Chodesh reading of uvayom hashabbat for the third [reader] [together with what the previous readers read — three verses from the previous parashah], which has only two verses. But when they read the weekly parashah in the Pinchas order, one should not stop after uvayom hashabbat lekatchila; and the same in other places when a small parashah occurred.
