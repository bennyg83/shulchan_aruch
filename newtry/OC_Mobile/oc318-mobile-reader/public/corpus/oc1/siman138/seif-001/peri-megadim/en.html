And the sign of Heaven’s Word is a sign of Heaven’s Word

And know that in Turei Zahav siman 22 he proved that there is no distinction, etc.; and a parashah of two verses — it is permitted to leave at the beginning — from Megillah 22a: uverashei chodsheichem — resolve the two [verses], etc.; and in the fourteenth of Pesach it is closed, etc. It is somewhat difficult for me, for there he does not finish and one does not leave, etc.; and the question is that the order concludes — and Rashi there, s.v. likri, etc. — his intent: and one does not leave; and apparently he does not hold like Tosafot, s.v. she'ani uvayom hashabbat; and if so, for us who rule like Tosafot on uvayom hashabbat, there is no proof. Nevertheless, Rashi also concedes to Tosafot on uvayom hashabbat. Also what Turei Zahav wrote that one may leave a parashah of two verses — see Peri Chadash 523: meaning specifically to leave; but to read the parashah of two verses — one is not permitted; and so it is proved from Tosafot, s.v. ein matchilin, on uvayom hashabbat; and even though those leaving are more of a concern than those entering (for one who is stringent regarding those leaving is lenient regarding those entering — ask, ask on Shabbat in the Gemara) — nevertheless here there is some proof: why did he not finish at the previous parashah? And if so, our custom on Rosh Chodesh that the third also reads uvayom hashabbat is likewise [permitted]; and it is somewhat difficult. And one can say that the fourth [reader], who has the obligation of the day, reads only what pertains to Rosh Chodesh and begins uverashei chodsheichem alone. In Chiddushei Megillah we expanded on this; and see there in siman 523; and Magen Avraham here from this.

See the Gemara and the the Master Heaven in the name of Heaven’s Word, “If there is a mistake in the Shabbat, and the name of the the Master will come to pass, and there will be a sign of the the Master.”

If they did not read the obligation of the day in the Festivals and Rosh Chodesh — plainly they must read [it] — except Chanukah, see siman 137 in Magen Avraham note 16, and in siman 684 seif 3. And if they read on Yom Tov the entire order, the fifth [reader] repeats and reads what was already read; and even for us there is a maftir following the enactment of Rav Amram.
