[1] He should not leave in the section, etc. It appears to me that if he transgressed and already recited the concluding blessing, he need not return and read more. This is also implied by Megillah 22a, which states that if a section contains five verses and the first reader read three, the second reads two from that section and three from the following section. It further appears that the next reader need not begin with an earlier verse. If the concern is for those entering, they see that he reads three verses; if the concern is for those leaving, his correction is ineffective, for they have already left. The cited Gemara, which says that the second reads two, is no proof, because there the section contains only five verses, and if he began one verse earlier he would begin within fewer than three verses of the section break. However, when the first reader concludes after two verses from the beginning of a section, such as where he began before the section began, it would seem at first that the second must begin from the beginning of the section because of the concern for those entering. Upon reflection, however, this is comparable to the levi on Rosh Chodesh who begins two verses after the beginning of the section, where the reason, as explained below in siman 423, is that anyone entering will ask. The rule that one should not begin within fewer than three verses of a section break appears to mean that the first reader should not conclude after two verses in a manner that would cause the second to begin within fewer than three verses of the section, or that one should not begin at the outset of the reading in such a position, as in Vayechal and Vehikravtem, which Tosafos discuss there. But here, where the first reader already read those two verses, we do not say that the second must return to the beginning of the section. So it appears to me, though this requires study. I later saw that Magen Avraham inclines this way. Shiurei Knesses HaGedolah writes that if an error is found in the Sefer Torah and another Torah is brought, even if only one verse remains, they read only what remains and are not concerned for those entering. Magen Avraham writes that where a section break occurs in the middle of a verse, as at the beginning of Pinchas, one may conclude even with the verse adjacent to it, since it is known that one does not conclude in the middle of a verse. Old siddurim likewise instruct concluding the kohen’s reading in Ve’Atah Tetzaveh one verse after the section break, for everyone knows that the kohen begins at the start of the sidrah. In Parashas Vayeilech too, they conclude with “in their ears,” although it is two verses before a section break, for everyone knows that no one reads after the third aliyah on a weekday. This is unlike newer siddurim that changed the placements. This differs from Responsa Nachalas Shivah, siman 23. Tosafos, s.v. shani, and Hagahos Ashri, chapter 3 of Megillah, write that if the reader before the last one on Pesach or Shavuos read until only two verses remained for the final reader, the final reader should go back and begin one verse earlier. He should not read two verses from that section and a third from a following section that is unrelated to the day. Chiddushei Halachos writes that this does not apply on Chol HaMoed Sukkos, where everything read concerns the day, since each day they read additional passages because of doubt regarding the day.
