Regarding cantors who read Torah on weekdays and Shabbat Mincha and end parshiyot as marked in the prayers — kohen, Levi, Israel — and sometimes end one verse before or after an open or closed parashah: know they err and do not act properly; I too who write rebuked them, sometimes in parashat Shoftim ending the parashah "the kohen whom you shall make" between two closed parshiyot — it should end at the verse before "which He gave you," as the Gemara teaches in the chapter HaKorei, etc. — Rav says skip (one verse retroactively); we hold like Rav; even on weekdays one may not begin or leave fewer than three verses — as David of Krakow wrote. Hagahot of R' David Tzimels of Krakow: I found a question whether one who cannot recite haftarah may be called to the Torah relying that another will say haftarah. Answer: forbidden — Beit Yosef siman 282 cites Rivash that if only one of those who went up knows haftarah and the shatz already said Kaddish, the one who wants to be maftir must return and read and bless for honor of Torah, lest those entering not see him read; if the maftir is the same as the last reader one might argue no return needed despite Kaddish interruption, but when another of the oleh is maftir he certainly must return and bless — complete proof one may not call maftir one who cannot recite haftarah; better call one who has not yet gone up even if he cannot haftarah and let one who went up haftarah — concern of those entering and leaving applies; Levush ruled leniently perhaps following Beit Yosef superficially — l'chatchila do not call one known unable to haftarah himself; b'dieved if they did not know and called him, he must go up as one called to Torah who does not go up shortens his days — he must go up and another says haftarah after. This is my humble opinion.
