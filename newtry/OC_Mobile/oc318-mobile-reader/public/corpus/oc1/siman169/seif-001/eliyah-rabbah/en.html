[1] Levushei Serad: Immediately to the attendant. Meaning, a small amount. Magen Avraham. This implies that even when the food has an aroma, a small amount suffices, and this is also somewhat implied in Kesubos 61a, where it says that he took some with his finger and placed it in his mouth. Magen Avraham writes that this applies specifically when the attendant stands and serves during the meal, but if he reclines at the table there is no concern. In siman 170, seif katan 8, he writes that this applies even if he does not serve during the meal but merely cooks the food, and even if his food is not the employer's responsibility. It appears that when he reclines at the table one need not give him immediately, but it is certainly improper not to give him anything at all. This requires study. Shelah, page 82, writes: It appears to me that if, at the outset when hiring him, one stipulates that he will not give him from the foods he eats, then even as an act of piety one need not give him from every kind, but only from a kind that has an aroma and that a person desires. We find similarly regarding a Hebrew servant that they said one may not have him put on his shoes, etc., yet we do all such things with our servants because of the stipulation. Magen Avraham holds that a stipulation is ineffective with regard to food because of the servant's distress. He further writes that our servants are treated like free persons. Sifrei expounds: “You may not rule over him ruthlessly,” but you may rule over a free person ruthlessly.
