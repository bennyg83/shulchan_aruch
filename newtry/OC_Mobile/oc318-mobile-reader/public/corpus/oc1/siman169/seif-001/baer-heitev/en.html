And from each kind. If, at the time of hiring him, he stipulated that he would provide him with food like the other members of the household, he need not give him from every kind, but only from a kind that has an aroma. For this reason, we are not accustomed to give the attendant from every kind. We also allow servants to carry our clothing after us to the bathhouse and to put on our shoes, because it is as though this was stipulated. Shelah. Magen Avraham writes: It appears to me that with regard to food, a stipulation is ineffective, since the reason is the servant's distress. This applies specifically to an attendant who stands and serves during the meal, as Rashi writes. If he reclines at the table, however, there is no concern. See there. In siman 170, seif katan 8, he writes that this applies even if he does not serve at the meal but merely cooks the food, and even if his food is not the employer's responsibility.
In such a case. The host does not object if one guest gives to another, since he invited them all. It is forbidden, however, to give to one whom he did not invite. Magen Avraham.
