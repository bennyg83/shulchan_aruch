1) [Seif 1] Anything that is brought, etc., one must give some of it to the attendant, etc. For we say in Kesubos 61a: all foods may be kept waiting before the attendant, except fatty meat throughout the year and aged wine during the season of Tammuz. The Gemara concludes that the general rule is that anything with an aroma or pungency must be given to him. Rashi, of blessed memory, explains: all foods may be kept waiting before the attendant serving at the meal. The diners may eat before him and delay feeding him until the guests arise, except for fatty meat, etc., which he craves and whose sight causes him distress. During the season of Tammuz, aged wine has a strong aroma, and the heat of the day burns within him like venom. The general rule is that anything with an aroma or pungency causes aching of the teeth and harms one before whom others eat while he does not eat. Avuh bar Ihi and Minyamin bar Ihi acted differently. One fed his attendant from every type of food, while the other fed him from only one type. Eliyahu would speak with one but would not speak with the other. There were likewise two pious men. One fed the attendant early, while the other delayed feeding him. Eliyahu would speak with the one who fed early but would not speak with the one who delayed. The Tur and Beit Yosef cite this.
