(1) That has an aroma. Or acidity, meaning a sour taste. Gemara.
(2) He must give, etc. Something with an aroma harms a person when others eat it in his presence, he desires it, and they do not give it to him immediately. Something without an aroma, however, may first be eaten, and afterward one gives some to the attendant, as explained below in siman 170, seif 3.
(3) To the attendant immediately. A small amount is sufficient, because that too settles his mind. The later authorities write that even if one stipulated with his servant at the time of hiring him that he would be exempt from this obligation, the stipulation is ineffective. This applies specifically to an attendant who stands and serves and does not eat together with them. If he too reclines at the table with them, there is no need to give him first. The Gemara states that if one smells food and desires it but cannot obtain it, he should not swallow the saliva that enters his mouth because of his desire for the food, since this may endanger him. Rather, he should spit it out.
(4) From every kind. Meaning, even a food that has neither aroma nor acidity, because he nevertheless experiences distress when he sees others eating and he does not eat.
(5) And he should not give him. This refers to one of the diners.
(6) In his hand. In the attendant's hand, lest because he is occupied taking the slice, he spill the cup he is bringing to the table. Some explain that even when the cup is in a guest's hand, one should not give the attendant at that moment, lest the guest notice that the host is looking at him disapprovingly, out of concern that there may not be enough for the other guests, causing him to tremble and spill the cup.
(7) In the host's hand. As above, lest the host object and, in his anger, spill the cup, causing disorder at the meal. Some say that even the host himself should not give to the attendant while the cup is in the attendant's hand, for the same reason: because the attendant is occupied taking the slice, he may spill the cup, causing disorder and embarrassment to those reclining at the meal.
(8) To give in such a case. Meaning, the other diners may give to one another while the cup is in the host's hand. Since he invited them, he does not object when they give to one another, and we are not concerned about disorder. Rabbeinu Yonah. This proves that it is forbidden to give to one whom he did not invite. Later authorities.
(9) To give him, etc. Meaning, to the attendant, because we are concerned that due to his preoccupation he may forget to wash his hands. Regarding another person, we are not concerned unless one sees that he wishes to eat without washing. Some are stringent even in that case. When one gives the food as charity, however, one may certainly be lenient, as explained below in the gloss to seif 2.
