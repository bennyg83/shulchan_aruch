To the attendant immediately. Meaning, a small amount. This is also implied by seif 3 and siman 170, seif 3. If, at the time of hiring him, one stipulated that he would provide him with food like the other members of the household, he need not give him from every kind, but only from a kind that has an aroma. For this reason we are not accustomed to give the attendant from every kind. We also allow servants to carry our clothing after us to the bathhouse and to put on our shoes, because it is as though this was stipulated. Shelah. Sifrei, however, expounds: “You may not rule over him ruthlessly,” but you may rule over a free person ruthlessly. Our servants are considered like free persons, since they may withdraw in the middle of the day and do not have the status of slaves. It appears to me that with regard to food, a stipulation is ineffective, since the reason is the servant's distress. This applies specifically to an attendant who stands and serves during the meal, as Rashi writes. If he reclines at the table, however, there is no concern. So it appears to me.
In his hand. Meaning, in the attendant's hand, for because he is occupied with the slice, the cup may spill. Some explain that it means while the cup is in the guest's hand, lest the host look at the guest with concern that there will not be enough for the other guests, causing the guest to spill the cup.
In the host's hand. Lest the host look intently and spill the cup.
It is permitted to give. The host does not object if one guest gives to another, since he invited them all. This implies that it is forbidden to give to one whom he did not invite.
Who washed his hands. This implies that specifically regarding an attendant we are concerned that because he is occupied he will forget to wash. This is also implied by Rashi. Rabbeinu Yonah, however, implies that this concern applies even to another person. See seif 2.
