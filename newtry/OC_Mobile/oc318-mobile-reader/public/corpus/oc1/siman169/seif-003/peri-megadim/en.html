As. Charity. See Taz. Rabbeinu Yonah discusses a doubt whether he will recite a blessing, and Taz explains that the same applies when there is doubt whether he will wash his hands. When it is given as charity, it is permitted, because a doubt does not override a certainty, and if one warns them they may become angry. This differs from an attendant. Bach, however, distinguishes between handwashing and a blessing. See Magen Avraham, letter 6, where this will be explained, with Hashem's help. Although the prohibition of “before the blind” here is Rabbinic, since handwashing and the blessing are themselves Rabbinic, we do not rule leniently in a doubt of this kind. See siman 163 and Magen Avraham there, seif katan 2, and it will be explained further in Magen Avraham, with Hashem's help.
