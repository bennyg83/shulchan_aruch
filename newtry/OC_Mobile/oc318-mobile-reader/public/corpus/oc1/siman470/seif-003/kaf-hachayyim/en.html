3) According to this, a child delivered by cesarean section and the child born afterward, neither of whom is considered firstborn for inheritance or redemption, as explained in Yoreh De'ah, siman 305, and Choshen Mishpat, siman 277, need not fast. Nevertheless, the practical law regarding the child delivered by cesarean section requires consideration. Perhaps that exclusion applies only there because Scripture specifically limits the laws: regarding inheritance it states, 'They bear him children,' while regarding redemption it states, 'The first issue of the womb.' Here, however, the fast commemorates the miracle of rescue from the plague of the firstborn, and such a child is ordinarily called a firstborn. The matter requires consideration. Chok Yaakov, there. In practice, since this is merely a concern that the person might be obligated to fast, one need not fast because of doubt, as stated below in subsections 6 and 7. See there.
