(א) Firstborns fast — as a remembrance of the miracle that they were saved from the plague of the firstborn.
(ב) Whether firstborn of father or mother — the plague of firstborns was on all of them, as stated in the Midrash; nevertheless, the eldest of the household was not required to fast, even though the plague was also upon them. One born after a miscarriage must fast, for he is a firstborn for inheritance; and this is specifically when the miscarriage was certain. But one born after a son of nine months who died within thirty days need not fast. Firstborn kohanim and leviim must also fast, even if they were only firstborns from the mother and are not firstborns for inheritance or redemption — nevertheless they are called firstborn. Maharil wrote that the fast of firstborns must be completed (until tzeit hakochavim, as in siman 249), for it is a public fast. It appears that if one has headache or eye pain, one need not fast. It also appears that if one for whom fasting is difficult cannot eat afterward except light things in very small quantity, it is better not to fast so he can fulfill matzah, maror, and drinking the four cups properly; nevertheless, in both cases it is better he eat only light foods. Firstborns say Aneinu in Shema Koleinu at Minchah when fasting; if ten firstborns pray together and one is shaliach tzibur when repeating the prayer, he says Aneinu in Shema Koleinu as the law of an individual fast — nevertheless l'chatchila the firstborn should not be shaliach tzibur when ten pray together, for some say it is not proper to mention the fast publicly in repetition of the prayer, since it is the month of Nisan.
(ג) Even a firstborn daughter — the plague of firstborns was also upon them, as stated in the Midrash.
(ד) And the custom is not so — for the Torah did not give sanctity of firstborn status to a female for any matter.
