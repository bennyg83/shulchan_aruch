From the mother. Baer Heitev. And he wrote in Shiyurei Shevet siman 17: one to whom a firstborn son was born on the night of the 14th — if born before midnight, the father must fast for him; but after midnight he does not fast, since the fast is remembrance of the salvation of the firstborns of Israel, and on those born after midnight the decree was not, even on Egyptian firstborns; and he brought proof from the beginning of Avot Rabba: "And it was at midnight," etc., and it is written "on the day the Egyptian," etc. — R' Yehuda said: even though they were struck at midnight, their souls were fluttering in them until morning. And if so, let us interpret "on the day the Egyptian" — those born from midnight onward; if so, on them there was no decree at all, and the first year will be clear, for in any case one need not be stringent in this custom, as Kaf HaChayyim wrote — see there. And in Beit Rachel he questioned this per his view that on a firstborn day-old his father fasts on erev Pesach — if so, what difference if born after midnight of erev Pesach night, since the plague of firstborns was on Pesach night? If so, also on one like this born after midnight of the 14th night there was the decree, only they advance the fast because it is impossible on Yom Tov, etc. But Korban Netanel in any case disagrees with Shevut Yaakov on this and holds that whenever the firstborn is not yet thirty days, one need not fast for him, since he has not left the category of doubtful miscarriage; and is it logical there should be more obligation on the father than redeeming his firstborn son, etc. — and a stringency that leads to leniency is that he fasts in Nisan on Yom Tov without need — end of his words. And in Birkei Yosef he wrote to advocate for Shevut Yaakov: redemption of firstborn is different, for thus said the Merciful One, etc., and one need not be concerned for Nisan and Yom Tov, for so is practiced for publicizing the miracle — see there. And it appears certainly there is no proof from firstborn, as Birkei Yosef wrote; unlike here, where the majority of offspring are not miscarriages, therefore he should fast, for the fast protects him and is remembrance of the salvation of the firstborns of Israel, as stated. And what Shevut Yaakov wrote similarly in Dagul MeRevavah — and see in Shev Yaakov: miscarriage within three months and the form of the offspring is recognizable — the one born after him is firstborn for inheritance and must fast on erev Pesach, as Magen Avraham wrote — see there.
