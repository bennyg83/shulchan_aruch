From the mother. See Ba'er Heitev. Shevut Yaakov part 1 siman 17 writes that if a firstborn son was born on the night of the fourteenth, if he was born before midnight the father must fast for him, but if after midnight he does not fast, since the fast commemorates the rescue of the firstborn of Israel, and those born after midnight were not included in the decree, even among the firstborn of Egypt. He brings proof from the beginning of Evel Rabbati: although they were struck at midnight, their souls fluttered until morning; if the decree included those born after midnight, it could have answered that "on the day I struck" refers to them. Therefore they were not included at all. In the first year he should be exempt, because in any case one should not be stringent in this custom, as Chok Yaakov writes. Birkei Yosef questions this, according to his view that for a one-day-old firstborn the father fasts on Erev Pesach: since the plague of the firstborn was on Pesach night, one like this, born after midnight on the fourteenth, would have been included, only we advance the fast because one cannot fast on Yom Tov. Korban Netanel in any case disagrees with Shevut Yaakov and holds that as long as the firstborn is not yet thirty days old one should not fast for him, since he has not left the doubt of being a nefel; can there be more obligation on the father than pidyon haben? It is a stringency that leads to leniency, for he fasts in Nissan on Erev Yom Tov without need. Birkei Yosef defends Shevut Yaakov: pidyon haben is different because the Torah said so, and there is no concern for Nissan and Erev Yom Tov because this is the custom for publicizing the miracle. It appears that there is certainly no proof from pidyon haben, as Birkei Yosef writes; here, since most infants are not nefalim, he should fast so the fast protect him, as a remembrance of the rescue of the firstborn of Israel.
What Shevut Yaakov wrote in part 2 is also written by Dagul MeRevavah. See Shev Yaakov regarding a woman who miscarried within three months and the fetal form was recognizable: the next child is a firstborn for inheritance and must fast on Erev Pesach, as Magen Avraham writes.
