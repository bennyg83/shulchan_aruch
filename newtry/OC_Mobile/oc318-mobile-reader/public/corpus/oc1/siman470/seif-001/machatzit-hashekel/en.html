(א) Magen Avraham wrote: it appears to me that one born after miscarriages must fast, for he is a firstborn for inheritance — as stated in Choshen Mishpat siman 276 section 6, and these are his words: one born after miscarriages — even though the head of the miscarriage emerged while alive, etc. And similarly a nine-month son whose head emerged and died — the one born after him is firstborn for inheritance. Semag and Shach wrote: that which he wrote "even though the head of the miscarriage emerged while alive" — not specifically the head emerged; rather, the whole emerged alive; nevertheless the one born after him is firstborn for inheritance, unlike responsum She'arit Yosef who holds only the head emerged — see there. Beit Shmuel in Even HaEzer siman 156 note 3: Rambam's view is a live nine-month son (but he wrote it is impossible to know he is nine months unless the husband explained and went far away, and if we fear he came by name — see there) — even if he remained thirty days and died within thirty and also hair and nails were not completed, likewise a complete offspring even rabbinically, and the one born after him is not a firstborn; but doubtful eight-month or nine-month, or even certainly eight-month if he remained thirty days, is offspring even rabbinically, for we say this seven-month is it and he remained in his mother's womb a month more — even if hair and nails were not completed, since he remained thirty days; but if he did not remain thirty days but hair and nails were completed, from the Torah he is also offspring, only rabbinically he is not offspring — see there; also Tosafot's view at least here regarding the fast. One born after an eight-month son whose hair and nails were completed even if he did not remain — need not fast.

(ב) He wrote in Knesset HaGedolah, etc., Aneinu in Shema Koleinu — and see above siman 119 in Magen Avraham note 7 that in Knesset HaGedolah he concludes with "Who answers in time of trouble" and "Who hears prayer." And it is not a conclusion in two; if he concluded only in Shema Koleinu, he has fulfilled.

(ג) For it is a public fast — and he meant: for we hold above siman 429 that even for Rabbeinu Tam there one does not decree a public fast in Nisan; but in Kaf HaChayyim he agreed with Knesset HaGedolah to say Aneinu in Shema Koleinu. In Sefer Ateret Roshe wrote: to resolve this from that siman 429 — in Sheyarei Knesset HaGedolah he reconciles that in siman 429 he said one does not decree a public fast from the month, because they are not obligated; unlike the fast of firstborns, who are obligated and standing. He further wrote in the name of Maharil that also a firstborn who is kohen or levi must fast.

(ד) See there siman 280 page 7 in Magen Avraham siman 280 note 4.

(ה) To eat at a milah meal, etc. — they were stringent; and Kaf HaChayyim wrote in the name of responsum Chut HaShani that baalei brit — meaning father of the son, sandak, and mohel — are permitted to eat and they fast in exchange another day, or release his vow that he regrets having accepted as a vow, and then he need not fast another day.

(ו) And in the name of Ateret Roshe wrote: if he is not a firstborn but fasts on behalf of his son, even if not baal brit — permitted; and Peri Chadash concludes that even one lenient to permit a firstborn who is not baal brit at a milah meal without release did not lose, as Shach wrote in Yoreh Deah siman 234 that one who fasts fasts with awareness it is custom; and behold the custom is to eat on erev Shabbat. But Kaf HaChayyim wrote: and in these countries where the custom is to eat the milah meal at night, one may not be lenient here, as Magen Avraham wrote below siman 559 end of note 11 and in siman 568 note 10; and in my humble opinion perhaps the case is not similar, for on erev Pesach they always make the meal by day, for it is difficult to make it at night.
