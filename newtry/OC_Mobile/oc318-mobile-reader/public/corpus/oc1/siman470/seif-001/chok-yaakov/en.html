Whether a firstborn from the father or a firstborn from the mother. Since in any case he is a firstborn, whether for inheritance or as peter rechem for pidyon; and for this reason Magen Avraham wrote that one born after miscarriages must fast, for he is a firstborn for inheritance, end quote. And according to this, one born by caesarean section (yotzei dofen) and the one born after him, who are firstborn neither for inheritance nor for pidyon, as explained in Yoreh Deah siman 305 and in Choshen Mishpat siman 216, need not fast. Nevertheless, regarding one born by caesarean section this requires further study for the practical law, for perhaps only there he was excluded from the verse: regarding inheritance it is written "and they shall bear him [children]," and regarding pidyon it is written "peter rechem," which is not so here, where it is a remembrance of the miracle that they were saved from Makkas Bechoros, and an ordinary firstborn is [included], and this requires investigation. And see Yoreh Deah siman 305 and Choshen Mishpat siman 277 regarding which is a firstborn for inheritance and which is a firstborn for kehuna. And Maharil wrote in his responsa, siman 14, that firstborn kohanim and Levi'im must fast on erev Pesach; and it is obvious that even if he is only a firstborn from the mother, so that he is neither a firstborn for inheritance nor for pidyon, even so he must fast; and so it is implied there in the responsum. And see Kol Bo at the beginning of the laws of Yom Tov, who wrote a novel and fitting reason for the firstborns' fast. However, the eldest of the household need not fast; even though the plague was upon them as well, we are not so stringent (Tur and Maharil).
And the custom is not so. Maharil: see Pesach Me'ubin siman 41, who reverses the reading in the words of the rav here, and thus it should read: Maharil; and the custom is not so; for "there is one who says even a female" is Maharil, as he wrote in his responsum siman 14 and in his derashos, and it is not mentioned in his words that the custom is not so; and he concludes there in Pesach Me'ubin that in his country the simple custom is for a female firstborn to fast, end quote. And the Bach also wrote that it is proper to practice thus; and in these countries the custom has spread like the Rama not to have a female fast at all.
