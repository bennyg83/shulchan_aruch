1) [Se'if 1] Firstborn sons fast, etc. The reason is to commemorate the miracle by which they were saved from the plague of the firstborn. Tur; Levush; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1.
2) There. Whether a paternal firstborn. This is because the plague also struck paternal firstborn sons. Tur in the name of Avi HaEzri; Olas Shabbos, subsection 1. See below, subsection 16. Chok Yaakov, subsection 2, gives another reason: in any event, such a person is a firstborn either for inheritance or as the first issue of the womb requiring redemption. In other words, if a man without children marries a woman who has already given birth, their first child is firstborn only for inheritance, because that child is the first of the father's strength. If a man who already has children marries a woman who has never given birth, their first child is firstborn only for redemption, because that child is the first issue of her womb, as stated in Yoreh De'ah, siman 305, se'if 17, and Choshen Mishpat, siman 277, se'if 8. See there.
3) According to this, a child delivered by cesarean section and the child born afterward, neither of whom is considered firstborn for inheritance or redemption, as explained in Yoreh De'ah, siman 305, and Choshen Mishpat, siman 277, need not fast. Nevertheless, the practical law regarding the child delivered by cesarean section requires consideration. Perhaps that exclusion applies only there because Scripture specifically limits the laws: regarding inheritance it states, 'They bear him children,' while regarding redemption it states, 'The first issue of the womb.' Here, however, the fast commemorates the miracle of rescue from the plague of the firstborn, and such a child is ordinarily called a firstborn. The matter requires consideration. Chok Yaakov, there. In practice, since this is merely a concern that the person might be obligated to fast, one need not fast because of doubt, as stated below in subsections 6 and 7. See there. See also below, at the end of subsection 8.
