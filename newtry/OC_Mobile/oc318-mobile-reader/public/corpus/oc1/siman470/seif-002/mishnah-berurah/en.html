(א) Erev Pesach on Shabbat — if it falls on erev Shabbat, they fast on that day.

(ב) On Thursday — and not on Friday, since it is not a better time to postpone to Friday. If the fast on Thursday is difficult because one must check for chametz at night and sometimes has many rooms to check and may not eat before checking, he tastes a little before checking or commands another to check and they eat.

(ג) That they do not fast at all — for this fast is only a custom, since when postponed it is postponed.

(ד) When the father is a firstborn — his fast counts for himself.

(ה) The mother fasts — and some decisors wrote she need not fast, for the father's fast also counts for the son. Where she suffers distress, one may be lenient, especially if pregnant or nursing; if she suffers from the fast, one may be lenient even if she has no husband to fast for her son. A woman who gives birth every thirty days need not fast for him in any case [per later authorities]. However, if she began once to fast for him, it is like a vow and requires release.

(ו) Until he grows up — while the son has not completed thirty days, she need not fast for him. Whether firstborns may eat at a seudat mitzvah depends on local custom; some are stringent. If one wishes to eat at a pidyon haben or brit milah, he needs release (custom is like a vow), except the mohel, sandak, and father of the boy may eat without release, for it is their Festival; they must still repay the fast after Pesach. Some firstborns are lenient and eat at seudat mitzvah; so is practiced today in many places in our country, including at a siyum masechet even if the firstborns did not learn the tractate, since at the one who completes it is a seudat mitzvah and they join his meal.
