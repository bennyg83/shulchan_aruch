(א) On Thursday. If erev Pesach falls on erev Shabbat, they fast on erev Shabbat. Maharil wrote in responsum siman 173 that the fast of firstborns must be completed, for it is a public fast — see above siman 249. And if the fast on Thursday is difficult because one may not eat before checking for chametz and some firstborns must check many rooms (and in Shulchan Gavoah it is a mitzvah so he checks well), he tastes a little or commands another to check — Maharil. Maharash HaLevi wrote in Chayei Adam siman 3 that firstborns may eat at a brit milah meal and one who is stringent will come to harm — end of his words. Magen Avraham wrote: and in our country they were stringent. In responsum Chut HaShani siman 25 he wrote that baalei brit, sandak, and father of the boy are permitted to eat at the milah and repay the fast afterward like the Ten Days of Repentance or those fasting on erev Rosh Hashanah, or release a vow. Peri Chadash wrote that one who is lenient with firstborns even without release at a seudat mitzvah did not lose — see there. R' Akiva Eiger wrote: when fasting on behalf of his son, permitted to eat at the milah even one who is not baal brit — see there.

(ב) The mother. Terumat HaDeshen and Maharil wrote she need not fast; where there is distress one may be lenient, especially pregnant or nursing — Magen Avraham and Kaf HaChayyim. However, if she fasted once it is like a vow (as stated in Shekalim, small tractate: if his father began weighing on his behalf, he does not stop). See Kaf HaChayyim. It appears to me simply that if his father fasts for a firstborn from another wife who died and he has another firstborn son to his mother from his second wife, the mother need not fast and his fast counts for both. And similarly if his father has distress and his mother fasts for herself — for those who practice fasting — if she is female, her fast also counts for her son. Kaf HaChayyim.
