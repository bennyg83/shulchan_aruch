One should follow the first opinion. And if erev Pesach falls on erev Shabbos, they fast on erev Shabbos. And he wrote. Maharil in a responsum, siman 173, that the firstborns' fast must be completed, for it is a public fast; see above siman 249. And if the fast is difficult for them on Thursday, since one may not dine before bedikas chametz, and there are firstborns who must check many rooms, he should taste a mere taste, or command someone to check, end quote of Maharil (and see there, that he also wrote in the name of R' Yechiel the matter of "minei targima" that is brought in Beis Yosef on this siman, and not like R' Shmuel HaLevi, part Orach Chaim, who disputed Beis Yosef on this; and see Kenesses HaGedolah in the Hagahot on Beis Yosef). And Maharash HaLevi wrote, part Orach Chaim siman 3, that firstborns may eat at a bris milah meal, and one who is stringent, may blessing come upon him. And in Responsa Chut HaSheni, siman 25, he wrote that the baal bris milah, mohalim, sandakim, and the father of the child are permitted to eat at the milah, and they should make up another fast, as during the Ten Days of Repentance, or those who fast Monday-Thursday-Monday (and see above siman 561 se'if 2 in the gloss, and Magen Avraham there), or have the vow released. And my brother-in-law the rav in Eliyah Zuta wrote that when they fast on behalf of their son, even those who are not baalei bris are permitted to eat at the milah, end quote. And it appears to my humble opinion that when the fast is deferred, for example when erev Pesach falls on Shabbos and they fast on Thursday, it is possible to fast and not complete [it], a fortiori from Tishah B'Av that is deferred, as explained below in siman 559 se'if 9; also it is possible to fast a fast on erev Shabbos; and in the responsa of R' Shmuel HaLevi there he ruled simply that in such a case one should not fast. And in these countries, where the custom is to eat the bris milah meal at night, one should not be lenient at all regarding the fast, as Magen Avraham wrote below in siman 599 at the end of s.k. 11; and see Rokeach siman 113.
Does the mother fast. And Magen Avraham wrote that she need not fast, and where there is distress one should be lenient, all the more so pregnant and nursing women, end quote. And so it appears to me that one should not be stringent regarding this fast, which is only a custom in remembrance of the miracle, and one remembrance serves both this way and that. And Magen Avraham further wrote: if she fasted once, it is like a vow, as stated in the first chapter of Shekalim: a minor whose father began to weigh on his behalf no longer stops; so wrote in Hagahot Semak siman 144, end quote. Behold, his words are correct, a matter with sound reason. But in Hagahot Semak I did not find [it], and this requires further study.
And if the father is not a firstborn. And it appears to me obvious that if his father is fasting for a firstborn from another wife who died, and he has another son who is a firstborn to his mother from his second wife, that his mother need not fast, and his [the father's] fast counts for both of them, for "leave it be, do not add upon it"; see above s.k. 5. And likewise if the father has distress and his mother fasts for herself, for those who are accustomed that if she is female she fasts, [her fast] also counts for her son.
