2) There. A Tzeduki is regarded as a Jewish person, etc. The same applies to Karaites. Levush. Although se'if 3 states that one who publicly desecrates Shabbos is an apostate even when violating a Rabbinic matter, the Tzedukim of the present era are nevertheless not regarded as apostates merely because they publicly violate Rabbinic laws. Their intentional transgression is comparable to an unintentional one because they continue the conduct of their ancestors, and they are like children taken captive among non-Jews. Beis Yosef; Taz, subsection 1; Magen Avraham, subsection 1. Responsa of Rabbi Betzalel Ashkenazi and Responsa Mabit, however, imply the reverse: according to all opinions these Tzedukim are considered complete non-Jews. See there. Shach and Peri Chadash in Yoreh De'ah, siman 2, and Knesses HaGedolah in the chapter Hadar, agree. Shiltei HaGibborim in the chapter Hadar likewise writes in the name of Ri'az that one who publicly desecrates Shabbos through a Rabbinic prohibition is regarded as a non-Jew and is unfit to participate in an eruv or renounce rights; rather, his rights must be rented like those of a non-Jew. See there. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 2, cites his words. The practical difference concerns Karaites in our time: their rights must specifically be rented, as with a non-Jew. See above, siman 382, subsection 80.
