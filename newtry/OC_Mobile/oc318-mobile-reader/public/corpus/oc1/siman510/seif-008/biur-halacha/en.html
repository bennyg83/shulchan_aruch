Even not for need, etc. — it is proven that for the need of Yom Tov he brought them. Behold the Mechaber began in the language of the Rambam and concludes in the language of the Tur; and the Tur wrote his words per Rashi, that it deals even with one who does all this for the need of a meal, and nevertheless we require that it be evident. And likewise wrote the Meiri and Avodas HaKodesh; and from the language of the Rambam it appears that all this deals with one who acts not for ochel nefesh need; but in truth it is very difficult to say the Rambam disagrees with all the poskim on this and permits without any change where it is for Yom Tov need; and also in the Gemara it says: these are the princes who lower their barrels, etc. — it is proven explicitly that even in carrying that is for Yom Tov need it is likewise. And perhaps the intent of the Rambam is also to forbid even when acting for Yom Tov need; and what he wrote: even though it was permitted, etc. — this is the explanation: even though they were so lenient in carrying even not for need at all, nevertheless in the manner of carrying they were stringent that one must always change, not as his act on a weekday, even if he carries for Yom Tov need. And all that we wrote is only to reconcile the view of the Rambam himself; but what the Mechaber concluded, that it must be evident that it is for Yom Tov need that he brought them — this is not precise per the view of the Rambam, for per him even not for Yom Tov need at all is also permitted (and only if he makes a change in his carrying and does not intend in his carrying to prepare this for tomorrow's need), as his view is brought in siman 518; see there in Beit Yosef and the Gra, and it requires further study.
