One may not make cheese on Yom Tov. For cheese is better when aged; and if so, he should have done it before Yom Tov. And even according to those poskim who hold in siman 495 that ochel nefesh itself is permitted even if it was possible to do it before Yom Tov, here it is forbidden because the usual way is to make it for many days, and it is weekday labor. And accordingly, even if it is impossible for him to make it before Yom Tov, one can say it is forbidden; however, through an alteration one should be lenient.
And one may not set, etc. That is, by means of rennet or other things so that the milk will gather and curdle and the whey will separate [and likewise it is forbidden to make siravoytke, namely putting a little vinegar into milk so that the whey will separate], for this is actual selecting, and the usual way is to do it for many days. And see Peri Megadim, who is uncertain whether it is permitted to do this himself for a patient who is not in danger.
And likewise one may not make butter, etc. The reason is also because of selecting, as explained above; and even through a non-Jew one should be stringent; and there are those who are lenient in this if it is for the need of simchas Yom Tov; and one should not be lenient except for a great need. And it is permitted for him himself to skim the cream floating on the surface of the milk that they call smetana, even on Shabbos; only when he reaches close to the milk he should be careful to leave a little of the smetana with the milk below, as written in siman 319, seif 14. And specifically when he needs to eat it on that same day; for otherwise it is forbidden because of preparation from Yom Tov to a weekday. And if he does not need it, but only fears it will spoil and become ruined, it is permitted to do it through a non-Jew, as written in siman 307, seif 5. And in Sefer Moed Katan he wrote that also regarding making the aforementioned butter it is likewise permitted through a non-Jew if he fears the smetana will spoil if he does not make it into butter.
