(Seif kaf alef) One rubs, etc. And there is a dispute, etc., as written siman 319, etc. Know that in Beitzah daf 12b Rava said: one rubs ears and breaks legumes on Yom Tov. And after this a baraisa was brought: one who rubs ears from erev Yom Tov for the next day blows from hand to hand. And on daf 13b it states: Abaye said: How does one rub? And the Amoraim dispute, and per all a change is required, only they dispute how the change should be. And the poskim divided on what the Gemara's "How does one rub" refers to. Tosafos and those who follow their view hold it refers to Yom Tov, and likewise the view of the Ran, that even though we say on Yom Tov it is permitted to rub, nevertheless a change is required. And if so, on Shabbat where it teaches "one who rubs ears from erev," etc., it implies on Shabbat it is forbidden, meaning even through a change. But the Rif, Rosh, and Rambam hold this "How does one rub" refers to Shabbat — that even though on Shabbat it is forbidden to rub, nevertheless through a change it is permitted. And per them, on Yom Tov one does not even need a change; and the Rav Beit Yosef ruled in Shulchan Aruch siman 315 like the Rif, Rosh, and Rambam, that through a change even on Shabbat it is permitted. Therefore he wrote here that on Yom Tov even without a change it is permitted. But the Rama wrote in siman 315: and there are those who are stringent and forbid on Shabbat even through a change; and per him, necessarily, that which we say "How does one rub" refers to Yom Tov as explained, and if so on Yom Tov it was not permitted except through a change — and this is the intent of Magen Avraham. And the Rama did not emend here "there is a dispute how they require a change" — because it is not explicit in the Mechaber that it is permitted even without a change, and the Rama relied on what he wrote in siman 315.
