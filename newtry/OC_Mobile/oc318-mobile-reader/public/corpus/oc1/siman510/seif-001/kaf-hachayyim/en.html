1) [Se'if 1] Ears of grain may be rubbed, etc. This refers to tender ears of wheat that one rubs with his hands. Legume pods may likewise be crumbled to remove the seeds. This is threshing performed in an unusual manner. Even on Shabbos it is prohibited only rabbinically, not under Torah law, and on Yom Tov the Sages did not impose the decree. Rashi, Beitzah 12b; Beis Yosef; Taz, subsection 1.
2) There. Ears of grain may be rubbed, etc. This is permitted although one could have rubbed them before Yom Tov. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1.
3) There. In their usual manner on Yom Tov. This means without an alteration, and not specifically with one's fingertips. Although above, siman 319, se'if 6, Shulchan Aruch states that an alteration is required and one must rub specifically with his fingertips, that ruling concerns Shabbos. On Yom Tov the Sages did not impose the decree. Maggid Mishneh, chapter 3, writes that although some disagree, this is the primary ruling. Ran, in the first chapter of Beitzah, likewise writes that according to some authorities, when the Sages said that ears of grain may be rubbed on Yom Tov, they meant specifically with an alteration. This is not the view of Rif, however, who permits rubbing on Yom Tov even without an alteration. See there. Beis Yosef cites this and writes that Rosh in the chapter Mefanin also holds this view, and this is the ruling. Therefore, Shulchan Aruch here rules that ears of grain may be rubbed in their usual manner on Yom Tov. Maharshal in Yam Shel Shlomo, first chapter of Beitzah, siman 38; Bach; Taz there; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1, rule likewise. Magen Avraham, subsection 1, however, cites the stringent opinion recorded by Ran. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1, likewise cites that opinion and writes that although the first view is primary, if it is possible to perform the act with an alteration, it is proper to be stringent and take the latter view into account.
4) There. He winnows a little at a time and eats. After the kernels have been separated from the ears but remain mixed with chaff, he may shake them from one hand to the other and blow away the chaff. He should not winnow a large amount together, because this appears as though he is winnowing for a weekday. Instead, he should winnow a little at a time and eat it immediately. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 2.
5) There. Even with a kanon or a tamchui. A kanon is a utensil with one broad end and another end formed like a short channel. One puts the legumes into the broad end and shakes it; the food rolls through the channel while the refuse remains in the utensil. A tamchui is a large bowl. Rashi, Beitzah 12b.
6) There. But not with a tray, etc. Using such a utensil appears as though one is preparing for the next day, because these utensils are ordinarily used only for large quantities. Rashi there; Taz, subsection 1.
