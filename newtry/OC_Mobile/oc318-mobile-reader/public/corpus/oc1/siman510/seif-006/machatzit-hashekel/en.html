(4) And kindling too is not present, for he roasts it in a bonfire, etc.

(5) And likewise the Bach copied. However I did not understand their words in what they wrote "and kindling too is not present" — therefore their intent is not what ends up kindling after the extinguishing. On this the Ran wrote, and likewise in Milchemes: since there is no extinguishing here, there is also no kindling — for it is as if it never was extinguished. And also on this does not help what they wrote that the bonfire was made for cooking need, but necessarily he intended at the beginning of kindling before he placed the meat on them; and therefore he wrote that it deals with one who made the bonfire for cooking need. And I did not understand why it should not be permitted to make a bonfire in order to roast on coals, for behold it is actual ochel nefesh — and what difference to me whether he makes a bonfire for cooking need or for roasting need? And necessarily they did not write that it is not need etc., but because he can roast it on a spit; and behold even if he roasted it on a spit he would need to make a bonfire — only they thought it is forbidden to roast on coals because he extinguishes, and this extinguishing is not need etc., for he can roast on a spit. And on this the Gemara answers that he does not extinguish, since in the end it kindles. And if because by roasting it on coals he must add extra kindling than if he roasted on a spit — that is, what ends up kindling after the extinguishing — therefore the Ramban and Ran wrote: since there is no extinguishing here, there is also no kindling, as explained. But to forbid making a bonfire l'chatchila in order to roast on coals — I did not know a reason; and what they wrote in the name of Ramban that it deals with a bonfire he made for cooking need — not on meat on coals did he write thus, but on smoking fruits mentioned in siman 518 seif 4; and he came to reject the explanation of the Baal HaMaor, who had the text like Rashi's version, that the Gemara answers the question from meat on coals. And it answers there: not possible — meaning on meat on coals it is not possible, because it is the best of roasting and is as if impossible in another way, and it is need etc.; unlike sweetening mustard, which is possible — meaning possible without sweetening; and therefore the Baal HaMaor holds meat on coals is need etc.; and the Ramban came to reject that necessarily meat on coals too is not called actual need etc., for it is possible to roast on a spit — rather the permission on coals is because there is no extinguishing, per the Rif's version. And one proof from the Ramban's view: for we say in Beitzah 22b regarding smoking fruits — Rav said forbidden because he extinguishes and in the end kindles, and there are two melachot; and he holds it is not called etc.; and Shmuel said permitted, for it is etc.; R' Yehuda: on a coal forbidden, on pottery permitted — for on pottery there is no extinguishing, and kindling the spices is not present, for it is kindling ke'lachar yad, and there is only a d'rabbanan prohibition. Rava said: even on a coal too it is permitted, similar to meat on coals, which also extinguishes and kindles and produces smell, and nevertheless it is permitted. And from this the Ramban proved that necessarily meat on coals is not ochel nefesh need, for it is possible to roast on a spit, as explained; and these are his words at the end: and moreover, your inference should not have been to say "even on a coal it is permitted" — for one who forbids on a coal and permits on pottery certainly would not permit to make a bonfire by itself, for he holds it is not etc. and not need etc.; and since so, Rava who added to permit because of ochel nefesh should not have been able to say "even on a coal it is permitted" — for it implies they permitted Rava on a coal similar to how they permitted R' Yehuda on pottery, not to make a bonfire by itself; rather it should read: Rava said, it is ochel nefesh need, etc.; but certainly he said: even though this is not actual ochel nefesh need, it is permitted to place on burning fire for the pot, because there is no extinguishing that ends up kindling; and since extinguishing is not present, kindling is not present, etc., similar to meat on coals, where all are in it, and it is permitted even though it is not etc., end of his words. From this he proved not like the Baal HaMaor — that necessarily meat on coals is not need etc., for otherwise what proof would Rava bring to permit smoking fruits on coals from meat on coals? Meat on coals is different, for it is ochel nefesh need, unlike smoking fruits, which is not need etc.; and why is it forbidden to make a bonfire by itself for smoking fruits, as explained — or that also in meat on coals it is not actual need etc., for it is possible to hang on a spit; and as explained, behold the Ramban did not forbid making a bonfire by itself except for smoking fruits, but in meat on coals we do not find anything from the Ramban. And it is reasonable that certainly it is permitted to make a bonfire for meat on coals, as explained.

(6) And the Bach wrote, etc. — for behold the plain mishnah, etc., and the mishnah did not distinguish if he was busy from erev Yom Tov that he need not change in crushing salt. Nevertheless, since it does not impair taste and all pots need salt, he should have done it from erev Yom Tov; and even if he was busy from erev Yom Tov the Sages did not distinguish.

(7) And it is possible he says the same here, etc. — and even though with salt, even if he was not busy on erev Yom Tov, it is permitted to crush on Yom Tov through a change — it must be said here with mustard it is inferior to salt; however I did not understand why he copied the words of the Bach, for the Bach did not permit except per Rashi's explanation, who holds the reason is that it should be done from erev Yom Tov; therefore when he was busy on erev Yom Tov it is permitted.
