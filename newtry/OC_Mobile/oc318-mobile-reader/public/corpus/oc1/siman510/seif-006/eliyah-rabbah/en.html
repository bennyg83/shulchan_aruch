[6] [Levush] Because he extinguishes it, etc. On Shabbos, folio 134, they ask: what differs from meat roasted on coals? And they answer: there it is not possible, here it is possible. Rashi explained: it is not possible to roast from yesterday, for on the day itself it is better; but mustard is possible from yesterday; until here. And the Taz did not look into Rashi's explanation, and likewise at the end of the Ramban's words in Milchemes Hashem on this he did not descend; see there. Also what he wrote, that meat roasted on coals spoils the taste, and so it is explained in Beis Yosef siman 511; until here: he was not precise, for Beis Yosef deals explicitly with smoking fruits, but meat roasted on coals does not spoil the taste, as proven from Rashi's explanation there and all the poskim. This is the language of Magen Avraham: the Bach wrote, if he was occupied on erev Yom Tov and could not do it, it is permitted to do it on Yom Tov; however in siman 504, seif 1, it implies it is forbidden regarding salt, and it is possible that here too he means through an alteration; until here. And one may say salt is different, for all pots need it, and they did not distinguish. But it is puzzling regarding Magen Avraham: behold the Bach ruled the opposite, that even if it was not possible on erev Yom Tov it is forbidden, since there is no need except for the pampered; and it is possible he infers from the Bach that here, in places where all the people of the city eat mustard, it is permitted when impossible on erev Yom Tov; and likewise Yam Shel Shlomo wrote in chapter 2, siman 37; and on this Magen Avraham asked: what differs from salt? In any case it is astonishing, for the essence of the Bach's ruling is missing; and furthermore, according to the Rif and the Rosh it appears forbidden in every case, as explained in the Bach and Maharshal there. Moreover, in Avodas HaKodesh, folio 41, these are his words: meat on coals is permitted, and this is not like sweetening mustard, for mustard is fit without extinguishing a coal, except for the pampered; and moreover it is possible with a coal of metal, and it is more fit and available; until here. And this is that the Gemara mentioned above explained: here it is possible in this way; and what he wrote "and it is fit," etc., he explained that also with meat it is possible to roast near the coals; therefore he explained "and it is more fit and available," which is not so with meat, which is more fit on coals. And in any case we hear a second reason that in every place mustard is forbidden even when impossible from erev Yom Tov. And furthermore the Ramban wrote that the way is to sweeten a great amount, like grinding wheat; if so, certainly forbidden even when impossible from erev Yom Tov, like grinding in siman 495, seif 2; and likewise in Rashi's explanation one may intend thus; consider carefully. And furthermore the Ramban wrote that sweetening mustard is a preparation and is not included in baking and cooking, but in grinding wheat; and in the manner they said: everything needs preparation; mustard needs sweetening, wheat needs grinding, even a person needs preparation; and therefore they forbade it; until here. Behold it is truly comparable to grinding. And incidentally I mention what the Bach wondered on the Ramban mentioned above, that baking is more of a preparation than grinding wheat; until here; as it seems he did not see what the Ran wrote at the beginning of Ein Tzadin in the name of the Ramban; see there, and I shortened.
