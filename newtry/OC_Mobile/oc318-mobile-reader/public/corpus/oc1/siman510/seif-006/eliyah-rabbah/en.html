[6] [Levush] Because he extinguishes, etc. On Shabbos daf 134 he asks: what is different from meat on coals? And he answers: there it is not possible, here it is possible. Rashi explained: it is not possible to roast from yesterday, for on the day of rest it is better; but mustard is possible from yesterday — until here. And the Taz did not look into Rashi's explanation, and likewise at the end of the words of the Ramban in his Milchemes on this he did not address it; see there. Also what he wrote that meat on coals impairs taste, and thus it is explained in Beit Yosef siman 518 — until here — he was not precise, for Beit Yosef deals explicitly with smoking fruits, but meat on coals does not impair taste, as is proven from Rashi's explanation there and all the poskim. This is the language of Magen Avraham: the Bach wrote, if he was busy on erev Yom Tov and could not do it, it is permitted to do it on Yom Tov; however, in siman 504 seif 1 it implies it is forbidden with salt, and it is possible he says the same here, through a change — until here. And one may say salt is different, for all pots need it — they did not distinguish. But one may wonder on Magen Avraham: behold the Bach ruled the opposite, that even if it was not possible on erev Yom Tov it is forbidden, since there is no need except for the pampered; and it is possible he infers from the Bach: here, in places where all the city's people eat mustard, it is permitted when impossible on erev Yom Tov, and likewise wrote Yam Shel Shlomo chapter 2 siman 37; and on this Magen Avraham asked: what is different from salt? In any case it is wondrous, for the essence of the Bach's ruling is missing; and moreover, per the view of the Rif and Rosh it appears forbidden in every case, as explained in Bach and Rashal there. Moreover, in Avodas HaKodesh daf 41, these are his words: meat on coals is permitted, and this is not like sweetening mustard, for mustard is fit without extinguishing coals except for the pampered; and moreover it is possible on coals of metal, and it is more fit and available — until here. And this is that the Gemara mentioned above explained here: it is possible in this way; and what he wrote "and it is fit," etc., he explained that also with meat it is possible to roast near coals; therefore he explained "and it is more fit and available" — which is not so with meat, more fit on coals. And in any case we hear a second reason that in every place mustard is forbidden even when impossible from erev Yom Tov; and moreover the Ramban wrote that the way is to sweeten a lot, like grinding wheat — if so, certainly forbidden even when impossible from erev Yom Tov, like grinding in siman 495 seif 2; and likewise in Rashi's explanation one may intend thus, and examine. And moreover the Ramban wrote that sweetening mustard is a repair, and it is not in every baking and cooking but in grinding wheat; and in the matter where they said: all need repair — mustard needs sweetening, wheat needs grinding, even a person needs repair — therefore they forbade it; until here — behold it is truly like grinding. And incidentally I mention what the Bach wondered on the Ramban mentioned above, that baking is more of a repair than grinding wheat — until here; as it seems he did not see what the Ran wrote at the beginning of Ein Tzadin in the name of the Ramban; see there, and I shortened.
