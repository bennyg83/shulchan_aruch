(1) One does not strain, etc. — see in Mishna Berurah what he wrote in the name of Magen Avraham, that if it was not possible to strain from erev Yom Tov it is permitted on Yom Tov, and its source is from the words of Tosafos on daf 137b, as he himself copied in his words, that this is the distinction between this law and there, where it deals with when it is impossible to do from erev Yom Tov, and therefore we are lenient there even though it is a complete melacha; and here, when it is possible. But per the view of the Mechaber above in siman 495, who holds that for ochel nefesh itself there is no distinction, and even when possible it is permitted — if so, necessarily, that which we forbid to strain mustard is because the way is to do it for many days, and per all it is forbidden, as the Ran wrote regarding sweetening mustard, and the same applies to its straining; and straining dregs that is permitted in his words, even though it is a complete melacha, is because he does it for that day to drink, and it is included among permitted melachot of ochel nefesh; and if so, there is no distinction at all between possible and impossible — for in straining mustard in every case it is forbidden because the way is to do it for many days, and with dregs in every case it is permitted; except that per the view of those who are stringent above in siman 495, one should be stringent regarding dregs if it is possible to do from the day before, but in straining mustard one may not be lenient even when impossible except through a change, as explained there.

(2) But on a metal pot it is permitted — see in Mishna Berurah what he wrote in the name of Magen Avraham; and in my humble opinion one should examine this well, for per what is written in the Rambam chapter 12 of the laws of Shabbat in the name of the Ramban, that for this reason they permitted in mustard to sweeten on coals of metal because mustard does not cause hardening — from this it implies that in water or liquid one may not be lenient; and even per the reasoning that the Maggid Mishneh brought for the view of the Rambam, that where one does not intend to make a vessel there is no concern of hardening, even though it is psik reisha, this applies only to coals, where when one does not intend there is no repair of a vessel at all, but in a kettle, which is a vessel, this does not apply; see in Peri Megadim siman 318 in the name of Lechem Mishneh, and likewise in Levushei Serad there; and if so, here too with a spit, which is a vessel per the poskim, for hardening is d'oraisa, one may not be lenient, for it is psik reisha.
