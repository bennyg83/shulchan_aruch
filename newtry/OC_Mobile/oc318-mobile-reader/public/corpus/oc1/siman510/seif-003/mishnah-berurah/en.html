One may not strain mustard, etc. For it appears like selecting, in that thereby he casts off its bran; but it is not actual selecting, for the refuse as well is fit to eat.
In his strainer. Apparently from this it implies that if he strains onto something else not in the usual way, it is permitted, similar to seif 2: they were stringent only with a sieve and a sifter, because thus is the weekday manner of selecting. Magen Avraham wrote: if it was not possible to strain from erev Yom Tov, it is permitted to strain even in a strainer; and see in Biur Halacha what we noted on this.
And one may not sweeten it with a coal. In the manner they are accustomed on a weekday to place a coal inside it, and thereby it becomes sweetened.
Of wood. Because of extinguishing. And even though it is for the need of ochel nefesh, behold it is possible to do it from yesterday, and they forbade it rabbinically. And accordingly, if it is impossible to do it from yesterday, it is permitted. And some are stringent that even in that case one should not do so except through an alteration.
With metal it is permitted. For it does not burn, and there is no Torah-level extinguishing. And even though there is a rabbinic prohibition of extinguishing, they permitted it for simchas Yom Tov [Acharonim]. And whether it is permitted by means of stones, see Machatzis HaShekel. And Magen Avraham wrote that for this reason it is permitted to place a whitened metal spit into a beverage so that it will become sour, if it is fit to drink on that same day. And see Biur Halacha: according to the poskim who hold that soldering is a Torah prohibition, one should not be lenient in this.
