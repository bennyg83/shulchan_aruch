One selects in his usual manner. The refuse from the food, and one need not alter to select the food from the refuse; for since the food is the majority, and as below, it is better to minimize toil and to select the refuse, which is the lesser amount.
If he wants to eat it on that same day. This does not come to exclude if his intent is to eat tomorrow, for in that case it is obvious that it is forbidden to make any preparation from Yom Tov to a weekday. Rather it teaches us that even if his intent is not to eat immediately but only after some time (for on Shabbos in such a case he is liable for a chatas, even when selecting food from refuse, and all the more so refuse from food), it teaches us that it is permitted: for there is no Torah prohibition of selecting on Yom Tov in this, since it is a need of ochel nefesh on that day, and it is comparable to baking and cooking. Only with a sieve and a sifter the Sages forbade, because it appears like one acting for tomorrow's need, for the way of selecting with these vessels is to do so for many days, as we wrote above in seif 1 [Magen Avraham and other Acharonim].
When the food is the majority. For then it is better to select the refuse, whose toil is lesser. And if they are equal in quantity, he selects whichever of them he wishes; however, if the food is fine, he should select the refuse.
Toil, etc., more. For example, when the refuse is very fine and there is great toil to select it.
The shell is not called refuse. And it is like one kind; and in whatever manner one prepares the food from its husk it is mere food-preparation, and he may select one from the other, whichever he wishes. And see the Acharonim who agreed that nevertheless even here one should select whichever is easier for him to select.
