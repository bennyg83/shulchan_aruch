(Seif bet) If, etc. — it is difficult: what does it teach us, etc.? For per the simple understanding and per our version, the Rama comes to be stringent and conditioned that we require specifically that he wants to eat it on that same day, but for tomorrow's need it is forbidden; and on this Magen Avraham asked: it is obvious.

(2) Therefore it appears to me it must read: even, etc.; and if so, the Rama comes to be lenient.

(3) Not like Rashba. The Maggid Mishneh in chapter 3 of the laws of Yom Tov wrote in the name of Rashba: the reason they forbade on Yom Tov nafah and sieve is because in this matter on Shabbat there is liability for chatat, and they did not want to permit it on Yom Tov; and he wrote, and these are his words: and from this reason it emerged to him that everything that on Shabbat has liability for chatat, such as borer not to eat immediately but to leave it for later that day — even on Yom Tov it is forbidden. And he disagrees with him and says there is no distinction on Yom Tov between borer for immediately and borer to leave for later that day; and its law is like a baker and cook, permitted in every case, end of his words; and likewise the Rama here: not like Rashba. And that which they forbade nafah and sieve — Rashi explained: it appears like one who acts for tomorrow's need, for the way with these vessels is only for much, end of his words.

(4) And in siman 506 seif 2 — the Rama cited there per Rashba that it is permitted to take by hand a pebble from the flour even though it is borer. And it must be said: Rashba per his view deals with when he needs the flour immediately; but per what the Rama ruled here, not like Rashba — if so, also regarding the pebble above it is permitted even if he needs it later that day and not immediately; nevertheless it is permitted.
