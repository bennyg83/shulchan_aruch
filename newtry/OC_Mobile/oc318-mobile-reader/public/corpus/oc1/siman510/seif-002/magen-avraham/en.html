If he wants, etc. It is difficult: what does this teach us? For even mere preparation is forbidden from Yom Tov to a weekday, all the more so selecting. Therefore it appears to me that it must read: even if he wants to eat it on that same day. And the meaning is: it is obvious that when he wants to eat it immediately it is permitted; rather, even if he wants to eat it later on that same day (for on Shabbos in such a case he is liable for a chatas; see siman 319), nevertheless it is permitted on Yom Tov; not like the Rashba, who forbids selecting to leave for later that day. And so it is explicit in Maggid Mishneh, chapter 3; and see siman 506, seif 2.
More toil. For example, when the refuse is very fine and there is toil to take it by hand.
And almonds, etc. This requires further study, for the Tur wrote thus according to his view: if the refuse is the majority it is forbidden to move it, and on that he wrote that almonds are permitted to move. But the Shulchan Aruch ruled like the Rambam, that they are always permitted to move, only there is a distinction in selecting; and if so, what differs regarding almonds and pistachios from other kinds? For even with two kinds of food, selecting applies, as written in siman 319. And Yam Shel Shlomo wrote, and these are his words: indeed it appears that it is not similar to selecting food from food, namely because those are two kinds, or refuse and food; but here it is one kind and there is no name of refuse upon it, and in whatever manner he prepares the food from its husk it is mere food-preparation and there is no name of melacha upon it, end quote. And this requires further study, for Beis Yosef wrote at the end of siman 321 in the name of the Yerushalmi that one who peels garlic and onions is selecting; and further: since we are concerned about toil, why should toil not be counted regarding almonds? Therefore it appears to me that also with almonds one should select in whichever manner is easier to select. Yam Shel Shlomo wrote that it is forbidden to place legumes or other seeds in water so that the refuse will float up or the dirt will fall down; and so appears the view of Rav Beis Yosef, since they omitted this in the Shulchan Aruch.
