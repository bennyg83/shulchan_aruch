(ב) It is not called refuse since their manner [of eating], etc. {Rama: This gloss is not with precision — for even though in Ashkenazi rite it is also written with this wording, nevertheless the meaning is not that they are not refuse at all, for certainly the shells are called refuse; rather the Tur wrote according to his view that with refuse in great quantity it is forbidden to move because the food is nullified in the majority and it is as if it is entirely refuse; on this he wrote that with nuts and pistachios it is permitted in any case to move, and the food is not nullified and one must borer specifically the food — so the Tur wrote explicitly here and in siman 319 that it is not called refuse in great quantity, but in any case it is called refuse.} And here in the Shulchan Aruch who ruled like the words of Rambam that also in other refuse the food is not nullified in the majority of refuse — there is no distinction between nuts and pistachios and other refuse at all; and in passing he was not precise, Rama, in this. Again I saw that Maharshal also followed the words of Rosh and wrote that there is no name of refuse upon it at all, and he gave it a higher status even more than food from within food, and in whatever matter one wants to prepare it it is permitted; and there is no basis for this — for since the matter depends on exertion, certainly one must borer that which has less exertion. The Maggid wrote chapter 3 that Rashba wrote: whatever on Shabbos one is liable for a sin-offering is forbidden on Yom Tov; and they learned from that they forbade with a sieve and winnowing basket; and from this emerged for him that it is forbidden to borer on Yom Tov except to eat immediately and not to leave for himself on the day, like on Shabbos siman 319; and likewise with two types of foods — and his honor rests in its place, for behold borer of refuse from food even by hand one is liable for a sin-offering, and on Yom Tov it is permitted; and they did not forbid a sieve and winnowing basket except that it appears like borer for many days; and so Rashi explained; and this is why they did not mention on Yom Tov at all that one must borer immediately, rather its law is like one who bakes and cooks who is permitted for himself on the day — end of quote. And one can ask that cooking is different, for it is impossible from erev Yom Tov for it spoils its taste, which is not so with borer that is possible from erev Yom Tov; and even though ochel nefesh itself is permitted even when one could do it on erev Yom Tov — that is d'oraisa, but d'rabbanan it is forbidden, as Ran wrote, brought in Beit Yosef siman 506 on that which 'we do not sift flour' — Rashi explained that it is possible from yesterday; and Ran wrote: explanation according to his explanation, and from Tosafos d'rabbanan — for d'oraisa they do not distinguish in ochel nefesh itself between possible and not possible. Also it is wondrous to me that which 'likewise' from that they did not mention on Yom Tov that one should not borer except to eat immediately; and furthermore from that they permitted to borer on Yom Tov refuse from within food what is forbidden on Shabbos — in one word these two proofs are rejected; namely that the poskim did not write the law of the prohibition of borer on Yom Tov because they relied on what was mentioned on Shabbos — presumably it is so on Yom Tov whenever it was possible from erev Yom Tov; and if so, when they permitted here to take refuse from food, it is to eat immediately as its law on Shabbos; and with this they distinguished between refuse in great quantity or the reverse; and so it appears from that which nearby 'we do not strain mustard in a strainer,' and Rashi explained that it appears like borer that one throws away his bran; and proof further from the words of Maharil that I mentioned in siman 695 that borer is forbidden on Yom Tov. And Maharshal wrote chapter 1 of Beitzah siman 42 on the words of Maharil; and I do not believe that these words came from his mouth — behold Beit Hillel permit borer on Yom Tov, only that one should beware l'chatchila to minimize exertion; and if so it is good to take the large crumbs of matza that minimize exertion — end of quote; and the view of Rashba was hidden from him, who wrote explicitly that borer is forbidden on Yom Tov, except that the Maggid disagrees with him. And therefore it appears to me that the words of Rashba are primary; nevertheless the difficulty that I challenged in siman 506 on the words of all of them in his explanation of the words of Ra'avad from the words of the Maggid — here it is difficult even according to Rashba; for here since there it refers to a fallen pebble today, and if so it was impossible from erev Yom Tov — why was sifting flour forbidden there, since it was impossible on erev Yom Tov? Certainly it is permitted like cooking — see there.
