(Seif yud alef) Cheese, etc. — and it appears to me the essence of the reason, etc., is because ochel nefesh itself is permitted even if it were possible to do from yesterday (only per the view of some that the Rama brought siman 495, a slight change is needed if it was possible from erev Yom Tov and it does not impair taste at all). And therefore he asks: what is different from kneading, etc.? In tractate Shabbos daf 134a he asks on that which they forbade making cheeses on Yom Tov: what is different from kneading, which is permitted? And he answers: hot bread is better, and it was not possible from erev Yom Tov; but cheese, on the contrary, aged is better, and it is possible from erev Yom Tov. And in the book Chamesh Ma'anos he asked per the words of Magen Avraham: if so, also per the explanation of Magen Avraham, why did he not ask from cooking, which is not done for many days, unlike cheese, which is done for many days? Nevertheless the Maggid Mishneh asked from baking, which is also done for many days, and nevertheless it is permitted; therefore it must be said to resolve Magen Avraham's difficulty as Rashi wrote, and these are his words: what is different from kneading — one kneads bread on Yom Tov, and this too is similar to kneading, end of Rashi — that in my humble opinion Magen Avraham's intent in his question is not why he asked from kneading and not from cooking; for what preference is there to ask from cooking over what he asks from kneading? And if he had asked from cooking, the Maggid Mishneh would reverse: why ask from cooking to ask from kneading? Rather Magen Avraham's intent is: why did the questioner specify kneading from other melachot of ochel nefesh? And Magen Avraham gave as an example cooking, and the same applies to other melachot done for ochel nefesh. And what superiority does kneading have over other melachot of ochel nefesh — it should have asked generally: what is different from all melachot permitted for ochel nefesh need? But certainly the essence of the reason in forbidding cheese is because it is done for many days, and similar to this, kneading is also done for many days, unlike cooking; and the same for what the Maggid Mishneh asked from baking. But in any case it is impossible to ask generally: what is different from other melachot done for ochel nefesh — for there are many melachot not done for many days and it is not similar to cheese. And he needed to specify: what is different from a melacha done for many days, and he took from kneading; and the same for what the Maggid Mishneh asked from baking — except this is certainly one matter: since in any case he must specify a melacha, there is no preference in baking to ask from it over what he asks from kneading. And likewise Rashi: "and this too is similar to kneading" — the intent is not that in cheese they do an act of kneading, but also his intent is as Magen Avraham wrote, that it is similar to kneading, which is done for many days. And in any case the Rav Beit Yosef brought poskim who hold there is no Torah prohibition in cheese; see there.
