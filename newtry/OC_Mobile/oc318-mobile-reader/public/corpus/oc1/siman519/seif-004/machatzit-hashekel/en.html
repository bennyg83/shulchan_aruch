(s.k. 4) Provided that, etc. It is implied that we are not dealing with a loose [fit]. For if it is loose, it is always obvious that there is no concern lest one wedge it in.
Since there is no building, etc., and even if he wedges [it in] there is no Torah prohibition.
Therefore they were lenient on Yom Tov. Nevertheless regarding shutters, if they have a hinge from the side, even on Yom Tov they decreed lest one wedge it in, as above, because regarding shutters it more resembles building.
And so appears to be the view of the Rambam, who in chapter 26 of Hilchos Shabbos copied the law of a menorah explained in siman 279 as a prohibition, and in Hilchos Yom Tov copied the law here. And therefore one must distinguish between Shabbos and Yom Tov.
And so wrote the Tosafos in Beitzah, folio 22a, s.v. "And Beis Hillel hold," etc., according to the first answer; see there.
And for this reason the Maharil wrote, etc. It is permitted to open it on Yom Tov, since there is no demolishing regarding vessels. And even so, specifically on Yom Tov was it permitted, but not on Shabbos; even to tell a non-Jew explicitly that he should open it is forbidden on Shabbos, as is written in siman 307, s.k. 20 in the Magen Avraham, and see there how one should act. And the reason is written there in the book Toras Shabbos, and this is his language: It is forbidden because of demolishing; and even though there is no building regarding vessels, nevertheless one should be stringent regarding it, until here ends his language.
