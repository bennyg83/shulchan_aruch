Shop shutters. That is, shops that are not attached to the ground, but are made like a wooden box or tower [and he closes it; and when he wishes to open it he removes the door from the hinge and places it in front of the shops and arranges upon it the peddlers' wares to sell, and afterward he puts away what remains in the shop and returns the doors to their place and goes]; but shops attached to the ground involve building and demolishing according to the Torah, as is written in siman 313, and it is forbidden to remove the door from the hinge and to put it back, as on Shabbos.
So that he may take out, etc. This refers to what was said above, that they permitted him to remove, so that he may take out, etc.; and putting them back, which they permitted, is because if we do not permit him to put them back he will not want to open them, lest they steal what remains in the shop, and he will come to refrain from the joy of Yom Tov; and all this is when the shops stand outdoors [but one must be careful not to mention the purchase price at the time of buying, as above in siman 517 and siman 323; see there]; but when the shops stand in the house it is forbidden to put them back, for there he is not concerned about theft.
A hinge in the middle. Meaning, in the middle of the wall there is a hole, and the door has a protrusion there opposite it that they insert into the hole, and it does not appear so much like building, and it is permitted even to put it back completely as needed, provided that he does not wedge it in with a peg forcefully; and if he wedged it in, it is a complete melachah.
A hinge from the side. That is, one end of the door protrudes above like a peg and one end opposite it below, and in the threshold there is a hole into which it is inserted, and above opposite it there is likewise a hole.
Forbidden. That is, to put it back completely as needed [after the board has slipped off the shop; for if one opens and closes while it is still fixed in it, it is certainly permitted, for if not so it would be forbidden to open and close the house on Shabbos]; but to put back part of it is permitted, and likewise to remove is permitted.
And that have no hinge at all. Thus the text should read.
Even in the house it is permitted to put back. For even though in the house there is no concern of theft, nevertheless it is permitted; for they forbade only when it has a hinge in the middle, as a decree on account of [the case of] one that has a hinge above and below, where there is concern lest one wedge it in; but when it has no hinge at all they did not decree at all.
