1) [Se'if 1] The shutters of shops may be removed, etc. This refers to shops that are not attached to the ground but resemble a wooden cabinet closed with a door. When the owner wishes to open it, he removes the door, places it before the shop, and arranges small merchants' wares upon it for sale. He may remove the door to take out spices and other items needed for Yom Tov. Although we rule that building and dismantling apply rabbinically even to utensils, the Sages permitted this for the joy of Yom Tov when needed for food preparation. He may also return the door to its place. Although replacing it is not needed for Yom Tov, the Sages permitted the concluding act because of the initial act. If they did not permit him to replace it, he would not open the shop and would refrain from contributing to the joy of Yom Tov. If he has a similar cabinet in his house, however, he may remove its door but may not replace it. In a house the concluding act was not permitted because of the initial act. Since the cabinet is in his own domain, he will not refrain from the joy of Yom Tov, because he is not afraid to leave it open in his house.
