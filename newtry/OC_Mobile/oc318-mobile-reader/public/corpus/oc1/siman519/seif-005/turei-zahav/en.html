To snip straw, etc. It is explained in the Asheri regarding straw that even with a knife it is permitted to snip, since it is animal food, as mentioned in siman 322; and regarding one who snips in order to smell, it is not mentioned explicitly that it is permitted even with a knife, but it appears to me that they share one reason, as I have written, and this is proven explicitly from what is stated in chapter HaMevi, folio 33: one beraisa taught, He snips it and smells it, and another taught, He may not snip it in order to smell it; R. Zeira said, There is no difficulty: this [permitting] is with soft ones, and that [forbidding] is with hard ones. Rav Acha bar Yaakov challenged this: Regarding hard ones, why not? For Rava bar Rav Ada and Ravin bar R. Elazar say, When we were at Rav Yehudah's [house], he would break off and give us stick after stick, even though they were fit for handles of axes and adzes. Rashi explained: He would break off, meaning when he lops off with his hand branches from a large tree, etc., like "and he broke me" [Iyov], he made me a fence of offshoots, with branches of forest trees around my path so that I not go out, as the people of the forests do; he would break off for us from detached [branches], and even though they are hard and fit to make from them alone a hatchet [handle]. And they conclude: that [forbidding beraisa] is R. Eliezer, who holds that one who snips for vessels is liable a chatas; here, regarding one who snips in order to smell, he is exempt but it is forbidden, as a decree lest he snip it for a vessel; and that of Rav Yehudah, who broke off in order to smell, follows the Rabbis, who hold that one who snips for a vessel involves only a shevus, for it is not a proper repair unless he cuts it and scrapes it smooth with a knife, and so there it would be exempt but forbidden; here, where it is for smelling, it is permitted l'chatchilah. Prima facie it is implied from Rashi, who wrote "when he lops branches with his hand," that with a knife it is forbidden; and so wrote the Maharshal, and this is his language: With a knife it is forbidden, since it is fit for a handle, for it is like an actual repair of a vessel; and "he snips it in order to smell," which [the Gemara] said, implies in every case, that is, with fragrant woods, small stalks that are not fit for use at all; and further, perhaps "he snips it in order to smell," which it said, also means by hand, but with a knife everything is forbidden, and you will not find a permit with a knife except regarding animal food alone, and that is the main [ruling], end quote. And according to my humble opinion this does not appear so at all, for if so, what forced the Talmud to establish the beraisos as Tannaim? It could have answered: this is by hand and that is with a knife; and all the more so it does not challenge anything from that of Rav Yehudah who broke off sticks, for there it was by hand; and furthermore, the reason for the permit regarding one who snips in order to smell is that even when snipping with a vessel there is only a shevus; and the same reason applies when snipping with a knife in order to smell as well, since even with a knife for vessels there is no liability of a chatas unless he scrapes it smooth; and if so, it is exactly like animal food. And what the Maharshal wrote, that "he snips it in order to smell" refers to small fragrant woods that are not fit for use: I was very astonished that a holy mouth would say so, for if so it would be obvious that [the Talmud] should have resolved the beraisos that way; rather the matter is clear that for smelling as well, even with a knife it is permitted, for it is one reason with animal food, since in both there is no liability of a chatas. And Rashi, who took "he lops with his hand," did not intend to forbid with a vessel; rather he explained the word, what the matter of "breaking off" is: it is a matter of uprooting with his hand something small from something large, like that of "and he broke me" that Rashi cited; and Rashi did not mention that "with his hand" at all regarding the case of Rav Yehudah, but only to explain the matter of breaking off that we find in the verse, that they pull a branch from the tree and make fences of them; and that [verse case] is not speaking of literally with his hand, but by means of a tool that he holds in his hand; and had Rashi not mentioned "with his hand," we would not understand what the matter of breaking off is; and had he intended to make Rav Yehudah's permit depend specifically on [doing it] by hand without a knife, he should not have written "when he lops with his hand branches," for that refers to breaking off in general; rather at the end, when Rashi mentioned "he would break off for us from the detached," which refers to Rav Yehudah, he should have written there "breaks off with his hand"; rather certainly the permit does not depend specifically on the hand, for fragrant woods for smelling are exactly like animal food. And so appears from the words of the Rosh, who wrote: And regarding a feeding trough of an animal, even with a vessel it is permitted, even to snip to pick one's teeth; for specifically regarding a wood chip, where there is liability of a chatas with a vessel, we decree by hand on account of with a vessel, but regarding a feeding trough of an animal we do not decree its vessel on account of the vessel of a wood chip; and so appears, since he compares it to breaking a barrel, which involves a vessel, end quote. And in the Gemara they explicitly compare snipping in order to smell to breaking a barrel, which implies that even by means of a vessel it is permitted since it is for smelling; for specifically when cutting to make a vessel did they decree even by hand on account of a knife, since the woods stand for that [use], so they decree making a vessel by hand because of the decree of a vessel with a knife; but regarding smelling there is no decree at all. And so appears from the words of the Tur, who wrote unqualified "they snip," which implies in every manner; and the Shulchan Aruch equated them explicitly: straw or hay and fragrant woods; and so it appears to me plain and clear. In the Maharil, Hilchos Yom Tov, he wrote in the name of the Maharash: If a non-Jew brought a letter to a Jew and it is sealed, it is permitted to break it and open it; and it appears that regarding a seal that has letters upon it, it is forbidden to break them, as is written in siman 340, se'if 3, regarding Shabbos, and the same applies on Yom Tov.
