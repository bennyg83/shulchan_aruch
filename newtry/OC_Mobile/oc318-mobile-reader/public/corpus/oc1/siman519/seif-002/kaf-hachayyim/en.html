2) Taz writes there that all this shows a distinction regarding replacing the door between shops and houses. According to Rashi, it is forbidden in houses whenever no public need is involved; consequently, when there is a public need, it is permitted even in houses. According to Rif, it is permitted even in houses when there is concern that the owner will not leave the cabinet open. Opening, however, is permitted in every case, even when no public need is involved. These are his words. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 3, however, writes that all the halachic authorities except Rashi give Rif's reason, unlike Taz, who rules according to both. These are his words. According to Eliyah Rabbah, even where a public need exists, the door may not be replaced in a house if there is no concern of theft should it remain open. Mishnah Berurah in Shaar HaTziyun, subsection 2, writes likewise. This also appears from the unqualified words of the later authorities: regarding houses, no distinction is made between a public and private need. Whenever there is no concern of theft if the cabinet remains open, its door may not be replaced.
