That it is asking for one's needs. That is to say: it is like tachanun. For even on erev Shabbos at Minchah one does not say Tachanun. And specifically the eighteen berachos one does say, for such is the formula of the berachos. And even on Shabbos itself it would be fitting to pray the eighteen, except because of burdening the congregation, as is in the Yerushalmi that Magen Avraham brought at the beginning of siman 268. And see siman 602 in the gloss, which wrote that if a milah falls between Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur, even though one does not say Tachanun, nevertheless they say Avinu Malkeinu. Thus Avinu Malkeinu does not have the law of Tachanun.
And the end of siman 604: if Yom Kippur falls on Shabbos. One should say Avinu Malkeinu on erev Shabbos at Shacharis. This implies that at Minchah even in such a case they should not say it. And this is a support for Maginei Eretz.
Selach u'mechal. Because mechilah is greater than selichah; and one should begin by asking for a small thing and afterward add asking a great request; and as above, siman 572, s.k. 4.
"Kera roa" with mercha tipcha, because they belong together; and thereby one interrupts between "roa" and "gezerah"; and as Taz wrote the reason in siman 622, s.k. 6; see there.
See what I wrote in siman 607, s.k. 3, who wrote in the name of the Shelah and Minhagim to say kapparah for chataim, selichah for avonos, mechilah for peshaim. Since pesha, which is rebellion, is more severe than avon, which is intentional but not rebellion to anger. Therefore one should say mechilah, which is greater than selichah, for peshaim, which are severe. And see there that I wrote that the Shelah wrote in the name of the Rema the opposite; for if so, according to Magen Avraham below, what he wrote that one should say "selach u'mechal" is fine, in order to begin with a small request as above. But according to what is written in the name of the Shelah in the name of the Rema, on the contrary selichah is greater than mechilah; so one should say the opposite: "mechal u'selach."
Magen Avraham also proved in siman 667, se'if 2, from the wording of Al HaChait: "forgive in the open and in secret, and willful and inadvertent," that if one asks for two things together, one may begin by asking about the more severe.
