And Shevus Yaakov, part 1, siman 140, wrote about one who had a slight ailment so that he could not go to the synagogue, and his sons and household urged him to let them blow for him and that he should eat, and he refused; and because of their urging he vowed not to eat until after the time of leaving the synagogue. And when they finished their prayer and wished to leave, a scholar came and delivered rebuke for about an hour or two, and they delayed leaving. And he wrote: and at first glance one may say that with nedarim one follows the language of ordinary people, and in ordinary language his intent is the time of leaving the synagogue, that is, after the time of prayer, and the time of Torah is separate, which did not enter his mind; and even if he were healthy and went to the synagogue he could leave after the time of prayer, for we hold the halachah is like Rabbi Yehudah at the end of the second chapter of Beitzah, who said "half for Hashem," etc. And if so, perhaps the vower's intent was only not to eat early because of the ailment he has, but not to delay so much. Yet one should compare it to what is written in Rosh Hashanah, folio 30, where Rabbi Zeira asked: if they stirred to stand, etc. If so, in our case, where he wrote that the people wished to leave and did not leave, there is likewise room to be in doubt similarly; and since it was left unresolved, a doubt of nedarim is treated stringently; and if they wish to release him, they may release him according to the law of nedarim that are for the need of Yom Tov; see there. And I do not understand the proof from Rosh Hashanah: there, so long as they are still sitting, the time of the beis din has not ended; which is not so here: that they did not leave is a delay that is not because of the time of leaving the synagogue from the end of the prayer, and something else caused them to be delayed; and by what is the leniency he first wrote deferred, that his intent was only the time of leaving from the prayer, and that time has already ended? Therefore it appears that if he has no one to release him, one should be lenient in this, especially for a man who has an ailment of illness and finds it hard to remain afflicted until the actual time of their leaving. And see below, siman 652, in Magen Avraham: even tasting is forbidden for one who is on the road and expects to come to a place of a lulav; he should wait until midday and not afflict himself more than half the day; see there. And it appears that nowadays, when the custom on Rosh Hashanah is to delay much after midday, if he has no shofar he must also wait, unless the fast is very hard for him.
