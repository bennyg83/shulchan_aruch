Se'if 1. One does not say Hallel on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur, etc. Marginal note: behold, I saw in Sefer Eliyah Rabbah that he asked, according to our praying for the reapers and the sick every day, and it is explicit in the Shas that that is because we hold like Rabbi Yosi that a person is judged every day: if so, one should never say Hallel; see there, where he was pressed on this. And in my humble opinion there is no difficulty at all: granted that a person is judged every day, that is on most days, but on the holy days such as Shabbos and Yom Tov he is certainly not judged, for there is no judgment in them; and when we say "every day," it is not precise, for the majority is like the whole, as Taz wrote above in the laws of Rosh Hashanah, siman 582. And that we pray for the reapers and the sick even on the holy days is because such is the formula of the berachos, in the manner of what is written in the Yerushalmi, brought in the poskim in the laws of Birkas HaMazon regarding whether one may say "Retzei na" on Shabbos, and he answered that such is the formula of the berachos; the same applies in this case. Granted, according to the one who holds that a person is judged only on Rosh Hashanah, it would not apply to say that they pray all year because it is a formula of a berachah for another day, and especially since on Rosh Hashanah there is no wording of this prayer at all. But according to Rabbi Yosi that a person is judged every day, if so it is fitting to pray for the reapers and the sick; and then, even though on Shabbos and Yom Tov a person is not judged, nevertheless they still pray this wording even on Rosh Chodesh and Chol HaMoed, for such is the formula of the berachos. And even though we say that in three periods the world is judged, that is other judgments such as produce; but judgments of souls, that the books of the living and the books of the dead are open before Him: that is not on Shabbos and Yom Tov and Rosh Chodesh; and examine carefully. So it appears in my humble opinion correct and clear. And see Magen Avraham, s.k. 2; and therefore it is proper to confess between the tekios quietly. Behold, at first glance this "quietly" has no preparation; and what difference is there before Him, may He be blessed, between a whisper and a raised voice? And see Taz, who wondered similarly in the laws of tefillin, siman 31, regarding the berachah of tefillin on Chol HaMoed. But it appears that Magen Avraham's intent is quietly so that he does not make it audible to his ears at all; and then every speech that is not audible to his ears is not considered speech but only thought, as is proven in the laws of nedarim, siman 210; see there. And therefore anything that does not have the status of speech but only thought: before the Accusing Angel, and all the more so the Satan, it is not considered at all and he does not know of it; but before Him, may He be blessed, even thought suffices. And therefore it is permitted to confess quietly, that is, without making it audible to his ears, and this is only thought. And behold, this is the intent of the verse in Tehillim: "If I saw iniquity in my heart, Hashem would not hear; but God has heard; He attended to the voice of my prayer." And behold, if the intent were according to its plain sense, it should not have said "if I saw in my heart," but rather "if I thought in my heart, Hashem would not hear." But it appears that this entire matter concerns vidui: for if one confesses only in the heart and not with the mouth, it does not avail, for vidui must be with a full mouth in such a way that it is considered speech and not thought. But that is in general, when it is possible for him to confess with the mouth: then thought alone does not suffice for him. But if mouth-vidui is impossible for him, even the heart suffices; and that is in the manner of their saying, of blessed memory: whoever intended to do a mitzvah and was forced and did not do it, Scripture ascribes it to him as if he did it. And therefore all year it is possible to confess with the mouth, so heart-vidui does not avail; but on Rosh Hashanah, since mouth-vidui is impossible, it is coercion, and then even the heart avails. Therefore he said "if I saw iniquity in my heart": that is, if I see in my heart that I have iniquity in my hand, "Hashem would not hear": this is not considered in Hashem's eyes as vidui. "But God has heard" thought that is in the heart: that is, "the voice of my prayer," meaning at the time of the tekios, which is a voice of prayer; then they confess quietly or in the heart; then, since it is impossible otherwise, even this avails; and it works out well. And see what I wrote in a drosh for Rosh Hashanah of the year 5593 for another reason why it is permitted during the tekios to confess; see there, and examine carefully. And behold, Magen Avraham wrote in the name of the Zohar that one should not say vidui on Rosh Hashanah; and it appears to me there is a support for this from what is written in Koheles: "Do not give your mouth to cause your flesh to sin, and do not say before the angel that it was an error; why should God be angry at your voice and destroy the work of your hands?" It appears that the intent is: the main prohibition of vidui is because of the Satan, so as not to open a mouth for the Satan; and for that reason they permitted confession between the tekios, for the Satan is already confused. And this is what the Wise One said: on Rosh Hashanah do not give your mouth to cause your flesh to sin, to obligate yourself to admit that you have sinned; and not only if you sinned deliberately should you not obligate yourself, but even "do not say before the angel that it was an error": even if you sinned inadvertently, do not say before the angel; for if you say so, the Satan will prosecute you and arouse wrath against you. And if it be so, "why should God be angry at your voice": this is the voice of the shofar; "and destroy the work of your hands": that is, the tekios that you do; for if there is wrath upon you, even the tekios will not rise favorably before the Holy One, blessed be He, Heaven forbid. And this is a correct allusion in my opinion, and it works out well; and examine carefully.
