One does not say Hallel. This requires some study regarding those who say Tehillim every day and happen to reach Hallel on the day of Rosh Hashanah: how may they say it? And it appears to me that since they do not say it by way of song but by way of entreaty and request, it is permitted; for it is a widespread custom to complete all of Tehillim on the night of Yom Kippur, and they also say Hallel; and there are also those who say Tehillim every single day, and Hallel is included; and we say "one who reads Hallel every day is as one who reviles and blasphemes," so it must be as I explained.
Avinu Malkeinu. Beis Yosef wrote in the name of the Kol Bo not to say "Avinu Malkeinu, we have sinned before You," for one does not say vidui on Rosh Hashanah; and so wrote the Zohar. But to say verses that mention sin there is no concern, since it is not in the manner of vidui (Kenesses HaGedolah, siman 238 [/237/]). And the Shelah wrote that not during prayer, or when he specifies his sins, it is permitted; therefore it is proper to confess quietly during the tekios, between one set and another. And in a commentary on the machzor I saw that it explains "Avinu Malkeinu, we have sinned before You" to mean: our fathers sinned in that they served idolatry, and we have no king but You; therefore deal kindly with us for Your name's sake; end quote. And with this our custom of saying it on Rosh Hashanah is settled. One should not say adjurations of angels except in the language of entreaty (Levush).
On Shabbos one does not say it. Some say the reason is that it was instituted corresponding to the middle berachos, and that applies only when it falls on a weekday (Levush); and according to his words, on erev Shabbos at Minchah it would be fitting to say it. But the Ran at the end of Rosh Hashanah wrote the reason that it is asking for one's needs; and so wrote Maginei Eretz that even when Rosh Hashanah falls on erev Shabbos one does not say it at Minchah; and so wrote the Rivash, siman 512, that there are places that practice thus, and places that are accustomed to rise for the watch in the night on Shabbos: each river follows its course. And see siman 602 in the Hagahos Asheri, and the end of siman 604. One should say "kaleh" with a tzeirei; "selach u'mechal," for mechilah is greater than selichah (see what I wrote in siman 607); "kera roa" with mercha tipcha: we wrote tipcha (Mateh Moshe); "erase and remove our sins and our willful transgressions," see what I wrote in siman 607, se'if 2.
Until midday. At the very least (there). And so wrote Yam Shel Shlomo on Beitzah, that on Rosh Hashanah one may lengthen beyond midday unless it falls on Shabbos; and it appears to me specifically with piyutim and prayers, and not with melodies; see siman 529, se'if 1.
