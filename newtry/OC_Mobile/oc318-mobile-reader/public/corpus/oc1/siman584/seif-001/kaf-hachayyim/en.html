1) [Seif 1] Hallel is not recited on Rosh Hashanah, etc. For R. Abahu said: The ministering angels said before the Holy One, blessed is He, "Master of the universe, why do the Jewish people not recite song before You on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur?" He said to them, "Is it possible that the King sits upon the throne of judgment, with the books of the living and the books of the dead open before Him, and the Jewish people recite song?" Rosh Hashanah 32b; Tur. Although we are confident that we will emerge meritorious in judgment, one must nevertheless be fearful and trembling from the dread of judgment, and through this we will merit to be found meritorious. Taz, s.k. 1; Mishna Berurah, os 1.
2) Ibid. Hallel is not recited on Rosh Hashanah, etc. Those who recite Tehillim every day, and whose daily portion includes Hallel on Rosh Hashanah, may recite it, since they do not recite it as song but as supplication and petition. Magen Avraham, s.k. 1; Eliyah Rabbah, os 4; Birkei Yosef, os 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, os 1; Chayei Adam, klal 139, os 11; Mishna Berurah, os 2.
3) Ibid., gloss. It is customary to recite Avinu Malkeinu, etc. The reason is that we learn in the third chapter of Taanis, 25b, that on one occasion a fast was decreed and they were not answered. R. Akiva descended before the lectern and said, "Our Father, our King, we have sinned before You," etc., and he was immediately answered. When that generation saw that he had been answered through that prayer, they added further requests and supplications and established it for the Ten Days of Repentance. Beit Yosef in the name of Kol Bo.
4) Ibid., gloss. It is customary to recite Avinu Malkeinu in its prescribed order. The Beit Yosef writes that the other requests recited in Avinu Malkeinu are said on Rosh Hashanah, but "We have sinned before You" and anything similar containing a confession of sin are not recited, for the reason that confession is not recited on Rosh Hashanah, end quote. Darkei Moshe, os 1, writes regarding this that the custom does not follow his words. This is what the Rama writes here, "It is customary to recite Avinu Malkeinu in its prescribed order," meaning the entire order without omission. However, the opinion of the Arizal in Shaar HaKavanos accords with the Beit Yosef, as written above, siman 582, os 16, see there. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, os 2, explains that those who omit "Our Father, our King, we have sinned before You" on Rosh Hashanah do so because confession is not recited on Rosh Hashanah, so as not to give the Accuser an opening. So too writes Mishna Berurah, os 3. The aforementioned words of the Beit Yosef are cited by Magen Avraham, s.k. 2, who writes that there is no concern with reciting verses that mention sin, since they are not recited by way of confession. So too writes Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ibid.; Mishna Berurah, ibid.
