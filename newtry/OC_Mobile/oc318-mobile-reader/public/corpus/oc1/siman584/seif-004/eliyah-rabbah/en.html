And Hallel is not recited, etc. Those who recite Tehillim every day and whose daily schedule brings them to the Hallel Tehillim on Rosh Hashanah may recite them, because they are not saying them as song, but as supplication and request. Magen Avraham. Sefer Amarkal asks that according to our ruling like Rabbi Yose, who holds that a person is judged every day, Hallel should never be recited. He answers that the judgment of the community is unlike the judgment of an individual, because each individual is judged separately. This is difficult. Rosh Hashanah 16 challenges the Mishnah, which says that all inhabitants of the world pass before Him on Rosh Hashanah, from Rabbi Yose’s opinion. Yet Rabbi Yose could agree regarding communal judgment. One must therefore say that the halachah does not follow Rabbi Yose. The practice of praying every day for the sick and afflicted is merely a customary practice. Thus far his words. In my humble opinion, our prayer practice relies upon the Gemara’s second answer, that crying out is beneficial to a person even after judgment has been decreed. Tosafos, Shabbos 12, also implies this. See also siman 32, siman 45, siman 67, siman 100.
