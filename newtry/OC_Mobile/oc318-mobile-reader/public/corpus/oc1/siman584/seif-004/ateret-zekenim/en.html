They circumcise, etc. If it is on Shabbos, they circumcise after Ashrei, and after the milah they say Yehallalu. See above, siman 521, se'if 2. And the reason they do not circumcise after the prayer before leaving the synagogue is because the zealous do mitzvos early; so it is written in the Hagahos Asheri on the first chapter of Rosh Hashanah, and so it is written in the Shiltei Gibborim around the Mordechai, in the Hagahos on Shabbos, folio 182b, in the name of several rabbis; and Binyamin Ze'ev, siman 290, also ruled that way in practice. All this is in a place where the custom is to circumcise in the synagogue; but in a place where they customarily circumcise each one in his house, they have the custom to circumcise after leaving the synagogue, and so is the custom in the land of Turkey and in all the land of Ishmael. And they also say the Geulah "Yom LiYabasha" when there is a milah on Shabbos, just as on other Shabbosos when a milah falls on them (Maharam Yaffe). But when a milah falls on Rosh Hashanah on a weekday, one does not say "Yom LiYabasha," and likewise on every Yom Tov except the last day of Pesach: when a milah falls on them, one says "Yom LiYabasha" even on a weekday, because the splitting of the Sea of Reeds was on that day.
