Avinu Malkeinu. See Baer Heitev; and see in Shin Chet in the book Birkhei Yosef, who challenged the Shelah, and his view is as Beis Yosef wrote: not to say that which is in the manner of vidui; and he wrote that so was the custom. And Kenesses HaGedolah wrote in the name of his teacher the rav, of blessed memory, that he would ridicule certain "wise men in their own eyes" who emended certain piyutim of Rosh Hashanah in which sin and iniquity are mentioned; for Maran Beis Yosef said only that one does not say vidui on Rosh Hashanah, such as "I have sinned, I have erred, I have rebelled," "forgive and pardon and erase"; but what is not in the manner of vidui is no better than Shabbos, etc. And so wrote the Peri Chadash; see there. And from this it is clear that where they are accustomed to skip "Avinu Malkeinu, we have sinned before You," one should also skip "selach," "mechal," and "mecheh," and it is not comparable to saying "VeHu Rachum" in Pesukei DeZimra, which they say even on Shabbos, for there it is not in the manner of request; and those who skip the first Avinu Malkeinu and say the rest in order are not doing well. And what he wrote that one should say "kaleh" with a tzeirei means the lamed with a tzeirei and the kaf with a patach. And Peri Chadash wrote that one must say "Avinu Malkeinu, remember that we are dust" with the zayin with a sheva and the kaf with a cholam, and not like those who say it with a kamatz and a shuruk. And regarding what he wrote to say "selach u'mechal": Shin Chet wrote that in all the siddurim and the common wording it is "mechol u'selach," and that is the main [wording]; and so they say in "Yehi Ratzon" before the Tamid section, and in the vidui "Mah Nomar," etc., and at the end of the Yom Kippur vidui; and so wrote the Rambam in the Order of Prayer, and the Radaz; and so in the Kol Bo it is written that the wording is "mechol u'selach." One should not object from the prayer wording "Selach lanu Avinu," etc., for here too they say Avinu Malkeinu corresponding to the two titles: against the title "Malkeinu" adjacent to it they say "mechol u'selach," corresponding to the title "Avinu" that is mentioned; see there. And in our siddurim now it is printed according to the wording of Magen Avraham. And also what Shin Chet wrote to settle from the wording of the Shemoneh Esreh that selichah comes first is forced; and on the contrary one may say that against "Avinu," which comes first, for the adjacent title "Malkeinu" they say "selach," and against the title "Malkeinu" they say "u'mechal," as in the Shemoneh Esreh. Therefore each person should not change from the wording he has been accustomed to from his early youth. And see Shevus Yaakov, part 3, siman 42, that one should say Avinu Malkeinu even as an individual, as the Rivash wrote; and so wrote Kitzur Shelah in the name of great authorities; see there.
