Avinu Malkeinu. Beis Yosef wrote in the name of the Kol Bo not to say "Avinu Malkeinu, we have sinned before You," for one does not say vidui on Rosh Hashanah; and so wrote the Zohar. But to say verses that mention sin there is no concern, since it is not in the manner of vidui. And the Shelah wrote that not during prayer it is permitted; therefore it is proper to confess quietly during the tekios, between one set and another. And in a commentary on the machzor I saw that it explains "Avinu Malkeinu, we have sinned before You" to mean: our fathers sinned in that they served idolatry, and we have no king but You; therefore deal kindly with us for Your name's sake; end quote. And with this our custom of saying it on Rosh Hashanah is settled. "Avinu Malkeinu, tear up the evil of our decree": it appears that one should say "evil of the decree" in one breath, for they belong together; that is, that Hashem tear away the evil that is in the decree, and what remains should be for mercy, for the recipients can transform Hashem's decree into mercy. And likewise "and repentance etc. remove the evil of the decree": one should not interrupt between "evil" and "decree" (Taz, siman 622, s.k. 7; see there). One should say "kaleh" with a tzeirei; "selach u'mechal," for mechilah is greater than selichah; see siman 607.
