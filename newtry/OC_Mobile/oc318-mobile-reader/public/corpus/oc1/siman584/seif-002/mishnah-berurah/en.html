(6) From "And Hashem visited." Because on Rosh Hashanah Sarah, Rachel, and Chanah were remembered.
(7) Seven. And they add making two sections: one "on the day Yitzchak was weaned," and two "in that he is there."
(8) The seventh. Until "a wife for Hashem." And if he erred and completed the section with four who were called up, or three, and he has not yet said Kaddish, he should complete the number of those called up in the Akeidah section that follows it. And if he said Kaddish: if only one is missing from the number, he need not read, and may rely on the Maftir; so wrote Shaarei Ephraim. And in Responsa Chasam Sofer, Orach Chaim, siman 29, he wrote that if he completed the section with four, he should not say Kaddish until after they read in the second Sefer Torah, and the Maftir will be the fifth who goes up; for we hold that the Maftir counts toward the number of those called up. If he erred and on the first day read the Akeidah section, he has fulfilled his obligation, and on the second day he reads the section "And Hashem visited" etc., and also the Akeidah section until the end of the sedra. Nevertheless, if he remembers while still reading the Akeidah section that he erred, and the last one has not yet recited the concluding berachah, he should roll [the scroll] and read with him the section "And Hashem visited" until its end, and afterward recite the concluding berachah (Mateh Ephraim and Shaarei Ephraim). Mateh Ephraim further wrote: it is proper for every man who fears Hashem to strive to have an aliyah on the Days of Awe; and even in places where they sell mitzvos, he should buy it with money according to his ability; and on the contrary there is more elevation in a mitzvah that comes to him with money than in a mitzvah that comes to him free, without silver.
(9) The one who blows. And there are places where it is customary to call also the one who prays Musaf; and likewise on Yom Kippur they call him. And one who takes payment for the blowing and for the prayer: they are not accustomed to call them (Eliyah Rabbah).
