They circumcise. See Taz in the name of the Levush: when Rosh Hashanah falls on Shabbos, one says "Yom LiYabasha" between Geulah and Tefillah, and on a weekday one does not; see Magen Avraham, letter 7. And before the shofar they advance the milah, to juxtapose the covenant of Avraham (the milah) to the binding of Yitzchak (the shofar alludes to the ram of Yitzchak). And on Shabbos they circumcise after the Torah reading: one says Ashrei Yoshvei Veisecha, and they circumcise, and afterward one says Yehallalu; and see the Levush and Ateres Zekenim. And that they do not circumcise on Shabbos after leaving the synagogue is because the zealous do mitzvos early, and not after midday; see the Levush and see Taz. There in the Levush: on Rosh Hashanah one says "haMelech Yoshev," not "haYoshev," which would imply one who regularly sits, whereas now He is not [merely] Sitting, but sits now. What he wrote about "Yom LiYabasha," that one does not say it on Yom Tov: for what would its novelty be? is difficult, for Yom Tov is a positive commandment and a negative commandment, and milah is a positive commandment; and God willing, more will be explained in Magen Avraham.
