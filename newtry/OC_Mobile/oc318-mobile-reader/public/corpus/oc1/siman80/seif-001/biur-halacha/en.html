It is better that he let the time of Keriat Shema and prayer pass—see in Mishna Berurah the reason that it is disgraceful, etc.; I collected this from the words of Eliyah Rabbah. And above in siman 38, seif 2, in Biur HaGra it implies that the main reason of the Shulchan Aruch is that Keriat Shema and prayer need to be with tefillin, and here it is impossible with tefillin—see there. Accordingly, in Mincha prayer, in which in any case we do not don tefillin, and likewise in the Keriat Shema of Maariv and its prayer—even though he cannot guard himself from flatulating—nevertheless he should recite Keriat Shema and pray at their time, and when he needs to flatulate he should stop until the odor passes. But according to everyone's words, the reason of the Shulchan Aruch is difficult: according to Eliyah Rabbah it is difficult—because of concern of flatulence should he cancel the mitzvah entirely? Is he not under duress in this? He should pray, and when the duress occurs he should stop in the middle until the odor passes, as Magen Gibborim truly challenged. And likewise the reason of the Gra is also difficult—for because he cannot fulfill Keriat Shema and prayer with tefillin, should he thereby cancel Keriat Shema and prayer entirely? But the Levush expanded for us the discussion on this in siman 58, and it is clear to him like an egg in a corner—that in a place where a person is under duress and cannot fulfill the mitzvah of tefillin, he should not cancel Keriat Shema and prayer at their time because of this; see there. And it is clear to me that because of this the Gra wrote in this siman that it does not seem so as halacha. But according to all opinions, this law of the Shulchan Aruch is not stated except if he will certainly flatulate in the middle; but from concern that perhaps he will flatulate, it is certainly not proper to cancel because of this—even communal prayer. And a person should initially only examine himself, and beyond that he has no obligation at all.
