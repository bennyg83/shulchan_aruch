[1] "Better that he let pass, etc." — so wrote the Tur; and he inferred from Rama's responsum siman 91 that since he did not say there is a prohibition in the matter but rather it is better for him to wait since he has a remedy to return and pray twice. However one who is accustomed to this and if he does not pray will be exempt from prayer forever — he is permitted to pray without a clean body, for a rabbinic prohibition does not come and override a Torah obligation or prayer which is a public mitzvah — until here. It is implied from this that the Tur and Rama hold that Keriat Shema also has compensation — for if not, why would the time of Keriat Shema pass; even one not accustomed would be permitted to read Keriat Shema. If so, it requires investigation: Beit Yosef and Shulchan Aruch above at the end of siman 58 wrote two opinions whether Keriat Shema has compensation and did not decide; and so he wrote in the Tur's words; and especially it is difficult on the Levush who ruled plainly below siman 108 se'if 2 that Keriat Shema has no compensation — if so his words here contradict. In truth in Rama's responsum he did not mention in the Tur's language the law of Keriat Shema but rather "better that the time of prayer pass"; perhaps intentionally he omitted it because in truth the time of Keriat Shema will not pass and he holds it has no compensation — and so in Rosh's responsum itself in general principle 4 of this law he wrote only "better that the time of prayer pass" — requires investigation. Also requires investigation in the law of prayer: it is implied from Rama's responsum mentioned above that specifically one accustomed forever; but when he knows it will stop from him in two or three days then it is permitted that its time pass. And it is difficult: for in siman 108 se'if 4 it is explained that prayer has compensation only at the time of prayer adjacent to it. Perhaps Rama did not mean literally forever but the same applies when he will have some days; or since in prayer it is possible to complete through a voluntary addition when one renews something in it — therefore specifically forever is required — requires investigation.
