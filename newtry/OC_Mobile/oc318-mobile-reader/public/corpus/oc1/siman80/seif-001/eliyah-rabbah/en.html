[1] "It is better that he let pass," etc.—so wrote the Tur. And he was precise in responsa Rama, siman 91, that he did not say there is a prohibition in the matter, but rather it is better for him to wait, since he has a remedy to return and pray twice. However, one who is accustomed to this, and if he would not pray he would be exempt from prayer forever—is permitted to pray without a clean body, for a rabbinic prohibition does not come and override a Torah obligation or prayer, which is a communal mitzvah—so far. From this it implies that it seems correct to the Tur and Rama that Keriat Shema also has compensation; for if not, why should he let the time of Keriat Shema pass—even one who is not accustomed should be permitted to recite Keriat Shema. If so, study is required, for Beit Yosef and Shulchan Aruch above at the end of siman 58 wrote two opinions whether Keriat Shema has compensation and did not decide; and so wrote in the words of the Tur. And all the more it should be difficult against the Levush, who ruled without qualification below in siman 108, seif 2, that Keriat Shema has no compensation—if so, his words here would contradict. But in truth in responsa Rama he did not mention in the language of the Tur the law of Keriat Shema, but only "it is better that he let the time of prayer pass"; and perhaps he omitted it intentionally for the reason I explained—that in truth he would not let the time of Keriat Shema pass, and it seems to him that it has no compensation. And likewise in the responsum of the Rosh itself, klal 4—the source of this law—he wrote only that it is better to let the time of prayer pass; study is required. Study is also required in the law of prayer, for it seems from the words of the responsum of Rama mentioned above that specifically when he is accustomed to this forever; but when he knows it will stop from him in two or three days, then it is permitted that he let its time pass. But it is difficult—for in siman 108, seif 4, it is explained that prayer has no compensation except in the time of prayer adjacent to it. And it is possible that Rama did not mean specifically forever; rather the same law applies when he will have some days; or alternatively, since in prayer it is possible to complete through a voluntary offering when one renews something in it, therefore specifically forever we require—and study is required. The law of one who has bladder-stone illness—see above, siman 78; and see the end of siman 66 what I challenged, that the Levush contradicts here.
