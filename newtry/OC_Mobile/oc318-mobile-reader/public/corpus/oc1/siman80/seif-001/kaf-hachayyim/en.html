(a) [Seif 1] One who is healthy such that he cannot stand, etc. — better that the time for Keriat Shema pass, etc. And all this is specifically when this incident occurred without purity on account of some food that harmed him and the like — so that in the evening he can certainly pray with a clean body. But if he has an illness on account of which he is compelled to belch every hour, he prays; and when belching comes to him he is silent and waits until the odor passes, and returns to pray from where he stopped. Peri Chadash, and Eliyah Rabbah brought it. And likewise Ateret Tzvi ot 1 in the name of responsum of Rama siman 98. Maamar Mordechai ot 1. Beit Yehudah ot 28. Petach HaDvir ot 1. And likewise that one may recite Shema — for a rabbinic [prohibition] does not come and uproot a Torah [obligation]. Mishnat Zahav ot 1. And likewise Petach HaDvir there. However, what Peri Chadash wrote there — that if he waited long enough to finish the entire prayer from beginning to end he must return to the beginning, as written in siman 104 seif 5 — Maamar Mordechai wrote on this: one should stammer over it, for when we return him to the beginning we are not assured that he will not belch and wait long enough to finish the entire [prayer], since he is accustomed to this — if so, to what is this similar? To what is explained below siman 101 regarding nowadays that we do not return on account of lack of kavanah, for even on return it is likely he will not concentrate — if so, why should he return? So far Tur z"l. And likewise here. And especially one for whom it is clear that he cannot restrain himself from belching many times frequently — he should not return, as stated. And in practice it requires study. And it appears that even Peri Chadash did not say that when he waited long enough to finish the entire [prayer] he returns to the beginning, but [only] when he knows in himself that if he returns a second time it can be without waiting long enough to finish the entire [prayer]; but if he knows in himself that such is always his way, that he must wait in order to finish the entire [prayer], even Peri Chadash would agree that he need not return to the beginning since his return accomplishes nothing. And see above siman 78 ot 1 regarding the law of one who constantly drips urine.
