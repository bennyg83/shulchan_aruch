(1) [Seif 1] One whose health is such that he is not able to stand, etc.—it is better that he let the time of Keriat Shema pass, etc. All this applies specifically when this incident of being not ritually clean occurred because of some food that harmed him and the like, so that in the evening he will certainly be able to pray with a clean body. But if he has an illness because of which he must flatulate every hour, he should pray; and when flatulence comes upon him he should be silent and wait until the odor passes, then resume praying from where he stopped. Pri Chadash—and Yad Aharon brought it in Hagahot Tur. And likewise Eliyah Rabbah, letter 1, in the name of responsa Rama, siman 98. Maamar Mordechai, letter 1. Beit Yosef, letter 28. Petach HaDvir, letter 1. And likewise it is permitted to recite Keriat Shema, for a rabbinic prohibition does not come and override words of Torah. Mishnat Zekenim, letter 1. And likewise Petach HaDvir there. However, what Pri Chadash wrote there—that if he waited long enough to complete all of it from the beginning of prayer until its end, he must return to the beginning, as written in siman 104, seif 5—Maamar Mordechai wrote on this there, and these are his words: one may hesitate about it, for when we return him to the beginning we are not assured that he will not flatulate and wait long enough to complete all of it, since he is accustomed to this; behold, to what is this similar—to what was explained below in siman 101, that nowadays we do not return because of lack of intent, for even upon return it is likely he will not have intent—if so, why should he return?—so far the words of the Tur, may his memory be a blessing. And likewise according to his view; and especially one for whom it is clear that he cannot restrain himself from flatulating many times in succession—he should not return as above; and in practice, requires study—so far. And it appears that Pri Chadash also did not say that when he waited long enough to complete all of it he returns to the beginning, but rather when he knows in himself that if he returns a second time it could be without waiting long enough to complete all of it; but if he knows in himself that such is always his way—that he needs to wait in order to complete all of it—Pri Chadash would also concede that he need not return to the beginning, since his return accomplishes nothing. And see above, siman 78, letter 1, regarding the law of one whose urine constantly drips. And Mishnat Zekenim in this siman; and in our words to siman 38, letter 5, in the matter one who regarding tefillin for one who cannot escape flatulence—see there.
