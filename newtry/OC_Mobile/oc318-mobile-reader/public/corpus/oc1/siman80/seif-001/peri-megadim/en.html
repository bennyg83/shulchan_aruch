"He should don"—Taz: certainly a tallit is permitted all day; even a large tallit is permitted with flatulence, but for the bathroom it is forbidden. However, if he did not don a tallit and it occurred to him between Ahavah and Keriat Shema, he may don it without a blessing, for donning is not an interruption—only in prayer. And see siman 66, letter 4, in Magen Avraham, in what he wrote there; and see the explanation here. And know that flatulence is a rabbinic prohibition, and nevertheless a foul odor without substance is permitted even regarding oneself, and for Keriat Shema as well—for it is the rabbis who decreed—and even so it is better that he let pass, for prayer and Keriat Shema have compensation in siman 58—opinions; Eliyah Rabbah stood on this. But if he has an illness such that he cannot manage without flatulence—Pri Chadash; and Eliyah Rabbah in the name of responsa Rama 98—it is permitted to pray and recite Keriat Shema, for a rabbinic prohibition does not come and override words of Torah; see there. And Pri Chadash wrote that he waits until the flatulence and odor pass; see there. And likewise prayer, according to one opinion, is rabbinic—a communal positive commandment. Now, tefillin require a clean body; and if one has an illness of flatulence, one could say that a rabbinic prohibition does not come and uproot forever a positive commandment, similar to musical instruments, as in siman 585 in Magen Avraham 4; and one could distinguish between an individual and the congregation—requires study. And see siman 38 in Magen Avraham, letter 2.
