(1) When he is certain. This excludes a case in which he is not certain, and he delays himself out of concern that he may pass wind in the middle. In doing so, he acts improperly.
(2) That he cannot, etc. Meaning, even if he were to recite only Krias Shema and the Amidah, he would still be unable to restrain himself from passing wind in the middle. Otherwise, it is better to do so than to omit the Amidah entirely.
(3) The time for Krias Shema. The later authorities agree that he should not allow the time for Krias Shema, which is D'Oraisa, to pass because of this. Moreover, the responsum of the Rosh, which is the source of this ruling, does not mention Krias Shema. The Magen Gibborim writes that the reference to Krias Shema is merely a conventional turn of phrase. His proof is that siman 38, seif 2, likewise records this ruling but mentions only the Amidah, and here as well the Mechaber concludes by referring only to the Amidah. The Mechaber's intent is merely to say that he must alter his normal practice of reciting Krias Shema and the Amidah at their proper time. Instead, he should recite Krias Shema by itself, together with its blessings, but should not recite the Amidah. During the Amidah one stands before the King, and it is a great disgrace to pass wind then. Krias Shema, however, is not like speaking before the King; rather, through it one fulfills Hashem's will. Although he will recite Krias Shema without tefillin, since tefillin require a clean body, he should not wait and allow the time for Krias Shema to pass in order to recite it while wearing tefillin. Rather, whenever he is able to put on tefillin during the day, he should put them on then.
(4) And recites the blessings over them. Immediately after Krias Shema he should remove them. He may, however, put on his tallis and recite its blessing before beginning the blessings of Krias Shema, and he may wear it throughout the day, since tzitzis do not require a clean body. If he did not put on the tallis beforehand and it became available to him between Ahavah and Krias Shema, he may put it on without a blessing, since putting it on does not constitute an interruption. All this applies when this condition occurred because of a food that harmed him or the like, in such a manner that by evening he will certainly be able to pray with a clean body. One who regularly suffers from an illness that forces him to pass wind at all times, however, and who would therefore be permanently exempt from prayer if he did not pray, may pray without a clean body. The prohibition against passing wind, which creates a foul odor without a tangible source, is only D'Rabbanan, and it does not override the D'Oraisa obligation of Krias Shema or prayer, which is a communal mitzvah. He should therefore recite Krias Shema and pray. The Rama writes this in responsum 98. The Peri Chadash rules likewise, adding that when he passes wind he should pause until the odor dissipates, just as one pauses for a foul odor without a tangible source, and then continue praying. The Peri Megadim further questions whether in such a case he may also put on tefillin, for how can he permanently uproot the D'Oraisa positive mitzvah of tefillin? See there. It appears to me that the Peri Megadim means only that he should put them on for a few moments, solely to fulfill the positive mitzvah. Wearing them longer is certainly forbidden. If he cannot restrain himself from passing wind long enough to put on both the arm tefillin and the head tefillin, he should put them on one at a time. Regarding the blessing, it appears to me that he should not recite one. The Rama writes in responsum 98 that one who has an illness causing urine to drip involuntarily at all times, and who would therefore otherwise never be able to pray, may wrap a garment around his organ, ensure that his outer garments are clean, enter the synagogue, and pray. When urine drips, he should pause, and afterward continue praying. This applies only if he did not need to urinate when he began praying, even though he knew that during his prayer urine would drip.
