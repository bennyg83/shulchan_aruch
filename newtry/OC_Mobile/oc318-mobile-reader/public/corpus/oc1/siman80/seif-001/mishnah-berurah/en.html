(א) When it is clear to him—to exclude if it is not clear to him and he restrains himself from concern that perhaps he will flatulate in the middle; in that case he is not acting correctly.

(ב) That he is not able, etc.—meaning, even if he would say only Keriat Shema and prayer alone, he still would not be able to restrain himself from flatulating in the middle; for if not so, it is better to do thus than to cancel the Eighteen Blessings entirely.

(ג) The time of Keriat Shema—the later authorities agreed that he should not let the time of Keriat Shema pass, for it is Torah law, because of this. Also in the responsum of the Rosh, from which this law is sourced, Keriat Shema is not mentioned there; and in Magen Gibborim he wrote that it is only a linguistic form, and proof that in siman 38, seif 2, this law also appears and only prayer alone is mentioned there; and here too he concludes only with prayer alone. The intent of the Shulchan Aruch is only to say that because of this his practice changes—that he always prays Keriat Shema and prayer at their time—rather he should recite Keriat Shema alone with its blessings and not pray, for in prayer he stands before the King and it is a great disgrace to flatulate then; but Keriat Shema is not like speaking with the King, rather he fulfills thereby the will of the Holy One, blessed be He. And even if he recites Keriat Shema without tefillin—for tefillin require a clean body—he should not wait and let the time of Keriat Shema pass in order to recite it with tefillin; rather, when he can don tefillin during the day, he should don them.

(ד) And bless over them—and immediately after Keriat Shema he should remove them. But the tallit he may wear and bless over it before he begins the blessings, and he may also wear it all day, for tzitzit do not require a clean body. However, if he did not don the tallit beforehand and it occurred to him between Ahavah and Keriat Shema, he may don it without a blessing, for donning is not an interruption. All this is when this incident occurred because of some food that harmed him and the like, so that in the evening he can certainly pray with a clean body. But one who is accustomed to this—that he has an illness because of which he must flatulate every hour, and if he would not pray he would be exempt from prayer forever—is permitted to pray without a clean body, for the prohibition of flatulence, which is a foul odor without substance, does not come and override Keriat Shema, which is Torah law, or prayer, which is a communal mitzvah; therefore he should recite Keriat Shema and pray [Rama in responsa, siman 98]. And Pri Chadash also wrote thus, except that he added that when flatulence comes upon him he should stop until the odor passes, as with a foul odor without substance—he stops and resumes praying. And Peri Megadim was further uncertain whether perhaps in such a case it is permitted for him to don tefillin as well—for how can he uproot forever the Torah positive commandment of tefillin?—see there. And it seems that the intent of Peri Megadim is only donning for a few moments, only in order to fulfill the positive commandment; more than that is certainly forbidden. And if he cannot restrain himself from flatulating long enough to don both the hand-tefillin and the head-tefillin, he should don them one by one. Regarding the blessing, it seems he should not bless over them. Rama wrote in responsa, siman 98: one who has an illness such that urine constantly drips from him—he is under duress—and if so he could never pray; one may permit making for him a garment around his organ, and the outer garments should be clean, and he may enter the synagogue and pray; at the time urine drips he should stop, and afterward pray—specifically when he did not need to drip at the hour he begins to pray, even though he knows that within his prayer he will need to drip, etc.; see there.
