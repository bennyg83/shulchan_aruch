But one who is accustomed such that he has an illness on account of which he is compelled to belch every hour — and if he does not pray he is exempt from prayer forever — it is permitted for him to pray without a clean body, for the prohibition of belching, which is a bad odor that has no substance, is only rabbinic; and it yields to Keriat Shema, which is Torah, or to prayer, which is a public mitzvah. Therefore he recites Shema and prays [Rama in responsum siman 98]. And Peri Chadash also wrote thus, except that he added that when belching comes to him he stops until the odor passes, as with a bad odor that has no substance — he stops and returns to pray. And Peri Megadim was further uncertain: perhaps in such a case it is also permitted to put on tefillin, for how can he uproot forever a Torah positive commandment of tefillin on account of this? And it seems Peri Megadim's intent is only donning for a few moments, and only in order to fulfill the mitzvah; beyond that it is certainly forbidden.
