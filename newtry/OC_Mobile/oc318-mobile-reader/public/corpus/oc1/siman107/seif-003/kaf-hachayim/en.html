3) There, he returns and prays—it implies that it is an obligation that he must return and pray. And similarly the Rashba in responsum, siman 91; and similarly Lechem Chamudot, chapter 10 of Laws of Prayer, law 6, according to R. Yitzchak. But in the innovations of the Rashba that he wrote in the name of a Gaon and Beit Yosef brought—it implies it is not an obligation that he must return and pray, but permission that if he wishes he returns and prays; and similarly agreed the Peri Chadash, letter 1 in the passage "and know"; and similarly Rashal in Chiddushei Halachot on Tosafot Berakhot 21a; see there. And even according to those who say there is an obligation to return and pray, Pri Megadim wrote in Eshel Avraham, letter 1, that in Maariv there is no obligation to return and pray, for that which they established Maariv as obligatory is certainly not in doubt. And similarly in the prayers of Shabbat and Yom Tov—even in Shacharit and Minchah—he does not return to pray because of doubt, for how can he make a condition that if he prayed it will be voluntary, since one does not pray voluntarily on Shabbat and Yom Tov; see there. And furthermore see in our words below, letter 8, and in siman 124, letter 54.
