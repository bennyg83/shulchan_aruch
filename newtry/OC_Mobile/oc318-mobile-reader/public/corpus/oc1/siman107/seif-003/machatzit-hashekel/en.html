Seif katan 3, He stops, since the ruling is stated without qualification, etc. The Rambam’s view in chapter 10 of Hilchos Tefillah is that in Maariv he does not stop. The reason one must stop is that he began praying with the intent that it be obligatory, and how could he finish with the intent that it be voluntary, making half the prayer obligatory when he was not actually obligated? Maariv, however, is optional, so even at the beginning, when he thought he had not yet prayed, it was only voluntary in character. The Raavad disagrees, because now that the Jewish people have accepted it as obligatory, it has the status of any other prayer.
