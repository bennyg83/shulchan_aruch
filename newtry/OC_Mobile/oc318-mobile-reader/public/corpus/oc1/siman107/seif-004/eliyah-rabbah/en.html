[4] He stops, etc. But if he stipulated at the outset, he completes it as voluntary and innovates something in it from that point onward. Malbushei Yom Tov wrote: I do not know from where he has this, that one must innovate something; and one who cannot innovate—if so, he loses. And all the more so there are those who say below that if nothing was renewed for him; and presumably the decisors said that doubt is like innovation—up to here. And similarly wrote Lechem Chamudot, page 20, and elaborated; see there. And it is possible that the Levush also only said this when he has something to innovate; and good advice is what is implied to us. Furthermore one may say that it seems plausible to him like the explanation of Bach in the name of R. Hai Gaon, that even in doubt one should not pray unless something was renewed in him; and the Levush was concerned for his view if he remembered in the middle of the prayer that he must innovate. And it appears that on this we also rely on the first view in the following seif.
