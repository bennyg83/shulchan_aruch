In the Taz, note 1. NB: See Beit Yosef, who elaborated and strained himself regarding what is the difference between the view of R. Yitzchak and R. Hai; and see also in Bach. And it seems to me that that for R. Yitzchak, R. Yitzchak refers to doubt whether one prayed; if so, one should interpret if one can innovate in it something per the Rosh—that something was renewed with him, as the Rema wrote in the gloss in seif 2. Not so for R. Hai, that R. Yitzchak refers to can innovate in it something—if so, it must suffice with can innovate, etc., and not that something was renewed with him; for if not, how could R. Yitzchak say about this would that he pray all day—is he sitting and waiting when trouble will pass upon the enemies of Israel and he will need to pour out speech? And this requires study.
