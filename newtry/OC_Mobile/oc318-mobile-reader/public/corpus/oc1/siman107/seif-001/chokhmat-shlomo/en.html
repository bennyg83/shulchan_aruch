Seif 1 — If he is in doubt, etc. NB: Behold the Radbaz wrote in a responsum regarding one who is seized [with compulsion] and permission is given you to perform one mitzvah—he should do what comes to his hand first and not wait until tomorrow lest a greater mitzvah present itself to him, for we do not pass over mitzvot. And see Chida (siman 106) what he objects on this and debates whether we are concerned for death or not. And see also Taz in Laws 12, who also wrote regarding Shehecheyanu that one must bless immediately out of concern lest one die. And it is astonishing that Chida did not bring the Talmud in Nedarim chapter 1, which says there regarding one who says I will not leave the world until I am a nazir—naziriteship falls upon him immediately from concern lest he die; see there. And now it is difficult to me from the Talmud Shabbat 69b, which says there: R. Huna was traveling on the road and does not know when is Shabbat—he counts six days and observes one day. Chiya bar Rav said: he observes one day and counts, etc., until the refutation of Chiya bar Rav. And it is difficult: why does the Talmud not say they argue about we do not pass over mitzvot—R. Huna holds we pass over mitzvot, therefore he counts six and observes one day; Chiya bar Rav holds we do not pass over mitzvot, therefore he holds he observes one day and counts, etc. And even more difficult: why does the Talmud challenge Chiya bar Rav—behold R. Yochai and the rabbis argue in the first chapter of Yoma whether we are concerned for death in a short time or not; let us say Chiya bar Rav holds like R. Yochai who is concerned for death, therefore he holds one observes one day and counts six, and the braita accords with the rabbis who are not concerned for death in a short time, therefore he holds one counts six and observes one day. And even though we do not hold like R. Yochai, nevertheless it is preferable to resolve thus than to say it was refuted—and this requires study. In any case it is proved from this that not like the Radbaz—for we hold one counts six and observes one day; and if it were like the creation of the world and it would be a superior mitzvah, it would be fitting to say we do not pass over mitzvot and he should do this immediately rather than wait afterward to do it more beautifully—and examine this well. There, seif 2 — innovation. That which we said, etc. NB: From the words of Rabbeinu Yonah in the first chapter of Berakhot in the mishnah in the passage one who wishes to be stringent on himself," etc.—from there it is proved that if he first prayed without juxtaposing redemption to prayer, then afterward he may pray voluntarily by juxtaposing redemption to prayer, and this is considered an innovation in it—what he juxtaposes redemption to prayer. And behold Rabbeinu Yonah there holds prayer with the congregation is preferable to juxtaposition of redemption to prayer, as is proved there at the beginning; see there. And nevertheless he holds juxtaposition of redemption to prayer is a matter of innovation; all the more so for us who hold later (siman 111) juxtaposition of redemption to prayer is preferable to prayer with the congregation—that this is considered innovation, what he juxtaposes redemption to prayer. Therefore if it happened he entered the synagogue and found the congregation praying and did not know the law that juxtaposition is preferable, and prayed with the congregation—if afterward he came to ask, one should instruct him to pray again as voluntary and juxtapose redemption to prayer, and this suffices for innovation and he need not another innovation, as appears to me—and examine this well. And while on this I saw in Rabbeinu Yonah astonishing things from what he wrote afterward (2a) in the passage and that which we say regarding Maariv is not precise but because Maariv is optional he took Maariv, etc.—and it is difficult why he needs this and why he did not resolve as his words above, since the way of the congregation is to recite Shema before Tefillah; let us say he would recite Shema and pray with the congregation and afterward read Shema alone in its time even without juxtaposition—therefore he teaches that even in Maariv one juxtaposes redemption to prayer and does so as Rabbeinu Yonah wrote at the end: first pray alone and then say Shema and its blessings and pray afterward and juxtapose redemption to prayer; therefore he took Maariv because there would be suspicion he do as Rabbeinu Yonah wrote at first—therefore he teaches the obligation to juxtapose redemption to prayer even in Maariv and to act as Rabbeinu Yonah wrote at the end—and this requires study; examine well.
