1) [Seif 1] If he is in doubt whether he prayed, he returns and prays. And even though in other matters a rabbinic doubt goes to leniency—in prayer, which is supplication, we go to stringency; and he may return and pray, for would that a person pray and seek mercy all day long (Levush, seif 3). And even though the Levush who wrote this reason holds prayer is rabbinic, as written above in siman 106, paragraph 7—nevertheless this reason applies also to the view of those who say prayer is from the Torah; for we wrote above in the cited paragraph that even according to those who say prayer is from the Torah, nevertheless the number of prayers is rabbinic. And if so, from the Torah one [need only pray] one prayer a day, and the rest of the prayers are rabbinic. And here we say plainly: if he is in doubt whether he prayed, he returns and prays—and it implies whether in Shacharit, Minchah, or Maariv; even though he already prayed and fulfilled his obligation from the Torah, if he is in doubt he returns and prays, and even in the other prayers that are rabbinic. And why does he return and pray—since we hold a rabbinic doubt goes to leniency? Rather one must say the reason is because prayer is supplication, we go to stringency, as the reason in Levush. And similarly Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 3; and he wrote not like Etz Yosef, paragraph 2; see there.
