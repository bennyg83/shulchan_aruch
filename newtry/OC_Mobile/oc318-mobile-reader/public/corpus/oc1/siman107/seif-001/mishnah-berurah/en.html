(א) If he prayed—for regarding Krias Shema and its blessings, if he is in doubt, see above in siman 67.

(ב) He returns and prays—and makes a condition and says: If I did not pray, let this be for my obligation; and if I prayed, let it be for my nedavah. And even though every doubt in a rabbinic matter we rule leniently, nevertheless here he returns and prays, because "Would that a person might pray all day" as nedavah and through innovation, as below. And here, when he returns and prays out of doubt, he need not innovate—for you have no greater innovation than fulfilling his doubt. Derech HaChaim wrote that for Maariv in doubt one need not return and pray the eighteen blessings, since they established it as obligatory with certainty, not in doubt. But from the words of the Peri Megadim it seems only that there is no obligation upon him according to all opinions—see there; but lechatchilah it is proper that he return and pray also for Maariv, for in truth even for Maariv, even for those who say it is optional, nevertheless lechatchilah it is a mitzvah; and automatically what fulfills his doubt is considered innovation also for Maariv. And so it seems to my humble opinion from the wording of the Rambam, ch. 10 of Hilchos Tefillah—see there. And Etz Yosef: it is good to innovate something when returning.

(ג) He does not return, etc.—for prayers were instituted corresponding to the daily offerings, and it is like offering two daily offerings as obligatory tamidim, which is forbidden because of bal tosif; and even if he prayed initially alone and now wishes to pray with the congregation, nevertheless it is forbidden.

(ד) And he prays as nedavah—meaning specifically as nedavah; but for obligation, even with innovation, it is forbidden. And that we require innovation in nedavah—through innovation it becomes recognizable from within that his prayer is for supplications and not for obligation; and without innovation he appears as one doing it for obligation. See Beit Yosef, who explained the Tur's view: likewise if one prays a prayer that is not in the manner of obligation and also did not intend it for any nedavah, and he innovates in it—it is fine, for through innovation it is recognizable that it is for supplications. See Biur Halacha.

(ה) The Musaf prayer—meaning even that of Rosh Chodesh and Chol HaMoed, since one says "and the additional offerings as prescribed," and additional offerings are brought only from the congregation's funds, and an individual cannot volunteer them. See the later authorities who wrote that therefore for Musaf prayer, and likewise on Shabbat and Yom Tov for all prayers—if he is in doubt whether he prayed them, he cannot return and pray them, for he must make a condition, as in siman kaf bet above; and here he cannot make a condition. See below in siman 124, seif 1 in Biur Halacha.

(ו) At all—because vows and voluntary offerings are not brought on them.

(ז) Even if he could, etc.—for the beginning of his prayer was presumably certainly for obligation, since he thought he had not yet prayed; and innovation in it does not help. Therefore even if he now wishes to finish it as nedavah it does not help, since he already began it for obligation—for one cannot bring an offering half obligation and half nedavah. All this when he began with intent that he had not prayed; but if he was in doubt whether he prayed and began conditionally, as in siman kaf bet above, and remembered in the middle that he prayed—one could say he need not innovate and may finish thus as nedavah, since he began properly with intent of doubt, and doubt is itself like innovation; further innovation is not needed. And in Levush it seems he finishes through innovation—perhaps this is on the side of greater stringency.
