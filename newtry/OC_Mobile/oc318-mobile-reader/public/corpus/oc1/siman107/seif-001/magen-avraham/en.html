"He returns and prays"—see the reason in the Gemara: "Would that a person might pray all day," unlike the first version; and he makes a condition and says: If I did not pray, let this be for my obligation; and if I prayed, let it be for my nedavah [Beit Yosef, Rashba].

"Except the Musaf prayer"—because it is a chatat and does not come as nedavah; and even though Shabbat Musaf was an olah, nevertheless we follow the majority of additional offerings (Rosh). I do not know why he needed to force an explanation—for it seems a clear reason that the morning prayer can be prayed in place of another offering, unlike Musaf where one says "and the additional offerings as prescribed," etc.; and so too siman 108, seif 6.

"He stops"—from the implication of the baraita it seems he holds like the Ra'avad, that even for Maariv, which is optional, one stops, since it has been established as obligatory.
