If he is in doubt, etc. Beit Yosef expanded in explanations and difficulties on the Tur's words; and what we mention here will settle everything, with Heaven's help. All hold that R' Yochanan does not disagree with Rav Yehuda—neither in the statement of one who remembered in the middle and stops, nor in the statement "if he knows how to innovate," etc.—but R' Yitzchak explains that R' Yochanan deals specifically with doubt whether he prayed and not when he certainly prayed; and he did not distinguish at all between nedavah and obligation. Automatically, when he certainly prayed it is forbidden even as nedavah, for prayers were instituted corresponding to the daily offerings and nedavah is not among the daily offerings; but in doubt it is permitted even as obligation, since it is doubt—he does not fulfill his obligation unless he has certainty that he prayed. And how can he say in this "Would that a person might pray all day"—there is prohibition in the matter if he certainly prayed once? They answered in the Rosh's responsum, general principle 4, that this is what it means: he is not concerned if he prays out of doubt—for even if he already prayed, from where [do we know]? Even if he prays all day out of doubt, automatically through innovation it is permitted even if he certainly prayed. And according to Rav Yehuda and Rif, in the manner of nedavah it is permitted in every manner for an individual, even without innovation, even if he certainly prayed—like an olah that was in the daily offering every day, which a person could volunteer as nedavah, except for Musaf; and in the manner of obligation it is forbidden unless he innovated. And what the Ramban wrote, which Beit Yosef brought—that for Rif he cited the congregation in the statement "if he can innovate" to inform us that specifically with the congregation one needs innovation, because he appears to pray with them for obligation, but not as an individual—end quote. He explained: as an individual one need not innovate, for he has permission anyway through the prayer of nedavah; but the congregation has permission only through innovation. But always, also the individual if he wishes to pray as obligation has permission also through innovation. With this is resolved what Beit Yosef challenged—that Rif should have brought this statement of Rav Yehuda next to what he explained, that R' Yochanan applies to the individual but the congregation may not pray, whether obligation or optional. And since he compared it in its own right, it implies it is not a matter of dividing between congregation and individual—for according to what is true, innovation helps also for the individual if he wishes to pray as obligation; but he need not do so, for he has permission anyway through nedavah. Also what he challenged: if he is considered like the congregation itself when praying with them, what does innovation help—for for the congregation innovation does not help? And if he is not considered like the congregation, even without innovating it is fine. And it is forced to say... in withholding from his honor—all this is not needed according to what is written: certainly the congregation does not give him any superiority in this matter or inferiority except for this matter—for if he prayed in solitude he would have permission to pray as obligation through innovation, but he did not need to, since he has permission through nedavah; but if he prays with the congregation he has no permission in nedavah, because he appears to pray with them for obligation; therefore he has permission only through innovation, and then it is permitted even as obligation. With this is also resolved what Beit Yosef and our teacher the Rosh wrote, who explains Rif's words per the Ramban—for then how did he write that per all opinions there is no distinction between finding a congregation praying or not—nevertheless one should not pray except through innovation? For per the Ramban, when he is not with the congregation he prays even without innovation—there is not even a scent of difficulty. Our teacher deals here with one praying as obligation; in this there is no distinction between individual and one praying with the congregation, as he wrote—except that afterward our teacher wrote: if he wishes to pray as nedavah, what is his law? According to Rif he may pray except Musaf, and then he need not innovate. And one could say: why did our teacher not write there that one praying with the congregation—nedavah does not help for him, but specifically innovation is required—for our teacher already wrote the words of R' Yitzchak, who said explicitly: one who enters the synagogue and finds the congregation praying—if he can innovate, etc., he prays; and if not, he should not pray. From the wording "he should not pray" it implies he has no remedy in the world since he has no innovation. And Rabbeinu Hai holds that even for nedavah one needs innovation, but for obligation innovation does not help; and the wording of Rav Yehuda supports him, who said: if he can innovate so his prayer be supplications—it implies that through innovation he prays as supplications, which is nedavah. And what the Ramban and Rosh in the name of Rabbeinu Hai wrote—that he prays all prayers as nedavah, even Musaf—this is through innovation; for otherwise it would be difficult for you: who forced Rabbeinu Hai to explain R' Yitzchak's words through innovation, since there is permission through nedavah? He should have explained R' Yitzchak's words thus, as the Rif truly [holds]—but rather Rabbeinu Hai must be speaking through innovation here; and so it is explained in the proofs the Ramban and Rosh bring for Rav Hai, that for nedavah alone there is no permission. With this is resolved what Beit Yosef challenged, namely: but if there is a difficulty in Rabbeinu Hai's words—why did he not write any dissent against Rif in what he wrote, that the individual cannot pray Musaf as nedavah, when R' Yitzchak and Rosh wrote that Rabbeinu Hai holds one may pray Musaf as nedavah? End quote. One could say: according to what is written, since he speaks through innovation—and it is possible this matter did not seem right to Rabbeinu Hai, for innovation does not fit well in Musaf, since it is not supplications but acknowledgment; therefore he did not bring his view on this. And even though Rabbeinu Hai did not mention the matter of nedavah in Rav Hai's words, this is nothing—for it refers to Rif's words, who explains R' Yitzchak's words regarding nedavah; and Rav Hai's words refer to this, that he adds one needs specifically innovation. Therefore Rabbeinu Hai afterward wrote: and according to Rav Hai one may not pray nedavah without innovation—end quote. Aimed at what is written; and everything is resolved, with Heaven's help.
