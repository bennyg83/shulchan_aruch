If he is uncertain, etc. The Beit Yosef discussed the Tur’s words at length, offering interpretations and raising difficulties. With what we explain here, everything will be resolved, with Hashem’s help. All authorities maintain that Rabbi Yochanan does not disagree with Rav Yehudah, either regarding the statement that one who remembers in the middle of the prayer stops, or regarding the statement that if he can introduce a new element, etc. Rabbeinu Yonah explains that Rabbi Yochanan discusses only one who is uncertain whether he prayed, not one who certainly prayed, and he makes no distinction between a voluntary and an obligatory prayer. Consequently, where one certainly prayed, it is forbidden even as a voluntary prayer, because the prayers were instituted corresponding to the daily offerings, and there was no voluntary daily offering. Where one is uncertain, however, it is permitted even as an obligatory prayer, because due to the uncertainty he has not fulfilled his obligation unless it is certain that he prayed. One might ask how Rabbi Yochanan could say, “Would that a person pray all day,” when it is forbidden to pray again after certainly praying once. The Rosh answered in responsum, rule 4, that Rabbi Yochanan means that one need not be concerned when praying because of uncertainty, for even if he already prayed, what difference does it make if he prays all day because of uncertainty? Thus, by introducing a new element, it is permitted even where he certainly prayed, in accordance with Rav Yehudah’s other statement. The Rif maintains that an individual may always pray voluntarily, even without a new element and even where he certainly prayed, just as the daily offering was an olah and a person may donate an olah, except for Mussaf. To pray as an obligation, however, is forbidden unless he introduces a new element. Rabbeinu Yonah, cited by the Beit Yosef, writes that according to the Rif, Rav Yehudah specifically mentioned a congregation in the statement, “If he can introduce a new element,” to teach that only with a congregation is a new element required, because he appears to be praying with them as an obligation, whereas an individual does not require one. This means that an individual does not require a new element because he is independently permitted to pray voluntarily. A congregation, however, has no permission except through a new element. Nevertheless, even an individual who wishes to pray as an obligation is likewise permitted through introducing a new element. This resolves the Beit Yosef’s question that the Rif should have cited Rav Yehudah’s statement next to his explanation that Rabbi Yochanan’s statement applies to an individual, whereas a congregation may not pray either obligatorily or voluntarily. The fact that he cites it separately seemed to imply that it is unrelated to the distinction between a congregation and an individual. There is no difficulty, because introducing a new element also helps an individual who wishes to pray as an obligation. He does not ordinarily need this permission, since he may pray voluntarily. This also resolves the Beit Yosef’s further question: If one praying with the congregation is considered part of the congregation, how does a new element help, since it does not help the congregation? And if he is not considered part of the congregation, he should be permitted even without a new element. With respect to his honor, none of this is difficult according to what I have explained. The congregation gives him neither advantage nor disadvantage except in one respect. Were he praying alone, he could pray as an obligation by introducing a new element, though he would not need to rely on this because he could pray voluntarily. When praying with the congregation, however, he cannot rely on a voluntary prayer, because he appears to be praying with them as an obligation. His only permission is therefore through introducing a new element, in which case he may pray even as an obligation. This also resolves the Beit Yosef’s statement that the Tur cannot interpret the Rif as Rabbeinu Yonah does, because the Tur wrote that according to all authorities there is no distinction whether he found a congregation praying or not, and he should never pray except with a new element, whereas according to Rabbeinu Yonah, when he is not with the congregation he may pray without a new element. There is not even the beginning of a difficulty, because the Tur is discussing prayer as an obligation, and in that respect there is no distinction between one praying alone and one praying with the congregation, as explained. Only afterward does the Tur discuss one who wishes to pray voluntarily and asks what his law is. According to the Rif he may do so, except for Mussaf, and then no new element is required. One might ask why the Tur did not state there that voluntary prayer does not help when praying with the congregation and that he specifically needs a new element. The Tur had already cited Rav Yehudah’s explicit statement: One who enters the synagogue and finds the congregation praying may pray if he can introduce a new element, but if not, he should not pray. The phrase “he should not pray” implies that he has no remedy at all when he lacks a new element. Rav Hai maintains that even a voluntary prayer requires a new element, whereas for an obligatory prayer a new element does not help. Rav Yehudah’s wording supports him, for he says that if one can introduce a new element so that his prayer will be supplication, he may pray. This implies that through the new element he prays as supplication, meaning voluntarily. Rabbeinu Yonah and the Rosh state in Rav Hai’s name that one may pray all prayers voluntarily, even Mussaf. This means through introducing a new element, for otherwise one could ask what compelled Rav Hai to interpret Rav Yehudah’s words as requiring a new element. Since voluntary prayer would itself provide permission, he should have interpreted Rabbi Yochanan’s statement that way, as the Rif did. It is therefore clear that Rav Hai means specifically with a new element. This is also explicit in the proofs that Rabbeinu Yonah and the Rosh bring for Rav Hai, showing that voluntary intent alone is insufficient. This resolves the Beit Yosef’s question: Why did the Tur cite no one who disagrees with the Rif’s ruling that an individual cannot pray Mussaf voluntarily, when Rabbeinu Yonah and the Rosh write that Rav Hai permits Mussaf voluntarily? According to what I have explained, Rav Hai permits it only with a new element. Perhaps the Tur did not accept this, because a new element does not properly apply to Mussaf, which is not supplication but thanksgiving, and he therefore omitted Rav Hai’s view on this point. Although the Tur did not mention voluntary prayer in his presentation of Rav Hai’s view, that is immaterial, because it follows the Rif’s explanation that Rav Yehudah’s statement concerns voluntary prayer, and Rav Hai adds only that a new element is specifically required. The Tur therefore later writes, “According to Rav Hai, one may not pray voluntarily without a new element.” This accords exactly with what I have written, and everything is resolved, with the help of Heaven.
