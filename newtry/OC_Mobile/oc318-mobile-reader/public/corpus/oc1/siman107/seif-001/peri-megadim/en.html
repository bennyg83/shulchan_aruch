If—The Taz expanded on this, and apparently there are three views: (1) R' Yitzchak, author of Tosafos; (2) Rif; (3) Rabbeinu Hai—and all hold according to R' Yochanan, and the two statements of Rav Yehuda are one halachah (Berachos 21a). R' Yitzchak, author of Tosafos, holds: nedavah without innovation is forbidden if he certainly prayed; and through innovation, obligation too is permitted; therefore if in doubt whether he prayed he must pray as obligation—and this is his innovation. And now the statement of R' Yochanan "Would that..." needs emendation as the Rosh in his responsum; and the statement of R' Yitzchak "one who remembered, stops"—one need not force that he thought he had not prayed and remembered he prayed, but he was in doubt and began with intent of obligation and remembered and stopped—meaning he is not able to innovate something, for otherwise he would finish through innovation. And according to Rif, even for nedavah when he certainly prayed it is permitted without innovation—and this is the statement of R' Yochanan (and the emendation of the Rosh is not needed); and through innovation obligation too is permitted. And this is what "he found the congregation praying" means—the congregation too is permitted thus, except the superiority that nevertheless he is considered with the congregation and forbidden in nedavah except through innovation, yet permitted even as obligation. And Rabbeinu Hai explained the statement of R' Yochanan also when he certainly prayed—through innovation; for nedavah without innovation is forbidden, and also through innovation obligation is forbidden. And that which the Taz wrote that the wording of R' Yitzchak "if he can innovate so his prayer be supplications" supports him in the Gemara—there is none; rather the version of the Rif is correct (Magen Avraham, ot dalet, and what he wrote there); and if so, for Rif this is difficult. And it would have been proper to expand, but time did not suffice; I stood on this. And the reason for the dispute of the poskim whether nedavah is permitted or not: one authority permits, following the offering—olah comes as nedavah; and one forbids, following the daily offerings—tamid is not nedavah; and automatically through innovation it is permitted even for obligation according to R' Yitzchak, author of Tosafos. But according to Rabbeinu Hai, nedavah is forbidden; through innovation it is permitted, so it be recognizable in his prayer that it is nedavah; but obligation through innovation is also forbidden. And I did not find it explained for Rabbeinu Hai that he would say obligation is forbidden through innovation.
