Magen Avraham, siman kaf bet. Nevertheless we follow the majority of additional offerings. Even if we say that on Shabbat and Yom Tov one may pray the morning prayer as nedavah—but per our ruling here in Shulchan Aruch that on Shabbat and Yom Tov one may not pray nedavah—this is fine. So it is explained in Beit Yosef. Nevertheless I have a question: for even if we say that on Shabbat and Yom Tov one may pray nedavah—as Beit Yosef wrote—since throughout the year one prays nedavah, on Shabbat and Yom Tov too one prays that nedavah; the Sages did not see fit to exclude Shabbats and festivals from the category of the rest of the days of the year in a matter that has no prohibition, but only as a mere example—see there. If so, why did Beit Yosef need to say that for Shabbat Musaf, since in other additional offerings there is a goat, one does not pray nedavah? He did not exclude Shabbat Musaf from the general rule—for from the simple sense of that which for Musaf in every case there is no nedavah, since generally there is a goat, and on Shabbat there is no nedavah—but for morning and afternoon he did not exclude Shabbats and festivals from the rest of the days of the year. And it requires study.
