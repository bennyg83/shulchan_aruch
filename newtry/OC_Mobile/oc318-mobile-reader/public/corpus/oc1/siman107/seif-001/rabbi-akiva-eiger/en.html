Magen Avraham, se'if katan 2. Nevertheless, we follow the majority of Musaf prayers. This is so even if we say that on Shabbos and Yom Tov one may also recite Shacharis as a voluntary prayer. According to what we rule here in the Shulchan Aruch, however, that one cannot recite a voluntary prayer on Shabbos or Yom Tov, the matter is otherwise readily understood. This is explained in Beit Yosef. Nevertheless, it is difficult for me: Even if we say that one may recite a voluntary prayer on Shabbos and Yom Tov, this is only for the reason stated by Beit Yosef; since one may recite a voluntary prayer throughout the year, he may recite that same voluntary prayer on Shabbos and Yom Tov as well, for the Sages did not exclude Shabbos and festivals from the rest of the year regarding something that entails no prohibition and is merely analogous to an offering; see there. If so, why did Beit Yosef need to say that regarding Shabbos Musaf, since the other Musaf offerings include a goat and therefore cannot be offered voluntarily, the Shabbos Musaf was not excluded from their general rule? It follows more simply from the fact that a voluntary prayer never applies to Musaf: ordinarily its offering includes a goat, and on Shabbos a voluntary Musaf likewise does not apply. By contrast, regarding Shacharis and Minchah the Sages did not exclude Shabbos and Yom Tov from the other days of the year. This requires further study.
