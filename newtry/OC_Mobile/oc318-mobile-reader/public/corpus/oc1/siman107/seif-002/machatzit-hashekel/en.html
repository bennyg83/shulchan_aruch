Seif katan 2, Except, etc. Because it includes a sin offering. Meaning, the Rosh challenged the Rif, who wrote that Mussaf may not be prayed voluntarily because the Mussaf offering cannot be brought voluntarily. Shacharis was instituted corresponding to the daily offering, and the daily offering likewise cannot be brought voluntarily. One must therefore say that since the daily offering was an olah, and an olah may be brought voluntarily, his prayer may be treated as corresponding to a voluntary olah rather than to the daily offering. If so, the same should apply to Mussaf, since the Mussaf offering was also an olah. He answered that Mussaf also included a goat as a sin offering, and a sin offering can never be brought voluntarily. Against this he asked that the Mussaf of Shabbos included no goat as a sin offering, but only olos. Although on Shabbos even Shacharis may not be prayed voluntarily, as stated in Shulchan Aruch, that is our ruling, because vows and voluntary offerings are not brought on Shabbos or Yom Tov. The Rosh’s question, however, was directed at the Rif, who maintains in a responsum that one may pray Shacharis voluntarily on Shabbos. Although vows and voluntary offerings are not brought on Shabbos or Yom Tov, this is because Shabbos may not be desecrated for them, whereas a voluntary prayer involves no desecration of Shabbos and is therefore permitted. The Rosh answered that we follow the majority of Mussaf offerings, which included a goat as a sin offering, and the Sages applied a uniform rule that even the Mussaf of Shabbos may not be prayed voluntarily, although it contains no sin offering.
In place of another offering. Namely, a voluntary olah, as explained above. Although the prayers were instituted corresponding to the daily offerings, the daily offering is not mentioned explicitly in Shacharis. In Mussaf, however, one says, “and the Mussaf offerings according to their law.” How then could he pray it as corresponding to a voluntary olah?
