(Siman kaf bet.) "Except..."—because it is a chatat. Meaning: the Rosh challenged the Rif, who wrote that one does not pray Musaf as nedavah, since the additional offering does not come as nedavah—yet the morning prayer corresponds to the tamid and was instituted, and the tamid also does not come as nedavah. Therefore one must say: since the tamid was an olah, and an olah comes as nedavah, his prayer should be in place of an olah and not in place of the tamid; thus also for Musaf one could say so—for the additional offering was also an olah. And one could answer: since in Musaf there was also a goat for a chatat, and a chatat does not come as nedavah at all, therefore one does not pray Musaf as nedavah. On this the Rosh challenged: but the Musaf of Shabbat had no goat for a chatat, only a plain olah; and even though on Shabbat one does not pray even the morning prayer as nedavah, as written in Shulchan Aruch—for us—the reason is that vows and voluntary offerings are not brought on Shabbat and Yom Tov. But the Rosh's difficulty to the Rif, who holds in his responsum that one may pray Shabbat morning as nedavah even though vows and voluntary offerings are not brought on Shabbat and Yom Tov—the reason is that we do not desecrate Shabbat for vows and voluntary offerings, unlike a prayer of nedavah in which there is no Shabbat desecration, so it is permitted. Therefore the Rosh answered that we follow the majority of additional offerings, which include a goat for a chatat; and the Sages did not distinguish, so as not to pray even Shabbat Musaf as nedavah, even though it has no chatat.

In place of another offering—meaning an olah of nedavah, as above. Even though prayers were instituted corresponding to the daily offerings, nevertheless the tamid offering was not mentioned in the morning prayer, unlike in the Musaf prayer where it is mentioned in the prayer "and the additional offerings as prescribed"—so how could one pray it as an olah of nedavah?
