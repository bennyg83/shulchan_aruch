There In a case of uncertainty, it is automatically permitted through introducing a new element. The ruling that one stops even in the middle of a blessing concerns one who cannot introduce a new element. The statement that a voluntary prayer is permitted in every case for an individual refers to Rabbi Yochanan’s dictum, “Would that a person pray all day.” The statement that an obligatory prayer is forbidden, etc., refers to the ruling that he stops even in the middle of a blessing, because he began with the intent that it be obligatory, and since he does not know how to introduce a new element, he may not complete it as a voluntary prayer. The Beit Yosef’s question, that the Rif should have cited this ruling, rests on the Beit Yosef’s view that, for an individual praying as an obligation, even a new element does not help, as implied by the ruling that he stops even in the middle of a blessing, which the Beit Yosef understands to apply even if he can introduce a new element. He understands the case of one who enters the synagogue to mean that he prays voluntarily, and that if he were not in the synagogue he would not need a new element. Since he is with the congregation in the synagogue, he needs a new element. Therefore the Beit Yosef asked that, since the ruling concerning an individual, Rabbi Yochanan’s dictum, distinguishes between an individual praying alone and one praying with the congregation, the Rif should have cited this statement where he wrote that Rabbi Yochanan’s ruling applies to an individual, whereas a congregation may not pray either obligatorily or voluntarily. It would then have taught that Rabbi Yochanan’s ruling contains a further distinction, namely that an individual praying alone does not need a new element, but one praying with the congregation does. From this question the Beit Yosef proves that an individual praying voluntarily requires a new element even when praying alone. See there. The Taz answers that a new element does in fact help an individual even when praying as an obligation, contrary to the Beit Yosef. Furthermore, one who enters the synagogue is not permitted to pray voluntarily because he appears to be praying with the congregation as an obligation. His only permission is therefore through introducing a new element, since a new element also helps for an obligatory prayer. Accordingly, there was no reason for the Rif to place the rulings together as the Beit Yosef asked, because Rabbi Yochanan discusses a voluntary prayer, whereas Rav Yehudah discusses an obligatory prayer. With regard to an obligatory prayer there is no distinction between praying alone and with the congregation. In both cases it is permitted with a new element and forbidden without one. When the Beit Yosef says that only with the congregation is a new element required, whereas alone it is not, he means that one praying alone may instead pray voluntarily. But regarding prayer as an obligation, there is no distinction between alone and with the congregation. And although an individual praying voluntarily alone does not need a new element while one praying with the congregation does, because he may not pray voluntarily there, this is not considered a substantive distinction between an individual and a congregation. It is only due to appearance, since he appears to be praying as an obligation, as the Taz later writes. Examine this carefully.
