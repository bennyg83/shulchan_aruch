"Something new occurred to him"—for otherwise, who is there who cannot innovate something in one of all the blessings—for healing, or livelihood, or his studies, etc. [Tur]. I was asked: if so, that which we say on page 29, "One who makes his prayer fixed—his prayer is not supplications," and Rabbah and R' Yitzchak explained it as "one who cannot innovate something in it"—this too is difficult: who is there who cannot innovate something? And there one cannot say that nothing occurred to him—for then he would never pray supplications unless something occurred to him, and this is not reasonable. And further, according to this, what is R' Zeira saying, "I can innovate a matter but I fear I will be distracted"? And the version of the Rif: "If he can innovate something so that his prayer be supplications, he may pray"—implies he holds like Rabbah and R' Yitzchak, that prayer is not called supplications without innovation. If so, here too regarding a fixed prayer one may pray it every day three times; but supplications one may pray all day. Therefore Rabbah said that if something occurred to him he may pray all day; and R' Zeira too says that even though something occurred to him he feared being distracted; and one who is sure he will not be distracted may innovate, as above. Alternatively, this is what it means: if he is a person who, even if something occurred to him, cannot innovate something in his prayer—his prayer is not supplications.
