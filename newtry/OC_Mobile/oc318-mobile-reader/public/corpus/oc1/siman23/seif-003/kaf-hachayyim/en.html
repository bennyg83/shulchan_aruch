3) [Seif 3] One who enters within four amot of a deceased person or a grave, etc. Even at a child's grave there is a concern of mocking the destitute, for perhaps it is the soul of a great person. Eliyah Rabbah, ot 3; and Yad Aharon in siman 45 in the name of Maharit Tzahalon. Peri Megadim, Eshel Avraham, ot 2, cites this and writes that it implies that at a woman's grave there is no concern of mocking the destitute, since during her lifetime she too was exempt. We are not so concerned that one should suspect reincarnation of a male soul. Pithei Olam, ot 4, cites his words. Mishneh LaMelech, however, in chapter 13 of Hilkhot Avel, law 9, writes that the prohibition of mocking the destitute applies to a woman as well; see there. Divrei Menachem, siman 45, ot 3, cites him. See Pithei HaDevir, siman 45, ot 3, who writes that Maharit Tzahalon too maintains that the prohibition applies to a woman, but Eliyah Rabbah did not cite all of Maharit Tzahalon's words and thereby caused Peri Megadim to state the opposite of Maharit Tzahalon's actual view; see there.
