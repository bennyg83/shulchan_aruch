[3] Within four cubits, etc. It implies in Beit Yosef that in a cemetery too it is permitted except within four cubits; likewise Bach siman 45 and Orach Chayim. That they specified "corpse" teaches that even when visible it is permitted beyond four cubits — so wrote Olas Tamid, Nachalas Tzvi, and Shiyurei Knesset HaGedolah. But Taz below siman 45 wrote that in a cemetery, even beyond four cubits, is forbidden; Magen Avraham there was stringent — his wording: even within four cubits of the cemetery is forbidden — so far. Indeed this is implied in Yoreh De'ah siman 282 and Tur there, and Rambam chapter 10 of Sefer Torah. However Yoreh De'ah siman 367 and below siman 45 and Orach Chayim imply like Taz; Yoreh De'ah siman 343 has some support for the first view — Rama ruled one may greet in a cemetery beyond four cubits from the grave — see there; requires investigation. Yoreh De'ah siman 367 explains these laws. Beit Yosef, Yoreh De'ah siman 282, wrote it is forbidden to carry a Torah scroll on one's arm in a cemetery, even wrapped in a cloth and not reading it. At the end of responsa R' Yom Tov Tzahalon he wrote: even if there is only a small grave, lo'eig lerash applies, for perhaps it is the soul of a great person.
