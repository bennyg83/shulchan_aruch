[3] Within four amos, etc. Beit Yosef implies that even in a cemetery it is permitted outside four amos of a grave. Bach writes the same in simanim 45 and 71. The reason the authorities mention a particular deceased person is to teach a novelty: even when the tzitzis are visible, it is permitted outside four amos. Olas Tamid, Nachalas Tzvi, and Shiurei Knesses HaGedolah write the same. Taz, however, below in siman 45, writes that in a cemetery it is forbidden even outside four amos. Magen Avraham there is stringent and writes: “Even within four amos of the cemetery it is forbidden.” This indeed appears from Yoreh Deah, siman 282, Tur there, and Rambam, chapter 10 of Hilchos Sefer Torah. On the other hand, Yoreh Deah, siman 367, and below, simanim 45 and 71, imply like Taz. Yoreh Deah, siman 343, offers some support for the first view, for Rama rules that one may greet another in a cemetery outside four amos of a grave. This requires study. These laws are explained in Yoreh Deah, siman 367. Beit Yosef in Yoreh Deah, siman 282, writes that it is forbidden to carry a Sefer Torah upon one's arm in a cemetery even when it is wrapped in a cloth and one is not reading from it. At the end of responsa Rabbi Yom Tov Tzahalon it is written that even where there is only the grave of a minor, the prohibition of mocking the deceased applies, for perhaps the soul is that of a great person.
