(Se'if katan 2) "where they are accustomed," etc. This is like the first explanation — meaning, Rosh wrote on what we say: although Shmuel holds tzitzis is an obligation of the garment, Shmuel concedes regarding an elder who made a tallis for his shrouds that he is exempt (see Magen Avraham siman 19). Gemara concludes there: "at that time we certainly throw them on him." Rosh explains "at that time" — when they dress the deceased in the tallis; we certainly require tzitzis on it, otherwise lo'eig lerash. He asked accordingly: why did R' Yochanan object to R' Yonasan, whose tzitzis dragged on graves, because of lo'eig lerash — behold the dead also have tzitzis? Answer: nevertheless there is lo'eig lerash, for in any case the dead are not commanded. Accordingly, even if they do not remove tzitzis from the deceased's tallis at home, if pallbearers wear tzitzis there is lo'eig lerash — the deceased is not commanded. Second explanation of Rosh: "at that time we certainly throw them on him" — when they dress the deceased in the tallis and take him out for burial; then because pallbearers might have tzitzis, and if the deceased had no tzitzis it would be lo'eig lerash — therefore the deceased's tallis too needs tzitzis. In the cemetery at burial they again untie the tzitzis. If so, R' Yonasan's case is not difficult, since the dead have no tzitzis. Accordingly, if the dead have tzitzis, even though not commanded, there is no lo'eig lerash. Therefore poskim contradict Rabbeinu Yonah back and forth: from what is stated here in this se'if, "where they are accustomed," it implies the main view is Rosh's second explanation; yet from what he ruled Yoreh De'ah siman 351, placing tzitzis, etc., yet ruled here forbidden to enter a cemetery — it implies the first explanation of Rosh.
