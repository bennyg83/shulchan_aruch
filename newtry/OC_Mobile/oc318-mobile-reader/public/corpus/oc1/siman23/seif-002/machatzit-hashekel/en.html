Seif katan 2: Where the custom is, etc. This follows Rosh's first explanation. Rosh writes concerning the Gemara's statement that although Shmuel maintains tzitzis is an obligation upon the garment, he concedes that an elderly person who made a tallis for his shrouds is exempt; see Magen Avraham, siman 19. The Gemara concludes, “At that time we certainly place them upon him.” Rosh explains that “at that time” means when the deceased is dressed in the tallis: we certainly attach tzitzis, because otherwise there would be mockery of the deceased. He asks why Rabbi Yochanan protested when Rabbi Yonasan's tzitzis dragged over graves because of mockery of the deceased, since the deceased also have tzitzis. He answers that mockery remains because the deceased are not commanded in the mitzvos. According to this, even if the tzitzis are not removed from the deceased's tallis at home, if those carrying him wear tzitzis there is still mockery of the deceased, because the deceased is not commanded. Rosh gives a second explanation: “At that time we certainly place them upon him” means when they dress the deceased in the tallis and take him out for burial. Since those carrying him have tzitzis, if the deceased had none there would be mockery. Therefore, the deceased's tallis must also have tzitzis. In the cemetery, at the time of burial, they remove the tzitzis. The account of Rabbi Yonasan is then readily understood, because the deceased have no tzitzis. According to this explanation, if the deceased does have tzitzis, although he is not commanded, there is no mockery. Beit Yosef's rulings therefore contradict one another. From what he writes here, “in a place where the custom is,” it appears that he treats Rosh's second explanation as primary. Yet in Yoreh Deah, siman 351, he rules that tzitzis are placed upon the deceased, while nevertheless ruling here that it is forbidden to enter a cemetery wearing visible tzitzis. This implies that he follows Rosh's first explanation.
