[2] And they did not accomplish, etc. See Levush's question upon Beit Yosef and my resolution in Eliyah Zuta. I later saw that Malbushei Yom Tov likewise agrees with Beit Yosef. He writes that even if this proof is not conclusive, Beit Yosef already demonstrated from another source that tying does not nullify the tzitzis, from siman 10, seif 3, as Levush himself began to explain. He also rejected the comparison to hanging them, and further proved that otherwise one would be wearing a four-cornered garment without tzitzis. He also distinguished properly between tzitzis and sukkah. Mordechai in Hilchos Tzitzis writes in the name of Ri: “When I die, I shall instruct my sons to tie the tzitzis to the corners of the garment so that in the future they will be ready, when the tied garment is opened, to hang automatically, and this is not something already made.” Bach in Yoreh Deah, siman 351, explains that this means tying the two tzitzis of two corners to one another. This implies that tying invalidates them, which is difficult according to Beit Yosef and Levush. Perhaps that case is different, because they are not intended to be untied while the person remains dead. Nevertheless, Mordechai writes that it is not something already made, and this requires study. Magen Avraham also did not address all of this. Bach writes there that it is proper either to tie the tzitzis of the deceased to one another or to cover them beneath the corners. This implies that a living person should take care not to do so. One may, however, force an explanation from Semak, who writes that in such a case there is no mockery of the deceased: if they are obligated, they have tzitzis; and if not, the tzitzis are covered. Examine this carefully, and see above, siman 8, in Magen Avraham.
