And they did not accomplish anything. Although they tie the tzitzis, the mitzvah of tzitzis is not nullified, as stated in siman 10. Furthermore, according to their own view that tying nullifies the tzitzis, when they later untie them it would become “you shall make,” and not something already made. Beit Yosef. Levush writes that this does not involve something already made, as stated at the end of siman 626: when one removes the roof from above a sukkah, it is not something already made. In my humble opinion, the cases are not comparable. There the invalidity is not in the sukkah itself, but in something external that prevents its validity; when he removes it, the sukkah is valid. Here, however, the invalidity affects the tzitzis themselves. The same is implied by siman 10, seif 6; see there and Beit Yosef. See also Sukkah 33a. Tosafos on Berachos 18a write: “The tzitzis of a deceased person's tallis should be tied and fastened within the corner. If the deceased are obligated in tzitzis, they have tzitzis; and if not, the tzitzis are covered.” This implies that even though they are tied, the mitzvah of tzitzis is not nullified. Perhaps tying two sets to one another is different.
