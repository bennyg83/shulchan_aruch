Se'if katan 1, "and it did not," etc. As siman 10, se'if 3: even if one folds and ties the corners so they appear to have no corners, one is nevertheless obligated — for he will untie the knot and the corner is not voided. The same here: tzitzis are not voided, since he will untie the knots. Moreover, according to their view — Rabbeinu Yonah also asked them: if so, according to their view they are at that moment wearing a four-cornered garment without tzitzis.

And Levush, etc. As siman 626: it is not ta'aseh velo min ha'asuy, since he first put sechach in validity — he removed the roof when putting sechach, only afterward they closed it; therefore when opened it returns to validity. The same here, since tzitzis were made in validity — when he unties the knots, tzitzis return to their original validity, and there is no ta'aseh velo min ha'asuy (but according to Levush's view there, he permits below to put sechach lechatchilah even before removing the roof — see there). As siman 10, se'if 6: according to Rambam, if one attached tzitzis upon tzitzis intending to add — even if he later cuts the second tzitzis, the first are disqualified because of ta'aseh velo min ha'asuy, even though their beginning was in validity — see there; in Rabbeinu Yonah you will find this explicitly.

And see Sukkah, etc. One must say the tannaim dispute whether there is delay (dechiyah) regarding mitzvos — it teaches: hadas whose berries are more numerous above and are black is invalid; if one reduced them it is valid, and one does not reduce on Yom Tov because of tikkun mana. If one passed and reduced them: Tanna Kamma validates; R' Eliezer b'R' Tzadok invalidates. Do they disagree, or does everyone hold lulav does not require binding, and ta'aseh velo min ha'asuy does not apply — for at binding they were invalid, and thus made in invalidity; when he reduced afterward it is ta'aseh velo min ha'asuy, since binding is not needed? If you say binding is required, you must say: although in sukkah ta'aseh velo min ha'asuy disqualifies, in lulav it does not — we do not derive lulav from sukkah; their dispute is only whether there is dechiyah in mitzvos. According to Levush it should rather say: everyone holds binding is required and ta'aseh velo min ha'asuy disqualifies — but it speaks when at binding the berries were green and valid, binding was first in validity, and on Yom Tov they blackened and afterward he reduced — no ta'aseh velo min ha'asuy, since binding's beginning was in validity; they dispute whether there is dechiyah; and although in such a case it is "seen and rejected," in this there is more reason to say there is dechiyah — unlike rejected from the outset, as explained there. Gemara means they dispute dechiyah from the outset — meaning, there the distinction between dechiyah from the outset versus seen-and-rejected was not yet settled; or Levush's reasoning does not hold.

When tying two, etc. Here Shulchan Aruch speaks of tying two tzitzis to each other; Tosafos speaks of tying each tzitzis by itself.
