1) [Seif 1] It is permitted to enter, etc. If the tzitzit drag over the graves, however, it is prohibited because this mocks the destitute, etc. The reason is that the souls of the dead stand over their graves, as stated in Shaar HaMitzvot, parashat Vayechi, and Shaar HaGilgulim, introduction 37, page 73a. Otherwise, there would be no basis for distinguishing between covered and uncovered tzitzit, since the deceased is in any event covered within the grave. Rather, the distinction exists because of the soul standing there above the grave, as stated. See Shitilei Zeitim, ot 3.
1) If. If one teaches Torah at the grave of a deceased person for the elevation of that person's soul, there is no prohibition of mocking the destitute, since he is acting for the deceased's honor. See Tosafot, Bava Kamma 16b, s.v. Sheheshivu Yeshivah, etc.; Pithei HaDevir, siman 45, ot 4; and Yoreh Deah, siman 367, seif 3.
2) There. But if they are covered, it is permitted. Likewise, a tallit katan worn beneath one's garments involves no prohibition of mocking the destitute, and one may enter a cemetery wearing it because it is covered. Beit Yosef; Bach, who writes that this is the custom; Perishah; Ateret Zekenim; Maamar Mordechai, ot 1. Birkei Yosef, ot 1, writes likewise in the name of Tiferet Shmuel, page 40d. It is surprising that he cites this law from Tiferet Shmuel without mentioning that it appears in Beit Yosef. Shaarei Teshuvah, ot 1, cites him. See our words below, siman 24, at the end of ot 4.
