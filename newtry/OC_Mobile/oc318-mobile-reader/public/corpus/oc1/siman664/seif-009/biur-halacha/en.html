If he did not stipulate about it from the outset. Seemingly his intent is like the law of sukkah decorations, where it is explained in siman 638 that he must stipulate before twilight that he is not separating himself from benefiting from them. However, according to what the Bikkurei Yaakov wrote in siman 653, it is enough that he stipulate before performing the mitzvah that it should not become prohibited through performance of the mitzvah, because he is not separating himself from benefiting from it. The same applies regarding hadas, which is prohibited to smell throughout the seven days because he set it aside for its mitzvah: it is also enough that he stipulate before performing the mitzvah that he is not separating himself from benefiting from it. However, regarding the basic law whether a condition helps, the Magen Avraham proved that this depends on a dispute. According to the Rashba and the Ran, and also according to Sefer Yereim, a condition helps even for that same day. Although he wrote that Sefer Yereim did not say that a condition helps, he had only the short Sefer Yereim; now that we have merited the printing of the manuscript of the complete Sefer Yereim, it is written there explicitly as the Hagahos Maimoniyos wrote in his name, that a condition helps, and the commentator there already preceded me in this. But according to Tosafos, a condition does not help in this. These are his words. It follows from him that regarding aravah, and likewise esrog, even if one set it aside after the first day, a condition does not help to benefit from it that same day. I do not understand his proof well. Here, where we are discussing aravah, which is only for the seventh day, from where do we know that according to Tosafos a condition does not help for that same day? We have proof from Tosafos only regarding the first day, because if it is deficient it is invalid on that day; if so, he cannot stipulate regarding it that he is not separating himself from benefiting from it at twilight, similar to sukkah wood. But on the other days, when he set them aside for them, and deficiency is valid for them, as with esrog, and likewise with aravah when its top was cut off, since its invalidity is because of hadar, and according to most poskim hadar is indispensable only on the first day, and especially with this aravah, where even if most of its leaves fell off it is certainly valid, then even according to Tosafos a condition should certainly help when he stipulates that he is not separating himself from taking a little from it. Although it is implied from Tosafos that what was set aside is only that one may not consume it entirely, but to take a little from it and leave the valid measure does not require a condition, for it was not set aside for that, nevertheless there is a major practical difference. According to Tosafos' reasoning, that it was set aside only to the extent of the valid measure, he is permitted only to take from it the amount beyond the valid measure, even before fulfilling the mitzvah; but to consume it entirely is prohibited even after fulfilling the mitzvah, for it was set aside for this, and sanctity rests upon it until the festival ends, since he did not stipulate. But when he stipulated that he is not separating himself from it, sanctity does not rest on it at all, similar to what we say in Beitzah 30b. The practical difference is that immediately after fulfilling the mitzvah he may take aravah wood, roast meat and fruit with it, and consume it entirely. Now, I saw in the Eliyah Rabbah that he resolved the Magen Avraham's difficulty and wrote that here, with aravah, since it is only a custom, everyone agrees that a condition helps. However, this can resolve only the words of the Shulchan Aruch. But one who examines the Beis Yosef, in what he brought in the name of the Hagahos Maimoniyos in the name of the Raam, will see that he is lenient even regarding the aravah in the lulav through a condition. Therefore we must answer that he is speaking of an aravah that he joined with the lulav after the first day, where a condition helps even according to Tosafos, as we wrote. And even if we say that the Hagahos Maimoniyos is speaking of an aravah that he joined with the lulav also on the first day, it is still understandable that he wrote 'he stipulated about it from the outset,' for perhaps the form of the stipulation is as Tosafos wrote, and as the Rav copied in siman 649:5 in the gloss: namely, that he stipulated on the eve of the first Yom Tov before the entire first day, since on that day deficient or lacking hadar is invalid and he cannot say that he is not separating himself from it, and that he is not separating himself from it for the entire twilight of the second night onward.
