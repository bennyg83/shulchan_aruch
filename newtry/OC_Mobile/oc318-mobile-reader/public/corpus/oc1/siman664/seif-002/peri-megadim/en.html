And we take. Aravah. See the Taz, Sukkah 44a, and see Beis Yosef, Bach, and Levush for the reason they chose, outside the Mikdash, specifically the seventh day. The Taz answered that on the seventh day, even in the Mikdash, they would circle seven times on the seventh day, etc. See siman 660: the custom of circling every day is this way; it is not the custom of the early authorities, who made a remembrance of the Mikdash only by taking aravah on the seventh day alone. See siman 489 regarding counting nowadays; some say it is a rabbinic remembrance of the Mikdash, because when the Beis HaMikdash stood it was a positive commandment even outside Eretz Yisrael, which is not so regarding aravah, one may say.
What. The Mechaber wrote, 'and we do not recite a berachah over it,' because it is a custom of the Prophets, and we do not bless over a custom. The Kiddush of the second night is the custom of our fathers; Hallel on Rosh Chodesh is a custom, and we bless, as in the end of chapter Lulav VaAravah, Sukkah 44. Or, customs that Chazal commanded, we bless over them; but aravah, the Prophets instituted it this way, and the people afterward practiced it on their own without a command. Therefore we do not bless over it; see there. See siman 422 and Eliyah Rabbah, letter 9.
