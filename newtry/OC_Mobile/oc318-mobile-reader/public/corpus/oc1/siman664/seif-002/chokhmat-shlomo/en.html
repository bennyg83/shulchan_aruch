[There, seif 2.] And we take an aravah on this day, etc. Note: Regarding the reason for taking the aravah on Hoshana Rabbah and beating it, there are certainly deep secrets, and I am brutish and not a man, and I do not know their extent. But to give a reason according to my small understanding, it appears based on what is explicit in Midrash Emor, that these four species allude to four classes of people, and the aravah alludes to the wicked, who have neither taste nor fragrance; and the Holy One, blessed be He, said to make them into one bundle. It appears that this applies specifically in this world, that one should pray for the wicked in this way, just as we offer bulls on the festival for the nations of the world so that the world not be harmed by them, and as the verse states explicitly in the Torah: 'You will not be able to destroy them quickly, lest the beasts of the field multiply against you.' So too now, we pray for the wicked and make them into one bundle, to protect one another. Therefore, specifically during the six days, which allude to the six thousand years that the world exists, one must make them into one bundle to protect one another. But on the seventh day, which alludes to the seventh millennium, then there will be separation from the bundle and the wicked will perish. Therefore we take the aravah by itself, to allude that on the seventh day there will be separation from the bundle, and the wicked will be judged by themselves and will be low down to the earth; therefore we beat them on the ground, to subdue them and lower them. Or it is in order to show the path of the success of the wicked: they succeed only so that they will be lowered, as written in the Midrash on the verse, 'and the enemies of Hashem are like the fat of lambs.' Therefore we show that the aravah merited being raised and made together with the lulav and its species, joining them together, only so that it would later be lowered to the earth; and this ascent is a descent for them. In conclusion, it appears correct to have intent at the time of beating the aravah to subdue insolent sinners and the wicked, and to fulfill the verse, 'You shall trample the wicked, for they shall be dust under the soles of your feet.' This, as it appears to me, is an explicit intent for this aravah.
