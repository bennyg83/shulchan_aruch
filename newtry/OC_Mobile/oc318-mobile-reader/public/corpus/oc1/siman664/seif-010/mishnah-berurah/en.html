(32) Even with the permission of the non-Jew - Because ordinary non-Jews' lands are presumed stolen from others, and their permission is not effective for this. It follows that when the Jew cuts it, he is the one stealing; rather, a non-Jew should cut it and give it to the Jew. The same applies when one Jew cuts it and gives it to another: it is permitted, as we wrote in siman 649:1 in Mishnah Berurah seif katan 11. See the Taz, who wrote that nowadays we do not say that their ordinary lands are presumed stolen. See above in siman 637:3 in the gloss, Mishnah Berurah, and Biur Halacha, and in siman 649:1 in the gloss, Mishnah Berurah, and Biur Halacha, for all of it is relevant to our matter.
