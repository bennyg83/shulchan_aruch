Seif 7: There is one who, etc., and if, etc. As the Maggid Mishneh wrote in chapter 7, that with another species it is not bal tosif according to the Sages, as written in chapter 10 of Sanhedrin. Nevertheless, ab initio it is prohibited, as written in chapter 3 of Sukkah: why do I need 'the four species,' etc.? With the same species, if it is not fit for the mitzvah, it is permitted ab initio, as it says there on 36b regarding bast and the root of a palm tree according to R. Yehudah. But with the same species, when it is fit, one even violates bal tosif, as written regarding two pairs of tefillin in the last chapter of Eruvin, and regarding offerings that require one application where he applied them with four applications in Zevachim and Rosh Hashanah. Rather, anything done for beauty, whether its own species or not its species, is permitted ab initio. For this reason, adding myrtle and binding the lulav even with another species is permitted according to the Sages, since according to the Sages the binding of the lulav is only because of 'I will beautify Him.' See what I wrote at the end of siman 691, that the view of the Geonim is that another species is prohibited even for beauty, except that since the binding is not in the manner of its growth, we are not concerned, since in any case it is prohibited only ab initio, as it appears to me. This is also the opinion of the Rashba in siman 75, that with its own species one violates bal tosif, and so the Rambam wrote.
