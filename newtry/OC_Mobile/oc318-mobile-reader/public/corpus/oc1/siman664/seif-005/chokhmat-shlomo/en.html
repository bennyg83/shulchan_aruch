(There, seif 5.) And it is taken only by itself, so that one should not bind anything else with it. Note: This matter, which emerges so in Shulchan Aruch, comes from the words of Tosafos in chapter 5 of Sukkah, daf 44b. In truth, the words of the Shulchan Aruch are difficult, for Tosafos, who wrote this, follow their own approach, since they hold that if one took the species one by one he did not fulfill the obligation; rather, we require that he take them all as one. According to them they were forced to explain this way, that if they are not bound together it is permitted to take the aravah, in order to resolve thereby the language of the Talmud, which answers that he lifted it and then lifted it again, as I wrote above in siman 651. But the Mechaber ruled above in siman 651 that if one took them one by one, he fulfilled the obligation. If so, it is possible to explain it simply, that 'he lifted it and then lifted it again' means each one literally by itself. If so, there is no proof to permit taking another object with the aravah when they are not bound together. According to his approach, from where does he know that it is permitted to take another object with the aravah, since he rules that if one took them one by one he fulfilled the obligation? His words contradict one another, and this requires investigation.
