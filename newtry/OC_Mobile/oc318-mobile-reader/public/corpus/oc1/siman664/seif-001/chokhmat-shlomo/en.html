Seif 1. “On the seventh day, which is Hoshana Rabbah, the custom is to add Tehillim as on Yom Tov.” Note: This implies that Mizmor Shir LeYom HaShabbos is also recited, as on Yom Tov, and this is indeed our practice. It follows that Chol HaMoed is also called Yom Tov, for Magen Avraham writes in siman 488 that Mizmor Shir LeYom HaShabbos is recited on Yom Tov because Yom Tov is also called Shabbos. Since it is recited on Hoshana Rabbah, even Chol HaMoed is evidently called Yom Tov. It therefore appears that on Chol HaMoed one should say in Bircas HaMazon, “May the Merciful One grant us the day that is entirely good.” Chok Yaakov in siman 487 writes that this should not be said because Chol HaMoed is not called Yom Tov, and Taz in siman 668 likewise rejects those who disagree with him on the ground that Chol HaMoed is not called Yom Tov. According to what is written here, however, our custom demonstrates that Chol HaMoed is called Yom Tov. It is therefore proper to recite this Harachaman, unlike Taz and Chok Yaakov.
