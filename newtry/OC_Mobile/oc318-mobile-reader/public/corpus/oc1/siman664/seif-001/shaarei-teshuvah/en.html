On the seventh day. In the Kuntres Acharon to Machazik Berachah, in the name of the book Mizbeach Adamah, he wrote in the name of Shulchan Melachim that one whose relative died during the festival was permitted by the rabbis of Yerushalayim, the holy city, to read the tikkun of the night of Hoshana Rabbah; see there. See Eliyah Rabbah, who wrote: they rise early to the synagogue, and it is one of the five early risings: Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur, Hoshana Rabbah, Purim, and Tishah BeAv. He wrote in the name of Maharil: once they thought dawn had arrived, but they erred, for the moon was still shining; afterward it became dark, and they waited almost an hour until dawn rose. On the eve of Rosh Chodesh Cheshvan, Maharil and the whole congregation decreed that they should fast, read Vayechal, and say Selichos because they had put on tallis and tefillin and said Pesukei DeZimrah while it was still night. See there. It appears that he was stringent in this because of berachos in vain that were said; if so, one should not be so stringent otherwise. See above, siman 18, and siman 30, regarding one who rises early to go on the road, etc. Regarding what is written that we increase the lights somewhat, the Matteh Moshe wrote that this is because it is the end of the festival, etc.; therefore the custom is to light the leftover candles of Yom Kippur. This is brought in Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah and Eliyah Rabbah. See Peri Megadim, that even Mizmor Shir LeYom HaShabbos is said on Hoshana Rabbah, and we do not say Nishmas because there is not so much addition of the extra soul. We say Na'aritzcha in the Kedushah of Mussaf, and we say Mimkomo, etc.; and before the Torah reading, Ein Kamocha, Shema Yisrael, and Al Hakol as on Yom Tov.
