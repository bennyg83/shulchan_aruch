1) [Seif 1] On the seventh day, which is Hoshana Rabbah. Why is it called Hoshana Rabbah? The Holy One, blessed is He, said to Avraham: I am unique and you are unique; I will give your children a unique day on which to attain atonement through My providence. This is Hoshana Rabbah, meaning: My Name Ehyeh has the numerical value 21, and you are at 21, namely, ten generations from Adam until Noach and ten generations from Noach until Avraham; and Hoshana Rabbah is the 21st day of the month of Tishrei. The Holy One, blessed is He, said to Avraham: If your children do not attain atonement on Rosh Hashanah, they will attain it on Yom Kippur; and if not, it will be on Hoshana Rabbah. This is, 'I made it unique through My Name.' Mateh Moshe, siman 957; Eliyah Rabbah, os 1. Regarding what he wrote, 'and you are at 21,' although there were only 20 generations, Noach is counted as two because he lived in two eras, before the Flood and afterward. One may further say that it is called Hoshana Rabbah because on that day one says Hoshana many times; thus Hoshana Rabbah means the day on which many Hoshanos are said. One may further explain this according to the custom of beginning supplications for mercy from Rosh Chodesh Elul, whether by blowing the shofar or by reciting Selichos, each according to his custom. From the first day of Rosh Chodesh Elul until Hoshana Rabbah there are 51 days. Therefore it is called Hoshana Rabbah, meaning: Save the great 51st day, which is the conclusion. The Levush wrote that the reason is because one increases the use of the aravah on that day, meaning, a great aravah.
2) However, one must understand why there are these three times at which people are judged: first, on Rosh Hashanah; second, on Yom Kippur; and third, on the night of Hoshana Rabbah. Indeed, our Sages, of blessed memory, said that on Rosh Hashanah people are inscribed, at Ne'ilah of Yom Kippur they are sealed, and on the night of Hoshana Rabbah they are sealed a second time. The inner meaning is as follows. Know that whenever the forces of severity are aroused, it is a time of judgment in the world. On Rosh Hashanah, the judgments are severed from Ze'er Anpin and placed upon her, meaning, upon the aspect of Malchus; then the judgments hold sway. Therefore, only the completely righteous are sealed on Rosh Hashanah, which is severe judgment. At Ne'ilah of Yom Kippur, the aspect of her Yesod is also formed together with the vessel of the severities; therefore, the world is judged then as well. On Hoshana Rabbah, the five severities are also placed within her; therefore, the world is judged at that time as well. Pri Etz Chaim, Shaar 29, beginning of chapter 4; so too is written in Shaar HaKavanos, 102c.
3) Therefore, one must remain awake throughout this entire night, since all created beings are judged on it, as stated above. This is the order that one should follow: During the first half of the night, read the entire sefer of Devarim, through the end of Parashas Vezos HaBerachah. This order is called Mishneh Torah, as is known. If one completes its reading before midnight, he should engage in Torah and in the hidden teachings of Kabbalah, should he wish, and not in anything else. The principal judgment takes place during the first half of the night. Therefore, only after midnight do people go out to observe their shadow in the moonlight, for the judgment has already been completed. This is the reason for those who customarily recite Selichos on the night of Hoshana Rabbah after midnight, near the morning watch. However, it is forbidden to recite Vayaavor and the Thirteen Attributes, or Selichos of a similar nature. Rather, one should say: Rachmana idkar lan kayamei, etc.; Elokeinu shebashamayim, etc.; de'anei la'aniyei, etc.; Hashem Hu HaElokim, etc.; Hashem Melech, etc.; Asei lema'an Shemecha, etc.; and similar passages may be recited. On Hoshana Rabbah during Shacharis, one should likewise follow the foregoing order, except for the Thirteen Attributes of Vayaavor, etc. Shaar HaKavanos, 103d. So too is written in Pri Etz Chaim, Shaar 29, chapter 4.
4) Therefore, some customarily read Sefer Devarim first on this night, as stated above, and afterward Idra Zuta, as the Tzemach wrote there in his glosses to Pri Etz Chaim, for it contains the hidden teachings of the Torah. If there is still time before midnight, they read Midrash Vezos HaBerachah until close to midnight, and then recite the order of Krias Shema before retiring. When midnight arrives, they recite the morning blessings, except for the blessing over Torah study, as written above in siman 46, os 49, see there. Afterward, they recite Tikkun Leah, as written above in siman 1, os 13, see there. They then recite the entire Tehillim, together with the prayers of the seven who entered the covenant, which are supplications for mercy.
5) He wrote there in Pri Etz Chaim that the third seal, which is formed on the night of Hoshana Rabbah, is the outer seal. It is formed from the illumination of the inner seal of Yesod and is of a lower order than it. The matter is that the seal of Yesod is formed from the three Names Ehyeh themselves, in the three spellings, with yuds, alefs, and hehs, whose numerical value is Chosam. At present, however, it is not a complete seal, for its secret derives from the three spellings of the three Names Ehyeh, whose numerical values are 140, 122, and 130, totaling 392; together with the three as a whole, the total is 395. This equals the numerical value of Mishneh of the King, for the principal seal is in the Yesod of Malchus, whereas this one is in the Malchus of Malchus; see there. Therefore, before reading Sefer Devarim, one should say: For the sake of the unification, etc. I hereby come to read on this night Sefer Devarim, which is Sefer Mishneh Torah, in order to draw down to the aspect of the Malchus of Malchus the outer seal, which consists of the three spellings of Ehyeh, with yuds, alefs, and hehs, together with the three as a whole, to rectify the root of this mitzvah and all 613 mitzvos included within it in the supernal realm. Vihi Noam, etc.
6) One whose relative died during the festival was permitted by the Rabbis of Yerushalayim, the Holy City, to read the order of the Tikkun for the night of Hoshana Rabbah. The author of Mizbe'ach Adamah in the name of Sefer Shulchan Melachim; Machazik Berachah in Kuntres Acharon, os 2; Shaarei Teshuvah.
7) Ibid. It is customary to increase the recitation of chapters of Tehillim, etc. One rises early to go to the synagogue. This is one of the five occasions for rising early: Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur, Hoshana Rabbah, Tishah BeAv, and Purim. Mateh Moshe, siman 958; Eliyah Rabbah, os 3; Shaarei Teshuvah. One should pray Shacharis and Mussaf with concentration and fervor, for aside from the obligation arising from the sanctity of the day, one must pray fervently so that sleep does not overpower him and cause him to make many errors in the prayer and its blessings, and to suffer many deficiencies, since he remained awake the entire night. Ben Ish Chai, Parashas Vezos HaBerachah, os 4.
8) The Maharil wrote concerning an actual incident that they thought dawn had broken and erred, for the moon was still shining. Afterward it grew dark, and they waited almost an hour until dawn broke. On the eve of Rosh Chodesh Cheshvan, the Maharil decreed that he and the entire congregation should fast, read Vayechal, and recite Selichos because they had recited Pesukei DeZimrah and donned a tallis and tefillin while it was still night. Eliyah Rabbah there; Shaarei Teshuvah. Therefore, with Hashem's help, one must be exacting whenever praying early, so as not to err in this matter.
9) Ibid. It is customary to increase the recitation of chapters of Tehillim, etc. In some places it is customary to blow the shofar, as Rabbi Pri HaAdamah wrote, volume 1, 36a. However, he wrote there on 38a that in the Holy City of Yerushalayim, may it be rebuilt and established, this is not the custom. So too wrote the author of Admas Kodesh, volume 2, Orach Chaim, end of siman 3, that in Yerushalayim, may it be rebuilt and established, it is not customary to blow the shofar on Hoshana Rabbah. Likewise, in Tzefas, may it be rebuilt, this is not the custom, as Eretz Chaim wrote in this siman. So too wrote Yafeh LaLev, volume 5, that in his locale this is not the custom.
10) Ibid. It is customary to increase the recitation of chapters of Tehillim, etc. This is because it is the end of the festival and of the circuits, the completion of the 70 bulls, and the confirmation of the sealing. Mateh Moshe, siman 960; Eliyah Rabbah, os 4.
11) Ibid. It is customary to increase the recitation of chapters of Tehillim, etc. The custom of the pious members of Beit El, may Hashem establish their community, who follow the teachings of the Arizal, is not to add anything whatsoever to the order of prayer beyond that of the other days of Chol HaMoed. The only change is in reciting the Hoshanos during the circuits. After completing the circuits and reciting the Hoshanos, they recite Kaddish Tiskabel, put down the lulav, and perform the beating with the aravah, as will be written below in the name of Shaar HaKavanos. Afterward, they read from the Torah and pray Mussaf as on every other day. This is likewise the custom of the holy congregation of Frankfurt: they make no change whatsoever, except that Adon Olam and the final Kaddish are recited to a melody, as Mateh Moshe wrote in siman 960; see there.
