He must, etc., lest it not be agreeable to them. Even though we hold that a house open to two courtyards — if he wishes he makes an eruv with both of them and is permitted in both of them, as below siman 378 — if so he should not make an eruv from his own, lest it not be agreeable to him; and if because it is agreeable to him more to make an eruv with the other courtyard — what loss is there in this eruv? Behold he can still make an eruv also with the other courtyard. Already Rambam wrote chapter 5 of Hilchot Eruvin the reason — that it does not appear to us to make an eruv with both of them, so as not to increase those entering and exiting through his house from two courtyards.

And if they are for one courtyard — as it should read. For in this certainly it is agreeable to him, for in any case he must make an eruv with this courtyard since he has no opening to another courtyard; therefore if he makes an eruv from his own in truth he need not inform; but if he makes an eruv from those who were added he must inform them, for for them it is the beginning of the eruv, and one does not make an eruv initially without the owners' knowledge as explained siman 367. That if he makes an eruv from his own he need not inform even in a house open to two courtyards. And requires study why — for behold he said lest it not be agreeable to them as written at the beginning of this s.k.
