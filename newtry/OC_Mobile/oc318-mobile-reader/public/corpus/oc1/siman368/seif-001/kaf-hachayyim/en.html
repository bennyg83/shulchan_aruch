1) [Se'if 1] They established an eruv and the eruv subsequently became deficient. This means that the food was reduced below its required measure, as stated in the Mishnah, Eruvin 80b.
2) There. And one of the courtyard's residents comes to restore it. This refers to the first Shabbos, when the eruv became deficient before the first Shabbos began. If this occurred for the second Shabbos, no restoration is necessary, for it is a remnant of an eruv and remains valid even if only any amount remains, as stated in se'if 4. Taz, subsection 1; Chemed Moshe, subsection 1; Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 1. Bach, however, writes that this refers to a case in which it became deficient after the first Shabbos began. Although any amount suffices for the remnants of an eruv, one might nevertheless wish to restore it in the optimal manner, or be concerned that not even the slightest amount will remain. See there. Mishbetzos Zahav, subsection 1, writes likewise. Ma'amar Mordechai, subsection 1, writes that this is also implied by Levush and that he found the same interpretation in the abridgment of Beis Yosef printed in the year 5359. See there. See below, subsection 8.
3) There. He need not inform them. This means that he need not ask whether they consent, for we maintain their previous status and assume that everyone will certainly consent to this same type of food, just as they consented initially. Levush.
4) There. But if he comes to establish an eruv using another type. For example, the original eruv consisted of wheat bread, and now he wishes to make it from rice bread. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1. See above, siman 366, se'if 8.
5) There. He must inform them, etc. They might not consent to establish the eruv using another type, and if they object, the eruv will be nullified. Levush. See below, subsection 10.
