2) There. And one of the courtyard's residents comes to restore it. This refers to the first Shabbos, when the eruv became deficient before the first Shabbos began. If this occurred for the second Shabbos, no restoration is necessary, for it is a remnant of an eruv and remains valid even if only any amount remains, as stated in se'if 4. Taz, subsection 1; Chemed Moshe, subsection 1; Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 1. Bach, however, writes that this refers to a case in which it became deficient after the first Shabbos began. Although any amount suffices for the remnants of an eruv, one might nevertheless wish to restore it in the optimal manner, or be concerned that not even the slightest amount will remain. See there. Mishbetzos Zahav, subsection 1, writes likewise. Ma'amar Mordechai, subsection 1, writes that this is also implied by Levush and that he found the same interpretation in the abridgment of Beis Yosef printed in the year 5359. See there.
