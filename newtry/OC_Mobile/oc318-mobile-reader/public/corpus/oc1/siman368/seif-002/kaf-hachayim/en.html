(2) There — and one of the courtyard residents came to restore and repair it. This deals with the first Shabbat that it was invalidated before the first Shabbat entered; but for the second Shabbat one need not repair, for behold it is the end of the eruv and is valid if something remained even any amount, as written seif 4 — Taz s.k. 1, Chemed Moshe part 1, Tosafot part 1; but Bach wrote it deals after the first Shabbat entered, and even though the remainder of the eruv is valid in any amount — that is if he comes to repair for the mitzvah in the best way or he is concerned lest nothing remain even any amount — see there; and likewise Machatzit HaShekel part 1, article part 1, and he wrote that such is implied in Levush, and likewise he found two abridgments of Beit Yosef printed in the year 359 — see there; and see below part 8.
