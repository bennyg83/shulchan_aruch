(s.k. 3) Diminished — and if the eruv broke, even before, etc. — meaning that breaking is lighter than diminishing; and the reason Magen Avraham wrote afterward.

And in responsum of Rambam, etc., and his reason, etc. — meaning that diminishing is valid; and since it diminished, therefore it broke — meaning it was whole; and if so breaking is worse than diminishing; and regarding diminishing he agrees according to this reason that it is valid even for the remaining Shabbats.

Or alternatively, anything that broke, etc. — and according to this reason breaking and diminishing are equal; and also regarding diminishing it was not permitted except for that Shabbat.

And behold Rabbenu Yonah ruled, etc. — and with this he rejects Magen Avraham's second reason.

Therefore it appears to me like Maharil. Meaning since he rejected Magen Avraham's two reasons of Rambam — if so necessarily breaking is not worse than diminishing; and also it was permitted even for the remaining Shabbats; and nevertheless he said specifically breaking after the first Shabbat entered like diminishing — on this he wrote that it appears to me like Maharil that breaking is lighter in this than diminishing, and even if it broke before the first Shabbat entered it is valid.

Lest it return, etc. — meaning that above siman 366 it is explained the reason one does not make an eruv with a slice is because of enmity — this one gives a slice and this one a whole loaf; but here where the eruv broke, behold all the eruv of all the courtyard residents are slices — and what enmity is there? Nevertheless it would have been fit to forbid as Magen Avraham wrote siman 366 s.k. 11 — we decree lest the matter return to its corruption, that this one gives a slice and this one a whole loaf; on this Magen Avraham wrote that here this concern also does not exist since at the time of placement it was whole.

And nevertheless it appears to me that if nothing remained, etc. — meaning even though regarding breaking we are lenient like Maharil, nevertheless regarding diminishing even if it is whole we are stringent in our time: (1) lest they forget, etc., and as the mishnah taught daf 82a, 2 — R' Yossi said in what case does this apply? At the beginning of the eruv; but regarding the remainder of the eruv even any amount; and they did not say to make an eruv in courtyards except so they not forget from the children — and Rashi explained: and they did not say to make an eruv in courtyards after they partnered (meaning that we hold we rely on partnership in place of eruv) except so they not forget eruv chatzerot from the young girl (who does not know about partnership since it is not done in their courtyard); therefore we are lenient in it, end of his words — behold that for this reason the remainder of the eruv all the more so, since otherwise there is partnership and the eruv is only so as not to forget; but for us it is invalid since we have only partnership and not eruv chatzerot at all, as Rama wrote above siman 366 seif 3 and below siman 387 — if so the partnership is from the law itself, and therefore one should not be more lenient in the remainder than at the beginning, except that in Chemed Moshe he challenged why Magen Avraham relied more on Rashi's words than what Rashi wrote daf 46b, 2 on that mishnah — so as not to forget eruv techumin from the children — and the author of that book understood according to this view that what the mishnah teaches 'and they did not say to make an eruv,' etc. is not a reason on what R' Yossi said initially 'remainder of eruv all the more so'; and if so also for us one may be lenient in the remainder of the eruv; but in my humble opinion Magen Avraham did not understand thus — for Tosafos wrote there according to this view that what the mishnah teaches 'and they did not say to make eruv in courtyards' means partnership of alleyways, and they call it thus in several places — and this is a difficulty for Magen Avraham: why, if not so as not to forget, would partnership of the alleyway be needed? And why — behold the reason is lest they come to confuse on Rosh Hashanah, for every courtyard facing the alleyway appears like a reshut ha-yachid that pertains only to one courtyard's residents, and the alleyway pertains to all the courtyards and appears to them like reshut ha-rabbim — therefore partnership is required; therefore Magen Avraham holds that also according to Rashi's explanation on daf 46 the explanation is thus — that it refers to partnership, as Tosafos wrote that the entire chapter deals with partnership of alleyways; and if so the beginning 'remainder of eruv all the more so' also deals with partnership; and on this 'and they did not say to make an eruv' means for partnership, etc.; and it is also the reason that remainder of partnership all the more so — because from the law itself partnership is not needed, for we hold we rely on eruv in place of partnership — namely if all the courtyards open to one another and all the courtyards made eruv together, then partnership is not needed; but nevertheless they partner in the alleyway so the young girl has recognition for eruv techumin (and this is unlike Maharsha on daf 46); and therefore we are lenient in remainder of partnership all the more so; and if so still Magen Avraham's question stands — for us who have only partnership alone one should not be lenient when it diminished.

And it is possible that this is Rambam's reason, etc. — meaning that Rambam also agrees that in the time of the Gemara breaking was valid, for there is no concern lest the matter return to its corruption; but nevertheless he holds it is not better than diminishing, unlike Maharil, since it changed from what it was initially whole; but nevertheless in the time of the Gemara when they needed to make eruv only so as not to forget, etc., they were lenient in breaking like in diminishing; but now that we have only partnership and it is from the law itself — if so like diminishing it is invalid in our time, as Magen Avraham wrote, so too breaking.

However there it implies that his reason, etc. — meaning he himself did not give this reason but because of lest it return, etc.; if so it implies according to him even in the time of the Gemara it was invalid — and this Magen Avraham already rejected above.
