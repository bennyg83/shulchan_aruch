1) [Se'if 1] Under Torah law, one is liable only when one uproots, etc. This is because it is not deemed a complete melachah unless one uproots an object from a private domain and places it in a public domain, or the reverse. If one performed only half of the melachah, or if it was performed by two people, with one uprooting and the other placing, they are exempt. For our Sages expounded in Shabbos 3a from the verse, 'When one of the people of the land does it': One who performs the entirety of it is liable, but not one who performs part of it; when an individual performs it, he is liable, but when two people perform it, they are exempt. Nevertheless, by Rabbinic law, each person is initially forbidden to perform half of the melachah, such as when one uproots and the other places, lest each come to perform the complete melachah, as Rashi writes at the beginning of tractate Shabbos.
