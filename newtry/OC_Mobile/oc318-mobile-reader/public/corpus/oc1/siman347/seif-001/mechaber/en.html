For which carrying is one liable from the Torah. And in it is one seif:
From the Torah one is liable only when one uproots an object from reshut ha-yachid and places it in reshut ha-rabbim, or the reverse; but if one extended his hand inward and an object was in his hand and his fellow standing inside took it, or if one extended his hand outward and an object was in his hand and his fellow standing outside took it — this one uprooted and that one placed — both are exempt; but it is forbidden to do so d'rabbanan. And if one extended his hand inward and an object was in his hand and placed it into the hand of his fellow standing inside, or if one extended his hand inward and took an object from the hand of his fellow standing inside and brought it out — it is found that the one standing outside alone uprooted and placed, he is liable and his fellow is exempt, but forbidden; and even if the one standing outside is a gentile it is forbidden because it is as if he gave it to him on condition to bring it out. And the same law applies to extend his hand outward and place it in the hand of his fellow standing outside, or to take from the hand of his fellow standing outside and bring it inside — the one standing inside is liable because he uprooted and placed, and the one standing outside is exempt, but forbidden:
