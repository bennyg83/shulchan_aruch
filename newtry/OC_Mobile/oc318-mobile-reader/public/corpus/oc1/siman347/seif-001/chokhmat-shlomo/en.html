Seif 1 from the Torah one is liable only when uprooting an object from reshut ha-yachid etc. but forbidden to do so d'rabbanan. Note: behold what he wrote d'rabbanan is superfluous, for it is obvious it is d'rabbanan, for if from the Torah they would be liable; and it is strained to say it deals with forbidden like chatat shiur that is forbidden from the Torah — he teaches us it is only d'rabbanan — this is strained. And it is more plausible he came to teach us what I saw from a certain sage who was in doubt from what Tosafos wrote in chapter 2 of Avodah Zarah daf 22 that also in a d'rabbanan prohibition "before the blind" applies; and he was in doubt whether the prohibition of "before the blind" is also d'rabbanan, or perhaps since there is already a d'rabbanan prohibition here it is again forbidden through "before the blind" from the Torah, for it is no less than other stumbling block and improper advice forbidden through "before the blind." And behold in the words of Shulchan Aruch here who was precise to say forbidden d'rabbanan — to say that also each one of what transgresses "before the blind" by causing his fellow to stumble is also only d'rabbanan; and especially since necessarily otherwise what does Shulchan Aruch teach us that it is forbidden d'rabbanan — and it would be better to teach us that there is also a Torah prohibition here for each one through "before the blind," as Magen Avraham wrote; and since it deals with "before the blind" it should have taught us thus at the beginning; and extra importance that even a Torah prohibition exists in this perforce — but in truth the prohibition of "before the blind" is also only d'rabbanan. And behold the aforementioned sage wanted to support from Tosafos' words to the opposite — therefore they needed to bring proof in Avodah Zarah that "before the blind" applies in d'rabbanan — it is obvious it is no less than a d'rabbanan prohibition than other improper advice that is forbidden to give him; and perforce their intent is to prove that from the Torah "before the blind" applies — it is not clear to me: one — from where for Tosafos is it so in truth — behold from the Talmud there it only implies there is "before the blind" here; but from where that it is from the Torah — perhaps in truth he asks from d'rabbanan. And further, what will the sage answer to the second proof they wrote from the Tosefta that says it is forbidden to sell to an Israelite suspected of selling to a gentile through "before the blind" — what proof, perhaps there he speaks d'rabbanan; and perforce Tosafos' intent is truly that from d'rabbanan there is "before the blind" here; and what they challenged — obvious that there is "before the blind" here, for it is no less than improper advice. Behold this is difficult for me, and the sage was ashamed — he is paid in what he wrote afterward that there are two matters of "before the blind": one who does not know at all that it is forbidden, and if he knew he would not do it; and one who knows it is forbidden and nevertheless the other transgresses "before the blind" if he assists him — and therefore Tosafos deals with a matter where he knows it is forbidden and is not concerned that his fellow will transgress "before the blind"; therefore they properly needed proof that "before the blind" applies in d'rabbanan, for otherwise it would be an assumption that they said and they said and only decreed on the doer himself, not on the assister; and thereby his question is resolved — behold it is no less than giving improper advice, for there it is all if he does not know it is a stumbling block for him; but if he knows this harms him and does it — certainly "before the blind" does not apply at all in monetary matters; therefore it would be an assumption that also in a d'rabbanan prohibition if he knows it is forbidden and is not concerned that his fellow will transgress "before the blind" — therefore he teaches us he transgresses "before the blind" and still only d'rabbanan; and nevertheless from their proof in the Tosefta that says there is "before the blind" in selling to an Israelite suspected of selling to a gentile — and behold this is only a d'rabbanan doubt, for he is suspected of selling to a gentile — nevertheless perhaps he sells to an Israelite too, as stated there explicitly that to an Israelite too he sells — if so what does he challenge, behold it is a d'rabbanan doubt and to be lenient from the Torah we should be concerned in this for a doubt of "before the blind"; and perforce from this it will be proven properly that the "before the blind" here is from the Torah. Therefore the essence of the prohibition here is d'rabbanan; nevertheless for this doubt whether the other may sell to him if he will transgress "before the blind" — this would be a Torah doubt; and there is proof as the sage's view. However in another responsum I wrote to resolve this question another way — and therefore one can say plainly they only speak d'rabbanan; and from Shulchan Aruch's words it appears certainly there is only a d'rabbanan prohibition here, since he did not teach extra importance as above — in my humble opinion now; and perhaps another time, with Hashem's help, I will explain more — understand well.
