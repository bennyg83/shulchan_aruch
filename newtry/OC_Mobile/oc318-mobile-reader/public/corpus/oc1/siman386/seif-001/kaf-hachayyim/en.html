1) [Se'if 1] The Sages prohibited, etc. Under Torah law, it is permitted to carry from the courtyards into the alleyway. The Sages prohibited it because the alleyway is considered a shared domain relative to the courtyards, and they imposed a decree on account of carrying from a public domain into a private domain. Tur.
2) There. From the courtyards into the alleyway. This applies specifically to utensils that began Shabbos in a house, as stated below in siman 388. See there. Utensils that were in a courtyard during bein hashmashos, however, share one domain with the alleyway, as stated at the beginning of siman 372. Orchos Chaim, subsection 1.
3) There. And they permitted it through a shituf. Just as an eruv chatzeiros combines all the houses of a courtyard, a shitufei mevo'os combines all the courtyards of an alleyway. Tur.
4) There. They collect bread or another item, etc. The reason for the distinction between eruv chatzeiros and shitufei mevo'os is that the former may be made specifically with bread, including every type of bread, whereas the latter may be made with bread or even other types of food and drink. An eruv chatzeiros serves to combine residences and make them one. A person's residence is not determined by wine or other beverages, but by bread. A shituf for an alleyway, however, serves only to combine the rights of the courtyards in the alleyway, not those of the houses, and a courtyard is not a residential house. Therefore, any food may be used to establish the shituf as a recognizable reminder. For this reason, it may be placed in the open area of a courtyard or in a house that is not four by four amos. Levush; Olas Shabbos, subsection 1; Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1. See above, siman 366, subsection 7.
5) There. Or in a house that is not four by four amos. Even if we say that since it is not regarded as a house, its enclosure and roof are considered nonexistent, it is nevertheless no less suitable than the open area of a courtyard. Sukkah 50b and Rashi there.
6) There. In the open area of the alleyway. This is invalid because it is not guarded. Tosafos on Eruvin 71b maintain that initially it is preferable to place a shitufei mevo'os in a courtyard rather than a house, and to establish it with other foods rather than bread, as a recognizable distinction so that people know it is a shituf. Rashi, however, does not agree, but maintains that a house and bread are preferable. Rosh writes likewise. Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 4. Maggid Mishneh, chapter 1, likewise writes that the custom is initially to establish a shituf with bread. Darkei Moshe, subsection 1.
