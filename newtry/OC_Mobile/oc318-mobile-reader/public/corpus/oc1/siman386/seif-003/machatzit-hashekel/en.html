(s.k. 3) Like an alleyway, etc., for in any case there must be a partition between them and the gentile—meaning it must deal that between those places, between one place and its fellow, there is an alleyway for the gentile, for if not, since they sit in the circuit of one wall, why can they not participate? Or an alleyway in which a gentile dwells interrupts between them, and therefore they are forbidden to carry from one to another, as Magen Avraham wrote s.k. 1; and on this Magen Avraham challenged: if so, in any case one must divide at the end of each alleyway with a partition so the gentiles do not forbid them—and if so it is obvious that it helps, etc.; and it would have been possible to deflect that it deals when they rented domain from the gentile, so no partition is needed for the gentile, or it deals where the gentile has an opening or window open behind his house for more than two se'ah, for the gentile does not forbid as below siman 389; but since Shulchan Aruch was general it appeared strained to Magen Avraham to establish it thus:
