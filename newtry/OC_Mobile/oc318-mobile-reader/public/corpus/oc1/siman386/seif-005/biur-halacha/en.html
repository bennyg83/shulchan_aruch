And one says one makes eruv with spices—see in the commentators who wondered at this opinion, for it is Rambam's opinion which is against the Gemara's sugya that says: if we say because it teaches in that mishna spices—and spices are not fit for eating—behold here too it teaches wheat and barley and they are not fit for eating while alive; behold the Gemara compares spices to living wheat and barley that all agree one does not make eruv with them; however in truth Rambam never mentions the law of wheat and barley [and what he wrote in Laws of Eruvin chapter 1 halacha 10: such as bread and types of grain—it is possible his intent is even while alive since he did not write cooked, and so in Beit Meir]; and it appears for Rambam it will be permitted to make eruv also with living wheat and barley, for regarding partnerships of alleyways he holds they were lenient in it, and as they were lenient that one need not specifically bread and its accompaniments—from Tosefta chapter 6 it is taught there at the head of the chapter: partnership of alleyway—how so? A person brings a barrel of wine and of oil and of grain and of dried figs and says: this is for all members of the alleyway; and accordingly one explains the sugya differently: if we say because it teaches, etc., and spices are not fit for eating—the explanation is: and for this reason it enters your mind that one does not make eruv with them; if so in that braita too it teaches wheat and barley, and they too are not fit for eating while alive, and nevertheless Rav said on it and so for eruv—behold we do not require that it be immediately fit for eating, but anything that is types of food; and the same with spices which are also designated for seasoning; and barley—and he did not bring any opinion here—it appears he holds that in this all agree one does not make eruv; and so in Sefer Or Zaru'a that wheat and barley are inferior to spices and all agree one does not make eruv—and it requires study; and see in Ma'aseh Rokeach and Beit Meir.
