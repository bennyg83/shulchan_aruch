(In Magen Avraham s.k. 5) As it is in Avodah Zarah folio 42, etc. and Rashi there wrote: perhaps he lifts it, for every ownerless thing (such as a find) is acquired by lifting; behold it is clear like Bach. But one may say that Magen Avraham's difficulty is from what we hold: that even an idol that has gentile owners, a Jew cannot nullify it; and therefore also Rashi means: since an ownerless thing is acquired by lifting, automatically even when it has owners we decree on account of [a case] where they have no owners; or perhaps he declares it ownerless and a Jew lifts it and afterward nullifies it. But the language of Magen Avraham does not imply so; and also from the reason he gives (because he is liable for its responsibility) it is clear that he holds that lifting helps him to be like his own; and according to what I wrote this is only because of a decree; and also what I wrote to explain that we decree on account of when it has no owners; this has no place, for still if he nullifies a gentile's before lifting, it is a decree upon a decree: perhaps he lifts before nullification, and perhaps he nullifies even when it has no owners. Therefore it appears to me in this like the view of Bach: that even though from the reason of a decree we say that nullification does not help, nevertheless since by Torah law nullification helps in such a case, "its measure is crushed" does not apply. And regarding Rashi's view it appears that he explains such as where a gentile lost [it] and a Jew found it, that it was acquired by him through lifting. And see Yoreh Deah siman 116, the view of Bach: that even if he did not acquire it (such as he found it before despair, or stole it) nevertheless it has no nullification by rabbinic law; and therefore he holds that nevertheless we do not say "its measure is crushed" in such a case, since he did not acquire it. And this requires further study according to what Taz wrote in Yoreh Deah there: that also when he acquired it only by rabbinic law, nullification does not help (see there. And what Magen Avraham wrote as a reason because of responsibility, and his proof from chametz) there is no proof, for there we amplify from "it shall not be found"; and also according to what I wrote in a responsum, regarding theft from a gentile there is no liability of responsibility even if stealing from a gentile is forbidden, for nevertheless "and he shall restore the stolen item" does not apply to it; and even though whatever the Merciful One said "you shall not do," if one did it it does not help; nevertheless if it was stolen or made [into something else] he is exempt; and so wrote Noda BiYehuda, Orach Chaim part, siman 88, and I elaborated there; if so it is obvious that there is no liability of responsibility here.
