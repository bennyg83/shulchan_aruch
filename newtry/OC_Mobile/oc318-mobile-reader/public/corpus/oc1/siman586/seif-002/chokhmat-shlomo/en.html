(Same section 5) One forbidden benefit from a shofar — another person may blow for him, etc. NB: Tosafot challenged in ch. 6 of Shevuot why we say mitzvot were not given for benefit — behold he enjoys being exempt from a perutah per R' Yosef — see there. In my humble opinion it fits well: what exempts him from giving a perutah to a poor person is only like chasing away a lion and paying his fellow's debt without his knowledge; therefore since for one forbidden benefit it is permitted to pay his debt without knowledge, likewise here what exempts him from a perutah to this poor person is not considered benefit. And even though paying his debt with knowledge is forbidden — there since nothing compels him and he does so knowingly he reveals his will that it pleases him and he acts as his agent; unlike one doing a mitzva — behold he is compelled to fulfill the mitzva, and if not compelled perhaps he would give more perutot to the poor and not do the mitzva; only the Torah compelled him to do the mitzva, and thereby he is automatically exempt from the poor person since through Torah compulsion it is as without knowledge. But for a paid guardian, since he has benefit, it is as with knowledge; therefore even chasing away a lion is benefit since it was with knowledge. But for a mitzva it is as without knowledge. Further, for one forbidden benefit the matter depends on Torah's will, and per Torah's will chasing away a lion is not considered benefit; but for a paid guardian the matter depends on his will, and as we find generally in that benefit that he trusts him — he concluded and submitted himself — therefore paying a debt is also benefit; therefore he is a paid guardian — and it fits well. And even for an unpaid guardian he is compelled to receive the lost object and guard it; nevertheless for the guarding itself he does not become a paid guardian as is known, only through exertion and arousal; and exertion and arousal are in his hand to do through another — then he would be liable to give a perutah to a poor person since he does nothing himself and is not exempt except when he does it himself; and that is also actual knowledge — therefore he is a paid guardian. With this the Bach's answer fits to what Magen Avraham wrote in his name, what is difficult from here to what is written in Yoreh Deah siman 221; and the Bach answered there is a distinction whether there is another or not — at first glance unclear; it must be that in pressing circumstances we rely on those who hold that for bodily benefit together with benefit of mitzva it is also not considered benefit, etc. Per the above one need not rely on that — only if there is another: if the other blows, since he does nothing he is liable for a perutah to a poor person and does not enjoy; only when he blows himself is he exempt; and if so if it is with knowledge and he enjoys R' Yosef's perutah it is forbidden entirely; but if there is no other, since he is compelled to blow it is again as without knowledge and only chasing away a lion — therefore permitted — and it fits well with divine help, examine well for it is correct. It is known the dispute of rishonim on what we hold mitzvot were not given for benefit — if bodily benefit together with benefit of mitzva is also not given for benefit, or for this mitzvot were given for benefit. Now it appears to me proof from Pesachim ch. Kol sha'ah in the sugya of Chizkiya and R' Eliezer who challenged from nazir that the Merciful One said he shall not eat etc., and we taught they may mix wine for a nazir etc. — why challenge? Behold the Rabbis' view in tractate Eruvin is that one mixes only for a matter of mitzva; one may say this mishnah goes like the view that one mixes only for mitzva and mitzvot were not given for benefit — therefore they mix wine for him — why challenge? Perforce it is proven that where there is bodily benefit together with benefit of mitzva it is given for benefit; if so here too at the start of eruv it is for mitzva, nevertheless since he placed the eruv he can go there afterward even for optional matters — for this it is given for benefit; and he challenged properly, examine. One may say those who hold even bodily benefit with mitzva is not given for benefit may say they hold like Razah that for rabbinic mitzvot it is given for benefit; if so even those who hold one mixes only for mitzva, nevertheless for rabbinic mitzva too they mix by force of in any case from Torah eruv is not needed and rabbinically eruv is required, and in rabbinic law too it is mitzva — therefore he challenged properly that behold rabbinic mitzva is given for benefit — and it fits, examine; and see above on this in another matter.
