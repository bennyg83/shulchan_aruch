One who steals a shofar; on Taz: in the Yerushalmi at the beginning of the chapter "A stolen lulav," apparently it explains that regarding the sound there is no law of theft, and "teruah" (however it may be) is "for you," which is not so regarding lulav and matzah, where we require "for you" as to the taking; and it holds that in the Yerushalmi they do not disagree with one another. And see the Rambam of blessed memory, chapter 1 of Shofar, and the objection of the Raavad of blessed memory; and this requires further study. And in the Bach: that "yehiyeh" implies a broadening [exclusion]; and in the Levush: "for you" refers to the day. And regarding a shofar it is not from that reasoning, for there is no theft in the sound; and see Magen Avraham 4; and with Hashem's help it will be clarified there regarding the blessing.
