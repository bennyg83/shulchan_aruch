Ibid. The law would be this way. It seems to me that he wrote this so that one should not ask why the Mechaber did not distinguish between nullifying it before Yom Tov and nullifying it on Yom Tov. Granted, after the fact there is no practical difference, since he rules that after the fact one fulfills the obligation even without nullifying it at all. Nevertheless, there would be a practical difference initially, for if it was nullified on Yom Tov it would in any event be forbidden initially, because of the question of Reish Lakish. Therefore he wrote that even initially there is no practical difference, because even if it was nullified before Yom Tov it is forbidden initially, since it is repulsive for the Most High, or for the mitzvah, as the Mechaber ruled in siman 154; see there. This law is also from the question of Reish Lakish there; see the Gemara there.
