(87) Outside the techum. That is, even beyond twelve mil, which according to several poskim is according to the Torah; and if so, according to them it is forbidden to tell a gentile to bring it, for in the previous seif they permitted only something that is rabbinic. Even so, if they brought it even for a Jew, it is permitted to blow with it; and the reason is that something that comes from outside the techum is permitted in carrying, and is forbidden only in eating or in benefit (namely to use it) as written in siman 515; and this, that he blows with it a tekiah of mitzvah, is not considered benefit, as we wrote in seif 5. Nevertheless it is forbidden to carry it in the street of a city whose alleyways are not fixed with a tzurat hapetach beyond four amos, as explained in siman 515 [for if they are fixed with eruvin properly it is considered like a house and it is permitted to carry throughout]. A Jew who sent a shofar to another city via a gentile before Rosh Hashana, and the gentile was delayed and brought it on Rosh Hashana from outside the techum, and the townspeople did not know that a shofar would be sent to them; one may not carry it, and even to blow with it [only] if another is available; and in a time of urgency there is [room] to permit; and the source of this law is explained at the end of siman 515 in the gloss (see there in Mishnah Berurah).
(88) If a gentile made, etc. Even for a Jew; and it deals with a case where the gentile made [it] from his own horn, for it is not muktzeh, since he did not divert his mind from it; but from a Jew's horn it is forbidden to carry it, and even to blow with it, for it is muktzeh of nolad; and even if he made it on the first day of Rosh Hashana it is forbidden also on the second day, like the law of an egg laid on the first [day], which is forbidden also on the second day. Nevertheless if they have no other shofar at all, one may rely on the view of the poskim who permit nolad on Yom Tov, and blow with it so as not to neglect the positive commandment; and it is permitted to blow with it even on the second day. And so long as he holds it in his hand it is permitted to blow with it all the tekiyos according to the custom; but if he blew the obligatory tekiyos and put the shofar down from his hand, he may no longer carry it in order to blow customary tekiyos with it [Mateh Ephraim]. The Acharonim wrote that all those shofaros of which we said they are invalid to blow with are invalid both on the first day and on the second day.
