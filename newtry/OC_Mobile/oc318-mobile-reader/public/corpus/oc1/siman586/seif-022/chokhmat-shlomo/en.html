Marginal note: See in Magen Avraham what he asked: for behold it is a complete "born," and even to move stones is forbidden (see there). And in my humble opinion there is no difficulty at all: for one who looks into the Talmud in the third chapter of Rosh Hashanah, which concludes the reason that therefore if a pile of stones fell upon it we do not clear (because Yom Tov is a positive commandment and a negative commandment) implying that if it were only a negative commandment it would override; and even though there [the prohibition] is rabbinic, necessarily whatever the Sages enacted they enacted like [a law] of the Torah, and even regarding a rabbinic [law] it is reckoned a positive and a negative, as Magen Avraham wrote in siman 445. But it appears that that is specifically what is forbidden also on Shabbos, then regarding that the Sages strengthened [it] like [a law] of the Torah to give it the law of a positive and a negative; but what on Shabbos is entirely permitted and only on Yom Tov they were stringent, such as muktzeh, for we hold above in siman 495 that on Yom Tov muktzeh is forbidden and on Shabbos we hold that muktzeh is permitted; and the same applies to "born," as written in the laws of Shabbos, siman 324: one may cut up a carcass before the dogs even if it became a carcass today, for it is "born"; and so it implies from Magen Avraham's words in siman 308, s.k. 55, that "born" is permitted on Shabbos. And if so, in such a case (since on Shabbos it is permitted) even though on Yom Tov, so that they not come to treat [Yom Tov] lightly, they were stringent, it certainly is not at any rate like a positive and a negative. And therefore it comes out well: regarding moving stones of something that is not a vessel, it is explicit in siman 308 that even according to us it is forbidden even on Shabbos; regarding that the Sages made [it] like [a law] of the Torah and it is a positive and a negative, and the positive commandment of shofar does not override; but regarding something that is a vessel, only that it is muktzeh and "born" regarding that, if it were Shabbos it would be entirely permitted, and only on Yom Tov so that they not come to treat lightly they were stringent; regarding that it suffices if they made it like a negative commandment alone but not also with a positive commandment; for it would be like a jest that on Shabbos it was entirely permitted and on Yom Tov it would be like a positive and a negative. Therefore it is only like a negative commandment alone, and then a positive commandment overrides a negative commandment. And therefore it comes out well that the Mechaber does not bring [otherwise], for he follows his own view in ruling that muktzeh is forbidden on Yom Tov and consequently according to him "born" is permitted on Shabbos, and certainly it is fine, for according to his view it is fine, and he ruled properly that it is permitted to blow with it. Only even according to the Rama's view in siman 495, who brought those who permit muktzeh on Yom Tov and wrote that according to their words "born" is forbidden even on Shabbos; nevertheless he did not gloss anything here; and that is because until here "born" is more severe than muktzeh only regarding "born" that is recognizable from its body, that we saw with the eye that yesterday it was not as it is now, and as it is now it has no permit (therefore it is truly "born" and more severe than muktzeh; but "born" of this sort) if the non-Jew had made it for himself it would be permitted, as Magen Avraham himself wrote, only if they made it for a Jew it is forbidden; and if so, regarding this, from the body of the act it is not recognizable for whom he is making it, whether for a Jew or a non-Jew; therefore whatever is not recognizable from its body does not have the law of "born" but [only] muktzeh, and is permitted on Shabbos and on Yom Tov according to this view. And what is written in siman 501 that it is forbidden; that is the Mechaber; and examine carefully. And even if it is forbidden on Shabbos, it is only by force of [concern of] substituting for a complete "born," so that they not come to treat truly-"born" lightly; and this is not a decree upon a decree, for sometimes we do decree so; therefore at any rate regarding this they were again not stringent to give it the law of a positive and a negative; therefore the positive commandment of shofar overrides it; and it is fine one way or the other, whether according to the Mechaber or according to the Rama. And one can explain the matter further: for necessarily it is proved that regarding this they did not make a reinforcement as of the Torah; for if they made a reinforcement as of the Torah, how is it permitted on Shabbos; from the Torah we do not find anything that is permitted on Shabbos and forbidden on Yom Tov; therefore what they made more stringent on Yom Tov than on Shabbos is not as of the Torah, for if as of the Torah it would be forbidden even on Shabbos; and since they did not make [it] as of the Torah, therefore automatically it is again only like a negative commandment and not like a negative and a positive; and examine carefully, for this is clear as a matter of reasoning.
