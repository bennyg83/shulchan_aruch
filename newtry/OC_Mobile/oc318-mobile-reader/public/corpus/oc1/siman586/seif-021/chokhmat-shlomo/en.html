A shofar of Rosh Hashanah; we do not desecrate Yom Tov for it, etc. Marginal note: Behold the source of this law is from the Talmud, the fourth chapter of Rosh Hashanah, in the Mishnah there: A shofar of Rosh Hashanah; we do not carry it beyond the techum. And Rashi explained: to go outside the techum to hear the tekios; and the Tur explained that the intent is that we do not bring the shofar from outside the techum to here. And see in the sefer Kapos Temarim what he pressed to reconcile wherein Rashi and the Tur disagree; and in my humble opinion one can say with room to spare: for in the Talmud it gave a reason that therefore it does not override, because Yom Tov is a positive commandment and a negative commandment, and a positive commandment does not override a negative and a positive. And it was asked in the Shelah (shin-lamed abbreviation uncertain) (brought in Magen Avraham in the laws of Pesach, siman 446) that anyway it would not be "at the same time"; and he answered that it is a positive commandment of the many and we do not require "at the same time"; and see Magen Avraham there who wrote that specifically where an individual takes the many out [of their obligation] it is reckoned a positive commandment of the many (see there). And according to this one may inquire: is the intent specifically where now he comes to take the many out [of their obligation], then it is reckoned a positive commandment of the many; but if now he does not come to take the many out it is not reckoned a positive commandment of the many; or one can say that even though now he does not take the many out, since at any rate this positive commandment has the power and connection to take the many out, it is immediately reckoned a positive commandment of the many. And in this Rashi and the Tur would disagree: for it was difficult for them why the Mishnah stated the law only regarding a shofar and not regarding a lulav, for all these laws apply regarding a lulav as regarding a shofar; and necessarily regarding a lulav, since it is not a positive commandment of the many, it was obvious to him that it does not override since it is not "at the same time" only regarding a shofar, which is a positive commandment of the many, one might have thought that it overrides; therefore it teaches us that this too does not override. And therefore the Tur holds: since the main novelty is by force of [the possibility] that one might think it is a positive commandment of the many, if so, fine regarding bringing a shofar from there to here, then it is proved that the shofar is needed to take the many out of their obligation and it is a positive commandment of the many; but if he goes to the shofar, then each one separately goes only for himself, and if so it is not a positive commandment of the many, and it is obvious that it does not override as regarding a lulav. But Rashi holds that even regarding an individual, since the positive commandment is found [able] to take the many out, it is reckoned a positive commandment of the many; therefore he explained that he goes to the shofar, and they disagree as a matter of law. And further it would appear to say regarding the Tur's reason based on what is written in Shaar HaMelech in the name of the Divrei Moshe: that the Sages do not have power to uproot a matter of the Torah even by remaining seated [passive], only where it is not "at the same time"; but where it is "at the same time" they do not; and I brought it in Sefer HaChayim in the laws of lulav. And I decided differently: that what the Sages made their words like a positive and a negative commandment is only where it is not "at the same time"; but where it is "at the same time" they made their words only like a negative commandment alone (see there). And according to this, in Nimukei Yosef, the second chapter of Bava Metzia, he brought there a dispute regarding one occupied with a mitzvah, whether it is reckoned "at the same time" or not; and I decided in Sefer HaChayim to distinguish in siman 11 between where he is occupied with the body of a mitzvah-object, such as a lost object (then it is reckoned "at the same time") and where he is not occupied with the body of a mitzvah-object; it is not reckoned "at the same time." And according to this it was difficult for the Tur: why did the tanna teach these laws regarding a shofar and not regarding a lulav; and necessarily the reason is because we do not carry it beyond the techum (and the intent is to bring the shofar from outside the techum; therefore granted regarding a shofar) even though he is occupied with the body of the shofar, it is not reckoned occupying himself with the body of the mitzvah, for the mitzvah is not in the shofar but in the hearing; and a proof: that a stolen shofar is kosher, and it is proved that the mitzvah is not in the body of the shofar but in the hearing; therefore he is not reckoned as occupied with the body of the mitzvah and not reckoned "at the same time"; and therefore even though regarding a positive commandment of the many we do not require "at the same time," nevertheless regarding this the Sages strengthened their words and it is a positive and a negative. But regarding a lulav, since a stolen one is invalid, the taking itself is the mitzvah, and if so he is occupied with the body of the mitzvah and it is "at the same time," and then it is not reckoned a positive and a negative, and it is proper that the positive commandment of lulav should override it, and even this would be a positive commandment of the many according to the Magen Avraham's other doubt; and chametz is different, for regarding overriding we do not combine (see there. Therefore the tanna cited a shofar. But if the intent were like Rashi's explanation that he goes to the shofar) then he himself is the body of the mitzvah and it is like being occupied with the body of the mitzvah, and if so it is "at the same time" and not a positive and a negative, and if so it should override; and if this too does not override, let him cite a lulav. And from this the Tur proved his approach, and not like Rashi's explanation; and examine carefully.
