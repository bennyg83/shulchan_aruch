"For the shofar of Rosh Hashanah one does not desecrate Yom Tov, etc." NB: The source of this law is the Mishnah in chapter 4 of Rosh Hashanah: for the shofar of Rosh Hashanah one may not pass beyond the techum. Rashi explained that this means going outside the techum to hear the blasts, while the Tur explained that it means one may not bring the shofar from outside the techum to here. See Kapot Temarim, who was pressed to explain the dispute between Rashi and the Tur. In my humble opinion one may explain it easily. The Gemara gives the reason that it does not override because Yom Tov has both a positive commandment and a negative commandment, and a positive commandment does not override a negative and a positive commandment. The Shibbolei HaLeket, brought by the Magen Avraham in Hilchot Pesach siman 446, asked that in any case the two do not occur simultaneously, and answered that it is a public positive commandment, where simultaneity is not required. See the Magen Avraham there, who wrote that only where one individual discharges the obligation of the many is it considered a public positive commandment. According to this, one may investigate whether this means specifically where he is now coming to discharge the obligation of the many, or whether, although he is not now discharging the many, since this positive commandment has the capacity and relevance to discharge the many, it is immediately considered a public positive commandment. Rashi and the Tur may disagree on this. They were troubled why the Mishnah stated this law only regarding shofar and not lulav, where all these laws also apply. Perforce, by lulav, since it is not a public positive commandment, it is obvious that it does not override, since it is not simultaneous; only by shofar, which is a public positive commandment, one might have thought it overrides, so the Mishnah teaches that even this does not override. The Tur therefore holds that since the main novelty is because one might have thought it is a public positive commandment, that is true when bringing the shofar from there to here, for then it is clear that the shofar is needed to discharge the obligation of the many. But if he walks to the shofar, each individual is walking for himself, and it is not a public positive commandment; it is obvious that it does not override, just like lulav. Rashi, however, holds that even for an individual, since this positive commandment can discharge the many, it is considered a public positive commandment, and therefore he explained that one is walking to the shofar. They disagree in practical law. Another reason for the Tur may be based on what the Shaar HaMelech wrote in the name of the Devar Avraham, that the Sages do not have power to uproot Torah law even passively except where the mitzvah and the issur do not occur simultaneously; but where they occur simultaneously, they do not. I brought this in Sefer HaChayim, Hilchot Lulav, and I decided in another way that when the Sages strengthened their words as a positive and negative commandment, that is only where they do not occur simultaneously; where they do occur simultaneously, they made their words only like a negative commandment. See there. Now the Nimukei Yosef in Bava Metzia chapter 2 brought a dispute whether one who is occupied with a mitzvah is considered simultaneous or not, and in Sefer HaChayim siman 11 I distinguished between where he is occupied with the object of the mitzvah, such as a lost object, which is considered simultaneous, and where he is not occupied with the object of the mitzvah, which is not. According to this, the Tur found it difficult: why did the tanna state these laws regarding shofar and not lulav? Perforce, because "one may not pass it beyond the techum" means bringing the shofar from outside the techum. By shofar, even though he is occupied with the shofar itself, he is not considered occupied with the object of the mitzvah, for the mitzvah is not in the shofar but in the hearing, as is evident from the fact that a stolen shofar is valid; this proves that the mitzvah is not in the body of the shofar but in the hearing. Therefore it is not considered simultaneous. Although with a public positive commandment simultaneity is not required, nevertheless here the Sages strengthened their words and made them like a positive and negative commandment. But with lulav, since a stolen lulav is invalid, the taking itself is the mitzvah; thus one who is occupied with the lulav is occupied with the object of the mitzvah, and it is simultaneous. It would then not be considered a positive and negative commandment, and the positive commandment of lulav should override it; this too would be a public positive commandment according to the other side of the Magen Avraham's doubt, while chametz is different because for overriding we do not combine. Therefore the tanna stated shofar. But if the meaning were like Rashi, that he walks to the shofar, then he himself is the body of the mitzvah, like one occupied with the object of the mitzvah; if so, it is simultaneous and not a positive and negative commandment, and it should override. And if even this does not override, it should have stated lulav. From here the Tur proved his view, unlike Rashi. Examine very carefully.
