[8] Levush: He should not sound it, etc. In a pressing circumstance he may sound it himself, as Bach writes in Yoreh Deah 221:13. Rabbeinu Yerucham writes: if one said, “This shofar is forbidden to me by a vow,” he may sound it. This resolves Ran’s question why Rava in Rosh Hashanah 28 also stated the law of one prohibited by vow from a shofar, after already ruling that one prohibited from benefiting from another person may use that person’s shofar. According to what I wrote, the novelty is that he himself may sound it. According to the reading that forbids it, one can say that this itself is the novelty. Ran in Nedarim 16 also writes that if one says, “A sukkah is forbidden to me,” since he did not mention sitting in the sukkah, he prohibited benefit and it is permitted. The same should apply to a shofar, and this requires study. See Beis Hillel to Yoreh Deah 221, where neither he nor his son in the gloss noticed Bach here.
