If it was pierced, etc. There is a difference of opinions on this between the Rambam and the Rosh. For it is taught in the Gemara: Our Rabbis taught: If it was pierced and he sealed it, whether with of its kind or not of its kind, it is invalid; R' Natan says: of its kind it is kosher, not of its kind it is invalid. And we hold like R' Natan; and we say according to him that that which he permits of its kind is specifically when its majority remained. And the Rambam explains that R' Natan refers to [a case] where it does not impede the blast (namely after sealing, its sound returned to what it had been initially) and even so it is kosher only of its kind; it turns out we require three [conditions] for excellence: it does not impede the blast, and of its kind, and its majority remained; but if one of these is lacking it is forbidden; this is the first opinion that he brings here. And the Rosh holds that R' Natan refers to [a case] where it impedes the blast, and even so it is kosher of its kind; automatically this is a leniency, for we require only two (namely of its kind and its majority) and even if it impedes; or its majority and it does not impede, even if not of its kind. And this is what he concluded here: and if it is a time of urgency, etc. that one may rely on the Rosh.
Even though it changed, etc. Meaning: even if a measure of tekiah did not remain in it (so wrote the Raem, whom Beis Yosef brought regarding the law of cracked lengthwise).
That one should not blow with a pierced shofar. But cracked is not included in this, and its law is explained afterward.
If its majority remained whole. And the Tur wrote that if the majority of the shofar did not remain (even though a measure of shofar remained from the place of the hole until the placing of his mouth) it is forbidden, even though regarding cracked across its width [that remaining measure] helps; and the reason is that nevertheless here there is the sound of a shofar with another matter, namely the sealing.
There is [room] to permit if it was sealed with of its kind. This is the view of the Rosh as written; and Beis Yosef wrote in the name of the Mizrachi: and accordingly, if the hole had impeded the blast before sealing, and when he sealed it the sound returned to what it had been initially, it is obviously kosher. And it is puzzling: what is different from plating it with gold on the outside, which is invalid because it is the sound of a shofar and another matter, and here he permits even though that sound after sealing is a shofar and another matter? And perhaps one may say: since through the sealing it returned to what it had been when whole (even though it had impeded before the sealing, and now it does not impede) the sound of the sealing is not called [another matter] (end quote). And in my humble opinion: since it is of its kind it is not called another matter at all, and it is not similar to plating it with gold; and so wrote the Rosh explicitly, as follows: but of its kind, even if its sound changed, another matter does not cause that the sound emerge, and all sounds are kosher (end quote. Further the Raem wrote: I have not heard a proper reason where it had initially impeded, and he sealed it, and it [still] impedes) why should it be invalid? For there is no sound of the sealing at all, since it returned to what it had been before the sealing and was kosher. And Beis Yosef answered: even though it was sealed, even though it still impedes the blast, it is impossible that it impede as much as it impeded before the sealing, and it turns out it is a shofar and another matter, for this sound is not like when it was whole nor like when it was pierced. And this is a light question: for if so (that it is impossible to be as initially) then even if now it is like the first hour, we should say it is impossible to be exactly as initially, since there is in any case a sealing; rather certainly one can make it exact. And the difficulties of the Raem; it appears to me that we are concerned that the sealing causes that the sound of the shofar that was before the sealing became spoiled because of the sealing, and a new sound is born because of the sealing. It is fine if now it returned exactly to the first sound: we say that through the sealing that was of its kind it became exactly the first creature, since the sound truly returned thus; which is not so if no [new] sound was born except as it was before the sealing (then it was not made like the first creature, and automatically the sound born now is another matter, and therefore invalid) as appears to me.
