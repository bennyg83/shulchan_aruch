Even though its sound changed, it is kosher. See in the Mishnah Berurah what he wrote, that we require that at least its majority remain, for if most of its length was pierced it is as if all of it [was pierced]; this is from the words of the Bach and the Peri Chadash; and see further there in the Bach, who wondered at the Tur for not distinguishing in this, and brought that Rabbeinu Yerucham wrote explicitly that [it is kosher] specifically when the majority remained (but if its majority did not remain, it is invalid if it hinders the tekiah (see there); and it implies that if it does not hinder the tekiah, it is kosher even if its majority did not remain; and perhaps he follows his own view there, that he holds that even when the hole was sealed, if it does not hinder the tekiah it is kosher even if its majority did not remain and even if [the seal] is not of its kind). But in truth I wonder: if a hole does not invalidate a shofar, then it is regarded as whole, so what do we care that its majority is pierced? And regarding [a case where] it was sealed, that case is different, for there we come either for the reason that a shofar and something else are mixed in it, or for the reason that it is like two shofaros; therefore we require that at least a majority [remain] without a defect, and we no longer heed the minority that has a defect in it, which is not so here, where this is no defect at all. And apparently one can answer that according to this, even when sealed it would suffice for us that its majority be without seals, but it could be full of holes; yet in the language of the poskim and the Mechaber it is clear that [it is kosher] specifically if its majority remained whole, and it implies that when punctured it is invalid even if the holes are open and were not sealed. But in truth one can reject this: that the poskim are dealing with [a case] where there was one large hole and it was sealed (see the Ramban and the Rosh), and regarding that they said [it is kosher] specifically when the hole was not so large that a majority of the shofar would not remain whole, for if the hole was, for example, half the shofar and it was sealed, then the majority would include the seal; but indeed, if the hole was a minority of the shofar and it was sealed, even if there are holes in the rest of the shofar as well it is kosher, for holes do not invalidate. And it is possible that Rabbeinu Yerucham as well, who mentioned in our topic that its majority remain, also meant that the hole should not be so large as to extend to half the shofar, for if it extended, and all the more so if it extended to its majority, then it is as if all of it [was opened], and the shofar is open throughout, and the name "shofar" does not apply to it; and if we invalidate when cracked, all the more so in this case. But when there are many small holes, even if the holes are over the entire shofar, what do we care, since holes do not invalidate, and between hole and hole there is shofar, so it is not cracked. Afterward I found in the Rosh, siman 6, who wrote: "And the beraisa teaches us," etc. it implies as well that when pierced over most of it it is kosher even though it hinders the tekiah, and even if a measure of a shofar did not remain in it. And in the Ritva I found that he wrote: And if they did not seal it at all, according to the words of the Yerushalmi it is kosher even if most of it was ruined and it has a measure of a shofar, for all sounds are kosher in a shofar; but according to our Gemara one should be stringent and rule, when they did not seal it at all, to invalidate if it hinders the tekiah (for our Gemara said that all sounds are kosher in a shofar only when it is in itself [sounding so] not because of a defect) end quote. And it implies from him that when it does not have a measure of a shofar, even according to the Yerushalmi one should be stringent, unlike the Rosh; and further it is heard from him that when it does not hinder the tekiah, even according to our Gemara one should be lenient even if most of it was ruined, if a measure enough for a tekiah remained in it.
That one should not blow with a punctured shofar. In Beis Yosef he brought this in the name of the Kol Bo and a responsum of the Rosh; and in truth this is found also in the Rambam's Commentary on the Mishnah, and this is its language: "And if it was pierced, there are laws regarding it, namely that he must seal the hole," etc. (and it clearly implies that he explains the Mishnah according to the second explanation of the Kol Bo that is brought in Beis Yosef) see there. And so too the Ritva wrote that the Bavli disagrees with the Yerushalmi on this, and according to the Bavli if they did not seal it at all it is invalid if it hinders the tekiah. And here I saw something of a wonder in the words of the Peri Megadim on our topic: for in s.k. 7 in Mishbetzos Zahav he leans toward holding that even according to the Mechaber's view who is lenient with a punctured shofar, nevertheless we require (even with a long shofar) that at least a majority of the shofar be whole without holes; and if so, according to this, the Rama who is stringent [holds that] even in that manner one should not blow with it ab initio; yet in s.k. 9 he leans toward holding that even according to the Rama's view, if its majority is whole one can say that we blow with it ab initio. And perhaps because the matter is not clear to him he leans this way and that. And we have already written in the main text that if the sound is clear one should not be stringent with it, and especially when its majority is whole.
Rather, its sound returned to what it was before it was pierced. This teaches us two things: one, that even though before it was sealed the hole was hindering the tekiah, it is kosher since now its sound has returned [and this is to exclude the view of some Rishonim who are stringent in this]; and further it teaches us that if its sound returned changed, as before the sealing, it is invalid, for the reason that it is impossible that the sound of the shofar should be the same after the sealing as before the sealing, and we are concerned that it may have changed somewhat or that a new sound was produced, and it is invalid for the reason mentioned above [later authorities].
