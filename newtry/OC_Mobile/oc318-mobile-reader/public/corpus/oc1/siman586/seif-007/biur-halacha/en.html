Even though its sound changed, it is kosher - See Mishnah Berurah, where I wrote that at least the majority of it must remain, for if most of its length was pierced it is as if all of it were pierced. This is from Bach and Peri Chadash; see there. See further in Bach, who wondered why the Tur did not distinguish this, and he brought that Rabbenu Yerucham wrote explicitly that this applies specifically when the majority remained. But if the majority did not remain, it is invalid if it prevents the blast; see there. It implies that if it does not prevent the blast, it is kosher even if the majority did not remain; perhaps he follows his own view that even where the hole was sealed, if it does not prevent the blast it is kosher even though the majority did not remain and even if it was sealed with a different material. In truth, however, this is difficult to me: if a hole does not invalidate a shofar, then it is like a whole shofar, and why should it matter that most of it is perforated? With sealing it is different, for there we come because shofar and another substance are mixed in it, or because it is like two shofarot; therefore we require at least a majority without defect, and then we no longer consider the minority that has a defect. Here, however, it is no defect at all. Seemingly one could answer that according to this, even in sealing it should suffice that the majority be without seals, though it could be full of holes. But the language of the poskim and the Mechaber indicates specifically that most of it must remain whole, implying that if it is pierced it is invalid even if the holes are open and not sealed. In truth, however, one may reject this, for the poskim discuss a case where there was one large hole that was sealed, see Ramban and Rosh; concerning this they said that this applies only when the hole was not so large that the majority of the shofar would not remain whole. For if the hole was, for example, half the shofar and was sealed, then the majority is with sealing. But indeed, if the hole was a minority of the shofar and was sealed, even if the rest of the shofar also has holes, it is kosher, for holes do not invalidate. Perhaps Rabbenu Yerucham, who mentioned in our matter that the majority must remain, also means that the hole should not be so large that it extends to half the shofar, for if it extends that far, and certainly if it extends through most of it, it is as if all of it is open, and the name shofar is not upon it. If we invalidate a split shofar, all the more so here. But where there are many small holes, even if the holes are throughout the shofar, what concern is there, since holes do not invalidate and between one hole and another there is shofar, so it is not split. Afterwards I found in Rosh, siman 6, that he wrote, "the baraita teaches us..." which also implies that if it is pierced through most of it, it is kosher even if it prevents the blast and even if the required measure of a shofar did not remain. In Ritva I found that he wrote: if he did not seal it at all, according to the Yerushalmi it is kosher, even if most of it was deficient and it has the measure of a shofar, for all sounds are valid in a shofar. But according to our Gemara one should be stringent and judge an unsealed hole invalid if it prevents the blast, for our Gemara said that all sounds are valid in a shofar only when it is so by itself and not because of a defect. End quote. It follows from him that if it lacks the measure of a shofar, even according to the Yerushalmi one should be stringent, unlike the Rosh. It also follows from him that if it does not prevent the blast, even according to our Gemara one may be lenient even if most of it is deficient, provided the measure for blowing remains.
That one should not blow with a pierced shofar - In Beit Yosef he brought this in the name of Kol Bo and a responsum of the Rosh. In truth, this is also found in Rambam's Commentary to the Mishnah: "If it was pierced, it has laws, namely that one must seal the hole..." It clearly implies that he explains the Mishnah like the second explanation of Kol Bo brought in Beit Yosef; see there. Ritva likewise wrote that the Bavli disagrees with the Yerushalmi on this, and according to the Bavli, if he did not seal it at all it is invalid when it prevents the blast. I saw something rather surprising in the words of Peri Megadim here. In s.k. 7 in Mishbetzot Zahav he tends to say that even according to the Mechaber, who is lenient with a pierced shofar, nevertheless even in a long shofar at least most of the shofar must be whole without holes. If so, according to this, Rama, who is stringent, would mean that even in this case one should not blow with it initially. Yet in s.k. 9 he tends to say that even according to Rama, if most of it is whole, perhaps one may blow with it initially. Perhaps because the matter was not clear to him, he inclined this way and that. We have already written inside that if the sound is clear, one should not be stringent, especially when most of it is whole.
Rather, its sound returned to what it had been before it was pierced - This teaches two things. First, even though before it was sealed the hole prevented the blast, it is kosher because now its sound returned; this excludes the view of some early authorities who are stringent in this. Second, it teaches that if its sound returned changed as it had been before the sealing, it is invalid, because it is impossible for the shofar's sound after sealing to be exactly the same as before sealing, and we are concerned that it changed somewhat or that a new sound was produced, making it invalid for the reason stated above by the later authorities.
