Seif 7: If not, etc. Yerushalmi there: R. Abba bar Zavda in the name of R. Zeira: that is when he sealed it, but if he did not seal it, it is valid, for all sounds are valid for a shofar.
However, etc. In Pesikta Rabbati: "Our teacher, teach us: what is the law of a shofar that was punctured and sealed?" Thus our rabbis taught: if it prevents the blowing it is invalid, and if not it is valid. What is the reason? For it is written, "and the Lord God will blow the shofar"; just as the shofar of the Holy One, blessed be He, is whole, so too the shofar of Rosh Hashanah must be whole. Rokeach, beginning of siman 203; see there.
One should not blow. Because there are several explanations of the above Yerushalmi. Responsa of the Rosh.
After sealing, etc. From what is written there, "and it was diminished," etc.; otherwise it is valid. In such a case it is certainly impossible that it would not prevent while still punctured. Tosafot there, s.v. nikav; see there.
And if he sealed it, etc. This is the Rambam's view, that Rav Natan refers to the latter clause of the Mishnah. The other poskim rule like the Rambam, and in pressing circumstances like Tosafot. The Rambam's proof is from what is written in the Yerushalmi there: a shofar that split and he glued it is invalid. For whom was this necessary? For Rav Natan. If he glued pieces of shofar it is invalid; this too is Rav Natan. If it was punctured and he sealed it: R. Chiyya in the name of R. Yochanan said this is Rav Natan, for it was taught: if it was punctured and he sealed it, whether with its own kind or not with its own kind, if it prevents the blowing it is invalid, and if not it is valid. Rav Natan says: with its own kind it is valid; not with its own kind it is invalid. Since he had to force the Mishnah as not with its own kind and according to Rav Natan, learn from this that Rav Natan refers to the latter clause, and the Rabbis invalidate in every case. Likewise "he glued," etc., is also not with its own kind, with glue according to Rashi. But Tosafot objected that in the first tanna's words it is explicit in the Yerushalmi there: "if it prevents," etc., and so too in the Tosefta explicitly like their words. One must say the Rambam did not have that reading. They explained that Rav Natan refers to the first clause, as is explicit in the baraita there, that the first tanna says "whether," etc., and Rav Natan says "with its own kind," etc. According to this, we always require that most of it remain, for otherwise it is as though it were removed. Tosafot understand the above Yerushalmi as saying: it is even Rav Natan, and all the more so according to the first tanna. Since in the first clause it says "for whom was this necessary," etc., meaning that according to the Rabbis, since they hold that if it prevents blowing it is invalid, why state "it split," etc., or "he glued pieces," etc.? But for Rav Natan it is necessary, since he validates with its own kind even if it prevents; glued with its own kind is as the Rosh wrote. All the more so according to the Ramban's explanation, that he glued it by fire until its edges adhered. It then asks from the latter clause, punctured and sealed, etc., which is the first tanna's baraita, and R. Chiyya in the name of R. Yochanan says this too is Rav Natan. It says: for it was taught, punctured and sealed, whether, etc.; not with its own kind is invalid. R. Chiyya in the name of R. Yochanan: read our Mishnah as "if it prevents the blowing it is invalid, and if not it is valid." Meaning, not as one might have thought, that Rav Natan does not distinguish at all between preventing and not preventing. Rather, Rav Natan, who invalidates not with its own kind, is like the Mishnah where it prevents. This is what R. Chiyya said: read our Mishnah, etc. He follows his view, wanting to establish the Mishnah like Rav Natan. Their words are primary.
