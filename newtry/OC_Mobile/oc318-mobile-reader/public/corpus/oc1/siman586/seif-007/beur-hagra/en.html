Seif 7: Unless, etc. The Yerushalmi there records in the name of Rabbi Abba bar Zevida in the name of Rabbi Zeira: this applies where he sealed it, but if he did not seal it, it is valid, because every sound is valid from a shofar.
Nevertheless, etc. Pesikta Rabbasi states: “Teach us, our master: what is the law of a shofar that was punctured and sealed? Our Sages taught: if the repair impedes the sounding, it is invalid; if not, it is valid. What is the reason? It is written, ‘Hashem God shall sound the shofar.’ Just as the shofar of the Holy One, blessed is He, is whole, so must the shofar of Rosh Hashanah be whole.” See Rokeach, beginning of siman 203.
One should not sound it. Because there are several interpretations of the Yerushalmi cited above. Responsa of Rosh.
After it was sealed, etc. This follows the statement there, “provided that it was diminished,” etc., which implies that otherwise it is valid. In such a case it is certainly impossible that the hole would not impede the sound while it remained open. Tosafos there, s.v. “It was punctured,” see there.
And if he sealed it, etc. This is Rambam’s view, that Rabbi Nasan refers to the latter clause of the Mishnah, and the other poskim explain Rambam accordingly. In a pressing situation one may rely upon Tosafos. Rambam’s proof is from the Yerushalmi there: “A shofar that split and was joined is invalid. For whom was this teaching necessary? For Rabbi Nasan, who says that joining fragments of shofaros is invalid. This too is Rabbi Nasan’s law concerning a shofar that was punctured and sealed.” Rabbi Chiya said in the name of Rabbi Yochanan that it is Rabbi Nasan’s view, for it was taught: if it was punctured and sealed, whether with its own material or with another material, if it impedes the sounding it is invalid, and if not it is valid; Rabbi Nasan says, with its own material it is valid, with another material it is invalid. Since the Yerushalmi had to interpret the Mishnah as referring to another material and according to Rabbi Nasan, it follows that Rabbi Nasan addresses the latter clause, while the Sages invalidate it in every case. Likewise, joining fragments is considered another material when glue is used, according to Rashi. Tosafos object that the first Tanna’s ruling, “if it impedes the sounding,” is stated explicitly in the Yerushalmi and likewise clearly in the Tosefta. One must say that Rambam did not have that reading. Tosafos therefore explain that Rabbi Nasan refers to the first clause, as the baraisa explicitly states: the first Tanna says, “whether with its own material,” etc., and Rabbi Nasan says, “with its own material,” etc. According to this, the majority of the shofar must always remain, for otherwise it is considered removed. Tosafos understand the Yerushalmi to mean that the ruling applies even according to Rabbi Nasan, and all the more so according to the first Tanna. Since the first clause asks, “For whom was this teaching necessary?” it means that according to the Sages, who hold that a repair impeding the sounding is invalid, there was no need to state separately that a split shofar or joined fragments are invalid. According to Rabbi Nasan, however, it was necessary, since he validates a repair with the same material even where it impedes the sounding, and a joined split is considered the same material, as Rosh writes, and certainly according to Ramban, who explains that it was heated in fire until its edges adhered. The Yerushalmi then challenges from the latter clause, “It was punctured and sealed,” which reflects the first Tanna’s view in the baraisa, and Rabbi Chiya said in the name of Rabbi Yochanan that this too is Rabbi Nasan’s view. It then cites the baraisa: “It was punctured and sealed, whether with its own material or another material; Rabbi Nasan says, with its own material,” etc. Rabbi Chiya said in the name of Rabbi Yochanan: “This is the proper reading of our Mishnah: if it impedes the sounding it is invalid, and if not it is valid.” Meaning, not as initially assumed that Rabbi Nasan makes no distinction between whether it impedes the sounding or not. Rather, Rabbi Nasan’s invalidation with another material follows the Mishnah only where it impedes the sounding. This is why Rabbi Chiya says, “This is the proper reading of our Mishnah,” and follows his view that the Mishnah should be established according to Rabbi Nasan. Their interpretation is primary.
