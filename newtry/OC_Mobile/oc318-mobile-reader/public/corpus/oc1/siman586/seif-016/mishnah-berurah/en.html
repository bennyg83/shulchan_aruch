(69) He plated it with gold, etc. This is the language of the baraita until the words "some explain."
(70) That is, the thickness of the shofar, etc. Meaning: that on the thickness of the shofar above there are two edges; one that inclines toward the hollow of the shofar and one that inclines toward the outside. According to the first interpretation, "the place of placing the mouth" means he plated it toward the inside, and its invalidity is because the breath of the sound enters the gold; and "not the place of placing the mouth" means the edge that inclines toward the outside around, and because of that small [amount] the sound does not change and it is kosher; but the entire length of the shofar from the outside is included in "he plated it with gold from outside" mentioned in the baraita. And according to the second interpretation, that whole upper edge that he inserts into his mouth is all called the place of placing the mouth; and "not the place of placing the mouth" means any amount adjacent to that place from outside; and they hold that because of that any amount also the sound is not apt to change even though it is on the wall from outside, and therefore the baraita stated plainly that it is kosher. And from that place downward to the end is called "he plated it with gold from outside," and it depends on whether the sound changed. And if he plated it with gold on the inside, in every manner it is invalid because he is blowing into gold.
(71) That is, the entire length of the shofar, etc. The Mechaber abbreviated this: for from his language it implies that the entire length has one law and it is kosher if he plated it with gold; and in truth that is not so, only the first any amount adjacent to the thickness that he inserts into his mouth is kosher, for ordinarily the sound is not apt to change because of that any amount; but from there downward until the end of the shofar, if he plated it, it depends on whether the sound changed because of this.
