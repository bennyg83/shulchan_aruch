Magen Avraham seif katan 4 And see above at the end of siman 454. It seems to me that his intention is to ask one question, as follows. In the Yerushalmi there are two reasons why one fulfills the obligation with a stolen shofar. One reason, brought in the Taz, seif katan 2, is that there is no derivation forbidding a stolen object. A second reason is that theft does not apply to sound. The practical difference between them is with a mitzvah performed through an act and not through sound, where there is no derivation excluding a stolen object. According to the first reason, one fulfills the obligation with a stolen object; according to the second reason, one does not, even though there is no derivation excluding it. The Rambam brought the reason that theft does not apply to sound, implying that in any mitzvah performed through an act one does not fulfill the obligation with a stolen object, even where there is no derivation excluding a stolen object. This also appears from the words of the Beit Yosef and the poskim in siman 691, who wrote that one fulfills the obligation with a stolen Megillah because theft does not apply to sound, implying that without this reason he would not fulfill the obligation. On this the Magen Avraham asked that at the end of siman 454, regarding stolen matzah, the Rosh did not say that one fails to fulfill the obligation with it except because there is a derivation from a verse, brought in the Taz there, which the Gemara derives by a gezeirah shavah from matzah to challah; see there. This seems to me to be the intention of the Magen Avraham, and it seems to me that this is itself the objection of the Raavad against the Rambam, brought in the Beit Yosef here; see there. This is easy to understand.
