(s.k. 6) When it was nullified, etc. (if so it was pushed aside, etc.) these are the words of the Mordechai, who is the "some are stringent" that the Rama wrote: that since in tractate Avodah Zarah folio 47a it is an unresolved inquiry whether there is "pushing aside" regarding a mitzvah (whether we say it returns and becomes fit again) therefore from doubt we go to stringency.
Since it is not in his power, etc. these are the words of Tosafos in tractate Avodah Zarah and in tractate Sukkah, who asked that it should have been resolved from what we learned: A hadas whose berries were more numerous than its leaves is invalid; and if he reduced them it is kosher; and one may not reduce them on Yom Tov; and nevertheless we say there that even if he reduced them on Yom Tov it is kosher, hence there is no "pushing aside" regarding mitzvos. And Tosafos answered, and this is their language in tractate Sukkah folio 32a, beginning of the s.v. "with an asherah that Moshe," etc.: And one can say that a hadas is different, for it is in his power to fix, even though it is forbidden to reduce on Yom Tov, nevertheless it is in his power to reduce; but regarding avodah zarah it is not in his power to nullify, for a Jew does not nullify avodah zarah of a non-Jew (end quote of Tosafos).
Even if it was fit and then pushed aside, etc. (and see Beis Yosef. Meaning: in Sukkah folio 33 we say that where the berries blackened before Yom Tov) for then they invalidate, since so long as they are green they do not invalidate (therefore when they blackened before Yom Tov, which is "pushed aside from the outset" before Yom Tov entered, when the pushing-aside begins) certainly it returns and becomes fit. But where they blackened on Yom Tov, which is "was fit and then pushed aside" (for when Yom Tov entered it was kosher, and on Yom Tov when they blackened they were pushed aside) in that [case] it was an inquiry to the Talmud whether if he reduced them they return and become fit. And nevertheless the Rambam ruled that in such a case too it returns and becomes fit; and so too the Rosh ruled (see there siman 646 what Beis Yosef wrote in reconciling the matter even though it is an unresolved inquiry); and see there also in Taz.
Exempt from covering unless it became revealed again, etc., so it should read. And one should have said he should cover from doubt, such as this: that we know he should not recite a blessing on the covering, as written there regarding a koy, which is a doubt in se'if 4; for in the Gemara, etc., it compares them to one another. Meaning: regarding covering it is not "pushing aside," for it is in his power to uncover it; and where it is in his power there is no "pushing aside," as written above. To this he wrote that necessarily this is not "in his power"; for in the Gemara it compares them to one another. Meaning: there in tractate Avodah Zarah Reish Lakish inquired regarding an asherah of a non-Jew that was nullified (whether its lulav is kosher for the mitzvah) whether there is "pushing aside" regarding mitzvos. And he sought to resolve from what we learned: If the wind covered the blood of a wild animal or bird, one need not cover unless it became revealed again; and Rav Papa said: Learn from this that there is no "pushing aside" regarding mitzvos. And it rejected: that Rav Papa himself is in doubt whether it is clear to him that there is no "pushing aside" even for a leniency, or perhaps he is in doubt and from doubt one must cover stringently; but regarding a lulav, which is a leniency, it is possible there is "pushing aside" and it was not resolved. And since he sought to resolve a lulav of an asherah from this [law] of covering, one must necessarily say that also regarding covering it is not "in his power"; for if it is called "in his power," how could he seek to resolve a lulav of an asherah, which is not in his power?
And so Tosafos wrote, etc. that this is called "not in his power"; and this is the language of Tosafos: That which it compares here this [law] of covering the blood, where it is in his power to uncover; nevertheless one should reckon it as "pushing aside," since once he was exempted from covering he is not obligated to take it out of being pushed aside in order to cover again (end quote. Meaning: specifically regarding reducing a hadas) if he were permitted to reduce them on Yom Tov; it is certainly called "in his power," for it is likely that he will reduce them so that he have a hadas to fulfill the mitzvah he is obligated in; and even though it is forbidden to reduce them on Yom Tov, it is [as if] a lion crouches upon him, and nevertheless it is called "in his power." And likewise regarding a lulav of an asherah, if a Jew could nullify, it would be called "in his power," for certainly he will nullify it in order thereby to fulfill the mitzvah of lulav. Which is not so regarding covering: since once the wind covered it he is not obligated to uncover it and cover it [again], it is likely that he will not uncover it; therefore it is not called "in his power."
And it appears to me that the view of the Tur and Beis Yosef, etc. Regarding this his intent is not to resolve his [prior] difficulty; rather it is a separate matter and does not refer to what is above it. And he comes to resolve the view of the Tur and Beis Yosef from what is difficult for them, as he writes afterward: for they hold that regarding avodah zarah of a non-Jew, even if it has not yet been nullified, since it is possible to nullify we do not say "its measure is crushed" and that is according to the initial assumption of Tosafos in Sukkah folio 32 mentioned above. But according to the conclusion of their words they proved like the view of the "some are stringent" that the Rama wrote, from the force of a difficulty. And if so, for the Tur and those of his view one must resolve Tosafos's difficulties. And that is: Tosafos in Sukkah initially wanted to say regarding what answers the difficulty against Rava who said a lulav of an asherah; he should not take it, and if he took it he has fulfilled; yet in the Mishnah it teaches that he has not fulfilled; and it answers that the Mishnah deals with avodah zarah of a Jew which has no nullification and its measure is crushed; implying that Rava deals with avodah zarah of a non-Jew which has nullification, that its measure is not crushed, and he has fulfilled even before nullification (for example where a Jew did not intend to acquire it; and it deals with the second day of Yom Tov where we do not require "for you"). And Tosafos rejected this approach: for if so, that which was an inquiry to Reish Lakish in tractate Avodah Zarah (whether a lulav of an asherah is kosher for the mitzvah, whether there is "pushing aside" regarding mitzvos) what was his inquiry? Behold it was never pushed aside, for even before nullification if he took it he has fulfilled. Therefore Rabbeinu Tam explained that even avodah zarah of a non-Jew before nullification he has not fulfilled; and Rava deals after nullification, and nevertheless ab initio he should not take it because it is repulsive. And nevertheless they asked: if so, resolve from Rava that there is no "pushing aside" regarding mitzvos, from Rava's saying that after nullification he has fulfilled; yet in tractate Avodah Zarah it implies that Reish Lakish's inquiry was not resolved whether there is "pushing aside," even for stringency. Therefore Tosafos concluded that Rava deals with [a case] where it was nullified before Yom Tov, so that the name of "pushing aside" never took effect upon it; and Reish Lakish's inquiry regarding avodah zarah deals with [a case] where he nullified on Yom Tov, which is "pushed aside from the outset" when Yom Tov entered, and that is like the view of the "some are stringent," specifically where it was nullified before Yom Tov. And if so, the Tur and those who stand in his approach, who hold that even before nullification he has fulfilled, need to resolve for their view Tosafos's difficulty from tractate Avodah Zarah: what was Reish Lakish's inquiry, behold it was never pushed aside?

That is ab initio; for we say there in Avodah Zarah: Rav Papa inquired; one who bows to an animal, what [is the law] of its wool for techeiles? And the Talmud discusses: techeiles of what? If techeiles of tzitzis (that is Reish Lakish's inquiry. And Magen Avraham holds that the Talmud's intent is: even though Rav Papa's doubt) since living beings are not forbidden for ordinary [use] (is whether we permit for a mitzvah need, or whether we say it is repulsive and forbidden or not) and that is Reish Lakish's inquiry regarding an asherah that was nullified, which is permitted for ordinary [use]: nevertheless he doubted regarding [use] toward Heaven, whether there is "pushing aside" regarding mitzvos and it is forbidden ab initio because it is repulsive, as above. And since it compared the wool of living beings that were worshipped with Reish Lakish's inquiry, therefore just as Reish Lakish inquired only regarding ab initio, as above, the same applies to techeiles for fulfilling [the mitzvah]: that too is included in this doubt, also only regarding ab initio.
