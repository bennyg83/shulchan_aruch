(1) Of Rosh Hashana. To exclude a fast day, on which they also blow with a shofar; we do not require of a ram and bent.
(2) Of a ram. As a remembrance of the Binding of Yitzchak. And included in this is also a female sheep; only that the most meticulous custom to seek specifically one of a ram, for it is more of a remembrance of the ram of Yitzchak.
(3) And bent. As a sign that they bend their hearts toward the Place [Heaven].
(4) And after the fact all the shofaros, etc. Meaning: even of other domestic animals or wild animals. Nevertheless if he has a shofar of a he-goat and a she-goat, he should give it precedence over a shofar of ibex (which is a kind of wild animal) and other wild animals, for it is closer to the species of "seh," as it is written "seh of lambs and seh of goats," and there is some remembrance of the Binding of Yitzchak.
(5) And the mitzvah is with bent ones, etc. Meaning: not specifically of a ram, but even of other domestic and wild animals, bent is also preferable to straight. And behold the Mechaber stated plainly and did not clarify: a straight ram versus bent of other species, which of them is preferable; however the conclusion of most poskim is that the virtue of bent is preferable; therefore if an ibex bent and a sheep straight happen to him, he should blow with the bent ibex, for the Rabbis enacted to blow with bent as a sign that they bend their hearts toward the Place in prayer, which is not so for a shofar of a ram: it is not a rabbinic enactment to blow specifically of a ram, but a custom that all Israel practiced from always as a remembrance of the Binding, and in this there is a preferred mitzvah. Nevertheless the commentators wrote that in this too there is at least the law of hiddur mitzvah; and therefore if they ask him to add for a shofar of a ram he should add up to a third, as of the kind explained below in siman 656.
(6) Of a cow. And the same applies to an ox; and the reason is that they are called "keren" and not "shofar."
(7) And likewise horns of most animals. Such as aurochs and gazelles and the like; and their invalidity is because a shofar must be hollow, for "shofar" is from the language of a tube. And these invalidities of a cow and of a single bone are according to the Torah; and even if he has no other shofar one may not blow with them.
(8) And likewise a shofar of an unclean animal. And as tefillin were not validated to be made from the hide of an unclean animal. And the Acharonim wrote that this law is not clear; therefore if he has no other shofar he should blow with it, but he should not recite a blessing over it, lest it be invalid by law; and when afterward a kosher shofar happens to him he needs to blow again, also without a blessing, lest he already fulfilled [his obligation] with the first shofar. And according to everyone a shofar of a neveilah or terefah is kosher to blow with.
