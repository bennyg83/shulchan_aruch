Of a ram and bent, etc. Even though the Shulchan Aruch grouped these two in one category, nevertheless they are not equal: for bent is the essence of the mitzvah l'chatchila, and they have not fulfilled their obligation l'chatchila with a straight [shofar]; but of a ram is not called l'chatchila, but rather a preferred mitzvah. So it is implied in the Tur, who wrote: it turns out there are three distinctions in it, namely, of a cow is entirely invalid even b'dieved: this is part one; and other species such as goats and ibex that are straight are kosher b'dieved: this is part two; and bent ones are kosher l'chatchila: this is part three. And afterward he wrote: nevertheless it is a preferred mitzvah to seek one of a ram, to remember the Binding of Yitzchak, and that is outside the count of the three he took first, for the three are from the side of law, but of a ram does not enter at all into the category of law except from the side of preferred mitzvah and extra carefulness. And so wrote Beis Yosef. And that the Tur wrote first, "A shofar fit to blow with (how so? That it be of a ram and bent, etc.") in this he also included preferred mitzvah in the general rule; and what he wrote afterward, "or that he has no ram and bent," he said by way of "not only this but also that": not only if he has no ram (obviously it is permitted b'dieved) but even if he has no bent; and therefore he concluded at the end: and l'chatchila we require that it be bent, even of goats. And nevertheless it is a preferred mitzvah to seek one of a ram, behold they are not equal, and one should not err and say that this "nevertheless it is a preferred mitzvah" is the third part, as some wrote, for that is not so: if so he should not have said "and nevertheless a preferred mitzvah etc." but "and a preferred mitzvah etc.," since this is one of the parts; rather certainly he taught us that even though this is not from the side of law, nevertheless it is from the side of fine choice for one who can. And so it is implied explicitly in the Asheri, who wrote: and l'chatchila the mitzvah is with bent ones, and with all bent ones they blow, etc. And that R' Abahu said, "Why do they blow with a shofar of a ram?" he did not take "ram" specifically, for other bent ones are also permitted l'chatchila; rather he stated the reason of the custom: and thus he meant: why do they blow with a shofar and seek after it to make a preferred mitzvah (so that He remember the Binding of Yitzchak) end quote. And it appears to me that not only of a ram, but likewise all kinds of sheep are included in this general rule to recall the Binding of Yitzchak; for in siman 583 he wrote that they eat a sheep's head, namely as a remembrance of the Binding of Yitzchak. And with this the words of the Rambam are fine to me, who wrote as follows: The shofar with which they blow, whether on Rosh Hashana or in the Jubilee, is the horn of sheep, bent; and all shofaros are invalid except the horn of a sheep (end quote. And it is difficult: why did he need to mention "except the horn of a sheep," since he already mentioned it first) he should have said "and all other shofaros are invalid"; and further, why did he not also mention in the latter clause the bent sheep? Rather it appears to me that he said two things: first he said l'chatchila it should be a bent sheep; and afterward he said b'dieved the rest are invalid except the horn of a sheep, namely even straight, which is kosher b'dieved; and in this "sheep" he included all species such as goats and ibex (and there is a proof for this from the verse "ox, sheep of lambs and sheep of goats; deer and gazelle and yachmur," and yachmur means ibex; and just as he counted ram after mentioning sheep of lambs, so he also mentioned yachmur which is in the category of goats, and its name proves it, for they call it Ashtenbak as Rashi explains, namely like goats, whose males are called bak; so too these are called Shtenbok, for its growth is on the rock which is stone). So wrote Beis Yosef, and so it is implied in the name of Kol Bo. And if you say that everything is included in the category of sheep like the Rambam (and even though Kesef Mishneh wrote that this is not in the implication of the Rambam's words and explained another way) what appears to me I wrote; and it is proven: for otherwise why did he not mention anything of the law of a ram as a remembrance of the Binding of Yitzchak? Rather certainly all these are included in the category of ram, which is a sheep, and he came only to exclude a cow and other animals. And if so the Rambam's method is like the method of the Rosh and the Tur; except that the most meticulous of the meticulous custom to seek specifically one of a ram, for it is more of a remembrance of the ram of Yitzchak, as appears to me.
