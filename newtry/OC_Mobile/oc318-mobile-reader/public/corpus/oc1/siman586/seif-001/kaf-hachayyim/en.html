1) [Seif 1] The shofar of Rosh Hashanah, etc. This excludes a communal fast, on which they also sound a shofar, for there it need not be from a ram or curved. Mishna Berurah, s.k. 1. Alternatively, it excludes Yom Kippur of the Jubilee year, regarding which there is an opinion that it must be straight, as explained in the Beit Yosef.
2) Ibid. Its mitzvah is with a ram's horn, and curved. It should be from a ram so that the Binding of Yitzchak may be remembered for us, since a ram was offered in his stead, and curved so that they will bend their hearts in prayer. Tur, from the Gemara; Magen Avraham, s.k. 1. A ram is from 13 months and 1 day old and upward, but before that age it is not called a ram, as stated in chapter 1 of tractate Parah; see there.
3) Ibid. Its mitzvah is with a ram's horn, and curved. The same applies to every type of sheep, meaning even a female, for they are included in this category so as to recall the Binding of Yitzchak. Indeed, in siman 583 it is written that one eats the head of a sheep, and that is in remembrance of the Binding of Yitzchak. Nevertheless, those who are meticulous customarily seek specifically a ram's horn, since a male more closely resembles the ram of Yitzchak. Taz, s.k. 1; Eliyah Rabbah, letter 1; Mateh Ephraim, letter 1; Mishna Berurah, s.k. 2.
4) Ibid. Its mitzvah is with a ram's horn, and curved. Although the Shulchan Aruch mentions these two requirements together, they are nevertheless not equal. A curved horn is part of the primary mitzvah l'chatchila, and one does not fulfill the obligation l'chatchila with a straight horn. By contrast, a ram's horn is not called a l'chatchila requirement, but rather the most choice manner of performing the mitzvah. Taz, ibid. However, Maamar Mordechai, letter 1, wrote that although it is evident from the words of Maran in the Beit Yosef, in his explanation of the Tur and the poskim, that the requirement of a ram's horn is only the most choice manner of performing the mitzvah and does not invalidate even l'chatchila so long as the horn is curved, nevertheless, the plain implication of his words here in the Shulchan Aruch is that all other shofaros are valid only b'dieved; see there. This is also the view of the Bach, that other shofaros which are not from a ram, even when curved, are valid only b'dieved; see there. A practical difference arises where one has a straight ram's horn and a curved horn that is not from a ram. According to the Taz, the curved horn that is not from a ram takes precedence, since being from a ram is only the most choice manner of performing the mitzvah, whereas a straight horn is valid only b'dieved. According to the Bach and Maamar Mordechai, the straight ram's horn and the curved horn that is not from a ram are equal, since each is valid only b'dieved. This also appears from what was written above in letter 2, that each one possesses one advantage. Yet since according to the Bach and Maamar Mordechai they are equal, while according to the Taz the curved horn that is not from a ram is valid even l'chatchila, the curved horn should be given precedence, since this accords with all opinions. So too wrote Mishna Berurah, s.k. 5, that the conclusion of most poskim is that a curved horn is preferable; see there.
5) All of this applies only on Rosh Hashanah itself, when the time for sounding has arrived and they are deliberating as to which shofar is more fit to sound. Before Rosh Hashanah, however, when the obligation of sounding has not yet taken effect, one may purchase a goat's horn even though a ram's horn is also available, but it must be purchased for a high price, and he knows that there will be no ram's horn in his city on Rosh Hashanah. Even so, there is no obligation to purchase the ram's horn for a high price. Rather, it is governed by the law of enhancing a mitzvah up to one-third, but no more, as explained below regarding the lulav. Bach; Ateres Zekeinim. Mateh Ephraim, letter 1; Mishna Berurah, s.k. 5. See below siman 656 and our words there, with G-d's help.
6) Ibid. B'dieved, all shofaros are valid. For example, those of ibexes and goats. Tur; Levush. An ibex is a species of wild animal, as stated in Parashas Re'eh, 'the wild ox, the ibex, and the antelope,' which Onkelos translates, 'the wild ox and the ibex.' The Ran likewise concluded in the chapter Ra'uhu Beis Din, unlike the Aruch, who explained it as a female sheep; see there. This is also explicitly proven in chapter 1 of Kilayim, Mishnah 6, unlike the Aruch; see there. Nevertheless, if one has the horn of a male goat or a female goat, he should give it precedence over the horn of an ibex or other wild animal, for it is closer to the species called seh, as it is written, 'a seh from the sheep and a seh from the goats,' and there is at least some remembrance of the Binding of Yitzchak. Mishna Berurah, s.k. 4. One can further say that this is because goats are fit to be offered upon the Altar, unlike wild animals.
7) Ibid. B'dieved, all shofaros are valid. The Rambam, at the beginning of chapter 1 of Hilchos Shofar, wrote that all shofaros are invalid except for the horn of sheep, and everyone challenged him regarding this. Tur. However, the Orchos Chaim wrote: It is possible that the Rambam's intent was only to exclude the horn of a cow, while the horns of a goat and a male goat are included in the category of sheep, and likewise the horns of ibexes; end quote. The Beit Yosef cited his words. So too wrote the Kesef Mishneh there, although he wrote that this is not the implication of the Rambam's words. The Taz, s.k. 1, cited his words; see there. The sefer Nechpeh BaKesef, folio 184, wrote that in a place where they accepted the rulings of the Rambam, if they do not have a curved sheep's horn they should not recite a blessing over other shofaros; see there. Birkei Yosef, letter 1, cited his words and wrote that this may be questioned. Since there are those who explain the Rambam's words as holding that other shofaros are valid, they may recite a blessing. Although Maran's explanation, and that of those who agree with him, regarding the Rambam's view appears correct, nevertheless the matter has not left the realm of doubt. Since the other poskim plainly hold that one recites a blessing, and there is at least some doubt that the Rambam also holds so, as the other commentators explained him, this is not included in their acceptance to rely upon the Rambam; end quote.
8) Ibid. It is a greater mitzvah with curved horns than with straight ones. This means that the rule is not limited specifically to a ram's horn. Rather, regarding other domesticated and wild animals as well, it is a greater mitzvah with curved horns than with straight ones. This is for the reason written above in letter 2, that a curved horn possesses an advantage; see there.
9) Ibid. The horn of a cow is invalid, etc. The reason is stated explicitly in the Mishnah, because it is called a keren. Although all shofaros are called keren, as it is written, 'And it shall be when the ram's horn is drawn out, when you hear the sound of the shofar,' the Gemara states that all other shofaros are called both keren and shofar, whereas that of a cow is called keren but is not called shofar. Beit Yosef; Levush.
10) Ibid. The horn of a cow is invalid, etc. The same applies to the horn of an ox. Levush; Olas Shabbos, letter 1; Magen Avraham, s.k. 2; Eshel Avraham, letter 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, letter 1; Chayei Adam, rule 140, letter 2; Mateh Ephraim, letter 1; Mishna Berurah, s.k. 6.
11) Ibid. The horn of a cow is invalid, etc. The horns of the buffalo are invalid for use as a shofar, for the buffalo that we call by that name is the wild ox, which we rule is a domesticated animal; see Yoreh De'ah, siman 80. If so, it is obvious that it is invalid. She'eilas Yaavetz, volume 1, siman 50; Shaarei Teshuvah, letter 3. This is the species called in Arabic jamus. So wrote Ibn Ezra on the Twelve Prophets, Amos 5:22; see there. His words were cited in Zevachei Tzedek on Yoreh De'ah, siman 28, letter 16. See there, where he brought further support for this; see there.
12) Ibid. The horn of a cow is invalid in every circumstance. This applies even b'dieved or when one has no other shofar. It goes without saying that the horns of most wild animals, which are not hollow but are one solid bone, cannot be used to fulfill the obligation even b'dieved or when one has no other horn. Levush. See below, letter 18.
13) A person sounding the shofar came with a curved horn in his hand to sound, prepared it in the manner of those who sound the shofar, and held it ready to recite the blessing and sound it. In the meantime, a Jew emerged holding a ram's horn. The designation of the first shofar, through his having prepared and readied it, is of no consequence, and he should sound the ram's horn. The same applies whenever a more choice means of performing a mitzvah becomes available to him before the mitzvah takes effect. Birkei Yosef, letter 2.
14) Ibid. Likewise, the horns of most wild animals, etc. For example, those of the re'em and the gazelle. Beit Yosef in the name of the Ran; Mishna Berurah, s.k. 7. As to whether the re'em is a kosher species, see Pri Chadash and Mateh Yosef, who wrote that it is a kosher wild animal, unlike Knesset HaGedolah in Hagahos Beit Yosef, who wrote as a simple matter that it is a non-kosher species; see there. So too wrote Magen Avraham, s.k. 3. Knesset HaGedolah himself also retracted, as written in the summaries of his responsa on Yoreh De'ah, printed at the end of the sefer Kol Ben Levi, siman 179; see there. Machazik Berachah, letter 2, cited this.
15) Ibid. Likewise, the horns of most wild animals, etc. In Lashon HaKodesh they are not called shofar, for only hollow horns that have a core inside, whose outer shell peels away from the core, are called shofar. They are invalid like the horn of a cow. Tur; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, letter 1. Even the horns of the offspring of a female goat upon which a gazelle mated are invalid, and it goes without saying that the offspring of a gazelle upon which a male goat mated is invalid. However, the horn of a hybrid produced from two valid species, such as one born from ibexes and sheep, is valid. Siddur Amudei Shamayim; Pis'chei Olam, letter 4.
16) Ibid., Rama's gloss: Likewise, a shofar from a non-kosher animal is invalid. Although we learned in Niddah, chapter 6, that any animal possessing horns possesses split hooves, indicating that a non-kosher animal has no horns, and thus how can a shofar from a non-kosher animal be found, Pri Chadash already wrote in this siman and in Yoreh De'ah, siman 80, that we find a dispute in the chapter Eilu Treifos, Chullin 59b, between the Sages and R' Dosa. According to the Sages, there are non-kosher domesticated and wild animals that possess horns. The Mishnah in Niddah and the discussion in the chapter Bameh Madlikin follow R' Dosa, whereas we rule like the Sages; see there. Mateh Yosef cited his words. So too wrote Olas Shabbos, letter 1. See Magen Avraham, s.k. 3. Be'er HaGolah wrote that the case is where a non-kosher animal gave birth to it, and that which emerges from a non-kosher animal is non-kosher, as we learned in the first chapter of Bechoros. Olas Shabbos cited his words there.
17) Ibid., Rama's gloss: Likewise, a shofar from a non-kosher animal is invalid. This is because it is stated, 'So that the Torah of Hashem shall be in your mouth,' meaning that everything used for the Torah of Hashem must be from that which is permitted in your mouth. Although it comes from a neveilah or another prohibition, it is nevertheless called 'in your mouth,' because its species is permitted in your mouth, as we say regarding tefillin and a Sefer Torah. The same applies to every sacred article, which must be from that which is permitted in your mouth. Levush; Ateres Zekeinim; Magen Avraham, s.k. 3. However, the Ran in the chapter Ra'uhu Beis Din wrote that the statement in the chapter Bameh Madlikin, folio 28, that only the hide of a kosher animal was deemed fit for sacred articles, and a shofar is also a sacred article since it comes for remembrance, is not a conclusive proof. Therefore, the matter requires further study; see there. The Beit Yosef and Olas Shabbos, letter 1, cited his words. Therefore, Olas Shabbos wrote there that if no other shofar is available, one should sound such a shofar without a blessing. So too wrote Mateh Ephraim, letter 3, and Mishna Berurah, s.k. 8.
18) If a valid shofar later becomes available to him, he should sound it as well without a blessing, because the invalidity of a shofar from a non-kosher animal is not entirely clear, and perhaps he already fulfilled the obligation through the shofar from the non-kosher animal. However, if he sounded a horn of an ox or cow, or the horns of wild animals that have no inner core, and a valid shofar later became available, he should sound it with a blessing, because those are certainly invalid. Therefore, even if he has no shofar other than one from an ox or cow, or the horns of such wild animals, he should not sound them at all. Mateh Ephraim, ibid.; Mishna Berurah, ibid. See above, letter 12.
