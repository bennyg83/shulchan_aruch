A ram. As a remembrance of the binding of Yitzchak, and bent so that they should bend their hearts before the Omnipresent.
A cow. The same applies to an ox, because their horns are called a keren and not a shofar.
From a non-kosher animal. R. Moshe Rivkash objects that a non-kosher domesticated animal has no horns, as stated in Niddah, chapter “Ba Siman.” He did not examine Chullin 59, where the statement refers to a wild animal. See there and Tosafos, s.v. “These are,” and likewise Rosh and Tur, Yoreh Deah 79, who write that horns exclude only a doubtful pig. Olas Shabbos writes that we do not rule according to the statement that only material fit for sacred service may be used, but he did not examine Shabbos 28. The reason is that the entire Torah is compared to tefillin, as stated in Kiddushin 35. It also appears to me that the re’em is kosher, because “te’o” is translated as “reima.” Moreover, it has horns, which according to one explanation are a sign of kashrus.
