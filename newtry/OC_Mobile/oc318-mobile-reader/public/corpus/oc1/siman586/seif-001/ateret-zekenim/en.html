The shofar of Rosh Hashanah — its mitzva is from a ram and not another horn, for every horn hints at the attribute of judgment which is kingship; only of a ram to hint at the ram of Isaac, for the reason that his portion was divided among them and then the rule of judgment turns to mercy and awakens the supernal shofar which is binah to turn from judgment to mercy, from which emerged the voice which is Torah complete of fire, water, and wind (Zohar Parashat Emor p. 289). And it is a mitzva to seek a ram's shofar to remember the binding of Isaac (Tur); and even though both are not before us, nevertheless it is a mitzva to seek a ram's shofar; and all this specifically on Rosh Hashanah itself; but before Rosh Hashanah, when the obligation of tekiah has not yet come upon him, he may buy a goat's shofar and need not buy a ram's shofar at great expense — rather he has the law of hidur mitzva up to a third and no more, as below regarding lulav (my teacher in Bach); and even a shofar from a carcass — nevertheless "in your mouth we read it" — from it is permitted in your mouth as we say regarding tefillin and a Torah scroll.
