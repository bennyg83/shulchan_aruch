(49) Cracked across its width. That is, around its circumference, and it comes through falling or a forceful blow.
(50) In its minority it is kosher. And it is not similar to cracked lengthwise, which he invalidated in seif 8 according to the first opinion, because there through the strength of the blast it can split further lengthwise, but not across the width.
(51) In its majority it is invalid unless, etc. For in this even if we reckon the minority as if it does not exist, and as if it was entirely cut off; nevertheless at least a measure of tekiah remained. And that excess should not be reckoned as an addition to invalidate thereby the whole shofar, as below in seif 11: for it is not called an addition except when it was initially a separate piece, which is not so here, for it never separated.
(52) There remained from the crack, etc. And if he glued it with glue, even if a measure of tekiah did not remain, it is also kosher, as we are lenient in seif 8 regarding cracked lengthwise when he glued it with glue [Eliyah Rabbah and Peri Megadim]. Nevertheless when there is another shofar it is better to blow with it (one reason: because of the view above in seif 8 that glue does not help, for it is considered not of its kind; and also according to R' Yehonatan that glue does not help in place of a crack).
(53) Toward his mouth. Specifically, for behold we view the cracked [part] as if cut off; and if a [sufficient] measure does not remain toward his mouth, if so he is blowing with a shofar that has no measure of tekiah.
(54) Namely four thumb-breadths. Of an average person; and one measures at the middle of the thumb and not at the tip of the thumb where it narrows; and that is the measure of a tefach.
(55) Even if it impedes the blast. For it is no worse than pierced and not sealed, which we permit above even when its sound changed.
(56) Even not toward his mouth. And even though this minority in his mouth interposes, and if so there is a separation between his mouth and the shofar; we are not concerned for this, for like-kind does not interpose, and we do not view it as if removed; and in a time of urgency it is possible one may rely on this view.
