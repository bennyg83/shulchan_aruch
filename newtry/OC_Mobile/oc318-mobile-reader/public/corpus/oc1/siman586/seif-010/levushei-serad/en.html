Seif katan 5 This raises no difficulty at all. For they asked there, and in Yevamos, why one fulfills the obligation with that of a non-Jew, since this too should be considered as though crushed away, for from the moment he lifts it he acquires it. They therefore had to answer that he does not intend to acquire it, and they did not answer that it has owners. Perforce, nevertheless, since the Jew intends to acquire it and keeps it under his responsibility, and becomes liable for accidents, it is considered his, just as chametz with respect to the prohibition of it being seen, where he transgresses if he is liable for its responsibility, above in siman 440. Therefore they had to answer that he does not intend to acquire it, meaning that he does not intend at all to steal it, but only wishes to blow it. For if he intends to steal it, it immediately stands under his responsibility. This is somewhat difficult, for it appears that even if he took it only to blow it and wishes to return it after the blowing, nevertheless, since it is without the non-Jew's consent, he is considered a robber with respect to being liable for its responsibility. If so, this means that the case is where the non-Jew permitted him to blow it, and if so the words of the Tur brought by the Taz in seif katan 3 are not resolved. This is easy to understand, but requires study.
