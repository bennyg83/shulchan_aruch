Se'if 3. But with that of avodah zarah of a non-Jew, and likewise with accessories of avodah zarah of a non-Jew, one should not blow; and if he blew with it he has fulfilled, etc. Marginal note: The reason is explained in the Talmud; that it is by force of [the principle] that mitzvos were not given in order to derive benefit. And behold, the words of the Baal HaMaor are well known, that he holds that rabbinic mitzvos were given in order to derive benefit. And from the words of Tosafos in the second chapter of Pesachim, in the sugya of Chizkiyah and R' Elazar, where it objects there from terumah, that we make an eruv for a nazir with wine, and Tosafos wrote there that there he does not go according to the view that we make an eruv only for a mitzvah matter, for mitzvos were not given in order to derive benefit, etc. And according to the Baal HaMaor's words it comes out well: for certainly even according to the view that we make an eruv only for a mitzvah matter, nevertheless even for a rabbinic mitzvah we make an eruv, for eruvin are rabbinic, and one way or the other; according to the Torah one does not need eruvin, and by rabbinic law this day itself is a mitzvah; and if so, a rabbinic mitzvah [was] given in order to derive benefit; necessarily he does not hold like the Razah. And Tosafos's words are astonishing: why must they say that he goes according to the view that we make an eruv for an optional matter; better they should have said that he goes according to the view that mitzvos were given in order to derive benefit, as R' Yochanan and Rava initially hold in tractate Rosh Hashanah (see there). And their core difficulty likewise is not difficult: one can say that if it were so that he holds we make an eruv only for a mitzvah matter, why did it cite "we make an eruv for a nazir with wine," etc.? It should have cited that we make an eruv for one who vowed benefit from something else, who is likewise permitted to another and forbidden in benefit to himself, and nevertheless we make an eruv; and from the fact that it did not cite so, it is proved that regarding something forbidden to him in benefit we do not make an eruv, and necessarily he holds we make an eruv for an optional matter and it is forbidden among forbidden benefits, and it comes out well; and examine this carefully with Hashem's help. And while speaking of this I will also mention what Maharsha asked regarding what it answers there "your harvest" implies two, and he asked: if so, regarding what it answered earlier concerning terumah that "your terumah" is written, it is likewise difficult that it should imply two. And in truth it comes out well: for Tosafos wrote earlier that for this reason a verse was needed regarding orlah for heating as a separate [case], for one might have thought that that is the manner of its destruction, etc. and if so, likewise here, if only "your terumah" had been written in the singular, since regarding terumah oil too applies, and that is the manner of its destruction; one might have thought that it permits only heating, for that is the manner of its destruction; therefore "your terumah" [plural] is written, and there are two broadenings to include other benefits as well, which is not so regarding chadash, which is only in produce, and in that [case] the manner of its destruction is not so; and if he destroys it by fire that would be "not in its manner," and permitted according to the Torah, therefore its law is one [single inclusion]; therefore it answers that [the plural] was needed for another matter; and examine carefully, and it comes out well with Hashem's help. And see in Magen Avraham s.k. 5 what he wrote: that even by way of theft it is avodah zarah of a Jew; and see what I wrote as a proof for this in my composition on Orach Chaim of the year 5609 (page 35 in the omissions and to the kuntres on "taking," he wondered) and it was already printed in my composition Shenos Chayim (see there); and examine carefully. And see in my composition Avodas Avodah on tractate Avodah Zarah, folio 54, in the omissions, what I wrote to resolve the approach of the Rosh and the Tur, may [peace] be upon them, with Hashem's help, and also to reject the reasoning from Rashi's explanation that I brought in Sefer HaChayim (see there); and examine carefully.
