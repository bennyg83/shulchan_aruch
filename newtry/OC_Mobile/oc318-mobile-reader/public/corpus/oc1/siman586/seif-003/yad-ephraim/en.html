(In Magen Avraham s.k. 6) When it was nullified before Yom Tov, etc. his words need clarification. And it appears to me that his intent is: initially he wrote to explain the words of Rama, that whatever is not in his hand is dichuy, and here it is not in his hand; and in truth even the first opinion does not dispute this, only that they hold that even if it was not nullified at all we do not say regarding it "its measure is crushed," since it is possible that it be nullified by a gentile; but if nullification were needed, they too admit regarding this, specifically when we know that it will be nullified before Yom Tov, for otherwise we would have to be concerned for dichuy. And this matter is necessary for the method of the Tur and those with him: for the Gemara brings the inquiry of Resh Lakish whether a lulav of an idol is permitted for a mitzvah, and says: where it is forbidden to a commoner, you need not ask; when you ask is where they planted it and afterward worshipped it according to the Rabbis, lest it be repulsive for the Most High; when Rav Dimi came he said: an asherah that was nullified is what is inquired for him (whether there is dichuy or there is no dichuy; and that same [type of] inquiry applies for R' Papa regarding covering and uncovering, etc.) that R' Papa himself inquires, perhaps it is specifically for stringency (see there). And behold according to the Tur and those with him it is apparently difficult: how did he write plainly to permit even without nullification, for even with nullification it is inquired whether its lulav is permitted for a mitzvah because it is repulsive, even though permitted to a commoner; and all the more so when forbidden to a commoner and he did not nullify. And one should not say that he holds that according to what Rav Dimi said regarding an asherah that was nullified it is inquired (he holds there is no [concern of] repulsive here) for nevertheless it is proven that we require nullification, and therefore he holds that there is dichuy; and according to the Tur who holds that we do not require nullification, if so there is no dichuy here at all, for it was never deferred; and so Tosafos asked in Sukkah folio 32 on this method. And one must say that he holds that certainly Rav Dimi explains Resh Lakish's inquiry of an asherah that was nullified, and he holds mitzvos were given for benefit, and therefore before nullification it is dichuy, for it is forbidden in benefit, and he inquires whether there is dichuy regarding mitzvos, which is not so for the Tur and those with him, who hold like what Rava said in Rosh Hashana and in Sukkah, that if he took it he has fulfilled [his obligation] because mitzvos were not given for benefit, if so there is no dichuy here at all, and even without nullification he has fulfilled. (But one should not explain that Rav Dimi holds that so long as he did not nullify we say "its measure is crushed" even though it is of a gentile and it is possible to nullify it, for if so whence does the Tur rule not like him, since we do not find anyone who disputes him on this; rather certainly the Tur holds that everyone admits on this, only that Rav Dimi's reason is because he holds mitzvos were given for benefit, and we find Amoraim who dispute this, and we hold like Rava.) And accordingly it is fine that the Tur explains Resh Lakish's inquiry like Rav Dimi, and he holds there is no concern of repulsive at all, only that he requires nullification because of the prohibition of benefit, and we hold that mitzvos were not given for benefit. And one should not say that the Tur holds that Resh Lakish's inquiry is like the first wording because of repulsive, only that Rava disputes and holds there is no concern of repulsive at all; this is not so, for whence that Rava disputes? Perhaps Rava holds like Rav Dimi's wording that regarding an asherah that was nullified we inquire; rather certainly in truth the Tur holds to rule as law like Rav Dimi's wording that we are not concerned for repulsive, only regarding that he holds "a mitzvah was given for benefit" we do not hold like him. And now thus is the order of Magen Avraham's words: after he explained the Mordechai that Rama brought (that here it is "not in his hand," since it is not in his hand to nullify it, etc.) he wrote that this requires further study in Yoreh Deah: that is, regarding both the Tur and Rama; why did they rule there that there is no dichuy, for according to what he ruled here, all of them necessarily hold that Resh Lakish's inquiry was stated regarding dichuy (whether there is dichuy for leniency or not) and they should have ruled also there so; and without the words of the poskim here we would have said that they hold the main [view] is like Resh Lakish's first wording, that he inquired regarding repulsive, but regarding dichuy the matter of R' Papa comes as its plain sense both for leniency and for stringency, and therefore he ruled there regarding covering plainly that there is no dichuy; but after they ruled here that if they blew with a shofar of an idol he has fulfilled [his obligation] even without nullification, they necessarily hold that Resh Lakish's inquiry is as Rav Dimi said, if so it requires further study why he ruled in Yoreh Deah that certainly there is no dichuy, and he should have said he should cover it only out of doubt, etc. And upon this he wrote that the view of the Tur and the poskim is as Tosafos wrote in Avodah Zarah: that Resh Lakish's inquiry is only l'chatchila; and the difficulties of Tosafos in Sukkah are answered, meaning: forever he holds like the first wording that Resh Lakish's inquiry is regarding repulsive, but regarding dichuy the matter of R' Papa comes as its plain sense that there is no dichuy even for leniency; and therefore he properly ruled in Yoreh Deah that there is no dichuy; and nevertheless what they ruled here is fine; that without nullification it is also permitted, and if so dichuy does not apply; and from the reason of repulsive one need not be concerned, for repulsive is only l'chatchila alone; and all the difficulties of Tosafos in Sukkah are answered (including also this difficulty they asked: if also without nullification [it is valid], then what did he inquire regarding dichuy) for this is not so, for one may say that Rav Dimi holds like Rav Yehudah in Rosh Hashana who holds mitzvos were given for benefit, and we do not hold those. And upon this Magen Avraham wrote: and if so the law is thus, etc. and l'chatchila he should not take even after it was nullified because it is repulsive, etc., meaning: since we now stand like the first wording, that this inquiry deals even where it is permitted to a commoner, nevertheless l'chatchila he inquired because it is repulsive, if so even though it was nullified it is also repulsive; and so wrote Tosafos there that Resh Lakish deals even when it was nullified, and even so he inquires perhaps l'chatchila it is forbidden because it is repulsive.
