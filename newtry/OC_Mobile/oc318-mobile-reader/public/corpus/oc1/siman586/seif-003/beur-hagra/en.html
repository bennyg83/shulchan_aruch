Some are stringent. This follows Rabbeinu Tam’s resolution in Tosafos, and the discussion in Avodah Zarah 47a: “When Rav Dimi came, he said regarding an asheirah,” etc., where the question was not resolved. Ran there writes, however, that it was nevertheless resolved in Sukkah 33b, and that the object is forbidden only if it was worshipped on Yom Tov, in which case it was fit and then became disqualified. Yet the question there was not resolved. See what I wrote above in siman 646, seif 2, that Rambam maintains that even where an object was fit and then became disqualified, the question was resolved that permanent rejection does not apply. Tosafos, however, follow their own view that the question was never resolved, and that the case there is different because it remains within his power to repair it. See Tosafos there, 33a, s.v. “Its top was severed,” and 32a, at the end of s.v. “Regarding an asheirah”: “You might ask, let it resolve,” etc. According to Tosafos’ view, the ruling in siman 646, seif 2, applies specifically where it was blackened from the outset. Ran likewise holds this view, as explained above, and the Rama did not gloss there at all.
