(s.k. 3) From an animal; he wrote: as is stated in Niddah folio 51b: We learned: Whatever has horns has hooves; and there are [animals] that have hooves and do not have horns. And even though there are two signs of taharah (cloven hooves and chewing the cud) nevertheless we say in Chullin that if it has cloven hooves, that suffices, and one need not know whether it chews the cud, provided he recognizes a pig: for there is no non-kosher animal that has cloven hooves and does not chew the cud except a pig; and once he recognizes that it is not a pig, the sign of cloven hooves suffices. And since whatever has horns has cloven hooves, it has left the doubt of tumah; for a non-kosher [animal] has no cloven hooves except a pig, and a pig has no horns. If so, you do not find a horn from a non-kosher animal.
And he did not look in Chullin. Certainly he looked in Chullin; for R' Moshe Rivkash concluded: "And it implies there that it is [accepted by] everyone, as Tosafos wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Tam in Chullin folio 59" (end quote). Thus the source of his difficulty is Tosafos of Chullin; rather Magen Avraham meant that he did not look in Chullin in the Ran. And that Magen Avraham contradicted his own words and did not mention the Ran is because this law (that a shofar of a non-kosher animal is invalid) is the words of the Ran; so R' Moshe Rivkash's difficulty is against the Ran. Therefore he wrote that he did not look in his words in Chullin. And this is what is stated there: Our Rabbis taught: These are the signs of a wild animal to permit its fat and to require covering [of the blood]: whatever has horns and cloven hooves. R' Dosa says: If it has horns, certainly it also has cloven hooves; but if it has cloven hooves, one must inquire whether it has horns. And we conclude there that those horns must be different from the horns of a domestic animal; but regarding cloven hooves it is not mentioned in the Talmud whether they must be different. And Tosafos wrote in the name of Rivam that the cloven hooves need not be different. And the first Tanna requires two things: horns, which are a sign that it is a wild animal, and cloven hooves to remove it from the category of a non-kosher wild animal. And R' Dosa holds that distinctive horns alone suffice, for it is certainly a kosher wild animal. And Tosafos wrote that according to this, the Mishnah of Niddah mentioned above necessarily comes only like R' Dosa and not like the first Tanna; for according to the first Tanna, even if it has distinctive horns one must know that it has cloven hooves, and we do not say that it certainly has cloven hooves; yet in the Mishnah of Niddah it teaches "whatever has horns has cloven hooves." Further they wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Tam according to the conclusion of their words: that the cloven hooves mentioned in the beraisa as well are distinctive cloven hooves different from those of a domestic animal; and according to the first Tanna one of them suffices to recognize that it is a kosher wild animal (either distinctive horns or distinctive cloven hooves; and what the first Tanna wrote "whatever has horns and cloven hooves") the vav of "and cloven hooves" that the first Tanna said is a disjunctive vav, as if he said "horns or cloven hooves." And R' Dosa is more stringent and said: whatever has distinctive horns certainly has distinctive cloven hooves; but if it has distinctive cloven hooves, that does not remove it from the doubt of being a domestic animal, and you must know whether it has horns to establish it as a wild animal. And according to this, neither the first Tanna nor R' Dosa spoke of signs that remove from a presumption of tumah; and one can say that everyone holds that with horns alone it leaves the presumption of tumah. And so Tosafos concluded, and this is their language: "And now the Mishnah of Niddah mentioned above comes according to everyone, for the Sages concede: whatever has horns has cloven hooves (that is, split and parted") end quote. And Maharsha wrote, and this is his language: "And know that according to this, horns and cloven hooves there (meaning in tractate Niddah) refer to any horns and cloven hooves (even if not distinctive), and regarding tumah and taharah; and this is what it means: whatever has horns, even of a domestic animal, has split cloven hooves and is tahor" (end quote. And he is right; for go and see) also if one says that the Mishnah of Niddah deals with distinctive horns and cloven hooves: how then did Tosafos write that the Mishnah of Niddah comes according to everyone? It could not come like the first Tanna, for the first Tanna holds that distinctive cloven hooves alone suffice. And anyway it is difficult: if the Mishnah deals with distinctive horns and cloven hooves, how does the end teach "and there are [animals] that have cloven hooves and do not have horns," and it is possible that it is non-kosher? For a non-kosher animal has no cloven hooves except a pig, and a pig's cloven hooves are not distinctive; and if so, since it has distinctive cloven hooves it has left the doubt of a pig and is certainly tahor. Therefore the Mishnah of Niddah must deal with cloven hooves that are not distinctive, and similarly those horns are not distinctive, and then it properly comes according to everyone. And according to this, per Rivam's explanation, Rivkash's difficulty does not arise; for according to Rivam the Mishnah of Niddah necessarily comes like R' Dosa. But according to the first Tanna we require two things: distinctive horns to remove the doubt that it is a domestic animal, and cloven hooves that are not distinctive to remove the doubt of tumah. If so, we find according to the first Tanna horns of a non-kosher [animal] if it has no cloven hooves. And if so, one can say that the Ran ruled like the first Tanna, and there is no difficulty at all. Therefore R' Moshe Rivkash concluded "and it implies there that it is [accepted by] everyone, as Tosafos wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Tam," etc. it implies that R' Moshe Rivkash took the main [view] like Rabbeinu Tam and asks well (not like what the Peles wrote in siman 79, who attributed to R' Moshe Rivkash an intention that does not appear so from his language (see there). And on this Magen Avraham wrote "and he did not look in Chullin") meaning in the Ran in Chullin, as above: that the Ran in Chullin brought only Rivam's explanation and did not bring Rabbeinu Tam's explanation at all. And if so, the Ran according to his own method is fine, and there is no difficulty at all.
And what Magen Avraham wrote "and so it is in the Rosh and Tur Yoreh De'ah" means: that also the Rama, who brought the Ran's words regarding a shofar of a non-kosher animal, is fine, for we too hold like Rivam's explanation; for they wrote there that horns only remove from the doubt of a pig; implying that even though it has horns, so that it is certainly not a pig, nevertheless it is possible that it is another non-kosher animal. And what Magen Avraham wrote "and he did not look," etc., refers to a wild animal, meaning the Mishnah of Niddah refers to a wild animal. And he came to resolve the Peri Chadash's difficulty in siman 80 at least against the Shulchan Aruch: he asked, since according to Rivam's explanation Rabbi stated a plain Mishnah in Niddah like R' Dosa, why did the poskim rule like the first Tanna (see there). Therefore Magen Avraham wrote that he holds that the Mishnah of Niddah that teaches "whatever has horns," etc., refers to a wild animal and deals with distinctive horns: that certainly has cloven hooves (which is what R' Dosa deals with) and it is a kosher wild animal; but if it has horns that are not distinctive, that was not mentioned either in R' Dosa's words or in the Mishnah as certainly having cloven hooves; rather it is possible that it has no cloven hooves and is non-kosher. And with this it is fine that we hold like the plain Mishnah of Niddah and like R' Dosa. And what is written in Yoreh De'ah siman 79 is fine: that horns only remove from the doubt of a pig, but it is possible that it is non-kosher. And what is written in Yoreh De'ah siman 80: a kosher wild animal; its fat is permitted, etc., and the Sages gave signs from tradition in their horns: if they are split, it is certainly a kosher wild animal. And the Shach wrote, and this is his language: when it chews the cud and has a cloven hoof it is certainly kosher; but it is obvious that the sign of being split does not indicate that it is kosher (end quote). And according to Magen Avraham's words this is not so, and the words of the Shulchan Aruch siman 80 are according to their plain sense: if it has split horns, that alone suffices that it is certainly a kosher wild animal, like R' Dosa and like the plain Mishnah of Niddah. And anyway the Shach's words there are imprecise in what he wrote that we require two things (chewing the cud and a cloven hoof) for a hoof alone suffices even according to the Shach's method, since there is no non-kosher [animal] that has a cloven hoof except a pig; and since it has horns it has left the doubt of a pig, and everything comes out correctly.
And what Eliyah Rabbah wrote, that we do not hold, etc. his intent is to challenge the Rama who ruled like this [view] of the Ran. For this is the language of the Ran: It is possible that specifically [shofaros] of kosher [animals are kosher], but non-kosher ones are not; for we say in the chapter "With what may one kindle," folio 28: R' Yosef taught: Only a kosher animal was made fit for the labor of Heaven; and a shofar too is a labor of Heaven, for we say in Rosh Hashanah folio 26a one reason that a cow's horn is invalid for a shofar (that a prosecutor does not become an advocate) so as not to mention the sin of the [golden] calf, for a cow is with the calf; for for that reason the Kohen Gadol does not serve inside in golden garments, so as not to mention the sin of the golden calf. And it objects: but the Kohen Gadol does serve in golden garments outside, and a shofar too is outside; and it answers: a shofar too, since it comes for remembrance (to bring up the remembrance of Israel before their Father in Heaven) is like inside (thus the Talmud compares blowing the shofar to the service inside; therefore we require that it be of a kosher [animal], for it is a labor of Heaven). Nevertheless it is not a complete proof, etc. (end quote). And even though the Ran left it as a doubt, the Rama ruled stringently to invalidate [one] from a non-kosher animal. And on this Eliyah Rabbah asked: the Ran's reason to invalidate is because R' Yosef taught "only [kosher animals] were made fit," etc. yet we do not hold like R' Yosef in this, since a verse was needed regarding tefillin that they be written on the hide of a kosher animal, as it is written "in order that the Torah of Hashem be in your mouth," and we expound "from what is permitted in your mouth"; and if it were so that we hold "only [kosher animals] were made fit for the labor," etc., why do we need the verse?
He did not look in Shabbos folio 21, where the Talmud asks regarding "only [kosher animals] were made fit" and says [it is] for tefillin. And it objects: regarding tefillin it is written explicitly "in order that the Torah," etc. (And Tosafos explained that this derivation was obvious and did not need to teach it; and it concludes [that it is] for the straps of tefillin, that they be of kosher hide. And Rashi explained that these [straps] do not derive from the verse "in order that the Torah," etc., for "Torah" is not written regarding them; and R' Yosef needed to teach us that since it is a labor of Heaven we require that they be of kosher [animals]. It is thus explicit that even R' Yosef who said "only [kosher animals] were made fit," etc., still requires the derivation of "in order that the Torah," etc. And for this reason he proved that R' Yosef necessarily does not deal with the hide for writing tefillin, for [that] was not needed, as above.)
And the reason that [all the Torah] was compared, etc. his intent is to resolve Eliyah Rabbah's difficulties and where his challenge goes: since we already know that only [kosher animals] were made fit, etc., why do we need the verse "in order that [the Torah] be," etc.? To this Magen Avraham wrote: "And the reason that [all the Torah] was compared," etc., for from where did R' Yosef himself know that only [kosher animals] were made fit, etc.? For it does not appear that it is a law to Moshe from Sinai. And also, to resolve Eliyah Rabbah's difficulty one must say that it is not a law to Moshe from Sinai, but rather since the verse wrote "in order that [the Torah] be," etc., and regarding writing tefillin we require a kosher [hide], from this we learn the entire Torah; for anyway we compare the entire Torah to tefillin regarding a positive commandment that is time-bound, from which women are exempt, as is stated in Kiddushin. And in the sefer Chemed Moshe he was astonished at Magen Avraham's words above and wrote that his words require great further study: for the comparison of the entire Torah to tefillin is from what is written "and it shall be a sign upon your hand" (that is tefillin) and it concludes "in order that the Torah of Hashem be," etc. that is the entire Torah; and since they are written in one verse we compare the Torah to tefillin. However, one only needs to learn the entire Torah from tefillin regarding a positive commandment that is time-bound; but regarding requiring a kosher hide, it is written in the Torah itself, for we expound from "in your mouth" (from what is permitted in your mouth) and the word "in your mouth" is written regarding "the Torah of Hashem" thus far his words. And in my humble opinion Magen Avraham's intent is not that we learn regarding the entire Torah that we require kosher [materials] from tefillin; rather his intent is: even though one might have thought that the Talmud's "in order that the Torah be," etc., refers only to writing the passages of tefillin and the like, regarding which "Torah" is written, as Rashi explained; nevertheless according to the conclusion the intent of the verse "in order that the Torah of Hashem be," etc., is: whatever is the Torah of Hashem, whatever mitzvah it may be, even if that mitzvah has no writing at all, nevertheless we require "in your mouth" from what is permitted in your mouth. Therefore even the straps of tefillin, which have no writing, nevertheless we require from what is permitted in your mouth. And a proof for this: we compare in Kiddushin regarding a positive commandment that is time-bound (that women are exempt) from tefillin, from what is written tefillin and Torah in one verse, namely "and they shall be a sign upon your hands" (that is tefillin) concluding "in order that the Torah be," etc. that is the entire Torah. Now are women exempt from a positive commandment that is time-bound only from a positive commandment that has writing in it? That is certainly not so; rather they are exempt from every positive commandment that is time-bound. Therefore all mitzvos are included in the phrase "in order that the Torah be," etc., even mitzvos that have no writing, and all are compared to tefillin to exempt women if they are positive commandments that are time-bound. And since "in order that the Torah be," etc., includes even mitzvos that have no writing, therefore even the straps of tefillin require the hide of a kosher animal. And this is the intent of the answerer in Shabbos: "It was needed only for the straps" for even though the derivation "in order that the Torah be," etc., was obvious, as Tosafos wrote above, nevertheless one might have thought that the verse's intent is specifically a mitzvah that has writing (namely the tefillin passage and the like) and we would still not know that straps require a kosher [hide]; R' Yosef teaches us that only [kosher animals] were made fit, etc., and reveals to us that the meaning of the verse is "in order that the Torah be," etc. (whatever is the Torah of Hashem, even if it has no writing, and even straps) must be from what is permitted in your mouth. And with this Eliyah Rabbah's difficulty is resolved: for if we hold "only [kosher animals] were made fit," etc., why the verse "in order that the Torah be," etc., as above? For if "in order that the Torah be," etc., had not been written, we would not know that only [kosher animals] were made fit; rather, since "in order that the Torah be," etc., is written, and R' Yosef revealed to us that the meaning of the verse is: whatever mitzvah it may be, even if it has no writing, nevertheless we require from what is permitted in your mouth; even straps. And this is Magen Avraham's intent in what he wrote "and the reason that the entire Torah was compared to tefillin as is stated in Kiddushin" meaning: the reason that straps, even though they have no writing, are also included in "in order that the Torah be," etc. and a proof for this is that the entire Torah was compared to tefillin regarding a positive commandment that is time-bound, even mitzvos that have no writing. And if so, one must necessarily say that "in order that the Torah be," etc., includes every mitzvah. One can further say that Magen Avraham's intent is as in the simple understanding: that the entire Torah was compared to tefillin, and from this we know that only [kosher animals] were made fit, etc. for the main verse comes for the mitzvah of tefillin, and "in order that the Torah be," etc., is giving a reason for the mitzvah of tefillin, as the Ramban wrote (that through tefillin you remember the Exodus from Egypt, as written at the end of the verse) and then you will know that Hashem is your Master who redeemed you from the house of bondage and you must listen to His voice. If so, presumably the word "in your mouth," which we expound as from what is permitted in your mouth, does not refer to "the Torah of Hashem," which is only giving a reason; rather one should more [readily] learn that it refers to the beginning (to the body of the mitzvah, namely tefillin) and it refers to the entire mitzvah of tefillin, even to the straps, for all is included in "and it shall be a sign upon your hand." And this R' Yosef teaches us, and from him we learn for the entire Torah that only [kosher animals] were made fit, etc. But the first explanation that I wrote appears to me the main one.
It also appears to me that a re'em is kosher, etc. he intended to disagree with Eliyah Rabbah, who wrote from what Tosafos wrote in Rosh Hashanah folio 26, s.v. "except," etc., regarding what we learned there: All shofaros are kosher except of a cow. And Tosafos wrote, and this is their language: Why did it not also teach "except of a re'em," etc.? And perhaps they are not hollow and are not fit for a shofar; until here his language. And Eliyah Rabbah wrote on this, and this is his language: And if the re'em is a non-kosher species, it is proved from their words that other non-kosher [animals] are kosher for a shofar (end quote). (And in truth according to his words he should have asked against the Ran who wrote that a shofar from a non-kosher animal is invalid; and afterward he wrote as Tosafos wrote, that for this reason the Mishnah did not teach "except of a re'em," because it is not fit for a shofar since it is not hollow (see there). And one must necessarily say that a re'em is kosher, from the fact that he did not say that anyway it is invalid since it is non-kosher, as Eliyah Rabbah inferred from Tosafos. Or one can say: since the Ran himself is not clear that a shofar of a non-kosher [animal] is invalid, therefore he offered an answer that certainly suffices, because it is not hollow; if so one can say the same regarding Tosafos.) Therefore Magen Avraham wrote that it is certainly kosher, and one cannot infer anything from Tosafos.
For we translate "and te'o," "reima." Therefore this is a scribe's error: for upon "te'o" the translator translates "torabla"; and it should read "and dishon and reima," for so the translator rendered upon "ako and dishon and yael" "and reima." And so too wrote in the sefer Chemed Moshe. And likewise Tosafos in Zevachim folio 113b, s.v. "orzila," etc., proved from the aforementioned Targum that a re'em is a kosher wild animal and its fat is permitted (see there).
And according to one explanation it is a sign of taharah. That is, according to Rabbeinu Tam's explanation above: for Tosafos wrote that according to Rabbeinu Tam the Mishnah of Niddah "whatever has horns has cloven hooves" comes according to everyone; and necessarily the Mishnah of Niddah deals even with horns of a domestic animal that are not distinctive, as Maharsha wrote and as I proved above. If so, even if you say that a re'em does not have distinctive horns but only domestic-animal horns, nevertheless it is proved that it is kosher since it has horns (therefore it also has cloven hooves and has left the category of tumah) only that there remains a doubt of a pig, and horns remove from the doubt of a pig, as above. And even though we hold like Rivam's explanation, and according to him the Mishnah of Niddah comes only like R' Dosa; and according to what Magen Avraham wrote above, the Mishnah of Niddah refers to a wild animal and deals with distinctive horns, as above (if so, if a re'em does not have distinctive horns there is no proof from the Mishnah of Niddah that it is kosher) nevertheless, since according to Rabbeinu Tam it is proved that it is kosher, and according to Rivam there is no proof that it is non-kosher, presumably Rivam also concedes that it is kosher; and especially since there is a proof from the Targum that it is kosher.
