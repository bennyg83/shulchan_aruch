Our rabbis taught: these are signs of chayah to permit its milk and require covering. Whatever has horns and hooves. R' Dosa says: if it has horns certainly it also has hooves; but if it has hooves one must investigate if it has horns. And we conclude there that these horns must be different from animal horns; but from hooves it was not mentioned in gemara if they need to be different. Tosafot wrote in the name of Ribam that hooves need not be different. Tanna kama requires two things: horns that are a sign it is chayah, and hooves to exclude tame chayah. R' Dosa holds that with different horns alone it suffices, for certainly it is a pure chayah. Tosafot wrote that per this the mishnah in Niddah above can only go like R' Dosa and not like tanna kama — for per tanna kama even if it has different horns one must know it has hooves, and we do not say certainly it has hooves; in mishnah Niddah it teaches whatever has horns has hooves. They further wrote in the name of Rabbenu Tam per their conclusion: the hooves the baraita mentioned are also hooves different from animal hooves; per tanna kama one suffices to recognize it is pure chayah — either different horns or different hooves; and what tanna kama said whatever has horns and hooves — the vav of ve-talafim that tanna kama said is the vav that divides, as if he said horns or hooves. R' Dosa is stringent and says whatever has different horns certainly has different hooves; but if it has different hooves this does not remove it from safek of animal and one must know if it has horns to establish it as chayah. Per this neither tanna kama nor R' Dosa spoke of signs that remove from presumption of tame, and one may say all hold that with horns alone it leaves presumption of tame. And so Tosafot concluded: and now the mishnah in Niddah above goes like all — the Rabbis agree whatever has horns has hooves, meaning split hooves — end of his words. Maharsha wrote: know that per this horns and hooves there (meaning in tractate Niddah) deal with all horns and hooves (even if not different), and regarding tame and pure; and this is what is taught whatever has horns, even of animal, has split hooves and is pure — end of his words. The law is with him that also in mishnah Niddah it deals with different horns and hooves; if so how did Tosafot write the mishnah in Niddah goes like all — it cannot go like tanna kama, for tanna kama holds different hooves suffice.
