And Bach wrote that it deals with idolatry of ownerless property; but if it has owners, even though he intended to acquire it he did not acquire it, and it is idolatry of a non-Jew, end quote. And it appears to us that we rule that a Jew does not annul idolatry of a non-Jew because of the decree lest he raise it for her and then annul it for her, and it becomes idolatry of a Jew, as is explained in Avodah Zarah 4, Mishna Berurah 12 — if so it is proven explicitly that even though it has owners it is called idolatry of a Jew. And so too in Tosafos Sukkah daf 31: if not, nothing would be difficult — for it should have said that it deals with idolatry that has an owner. And so too in Yevamos daf 104. And the reason: since the Jew is liable for it, it is called his; and so we find regarding chametz, and greater than this Bach wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 146: some say that even if he did not acquire it, whenever his name is called upon it, it has no annulment. And even though we do not rule thus, and the proof he brings from "they participated" is not [valid], as explained in Kiddushin 17 — it is proven there that when they participated he acquired it before it came to his hand, see-there; nevertheless, when he acquired it, according to all it is forbidden even as theft:

When it was annulled from erev Yom Tov. But when it was annulled on Yom Tov — then it was rejected when Yom Tov entered, and again it is not seen, since he has no ability to fix it, for a Jew cannot annul it. But in a matter that he has ability to fix — even if it was seen and rejected, there was no deferral, as written siman 646 seif 2 (and in Beit Yosef): although it is forbidden to diminish them on Yom Tov, nevertheless it was in his power to diminish. And tzarich iyun in Yoreh De'ah siman 28 seif 1, that ruled like "the wind exempts" — even (if) it returned and was revealed (he is liable), for there is no deferral regarding a mitzvah; and it should have said that he should cover it (only) out of doubt, as written here — for in the gemara in Avodah Zarah daf 46 he compares them to each other. And so too Tosafos in Sukkah daf 33, see-there: this is called "not in his power," see-there on daf 32. And it appears that the view of Tur and Beit Yosef and the poskim is as Tosafos wrote in Avodah Zarah: the statement of Rabbi Levi is only l'chatchila; and with this all the difficulties that Tosafos challenged in Sukkah, see-there, are resolved. And if so the law is thus: b'dieved he fulfilled even without annulment, and l'chatchila he should not take it even after it was annulled, for it is repugnant before Heaven. And so too siman 154 seif 1. And according to this it must be that that which is written in siman 11 seif 8 "wool is invalid" — that is l'chatchila, for b'dieved he fulfilled; and the same applies that a horn is invalid l'chatchila for a shofar, and there it deals with idolatry of a Jew, except that through a non-Jew it is not forbidden, and nevertheless it is repugnant before Heaven:
