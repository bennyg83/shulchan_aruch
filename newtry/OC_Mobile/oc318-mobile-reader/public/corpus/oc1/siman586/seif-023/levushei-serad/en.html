The explanation of their words appears to be as follows: where one says, 'benefit from a shofar or sukkah is konam upon me,' we explain that his intent refers to the primary benefit. By contrast, blowing or dwelling for the sake of a mitzvah, which were not given for benefit, are not included in the benefit that he forbade upon himself. But when he did not mention benefit, but rather said, 'the blowing of a shofar is upon me,' or 'dwelling in a sukkah is upon me,' then we also explain that his intent is that he should not benefit from them. Although he did not mention benefit, nevertheless an unspecified vow is from benefit. For this reason there is a distinction: with dwelling in a sukkah, his wording includes even dwelling for a mitzvah, for even though its primary dwelling is only for the mitzvah, nevertheless, since there is also benefit, it is included in the category of dwelling in a sukkah which he forbade upon himself. Since he did not say, 'benefit from a sukkah is upon me,' but rather, 'dwelling in a sukkah is upon me,' even benefit from dwelling whose primary intent is not benefit, such as for a mitzvah, is included. This is not so with a shofar, for when he blows for the mitzvah there is no benefit at all, and we explain that his intent was to forbid blowing a shofar as music, but blowing for the mitzvah is not included. This, in my humble opinion, is the explanation of their words. Examine it carefully.
