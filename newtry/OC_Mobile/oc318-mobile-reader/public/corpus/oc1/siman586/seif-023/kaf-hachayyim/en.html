23) It appears that one may take another person's shofar without his knowledge and recite a blessing over it, as written in siman 649, seif 5. Although a shofar is somewhat damaged through sounding it, a tallis is also somewhat damaged through its use, and nevertheless it is permitted, as written above in siman 14. There is no concern that he will sound it excessively, as there is regarding sefarim, above siman 14, letter 31, for why would he sound more than is required for his obligation? Magen Avraham, ibid.; Eliyah Rabbah, ibid.; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, letter 5; Chayei Adam, ibid. Nevertheless, he may sound 100 blasts with it, since the custom is to sound that number. Mateh Ephraim, letter 5; Mishna Berurah, ibid. It is implied, however, that it is forbidden to take it without the owner's knowledge in order to practice, unless he knows that the owner does not object to this. Chayei Adam, ibid. The permission to take another person's shofar applies only to sounding it in the place where it rests. It is forbidden, however, to remove it from his house to the synagogue, or the reverse, for it is possible that he objects to this, and it constitutes theft, as written above in siman 14, seif 4, and in our words there, letters 19 and 22; see there. So too wrote Magen Avraham, siman 649, s.k. 16, regarding a lulav; see there. So too wrote Mishna Berurah in this siman, Shaar HaTziyun, letter 20.
