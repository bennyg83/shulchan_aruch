[4] From a non-kosher animal, etc. Ran writes this. R. Moshe Rivkash objects that a non-kosher domesticated animal has no horns, as stated in the Mishnah in the chapter “Ba Siman.” Magen Avraham answers that the Mishnah there refers to a wild animal. According to this, however, the difficulty applies to Levush as well, for he states the same law regarding a wild animal. In my work on Yoreh Deah, siman 80, I explained at length that they erred in two respects. First, Ran in Chullin explains that the Sages disagree with that Mishnah, and Rashba and Beit Yosef rule accordingly. Second, I proved there that Rambam and Rosh understand the Mishnah as giving a sign distinguishing a wild animal from a domesticated animal, but a non-kosher species can have horns. Tosafos Yom Tov in Niddah was also imprecise in this matter. His difficulty with Rashi for not explaining the Mishnah there as referring to horns, as Rashi does in Chullin, is likewise unfounded, for Rashi follows Rabbeinu Tam’s view in Tosafos, Chullin 59, that even a non-kosher domesticated animal has no horns at all, as Maharsha explains there.
