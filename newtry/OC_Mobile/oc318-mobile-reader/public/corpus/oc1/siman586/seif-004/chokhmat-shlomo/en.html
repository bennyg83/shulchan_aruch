(Same section 22) The same if a gentile made a shofar on Yom Tov — it is permitted to blow with it. NB: see in Magen Avraham what he challenged — behold it is fully nolad and even to move stones is forbidden on Yom Tov — see there. In my humble opinion there is a distinction: one who examines in gemara ch. 3 of Rosh Hashanah concludes the reason that therefore if a pile of stones fell on him we do not clear him — from the fact that it is Yom Tov performance and lav she-ein melakha; it implies that if it were lav alone it would be pushed aside; and although there it is rabbinic, perforce whatever the Sages enacted like Torah enactment, and even rabbinically it is considered performance and lav. But it appears this is specifically what is forbidden also on Shabbat — then the Sages likened it to Torah to have the law of performance and lav; but what on Shabbat is entirely permitted and only on Yom Tov they were stringent, such as muktzeh — we hold above siman 495 that on Yom Tov muktzeh is forbidden and on Shabbat we hold muktzeh is permitted; and likewise nolad as written in laws of Shabbat siman 324 that they cut the carcass before dogs even if it became nevelah today which is nolad; and so it appears from Magen Avraham siman 308 s.k. 55 that nolad is permitted on Shabbat; if so in this case since on Shabbat it is permitted, on Yom Tov they were stringent so people would not treat lightly — certainly in any case it is not like performance and lav; therefore it fits well that moving stones in a matter that is not a vessel is explicit in siman 308 that even for us it is forbidden even on Shabbat — in this the Sages enacted like Torah and it is performance and lav and the performance of shofar does not push it aside. But in a matter that is a vessel only that it is muktzeh and nolad — if it were Shabbat it would be entirely permitted, only on Yom Tov they were stringent so people would not treat lightly; in this if they made it lav alone; but not also performance — behold it would be like a hollow and a pebble: on Shabbat it would be entirely permitted and on Yom Tov like performance and lav; therefore it is only lav alone and again performance pushes aside lav; therefore it fits well that the Mechaber does not bring it — he goes to his view that he rules muktzeh is forbidden on Yom Tov and automatically per his words nolad is permitted on Shabbat; certainly there is no difficulty for his view. And he ruled well that it is permitted to blow with it; even per Rama in siman 495 who brought there are those who permit muktzeh on Yom Tov and wrote that per their words nolad is also forbidden on Shabbat — nevertheless he did not emend anything here, and the reason is until here nolad was not stricter than muktzeh except nolad that is recognizable from its body that we saw with our eyes that yesterday it was not as it is now — per its present state it has no permission, therefore it is truly nolad and stricter than muktzeh. But nolad thus — if the gentile made it for himself it would be permitted as Magen Avraham wrote himself; only if he made it for an Israelite it is forbidden; if so from the body of the act it is not recognizable for whom he makes it, Israelite or gentile; therefore whatever is not recognizable from its body does not have the law of nolad, only muktzeh, and is permitted on Shabbat and on Yom Tov per this view. What is written in siman 501 that it is forbidden — that is the Mechaber, examine. And even if forbidden on Shabbat it is only because of concern it might be exchanged for full nolad so people would not treat lightly with true nolad — and this is not a decree upon a decree, for sometimes we decree thus; therefore in any case here too they did not make it stringent to have the law of performance and lav; therefore the performance of shofar pushes it aside; and there is no difficulty either for the Mechaber or for Rama. One can explain the matter more: perforce it is proven that here they did not make a strengthening like Torah, for if they made strengthening like Torah how is it permitted on Shabbat? Behold from Torah we do not find anything permitted on Shabbat and forbidden on Yom Tov; perforce what they were stringent on Yom Tov more than Shabbat is not like Torah — for if like Torah it would be forbidden even on Shabbat; and since they did not make it like Torah, therefore automatically it is only lav and not lav and performance — examine well for this is clear in reasoning:
