A curved horn is part of the primary mitzvah l'chatchila, and one does not fulfill the obligation l'chatchila with a straight horn. By contrast, a ram's horn is not called a l'chatchila requirement, but rather the most choice manner of performing the mitzvah. Taz, ibid. However, Maamar Mordechai, letter 1, wrote that although it is evident from the words of Maran in the Beit Yosef, in his explanation of the Tur and the poskim, that the requirement of a ram's horn is only the most choice manner of performing the mitzvah and does not invalidate even l'chatchila so long as the horn is curved, nevertheless, the plain implication of his words here in the Shulchan Aruch is that all other shofaros are valid only b'dieved; see there. This is also the view of the Bach, that other shofaros which are not from a ram, even when curved, are valid only b'dieved; see there. A practical difference arises where one has a straight ram's horn and a curved horn that is not from a ram. According to the Taz, the curved horn that is not from a ram takes precedence, since being from a ram is only the most choice manner of performing the mitzvah, whereas a straight horn is valid only b'dieved. According to the Bach and Maamar Mordechai, the straight ram's horn and the curved horn that is not from a ram are equal, since each is valid only b'dieved. This also appears from what was written above in letter 2, that each one possesses one advantage. Yet since according to the Bach and Maamar Mordechai they are equal, while according to the Taz the curved horn that is not from a ram is valid even l'chatchila, the curved horn should be given precedence, since this accords with all opinions. So too wrote Mishna Berurah, s.k. 5, that the conclusion of most poskim is that a curved horn is preferable; see there.
