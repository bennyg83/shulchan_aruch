One who is forbidden by neder from benefit of a shofar; another person blows with it, etc. Marginal note: Behold Tosafos asked in the sixth chapter of Shevuos: why do we say mitzvos were not given in order to derive benefit (behold he benefits in that he is exempt from the prutah of Rav Yosef) see there. And in my humble opinion it comes out well: for what exempts him from giving a prutah to a poor person is only akin to "driving away a lion" and like one who pays his fellow's debt without his knowledge; and therefore, since regarding one forbidden by neder from benefit it is permitted to pay his debt without his knowledge, likewise here what exempts him from a prutah to a poor person is not reckoned as benefit. And even though one who pays his debt with his knowledge is forbidden; that is because there, since nothing forces him to do so and he does it of his own will, he reveals his mind that it is agreeable to him and he acts as his agent, which is not so when performing a mitzvah: he is forced to fulfill the mitzvah, and if he were not forced it is possible he would rather give the prutah to the poor person and not do the mitzvah, only that the Torah forced him to do the mitzvah, and thereby he is automatically exempted from the poor person; since it is through the Torah's compulsion, it is like [doing it] without his knowledge. But a paid watchman, since he has this benefit, it is like with his knowledge; therefore even "driving away a lion" is benefit since it is with his knowledge. But regarding a mitzvah it is like without his knowledge. And further: regarding one forbidden by neder from benefit the matter depends on the Torah's viewpoint, and in the Torah's viewpoint driving away a lion is not reckoned as benefit; but regarding a paid watchman the matter depends on his own viewpoint, as we find generally regarding that benefit that because he trusts him he commits himself, and therefore in his viewpoint paying a debt is also benefit, so he is a paid watchman; and it comes out well. And even though regarding a watchman of a lost object he is likewise forced to accept the lost object and to watch it; nevertheless for the watching alone he does not become a paid watchman, as is known, but only through spreading and shaking [the garment]; and spreading and shaking it is in his power to do through another, and then he would be obligated to give a prutah to a poor person, for he himself does nothing and is exempt only when he himself does it, and that too is with his knowledge entirely; therefore he is a paid watchman. And with this Bach's answer comes out well (what Magen Avraham wrote in his name regarding what is difficult from here to what is written in Yoreh De'ah siman 221) and Bach answers that there is a distinction between whether there is another [person] or not; and apparently it is not understood, and one must say that in a time of need we rely on those who hold that bodily benefit together with mitzvah-benefit is also not reckoned as benefit, etc. And according to the above we need not that: only if there is another, then if the other blows (since he himself does nothing) he is obligated in a prutah to a poor person and does not benefit except when he himself blows, [when] he is exempt; and if so, if it is with his knowledge and he benefits with the prutah of Rav Yosef, therefore it is forbidden. But if there is no other, then since he is obligated to blow it is again like without his knowledge and is only "driving away a lion," therefore it is permitted; and it comes out well with Hashem's help; and examine carefully, for it is correct. And behold the dispute of the Rishonim is well known regarding what we hold that mitzvos were not given in order to derive benefit, whether when there is bodily benefit together with mitzvah-benefit we also say they were not given in order to derive benefit, or in that [case] mitzvos were given in order to derive benefit. And now it appears to me a proof from Pesachim, chapter "Kol Sha'ah," in the sugya of Chizkiyah and R' Elazar, where it objects there from a nazir (the Merciful One said he shall not eat, etc., and we learned we make an eruv for a nazir with wine, etc.) and it is difficult: what does it ask? Behold the view of the Rabbis in tractate Eruvin is that we make an eruv only for a mitzvah matter; and if so one can say that this Mishnah comes like the view that we make an eruv only for a mitzvah matter, and mitzvos were not given in order to derive benefit, therefore we make an eruv for him with wine, so what does it ask? And necessarily it is proved that where there is bodily benefit together with mitzvah-benefit, [mitzvos] were given in order to derive benefit; and if so, likewise here, even though the beginning of the eruv is for a mitzvah matter, nevertheless once he placed the eruv he can again go there even for an optional matter wherever he wishes; therefore in that [case mitzvos] were given in order to derive benefit, and it asks well; and examine carefully. However one can say that those who hold that even when bodily benefit is together with a mitzvah we also say they were not given in order to derive benefit; they hold like the Razah's view that regarding rabbinic mitzvos [they] were given in order to derive benefit; and if so even those who hold we make an eruv only for a mitzvah matter; nevertheless for a rabbinic mitzvah too we make an eruv, by force of one way or the other: according to the Torah one does not need an eruv, and by rabbinic law an eruv is required, and by rabbinic law too it is a mitzvah; and if so it asks well that a rabbinic mitzvah was given in order to derive benefit; and it comes out well; and examine carefully; and see what I wrote above on this in another matter.
