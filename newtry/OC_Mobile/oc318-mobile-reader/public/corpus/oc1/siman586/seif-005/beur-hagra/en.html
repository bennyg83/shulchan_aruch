But if, etc. See Nedarim 16b and Tosafos in Rosh Hashanah. As Ran and Rosh explain there, Rava disagrees with Abaye only when one prohibits benefit, because mitzvos were not given for personal benefit. But if he says, “the sitting,” etc., the prohibition takes effect, since he expressly prohibited the act upon himself, just as one may prohibit himself from throwing a stone into the sea even though he derives no benefit from it. Ran writes that if he merely says, “This sukkah is forbidden to me,” he may sit in it for the mitzvah, because he did not expressly prohibit the act of sitting. This is why the ruling here speaks of “its sounding being forbidden to me,” for he expressly mentioned its sounding. Ran further asks that vows do not take effect upon something intangible, and answers that the case is where he said, “This sukkah is forbidden with respect to its sitting.” Tosafos write that even if he says, “Sitting in this sukkah is forbidden to me,” it is effective because he mentions the tangible object. This depends upon the ruling at the end of the first chapter, seif 13, concerning one who says, “My mouth shall be forbidden,” etc. Ran likewise writes that the statement is treated as though he said it in that form, and this is the ruling in Yoreh Deah at the beginning of siman 213. See the gloss there concerning one who says, “My speech,” etc. This is also implied by the words of Tosafos cited by Ran concerning sitting in a sukkah. The same applies here: he must say, “The sounding of this shofar is forbidden to me.” See Beit Yosef in siman 239. Nevertheless, it is prohibited D’Rabbanan even if he did not mention the tangible object, as stated there on 14a and as Ran writes there at the end of the first chapter.
