(21) One forbidden benefit from a shofar. Such as if he said, "Konam: the benefit of a shofar upon me."
(22) Another person, etc. Meaning: even though he cannot release himself from the mitzvah of shofar because of this neder (for a mitzvah is not called benefit, as above) nevertheless he should hear the blowing of a shofar from another and not blow himself; and the reason the poskim wrote is that there are many people who have enjoyment when they blow, and bodily enjoyment cannot be permitted because of the reason that mitzvos were not given for benefit. Nevertheless the Acharonim wrote that in a time of urgency when he has no other, it is permitted for him himself to blow, only that he should not blow except ten sounds, namely Tashrat, Tashtat, Tirtat, which are from the essence of the law.
(23) Konam regarding its blowing upon me. He did not mention benefit; meaning he said, "Konam: the shofar for blowing it upon me"; and therefore it takes effect, since he specified to forbid upon himself the shofar to blow with it, like one who forbids upon himself a pebble to throw with it even though he has no benefit; but if he said plainly, "Konam: a shofar upon me," it is permitted to blow with it a tekiah of mitzvah.
(24) It is forbidden to blow, etc. And even if he specified in his neder a tekiah of mitzvah; and the reason is that nedarim are a prohibition of the object and take effect even on a matter of mitzvah; and even hearing the blowing of a shofar from another is also included in this prohibition. And know that there are poskim who mean that the neder does not take effect unless he forbade the shofar upon himself (such as if he said, "A shofar upon me as a konam to blow with it"; but if he forbade upon himself the blowing) such as if he said, "Blowing of a shofar upon me as a konam" it does not take effect, for the blowing has no substance, and nedarim do not take effect on something without substance. However several poskim hold that it takes effect even in this, since in any case he mentioned in his neder an object; ordinarily his intention is to forbid the object upon himself. And all this regarding a Torah prohibition; but by rabbinic law there is a prohibition in every manner, even in a neder that has no substance, as explained in Yoreh Deah siman 213; and therefore it is forbidden to blow with it before he has the neder permitted. And it is explicit in the poskim that all this is regarding a neder; but regarding an oath he cannot release himself from the mitzvah of blowing the shofar, for he is sworn and standing from Mount Sinai to fulfill the mitzvos of the Torah, unless he included in his oath to forbid himself from hearing every tekiah, which implies even a voluntary tekiah; and in this his oath takes effect comprehensively to forbid also one of mitzvah, and he should strive to permit his oath. And even regarding nedarim: if he forbade himself to hear a shofar from so-and-so, he is not released thereby from the mitzvah of blowing the shofar, and he must seek another person to hear a shofar from him; and if he does not find another, it is forbidden to hear from this person (even if he forbade himself by an oath) for he is not like one sworn to cancel the mitzvah, since when the oath left his mouth it was not to cancel, for behold he is not commanded to hear specifically from this person.
