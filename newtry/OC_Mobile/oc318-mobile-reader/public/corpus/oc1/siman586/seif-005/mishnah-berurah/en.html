(24) It is forbidden to blow etc. — and even if he specified in his vow tekiah of a mitzva; the reason is that vows are prohibition of the object and take effect even on a matter of mitzva, and even to hear another's shofar blowing is also included in this prohibition. Know there are poskim that R' Eliezer means the vow does not take effect unless he forbade the shofar upon him, such as if he said shofar upon me in konam to blow with it; but if he forbade upon him the blowing, such as if he said tekiah of shofar upon me in konam — it does not take effect, for tekiah has no substance and vows do not take effect on something without substance. However several poskim hold it takes effect even in this, since in any case he mentioned in his vow the object implicitly — his intention is to forbid the object upon him. All this regarding Torah prohibition; but rabbinically there is prohibition in every case, even in a vow without substance as explained in Yoreh Deah siman 213; therefore it is forbidden to blow with it before the vow is permitted. It is explicit in poskim that all this is regarding a vow; but regarding an oath he cannot release himself from the mitzva of tekiah of shofar, for he is sworn and standing from Mount Sinai to fulfill Torah mitzvot — unless he included in his oath to forbid himself from hearing any tekiah, which implies even optional tekiah; in this his oath takes effect comprehensively to forbid also mitzva tekiah, and he should strive to permit his oath. Even regarding vows: if he forbade himself to hear shofar from so-and-so, he is not released from mitzva of tekiah of shofar and must ask another person to hear shofar from him; and if he does not find another, it is forbidden to hear from this person even if he forbade himself by oath that he is not like one sworn to cancel the mitzva — for when the oath left his mouth it was not to cancel, for behold he is not commanded to hear from this person specifically:
