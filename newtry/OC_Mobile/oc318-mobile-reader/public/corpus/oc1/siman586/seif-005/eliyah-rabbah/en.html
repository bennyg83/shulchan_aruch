[5] It is invalid, etc. This requires study. Darkei Moshe writes: “Ran leaves the matter requiring study, and it is proper to be stringent.” This implies that one may be lenient when no other shofar is available. Why, then, did Rama later rule without qualification that it is invalid? It appears to me that Ran’s essential uncertainty is whether the rule that only material fit for sacred service may be used can be derived for shofar, because Shabbos 28a asks what conclusion was reached concerning the tachash that existed in Moshe’s time. One may answer that a temporary case is not derived from a law applying for generations, namely tefillin, whereas shofar can be derived from it because shofar too applies for generations. Olas Shabbos questions this ruling, arguing that if a verse was needed for tefillin, “so that the Torah of Hashem shall be in your mouth,” we evidently do not accept the rule that only fit material may be used. His question itself is difficult. If so, why does the Gemara ask, after concluding that this rule concerns tefillin, that tefillin are explicitly written? It should have asked earlier, when it assumed that the rule concerned other matters, that this cannot be so because a verse was required for tefillin. Rather, it is clear that the general rule is itself derived from the verse concerning tefillin, as Levush implies.
