(1) One forbidden benefit from a shofar — another person blows for him and he fulfills, etc.; but he himself is forbidden to blow because many people enjoy when they themselves blow, and wherever there is bodily benefit it cannot be permitted because of the reason mitzvot were not given for benefit (girsa of Rif and Baal HaMaor). And some say he himself is permitted to blow (Tur and girsa of Rosh, Rambam, Rabbeinu Yerucham, and Agudah); however in practice we rule stringently (my teacher in Bach). But when there is no other who can blow for him, Tur, Rosh, Rivash, Rambam, and Agudah are fit to rely on them in time of need; see Yoreh Deah siman 221 seif 13 and siman 215 seif 1.

(2) But if he said konam [this] shofar for my blowing upon me, etc. — specifically that he said in the language of a vow, for vows take effect on a matter of mitzva as on a voluntary matter; but in the language of an oath not, for an oath does not take effect on a matter of mitzva unless in a general statement as explained in Hilchot Nedarim (Ran Kol Bo). Therefore one who says an oath that I will not hear the sound of a mitzva shofar — the oath does not take effect to nullify the mitzva; he is lashed and hears a mitzva shofar. Only when he says I will not hear the sound of a shofar stam — since it takes effect on the sound of a shofar that is not a mitzva, it also takes effect on the sound of a mitzva shofar (Beit Yosef, Ran, Maharam of Padua).
