Another person blows. For mitzvos were not given for benefit; but he himself is forbidden to blow with it even [a blast] of mitzvah, for there are many people who enjoy when they themselves blow.
Konam regarding its blowing upon me. This is the language of a neder, forbidding the object upon him; and nedarim take effect regarding a mitzvah-matter as regarding a voluntary matter, as written in Yoreh Deah siman 215. Automatically, if he said that he will not hear the sound of a shofar, that is the language of an oath, forbidding himself regarding the object, and an oath does not take effect regarding a mitzvah-matter. And Beis Yosef brought in the name of Tosafos who wrote in the second chapter of Nedarim regarding a sukkah: that if he says, "Sitting in a sukkah upon me as a konam," even if he says that he will not sit in a sukkah, it is forbidden to sit in a sukkah of mitzvah (so too regarding a shofar: if he said, "Shofar blasts upon me as a konam," it is forbidden to blow) unless one distinguish that regarding a sukkah even without a mitzvah there is enjoyment in sitting, but a shofar without a mitzvah has no enjoyment in blowing, for he does not intend song (end quote). And if he says regarding another person that he will not hear the sound of a shofar from him, or "his blowing upon me (as a konam)," it appears simple that it takes effect according to everyone, for it is possible to hear from another.
