(ב) Bach wrote etc. but one may say owners etc. and it would be gentile idolatry. It implies Bach holds in such a case that through nullification it is permitted. However this is not so, for Shach wrote in Yoreh Deah siman 146 in the name of Bach that even if an Israelite did not acquire it, such as if he stole it, nevertheless it has no nullification rabbinically, for they decreed lest Israelite idolatry — see there; perforce it must be that it has no nullification in such a case, nevertheless it is permitted because it is not called crushing its hide, since from Torah it has nullification. I found support for this reasoning in responsum Sha'ar Efrayim siman 38 who wrote to explain Tosafot's words in Sukkah daf 35a s.v. lefi etc. — they challenged why gemara said for this reason we do not fulfill with etrog of orlah, because there is no permission of eating or monetary law — answer: crushing its hide; for its mitzva is in burning; and he wrote: it is possible that even d'oraisa orlah is in burning, for we learn it from kilayim of vineyard etc. — end of Tosafot; and he wrote in that responsum: this is what Tosafot inferred "and it is possible that even d'oraisa it is in burning" — for if it were rabbinic, crushing its hide would not apply — end of his words:

(ג) In my humble opinion behold we hold etc. as stated in Avodah Zarah etc. — the language is behold we hold it must be. So it is stated there: idolatry that broke by itself — R' Yosi holds there is no nullification; R' Eliezer holds there is nullification. Gemara challenged R' Eliezer: from what we taught Israel cannot nullify gentile idolatry — why should it be like idolatry that broke by itself? Abaye answered: the mishnah deals with one who bowed to idolatry before him and did not break it — through gentile in such a case there is nullification, but not through Israel. Rava establishes it even if Israel broke it — nevertheless it does not help because they decreed lest he elevate it etc.; behold Rava did not need this decree except for R' Eliezer, but for R' Yosi whom we hold like regarding idolatry that broke by itself it is forbidden for there is no nullification — he does not need this decree; and by law Israel cannot nullify gentile idolatry; if so why did Magen Avraham write behold we hold etc.? However his intention is certainly from R' Eliezer we hear for R' Yosi, since for R' Eliezer perforce even idolatry that has an owner — if Israel acquired it, it has no nullification; certainly R' Yosi does not argue on this except that one should not say Abaye goes like R' Eliezer after he bowed — and since he did not establish like Rava because of the decree, one might say Abaye holds as Bach wrote that even if Israel elevates it, it has nullification since it has an owner; if so even per R' Eliezer according to Abaye the Bach's law applies. Thus Magen Avraham behold we hold etc. — R' Eliezer that after Abaye's answer Abaye argues with Rava on this; nevertheless we hold like Rava against Abaye (even though they do not argue regarding themselves); if so for us who hold like Rava, perforce even if it has an owner, if Israel acquired it, it has no nullification. And even though I wrote above Bach concedes rabbinically it has no nullification as in Yoreh Deah — nevertheless since Bach holds it has no nullification except because of decree lest Israelite idolatry as above — if so again one should not decree lest he elevate it etc. which would be decree upon decree; perforce if Israel acquired it, it has no nullification from Torah:
