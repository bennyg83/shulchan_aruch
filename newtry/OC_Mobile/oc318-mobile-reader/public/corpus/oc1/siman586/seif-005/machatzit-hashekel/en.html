(s.k. 5) He intended to acquire, etc. And specifically regarding avodah zarah of a non-Jew: if a Jew acquired it, it has no nullification. But accessories or adornments of avodah zarah of a non-Jew (even if a Jew acquired them) Beis Yosef ruled in Yoreh De'ah siman 146, se'if 2, that they have nullification; but the Bach there disagrees, as the Shach wrote in his name, s.k. 1.
And the Bach wrote, etc. But one can say [it has] owners, etc., and it is avodah zarah of a non-Jew. And it implies that in such a case the Bach holds that one fulfills with it because it has nullification. However this is not so; for the Shach wrote in Yoreh De'ah at the beginning of siman 146 in the name of the Bach that even if a Jew did not acquire it (for example he stole it) nevertheless it has no nullification by rabbinic law, for we decree on account of avodah zarah of a Jew (see there). Therefore one must say: even though in such a case it has no nullification, nevertheless one fulfills with it, for it is not called "its measure is crushed," since according to the Torah it has nullification. And I found support for this reasoning in the Responsa Shaar Ephraim, siman 38, who wrote to explain Tosafos's words in Sukkah folio 35a, s.v. "because," etc., who asked on what the Talmud said that for this reason one does not fulfill with an esrog of orlah (because it has no permission to eat or law of monetary [use]) [why not rather] say that its measure is crushed, for its mitzvah is by burning; and they wrote, and this is their language: "And it is possible that even according to the Torah orlah is [required to be] burned, for we learn it from kilayim of the vineyard," etc. (end quote of Tosafos). And he wrote in the aforementioned Responsa Shaar Ephraim, and this is his language: "And this is what Tosafos were precise: 'And it is possible that even according to the Torah it is [required to be] burned' (for if it were only by rabbinic law, 'its measure is crushed' would not apply") end quote.
And it appears to me that we hold, etc., as is stated in Avodah Zarah, etc. The language "for we hold" requires explanation. For this is what is stated there: Avodah zarah that broke by itself. R' Yochanan holds it is not nullification, and Reish Lakish holds it is nullification. And the Talmud objects against Reish Lakish from what we learned: A Jew cannot nullify avodah zarah of a non-Jew; and why; it should be like avodah zarah that broke by itself. And Abaye answers that the Mishnah deals with [a case] where he chipped the avodah zarah in its presence and did not break it, for through a non-Jew such a case is nullification, but not through a Jew. And Rava establishes it even where a Jew broke it: nevertheless it does not avail, because we decree lest he lift it up, etc. Thus Rava needed this decree only according to Reish Lakish; but according to R' Yochanan, with whom we hold (that avodah zarah that broke by itself is forbidden, for it is not nullification) we do not need this decree, and by law a Jew cannot nullify avodah zarah of a non-Jew. And if so, why did Magen Avraham write "for we hold," etc.? However his intent is: certainly from Reish Lakish we hear [the law] for R' Yochanan, since according to Reish Lakish one must say that even avodah zarah that has owners, if a Jew acquired it, has no nullification; and certainly R' Yochanan does not disagree with him in this. Only: lest you say that Abaye answered Reish Lakish with a different answer (that he chipped it) and since he did not answer like Rava because of a decree, you will say that Abaye holds as the Bach wrote, that even if a Jew lifts it up it has nullification since it has owners (and if so even according to Reish Lakish via Abaye there is the Bach's law. On this Magen Avraham wrote "for we hold," etc.) meaning: even if you say Abaye disagrees with Rava in this, nevertheless we hold like Rava against Abaye (even though they do not disagree according to their own views [in personal debate]); and if so, for us who hold like Rava, necessarily even if it has owners, if a Jew acquired it it has no nullification. And even though I wrote above that the Bach concedes that by rabbinic law it has no nullification, as written in Yoreh De'ah (nevertheless since according to the Bach it only has no nullification because of a decree on account of avodah zarah of a Jew, as above) then we should no longer decree lest he lift it up, etc., for that would be a decree upon a decree. Therefore [one must say]: if a Jew acquired it, it has no nullification according to the Torah.
And so it appears in Tosafos of Sukkah. For regarding what we learned there: An esrog of an asherah (he should not take it, and if he took it he has not fulfilled; and it objects: but Rava said a lulav of avodah zarah) he should not take it, and if he took it he has fulfilled; and it answers: the Mishnah is of an asherah of a Jew, which has no nullification and its measure is crushed. And Tosafos asked: even though Rava deals with avodah zarah of a non-Jew which has nullification, nevertheless once he lifted it he acquired it and it becomes avodah zarah of a Jew, as is stated in tractate Avodah Zarah, lest he lift it up and afterward nullify it, as above; and they answered that it deals with [a case] where he lifted it in order not to acquire it. And it is impossible according to the Bach's words that one should say Rava deals with [a case] where it has owners (for that is the usual case. And so it appears in Yevamos folio 104: Tosafos asked similarly regarding what we say there) a shoe of avodah zarah of a non-Jew; she should not perform chalitzah with it, and if she performed chalitzah her chalitzah is valid; and Tosafos asked also: since she lifted it she acquired it and it becomes avodah zarah of a Jew. And according to the Bach one could say that it has owners.
And the reason is: since a Jew is liable, etc. In truth the plain language of Rashi in Avodah Zarah there implies like the Bach: he wrote, and this is his language, s.v. "rather," etc.: a decree lest he lift it up and acquire it by lifting, for every ownerless thing such as a find is acquired by lifting (end quote). And from his need for the reason "for every ownerless thing," etc., it implies like the Bach that it deals specifically with ownerless [property]. And Magen Avraham must hold: from the fact that Rava did not say explicitly "we are dealing with ownerless avodah zarah," therefore it deals even when it has owners; nevertheless, once he lifted it, it becomes avodah zarah of a Jew since he is liable for responsibility. But why should we say that through the lifting he becomes liable for responsibility? On this Rashi wrote that he acquires it by lifting so as to be liable for accidents; for every ownerless thing is acquired by lifting with a complete acquisition; therefore at least when it has owners (even though he does not acquire a complete acquisition through lifting) nevertheless he acquires it regarding responsibility to be liable for accidents.
And so it appears regarding chametz in siman 440: a non-Jew who deposited his chametz with a Jew, if the Jew is liable for responsibility, it is like a Jew's chametz and he must destroy it.
Beis Yosef wrote in Yoreh De'ah in the Tur according to some versions in the Tur.
And the proof he brings from "they became partners," etc. And this is the language of Beis Yosef: There is a proof from what we learned in chapter 6 of Demai: A convert and a non-Jew who inherited from their non-Jewish father; [the convert] may say to the non-Jew, "You take the avodah zarah or yayin nesech and I [will take] money," etc. And we conclude in tractate Avodah Zarah folio 64 that specifically regarding a convert's inheritance they were lenient so that he not return to his corruption by losing half his inheritance; but for a [born] Jew it is forbidden (because he wants the continued existence of the avodah zarah and yayin nesech, for if they are ruined he loses against them half the money). And a beraisa likewise teaches: When is this said? When they inherited; but if they became partners it is forbidden. And Beis Yosef wrote: From its teaching "forbidden" without qualification it implies that there is no remedy even through nullification. And here [is a case] where he has not yet acquired it (similar to inheritance.. before it came into the convert's possession) and it teaches that it is forbidden and nullification does not avail since his name is called upon it, etc. (end quote of Beis Yosef. And on this Magen Avraham wrote: even though there in tractate Avodah Zarah one can say as Beis Yosef wrote) that "they became partners" deals with [a case] where he did not acquire it, and nevertheless it has no nullification since his name is called upon it, and it is forbidden because he wants its continued existence, and only regarding inheritance they were lenient so that he not return to his corruption; nevertheless in Kiddushin folio 17 it is proved that this "they became partners" deals with [a case] where he acquired it. For this is what is stated there: Rava said: A convert who inherits from his non-Jewish father is only by rabbinic law so that he not return to his corruption. For we learned: A convert and a non-Jew who inherited from their non-Jewish father (he may say to him, "You take the avodah zarah," etc. And if you would think a convert's inheritance is according to the Torah) even [when the items] have not [yet] come into the convert's possession; why is it permitted to say "You take the avodah zarah," etc.? Behold he is taking substitutes of avodah zarah (Rashi explained: for when his father died he acquired half, and now he exchanges them). Therefore [one must say] he inherits only by rabbinic law. And Tosafos Yeshanim wrote: now they are not substitutes of avodah zarah, for the Sages only enacted inheritance for him regarding what is permitted, etc. A beraisa likewise teaches: When is this said? When they inherited; but if they became partners it is forbidden (end quote of the Gemara). And if so, one must necessarily say that "they became partners" deals with [a case] where he acquired it; for now it properly brings "a beraisa likewise teaches": since once they became partners it is forbidden because he acquired it, likewise if a convert's inheritance were according to the Torah it would be as if he acquired it and it would be forbidden because of substitutes of avodah zarah. And if [you say the concern is] lest he return to his corruption, for that reason the Gemara holds it as a simple reasoning that one may not permit a Torah prohibition; and it brought a proof only that it is forbidden to exchange in such a case. Therefore it must be that a convert's inheritance is by rabbinic law; and therefore specifically "they became partners," where he acquired it and it is his according to the Torah, so the exchanges are forbidden, which is not so regarding inheritance which is only by rabbinic law, [where] they were lenient. But if "they became partners" deals with [a case] where he did not acquire, as Beis Yosef wrote, and its prohibition is because his name is called upon it, then what proof does it bring from "they became partners" that a convert's inheritance is by rabbinic law? For if not, what difference is there between inheritance and becoming partners? And according to your reason, even if you say a convert's inheritance is by rabbinic law, nevertheless his name is called upon it, and it would still be difficult why they were lenient regarding inheritance and forbid becoming partners. Therefore one must say they were lenient regarding inheritance so that he not return to his corruption, which does not apply regarding becoming partners; if so, from where [do we know] that his inheritance is by rabbinic law; perhaps it is always according to the Torah and they were lenient so that he not return to his corruption? And if it holds as a simple reasoning that for that reason one may not be lenient regarding a Torah prohibition, then one does not need a proof from "they became partners." Therefore "they became partners" must deal with [a case] where he acquired it, and it brings a proof that where he acquired it according to the Torah it is forbidden to say "You take the avodah zarah," etc., as above. But I looked at Tosafos in tractate Avodah Zarah, s.v. "a beraisa likewise teaches," etc., who wrote, and this is their language: "And therefore in the first chapter of Kiddushin, after it proved that a convert's inheritance is by rabbinic law from [the law of] a convert and a non-Jew, it needed to bring 'a beraisa likewise teaches' so that you not reject its proof toward the reason of bererah" (end quote) meaning: one could have said that even if a convert's inheritance is according to the Torah, nevertheless it is not called substitutes of avodah zarah, for the matter was clarified retroactively that the permitted [item] this one takes was his share from the outset and not the forbidden [item]; but according to this, becoming partners too would be proper to permit for the reason of bererah; and according to this one cannot reject Beis Yosef's proof from Kiddushin: for even if "they became partners" deals with [a case] where he did not acquire, nevertheless it properly brings a proof, for certainly as a matter of reasoning it is obvious to him that if it were according to the Torah they would not permit the exchanges so that the convert not return to his corruption; only lest you say that as a matter of law it is permitted for the reason of bererah, therefore it brings a proof from "they became partners" that it is forbidden; and if it deals with [a case] where he did not acquire and its prohibition is only by rabbinic law because his name is called upon it, all the more so is its proof good: for regarding becoming partners where there is only a rabbinic prohibition they do not permit for the reason of bererah, all the more so if a convert's inheritance is according to the Torah, one should not permit for the reason of bererah. But according to what Tosafos wrote in Kiddushin, s.v. "substitutes of avodah zarah he takes," this is not so: they wrote, and this is their language: "And one should not say the reason is because of bererah," etc., "for if so, even when they came into his possession it should likewise be permitted for that reason" (end quote). Thus they disagree with the words of Tosafos of tractate Avodah Zarah; and for this one needs a proof from the beraisa that the reason is not because of bererah. And if so, one must necessarily say the Gemara's words and its proof are as above; and Magen Avraham's words are fine.
Nevertheless when he acquired, etc., Meaning: even though when he did not acquire we do not hold like those "some say," nevertheless for us as well, at least when he acquired, one can say it has no nullification.
