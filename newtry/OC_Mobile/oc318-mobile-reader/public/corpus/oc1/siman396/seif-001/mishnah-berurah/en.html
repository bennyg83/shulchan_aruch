(א) In which a man is under him from here etc. — explanation: it is written "under him" as under him — his body three amot and an amah to extend his hands and legs, as explained in siman 349 — see there in Mishna Berurah; but there we deal with carrying some object in reshut ha-rabbim, and here we deal with one who went outside the techum, that he has permission to walk four amot:

(ב) Even if he went etc. — for Moses said this to those who went outside the techum "in which a man is under him" [Tur]:

(ג) With his amah — see above 349 in Mishna Berurah s.k. 3:

(ד) Dwarf in his limbs — meaning his body is average and his arm short, as I explained above in 349 s.k. 4 and in Shaarei Tzion there:

(ה) And these four amot etc. — meaning whether we measure for each person with his amah or we measure four average amot:

(ו) Wide — meaning they should measure him with space, not cramped. And know that four amot are given him square toward the direction he wishes to walk — meaning he can walk in them also at the diagonal, which adds another amah and three-fifths:

(ז) And he is in the middle of them — printing error; should read this after the words "to each side," as we will explain below [Taz and Gra]:

(ח) And there is one who says he may walk etc. — for per the first view he only has four amot in the direction he chose; and if he chose one place he cannot return and choose another place; per this view he has four amot to each side around him — meaning eight by eight:

(ט) To each side and he is in the middle of them — so it should read; meaning from the place he stands he has four amot to each side, and behold it is eight by eight as we wrote; and see Bach who wrote one may rely on this view in practice:

(י) But for carrying etc. — meaning there are those who say they dispute only about walking, but regarding carrying all agree he is only permitted up to four amot with the diagonal; and see above in siman 349 that those four amot he chose for him — all agree he may carry at the diagonal even if it extends more than five amot:
