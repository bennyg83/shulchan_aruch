(1) Piyyutim, etc. The Rosh at the end of the chapter "Ein Omdin" brings proof — these are his words: R' Eliezer HaKallir, who was from the Land of Israel, from Kiryat Sefer; and in his days the Temple stood by sight, for behold he did not fix any krovetz for the second day. And some say he was a tanna, as stated in Pesikta: when R' Eliezer ben R' Shimon died, his generation called him "from all the dust of merchants," for he was a tanna, krovetz, darshan, and paytan; and he instituted the krovetz to say in the first three [blessings] — until here are his words. And one may wonder: in our machzorim we find him on the second day of Pesach and on the second day of Sukkos, where he is signed in a piyyut before "El Na." And Maadanai Melech, page 42, answered that it is possible he established two piyyutim and they would see which one was fit, and afterward they practiced both — until here; and this is strained and requires examination. He also asked: in many piyyutim on the four parshiyot and on Purim and Tal, the rhyme endings are signed "R' Eliezer b.Ribbi Kallir from Kiryat Sefer," and he wrote that it is possible his grandfather was called Kallir; however, in Hagahot Maimoni it implies that he was R' Eliezer ben Rashbi. And in Aruch he explained Kallir as the language of "ugah" (cake) — that he ate a cake in which an amulet was written and he became enlightened; and Maadanai Melech wondered at him: if so, he must say that Ribbi returned the title to himself; and it is known that Rashi explains that it is the language of "great one of the generation," and it is impossible to say that he would sign himself "great one of the generation" — until here. And to me it appears that "ugah" means this: not to attribute goodness to himself, but rather that his greatness came to him from the power that he ate a cake with an amulet of holy names. Shibalei Leket wrote: I heard from my father, my teacher, who heard from his teachers, the geonim of Lotir, that when R' Eliezer paytaned "Chayot asher hena merubbaot," once on the throne fire flared around him; and also R' Shimon the Great, who was learned in miracles, would say it every day.
