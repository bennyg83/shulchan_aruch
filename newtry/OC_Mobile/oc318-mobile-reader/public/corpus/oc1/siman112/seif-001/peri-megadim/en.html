What the Mechaber wrote. Regarding the kerovos, see Peri Chadash, who strongly warned not to miss the times for Krias Shema and prayer because of them, and also not to converse. See siman 68. See Eliyah Rabbah in the name of Lechem Chamudos: the paytan Kalir is described in the Aruch under the entry “Kalir” as having eaten a cake upon which an amulet was written, after which his understanding was opened. Regarding the piyyut attributed to him for the second day of Pesach, Maadanei Yom Tov answers that he composed two piyyutim for the first day, and the public divided them, selecting one for the first day and one for the second day. See there.
