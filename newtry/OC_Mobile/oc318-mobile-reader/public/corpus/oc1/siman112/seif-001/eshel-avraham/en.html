In the first three blessings. The resurrection of the dead is mentioned six times. Four classes are considered as dead, as stated in the Gemara, in addition to natural death, and a wicked person is called dead even during his lifetime. The phrase “Who revives the dead with abundant mercy” refers to the unlearned, who by strict law would not merit resurrection, but merit it by benefiting Torah scholars through commerce. See the thirteenth chapter of Kesubos. The phrase “See, please, our affliction” does not refer to redemption from exile, for that has already been mentioned in the blessings Boneh Yerushalayim and Tzemach David. Rather, it refers to constant misfortunes. See the beginning of the third chapter of Megillah. The blessing Hashivah alludes to the verse, “I will betroth you to Me with righteousness, justice, kindness, and mercy,” as stated in the Midrash. Because Avraham practiced righteousness and justice, Hashem granted his descendants kindness and mercy. When they ceased practicing righteousness and justice, these ceased, and when they return to practicing righteousness and justice, as stated, “Tzion will be redeemed through justice,” the Holy One, blessed is He, adds mercy and kindness for them. The phrase “Sound a great shofar” means a shofar great in distinction, which is a sign of freedom for Israel and of the return of the Land. See the additions in Yad Aharon to Orach Chaim, simanim 515 through 519, at length.
