(3) Some permit, etc. Responsa Radbaz, volume 3, siman 532, also discusses the matter at length and permits it. He writes that he possessed several questions that one of the Rishonim had asked from Heaven through seclusion, prayers, and invocations of Divine Names, and that answers were given to his questions. He asked about this matter as well, and they answered that it is permitted. See there, where he strongly warns against changing the custom. See also Bach, siman 68, who discusses this at length, and Responsa Chavos Yair, siman 238, which rules that the piyyutim should be recited and the custom should not be changed.
(4) To recite them. See Peri Chadash, who strongly warns that because of them one must not allow the times for Krias Shema and prayer to pass, nor prolong them until after midday. See there. If one sees that the congregation will miss the time for Krias Shema because of the length of the piyyutim, he should not wait for them. As soon as the chazzan begins Yotzer Or, he should begin quietly on his own and pray in order until after “Hashem your God is true.” There he should wait silently and should not speak, even words of Torah, until the chazzan reaches that place, and then continue praying with him in order.
