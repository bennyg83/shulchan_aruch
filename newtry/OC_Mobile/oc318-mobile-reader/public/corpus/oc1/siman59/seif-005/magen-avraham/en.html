The second person need not. This implies that if he wishes to begin from the start, he may do so, as Beit Yosef writes. Darkei Moshe, however, writes that he may not, because the later authorities rule like the Yerushalmi. Consequently, if he begins from the start, it is a blessing in vain.
From Kedushah onward. The students of Rabbeinu Yonah write in the fifth chapter of Berachos: If he erred in the ofanim, or from the ofanim until the end of the blessing, he need not return except to ‘To the blessed God,’ for from there onward it is considered the beginning of a blessing. This implies that if he erred within the ofanim, he begins again with the ofanim. The language of the Yerushalmi likewise indicates this: once he completed ‘Holy,’ he completed the blessing. Levush writes likewise, and this is also implied by Shulchan Aruch's wording, ‘If he erred from Kedushah onward,’ contrary to Bach, who disagrees.
