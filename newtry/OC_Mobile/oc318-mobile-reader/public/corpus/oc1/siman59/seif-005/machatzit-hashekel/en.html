(Note 5) With fewer than ten [men]. Thus also in Darkei Moshe, and this is his language... The intent of Magen Avraham from Rama's words that the shaliach tzibbur fulfills [the obligation] for an individual even if he is expert. However, that the shaliach tzibbur does not fulfill with fewer than ten seems to refer specifically to an expert. For Beit Yosef discusses above from this perspective that one who is not expert is fulfilled even with fewer than ten; therefore Magen Avraham writes that this is not so — for certainly this is not Rama's intent. For this law originates from Terumat HaDeshen, and there it is explained that even for one who is not expert the shaliach tzibbur does not fulfill with fewer than ten. If so, it emerges that Rama's intent is also that even for one who is not expert ten are required to fulfill his obligation.

Although "with those in the fields," etc. — know that Rivash wrote on what the Tur cites in the name of the responsum of the Rosh, that the blessings of Yotzer Or and Aravit should be recited with the shaliach tzibbur at a leisurely pace, etc., as written in Shulchan Aruch. He concluded with his reason, and this is his language: for a person cannot constantly concentrate with the shaliach tzibbur in silence; and even if he were concentrating, in the middle of the blessing his heart would turn to other matters — he has lost his concentration, for he interrupted in the middle. But when a person recites with his mouth, even if he recites part of it without concentration he has fulfilled his obligation, etc. — thus far the Rosh. Rivash challenged this: behold, they conclude at the end of tractate Rosh Hashanah in the dispute of Rabban Gamliel and the Rabbis regarding blessings on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur, that the Sages hold that the shaliach tzibbur fulfills only for one who is not expert, and Rabban Gamliel holds that he fulfills even for the expert — and that is when one hears the blessing from the mouth of the shaliach tzibbur. They conclude that the halakhah follows Rabban Gamliel on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur because they are long blessings and not everyone is expert in them; and the halakhah follows the Sages on the rest of the days of the year, that the shaliach tzibbur fulfills only for one who is not expert. If so, why does the Rosh need the reason that he did not rely on hearing the blessing from the mouth of the shaliach tzibbur lest his heart turn in the middle to other matters? Tell [us] from the law itself: the shaliach tzibbur does not fulfill for an expert on the rest of the days of the year. Rivash answered, and this is his language: and perhaps one could say that specifically regarding blessings of tefillah, which are supplications (meaning that it is fitting for each person to pray for himself, for a person's mind is more settled when he prays with his mouth), but regarding blessings of Keriat Shema and the like, by hearing from the shaliach tzibbur one fulfills his obligation — provided that he concentrates on all his words — thus far Rivash. And so ruled Shulchan Aruch: behold, for Rivash the blessing of Keriat Shema is lighter than tefillah; for in the blessing of Keriat Shema, even on all the days of the year the shaliach tzibbur fulfills even for an expert.

But in Terumat HaDeshen it is explained to the contrary — on the contrary, blessings of Keriat Shema are stricter than tefillah for this matter: for that which we learned in tractate Megillah 23b, "One does not recite the Shema with fewer than ten" — Terumat HaDeshen explains in the name of Rambam, chapter 3 of Berakhot, and this is his language: although "with those in the fields," etc., as Magen Avraham wrote here — all the language of Terumat HaDeshen there, and his intent is that we say there in tractate Rosh Hashanah regarding "with those in the fields" who are under duress and cannot come to the city, the shaliach tzibbur fulfills them even if they were in the fields and did not hear the tefillah from the mouth of the shaliach tzibbur (not like Rashi's explanation there in tractate Rosh Hashanah). He explains that which we do not recite [Shema], etc., mentioned above — meaning those people who did not hear from the mouth of the shaliach tzibbur the blessing of Keriat Shema, who were under duress and could not come to the synagogue when the shaliach tzibbur blessed; now, when one wishes to fulfill them by having the shaliach tzibbur recite the blessing of Keriat Shema and they hear and answer Amen, ten are required — and on this Terumat HaDeshen wrote "although with those in the fields who are under duress," etc. He holds that this law of "with those in the fields" who are exempt because of duress applies without distinction between Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur and the rest of the days of the year, for the reason for the exemption is duress; if so, there is no reason to distinguish (not like the view of the Rosh, who holds that specifically on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur those in the fields are exempt). If so, why must one recite Shema for those people who were not in the synagogue when the shaliach tzibbur blessed Keriat Shema? They should fulfill their obligation through the blessing of the shaliach tzibbur even if they were not in the synagogue, since they were under duress (for one who was negligent and did not come to the synagogue alone — certainly the Sages did not enact a remedy for him ab initio), like "with those in the fields." On this Terumat HaDeshen answered that the blessing of Keriat Shema is stricter than the blessing of tefillah, and the shaliach tzibbur cannot fulfill for those in the fields through the blessing of Keriat Shema until they come to the synagogue and hear from the mouth of the shaliach tzibbur and answer Amen.

Therefore Shulchan Aruch wrote "one does not recite Shema," etc. — behold, according to Terumat HaDeshen, on the contrary the blessing of Keriat Shema is stricter than tefillah regarding the shaliach tzibbur fulfilling [the obligation], not as Rivash proved from the Rosh as above.

It implies that it deals with one who is not expert — for otherwise, even in tefillah he would not fulfill his obligation. Meaning, according to Terumat HaDeshen, who holds that blessings of Keriat Shema are stricter than tefillah regarding the shaliach tzibbur fulfilling [the obligation], not like Rivash. If so, from what Terumat HaDeshen wrote that they are not exempt except through hearing and answering Amen, it implies that in any case through hearing from the mouth of the shaliach tzibbur they fulfill their obligation — and we have established (in siman 124) on the rest of the days of the year like the Rabbis of Rabban Gamliel, that the shaliach tzibbur does not fulfill in tefillah for the expert, as above. All the more so he does not fulfill for an expert in the blessing of Keriat Shema, which are stricter for this matter according to Terumat HaDeshen. If so, it emerges that Terumat HaDeshen deals with one who is not expert — for even in tefillah the shaliach tzibbur can fulfill all year round, and the same applies to the blessing of Keriat Shema even though they are stricter than tefillah; nevertheless "one does not recite," etc., with fewer than ten, and specifically ten are required — behold, even one who is not expert, the shaliach tzibbur does not fulfill except with ten.

"The one who knows how to bless," etc. — meaning two who ate together and one of them knows how to bless. Meaning, one knows how to bless Birkat ha-Mazon and the other is ignorant and does not know how to bless Birkat ha-Mazon, as explained below in siman 193, that blessings of enjoyment are different — they do not require ten.

And in Ran, etc. — therefore he challenged the Geonim, who explained that which "one does not recite Shema with fewer than ten" — they do not begin the blessing of Keriat Shema so that one blesses and the others fulfill through his blessing except with ten, and this is because of the Kedushah that is in the blessing of Yotzer. And even in the blessing of Keriat Shema of Aravit, in which there is no Kedushah, nevertheless the Rabbis did not distinguish. And therefore Ran wrote, and this is his language: I am astonished at this explanation — for if so, it implies that in tefillah, wherever there are ten, the shaliach tzibbur fulfills for the expert; whereas at the end of Rosh Hashanah it implies that in tefillah, wherever there are ten, the shaliach tzibbur does not fulfill for the experts — thus far. And this is because he understood that the Geonim deal with an expert, and therefore he was astonished at them. But according to what Magen Avraham wrote, to explain the words of Terumat HaDeshen that it deals specifically with one who is not expert — the same applies, that one can explain the view of the Geonim that they deal with one who is not expert, and there is room to decide between them. See section 6 in the name of Levush, who holds that the blessing of Shacharit also requires ten and deals with an expert.

And siman 594, thus it is.
