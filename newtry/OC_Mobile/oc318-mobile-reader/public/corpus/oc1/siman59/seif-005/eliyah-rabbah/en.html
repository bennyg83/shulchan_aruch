[5] That he begins from Kedushah etc. Meaning in the "opanim" — he erred in the "opanim" he should begin; and the "opanim" (Levush). And it seems to me "Kadosh Kadosh" is at the end of the blessing; and in Lechem Chamudot he wrote page 42 that Levush, in excusing his honor, erred in the "opanim," for behold whoever did not answer is also "Baruch the Omnipresent m'mekomo" — is that finishing a blessing? He must begin from the head; but nevertheless he ruled like Levush. And I also found in Prisha that he agreed to Levush's explanation; and so it implies from the Yerushalmi that Beit Yosef brought, and this is its language: since you answered "Kadosh Kadosh" etc., and so Rabbeinu Yonah brought — it implies "Kadosh" is also finishing a blessing. And the reason seems to me: in chapter Gid HaNashe it says "Baruch kevod the Omnipresent m'mekomo" — "opanim" they call it; and if so for angels "Kadosh" is a conclusion. Levush further wrote: if he erred from there onward he begins from "Kel Baruch" — "Baruch kevod the Omnipresent" is at the end of the blessing, it seems to me; and so I found in Rabbeinu Yonah, Agudah, and Sefer Zichron for the Gaon R. Yitzchak HaKohen chapter Ein Om'din — explicitly. Menachem Azariah wrote in responsum siman 102: in Kedushah of "me'yushav" — if he was sitting and stood, behold this is an error; and if he was standing and sat, it is evident he is not wise — until here; and see above sk 3. And perhaps Menachem Azariah speaks of Kedushah and "Uva leTziyon"; but in Knesset HaGedolah it implies he speaks of this Kedushah; and so it implies in the responsa there in what he wrote "me'yushav" etc.:
