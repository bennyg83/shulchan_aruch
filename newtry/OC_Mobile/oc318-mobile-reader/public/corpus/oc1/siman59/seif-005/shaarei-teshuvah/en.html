Se'if 1. See Be'er Heitev. See Birkei Yosef, who explains that according to Beit Yosef, even after Yotzer HaMe'orot one should not answer Amen; rather, he should conclude together with the shaliach tzibbur. This is contrary to the practice of some who answer Amen after Yotzer HaMe'orot but not after HaBocher; see there. This indeed appears to follow from Beit Yosef's wording. The plain wording of Shulchan Aruch, however, implies that this applies specifically after HaBocher, because that blessing immediately precedes Keriat Shema, so answering would constitute an interruption between the blessing and the matter over which it was recited. This requires study. Beit Yosef explains that if one concluded before the shaliach tzibbur, he answers Amen. He should not, however, hurry to conclude first; rather, he should finish together with the shaliach tzibbur and then not answer Amen, because that would be like answering after his own blessing. See below, siman 81, in Darkei Moshe, that when answering Amen he should intend the words El Melech Ne'eman. Nevertheless, to complete the additional three words, he should rely upon what he hears from the shaliach tzibbur when the latter repeats, “the Omnipresent your God; true.” An individual, however, should say El Melech Ne'eman, because the Zohar calls one who omits the required completion “distorted,” etc.; see there. Shelah writes that the Sephardic practice appears correct: one should not answer Amen but should conclude together with the shaliach tzibbur, so as not to interrupt between Ahavah and accepting the yoke of the kingdom of Heaven. Therefore, both at Shacharit and at Ma'ariv the blessing concludes with “in love,” and immediately after the first verse of Keriat Shema one begins again, “You shall love”; see there. Nevertheless, if one concluded before the shaliach tzibbur, he should answer Amen and intend El Melech Ne'eman, as written in siman 61. See Perach Shoshan, klal 1, siman 15, for the reason one should not answer Amen. Although it is permitted to interrupt out of fear or honor, as Tur writes in siman 66, while one is occupied with praising the Omnipresent he should not interrupt for a different praise. See there, where he writes that one who needs to put on a tallit and tefillin after Baruch She'amar should initially recite several verses of Pesukei DeZimra and put them on between one psalm and another. After the fact, if he put them on immediately between Baruch She'amar and Pesukei DeZimra, he did not lose the blessing. See there.
