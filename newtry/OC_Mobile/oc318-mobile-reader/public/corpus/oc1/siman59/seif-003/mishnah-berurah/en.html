Rather, he says, ‘and they say with awe: Holy,’ and, ‘and the Ofanim,’ and so forth, ‘say: Blessed.’ Peri Chadash writes that even when one could have prayed with the congregation but is praying alone because he arrived late, since a congregation is present he recites these passages, even quietly, according to all opinions.
The widespread custom, and so forth. Biur HaGra agrees in practice with the latter opinion. Since one should not depart from the custom, when reciting it alone it is proper to endeavor to chant it with the melody and cantillation used when reading verses. Peri Megadim writes similarly in the name of Levush.
And when they answer, and so forth. If possible, it is preferable to recite this Kedushah while seated.
Aloud. This applies specifically with a congregation. An individual says it quietly, according to Eliyah Rabbah. Shaarei Teshuvah, however, writes that there is no objection even if an individual says it aloud.
