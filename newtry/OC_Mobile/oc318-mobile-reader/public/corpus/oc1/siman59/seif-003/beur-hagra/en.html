Seif 3: Some say, and so forth; and some say, and so forth. This also appears from the Mishnah in Megillah 23b; see Rashi there, s.v. ‘One may not divide,’ and so forth. Seemingly, the first blessing does not require ten except because of Kedushah, and Ran writes likewise there. Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe already addressed this in siman 69; see there. All their explanations are strained. This is also the view of the Geonim and Rambam, as Ran proves there in Megillah. The students of Rabbeinu Yonah further prove this in the third chapter of Berachos from the Yerushalmi's statement, ‘They began with ten and some departed,’ and so forth. This implies that they began with dividing Krias Shema, namely the blessing. What then does ‘they complete’ mean? Although he rejects that proof there, as cited in Beit Yosef there and as I write below, the first opinion supported itself from the end of chapter 16 of Maseches Soferim, cited by Mordechai in the third chapter of Berachos. It states there: ‘A minor who may not divide Krias Shema cannot say the Kedushah of Yotzer when alone, but in public he responds with them. An adult who divides Krias Shema may say Kedushah, because it is part of the order. But the Kedushah of the Amidah, since one must say, “We shall reverence You and sanctify You,” and so forth, should not be recited with fewer than ten.’ Yet that source appears to prove the opposite. At first glance it is difficult, for when it says, ‘An adult who divides,’ and so forth, it necessarily refers to ten. Why then does it add, ‘because,’ and so forth, but contrasts this with the Amidah? Rather, its meaning is as follows. Although six who did not yet hear suffice for dividing Krias Shema, as stated there at the end of chapter 10, the Kedushah of the Amidah requires ten who did not yet hear it, as stated in the Mishnah in Megillah and in Maseches Soferim there. Kaddish and Barechu may not be recited with fewer than ten. Our teachers in the West say seven, and give a reason for their position. This implies that according to the first view, precisely ten who have not heard are required. Even according to our teachers, this applies only to Barechu, which they derive from a verse, whereas Kedushah remains governed by the original rule. Thus, when it says that a minor cannot, and so forth, it means that since he cannot divide Krias Shema, he also cannot say Kedushah. In public, however, and so forth. An adult, and so forth, may likewise say Kedushah with seven or six, because it is part of the order of dividing Krias Shema, unlike the Kedushah of the Amidah. The Zohar states this explicitly, and Beit Yosef cites it. Darkei Moshe, however, writes that since the matter is explicit in Maseches Soferim, one need not be concerned for the Zohar. This is the meaning of Rama's gloss, ‘It has already,’ and so forth. Yet I have already written that Maseches Soferim itself maintains the opposite. The earlier sages likewise instituted reciting baraisos in place of Kedushah because they accepted the latter opinion. According to the first opinion, there would be no need for this, since it is no better than the Kedushah of U'Va LeTzion and Yotzer.
And when they respond, and so forth. This is from the Yerushalmi and Tosefta in the first chapter: ‘One does not respond together with the one reciting the blessing. Rabbi Yehudah would respond together with the one reciting the blessing: Holy, holy, holy; Hashem Tzevakos; the whole earth is filled with His glory.’ In certain manuscripts, all of these were said by Rabbi Yehudah together with the one reciting the blessing. Raavyah explains that ‘respond’ means to answer aloud, as in ‘Miriam answered them,’ and ‘the Leviim shall answer.’ Hagahos Maimoniyos cites this in the text of the prayers, seif katan 3.
