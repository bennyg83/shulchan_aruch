Siman 63—some say, etc., and some say, etc. And so too in the Mishnah in Megillah 23b and Rashi there s.v. ein porsin, etc.; and apparently the first berakhah does not require ten except on account of kedushah—and so wrote the Ran there; and Beit Yosef already noted doubt in this at siman 69—see there; and all their words are strained. And so ruled the geonim and Rambam, and the Ran proved it in Megillah there; and further the Tur proved it in chapter 3 of Berakhot from what is stated in the Yerushalmi: they began with ten and left, etc.; and he explained that they began with the recitation of Shema—that is, the berakhah—and what does "finish" mean? Even though he rejected it there as Beit Yosef wrote there, and as written below; and the first view is supported from the end of chapter 3 of Berakhot in the Mordekhai, and thus: there, a minor who does not recite Shema cannot say kedushah in Yotzer by himself, but in a congregation he answers with them; and an adult who recites Shema may say kedushah because it is in order—but kedushah of the Amidah, since it must say "ne'aritzekha venakdishkha," etc., the law is not to say it with fewer than ten; but from there it implies the opposite—for apparently it is difficult, for it says "and an adult who recites," etc.; therefore it deals with ten. If so, what is "because it is in order"—but, etc.; rather this is what it says: even though for reciting Shema six suffice who did not hear, as written there at the end of chapter 3, nevertheless for kedushah of the eighteen [blessings] ten are required who did not hear, as in the Mishnah in Megillah and in Mishnat Rabbi Eliezer there; and one cannot say Kaddish and Barekhu with fewer than ten. Our rabbis in the West say them with seven, and it is possible to reconcile their words: apparently at the beginning one needs ten specifically who did not hear, and even per our rabbis—specifically for Barekhu, which is derived from a verse; but kedushah remains on the first law. And this is what it says: "and a minor," etc.—meaning, since he cannot recite Shema he cannot say kedushah either; but in a congregation, etc.; "and an adult," etc.—one could also say kedushah with seven or six, since it is in the order of reciting Shema, unlike that of the eighteen [blessings]. And so explicitly in the Zohar, and Beit Yosef brought it; but in Darkei Moshe he wrote: since it is explicit in Mishnat Rabbi Eliezer, one need not heed the Zohar; and this is what the hagahah says: "and already," etc.; but I have already written that Mishnat Rabbi Eliezer also holds the opposite, and so the ancients instituted to say in place of kedushah beraitot, because they hold like the last view—for the first view has no need, for it is not better than the order of kedushah and Yotzer.

And when they answer, etc. Yerushalmi and Tosefta chapter 1—and one does not answer with the one who blesses. Rabbi Yehuda would answer with the one who blesses one hundred, one hundred, one hundred, ten, ninety—how much more so; and in Bekhorot all these Rabbi Yehuda would say with the one who blesses. Rabbi Avi haEzrachi explains that he means answering in a loud voice—from the language "and Miriam answered them," etc., and "and the Levites answered," etc.; and they brought this in Hagahot Maimoniyot in the formula of prayers, section 3—and see what is written in siman 54.
