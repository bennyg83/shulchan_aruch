Aloud. See Be'er Heitev. Magen Avraham writes that it is recited specifically while sitting, and Sefer HaKavanot writes likewise. Regarding the phrase “and with a holy melody,” with kedoshah vocalized with a cholam, Shaliach Tzibbur writes that in a manuscript of Sefer HaKavanot by Rabbi Chayim Vital, kedushah is vocalized with a shuruk and refers to all of them together, etc. This also appears in old books. Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah writes that it has a cholam, as it appears in Sefer Heikhalot, etc.; see there. Shaliach Tzibbur writes that the text should read, “Opposite the seraphim; opposite them they offer praise,” as the Arizal, of blessed memory, wrote. He further writes: I heard grammarians say that when one recites Ba'al Milchamot in prayer, he must strongly articulate the ayin in ba'al, so that it not sound as though he says bal, “not,” Heaven forbid, etc. In the word meherah, he should likewise articulate it so that it not sound as though it is written with an alef; this is the language of Rabbi Chayim Vital. Similarly, in the verse “He shall receive a blessing from the Omnipresent,” one should extend the reading of the alef in me'et clearly, so that, Heaven forbid, it not be understood as met, “dead.” Levush explains that the congregation answers this Kedushah aloud so that they will recite it together, like the angels; see there. This applies specifically in a congregation. For an individual, siman 132 explains that it is recited quietly, as Eliyahu Rabbah writes; see there. There I wrote in the name of Shaliach Tzibbur that regarding Kedushah DeSidra, there is no objection if an individual recites it aloud; see there. The same applies here.
