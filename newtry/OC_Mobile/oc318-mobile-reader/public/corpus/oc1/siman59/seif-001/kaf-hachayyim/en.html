1) [Seif 1] "Blessed are You, Hashem our God, King of the universe, Who forms light," etc. The reason a blessing is recited every day over the light of the sun, unlike the blessing over fire, which is recited only on Motzaei Shabbos, is that sunlight is renewed each day. Tosafot writes likewise in Perek Makom Shenahagu. Pri Chadash, letter 1; Shalmei Tzibbur, page 90c. 2) There, "Who forms light," etc. When beginning this blessing, one should touch the arm tefillin, which correspond to Beriah in the aspect of action, corresponding to the prayer of Yotzer, which is in Beriah in the aspect of speech. Shaar HaKavanos, discourse on Yotzer and Kriat Shema, page 18d, and in the prayer text, page 51b. This was cited above in siman 48. See also what we wrote in siman 25, letters 27 and 32, and siman 48, letter 1, beginning "Know also this introduction." When touching the arm tefillin during Yotzer Or, one should touch it when saying the word "u'vorei" in "u'vorei choshech," because that word alludes to Beriah, as stated in Shaar HaKavanos, page 19a. One should not touch the head tefillin now at the beginning of Yotzer Or. Rather, when he reaches Shemoneh Esrei he should touch the head tefillin, because both correspond to Atzilut: the head tefillin in the aspect of action and the prayer in the aspect of speech. This is explained in Shaar HaKavanos, the first and third discourses on the morning prayer and the first discourse on the Amidah. I likewise saw written in the name of Rabbi Shmuel Vital, in manuscript on the margin of Pri Etz Chaim, Shaar Kriat Shema, chapter 1, regarding the Arizal's statement that at the beginning of the ascent of Beriah, namely Yotzer Or, one should hold and touch the arm tefillin. It says: "This is correct, because Yotzer and the arm tefillin are both in Beriah, as explained by us. Therefore one should touch specifically the arm tefillin at Yotzer Or and not the head tefillin until the beginning of the Amidah. For this reason the Rav omitted touching the head tefillin at u'vorei choshech." End quote. Shalmei Tzibbur likewise writes in the name of the Rav that before beginning Yotzer Or one should touch the arm tefillin, and before beginning the Amidah one should touch the head tefillin. Chesed LaAlafim similarly writes: "When saying Yotzer Or, one should touch the arm tefillin. One should not touch the head tefillin until immediately before the silent Shemoneh Esrei. This is the correct practice according to the mystical tradition and the custom of the community of pietists in the Beit El study hall in Jerusalem." Kaf HaChaim by Rabbi Chaim Palagi and Ben Ish Chai write likewise. This is contrary to Mishnat Chassidim, cited by Yad Aharon and Baer Heitev, which states that one touches the arm tefillin at Yotzer Or and the head tefillin at u'vorei choshech. Rather, one should not touch the head tefillin until Shemoneh Esrei. One should touch it after saying, "Hashem, open my lips," and before beginning the Amidah, for there begins the aspect of Atzilut, as explained in Shaar HaKavanos, first discourse on the Amidah, page 28d. 3) There, "Who forms light." The blessing Yotzer Or, Shemoneh Esrei, and Birkat HaMazon each contain every letter of the alphabet except the final form of the letter peh. This teaches that one who concentrates during these 3 is protected so that Satan, an evil occurrence, or any prosecutor, even when wrath surges, has no power over his prayer or his meal. Ateret Zekenim; Shalmei Tzibbur, page 90b. 4) There, "Who forms light." One must pause between Yotzer and Or so that the words do not sound like "Yotzeror." Ateret Zekenim; Shalmei Tzibbur. The later authorities write likewise. See what we wrote above in siman 48, letter 1, in the name of Shaar HaKavanos regarding points of precise pronunciation that pertain to this blessing. 5) "Alone, from then." When saying the word me'az, one should intend the union represented by the combined Divine Name Yahadonai, because the numerical value of az is 8, corresponding to the 8 letters of that Name. Shaar HaKavanos, discourse on the intentions of Yotzer, page 19a; Shalmei Tzibbur, page 90d; Kaf HaChaim by Rabbi Chaim Palagi, siman 14, letter 4.
