Seif katan 1, ‘and he did not say,’ and so forth. This is not comparable to what is written in siman 209, that if he concluded properly he fulfilled his obligation. That case concerns one who held a cup of beer, erred and said, ‘Blessed are You, Hashem our God, King of the universe, Who creates the fruit of the vine,’ and within the time required to utter a brief phrase remembered and also concluded, ‘by Whose word all things came into being.’ Thus, his full recitation was, ‘Blessed are You, Hashem our God, King of the universe, Who creates the fruit of the vine, by Whose word all things came into being,’ and he fulfilled his obligation.
For there the formula is brief. Meaning, there the blessing contains only its concluding formula, and nothing was instituted in its middle. He corrupted its conclusion by saying ‘Who creates the fruit of the vine.’ Since he then said ‘by Whose word all things came into being,’ he corrected the conclusion that he had corrupted. Here, however, the Sages instituted a lengthy formula, meaning that words are added between the opening and the conclusion. He corrupted the formula in the middle of the blessing. Even though he concluded properly, he did not correct the middle of the blessing.
It appears to me that if he did not remember. Meaning, wherever he must repeat the blessing Yotzer Or according to what is explained here in Shulchan Aruch, but did not remember that he must repeat it until after he completed the blessing Ahavah Rabbah, he should not interrupt between Ahavah Rabbah and Krias Shema to recite Yotzer Or, as explained in seif 4.
