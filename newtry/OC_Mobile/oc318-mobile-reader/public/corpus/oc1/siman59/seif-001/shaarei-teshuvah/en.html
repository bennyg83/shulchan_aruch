Light. See Be'er Heitev. See Ateret Zekenim, who writes not to say, “and King of the universe,” but only “King of the universe,” etc. Abudraham, however, gives the text “and King of the universe,” and this is also the wording of the verse in Jeremiah. Shaliach Tzibbur writes that this proves what Lechem Rav writes in siman 1: specifically in prayer one must express himself in clear language, but this is unnecessary regarding titles of the Omnipresent. It is sufficient to recite the titles as they are written in the Torah; see there. Be'er Heitev cites Lechem Rav concerning the word koneihem and writes that the Arizal likewise used this form, etc. If so, here too one need not object, and one may say, “and King of the universe.” See Lechem Rav, who explains that “the will of their Maker” appears in the plural as an honorific, like “holy Gods,” because that is the language of Scripture. It appears to me that this explanation is unnecessary: the plural refers to those who were acquired, in the manner of the verse, “to their masters, the king of Egypt.” See there.
