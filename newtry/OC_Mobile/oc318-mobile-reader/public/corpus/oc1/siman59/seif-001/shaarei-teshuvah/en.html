Or — Be'er Heitev. And see in Ateret Zekenim not to say "u'melekh olam" but only "melekh olam," etc. And in Avudraham he wrote "melekh olam." And so it is in Scripture in Jeremiah. And he wrote in Sha'ar HaTziyun that from here it is proven, as Lechem Rav siman 1 wrote, that specifically in tefillah one must speak in clear language, but in designations of the Holy One, blessed be He, one need not do so — it suffices that we say the designations as written in the Torah, see there. And Be'er Heitev wrote in the words of Lechem Rav regarding the word "konem" — so wrote the Arizal, etc. And if so, also in this there is no need to be strict, and one may say "u'melekh olam," see there in Lechem Rav, for the intent of "konem" comes in plural language for glory, like "Elohim kedoshim," for such is the language of Scripture. And it seems to me one need not for this, for the plurality is upon those acquired; and per the verse "to their masters, to the king of Egypt," see there.
