Yotzer Or. One must pause between "yotzer" and "or," because where it is not heard it sounds like "yotzeror." "Meshartim ve'asher meshartav"—acronym mem-vav-mem, and it is the last name of the seventy-two-letter name; therefore one must be careful not to skip the vav of "ve'asher." When one says "yotzer or" one switches to the hand tefillah, and when one says "u'vorei choshekh" one switches to the head tefillah. Ma'aritzim umekadishim—all with mem, and some read with nun; and those who say with mem need to pause between "mamlikhim" and "et shem haEl haMelekh." "Ratzon konem" and not "ratzon konem"—Lechem Rav siman 1, and likewise the Arizal z"l and many poskim; and some say "ratzon konem," see Atzei Tzafon. "Ahuvim berurim giborim"—acronym alef-bet-gimel, and it is the first name of the forty-two-letter name. Oshehum be'eimah—acronym ayin-bet; and whoever intends these names is not harmed all that day (Ateret Zekenim). "L'El barukh"—the sweetness of the lamed with kamatz; "l'melekh El chai"—the lamed with patach; and the Arizal z"l wrote all with kamatz. See Yad Aharon.
