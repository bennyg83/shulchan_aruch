In the blessing, ‘Blessed are You, Hashem, Who forms light,’ and so forth: We find that the blessing of Yotzer Or, the Amidah, and Bircas HaMazon each contain every letter of the alphabet except final peh. This teaches that one who concentrates properly during these three is not subject to Satan, harmful occurrence, or any accuser, even if anger and wrath have surged forth; they have no power over his prayer or his meal. This is cited by Eliyah Mizrachi in the name of Maharil. One must pause between ‘Yotzer’ and ‘or,’ so that it not sound like a single word, ‘yotzeror.’ The initial letters of ‘mesharsim va'asher mesharsav’ spell mum, which is the final Name among the seventy-two Names. One must therefore take care not to omit the vav of ‘va'asher.’ One should not say ‘u'Melech olam,’ with a vav, but rather ‘Melech olam’ without a vav, because ‘u'Melech’ could imply, Heaven forbid, two authorities. This is from Maharam. The initial letters of ‘ahuvim, berurim, gibborim’ spell Abag, one of the Names within the forty-two-letter Name. The initial letters of ‘osim b'eimah’ have the numerical value seventy-two. Whoever concentrates upon these Names will not be harmed throughout that day. ‘Retzon Koneihem’ is written without a yud between the nun and the heh. Some do not include the word ‘b'eimah,’ because awe applies to those who are distant, whereas fear applies to those who are near. This is from Maharshal. I have already written many times, however, that one should not corrupt the wording of all the siddurim. Regarding ‘meshabchim u'mefa'arim,’ some versions spell all these words with a final nun. Their numerical value is three hundred, corresponding to the Name Matzpatz, and also equals the numerical value of ‘b'rachamim.’ Other versions spell them all with a final mem. Those who say them with a mem must pause between ‘mamlichim’ and ‘es Shem haKel haMelech.’ In ‘laKel Baruch,’ the lamed has a kamatz, while in ‘u'leMelech Kel chai’ it has a patach. This is from Lechem Min HaShamayim.
