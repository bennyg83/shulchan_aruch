In Beit Yosef on Yotzer Or, etc.: Behold we find in the blessing Yotzer Or, in the Amidah of eighteen [blessings], and in Birkat HaMazon every letter of the alef-bet except peh—to tell you that whoever has proper kavanah in these three [prayers] has no satan, no evil mishap, and no accuser, even though an angry king, etc., rules over his prayer and also over his meal (Magen Avraham, Maharil). One must pause between "yotzer" and "or," because where it is not heard it sounds like "yotzeror." "Meshartim ve'asher meshartav"—acronym mem-vav-mem, and it is the last name of the seventy-two-letter name; therefore one must be careful not to skip the vav of "ve'asher." One should not say "u'melekh olam" but only, without vav, "melekh olam," because "u'melekh" implies two authorities, heaven forbid (Maharam). "Ahuvim berurim giborim"—acronym alef-bet-gimel, and it is one name of the forty-two-letter name. "Oshehum be'eimah"—acronym ayin-bet. And whoever has kavanah in these names is not harmed all that day. "Ratzon konem" without yod between nun and heh. There are those who do not read "be'eimah," because awe applies to distant ones and fear to near ones (Maharshal). And I have already written many times that one should not disturb all the established arrangements. "Meshabchim umefarim"—some read everything with nun, and they add up to vav-nun in gematria shin, like the count of the name Mets-Tzadi-Peh-Tzadi; also in gematria "b'rachamim." And some read everything with mem; and those who say with mem need to pause between "mamlikhim" and "et shem haEl haMelekh." "L'El barukh" with kamatz under the lamed, and "l'melekh El chai" with patach (Lechem min haShamayim).
