"To fulfill," etc. Mahari Abuhav gives another reason: if we did not mention the attribute of night during the day, it would appear that darkness is evil, but this is not so, for "He made everything beautiful in its time." The Shulchan Aruch omitted this reason because Beit Yosef objected that according to it we should not need to mention the attribute of day at night. In my humble opinion there is no difficulty. Mahari Abuhav's intent is that whenever we bless over 1 matter alone, it can imply that its opposite is bad. Accordingly, if we failed to mention day at night, it could similarly appear that light is bad. This is easy to understand.
