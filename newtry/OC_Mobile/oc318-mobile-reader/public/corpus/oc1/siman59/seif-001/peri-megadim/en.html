What he wrote on Rambam chapter 1 of Laws of Keriat Shema, siman 13, Magen Avraham note 7, and Pri Chadash. And according to Rambam's view in Mishneh Torah — the whole day is its time, and rabbinically they said one who reads at its time has reward like one who reads the Torah but not specifically; rather he has reward for Keriat Shema. And Shulchan Aruch ruled until four hours. And because of this there is no proof that Torah Keriat Shema [applies] all day and the berakhot are rabbinic — they said only until four hours. Furthermore, the rabbis uprooted Keriat Shema from it as written in note 2. And Levush wrote that the fourth hour is also a bit of the time of rising — it implies that one fulfills the fourth hour in Keriat Shema. And I do not know from where he has this. And Eliyahu Rabba challenged him. And regarding whether there is makeup for Keriat Shema and its berakhot it will be explained elsewhere in Magen Avraham note 8.

The author brought in his second explanation the explanation of Rabbenu Yonah; but Prisha held to the words of Mahari"l that if we were saying only "Yotzer Or" it would imply that we give thanks that He did not create darkness, and at night we give thanks without [saying] He created light but only "night is rest for the body," etc. And Rabbi Akiva Eiger did not see Prisha's words. A blind person is obligated in the berakhah Yotzer Or — see siman 69. And he can fulfill others; but with the Havdalah candle he is exempt, for we require "that one benefit from its light," etc., siman 298. And if he fulfills others — he left in doubt in Eliyahu Rabba 298 note 23 and 397 — one who cannot smell — fulfilling others is in doubt, for whoever is not obligated in a matter cannot fulfill others. And one whose mouth pains him (Magen Avraham 41 — in tefillin his hand was cut off, and possibly the same if born thus) — if so, a deaf person whose hearing was restored afterward, why does he not fulfill others in blowing shofar? And it is in doubt now, and it will be explained elsewhere. That which they bless three berakhot in Keriat Shema in the morning and four at night — Levush wrote in siman 58, and I brought it in Taz 1 and Pri Chadash siman 67, in Yerushalmi end of chapter 1 of Berakhot: "You recited by day and by night — you recited night like day" — six in the morning and six in the evening, three berakhot and three parshiyot; and at night one does not [recite] tzitzit even though we do not say tzitzit at night, the enactment did not move from its place. And in Eliyahu Rabba 58:1 — seven "Ga'al Yisrael" — what is it? For it is one berakhah with "emet veyatziv"?

If thus he mentioned the reasoning that the conclusion requires [to be] similar to the opening (me'ein petichah), and therefore even if he concluded "Who has redeemed us, our God, King of the world, Creator of the luminaries" — all that at the beginning he did not say except "Ma'ariv aravim" alone — he has not fulfilled, for the conclusion is not similar to the opening. And see Prisha — Eliyahu Rabba brought note 2. And for Bach, in such a case he has fulfilled, for there is "shem u'malkhut" in "Yotzer ha-me'orot," see there. And behold in Shulchan Aruch that wrote "and he remembered immediately and said Yotzer Or" — if not within k'dei dibbur he has not fulfilled; and as Bach wrote that "shem u'malkhut" does not refer to Yotzer Or. And afterward he wrote "he said Ma'ariv aravim and did not say Yotzer Or" — the implication is only after k'dei dibbur he has not fulfilled. And conversely, Yotzer Or and Ma'ariv aravim — whether immediately or not immediately — l'adif tfei, and he has fulfilled if he concluded properly. And Av Beit Chadash: if in the conclusion he mixed Ma'ariv aravim and Yotzer ha-me'orot he has not fulfilled, for the conclusion needs only Yotzer ha-me'orot — and see further elsewhere in Magen Avraham note 1. Behold what Bach wrote: if he concluded with "shem u'malkhut" Yotzer ha-me'orot even though at the beginning he said only Ma'ariv aravim — he has fulfilled even though he shortened. And as they said to lengthen — one is not permitted to shorten — one could say bedieved he has fulfilled. I again saw in Pri Chadash likewise according to his method that he holds everything follows the conclusion at the beginning, see there. And we have only the words of the author and Ra'avad z"l, and it will be explained elsewhere in Kaf Pei Zayin siman 61 "Barukh Rachmana Marei dehai pita" whether a long berakhah if he shortened whether he fulfills bedieved — and see in Magen Avraham note 1 from this.

Pri Chadash wrote that "u'vene'imah kedushah" — the dalet with cholam, and "u'vene'imah shel kedushah" — and here there is somewhat of a pause in the phrase, for if kedushah continues through all of them it should have said "be'safah berurah u'vene'imah" without "ve" before ne'imah. And the explanation of what Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Orchot Chayim — likewise the explanation, see there. And likewise in Sefer Shokena Neged. And Eliyahu Rabba note 3 (and Darkei Moshe does not imply thus). And I do not know — if so, in "be'kedushah u'vetaharah beshirah uvizimrah" it should also say "be'kedushah u'vetaharah veshirah vizimrah" without bet? And Tosefta Chagigah 13b s.v. "mezi'atan" — Pri Chadash brought that they all wrote kedushah — there is no proof neither for here nor for there. And in the siddur edition of the great grammarian Rabbi Zalman Henna z"l — kedushah with small shuruk and all the kafs are dagesh. I do not know why — if the word kedushah continues through all of them, the kaf should be raphe after alef-hei-vav-yod; whereas if kedushah is a pause, it is reasonable.

"L'melekh" with patach, "le'El" the lamed with kamatz — see in the siddur mentioned above. And Eliyahu Rabba wrote both with kamatz. Behold in that siddur he wrote regarding this that the Sephardim do not distinguish in their reading — and this is not a sufficient answer, for certainly even according to them there is some distinction between kamatz of ta'am gedol and patach; and furthermore, why would they need to change — let it be explicit: this patach and this kamatz.
