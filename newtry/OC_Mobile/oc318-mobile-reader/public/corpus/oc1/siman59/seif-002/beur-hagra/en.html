Seif 2: If he erred, and so forth; and the same applies, and so forth. The Tur's words are explained as requiring two conditions. First, in the opening portion of the blessing he must mention ‘Who forms light,’ either at the beginning or in the middle. Second, the conclusion must be proper. If either condition is lacking, he has not fulfilled his obligation. This requires study, for the Rosh's words do not appear to indicate this, nor does the Gemara appear to say so. The Gemara answers, ‘There it is different,’ and so forth, which indicates that the conclusion alone suffices. Even according to the final answer, ‘Rather, since,’ and so forth, it likewise appears that the conclusion suffices. His interpretation, however, follows the ruling that the unresolved question is decided leniently, and reads the baraisa in its straightforward sense because he returned and said ‘Who forms light,’ according to the Rif's explanation. Yet the baraisa's wording, ‘Its general principle is this,’ and so forth, implies that this refers to the conclusion of the blessing. Therefore, the primary ruling is that if he concluded ‘Who forms the luminaries,’ then even though he initially said the entire text of ‘Who brings on evenings,’ he has fulfilled his obligation according to every interpretation in the Gemara.
