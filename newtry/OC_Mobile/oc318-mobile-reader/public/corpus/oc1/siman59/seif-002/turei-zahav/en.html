If he erred, etc. Shulchan Aruch added some matters to the language of the Tur; and regarding the law, all his words are true — only in explaining the language of the Tur it seems to me somewhat in one direction, for it seems to our rabbi that what the Gemara said "everything follows the conclusion" means: if he concluded Ma'ariv aravim in the morning in every manner he has not fulfilled even though he said at the opening and also in the middle Yotzer Or; but if he concluded Yotzer ha-me'orot then he requires that he mention at least the berakhah Yotzer Or either at the opening or in the middle besides the conclusion — and our rabbi proved this from what the Talmud said: opened with Ma'ariv aravim and concluded with Yotzer [Or] — he has fulfilled; for if it had said "said Ma'ariv aravim and concluded with Yotzer" we would learn that in this he has not fulfilled, but rather he needs to open separately with Ma'ariv aravim and immediately remember and say Yotzer Or, also conclude properly — then he has fulfilled. The reason is that we require in the conclusion [to be] similar to the opening or similar [to what is] adjacent to the conclusion. And if so, also what the Talmud said: opened with Yotzer Or and concluded with Ma'ariv aravim — he has not fulfilled — meaning specifically if the opening alone was Yotzer Or and everything else Ma'ariv aravim; but if he said everything in Yotzer Or and concluded only Ma'ariv aravim alone, I would say here too he has fulfilled. There is no contradiction — rather the opposite, this is a stringency: that even though after the opening he said Ma'ariv aravim and also concluded in Ma'ariv aravim which is adjacent to the conclusion, nevertheless he has not fulfilled; and all the more so if he said everything in Yotzer Or and the conclusion alone in Ma'ariv aravim — for the conclusion was not similar to what was mentioned at all — he has not fulfilled. And nevertheless our rabbi said that if he opened with Yotzer Or and afterward said Ma'ariv aravim and also concluded in Ma'ariv aravim he has not fulfilled — meaning as a stringency as written; and all the more so if the conclusion alone is in Ma'ariv aravim. And what he wrote that if he opened and concluded in Yotzer Or he has fulfilled even though in the middle he said [part] of Ma'ariv aravim and it was not adjacent to the conclusion similar to the conclusion — from where did our rabbi [derive this]? And even though it was similar to the opening — from where is it effective? One could say that if so the Talmud should have said the stringency in the place that one says opened with Yotzer Or and concluded with Ma'ariv aravim — he has not fulfilled even if he concluded with Yotzer Or — for he did not say in the middle [part] of Yotzer Or; but we learn that in such a case he has fulfilled, for it is evident it is preferable more since the opening and conclusion are proper — so is the intent of our rabbi, etc.
