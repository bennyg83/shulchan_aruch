See also what we wrote above in siman 25, paragraphs 27 and 32, and in siman 48, paragraph 1, beginning with the words "Know also this introduction," etc.; see there. When touching the arm tefillin during the blessing Yotzer Or, one should touch it while saying the word "u'vorei" in the phrase "u'vorei choshech," because that word alludes to the world of Beriah, as stated in Shaar HaKavanos there, page 19a; see there. One should not, however, touch the head tefillin now, at the beginning of Yotzer Or. Rather, when he reaches Shemoneh Esrei he should then touch the head tefillin, for both are in Atzilus: the head tefillin in the dimension of action and the prayer in the dimension of speech. This is explained in Shaar HaKavanos, the first and third discourses on the morning prayer and the first discourse on the Amidah, and in our aforementioned comments; see there. I likewise saw the following written in the name of Rabbi Shmuel Vital, of blessed memory, in a handwritten marginal gloss to Pri Etz Chaim, Shaar Krias Shema, chapter 1. Concerning the Arizal's statement there that at the beginning of the ascent of the world of Beriah, namely Yotzer Or, one must take hold of the tefillin and touch the arm tefillin, etc., Rabbi Shmuel Vital writes: "Yotzer and the arm tefillin are both in the world of Beriah, etc., as explained by us. One should therefore specifically touch the arm tefillin during Yotzer Or, but not the head tefillin until the beginning of the Amidah. This is why the master omitted the practice of touching the head tefillin at 'u'vorei choshech.'" End quote from the manuscript. Shalmei Tzibbur, pages 90b and 108d, likewise writes in the Arizal's name that before beginning Yotzer Or one should touch the arm tefillin, and before beginning the Amidah one should touch the head tefillin; see there. Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 1, similarly writes: "When saying Yotzer Or, one should touch the arm tefillin. He should not touch the head tefillin until immediately before the silent Shemoneh Esrei. This is the correct practice according to the mystical tradition, and it is the practice of the community of pietists in the Beis El study hall here in the holy city of Jerusalem, may it be built and established speedily in our days." End quote. So too Kaf HaChaim by Rabbi Chaim Palagi, siman 14, paragraph 2, and Ben Ish Chai, parashas Shemos and parashas Beshalach, paragraph 1. This is contrary to Mishnas Chasidim, in the prayer of Beriah, chapter 1, paragraphs 7 and 8, cited by Yad Aharon in Hagahos HaTur and by Be'er Heitev, seif katan 1, which states that at Yotzer Or one touches the arm tefillin and at "u'vorei choshech" one touches the head tefillin; see there. Rather, one should not touch the head tefillin until Shemoneh Esrei, as stated above. He should touch it after saying the verse "Hashem, open my lips," etc., and before beginning the prayer, for that is the point at which the world of Atzilus begins, as explained in Shaar HaKavanos, the first discourse on the Amidah, page 28d; see there.
