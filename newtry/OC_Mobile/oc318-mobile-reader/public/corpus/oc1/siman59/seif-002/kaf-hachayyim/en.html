Shaar HaKavanos, discourse on Yotzer and Kriat Shema, page 18d, and in the prayer text, page 51b. This was cited above in siman 48. See also what we wrote above in siman 25, letters 27 and 32, and in siman 48, letter 1. When touching the arm tefillin during Yotzer Or, one should do so when saying the word "u'vorei" in the phrase "u'vorei choshech," because it alludes to Beriah, as stated in Shaar HaKavanos, page 19a. One should not touch the head tefillin now at the beginning of Yotzer Or. Rather, when he reaches Shemoneh Esrei he should touch the head tefillin, because both correspond to Atzilut: the head tefillin in the aspect of action and the prayer in the aspect of speech. This is explained in Shaar HaKavanos, the first and third discourses on the morning prayer and the first discourse on the Amidah. Rabbi Shmuel Vital likewise wrote in manuscript that because Yotzer and the arm tefillin both correspond to Beriah, one should specifically touch the arm tefillin during Yotzer Or and not the head tefillin until the beginning of the Amidah. Shalmei Tzibbur writes similarly in the name of the Rav: before beginning Yotzer Or one touches the arm tefillin, and before beginning the Amidah one touches the head tefillin. Chesed LaAlafim writes: "When saying Yotzer Or, one should touch the arm tefillin. One should not touch the head tefillin until immediately before the silent Shemoneh Esrei. This is the correct mystical practice, and this is the practice of the community of pietists in the Beit El study hall in Jerusalem." Kaf HaChaim by Rabbi Chaim Palagi and Ben Ish Chai likewise rule this way. This is contrary to Mishnat Chassidim, cited by Yad Aharon and Baer Heitev, which states that at Yotzer Or one touches the arm tefillin and at u'vorei choshech one touches the head tefillin. Rather, one should not touch the head tefillin until Shemoneh Esrei, as stated above. One should touch it after saying, "Hashem, open my lips," and before beginning the prayer, because that is where the aspect of Atzilut begins. 3) There, "Who forms light." We find that Yotzer Or, Shemoneh Esrei, and Birkat HaMazon each contain every letter of the alphabet except the final form of peh. This teaches that one who concentrates during these 3 is protected so that Satan, an evil occurrence, and any prosecutor, even when wrath surges, has no power over his prayer or meal. Ateret Zekenim; Shalmei Tzibbur. 4) There, "Who forms light." One must pause between Yotzer and Or so that they do not sound as a single word. Ateret Zekenim; Shalmei Tzibbur. The later authorities write likewise. 5) "Alone, from then." When saying me'az, one should intend the union represented by the combined Divine Name Yahadonai, because the numerical value of az is 8, corresponding to the 8 letters of that Name. Shaar HaKavanos; Shalmei Tzibbur; Kaf HaChaim by Rabbi Chaim Palagi.
