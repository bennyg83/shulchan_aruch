(א) And he recalled immediately — see in Mishnah Berurah the law that Peri Megadim wrote in the name of Bach and the Prisha and his reason for it. In truth his words are not understandable, for this would be more than within the time of speech; and even if we say his intent is that one combines the word "yotzer or" with the word "asher bedevaro," and it is as if he said "asher bedevaro yotzer or," nevertheless his words are astounding, for his reasoning applies only per the view of the gemara that wanted to resolve the question [and per the text of the Rosh and his explanation see there] — if he held a cup of beer in his hand and thought it was wine and opened with "Baruch Atah the Omnipresent" in the first three blessings and recalled and concluded "shehakol nihyeh bidvaro," whether he fulfills his obligation thereby — who would combine the word "shehakol" with the Name and kingship that he said at the beginning from the baraita that says if one opened in the morning with "ma'ariv aravim" and concluded with "yotzer or" [i.e., he said "Baruch Atah the Omnipresent Elokeinu Melech ha'olam asher bedevaro ma'ariv aravim yotzer or u'vorei choshech" etc. until the end of the blessing "yotzer hama'orot"] — he fulfills thereby; thus we combine his saying of "yotzer or" with the Name and kingship he said at the beginning, and we are not concerned for what he said in the middle, "asher bedevaro ma'ariv aravim," which are words not to the point. There the reasoning of Bach applies, that the baraita speaks specifically within the time of speech, for otherwise we certainly would not say combination, as Shulchan Aruch ruled below in siman 209 seif 2. But according to what the gemara rejected the question and holds that generally we do not say combination, and there since Rabbah bar Osha said one must mention the attribute of day at night and of night by day — when he says "berachah u'malchut" at the outset he says both — why do we need within the time of speech? And we cannot reject our words, for since in truth we rule the question leniently as the Rif wrote, therefore the baraita's reasoning is as its plain sense from the reason of combination, and we do not further say the reasoning that at the outset he says both; that is, the Rif's reason is that since the question was not resolved we go leniently in a rabbinic matter; if so, whence should we be stringent in our matter? On the contrary, go to the other side. Also in Beit Yosef it implies he fulfills even if he did not recall immediately; also from Shulchan Aruch's wording that wrote afterward "and did not say yotzer or" it does not imply as Bach, that for him it would need to inform us of greater novelty that even if he said it but not immediately. And Peri Megadim's answer in this is forced. And that Shulchan Aruch wrote "and recalled immediately" — truth guides his way that it is because of proper conduct, as he himself wrote in Beit Yosef; and especially since there are several opinions among the Rishonim that even if he did not say yotzer or at all, only that he concluded "yotzer hama'orot," he fulfills, as we wrote below. In any case, certainly one should combine their views to be lenient regarding if he did not say yotzer or immediately, as we wrote in Mishnah Berurah sk 2 in the name of Darkhei Chaim:

(ב) And did not say yotzer or — see in Mishnah Berurah; and behold all this is according to Shulchan Aruch and all the latter poskim [except Peri Chadash who has another view in all this] that we require both — that they be as one law: (1) opening of the blessing or its middle, (2) conclusion of the blessing. And the Gra in his Biur challenged Shulchan Aruch's ruling with several proofs and ruled primarily like the Rashba brought in Beit Yosef, that it depends only on the conclusion of the blessing alone — if he concluded properly he fulfills. Also in Milchamot HaRamban it is explained per his text like the Rashba, that if he concluded "yotzer hama'orot" even though he did not say at the beginning only "ma'ariv aravim," nevertheless he fulfills:

(ג) If he concluded "ma'ariv aravim" etc. — see in Mishnah Berurah sk 6 that Bach's view is it does not help even if he immediately concludes "yotzer hama'orot."
