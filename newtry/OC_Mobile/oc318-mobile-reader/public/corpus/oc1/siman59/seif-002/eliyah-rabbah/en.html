[2] And he recalled immediately and said etc. It implies that if he did not recall immediately but only after the time of speech he does not fulfill; and Bach wrote the reason — the blessing he opened "Baruch Atah the Omnipresent Elokeinu Melech ha'olam" does not refer to what he returns and says "yotzer or" since they did not say it immediately; therefore even if he concluded with "yotzer or" he does not fulfill, for in the conclusion there is no malchut. And according to this, if he concludes "Baruch Atah the Omnipresent Elokeinu Melech ha'olam yotzer hama'orot" he fulfills. But I saw in Derishah that he explained we require in every blessing an opening similar to the conclusion, and all the more the conclusion itself should be similar to the opening; and if one does not say it within the time of speech it is as if he did not say it in the blessing — see there. And according to this it is fine that even if he concluded "Baruch Atah the Omnipresent Elokeinu Melech ha'olam yotzer hama'orot" he does not fulfill, for it is not similar to the opening. Magen Avraham wrote: it seems to me if he did not recall until he finished Ahavah Rabbah he should not interrupt but say "yotzer or" after the eighteen — until here.
