[2] And he remembered immediately and said, and so forth. This implies that if he did not remember immediately, but only after the time needed to utter a brief phrase, he has not fulfilled his obligation. Bach explains that the opening formula, ‘Blessed are You, Hashem our God, King of the universe,’ does not apply to his later words ‘Who forms light,’ because he did not say them immediately. Therefore, even if he concluded with ‘Who forms light,’ he has not fulfilled his obligation, since the conclusion lacks mention of kingship. According to this, if he concludes, ‘Blessed are You, Hashem our God, King of the universe, Who forms the luminaries,’ he has fulfilled his obligation. I saw, however, that Derishah explains that every blessing requires wording related to its opening close to its conclusion, and all the more so the conclusion itself must relate to the opening. If he does not say it within the time needed to utter a brief phrase, it is as though he did not say it as part of the blessing. See there. According to this, it is understandable that even if he concludes, ‘Blessed are You, Hashem our God, King of the universe, Who forms the luminaries,’ he has not fulfilled his obligation, because the conclusion does not relate to the opening. Magen Avraham writes: It appears to me that if he did not remember until after completing Ahavah Rabbah, he should not interrupt, but should recite Yotzer Or after the Amidah. This is not convincing, because all agree that Yotzer Or was initially instituted before Krias Shema. Rather, they should interrupt and recite Yotzer Or and Ahavah Rabbah, as stated in siman 60.
