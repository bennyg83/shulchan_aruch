If one erred and said, 'who by His word brings on evenings,' then immediately remembered and said, 'who forms light,' and also concluded, 'who forms the luminaries,' he has fulfilled his obligation. But if he said, 'who by His word brings on evenings,' and did not say, 'who forms light,' or did not conclude, 'who forms the luminaries,' he has not fulfilled his obligation. If he said, 'who forms light and creates darkness, who by His word brings on evenings,' and also concluded, 'who brings on evenings,' he has not fulfilled his obligation. {Rama: The same applies if at the beginning he said only, 'who forms light': if he concluded, 'who brings on evenings,' he has not fulfilled his obligation. [Rama's own ruling, explaining the Tur and Rosh]} But if he concluded, 'who forms the luminaries,' then since he began with 'who forms light,' he has fulfilled his obligation even though he interrupted with the words concerning bringing on evenings.
