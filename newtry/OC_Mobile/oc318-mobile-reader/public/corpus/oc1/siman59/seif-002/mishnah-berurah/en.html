(2) Immediately — specifically immediately, meaning within the time of an utterance; but after the time of an utterance he has not fulfilled his obligation, for the blessing that he opened with "Barukh Atah... Elokeinu" does not refer to what he returned and said "Yotzer Or," since he did not say it immediately. And what he wrote afterward "and he did not say Yotzer Or" — the same applies if he said it but not immediately; so wrote Pri Megadim in the name of Bach and Prisha. But in Derekh ha-Chayim he rules that even if he did not remember until near the end of the blessing, nevertheless, since he did not finish it, he need not begin the blessing of Yotzer Or anew from the start; he only says "Yotzer Or u-vorei choshekh," etc., until the end of the blessing "Yotzer ha-me'orot," and fulfills his obligation thereby. And so is the practice, as we wrote in Beur Halakhah.

(3) And he did not say, etc. — explanation: even though he concluded with "Barukh Atah... Yotzer ha-me'orot," since his beginning and middle were not proper he has not fulfilled his obligation and must return to the beginning — "Barukh Atah... Elokeinu" in order, until after "Yotzer ha-me'orot." See Beur Halakhah.

(4) And the same applies — all agree thus, as explained in Beit Yosef, that the conclusion certainly must be proper. And what Shulchan Aruch cited at the beginning "or did not conclude Yotzer ha-me'orot" — his intent is not specifically in the manner that he erred and said at the beginning "asher be-dvaro ma'ariv aravim," but he cited it to conclude the matter of the beginning. And also what he cited "and if he said Yotzer Or u-vorei choshekh asher be-dvaro ma'ariv aravim," etc. — this is because he wished to conclude at the end that if he concluded "Yotzer ha-me'orot" he has fulfilled his obligation even though he interrupted with "ma'ariv aravim," as explained in Beit Yosef.

(5) Only "Yotzer Or" — explanation: and any formula of the blessing alone in which he erred in the conclusion — nevertheless he has not fulfilled his obligation, for he did not bless in the conclusion on the light that begins to shine on this day.

(6) He concluded "ma'ariv aravim" — and if within the time of an utterance he remembered and said "Yotzer ha-me'orot," he has fulfilled his obligation; but if he paused after saying "ma'ariv aravim" for the time of an utterance, he has not fulfilled his obligation even if he said "Yotzer ha-me'orot" after the time of an utterance, and he must say again from the blessing of Yotzer Or from its beginning.

(7) He has not fulfilled his obligation — and if he erred and did not remember until he began Ahavah Rabbah, he should not interrupt in the middle but should finish in the order of the blessing Ahavah Rabbah, and afterward say Yotzer Or before Keriat Shema.

(8) He has fulfilled his obligation — the essence of this section is that there are two matters in the blessing of Yotzer Or of the morning that impede [fulfillment]: (1) the opening of the blessing or its middle must be proper; (2) the conclusion of the blessing. And know that the same applies in the blessing of "ma'ariv aravim" of the evening, as explained in Berakhot 12a — see there.

(9) That he interrupted in "ma'ariv aravim" — within the time of an utterance of the opening of Yotzer Or, and he said the entire formula of the evening except the conclusion — nevertheless we do not say that the first words of Yotzer Or were uprooted thereby.
