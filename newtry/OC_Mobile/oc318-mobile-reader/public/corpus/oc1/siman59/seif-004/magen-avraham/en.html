With fewer than ten. Darkei Moshe writes this in the name of the students of Rabbeinu Yonah in the third chapter of Berachos. Their words are: Although the chazzan discharges the obligation of those in the fields who are prevented from attending with regard to prayer, they are nevertheless not exempted from Krias Shema except through listening and answering Amen, and he discharges their obligation only with ten. This is also cited by Beit Yosef in siman 69. It implies that the case concerns one who is not proficient, for otherwise he does not fulfill his obligation even regarding prayer, as stated in siman 124. Thus, even one who is not proficient requires ten for Krias Shema. Abudraham and Rabbi Moshe Alashkar, siman 10, write likewise. Kesef Mishneh, in chapter 8 of Hilchos Tefillah, writes that the statement that an educated person recites a blessing and an unlearned person fulfills his obligation must be distinguished as applying specifically to blessings over enjoyment. Ran in the chapter HaKorei Omed did not understand it this way and therefore questioned what distinction the Geonim could draw. Nevertheless, regarding other blessings, all authorities agree that one who is not proficient does not require ten. This also appears from Hagahos Ashri at the end of the third chapter of Rosh Hashanah. See what I wrote at the end of siman 6 and in siman 564.
