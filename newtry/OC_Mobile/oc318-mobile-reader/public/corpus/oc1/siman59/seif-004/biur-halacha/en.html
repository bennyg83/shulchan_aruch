(א) With the shaliach tzibur — see in Mishnah Berurah. And see in Tur and Beit Yosef that wrote in the name of a responsum of the Rosh, that if he intended the words of the shaliach tzibur in silence and in the middle of the blessing turned his heart to other matters, he lost the intention because he interrupted in its middle; for all who rely on the shaliach tzibur must direct his heart to everything he says and not turn his heart to other matters, for otherwise he does not fulfill. But when he recites with his mouth, even if he read part of it without intention he fulfills. And it is astounding: according to what we rule "shome'a ke'oneh" and it is as if he said literally with his mouth, as is proven in Berachot 20b in Tosafot s.v. "kede'ashkachan" — if so, even if we consider that in the middle he turned his heart to other matters, it is as if he skipped in the middle — what of it? Yet the Rashba wrote in Berachot chapter 1, and part of it was brought in siman 187, that if he changed and skipped much in the middle of long blessings he fulfilled his obligation; and he did not say the mishnah "from the coin the Sages coined" applies only when one changes in their opening or conclusion, but the rest of the formula is not me'akev, only the words the Sages specified, such as what they said "brit, Torah, u'malchut" in the morning blessing, and such as mention of the Exodus, malchut, splitting of the sea, plague of the firstborn in "emet v'yatziv" and all similar. And one could say we consider what in the middle he turned his heart to other matters as if he actually stopped with his mouth, as we consider the entire blessing as if he said with his mouth; and one should be precise somewhat in the Rosh's wording that he interrupted — see there. But one could say: first, even if he actually stopped in the middle of a long blessing, the law is not clear that he must return and bless — see in Nishmat Adam klal 25; and further, granted the beginning and end of the blessing that he heard from another, since he uttered with his mouth and intended to utter it thereby, it is fine what we consider him also as if he uttered with his mouth; but what in the middle he turned his heart to other matters is only mere thought, and even one who holds thought is like speech — that applies only when one thinks in his thought specific words of Keriat Shema or prayer and the like, but not mere thought about some matter; and also the Rosh himself ruled in chapter Mi shemeito that thought is not like speech. And perhaps the Rosh's intent is to say he does not fulfill the mitzvah in the optimal way, similar to what Beit Yosef wrote in siman 58 in the name of the Rashba on what they said "one who recites Shema with the men of the mishmar does not fulfill" — see there:

(ב) In a pleasant tone — and see in Mishnah Berurah. And it seems to me the Rosh did not say this except in his time when the world still practiced to fulfill through the blessing of the shaliach tzibur, as the Gra wrote; therefore he was stringent on himself to say with his mouth together with the shaliach tzibur — he needed to say in a pleasant tone so as not to raise his voice and thereby spoil it for the rest of the people who listen and intend to fulfill through the blessing of the shaliach tzibur. And it is also possible that for this reason he would hurry to finish the blessing and answer Amen after the shaliach tzibur, as brought in Tur, so as not to separate himself from the congregation who all need l'chatchilah to answer Amen after the shaliach tzibur; and it is also possible even for delay, for otherwise they would not fulfill through his blessing, as Rabbenu Yonah wrote in chapter Mi shemeito and was brought in this siman in Magen Avraham sk 5 — unlike today when the practice is that each one blesses Keriat Shema for himself:

(ג) With fewer than ten — see in Mishnah Berurah in what he wrote regarding the morning blessing in the name of Levush, and he at the end of siman 6 brought Magen Avraham there sk 10 and Magen Avraham in this siman sk 1 disagrees with him, for he wrote that according to all, in other blessings one who is not expert does not need ten; and Peri Megadim there wrote that perhaps Levush speaks specifically of an expert, and even regarding an expert Peri Megadim in siman 8 wondered whence he has this distinction from the blessing over mitzvot, where we rule "one who fulfilled exempts another" and does not need ten — see there; and see in Chayei Adam klal 5 that the default is like Levush's words, and it implies from him that Levush is stringent in every case; and in truth so it implies in Levush in siman 59 seif 4 — see there also the reason for this:

(ד) And he should not answer Amen — it appears we can say Beit Yosef's intent is specifically if he prays together with the shaliach tzibur; and the same if he prays alone, to exclude those who practice always to answer Amen after the chozer, as mentioned in Tur in siman 61. And in this manner also after the blessing "yotzer or" one should not answer, for it is answering Amen after oneself. But if he finished before the shaliach tzibur, even after the chozer's blessing he must answer Amen after the shaliach tzibur, and in this we are not concerned for interruption; and so it seems Birkei Yosef and Shulchan Tuki understood, and so it implies somewhat in Beit Yosef at the end of s.v. "u'mashma" that he concludes in Rama's view; and Rama's intent in what he concluded "and see" is in what he wrote there that even alone we are not concerned for interruption in this. But it is more correct to say that since Beit Yosef is silent in Shulchan Aruch to teach that his view is not like Rama, only Rabbenu Yonah's words as their plain sense, that even if the chozer finished before the shaliach tzibur he should not answer Amen on the shaliach tzibur because of interruption; and Beit Yosef's first answer — see there — and this applies only to the chozer's blessing; but after "yotzer hama'orot" one answers Amen if b'dieved he finished before the shaliach tzibur; but l'chatchilah he must finish together with the shaliach tzibur so he need not answer Amen, and so Beit Yosef holds — see there [unlike Rama's view here in Hagahah]. And that Rama wrote "and see" means that the practice of all is like the Rosh's view to finish even the blessing Ahavah Rabbah before the shaliach tzibur and answer Amen after him, as Darkei Moshe wrote; and so it implies in the Gra's Biur per this second explanation we wrote, that he wrote in sk 12 that there he ruled like the Rosh and Rama etc., implying Beit Yosef in Shulchan Aruch disagrees also with Rama; and in my humble opinion in Beit Yosef there is no proof he himself holds like Rama, only that he brings proof from him for his words; and Beit Yosef held it is better to finish together to fulfill according to all, and thereby the wonder of Sha'arei Teshuvah for the inquirer is removed:
