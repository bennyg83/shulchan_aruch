Seif 4, the blessing, etc. So wrote the Rosh, that this was his practice because of lack of concentration; one may lose the entire blessing since he did not hear it. But with the recitation itself he does not lose, as stated at the beginning of chapter 2: from here onward is the mitzvah of recitation. See Tosafot, Rosh Hashanah 16b, s.v. ve-iyyun, and the Yerushalmi there.
Quietly, etc. This appears from the above Tosefta according to the Hagahot Maimoniyot, who explains that they answer aloud. But the Rosh does not explain it so, for from his words it appears that were it not for the above concern he would not have recited the blessing, and it appears that in his days they did not recite it individually; rather, everyone listened to the shaliach tzibbur, and he was concerned about this. From him our custom developed. The Tosefta follows its plain sense, as it says there, "all these he would say," implying that the others did not say them. The reason he would say all this is as I wrote above in siman 49 in the name of the Rosh, because one cannot discharge others' obligation orally, as written in the Tur there. He therefore says it quietly, since the essential law is to hear it from the shaliach tzibbur.
And he should hasten, etc. As I wrote above at the beginning of siman 51.
Nevertheless, etc., that blessings, etc. As written in the Tosefta. Even Ri would say only the Kedushah. The Ran wrote there in Megillah in the name of the Geonim that "we do not divide," meaning that we do not begin the blessings of Shema by having one person bless and the others fulfill through his blessing except with ten. This differs from other blessings, such as Kiddush, Havdalah, and the like, where even with two one may exempt the other, except for Birkat Hamazon, which requires three; see there for the reason. He wrote that the reason is because of the Kedushah contained in them, and even at Maariv the Sages did not distinguish in their enactment. He further wrote that one who does not know may be exempted with fewer than ten except for its Kedushah, as with Birkat Hamazon in chapter 7 of Berachot. Talmidei Rabbeinu Yonah likewise wrote in chapter 3 of Berachot, because of the above difficulty, that since they hold that an individual recites this Kedushah, they explained that people are exempted only by answering amen, and one exempts only with ten. He alludes to what is stated in chapter 3 of the Yerushalmi, that with Shema it is not reasonable that each person should repeat it with his mouth. See below siman 594 in the gloss, and see Magen Avraham.
And he should not answer, etc. As stated, the reason is that one who answers amen after his own blessings is considered ignorant, because one must not interrupt between the blessing and the matter over which he blesses.
And see below, etc. There he ruled according to the Rosh and Ramah to answer amen. The reason is that the blessings are not upon Shema, as above. But in Rambam it is written that after Ahavah Rabbah one should not answer amen after himself, and this must be for the above reason. See Beit Yosef.
