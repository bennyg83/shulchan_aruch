Siman 64—blessing, etc. So too the Rosh—for thus he practiced on account of lack of kavanah, and one loses the entire berakhah since he did not hear; but in keriah one does not lose, as written at the end of chapter 2 from here onward is the mitzvah of keriah; and Tosafot Rosh Hashana 16b s.v. ve'iyun, etc., in the Yerushalmi, etc.

Quietly, etc. As in the Tosefta cited above, per the explanation of Hagahot Maimoniyot—that they answer in a loud voice; but the Rosh does not explain thus, for from his words it appears that the above concern did not apply—he would not bless, and he wrote that in his days they would not bless but everyone would hear from the shaliach tzibbur, and he was concerned about this; from him our custom spread. And the Tosefta accords with its plain meaning, as written: "all these Rabbi Yehuda would say," etc.—he explains that the rest would not say; and the reason he would say all this is as explained in siman 49 in the name of the Rosh—because one cannot fulfill the eighteen [blessings] by heart, and as the Tur wrote there; rather one says quietly because the main law is to hear from the shaliach tzibbur.

And he should hurry, etc. As written above, siman 51.

Nevertheless, etc., in Berakhot, etc. As in the Tosefta; and even Rabbi Yehuda would say only kedushah. The Ran wrote in Megillah, in the name of the geonim: ein porsin, etc.—meaning, one does not begin with the blessings of Keriat Shema that the first blesses and the others fulfill through his berakhah except with ten; unlike other berakhot such as Kiddush and Havdalah and the like, where even one fulfills his fellow's obligation except for Birkat HaMazon, which requires three—see there the reason. And he wrote the reason is on account of kedushah in them; and even at Ma'ariv the rabbis did not disagree with their enactment. And he wrote: this is when one does not know—one fulfills him with fewer than ten except for the kedushah in it; and as written in Birkat HaMazon at the end of chapter 7 of Berakhot, and likewise the Tur in chapter 3 of Berakhot on account of the above difficulty—for he holds this kedushah an individual says; therefore he explains that one is not exempt except by answering amen, and one does not exempt except with ten; and he hints at what is written in chapter 3 of the Yerushalmi: it is not reasonable in Keriat Shema that each one recites from memory—and see below siman 294 in the hagahah, and Magen Avraham.

And he shall not answer, etc. As the reason is explained—that he answers amen after his berakhot; this one is a boor because one may not interrupt between the berakhah and the matter over which he blesses.

And see below, etc. For there it rules like the view of the Rosh and Rema to answer amen; and the reason is because these berakhot are not upon Keriat Shema, as above; but in Rambam he wrote that after Ahavah Rabbah one does not answer amen after oneself, and it must be for the same reason as above—and Beit Yosef.
