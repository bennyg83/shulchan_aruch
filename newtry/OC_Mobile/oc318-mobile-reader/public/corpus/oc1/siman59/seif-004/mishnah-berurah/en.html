(14) The blessing of Yotzer — and the same applies to all the blessings of Keriat Shema.

(15) With the shaliach tzibbur — for although according to the law one can intend to fulfill through the shaliach tzibbur's blessing even if he is expert — specifically in tefillah the shaliach tzibbur does not fulfill for one who is expert, as will be explained below in siman 124, because they are supplications and each one must seek mercy for himself — nevertheless one should be accustomed to say with the shaliach tzibbur quietly, for since they are long blessings a person cannot regularly intend to fulfill with the shaliach tzibbur in silence. And today they are not accustomed to be careful to say specifically quietly. See Beur Halakhah.

(16) Before — for in the middle it is forbidden to answer amen, as explained in siman 66.

(17) And he answers amen — but in "Barukh Hu u-varukh Shemo" he should not say, for even in verses of song one may not interrupt for this; as Magen Avraham wrote in siman 124.

(18) After the shaliach tzibbur — and the same applies if he heard the end of a blessing from another person, whether this blessing or another blessing.

(19) He heard from the shaliach tzibbur — and specifically if he intended to fulfill and the shaliach tzibbur intended to fulfill for him, as above in siman 6, seif 4.

(20) These — of Keriat Shema; and the same applies to all blessings, as above at the end of siman 6 [and see there in Pri Chadash, and in siman 8 seif 5, see there in Machatzit ha-Shekel and Eretz ha-Chayyim, and siman 219 seif 5, and siman 298 seif 14] — except for the blessings of tefillah, for the shaliach tzibbur does not fulfill for one who is expert, as will be explained below in siman 124.

(21) That he is expert — and even if he already fulfilled, he can fulfill for another, as will be explained below in siman 69; and the same applies in all blessings, as will be explained below in siman 585 and siman 592 — except for the blessing over enjoyment, for one who already fulfilled cannot fulfill for another who is still obligated in the berakhah, as will be explained below in siman 213 seif 2, and even for one who is not expert.

(22) The individual — even if he is not expert.

(23) With fewer than ten — and this is specifically regarding the blessing of Keriat Shema; and also for the blessing of Shaḥar Levush is stringent like Keriat Shema [and see Beur Halakhah]. But other blessings such as berakhah over mitzvot and berakhah over enjoyment — even one individual can fulfill for another individual, even for one who is expert, as above in siman 8. But in berakhah over enjoyment there are several distinctions: in the first berakhah over bread and wine, according to all opinions the law is as we wrote, as brought at the beginning of siman 193 seif 1; but they must establish themselves for this, meaning that they sit together. And regarding birkat ha-mazon — if only two ate, it is a mitzvah to separate and each bless for himself, unless one is not expert, and then the expert one fulfills for him, as brought in siman 193 — see there. And regarding other things besides bread and wine, both in the first berakhah and in the latter berakhah, there are opinions among the poskim whether one can fulfill for his fellow, as will be explained below in siman 213 — see there.

(24) That it constitutes an interruption — between Keriat Shema and the berakhah, as it is forbidden to interrupt between any matter of mitzvah or enjoyment over which one blesses and the berakhah before it. But in the berakhah of Yotzer Or he also admits to the law of Rama in this: if he finished before the shaliach tzibbur, he may answer amen after him. See Beur Halakhah.

(25) Siman 61 — in siman 63 in the Rama gloss there it was explained that also in the blessing HaBocher the custom is to finish before the shaliach tzibbur and answer amen after the shaliach tzibbur, as Darkei Moshe wrote in this siman. The reason is that the Keriat Shema blessings are not like other mitzvah blessings, for one does not bless "Who commanded us regarding Keriat Shema" but they are separate blessings and they enacted to bless them before Keriat Shema; therefore there is no concern in what interrupts between them and Keriat Shema. See in the Acharonim who all copied the words of Rama as the law: if he finished the blessing HaBocher before the shaliach tzibbur he should answer amen after the shaliach tzibbur; but lekhatchilah it is better that he finish together with the shaliach tzibbur and not need to answer amen after him. See in Da'at Hachamim who wrote that one should not answer amen after another berakhah between Ahavah Rabbah and Shema.
