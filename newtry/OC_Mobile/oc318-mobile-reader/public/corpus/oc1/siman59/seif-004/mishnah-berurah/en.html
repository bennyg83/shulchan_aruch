Regarding other blessings, such as blessings over mitzvos and blessings over enjoyment, one individual can discharge another individual, even one who is proficient, as stated above in siman 8. Blessings over enjoyment, however, involve several distinctions. Regarding the first blessing over bread and wine, all agree with the rule stated above, as explained at the beginning of siman 193, seif 1, but they must establish themselves together for this purpose, meaning that they sit together. Regarding Bircas HaMazon, if only two ate together, it is a mitzvah for them to separate and each recite it independently, unless one is not proficient, in which case the proficient person discharges him, as stated in siman 193. Regarding foods other than bread and wine, both for the initial and concluding blessings, the authorities dispute whether one person can discharge another, as explained below in siman 213.

Siman 61. This refers to seif 3 in the gloss, where it is explained that regarding the blessing ‘Who chooses His people Israel with love,’ the custom is likewise to conclude before the chazzan and answer amen after him, as Darkei Moshe writes in this siman. The reason is that the blessings of Krias Shema are unlike other blessings over mitzvos. We do not say, ‘Who sanctified us with His commandments and commanded us to recite Krias Shema.’ Rather, they are independent blessings instituted before Krias Shema. Therefore, interrupting between them and Krias Shema is not a concern. The later authorities all cite Rama's ruling that if one concluded ‘Who chooses His people Israel with love’ before the chazzan, he should answer amen after him. Initially, however, it is preferable to conclude together with the chazzan so that he need not answer amen. Derech HaChaim writes that one should not answer amen to another blessing between Ahavah Rabbah and Krias Shema.
