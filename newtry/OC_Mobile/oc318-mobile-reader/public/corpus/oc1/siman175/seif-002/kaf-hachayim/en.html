2) There, but he recites over it ha-tov, etc. If he made kiddush over raisin wine and afterward, during the meal, they brought him wine, he must recite borei peri ha-gefen over it, and should not recite ha-tov veha-metiv over it, for it is not usual to drink raisin wine during the meal, and it is considered that he changed his mind. Responsa Beit Yehudah siman 49. So too wrote Birkei Yosef ot 1. However, if during kiddush he intended to exempt the water and beer within the meal, the Birkei Yosef wrote there that although he does not want to drink more raisin wine during the meal, it is not considered that he changed his mind; and if so, the wine that comes to him is also exempted from borei peri ha-gefen, and he must recite ha-tov veha-metiv. But he concluded that this requires deliberation; see there. Therefore, it appears that since there are sides to this, if possible one should have another person who is not among the members of the meal recite borei peri ha-gefen and intend to exempt them, or they should not drink until after birkat hamazon, in order to remove themselves from doubt. If this is not possible, such as where they need to drink, then one may permit it by reciting the berachah in his heart and relying on those who permit this, as written above in siman 74 ot 49; see there. There is no concern of a berachah in vain in this, as written above in siman 107 ot 10; see there.
