Beit Yosef writes that all agree that HaTov VeHaMeitiv is not recited over wine of the latter sort. He further distinguishes whether it is slightly or greatly inferior. The Tur implies that even if only slightly inferior, HaTov VeHaMeitiv is not recited, unlike Rabbeinu Tam, who distinguishes between greatly and slightly inferior wine. The Shulchan Aruch rules like the Tur. Regarding white wine, Rama writes that the blessing is recited even if it is more inferior, based on Mordechai, chapter Arvei Pesachim, according to Rabbeinu Tam, because white wine possesses an advantage even when much inferior. Other wine has an advantage only when slightly inferior. Bach therefore challenges Rama: since our practice is not to recite the blessing even over slightly inferior wine, over white wine we should recite it only when slightly inferior, not when greatly inferior. There is no difficulty. Since this is based on the rationale that white wine possesses a health benefit, why should we not rule like Rabbeinu Tam in this respect? Bach writes that if the red wine is superior to the white and he drank the white first, he recites HaTov VeHaMeitiv over the red; the health advantage does not override the superior quality.
