Lechem Chamudos writes: If Reuven and Shimon each drink his own wine, and Reuven later gives Shimon his cup to drink, it requires study whether Shimon recites HaTov VeHaMeitiv, since only Shimon has experienced a change of wine. See Magen Avraham. Responsa Ginas Veradim, principle 1, siman 40, writes that even if another person is with him but did not drink from the first wine, and a second wine is then brought and both wish to drink it together, HaTov VeHaMeitiv is not recited. There is no benefit to another, since his companion did not drink the first wine. If, however, after the second wine arrives they decide also to drink from the first, he recites HaTov VeHaMeitiv. He writes that this resolves the question of Lechem Chamudos. Responsa Perach Shoshan, principle 4, siman 12, discusses at length both the case where Reuven and Shimon drank from the first wine but only one drank from the second, and the reverse case, and concludes that all these cases remain doubtful. See there. In a doubt concerning blessings, one is lenient. See Magen Avraham, seif katan 5.
No. Mordechai, at the end of the chapter HaRoeh, writes that a lodger and a guest should not recite HaTov VeHaMeitiv, because this is not their benefit since the wine is not theirs. Beit Yosef explains that only the guest should not recite it, while the host should, and he disagrees: Why should the guest not bless Hashem for having provided him different wines to drink, even though they are not his? Bach explains that Mordechai holds that the guest does not recite it, but the host recites it and the guest fulfills his obligation through the host. Bach rules accordingly, as does Shiltei HaGibborim. Magen Avraham disagrees and writes that, because of the doubt concerning blessings, neither the guest nor the host should recite it when no member of the host's household is at the table. Nevertheless, if the host places the pitcher on the table, as is done at large meals, all participants are equally included in the benefit and recite HaTov VeHaMeitiv. If they do not drink together, but each drinks in a separate room, it is obvious that the blessing is not recited. Responsa Perach Shoshan, principle 1, siman 12, challenges Magen Avraham at length and concludes that the guest may recite HaTov VeHaMeitiv, as may the host who drinks with him. He writes that he saw guests recite the blessing in the presence of the leading authorities of the generation. Or Chadash, page 29, seif katan 8, likewise rules this way and explains that it is as though the entire wine brought was given to the guest as a gift, so it is considered his. Therefore, to avoid doubt, when the host gives the guest the honor of reciting the blessing, either the guest should say to the host, or the host to the guest, that through giving him the honor of reciting the blessing, all the wine becomes his, enabling him to recite HaTov VeHaMeitiv according to all opinions. Alternatively, he should say that half the wine becomes his, for if he gives him all of it, the host would no longer be able to recite HaTov VeHaMeitiv over it. See Perach Shoshan, siman 12.
