Seif katan 4: One does not, etc. Lechem Chamudos writes, etc., “since only he experiences a change of wine,” etc. If so, even if Shimon also gives Reuven a cup of his wine, so that each has two varieties, it is still possible that the doubt remains. The cup over which each one recites the blessing represents a change only for the one reciting it.
He disagrees with him, for Beit Yosef writes that he did not find this ruling in the Rif. Bach writes that Mordechai may have found it in a responsum of the Rif.
The blessing HaTov VeHaMeitiv is not recited over rainfall when the rains had initially ceased and later fell again. This is unlike the Rosh there, who holds that even if one owns a field alone, without partnership with another, he nevertheless recites HaTov VeHaMeitiv because owners of other fields share in the benefit of the rain.
“Unlike partners, regarding whom we rule that there is no retroactive clarification.” The phrase “we rule” is not precise. First, the blessing is Rabbinic, and in Rabbinic matters we rule that there is retroactive clarification, as Magen Avraham writes in siman 153, end of seif katan 47, and at the end of siman 413. Even if one insists that the wine itself involves a monetary law, for if one partner wished to take the half-cup taken by his fellow he would be entitled to do so, and monetary law is Torah-level, where we rule that there is no retroactive clarification, nevertheless we are discussing the blessing. Furthermore, according to the opinion that there is retroactive clarification, there would never be a case in which partners recite HaTov VeHaMeitiv over wine. Necessarily, Magen Avraham means that since there is a basis to say there is no retroactive clarification, even where we rule there is, HaTov VeHaMeitiv may be recited because it appears somewhat as though each has a share in the other's portion.
Nevertheless, etc. “He places the pitcher,” etc. Accordingly, regarding the question of Lechem Chamudos cited by Magen Avraham at the beginning of this seif katan, concerning Reuven and Shimon, etc., and Reuven later giving, etc., Lechem Chamudos wrote according to Beit Yosef. According to Magen Avraham, in the case described by Lechem Chamudos he certainly does not recite the blessing, unless Reuven afterward places his pitcher on the table. In such a case the question of Lechem Chamudos applies.
And if each one does not drink, etc. Meaning, even if they are partners in the wine, but each drinks the jointly owned wine in a different room.
And what Tosafos write, etc. Tosafos reject the opinion that even during a meal each person may not recite HaTov VeHaMeitiv independently, because the blessing applies only when one person discharges others through his blessing. Their words are: “This is implied by ‘Who is good to Himself and bestows good upon others,’ meaning that they drink from it. Rabbeinu Yechiel would say that this is incorrect,” etc. The reason that “Who is good to himself and bestows good upon others” does not require one to discharge others is that HaTov VeHaMeitiv applies even when the beneficiaries are not present, as when they told him, “Your wife has borne a son.” It is good for him and beneficial for his wife even though she is not with him, as the wording “they told him” implies that he did not know until informed.
Rather, Tosafos bring proof, etc. Meaning, the case of one's wife giving birth is stronger, as Magen Avraham writes, because he may recite the blessing even though his wife is not with him. At least it proves that the phrase “bestows good upon others” can apply even where one does not discharge others through his blessing. In this respect there is no distinction between the birth of a son and wine.
