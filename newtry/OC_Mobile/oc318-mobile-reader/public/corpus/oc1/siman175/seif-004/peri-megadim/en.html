And over. The preferred wine first. See Taz, and Magen Avraham, letter 2. See what I wrote in letter 1. I already wrote there that Rama reconciles seif 1 and seif 3, while Bach explains that seif 1 concerns one who transgressed, etc. Taz, however, explains that seif 1 concerns uncertainty and this seif a case in which it is known to be inferior. His lucid wording reflects profound wisdom, as is his way. Beit Yosef, after citing the Tur, writes, “But Mordechai wrote,” etc., treating them as conflicting. Why, then, did he repeat both rulings in seifim 1 and 3 of the Shulchan Aruch? This would imply a retraction, which is difficult. Taz therefore explains that the Tur rules that even if both wines are before him, Rashbam limits the blessing to a case where one is known to be superior, whereas Tosafos apply it even in a case of uncertainty. According to Rashbam there is certainly a dispute with Mordechai. The Tur, however, holds that Mordechai's question is not compelling, since in such a case we do not minimize blessings, similar to siman 211, seif 3. One might distinguish that there it is initially proper to recite the blessing appropriate to each item. See siman 206, where, with Hashem's help, this will be explained. But according to the Mechaber, who follows Tosafos that the blessing is recited even in uncertainty, there is no contradiction. In a case of uncertainty, preference is inapplicable because he does not know which is superior. Since there is an abundance of wines, it is proper to offer multiple praises, analogous to siman 211, seif 3, rather than determine which is inferior. In seif 3, however, where one wine is clearly superior, he recites Borei Peri HaGafen over the superior wine. This differs from siman 211, seif 3, as explained above. This is the substance of Taz's intent. Accordingly, where both wines were before him, uncertainty is certainly preferable. According to Rashbam, known superiority is certainly preferable, as explained. If both are known to be equal, he does not recite HaTov VeHaMeitiv; he does so only where their quality is unknown.
