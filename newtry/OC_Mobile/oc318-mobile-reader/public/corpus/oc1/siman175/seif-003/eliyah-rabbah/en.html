[6] And he should not recite, etc. Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah writes that if he is uncertain which wine is superior, he recites Borei Peri HaGafen over one and HaTov VeHaMeitiv over the other. It appears that he need not remove either from before him, consistent with what I explained in seif katan 3. Sefer Beis Hillel writes that even if several varieties of wine are brought to the table, one recites HaTov VeHaMeitiv over each one. Nevertheless, when reciting HaTov VeHaMeitiv over one wine, it is proper to remove from the table another wine over which he intends later to recite HaTov VeHaMeitiv. He further writes that if two people are drinking and one has not drunk this wine within thirty days, it is preferable that he recite HaTov VeHaMeitiv. Orchos Chaim writes that one who has already recited Bircas HaMazon, or who has not yet drunk wine with those seated and drinking, should not recite HaTov VeHaMeitiv. It further writes that if the host gives the guest the honor of reciting HaTov VeHaMeitiv, he should declare that the wine belongs to him so that he can recite the blessing. This is because Bach and Perishah agree that the guest should not recite HaTov VeHaMeitiv for himself, since the wine is not his. Perishah writes that the same applies to a lodger who remains at an inn for an extended period. Magen Avraham explains that even the host should not recite the blessing unless the person drinking with him has a share in that wine, as explained below in siman 221. If the host places the pitcher on the table, as is done at large meals, it is as though everyone has a share in it.
