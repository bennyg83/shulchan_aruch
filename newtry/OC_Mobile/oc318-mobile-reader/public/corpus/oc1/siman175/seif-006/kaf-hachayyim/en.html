6) There, gloss: Although he has no more, etc. Maran, of blessed memory, writes this in Beis Yosef based on his own reasoning. Radbaz, volume 1, siman 125, however, writes that the blessing is not recited if none of the first wine remains, in accordance with the words of Rabbeinu Yerucham cited by Beis Yosef. Baal HaNesivos was uncertain about the matter, and Radbaz effects a compromise: If they possess some of the wine they drank initially but did not wish to bring it, and instead brought this different wine, they certainly brought it because of the change in wine and recite HaTov VeHaMeitiv. If they had no more of the first wine and brought this one, although it is different, they did not bring it because of a change in wine and do not recite the blessing. In such a case, Raavad's reasoning appears correct. Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef, Magen Avraham, paragraph 1, and Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 11, cite this. Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef, paragraph 13, likewise writes that it is proper to follow Rabbeinu Yerucham; see there. Birkei Yosef, paragraph 2, writes that had Maran heard that Raavad held the opposite view, it is possible he would have ruled in accordance with Raavad. He concludes that one should take the views of Raavad and Radbaz into account and not recite the blessing; R' Yaakov Castro and the Shelah write the same.
