And Who bestows good. Maharil writes: Some say that HaTov VeHaMeitiv is not recited on the night of Pesach, so as not to appear to add to the required cups. Magen Avraham writes that, in his view, one should not drink two varieties of wine. But if another variety was brought to him, he must recite the blessing, since this blessing is mentioned in the Gemara. If he already recited Bircas HaMazon over the wine, he need not recite HaTov VeHaMeitiv, for he has already said HaTov VeHaMeitiv in Bircas HaMazon. Magen Avraham.
The first. Knesses HaGedolah cites authorities who hold that the blessing is not recited. Radbaz, siman 125, rules that if they still have some of the first wine but do not desire it, and another wine is brought, they recite the blessing. See there.
When he recited the blessing. Rama wrote this to reconcile two rulings of the Shulchan Aruch. Here it rules that if he initially had two wines, he recites HaTov VeHaMeitiv, whereas in seif 3 it does not rule this way. Rama therefore explains that the present seif refers specifically to a case in which both wines were not before him at the time of the blessing. If they were both before him, the case is that of seif 3. Bach explains that seif 1 discusses one who transgressed and recited the blessing over the inferior wine. Taz explains that seif 1 concerns uncertainty as to which wine is superior, while seif 3 concerns a case where it is known that one is inferior. He concludes that one who is uneasy about reciting two blessings in a case of doubt should remove one wine from the table, recite Borei Peri HaGafen over the other, and afterward recite HaTov VeHaMeitiv over the second. In this way he satisfies all opinions. See there. See also Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah and Perach Shoshan, principle 1, siman 12, who discusses this at length and concludes like Taz.
