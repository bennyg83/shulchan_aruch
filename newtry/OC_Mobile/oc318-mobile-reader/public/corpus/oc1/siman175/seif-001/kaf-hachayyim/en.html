1) [Seif 1] They brought them another wine, etc.; one recites HaTov VeHaMeitiv over it, etc. Why is HaTov VeHaMeitiv recited upon a change of wine, while no blessing is recited upon a change of bread? Wine brings gladness and sustenance, whereas bread brings sustenance but not gladness. Furthermore, we find song recited only over wine. Also, since wine is significant and establishes a blessing for itself, it is fitting to recite HaTov VeHaMeitiv over it. Alternatively, HaTov VeHaMeitiv was instituted in Yavneh: HaTov, because the slain did not decompose, and VeHaMeitiv, because they were permitted burial. Since the non-Jews fertilized their vineyards for seven years with the blood of slain Jews and surrounded their vineyards with their bodies, the Sages instituted its recitation over wine, which is red like blood. Rosh, Berachos, chapter HaRoeh; Beis Yosef at the end of the siman. Tosafos in Berachos 59b and Pesachim 101a likewise give these reasons; see there. Perishah, paragraph 1, cites them. This is the text of the blessing: Blessed are You, Hashem, our God, King of the universe, Who is good and does good. Rambam, chapter 4 of Hilchos Berachos; Levush; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1.
2) There: One recites HaTov, etc. If one recited Kiddush over raisin wine and wine was then brought during the meal, he must recite Borei Pri HaGafen over it and should not recite HaTov VeHaMeitiv, since people do not ordinarily drink raisin wine during a meal and he is considered to have formed a new intention. Responsa Beis Yehudah, siman 49; Birkei Yosef, paragraph 1, writes the same. If, however, when reciting Kiddush he intended to cover the water and beer consumed during the meal, Birkei Yosef writes there that although he does not wish to drink more raisin wine during the meal, he is not considered to have formed a new intention. If so, the wine that arrives is also covered by Borei Pri HaGafen and he must recite HaTov VeHaMeitiv. He concludes, however, that this requires deliberation; see there. Therefore, since there are grounds for uncertainty, if possible, one should have someone who is not a member of the meal recite Borei Pri HaGafen and intend to discharge them, or they should not drink until after Birkas HaMazon, thereby avoiding the doubt. If this is impossible, such as when they need the drink, it may be permitted by reciting the blessing in one's heart and relying upon those who permit this, as written above in siman 74, paragraph 49; see there. This does not involve concern for a blessing in vain, as written above in siman 157, paragraph 10; see there.
3) There: One recites HaTov, etc. Maharil writes that some say HaTov VeHaMeitiv is not recited on Pesach night, so as not to add to the cups. Magen Avraham at the beginning of the siman cites this and writes that initially one should not drink from two types. If another type was brought to him, however, he must recite the blessing, since it is mentioned explicitly in the Gemara. He means that if another type was brought between the first and second cups, when he may drink as stated in siman 473, seif 3, and he desires to drink, he must recite the blessing because HaTov VeHaMeitiv is mentioned in the Gemara. Avudraham writes the same in the name of Maharam, and the Tur in siman 473 states that one may recite HaTov VeHaMeitiv on Pesach night and this is not considered adding to the cups; see there. Magen Avraham there cites their words.
4) If, after reciting the blessing on the first cup on Pesach night, they saw that the wine was inferior, they may send for superior wine for the three remaining cups, to perform the mitzvah in the preferred manner, and recite HaTov VeHaMeitiv. There is no need to be concerned for the view cited by Maharil that HaTov VeHaMeitiv is not recited on Pesach night. First, most great authorities disagree with him. Furthermore, the reason there is to avoid adding to the cups, whereas in this case the wine is specifically for the cups and nothing more. This is obvious. Birkei Yosef, paragraph 3; see there. Shaarei Teshuvah, paragraph 1, cites this. Erekh HaShulchan, paragraph 1, writes the same, contrary to Beis Yehudah, siman 53, who writes that no blessing should be recited because mitzvos were not given for benefit; see there. Birkei Yosef implies that this applies specifically if some of the first wine remains before them. If none remains, HaTov VeHaMeitiv should not be recited, as stated below in paragraph 6; see there.
5) There: One recites HaTov, etc. If one recited Birkas HaMazon over another wine, he need not recite HaTov VeHaMeitiv, since he already recited HaTov VeHaMeitiv within Birkas HaMazon. Magen Avraham at the beginning of the siman; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2; Yad Aharon, Hagahos HaTur. Although for Birkas HaMazon, since he must recite Borei Pri HaGafen, he is considered to have formed a new intention, and one who forms a new intention recites only Borei Pri HaGafen, as stated below in paragraph 7, one may say that here all agree that he recites only Borei Pri HaGafen. See there and understand this.
6) There, gloss: Although he has no more, etc. Maran, of blessed memory, writes this in Beis Yosef based on his own reasoning. Radbaz, volume 1, siman 125, however, writes that the blessing is not recited if none of the first wine remains, in accordance with the words of Rabbeinu Yerucham cited by Beis Yosef. Baal HaNesivos was uncertain about the matter, and Radbaz effects a compromise: If they possess some of the wine they drank initially but did not wish to bring it, and instead brought this different wine, they certainly brought it because of the change in wine and recite HaTov VeHaMeitiv. If they had no more of the first wine and brought this one, although it is different, they did not bring it because of a change in wine and do not recite the blessing. In such a case, Raavad's reasoning appears correct. Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef, Magen Avraham, paragraph 1, and Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 11, cite this. Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef, paragraph 13, likewise writes that it is proper to follow Rabbeinu Yerucham; see there. Birkei Yosef, paragraph 2, writes that had Maran heard that Raavad held the opposite view, it is possible he would have ruled in accordance with Raavad. He concludes that one should take the views of Raavad and Radbaz into account and not recite the blessing; R' Yaakov Castro and the Shelah write the same. Zechor LeAvraham, under the letter heh, paragraph 2; Beis Menuchah, paragraph 1; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, siman 49, paragraph 12.
