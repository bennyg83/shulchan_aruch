Who is good and does good. See Baer Heitev. Birkei Yosef writes that many authorities disagree with Maharil. See Tur, siman 473. Maamar Chametz of Tashbetz writes that one may recite the blessing HaTov VeHaMeitiv. Birkei Yosef records an incident in which, on Pesach night after the Kiddush cup, they discovered that the wine was poor. They were uncertain whether to send for better wine because this might create a doubt regarding the blessing HaTov VeHaMeitiv. He answered that there is certainly a personal obligation to perform a mitzvah in the best manner, and he brought proof from Responsa Chacham Tzvi, siman 45. See there. He writes that even Maharil agrees in this case, because bringing better wine does not increase the number of cups. They require wine for the mitzvah cups in any event, and they still have some of the original wine. They may therefore recite HaTov VeHaMeitiv according to all opinions. This implies somewhat that if none of the original wine remained, perhaps they should not bring new wine, so as not to enter the doubt regarding HaTov VeHaMeitiv. Nevertheless, it appears that for the sake of performing the mitzvah in the best manner it is proper to bring better wine, because a mitzvah is precious at its proper time, even if they refrain from reciting HaTov VeHaMeitiv because of the doubt. Birkei Yosef writes that Raavad and Radbaz hold that the blessing should not be recited and that their opinion should be taken into account. Maharikash and Shelah rule similarly. See there. The first. See Baer Heitev and the preceding seif katan, where it is stated that in cases of doubt concerning blessings one rules leniently. Birkei Yosef writes in the name of Mahari Molcho that if newly arrived wine was mixed into the wine already present, one recites HaTov VeHaMeitiv only if most of the mixture consists of the new wine. He compares this to the rulings in Shulchan Aruch, simanim 208 and 217. See there. In my humble opinion this requires study. The essence of this blessing is that he now tastes a superior flavor compared with what he tasted initially. This does not depend upon majority and minority, but upon whether the mixture has improved. If he now tastes a superior wine flavor, even when that improvement results from a minority of newly added wine, the blessing should be recited. It is easy for one who understands to distinguish between the law here and the cases in simanim 208 and 217. See below, siman 202, regarding cooked wine mixed with uncooked wine.
