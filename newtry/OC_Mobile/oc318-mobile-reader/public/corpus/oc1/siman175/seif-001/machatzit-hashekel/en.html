Magen Avraham wrote that Maharil says some do not recite HaTov VeHaMeitiv, etc. This is so as not to appear to add to the prescribed cups. Certainly, during the meal one may drink as much wine as desired, and this does not appear to add to the four cups instituted by the Sages on Pesach night. Since no separate blessing is recited over wine drunk during the meal, it is evident that he drinks only because of thirst or a similar need. A cup over which one recites a blessing, however, resembles the four cups and may appear to be an addition. Nevertheless, if he wishes to drink from a new variety of wine that has now been brought, he must recite HaTov VeHaMeitiv. One should not think that because he wishes to drink it, it is preferable to permit drinking without the blessing so that it not appear to add another cup. Since HaTov VeHaMeitiv is required by the law of the Gemara, he must recite it, and we are not overly concerned that this appears to add to the cups. It is nevertheless preferable not to drink the newly brought wine. Magen Avraham discusses a case where he needs much wine because he is very thirsty and has no other wine. See Magen Avraham, siman 174, seif katan 2. See also siman 472, seif 7, and the Tur there. The Tur writes that since the four cups must be drunk while reclining, one who drank them without reclining must drink them again while reclining, even though this appears to add to the cups. Since reclining is a law of the Gemara, we are not concerned about that appearance. Rama rules below that regarding the final two cups, where an additional cup appears more clearly to be an addition, one should not drink again, because after the fact one may rely on Raavyah, who holds that reclining is not required nowadays. Were it not for Raavyah's reasoning, Rama would agree that one must drink again while reclining, despite the appearance of adding, because reclining is required by the Gemara. The Tur likewise writes in siman 473 in the name of Maharam of Rothenburg that one may recite HaTov VeHaMeitiv on Pesach night, because it appears to add only when one recites Borei Pri HaGafen as was recited over the prescribed cups.
It appears to me that when he recites Birkat HaMazon, etc. Meaning, he should not drink the new variety during the meal. Rather, he should recite Birkat HaMazon over that wine afterward. This is not limited to Pesach night; the same law applies throughout the year.
