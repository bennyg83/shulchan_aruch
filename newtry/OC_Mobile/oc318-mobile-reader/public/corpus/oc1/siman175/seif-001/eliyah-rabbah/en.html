Even though he has none of the first wine left, etc. The Shelah, folio 91, is stringent because of doubt regarding blessings and rules that one should not recite HaTov VeHaMeitiv unless some of the first wine remains. Magen Avraham rules likewise in the name of the Radbaz. However, if some of the first wine remains but they no longer desire it and bring another wine, one does recite the blessing. From all this you can understand the Rama and Levush's statement, 'even though he has none of the first wine left.' The novelty may concern HaTov VeHaMeitiv because some poskim hold it should not be recited in that case. The Shulchan Aruch's statement, 'and it is not specifically where it was newly brought to him,' means that he did not initially intend to drink it. Rather, even if he intended from the outset to drink it but it was not yet prepared before him, he recites the blessing, as will be explained in seif katan 3. This is clear, unlike the Taz's strained and unusual interpretation of the Shulchan Aruch.
