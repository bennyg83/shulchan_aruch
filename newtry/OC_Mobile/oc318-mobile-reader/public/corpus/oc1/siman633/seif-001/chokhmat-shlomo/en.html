Se'if 1. A sukkah that is higher than twenty amos is invalid, etc. Note: He ruled like Rava in the Talmud, who holds this. Now the Rif wrote as follows: "There is one who says that the halachah follows Rabbah, for he acted in all matters like Rav, etc." These are his words. At first glance, this has no preparation at all. One way or the other: if he holds that until Abaye and Rava the halachah follows the earlier authorities, let him derive it without this, for Rabbah was Rava's teacher and the halachah follows him. And if he holds that from Abaye and Rava onward the halachah follows the later authorities, and that Abaye and Rava themselves are included in the rule, and therefore it would be fitting that the halachah follow the later authority, Rava, then what help is it that he acted in all matters like Rav? In the end, the halachah follows the later authority, Rava. One cannot say that his intent is that since Rav also holds this way, Rava is one against two, since Rabbah and Rav hold unlike him. For if so, in every place where Rabbah disagrees in some law, he should decide that the halachah follows Rabbah because Rav holds that way; and in Bava Basra, chapter 3, where we say that the halachah follows Rabbah regarding land and Rav Yosef regarding money, and because it is uncertain like whom the halachah follows we therefore say that the burden of proof rests on the claimant, why do we not rule even regarding money like Rabbah, because Rav also holds that way in several places? To explain his words, it appears to me as follows. We have the text in the Talmud that Rav Miyasha said in the name of Rav: the dispute is when the walls do not reach the s'chach, but when the walls reach the s'chach, all agree that it is valid. Like whom? Like Rabbah. Afterward it brings there that Rav Huna said in the name of Rav: the dispute is when it has only four amos by four amos, but if it has more than four amos, even above twenty it is valid. Like whom? Like Rabbi Zeira. Afterward it says there that Rabbi Chanan said in the name of Rav: the dispute is when it holds only his head, most of him, and his table, but if it holds more than this, it is valid. Like whom? Like none of them, etc. Now, in any event, both according to Rav Huna and according to Rabbi Chanan, according to both of them, if the sukkah was wider than four amos, it is valid even if it is higher than twenty, even when the walls do not reach the s'chach. If so, it would have been proper to say that they are many against Rav Miyasha, and the halachah should follow them in the name of Rav. If so, it is proven that Rav does not hold like Rabbah. And if so, although Rabbah holds this, the halachah should follow Rav, who is earlier, and the halachah follows the earlier authorities until Abaye and Rava. Do not say that Rabbah and Rav Miyasha are also two, for Rabbah did not say this in the name of Rav. If so, regarding Rav's view in this matter, Rav Miyasha is an individual, while Rav Huna and Rabbi Chanan are many according to Rav. In any event, the halachah should follow them, that Rav holds that they do not disagree when the walls do not reach the s'chach and because of the reason that the eye controls it. Since Rav does not hold like Rabbah, the halachah does not follow a student in place of the teacher. Therefore they said: since the master acted in all matters like Rav, automatically everything Rabbah says in this matter is presumably in the name of Rav, and not that he disagrees with him; rather, certainly Rav also holds so. If so, it is again as though Rabbah too said so in the name of Rav, and he and Rav Miyasha are also two. Therefore the halachah again follows Rabbah, for he and Rav Miyasha are many against Rav Huna in the name of Rav. And although there is also Rav Chanan, nevertheless since the Talmud says that Rav Chanan's view is like none of them, it is certainly not like Rav Chanan in the name of Rav, since all of them disagreed with him. What remains is only the view of Rav Huna in the name of Rav, and Rabbi Zeira was not said because of Rav. If so, in any event, regarding Rav's view, Rav Huna is an individual, and Rabbah and Rav Miyasha are many as Rav's view; and the halachah follows Rav against Rava, because the halachah follows the earlier authorities until Abaye and Rava, and Abaye and Rava are not included, for there are differing views on this, and I have discussed this at length elsewhere. Therefore these poskim will hold that Abaye and Rava are not included. So it appears in my humble opinion to explain their words. I do not presently have time to elaborate on this; examine it well. Now regarding what is stated in Shulchan Aruch, that a sukkah that is higher than twenty amos is invalid: one who examines the Talmud sees three reasons for this. The reason of Rabbi Zeira is because it is written, "and a sukkah shall be for shade by day"; until twenty amos a person sits in the shade of the sukkah, etc. The Talmud says there that all of them did not say like Rabbi Zeira because that verse refers to the days of Mashiach. Rashi explained: those sukkos will be for shade of delight and for shelter, but the mitzvah-sukkah is not for shade. It is proven there that this is what they dispute: according to Rabbi Zeira, a mitzvah-sukkah is for shade, while according to the other authorities it is not for shade. So Rashi later wrote regarding Rav Huna's two points, and this is his language: the name sukkah applies only to what is made for shade; that verse about the days of Mashiach, because it is made for shade, is called a sukkah. These are his words. From this it implies that if according to Rabbi Zeira a sukkah is only that which is made for shade, it is proven that according to the other authorities, who did not say like Rabbi Zeira, we do not require a sukkah to be made for shade. This is very difficult for me from what is written later in the Talmud, page 8, regarding the sukkah of gentiles, women, animals, and Kutim, and of shepherds, fig watchers, city guards, and fruit guards, where it teaches there: provided that it is roofed according to its law. Rav Chisda explains that this means: provided that he made it for the shade of a sukkah. Rashi explains there that although we do not require a sukkah to be made for the sake of the festival, we do require it to be made for the sake of sukkah; and it is because of shade that it is called a sukkah, for it shelters from the heat. These are his words. It is proven and implied from this that we require a sukkah to be for the sake of shade, and so we rule in siman 635. But this is the view of Rabbi Zeira, whereas we rule like Rabbah and like Rava in this matter; if so, we should not require a sukkah to be for the sake of shade. Also, how will Rabbah and Rava explain the Baraisa of these sukkos, where it teaches: provided that it is roofed according to its law, and Rav Chisda explains that this means for the sake of shade? According to them, we do not require it to be for the sake of shade. Against the Talmud itself this is not difficult, for one can say that they hold like Rav Masna later on page 6, that Rabbi Shimon derives from "and a sukkah shall be for shade by day," etc.; and perforce Rabbi Shimon does not hold that the verse is speaking for all generations. Therefore, according to him, we also require it to be for the sake of shade, and that Baraisa, "that it be roofed according to its law," follows Rabbi Shimon. But we rule like the Sages, who hold that that verse refers to future generations. Still, the poskim who ruled that we require it to be for the sake of shade require analysis. Examine this carefully.
