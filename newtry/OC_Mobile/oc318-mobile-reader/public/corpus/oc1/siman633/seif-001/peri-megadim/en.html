Whether. See Turei Zahav at the beginning of Sukkah, like Rava. See Choshen Mishpat, siman 72, in Sema, letter 7, regarding the expression "whether... whether"; his way is to take the simpler case last, that is, "this, and needless to say this." Therefore he takes "whether large," and needless to say small, and needless to say when the walls do not reach the s'chach. One can say the reverse: that when they do not reach the s'chach it is valid, because it does not require permanence, while when they reach the s'chach they are invalid, for then the way is to make it permanent; nevertheless it is invalid in every case. See Bach. Perhaps this is the intent of the Levush, of blessed memory: "whether large and whether small, there is no difference whether they reach or whether they do not reach," and not "whether" regarding reach and do not reach, for I do not know so clearly which is "this" and which is "needless to say this," as stated. See Levush and Chiddushei HaRan, from "You shall dwell in sukkos for seven days," and with Hashem's help below, and in Magen Avraham, this will be explained further.
