1) [Seif 1] A sukkah that is higher than, etc. This means that the interior space of the sukkah is more than twenty amos high, as the Shulchan Aruch states later in this seif.
2) Ibid. A sukkah that is higher than twenty amos, etc. The amos are measured as six-tefach amos, but they are measured exactly, meaning compressed rather than expanded. Beit Yosef; Magen Avraham at the beginning of the siman. This applies stringently to the maximum height of twenty amos. For the minimum height of ten tefachim or minimum area of seven by seven tefachim, however, expanded measurements are required as a stringency, as explained in siman 363. Levushei Serad; Eshel Avraham at the beginning of the siman. An expanded amah exceeds a compressed amah by half a fingerbreadth. Eshel Avraham, ibid. See above, siman 363, seif 26, and in our words there, os 143 and os 144.
3) The aforementioned fingerbreadth is the width of the thumb of a person of average physical build, neither one whose limbs are dwarfed nor one whose limbs are unusually large, and not one who is still young, but one of average build who has reached full growth, at age 35 or older. Maaseh Rokeach on the Rambam, beginning of chapter 4 of Hilchos Sukkah, in the name of R' Avraham, son of the Rambam.
4) Ibid. It is invalid. Since it is so high, one must construct it as a permanent dwelling, whereas we require a temporary dwelling. If it is lower than twenty amos, however, it is valid even if he constructed it sturdily. Magen Avraham, s.k. 1. For we require only that it be capable of being made as a temporary dwelling. Machatzis HaShekel.
5) Ibid. Whether it is large, etc. This excludes the opinion that if it is larger than four amos it is valid, and also excludes the opinion that if the walls reach the s'chach it is valid, as explained in the Beit Yosef; see there.
