(Small paragraph 8) Three handbreadths, etc. But the Tur wrote: specifically inside the dug-out area, etc. However, the Beis Yosef wrote about him: I do not know why. How is it different from a sukkah that was higher than twenty and he built a platform in it, where Rashi validates even from the platform and onward? These are his words. Therefore he wrote plainly in Shulchan Aruch, implying that even outside the dug-out area it is valid, unlike the Tur. The Bach explained the view of the Tur and agreed with him in practice. He distinguished between the case here and the law outside the platform: "s'chach that extends from the sukkah" is validated only where it has complete partitions and valid s'chach, except that it has some deficiency, like the case explained in siman 631, se'ifim 6 and 7. If so, granted, outside the platform it has complete partitions and valid s'chach; even though it is above twenty, nevertheless it has the law of valid s'chach, as the Magen Avraham wrote above in small paragraph 5 in the name of the Rosh, and therefore it is judged as "s'chach that extends from the sukkah." But here, outside the dug-out area, since it is not ten high, it has no partition at all, and it does not have the law of "s'chach that extends from the sukkah."
If so, all of this requires analysis, for he permits sleeping under the walls, also because of "s'chach that extends from the sukkah," even though the partitions are not ten high.
The same applies if it was removed, etc. This is a separate matter. He teaches us a new law, for in Shulchan Aruch it is explained only where one dug into the ground and completed it to ten, and the Magen Avraham teaches us that the same applies if one did not dig into the ground, but the s'chach was thick and he removed from the valid s'chach below, so that the interior space of the sukkah increased and became ten high; this too is valid according to us, for we rule that such a case is valid in siman 635 regarding one who hollows out a haystack. See there in Magen Avraham, small paragraph 2.
