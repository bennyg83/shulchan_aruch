See Turei Zahav and Rashi, Sukkah 4a, and the Rosh there, to answer the question of Rabbeinu Yeshayah: in siman 631 we require in the Mishnah one wall with it, while here there is no wall at all, and nevertheless it is valid; see there. See Magen Avraham, letter 5, and with Hashem's help it will be explained there further. Know that our great master, in Pnei Yehoshua, on what Rashi wrote that the platform is seven by seven handbreadths and something more, wrote that specifically a little more is required according to Rava; see there what he wrote about Maharsha, of blessed memory. To me, the poor one, this requires analysis, for one can say that the essential concern is with the s'chach: whenever it is only slightly more than twenty, it is valid. So too, when he reduced it with earth and straw, there is no need to say that it is as though he placed the walls upon the earth with which he reduced it. So it also appears from the plain words of the Mechaber: "the measure of a sukkah," implying seven by seven handbreadths alone. Rashi perhaps took the ordinary case, since it is not easy to be so exact; when he took seven by seven handbreadths, he wrote "and something more." By contrast, regarding where one built a pillar, the Turei Zahav below, letter 9, takes seven by seven handbreadths and something more, for even so we do not say "extend upward." If there are only exactly seven by seven handbreadths, one can say that everyone agrees that we do not say "extend upward," as Pnei Yehoshua, of blessed memory, wrote there.
