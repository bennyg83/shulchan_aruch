(19) At the side. Meaning alongside one wall, while nevertheless reaching the middle wall; it therefore has two walls.
(20) If it is less than four amos, it is valid. This is through the principle of a bent wall: whenever the distance is less than four amos, we view the wall and sechach together as one wall bent and sloping from the floor to the place of valid sechach, for a wall ordinarily bends up to this measure. The sukkah consequently has three walls. This applies only where the walls reach the sechach. If they do not, we cannot view wall and sechach as one wall, since they do not touch at all.
(21) Only upon the platform. The area beyond cannot be validated through the platform as above, because no valid sechach remains there: that sechach has been treated as part of the wall, so one would be sitting beneath the wall. Toward the entrance, however, one may sit even beyond the platform, for valid sechach exists there, only higher than twenty amos, and it is validated through the platform as in the preceding se'if.
