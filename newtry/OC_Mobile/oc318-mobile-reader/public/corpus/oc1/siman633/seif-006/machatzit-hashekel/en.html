(Small paragraph 6) Upon the platform, etc. "This is not comparable to what is written at the end of siman 632." Meaning, to what the Magen Avraham wrote there in small paragraph 4 in the name of the Tur.
But here, etc. Meaning: specifically at the end of siman 632, where there are two places and two people, one can well say that this person sitting in the inner section considers it s'chach, while the one sitting in the outer section considers it a wall. But here, regarding one person who sits outside the platform, one would have to say that it is considered both wall and s'chach, and we do not say this, for it is two contradictory things. For the person sitting outside the platform wants to validate his place because it is "s'chach that extends from the sukkah," and therefore one must say that he considers what is over the platform to be a valid sukkah, even though the walls are distant from the platform.
One must say that we say "bent wall," and if so he considers it a wall. If so, how can he himself consider it s'chach in order to sit outside the platform? Accordingly, on the side of the platform that is toward the opening of the sukkah, he may sit because of "s'chach that extends from the sukkah." Even in the following se'if, where he built a platform in the middle and we say "bent wall" from every side, nevertheless on the side of the opening it is fit to sit, for there one does not need "bent wall." Also, "bent wall" is not needed there, for it has three walls. Seemingly, one could also say that from one of the sides of the sukkah, outside the platform, if the platform is longer than seven handbreadths, it is permitted to sit at the side outside the platform, after the seven handbreadths of the platform have ended, for from there onward one need not say "bent wall," and it is considered s'chach, so it is "s'chach that extends from the sukkah." However, it appears that "s'chach that extends from the sukkah" is validated only when it is opposite the s'chach of the sukkah itself. This is straightforward. For that which is at the end of siman 631 regarding when he made it like a hut, as the Rosh wrote, he brought proof from chapter 7 of Ohalos.
