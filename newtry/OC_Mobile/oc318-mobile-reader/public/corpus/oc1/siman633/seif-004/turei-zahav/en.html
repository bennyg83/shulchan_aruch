With straw, and he nullified it. This means explicitly, as he concludes afterward. The rule in both cases, whether with earth or with straw, is that it specifically requires explicit nullification. In the Gemara we say that with straw, when he does not intend to remove it, meaning we know that he does not need it, and with unspecified earth, there is a dispute between the Sages and Rabbi Yosei: according to the Sages, it is not nullified even in this case until he nullifies it explicitly, while according to Rabbi Yosei this helps like explicit nullification. The Beis Yosef wrote that it is known that the halachah follows the Sages, and so the Rosh wrote. But the Tur wrote in this case that it is a reduction, and if so he ruled like Rabbi Yosei, which is astonishing. In the edition of Mahara of Prague, another text is written in the Tur, and this is its language: "Likewise, if he reduced it with straw and unspecified earth, where we do not know whether he needs it or not, or even if we know that he does not intend to remove it, such as where he does not need it, it is not a reduction unless he nullified it explicitly, etc." These are his words. This accords with the Sages, and this text is primary. It also seems that this was the text before the Beis Yosef, since he wrote nothing about the words of the Tur, but simply brought that the halachah follows the Sages. The Levush copied the text of the Tur that is before us, but it is not primary.
