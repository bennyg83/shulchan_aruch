(s.k. 2) They descend, etc. — nevertheless hutzin are not so much and require their shade be greater than their sun — from this wording it appears Magen Avraham holds like Rosh that they descend into the interior space; meaning below twenty. Although we teach "the interior of the sukkah," as written in seif 1 in Shulchan Aruch — nevertheless hutzin are inferior. However this is not so, neither in Rosh nor in Ran. For Rosh wrote in tractate Sukkah: if it was, etc., and hutzin, etc., and although part of the sechach is above, we say in the first chapter of Eruvin "the interior of the sukkah we teach"; the explanation: hutzin descend into the interior — meaning they reach the interior space and it is valid; if not (meaning the hutzin's shade is not greater than their sun) it is invalid as if they do not exist. It follows the sechach does not reach the interior space — end of his words. It is explicit he does not require hutzin descend within the interior, only until the interior like generally. Ran also implies so. Why did Beit Yosef change Shulchan Aruch's wording from the gemara's "hutzin descend within the interior" to simply "descend downward" — meaning until the measure explained in seif 1, i.e., until the interior. Therefore it appears there is a scribal error here and it should read: they are not so much and require their shade be greater than their sun — these are Ran's words who asked: since hutzin descend downward why require hutzin alone without combining sechach above still have shade — we say in Eruvin "the interior of the sukkah we teach"; therefore even sechach placed above twenty reaching the interior is valid even if little lower sechach reaching the interior alone has sun greater than shade — for we combine with sechach above twenty; so too with hutzin we could say thus. And thereby answer: hutzin are not so much and require they themselves have shade nevertheless; for otherwise as if they do not exist, and the sechach above twenty would be invalid for now; Rosh explains thus briefly as above.
