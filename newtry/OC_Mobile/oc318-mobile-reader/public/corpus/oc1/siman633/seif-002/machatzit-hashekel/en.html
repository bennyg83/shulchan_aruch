(Small paragraph 2) They descend, etc. Nevertheless, small branches are not so significant, and we require that they descend into the space of twenty. From this language it implies that the Magen Avraham holds that what the Rosh wrote, "until they descend into the space of twenty," means below twenty. Although we learned "the interior space of the sukkah," as written in se'if 1 of Shulchan Aruch, nevertheless small branches are worse. However, this is not so, neither in the Rosh nor in the Ran. For this is the language of the Rosh in tractate Sukkah: if it was, etc., and small branches, etc., and although some of the s'chach is above twenty, we say in the first chapter of Eruvin, "we learned the interior space of the sukkah." This is the explanation: the small branches descend into twenty, meaning that they reach the space of twenty, and it is valid. But if not [meaning that the shade of the small branches is not greater than their sunlight], it is invalid, for it is as though they are not present; it emerges that the s'chach does not reach the space of twenty. These are his words. Thus it is explicit that we do not require the small branches to descend inside twenty, but only until the space, as in general. So too it is implied in the Ran. For this reason the Beis Yosef in Shulchan Aruch changed the language of the Talmud: in the Gemara it says, "and the small branches descend inside twenty," while in Shulchan Aruch he wrote simply, "descend below," meaning until the measure explained in se'if 1, namely until the space of twenty. Therefore it appears in my humble opinion that there is a scribal error here, and this is how it should read: "they are not so significant, and we require that their shade be greater than their sunlight." These are the words of the Ran, who asked: since the small branches descend below, why do we require that the small branches alone, without joining the s'chach above, have shade greater than sunlight? Did we not say in Eruvin that "we learned the interior space of the sukkah," and therefore even if the s'chach is placed above twenty and reaches the space of twenty, it is valid, even though the small amount of lower s'chach that reaches the space of twenty alone has more sunlight than shade, because the s'chach above twenty joins with it? So too with small branches, let us say this. Through this he answered that small branches are not so significant, and we require that they themselves have shade greater than sunlight; for otherwise they are as though they do not exist, and the s'chach is above twenty. This is the content of his words, and in the Rosh this is explained briefly as above.
