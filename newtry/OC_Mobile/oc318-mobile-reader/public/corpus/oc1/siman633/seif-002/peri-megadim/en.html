If. See Turei Zahav, Eruvin 3a. In practice, whenever the interior space is only twenty amos, it is valid. However, analysis is required whenever there is a handbreadth of space between one layer of s'chach and another, a handbreadth-tent, whether it joins to validate up to three handbreadths by lavud. In siman 628, Turei Zahav, letter 1, there it has ten handbreadths, and therefore we require that the upper one stand within twenty from the ground. However, what he wrote there, that if the upper one has no walls but only s'chach, requires analysis. Now I have seen: whenever the upper s'chach has seven by seven handbreadths, and there is between them a measure of ten handbreadths so that the upper s'chach is fit to be called s'chach, even though the upper one has no partitions, it invalidates the lower one, for s'chach alone is called sukkah; see Chiddushei HaRan at the beginning of Sukkah. In this way the Torah invalidated two layers of s'chach. However, it is necessary that the lower one be capable of receiving cushions and pillows, for otherwise the upper one is not fit to be called s'chach and to protect him, since it is impossible to lie on the lower one. But that the upper one must be valid specifically according to the law is not so. This is what Rashi wrote in Sukkah 10a, "to make it valid," meaning the fitness of a sukkah, not a valid sukkah, as stated. But if both are invalid, or the upper one stands above twenty, even according to Rashi it is invalid s'chach. This, however, can be discussed, but the truth is as I wrote.
