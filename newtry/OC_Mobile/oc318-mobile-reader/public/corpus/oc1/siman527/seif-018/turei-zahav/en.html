He baked and did not cook. This is Ravad's wording in Beit Yosef; it appears his intent is Abaye's formulation: he began his dough — finish; Rosh wrote there is room to doubt whether he finishes only this dough he began and will not cook or insulate, or perhaps since he began dealing with one Shabbat need permissibly he finishes all Shabbat needs; the matter is in doubt until a righteous teacher comes; Ravad holds "finish" means even other things of that type — if he baked permissibly he may bake more even after the eruv was lost, but it does not help him to cook afterward in any way for Shabbat need; and if he cooked permissibly it helps only for cooking. He challenged: if he baked first for Shabbat need it is obvious he may afterward bake and also cook for Yom Tov — therefore he explained the first baking was for Yom Tov need and he had not yet prepared anything for Shabbat; afterward the eruv was eaten — he takes what he baked for Yom Tov and leaves it for Shabbat and bakes again for Yom Tov; it teaches that it would be forbidden as baking for Shabbat — it teaches permitted since nevertheless the baking was permissibly; similarly for cooking — if he cooked first permissibly for Yom Tov it helps afterward for cooking for Shabbat but not for baking for Shabbat at all. Levush wrote: he finishes only that dough he began, no more; and likewise if he began to cook he finishes; if he baked or cooked for Yom Tov and the eruv was eaten, behold the baking or cooking was done permissibly — he may leave it for Shabbat need and may bake and cook anew for Yom Tov; his words contradict each other — at first he permitted only that dough, at end he permitted baking anew for Yom Tov; if so all the more permitted to bake anew for Yom Tov at first since he began for Shabbat permissibly — he mixed Ravad's view with Rosh's view:
