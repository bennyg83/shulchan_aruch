He baked but did not cook. This is the language of the Raavad in the Beit Yosef. It seems to me that his view is as follows. Abaye said, we have a tradition: if he began with his dough, he may finish. The Rosh wrote that there is room to doubt whether this means he may finish only the dough that he began and then may not cook or insulate, or perhaps, since he began engaging in one of the Shabbos needs permissibly, he may complete all Shabbos needs. The matter is balanced until a righteous teacher comes. But the Raavad holds that 'he may finish' means even another item of that same category. Thus if he baked permissibly, he may also bake afterward once the eruv was lost, but it does not help him to cook afterward in any way for Shabbos. Likewise, if he cooked permissibly, it helps only for cooking. This is difficult: if he first baked for Shabbos, obviously he may afterward bake and also cook for Yom Tov. Therefore one must explain that the first baking was for Yom Tov, and he had not yet prepared anything for Shabbos. Afterward the eruv was eaten; he takes what he baked for Yom Tov and leaves it for Shabbos, and bakes again for Yom Tov. One might have thought this is forbidden, because it is like baking for Shabbos. Therefore it teaches that it is permitted, since in any case the baking was done permissibly. The same applies to cooking: if he first cooked permissibly for Yom Tov, it afterward helps for cooking for Shabbos, but not for baking for Shabbos at all. The Levush wrote that he completes that dough which he began, but no more, and similarly if he began to cook, he completes it. He then wrote that if he baked or cooked for Yom Tov and the eruv was eaten, the baking or cooking was done permissibly, so he may leave it for Shabbos and then bake and cook afterward for Yom Tov. His words contradict one another, for at the beginning he permitted only that dough, whereas at the end he permitted baking anew for Yom Tov. If so, all the more should it be permitted in the first case to bake anew for Yom Tov, since he began for Shabbos permissibly. He mixed together the view of the Raavad with the view of the Rosh.
