(Seif katan 18) It is permitted, etc., following Rosh, and this is also implied by the Gemara, etc. Beitzah 22b states that one who made no eruv tavshilin may have one loaf baked for him, one pot cooked for him, and one lamp lit for him. Rosh writes that “one lamp” is intended, just like one loaf and one jug, although the Gemara did not state this explicitly. Therefore the custom is to include lighting in the eruv formula. Although the eruv itself was instituted only with bread and a cooked dish, the principal components of a meal, the formula must mention every act forbidden without an eruv. Thus, even if Rif and Rambam disagree with Rosh and require mentioning only baking and cooking, they agree that one who made no eruv at all may light only one lamp, as Rosh inferred by comparing it to one loaf and one jug.
Moreover, initially, etc. Even if one accepts Bach's view that according to Rif and Rambam additional lamps may be lit, Bach himself permits relying upon them only for a mitzvah, namely the Shabbos lamp. Magen Avraham therefore notes that one lamp is permitted according to all views, while there is no obligation to light more than one. Although additional lamps increase joy and domestic peace, as stated in siman 263, seif 8, that is insufficient reason to disregard the authorities who prohibit them.
