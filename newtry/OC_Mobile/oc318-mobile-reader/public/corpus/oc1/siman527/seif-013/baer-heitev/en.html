That is, where Yom Tov falls on Thursday and Friday; which is not so if it falls on Friday and Shabbos, for it is obvious that it is permitted to cook on Friday, which is the first day of Yom Tov, by means of eruv tavshilin. And Maharshal wrote that even if he transgressed and cooked on the first day, that is on Thursday, it is forbidden to eat it on Shabbos. And the Taz disagrees with him and permits eating it on Shabbos, and all the more so there is no prohibition to eat it at least on Yom Tov; see there. If Yom Tov falls on Friday and Shabbos, one should say in the eruv tavshilin formula, 'to do all our needs from one Yom Tov to the next and from Yom Tov to Shabbos'; see the Taz for the reason. And see Magen Avraham, who left it requiring further study: if he knows that on the second day he will have a forced circumstance so that he cannot bake, for example on their festival day, whether it is permitted to bake or cook on the first day if that was his intent at the time of the eruv; see there.
