From the first day of Yom Tov for Shabbos. Meaning, when Yom Tov falls on Thursday and Friday. Since one can bake, cook, and slaughter on Friday, which is only observed because of the custom of our fathers, why should he do so on the first day of Yom Tov, which is certainly holy? But if Yom Tov falls on Friday and Shabbos, it is obvious that one may cook on Friday, the first day of Yom Tov, by means of an eruv tavshilin, since he has no alternative. The Maharshal wrote that even if he transgressed and cooked on the first day, meaning Thursday, it is forbidden to eat it on Shabbos. It is not comparable to what is written later in this siman, that if one transgressed and cooked it is permitted, because there he has nothing to eat, but here, since he can cook the next day for Shabbos, it is forbidden to benefit from it until after Shabbos. I do not recognize this distinction at all, and certainly there is no prohibition to eat it on Yom Tov. The Hagahot Maimoniyot wrote that it is stated in the Yerushalmi, 'from one Yom Tov to the next, and from Yom Tov to Shabbos.' The Raavyah explained this as a case where the two days are Shabbos and Erev Shabbos, and it teaches that before Yom Tov one must mention both. The Beit Yosef brought this and wrote that this means he must say in the eruv, 'through this it shall be permitted to cook from Yom Tov to the next and from Yom Tov to Shabbos,' even though he cooks only on the first day for the first day, since that day for which he is cooking has two names and both must be mentioned. He questioned the wording, because it sounds as though he wants to cook on two days for two other days, which is not so. In my humble opinion the words are simple: if Yom Tov is Friday and Shabbos, saying 'to do all our needs from Yom Tov to Shabbos' is not enough, because every person cooks on a Yom Tov that is also Shabbos more than he needs for Shabbos alone. Thus the extra that he cooks for tomorrow, which does not relate to Shabbos alone, is not included in the phrase 'from Yom Tov to Shabbos.' Therefore he should mention both, to include that he cooks today also for Yom Tov, in the excess over what is needed for Shabbos. It appears to me correct and proper to say this whenever the first day of Yom Tov is on Friday.
