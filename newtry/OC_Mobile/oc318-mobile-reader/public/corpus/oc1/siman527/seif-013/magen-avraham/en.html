He cannot cook. For the second day of Yom Tov is certainly a weekday according to the Torah, and therefore, when he can do it on the second day, it is forbidden to do it on the first. Also, a nearby shevus was permitted, but a distant shevus was not permitted, as stated at the end of chapter 15 of Shabbos. It seems to me that if he knows he will be unable on the second day to bake, for example because it is their festival day, it is permitted to bake or cook on the first day, provided that this was his intent at the time of the eruv. This follows the first reason. But according to the second reason it is forbidden, for certainly if Yom Tov is on Thursday and Friday is a weekday, and there is a circumstance preventing him from baking on Friday, it is forbidden to bake on Thursday for Shabbos by means of an eruv. So too here, and this requires study. Initially one should make the eruv only on Erev Yom Tov, so that he will select a fine portion for Shabbos; see seif 1.
