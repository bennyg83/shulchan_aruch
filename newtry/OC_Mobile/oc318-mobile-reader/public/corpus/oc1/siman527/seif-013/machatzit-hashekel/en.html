(א) (sk 13) Eretz Yisrael to cook, etc. — certainly weekday d'oraisa, for we know the first day is primary and holiness applies on the second due to custom; also shevut is close and this shevut (distant) — Magen Avraham this siman: we hold d'oraisa Shabbat needs are not done on Yom Tov; thus eiruv cannot permit d'oraisa prohibition except we hold Rabbah ho'il; still d'rabbanan prohibition exists — Chazal permitted through eiruv only close shevut — cooking on Yom Tov close to Shabbat, day 6; distant shevut — cooking Thursday when Shabbat is far — not permitted where possible to cook close, day 6. Ran and Maharshal cited this reason but wrote close Shabbat, etc. (see Chayei Adam) — same intent: close Shabbat permitted shevut, not distant.

(ב) And as cited Shabbat ch. 15 — Magen Avraham added from his own view, not in Ran. Magen Avraham: as on every erev Shabbat they blew to announce Shabbat entry and melacha prohibition; likewise Yom Tov on erev Shabbat though Yom Tov also forbids melacha — still blow to announce Shabbat forbids even melacha permitted on Yom Tov such as ochel nefesh. Yom Kippur on erev Shabbat — no Shabbat blast, no comparison. They challenge: still blow so Shabbat is stricter than Yom Kippur — Yom Kippur permits picking vegetables from minchah onward — Rashi: detach leaves from stalks to prepare cutting at night (see 611 Magen Avraham sk 2). R' Yosef: we do not push aside shevut to permit. Rashi: shofar blast is shevut. You say to permit vegetable picking (Rashi: picking vegetables on Shabbat is obvious; Yom Kippur reason does not apply Shabbat). Rav Sheshet baraita R' Eliezer: close shevut they permitted, distant not (Rashi: always push shevut to permit when needed today; here no need for Yom Kippur vegetable picking on erev Shabbat — they will not cook on Shabbat; only prepare after Yom Kippur). Blast would only inform permission another year when Yom Kippur midweek — shevut obligation permitted for close need, not distant. They challenge: close shevut permitted. Mishnah: Yom Tov on erev Shabbat — blow; Yom Tov after Shabbat — do not blow. Why not blow so they know immediate slaughter is permitted (and all ochel nefesh) — clear per R' Yosef — Gemara. Thus reject R' Shimon baraita R' Eliezer who holds even to permit they permitted close shevut; rather to forbid they permitted no shevut even close. His view is true: permitted shevut is only close; R' Shimon in R' Eliezer not rejected. See Pesachim 47a and Chayei Adam at eiruv time — second reason: distant shevut not permitted; only where close shevut can fix on second Yom Tov is distant shevut also permitted.
