(Seif katan 7) And it was lost, and we do not say, etc. This resolves why the ruling is not obvious. Even one who made no eruv at all, where there might have been some basis to penalize him, may rely upon the leading scholar of the city if he did not act deliberately. Certainly, then, one who acted according to the Sages' enactment but whose eruv was lost should be able to rely upon him. The answer is that one might have thought the reverse, that one who made an eruv and lost it is worse because he revealed his intent, etc. Beit Yosef explains likewise.
