And it is a mitzvah upon every leading scholar. It is obvious that whether when he includes himself in this eruv and says 'for me and for the people of my city' and acquires for them a share in his cooked dish, or when he already made an eruv he may also make an eruv on their behalf separately; only I am in doubt whether he may recite the blessing, since it is not known to him whether they will need this according to the Mechaber's view that each one must make an eruv for himself. And further one should examine: when he already made an eruv for himself and wishes to make an eruv on behalf of the townspeople, whether he needs another cooked dish, or whether he may acquire for them with the cooked dish with which he already made an eruv, since one eruv suffices for a hundred; and Tosafos Yerushalmi is in doubt about this; see there.
