7) There. Through an eruv he may initially cook for Shabbos. The Sages associated this with the verse, 'Remember the Shabbos day to sanctify it': remember it when another day comes to cause it to be forgotten. What is the reason for the enactment? Rava says it is so that one will select a fine portion for Shabbos and a fine portion for Yom Tov. Rav Ashi says it is so that people will say: If one may not bake from Yom Tov for Shabbos without an eruv, certainly one may not bake from Yom Tov for a weekday. A Tanna derives it from here: 'That which you will bake, bake, and that which you will cook, cook.' Rabbi Elazar says: One may bake only in reliance upon that which was already baked, and may cook only in reliance upon that which was already cooked. From here the Sages found a Torah allusion for eruv tavshilin. Beitzah 15b. A practical difference between the two reasons is that according to Rava, the eruv must specifically be prepared on erev Yom Tov so that one will select a fine portion for Shabbos. According to Rav Ashi, it may be prepared even before erev Yom Tov. If Sukkos falls on Thursday, one could prepare before Yom Tov a single eruv for that Shabbos and the following Shabbos. Rosh, at the beginning of the second chapter of Beitzah. Yam Shel Shlomo, chapter 2 of Beitzah, siman 1, however, disputes this and writes that even according to Rav Ashi one must prepare an eruv every erev Yom Tov. If it was prepared before erev Yom Tov, it is ineffective even according to Rav Ashi. He writes that this is the common practice. See there. Beis Yosef cites the dispute and concludes that initially we adopt the stringent ruling, while after the fact we are lenient because the matter is rabbinic. See there. Shulchan Aruch rules accordingly below, se'if 14. Levush rules likewise there, Bach agrees, and Magen Avraham, subsection 13, writes likewise. This is also the view of the later authorities, as will be explained there, with Heaven's help.
