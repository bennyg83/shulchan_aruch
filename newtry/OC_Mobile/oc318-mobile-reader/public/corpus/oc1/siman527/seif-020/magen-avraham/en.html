That he give his flour. Through pulling it into possession, and not through a symbolic acquisition. See what I wrote in siman 307.
And if there is no one there, etc. This implies that if there are others there but he does not want to transfer ownership to them, it is forbidden to bake even an exact amount. So ruled the Darkei Moshe. But in the Beit Yosef he wrote that nevertheless it is permitted to bake an exact amount, and so ruled the Yam Shel Shlomo.
The labors explicitly. The Maharshal wrote in responsum 78 that it is forbidden to slaughter from Yom Tov for Shabbos, since he did not explicitly specify in the eruv, 'and to slaughter.' The Bach wrote, may his Master forgive him, for all labors that precede cooking are included in 'to cook,' such as skinning and salting. It seems to me that he later retracted, since he did not write this in his main work. In the responsa of the Rosh, klal 23, chapter 4, it is explicit that it is permitted, and the Beit Yosef cites it. See what I wrote in siman 503, siman 498 seif 8, and siman 528. Nevertheless, it seems to me that it is good to say explicitly, 'and to slaughter.'
It is forbidden to cook for others. Since he cannot cook for himself, one may not permit it on the basis of ho'il, that he could request release from his vow, for we have not found that cooking is permitted because of ho'il except where it is permitted to cook for oneself without ho'il, such as baking challah, and likewise one who bakes from Yom Tov for a weekday. He further says that one who fasts on Rosh Hashanah must learn the entire day, since he is not fulfilling the half that is 'for you,' he must fulfill all of it as 'for Hashem,' and in this Rabbi Yehoshua agrees with Rabbi Eliezer. Thus are the rulings of Mahari Weil, siman 55. According to what I wrote at the end of siman 512, initially we do not permit because of ho'il. See Tosafot in the third chapter of Pesachim. It follows that even a maidservant is forbidden to cook, unlike what the Levush wrote in siman 597; see what I wrote there. When Yom Tov falls on Friday, it is forbidden to cook from Yom Tov for Shabbos, and even an eruv helps only because of ho'il, that guests may arrive, as written in seif 1. Here, since he is forbidden to cook for others, that reasoning does not apply. Nevertheless, others may bake and cook for him.
