{56} One who did not make an eruv, etc. And specifically one of the townspeople who is not secondary to his fellow's dough and cooked dish; but a person's wife and household members, since the householder made an eruv, are secondary to him and are permitted to cook even though they did not make an eruv for themselves at all, and he also did not acquire on their behalf through another.
{57} For others, and even in their homes. It teaches us that we do not say that since they made an eruv and can cook for themselves they can appoint an agent for themselves; for since he did not make an eruv, a prohibition rests upon him to cook.
{58} And also others, etc. Meaning: even in their homes.
{59} To cook for him. That is, from his flour; for even though they made an eruv, it was not permitted for them to take his flour, for his flour too is forbidden so long as it is his.
{60} To others who made an eruv, as a gift. That is, by drawing into their domain or by lifting in any place; for acquisition by a sudar is forbidden (later authorities).
{61} And if there are no others there, etc. And the later authorities agreed that even if there are others there, only he does not wish to transfer ownership to them for some reason, he is likewise permitted to bake one loaf, etc.; however in truth it is very proper that he transfer ownership of his flour to others so that they can bake for him for lechem mishneh.
{62} Scantily, one loaf. That is, even a large loaf that will suffice him for the whole Shabbos; and they permitted him all this for his livelihood because of the honor of Shabbos. And it is obvious that if he has one loaf left over from Yom Tov it is forbidden for him to bake for the sake of lechem mishneh.
{63} He is like one who did not make an eruv, etc. For so the Sages enacted that he specify the main melachos; and if he specified only part, for example he said only 'to bake' and did not mention cooking and burying food to keep warm, those are forbidden. And some disagree with the essence of this law and hold that the saying of 'with this' is only a mitzvah l'chatchilah, but b'dieved, since he set the cooked dish for the sake of an eruv, even if he did not say the formula of 'with this' at all, etc., it does not withhold, and it is permitted to rely on it to do all the melachos from Yom Tov for Shabbos. And for practical law, l'chatchilah one should be stringent like the first reason; therefore if he remembered before the onset of the night of Yom Tov he should go back and take the eruv in his hand and say the formula 'with this,' etc., and he should not go back and recite the blessing again if he already recited it at the beginning. And if he remembered on erev Shabbos he should transfer ownership of his flour to others and they will cook and give to him; and if he has no one to whom to transfer ownership, he may rely on the latter reason.
{64} At all. And if he said the formula 'with this' but did not recite the blessing at the beginning, it does not withhold, for blessings do not withhold.
{65} For he is like one who did not set an eruv, etc. Meaning: just as we hold that that person who did not make an eruv is forbidden to cook from Yom Tov for Shabbos even for the sake of others, since it is forbidden to cook for himself, so too regarding Yom Tov itself, one who is fasting is forbidden to cook for himself, since he does not need it; consequently it was not permitted for him to cook also for the sake of others. And Magen Avraham wrote that consequently if this occurs on Yom Tov adjacent to Shabbos, it is forbidden for him to cook himself l'chatchilah for Shabbos even though he set an eruv; but his household members or others bake and cook for him from what is his, for his flour is not forbidden since he set eruv tavshilin. And see the later authorities who cast doubt on the essence of the Rama's ruling; therefore on Yom Tov adjacent to Shabbos, if he has no others to cook for him, he is permitted to cook himself; and likewise a serving-woman who fasts on Yom Tov may also cook for the needs of the householder.
