Even in their homes. The Tur gives the reason that people do not know he is cooking for others. The Beit Yosef objected: what do people know about whether this person made an eruv or not? Furthermore, even if they know, since he is not cooking in his own home but in the home of others, it is evident that he is cooking for others. It seems to me that one difficulty answers the other. The Tur means that since it is evident that this person did not make an eruv, people will assume he is cooking for himself, only that he is not permitted to cook for himself in his own home because he did not make an eruv, but in another person's home he may cook even for himself, for that is the law according to the opinion of the Ri'a. Therefore they prohibited it entirely. This appears correct to me.
It is like one who did not make an eruv at all. This is astonishing to me, for if one placed an eruv tavshilin and did not recite the blessing at all, it helps, since blessings do not prevent fulfillment anywhere in the Torah, as the Maharshal wrote, cited in seif 6. Why then should it not help here if he said only 'all our needs'? Is this worse than having no blessing at all? Although the enactment of the Sages was that the phrase 'to do all our needs' alone does not suffice, this is so that the blessing should be made in the proper manner and for the reason mentioned above. Nevertheless it is not worse than one who did not bless. The Rama himself wrote in siman 395 that the blessing does not prevent fulfillment. It seems to me that one should not rely on this ruling to repeat the blessing.
