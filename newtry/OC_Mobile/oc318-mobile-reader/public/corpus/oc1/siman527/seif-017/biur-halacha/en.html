He finishes that dough. See Beis Yosef, that the Rosh and the Ran were in doubt about this, and the Mechaber ruled anonymously to be stringent, that specifically that dough; and so the later authorities ruled anonymously; and so Korban Nesanel brought in the name of the Raavan [and especially according to what Nehar Shalom brought in the name of Orchos Chaim in the name of the Raavad, that the Raavad's view is also explicitly thus]. However there are those who are lenient in this: Birkei Yosef in the name of the Radbaz, that if he began he does all his needs; and likewise his student, Shittah Mekubetzes, who inferred carefully from Rashi's commentary, who wrote: he began to prepare it and to bake it, he finishes everything; and Shittah Mekubetzes inferred that his intent is that he finishes even other cooked dishes; and in the Rashi before us this is not written, only in the Rashi in the Rif; and as it appears, before the Ran too there was this girsa, and with a slight variant: 'to bake or to cook, he finishes everything'; however the Ran, even though he wrote thus, nevertheless was in doubt about other cooked dishes, and it appears that he explains 'he finishes everything' as meaning all the melachos of kneading [for example arranging and rolling] or all the melachos of baking of that sort, but regarding other cooked dishes it is doubtful. And from the Rambam's wording, who wrote as follows: that his eruv must remain intact until he finishes all that he needs to bake and cook; if the eruv was eaten or lost or burned before he bakes or cooks, he is forbidden to bake and cook, end quote: behold, at first he wrote explicitly that it must remain intact until he finishes everything; and also from the end of his words, 'before he bakes or cooks,' it is apparently compelled that even if he did one thing, baking or cooking, and only the second is lacking, it is forbidden; and if so, at the end, where he concludes 'he began with his dough or with his cooked dishes, he finishes,' that is of necessity that dough and that cooked dish; so it appears apparently from his wording. And also in Rashi on the Mishnah he likewise wrote explicitly: they ate it or it was lost before he cooked all the needs of Shabbos; it is likewise clear not like what Shittah Mekubetzes inferred, unless we say that Rashi explains that Abaye, who said 'we hold,' disagrees with the Mishnah, and we hold thus for practical law; or that Abaye explains the Mishnah thus, not according to its plain understanding. And nevertheless it appears to me that since in the Raavad [according to what Orchos Chaim copied] it is explicitly implied as a prohibition, and likewise the implication of the Rambam in my humble opinion as above, and likewise in the Raavan brought in Korban Nesanel, and so it is in Rashi's wording on the Mishnah, it is not proper to move from the ruling of the Shulchan Aruch and later authorities.
