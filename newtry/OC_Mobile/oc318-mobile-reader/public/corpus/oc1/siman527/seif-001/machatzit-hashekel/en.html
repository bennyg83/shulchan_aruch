(s.k. 1) Magen Avraham wrote, Ran wrote, etc. — I need to set forth the words of the Talmud briefly. We learned in chapter 2 of Beitzah: Yom Tov that falls on erev Shabbat — one may not cook initially on Yom Tov for Shabbat need unless he placed eruv tavshilin from erev Yom Tov. They said: why must one place eruv tavshilin? Rava said: so that he will set aside a fine portion for Shabbat, etc. — meaning if he does not place eruv tavshilin he might forget that Shabbat follows Yom Tov and eat everything on Yom Tov and have nothing to eat on Shabbat; through placing eruv tavshilin he will remember and set aside for Shabbat as well. R' Eliezer: so they will say one does not bake from Yom Tov to Shabbat — a fortiori from Yom Tov to weekday is forbidden — meaning it appears forbidden to cook from Yom Tov to Shabbat only through eruv tavshilin, for through placing eruv tavshilin for Shabbat it appears he began preparing from erev Yom Tov for Shabbat and then may finish all Shabbat needs on Yom Tov; but if he did not begin through eruv tavshilin from erev Yom Tov it would be forbidden to cook for Shabbat. A fortiori from Yom Tov to weekday is forbidden even to finish, meaning even if he began on Yom Tov nevertheless forbidden for weekday. In Pesachim daf 46b it was stated: one who bakes from Yom Tov to weekday — Rabbah said he is not lashed, for they say: since guests may happen upon him it is fit for him; even though for himself he does not need what he baked, nevertheless it is fit to give to arriving guests. R' Chiya said he is lashed, for there is no "since" etc. Rabbah said to R' Chiya: per your view that there is no "since," how does one bake from Yom Tov to Shabbat? He said: through eruv tavshilin. He said: and through eruv tavshilin we permit a Torah prohibition? In the end R' Chiya said: from Torah Shabbat needs are done on Yom Tov, and Rabbanan decreed on Yom Tov lest they say one bakes from Yom Tov to weekday; since Rabbanan required eruv tavshilin he said to him: you have recognized it. Thus the words of the Talmud; most poskim ruled like Rabbah who said "since." Ran wrote, etc., that from Torah it is forbidden, etc. — meaning only per R' Chiya who has no "since" must one say that from Torah Shabbat needs are done on Yom Tov; but for us who hold like Rabbah who said "since," nevertheless one may cook from Yom Tov to Shabbat through eruv tavshilin — the external reason remains that Shabbat needs from Torah are not done on Yom Tov except through "since"; and so is proven, for Rabbah holds Shabbat needs are not done on Yom Tov from Torah, since he challenged R' Chiya and through eruv tavshilin we permit Torah prohibition as above. Further, we hold like Rabbah at the beginning of Beitzah that preparation and an egg born on Shabbat after Yom Tov is forbidden from Torah because it was completed from yesterday — behold even preparation through Heaven is forbidden from Torah from Yom Tov to Shabbat; a fortiori human preparation, namely kneading and cooking, is forbidden from Torah from Yom Tov to Shabbat; see Magen Avraham s.k. 7 in name of Tosafot beginning of Beitzah. Nevertheless prohibition exists — meaning so Rabbah's answer to R' Chiya should not be difficult; therefore Rabbah said only through "since" he is not lashed but nevertheless prohibition exists, therefore eruv tavshilin is required.
