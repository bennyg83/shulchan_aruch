The Ran wrote in the third chapter of Pesachim that according to the Torah it is forbidden to cook from Yom Tov for Shabbos, and all the more so for a weekday. It is permitted only through the principle of ho'il, that if guests would happen to come, it would be fit for them now as well. Nevertheless, there remains a prohibition, and through an eruv it is permitted, and so it appears in the Gemara; see the end of siman 512. Tosafot in Pesachim 46 wrote that if one cooked close to nightfall, when it cannot be said that guests may come, he receives lashes. So too wrote the Mordechai at the beginning of the second chapter of Beitzah, and the Beit Yosef in the name of the Hagahot Maimoniyot. What the Beit Yosef wrote, that it is explicit in the Gemara that according to the Torah the needs of Shabbos may be done on Yom Tov, has already been challenged by the Yam Shel Shlomo, for that is specifically the opinion of Rav Chisda, whereas we rule like Rabbah. It seems to me that the Rambam's view is also so, and therefore there is no dispute in this matter. He holds like what the Baal HaMaor wrote, that through ho'il it is initially permitted to cook from Yom Tov for Shabbos, since it is considered like a pressing circumstance, only the Sages prohibited it lest people come to cook from Yom Tov for a weekday, or so that he will remember to select a fine portion for Shabbos, as stated in the Gemara. Accordingly it is forbidden to cook close to nightfall, since it is a Torah prohibition and ho'il does not permit it then. Although Tosafot at the beginning of Beitzah wrote that preparation does not apply to cooking, they wrote this only according to the one who does not hold of ho'il; see there. Apart from this, their words are difficult and require reconciliation with the Gemara in Pesachim; see there. But we rule that according to the Torah it is forbidden to cook from Yom Tov for Shabbos close to nightfall. Therefore, when Yom Tov falls on Friday, the practice is to pray Maariv early. See siman 416, seif 2, that even slight preparation is forbidden, and see the end of siman 667. Doubtful darkness. If he accepted Shabbos upon himself, such as by saying Barchu, it is forbidden; see the end of siman 261. It seems to me that one who is in the synagogue and did not make an eruv tavshilin, and if he goes home to make one the time for Minchah will pass, should pray Minchah and not make the eruv, for he can transfer ownership of his flour to others.
