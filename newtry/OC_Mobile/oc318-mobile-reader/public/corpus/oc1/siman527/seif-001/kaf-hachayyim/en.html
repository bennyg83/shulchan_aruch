1) [Se'if 1] When Yom Tov falls on Friday, etc. There is no distinction between the first day of Yom Tov and the second day observed in the Diaspora. When the second day of Yom Tov in the Diaspora falls on Friday, it is likewise forbidden to cook for Shabbos in a separate pot without an eruv tavshilin prepared before the first day of Yom Tov, as written below, se'if 22. See there.
2) There. One may not initially cook for Shabbos, etc. In Beitzah 2b, Rabbah derives this from the verse, 'On the sixth day they shall prepare that which they bring.' A weekday prepares for Shabbos, and a weekday prepares for Yom Tov, but Yom Tov does not prepare for Shabbos, etc. Tosafos there ask: Since preparation is a Torah prohibition, how may one bake and cook on Yom Tov for Shabbos? If the answer is that an eruv tavshilin permits it, how can a rabbinic enactment come and uproot a Torah prohibition against preparation? They answer that Rabbah follows his own reasoning in Pesachim 46b, where he applies the principle that since the food would be fit if guests happened to arrive, it is presently considered fit as well. See there.
3) If one baked or cooked on Yom Tov for a weekday, the Amoraim disagree in Pesachim 46b. Rabbah says that he is not lashed, because we apply the principle that since guests might happen to arrive, the food is fit for him. See there. Rif and Rosh there rule according to Rabbah. Rambam, chapter 1 of Hilchos Yom Tov, law 15, rules likewise, as do Ramban in Milchamos, Raah in Sefer HaMaor, and Ran there in the third chapter of Pesachim. Sefer HaMaor writes there that although Rabbah disagrees regarding lashes, he concedes that initially one may not bake. See there. See below, subsection 7.
4) Tosafos write there in Pesachim that if one cooked close to nightfall, when it cannot be said that guests might arrive, he is lashed. Mordechai at the beginning of the second chapter of Beitzah writes likewise, as does Beis Yosef in the name of Hagahos Maimoniyos. Magen Avraham at the beginning of the siman cites them and writes that for this reason the custom when Yom Tov falls on Friday is to recite Maariv early. See there. He means that this prevents people from cooking for Shabbos close to nightfall, as Machatzis HaShekel writes. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 8; Mikraei Kodesh, siman 32, subsection 8, write likewise. Based on this, Mishbetzos Zahav at the end of siman 259 writes that when Yom Tov falls on Friday, it is proper to place the cholent in its insulated setting early enough that before nightfall it will be cooked at least to the degree of maachal ben Derusai, half cooked. See there. Some authorities, however, maintain that preparing the needs of Shabbos on Yom Tov is permitted under Torah law, as written in Beis Meir, Chemed Moshe, subsection 1, and Mishnah Berurah in Biur Halachah. Mishnah Berurah, subsection 3, therefore writes that in pressing circumstances one may be lenient on the second day of Yom Tov that falls on Friday if the food for Shabbos was delayed until close to Shabbos. Even on the first day of Yom Tov, it is possible that one may be lenient in pressing circumstances. Initially, however, one must certainly take care regarding this, especially on the first day of Yom Tov, whose sanctity is under Torah law. See there.
5) There. In a separate pot. In one pot, however, he may cook as much as he wishes. Tur and Beis Yosef; Taz, subsection 1; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1; Chemed Moshe, subsection 1. See above, siman 503, se'if 1, and our words there, with Heaven's help.
6) There. But he may cook several pots for Yom Tov, and if he has leftovers, etc. This applies even if an entire pot remains, provided that at the outset he thought it would be needed for Yom Tov and did not employ subterfuge, as explained below, se'if 24. See there. See above, siman 503, se'if 1, in the gloss, and our words there, with Heaven's help.
7) There. Through an eruv he may initially cook for Shabbos. The Sages associated this with the verse, 'Remember the Shabbos day to sanctify it': remember it when another day comes to cause it to be forgotten. What is the reason for the enactment? Rava says it is so that one will select a fine portion for Shabbos and a fine portion for Yom Tov. Rav Ashi says it is so that people will say: If one may not bake from Yom Tov for Shabbos without an eruv, certainly one may not bake from Yom Tov for a weekday. A Tanna derives it from here: 'That which you will bake, bake, and that which you will cook, cook.' Rabbi Elazar says: One may bake only in reliance upon that which was already baked, and may cook only in reliance upon that which was already cooked. From here the Sages found a Torah allusion for eruv tavshilin. Beitzah 15b. A practical difference between the two reasons is that according to Rava, the eruv must specifically be prepared on erev Yom Tov so that one will select a fine portion for Shabbos. According to Rav Ashi, it may be prepared even before erev Yom Tov. If Sukkos falls on Thursday, one could prepare before Yom Tov a single eruv for that Shabbos and the following Shabbos. Rosh, at the beginning of the second chapter of Beitzah. Yam Shel Shlomo, chapter 2 of Beitzah, siman 1, however, disputes this and writes that even according to Rav Ashi one must prepare an eruv every erev Yom Tov. If it was prepared before erev Yom Tov, it is ineffective even according to Rav Ashi. He writes that this is the common practice. See there. Beis Yosef cites the dispute and concludes that initially we adopt the stringent ruling, while after the fact we are lenient because the matter is rabbinic. See there. Shulchan Aruch rules accordingly below, se'if 14. Levush rules likewise there, Bach agrees, and Magen Avraham, subsection 13, writes likewise. This is also the view of the later authorities, as will be explained there, with Heaven's help.
8) There, in the gloss. An explanation of the concept of eruv, etc. Another explanation for the name eruv tavshilin is that through it the cooked foods of Yom Tov and Shabbos are joined together, since one cooks for both on one day.
9) There, in the gloss. It is permitted to place this eruv even during twilight. The period of twilight was explained above, siman 261, subsection 1. It is explained there that when three medium-sized stars are visible, it is certainly night. See there. Mishnah Berurah, subsection 4, writes likewise.
10) There, in the gloss. Even during twilight. Shulchan Aruch rules likewise above, siman 393, se'if 2, regarding eruv chatzeros and shitufei mevo'os. See there. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 9, gives the reason: a doubt concerning a rabbinic law is treated leniently, and we presume that it is still daytime. These are his words. Regarding whether one should recite a blessing over an eruv placed during twilight, see above, siman 393, subsection 11. There we wrote in the name of Responsa Noda BiShe'arim, at the end of siman 6, that the question remains unresolved. See there. Therefore, one should recite the blessing without the Divine Name and Kingship, and should say only the Leshem Yichud, etc.
11) There, in the gloss. Even during twilight. If he accepted Yom Tov upon himself, such as by answering Barechu, he may no longer prepare the eruv, as written at the end of siman 261. Magen Avraham, subsection 1. Taz, subsection 3, however, writes in the name of Raavan, siman 168, that even if one did not prepare an eruv until after Barechu, he may do so as long as the congregation has not recited the Yom Tov prayer, because Yom Tov is not accepted until the Yom Tov prayer. See there. Ateres Zekeinim writes likewise in that name, and Nesiv Chaim concludes accordingly. Yad Aharon in Hagahos HaTur distinguishes: Raavan's ruling applies when the congregation said Barechu but the individual himself did not. He may then prepare the eruv after Barechu. If he himself said Barechu, however, he has already accepted Yom Tov and may not prepare the eruv. These are his words. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 9, writes that if most of the congregation has already said Barechu, he is drawn after the majority, Yom Tov sanctity takes effect upon him as well, and he may not prepare an eruv tavshilin, just as on Yom Tov itself. Certainly, if he personally accepted Yom Tov, such as by reciting the Yom Tov Amidah, he may not prepare an eruv even if the congregation has not yet said Barechu. These are his words. Chayei Adam, rule 102, subsection 2, likewise writes that if he has already accepted Yom Tov, such as by saying Barechu, or the congregation has already said Barechu, he may not place the eruv. Nevertheless, see above, siman 263, subsection 15, where we wrote that in a city with many synagogues, one congregation is not drawn after another, even if one synagogue contains the majority. See there. In such a city, an individual is not drawn after the majority as long as he has not answered Barechu, because he can pray in the later synagogue. Consequently, he may also prepare the eruv as long as he has not yet answered Barechu. Likewise, if he said Barechu but there are others who have not yet done so, he may ask them to prepare the eruv for him, as written above, siman 263, se'if 17, and our words there, subsection 100. See there.
12) If one placed an eruv tavshilin before the congregation recited the Amidah, then although they already said Barechu, his eruv is valid, because one may rely upon Raavan. Chayei Adam there. Mishnah Berurah, subsection 4, likewise writes that in pressing circumstances, even after Barechu, as long as the congregation has not recited Maariv and it is not yet dark, one may prepare an eruv. All this applies in a city with only one synagogue. If there are many synagogues, the individual is not drawn after a congregation that has already prayed, as stated above.
13) One who is in the synagogue and did not prepare an eruv tavshilin, and who would miss the time for Minchah if he went home to prepare it, should pray Minchah and not prepare the eruv, because he can transfer ownership of his flour to others. Magen Avraham, subsection 1; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 3; Yad Aharon, Hagahos HaTur; Chemed Moshe, subsection 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 10; Chayei Adam there; Mikraei Kodesh, siman 32, subsection 11; Mishnah Berurah there. See below, se'if 20.
14) Nevertheless, if he can prepare his eruv through an agent, he should send an agent to prepare it before praying Minchah. Mishnah Berurah there. The agent should say: 'Through this eruv, may it be permitted for so-and-so son of so-and-so and all members of his household to bake, etc., and to perform all their needs from Yom Tov for Shabbos.' Regarding whether the agent may recite the blessing, see below, subsection 58.
15) A person was sitting in the synagogue and remembered that he had not prepared an eruv tavshilin. In the synagogue he recited the blessing over the mitzvah of eruv and said, 'Through such-and-such cooked food in my house, may it be permitted for me to bake, etc.' It is obvious that he acted irrationally and accomplished nothing, because he must specifically hold the cooked food in his hand and say, 'Through this, etc.' Chaim Sha'al, volume 1, siman 29; Zechor LeAvraham, volume 3, subsection 27. Halachah Gevurta, printed in Tiferes Yisrael on Mishnayos, chapter 2 of Beitzah, mishnah 1, however, writes that this is effective. One may even initially do so if he is in the synagogue close to nightfall, lacks time to go home, and it is still before Barechu. He should say: 'The bread and cooked food that I will take when I come home shall from now be designated for an eruv tavshilin. Blessed are You, Havayah, our Elokim, King of the universe, etc.' See there. Mikraei Kodesh there, Likutei Rima, subsection 16, cites this. See below, se'if 6.
