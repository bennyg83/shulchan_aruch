(א) And through eiruv, etc. — see Mishna Berurah: the prohibition is only d'rabbanan because of the reasoning of ho'il, since guests may arrive; therefore eiruv helps — so wrote Magen Avraham in the name of Ran, like Rabbah in Pesachim 47 and Milchamot in Beitzah [Magen Avraham wrote in the name of Maor and Chakham Tzvi challenged — the Maor wrote so only per Rif, but Milchamot holds so]. So too Tosafot Eruvin 48 first answer: for this reason baking and cooking are permitted and we are not concerned for hachanah d'oraisa; where ho'il does not apply, e.g. close to dark, Tosafot Pesachim 46 wrote it is d'oraisa — cited also in Rashba's chiddushim in Beitzah; Magen Avraham brought Mordechai: close to dark is forbidden, ho'il does not apply, eiruv does not help — so Hagahot Maimoniyot and Semag: all his French teachers hold Shabbat melachot are not done on Yom Tov except through ho'il; where ho'il does not apply it is d'oraisa. For these poskim melacha prohibition and hachanah are one, as Milchamot explains. However Ritzba in Eruvin 48 [Tosafot at the start of Beitzah in Stam is only Ritzba's view; Semag cited this only as a lone view] holds hachanah applies only to a new matter, not baking/cooking which is mere repair; therefore eiruv helps [if for melacha, his view is like Rav Chisda that Shabbat melachot are done on Yom Tov — see Ittur]. Meiri also distinguishes hachanah from melacha; melacha was permitted only due to ho'il per Tosafot, Semag, Ramban; for them close to dark is forbidden — Magen Avraham, Chemed Moshe, Gra wrote we therefore pray Maariv early when Yom Tov falls on erev Shabbat; Ateret Zekenim in Levush. However Rabbenu Efraim and Maor rule fully like Rav Chisda: Shabbat melachot on Yom Tov are d'oraisa with no distinction between earlier and close to dark. Rambam: although he rules like Rabbah that one who does melacha from Yom Tov to weekday is not lashed for ho'il, for Shabbat he adopts Rav Chisda that d'oraisa Shabbat melachot are done on Yom Tov, only d'rabbanan lest they be lenient also from Yom Tov to weekday [it appears even Yom Tov to weekday, though not lashed, is still d'oraisa — see his wording in halachah 9 and 13 ch. 1; Chemed Moshe elaborated]. Hagahot and Semag understood Rambam so; Beit Yosef on Rambam; plain wording; Chemed Moshe disagrees with Magen Avraham's diversion; Beit Meir concludes Rambam follows Rav Chisda: Shabbat needs are done on Yom Tov d'oraisa, unlike Maharshal and Magen Avraham — because Rav Ashi's plain words imply: lest they say from Yom Tov to Shabbat is forbidden, kal vachomer to weekday — thus d'oraisa permitted for Shabbat, per Rav Chisda, not ho'il [similarly Yerushalmi Yom Tov: by Torah it is forbidden and eiruv tavshilin permits; R' Avahu: by right one should bake/cook Yom Tov to Shabbat — if you say so, also Yom Tov to weekday]. Although Ramban reconciled Rav Ashi also to Rabbah — Milchamot — plain words follow Rav Chisda as Maor wrote; Pirush Chadash on Rav Chisda in Pesachim matches Rav Ashi in Beitzah — one view; this is Rambam's view; Ritzba in Tosafot Eruvin and Beitzah requires halacha like Rav Chisda [Chemed Moshe and Beit Meir: still melacha prohibition — see their discussion]. Thus l'chatchila one must be strict per these authorities on a d'oraisa matter as Acharonim wrote; b'dieved in pressing need one may rely on them for close to dark; Rosh and Tur did not cite Tosafot on close to dark [see Perishah]; Stam of Talmud permits with eiruv without distinction — all the more on second day of Yom Tov which is d'rabbanan one may rely leniently in need.
