And by means of an eruv, etc. See Mishnah Berurah, what he wrote: that the prohibition is only rabbinic, because of the reason of ho'il (since guests may happen to come), and therefore the eruv is effective; so wrote Magen Avraham in the name of the Ran, and that is like Rabbah's view in the Gemara, Pesachim folio 47, and so it is in the Milchamos in Beitzah [and in Magen Avraham he wrote in the name of the Meor, and Chemed Moshe noted about him that in truth the Meor wrote thus only according to the Rif's view, but the Milchamos holds thus]. And so is the view of Tosafos in Eruvin 48 in the first answer, that for this reason it is permitted to bake and cook, and we are not concerned about hachanah which is forbidden according to the Torah; and in a place where the reason of ho'il does not apply, for example close to nightfall, Tosafos wrote in Pesachim folio 46 that it is forbidden according to the Torah, and Rashba's novellae in Beitzah also copied their words; and Magen Avraham likewise brought in the name of the Mordechai that close to nightfall it is forbidden, for ho'il does not apply, and an eruv does not avail; and so it is in Hagahos Maimoniyos and Sefer Mitzvos Gadol, that the view of all his French teachers is that the melachos of Shabbos are not done on Yom Tov, and only by reason of ho'il it is permitted; and consequently we learn that where ho'il does not apply it is forbidden according to the Torah. And according to these poskim, the prohibition of melacha and of hachanah are one, for in every melacha hachanah applies, and so it is explained explicitly in the Milchamos. However, the view of Ritzba in Eruvin 48 [and what Tosafos wrote anonymously at the beginning of Beitzah is only Ritzba's approach, and likewise in Sefer Mitzvos Gadol he copied this only as an individual view] is that the prohibition of hachanah applies only to something new, and not to the melacha of baking and cooking, which is mere fixing; therefore the eruv is effective, for regarding these the prohibition of hachanah does not apply [and if we say because of melacha, it appears that his view is like Rav Chisda, that the melacha of Shabbos is done on Yom Tov; and so too appears the view of the Itur; see there]. And so the Meiri wrote to distinguish between hachanah and melacha; however his view is that the prohibition of melacha was permitted only because of ho'il, like Tosafos and Sefer Mitzvos Gadol and the aforementioned Ramban; and according to them, close to nightfall it is forbidden; and so wrote the later authorities, Magen Avraham and Chemed Moshe and the Alter Rebbe, and they wrote that for this reason the custom is to pray Arvis early on Yom Tov that falls on erev Shabbos; and so it appears from Eliyah Rabbah in the name of the Levush. However there are also views of Rishonim who are lenient in this, namely Rabbeinu Ephraim and the Meor, who ruled entirely like Rav Chisda, and the melacha of Shabbos is done on Yom Tov according to the Torah; and according to them there is no difference between while it is still day and close to nightfall. And likewise the Rambam: even though he ruled like Rabbah that one who does melacha from Yom Tov to a weekday is not flogged because of ho'il, nevertheless regarding Shabbos he adopted Rav Chisda's reasoning, that according to the Torah the melacha of Shabbos is done on Yom Tov, and only rabbinically [they forbade] so that they not be lenient also from Yom Tov to a weekday [and it is implied that he holds that even from Yom Tov to a weekday, even though he is not flogged, nevertheless there is a Torah prohibition; and so it is implied further in his wording in hilcha 9 in chapter 1 and in hilcha 13 there; see there; and Chemed Moshe already elaborated on this]. And so the Hagahos and Sefer Mitzvos Gadol understood his view; and so too is Beis Yosef's view of the Rambam's intent; and so is the plain sense of his words, as Chemed Moshe wrote, and he disagrees with Magen Avraham who shifted the Rambam's words to another view. And likewise in Sefer Beis Meir he elaborated and concludes that the Rambam stands in Rav Chisda's approach, that the needs of Shabbos are done on Yom Tov according to the Torah, not like Maharshal and Magen Avraham; and his reason is that the plain sense of Rav Ashi's words indicate thus, for he said, 'so that they will say: from Yom Tov to Shabbos it is forbidden, a fortiori to a weekday,' hence according to the Torah it is permitted for Shabbos, like Rav Chisda's view, and not because of ho'il [and similarly it is in the Yerushalmi, chapter of Yom Tov, and this is its wording: come and see: according to the Torah it is forbidden, and eruvei tavshilin permit; R' Abahu said: by law one should bake and cook from Yom Tov for Shabbos; if you say so, he will also bake and cook from Yom Tov to a weekday, end quote]. And even though the Ramban toiled to reconcile Rav Ashi's words also according to Rabbah's view (see there in the Milchamos), nevertheless the plain sense of the words appears that Rav Ashi stands in Rav Chisda's approach, as the Meor wrote; and in my humble opinion, from Rabbeinu Chananel's commentary too it is so implied, for he copied upon Rav Chisda's words in Pesachim exactly the wording of Rav Ashi in Beitzah, hence he holds that it is one approach; see there; and this is the Rambam's reasoning as well. And likewise Ritzba in Tosafos Eruvin and Tosafos at the beginning of Beitzah compel that the halacha follows Rav Chisda [as Chemed Moshe and Beis Meir wrote: for otherwise, granted that there is no issue of hachanah, still there remains a prohibition of melacha; see their words where they elaborated]. Therefore it appears that even though l'chatchilah one must certainly be careful like the view of all these aforementioned authorities, and that it is a matter of Torah law, as the later authorities wrote, nevertheless at a time of pressure one may say that these early teachers of ours are worthy to rely upon regarding close to nightfall; also because the Rosh and the Tur did not copy Tosafos's words regarding close to nightfall [see Prisha]. And also because the Gemara stated this law anonymously, that with an eruv it is permitted, and they made no distinction at all in this; and all the more so on the second day of Yom Tov, which is rabbinic, certainly one may rely to be lenient when needed.
Doubtful nightfall. See Mishnah Berurah regarding if he accepted Yom Tov upon himself; and see siman 263, that lighting a Shabbos lamp is considered acceptance for women, and it requires further study what the law is here. And apparently if the women lit a Yom Tov lamp, it is then impossible to make an eruv on their behalf even though it is still day, since they already accepted Yom Tov upon themselves. And it is somewhat implied there that lighting the lamps has the status of communal acceptance, since that is the custom; see there, for some say that a condition does not avail. Only one may discuss: perhaps specifically on Shabbos, when everyone lights at one time and they were accustomed to accept Shabbos thereby; but on Yom Tov, when not everyone lights at one time, possibly they were not accustomed to accept thereby, and it has the status of an individual who lights, who does not accept Shabbos by his lighting. And even if we say that a woman's way is to accept even on Yom Tov by her lighting, nevertheless on Yom Tov possibly there is in this only the status of an individual's acceptance, since they do not light at one time, and things that are permitted to do in doubtful nightfall are still permitted. However l'chatchilah one must certainly be careful to make the eruv before she lights the lamps.
