Se'if 22: If he remembered, etc. There, folio 6a.
And some say, etc. The Ran wrote that so it is in the Yerushalmi, and this is its wording in chapter 2 of Yom Tov, halacha 1: R' Chiyya the Great went up to his house; they said to him, we forgot in the West; he said to them, are there lentils here from yesterday? They said to him, yes. This teaches that even one kind [suffices]. That is to say: and if so, unlike the tanna of the beraisa, that even according to Beis Hillel two cooked foods are required. Were there beets among them? This teaches that even though there is not a kezayis in them [it works]. Was there [the required] measure among them? This teaches that one need not stipulate a condition. That is to say: and one need not stipulate a condition regarding eruvei tavshilin from one Yom Tov to the next; rather he relies on a cooked dish that he has from erev Yom Tov even though he did not rely [on it] from erev Yom Tov. And they answer: R' Chisda said, R' Chiyya the Great had a condition; that is to say, that he had stipulated from one Yom Tov to the next, and even so he said, 'are there lentils here from yesterday.' And the first reason appears to explain 'this teaches that one need not stipulate' as meaning that he need not rely on it from erev Yom Tov; R' Chisda said, etc., meaning that in truth he relied on it from erev Yom Tov and stipulated that if he forgets to make an eruv he will rely on the cooked dish that he has from before the festival; and so it is there in the Mordechai.
