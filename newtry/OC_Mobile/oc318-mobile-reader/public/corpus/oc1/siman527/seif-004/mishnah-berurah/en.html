{11} To dip as relish. For example meat and fish and eggs and other kinds of cooked relish that are customarily used to dip bread into. Maharil would place the meat on a plate and not on the challah because of repulsion, and he also took a whole loaf and would afterward take it for lechem mishneh and cut over it at the third meal, since one mitzvah was done with it, let another mitzvah be done with it.
{12} To exclude porridge. That is, barley groats that are called gagelach, which people are not accustomed to use to dip bread into, but rather eat them by themselves to be satisfied like bread. And even in places where they are accustomed to dip bread into them, their intent is nullified against everyone; and other kinds of legumes depend on the local custom: in a place where they do not dip bread into them, it is forbidden to make an eruv with them.
