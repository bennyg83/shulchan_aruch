Smoked. See Baer Heitev; and see Chacham Tzvi, siman 100, according to the view whose opinion is that l'chatchilah one should not set eruv tavshilin except with [food] cooked on an actual fire, and b'dieved one may rely either on pickled or on salted [food] that was not cooked, for eruv tavshilin is rabbinic and many were lenient regarding it. Therefore if one set eruv tavshilin with herring it is effective b'dieved, and even l'chatchilah when he has no other option, for a time of pressure is treated like b'dieved, and he should not recite the blessing, but only say the customary condition, 'with this it shall be permitted,' etc. And likewise with pickled [food] one should conduct oneself thus: not to set it except at a time of pressure, and to stipulate without a blessing; see there.
