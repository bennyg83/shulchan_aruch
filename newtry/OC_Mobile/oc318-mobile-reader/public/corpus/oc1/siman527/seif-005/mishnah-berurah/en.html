{13} Roast. And specifically actual roast; but salted, even though it is considered like boiling-hot roast, nevertheless one may not make an eruv with it, and it is worse than pickled below; therefore one may not make an eruv with salted fish called herring even though it is fit to eat as it is raw; however those lying at the bottom of the barrels that are pickled in brine have the status of pickled, and one may make an eruv with them.
{14} Thoroughly cooked. Cooked much more than needed.
{15} Pickled. For pickled foods are considered like cooked; and the measure of pickling in water and other liquids is from evening to evening; and in vinegar and brine, if it was only soaked long enough that if placed on the fire it would begin to boil, it is considered like cooked; and all this if it is fit to eat by means of the pickling.
{16} Or smoked. And this too where it is fit to eat it thus.
{17} A kind of small fish. That is, Spanish kolyas in the Gemara, which are soft and in hot water are finished for eating, and cooking is credited to them.
{18} Cooked apples, and the same applies, etc. And all this specifically if the practice of that place is to dip bread into them (Magen Avraham and other later authorities).
{19} That he cooked them. Or roasted them (Gemara); that is, even if they were salted initially and were fit to eat raw and need no cooking, one might have thought that cooking them counts as nothing; it teaches us [otherwise]; and this novel point likewise applies regarding cooked apples, even though they were fit to eat raw.
