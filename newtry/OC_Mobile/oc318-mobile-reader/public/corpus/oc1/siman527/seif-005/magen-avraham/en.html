Pickled. Rashi explains, in vinegar or mustard. In Yoreh De'ah, siman 87, it is explained that if something soaked for a full day in a liquid, it is called pickled. See the Darkei Moshe and the Beit Yosef. It seems to me that if it is not fit to be eaten through the pickling or smoking, one may not use it for the eruv. But salted food may not be used even if it is fit to eat, as we say regarding small fish. Although in Chullin it says that salted is like boiling hot, and pickled is like cooked, this implies that salted is not like cooked. However, if it was pickled in brine or vinegar and remained there long enough that, if placed on the fire, it would begin to boil, it is like cooked if it is eaten that way; see Yoreh De'ah at the beginning of siman 105.
Cooked fruit. But not raw fruit. However, from the Maggid Mishneh it appears that even cooked fruit is forbidden if it is not the normal way to use it as an accompaniment to bread. As for raw fruit, he did not need to say it, for it is obvious that a cooked dish is required; see seif 4.
