{67} And leave it burning. And afterward he may carry it and place it wherever he wishes.
{68} And some say that even, etc. And so the world is accustomed to be lenient like this view; and see above in siman 503, se'if 1 in the gloss, and in Mishnah Berurah, where this law was explained at length.
{69} Several pots, etc. It does not mean that he cooks different kinds in several pots for the needs of Yom Tov and increases in each for the sake of Shabbos, for that is permitted even according to the first view; rather it comes to add that even if he cooks them only for the sake of Shabbos, and only in order to permit cooking he eats a bit of each one on Yom Tov, that too is permitted.
{70} It is permitted. That is, that in each one there be a different kind by itself; for otherwise the artifice is recognizable, for what he will eat a bit from each pot he can taste from one.
{71} Provided that he eats, etc. The Taz wrote that this is l'chatchilah; but if it happened that he did not eat at all of one kind, it is not forbidden for Shabbos, since at any rate at the time of cooking he intended to eat it today, so his act was done permissibly. And Beis Meir disagrees with this, for since the essence of his cooking is for the needs of tomorrow and he does not need it for Yom Tov, and the whole permit is only that he at least eats a bit of it on Yom Tov, for thereby it is not recognizable artifice; and if afterward he will not eat, the artifice is recognizable to all, for it turns out that all his cooking was only for the needs of tomorrow alone. And so it appears to be stringent in this, after several later authorities hold that the essential law follows the first view, that one should not permit cooking except in that pot which he truly cooks for the needs of Yom Tov, only that he also increases in it for the sake of Shabbos; only that the world was accustomed to be lenient like the latter view as above; at any rate one should not add leniency upon leniency.
