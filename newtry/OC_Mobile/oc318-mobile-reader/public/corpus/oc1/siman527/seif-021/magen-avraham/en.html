If he remembered, etc. The Yam Shel Shlomo wrote that if he made a cooked dish specifically for eruv tavshilin, or set it aside for that purpose, even if he intended to bless on it and forgot, it is permitted to cook, for blessings do not prevent fulfillment; even if his wife set it aside without his knowledge, it is permitted. This requires study, for is this better than one who said the formula but did not specify the labors explicitly, which is forbidden as written in seif 20? What he wrote, that blessings do not prevent fulfillment, means where he said, 'through this it shall be permitted,' but did not say the blessing. Although by eruv chatzeiros it is permitted after the fact without any declaration, there the main action is that they join together, but here the declaration is central, so that he will select a fine portion for Shabbos. Therefore it seems to me that one should not rely on this. He further wrote that nowadays if one forgot and did not make an eruv, it is permitted, for the formula of the eruv says, 'for us and for all Israel,' so each person makes an eruv for all Israel. The Bach wrote likewise. But they did not carefully observe people's practice, for we rule that one must grant ownership, as written in seif 10, and no one does this unless he is knowledgeable. Therefore one should not rely on this; see seif katan 9.
And there are those who say that even, etc. See siman 503, that even on an ordinary Yom Tov it is permitted.
