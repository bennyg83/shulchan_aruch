One who did not make an eruv, etc. See Mishnah Berurah, that a person's household members are secondary to him; so wrote Yam Shel Shlomo, and the later authorities copied him. However regarding his Hebrew servant I do not know; for according to the Rosh's reasoning in chapter 2, siman 16, the law of who needs an eruv for himself depends on that person through whom they acquire; and since we hold that through his Hebrew slave and maidservant he can acquire for others, consequently apparently they need an eruv for themselves and are not secondary to him unless he gave the eruv into their hands or acquired for them through another. And according to the words of Machatzit HaShekel in letter 11 it is implied that since they eat from his foods and cooked dishes, consequently the eruv that he prepared while it was still day is also dragged upon them; and possibly the Rosh too agrees to this reason; and this requires further study.
