(Seif katan 10) They rely, etc. He need not inform him; see seif 7. This prevents misunderstanding Rama's statement that they may rely upon him as meaning that everyone may initially rely upon the leading scholar merely because this is his regular practice. Magen Avraham rejects that reading, for one who initially relies upon him is certainly considered negligent, as stated in this seif, regardless of the scholar's practice. Rather, Rama comments on Mechaber's case of one who was prevented by circumstances, who may rely upon the leading scholar and need not have known that the scholar set it aside for him. Ordinarily, however, before the prevented person cooks, the scholar must inform him, implying that until then he may not cook for Shabbos and may not assume that the scholar certainly included the entire city, despite the mitzvah upon the scholar to do so. Rama therefore adds that where the scholar regularly makes an eruv for everyone, one may rely upon him without specific notice. The person prevented by circumstances need not be informed and may assume that the scholar included him, since this is his regular practice.
