{33} He must acquire on their behalf. So that they have a share in the cooked foods and can rely on this to cook.
{34} They acquire through him for eruv tavshilin. And regarding those about whom there is a dispute there, for example his wife whom he supports with sustenance, or his adult son who is dependent on his table, where we conclude there that b'dieved we rely to be lenient, the same applies here. And the later authorities wrote that all this is regarding his wife who eats with him; but if he supports her with sustenance and she cooks separately, then she is not secondary to the husband's pot and needs an eruv on her own; and since she acquires for herself, he can also acquire through her for others.
