One who makes an eruv for others, etc. In the Yam Shel Shlomo it is written that if his wife does not eat with him, even though he provides her support, she requires an eruv, since she does not eat from his cooked dish. Therefore he may grant ownership through her, for since she can acquire for herself, she can acquire for others. This is not comparable to eruv chatzeiros, for there the matter depends on the head of the household, whereas here anyone who eats from another cooked dish needs another eruv. Accordingly, one must place an eruv on behalf of widows who do not know how to make an eruv themselves, but those who know must make one themselves.
