(1) (Seq. 11) The West, K. Afi, who raises food for her is a mother who gives her ma'at, and she takes bread and meat and all eating needs from the ma'at given to her by the husband. And while the husband gives her flour and meat and eating necessities, she will cook for herself and eat on her own. Since she does not eat her husband's dishes, she has to make dinner for herself and does not go out with her husband. And GK on behalf of what is explained in Sec. 8, because Dantan Ra'a thought that it would appear that it was not allowed to cook on the Shabbat for Shabbat. And how should it be said about a woman who eats for herself and does not eat from her husband's dishes. As for the woman who started to cook for Shabbat, that is, the mixed dish, she is not allowed to eat from the mixed dish. But surely the other members of the household who eat at the house owner's request, who sometimes cook their own A dish and not per the Hebrew the Gemara, can rely on the Old Testament, can eat from the Old Testament.

(ב) And in the view of Orach Chayim: Shulchan Aruch 308:6 — a woman receiving sustenance from her husband does not forbid and need not make eiruv for herself; 366:10; Magen Avraham sk 18 on almanot. Custom: almanot have no separate home and live with others; still they cannot rely on the homeowner's eiruv since they do not eat from him — one who does not know, the homeowner grants eiruv and leaves eiruv for them; those who know must make their own eiruv and cannot rely on what he granted — as seif 7.
