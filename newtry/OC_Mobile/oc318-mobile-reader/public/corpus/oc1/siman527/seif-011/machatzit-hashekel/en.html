(Seif katan 11) One who makes an eruv, etc. Although the husband provides her maintenance, whether by giving her money with which she buys bread, meat, and all her food, or by giving her flour, meat, and other provisions which she cooks and eats separately, since she does not eat her husband's cooked food she must make her own eruv and does not fulfill her obligation through his. This also follows from the reason explained in seif katan 8. Rabbi Eliezer's rationale for eruv tavshilin is to show that cooking from Yom Tov for Shabbos is permitted only because preparation for Shabbos began before Yom Tov through the eruv. That rationale cannot apply to a woman who eats separately and may not eat from the husband's eruv dish. Other household members who eat at the householder's table, however, may rely upon his eruv even if they occasionally cook a dish of their own, because they may eat from his eruv and ordinarily eat his food. For them, the eruv properly constitutes the beginning of Shabbos cooking before Yom Tov.
This is not comparable to eruv chatzeiros. Siman 370, seif 6, states that a woman who receives an allowance from her husband does not prohibit carrying and requires no separate eruv chatzeiros. The same appears in siman 366, seif 10; see Magen Avraham, seif katan 18. The reference to widows describes the ordinary situation, since widows often lack separate dwellings and live with others. Nevertheless, they cannot rely upon the householder's eruv tavshilin because they do not eat his food. If they do not know how to make an eruv, the householder confers a share upon them and makes the eruv for them as well. Those who know how must make their own eruv and may not rely upon the share conferred upon them, as stated in seif 7.
