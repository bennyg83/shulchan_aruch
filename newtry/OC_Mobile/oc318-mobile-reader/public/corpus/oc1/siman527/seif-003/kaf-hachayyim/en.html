20) [Se'if 3] The required quantity of this cooked food is a kezayis, etc. One should take care not to use inferior bread and a lowly food item, thereby performing the mitzvah in a degrading manner and possibly throwing it away afterward. Rather, every person who rejoices in a mitzvah should take bread prepared for the Shabbos meal and an important cooked food, such as a fine piece of fish, meat, or a dish prepared for the morning meal. Shelah; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 5. Rashi writes in Beitzah 16a that an item not eaten every day is required, so that it is evident that one acts in honor of Shabbos. Magen Avraham, subsection 2; Eliyah Rabbah there. This requirement is not conclusive from the aforementioned words of Rashi. See there. It is possible that they intended to derive from Rashi only that when possible, this is the preferable practice, but not that the law requires it. Under the law, anything eaten as an accompaniment to bread is permitted, as explained in the Talmud, Shulchan Aruch, and the halachic authorities. See Nishmas Adam, rule 102, subsection 2, and examine the matter carefully.
21) Mahari Weil would place the cooked meat upon a platter and not upon the loaf, so that the meat would not make the loaf repulsive. Derashos Mahari Weil, Hilchos Eruv Tavshilin; Sheyarei Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef, subsection 2; Eliyah Rabbah there; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 11. It is proper to use hard-boiled eggs for the cooked food, because they keep in hot climates, whereas meat will not keep in hot weather for two days and two nights. Ben Ish Chai, parashas Tzav, subsection 1. If eggs are unavailable, one should use roasted meat, because well-roasted meat keeps longer, as is known.
22) There. Whether at its end. This means that if the eruv was eaten and a kezayis remained, one may cook and bake. One might ask that this follows a fortiori: if a kezayis is sufficient initially, it is certainly sufficient after the fact. The teaching informs us that even after the fact, less than a kezayis is ineffective. Olas Shabbos, subsection 3; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 9. See below, se'if 15.
23) There, in the gloss. This is likewise the initial practice. The term 'initially' applies only before one prepares the eruv. If he already prepared it, he need not repeat the procedure. Chemed Moshe, commentary, subsection 3; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 10.
