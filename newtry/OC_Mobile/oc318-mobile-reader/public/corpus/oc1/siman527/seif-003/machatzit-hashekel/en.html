(Seif katan 3) Pickled. Rashi explains, etc. He follows his own view, meaning that Rashi maintains that pickling is effective only in vinegar. This is cited in Beit Yosef at the beginning of Yoreh Deah, siman 105; see there.
With a liquid substance, even in water, unlike Rashi; see Beit Yosef, etc. But regarding salted food, etc. Meaning, Beit Yosef writes in explanation of why the Tur omitted the ruling that one may make an eruv with pickled food that the Tur maintains that pickling applies even in water. Since we rule that one may not make an eruv with small salted fish unless they were cooked, the same should apply to pickled food, because salted food is considered pickled. According to Beit Yosef, the baraisa permitting an eruv with pickled food is therefore rejected from the halachah.
Magen Avraham disagrees. Although one may not make an eruv with salted food, as stated in Shulchan Aruch, even small fish rinsed in hot water, etc., refers to koleis ha'ispanin, as Beit Yosef writes. Those fish are salted, and rinsing them in hot water constitutes their cooking. Without such rinsing, however, one may not make an eruv with them. Nevertheless, one may make an eruv with pickled food, because salted food is less cooked than pickled food. Chullin 97 states that salted food is like something hot from roasting, whereas pickled food is like cooked food. One might ask that although salted food is not considered cooked, it is considered roasted, and since one may make an eruv with roasted food, salted food should likewise be permitted. The answer is that the Mishnah requires a cooked dish, and Rabbi Eliezer supports this from the verse, “Bake what you will bake and cook what you will cook,” teaching that one may bake only on the basis of something baked and cook only on the basis of something cooked. Neither the Mishnah, the baraisa, nor the verse mentions roasting, yet roasted food qualifies because roasting is included in cooking and is itself called cooking, as the verse says, “They cooked the Pesach offering,” as Tosafot write in Chullin 96b. Salted food, however, although it is hot like roasted food, is nowhere called cooked and is therefore not included in the cooked dish mentioned by the verse, Mishnah, and baraisa. Chacham Tzvi, responsum 130, agrees with Beit Yosef that salted and pickled food have the same law. After the fact, one who used either for eruv tavshilin has fulfilled the requirement. Initially, however, one should use neither unless there is pressing need. In pressing circumstances one may use pickled or salted food, but should omit the blessing and recite only the eruv formula, “Through this it shall be permitted,” etc.
The time needed for them to place, etc. This is the measure of pickling in vinegar or brine.
See Yoreh Deah, siman 105, for this is Beit Yosef's view there. Shach disagrees and maintains that this measure suffices only for brine, while pickling in vinegar requires a full twenty-four hours. Magen Avraham writes above, siman 447, seif katan 28, that strong wine vinegar requires only the time needed to place water, etc., whereas weak wine vinegar or beer vinegar requires twenty-four hours.
