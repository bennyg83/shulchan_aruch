On this Magen Avraham disagrees — for in salted fish one does not make eruv tavshilin as Shulchan Aruch wrote, even small fish rinsed in hot water, etc., which are koleis that fishermen catch as Rabbenu Yonah wrote and they are salted; therefore rinsing in hot water is their cooking. But if he did not rinse them one does not make eruv tavshilin with them. Nevertheless with pickled one makes eruv tavshilin, for salting is lesser than pickling, for they say in Chullin daf 97: salted is like roasted that is roasted, and pickled is like cooked. If so salted is not like cooked; nevertheless it is like roasted, and as one may make eruv tavshilin with roasted it is fit to permit with salted as with pickled since it is like cooked. Therefore one may say: in the Mishnah it teaches we require a cooked dish, and in the baraita R' Eliezer supports from the verse "that which you will bake, bake" and "that which you will cook, cook" — from here R' Eliezer: one bakes only on the baked and cooks only on the cooked; roasted is not mentioned in Mishnah, baraita, and verse, only cooking; nevertheless roasted is included in cooking, for roasted too is called cooked as written "and they cooked the pesach," and as Tosafot wrote in Chullin daf 96b. But salted — since it is like roasted that is roasted, nevertheless we do not find it called cooked; it is not in the category of cooked mentioned in verse, Mishnah, and baraita. However in responsum Chacham Tzvi siman 130 he agreed with Rabbenu Yonah that salted and pickled share the same law, and b'dieved if he placed eruv tavshilin with salted or pickled he fulfills; but l'chatchila one should not make eruv with salted or pickled except in pressing need — then l'chatchila permitted with pickled or salted, only he should not bless but say the eruv tavshilin formula "may it be permitted," etc.:
