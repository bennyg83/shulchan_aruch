And to light the lamp. This implies that if he did not make this stipulation, it is forbidden to light from Yom Tov for Shabbos. So wrote Tosafot in the second chapter, daf 22, from the statement that one who did not place an eruv tavshilin has a lamp lit for him, implying that one must mention in the blessing of eruv tavshilin: it shall be permitted to us to bake, to cook, and to light, etc. If he did not stipulate for lighting, it appears from here that he may have only one lamp lit for him, since we see that it was necessary to mention lighting the lamp. This is difficult: why must we mention lighting, since afterward he says, 'and to do all our needs'? It seems to me that we learn this from the discussion in the second chapter: Beis Shammai say that one may not insulate hot food unless there were hot foods insulated from before Yom Tov. The Gemara asks, if he placed an eruv tavshilin, why is it forbidden according to Beis Shammai? Rava answers that insulation is different, because it is evident that he is doing it for Shabbos. Abaye challenged him from Chananyah: Beis Shammai say one may not bake unless he made an eruv with bread, may not cook unless he made an eruv with a cooked dish, and may not insulate unless he had hot foods insulated from before Yom Tov. This implies that if he did have them, he may insulate, even though it is evident. Rather, Abaye answered that the case is where he made an eruv for this but not for that. Rashi explains that he baked and cooked from before Yom Tov for the sake of the eruv, but did not insulate hot foods, and this is Chananyah according to Beis Shammai. From the words of Beis Shammai and Beis Hillel we learn that even according to Beis Hillel, who require only one cooked dish for everything, nevertheless with respect to the blessing one must mention the matters. If he did not bless at all, it helps for all matters, because blessings do not prevent fulfillment, as I wrote above. But if he blessed and mentioned one thing, that is worse, for he explicitly shows that he made the eruv only for that thing. Therefore it would have been proper for the phrase 'and to do all our needs' to include everything. However, since Rabbi Eliezer derived from the verse, 'what you will bake, bake,' that one may bake and cook only by means of what is baked and cooked, one must explicitly mention these two matters, baking and cooking, because they are stated in the verse on which eruv tavshilin is based. Consequently one must also mention the other matters in the blessing, such as insulating and lighting; otherwise it is as if he made an eruv for this and not for that. The phrase 'and to do all our needs' comes to include slaughtering, which is not included in the explicitly mentioned details. One should not ask why insulating and lighting are not also included in that general phrase. It is because, according to Rava's answer, insulation is evident as being done for Shabbos. Although Rava's answer was rejected regarding whether eruv tavshilin helps for insulation, the logic was not rejected regarding the blessing: everyone agrees that one must mention when he is making an eruv even for something evident, such as insulation. The same applies to lighting a lamp, which is evidently for Shabbos, since he lights while it is still day, and what benefit is a lamp in daylight?
