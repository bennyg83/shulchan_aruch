Regarding what he writes concerning Yom Tov falling on Friday and Shabbos, it is proper on Erev Yom Tov to say, “from one Yom Tov day to the next,” thereby including what he cooks on Friday for Shabbos in honor of Yom Tov. The ordinary writer says: I saw fit to write briefly concerning eruv tavshilin. It is proper to take a whole loaf the size of an egg together with a kezayis of cooked meat or fish that is fully fit to eat, rather than only half-cooked. One should also ensure that mitzvos are not treated contemptuously. The formula is set out in the siddurim.
Question. One set aside bread and a cooked egg for eruv tavshilin. It appears that this fulfills the requirement of a food eaten with bread, as in the Mishnah's case of a fish with an egg upon it. On Yom Tov, another egg was laid, cooked, and became mixed with many permitted eggs. Since an item that will later become permitted is not nullified, perhaps here it is nevertheless not treated as such, for otherwise he would be forbidden to cook for Shabbos, which would be more severe than a case in which the eruv was spoiled. Alternatively, since he loses nothing by waiting to eat this cooked food, it remains forbidden and cannot serve as an eruv; it is as though the eruv were lost, because it may not be eaten on Yom Tov. Similarly, if on Erev Pesach a broken matzah became mixed with chametz matzah, then on Yom Tov the dry mixture would be forbidden according to the opinion that the prohibition reawakens on Pesach. In such a case one may not rely upon it as an eruv. This requires study. If one has no Yom Tov food other than an item mixed in this manner, it also requires study whether it retains the status of an item that will later become permitted. See siman 529. The obligation of rejoicing on Yom Tov is equivalent to that of Shabbos, and fasting is likewise forbidden on Yom Tov.
