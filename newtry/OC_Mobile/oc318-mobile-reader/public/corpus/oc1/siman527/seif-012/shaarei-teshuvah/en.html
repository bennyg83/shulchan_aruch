(In Shulchan Aruch se'if 12) He then takes immediately from the one who acquires, etc. See Birkei Yosef in the name of Tur Barekes, who wrote: my teachers did not practice thus; rather the one through whom he acquires stands with him, and the one making the eruv takes the eruv in his hand and says, 'With this eruv,' etc., 'and for all the people of the province,' and afterward he places the eruv in the hand of the one who acquires and says to him, 'Acquire,' etc. But the Shibbolei HaLeket and the Tur wrote as the Shulchan Aruch; see there. See Gan HaMelech, siman 62, whose opinion is that one who is accustomed to make an eruv for others need not [explicitly] have them in mind for others, and the proof is from the end of chapter 'Kol HaGet,' where we establish the case regarding those familiar with the priesthood, etc.; see there. And in Kuntres Tov Ayin (at the end of Shem HaGedolim composed by Mahari Azulai) he wrote that there is someone who disputes him and rejected his proof according to the Rashba and the Ritva, of blessed memory; and the author wrote that b'dieved one may rely on what is written in Gan HaMelech; see there.
His head, see Baer Heitev; and the Radbaz wrote, siman 594 (brought in Birkei Yosef): kinds of fowl and kinds of meat which, if slaughtered on the day they are slaughtered, are not as good to eat as those slaughtered from the day before: it is permitted to slaughter them on the first day; see there. And it appears that his meaning is when he needs them on the first day, or that he will eat a bit of them.
