16) [Se'if 2] This eruv is prepared with bread and a cooked food, etc. This is because the matter is disputed. Some say that bread and a cooked food are required, because the verse states, 'That which you will bake, bake, etc.' One may bake only in reliance upon that which was already baked, and may cook only in reliance upon that which was already cooked. Others say that a cooked food alone permits both baking and cooking, because all these activities are included in cooking. The custom is nevertheless to be stringent and prepare the eruv with bread and a cooked food, as explained in Tur and Beis Yosef. See there. Shulchan Aruch therefore rules that initially bread and a cooked food are required, but after the fact, if one prepared it only with a cooked food, it is permitted.
17) There. With bread and a cooked food. It appears that this applies specifically when one wishes to bake bread on Yom Tov. If one wishes only to cook and not to bake, even Rabbeinu Tam holds that a cooked food alone is sufficient. This appears from Tosafos, Beitzah 17b. Olas Shabbos, subsection 2; Bigdei Yesha; Biurei HaGra; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 6, write likewise.
18) There. If he prepared it only with a cooked food, it is permitted. Bread alone, however, is ineffective for cooking for Shabbos. Ateres Zekeinim. This implies that it does permit baking. Shibolei HaLeket writes likewise. See Tosafos, Avodah Zarah 37b, who write that bread alone permits cooking as well. See there. Olas Shabbos, subsection 4, however, writes that bread alone should not be relied upon even for baking. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 4; Mishbetzos Zahav, subsection 4; Bigdei Yesha; Chemed Moshe, subsection 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 3; Shaarei Shamayim; Mikraei Kodesh, siman 32, subsection 2, write likewise. See below, subsection 25.
19) There. But with a cooked food alone, it is permitted. It is permitted to bake and cook on Yom Tov for Shabbos. Nevertheless, if one remembers before nightfall, he must add bread as well and say, 'Through this eruv, etc.,' but he should not recite the blessing again. Chemed Moshe, commentary, subsection 3; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 7.
