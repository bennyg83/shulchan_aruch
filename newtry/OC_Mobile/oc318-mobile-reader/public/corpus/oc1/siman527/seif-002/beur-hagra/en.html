(2) This eruv, etc. Yerushalmi Eruvei Tavshilin: thus they are — bread like an egg and cooked dish like a kezayit; one says: in the land of Israel, etc., on the mitzvah of eruv, and stipulates and says: by law may it be permitted to us to cook and bake from Yom Tov for a friend and from Yom Tov to Shabbat, and for those who rely on this eruv until motza'ei the last Yom Tov, provided his eruv is guarded until motza'ei the last Yom Tov; if they ate it or it was lost he must make eruv on the other Yom Tov — end Yerushalmi. Hagahot Maimoniyot and Mordechai brought it. That which it says "from Yom Tov for a friend" — Rabeinu Yehudah explained: for example if Yom Tov falls on erev Shabbat and Shabbat; in the Gemara chapter 2:15b: one bakes only on the baked, etc.; Beit Hillel: one cooked dish — meaning for one cooked dish; and what the Gemara says "there is only cooked," etc. — meaning if he does not wish only to cook:

(ב) And if he did not do so, etc. — the plain meaning of the Mishnah and Beit Hillel: one dish; and the Gemara's wording: only a dish, etc., implies that a dish alone suffices. There 17b and Beit Hillel: one mixes in one dish and cooks, etc.; and Beit Shammai: one does not bake, etc.; and above it says Beit Hillel mixes, etc., and we do not hold like that Yerushalmi; and what he brought as proof from the baraita he brings it from here, etc. — it is R' Eliezer, and R' Yehoshua disagrees in Yerushalmi ch. 2: R' Eliezer permits baking, etc., and R' Yehoshua says one bakes and cooks on what is cooked, etc. — see there; and the Gemara did not care to bring only the beginning of the baraita — it relied on eiruv tavshilin. Tosafot wrote; nevertheless Or Zaru'a was not satisfied, etc., and so is custom; and b'dieved one relies on Ri and other poskim, but l'chatchila one acts per Rabbenu Tam. What he challenged — that R' Yehoshua disagrees — nevertheless the Stam of the Talmud brings R' Eliezer; and it appears support for his view from what is written: if he placed, etc., rather Abaye said, etc., and Chanania, etc.; and if so, why specifically one may not insulate — one may not even bake? Rather that even for Beit Hillel one also needs bread; but the view of Ri, Ran, Rambam, and Shulchan Aruch is that a dish alone suffices, and so explicitly in Tosefta: one who makes a dish from Yom Tov bakes and cooks on it for Shabbat and insulates hot water on it, and alley residents may make a dish and bake and cook on it; and R' Eliezer is the Stam author, but Beit Hillel disagrees explicitly as above. What is written one may not insulate hot water, etc. — one may say he stated a stringency: even hot water, which is like a dish, is nevertheless forbidden; and so is the main point.
