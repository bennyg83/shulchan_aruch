(There seif 24) If he made an artifice to cook two pots for today's need and left one for tomorrow — it is forbidden to eat it. NB: Maayan Ganim in Rivash responsum 254 proves a novel law: artifice is worse than intentional only if at first cooking he made the artifice or intended to say I leave this for tomorrow and will cook again today; but if at first cooking he intended to eat today and later decided to artifice — this is not called artifice and is permitted; see there. Thus one may say regarding bread baked on erev Yom Tov: I do not want it and will bake hot bread on Yom Tov, as Rashi Beitzah 9 on dough wheel — one may say I eat hot bread; Shulchan Aruch 526: if he has clean bread he need not bake — deals with returned bread; see Mechaber's end and Tosafot — this is forbidden as the artifice is obvious; but where artifice is not obvious, if at first baking he did not intend artifice, from Mechaber it is also clear: if household usually eats returned bread it is permitted; see Yerushalmi Pesachim ch. 3 and investigate. See Mishnah commentary Temurah ch. How to make artifice — Tosefot Yom Tov: artifice is what is permitted; deceit is forbidden. I wonder how they missed Talmud and poskim here calling it artifice and forbidding — Bava Metzia ch. 1 fears deceit; investigate.
