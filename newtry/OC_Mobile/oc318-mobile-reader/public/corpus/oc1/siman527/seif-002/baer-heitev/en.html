Cooked dish. For it is stated that R' Eliezer learns from the verse "that which you will bake, bake" and "that which you will cook, cook" — from here one may bake only on what is already baked and cook only on what is already cooked; therefore here bread is needed for what they will bake for Shabbat need and a cooked dish for what they will cook. R' Yehuda holds that through a cooked dish everything is permitted, and most poskim hold like R' Yehuda; only Rabbenu Tam and some views hold like R' Eliezer. Therefore it is valid for everyone, at least b'dieved like R' Yehuda; but bread alone does not help for anyone, even for baking. It is forbidden to make eruv tavshilin before Yom Tov, only on Yom Tov; but b'dieved it is permitted — see Taz s.k. 12 and Magen Avraham s.k. 13. It is written in Yeshuot Shani: if he made a special dish for eruv tavshilin or designated it for that purpose, even if he intended to bless on it and forgot — permitted to cook, for blessings do not obstruct; and even if his wife designated without his knowledge — permitted — end quote. Magen Avraham in s.k. (53) [23] disagrees and wrote one may not rely on this — see there. See Taz s.k. (17) [18]. Maharil would place the meat on the tablecloth and not on the loaf because of repulsiveness. He also took whole bread and used it for lechem mishneh on Shabbat night, and the next day at the third meal he would break on it, since one mitzvah was done with it, let another mitzvah be done with it.
