From a cooked dish. For it is stated that R' Eliezer learns from the verse, 'that which you will bake, bake, and that which you will cook, cook': from here [we learn] that one may bake only upon that which is baked, and cook only upon that which is cooked; therefore here one needs bread for what he will bake for the needs of Shabbos, and a cooked dish for what he will cook. And R' Yehoshua holds that on account of a cooked dish everything is permitted; and most poskim hold like R' Yehoshua, only Rabbeinu Tam and a few opinions hold like R' Eliezer; therefore it is valid for everyone at least b'dieved like R' Yehoshua, but bread alone does not avail according to anyone, even to bake. It is forbidden to make eruv tavshilin before erev Yom Tov, only on erev Yom Tov; but b'dieved it is permitted; see Taz s.k. 12 and Magen Avraham s.k. 13. It is written in Yam Shel Shlomo: if he made a cooked dish specially for eruv tavshilin, or set it aside for that purpose, even if he intended to recite the blessing over it and forgot, it is permitted to cook, for blessings do not withhold [the validity], and even if his wife set it aside without his knowledge it is permitted, end quote. And Magen Avraham in s.k. (53) [23] disagrees with him and wrote that one should not rely on this; see there. And see Taz s.k. (17) [18]. Maharil would place the meat on a plate and not on the challah because of repulsion. He also took a whole loaf and used it for lechem mishneh on Friday night, and on the morrow at the third meal he would cut over it, since one mitzvah was done with it, let another mitzvah be done with it. In the Shlah he wrote not to take a lowly bone, but rather take an important piece, and do not regard this mitzvah as light in your eyes.
