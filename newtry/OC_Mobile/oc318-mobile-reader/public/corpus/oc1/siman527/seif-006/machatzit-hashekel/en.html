(Seif katan 6) One who forgot, etc. This requires study. Siman 108, seif 8, rules that one who inadvertently or unavoidably failed to pray may make up the prayer at the next prayer service, whereas one who deliberately omitted it may not. It further states that one who failed to pray because he thought sufficient time would remain after finishing his present activity, but meanwhile the time passed, which occurred through forgetfulness, is considered inadvertent and may make up the prayer. Beit Yosef cites this from Nimukei Yosef in the name of Ramah in the chapter Keitzad HaRegel. Ramah derives it from Rava's ruling that if a stone lay in one's lap and fell and caused damage, he is exempt from four categories of damages. Ramah explains that initially placing the stone in his lap was not negligence because it could be guarded, while its later fall through forgetfulness is considered unavoidable. Thus, both regarding prohibition and monetary law, forgetfulness is treated as unavoidable rather than negligent. See Magen Avraham there.
