Even on lentils, etc. It is written in the Mordechai that this is specifically after the fact. The Beit Yosef disagrees and writes that even initially, if on Erev Yom Tov he has no cooked dish except lentils left at the bottom of the pot or that fat stuck to the knife, he may make an eruv with them, namely if he scraped them off, as written here in Shulchan Aruch. It seems to me that even the Mordechai agrees, for such a case is like after the fact since he has no other cooked dish, and the Mordechai speaks only where he has another cooked dish. The Maharshal wrote that since the Gemara did not say, 'one may make an eruv with lentils,' but rather, 'one relies on the lentils,' this implies specifically after the fact. My father-in-law the Bach wrote that this is no proof, for the Mishnah also uses this language: one makes a cooked dish on Erev Yom Tov and relies on it for Shabbos, and that is even initially. In any case, for beautification of the mitzvah it is proper to take a complete cooked dish according to all opinions. The Maharshal wrote further that it appears that the need to stipulate on Erev Yom Tov applies only to a cooked dish that remained unintentionally, such as what is at the bottom of the pot and the like. But one who made a dish specifically for eruv tavshilin, or set it aside for that purpose, even if he intended to stipulate and bless upon it and forgot to stipulate and bless, may rely on it after the fact, as in the language of the Mishnah, 'one makes a cooked dish on Erev Yom Tov and relies on it.' The blessing that one must recite initially and the stipulation do not prevent fulfillment, for blessings do not prevent fulfillment. What he wrote, that the language of the Mishnah indicates relying after the fact because he did not stipulate or bless, is not reasonable, for the tanna is not discussing blessings at all; rather, 'he relies on it' means initially. Nevertheless, the law itself is true: since he intended to stipulate upon it, it is permitted after the fact, for blessings never prevent fulfillment. See seif 20.
