(Seif katan 14) It was eaten, etc. If the bread was eaten, this is of no consequence, as stated in seif 2, because after the fact a cooked dish alone suffices.
He need not make another eruv; see seif 22 and Yam Shel Shlomo. One should not argue that this should be treated as an initial case, since he can make an eruv on the first day with a stipulation, as stated in seif 22. Yam Shel Shlomo explains that Rambam maintains that nowadays no stipulation should be made at all, as cited by Tur in siman 528. Since we know the calendar and know that the first day is Yom Tov and the second is a weekday, the Sages established the second day as definite Yom Tov because of ancestral custom, not because of doubt. One therefore cannot truthfully stipulate, “If today is sacred,” etc., when he knows the second day is a weekday. Although we do not rule like Rambam, there is no reason initially, without necessity, to act against his view.
