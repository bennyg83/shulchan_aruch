(Ibid., se'if 24) If he used artifice to cook two pots for the needs of the day and left over one for the needs of tomorrow, it is forbidden to eat it. Note: Behold, one who examines Responsa Rivash, siman 254, will see a novel ruling there, that the reason artifice is more forbidden than willful [violation] is if at the time of the first cooking he employed this artifice, or intended to employ artifice to say, 'this I am leaving for tomorrow and I will cook a second time today'; but if at the time of the first cooking he intended simply to eat today, and afterward reconsidered to employ artifice to say, 'this I am permitting for tomorrow and I will cook a new [dish] for today,' this is not called artifice and is permitted; see there. And if so, it appears that the same applies that it is permitted to say of bread baked on erev Yom Tov, 'it is not satisfactory to me,' and I will bake hot bread on Yom Tov, as Rashi explained in Beitzah folio 9 regarding one who rolled dough, that he may say, 'I eat hot bread.' And what is written in Shulchan Aruch siman 506, that if he has fine bread he may not bake, deals with coarse bread, as appears from the end of the Mechaber's wording; and in Tosafos the source of this ruling is explicit thus; namely, that this is forbidden where the artifice is recognizable, for it is not the way to leave fine bread and eat coarse bread because it is hot; but where the artifice is not recognizable, for example where this is equal to that, it is permitted to say so if at the beginning of the first baking he did not intend artifice. And from the body of the Mechaber's words as well it is evident thus: that if his household members are accustomed to eat coarse bread it is permitted; yet in the end they are also accustomed to eat fine bread, and it is possible for him to feed them fine bread, for of necessity it deals with a case where he has fine bread sufficient for their needs, for otherwise even fine bread is permitted to bake if he lacks it for all of them; if so, he should give them fine bread; of necessity, since at least the artifice is not recognizable, it is permitted; and this is clear and simple. And behold, so I found in the Yerushalmi as well, chapter 3 of Pesachim, halacha 3, and this is its wording: how does he do it according to R' Eliezer? He uses artifice and says, 'this I wish to eat, this I wish to eat'; when he removes [the loaves] he uses artifice and says, 'this I wish to set aside, this I wish to set aside,' and he leaves one over; see there. And the matter is clear as I wrote; examine carefully. And see the commentary on the Mishnayos in tractate Temurah in the chapter 'How do they use artifice,' and Tiferes Yisrael there, that artifice is what is permitted and deceit is what is forbidden. And I am astonished at them, how the wording of the Gemara in Beitzah and the poskim here escaped them, who called [this] artifice and forbade it; also in the first chapter of Bava Metzia we are concerned lest he is using artifice, and if he said two, etc., behold it is forbidden likewise because of 'distance yourself from a false matter,' as is written in the Gemara and Shach, siman 75; see there and examine carefully.
