If he acted with evasion, etc. Rashi explains that they were more stringent with evasion than with intentional transgression because they were strict in this matter so that the law of eruv not be uprooted. One who intentionally transgresses is wicked for violating the words of the Sages, and others do not learn from him; he himself also takes it to heart and repents, so the enactment of eruv is not uprooted. But one who acts by evasion thinks he is acting permissibly, others learn from him, and the enactment of eruv is uprooted. Therefore the Sages penalized him. This is difficult with what Shulchan Aruch wrote here, that evasion through two pots is forbidden even when needed for that day. This must refer to before the meal. Yet above in seif 21 he brought an opinion that it is permitted before the meal because he will eat from each one. Although the Tur also wrote this here, that is not difficult, because the Tur does not hold of this remedy of eating a bit from it, as I wrote in siman 503, that there is no greater evasion than this. But Shulchan Aruch, who ruled that it helps, why did he prohibit here? It is not proper to say that here he retracted from the opinion he brought nearby. Nor can one say that here he prohibited if he did not eat a bit from it, for then he should not have written 'it is forbidden,' but rather 'he should eat a bit from it.' Furthermore, above he should have written, 'and if he did not eat, it is forbidden.' In addition, this law would certainly be false, that it becomes forbidden because he did not eat, since in any case the cooking was done permissibly, as I wrote in seif 19. Therefore it seems to me that here he is discussing one who cooks one type in two pots; in such a case it does not help that he wants to eat a little from it, because both have the same taste. But in truth, if there is a difference in taste between them, it helps even with two pots before eating, through this evasion of eating a little from one, as I wrote. I also wrote this distinction in siman 503, with the help of Heaven, and it is correct.
