But he may rent him a donkey to carry chametz upon it. The rent is permitted. Bach and Kenesses HaGedolah disagree, asking why this differs from rent for an oven or a house used for chametz, which is forbidden. Responsa of the Geonim, siman 243, likewise forbids the rental. Magen Avraham answers that here the donkey was rented without specification, and the Jew did not expressly state that it was to carry chametz. This is contrary to the plain wording of the authorities and Tosafot, who expressly say “to carry chametz,” implying that even an explicit rental is permitted. Beit Yosef and Rama must hold that since Tosafot explicitly permit the donkey case, one should not be stringent where chametz does not render its proceeds forbidden. Yet the ruling should not be extended, because perhaps a donkey is different: the non-Jew brings it into his own domain and acquires its use through the rental. An oven or house, by contrast, remains identified with the Jew, and therefore requires stringency. A utensil may not be rented for cooking chametz because the Jew desires the continued existence of the prohibition so that the utensil not break; accordingly, even lending it without payment is forbidden, as the later authorities explain. Nevertheless, one should also be stringent not to rent a donkey expressly for carrying chametz, following the later authorities. See my Minchas Yaakov, klal 85, seifim ketanim 14 and 16, and Yoreh Deah, siman 133. Magen Avraham explains the prohibition against purchasing chametz during Pesach as likewise based on desiring its continued existence. Rivash gives other reasons: a non-Jew has no halachic agency, so the Jew himself acquires it, and there is concern that he may eat from it. Shaar Ephraim, responsum 6, in the gloss, writes that even without touching the chametz we fear that he may eat from it. See what I wrote above at the end of seif katan 109.
