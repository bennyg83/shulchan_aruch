Forbidden to rent a utensil to a non-Jew on Pesach so he cook chametz in it, for he profits from issurei hana'ah; but permitted to rent a utensil before Pesach with Pesach days absorbed, such as renting for three or four weeks to do in it what he wishes, even though it is known the non-Jew will cook chametz in it during Pesach — for he will consider all his wage for days before Pesach and after Pesach, and there is no profiting from issurei hana'ah. One stringent even in this — see there (Maharam MiRotenburg). Those holding an estate and taking from them measures of maltz from every beer wage cooked in cauldrons — the law changes by the estate arrangement: if the cauldrons belong to the lord who rents and the Jew must repair cauldrons when broken, and even if cauldrons age the Jew must buy new ones himself and deliver complete at estate payment time — then cauldrons are in the Jew's possession; needless to say forbidden to take the measures themselves, for the Jew acquired them as cauldron-wage and it is actual chametz over which Passover passed; but even money forbidden, like benefiting from actual chametz money, since their custom is to give chametz itself as cauldron-wage, as explained in responsum of R' Shimon. Even when he takes nothing it would appear forbidden because of wanting its continued existence; but since he cannot prevent cooking because they pay his wage per the estate, he is compelled — if he can prevent their cooking, forbidden l'chatchila to let them cook because of wanting its continued existence; and it was explained permission is through sale — sell before Pesach to a non-Jew all measures coming from the week of Pesach and take money immediately; even though the non-Jew sells and returns measures after Pesach, permitted. But if an estate where the Jew need not repair cauldrons at all — they cook in their own, only took measures from every cooking — then similar to buying the priests' allotment: stipulate with the non-Jew before Pesach to give money; b'dieved may receive measures even without stipulation; if he does not know who cooked, stipulate with him — like b'dieved (my teacher in Bach).
