(א) So that he cook chametz in them — because he wants the chametz's continued existence [that if he pours chametz from the pot while on the fire the vessel may crack] — and behold it is like benefiting from chametz.

(ב) But one may rent him a donkey — for he does not care if the chametz is lost. Acharonim challenged: why forbid both utensils and donkey because of profiting from issurei hana'ah forbidden l'chatchila as previous seifim; they conclude this law is for the view that profiting from issurei hana'ah is forbidden only for idolatry and the like; but according to what we are stringent to forbid profiting l'chatchila everywhere, likewise forbidden for donkey to bring chametz. Know: even for this view, specifically when he rented explicitly to bring chametz; but if he rented unstipulated for time and brought chametz, permitted — for if he carried nothing he would still pay wage, as we wrote above regarding oven.

(ג) And some permit heating etc. — meaning: do not say this too is like wanting continued existence of chametz absorbed in vessel walls for the vessel's sake [when using it he wants it whole, and thereby like wanting chametz absorbed — for one cannot be without the other]; we do not say so for absorbed chametz not b'ein.

(ד) And to wash in them — or to wash the head, for he benefits only from the vessel, not from chametz absorbed though it exudes into the water. Acharonim wrote: specifically vessels designated for washing and laundering not usually used for eating; but vessels sometimes used for eating and drinking must be stored modestly and not used even for washing — decree lest used for eating. Nevertheless temporary use — no stringency; explained later siman 451.

(ה) And such is the custom — regarding selling chametz vessels to a non-Jew on Pesach, some permit, also for the reason above — he benefits only from the vessel body, not absorption [unless the non-Jew pays more because of absorption and there is difference — then he benefits from chametz]; some forbid. Acharonim wrote: a Jew holds an estate from a lord with a brewery house where they make beer called maltz; custom is whoever needs beer makes it in that house and the Jew takes rent; if he lets non-Jews make beer on Pesach, he wants chametz's continued existence because of vessels in the maltz [if maltz spills the cauldron cracks on the fire]; and when he receives rent he profits from issurei hana'ah; further, since custom is always to give a measure of maltz as wage not money — when the non-Jew makes beer the Jew already acquired the maltz measure, and even if given after Pesach forbidden — chametz of a Jew over which Passover passed. They wrote he should sell his right in the maltz to a non-Jew or rent for some weeks so Pesach days are absorbed and permitted; if he did not lease the maltz itself from the lord but only the measure-wages, sufficient to command brewers not to separate measures on Pesach but record they ground on Pesach and collect after — same for one holding mills on an estate.
