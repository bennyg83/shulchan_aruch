(There, seif 7) It is forbidden to rent a utensil to a non-Jew on Pesach so that he will cook chametz in it, etc. Note: Regarding selling the grain and all the utensils connected with it, whether it is permitted for the non-Jew to make from it whiskey or beer on Pesach, this is a simple allowance in my humble opinion. It is not necessary to say that if he sells the malt that is already complete chametz, it is certainly permitted, for this entire sale is only rabbinic, since according to Torah law nullification alone suffices. If so, what difference is there whether he sells chametz this way or whether the non-Jew does with it whatever he wants? Especially since the common practice is to sell whiskey and pour it in a Jew's house, and there is no greater publicity and maris ayin than that. If so, what prohibition is there in making whiskey or beer more than in pouring it? Even if he sells him grain as it is and the non-Jew makes malt from it on Pesach - aside from this I was told that it is impossible to make whiskey and beer except from malt that was already made a quarter of a year earlier - there is still no prohibition. One should not say that since beforehand it was not chametz and was not included in the nullification, sale no longer helps for it, because this is only a legal fiction and would be in a Torah matter. This too is not so. First, several later authorities are lenient even in selling animals so that they be fed chametz, and they challenged the Tevuos Shor of blessed memory; thus they hold that legal fiction helps even in Torah matters, and this is the common practice, for it is impossible to maintain the religion properly and forbid this. If so, this is no worse than selling animals, which is certainly a Torah prohibition. Furthermore, it is true that Chok Yosef in siman 434 wrote that what became chametz on Pesach is not included in the nullification, but this is an error. Since in any event such nullification would not help, for we require it before three, and nullification in the heart also does not help; rather, the main reason is as the Ran wrote, that since in any event it is not in his domain, mere disclosure of intent that he does not want it is enough. If so, such a disclosure of intent helps even before it becomes chametz, since at the time it becomes chametz it will not be in his domain, except that the Torah made it as though it were in his domain. If he disclosed beforehand that he does not want the chametz, then since there was prior disclosure of intent, the verse does not afterward make it as though it were in his domain. An explicit proof to this is from Pesachim 7, where the Gemara answers: what case are we dealing with? A student sitting before his teacher who remembers that he has rolled dough in his house and fears that it may become chametz; he proceeds and nullifies it before it becomes chametz, etc. If so, how does the baraisa there state that he nullifies it in his heart? Nullification in the heart does not help, since it is still permitted. Necessarily, it helps even for a later time. Thus it is proven that it helps even for chametz that became chametz during Pesach, since he disclosed his intent from the outset. One should not say that there he had this specific item in mind, but chametz that he did not have in mind would not be affected by the nullification; perhaps this was the intent of Chok Yosef. In truth this is not so, because we come to it through disclosure of intent. What difference is there between this and that? Since the verse did not make it in his domain where he disclosed that he does not want it, so too here, once he disclosed that he does not want chametz, automatically the verse did not make it as though it were in his domain. Furthermore, he says 'that I have seen and that I have not seen, that I have destroyed and that I have not destroyed,' so everything is included. And even if this were the intent of the poskim, that would be only where some chametz became chametz on Pesach and he did not know at the time of nullification that it would become chametz. But here he knew beforehand that it would become chametz on Pesach, so it is exactly the law of rolled dough, where it is explicit that nullification helps. If so, the sale is again only rabbinic. On the contrary, it seems to me that on Pesach the sale helps even for doing melacha on Shabbos. It is not only on Yom Tov, where Chok Yaakov is lenient in any event in this siman; even on Shabbos it is permitted, because here there is no concern of maris ayin, since it is known that everyone sells their chametz with the land for Pesach, even during the weekdays, meaning Chol HaMoed. Since people have practiced leniency in this regarding pouring whiskey and were not concerned for maris ayin because everyone sells the chametz, what difference is there between Shabbos and weekday? Therefore this Shabbos is better than Shabbosos of the rest of the year. This is clear. I needed to write this only to exclude the view of the heretics who say the opposite; I wrote this as a remembrance, and may the mouths of speakers of falsehood be stopped. Analyze it.
