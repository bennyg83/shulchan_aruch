There, seif 7. "It is forbidden to rent a vessel to a non-Jew on Pesach so that he cook chametz in it, etc." NB: Regarding whether one may sell the grain and all vessels connected with it, and whether the non-Jew may make liquor or beer from it on Pesach, in my humble opinion this is a simple heter. Not only if he sells malt that is already complete chametz is it certainly permitted, for the entire sale is only rabbinic, since by Torah law mere nullification suffices; if so, what difference is there between selling chametz this way or letting the non-Jew do with it whatever he wishes? Especially since common practice is to sell liquor and pour it in a Jewish house, and there is no greater publicity and marit ayin than that; what greater issur is there in making liquor or beer than in pouring it? Even if he sells him grain as it is and the non-Jew makes malt from it on Pesach, there is no issur. One cannot say that since beforehand it was not chametz and was not included in the nullification, the sale does not help and is only an evasion in a Torah matter. This too is not so. First, several later authorities are lenient even with selling animals so they may be fed chametz, and they disagreed with the Tevuot Shor; they therefore hold that evasion helps even in a Torah matter, and this is the common practice, for it is impossible to uphold the religion properly and forbid this. If so, this is no worse than selling animals, which involves a definite Torah issur. Furthermore, although the Chok Yosef in siman 434 wrote that chametz that becomes chametz during Pesach is not included in bitul, this is a mistake. Since such bitul would not otherwise help, for it must be before three people, and nullification in the heart does not help, the main reason is as the Ran wrote: since in any event it is not in his domain, a mere disclosure of intent that he does not want it suffices. If so, such a disclosure of intent helps even before it becomes chametz, since when it becomes chametz it will not be in his domain except that Scripture made it as though it were in his domain; if he disclosed beforehand that he does not want chametz, then because there was an initial disclosure of intent, Scripture later does not make it in his domain. There is explicit proof from Pesachim 7, where it answers that the case is a student sitting before his teacher who remembers that he has rolled dough at home and fears it may become chametz; he should nullify it before it becomes chametz. How can the baraita there say he nullifies it in his heart? Nullification in the heart does not help while it is still permitted. Perforce it helps for the later time, and this proves that it helps even for chametz that becomes chametz during Pesach once he disclosed his intent beforehand. One cannot say that there his mind was on that particular dough, whereas chametz that was not in his mind to become chametz is not covered by the bitul, and perhaps this was the Chok Yosef's intent. In truth this is not so, for we come by way of disclosure of intent; what difference is there between this and that? Since Scripture does not make it in his domain where he disclosed that he does not want it, so too here, since he disclosed that he does not want chametz, Scripture does not make it as though it is in his domain. Moreover, he says in the formula, "that I have seen and that I have not seen, that I have removed and that I have not removed," so everything is included. And even if that were the intent of the poskim, that would be only where some chametz became chametz on Pesach and he did not know at the time of nullification that it would become chametz. But here he knew beforehand that it would become chametz on Pesach; this is exactly the law of rolled dough, where it is explicit that bitul helps. If so, the sale is again only rabbinic. On the contrary, it appears to me that on Pesach the sale helps even for work on Shabbat, not only on Yom Tov, where the Chok Yaakov is lenient in any event in this siman, but even on Shabbat. Here there is no concern of marit ayin, since it is known that everyone sells their chametz with the land during Pesach, even on weekdays, meaning Chol HaMoed. Since people practiced leniency with pouring liquor and were not concerned for marit ayin because everyone sells the chametz, what difference is there between Shabbat and weekday? Therefore this Shabbat is better than other Shabbatot of the year. This is clear. I only needed to write this to exclude the view of the heretics who say the opposite; I wrote it as a reminder, so that the mouth of those who speak falsehood be closed. Examine carefully.
