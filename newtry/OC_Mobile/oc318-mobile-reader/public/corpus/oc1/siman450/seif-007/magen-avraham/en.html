It is forbidden to rent, etc. The reason is that he wants the chametz to continue existing through something else, so that the utensil not crack [Tur]. It seems to me that this means he wants the chametz to remain and continue inside it, because if he removes the chametz from the pot while it is still on the fire, the utensil will crack near the fire; if so, he benefits from chametz. But this does not apply to an oven, as it seems to me, unlike the Beis Yosef. Another reason: since the utensil itself is forbidden in benefit, it is forbidden to rent it ab initio [Tur], meaning that the pot absorbed chametz. But an oven, when they bake in it, has already been fired and is not absorbed with chametz. This is difficult: it should be forbidden because he profits from items forbidden in benefit, as in seif 4. One must say that specifically there, where they give the chametz itself as payment for the oven, for that was their custom, those coins are also the value of chametz. But with a utensil, the main rent is money; the chametz merely caused him benefit, and they were not stringent to forbid it. The same applies to a donkey. Nevertheless, it seems to me that even with a donkey it should be forbidden [Bach]. His words require analysis, for the Rashba brings proof from yayin nesech that its rent is forbidden, and there the main rent is money. There is another difficulty: with a house too the main rent is money, and nevertheless it is forbidden, as written in seif 5 in the gloss. If so, why did he permit regarding a donkey? Therefore it seems to me that all these cases speak where he rents to him without specification, only knowing that the non-Jew will use it for chametz. Therefore with an oven it is forbidden, for since he rents it for baking and ordinary baking is chametz, he profits from items forbidden in benefit; therefore he must say that it is for baking matzah. But with a utensil and donkey, they are not ordinarily designated for chametz, so it is permitted to rent them without specification for cooking; a utensil is forbidden for another reason, as above, while a donkey is permitted. What the Rema wrote regarding a house, that he rents it to dwell in, means that he rents it without specification, as above. In Beis Yosef, siman 440, in the name of the Rashba, it implies that with a house it is specifically where he sits and guards it that it is forbidden.
And some permit, etc. At first glance this requires analysis: what is this matter doing here? But in Darkei Moshe he wrote that according to the Tur, who forbids renting it because the utensil itself is absorbed with chametz, all the more so it should be forbidden to heat hot water in it and benefit from it oneself. However, the custom is to permit, and so Tosafos wrote, end quote. This requires analysis, for the Mordechai permitted selling to a non-Jew a chicken in which a wheat kernel was found, because he has no benefit from the chametz, and on this Mordechai brings proof that it is permitted to heat hot water in a chametz utensil, because one does not benefit from what is absorbed, etc.; see there. But we hold that a cooked dish in which chametz is found is entirely forbidden in benefit, as written in siman 447, and for the reason see siman 442, seif 2; it is forbidden to sell it to a non-Jew, all the more so it should be forbidden to bathe in it oneself. Also, Tosafos permitted only placing something dry into it, and in this the Tur does not disagree, for he ruled in Yoreh Deah, siman 94, that a pot absorbed with meat and milk may be used to place fruit or cold food into it. The Issur VeHeter, klal 21, also wrote that wherever the water is permitted in benefit, one may sell it to a non-Jew. If so, since here it is permitted to bathe in it, we should likewise say that a dish cooked in a chametz utensil is permitted to be sold to a non-Jew. In Ma'aglei Tzedek it is written that these utensils are not eating utensils, but utensils designated for washing and laundering; although in any event they absorbed chametz, nevertheless by law one need not hide them from sight like other utensils; see the beginning of siman 451. Nevertheless, one must say that now they are not used for any use on Pesach, end quote. Still, even according to Ma'aglei Tzedek it implies that since they absorbed only a trace of chametz and are not ben yomo, they are not forbidden in benefit. If so, if a cooked dish was cooked in them, it should be permitted to sell it to a non-Jew. See Toras Chatas, klal 85, who ruled regarding a pot absorbed with meat and milk that it is permitted to use it to make hot water for washing the head, yet it is forbidden to sell it to a non-Jew. One must say that something intended for eating, when the non-Jew eats and benefits from it, is considered as though the Jew benefits from it, for he gives benefit to the non-Jew, as written at the end of siman 448. But washing the head with it is permitted, because he does not benefit from the chametz within it; furthermore, it is not the normal manner of benefit. See what I wrote in siman 466. In Yam Shel Shlomo on Chullin he wrote that only hot water for washing the head is customarily permitted, but what is fit for food and drink is forbidden; see there. If so, the same applies here, that it is forbidden to sell it to a non-Jew. The Bach wrote: those who rent from the ruler vats and mills, to take from every beer that they brew a measure of chametz, if the Jew is obligated to repair the vats and mills when they break, then they are in the Jew's domain and it is forbidden to receive even money as his wages, since their custom is to give chametz, and it is like benefiting from the value of chametz. If he has the power to prevent them from brewing at all, he is obligated to prevent them, for he wants its continued existence. Through a sale it is permitted, meaning that before Pesach he should sell to a non-Jew all the measures that will come from that week and take the money; and although the non-Jew later gives him the measures, it is permitted, as written in siman 448, seif 3. It seems to me that he should also sell the vats to the non-Jew. If the Jew need not repair the vats and mills, it is similar to one who bought the priests' allotment, as written in seif 2, and it is enough that he stipulate before Pesach. If he has no one with whom to stipulate, it is like after the fact and permitted without a condition, end quote. It does not seem so to me, for the priests' allotment is not rent, but merely a tax that they give them, and if they do not give it today, they give it tomorrow. But here he gives it to him as rent for the mills and vats, and the non-Jew separates it; if so, it is exactly like the oven, where even money is forbidden to receive as its rent, for he profits from items forbidden in benefit. Although he need not repair them, nevertheless they are his for the time being, for rental for its day is considered a sale. What does the ruler care whether the Jew leaves them empty or allows people to brew and grind with them? Therefore it seems to me that he has no permission except to sell the mills and vats to a non-Jew before Pesach; see the end of siman 243 in the gloss. One must say that it speaks where the Jew does not rent the mill itself, but rents the right due to the ruler from the mill, meaning the measures. Nevertheless, it seems to me that this is not comparable to the priests' allotment, because as soon as they grind the chametz and separate the measure and place it in the mill, the Jew has acquired his measure that is due to him. Therefore he must sell it to a non-Jew, or command the grinders not to separate the measures on Pesach, but rather that the non-Jews write down that they ground on Pesach and he collect from them after Pesach. So it seems to me.
