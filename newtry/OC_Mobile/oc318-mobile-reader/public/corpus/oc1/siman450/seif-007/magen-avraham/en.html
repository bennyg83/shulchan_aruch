(א) Forbidden to rent etc. Because he wants its continued existence through something else so the vessel not crack (Tur). It appears the reason is he wants the chametz remain and be sustained inside — if he removes chametz from the pot while on the fire the vessel cracks at the fire and then he benefits from chametz; but in an oven this does not apply, unlike Beit Yosef. And further, since the vessel itself is forbidden in benefit, forbidden to rent l'chatchila (Tur) — meaning the pot absorbed chametz; but an oven when one bakes in it is already fired and does not absorb chametz. It is difficult — it should be forbidden because of profiting from issurei hana'ah as seif 4; it must be said specifically there they give chametz itself as oven-payment, for such was their custom — those monies are also chametz money; but for utensils the main wage is money, only chametz caused him benefit — they did not be stringent to forbid; the same for a donkey. Nevertheless it appears to forbid even for a donkey (Bach); and his words require further study, for Rashba brings proof from yayin nesech that renting is forbidden and there the main rental is money; and one may challenge: a house too — main rental is money and nevertheless forbidden as seif 5 in the gloss. Therefore it appears all these deal when he rents unstipulated only knowing the non-Jew will use it (for chametz); therefore for an oven it is forbidden, since he rents it for baking and unstipulated baking is chametz — he profits from issurei hana'ah, therefore he must say to bake matzah; but for utensils and donkey they do not presume chametz, therefore unstipulated permitted to rent for cooking; forbidden for utensils for another reason as above; for donkey permitted. And what Rama wrote regarding a house rented to dwell — when rented unstipulated as above; and in Beit Yosef siman 440 in the name of Rashba it implies specifically for a house where he sits and guards it is forbidden.

(ב) And some permit etc. At first glance it requires further study what this has to do here; but Darkei Moshe wrote: according to Tur who forbids renting because the vessel itself absorbed chametz — all the more forbidden to heat in it for his own benefit. Nevertheless the custom is permitted, and so Tosafot (end of his words); and it requires further study, for Mordechai permitted a chicken in which wheat was found to sell to a non-Jew since he has no benefit from the chametz — and on this Mordechai brings proof that heating water in chametz vessels is permitted since one does not benefit from the absorption etc. (end of his words); and we hold a dish in which chametz was found — everything is forbidden in benefit, as siman 447 (and the reason — see siman 442 seif 2); and forbidden to sell to a non-Jew — all the more forbidden to wash in it; and in Tosafot they permitted only to put something dry inside — Tur does not dispute, for he ruled Yoreh De'ah siman 94 that a pot absorbed meat in milk may have fruit or cold placed in it; and Or Zarua wrote everywhere water permitted in benefit may be sold to a non-Jew — if so, since here permitted to wash, we may say likewise a dish cooked in chametz utensils may be sold to a non-Jew. In Ma'agalei Tzedek it is written these utensils are not eating utensils but specifically for washing and laundering — even though they absorbed chametz, by law they need not be hidden from sight like other vessels (Arukh, Tanakh) — nevertheless it must be that now they are not used at all on Pesach (end of his words). Nevertheless even according to Maharitz it appears since they absorbed somewhat and are not b'ein, they are not forbidden in benefit; if so, if a dish was cooked in them it may be sold to a non-Jew; and see Taz chapter 80 who ruled regarding a pot absorbed meat in milk that it is permitted to heat water to wash the head, and nevertheless forbidden to sell to a non-Jew; it must be said a food item — when the non-Jew eats and enjoys it, it is as if the Jew benefits, for his benefit is from the non-Jew, as siman 448; but to wash the head with it is permitted since he does not benefit from chametz inside; and further it is not the usual manner of their benefit, as siman 466. In Yeshuot Yaakov on Chullin he wrote: specifically hot water to wash the head — custom is lenient; but what is fit for food and drink is forbidden (end of his words); if so, here too forbidden to sell to a non-Jew. Bach wrote: those who rent from the lord cauldrons and mills take a measure of chametz from every beer wage cooked in cauldrons — if the Jew must repair cauldrons and mills when broken, they are in the Jew's possession and forbidden to receive even money as wage, since their custom is to give chametz — like benefiting from chametz money; and if he can prevent them from cooking he must, for wanting its continued existence; and through sale permitted — sell before Pesach to a non-Jew all measures from this week and take money, even though the non-Jew gives measures afterwards, as siman 448 seif 3 (and it appears he should also sell the cauldrons to the non-Jew). If the Jew need not repair cauldrons and mills — like one who bought the priests' allotment as seif 2; it suffices to stipulate before Pesach; if he has no one to stipulate with, b'dieved permitted without condition (end of his words). It appears the priests' allotment is not rental but a mere tax — if they do not give today they give tomorrow; but here he gives wage for mills and cauldrons and the non-Jew separates it — it is actual money like an oven; even money forbidden as wage, profiting from issurei hana'ah; and even if he need not repair, they are his for the hour — rental is daily, not sale; what does the lord care if the Jew leaves them empty or lets his cook grind — therefore no permission except selling mills and cauldrons before Pesach (end of his words, siman 443 gloss); it must deal when the Jew does not rent the mills themselves but rents the lord's allotment from the mills, i.e. the measures; nevertheless it appears unlike the priests' allotment: immediately when they grind chametz and separate the measure and leave it in the mill the Jew acquired his measure — therefore he may sell to a non-Jew or command the grinders not to separate measures on Pesach but write the non-Jew ground on Pesach and collect after Pesach, as above.
