Seif 4, a Jew, etc., for it is considered, etc., and if, etc. Avodah Zarah 62a: What is the reason for his wage, etc.; and we learned, 'if he sold them,' etc. See Tosafos there, s.v. 'But did we not learn.' However, it is astonishing, etc.
And if he received, etc., it is permitted. It implies that this is so even if he himself received it, in accordance with the Rambam's view, which he wrote at the end of siman 443. So too is implied by the plain meaning of the Gemara in Avodah Zarah there. See Tosafos there, s.v. 'we learned'; 'it is astonishing,' etc., but with this it is resolved. See Ran at the beginning of chapter 5 of Avodah Zarah, who brought proof from the Yerushalmi and wrote there in the name of the Ramban that what we conclude there, that it is a fine they imposed on donkey-drivers and sellers of yayin nesech, applies specifically to yayin nesech and shevi'is, which transfer their prohibition to their money; and he relies on the first reasoning, that it transfers its prohibition to its money, etc.; see there. Seif 5, there is. There: he hired her to sit, etc. See the Gemara there, 65a: this is not difficult, this case, etc. The same applies here, where he takes nothing for the chametz, for even if he does not bake at all, he will pay him.
And likewise it is permitted. This is learned from the above. But to rent to him so that he will bring chametz into it is forbidden, as written in the Yerushalmi, chapter 2, halacha 2: a Jew may not rent his house to a non-Jew to place chametz in it. Although they said in the first chapter of Pesachim, 'he designated a house for him,' etc., here it speaks during Pesach or on erev Pesach after it has already become forbidden in benefit, and therefore he is profiting from items forbidden in benefit, as above. So too it appears in the Yerushalmi there that it speaks during Pesach: 'during Pesach, what is the law,' etc.; 'but was it not taught: he may not rent,' etc. See Taz and Magen Avraham, and see Beis Yosef in the name of Agur. It seems to me that the case in the first chapter of Pesachim also speaks during Pesach, as written there: the Sages taught, a non-Jew who entered, etc.; necessarily this is during Pesach. About that it says: if he deposited it, etc.; if he designated, etc.; does this mean, etc.? Rather, there it speaks where he designated a house for him not in order to bring chametz into it, and it is permitted even when he brings it into it, as above. On this they said there, 'when the non-Jew stands,' etc.; does this mean, etc.; but was it not taught, etc.? There too he rents it as a dwelling house, and nevertheless it is forbidden. The Geonim explain unlike Rashi, that 'he designated a house for him' means that he rents him another house, not in his own house. This is what it means by 'a non-Jew who entered,' etc.: since he is together with the chametz, if he deposited it, etc., even without responsibility; if he designated for him, etc., without responsibility, since it is in another house. So too it is implied in the Yerushalmi there: a non-Jew's chametz during Pesach, what is the law? Rabbi Yirmiyah said it is permitted; Rabbi Yissa said it is forbidden. But was it not taught: one may not rent his donkey to a non-Jew to bring chametz on it? It answers that this is where he comes together with it. But was it not taught: one may not rent his house, etc.? Can you say that this is where he dwells together with him? Thus, even according to the one who permits, it is forbidden when he is together with him in the house. This is what is written in Terumas HaDeshen and above, siman 448, seif 3, where it is outside the house.
