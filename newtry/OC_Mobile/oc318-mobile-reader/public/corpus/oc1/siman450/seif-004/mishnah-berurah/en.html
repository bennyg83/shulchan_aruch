(7) And non Jews baked in it, etc. This implies that the ruling applies even when they did so without the owner’s knowledge, and the later authorities rule accordingly.
(8) Even money. All the more so, he may not accept loaves from those baked during Pesach, even after Pesach.
(9) Since he profits, etc. Initially, rabbinic law prohibits profiting from any item from which benefit is forbidden, even if the money is paid before Pesach or after Pesach. This is unlike seif 3, where the oven was jointly owned and, when the partners divided its use by time, the oven belonged entirely to the non Jew during his allotted period.
(10) It is permitted to benefit from the money. After the fact, profiting from an item from which benefit is forbidden is not prohibited in other areas of halacha, since the money is neither the chametz itself nor an exchange for it; it merely came about through the chametz. The Sages were stringent about this only regarding wine used for idolatry, because of the severity of idolatry. This applies when he received money. If they gave him loaves from those baked in his oven, he may not benefit from them, because he acquired them during Pesach as payment for the use of his oven, where the ordinary practice is to pay with loaves. They are therefore chametz belonging to a Jew that remained through Pesach.
