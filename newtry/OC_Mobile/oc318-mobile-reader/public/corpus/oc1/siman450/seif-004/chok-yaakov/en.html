Taz writes in seif katan 3 that the two seifim reflect differing opinions and expresses surprise at Shulchan Aruch. Kenesses HaGedolah demonstrates at length that there is no dispute, and this is the correct understanding.
For he profits from an item forbidden in benefit. See the end of this siman. Bach discusses the custom in Russia and Volhynia whereby Jews lease estates that include kettles and mills, receiving a measure from each batch of beer brewed and a measure of malt from each person who grinds malt. If the Jew must repair the kettles and mills when they break, they are considered in his domain, and he may not receive even money as payment, since the custom is to pay him in chametz and he thereby benefits from the value of chametz. If he has the authority to prevent all brewing, he must do so, for otherwise he desires the continued existence of the prohibition. A sale resolves the issue: before Pesach he should sell to a non-Jew all the measures that will become due that week and take the money, and it is permitted even if the non-Jew later delivers those measures to him. If the Jew is not responsible for repairing the kettles and mills, it is enough to stipulate before Pesach as in seif 2. If he has no one with whom to stipulate, it is considered after the fact and is permitted without a stipulation. Taz is stringent even where the Jew bears no responsibility for repairs. Magen Avraham at the end of the siman inclines toward leniency because the mill serves the entire city and the leaseholder has no power to stop the non-Jew from grinding that day; he can therefore say that he does not wish to acquire the payment when it is forbidden. I have not elaborated further because the local practice concerning such estate leases is unknown to me, as they are uncommon in these lands.
