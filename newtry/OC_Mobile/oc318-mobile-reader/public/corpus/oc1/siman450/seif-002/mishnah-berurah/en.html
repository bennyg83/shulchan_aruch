(3) He should tell him beforehand. Since the non Jew is obligated to give him a loaf every week, if the non Jew designates a loaf during Pesach for the Jew, it is as though the Jew has already acquired it and as though his chametz remained through Pesach. He must therefore stipulate that for the week of Pesach he does not want loaves, but rather their flour or their monetary value, or loaves that will be baked after Pesach. Then, even if the non Jew prepares loaves for him on Pesach, there is no concern, since they are not encumbered to the Jew at all and presently remain entirely in the non Jew’s possession.
(4) Chametz loaves. This includes even the loaves the non Jew prepared for him during Pesach. The later authorities agree that all this applies initially. After the fact, even if he made no stipulation, he may receive loaves after Pesach in payment for the week of Pesach. Although the non Jew designated them for the Jew during Pesach, the Jew did not acquire them before they entered his possession. The non Jew merely owed him an ordinary debt of loaves, not those specific loaves, and they remained the non Jew’s property throughout Pesach.
