He should tell him. And b'dieved, even if he did not tell him and the non-Jew brought him [payment] after Pesach it is permitted, for the Jew did not acquire it on Pesach (Bach, Magen Avraham). And Kaf HaChayyim wrote that l'chatchila it is good to stipulate thus; and if he stipulated thus, even if the non-Jew later gives him chametz over which Passover passed, it is also permitted; and if he did not stipulate with him thus, if the non-Jew gives him afterwards flour or money or chametz made after Pesach it is permitted. And the same law applies to one who purchases the priests' allotment who give them fixed bread every week — see Taz (who was inclined to be stringent and requires a condition, and concluded that if they did not stipulate at first the transaction is voided and he stipulates — see siman 245 above). And one should not be stringent in these laws, for in any event a Jew does not acquire without pulling according to most poskim (Kaf HaChayyim).
