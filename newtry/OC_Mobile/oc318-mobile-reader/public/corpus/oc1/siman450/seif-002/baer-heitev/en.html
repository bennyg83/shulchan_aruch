He should tell him. After the fact, even if he did not tell him this and the non-Jew brought it to him after Pesach, it is permitted, because the Jew did not acquire it during Pesach. Bach; Magen Avraham. Chok Yaakov writes that initially it is proper to stipulate this. If he did stipulate it, then even if the non-Jew afterward gives him chametz that remained over Pesach, it is permitted. If he did not stipulate it, and the non-Jew later gives him flour, money, or chametz produced after Pesach, it is permitted. The same law applies where one purchased the fixed due of priests who receive a set amount of bread each week. See Taz, who inclines toward stringency and requires an express stipulation. He concludes that if they did not stipulate initially, the transaction should be annulled and then renewed with a stipulation. See above, siman 245. One should not be stringent in these laws, because according to most poskim a Jew does not acquire without meshichah in any event. Chok Yaakov.
