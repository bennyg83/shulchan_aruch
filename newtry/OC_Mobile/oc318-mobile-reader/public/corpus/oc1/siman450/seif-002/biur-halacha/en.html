He should tell him before Pesach — see Taz who divided here: stipulation before Pesach does not help except when he did not lend for a fixed time but each week holding his money pays him interest — with this stipulation before Pesach helps, like a gift at loan start, for if he wanted he could remove his money; but if he lent for a term and within the term neither can withdraw and he is obligated to give loaves each week — stipulation before Pesach does not help, only stipulate at loan start, like siman 245 regarding Jew-non-Jew partnership for Shabbat. However, from Magen Avraham s.k. 2 it implies no division here; see Machatzit HaShekel and Peri Megadim; and so Noda BiYehudah and Chamed Moshe rejected Taz's words (see there). It appears their words are not compelled except when reconciling both that on Pesach he gives money; but when the Jew stipulates alone and the non-Jew does not agree to change prior terms, there may well be room for Taz — the Jew cannot change initial terms. See Peri Megadim who also hesitated on Taz and wrote the first condition is not strong since there was no pulling and it is something not yet come (see there); concludes nevertheless where dina demalkhuta is that conditions stand, be stringent like Taz; b'dieved requires further study (see there). In my humble opinion even there no stringency b'dieved: though dina demalkhuta obligates giving loaves, he has only a debt of loaves, not that the loaves themselves belong to the Jew even before entering his possession, as we wrote in Mishna Berurah; and so we found in Beit Meir (end of his words).
