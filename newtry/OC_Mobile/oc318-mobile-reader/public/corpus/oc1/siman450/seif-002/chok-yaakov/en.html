Since he stipulated with him in this manner. Although there are some differing opinions, the principal ruling appears to follow the language of Shulchan Aruch and Levush: initially it is preferable to make this stipulation. See Rokeach, siman 268. If he stipulated, then even if the non-Jew afterward gives him chametz that remained over Pesach, it is permitted. If he did not stipulate, but afterward the non-Jew gives him flour, money, or chametz made after Pesach, it is permitted, because exchanges for chametz are permitted, like chametz belonging to habitual transgressors, as explained above in siman 443. The same applies where one receives the non-Jew’s allotted share, such as a fixed quantity of bread given weekly. See Tur and Beit Yosef. One should not impose stringencies in these laws, especially because according to most authorities a Jew does not acquire from a non-Jew without meshichah. See Choshen Mishpat 194 and Shach there, seif katan 1.
