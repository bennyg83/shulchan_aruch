(S.k. 1) He must, etc. Although ab initio, etc.; that is to say, although we hold that chametz does not transfer its prohibition to its money, and its exchanges are permitted, as written at the end of siman 443, nevertheless ab initio it is forbidden, etc.
The first is not intact, etc. According to this, Chok Yaakov wrote that even if he pays him on Pesach with something that is not chametz, it is permitted. This is also implied by Tosafos in Avodah Zarah, from where this law is learned. We say at the beginning of Perek HaPoalim that the house of Rabbi Yannai would borrow shevi'is produce from the poor during shevi'is, for the poor had much shevi'is produce and needed to consume it before the time of bi'ur; see Tosafos there. For the benefit of the poor they borrowed those fruits from them and paid the poor in the eighth year with eighth-year produce, and it was not considered exchange produce of shevi'is. Although shevi'is transfers its prohibition to its money, nevertheless, since at the time of repayment the shevi'is produce is no longer intact, it is not called exchange produce of shevi'is. All the more so with chametz, which does not transfer its prohibition to its money. Tosafos wrote there that the same applies if they paid them during shevi'is with other produce that was not shevi'is, if they had already eaten the produce they borrowed from the poor, it is permitted. So too appears from the words of the Rosh, and all the more so with chametz. Knesses HaGedolah wrote that there are places that had the custom, etc.; Chok Yaakov wrote in his name that they were concerned because of the prohibition of interest, for it is like se'ah for se'ah, which is forbidden. So wrote Darkei Moshe as well, and not because of a chametz prohibition. According to this, based on what the Rema wrote in Yoreh Deah, siman 162, that our custom is to be lenient with loaves because people are not particular about such a small matter, the custom mentioned by Knesses HaGedolah does not apply.
