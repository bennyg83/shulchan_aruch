(א) Before Pesach he must repay him — meaning even if he borrowed this loaf on erev Pesach near the time of burning: if he had not borrowed it, he would have had to burn it, and if he did not burn it it would be forbidden in benefit — nevertheless he must return another loaf after Pesach, for in any event he borrowed at a permitted time when the chametz was full property still before burning time. Acharonim wrote: even if the Jew transgressed and borrowed and kept the chametz until after Pesach and it became forbidden, nevertheless he must pay and we do not say the lender cannot receive its value because of chametz substitutes as above siman 443, for this is not chametz substitutes — since he borrowed the loaf it is his and the lender has only monetary claim. A Jew who lent chametz to a non-Jew before Pesach and repaid chametz like it after Pesach — since he does not return the first chametz in its existence it is permitted [so Shelah in the name of Riaz]; and from his words it implies if he returns that chametz it is forbidden. Peri Chadash disagrees — it is not called chametz over which Passover passed, for until now it belonged to the non-Jew; since he lent it, it stood in his possession and it is chametz of a non-Jew over which Passover passed, permitted even for eating. In practice one should be stringent like the first opinion — it appears as chametz of a Jew over which Passover passed since he returned that chametz itself.

(ב) If he does not repay him — and because of concern for interest, for it is forbidden to lend se'ah bese'ah — there is no such concern with loaves because the difference is a small matter according to most, and people are not particular about this (Acharonim).
