1) [Se'if 1] A Jew who had already borrowed, etc. In some places, the custom is that a woman does not borrow a loaf from another woman before Pesach to repay her after Pesach. It appears to me that this custom was adopted because of a concern for ribbis, since one who lends another person one se'ah in exchange for one se'ah, or one loaf in exchange for one loaf, and fixes repayment for a particular date, acts unlawfully. Ri'az, cited by Shiltei HaGibborim in the chapter Kol Sha'ah; Darkei Moshe, subsection 1; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur; Magen Avraham, subsection 1. In light of Rema's ruling in the gloss to Yoreh De'ah, siman 162, se'if 1, that one may borrow one loaf in exchange for one loaf, it would appear possible to rule leniently. However, Ri'az himself did not definitively state that this was the reason for the custom, but wrote only that it appeared so to him. Therefore, one should not rule leniently in a place where the prohibitive custom exists, for perhaps the custom was adopted for another reason. Even in a place without such a prohibitive custom, it appears proper initially to be stringent not to borrow before Pesach for repayment after Pesach. This also appears from the language of the Shulchan Aruch, 'A Jew who borrowed,' implying that specifically when one already borrowed and did not repay must one repay after Pesach. Initially, one should repay before Pesach if possible.
2) There. One must repay it, etc. This does not involve the prohibition against chametz that remained over Pesach, because the original chametz was not extant when the prohibition applied. Tur; Levush; Magen Avraham, there; Chok Yaakov, subsection 1. Chok Yaakov writes there that one may repay with money instead of a loaf even during Pesach. See there. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1; Eshel Avraham, subsection 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1, write likewise.
3) There. One must repay it, etc. This applies even if the original loaf remains extant because the borrower deliberately or inadvertently failed to eliminate it. The borrower must repay the lender by giving another loaf after Pesach. Ma'amar Mordechai, subsection 1; Mekor Chaim, subsection 1; Eshel Avraham, subsection 1; Chayei Adam, rule 124, subsection 22.
4) There. One must repay it, etc. If a Jew lent chametz to a non-Jew before Pesach, the non-Jew may repay with equivalent chametz after Pesach. Since the non-Jew does not return the original chametz itself, it is permitted. Ri'az, cited by Shiltei HaGibborim in the chapter Kol Sha'ah; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur. Peri Chadash, however, challenges this and writes that even if the non-Jew returns the original chametz, it is permitted, including for consumption. Once the Jew lent it to the non-Jew, it entered the non-Jew's ownership and is chametz belonging to a non-Jew that remained over Pesach, which is permitted even for consumption. Eshel Avraham, there, and Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 2, agree.
