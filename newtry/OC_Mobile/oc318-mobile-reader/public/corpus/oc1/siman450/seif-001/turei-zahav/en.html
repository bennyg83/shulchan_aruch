So too Tur in the name of Avi HaEzri; and Beit Yosef wrote in Hagahot Maimoniyot and Mordechai regarding a Jew who bought the priests' allotment — he takes after Pesach what was withheld on Pesach; it implies no need to stipulate thus initially. The reason: here there is no prohibition but a fine for bal yera'eh, and here fining does not apply; he ruled to be stringent. It appears there is a difference in timing of the condition: in this law where he receives interest every week, each week is a separate debt — if he wants he can remove his money each week; when the Jew stipulates at week's start it is like a gift from the outset, unless he fixed interest once and cannot remove until the set time — then stipulation before Pesach does not help; learn from siman 245 regarding partnership with a non-Jew for Shabbat. Likewise here: since the Jew already acquired the loaf of the week of Pesach, what does stipulation help to remove himself — like selling chametz before Pesach he had on Pesach; not so if he stipulated initially the Jew did not acquire that loaf at all. Therefore one who buys the priests' allotment at once for a fixed term must stipulate at acquisition time for the reason mentioned, not later before Pesach; if he did not stipulate initially, his remedy is to void the transaction and stipulate then, as for Shabbat there. In a partnership oven with a non-Jew written afterwards — stipulation before Pesach helps there too: since half the oven is the Jew's he can always tell the non-Jew he does not want to participate on Pesach except on condition the non-Jew give him money for his share — this helps well in my humble opinion.
