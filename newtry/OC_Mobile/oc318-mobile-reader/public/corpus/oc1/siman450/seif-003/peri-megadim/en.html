Even. See Taz. See what I wrote in letters 1 and 2. The Tur brought a responsum of Rashi, that he should take money, etc.; this is what the Mechaber copied in seif 3, though he omitted 'he should take money,' relying on what the Mordechai wrote in the first chapter of Avodah Zarah, siman 808: Maharam was asked about a Jew and a non-Jew who have an oven in partnership, etc., and he answered that if he stipulates before Pesach it is permitted. See letter 2 regarding the Taz's difficulty, and according to what I wrote one can answer. Afterward the Tur wrote that Rabbi Shimshon ben Avraham forbade in an incident, etc.; this is what the Mechaber copied in seif 4, as Beer HaGolah noted. Since the Tur writes 'and the Rashba,' with a vav, it implies that he disagrees with Rashi's responsum. But the two cases are not connected: in Rashi there was a condition before Pesach, whereas the Rashba speaks of a Jew's oven, etc. Also, regarding the loaves they brought that were baked on Pesach, which he acquired as rent for his oven, it is obvious that Rashi would agree that they are forbidden, and even the money is exchange money for chametz. What he wrote 'ab initio' is according to the Rashba of blessed memory, who ruled that after the fact it is permitted; see Tevuos Megadim, letter 5. Therefore one must say that the dispute between Rashi and the Rashba is that according to the Rashba, a non-Jew's chametz is forbidden in benefit during Pesach, so this is profiting from items forbidden in benefit. According to this, what the Rashba forbids - taking money - is forbidden even before Pesach, and with Hashem's help this will be explained in Magen Avraham, letter 4. Rashi holds that a non-Jew's chametz during Pesach is permitted in benefit, and therefore this is not profiting from items forbidden in benefit, etc. If so, at first glance the rulings of the Shulchan Aruch contradict one another: in seif 4 versus seif 3, even with a partnership oven, and even where he can remove the non-Jew at any time and say, 'I do not want to be your partner during the week of Pesach,' so that the oven will stand empty or they will bake other things, nevertheless, although for this purpose it is like the beginning of the partnership, as I wrote at the end of letter 1, it is still profiting from items forbidden in benefit, for in the end he is taking rental money for things forbidden in benefit. This is at least forbidden ab initio with other items forbidden in benefit, and there is no remedy for the case of seif 3 except that at the beginning of buying the oven they stipulate that during the days of Pesach the oven will belong to the non-Jew and one week after Pesach to the Jew; then it is permitted, but not otherwise. This is the explanation of his words in this matter, as it seems to me. Nevertheless, it is difficult to say that Rashi holds a non-Jew's chametz is permitted in benefit during Pesach, for in Pesachim 29a the Gemara concludes that Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov retracted. It would be better to explain that Rashi holds that with other items forbidden in benefit, one may profit from them, as I wrote above. However, as a matter of law one can say that the two seifim do not contradict: a partnership oven is different, and whenever he stipulates before Pesach it is as though he stipulated at the beginning of the partnership, and this is proper. With this you will understand the words of Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah in Hagahos Beis Yosef, letter 4; see there. In siman 245, where a condition before Shabbos does not help except at the beginning of the partnership, there he appears like the non-Jew's agent, as Magen Avraham wrote in letter 3, and the Taz follows his own view in letters 1 and 2. This is unlike seif 4, with a Jew's oven, where a condition does not help; even before Pesach it is forbidden to take money, for ultimately he profits from items forbidden in benefit. The case of one who rents a donkey in seif 7 can be explained as Magen Avraham wrote in letter 11. All this is where they have an oven in partnership. But if they rented an oven from a non-Jew to bake in it, and each one has permission to withdraw whenever he wants, even the Taz agrees that a condition before Pesach helps, so that the non-Jew bakes during the week of Pesach, and there is no profiting from items forbidden in benefit, as stated by Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah.
And what he wrote. The difficulty raised against the Rashba, that one is not pleased to acquire a prohibition, was not against the body of the law that it is forbidden because he profits from items forbidden in benefit. Rather, it was against what he wrote that the loaves are forbidden because his oven acquired them like his courtyard, making them a Jew's chametz over which Pesach passed, and therefore forbidden by law. But one is not pleased to acquire a prohibition. To this he answered that he is pleased with this, for he allowed the baking, etc.; see there. See Magen Avraham, and with Hashem's help it will be explained.
