Even money is forbidden, etc. In the Tur, after Responsum 260, which is seif 3, it is written as follows: the Rashba wrote: an incident came before me involving a Jew who had an oven, and non-Jews baked chametz in it on Pesach. The baker, the Jew's treasurer, came and brought him loaves after Pesach from the profit of the oven, and I forbade him to receive them. It is not necessary to say that chametz is forbidden, for the Jew acquired it through the profit of his oven and it is a Jew's chametz; even money is forbidden to receive as his wage, for he profits from items forbidden in benefit, since chametz on Pesach is forbidden in benefit, and even a non-Jew's chametz is forbidden for him to benefit from on Pesach, end quote. At first glance, from the Tur's language, 'and the Rashba wrote,' it implies that there is a dispute between him and Rashi, namely that the Rashba forbids taking the money that Rashi permitted. This is not so, for Rashi permitted because he stipulated before Pesach. According to the Rashba, a condition does not apply, and the loaves baked on Pesach were automatically actually the Jew's; therefore it is forbidden ab initio to take even money for them. Rather, the main dispute is in the manner that the Beis Yosef wrote in the name of the Rivash: there is one who says one may not rent his animal to a non-Jew to bring chametz on it, because he holds a non-Jew's chametz is forbidden in benefit; and there is one who says one may rent to a non-Jew, because a non-Jew's chametz is permitted in benefit. Therefore, since Rashi permitted taking money although the money comes because of the non-Jew's chametz, he must hold like the one who says a non-Jew's chametz is permitted in benefit. But the Rashba wrote explicitly like the one who says even a non-Jew's chametz is forbidden. If so, there is a great wonder on the Shulchan Aruch, who brought the words of Rashi in the previous seif and here brings the words of the Rashba. According to the Rashba, there is no remedy for a Jew's oven, even in partnership with a non-Jew, unless they stipulated at the time of buying the oven that during the week of Pesach it belongs to the non-Jew, like the case of siman 245. Do not ask on what the Rashba wrote, that the Jew acquired the loaves that the treasurer brought him after Pesach and they are forbidden: why do we not say here too that he is pleased to acquire permitted things and not forbidden things, as written in siman 448 regarding a non-Jew who brings chametz on Yom Tov, where the Jew can afterward say so and it is permitted? There it is different, because we have no proof that he wants it acquired for him on Yom Tov, so we say he certainly does not want the prohibition. This is unlike here, where the chametz comes because of today's act, that he baked in his oven today, when he gives the oven to bake in today; he certainly wants this. This is simple in my humble opinion; see what I wrote at the end of this siman.
