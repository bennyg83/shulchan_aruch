Seif 3 — A Jew and a non-Jew with partnership oven etc. Note: see Magen Avraham s.k. 3 who wrote on four yom tov days forbidden to tell him take yours etc.; see Kaf HaChayyim what he challenged; see Chiddushei Orach Chayim in Choshen Mishpat siman 330 what he wrote. Further his words are wondrous from explicit baraita Avodah Zarah 21 — explicit in baraita one may not rent a bathhouse to a non-Jew where the non-Jew does melacha on Shabbats and yom tov — therefore yom tov too is stated; on yom tov burning is permitted for the Jew — if we say this burning not for equal need of all souls is forbidden to him, therefore we are concerned for the non-Jew — if so here too when the non-Jew bakes for himself, all the more if he bakes chametz — if the Jew did so for the non-Jew, especially chametz, it would be doing a Torah prohibition "for you and not for non-Jews" — therefore concern even for non-Jew. Possibly Kaf HaChayyim's intent: since truly renting oven to non-Jew or saying take yours etc., he is not his agent and no prohibition only because of melacha as siman 443 and 244 — on Shabbat forbidden; in bathhouse it is recognizable and marit ayin; here not recognizable if non-Jew bakes for himself or Jew — no marit ayin, onlooker says for Jew's need permitted for Jew, especially since anyway can be attributed to Jew; two leniencies — like field permitted; see Taz siman 244 and understand. However possibly one may say even Magen Avraham was stringent only on Pesach where erev Pesach mostly chametz vs matzah recognizable by crack or blushing faces; also Jews do not bake through non-Jews on Pesach, they guard their dough — then like bathhouse where Gemara Avodah Zarah says bathhouse for guests people do not do — likewise on Pesach not through non-Jews, therefore known he bakes for non-Jew's need and marit ayin concern; but other yom tov possibly Kaf HaChayyim is right that on yom tov permitted to say take yours — no marit ayin they will say for Jew's need — two leniencies like field; to reduce dispute, better to decide for practical law; see Chiddussei Avodah Zarah what is written; see my composition on Orach Chayim mishnah 592 daf 68 what reconciles his words (end of his words); examine.
