Seif 3. A Jew and a non-Jew who have an oven in partnership, etc. Note: See Magen Avraham, s.k. 3, who wrote that on the four days that are Yom Tov it is forbidden to say to him, 'You take,' etc. See Chok Yaakov, who challenged him on this, and see my novellae to Orach Chaim, in the laws of Chol HaMoed, siman 530, where I wrote about this. His words are also astonishing from an explicit baraisa in Avodah Zarah, folio 21, where the baraisa explicitly states that a person may not rent a bathhouse to a non-Jew, because the non-Jew does melacha in it on Shabbosos and Yomim Tovim. Thus it mentions Yom Tov too. But on Yom Tov kindling is permitted for a Jew. If we say that since in any event this kindling is not for a need equally relevant to every person, such kindling is forbidden to him, and therefore we are concerned when a non-Jew does it, then the same should apply here when the non-Jew bakes in it for himself, and all the more so if he bakes chametz in it. For if the Jew did so for the non-Jew, especially with chametz, he would be committing a Torah prohibition of 'for you' and not for non-Jews. If so, we should be concerned even with a non-Jew. Perhaps the intent of Chok Yaakov is that since in truth he rents the oven to the non-Jew, or says to him, 'You take,' etc., and he is not his agent, there is no prohibition here except that the main prohibition is because of maris ayin, as written in simanim 243 and 244. If so, it is understandable with a bathhouse, which is recognizable as a bathhouse and involves maris ayin; but here it is not recognizable whether the non-Jew is baking for himself or for the Jew. Therefore there is no maris ayin, for the observer says he is baking for the Jew, and for a Jew this is truly permitted. Especially since in any event one can attribute it to the Jew, there are two factors for leniency, so it again resembles a field, which is permitted. See Taz, siman 244, and you will understand. However, according to this, one can say that even Magen Avraham was stringent only on Pesach. In that case, generally, chametz and matzah are well recognizable, either through a crack or through the whitening of its surface. Also, Jews do not ordinarily bake through non-Jews on Pesach, and they themselves guard their doughs on Pesach. If so, this resembles a bathhouse, about which the Talmud in Avodah Zarah says that people do not ordinarily run a bathhouse as sharecropping. So too on Pesach, people do not bake through non-Jews, and therefore people know that he is baking for the non-Jew's need, and there is a concern of maris ayin. But on other Yomim Tovim, perhaps Magen Avraham agrees with Chok Yaakov that on Yom Tov it is permitted to say, 'You take,' etc., for there is no maris ayin: people will say it is for the Jew, and there are two advantages for leniency, so it becomes like a field. It is preferable to minimize the dispute, as seems to me in deciding the practical law on this. See my novellae to tractate Avodah Zarah, where I wrote about this, and also see my work on Orach Chaim from the year 592, folio 68, where I wrote to reconcile his words; see there and analyze it.
