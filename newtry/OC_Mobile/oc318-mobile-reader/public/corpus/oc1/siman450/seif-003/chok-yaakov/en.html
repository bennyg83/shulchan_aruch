And afterward I will take my share. He may then take even chametz. Beit Yosef derives this from Mordechai in the first chapter of Avodah Zarah, and Agudah writes likewise. See the later authorities concerning the distinction between this case and siman 245, where a Jew and non-Jew who jointly own a field may not say, “You take the Shabbos portion and I will take the weekday portion.” Bach writes that here too one should be stringent unless the Jew takes the money due for his share before Pesach, and the later authorities agree in practice. Nevertheless, regarding a Shabbos that falls during Pesach, merely saying, “You take your portion,” does not suffice, as in other Shabbosos of the year in siman 245, unless they stipulated at the beginning of the partnership. Magen Avraham writes that during the four Yom Tov days it is at least forbidden to say, “You take your portion,” as in siman 245. I am astonished at this. Had it been correct, one of the authorities would surely have recorded it here, yet all permit without qualification during Pesach, implying that even on Yom Tov itself it is permitted. The case is entirely unlike siman 245, where the prohibition is that the non-Jew appears to perform forbidden melachah as the Jew’s agent. That applies to field labor or renting an oven on Shabbos. On Yom Tov, however, the Jew himself may kindle for a permitted need, so certainly there is no agency concern when the non-Jew does it. The only issue here is exchange for chametz, and saying, “You take your portion,” is sufficient.
