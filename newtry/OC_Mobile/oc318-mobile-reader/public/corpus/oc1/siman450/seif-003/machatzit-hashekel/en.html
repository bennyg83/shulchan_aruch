(א) (s.k. 3) He says etc. — but here, when he takes etc.; meaning: here there is no prohibition, for it does not appear as trouble for the Jew — what of it if he troubles for the Jew, for it deals with chol hamoed as Magen Avraham wrote in that s.k. 7; and even though on chol hamoed melacha not needed for the festival is forbidden, nevertheless it is not so severe and there is no marit ayin here; the prohibition is only from receiving chametz on Pesach as his wage — therefore by taking the money first it is permitted, for then he sells the chametz before Pesach and then it is permitted to sell it, as explained below.

(ב) For Rambam and the Rosh: the Rosh's view regarding a Jew and a non-Jew who were partners in trees — even if they did not stipulate before partnering, the Jew may tell the non-Jew: take for yourself three years of orlah and I will take correspondingly three other years; for what should we be concerned — if because of marit ayin when the non-Jew works the orlah years it appears he is the Jew's agent? What of it — the Jew himself may work the tree in orlah years. And if because the Jew takes three years of fruit against three years of orlah fruit — that would be orlah substitutes. They say: such is the custom — whoever works the tree eats its fruit; and since the non-Jew passes the orlah years the fruit is all the non-Jew's, and the three years the Jew works the fruit is all his — there are no orlah substitutes at all. Therefore likewise here regarding chametz — this is Mordechai's view like the Rosh there; but Rambam holds that even for orlah one must stipulate specifically before they partnered, but afterwards saying "take yours" does not help — even though the non-Jew works alone in orlah years and the Jew alone in the other three years, nevertheless it is orlah substitutes. The same applies — one should forbid chametz here; and Hagahot Maimoniyot holds like Rambam. Therefore he holds one must take the money first, and that suffices, as written afterwards.

(ג) Only that he sells the chametz to the non-Jew etc. — meaning: if he sells chametz to the non-Jew before Pesach — even though there is reason to permit, for then the chametz is still permitted to eat, all the more so to sell — nevertheless Magen Avraham holds this reason does not help: specifically to benefit and to sell chametz he already acquired is permitted, but here he sells and benefits from chametz that will be acquired on Pesach at the time of prohibition — that is benefiting on Pesach from issurei hana'ah; and so Bach wrote (end of his words). Therefore Magen Avraham wrote we require selling him the oven; and Hagahot Maimoniyot's view is that we require taking money first. It must be said that saying "take yours on Pesach" does not help — through this we say he sells him the oven since the oven already belongs to the Jew. And why taking money first helps — I do not know a clear reason; possibly: since chametz substitutes are b'dieved permitted but l'chatchila forbidden to receive substitutes, and since the substitute-buyer, i.e. the money, is before Pesach, before he acquired chametz, it is permitted even l'chatchila; and Magen Avraham for the practical law is stringent that we require sale of the oven and also taking money before Pesach.

(ד) And on the four days that are yom tov etc. — and Kaf HaChayyim wondered: on yom tov burning is permitted — what of it if they say the non-Jew acts as the Jew's agent; they forbade only on Shabbat in siman 245. And he answered well in Sefer Ateret Roshei: likewise on yom tov it is forbidden since he bakes chametz in it for the non-Jew's need.
