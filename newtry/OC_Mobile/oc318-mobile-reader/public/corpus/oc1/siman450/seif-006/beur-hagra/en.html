Seif 6, it is permitted, etc. Avodah Zarah 63: a person may say to his donkey-drivers, etc.
And some permit. There, Rav Pappa said, etc.; Rav Ashi, etc. The first opinion also holds this way, except that it relied on what it wrote in Yoreh Deah.
And it is forbidden. Lest he come to eat it, as written in the first chapter of Pesachim 10-11, and as written in the Yerushalmi cited above, that according to the one who permits, he establishes it where he comes with it, as above. Even the Sages disagree only because he himself, etc. See above, siman 546, seif 3: 'he pushes it with a reed,' specifically. Furthermore, there is no agency for a non-Jew, as written in Bava Metzia 71b. Although the Jew does not intend to acquire it, nevertheless he is responsible for it and transgresses over it. Furthermore, he wants its continued existence, as written in Avodah Zarah 64a: 'but he wants,' etc.
