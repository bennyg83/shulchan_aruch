(6) There, and since he stipulated etc. — implies but if he did not stipulate, forbidden; as Beit Yosef in Avi HaEzri (see there). And so Levush. But Bach wrote l'chatchila concern Rabeinu Yerucham's view but b'dieved permitted even without any stipulation (see there). Bach, Magen Avraham s.k. 2; Chak Yosef Orach Chayim 2; Mekor Chayyim Orach Chayim 2; Chayei Adam general rule 124 14. Peri Chadash wrote no need to say anything beforehand — from default receives after Pesach chametz loaves that would have been given on Pesach, as Hagahot Maimoniyot and Mordechai Beit Yosef brought (see there). Kaf HaChayyim Orach Chayim 3 wrote main point as Shulchan Aruch and Levush — l'chatchila good to stipulate; if stipulates, even if non-Jew later gives chametz over which Passover passed, permitted; if not, if non-Jew gives flour, money, or post-Pesach chametz, permitted — chametz substitutes permitted as above siman 443; same for priests' allotment (meaning king gave priests their bread ration weekly, sold to Jew at year start for whole year's bread) giving fixed bread weekly; do not be stringent, Jew does not acquire without pulling per most poskim — Choshen Mishpat 194 and Shach s.k. 1 (end). However R' Zalman Orach Chayim 3 ruled like Bach — l'chatchila must stipulate; b'dieved if not, even if non-Jew brings after Pesach for Pesach week loaves that became chametz on Pesach, permitted to receive and eat. Orach Chayim 4 wrote same for priests' allotment — l'chatchila stipulate, b'dieved if not and after Pesach they give chametz bread for Pesach week, permitted to receive and eat (see there).
