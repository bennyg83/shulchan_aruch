(א) To a worker — even though his sustenance is on him, since he did not tell him to acquire chametz.
(ב) Even though he knows he will acquire etc. — and if he is hired for a year he should not eat in his house because of suspicion (Agudah).
(3) Hamz - But he should not say to him, "Go this dinar and buy some chametz for you from the hanoni and eat it, even if he is a non-Muslim, because his food is not on him, that the Israeli himself is not allowed to buy chametz on Passover, he is not allowed to tell the non-Muslim to buy him a daff that the foreigner buys for himself and not for Israel, because the Israelite gives him a dinar and tells him to buy some chametz with it, and the non-Muslim buys it in his speech, then it looks like he is sending, but it is allowed to say to him, buy it for you chametz in your dinar and eat:
(ד) Go and eat etc. — and specifically if he told him go and eat chametz, or at least he knows certainly he will eat chametz. The reason: since the shopkeeper gives the worker on credit that the master will pay, the shopkeeper is his agent and it is as if he feeds him chametz by hand; and since his sustenance is on him, he benefits from the chametz he feeds him.
(ה) And some permit etc. — since he has not yet given money to the shopkeeper, what the shopkeeper gives his worker is not considered his chametz but a mere debt — he became obligated to the non-Jew for guaranteeing him. For practical law the main view is this; nevertheless l'chatchila proper to be concerned for the first view regarding his worker and maidservant whose sustenance is on him — not to tell him to go buy on credit but to give cash and he buys what he wants.
(ו) Also in this — according to this view permitted also to tell the shopkeeper: give workers chametz and I will pay, for the same reason. Regarding a child who must eat and drink chametz — he should bring him to a non-Jew's house and ask the non-Jew to feed chametz to the child [but beware they not take it in hand, for he acquires it]; and if the non-Jew does not want to give free, he may promise to pay afterwards, for permitted by essential law as above; but he should not give money beforehand or when giving to eat, for he buys chametz thereby and forbidden to all; and if impossible to carry out and the non-Jew must bring chametz to the Jew's house and feed there, he may tell the non-Jew to gather leftover chametz and take to his house, and when needing to eat again return and bring and feed; and if no non-Jew each time, there is room to be lenient that the non-Jew leave chametz in the house for several times and explicitly say he does not want to acquire the chametz [otherwise he acquires for him], as above siman 448; and command a minor to feed the child — but he himself should not feed: forbidden l'chatchila to touch, as siman 446; and further because of acquisition concern. If he can hide chametz where sold chametz is kept, hide there and bring the child to eat; if not, make a ten-[tefach] partition before chametz, or at least cover with a vessel; all this if the child is not endangered — if danger, no need to be exact to hurry feeding, as siman 328.
(ז) Unless he gave dinar beforehand — to the shopkeeper and commanded him to give sustenance to his worker when he comes — then it is like feeding chametz by hand, for the shopkeeper is the master's agent; with this forbidden even if he does not know certainly he will buy chametz; and see Yoreh De'ah siman 332 seif 4 who wrote: even giving dinar beforehand is not forbidden unless he said this dinar is in your hand until you give my worker; but if he permitted him to spend now, permitted — for when he gives the worker there is no dinar acquiring for the master. Many Acharonim disputed and agreed forbidden in all cases. Know: giving dinar beforehand is not precise — likewise if he gave dinar when the shopkeeper gave chametz to his worker, also forbidden; and this comes only to exclude if he paid afterwards.
(ח) Or he lifted and gave by hand — meaning the master himself took chametz from the non-Jew shopkeeper for his worker and gave him; with this, even if he gave no money at all, nevertheless forbidden, for he acquired chametz by pulling from the non-Jew.
(ט) And forbidden to buy etc. — there is concern lest he eat from it; and further he wants the chametz's continued existence; and further there is no agency for a non-Jew and the Jew ends up buying them and transgresses bal yera'eh; and even if he does not pull chametz from the seller's domain but only gives money on Pesach, also forbidden — for some say a Jew acquires movable property from a non-Jew with money alone.
(י) Even with his money etc. — nevertheless if he did not intend to buy for himself and also took no liability, there is room to be lenient for eating and benefit after Pesach, as Peri Megadim wrote; and see Sha'arei Teshuvah.
(כ) Of the non-Jew — likewise forbidden to tell a non-Jew on chol hamoed Pesach to buy chametz for him, even if he does not draw chametz into the Jew's house — some poskim hold there is agency for a non-Jew stringently; b'dieved if he bought and did not draw to his domain, no prohibition after Pesach [from Chiddushei R' Akiva Eiger, end of his words]. Peri Megadim wrote regarding a non-Jew buying grain from a non-Jew with some souring — permitted for a Jew to be broker between them, for he does not pay for the sour part. If the non-Jew's animal was laden with chametz, permitted to unload where there is pain for the animal on a Torah level — even though the non-Jew holds a favor for this [and this is generally wanting continued existence of chametz so he holds a favor] — since the Jew does not intend he hold a favor, permitted.
