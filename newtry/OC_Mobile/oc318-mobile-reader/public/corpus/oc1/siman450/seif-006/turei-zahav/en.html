Forbidden to rent etc. Tur's reason: wanting continued existence of chametz so the vessel not crack; and further since the vessel itself is forbidden in benefit, forbidden to rent l'chatchila. Do not say what the Tosefta teaches: one may rent one's donkey to a non-Jew for chametz — there the donkey is not forbidden but here the vessel is forbidden; this seems Rashba's view nearby, but Rashi's view we mentioned — for partnership oven permitted to rent when taking money — we could permit here too, but Tur's conclusion seems like Rashba. After the rules of this siman, proper to examine what is practiced in Russia and Volhynia: Jews rent an estate from the lord with cauldrons and mills; they take a measure of maltz from everyone who grinds maltz; maltz is full chametz punishable by karet — what to do on the week of Pesach. And a question: he wrote one should do through sale or giving to a non-Jew as siman 441; in my humble opinion there is doubt: one, sale does not apply to something not yet in existence, as Bava Metzia chapter 1 — "what my trap will bring, sell to you" — he said nothing; two, they permitted sale only when eating is permitted — "for me sale is like eating," as Mishnah: every hour eating is permitted, selling is permitted; so for chametz coming when eating forbidden, no proof sale is permitted — sale applies only until chametz exists, then eating forbidden. Further difficulty: do we have only one Pesach a year — we have more than fifty Pesachs, all Shabbats as Hillel the Elder said in chapter Elu Devarim regarding Pesach slaughter on Shabbat; we have more than two hundred Pesachs when Shabbat is overridden. We too say so here — how permit taking Shabbat wage on Shabbat, what the mill grinds on Shabbat — a measure for it — Shabbat wage clearly forbidden as explained in many places; we leave no permission for estate-holders and never heard Sages objected. Permission in my humble opinion like Yoreh De'ah siman 317 regarding raising lord's pigs — all belongs to the lord but the Jew takes from him; similar to buying priests' allotment at start of this siman — for views permitting even without stipulation, permitted to take the lord's allotment though not stipulated with anyone; and as we hold there stipulation required, here too for weekly interest mentioned above. For mills there is no fixed amount how much they grind; initially when buying he enters doubt whether they grind — then he takes each grinding automatically; each measure from the grinder is a new acquisition, as buying then from the lord — initial acquisition did not take effect until purchase existed, unclear at acquisition time; properly called non-Jew's chametz over which Passover passed when the Jew takes after Pesach; likewise after each Shabbat as intended initially — no fine applies, for R' Shimon fined only for bal yera'eh not applicable to the Jew on Pesach and Shabbat days. On Pesach itself the Jew acquired chametz after it came to existence — the Jew cannot say he is not pleased to acquire then, unlike siman 448; and though we wrote seif 4 in Rashba regarding oven praise — do not apply siman 448 there, different — oven is his and he willingly gives to bake that day, certainly pleased; not so mills belonging to the whole city, estate-holder cannot protest the non-Jew grinding that day — properly he can say he is not pleased to acquire at prohibition time since he can come after Pesach; certainly times mills are free from maltz grinding and whoever needs grinds also after Pesach — all applies to the lord's mills and Jew renting from him; but a Jew with his own mills certainly cannot give to grind on Shabbats and Pesach — like Rashba we mentioned: since he can protest and do as he wishes, no remedy except sell to a non-Jew for the year or half-year with Shabbat and Pesach absorbed between; unlike lord's mills — even though Jew already paid, sale did not take effect until it comes, since doubt at sale time what will come (in my humble opinion) to learn full permission; one who wants to be stringent should stipulate on Pesach that whoever wants to grind maltz gives a measure from other grain without chametz — then if maltz given after Pesach it is substitute for other grain, permitted as above.
