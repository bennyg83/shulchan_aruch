It is forbidden to rent, etc. The reason in the Tur is that he wants the chametz to continue existing so that the utensil not crack. Furthermore, since the utensil itself is forbidden in benefit, it is forbidden to rent it ab initio. This is not comparable to what is taught in the Tosefta, that a person may rent his donkey to a non-Jew for chametz, because there the donkey is not forbidden, whereas here the utensil itself is forbidden. It seems that this follows the view of the Rashba nearby. But according to Rashi's view that we mentioned, that with a partnership oven it is permitted to rent it when he takes money, we should permit this as well; but it seems that the Tur's conclusion follows the Rashba. After the principles stated in this siman, it is fitting to examine the practice in Russia and Volhynia, where Jews rent orendas from the authorities and take measures of malt from everyone who grinds malt, and the malt is complete chametz, punishable by kareis. What should be done during the week of Pesach? My father-in-law of blessed memory wrote about this that it should be done by way of sale or gift to a non-Jew, as written in siman 441. In my humble opinion there is a difficulty with this. First, a sale does not apply to something that has not yet come into existence, as stated in the first chapter of Bava Metzia: if one says, 'what my trap will catch is sold to you,' he has said nothing. Furthermore, they permitted sale only at a time when it is permitted to eat, because sale is like eating in this respect, as the Mishnah says: whenever it is permitted to eat, it is permitted to sell. This refers to chametz that he has at the time of the sale. If so, with chametz that will come to him at a time when it is forbidden to eat, you have no proof that it is permitted to sell, since the sale does not take effect until the chametz exists, and then it is forbidden to eat. There is another difficulty: do we have only one Pesach in the year? There are more than fifty Pesachs in the year, namely all the Shabbosos, as Hillel the Elder said at the beginning of Perek Eilu Devarim regarding slaughtering the Pesach offering on Shabbos; and we have more than two hundred Pesachs that override Shabbos. We too should say so in this matter: how is it permitted to take Shabbos wages, meaning that what the mills grind on Shabbos, he takes a measure for it? Shabbos wages are obviously forbidden, as explained in several places. If so, you have left no allowance for all holders of orendas, and we have never heard that the Sages protested against them. In my humble opinion there is an allowance similar to what we mentioned in Yoreh Deah, siman 117, regarding raising pigs in orendas. So too here: everything belongs to the ruler, except that the Jew takes from him. This is similar to what was written regarding one who buys the priests' allotment, which we mentioned at the beginning of this siman; according to those views that hold it is permitted even without stipulating, here too it is permitted to take the allotment due to the authority, even though he stipulated with no person. And according to what we hold there, that a condition is required, it seems that they would agree here that it is permitted. There, with the priests, it is a known and fixed thing, that they give them one loaf each week; it follows that when he bought from them the allotment for the whole year, it was as though he bought from them fifty loaves, including that of Pesach. Likewise with the weekly interest mentioned above, a condition is therefore required. But with the mill there is no fixed amount for how much they will grind. From the beginning, when he bought it, he entered into uncertainty whether they would grind, for only then would he take automatically each time they grind and a measure is due from the grinder. It is a new acquisition, as though he buys it at that time from the authority, for the acquisition at the beginning did not take effect unless the purchase came into existence, since it was not clear at the time of acquisition. Therefore it is properly called a non-Jew's chametz over which Pesach passed when the Jew takes it after Pesach, and likewise after each Shabbos, for this was the intent from the beginning. The fine that Rabbi Shimon imposes because one transgressed bal yera'eh does not apply here, because it did not belong to the Jew during Pesach or during the Shabbosos. Do not say that on Pesach itself the Jew acquired the chametz after it came into existence. This is not so, for the Jew can say, 'I do not want to acquire it at that time,' as above in siman 448. Although we wrote in seif 4, in the case of the Rashba regarding the profit of the oven, that this is not comparable to siman 448, there it is different: the oven is his, and he willingly gives it to bake in on that day, so he certainly wants it. This is unlike a mill that belongs to the whole town, where the holder of the orenda cannot protest that the non-Jew not grind on that day. Therefore he can properly say that he does not want to acquire it at the time of prohibition, since it is possible for it to come after Pesach, for there are certainly times when the mill is free from grinding malt, and anyone who has grain to grind can grind even after Pesach. All this applies to the ruler's mill that the Jew rents from him. But a Jew who has a mill fully his own certainly has no basis to allow grinding in it on Shabbosos and Pesach; it is comparable to the case of the Rashba that we mentioned, since he can protest and do as he wants. He has no remedy except to sell it to a non-Jew for the entire year or half-year, so that Shabbos and Pesach are included by havla'ah among them. This is unlike the ruler's mill: although the Jew already gave him the money, nevertheless the sale does not take effect until it comes, since at the time of the sale there was uncertainty what would come afterward. So it seems to me, to derive a full allowance in this. However, one who wants to be stringent should stipulate on Pesach that anyone who wants to grind malt should give him a measure from other grain that contains no chametz; then, if he gives him malt after Pesach, it is an exchange for other grain and is permitted, as above.
