That he acquire chametz. If he is hired by him for the year, he should not eat in his house because of suspicion [Agudah].
Go and eat. This is specifically where he said to him, 'Go and eat chametz,' or where he knows with certainty that he will eat chametz.
Unless he gave beforehand, etc. This is the primary ruling, and specifically where the dinar is still intact in the shopkeeper's hand at the time when he eats, as written in Yoreh Deah, siman 132, seif 4. If he did not give him a dinar beforehand, it is permitted to say, 'Give the workers chametz and I will pay.' If so, one who has a child who needs to drink chametz may say to a non-Jew, 'Give him chametz to drink and I will pay'; see siman 343. This is not considered benefit from the fact that his son drinks chametz, as written in Yoreh Deah, siman 221, seif 4; see there. Furthermore, the workers' food is also his responsibility, and nevertheless it is permitted, as the Mordechai wrote; see there. But he himself should not take the chametz in his hand; see siman 616, seif 1 in the gloss, and as written in siman 446, seif 3. For a dangerously ill person, everything is permitted.
And it is forbidden to buy. For there is no agency for a non-Jew, and it turns out that the Jew acquires it. Also, there is concern lest he eat from it [Rivash; Beis Yosef]. It further seems to me that it is forbidden because he wants its continued existence; see Yoreh Deah, siman 133, seif 4, gloss 6. Tosafos likewise wrote in Pesachim 102b that the fact that the non-Jew will feel gratitude toward him is considered like wages, unless it is not in the non-Jew's presence; see there. It is implied from their words that if a non-Jew's animal was loaded with chametz, it is forbidden to unload it except outside the non-Jew's presence. But according to the first answer, it is permitted, since animal suffering is a Torah concern, and he does not intend that the non-Jew feel gratitude to him; it is a case that cannot be otherwise and he does not intend it, which is permitted. So it also appears in Choshen Mishpat, siman 272, in the Tur; see there.
