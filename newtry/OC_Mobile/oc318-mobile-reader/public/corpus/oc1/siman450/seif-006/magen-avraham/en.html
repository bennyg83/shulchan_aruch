(א) That he acquire chametz. And if he is hired by him for a year he should not eat in his house because of suspicion (Agudah).

(ב) Go and eat. And specifically if he told him go and eat chametz, or he knows certainly he will eat chametz.

(ג) Unless he gave beforehand etc. And so is the main ruling; and specifically when the dinar is still in existence in the shopkeeper's hand when he eats, as Yoreh De'ah siman 332 seif 4; and if he did not give dinar beforehand, permitted to say: give the workers chametz and I will pay. If so, one who has a child who needs to drink chametz may tell a non-Jew: give him to drink chametz and I will pay (and see siman 443); and this is not benefit in what we built that he drinks chametz, as Yoreh De'ah siman 221 seif 4 (end of his words); and further workers' sustenance is on him and nevertheless permitted as Mordechai wrote (end of his words); but he should not take chametz in his hand (and siman 326 seif 1 in the gloss); as siman 446 seif 3; and for a sick person in danger everything is permitted.

(ד) And forbidden to buy. For there is no agency for a non-Jew and the Jew ends up buying them; and further there is concern lest he eat from it (Rivash, Beit Yosef); and further it appears forbidden because he wants its continued existence, as Yoreh De'ah siman 332 seif 4, end of his words; and so Tosafot in Pesachim 102b — what the non-Jew holds as a favor is considered wage unless it is not in the non-Jew's presence (end of his words); and from their words it implies: if the non-Jew's animal was laden with chametz it is forbidden to unload except not in the non-Jew's presence; but for the first version it is permitted since there is pain for the animal on a Torah level and he does not intend the non-Jew hold a favor for him — it is not possible and not intended, and permitted; and so Choshen Mishpat siman 272 in Tur (end of his words).
