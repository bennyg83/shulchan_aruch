Seif 61, that he is, etc., even, etc. There R' Eliezer said, since, etc., and the Rosh explained: anyone obligated to mourn — not like Rabbeinu Tam.
And even if, etc. Yerushalmi there teaches: if he wants to be stringent upon himself, they do not listen to him — either because of honor for the deceased or because he has no one to carry his burden. What emerges from between them? If he had someone to carry his burden, it is not surprising that because of honor for the deceased it is forbidden; but if you say because he has no one to carry his burden — behold, he has someone to carry his burden. And it taught: exempt from taking the lulav — resolve it on a weekday. And it taught: exempt from blowing the shofar — you can say on a weekday but not on Yom Tov. R' Chiya said: since he needs him to bring him a coffin and shrouds, as we taught there, they travel beyond the techum to inspect, etc. — he is considered like one carrying his burden. And in Yoreh De'ah siman 341 it explains as the reason of honor for the deceased. But Hagahos Maimoniyos and Mordechai wrote in the name of Ravyah: since it was not resolved and it is a rabbinic doubt, they do not object to him; and so too the Beit Yosef here. And this is what he said, and if there is, etc.
