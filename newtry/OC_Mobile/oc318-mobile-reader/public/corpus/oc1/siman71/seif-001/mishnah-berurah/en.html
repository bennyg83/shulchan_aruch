(1) That he is obligated — they are seven relatives: father, mother, son, daughter, brother, sister, and his wife.

(2) It is not laid upon him — such as if his married sister died, for the husband is obligated to attend to the burial.

(3) He is exempt, etc. — and if he read [Shema] he has not fulfilled his obligation and must return and read after the burial. [Chiddushei Ra'ah].

(4) And he prays — and likewise from all blessings, even blessings over benefit, and from all mitzvot stated in the Torah. And if he wishes to eat bread, even though he need not recite HaMotzi, nevertheless he must wash his hands and not recite a blessing over the washing [Birkhei Yosef, Yoreh De'ah 341]. And if there is a society in the city whose custom is that designated bearers take out the deceased, and after the relatives have attended to the needs of the deceased they hand him over to them and they bury him — from the moment they hand him over he is immediately obligated in all mitzvot, even before they remove him from the house, since it is no longer laid upon them. Therefore onenim do ill to wait to pray until after burial, for as soon as they have settled with the chevra kadisha regarding the burial place and given them all expenses and shrouds, they are obligated to pray. However, sometimes they have not yet agreed where he will be buried, and it is for the honor of the deceased, or such as when they eulogize him — then he is exempt; and immediately thereafter it is permitted to pray and he is obligated. But in a place where there is no society designated for this, even if he has someone to attend to the burial for him, onenut continues until he is buried and they throw earth upon him; and as soon as they begin to throw earth upon him the mourner goes to the house at the cemetery and reads [Shema] and prays there. And if onenut continues until near the end of the time for Shema, which is until a quarter of the day per the proportional hours of the day — and if he prays in order he will pass the time for Shema — he reads Shema without blessings and afterward prays in order and reads Shema again with blessings. And if onenut continued until after four hours, the Magen Avraham holds that the morning blessings — that is, what precedes Baruch She'amar — he does not say even if he has leisure, since at the time of the main obligation, which is in the morning, he was then exempt; and some dispute this, and regarding doubtful blessings we are lenient, except for the blessing on the Torah and the blessings that He did not make me a gentile, a slave, or a woman — those blessings even per the Magen Avraham he may recite [and he does not don tefillin — see above in siman 38, seif 5]. And he prays the eighteen blessings alone; but the blessing of Shema seems from the Shulchan Aruch above in siman 58, seif 6, that their time has already passed. And according to what I wrote above at the end of siman 58 in Mishna Berurah, he is permitted to say even the blessing of Shema, since through coercion the time passed; and according to all opinions it is possible he may say the verses of song — see above in siman 52, Chiddushei Ra'ah. And if the four hours have not yet ended, which is a third of the day, only that he is near that time — and if he prays in order he will pass the time for prayer and need to pray Shacharit after a third of the day — he skips the verses of song, as explained above in siman 52 regarding one who came late to the synagogue; but he does not skip Shema and its blessings at all, even though thereby he will be forced to pray Shacharit after a third of the day [Darkei Moshe Chayyim — see there his reason; and one should examine in light of what was written at the beginning]. The Darkei Moshe Chayyim further wrote that those blessings — that He did not make me a gentile, slave, or woman, and the blessing on the Torah — their time is all day, and even after midday if he was buried he says them; but the prayer of eighteen [blessings] he does not pray except until midday and no more. Also he need not complete that prayer in the adjacent prayer — such as if he did not pray Shacharit he does not complete it in Minchah, for it is not comparable to one who forgot or was compelled and did not pray Shacharit, whom we complete in Minchah, for there he was truly obligated only that through a circumstance he did not pray; not so here, where he was exempt by law. And the same applies to Minchah and Maariv. And this is only if his dead was laid before him from the time the obligation for prayer began until the completion of its time — such as his dead was laid before him from dawn and the entire eastern horizon brightened until after midday. But if he died after the obligation had already begun upon him — such as after the entire eastern horizon brightened — even if they did not bury him until after midday, he completes it at the time of Minchah, since the obligation fell upon him from the beginning of the time when the time for prayer arrived, even if he had no leisure to finish the prayer; nevertheless, since the obligation fell upon him, he is obligated afterward to complete the prayer at the time of Minchah. And it is possible that in this he is also obligated to recite the morning blessing if he did not bless it in the morning when he rose from his bed, for at the time of their obligation he was obligated — see above in siman 52 in Biur Halacha.

(5) To be stringent — meaning, and it deals with one who has no one to attend to the needs of the deceased; alternatively, that it deals in every case where they forbade him for the honor of the deceased, so they should not say this deceased is trivial in the eyes of the onen and he is not anguished over his death. And this is the view of the author himself from the fundamental law; and what he wrote afterward, "we do not protest" — meaning, nevertheless we cannot protest against one who practices the opposite, for he has whom to rely upon where he has someone who will strive on his behalf in burial needs.

(6) On his behalf — meaning, that even if it is laid upon him to bury him, only that he has other people who will attend on his behalf, and he wishes to be stringent upon himself — we do not protest against him. This is the view of Ravyah; and Rashal wrote in a responsum, siman 70, that nowadays they do not practice according to Ravyah.

(7) Siman 341 — for there he ruled that he is not permitted to be stringent in every case; and such is the essential [view of the later authorities]. Nevertheless, a son who is an onen over his father and there are attendants aside from him — it is permitted for him to go to the synagogue to say Kaddish for him, for this is the honor of his father; and it is permitted according to all opinions, and all the more so on Shabbat and Yom Tov. And specifically when there are no other mourners; but when there are other mourners, no [Peri Megadim and Darkei Moshe Chayyim].
