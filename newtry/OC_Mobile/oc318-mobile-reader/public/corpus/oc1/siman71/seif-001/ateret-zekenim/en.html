If the person upon whom the obligation to bury does not rest is not in the city, he may recite Krias Shema. Some say that the reverse is likewise true: when the person upon whom the obligation to bury does not rest is in the city, while the person upon whom the obligation does rest is in another city, the one who is in the other city may recite Krias Shema. If none of the relatives are in the city, they must all leave their city to attend to the burial arrangements, and they are exempt from Krias Shema and prayer. If he has someone who will attend to the burial arrangements on his behalf and he wishes to be stringent with himself and recite, one does not protest, as stated above. My teacher, the Bach, writes this in the name of Rabbeinu Tam; see Yoreh Deah, siman 341. The text must also be emended to read: 'Some say that one does not protest.' The principal view, however, is that because of the honor due to the deceased he should not do so, lest people attribute his conduct to stringency and say that the deceased is insignificant in his eyes, and that for this reason he is neither preoccupied nor distressed by the death. This would be a disgrace to the deceased. Therefore, even if he has someone who will attend to the burial arrangements on his behalf, he may not be stringent and recite. All the later authorities of our generation agreed to this, including Maharam Yafeh and my teacher, the Bach.
