One who had a relative die, etc. Therefore even nowadays, when everyone is not scrupulous about intent, he is likewise not permitted to be stringent upon himself and recite Kriat Shema because of honor for the deceased. If one upon whom it is not incumbent to bury the deceased is not in the city, he may recite Kriat Shema (and some say the reverse also applies: if one upon whom it is not incumbent to bury him is in the city and the one upon whom it is incumbent to bury him is in another city, the one who is in the other city may recite Kriat Shema). If all the relatives are not in the city, they all must leave their city to attend to burial needs and are exempt from Kriat Shema and prayer. If he has someone who will act on his behalf regarding burial needs and wants to be stringent upon himself and recite, they do not object to him as above (my teacher in the Bach in the name of Rabbeinu Tam; see Yoreh De'ah siman 341). It also needs emendation: and some say they do not object. The essence is that it is proper because of honor for the deceased, so that people should not attribute his reason to stringency he imposed on himself, but rather say that this deceased was trivial in his eyes — therefore he is not troubled and not alarmed at his death — and this is a disgrace to the deceased. Therefore even if he has someone who will act on his behalf regarding burial needs, he is not permitted to be stringent and recite. To this all the later authorities in our generation agreed, including the Maharar"m"i and my teacher in the Bach.
