Where the time for Minchah had already arrived before his relative died, and he occupied himself with the burial until after the time for Maariv arrived, responsum Beis Yaakov, siman 42, concludes that he cannot pray Maariv twice. The obligation of Minchah does not fully take effect until the final moment of the day, since prior to that moment the obligation is not so immediate, as he can still fulfill it by praying later. See there. Rabbi Moshe Aharon and Rabbi Chaim Assael, however, maintain that he is obligated, and they bring many proofs from the poskim and from the Gemara's case of one who was lame on the first day and recovered on the second day. See there.
We do not prevent him. See responsum Beis Yaakov, siman 41. Where he prays because of an error, believing that he is obligated, others are required to prevent him. A proof is brought from the chapter HaPoalim, page 91: thieves stole rams from Rabbah through a tunnel and later returned them, but he did not accept them because they had acquired them through the change in value. Tosafos asks that they were nevertheless obligated to pay in order to fulfill their duty toward Heaven. Tosafos answers that they believed they were legally obligated, and had they known that they were exempt under the law, they would not have returned them.
