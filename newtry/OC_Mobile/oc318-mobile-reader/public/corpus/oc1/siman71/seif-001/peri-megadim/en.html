Even so — the Taz brought the words of the Rosh, that even in another city he is exempt and not permitted; and see Yoreh De'ah 341; and in the Shach, note 5, not like the Bach who ruled that in another city he eats meat and drinks wine when her husband is in the city — see there. Behold, the Rosh and Rambam in chapter 4 of Keriyas Shema disagree: per the Rosh, meat and wine are forbidden, for the Sages did not distinguish; and per the Rambam the reason is that his heart is not free until he buries him. And this regarding Shema, which requires intent, not so meat and wine — even now, in any case, one cannot have intent. And the Taz wrote that this depends on the reasons written in the Yerushalmi in note 2: it is explained that for the reason of one who carries his burden — such as Rabbeinu Tam — meat and wine are permitted and there is obligation in mitzvot; and for the reason of the honor of the deceased — that they should not say this deceased is trivial in the eyes of the onen and he did not anguish over his death and is not distraught, as the Levush wrote — therefore even in another city, when her husband is in the city, meat and wine are forbidden and he does not read Shema (see in Hagahot Levush — it is not from the words of the Levush, and it is per the Rosh's explanation: honor of the deceased, that he should be free to think about burial needs; and therefore in another city he needed to say they did not distinguish).
