One who had a relative die, etc., is exempt from Kriat Shema and from prayer — and likewise from all mitzvos, and specifically positive mitzvos; but negative mitzvos he is obligated in, even in something that is rabbinic in general [Birkhei Yosef]. The Darkhei Chaim wrote: if the deceased was buried after he ate and the time for the food to be digested has not yet passed, he must bless Birkas HaMazon; and likewise Asher Yatzar — if he relieved himself while he was still an onen, he must bless Asher Yatzar after the deceased was buried, even the entire day there. His reason: unless he still intends to relieve himself again, he need not now bless, but rather one blessing covers both, as above in siman 77 in Mishna Berurah — see there. He also wrote there: a place where they convey the deceased from city to city — if the place is near, he is an onen and exempt from Kriat Shema and prayer; but if the place is far, such as a two-day journey, he is not an onen until they arrive at the city of burial — see there.
