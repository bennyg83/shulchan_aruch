Seif 1 — one whose relative died, etc. N.B.: By law if he was an onen until after chatzot it is proven from all decisors that after chatzot is not prayer time and prayer applies only as makeup — since at his obligatory time he was exempt, makeup does not apply. A new book Mishkanot Yaakov proposed division from Pesach Sheni — those impure from corpse were occupied in mitzvah yet obligated in second — proving onen after chatzot owes makeup. I wonder how he disputed Rosh; the proof fails: views differ on bearers of Joseph vs Mishael and Elzaphan; they could purify but did not — inadvertent is exempt; Talmud Sukkah 20 proves only R' Yehudah's view. Rosh deals with occupation at time of obligation exempting truly — not comparable to Pesach impurity at wrong time. Rosh stands: occupation in mitzvah like onen or communal needs is true exemption without makeup. Mishkanot Yaakov's prayer proof from synagogue exempt from mezuzah is flawed — prayer exists in Torah regardless. Gaon of Lissa on onen completing Minchah after daybreak — no proof; lame person differs as bodily coercion. Taz/Magen Avraham distinction on communal needs is sound. Rosh's lame-person proof exempts onen; does not obligate. Festival is one extended day; prayer times are separate — no makeup after exempt period. Burial on second day and tefillin — Jerusalem Talmud Berakhot 3 proves counting from burial. Responsa on onen discharging others — Yoreh De'ah 341. Onen ate before burial — see Lissa siddur. Mourner exempt even without burial obligation — Yoreh De'ah 341; Taz on rabbinic doubt lekhatchilah. Mitzvah permits rabbinic doubt lekhatchilah — Orach Chaim 426. Distinguish obligation before death vs after. Zikhron Yitzchak on Yom Tov before daybreak. Novel law: onen may intend to fulfill shofar/Megillah by hearing without action — no dishonor to dead.
