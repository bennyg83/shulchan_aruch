(א) Exempt, etc. And it seems that before chatzos, etc. — it goes according to his reasoning later in siman 89 note 5, who disagrees with the Bach and Taz who hold that even half an hour after chatzos is the time of the morning tefillah. But Birchos HaShachar must be the blessings explained above in siman 46.

(ב) As written in Yoreh De'ah, etc., regarding Havdalah. Therefore the explanation is that this refers to what is written above that one prays until chatzos. Or something is missing in the Magen Avraham. For there in Yoreh De'ah it is explained — one whose relative died on Shabbat eats at night without Havdalah, for an onen is exempt from Havdalah; and after burial he makes Havdalah until the end of the third day on Shabbat. But the Maariv tefillah he does not pray as compensation, as we rule — if one erred and did not pray Maariv he prays Shacharis twice, as below siman 108 — because at Maariv, in what is written, then was the time of his obligation he was exempt and therefore he need not compensate. And this is specifically the Maariv tefillah whose time is specifically at night; in the day it comes only as compensation. But Havdalah — its beginning time the Sages instituted until the end of the third day, and it is not as compensation; therefore even though at night he was exempt, nevertheless he must make Havdalah by day. And if so, the Shacharis tefillah has the law of Havdalah upon it, for its beginning time is until chatzos; but Birchos HaShachar the Magen Avraham holds has the law of the Maariv tefillah there — that one does not complete, since at the time of his obligation he was exempt; for it was an enactment of the Sages that they fixed for each of the blessings a fixed time, as stated in the Gemara. And likewise in the Tur above siman 46 — when he awakens from his sleep he says Elokei Neshamah; when he hears the voice of the rooster he says "who gives understanding to the rooster," etc.; and so all of them. Only that nowadays because hands are not clean they instituted to arrange them in the synagogue, as above siman 46. Or it can be said well — since at the time of his obligation he was exempt, etc.; but nevertheless I did not know why proof is needed from Havdalah that one prays Shacharis until chatzos — behold it is explained explicitly there in Yoreh De'ah. And perhaps the words "regarding Havdalah" are a scribal error, and it should read "regarding the Maariv of what is written," and he does not bring proof except that he is exempt from Birchos HaShachar. However, the blessing of the Torah — in my humble opinion he is certainly obligated to bless, for it is similar to Havdalah. Also if his relative died in the morning after he rose from his bed before he blessed Birchos HaShachar — he is also obligated to bless Birchos HaShachar after burial, for at the time of his obligation he was obligated. In this too I am uncertain and ask — even if his relative died at night and tomorrow after burial, if he is not obligated to bless the three blessings "who did not make me a gentile," slave, woman — mentioned in tractate Menachos p. 42, and in Shas no fixed time was established for them; although they are blessed in the morning from the side of the mitzvah of alacrity, their time is all day, and it is similar to Havdalah or the morning tefillah until chatzos. And per the kabbalists whom the Magen Avraham brought siman 46 note 14 — that the blessings were instituted so that at night his soul should not cling to the soul of gentile, slave, woman — it can be said well that their primary time is in the morning, which is not so for the Shelah and Bach brought there that they were instituted for the beginning of creation — I do not know if their primary time is in the morning. Or it can be said — since in the morning a new creature was made, as above at the beginning of siman 4 — if so one should bless on the beginning of creation in the morning; if so their primary obligation is in the morning.
