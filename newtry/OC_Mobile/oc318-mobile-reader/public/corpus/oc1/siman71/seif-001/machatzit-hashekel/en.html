Seif katan 1: Is exempt, etc. His statement, 'It appears to me that before midday, etc.,' follows his own view below in siman 89, seif katan 5, where he disagrees with the Bach and Taz, who maintain that even the half hour after midday remains a time for the Shacharis prayer. However, regarding Bircas HaShachar, he should recite the blessings explained above in siman 46.
As written in Yoreh Deah, etc. Regarding Havdalah. He must mean that this refers to what he wrote above, that one may pray until midday; alternatively, something is missing from the Magen Avraham. In Yoreh Deah it is explained that one whose relative died on Shabbos should eat that night without Havdalah, because an onen is exempt from Havdalah. After the burial, he should recite Havdalah until the end of Tuesday. He should not, however, pray Maariv as a compensatory prayer. Although we rule that one who erred and did not pray Maariv prays Shacharis twice, as below in siman 108, that is because during Maariv on Motzaei Shabbos, when the obligation took effect, he was exempt, and therefore he need not compensate for it. This applies specifically to Maariv, whose prescribed time is only at night, while during the day it can be recited only as compensation. Havdalah, however, was initially instituted by the Sages through the end of Tuesday and is not compensatory. Therefore, although he was exempt at night, he must nevertheless recite Havdalah during the day. Accordingly, Shacharis resembles Havdalah, since its initial prescribed time extends until midday. Bircas HaShachar, however, in the Magen Avraham's view, resembles Maariv there and is not made up, because he was exempt when its obligation took effect. The Sages assigned each blessing a fixed time, as stated in the Gemara and likewise in the Tur above, siman 46: when one awakens from sleep he should say Elokai Neshamah; when he hears the rooster he should say, 'Who gives the rooster understanding,' and similarly for all the blessings. Only now, because hands are not clean, they instituted arranging them in the synagogue, as above in siman 46. Alternatively, it can indeed be said that since he was exempt when the obligation took effect, etc. Nevertheless, I do not know why proof from Havdalah is needed to show that he may pray Shacharis until midday, since this is stated explicitly there in Yoreh Deah. Perhaps the words 'regarding Havdalah' are a scribal error and should read, 'regarding Maariv of Motzaei Shabbos.' He would then be bringing proof only that he is exempt from Bircas HaShachar. In my humble opinion, however, he is certainly obligated to recite Bircas HaTorah, because it resembles Havdalah. Likewise, if his relative died in the morning after he arose but before he recited Bircas HaShachar, he must recite Bircas HaShachar after the burial, since he was obligated when their time arrived. I am also uncertain and ask whether, even if the relative died at night, he is not obligated after the burial the next day to recite the three blessings, 'Who has not made me a non-Jew,' 'a slave,' or 'a woman.' These are mentioned in Menachos 42, and the Gemara assigns them no fixed time. Although they are recited in the morning because of the mitzvah of acting promptly, their time extends throughout the day, and they therefore resemble Havdalah or Shacharis until midday. According to the kabbalists cited by the Magen Avraham in siman 46, seif katan 14, who explain that these blessings were instituted because the soul of a non-Jew, slave, or woman did not attach itself to his soul during the night, one may indeed say that their principal time is in the morning. According to the Shelah and Bach cited there, however, who explain that they were instituted for the beginning of creation, I do not know whether their principal time is specifically in the morning. Alternatively, since in the morning he becomes a new creation, as above at the beginning of siman 4, he must bless Hashem for the beginning of his creation in the morning, and their principal obligation is therefore then.
