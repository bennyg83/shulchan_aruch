To bury him. Such as if his sister died and she has a husband, for the husband is obligated to attend to her burial — as opposed to Rabbeinu Tam, whose sister died — his married sister, mother of R' Yitzchak — and he ate meat and drank wine since she had a husband. And the Rosh disagrees with him. See Taz.

And from prayer. Immediately when they begin to throw earth upon the deceased he recites Kriat Shema and prays, and so did the Maharil. The Magen Avraham wrote, and it seems to me that before midnight he prays Shacharit as stated in siman 89, but he does not say Birchas HaShachar since at the time of its obligation he was exempt, as stated in Yoreh De'ah siman 341 seif 2 regarding havdalah.

To be stringent. Meaning it discusses when he has no one to engage in the needs of the deceased.

On his behalf. Meaning that even if it is incumbent upon him to bury the deceased, only that he has other people who will engage on his behalf, and he wants to be stringent upon himself — they do not object to him; this is Ravyah's view. Rashal wrote in responsum siman 70 that nowadays they do not practice according to Ravyah. The Taz wrote: nevertheless it seems to me that a son who is an onen for his father and there are those engaging in burial — he should go to the synagogue and say kaddish because this is honor for his father, and it is permitted for everyone there. See Yoreh De'ah siman 376 where the Taz wrote there that on Shabbos he says kaddish before the burial, but not on a weekday there. And it must be said that there it discusses when he has no one engaging. See further there what he wrote in siman 341 seif 2.

341. For there he ruled that he is not permitted to be stringent, and such is the essence. Taz and Bach in siman 98: (and so wrote in Eliyah Rabbah that he saw such in practice from his grandfather the gaon, may his righteous memory be blessed). And also the Beit Yosef's view is so that he is not permitted to be stringent, only that the court does not object to his hand there.
