To bury him. For example, his sister died and she has a husband, since the husband is obligated to attend to her burial. This excludes the case of Rabbeinu Tam, whose married sister, the mother of the Ri, died, and he ate meat and drank wine because she had a husband. The Rosh disagrees with him. See Taz.
And from prayer. As soon as they begin casting earth upon the deceased, he should recite Krias Shema and pray; Mahari Weil acted accordingly. The Magen Avraham writes: It appears to me that, before midday, he should pray Shacharis, as stated in siman 89. He should not, however, recite Bircas HaShachar, since he was exempt when their obligation took effect, as stated in Yoreh Deah, siman 341, seif 2, regarding Havdalah.
To act stringently. Meaning, this refers to a case in which he has no one else to attend to the needs of the deceased.
On his behalf. Meaning, even where the obligation to bury the deceased rests upon him, if he has other people who will attend to it on his behalf and he wishes to act stringently upon himself, we do not prevent him. This is the view of the Raavyah. The Maharshal writes in responsum 70 that nowadays the practice does not follow the Raavyah. The Taz writes: Nevertheless, it appears to me that a son who is an onen for his father, where others are attending to the burial, should go to the synagogue and recite Kaddish, for this is an honor to his father and is permitted according to all opinions. See there. See Yoreh Deah, siman 376, where the Taz writes that on Shabbos he should recite Kaddish before the burial, but not on a weekday. See there. One must say that there he is discussing a case in which no others are attending to the burial. See also what he wrote there in siman 341, seif katan 2.
341. For there it is ruled that he is not permitted to act stringently, and this is the primary ruling. Taz, Bach, and Peri Chadash. (Eliyah Rabbah likewise writes that he saw this implemented as practical halachah by his grandfather, the Gaon, of blessed memory.) He further writes that this is also the view of the Beit Yosef, namely, that he is not permitted to act stringently, although beis din does not prevent him. See there.
