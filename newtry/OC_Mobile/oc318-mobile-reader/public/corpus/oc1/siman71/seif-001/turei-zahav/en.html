Even if it is not laid upon him, etc. — meaning, to exclude Rabbeinu Tam, whose married sister died — the mother of R' Yehuda — in another city; and Rabbeinu Tam ate meat and drank wine, since she had a husband who is laid upon him to bury her. And the Rosh disagreed with him that the Sages did not distinguish — see there. And it appears that this dispute depends on the reasons that Tosafos and the Rosh wrote in the beginning of Keriyas Shema: why is an onen exempt from Shema and prayer, in the name of the Yerushalmi mentioned nearby.

And even if he wishes to be stringent — in the Yerushalmi we say thus, because of the honor of the deceased, or because he has no one to carry his burden. And there is a practical difference if he has one to carry his burden: for the first reason it is forbidden even in this case. And the Beit Yosef [holds] that one who wishes to be stringent when he has one to carry his burden — to read is in his discretion, since it is a rabbinic doubt; and this is not the matter of that groom in the siman before this, where we ruled that they are not obligated to read — for there the matter depends on intent, not so here. And the Mordechai wrote in the name of Ravyah that on the second reason onenim rely, who go to the synagogue and pray in a place where there are attendants who guard the deceased — end quote. So I found in the Mordechai, corrected edition, and in the Beit Yosef he copied the words of Ravyah and wrote that they rely who go, etc.; and there is missing there the word "onenim," and so too in the Agudah. And Rashal wrote in a responsum, siman 70, that nowadays they do not practice according to Ravyah in this; nevertheless it appears to me that a son who is an onen over his father and there are attendants — he goes to the synagogue and says Kaddish, for this is the honor of his father; and it is permitted according to all opinions.

And see Yoreh De'ah, siman 341 — meaning, that there the Shulchan Aruch did not write thus, but rather he is not permitted to be stringent at all; and although in Yoreh De'ah there too the Rama wrote — see in Orach Chayyim, siman 71 — nevertheless the essential [view] appears as his words in Yoreh De'ah, that they are not lenient in a rabbinic doubt except after the fact, and not ab initio. But here, why is it said ab initio that he should make a prohibition against the reason of the honor of the deceased? And "return and do not do" is preferable.
