Necessarily, when one opinion is stated without qualification and the contrary opinion is explicitly recorded afterward, the halachah follows the first opinion; see what I wrote in the rules of issur veheter in Peri Chadash on Yoreh Deah. Regarding the statement that one should not actively create a case of Rabbinic doubt, there is a general rule that one should not intentionally create a double doubt or a Rabbinic doubt at the outset. See Magen Avraham, siman 467, seif katan 2. Nevertheless, one might say that Krias Shema is a positive Torah commandment, so the opposite rule should apply, namely, that in a Torah doubt one must be stringent. This, however, is incorrect, for the question concerns only whether he may choose to be stringent. Since he is exempt even when another person will bear his burden, there is no remaining Torah doubt. Still, one who examines subsection 1 will see that this matter requires further consideration. Know also that one should not distinguish between Krias Shema, where there is no concern, and blessings, where there is concern for taking Hashem's Name in vain, and for that reason say that Yoreh Deah rules stringently. The prohibition of taking Hashem's Name in vain may apply only to an unnecessary blessing, whereas when one is exempt there may be no such prohibition, just as women may recite blessings over time-bound positive mitzvos. We discussed this in our novellae at the beginning of the laws of Krias Shema.
