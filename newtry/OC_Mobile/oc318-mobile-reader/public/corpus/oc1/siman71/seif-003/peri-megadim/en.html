And see Yoreh De'ah — the Taz in Yoreh De'ah brought only one view that he is not permitted, and it implies even if there is one to carry his burden (for presumably we say the halacha follows the first view in an unspecified dispute if the opposite is clear — see in Hanhagot Orach Chayyim in Pri Chadash to Yoreh De'ah, see there). And in what he wrote that one should not make a rabbinic doubt with one's hands — and the general principle is siman 66, and a rabbinic doubt one should not make ab initio; and Magen Avraham, siman 467, letter 2, see there. However, one could say that Shema is a Torah mitzvah, and therefore we reverse a Torah doubt to be stringent; however, this is regarding a blessing specifically, regarding whether he wishes to be stringent it is required — behold, he is exempt even when there is one to carry his burden, so there is no longer a Torah doubt. Nevertheless, one who examines note 1 should examine this. And know that one should not distinguish between Shema, where there is no concern, and blessings, where there is concern of "you shall not take [God's name in vain]"; and therefore he was stringent there in Yoreh De'ah, for one could say that "you shall not take" applies only to a blessing that is required; but regarding exemption there is no "you shall not take," similar to a woman whose husband exempted her from blessings; and in our novellae at the beginning of what we wrote, we wrote from this.
