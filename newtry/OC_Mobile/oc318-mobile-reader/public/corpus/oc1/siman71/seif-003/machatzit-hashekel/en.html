(א) Obligated, etc. And the Bach ruled, etc. — that the Rabbeynu Yosef challenged the words of the Tur against each other — that here in Orach Chayyim he wrote for evening time, since he can wait until dark he is exempt, implying even if he does not wait; and in Yoreh De'ah siman 381 he wrote "if he waited" — implying if he does not wait he is obligated; and therefore the Bach explained and ruled so — that there it deals with other mitzvos that do not require kavanah; therefore if he does not wait, even though he is somewhat preoccupied since he can wait, nevertheless he is obligated. But here it deals regarding Kriat Shema which is recited with awe and requires extra kavanah; therefore the Tur holds that even if he does not wait until dark, since he can wait and is somewhat preoccupied — he is exempt.

(ב) And it requires investigation, for the Rabbeynu Yosef wrote, etc. — that the Rabbeynu Yosef wrote to resolve his difficulty mentioned, and therefore he added to challenge that regarding Kriat Shema it is not relevant at all to say exempt or obligated — in any case, etc., as the Magen Avraham wrote — only that we do not learn from here to all mitzvos that one is obligated in them every Shabbat and Yom Tov even at the hour he waits at the boundary; therefore he wrote obligated only until evening, etc.; and since he did not write so because of what is necessary for the laws of Kriat Shema he was not concerned to clarify his words and relied on what the Tur wrote in Yoreh De'ah which is his primary place, so far the Rabbeynu Yosef. And if so it can be said that the Rabbeynu Yosef here in Shulchan Aruch too has this intention; and so he wrote in responsum Shevut Yaakov part 1 siman 8, see there. But nevertheless on the Bach the Magen Avraham challenged — it is impossible to say as the Bach wrote that here it deals regarding Kriat Shema, for regarding Kriat Shema you do not find that they practice reciting by day; if so it deals with the Kriat Shema of Maariv whose time for us is while it is still day — nevertheless even if he does not wait until dark he is exempt.

(ג) And nevertheless in what is written "everyone," etc. Explanation: that it was not permitted on a weekday to recite while it is still day except because of the trouble of the congregation and they rely on Kriat Shema on one's bed, as explained below siman 236; and if so for an individual it was not permitted to recite while it is still day. And all the more in what is written that the congregation too recites at night — if so its time is after ninety [minutes] and the difficulty of the Magen Avraham returns on the Bach to its place. And for the practical law the Taz wrote that in such a case if he does not wait until dark he recites Kriat Shema while it is still day like on a weekday. And in responsum Shevut Yaakov vol. 1 siman 8 he brought that Moreh one ruled like the Taz and further permitted to pray Shemoneh Esreh on Shabbat after plag haMinchah, as written below siman 293 that in a case of coercion one may pray Shemoneh Esreh after plag haMinchah and also make Havdalah; and in the responsum mentioned he disagreed with Moreh — that even if we say he recites Kriat Shema, nevertheless certainly to pray and make Havdalah is not correct, for it is like hitching two animals to one wagon — since you obligate him in tefillah because it is Shabbat and aninus did not apply to him, how will he pray a weekday tefillah? For if it is a weekday, aninus applies to him and he is not permitted to pray, so far. And I in my humble opinion did not understand — for as long as he is not permitted to occupy himself with the needs of the deceased, aninus did not apply to him, and the reason is not because of the sanctity of Shabbat; and if so behold even if he prays a weekday tefillah and makes Havdalah, nevertheless melacha is forbidden until night as below siman 293; if so even though he prays a weekday tefillah, nevertheless aninus did not apply to him since he still cannot occupy himself with the needs of the deceased. Further the Taz wrote that even on a weekday if there is to a son someone who will occupy himself with the needs of his father and mother — he goes to the synagogue and says Kaddish, for this is the honor of his father and mother. And in Sefer Eliyah Rabbah he challenged the words of the Taz — his words against himself: in Yoreh De'ah the Taz wrote specifically on Shabbat he says Kaddish, implying but not on a weekday; and in my humble opinion it is not a difficulty, for there it deals with the present situation where he has no son who will occupy himself with their needs — who will be more concerned to occupy himself with their needs like the son; therefore he wrote specifically on Shabbat and Yom Tov. But regarding the essential law they disagreed with the Taz in Nekudas HaKesef and Avodas HaGershuni — for Kaddish was instituted to save him from Gehinnom, and before burial there is still no law of Gehinnom. And in the responsum Shevut Yaakov mentioned he agreed with the Taz — on the contrary it is fitting to precede the repair to the trouble, as written that one should precede prayer to trouble. However he concluded in the responsum mentioned that he did not see practiced so; but one who instructed a student to say Kaddish — we do not return the practice. See there further.
