[3] If he does not travel beyond the techum, etc. So wrote the Tur in Yoreh De'ah; see Beit Yosef who raises a difficulty between the Rosh and Tur — for in this siman he wrote "if he can travel beyond the techum," implying even if he does not travel, since he could travel, he is exempt. The Bach answers that in Yoreh De'ah siman 341 it discusses other mitzvos — he is not exempt except when he must travel beyond the techum; but here the Tur discusses Kriat Shema which requires great intent, therefore even when he need not travel he is exempt; and he concludes that thus we hold, unlike the Shulchan Aruch. You might wonder: when he can travel beyond the techum there is no time for Kriat Shema; and even when praying Maariv of Motzei Shabbos on Shabbos, in any case they do not recite Kriat Shema until stars emerge, as the Tur himself wrote below siman 293; and so I found in Derishah that he wrote the Tur wrote this law regarding other mitzvos and not regarding Kriat Shema, for there is no time for Kriat Shema — until here. And this is unlike the Taz who ruled that an onen recites evening Kriat Shema while it is still day. On the main difficulty Derishah extended greatly to settle it — and this is the essence of his words: the Rosh and Tur are plausible that whenever they must travel beyond the techum for the deceased, even if he himself does not travel he is exempt — similar to a weekday, that even if he has others who will act in burial needs he is exempt. Therefore the Rosh and Tur wrote here "he can travel beyond the techum," meaning even if he need not travel himself; and the same law when no person travels but they ride on Motzei Shabbos quickly to bring the needs of the deceased — nevertheless, since the needs of the deceased are not in the city and it is fitting to travel, he is exempt because of honor for the deceased. But when they need not travel at all because all the needs of the deceased are in the city, then he is obligated in Kriat Shema and all mitzvos. Therefore the Tur wrote in Yoreh De'ah "if he must travel" — until here his words briefly; understand carefully that everything is settled and so one should rule.
