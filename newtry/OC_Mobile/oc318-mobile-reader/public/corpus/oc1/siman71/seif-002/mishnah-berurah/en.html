(8) He is obligated — in all blessings and in all mitzvot [except matters in private] and it is permitted to go to the synagogue.

(9) The boundary — and the same applies in a place where one must seat gabbaim after Minchah to negotiate regarding a burial place or to engage in other needs of the deceased [Chacham Adam].

(10) He does not make it dark, etc. — the Taz wrote that since he has an obligation at that time, he may read the evening Shema while it is still day, even though the congregation does not read Shema except at night; nevertheless, that he will be exempt at night — he should not nullify from himself the yoke of the kingdom of heaven. But from the Magen Avraham, note 3, it seems he does not hold thus; and likewise the Eliyah Rabbah, that he should not read Shema and blessings while it is still day, and all the more so that he should not pray the prayer of eighteen [blessings]. And it appears that he reads Shema without blessings; and on Motzei Shabbat he eats without Havdalah and does not pray, and after he buries the deceased he makes Havdalah even until the end of the day.

(11) His law is like weekday — and even if he does not wish to bury him, nevertheless all laws of onenut apply to him; and specifically when he is able to bury him but he does not wish to. But if it is impossible to bury him due to a gentile festival or another coercion, onenut does not apply to him. And Chacham Adam wrote that if his deceased died on Yom Tov in the morning at the time of prayer, in a place where the custom is to seat gabbaim and he cannot gather them until after prayer — onenut does not apply to him then, and he is obligated to pray, for it is like coercion. And likewise on the night of Yom Tov, even of the second day of Yom Tov, most of the later authorities agreed that he sanctifies and prays everything, and the law of onen does not apply to him, because there is no way to bury at night; and he is permitted in everything except Torah study, because it gladdens. And the same for Yom Tov. But regarding weekday, whether at night or by day, even if there is some hindrance from the gabbaim that they do not gather — nevertheless onenut applies. One who began to pray or to read Shema and suddenly became an onen — whether he finishes or stops — there are opinions on this among the later authorities (see in Chacham Adam, general rule 139, seif 5, that his view is like the Shulchan Aruch; and in Sha'arei Teshuvah he brought many later authorities who require stopping).
