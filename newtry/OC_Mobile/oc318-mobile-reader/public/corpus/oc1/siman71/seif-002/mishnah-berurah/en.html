8. He is obligated. In all blessings and mitzvos, except for private practices of mourning, and he may go to the synagogue.
9. The boundary. The same applies where it is necessary to seat communal officials after Minchah to reach an agreement concerning a burial place or to attend to other needs of the deceased. [Chochmas Adam.]
10. He does not go beyond the boundary, etc. The Taz writes that since he is obligated at that time, he should recite the evening Krias Shema while it is still daytime. Although the congregation does not recite Krias Shema until night, this person, who will be exempt at night, should not thereby lose acceptance of the yoke of the Kingdom of Heaven. The Magen Avraham, seif katan 3, however, appears not to agree, and Eliyah Rabbah likewise writes that he should not recite Krias Shema and its blessings before nightfall, and certainly should not pray the Amidah. It appears that he should recite Krias Shema without its blessings. On Motzaei Shabbos he should eat without Havdalah and should not pray. After the burial he may recite Havdalah through the end of Tuesday.
11. Has the same law as a weekday. Even if he does not wish to bury the deceased, all the laws of aninus apply to him. This applies specifically where burial is possible but he does not wish to arrange it. If burial is impossible because of a non-Jewish holiday or another unavoidable circumstance, aninus does not apply. Chochmas Adam writes that if a relative died on Yom Tov morning during the time of prayer, in a place where the custom is to convene communal officials, and he cannot gather them until after prayer, aninus does not yet apply and he is obligated to pray, because this is considered an unavoidable circumstance. Likewise, on the night of Yom Tov, even the second day of Yom Tov, most later authorities agree that he should recite Kiddush and pray in full, and the laws of an onen do not apply, because burial is not normally performed at night. He is permitted in all matters except Torah study, because it brings joy. All this concerns Yom Tov. On a weekday, however, whether at night or during the day, even if there is some impediment because the communal officials have not gathered, aninus nevertheless applies. If one began praying or reciting Krias Shema and suddenly became an onen, the later authorities disagree whether he should complete it or stop. See Chochmas Adam, klal 139, seif 5, who follows Shevus Yaakov, while Shaarei Teshuvah cites many later authorities who require him to stop.
