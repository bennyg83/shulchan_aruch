We do not object, etc. The Levush wrote: his words are astonishing, for if even one upon whom burial is not incumbent, such as his married sister, is not permitted, certainly one upon whom burial is incumbent, though he has someone else, is not permitted. End quote. Lechem Chamudos, page 16, wrote that the Levush did not notice the contradiction between the Shulchan Aruch here and Yoreh Deah, siman 341, where he wrote, some say that even if he wishes to be stringent he is not permitted, and he did not write the dissenting opinion at all. End quote. In my humble opinion, the Levush holds that this is no contradiction at all. For in the first opinion there he wrote that he is exempt from Krias Shema, but did not conclude that even if he wishes to be stringent he is not permitted, as he concluded later. This implies that the exemption mentioned means that there is no obligation, but if he wishes to be stringent, permission is in his hand. On this the Shulchan Aruch there wrote, and some say that even if he wishes to be stringent he is not permitted. Thus he wrote both opinions in Yoreh Deah. It is already known that the first opinion brought by the Shulchan Aruch without qualification is what he holds primary for practical halacha, and as he ruled here. So it appears to me. Regarding the Levush's difficulty, Lechem Chamudos answers that an error fell into the text of the Shulchan Aruch, and it should read: and some say that if he has someone to involve himself, etc. But this is forced, and what the Magen Avraham wrote on this is also forced. My master, my father the Rav, may his light shine, would answer the difficulty of the Levush and the questions of Lechem Chamudos with one approach: that what the Shulchan Aruch wrote, if he has someone to involve himself on his behalf, etc., refers back above to a man upon whom burial is not incumbent; and he says that if he also has someone to involve himself on his behalf, so that there are two favorable factors, he may be stringent. But with only one favorable factor, such as where burial is incumbent upon him even though he has someone to involve himself, or where burial is not incumbent upon him but he has no one to involve himself, the Shulchan Aruch holds that he may not be stringent, as he ruled in Yoreh Deah. His words are correct, except that this distinction is not mentioned in the Beit Yosef or the poskim. On the contrary, a married sister presumably has people to bury her, and nevertheless they wrote that he may not be stringent. Now the Olas Tamid wrote: there is a difficulty with the Shulchan Aruch; since this was left as a question in the Yerushalmi, he should have ruled leniently in a doubt regarding blessings like the Levush, that one may not be stringent upon himself because of a blessing in vain. End quote. According to the explanation of my master, my father the Rav, may his light shine, this is not difficult at all, for we do not find a question in the Yerushalmi in a case with two favorable factors, and examine this well. Furthermore, I say that in any case the words of Olas Tamid are astonishing. A blessing in vain is not relevant here at all, for on the contrary, he is obligated in the blessings; rather, they were concerned for the honor of the deceased, lest he become sluggish in burial, as the Beit Yosef wrote. But in the blessing itself there is no concern or flaw at all. Know this, for many poskim wrote regarding the aforementioned unresolved question that since it was not resolved, one blesses and prays; see Beit Yosef. Furthermore, it is only regarding D'Rabbanan blessings that we rule leniently, but with Krias Shema, which is according to the Torah, we hold in siman 67 that a doubt is ruled stringently. Nevertheless, practically, the Bach, Nachalas Tzvi, and Taz ruled like the Levush, that he may not be stringent, unlike the Shulchan Aruch, and so I saw done by my grandfather, the gaon, of blessed memory. It also appears to me that the difficulties of the Levush and Lechem Chamudos above against the Shulchan Aruch may be answered in another way: the Shulchan Aruch also holds that he may not be stringent, but holds that if one wishes to be stringent, we do not object to him, meaning that beis din does not have power to object to him, since he has upon whom to rely among the great poskim who ruled that he may be stringent. But in truth, one who comes to ask is instructed that he may not be stringent. So it also implies in the Beit Yosef, and so it implies in Responsa Avodas HaGershuni, siman 42. The Taz wrote that a son who is an onen and others are attending to the burial should go to the synagogue to say Kaddish according to all opinions. End quote.
