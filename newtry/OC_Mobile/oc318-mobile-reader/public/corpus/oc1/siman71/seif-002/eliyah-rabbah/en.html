[2] They do not object, etc. The Levush wrote — his words are astonishing: if even one upon whom it is not incumbent to bury him, such as his married sister, is not permitted, all the more so one upon whom it is incumbent but he has another — until here. Lechem Chamudos page 16 wrote: the Levush did not notice the contradiction of the Shulchan Aruch, for here versus Yoreh De'ah siman 341 he wrote that some say that even if he wants to be stringent he is not permitted, and he did not write the dissenting view at all — until here. In my humble opinion the Levush is plausible — there is no contradiction at all, for in the first reasoning he wrote there that he is exempt from Kriat Shema and did not conclude that even if he wants to be stringent he is not permitted as he concluded here; the exempt he mentions means there is no obligation, but if he wants to be stringent the permission is in his hand. Therefore the Shulchan Aruch wrote upon it there that some say that even if he wants to be stringent he is not permitted — and if so he wrote both views in Yoreh De'ah. And it is already known that the first reasoning the Shulchan Aruch brought anonymously is the main ruling in law, and as he ruled here — so it seems to me. On the Levush's astonishment, Lechem Chamudos answers that a mistake fell in the Shulchan Aruch and one must read that and some say that if he has someone who will act, etc. — and this is strained; also what Magen Avraham wrote here is strained. My father the rabbi, may his light shine, would answer the Levush's astonishment and Lechem Chamudos's difficulties with one reed: what the Shulchan Aruch wrote, "and if he has someone who will act on his behalf," etc., refers above to a man upon whom it is not incumbent to bury him, and says if he also has someone who will act on his behalf — when there are two positive factors he is permitted to be stringent; but in one positive factor, such as when it is incumbent upon him to bury him even though he has someone who will act, or when it is not incumbent upon him to bury him and he has no one who will act — the Shulchan Aruch is plausible that he is not permitted to be stringent, as ruled in Yoreh De'ah. His words are correct, only that this distinction was not mentioned in Beit Yosef and poskim; on the contrary, his married sister presumably has those who bury her, and even so they wrote he is not permitted to be stringent. In Olas Tamid he wrote astonishment at the Shulchan Aruch's ruling that this is an unresolved question in Yerushalmi — he is inclined to rule leniently in doubt of blessings like the Levush, that he is not permitted to be stringent upon himself because of a blessing in vain — until here. According to my father the rabbi's explanation, nothing is difficult, for we do not find an unresolved question in Yerushalmi with two positive factors — understand carefully. And further I say that in any case Olas Tamid's words are astonishing, for a blessing in vain does not apply here at all — on the contrary he is obligated in blessings; rather they were concerned because of honor for the deceased that he not be lazy in his burial, as Beit Yosef wrote, but in the blessing itself there is no concern or flaw at all. And you should know that many poskim wrote on the unresolved question mentioned above: since it was not resolved, they bless and pray — see Beit Yosef. And further, that specifically regarding rabbinic blessings we rule leniently, but regarding Kriat Shema which is biblical we hold in siman 67 that doubt is stringent. However for practical law the Bach, Nachalas Tzvi, and Taz ruled like the Levush that he is not permitted to be stringent, unlike the Shulchan Aruch; and so I saw in practice with my grandfather the gaon, may his memory be for a blessing. It further seems to me to resolve the astonishment of the Levush and Lechem Chamudos mentioned above on the Shulchan Aruch: also the Shulchan Aruch's view that he is not permitted to be stringent — only that he is inclined that one who wants to be stringent, they do not object to his hand; meaning the court has no power to protest him since he may rely on great poskim who ruled he may be stringent — but in truth one who comes to ask, they instruct him he is not permitted to be stringent; and so it implies in Beit Yosef and in responsum Avodas HaGershuni siman 42. The Taz wrote that a son who is an onen and there are those engaging in burial should go to the synagogue to say kaddish for everyone — until here. And it is somewhat difficult regarding what the Taz wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 376 — see there; and see above siman 133 seif gimmel from this.
