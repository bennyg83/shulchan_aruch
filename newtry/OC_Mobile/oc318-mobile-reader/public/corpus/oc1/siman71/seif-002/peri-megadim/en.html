And even so — the Taz, in what he wrote "and it is appropriate for a groom," for we ruled that they are not obligated to read — apparently the word "they are not mistaken" refers to siman 377, where the Shulchan Aruch ruled that nowadays one reads — which implies obligation (see there). If so, why here is there no distinction between the time of the Talmud and now? And one should explain that there, regarding intent, now it would be conspicuous if he did not read; not so here, where he is exempt because there is no one to carry his burden or for his honor — there is no distinction in this time. And if we read "they are not obligated," it refers to what the Shulchan Aruch ruled in 377 per Talmudic law that he is exempt; and "they are not obligated" refers to what the Shulchan Aruch ruled in 377 per Talmudic law that he is exempt — behold, if he wishes he reads even per Talmudic law, and now he is obligated; and this is like the Rambam, that all who are exempt from Shema, if he wishes he reads, and an onen is also included, as the Beit Yosef wrote in siman 377. If so, how did he rule here that he is not permitted to be stringent, at least when there is no one to carry his burden? For here, where the reason is his honor or his burden, he ruled not like the Rambam; not so above, because of intent, if he wishes he is permitted. And Orach Chayyim and the Taz, in what he wrote that an onen goes to the synagogue and says Kaddish when there are attendants — there is only because of his honor, and this is his honor that he says Kaddish. And in Ohr Chadash, 433, letter 50, and Yoreh De'ah 376 in the Taz, note 4 — on a weekday one does not say Kaddish deals with when there are no attendants; only he, when there is a burden, is forbidden to say Kaddish. And in Nachalat Binyamin, Yoreh De'ah 27 — when the deceased has not been buried there is no law of Gehinnom and no Kaddish to save from Gehinnom; and it appears that if there are no other sons, one who is not a robber is permitted to say Kaddish in such a case, as the Taz wrote.
