And even. See the Taz. Regarding his words, 'This is unlike a groom, concerning whom we rule that they are not obligated to recite,' it appears that the word 'not' is a scribal error. His meaning is that in siman 70 the Shulchan Aruch rules that nowadays a groom recites, which implies an obligation. If so, why is no distinction made here between the Talmudic period and our time? He answers that there the matter depends upon concentration, and nowadays not reciting would be presumptuous, whereas here the exemption is because no one else will bear his burden or because of the honor of the deceased, so there is no distinction between our time and earlier times. If we retain the reading 'they are not obligated,' it refers to the Shulchan Aruch's ruling in siman 70 according to the strict Talmudic law that a groom is exempt and not obligated. Yet if he wishes, he may recite even under Talmudic law, and nowadays he is obligated. This follows the Rambam, who holds that anyone exempt from Krias Shema may recite if he wishes, and includes an onen as well, as the Beit Yosef writes in siman 70. If so, how does the Shulchan Aruch rule here that he may not be stringent? At least where no one else will bear his burden, this requires explanation. The answer is that here, because the reason is the honor of the deceased or the burden of burial, the ruling does not follow the Rambam. Above, however, where the exemption is because of concentration, he may recite if he wishes. See the Bach and the Taz. Regarding the Taz's statement that an onen should go to the synagogue and recite Kaddish when others are attending to the deceased, there remains only the concern for the honor of the deceased, and reciting Kaddish is itself an honor. See Eliyah Rabbah, siman 133, subsection 50, and the Taz in Yoreh Deah, siman 376, seif katan 4. His statement there that on a weekday he does not recite Kaddish refers to a case in which no one else is attending to the burial, so the burden rests upon him and he may not recite Kaddish. The Nekudas HaKesef in Yoreh Deah writes that until the deceased is buried there is no judgment in Gehinnom, and Kaddish is therefore not needed to save him from Gehinnom. It appears that where there are no other sons and he is not depriving anyone else, he may recite Kaddish in the situation described by the Taz.
