Seif 62, if he travels beyond the techum, etc. So that the Yerushalmi cited above should not contradict the Gemara. And likewise in Yerushalmi there: this that you might say on a weekday, but on Shabbos he reclines and eats meat, etc. And the Rosh explained that the Yerushalmi cited above discusses such a case, as stated, since he is, etc. And further the Rosh explained that this is what it said: since it is permitted to bury him through gentiles, and also to travel beyond the techum — and some say this is a rabbinic prohibition — therefore it applies to him, unlike on Shabbos. And this is what he said, and the first Yom Tov, etc., and if he is not, etc., and it says the second Yom Tov, etc., as stated in chapter 1 of Yom Tov. The second Yom Tov regarding a deceased, etc. And in Moed Katan he agreed with the explanation cited above that the plain meaning of the language of mem is because he travels beyond the techum; nevertheless he wrote that by reasoning the second Yom Tov and first Yom Tov through gentiles is no worse than traveling beyond the techum on Shabbos.
