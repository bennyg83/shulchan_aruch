Se'if 2: “If nightfall approaches,” etc. This prevents the previously cited Yerushalmi from contradicting the Gemara. The Yerushalmi there states: This applies on a weekday, but on Shabbat he reclines and eats meat, etc. The Rosh answers that the cited Yerushalmi concerns a case of this kind, as indicated by the words, “Since he is,” etc. He offers another answer: The meaning is that since it is permitted to bury the deceased through non-Jews and also to wait at the boundary toward nightfall, and these involve only rabbinic restrictions, the law of an onen applies; this is unlike an ordinary Shabbat. This explains the words “and the first day of a festival,” etc., and “if he is not,” etc. The question remains regarding the second day of a festival, etc., as stated in the first chapter of Beitzah: “The second day of a festival with respect to a deceased,” etc. In Moed Katan, the Rosh agrees with the preceding explanation, because the plain language indicates that the reason is that he may wait toward nightfall. Nevertheless, he writes that logic dictates that the second day of a festival (and the first day of a festival when burial is performed through a non-Jew) can be no less subject to this law than waiting at the boundary toward nightfall on Shabbat.
