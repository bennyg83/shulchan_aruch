Evening. The Taz writes: It appears to me that, since the obligation rests upon him at that time, he should recite the evening Krias Shema while it is still day. Although the congregation does not recite Krias Shema until night, nevertheless, since he will be exempt at night, he should not thereby lose acceptance of the yoke of the Kingdom of Heaven when he can recite it before nightfall. After all, every day we recite the evening Krias Shema even while it is still fully daytime. He may therefore rely upon this and recite it on that Shabbos before Shabbos ends. The responsa Shvus Yaakov, volume 1, siman (100) [8], disagrees and writes that one who examines the Beit Yosef will see that the statement that he becomes obligated toward evening refers only to the other mitzvos, not to Krias Shema. Even were we to say that it also refers to Krias Shema, he certainly should not pray Maariv, for since he wishes to pray the weekday Maariv, it is already weekday for him, the status of aninus takes effect upon him, and an onen is exempt from prayer. See the Bach and Magen Avraham. Regarding one who began praying or reciting Krias Shema and suddenly became an onen, whether he should finish or interrupt, Shvus Yaakov, volume 1, siman 7, rules that he should not interrupt but should finish, since the rule that an onen may not recite Krias Shema or pray is only D'Rabbanan.
