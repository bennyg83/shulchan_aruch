Seif katan 2: We do not, etc. His question, 'Why is this different from his married sister, etc.?' means that the Yerushalmi gives two reasons why an onen is exempt from mitzvos. First, because of the honor of the deceased, for if he occupies himself with another matter, it appears that he is unconcerned with the deceased's needs. Second, because there is no one else to bear his burden and attend to the burial needs if he occupies himself with a mitzvah or another matter. The Yerushalmi says that the practical difference is where someone else can bear his burden. According to the second reason, he is then obligated in all mitzvos, while according to the first reason, the honor of the deceased, he remains exempt. Rabbeinu Tam acted accordingly when his married sister died, ruling that since she had a husband who was obligated to bury her, there was someone else to bear his burden, and the laws of aninus did not apply to her brother. He thus followed the second reason of the Yerushalmi. The Beit Yosef, however, rules that the exemption applies even where the obligation to bury does not rest upon him, because he follows the Rosh, who rules according to the view that the reason is the honor of the deceased. He therefore writes that even if the onen wishes to be stringent, he may not do so. It is consequently difficult why he then writes that if someone else will attend to the burial needs, the onen may be stringent, which appears to follow Rabbeinu Tam. The answer is that he appointed another person in his place. Meaning, even according to the reason of honoring the deceased, there is no affront when he personally appointed someone else, for a person's agent is like himself, and it is apparent that he is concerned for the deceased's honor. In the first case, however, although the sister has a husband upon whom her burial rests, there remains an affront to the deceased when her brother also does not participate in her burial.
And furthermore, etc. We do not prevent him. In the first clause, he comes to ask beis din, and they instruct him that he may not be stringent. In the latter clause, he does not ask, but acts stringently on his own, and beis din does not prevent him. The same would apply in the first clause concerning his married sister: if he does not ask and acts stringently on his own, they do not prevent him. Nevertheless, according to this explanation, it requires clarification why the law of one upon whom burial does not rest and the law of one who has someone else to attend to it were divided into two clauses, since their rulings are identical.
