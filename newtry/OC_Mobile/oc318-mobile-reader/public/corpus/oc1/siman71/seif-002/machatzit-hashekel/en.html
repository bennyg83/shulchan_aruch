(א) There is no [prohibition], etc., from his married sister, etc. Explanation: that in the Yerushalmi there are two reasons he is exempt from mitzvos. One — because of the honor of the deceased, that if he occupies himself with something else it appears he does not care for the needs of the deceased. Reason two — because he has no one to carry his burden and exert himself in burial needs if he occupies himself with a mitzvah or something else. And he said there — if so, if he has someone to carry his burden, according to reason two he is obligated in all mitzvos; but according to reason one, because of the honor of the deceased he is exempt. And Rabbenu Tam acted — his married sister died and he ruled: since she has a husband who is obligated in her burial, he has someone to carry his burden and the law of aninus does not apply to her brother, like reason two of the Yerushalmi. But the Rabbeynu Yosef ruled even if he is not responsible to bury him — because he ruled like the Rosh who rules like the one who says because of the honor of the deceased he is exempt; and therefore he wrote that even if he wants to be strict, etc., he is not permitted; and if so it is difficult why he wrote "and if one will exert himself he may be strict" — and this is per the view of Rabbenu Tam who established, etc. Explanation: that even for the reason because of the honor of the deceased it does not apply since he established another in his place — his agent is like him — and it appears he was concerned for the honor of the deceased; not so in the first case where one can say the husband is responsible for her burial. Nevertheless there is disgrace to the deceased when her brother too does not exert himself in her burial.

(ב) And further, etc. — they do not protest. In the first section it deals with when he comes to ask the court — they instruct him he is not permitted to be strict; in the conclusion it deals with when he does not ask and on his own he is strict — the court does not protest against him. And the same in the first section regarding his married sister — if he himself does not ask and is strict, they do not protest; and nevertheless it requires clarification: why did we divide them into two sections — "he is not responsible to bury him" and "he has someone who will exert himself" — since their laws are equal?
