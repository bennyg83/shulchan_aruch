If — an onen who blessed and others heard him, do they fulfill [their obligation]? Or shall we say: anything one is not obligated in is called [exempt], since for the present he is exempt — we wrote this in a comprehensive introduction from this. However, one engaged in guarding the deceased who stopped and read and others heard — they fulfill well, for he is not truly exempt but engaged in another mitzvah. And see Yoreh De'ah 341 in the Taz, note 5, and Magen Avraham 393, letter 5; and there it will be explained.

See Yoreh De'ah 341 — even though there is someone who will attend to burial needs, meat and wine are forbidden and he is exempt from all mitzvot; but if they handed him over to bearers designated for this, meat and wine are permitted; and it appears that likewise he is permitted to pray and read Shema without tefillin. In siman 38 we wrote that on Chol HaMoed he dons tefillin, but on the first day; and in Ohr Chadash there; and there is room to examine it, for nevertheless it is a bitter day for him; and all the more so the view of the author in siman 31, that in any case on Chol HaMoed one does not don tefillin; and even though there is no mourning on the festival, onenut applies on Chol HaMoed, even on the second day of Yom Tov through Israel or the first day of Yom Tov through gentiles; and there, in 348, this will be explained further.
