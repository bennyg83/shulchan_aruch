If. If an onen recited a blessing and others heard him, whether they thereby fulfill their obligation, or whether he is considered one who is not obligated in the matter because he is presently exempt, is discussed in the general introduction. However, if one who was engaged in communal needs stopped and recited, and others heard him, they certainly fulfill their obligation, because he is not truly exempt but is merely occupied with another mitzvah. See Yoreh Deah, siman 341, Taz, seif katan 5, and Magen Avraham, siman 93, seif katan 5. With Hashem's help, this will be explained there.
See Yoreh Deah. In siman 341, seif 3, it is ruled that although someone else is available to attend to the burial needs, he is forbidden to eat meat or drink wine and is exempt from all mitzvos. If, however, the deceased was entrusted to professional pallbearers designated for this purpose, he may eat meat and drink wine. It appears that he may likewise pray and recite Krias Shema, though without tefillin. In siman 38 we wrote that on Chol HaMoed he puts on tefillin. However, on the first day of mourning, see Eliyah Rabbah there, this matter requires examination, for it is nevertheless a bitter day for him. This is certainly so according to the Mechaber in siman 31, who in any event rules that tefillin are not worn on Chol HaMoed. Although mourning does not apply on the festival, aninus does apply on Chol HaMoed, and even on the second day of Yom Tov when burial is performed by Jews, or on the first day when burial is performed by non-Jews. With Hashem's help, this will be explained further in siman 548.
