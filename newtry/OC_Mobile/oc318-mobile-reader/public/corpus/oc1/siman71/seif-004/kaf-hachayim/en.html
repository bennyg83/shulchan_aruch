(א) (4) There. Exempt from Kriat Shema and tefillah. And immediately when they begin to throw earth upon the deceased he recites and prays. And so did Maharil. And it seems that before chatzos he prays Shacharis as written in siman 89. Magen Avraham end of note 1. Sha'ar HaTziyun note 2. Rabbenu Zalman note 1. And likewise in responsum Devar Moshe vol. 3 siman 13, and they brought it in Shulchan Aruch HaRav p. 282 side a. Sha'arei Teshuvah note 1. But the Zera Emes vol. 1 siman 11 disagreed with the words of the Magen Avraham mentioned, and he ruled practically that when the mourner returns from the house of graves after a third of the day he should not say even the prayer of the eighteen [blessings], only it is fitting that he recite Keri'as Shema alone without blessings to accept upon himself the yoke of the kingdom of heaven; and he wrote that such is the custom in the city of Modena, see there. And they brought it from Machberes note 1 in Shulchan Aruch HaRav there. Sha'arei Teshuvah there. Siddur Beit Yosef in the laws of Keri'as Shema of Maariv note 10. And likewise the Chesed LeAvraham note 1. And he need not complete the tefillah after it since at the time of his obligation he was exempt, as written in Yoreh De'ah siman 341 seif 2; and likewise Eliyah Rabbah note 1. Rabbenu Zalman there. Sha'arei Teshuvah there. Chesed LeAvraham there. And see in our words above siman 58 note 25.

(ב) (4) And he wrote there — the Magen Avraham: but Birchos HaShachar he should not say, since at the time of his obligation he was exempt, as written in Yoreh De'ah siman 341 seif 2 regarding Havdalah, so far. And they cited Eliyah Rabbah there, Sha'ar HaTziyun there. And likewise Rabbenu Zalman there — that he should not say all Birchos HaShachar even if he was buried before the time of Keri'as Shema and tefillah passed, since at the hour when the obligation of the blessings applied — that is, when he rose from his bed — he was exempt from them; he need not complete them afterwards, and if he blesses he blesses in vain, so far. And likewise ruled Devar Moshe in his responsum vol. 3 siman 13 per the view of the Magen Avraham mentioned; only to distinguish — the three blessings "who did not make me a gentile," "who did not make me a slave," and "who did not make me a woman," and the blessings of the Torah that are upon a person to say them all day — also the mourner after his relative was buried — at any time he is obligated to say them, see there. And they brought Shulchan Aruch HaRav there. And see Levush and Machatzis HaShekel what they wrote on the words of the Magen Avraham mentioned; and it is implied from their words that these words of the Magen Avraham apply specifically per those who say that these blessings — if one was not obligated in them — one does not bless them; but per those who say they were instituted for the conduct of the world and even if one was not obligated in them one blesses them, as written in siman 46 seif 8, one may bless them also after chatzos, see there. And likewise the Peri Megadim in Eshel Avraham note 1, and likewise the Nahar Shalom note 1, and likewise the Maamar Mordechai note 3 — only that he wrote the matter needs study, see there. And likewise Rabbi Meir Chaim z"l p. 244 sides b and c — that the mourner may bless them all day long; and he wrote that in his days they did so in practice with the consent of the gaon Maharit, author of Erech HaShulchan, see there. And see in Siddur Nehora HaShalem in Derech HaChaim in the laws of Pesukei DeZimra and Birchos HaShachar — he wrote in the name of Sefer Ma'aseh Rav from the gaon Maharal of Vilna z"l — that one who forgot and did not say Birchos HaShachar before tefillah may say them afterwards all day, and also at night until sleep, except the blessing of the Torah which already departed in Ahavah Rabbah and the blessing Elokei Neshamah which departed in the blessing Mechayeh HaMetim, see there. And so is implied from the words of the Maamar Mordechai siman 46 note 10 — only that he is uncertain in this; only he wrote it seems simple that until the end of three hours of the day one may bless with simplicity, for such is the way of princes to stand three hours and it is still the hour of rising, and many people do not rise until that hour — and if so there is no law and no judge who can bless; but for the rest of the day there is room to doubt, see there. And likewise in Siddur Nehora HaShalem in Derech HaChaim in the laws of onen note 4 — if the aninus continued until after four hours of Birchos HaShachar, that is, what is before Keri'as Shema, he should not say them even if he has leisure, since they said in the Gemara for all these blessings there is a time, as written — when he puts on his shoes he blesses, etc.; and even though we rule in siman 46 that for the needs of the world one blesses, and even if one was awake all night one blesses all the needs of the world — that is, until the time of rising which is a third of the day, like Keri'as Shema — see siman 58; except the blessing of the Torah and the blessing "who did not make me a gentile," "who did not make me a slave," and "who did not make me a woman" — those blessings one blesses all day, and even after chatzos if he was buried he says them, so far. And see the gaon Maharshak z"l in Sefer Shenos Chaim siman 80 p. 22 side d — he wrote that one who did not bless Birchos HaShachar until chatzos may not bless afterwards, see there; and his reason seems to me that they instituted them to be said until chatzos when the strength of the day still ascends, and not after chatzos when the shadows of evening begin to incline. And the Zera Emes there wrote that even after tefillah all day long he may say them, as written in siman 52, see there. It turns out that in the view of the plain speakers there are three disputes in the matter: some say the time of Birchos HaShachar is until a third of the day like the time of Keri'as Shema; and some say until chatzos like the time of the morning tefillah; and some say all day long is its time. However, what the Magen Avraham wrote and his colleagues regarding onen that he should not say them afterwards even if he was buried before the time of Keri'as Shema and tefillah passed — we do not rule like him, since we ruled above in siman 46 note 49 per the Ari z"l and the later authorities that for the conduct of the world they are said; and if so the matter is not dependent on him but on any time the world can say them — he too can say them if his dead was buried before that time. However, the words of the Ari z"l remain for us in Sha'ar HaKavanos p. 2 side b — he wrote the blessing "who gives strength to the weary" is one of these eighteen blessings instituted before tefillah, etc.; and on p. 1 side c he wrote these eighteen blessings are in Ze'ir Anpin of Asiyah; and likewise in Peri Etz Chaim sha'ar HaBerachot ch. 3, see there. And further see in Nahar Shalom p. 11 side c on the Noda BiYehudah in the matter "in general the first three blessings," etc.; and in our words to siman 46 note 18 in the matter "and this," see there — that it is implied they have no place after tefillah, since they were instituted for all aspects of the three lower worlds in Yetzirah and ascended to Atzilut and the mochin are drawn through tefillah — how can the repair come afterwards, as the elder asked the children. Therefore it seems for the practical law that if one delayed and has not yet prayed — since he can pray until chatzos of the day, he can also bless Birchos HaShachar, since the repair of tefillah has not yet been done through it he can bless Birchos HaShachar before tefillah; and the same for onen if his dead was buried before chatzos that he can pray afterwards — only per the reasoning of the Zera Emes and his colleagues who wrote he cannot pray except until a third of the day — he also should not bless Birchos HaShachar except until a third of the day. And all other people — if one forgot to bless them before tefillah and wants to bless them afterwards, if it is before a third of the day we do not neglect him, but not afterwards because there is a dispute in this as mentioned — requires investigation, and even though the Ari z"l wrote they were instituted for the conduct of the world and a person must bless them every day — requires investigation; we do not say "requires investigation" against the words of the Ari z"l — that is, specifically regarding whether he was awake all night or did not remove his turban, etc., but not regarding that he should bless them at any time, for their time is before tefillah as mentioned. However, the blessing of the Torah one may bless at any time of the day if he did not bless it, because it has two kavanos as written in siman 46 note 29; and further see in our words to siman 46 note 9.
