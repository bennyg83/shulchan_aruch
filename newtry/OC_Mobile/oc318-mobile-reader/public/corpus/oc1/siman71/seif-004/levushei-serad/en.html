(Magen Avraham, seif katan 1) Havdalah. It is true that there the Shulchan Aruch rules that he recites Havdalah after the burial until the third day, so the Magen Avraham would appear to be citing a proof that contradicts his own ruling. His intent, however, is as follows. The Beit Yosef there records a dispute: the Rosh and those who follow him maintain that he is exempt from Havdalah because he was exempt when its obligation first took effect. They bring a proof from Chagigah, where one who was lame on the first day of the festival and recovered on the second day is exempt from appearing in the Temple. Although the mitzvah of appearing applies throughout all seven days, all later days are compensatory for the first. Since he was exempt on the first day, he remains entirely exempt. The same would apply to Havdalah. Other poskim disagree, because here there is no concept of compensation at all. Rather, the period of obligation for Havdalah extends from Motzaei Shabbos until the third day, not as compensation. For the same reason, one may pray Shacharis until midday even if he was exempt in the morning, because this is not compensation; rather, the time for prayer extends from morning until midday. The Shulchan Aruch therefore rules there that he recites Havdalah, in accordance with those poskim. The Magen Avraham maintains that Bircas HaShachar resembles the case of the person who was lame, because the Gemara says, 'When he hears the rooster,' and similarly regarding each blessing. The poskim, as cited in the Shulchan Aruch in siman 46, write that nowadays, because the hands are not clean and also because of the unlearned, the practice is to arrange the blessings in the synagogue. Consequently, their later recital is considered compensatory, since the obligation originally attaches to a specific event, such as hearing the rooster, and by strict law one must bless immediately upon hearing it. This appears to me to be the Magen Avraham's intent, and it is straightforward.
