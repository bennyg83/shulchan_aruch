And see Yad Eliyahu, siman 16 — if it is near Shabbat and there is no time to bury him, he is obligated in Minchah with intent, for the reason is so that he should attend to it, and here it is not possible — see there. And so wrote in Shirrei Kenesses HaGedolah, Yoreh De'ah, siman 341, in Shabbos Shel Pesach — if his deceased died on Shabbat and he is an onen, he is not less than a mourner on a weekday, etc. So too an onen on Shabbat — if there is no one there to pray, the mourner may pray and discharge the obligation of the many. And if there is another there, he cannot — see there. And we practice that a mourner on a weekday prays even when another is present; and this is when they are commanded to pray for his father and mother. Therefore an onen on Shabbat does not pray when another is present. And see below, siman 133, regarding Kaddish; a minor onen on Shabbat; and in what he wrote from Shevut Yaakov regarding one who suddenly became an onen. And see in Shabbos Shel Pesach in the name of Motzal Me'esh, volume 2, and in a letter to David — there are those whose view is that he should not finish only that blessing, but he should not begin another blessing. And in Be'er Heitev here he elaborated on this and wrote in Shabbos Shel Pesach that it appears this is also his view, that he must stop, only he did not decide; and in Shabbos Shel Pesach wrote Shevet Yehudah that his view is he must stop; and it seems to me likewise that this is the view of the Maggid Mishneh, because if he finishes there is concern of a blessing in vain — return; and if you ask, stopping is preferable — see there. And see in Shevut Yaakov, volume 2, siman 188, regarding one who died on Rosh Chodesh — he does not pray Musaf if seven hours of the day have already passed; and in Be'er Heitev in Yoreh De'ah, siman 341, he disputes him — see there; and it will be explained in the laws of prayer.
