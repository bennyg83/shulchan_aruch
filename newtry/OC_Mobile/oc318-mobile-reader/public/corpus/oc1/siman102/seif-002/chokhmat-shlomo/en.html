Seif 2. There are those who say that if one sitting beside the one praying is weak, it is permitted. NB: See in Taz s.k. 4 what he challenged. His words are astonishing—for one can say the leniency for an elder and an ill person applies only to the ill person and the elder: if the one praying already came and stood there, the ill person and the elder need not stand since he is ill and elderly; but the one praying commits a prohibition by standing there to pray near him, and behold he can stand on another side—and therefore the prohibition is upon the one praying. And one can say this view explains per the explanation of Tosafot that Or Zarua brought above on "with You, Heaven"—therefore it is well said that if it were not within four amos she would not be standing to pray there, for there is no leniency because he is elderly; and Taz wrote this reasoning later regarding one who enters his boundary, and here he did not notice this—it requires analysis; understand.
