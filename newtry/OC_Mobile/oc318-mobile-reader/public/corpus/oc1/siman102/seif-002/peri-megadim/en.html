Whether. See Taz. These are the words of Darchei Moshe. Eli certainly would not have stood behind a woman; see Peri Chadash. The Taz here appears to agree with Magen Avraham, seif katan 6, that sitting before the person praying is forbidden but standing is permitted. If so, from where do we know that the lateral sides are forbidden? The answer is that Eli may have stood at her side, whereas he would not have stood behind her. According to Tosafos, however, who derive from “with you” that he sat in the fifth amah, there is no proof regarding the sides, even if we say that standing before him is forbidden, all the more so passing. Eli was outside four amos. This will, with Hashem’s help, be explained under Magen Avraham, seif katan 6. Know that if a raised platform or separate domain intervenes, one may sit even within four amos. This is obvious, and even according to the Taz’s reason in seif katan 3, a separate domain is permitted.
