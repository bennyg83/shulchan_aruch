Whether on the lateral sides. The Tur likewise writes this and says nothing about behind him. He gives the mnemonic that the word “this” has the numerical value twelve, corresponding to twelve amos in three directions: before him and the two sides. Behind him, in the Tur’s view, one may sit even within four amos. A reason may be that the law is derived from Eli, concerning whom it says, “Eli watched her mouth.” Such wording applies only when he was before her or at her side, not behind her. The Rosh, however, writes that one must distance himself four amos both before and behind the person praying, and connects “with you in this” to the numerical value twelve, meaning four amos in every direction. One must therefore say that the two lateral sides are counted as a single direction, as Darchei Moshe explains. It is surprising that the Tur does not cite his father’s dissenting opinion. Tosafos and Mordechai likewise write that one must distance himself four amos behind the person praying, and the Rama rules accordingly.
