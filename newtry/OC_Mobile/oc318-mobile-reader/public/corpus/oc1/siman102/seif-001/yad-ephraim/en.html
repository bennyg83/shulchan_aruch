Magen Avraham, seif katan 4: Into his domain, etc.; but in a synagogue, etc. It appears that the Bach holds that even when each person has a fixed place in the synagogue, if he is presently sitting idle and another comes to pray, the latter is not considered to have entered his private domain. This is implied by the case of Eli, who presumably had a fixed place in the sanctuary, and the Bach uses this to resolve the Tur’s question. It is possible, however, that where each person has purchased a fixed seat, and the person who stands beside him to pray has his own place elsewhere or is not a member of that synagogue and entered only incidentally, the latter is indeed considered to have entered his domain, and the seated person need not rise. Perhaps even this is not called entering his domain, because the entire purpose of a fixed place in a synagogue is prayer, and when he is not praying it is not presently his domain. This seems limited to a synagogue, comparable to the sanctuary. In our batei midrash, where people sit all day and attend to their needs, fixed places are not considered designated solely for prayer. A person’s place in the beis midrash is like his home. If he has completed his prayer and is sitting, and another stands beside him to pray, he need not rise, just as in his home. This applies even in a public beis midrash where people remain all day and use the place for sacred purposes, prayer and study, as well as permitted mundane uses such as eating, drinking, and sleeping, for “the house of the scholars is their home,” as explained in siman 151. All the more so in a private beis midrash where the owner grants each person use of his place for whatever he needs. The Bach too would agree that he need not rise there, except perhaps as an act of piety, as the Rama writes. In my Kuntres Misped Rav I explained the passage in Moed Katan where they wished to place a Sefer Torah on a bier, and Rabbi Chiya said, “Something he did not accept during his lifetime, shall we now arise and do?” One may not sit on a bed upon which a Sefer Torah rests. I explained there, based on the Taz’s reasoning here, that although the Sefer Torah entered his domain and he need not stand, there remains a prohibition against sitting. See there, and with Hashem’s help you will find the explanation sound.
