It is forbidden. In my novellae to Berachos 31a-b I wrote that Eli rebuked Channah because an intoxicated person may not pray, a Rabbinic prohibition according to Tosafos there. He knew she was praying only according to the Taz’s explanation, that she prayed so quietly that even her own ears could not hear, while only her lips moved. This supports the ruling in siman 101, seif 2. One may also say that after Channah answered him she prayed again. According to Tosafos, who derive the law from the extra hei in “and Eli was sitting,” the matter is clearer: where another person does not know that she is praying, he is certainly not considered one who transgresses before a blind person. Even if he knows, it was an act of piety for a woman as righteous as Channah to distance herself.
