Within four amos. According to Tosafos, it is understandable that one must distance himself even behind the person praying, because “with you” means that Eli sat in the fifth amah. According to the Rosh, who derives the law from the numerical value twelve, it is difficult, for four amos in each of four directions totals sixteen. The Bach answers that Channah presumably prayed near a wall and therefore no distance was needed before her. In truth, if one prays in the middle of a room, sitting before him is also forbidden. It appears to me, however, that the words are literal: one must distance himself four amos around the person praying in a circle. It is known that the circumference associated with four amos is twelve amos, as stated in the first chapter of Sukkah. Although here the span is four amos in every direction, making an area eight by eight, the calculation refers only to the equal line. According to the “there is one who says,” there is no difficulty, because the numerical allusion does not discuss the area before him at all.
