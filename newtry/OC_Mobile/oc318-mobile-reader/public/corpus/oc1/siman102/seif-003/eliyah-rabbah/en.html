[3] There is one who says, etc. The Beit Yosef cites this only in the name of Ohel Moed and therefore presents it as “there is one who says.” The Perishah, however, writes that this also follows from the Tur. The Tur writes at the end of the siman that one may not pass before people praying within four amos. If the prohibition stated here against sitting were limited to four amos, standing there would be permitted; why, then, would passing before him be forbidden? It is unreasonable that passing should be worse than standing. Moreover, the prohibition against passing follows a fortiori from standing, as the Beit Yosef writes below. It must therefore be that sitting before him is forbidden as far as the eye can see, whereas within four amos even standing and passing are forbidden. It appears that there is a slight omission in his words. His intent is that if sitting before him were forbidden only within four amos, standing within those four amos would be permitted, as implied by the specific mention of sitting and by the derivation from “I am the woman who stood with you,” where Eli stood. Passing would then certainly be permitted. It must therefore be that sitting is forbidden as far as the eye can see, while standing and passing within four amos before him are forbidden. Yet his proof is questionable. Standing may be permitted even directly before him and within four amos, whereas passing is forbidden because the movement disrupts his concentration. As for his citation of the Beit Yosef that passing is derived a fortiori from standing, in my humble opinion he did not fully grasp the Beit Yosef’s words. The Beit Yosef’s alternative argument, that if standing is permitted then passing is certainly permitted, refers to the lateral sides, where the concern is not disruption of concentration but the appearance that one does not accept the yoke of Heaven. If standing there is permitted, passing surely does not create that concern. Directly before him, however, where disruption of concentration applies, passing specifically is forbidden, while standing may be permitted, since merely standing does not disrupt him and does not appear to reject the yoke of Heaven because the person is not sitting. Nor can proof be brought from the Tur and Levush in seif 7, who write that standing at the sides is permitted, implying it is forbidden before him. Had that been a proof, the Perishah should have cited it. Plainly, it is not a proof, as explained below.
