Even. See Taz, who gives another reason: the place is holy because the person praying stands before the King. This differs from Krias Shema, which is praise and is not standing before the King, and therefore one need not cover his heart for Krias Shema. Even according to this reason, the Beit Yosef’s distinction is sound: mere contemplation does not suffice despite its holiness, and one must articulate the words. According to the Tur’s reason, that he appears not to accept the yoke of Heaven, contemplation plainly does not help. See Magen Avraham, seif katan 2. In siman 65, one who encounters a congregation reciting Krias Shema must recite the first verse with them. If this applies to song and praise, it should certainly apply to words of Torah. Perhaps the difficulty is that even beside an individual reciting Krias Shema it should be forbidden to sit. In truth, during prayer one may stand, and likewise during Krias Shema when both are sitting. Perhaps the question concerns a case where one stands and recites Krias Shema, whether another may sit idly. This requires study; see Chemed Moshe. Beer HaGolah, note 5, attributes the permissive opinion regarding Torah study to Terumas HaDeshen, but one who examines siman 3 there finds the opposite implication: song and praise, but not ordinary Torah study. See Beit Yosef, siman 65. Here only the Semag and Semak write this, as cited by the Beit Yosef. With Hashem’s help this will be explained further under Magen Avraham.
