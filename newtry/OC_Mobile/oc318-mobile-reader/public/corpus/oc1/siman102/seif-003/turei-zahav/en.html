This is the Tur’s view, based on his explanation that sitting within four amos of a person praying appears as though the other accepts the yoke of Heaven while he does not. When he is engaged in Krias Shema and its blessings, this concern no longer applies; if he is engaged in Torah, however, it remains. This is difficult. According to that reason, the same should apply beside one reciting Krias Shema, and it should be forbidden to sit silently within his four amos, for Krias Shema is the primary acceptance of the yoke of Heaven, whereas prayer consists of requests and supplication. Further, how does distancing four amos remove this concern? And from where did the Tur derive this reason, when the law is learned from Channah? Another reason may be offered. The place where one prays is holy ground so long as he is praying. One who is within four amos must therefore honor it. If he sits without occupying himself with anything sacred, he treats it as an ordinary place and must stand to demonstrate its sanctity. If he is engaged in prayer or Torah, even while sitting, he too bears sanctity. This is a sound reason for those who hold that ordinary Torah study suffices. The Beit Yosef nevertheless distinguishes that one must articulate the Torah with his lips, for that creates greater sanctity, unlike mere contemplation. This is a proper distinction.
