It is forbidden to pass directly before him, etc. See the reason in Mishnah Berurah. Eliyah Rabbah, citing the Shelah, writes that one must likewise be careful not to pass before someone while he is reciting “Shema Yisrael.” Maamar Mordechai writes: The reason is that doing so disrupts the concentration of the person praying. According to this, it would seem that if the person praying lowers his tallis over his face, as many customarily do, one may pass before him. A parallel appears below in siman 128, seif 3, where the Rama writes that the kohanim likewise should not look at their hands, and therefore they customarily lower the tallis. It further states there regarding physical blemishes that, where the kohanim customarily lower the tallis, a kohen may recite Bircas Kohanim even if he has several blemishes. Here too, lowering the tallis could therefore be effective. Nevertheless, it is proper to be concerned and act stringently. This is especially so according to Chayei Adam, who explains that the prohibition is because one interposes between the person praying and the Shechinah; according to that reason, one certainly should not be lenient.
