Beside the person praying, etc. The Beit Yosef cites this ruling from Orchos Chayim in the name of Baal HaTamid. Orchos Chayim comments that his words are astonishing and require study. The Beit Yosef responds that there is no difficulty, because the person’s weakness demonstrates why he sits. In my humble opinion, the difficulty remains. How does weakness justify sitting specifically within four amos? He could move one additional amah. Further, it appears that weakness does not help, for an elderly person and an ill person are treated alike everywhere, as in siman 113, seif 5, regarding bowing at Modim, and regarding leaving a table where an elderly or ill person remains, as stated in Pesachim. Eli was certainly very old at that time, as Scripture implies and as Chullin 21a says concerning Eli, “old age is different.” Why, then, did he have to stand within four amos of Channah? It therefore requires study whether one may rule leniently in this matter.
