Beside him. See Taz, who resolves the Bach’s question by explaining that in such a case the person praying must distance himself. See seif katan 3; according to both reasons the ruling is understandable. In a synagogue, however, where each person has his own place, it is forbidden, as the Bach and Eliyah Rabbah write. Magen Avraham, seif katan 4, says likewise. Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 5, cites Olas Tamid that this applies only at his side, whereas directly before him it is forbidden even if he was sitting first. Eliyah Rabbah observes that, if so, the permission for a weak person may likewise apply only at the side. This resolves the Taz’s words in seif katan 4. Yet the plain wording of the poskim does not imply this. According to the Taz’s reason in seif katan 3, that the prohibition is due to the sanctity of the place, partial leaning may perhaps be permitted. This is not because he appears not to accept the yoke of Heaven. See Magen Avraham, seif katan 7. There is no need to elaborate.
