Beside him, etc. The Tur writes this in the name of the Rosh and asks that the law is derived from Eli, who had already been sitting, as it says, “and Eli was sitting,” yet he nevertheless had to rise. The Beit Yosef cites Mahari Abuhav that this question applies only according to the first interpretation at the beginning of the siman, that Eli had been standing. According to Tosafos, that Eli was sitting and the proof is from the extra hei, there is no question. At first glance this is puzzling, for even according to Tosafos the question remains: Channah said, in effect, “I am praying outside your four amos, and therefore you may sit. Had I been within your four amos, sitting would be forbidden,” although Eli had already been sitting. On closer analysis, however, Mahari Abuhav is correct. If one person was already sitting and another enters his four amos, the first person has no obligation to move, since the second entered his domain. The second person acts improperly by beginning to pray within four amos of one who was already sitting lawfully. Channah therefore said correctly, “I am the woman standing to pray lawfully, since I am outside your four amos. Had I been within four amos, the prohibition would have been mine because I began praying within your area, but you would have committed no prohibition because you were already sitting.” Thus there is no question. My father-in-law the Bach criticized Mahari Abuhav sharply, but he did not analyze the matter correctly, for Mahari Abuhav’s words are sound.
