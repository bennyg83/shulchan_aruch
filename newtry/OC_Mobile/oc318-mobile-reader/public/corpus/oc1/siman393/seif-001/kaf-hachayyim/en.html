1) [Se'if 1] One may not establish an eruv, etc. This means in order to carry on the following day, because it appears as though one is repairing something on Yom Tov. Rashi on Beitzah 16b; Levush.
2) There. But if he forgot and did not establish an eruv, etc. If he forgot before Yom Tov, remembered on Friday, and mistakenly thought that one may establish an eruv on a Yom Tov that occurs on Friday, and he did establish one, it requires examination whether the eruv is effective after the fact. Mishbetzos Zahav, subsection 1. It appears that if this occurred on the second day of Yom Tov and he mistakenly established the eruv, one may rule leniently. We are expert in determining the calendar, and the observance of the second day is only an enactment of our Sages. The eruv itself is also only an enactment of our Sages, making this a twofold Rabbinic issue. Therefore, if he already acted without knowing the law and there is some need, one may be lenient.
3) There. He should establish an eruv conditionally on Yom Tov, etc. Rambam writes in chapter 6 of Hilchos Yom Tov, halachah 15, that nowadays, when we are expert in determining the calendar, one may not establish an eruv conditionally, whether an eruv tavshilin, eruv chatzeiros, or another eruv. See Beis Yosef, who writes that Ran and Rashba challenge this ruling. Birkei Yosef, subsection 1, also questions Rambam from the Gemara, for they were expert in determining the calendar and nevertheless made conditional stipulations on Yom Tov observed outside Eretz Yisrael. See there. The later authorities likewise agree with the Shulchan Aruch's ruling that one may make a conditional stipulation during the two days of Yom Tov observed outside Eretz Yisrael and establish an eruv conditionally, even nowadays when we are expert in determining the calendar.
