Although only the first verse is from the Torah and the rest is rabbinic — and all the more so the blessings — and a rabbinic doubt does not require returning, one could say that such was the enactment: whenever one reads, one is obligated to read as the essence of the enactment with its blessings, unless due to bitul Torah when the first verse suffices, as Kessef Mishneh wrote, and see above siman 63 note 6. And it is implied in Beit Yosef siman 40 that if he knows he said the first verse and is in doubt whether he read the rest, he need not return and read; however in siman 64 seif 3–4 it is implied that he must return — and it seems that initially they enacted thus that he should return. And if he is in doubt whether he said parashat tzitzit and also emet veyatziv, he must return per all opinions, for they are obligated to mention the Exodus from Egypt by Torah law; and it is implied in Tosafot Berakhot page 21 side 1 s.v. safek, etc., that he says emet veyatziv a second time — see there. And on page 20 side 2, Tosafot s.v. baal keri, etc., imply that it is better he say Keriat Shema again, for in it are two matters: acceptance of the mitzvot and the Exodus from Egypt — see there. And per the explanation of Rosh it is implied that he should say parashat tzitzit and also emet veyatziv, for they are all one matter. But if it is clear to him that he said parashat tzitzit and he is only in doubt whether he said emet veyatziv, he does not return — for once he said parashat tzitzit, then emet veyatziv is rabbinic, since the Exodus from Egypt was already mentioned in parashat tzitzit. And it is stated in the Gemara page 13 side 2 that if he said a halachah in which the Exodus from Egypt is mentioned, he has fulfilled. And Magen Avraham wrote, and it seems that all the more so if he said Shirat ha-Yam that he has fulfilled — see there; and see Taz. And it is plain in the Gemara that also at night one must mention the Exodus from Egypt. And what Taz wrote, like in separating demai — see there — is against the mishnah in tractate Demai chapter 1 mishnah 4, "and they separate it naked," etc. — see there; and perhaps it is a scribal error in the words of Taz. {Rama: And if he is in doubt whether he recited Keriat Shema and he knows he said emet veyatziv — Tosafot wrote that he does not return, even when he knows he contemplated emet veyatziv; he does not return — so too in the responsum of Beit Yaakov siman 138.}
