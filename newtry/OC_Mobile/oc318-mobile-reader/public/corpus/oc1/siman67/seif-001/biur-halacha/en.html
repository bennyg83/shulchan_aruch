And this is the wording of Kessef Mishneh in the name of Rashba; and it seems plain that this is specifically where he is in doubt also regarding the blessings; but if he knows he blessed the blessings and the doubt is on Keriat Shema itself — whether he had intent in the first verse, and the like — although he must return and read it, nevertheless he has already discharged the obligation of the blessings, for they are not connected to Keriat Shema; and even if it becomes known to him with certainty that he did not have intent in his heart in the first verse, he need only return and read the entire parashat Shema so that he not read it out of order, as above in siman 64 — see there. Afterward I found this in Shulchan Shelomo. I also wrote in Mishnah Berurah that if he knows he read the first verse and is in doubt about the rest, etc. — initially they enacted that he should return and read it; and it seems that in this case it is better he read it without the blessings, for in any event Gra and Pri Megadim questioned the essence of the ruling of Shulchan Arukh — why is this different from any other mitzvah that one is obligated to perform out of doubt, that one does not bless on it. And although Kessef Mishneh already settled this in the name of Rashba, as we copied in Mishnah Berurah — nevertheless do not add to it, for it is possible they enacted the blessings only if at the time the doubt arose he had a Torah doubt about the mitzvah itself. I also wrote in Mishnah Berurah in the name of Shulchan Arukh HaRav: regarding if he is in doubt whether he said parashat tzitzit and emet veyatziv — that he should say only parashat tzitzit; although in Magen Giborim he settled his difficulty on Magen Avraham, and Beit Chadash and Taz hold that he returns and says both — nevertheless I leaned to decide like him, because the essence of the law — even where he is in doubt about all of Keriat Shema whether he must also bless — is not so clear, as above in the name of Gra and Pri Megadim.
