He returns and blesses — because anything that is a Torah doubt — one blesses on it, as in separating demai. But on account of blessings alone one does not return and bless, for blessings are rabbinic. And although we say in the Gemara that for Shmuel emet veyatziv was from the Torah — meaning, since it contains the Exodus from Egypt, as it is written "so that you remember the day of your departure," etc. — and R. Eliezer does not disagree with him except regarding Keriat Shema; nevertheless it is clear in Tosafot and in the words of Rabbenu Yonah that emet veyatziv was not from the Torah, but that he also did not say parashat tzitzit — for parashat tzitzit pertains to emet veyatziv, for in both the Exodus from Egypt is mentioned. But if it is clear to him that he said parashat tzitzit — emet veyatziv is rabbinic according to all; therefore Tur and Shulchan Arukh ruled that on account of emet veyatziv he does not return — they deal with where he certainly said parashat tzitzit, only he is in doubt whether he said emet veyatziv; in that case he does not return. But in truth, if there is doubt also in parashat tzitzit — even though he certainly read the two paragraphs of Keriat Shema — he certainly returns; for then emet veyatziv would be from the Torah. This is plain to one who examines the topic.
