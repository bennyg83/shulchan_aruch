Av Beit HaTev; and see in Peri Chadash who wrote that also "and it shall be if you heed" is from the Torah; and Sha'agat Aryeh siman 2 challenged him and raised that "and it shall be if you heed" has no essence from the Torah and its reading is only rabbinic — but parashat Shema entirely is from the Torah, and the first verse alone does not suffice — see there. And nevertheless it appears from his words that in doubt he returns and reads also "and it shall be if you heed," even though it is rabbinic — for in any event, if it is clear to him that he read the first paragraph from the Torah, since he began to engage in Keriat Shema he should fix even rabbinic matters out of doubt. And if he is also in doubt about the first paragraph — he must read out of doubt the first paragraph, etc. — see there; and in siman 3 I wrote from this above siman 58 — see there. And see there siman 10 regarding remembrance of the Exodus from Egypt whose time is all day: if he did not mention all day and remembered during bein hashemashot, he must mention it, since during bein hashemashot it is a doubt of day — a Torah doubt is strict. And the time of the Exodus from Egypt at night — its time is all night, from the appearance of the stars until dawn; and afterward, no. Although the time of Keriat Shema of Maariv extends until sunrise — and if he mentioned before the appearance of the stars, even during bein hashemashot, it is a doubt and from the Torah, and he must return and mention from certainty onward. And see there siman 11, where he brought the words of Magen Avraham on this — that if in doubt whether he said emet veyatziv, what he brought from the words of Tosafot — and he challenged at length and raised that if in doubt whether he said Keriat Shema and its blessings, or not — he does not say; since on account of doubt in Keriat Shema he must say, he returns and says the blessings as well; and there is no distinction between Shacharit and Maariv. And where it is clear to him that he said Shema and "and it shall be if you heed" and he is in doubt about vayomer, emet veyatziv — then in the morning he returns and says the paragraph vayomer and emet veyatziv; he need not say out of doubt. And in the evening he returns and says emet ve'emunah, and need not return and read parashat tzitzit out of doubt. And if it is clear to him that he read all three paragraphs and he is in doubt only about emet veyatziv of morning or emet ve'emunah of evening — both have the same law that he need not return and say out of doubt — see there. And what Be'er Heitev wrote in the name of the responsum of Beit Yaakov — that even when it is known he contemplated emet veyatziv in his heart he need not return out of doubt — see Sha'agat Aryeh siman 13, who elaborated to prove from the Gemara that one does not fulfill remembrance of Egypt through contemplation; and see there that he brought the words of Ramban on the Torah on "remember what the Omnipresent your the Omnipresent did to Miriam" — that it is a warning against lashon hara, etc.; and this remembrance is a positive commandment like remembrance of Shabbat and Amalek; and he brought there from the words of Sifrei that it is with the mouth specifically — and the world is not careful. And see above siman 60 note 1.
