Although only the first verse is from the Torah and the rest is rabbinic — and all the more so the blessings — and a rabbinic doubt does not require returning, one could say that such was the enactment: whenever one reads, one is obligated to read as the essence of the enactment with its blessings, unless due to bitul Torah when the first verse suffices (Kessef Mishneh and Rashba in responsum siman 372) Av Beit Din siman 28. And it is implied in Beit Yosef siman 40 that if he knows he said the first verse and is in doubt whether he read the rest, he need not return and read; however in siman 64 seif 3 it is implied that he must return — see there. And it seems that initially they enacted thus that he should return. And if he is in doubt whether he said parashat tzitzit and also emet veyatziv, he must return per all opinions, for they are obligated to mention the Exodus from Egypt by Torah law (Beit Chadash and Shakh in the name of poskim). And see Knesset HaGedolah who elaborated on this; and see in Sefer Tomat Yesharim siman 13. And see in Berakhot page 14 side 2 and page 20 side 2 in Tosafot s.v. baal keri — it is implied that it is better he say Keriat Shema again than say emet veyatziv, for in it are two matters: acceptance of the mitzvot and the Exodus from Egypt — see there. But on page 21 s.v. safek, etc., it is implied that he says emet veyatziv again — because if he had said emet veyatziv, then presumably he also recited Keriat Shema. But if he said Keriat Shema, there is still doubt whether he said emet veyatziv — see there. But per the explanation of Rosh it is implied that he should say parashat tzitzit and also emet veyatziv, for they are all one matter. And it is stated in the Gemara page 13 side 2 that if he said a halachah in which the Exodus from Egypt is mentioned, he has fulfilled. And it seems that all the more so if he said Shirat ha-Yam that he has fulfilled — see there. And it is plain in the Gemara that also at night one must mention the Exodus from Egypt.
