His tzitzis. Of this it is said, "and I will sweep it with the broom of destruction." Sefer Mitzvas Semuros elaborates: there is no prohibition here of bizuy mitzvah (disrespect to a mitzvah), but rather lest one step on them and they tear. Magen Avraham challenged: in siman 23 he wrote that if tzitzis are dragged over graves it is forbidden because of "mocking the poor," which implies that otherwise it is permitted. So too the case of tzitzis of a pillow-cover, and the author of Yad Aharon reconciles this per the distinction of Taz that I wrote in se'if katan 3 regarding the bathroom. Here too, regarding tzitzis being dragged on the ground, there is a difference between a large tallit and a small one: for a large tallit designated only for prayer, it is not fitting and proper that its tzitzis be dragged on the ground. The cases of graves and pillow-cover tzitzis concern tzitzis on one's garments; see Shakh, Nekudas haKesef.
