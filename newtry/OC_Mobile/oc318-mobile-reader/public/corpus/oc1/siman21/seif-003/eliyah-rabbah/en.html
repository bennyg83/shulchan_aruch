[3] It is permitted to enter, etc. Taz writes that this appears to apply specifically to a garment made for ordinary wear. Regarding our talleisim designated solely for prayer, however, it is improper to enter the privy while wearing them. Although there is no clear proof of prohibition, it is at least improper conduct, as the Sages say: garments in which one cooked for his master should not be used to pour him a cup. Likewise, those who go to relieve themselves on Yom Kippur should remove the kittel, because that garment is designated for prayer. Thus writes Taz. I found in Sefer Chassidim, siman 777: “When Shaul relieved himself, his mantle was not upon him. Therefore, some remove their outer coat when relieving themselves in the privy, and likewise their tallis that has tzitzis.” This requires study.
[4] Not to sleep, etc. Later kabbalists write that many scrupulous people sleep wearing tzitzis so that they not be without the mitzvah of tzitzis even for a single moment. This applies even when sleeping with one's wife, and it is permitted to have marital relations while wearing it. Magen Avraham questions the proof of the Arizal. David said when he saw himself naked in the bathhouse, “Woe to me, for I am naked of mitzvos,” but did not say this at night while lying down. Magen Avraham answers that at night he had a mezuzah. In my humble opinion, the proof may be brought from the nights of Sukkos, when one is obligated to sleep in a sukkah, which is exempt from mezuzah, as stated in Yoreh Deah, siman 286. This is not a proof, however, because the sukkah itself is a mitzvah. Rather, the proof appears from when one lies with his wife during the year, for the room is exempt from mezuzah. Rama writes in Yoreh Deah, siman 286, that if a door separates the area, everyone agrees it is obligated in mezuzah. Perhaps because a door separates the area, he is considered naked of mitzvos.
