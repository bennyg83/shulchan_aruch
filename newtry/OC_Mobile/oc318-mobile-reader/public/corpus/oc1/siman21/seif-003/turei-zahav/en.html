It is permitted to enter, etc. It appears that this applies specifically to a garment made for ordinary wear that has four corners and therefore became obligated in tzitzis. Regarding our talleisim designated solely for prayer, however, it is improper to enter the privy while wearing them. A comparison may be drawn from the verse, “He shall remove his garments and put on other garments,” from which the Sages derive proper conduct: garments in which one cooked for his master should not be used to pour him a cup. This applies all the more so to a garment designated specifically for a sacred purpose. Indeed, in the preceding seif the Shulchan Aruch imposes greater stringency upon a worn-out tallis used for a mitzvah than upon damaged tzitzis, according to his own view in seif 1. For this reason Tur writes in siman 8 about one who removed his tallis in order to enter the privy. Although Beit Yosef writes there that Tur did not mention this because of any prohibition, but only because the case was one in which he could not relieve himself without removing the tallis, the explanation I have given appears more correct. Even if there is no proof of prohibition, it is at least no less than the proper conduct mentioned above. A tallis katan worn beneath one's clothing, as is our practice, may certainly be worn while relieving oneself, because it is covered. It further appears that those who leave the synagogue on Yom Kippur to relieve themselves while wearing a kittel must remove the kittel, since this garment is designated specifically for prayer.
