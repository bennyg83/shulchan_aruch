It is permitted to enter, etc. It seems this applies specifically to a garment made for wearing, with four corners, by which one becomes obligated in tzitzis; but tallisos used for a mitzvah, which we use only for prayer — it is not proper to enter a bathroom wearing them. An example: "He removed his garments and put on other garments" — we expound "other garments" — garments in which one cooked a pot for his master, one should not mix wine in them; and how much more so this matter, designated only for something holy. Did not the previous se'if also give stringency to a tallis used for a mitzvah that wore out, more than tzitzis that became ruined, per Shulchan Aruch se'if 1? Therefore the Tur wrote siman 8 regarding a tallis one removes in order to enter a bathroom wearing it; and although the Beit Yosef wrote there it is not because of prohibition, our Rabbi recorded it thus — but he speaks when one cannot attend to one's needs without removing the tallis. In my humble opinion the way I wrote is more correct; and even though there is no proof for prohibition, nevertheless it is no less than that matter of derech eretz we cited. However, the tallit katan worn under the garment, as we practice — in this case it is certainly permitted to enter for one's needs, since it is covered. I also think that those who go from the synagogue on Yom Kippur to relieve themselves while wearing a kittel must remove the kittel, since that garment is designated for prayer, as above.
