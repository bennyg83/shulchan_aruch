Tzitzis strings — see Taz. See Tosafos Shabbat 22a, s.v. sukkah, and Maharsha there, who explained: it teaches them separately — if they fell on chol hamoed they are permitted; likewise Gra there — if they fell on Yom Tov they are forbidden; if on chol hamoed they fell, permitted. There, in our novellae, we asked from Sukkah 46b: esrog — all eight days forbidden; one may say: carrying for disrespect is forbidden on chol hamoed, and on Shemini Atzeret because of muktzeh; but Taz holds that if they fell on chol hamoed they are permitted, and likewise in Turei Zahav on the Tur explicitly in the name of Ba'al Halachos: if they fell on chol hamoed, forbidden to eat but permitted to move; and in Shulchan Aruch 665, even if disqualified, forbidden all seven days — see there. Taz holds the reason is that chol hamoed is also somewhat Yom Tov, and with combination of reasons — disrespect and muktzeh — it is forbidden even if sukkah decorations fell and esrog became disqualified, even on chol hamoed; and tzitzis and esrog (and possibly sukkah too; "fell" is not precise — likewise one going on a trip who is not performing the mitzvah now has no disrespect like "fell") — after one took the esrog it is like "fell," for he does not now want the mitzvah; likewise tzitzis, for it is not an obligation on the person that he must go specifically in a tallis with tzitzis — bizuy mitzvah does not apply. No comparison to Chanukah candle, etc. Esrog and sukkah decorations are different, for since they were designated on Yom Tov they were somewhat designated for chol hamoed, and with combination of bizuy mitzvah it is forbidden even if disqualified and fallen; unlike tzitzis, where there is no muktzeh on plain weekday, and he does not now want the mitzvah — no bizuy mitzvah and no combination — therefore the Tur permits; nevertheless he wrote one should be stringent. It implies somewhat that on Shabbat and Yom Tov by law it is forbidden to use tzitzis, for he removes his mind from them. When they are not on him but only placed in a box, it is permitted even according to Sha'alos u'Teshuvos; but later authorities do not hold thus — rather they are forbidden as long as they are in the garment.
