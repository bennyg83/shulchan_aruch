But as long as they are attached to the tallit, it is forbidden to use them. {Rama: On this — because it is forbidden to use for one's needs something set aside for a mitzvah, such as threads attached to a tallit to tie something with them; likewise a hoshana for food, or an etrog that must be eaten (Tur, citing Sha'alos uTeshuvos, parashas Shelach Lecha). And "hoshana" as written — the explanation is the hadas (myrtle); so wrote Ra'avad in his Hagahos, for hadas, lulav, and aravah are all called "hoshana," as we find in several places in the chapter "Lulav haGazul"; they are called hoshana because they are waved when reciting Hoshanos (Maharil in his derashos on the laws of lulav). It is also explicit that the baraisa compares them to one another, for it teaches: "These are objects of a mitzvah: sukkah, lulav, shofar, tzitzit" — we see that sukkah and lulav are forbidden during their mitzvah time, which implies the same for tzitzit. Even when one is not wearing them, it is forbidden to use them, since they stand ready to fulfill a mitzvah with them, as above — the baraisa compares them to one another. Just as sukkah and lulav are forbidden throughout the seven days of Sukkos, even not at the time of waving or while sitting in the sukkah — so too tzitzit. Therefore they did not permit untying tzitzit from a tallit in order to sell it, because one thereby nullifies the mitzvah — just as it is forbidden to remove a mezuzah from a house when one moves out, because one nullifies the mitzvos. They only permitted untying from this garment and immediately placing tzitzit on another garment, as implied in Sha'alos uTeshuvos Shelach Lecha. The same applies to anything that stands ready to fulfill a mitzvah with it — it is forbidden to use it. The Rav elaborated on this in the Taz; see his book.}
