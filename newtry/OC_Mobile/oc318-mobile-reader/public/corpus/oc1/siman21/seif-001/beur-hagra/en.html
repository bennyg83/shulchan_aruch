Seif 1: That were severed, etc. Meaning, that is the case discussed there, as the conclusion states. The same principle applies to Chanukah lights, sukkah, and blood in the second chapter of Shabbos, and to other mitzvos such as an esrog and similar objects. We likewise say at the end of Rosh Hashanah that urine is forbidden, etc. The baraisa there also lists sukkah, lulav, and shofar.
But so long as, etc. As stated in the second chapter of Shabbos: blood is the source for all of them. See Toras HaBayis HaAruch, page 32b.
And some say, etc. Their meaning is that they may be discarded automatically, in contrast to sacred appurtenances, which require burial. In Maseches Tzitzis, however, it says that one may make them into a shroud for a deceased person or a saddlecloth for a donkey.
And there are, etc. This is derived from the statement, “Since one mitzvah was performed with it, etc.,” which shows that one should treat them respectfully. This is the meaning of the reference below to siman 26. See Magen Avraham and Taz.
