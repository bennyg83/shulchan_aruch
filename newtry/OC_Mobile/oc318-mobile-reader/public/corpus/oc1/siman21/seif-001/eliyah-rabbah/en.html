[1] It is forbidden to use them, etc. But it is permitted to use a tallit after untying the tzitzis from it — just as it is permitted to sell it to a non-Jew — provided the use is not degrading; and all the more so it is permitted to make another garment from it without four corners, as the poskim imply. However, as long as tzitzis remain on a tallit, even if one is not wearing it; likewise a shofar even after Rosh Hashanah; likewise an old lulav after Sukkos that stands ready for the mitzvah — Beit Yosef wrote that it is forbidden to use them and to treat them in a degrading manner. Regarding the tallit itself while tzitzis are still on it, Olas Tamid wrote that it is permitted to use it so long as the use is not degrading — they only forbade using the tzitzis, not the tallit — end quote. It is somewhat difficult: why was it necessary to prove above, siman 15, that when tzitzis are untied it is permitted to use the tallit — behold, even while it is on him it is permitted? R' Eliyahu Mizrachi wrote in a responsum, siman 81: It is forbidden to make a tallit used for a mitzvah from ornaments of pagan priests, or to buy from them beams and stones to build a synagogue; but it is permitted to wear clothing with which they adorn themselves, and to enter their house of worship and pray there. See Yoreh De'ah siman 130, se'if 10.
