[1] It is forbidden to use them, etc. It is permitted to use the tallis after removing its tzitzis, just as it may be sold to a non-Jew, provided that the use is not degrading. All the more so, one may make it into another garment without four corners. This is implied by the poskim. However, so long as the tzitzis remain attached to the tallis, even when it is not being worn, and likewise regarding a shofar after Rosh Hashanah or an old lulav after Sukkos that remains designated for a mitzvah, Bach writes that it is forbidden to use them or treat them disrespectfully. Regarding the tallis itself while its tzitzis are still attached, Olas Tamid writes that it may be used so long as the use is not degrading, because the prohibition against use applies only to the tzitzis and not to the tallis. This is somewhat difficult, for if so, why was it necessary to prove above in siman 15 that once the tzitzis are removed it is permitted to use the tallis? According to this view, it is permitted even while the tzitzis remain attached. Responsa Rabbi Eliyahu Mizrachi, siman 81, writes that it is forbidden to make a tallis for a mitzvah from ornaments used by idolatrous priests, or to purchase from them beams and stones to build a synagogue. It is, however, permitted to wear a garment with which they adorn themselves for idolatry, and one may enter a synagogue and pray while wearing it. See Yoreh Deah, siman 130, seif 10.
