(1) Tzitzis strings, etc. — The same applies to all objects of mitzvah use, such as sukkah, lulav, shofar, and the like, after their mitzvah has ceased and they no longer stand ready for their mitzvah.

(2) That became detached — or that one untied them from the tallis; and the same applies as stated earlier when one attached them to a garment — mere designation is not a [halachically significant] matter.

(3) To the trash — but it is forbidden to use them in a degrading manner; tzitzis are not less than the tallis itself, as below in se'if 2.

(4) Attached to a tallis — even if one is not wearing the tallis now, and even at night, since it stands ready to be worn and to fulfill the mitzvah of tzitzis with it. Likewise a shofar, even after Rosh Hashanah; likewise an old lulav if they still stand ready for the mitzvah next year — it is forbidden to use them because of bizuy mitzvah. See Biur Halacha.

(5) To use them — regarding tzitzis, a stipulation does not help if one says "I am not separating from them," as in siman 638, se'if 2, because it is bizuy mitzvah — for tzitzis are necessarily made for the sake of the mitzvah, as one blesses upon them "to wrap oneself."

(6) One should not treat them disrespectfully — likewise sukkah sechach, lulav, and shofar after their mitzvah has ceased: one should not throw them in the trash, nor anything else that is not in honor of the mitzvah that has passed. Pri Megadim wrote it is proper not to make degrading use even of sukkah walls.

(7) To throw them away — meaning, it is forbidden to throw them in the trash by hand; but if they were thrown away on their own without burial, one need not be concerned.

(8) To bury them — Maharil wrote that one places tzitzis inside a book as a marker, or uses them for some mitzvah purpose, since once one mitzvah was done with them, another may be done with them.
