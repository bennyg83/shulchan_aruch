(1) Tzitzis threads, etc. The same law applies to all objects used for a mitzvah, such as a sukkah, lulav, shofar, and the like, after their mitzvah use has ended and they are no longer designated for that mitzvah.
(2) That were severed. Or that were removed from the tallis. The same applies before they were attached to the garment, because mere designation has no halachic effect.
(3) Into the refuse. It is nevertheless forbidden to use them for a degrading purpose, because tzitzis are no less sacred than the tallis discussed below in seif 2.
(4) Attached to the tallis. Even when he is not presently wearing the tallis, and even at night, since it remains intended for wear and for fulfilling the mitzvah of tzitzis. The same applies to a shofar even after Rosh Hashanah, and to an old lulav if they remain designated for the mitzvah in the coming year. Using them is forbidden because it disgraces a mitzvah object. See Biur Halacha.
(5) To use them. Regarding tzitzis, a stipulation that one does not separate himself from them is ineffective, unlike the case in siman 638, seif 2, because the concern is disgrace to the mitzvah. Tzitzis are necessarily made for the sake of the mitzvah, since one recites a blessing over wrapping in them.
(6) One should not treat them. Likewise, after the mitzvah use of sukkah covering, lulav, or shofar has ended, one should not throw them into refuse or otherwise treat them in a way that dishonors a mitzvah that has passed. Peri Megadim writes that it is proper not to use even the walls of a sukkah for a degrading purpose.
(7) To throw them. Meaning, one may not actively throw them into refuse. If, however, they were not stored away and were subsequently discarded on their own, there is no concern.
(8) To store them away. Maharil writes that one should place the tzitzis inside a sefer as a marker, or use them for another mitzvah, because an object through which one mitzvah was performed should be used for another mitzvah.
