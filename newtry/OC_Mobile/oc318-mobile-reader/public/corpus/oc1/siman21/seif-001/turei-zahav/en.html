Tzitzis strings that became detached. Our Rabbi the Tur brings in the name of Sha'alos u'Teshuvos that while tzitzis remain in their mitzvah state it is forbidden to use them; likewise an esrog — to eat it — and hosha'anah — to smell it — we derive from blood, as it is said "and he shall pour out and cover" — with what he poured he shall cover, that he should not cover with his feet, so that mitzvos should not be disgraced through him. He wrote upon this, and it is possible to distinguish that blood is different — when one covers with his foot he makes the mitzvos in a degrading manner, unlike here; but it is good to be stringent. The Beit Yosef wondered: why did he mention tzitzis strings that are permitted, unlike esrog which is forbidden to eat? He also asked from chapter 2 of Bava Metzia, where we say regarding a Chanukah candle that it is forbidden to count money opposite it, and they derive from blood — so mitzvos should not be disgraced through him; and likewise sukkah decoration — it is explicit that benefit from a matter of mitzvah before its mitzvah has passed is considered bizuy mitzvah. Maharach Or Zarua explained that the Tur did not come to permit while wearing them, but only when placed in a box — meaning, even though they stand ready for wearing, nevertheless since there is no obligation except in a tallis one wishes to wear, it is permitted, etc. This is puzzling — for in this case certainly Sha'alos u'Teshuvos also concedes they are permitted; and from where do we know that Sha'alos u'Teshuvos forbids this? In my humble opinion the Tur's words are clear and follow Tosafos on Bava Metzia 22b, who wrote on sukkah decoration: there are two reasons for prohibition — one because of muktzeh and one because of disrespect — and both are needed; for if only because of muktzeh it would be forbidden only on Yom Tov and not on chol hamoed, and if only because of disrespect it would be permitted when fallen; but now that both apply, it is forbidden when fallen even on chol hamoed. Here you have that because of the combination of Yom Tov reasons it is forbidden even on chol hamoed. If so, the Tur speaks well to permit use of tzitzis even while on him — for muktzeh does not apply here, for that applies specifically on Shabbat and Yom Tov, i.e. what they designated on Yom Tov shel rabbanan; and bizuy mitzvah does not apply here, for at the hour he uses them he is not then seeking that the mitzvah name remain upon him. If so, no comparison to all the prohibitions. For Chanukah candle — there it speaks when one wants to use it at the hour there is obligation for the candle to be lit, and one cannot say he does not seek to perform the mitzvah then, for otherwise he would need later to extinguish and relight anew, for the lighting fulfills the mitzvah — but certainly at that hour he also wishes to engage in the mitzvah, and thus he himself makes the mitzvah in disrespect, like blood. Also no comparison to eating esrog and smelling hosha'anah — for these are not forbidden because of bizuy mitzvah, for likewise he would not then wish to perform the mitzvah, like tzitzis; rather they resemble sukkah decoration, which has prohibition because of muktzeh on Yom Tov, and this causes prohibition even on chol hamoed, as Tosafos wrote. The general principle: Chanukah candle and sukkah decoration — when one wishes to use them at the time of performing the mitzvah — resemble blood; and even sukkah decoration that fell, though not resembling blood since there is no disrespect there, nevertheless it is forbidden even on chol hamoed because of muktzeh, since it was already forbidden on Yom Tov, as the Tosafos I cited — and it is in this respect like esrog one wishes to eat and hosha'anah to smell, where then he is not performing the mitzvah but it is forbidden because of muktzeh. It turns out the Tur spoke well, unlike Sha'alos u'Teshuvos, who holds esrog and hosha'anah are derived from blood — if so we should also forbid use of tzitzis; but in truth esrog and hosha'anah are not derived from blood, for there one makes the mitzvah in disrespect; whereas tzitzis has two reasons for leniency as stated — therefore there is full permission even while on him to make use of them and later return them to their mitzvah state. This is clear and very correct, be-siyata di-shmaya. Only our Rabbi the Tur, in his humility, set aside his view and wrote it is good to be stringent, as in my humble opinion.

In a degrading place. He concludes in the name of Maharil, who was accustomed to bury them and place them inside a book, or to use them for some mitzvah purpose. Accordingly it seems the same applies to sukkah sechach: it is proper not to use it for a purpose that is not in honor of the mitzvah that has passed.
