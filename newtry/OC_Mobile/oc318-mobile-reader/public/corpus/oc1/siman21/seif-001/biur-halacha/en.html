But so long as they are attached, etc. See Mishnah Berurah. This is from Bach, whose words are cited by Olas Tamid and Eliyah Rabbah. Peri Megadim likewise agrees with them, unlike Taz, who writes that everyone agrees it is permitted. See below, siman 638, Magen Avraham, seif katan 2, concerning a sukkah built from year to year. His words imply that throughout the year, and even when Sukkos arrives, so long as one has not yet sat in it, it has only the status of mere designation. This clearly shows that he agrees with Taz that it is still permitted to use it, and there is no degradation of the mitzvah, unlike Bach. Shaarei Teshuvah there cites Birkei Yosef in the name of Mahari Molcho, who likewise forbids it, but rejects his words based upon Magen Avraham. It is surprising that he did not note that Bach agrees with Mahari Molcho. One may further distinguish between tzitzis, which stand ready every day for fulfillment of the mitzvah. Therefore, even when one removes the garment, the mitzvah has not ceased from it. A sukkah, however, after the seven days of the festival, has no mitzvah until the following year. Even if one intends to use it again, it has only the status of designation. According to this distinction, Magen Avraham would necessarily disagree with Bach at least regarding lulav and shofar.
