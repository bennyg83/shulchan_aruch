And rejoice at the arrival of Shabbos. Whoever increases the honor of Shabbos, whether through his person, his clothing, or food and drink, is praiseworthy. See siman 242. The Zohar and the kabbalists strongly warned that there should be no dispute on Shabbos, God forbid, especially between a husband and wife. This is also evident in Gittin, folio 52a, from the incident of two people who regularly quarreled with one another; see there. Tosefes Shabbos.
Similarly, etc. In some places the custom is to leave the synagogue for the entrance hall and say, 'Come, let us go out,' etc. Knesset HaGedolah. Our custom is to turn toward the west when saying, 'Come in peace,' etc. The custom is then to stand and make a gesture resembling the reception of a distinguished person. The custom is not to recite the usual Kabbalas Shabbos on Yom Kippur that falls on Shabbos. Rather, one recites 'A psalm, a song for the day of Shabbos' before Barechu. This applies even among those whose practice on other festivals that fall on Shabbos is to add several psalms in honor of Shabbos.
Immediately after, etc. Women also have the custom, before lighting the lamps, to wash themselves and put on Shabbos clothing, and fortunate are they. However, on short days, some remain late in the shop, and afterward wash and dress. Meanwhile they may, God forbid, enter into a possible desecration of Shabbos. It is therefore proper to warn them to come home earlier to wash and dress. If a woman is delayed, it is a greater mitzvah for her to light while still wearing weekday clothes than to enter, God forbid, into a possible desecration of Shabbos. If her husband sees that she is delayed, it is a great mitzvah for him to light the lamps, without regard to his wife's quarrel. It is a greater mitzvah to sit in darkness than to desecrate Shabbos, God forbid. Chayei Adam.
