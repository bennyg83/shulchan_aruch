(8) He should wear, etc. He should continue wearing them until after Havdalah on Motzei Shabbos.
(9) And rejoice at the arrival of Shabbos. Whoever increases the honor of Shabbos, whether through his person, his clothing, or food and drink, is praiseworthy. See siman 242. The Zohar and the kabbalists strongly warned that there should be no dispute on Shabbos, God forbid, especially between a husband and wife. This is also evident in Gittin, folio 52a, regarding those two people who would quarrel with one another; see there. Tosefes Shabbos.
(10) Similarly, etc. In some places the custom is to leave the synagogue for the entrance hall and say, "Come, let us go out," etc. Knesses HaGedolah. Our custom is to turn toward the west when saying, "Come in peace," etc. The custom is then to stand and make a gesture resembling the reception of a distinguished person. The custom is not to recite the regular Kabbalas Shabbos on Yom Kippur that falls on Shabbos. Rather, one recites "A chapter of Tehillim, a song for the day of Shabbos" before Barechu. This applies even among those who, on other Yamim Tovim that fall on Shabbos, customarily recite additional Tehillim in honor of Shabbos.
(11) Immediately after, etc. Women also customarily wash themselves and put on Shabbos clothing before lighting the lamps, and fortunate are they. However, on short days, some remain late in the shop and afterward wash and dress. Meanwhile they may, God forbid, enter into a possible desecration of Shabbos. It is therefore proper to warn them to come home earlier to wash and dress. If a woman is delayed, it is a greater mitzvah for her to light while still wearing weekday clothing than to enter, God forbid, into a possible desecration of Shabbos. If her husband sees that she is delayed, it is a great mitzvah for him to light the lamps and not be concerned about his wife's anger. It is a greater mitzvah to sit in darkness than to desecrate Shabbos, God forbid. Chayei Adam.
(12) Close to evening. This refers to one who washes himself somewhat at home, as related in the Gemara, Shabbos 25b, regarding R' Yehuda son of R' Ilai; see there. When he goes to the bathhouse, however, it is certainly proper to go earlier so that he will not come to desecrate Shabbos, especially during the winter when the days are short. See above, siman 260, seif katan 1 in the Mishnah Berurah, where we discussed this.
