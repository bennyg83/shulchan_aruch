One should clear cobwebs from the house. Rabbeinu Chaim, may he rest in peace. The Arizal was very particular to eat at a four-legged table, corresponding to the table in the Temple, and to recite the blessing over two bundles of myrtle at each of the three Kiddushim. On Friday night he placed on the table the cup used for Birkat HaMazon, with a little wine and a few crumbs, but not a whole loaf. See Yoreh De'ah at the end of siman 178, and see siman 180.
And he should spread the beds. This refers to those upon which people sit. The Darkei Moshe writes as follows: Mahariv wrote in his rulings that it is permitted to throw something away from the table, and one need not be concerned for those who say that by doing so one drives away the angel. End quote. It is possible that their words are metaphorical: if one throws something away and will be unable to sweep the house on Shabbos, the house will become dirty and the good angel who accompanies him will depart. This is correct. However, I have not seen people concerned for this, because they later sweep the house in a permitted manner, as stated in siman 337. End quote. Accordingly, in a place where people are not accustomed to sweep on Friday night, it is forbidden to throw things away from the table. See Responsa Maharil, siman 163. The Maharshal had two tablecloths on the table in addition to the upper covering, because when the cloth was shaken out the table would otherwise be left uncovered. Furthermore, the cloth hangs a handbreadth below the table, and spreading it forms a tent. He therefore left the lower cloth in place at all times and placed another over it. Mateh Moshe. As stated in siman 315, se'if 11, this does not constitute a tent.
