One should clear spiderwebs from the house (R. Chaim, peace be upon him). The Ari was very particular to eat at a four-legged table, corresponding to the Temple table, and to bless over two bundles of hadas at all three kiddushes. On Friday night he would place on the table the cup of birkat hamazon with a little wine and some crumbs, but not a whole loaf. See Yoreh Deah at the end of siman 178 and see siman 180.
And arrange the beds. Meaning the beds on which they sit. These are the words of Darkei Moshe: Mahariv wrote in his rulings that it is permitted to throw something off the table, and one need not be concerned for those who say that one thereby throws away the angel; end quote. Perhaps their words are metaphorical: if one throws something and cannot sweep the house on Shabbat, the house will be disgraceful and the good angel that accompanies him will leave; the matter is correct. But I have not seen people concerned for this, and afterward they sweep the house with something permitted, as written in siman 337; end quote. If so, in a place where the custom is not to sweep on Friday night, it is forbidden to throw. See Responsa Maharil, siman 163. Maharshal had two tablecloths on the table besides the upper one, because when one shakes out the tablecloth the table is found uncovered, and also because the tablecloth hangs a tefach over the table, and when one spreads it he makes an ohel. Therefore he would always leave the lower cloth in place and another on top of it (Mateh Moshe), as written in siman 315 seif 11, that this is not considered an ohel.
