Se'if katan 1: And he should spread the beds upon which they sit. This means also the beds upon which they sit, and the same applies to the beds upon which they sleep. The Bach derives this from the wording of the Tur, which, like the Shulchan Aruch, says 'and he should spread the beds' in the plural. Although the Gemara mentions only 'a set table and a made bed' in the singular, the Tur nevertheless uses the plural. For even regarding arranging provisions for a debtor, we rule that the debtor is left two beds, one upon which to sit and one upon which to sleep, as explained in Choshen Mishpat, siman 97. These are the words of the Gemara in Bava Metzia, folio 113. The reason explained there is that if a person does not walk four cubits after eating, it is dangerous for him. He is therefore given two beds, and the bed upon which he sleeps is placed four cubits from the bed upon which he sits while eating, so that he will become accustomed to walking four cubits after eating. Accordingly, here too there should be two prepared beds. The Gemara was not exact in using the singular. This is the substance of the Bach's words.
And the good angel will depart. This means that two angels accompany a person on Friday night when he leaves the synagogue, one of them a good angel, etc.
And Teshuas Shlomo writes that according to the wording of the hymn recited on Friday night when leaving the synagogue, whose final stanza is 'Depart in peace,' etc. It would seem that the angel departs immediately after the person arrives from the synagogue. If so, the words of Mahariv are incorrect, for according to Mahariv the angels must remain with the person throughout Shabbos. He writes that he saw a printed siddur that omitted the text 'Depart in peace' based on the direction of a great authority; see there for his resolution. In my humble opinion, it is simple that although the angel remains with the person throughout Shabbos, one should not alter the text 'Depart in peace.' It is analogous to 'Blessed shall you be when you enter, and blessed shall you be when you depart.' Although the angels are not departing now, one says 'Depart in peace' concerning the time of their departure, whenever it will occur.
The Maharshal had two tablecloths, etc., and when he spreads it, etc. This means that when he removes the cloth to shake it out and then spreads it again afterward, he forms a tent. See what is written in siman 315, se'if 11, concerning a canopy whose roof is not a handbreadth wide, etc., where it is permitted to spread a sheet over it, since in such a case it does not constitute a tent, provided that the sheet does not hang a handbreadth below the bed. The bed is itself a handbreadth wide and thus forms a roof, so if the sheet hangs a handbreadth below the bed it forms a tent. The Magen Avraham writes there that this is forbidden specifically with a canopy, because the sheet was spread to provide shade. With our beds, however, even if the sheet hangs a handbreadth below the base, there is no concern, since the sheet was not made to provide shade and the prohibition of forming a tent does not apply. The same should therefore be true of a tablecloth, since it is not spread to provide shade. Thus only one reason remains: if the cloth is removed, the table will be left uncovered, which is not respectful to Shabbos.
