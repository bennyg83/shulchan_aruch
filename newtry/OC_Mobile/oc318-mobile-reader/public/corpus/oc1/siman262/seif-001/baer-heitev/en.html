His table. The Arizal was very particular to eat at a four-legged table, corresponding to the table in the Temple, and to recite the blessing over two bundles of myrtle at each of the three Kiddushim. On Friday night he placed on the table the cup used for Birkat HaMazon, with a little wine and a few crumbs, but not a whole loaf. See siman 180. One should clear cobwebs from the house. Rabbeinu Chaim, may he rest in peace.
When he comes from the synagogue. On Friday night, when his mother blesses him, he should kiss her hands. The mystical reason is explained in the teachings of the Arizal.
Set. The Maharshal had two tablecloths on the table in addition to the upper covering over the bread, because when the tablecloth is shaken out the table would otherwise be left uncovered. Furthermore, the cloth hangs a handbreadth below the table, and when he spreads it he forms a tent. He therefore left the lower cloth in place at all times, so that spreading the other cloth merely adds to a temporary tent and is permitted. Mateh Moshe. See siman 315, se'if 11.
