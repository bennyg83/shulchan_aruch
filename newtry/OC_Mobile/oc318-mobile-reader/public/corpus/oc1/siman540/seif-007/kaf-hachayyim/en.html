7) There. Likewise, if he makes a parapet for a roof, he builds it with unskilled workmanship. This means that he places stones one upon another, etc., or fences it with reeds, etc., as stated above. See Ritva's novellae, where he writes that the mishnah concerns a roof upon which people do not ordinarily walk. It therefore is not obligated in a parapet, but the owner wishes to make one for protection. If the mitzvah of making a parapet applied, why should he not construct it in the ordinary manner? See there. The other halachic authorities, however, write without qualification, implying that no distinction is made. Nevertheless, it appears that if small children are present and walk near the parapet, and there is concern that they might demolish it if it is made of a light material, creating danger, Heaven forbid, one may construct it from a strong material that the children cannot demolish.
