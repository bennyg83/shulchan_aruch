But first, etc. The Bach asked why it did not explain that this is specifically when the levi is greater than the Yisrael or they are equal, but when the Yisrael is greater than the levi, the Yisrael reads first. So too the Taz and Magen Avraham ruled, and so appears primary. So too they wrote in Maharil and Matteh Moshe, that this is the practice. But regarding the Bach's objection to the essence of this law on the basis of Matza Kasav, cited by the Beis Yosef, that according to everyone he reads first, for from Rashi's language in his first explanation, where he explained that he should not read at all, it implies that even first, a levi should not be called at all, lest they come to elevate him to the status of a kohen, and that Abudraham wrote that this is the practice, and Nachalas Tzvi agreed, in my humble opinion the main view is like the Rama. What Rashi explained, that he should not read at all, means that he should not read at all after a Yisrael, even when the Yisrael is greater than he, but first a levi may read. There is no room to err about them, just as we are not concerned when a Yisrael reads first. This appears to be the view of Abudraham, and so it also appears in Responsa Rashba, siman 214. Know this, for otherwise the custom would contradict the custom, since Sefer Tanya wrote that the custom is that he reads first, as Maharil and Matteh Moshe wrote. I also found explicitly in Responsa Maharam of Rothenburg, siman 555, like the Matza Kasav cited in Beis Yosef; see there, where it is explained more fully.
