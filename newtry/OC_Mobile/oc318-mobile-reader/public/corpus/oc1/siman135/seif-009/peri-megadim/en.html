They practice. The Taz — I must expand here on the Gemara in Gittin 59b: kohen after kohen — the first is flawed; Levi after Levi — both are flawed, as written in letter 8. And if so, kohen after kohen with Levi and Israel in between — there is no first concern, but there is a second concern that he is a challal and goes up fourth. Beit Yosef gives three answers: (1) that afterward there are also Levi and Israel, and therefore the last one or the maftir is forbidden (and one must say regarding Levi in our case that he too may not go up, unlike R' Yehudah in Beit Yosef, for according to two of the explanations he may go up; and the Mechaber in siman 66 ruled that a Levi may not go up — it seems the fourth explanation also may not; and if he omitted the word "all," see in letter 6 that one need not distinguish whether there is a kohen or not). Peri Chadash questioned: the concern is about those leaving between one reader and the next (apparently a scribal error in "first" — and one can say "the first" means the second kohen and the second Levi; for certainly when they are in synagogue they know the kohen is valid, otherwise how did they call Levi after Israel; and likewise the second Levi is valid, otherwise how did they call Israel after kohen; and for two flawed ones we are not concerned, but the concern is those leaving who will say the first kohen is the second and is flawed). For in the Gemara too it says regarding those leaving between one reader and the next that it is permitted to leave — or they leave before the rolling, for they assume he is the sixth, see there. Bach questioned: since Tur writes "with Israel in between," it implies when there is no Levi and the kohen read twice; and he stated a greater novelty that even though only one interrupts, we are not concerned (and greater than this — Levi after Levi with Israel in between — we are not concerned for the first; and Beit Yosef hinted to this when he wrote "and if there is no Levi he reads twice," implying that Israel's interruption alone is meant; and if so the second kohen has concern of being flawed when there is no Levi; and he holds that we do not permit him to read twice in all this — Magen Avraham 14; and one can explain that Tur discusses a case like the Gemara where there is no second concern, and in the Gemara it is one established as kohen through his father alone, and Tur is one established through father and mother as the second, and only one flaw; and with interruption it is permitted — and therefore the last maftir is permitted). Another answer of Beit Yosef: the kohen should say on the second, and by law of the Gemara it is forbidden because of those entering; therefore Levi after Levi — both are flawed; but in our time, thank God, there is no concern of those entering, and it helps for a second kohen and likewise for Levi; and we require Israel's interruption so we do not disagree with the explicit law of the Gemara. Behold in Shulchan Aruch — necessarily the second answer: (1) that he did not mention kohen-Levi-Israel on the second; and (2) Levi after Levi with Israel in between — the second flaw is Israel; and what is "and therefore" that Rama wrote — on the contrary, the last and maftir are forbidden — we learn like Beit Yosef's second answer, even though he is a kohen, and it helps for Levi after Levi, and there is no first flaw because immediately they would murmur. However, one can say only regarding the second that even though he is a kohen there is no flaw at all. Perishah explains similarly in another manner: nowadays kohen after kohen is forbidden because of the first flaw as in the days of the Gemara, and on the second there is no flaw for the kohen to say (even without "even though"); but with Israel's interruption they would immediately murmur. And the question on Levi after Levi who said "both are flawed" — the Levi should say on the second; and one can say without the interruption there is proof that the first is flawed, not so with interruption. The third answer: Beit Yosef wrote, and one can also say this custom, etc.; and it appears from the Taz that this is another answer, not the first. And now the words of the Taz are explained: he brought Beit Yosef's question and answer 1; and questioned Bach's difficulty, and Beit Yosef hinted to this from what he wrote "and if there is no Levi the kohen reads twice" (what Bach questioned — we wrote Bach's explanation that it discusses one established through father and mother, like the Gemara); and he brought a third answer (as he understood it) and questioned why such a case is not forbidden, and resolved that the Sages instituted in the mishnah kohen-Levi-Israel and whoever changes causes error, etc.; and likewise Levi after Levi when there is a kohen is permitted, and when there is no kohen it is forbidden as in siman 66 and seif 138 (1).
