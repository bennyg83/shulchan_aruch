I must elaborate here concerning Gittin 59b: when a kohen is called after another kohen, there is concern for a defect in the first; when a levi is called after another levi, there is concern for a defect in both, as I wrote in seif katan 8. Accordingly, when a second kohen is called after an intervening levi and Yisrael, the first concern is absent, but the second concern remains, namely that the second kohen may be a chalal, although he is receiving the fourth aliyah. Beit Yosef gives three answers. First, afterward another levi and Yisrael are also called. According to this, calling the last aliyah or maftir to a kohen would be forbidden. The same should apply to a levi in our case, unlike Rabbi Yehudah cited by Beit Yosef, who permits him according to two explanations. The Mechaber in seif 6 rules that a levi may not be called. This appears to mean even third or fourth, although he omitted the word “at all.” See what I wrote in seif katan 6, that there is no reason to distinguish between a case where a kohen is present and one where he is absent. Peri Chadash asks that there remains concern for those who leave between one reader and the next. The word “first” there appears to be a scribal error. It may mean the first, namely the second kohen, and the second, namely the second levi. Those present in the synagogue know that the kohen is valid, for otherwise how could a levi have been called after a Yisrael? Likewise they know the second levi is valid, for otherwise how could a Yisrael have been called after a kohen? We are not concerned that two people are disqualified. The concern is only for those who leave, who may say that the first, meaning the second kohen, is disqualified. In the Gemara too, the concern is for those who leave between one reader and the next, since it is permitted to leave then. Alternatively, they leave before the scroll is rolled, assuming that he is the sixth reader. See there. Bach asks that since the Tur writes that a Yisrael intervenes, it implies that no levi was present and the kohen read twice. The Tur therefore teaches a greater novelty, that even though only one Yisrael intervenes, we are not concerned. An even greater case would be a levi called after another levi with only a Yisrael between them, where we are not concerned for a defect in the first. Beit Yosef alludes to this when he writes, “If there is no levi, the kohen reads twice,” implying that the interruption of a Yisrael alone is enough. According to this, however, there should be concern that the second kohen is disqualified, since no levi is present. Bach holds that we do not permit so much as allowing the second kohen to read twice. See Magen Avraham, seif katan 14. He answers that the Tur discusses a case resembling the Gemara, where there is no second concern. In the Gemara, the kohen was established only through his father; in the Tur’s case, the second kohen was established through both his father and mother, so only the first defect is possible, and an interruption removes the concern. According to this, the last aliyah or maftir may be given to a kohen. Beit Yosef gives a second answer: the gabbai announces regarding the second, “Although he is a kohen.” Under the strict law of the Gemara this would still be forbidden because of those entering, and this is why a levi after a levi creates concern for both. Nowadays, however, regrettably, there is no concern for those entering. This announcement therefore suffices for a second kohen and likewise for a levi. An intervening Yisrael is still required so that we not openly depart from the explicit law of the Gemara. The Shulchan Aruch necessarily follows this second answer. First, it does not require a kohen, levi, and Yisrael after the second kohen. Second, in a case of a levi after a levi with a Yisrael between them, the concern regarding the second is that he is a Yisrael, yet Rama writes “therefore,” whereas according to the first answer the last aliyah or maftir should be forbidden. This shows that he follows the second answer. The announcement “although he is a kohen” also removes the concern in a case of levi after levi. The concern for a defect in the first is absent because people would immediately have protested. Yet this explanation is incomplete. Rather, regarding the second person, the announcement “although he is a kohen” removes any concern for either of them. Perishah offers a similar but different explanation. Nowadays, kohen after kohen remains forbidden because of concern for the first, just as in the days of the Gemara. There is no concern regarding the second because they announce that he is a kohen, without the word “although.” When a Yisrael intervenes, people would immediately have protested. The difficulty remains regarding levi after levi, where the Gemara says there is concern for both; why not announce regarding the second that he is a levi? The answer is that without an interruption there is proof that one of them is disqualified, whereas with an interruption there is no such proof. Beit Yosef’s third answer begins, “It may also be said that this custom,” etc. Taz understands this as a distinct answer, not part of the first. Taz’s words are now clear. He cites Beit Yosef’s question and first answer, raises Bach’s objection, and notes that Beit Yosef alluded to it by writing, “If there is no levi, the kohen reads twice.” We explained Bach’s objection as referring to one whose father and mother are established, parallel to the Gemara. Taz then cites the third answer, as he understands it, and asks why the case should not still be forbidden. He answers that Chazal instituted the order kohen, levi, Yisrael in the Mishnah, and any deviation causes misunderstanding. Likewise, levi after levi is permitted when a kohen is present, but prohibited when no kohen is present, as in seif 6. See siman 138, seif 1.
And in the sacred words of Levush, he omitted the case of a levi called after another levi with a Yisrael intervening, because he explicitly adopts Beit Yosef’s first answer there, and that answer does not resolve the case of levi after levi. Namely, in a case of kohen after kohen there remains concern for a second levi among those entering, who may see the second levi and not know that a kohen was called before him.
