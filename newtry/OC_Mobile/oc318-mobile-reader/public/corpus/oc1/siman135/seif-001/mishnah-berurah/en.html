The Rambam wrote in chapter 12 of Hilkhot Tefillah: Moshe Rabbeinu enacted for Israel that they read from the Torah publicly on Shabbat, and on Monday and Thursday mornings, so that they should not remain three days without hearing Torah. Ezra enacted that they also read at Minchah every Shabbat because of the idlers, and he also enacted that on Monday and Thursday three people read and that they not read fewer than ten verses. In Moshe Rabbeinu's time the enactment was not specifically that three read and that exactly ten verses be read; Ezra came and enacted this. In the Yerushalmi it says: Moshe enacted for Israel that they read from the Torah on Shabbatot, Yomim Tovim, Rosh Chodesh, and Chol HaMoed; the Rif brings this in Perek HaKorei Omed, and so it is in the Rambam, chapter 13, halakhah 8.
(1) On Monday and Thursday: in the morning. Although after the fact its time is all day, nevertheless the mitzvah initially is to do it early.
(2) And we do not add to them: because of the people's loss of work. For this reason we also do not read a haftarah.
(3) It is permitted to add: so that both of them will be able to ascend to the Torah. As a matter of law, the later authorities wrote that this is not the practice, and all the more so regarding baalei berit one certainly should not practice this way.
