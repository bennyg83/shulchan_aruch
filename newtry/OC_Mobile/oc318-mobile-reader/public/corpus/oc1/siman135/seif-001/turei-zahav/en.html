And they do not add. The reason is because of interruption of labor, as stated in the Gemara, chapter HaKorei Es HaMegillah: This is the rule: whenever there is interruption of labor for the people, such as a communal fast and Tisha B’Av, three people are called. Rashi explains that because they remain longer in the synagogue, labor is interrupted. When there is no interruption of labor for the people, such as Rosh Chodesh and Chol HaMoed, four people are called. Rashi explains that on Rosh Chodesh women do not perform labor. For this same reason there is no maftir, according to Rashi.
And if there were two bridegrooms, etc. Mordechai, chapter HaKorei, writes this in the name of Ritzba: “It appears to me that it is like a festival, as we say in the first chapter of Kesubos that a bridegroom is compared to a festival, and the days of his celebration are considered a regel. Just as they said that although mourning does not apply on a festival, private mourning practices do apply, so too regarding a bridegroom, mourning does not apply to him.” This implies that all seven days of celebration are equivalent to the wedding day, since mourning does not apply throughout them, as stated in the first chapter of Kesubos. See what I wrote in siman 131, seif 4. I am astonished how Ritzba compares the personal festival of a bridegroom to a festival of all Israel. On the contrary, the opposite is evident. The reason additions are not made is interruption of the public’s labor, as written above. What benefit is the bridegroom’s festival when the entire congregation suffers interruption of labor and for them it is no festival? Ritzba himself writes there that if only one bridegroom is present, four are not called, since it is a festival only for the bridegrooms themselves and not for others. The same reasoning should apply here: interruption of labor harms the public. Moreover, if so, the bridegroom should also read maftir, since for him it is Yom Tov. Rather, it is clear that we do not follow his personal status, and therefore the bridegroom does not read the haftarah. Rama further compares participants in a bris to this case. Levush writes that this is not the custom, and it appears to me that those who do not follow this practice act correctly.
