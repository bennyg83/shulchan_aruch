Magen Avraham. The Rif states, and so forth: Moshe instituted for Israel that they should read from the Torah on Shabbos, Yom Tov, Rosh Chodesh, and Chol HaMoed, as it is stated, “And Moshe spoke,” and so forth. Ezra instituted for Israel that three people should read from the Torah on Monday, Thursday, and Shabbos at Minchah. End quote. This requires study, for in the chapter Merubah it states, and the correct text should read, that the Magen Avraham’s question is why he wrote that Ezra instituted the reading on Monday and Thursday. In Merubah it states that the prophets in the days of Moshe instituted Monday and Thursday, while Ezra added Shabbos at Minchah.
And Tosafos, etc. We say that Rabbi holds that Krias Shema must be read in Lashon HaKodesh, for it is written “and they shall be,” meaning they shall remain in their original form. The Gemara concludes that Rabbi necessarily holds that the entire Torah may be read in any language, for if it could be read only in Lashon HaKodesh, why would the verse “and they shall be” be needed regarding Krias Shema? Is Krias Shema inferior to the rest of the Torah? Rashi explains “the entire Torah may be read in any language” to mean reading from the Torah. Tosafos write that this explanation is unsatisfactory, for Ezra instituted Torah reading, and before Ezra came, why would the verse “and they shall be” have been needed? End quote. Bach asks how Tosafos could write that Ezra instituted it, since the prophets in the days of Moshe instituted it. Their principal question, however, is, etc., for then it is inapplicable, etc. This excludes the words of Bach in siman 685, who writes that if it had been instituted in the days of Moshe it would have been D’Oraisa, and therefore the verse “and they shall be” was necessary. His words are astonishing, for the Gemara states that the prophets arose and instituted it, showing that it was only an enactment and not D’Oraisa.
The number of those called to the Torah, as stated in the Gemara. In the chapter Merubah too it says that Ezra’s enactment was to read on Monday, Thursday, and Shabbos at Minchah. The Gemara asks: But was not Monday and Thursday already an enactment of the prophets in the days of Moshe? It answers that originally they instituted one person reading three verses, and Ezra came and instituted three people and ten verses.
If so, Moshe instituted the number, etc. Meaning, the Rif lists the enactment of Moshe and the enactment of Ezra in parallel. Just as regarding Ezra he means the number of those called, so too regarding the enactment of Moshe.
Seif katan 1: And one may not, etc. Because of loss of work. Although at Shabbos Minchah there is no loss of work, since they expounded until close to nightfall, the Sages did not wish to burden the congregation by adding aliyos. So Rashi explains there in Megillah.
