If the Sefer Torah is open, etc. In siman 66 I concluded that one may be lenient if he is engaged in the blessings of Krias Shema; see there. One should not object that where he does not interrupt to be called to the Torah there is concern for his reputation. It appears to me that where an aveirah is involved, Chazal were not concerned for his reputation. A parallel is found in the chapter Kol HaYad: It is preferable that people cast suspicion upon his children than that he make himself wicked before Hashem for even one moment.
And it is proper that the kohen leave. Beit Yosef proves that he need not leave, since the Gemara does not mention that kohanim would leave the synagogue. Rama therefore writes merely that it is proper for him to leave, implying that this is not required by law. In my view, this is no proof at all. He certainly must leave, lest people say that it became known that he is a chalal and disqualified from the kehunah. In the Gemara they were not concerned for this, because the custom there was that a Torah scholar would read in place of a kohen. Nowadays, however, an ignorant kohen always precedes a Torah scholar who is a Yisrael. If the kohen remains in the synagogue, people will not know that he did not fast and will instead say that he is a chalal. See what I wrote in siman 566.
