(16) If the Sefer Torah is open. The same applies even if the Sefer Torah has not yet been taken out: they do not delay taking it out until the kohen finishes his prayer, because of burdening the congregation.
(17) He is not permitted. Even according to the first view in siman 66, seif 4, that he interrupts, this applies only if he was already called, because of the honor of Torah. Initially, however, he should not be called. There is no concern for casting doubt on his kehunah, for everyone sees that he is occupied with Krias Shema and prayer. The later authorities agree that this is not limited to Shema, but applies also while he is reciting Birchos Krias Shema. See above, siman 66, Mishnah Berurah seif katan 26, where we concluded that one may be lenient and call him initially when he is between sections and there is no other kohen, and certainly when he is occupied with Pesukei DeZimrah.
(18) It is preferable that the kohen leave. Strictly speaking, even if he does not leave, a Yisrael may be called in his place because he is unable to receive the aliyah. Initially, however, it is preferable that he leave the synagogue, lest there be concern for his status since not everyone knows that he is not fasting. If another kohen who is fasting is present, all agree that he need not leave; he should merely inform the chazzan not to call him. A congregation had an ancient practice that on Shabbos Bereishis a congregant donated money for synagogue needs in order to be called first at the beginning of the Torah. If the kohen refuses to waive his precedence, his objection is disregarded and the congregation follows its custom, since it is an ancient practice instituted for the honor of Torah. Maharik, shoresh 9; see there at length in praise of this custom. Knesses HaGedolah and Peri Chadash agree that even if the kohen refuses to leave, he is disregarded and the donating Yisrael is called. This applies specifically where the custom was ancient, universally accepted, and established with the consent of the kohanim, whose longstanding practice was to leave when the Yisrael was called, because of the enhancement and honor of Torah created by large donations for the opening aliyah. It does not apply where a city seeks to establish a fixed practice of selling the mitzvos without the kohanim’s consent and requiring them to leave during Krias HaTorah, which they have no authority to impose. Responsa Chasam Sofer 24; likewise Responsa Shivas Tzion.
