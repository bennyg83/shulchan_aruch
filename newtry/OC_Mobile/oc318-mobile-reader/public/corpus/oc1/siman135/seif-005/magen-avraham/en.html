He is not permitted. Even according to the view in siman 66, seif 4, that one may interrupt, that applies when he was already called. Initially, however, he should not be called. There is no concern that people will question his status as a kohen, since he is occupied with Krias Shema. They do not wait until he finishes because of burdening the congregation. This is not limited to Krias Shema. The same applies whenever he is engaged in a section in which he may not interrupt, such as the blessings of Krias Shema. Beit Yosef, Rashba, and Maharik. It is possible, however, that while he is in Pesukei DeZimrah he may be called. See what I wrote in siman 139, seif 8.
And they call a Yisrael. They do not wait for the kohen because of the honor of the congregation. Rashba and Maharik. If no kohen is present, they follow the person greatest in wisdom and stature, even if another is older. Binyamin Ze'ev, simanim 243 and 253. If the local custom is that the first aliyah at the beginning of Bereishis is purchased for money, and a Yisrael purchases it, the kohen waives his honor and leaves the synagogue. On one occasion a kohen refused to leave the synagogue. It was permitted to compel him through the authorities to leave so that the custom and the honor of the Torah would not be nullified. Beit Yosef and Maharik. Knesses HaGedolah writes that even if he does not leave, the Yisrael should read. However, this does not appear from Maharik’s wording, for if so there would be no need to compel him through the authorities.
