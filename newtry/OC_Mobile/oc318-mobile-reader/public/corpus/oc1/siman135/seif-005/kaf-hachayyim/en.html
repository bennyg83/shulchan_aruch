5) There, gloss: If they omitted one Shabbos, etc. And the Maharam Mintz, siman 85, wrote that specifically two sidros they read if the reading of one Shabbos was omitted, since in any case we find that sometimes they read two sidros on one Shabbos in ordinary years; but if two Shabbosos were omitted, they do not read three sidros on the coming Shabbos, for we do not find any place where they read three sidros on one Shabbos. And likewise, if the parshiyos were joined on the previous Shabbos, there is no making them up, for there is no reasoning to divide them and read half the obligation on the coming Shabbos. And likewise, if they omitted parashas Vayechi, one should not read it with parashas Shemos, for when one reads two sidros, one man must read from the end of the first sidra to the beginning of the second sidra so that they be joined, and this is not proper to do with two separate scrolls; see there. And the Knesses HaGedolah cited him in the Hagahos HaTur, siman 282, and wrote that according to his words, if they omitted [reading] on a Shabbos whose parshiyos are not joined, and the coming Shabbos has joined parshiyos, one should not read the parashah of the previous week; see there. And so wrote the Olas Tamid there, ot 4; Ateres Zekeinim in this siman, ot 2; Magen Avraham, s.k. 4; Yad Aharon in the Hagahos HaTur; Soles Belulah, ot 1. However, in the Hagahos Minhagim he wrote concerning the aforementioned words of the Maharam Mintz that his words do not appear correct in his eyes; and the Eliyah Rabbah cited him, ot 2, and wrote that his reasoning is logical, that one should not skip any parashah, and all the more so when two are joined on this Shabbos, that they also read the sidra of the previous Shabbos; and the same applies when they omitted two or three Shabbosos, they read all the parshiyos that they omitted at one time; see there. And such is the view of the Bais Dovid in the laws of Shabbos, siman 106; and see there, where he refuted all the aforementioned words of the Maharam Mintz. And so wrote the Mateh Yehudah in siman 282, ot 14. And so he concluded in the responsa Devar Moshe, Orach Chaim, siman 50, and wrote that a shaliach tzibbur who erred on Shabbos Vayakhel-Pekudei, thinking that they were not joined, and read Vayakhel alone: he should read Pekudei on Shabbos Vayikra, along with parashas Vayikra, like the sefer of the Bais Dovid; and one should not say in this 'safek berachos lehakel,' since in every place where one reads from the Torah there is an obligation to bless; see there. And so wrote the Emes LeYaakov in the laws of error, siman 168, ot 2; Ledovid Emes, siman 9, ot 2. And one must read them in their order, on account of 'the Torah of Hashem is perfect': first the parashah that they omitted, and afterward the obligation of the day; and the majority of the seven who ascend should ascend for the obligation of the day. And it is better that one of those who ascend read the parashah that they omitted, and the rest for the obligation of the day, like the Rashal in the name of the sefer Leshon Chachamim; and see there. And in my humble opinion it appears that the one should read the entire omitted parashah, and also begin to read three verses in the parashah of the day's obligation, so that all of them read and bless for the day's obligation. And afterward I saw that so wrote the sefer HaShomer Emes, siman 15, ot 1; and the Pesach HaDvir cited him, ot 4, and concurred with his words, and wrote that it appears all agree in this; see there well. Only if the omitted parashah was the end of a sefer, such as Vayechi or Pekudei, then the one should not begin to read in the second sefer, which is Shemos or Vayikra, because it is not proper for one man to read in two separate scrolls, as mentioned above in the name of the Maharam Mintz; and see the Pesach HaDvir there. However, on Monday and Thursday they read the weekly parashah like the rest of the congregation, as stated below in ot 9; and further see below in siman 144, ot 2.
