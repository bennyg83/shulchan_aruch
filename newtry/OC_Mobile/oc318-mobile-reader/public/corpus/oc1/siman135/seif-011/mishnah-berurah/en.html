(38) A kohen or levi. If a Yisrael was called and is not present, another Yisrael may be called by name. There is no concern for casting doubt on a Yisrael’s status, since even a mamzer may receive an aliyah, as explained below in siman 282, seif 3.
(39) And he is not present. If that kohen is praying, another kohen may be called by name, since everyone sees that the first cannot receive the aliyah because he may not interrupt his prayer. Magen Avraham.
(40) Because of casting doubt on him. People may think that it was discovered in the interim that he was disqualified. The later authorities write that his son may nevertheless be called by name, because if the father were disqualified the son would also be disqualified. His father, however, may not be called, lest people say that the father had long ago married a divorcee, so that the father remains valid while the son is a chalal.
(41) He should ascend on his own. Shaarei Ephraim writes that if a kohen or levi was called for the final aliyah or maftir and is not present, another person may be called. Even if the replacement is also a kohen or levi, he may be called by name, because there is no basis here to say that the first person was found disqualified, since a Yisrael may also receive the final aliyah or maftir.
(42) He may call, etc. There is no concern that this casts doubt on the chazzan’s own status, since everyone understands that, because he is the chazzan, it is unreasonable to say that he would exclude all the other kohanim from aliyos. Thus, his omission is not because he is disqualified. Even if the gabbai is a kohen and has authority to call whomever he wishes, or in a place where aliyos are purchased and the purchaser is a kohen or levi, he may direct that another kohen or levi be called. This does not cast doubt on him, because everyone knows that the accepted way is to honor others.
