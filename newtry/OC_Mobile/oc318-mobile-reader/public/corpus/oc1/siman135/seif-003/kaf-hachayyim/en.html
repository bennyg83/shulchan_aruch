12) [Seif 3] A Kohen reads from the Torah first, etc. For so the Sages of blessed memory instituted, on account of the ways of peace, so that they not come to quarrel and each one say, 'I will read first'; and they also supported it with a verse, as it is written, 'And Moshe wrote this Torah and gave it to the Kohanim, the sons of Levi' -- and we ask: is it not so that I already know that the Kohanim are the sons of Levi? And we answer: rather, a Kohen first; and as it is also written regarding a Kohen, 'and you shall sanctify him,' for every matter of holiness, to be first, etc., and afterward the Levi, and afterward all the people, etc. And that which they needed to say 'on account of the ways of peace,' since they supported it with a verse: this is to say that even if the Kohen wishes to forgo his honor and give honor to his teacher or to one greater than him, he is not permitted. Levush; and it is from the Gemara Gittin, folio 59b. And it is implied from the words of the Levush that it is rabbinic and the verse is a support. However, see the Magen Avraham, siman 201, s.k. 4, who brought a dispute in this, and there are those who say that it is from the Torah; see there.
13) And this that we say that the Kohen is not permitted to give honor to one greater than him -- they said there in the Gemara: we said this only on Shabbosos and Yom Tov, when the multitude is present, but on Monday and Thursday, no. And we ask: but did not Rav Huna read as a Kohen on Shabbosos and Yom Tov? And they answered: Rav Huna is different, for even Rabbi Ami and Rabbi Asi, the distinguished Kohanim of Eretz Yisrael, would subordinate themselves to him. Until here. And concerning that which the Gemara distinguishes between Monday-and-Thursday and Shabbosos-and-Yom-Tov, the Tosafos wrote there in the name of Rabbeinu Chananel that this applies to them, for on a weekday they were busy with their work and were not present, but for us they are present on Monday and Thursday as on Yom Tov. End of his language. And the Beis Yosef cited him, and wrote: however, even on Shabbosos and Yom Tov, if a Kohen comes to give honor to a great and outstanding man, to whom all the Kohanim subordinate themselves, to ascend first, he is permitted, as we find that Rav and Rav Huna would read in place of Kohanim; only that in these generations the great ones were not particular to ascend first, and it is preferable for them to ascend at the end of those who ascend, and always the greatest in the congregation ascends at the end; and through this the Kohanim did not need to forgo their honor to the great ones of the generation. End of his language. And this is unlike the Taz, s.k. 3, who wrote that even granting permission is of no avail; see there. And see the Maamar Mordechai, ot 4, what he elaborated on this, and deliberated in the words of the Bach and the Derishah, and held firmly to the words of Maran, of blessed memory, and wrote concerning the aforementioned words of the Taz that they are not compelling; see there. And see the following ot, and further see below, ot 19, and siman 137, ot 4.
14) And the Maharik wrote in shoresh 9 concerning a congregation that had the custom that on Shabbos Bereishis one of the congregation would donate money for the needs of the synagogue in order to read first at the beginning of the Torah; and they also had the custom that if there is a Kohen there, he purchases that mitzvah or forgoes his honor and leaves the synagogue; see there. And the Beis Yosef cited him, and the Magen Avraham, s.k. 7. However, the Beis Yosef there disagreed with him, and wrote that if the Kohen forgoes his honor in order that the congregation fulfill their custom, he need not leave; and see there, where he brought supports for this from the Gemara, and wrote that we do not find that the Kohen must leave outside the synagogue except specifically regarding nesias kapayim, because it contains three positive commandments, and even that only if they said to him to ascend, as stated in siman 128; see there. And the Pri Chadash concurred with his words, ot 3. And so wrote the Knesses HaGedolah in the Hagahos Beis Yosef, that he found written in manuscript by the Maharar Levi Trivush the Frenchman, of blessed memory, that even if the Kohen does not leave the synagogue, the Yisrael who donated may ascend to read first; and the Emes LeYaakov cited him in the law of the ascent to the Sefer Torah, ot 46; Ledovid Emes, siman 5, ot 46 (and see the responsa Chasam Sofer, siman 24). And so wrote the Birkei Yosef, ot 5, in the name of the Mahar"sh Sha'ar Aryeh in manuscript, that there are those who have the custom that on the day of Shavuos the rav of the place reads in place of the Kohen, for the honor of the Torah, and the Kohen need not leave, and the shaliach tzibbur says explicitly, 'Let the rav stand,' etc. End of his language. And so wrote the Gaon Mahar"sh Kluger at the end of the sefer StaM, in the kuntres 'Prat V'Olelos,' in the addenda to the laws of Sefer Torah, folio 86a, that the Ashkenazim in Eretz Yisrael have the custom that a Yisrael reads in place of a Kohen, and sometimes the chazan says it even though there is a Kohen here, because guests who need to be given an aliyah to the Sefer Torah are frequently present among them, and presumably the Kohanim forgo to them, since it is known that it is specifically for the honor of the guests; there is no concern of coming to quarrel, for at a time when there are no guests they do not forgo, etc.; and further, great is human dignity, etc., since the way is to give aliyos to the guests; see there well. And our friend the Rav Eretz Chaim, may God protect and preserve him, cited him in this siman.
