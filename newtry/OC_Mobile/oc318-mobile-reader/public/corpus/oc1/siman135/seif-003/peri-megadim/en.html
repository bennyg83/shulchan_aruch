A kohen. See Taz and the commentary to the Yerushalmi, Gittin, chapter 5, where Rabbi Yehudah follows his own view and derives the law from “the kohanim, the sons of Levi, shall approach, and all the elders of Israel.” This indicates that the kohen precedes the elders of Israel. By contrast, according to “and you shall sanctify him,” one may say that the sanctity of a talmid chacham and of Torah is greater. See there. Levush cites Rav Mattanah’s derivation together with “and you shall sanctify him,” whereas Nechalas Tzvi, seif katan 5, cites only “and he gave it to the kohanim” in Devarim 31. I do not know why all of them also cite “and you shall sanctify him,” since there are many practical differences, as I wrote at the beginning of the siman. Bach and Perishah answer that we require a case where everyone submits to the scholar because of his extraordinary wisdom, something not found nowadays. If they submit to him for some other reason, it does not suffice. If he is preeminent in his generation, the lesser person must acknowledge that he is greater. See there. Therefore, the custom is that the kohen receives the first aliyah. In seif 4 the Mechaber omitted the word “today,” which appears in Rambam, following the approach by which he resolved this in Beit Yosef. Even if the kohen waives his honor, he may not do so nowadays, even on Monday and Thursday.
