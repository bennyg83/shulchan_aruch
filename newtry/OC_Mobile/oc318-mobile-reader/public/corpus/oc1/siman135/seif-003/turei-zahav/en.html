A kohen reads, etc. The Tur writes that the Rosh’s words imply that if a Yisrael is exceptionally great and distinguished, he may read before a kohen, for in the chapter HaNizakin we find that Rav read in place of a kohen, and likewise Rav Huna, because everyone was subordinate to them. Rav Amram, however, writes that whenever a kohen is present, a Yisrael has no permission to read before him, even if he is the nasi of Israel. Rav Natronai likewise writes that even an ignorant kohen precedes a Torah scholar who is a Yisrael. Beit Yosef discusses this at length and explains that Rav Natronai and Rav Amram refer to a case where the kohen did not waive his honor. This is incorrect, for if so they should have said, “unless the kohen grants him permission.” Moreover, the Rosh too speaks specifically where the kohen grants permission, as is explicit from the fact that his discussion follows the phrase, “if he granted permission,” etc. This implies that according to Rav Amram, permission is ineffective. Nor can one say that Rav possessed a unique superiority over the entire world, and likewise Rav Huna, so no law may be derived from them. The Talmud made the matter depend only on everyone being subordinate to them, and it is obvious that people are subordinate to the nasi of Israel. It appears to me that Rav Amram and Rav Natronai hold that since Rabbi Chelbo said there in the Gemara, “Who follows them?” meaning after the kohen and levi, “Torah scholars appointed as leaders over the community,” it follows that even though he is a Torah scholar and communal leader, he reads only after the kohen and levi. They therefore disagree with the precedent of Rav, and Rav Amram and Rav Natronai rule accordingly. Beit Yosef himself later cites Sefer HaManhig, who writes that Rav Natronai brought proof from this passage. According to this, even the kohen’s permission is ineffective.
