But Taz — per my simple understanding of our master's view — since he had a special intention, as I will say. For one who examines Rashi Gittin 59b s.v. "the bundle separated" finds two explanations, and in each there are three explanations per the plain language: the first wording is a stringency — Levi should not read at all in the fourth [aliyah]; the reason: although an Israelite may ascend in place of a priest, nevertheless a Levi has importance, and if they accustom thus they will say his importance is that he is a priest. The second wording is a full leniency — Israel reads first and Levi reads second; and that of Ketubot refers to equals (see Tosafot s.v. "the bundle separated"); and this is Rosh's view as Bach understood, and what Rosh wrote "Levi should not read in his place" means in the priest's place, etc. Rosh's proof from R. Yehuda bar Nachmani to say "equals" — Levi first is not proven from Ketubot; but Taz, per force, considered all Rosh meant as Bach, explaining "in his place" plainly as second place with two stringencies. First wording: Levi should not read in fourth at all; second: should not read in his [second] place, but first may be whichever he wishes — Israel, and Levi should not read after him, or Levi reads first; and from Ketubot there is no proof, only from R. Yehuda bar Nachmani (and Rosh's "equals" is somewhat difficult — before priest is not his place here). Therefore for wording two "in his place" — the fourth reads. Third: wording one — no reading in fourth, including even a great Israelite — for the first reads Levi; wording two — Levi reads second with a great Israelite; Ketubot — equals; and this is as Beit Yosef found written (and Magen Avraham siman 10). Taz left Rosh as two stringencies in wording one and two; therefore he raised three difficulties: (1) should have written "and some say"; (2) for wording two the fourth is permitted, as he derives from "in his place"; (3) should have conditioned "equals" specifically for Levi first per Rosh. Thus Rama ruled all like stringency of fourth — Levi does not read even with a great Israelite, lest they say the Levi is a priest; but when equals, he relied to be lenient per second wording — Levi reads first specifically; and "may" means it can sometimes be so that Levi precedes, but when Israel is great Levi does not read first; also fourth does not read — not like Rosh; therefore he did not write "and some say." Magen Avraham's view is as we said there and here; we cannot elaborate.
