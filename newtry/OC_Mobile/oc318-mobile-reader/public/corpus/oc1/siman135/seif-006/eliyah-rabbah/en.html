If the kohen is not fasting, etc. If they erred and called someone who is not fasting, the Bach in siman 566 wrote that he should go up and bless, and so I found in Responsa Emunas Shmuel, siman 46, unlike what is implied by Nachalas Tzvi in this siman. However, in the Beis Yosef there it implies that there is even a prohibition if he goes up, and so the Taz there ruled, and so it seems to me. Maharik himself, shoresh 7, wrote that although the fasts of Monday, Thursday, and Monday are only a mere custom, nevertheless we set aside the honor of the kohanim in order to uphold this custom, etc.; examine this carefully. Emunas Shmuel further wrote there that at Shacharis one initially calls him, since its reading is from the enactment of Ezra, and that the Bach wrote likewise in siman 566. This is astonishing, for this is not stated explicitly there. On the contrary, from his writing that the custom is that he does not go up when they read Vayechal, it implies even at Shacharis, because of the custom. So too the Magen Avraham there wrote this as his own view. It also seems to me that the responsum of Maharik cited above is speaking of Shacharis, where in any case they read because of the fast; but at Mincha, even though the fast is by custom, it would not enter the mind of the kohanim to read, since the reading is because of the fast. Examine this carefully. To explain it as referring to Mincha is very forced. Therefore, initially one should be concerned for this, but if they called him, he goes up at Shacharis of a market day. The Magen Avraham wrote there that if there is another kohen, he should enter the synagogue and tell the chazzan not to call him.
