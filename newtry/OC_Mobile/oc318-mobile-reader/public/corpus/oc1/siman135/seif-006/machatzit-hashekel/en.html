Seif katan 6: He does not, etc. Even according to the opinion, etc. Meaning, Beit Yosef in siman 66 cites two opinions whether one may interrupt. Although Beit Yosef there decided in accordance with the view that one may not interrupt, he should not have ruled here without qualification. He should likewise have recorded the dispute and then ruled in accordance with the view that one may not interrupt. A practical difference may be found in Shach, Yoreh Deah, end of siman 242, in the principles of deciding questions of prohibition and permission.
There is no concern for discrediting him. Meaning, people will not say that he is not a kohen because he was not called, thereby casting doubt on his kehunnah, since everyone knows that he is standing at a point where he may not interrupt.
And we do not wait, etc. Meaning, the congregation is not obligated to wait in order to give him precedence in being called, despite the obligation from the verse “You shall sanctify him,” as explained below at the end of siman 201. Rashba answers that concern for burdening the congregation overrides this.
For burdening the congregation is weightier than the honor of the kohen. Strictly speaking, even a learned convert precedes an ignorant kohen. All the more so, concern for burdening the congregation overrides the honor of a kohen. See Beit Yosef, who cites the words of Rashba.
During Pesukei DeZimrah it is permitted, etc. Although interruption is forbidden, as stated above in siman 51, seif 4, it is nevertheless not as stringent there as in other places where interruption is forbidden.
