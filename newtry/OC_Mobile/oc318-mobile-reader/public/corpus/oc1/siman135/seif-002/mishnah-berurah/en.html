(4) At Minchah and on Monday and Thursday. If they erred and read for the three aliyos from the first parashah of the previous week’s order, or from the parashah of the week following the coming week, whether this invalidates the reading after the fact is discussed in Pischei Teshuvah, which cites the views of the later authorities.
(5) If they failed, etc. This refers to a case where the reading was omitted the entire day because of a quarrel or the like. If they omitted only the Shacharis reading, but by Minchah calm had been restored, then if there is time to read the entire parashah they should read the entire parashah at Minchah and call seven men, because the entire Shabbos day is a time for the reading. If there was no calm even at Minchah, they should not read the entire parashah from the previous Shabbos on Monday or Thursday, because weekday reading would cause the people to lose work. Rather, they read only the passage from the coming Shabbos’s order, not from the previous Shabbos. Dagul Merevavah; see there for the reasoning.
(6) One Shabbos. If they failed to read for several Shabbosos, some say that on the following Shabbos they read only the last parashah immediately preceding the current one. Others say that the congregation must make up all the parashiyos they omitted. Biur HaGra implies that he agrees with the first view.
(7) The reading of the parashah, etc. If two parashiyos were joined on the Shabbos when the reading was omitted, Maharam Mintz holds that they are not made up at all, because we never find three parashiyos read on one Shabbos. Nor do we say to read only one of them, because there is no partial remedy. The same applies if two parashiyos are joined on the present Shabbos: the previous Shabbos’s parashah is not made up, for the same reason. Hagahos Minhagim disputes all this, and Eliyah Rabbah agrees that whatever was omitted on the previous Shabbos must always be made up. Magen Giborim also inclines to this view. Some say that if Parashas Vayechi was omitted, it should not be read together with Parashas Shemos, because when two parashiyos are read one man must read from the end of the first into the beginning of the second so that they are joined, and this should not be done from two separate Sifrei Torah. The same applies to Pekudei and Vayikra and similar cases. Others disagree and hold that it is always made up. Shulchan Atzei Shittim writes that one who follows this practice should not be protested. Shaarei Ephraim writes that if the reading was omitted in one synagogue but most of that congregation heard Krias HaTorah in another synagogue, they need not make it up. If most of that congregation did not hear the reading at all, then even though other synagogues in the city read properly, those who did not hear it and comprise the majority of this congregation must make up the reading.
(8) They read that parashah. They read in the order written in the Torah, first the parashah of the previous Shabbos and afterward that of the present Shabbos. Or Zarua. The reason given there is that from the days of Moshe Rabbeinu the enactment was to read the Torah in its parashiyos and complete it, in order to complete for the people the mitzvos and statutes.
