If they missed one Shabbat, etc. This is specifically where on the previous Shabbat they did not read only one parashah; then on the next Shabbat they read that parashah together with the parashah belonging to that Shabbat, because in any case we sometimes find that two parashiyot are read on one Shabbat. But if two Shabbatot were missed, and likewise if on the Shabbat they missed they were supposed to read two parashiyot, on the second Shabbat they do not read three parashiyot, for nowhere do we find that three parashiyot are read on one Shabbat (Responsa Maharam, siman 85). Likewise, if that Shabbat already requires reading two parashiyot, they also do not read the missed parashah, for the same reason, as appears to me. It seems that one should distinguish: if two Shabbatot were missed, each time one parashah, then it must be that they have permission on the next Shabbat to read one missed parashah together with the parashah belonging to that Shabbat. But if one Shabbat was missed on which two parashiyot were read together, they should not read the parashah belonging to that Shabbat together with one of the two missed parashiyot, because since they were joined on the previous Shabbat they are like one parashah, and there is no logic at all to divide and read on the next Shabbat half of the prior day's obligation. Also, even where one parashah was missed, we said that on the following Shabbat that parashah is read with the parashah belonging to that Shabbat only if that parashah was not at the end of one of the five books of the Torah. But if it was one of the parashiyot at the end of a book, such as Vayechi, Pekudei, and the like, then even if only one parashah was missed it should not be read with the parashah belonging to that Shabbat, for there is no logic in reading from one book to another on one Shabbat, as appears to me from the words of Maharam Mintz.
If the sefer Torah is open and the kohen is reciting Shema, and likewise if he is praying, the law is the same. If there are other kohanim there, it is permitted to call them by name, for there is no concern here of blemishing his status, since everyone sees that he is not ascending because he is reciting Shema or because he is praying (Mahar"r Mordechai Yaffe). For this reason, on a fast day, if they call a kohen who did not fast and he says that he did not fast, there is also no concern of blemishing his status; therefore he need not leave, as appears to me. Nevertheless, it is better for him to leave, so that he not enter the category of shortening his days, where they call him to bless and he does not bless, as appears to me. Some wrote that even one who is not fasting may ascend to the Torah (my teacher in the Bach). A kohen may not waive his honor, but at a meal he may waive it. If a scholar gives precedence to an ignorant kohen to bless before him as a fixed law of the priesthood, about this it is said, "those who hate Me love death." But if the scholar gives permission to an ignorant kohen, there is no concern, for a scholar can give permission even to an Israelite (Mordechai, Perek Bnei HaIr).
