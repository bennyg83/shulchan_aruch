They read it, etc. In Hagahos Minhagim, Laws of Shabbos, it is written as follows: Maharam Mintz disagrees, saying that this applies specifically with two parshiyos, but not with three parshiyos, such as where the previous Shabbos had doubled parshiyos; see there. His words do not appear correct to me. In Kenesses HaGedolah and Olas Tamid, later in siman 282, they brought Maharam's words as halacha and did not mention the words of the Hagahos cited above, and it seems to me that they escaped their attention. His reason is also logical, for no parsha should be skipped. Furthermore, I found a reason in the marginal gloss to the small Mordechai at the beginning of Berachos, as follows: From the days of Moshe Rabbeinu, peace be upon him, it was instituted to read the parshiyos in order to make them heard to the public. This implies that there is no distinction at all, and all the more so where two parshiyos are joined on this Shabbos, that they also read the sidra of the previous Shabbos. Accordingly, it seems to me that the same applies if they missed two or three Shabbosos: they read all the parshiyos that they missed at one time, unlike what is implied by Maharam's responsum there at the end of chapter 5. The same applies if they began and did not finish because of a quarrel. Maharam further wrote there that if the previous sidra was the end of a book, they do not read it. Olas Tamid wrote there in the name of Maharam's responsum that in a place where there is more than one synagogue, if they read in one synagogue, even though they did not read in the other synagogues, this is not called nullifying the continual practice and they need not complete it. It seems to me from the body of the responsum there that this applies specifically when the congregation of this synagogue was reading, only in another synagogue; but if this congregation did not read at all, they must complete it. Mishpetei Shmuel, siman 3, wrote: If they prayed in the synagogue and read from the Torah, and part of the congregation was not in the synagogue, they should not take out a Sefer Torah again and read from it, because of the blemish to the first one. It seems that this is specifically where the first ones prayed at the proper time for prayer and the latter ones did not come at the time of prayer; Olas Tamid, end of siman 144. It is written in the margin of Magen Avraham that if the Torah reading was missed on Monday because of circumstances beyond their control, they should read on Tuesday, so that they should not go three days without Torah. But if they missed it on Thursday, or even on Monday but intentionally, there is room for doubt. From the poskim cited above it does not seem that one completes except on Shabbos morning, where the sidra would be completely missed; this case, however, is completed on Shabbos morning.
