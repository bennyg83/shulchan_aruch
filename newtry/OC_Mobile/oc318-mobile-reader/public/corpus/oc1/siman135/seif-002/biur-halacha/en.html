If they annulled, etc. — see Mishna Berurah; and see in Pri Megadim that he is uncertain whether specifically when inadvertent or even when intentional one must complete; and in Peri Megadim and Shulchan Atzei Shetim they wrote that the conclusion of the poskim implies that even when intentional they complete. However, from Biur HaGra's explanation, who brings the reason of this law from the law of prayer — that we hold one who erred and did not pray a certain prayer completes it in the adjacent prayer — it appears at first glance that it is specifically when inadvertent, as there, that one has no make-up when intentional, as explained in siman 108; see there.

One Shabbat, etc. — specifically Shabbat; but if they annulled the reading on Monday and Thursday, it has no make-up on the following day [later authorities, not like Ateret Zekenim].

The reading of the parasha, etc. — Magen Avraham wrote similarly: if they began and did not finish because of a fight; see in Shaarei Ephraim who justifies himself not to begin on the coming Shabbat from the place they stopped, but to begin with the priest from the start of the order of the past Shabbat and read with him all he read on the past Shabbat and add three verses or more of what was not read on the past Shabbat, and there the priest stops and blesses after it, and again they read as usual; and even if nothing remains in the first order after the priest finishes his reading enough for Levi and Israel and joining the orders through the fourth — rather they must join orders through the second or third — this does not impede, end of his words.
