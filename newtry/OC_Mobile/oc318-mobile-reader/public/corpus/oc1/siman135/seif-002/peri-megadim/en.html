And if. See Taz in siman 131, seif katan 10, that all seven days of celebration are like a festival. The same applies to a widower and widow for three days. One who remarries his divorcee may likewise have the status of a festival for himself, even if she had married another in the interim. Although siman 546, seif 2, permits remarrying one’s divorcee during the festival, see Magen Avraham there. This requires study. Regarding what he wrote in the name of Ritzba, see Beit Yosef in the name of Mordechai: where there are three bridegrooms, one kohen, and two Yisraelim, the kohen reads twice. Hagahos Maimoniyos, in the name of Ritzba, discusses two or three bridegrooms who are Yisraelim. It appears that he disagrees with Mordechai, for according to Mordechai no other person may be called and the kohen reads twice, so among the Yisraelim only one would receive an aliyah. According to Hagahos Maimoniyos in the name of Ritzba, the kohen and levi are called, followed by the two bridegrooms. Since the kohen and levi are properly called, both bridegrooms may also be called. If there is only one bridegroom, however, one should not add a fourth aliyah merely for him, since there is no necessity to do so. Rather, let him receive the third aliyah. Moreover, a bridegroom precedes other Yisraelim, as Peri Chadash proves from Moed Katan: from where do we know that a bridegroom sits at the head? As it is stated, “like a bridegroom who wears priestly splendor.” Therefore, where there is no kohen, the bridegroom is called first. See there. If one married a divorcee and another a chalutzah, the two are equal, unlike Knesses HaGedolah, because both are Yisraelim. If one married a virgin and the other a non-virgin, they may likewise be equal according to Beit Yosef. Levush writes that the custom is not to follow this rule. This appears to refer to the baalei bris, as Peri Chadash also implies. Magen Avraham, seif katan 2, however, understands that even regarding a bridegroom the custom is not to follow it, and Bedek HaBayis likewise states that the custom is not to do so even for a bridegroom. See Even HaEzer, siman 64, seif 2. It requires study whether a widower who marries a non-virgin is forbidden to perform work and whether the days are regarded as a festival for purposes of mourning, for a baal bris observes mourning during the seven days. See Eliyah Rabbah, siman 132, seif katan 4, and Peri Chadash here. This requires study regarding several practical laws.
