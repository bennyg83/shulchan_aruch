(Ibid.) Seif katan 7, “and the honor of the Torah.” Note: See Responsa Chasam Sofer, Orach Chayim, siman 25. There he addresses a city with only one kohen who refuses to waive his right to the first aliyah, and this has already caused much contention and dispute. At times, several people with obligations to receive aliyos are present on the same day, among them the local rav and communal leaders, and they wish to enact a permanent regulation that when several such obligations occur, including distinguished residents of the city, those persons should be called first and the kohen's status should be disregarded. He was asked whether they may enact such a regulation. He answered that if they can appease the kohen verbally or through some other inducement so that he agrees to the regulation, as is proper and fitting, then their enactment may be established permanently. If afterward he, or another kohen, refuses to comply with the established regulation, they may remove him against his will or call a Yisrael before him even while he is present in the synagogue. Although a kohen who wishes to waive his precedence may not do so, that applies when waiving it for a private individual, because this may lead to quarrels, with others saying, “I too am as important as he,” as Rashi explains at the end of HaNizakin. But where he waives his precedence to the congregation so they may establish a regulation for a defined circumstance such as this, there is no concern. Nevertheless, it is proper to honor him at least with taking out and returning the Sefer Torah, in order to fulfill “and you shall sanctify him” at least in part. But if it is impossible to appease him, we have no authority to override his will forcibly.
