5) [Seif 2] A place where they stop on Shabbos, etc. And if they erred on Monday and Thursday and read in a different sidra, three men, one need not return to read the weekly parashah a second time. Beis Oved, in the laws of Krias Sefer Torah, ot 2; and the Yafeh LaLev cited him in his Kuntres Yosher Levav, ot 1; and see there the reason.
5) There, gloss: If they omitted one Shabbos, etc. And the Maharam Mintz, siman 85, wrote that specifically two sidros they read if the reading of one Shabbos was omitted, since in any case we find that sometimes they read two sidros on one Shabbos in ordinary years; but if two Shabbosos were omitted, they do not read three sidros on the coming Shabbos, for we do not find any place where they read three sidros on one Shabbos. And likewise, if the parshiyos were joined on the previous Shabbos, there is no making them up, for there is no reasoning to divide them and read half the obligation on the coming Shabbos. And likewise, if they omitted parashas Vayechi, one should not read it with parashas Shemos, for when one reads two sidros, one man must read from the end of the first sidra to the beginning of the second sidra so that they be joined, and this is not proper to do with two separate scrolls; see there. And the Knesses HaGedolah cited him in the Hagahos HaTur, siman 282, and wrote that according to his words, if they omitted [reading] on a Shabbos whose parshiyos are not joined, and the coming Shabbos has joined parshiyos, one should not read the parashah of the previous week; see there. And so wrote the Olas Tamid there, ot 4; Ateres Zekeinim in this siman, ot 2; Magen Avraham, s.k. 4; Yad Aharon in the Hagahos HaTur; Soles Belulah, ot 1. However, in the Hagahos Minhagim he wrote concerning the aforementioned words of the Maharam Mintz that his words do not appear correct in his eyes; and the Eliyah Rabbah cited him, ot 2, and wrote that his reasoning is logical, that one should not skip any parashah, and all the more so when two are joined on this Shabbos, that they also read the sidra of the previous Shabbos; and the same applies when they omitted two or three Shabbosos, they read all the parshiyos that they omitted at one time; see there. And such is the view of the Bais Dovid in the laws of Shabbos, siman 106; and see there, where he refuted all the aforementioned words of the Maharam Mintz. And so wrote the Mateh Yehudah in siman 282, ot 14. And so he concluded in the responsa Devar Moshe, Orach Chaim, siman 50, and wrote that a shaliach tzibbur who erred on Shabbos Vayakhel-Pekudei, thinking that they were not joined, and read Vayakhel alone: he should read Pekudei on Shabbos Vayikra, along with parashas Vayikra, like the sefer of the Bais Dovid; and one should not say in this 'safek berachos lehakel,' since in every place where one reads from the Torah there is an obligation to bless; see there. And so wrote the Emes LeYaakov in the laws of error, siman 168, ot 2; Ledovid Emes, siman 9, ot 2. And one must read them in their order, on account of 'the Torah of Hashem is perfect': first the parashah that they omitted, and afterward the obligation of the day; and the majority of the seven who ascend should ascend for the obligation of the day. And it is better that one of those who ascend read the parashah that they omitted, and the rest for the obligation of the day, like the Rashal in the name of the sefer Leshon Chachamim; and see there. And in my humble opinion it appears that the one should read the entire omitted parashah, and also begin to read three verses in the parashah of the day's obligation, so that all of them read and bless for the day's obligation. And afterward I saw that so wrote the sefer HaShomer Emes, siman 15, ot 1; and the Pesach HaDvir cited him, ot 4, and concurred with his words, and wrote that it appears all agree in this; see there well. Only if the omitted parashah was the end of a sefer, such as Vayechi or Pekudei, then the one should not begin to read in the second sefer, which is Shemos or Vayikra, because it is not proper for one man to read in two separate scrolls, as mentioned above in the name of the Maharam Mintz; and see the Pesach HaDvir there. However, on Monday and Thursday they read the weekly parashah like the rest of the congregation, as stated below in ot 9; and further see below in siman 144, ot 2.
6) If on one Shabbos they began to read the parashah and some of the olim read in it but did not finish the sidra on account of a quarrel, they finish it on the coming Shabbos together with the parashah belonging to that Shabbos; and if they finished the sidra, whether in another Sefer Torah or in another synagogue, one need not read it again on the coming Shabbos. Maharam Mintz there; Knesses HaGedolah there; Olas Tamid there; Magen Avraham there; Yad Aharon there; Soles Belulah there; Emes LeYaakov there, ot 3. And this is specifically when the congregation of this synagogue read it, only that it was in another synagogue; but if this congregation did not read it at all, one must complete it. Eliyah Rabbah there; Ledovid Emes there, ot 3. And this one that they did not finish on account of a quarrel: one must begin on the coming Shabbos from the head of the parashah of the previous Shabbos and read it with one person, and he should also begin to read from the parashah of the day's obligation, as mentioned in the previous ot.
7) One congregation who thought that parashas Matos and Masei were joined, as in most years, and read the two parshiyos on one Shabbos, and after they departed it became known to them that they were not joined: one need not return and read a second time in parashas Matos, seven men, even though in truth they did not read seven in the matter of the day, for it is not an obligation to read the weekly parashah but a custom; and on the Shabbos following it they return and read parashas Masei. Emes LeYaakov there, ot 4, in the name of the Pri Ha'aretz; and he wrote that the same applies on Monday and Thursday, if the congregation erred and read in a different sidra, three men, that one need not return a second time to read the weekly parashah, for in a matter of enactment we do not repeat the act; see there. And so wrote Ledovid Emes there, ot 4.
8) There, in the gloss: If they omitted one Shabbos, etc. The Ateres Zekeinim, ot 1, wrote that if they omitted on Monday the reading in the Sefer Torah due to circumstances beyond their control, they may read the reading of Monday on Tuesday, so that Ezra's enactment not be nullified and so that they not go three days without Torah; which is not the case if they omitted on Thursday, for then they have already gone three days without Torah; see there. But the Rav Mas'as Moshe in his responsa, part 2, siman 1, concluded from the responsum of the Maharik, shoresh 177, that every matter that is on account of an enactment, what was, was, and we do not repeat the act; see there. And the Emes LeYaakov cited him there, ot 1. And likewise the Eliyah Rabbah, ot 2, contested the aforementioned words of the Ateres Zekeinim; see there. And likewise the Birkei Yosef, ot 2, although he wrote to refute the proofs of the aforementioned Rav Mas'as Moshe, nonetheless concurred with him regarding the law; see there. And so he ruled in his sefer Ledovid Emes there, ot 1. And so is implied from what was stated above in ot 1 in the name of the Arizal, of blessed memory, that there is no place for reading the Sefer Torah on a weekday except on Monday and Thursday alone; see there. However, it appears that they may read at Minchah if they omitted at Shacharis on Monday and Thursday; and so it is written in the responsa Yehudah Ya'aleh, siman 51, and the Tosafos Chaim cited him, ot 1, and wrote that possibly all agree in this; see there.
9) There, in the gloss: If they omitted one Shabbos, etc. And if they omitted the reading on Shabbos morning and at Minchah found an opportunity: the Degel Machaneh Reuven wrote that if there is leisure to read the entire sidra, they read the entire sidra at Minchah and read seven men, for the entire Shabbos day is the time for the reading; but if there was no opportunity at Minchah either, they should not read the entire sidra of the previous Shabbos on Monday and Thursday on account of interruption of labor for the people, but rather they read from the parashah of the coming Shabbos; see there. And the Bar Yochai, folio 109b, cited him on this seif, and wrote that although in the Zohar, parashas Vayakhel, folio 206, end of side b, it is implied that one should not read seven men at Minchah at all, and also that one should not read the entire sidra at all, nonetheless, according to the Gemara it is explained thus; see there. However, the Chida, in the sefer Chaim Sha'al, part 1, siman 71, ot 5, and part 2, siman 16, wrote simply that one should not read even at Shabbos Minchah the parashah that they omitted, and even without adding readers; only on the coming Shabbos do they read two parshiyos, as he wrote in his Birkei Yosef; see there. And the Ikrei HaDaT cited him, siman 6, ot 60; Beis Menuchah in the laws of error, siman 168, ot 5. And see below siman 137, ot 18. However, on Monday and Thursday they read the parashah of the coming Shabbos like the entire congregation, on account of 'lo sisgodedu.' Pesach HaDvir, ot 2; and see there well.
10) There, in the gloss: If they omitted one Shabbos the reading of the parashah, etc. But if they omitted the reading of the Haftarah, it has no making-up. Pesach HaDvir, ot 5; see there.
11) A congregation that agreed not to pray with a minyan unless their money is paid to them: they cannot read the Sefer Torah and say Kaddish after it, for in the language of people every matter that requires a minyan is called 'tefillah.' The Rosh in a responsum, klal 3, siman 9; Knesses HaGedolah in the Hagahos HaTur; Pri Chadash, ot 1; Emes LeYaakov, siman 1, ot 4; Ledovid Emes, siman 1, ot 4. And likewise, if they agreed not to pray with Reuven, they do not read from the Torah if Reuven is in the congregation. Responsum of the Rosh there; Emes LeYaakov there, ot 9; Ledovid Emes there, ot 9.
