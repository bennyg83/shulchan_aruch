(28) But not another kohen. Likewise, a Yisrael should not be called in place of a levi, lest people say that the first person is not a kohen or is disqualified because no levi was called after him, and lest they mistakenly think that this Yisrael is a levi.
(29) That the first is disqualified. The text does not say, “that one of them is disqualified,” as it does below in seif 9, because this case applies even where the father of both is established as a kohen. Nevertheless, regarding the first, people may assume that after he was called it became known that his father had married a divorcee, or that his mother had secretly committed adultery, so that his offspring later lost the sanctity of kehunah, and therefore another kohen was called. Regarding the second there is no such concern, for if he were a chalal he would not have been called even in place of a levi. The later authorities write that even on a fast day, if the levi is not fasting, the kohen recites the blessing in place of the levi and a Yisrael is not called.
