And know that Rashi to Gittin 59b, s.v. aval, writes that when no kohen is present, the levi may waive his honor for another levi, so that the other leviim or Yisraelim do not come to quarrel, but a levi may not waive his honor for a Yisrael. It is true that the Mishnah states that a kohen, then a levi, then a Yisrael are called because of peaceful relations, implying that the order between levi and Yisrael is also for peaceful relations. If a kohen is present, however, a Yisrael may not be called immediately after him in any event, lest people say that the kohen is disqualified, while the kohen cannot simply be called twice. See Levush. The Mishnah must therefore refer to a case where no kohen is present and the levi and Yisrael are equal. In that case the levi precedes as a matter of law, as I wrote in seif katan 6, and he may not waive his honor for the Yisrael because of peaceful relations. This supports what I wrote there, that according to all explanations the levi is called first. It requires study why the poskim did not mention this. See Rashi there, who explains that “the bundle is separated” is a tradition from our ancestors. At first glance, the phrase “we have a tradition” seems to arise from this discussion. The Gemara asks whether the rule enacted for peaceful relations is actually a Torah law, namely according to the school of Rabbi Yishmael, which derives it from “and you shall sanctify him.” The other derivations are homiletical supports, whereas “and you shall sanctify him” is substantive, and Rambam counts it as positive commandment 32. See Magen Avraham, seif katan 20, and Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 4. If so, from “and you shall sanctify him” there is no proof that a levi has any particular importance. Thus, where no kohen is present, “the bundle is separated” would mean that the levi loses his distinction. According to the other derivations, however, it is evident that the levi precedes a Yisrael. If so, where no kohen is present he has not lost his honor; on the contrary, he is called first. Yet Rashi did not explain it this way. Understand this.
I am uncertain whether a kohen who is a petzua daka or kerus shafchah retains his sanctity and receives the first aliyah, and likewise regarding a blemished kohen or a minor kohen. If another kohen is present in the synagogue, it is obvious that one may not call the petzua daka or kerus shafchah first. If no other kohen is present, the matter is doubtful. According to the opinion that “and you shall sanctify him” is only an asmachta, this is a D’Rabbanan doubt and one may be lenient, for the concern of peaceful relations applies only where the kohen waives his honor, etc. See Even HaEzer, siman 5, Chelkas Mechokek seif katan 1, and Beis Shmuel seif katan 1. Some say that he may marry a divorcee and has the status of a non-kohen. See Rambam, Hilchos Bi’as HaMikdash, chapter 6. See also Magen Avraham, siman 282, seif katan 6, that a minor is not included in “and you shall sanctify him,” because he is not fit for the avodah. According to this, a blemished kohen is likewise not fit for the avodah. One might answer that the phrase “he offers the bread of your God” includes eating, but a minor also eats. The fact that he does not receive a portion is immaterial. See Rambam, chapter 10, halachah 17. It therefore requires study why a minor differs from a blemished kohen. See Menachos 50a.
