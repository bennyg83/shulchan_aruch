But not another kohen, etc. All the more so he should not call a Yisrael after him, for not everyone knows that there is no levi in the synagogue, etc.; Levush. According to this, on a fast day, when there is no levi who is fasting, it would have been permitted to call a Yisrael in place of the levi, as I wrote in seif 5, for people would say that there is a levi but he is not fasting. However, since this kohen is standing above, there is greater concern for blemish to him. There is also room to distinguish further. Moreover, above he wrote that it is good that he leave, and according to the Taz there it is an obligation that he leave. Therefore it seems to me that this kohen too should read in place of the levi, and the levi should leave the synagogue like the kohen above; see seif katan 4.
After a kohen, etc. So wrote Mahari Abuhav in the name of the Rashba. The Beis Yosef wrote: I am astonished, for regarding a levi after a levi he wrote in his name that even if a Yisrael interrupts between them, it is forbidden, unless we say that Rashba here and the Rashba there are two different authorities; these are his words. His words are forced. Furthermore, it is astonishing, for I saw two contradictory matters written in Responsa Rashba, one in siman 214 and one in siman 543. It is also astonishing how Mahari Abuhav himself could contradict himself. I am also astonished, for in my humble opinion there was no difficulty from the outset. That which he forbids is when the order afterward is not kohen, levi, Yisrael, as is explicit from his language there, for then it will be understood as a blemish to the first kohen or second levi. This is not so when they call a kohen and then a levi, where there is no concern even for a blemish to the levi. One can further say that the Rashba and Mahari Abuhav who permit are speaking of a wedding, where the blemish is not so strong, as the Beis Yosef wrote in the name of Shibbolei HaLeket and the Darchei Moshe, because it is recognizable that it is because of the wedding. The Bach asked: since the Tur wrote, 'because a Yisrael is called after him,' this shows that he is speaking where there was no levi and he read twice, and after him a Yisrael read. Now there is concern for a blemish to the latter. Because of this he objected to the Beis Yosef, saying that in truth we are concerned for blemish when it is not known and established that the second one is not the son of a divorcee or chalutzah; see there. This does not appear correct, for several reasons, and this is not the place to elaborate. Rather, it seems to me to answer the difficulty as follows: the latter kohen also reads twice in place of a levi, and if so there is no concern for blemish, for it is recognizable that he is a kohen since he reads twice. All this follows the Beis Yosef's explanation of the words of the Tur, namely specifically where a levi reads after him. But I found in the Perishah that he objected to him, since the Tur made the reason depend on the interruption of a Yisrael; this implies even when a levi is not called after him, or when he is called last and there is no concern for blemish, since they announce that he is a kohen. The Taz wrote similarly; see there. It seems to my humble opinion that the Beis Yosef himself also reached this conclusion, as he wrote at the end: perhaps specifically in the time of the Gemara, etc., but nowadays those remaining in the synagogue know that the chazzan announced that he is a kohen. If so, even if a levi does not read afterward it is permitted, and the same applies when a levi reads after a Yisrael. Now it is understandable that the Shulchan Aruch wrote without qualification that he went up with the interruption of a Yisrael, and so it also appears from what the Rama wrote on the Shulchan Aruch: therefore it is also permitted to go up for maftir, etc. Examine this carefully. According to this, what the Rama wrote, that in a place of need and pressure one may rely upon them, applies likewise in this case where a levi is not called after him. However, from the Levush it implies that we do not practice this way even in pressing circumstances except for the last aliyah. But regarding a kohen after a levi, Kenesses HaGedolah wrote in the name of Mishpat Tzedek that he should not be called.
