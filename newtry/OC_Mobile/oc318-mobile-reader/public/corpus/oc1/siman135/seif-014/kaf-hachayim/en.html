(14) And Mahari Kilon wrote in shoresh 9 regarding a congregation that practiced that on Shabbat Bereishit one of the congregation would volunteer money for synagogue needs so he would be called first at the beginning of the Torah; and they also practiced that if there is a Kohen there he buys that mitzvah or waives his honor and leaves the synagogue — see there; and they brought in Beit Yosef and Magen Avraham seif 7; however Beit Yosef there disagreed and wrote that if the Kohen waives his honor so the congregation may keep their custom, he need not leave; and see there that he brought support for this from the Gemara and wrote we do not find the Kohen must leave outside the synagogue except specifically regarding a mourner because there are three positive commandments involved, and even this is specifically when they told him to ascend, as written in siman 128 — see there; and Peri Chadash agreed with his words orach 3; and likewise Kenahag in Hagahot Beit Yosef found written in a manuscript of Maharal Levi Treves z"l that even if the Kohen does not leave the synagogue they call the Israelite who volunteered to ascend first; and Bach Lechem Dvira at the ascent of the Torah scroll orach 46, Lechem Dvira siman 5 orach 46 (and see teshuvah Chatham Sofer siman 24); and likewise Birkei Yosef orach 5 in the name of Maharash Shaar Aryeh in a manuscript that some practice on Shavuot that the rabbi of the place reads in place of the Kohen for honor of the Torah, and the Kohen need not leave, and the shaliach tzibbur says explicitly "the rabbi shall stand," etc. — end of his words; and likewise the Gaon Maharash Kluger at the end of Sefer Stam in the section Paret U'olelot in omissions to laws of Torah scroll p. 86a that Ashkenazim in Eretz Yisrael practice calling Israel in place of Kohen, and sometimes the chazzan says even though there is a Kohen here, because guests who need to be called to the Torah are always found with them, and presumably the Kohanim waive to them, since it is known it is specifically for honor of guests there is no concern for quarreling, for when there are no guests they do not waive, etc.; and further "great is human dignity," etc., since the way is to bring guests to the Torah — understand. And our friend Rabbi Eretz Chaim n"r brought this in this siman.
