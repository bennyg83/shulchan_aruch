We do not bring, etc. — the source is Yerushalmi brought in Beit Yosef, and this is his language there: everywhere you say "go after the Torah," and here you say "bring the Torah to them" [meaning what is stated in Yoma: the chazzan of the synagogue gives the Torah scroll to the head of the synagogue, etc.] — only because they are people great in Torah, the Torah was elevated in them, etc., see there in Beit Yosef. And truly the matter is astonishing — for Yerushalmi refers to when it is possible to go to the place where the Torah scroll rests, and therefore it is contempt when they bring a Torah scroll to them; not so here, that they are compelled and want to fulfill the mitzvah of public Torah reading — why not bring it to them? What contempt is there to the Torah scroll when people seek it to read in it, if we bring it to them since they cannot go after it? Afterwards I found in Peri Chadash who also wondered at this, and truly so is the view of Or Zarua, that for a sick person it is permitted to bring a Torah scroll to him to read in it because he is compelled, as Darkei Moshe wrote in his name, and in Or Zarua itself his reason is explained, and this is his language: that if for those we bring because of their honor, all the more so we bring to his house because he is compelled, see there. And therefore the same applies to prisoners in prison — what is different? And for practical law it appears that even for Mordechai who forbids even when compelled — it refers only to individuals imprisoned who want to gather ten there for public Torah reading — in this he forbids [and not like Or Zarua, who implies permitting a sick person to gather ten there and bring the Torah scroll to him]; and from the law one can say that the mitzvah of public Torah reading does not apply to an individual when he cannot go to the synagogue; but when there are ten there, since the obligation of reading applies to them and they cannot leave to go after it — Mordechai also concedes they must bring them a Torah scroll to read in it. And I found in Sefer A"R that he also wrote one answer to Mordechai: he does not refer to when there are ten there, as it appears to me for practical law.

And if he is an important person, etc. — see in Mishna Berurah what we wrote, that specifically one important in Torah — and this is from Biur HaGra in the name of the responsum of Maharam Padua; see there that its source is from Yerushalmi from that which questions from the Exilarch, even though he is certainly among the important and wealthy — therefore specifically important in Torah we require. And I saw in Teshuvot Ketav Sofer siman 289 who wrote the opposite of this, and this is his language: in Yerushalmi chapter Ba Lo, here you say go after the Torah, and here you say bring the Torah to him — only through people great in Torah the Torah is elevated; from here proof that there is no prohibition to call a wealthy respected person to the Torah before a Torah scholar, because through great people the Torah is elevated, end of his words — behold from his words it implies the "great people" mentioned in Yerushalmi are not specifically Torah scholars; and I did not copy his view in Mishna Berurah because his words are difficult, since from the end of Yerushalmi's words it is explained the opposite of this, as above. However, after consideration I saw that Rama in siman 282 brought the law of Teshuvot Ketav Sofer as halachah [and what is printed there Or Zarua is an error, for it is not found at all in Or Zarua, only in the above Teshuvot Ketav Sofer, whose language is exactly this, as above]. And perhaps Rama distinguishes between calling him to the Torah and bringing a Torah scroll to him — for the latter we require one most important; and although Teshuvot Ketav Sofer itself derived its law only from the above Yerushalmi, Rama decided his law only from general reasoning; and this requires further study.
