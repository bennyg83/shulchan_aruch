13) And this that we say that the Kohen is not permitted to give honor to one greater than him -- they said there in the Gemara: we said this only on Shabbosos and Yom Tov, when the multitude is present, but on Monday and Thursday, no. And we ask: but did not Rav Huna read as a Kohen on Shabbosos and Yom Tov? And they answered: Rav Huna is different, for even Rabbi Ami and Rabbi Asi, the distinguished Kohanim of Eretz Yisrael, would subordinate themselves to him. Until here. And concerning that which the Gemara distinguishes between Monday-and-Thursday and Shabbosos-and-Yom-Tov, the Tosafos wrote there in the name of Rabbeinu Chananel that this applies to them, for on a weekday they were busy with their work and were not present, but for us they are present on Monday and Thursday as on Yom Tov. End of his language. And the Beis Yosef cited him, and wrote: however, even on Shabbosos and Yom Tov, if a Kohen comes to give honor to a great and outstanding man, to whom all the Kohanim subordinate themselves, to ascend first, he is permitted, as we find that Rav and Rav Huna would read in place of Kohanim; only that in these generations the great ones were not particular to ascend first, and it is preferable for them to ascend at the end of those who ascend, and always the greatest in the congregation ascends at the end; and through this the Kohanim did not need to forgo their honor to the great ones of the generation. End of his language. And this is unlike the Taz, s.k. 3, who wrote that even granting permission is of no avail; see there. And see the Maamar Mordechai, ot 4, what he elaborated on this, and deliberated in the words of the Bach and the Derishah, and held firmly to the words of Maran, of blessed memory, and wrote concerning the aforementioned words of the Taz that they are not compelling; see there.
