And so they practice, etc. The Levush wrote that the practice is not to call any kohen or levi, even when they call more than seven, except as the last aliyah. Even if the last one is a kohen, they call a kohen for maftir, for Kaddish interrupts between them. This implies that this is specifically when they call more than seven, but when they call only seven he should not be called as the seventh, and likewise on Yom Tov. A case came before me on Yom Tov where they erred and called a kohen for the fifth aliyah, and I ruled that if it is possible to switch him to maftir, he should stand there and be maftir, and they should call the last aliyah and then he should be maftir, as above in seif 7. But if this is impossible, since he already went up he should not go down, for we are not concerned for a blemish to the first kohen, since they called a levi after him, as the Beis Yosef wrote. As for his own blemish, it seems that since he is last, it is not such a blemish. Furthermore, I wrote above that there are places that practice this way initially. Although we do not practice this way, nevertheless, since he already went up, one may rely upon them.
