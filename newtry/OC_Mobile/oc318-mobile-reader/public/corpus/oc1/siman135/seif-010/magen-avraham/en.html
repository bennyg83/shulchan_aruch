With an intervening Yisrael. This means that the order was kohen, levi, and Yisrael. After the levi, however, another kohen may not be called. Knesses HaGedolah, Matzas Shimurim, and Rabbi Yosef Levi. If no levi is present, the kohen reads twice, followed by a Yisrael, and then another kohen may be called. There is no concern that people will discredit the first kohen, for had he been disqualified they would immediately have raised objections and called another in his place. Nor is there concern that people will say the second kohen is a chalal and therefore was called in place of a Yisrael, because they would then also have to say that the levi called after him was disqualified, and we do not entertain such an extensive concern. Beit Yosef. Bach asks what the law is when no levi is present. In my view there is no difficulty, for if no levi is present the second kohen must also read twice, so that people should not think the Yisrael called after him is a levi. They therefore cannot say that the kohen is a chalal and was called in place of a Yisrael, because if so, why did he read twice? It is nevertheless possible that we do not permit so much, namely calling a kohen in the middle and having him read twice.

In a place where it was instituted that the city’s Torah scholar should read the Thirteen Attributes, to prevent quarrels among the rest of the people, he may be called third even if he is a kohen. Matzas Shimurim, part 2, siman 9, and Rabbi Yosef Levi, siman 29.
Within the count of seven. The same applies on a fast day: a kohen or levi should not be called for maftir, because maftir is included in the required number of readers. If no Yisrael present knows how to read the haftarah, they may prompt him word by word. Responsa Maharam, siman 64. See the end of siman 284, seif 4. Levush writes: “Our practice is to call him only for the last aliyah, while in Pozna he is called only for maftir.” This also appears from Mordechai and Mahariv, siman 3. Responsa Maharam, siman 17, discusses two grooms on one Shabbos, both of whom were kohanim. Half the parashah was read with one groom and his attendants, and afterward a kohen, levi, the second groom, and his attendants were called. This implies that otherwise it would be forbidden. As for the practice of calling one kohen after another, one for the last aliyah and one for maftir, it may be said that since Kaddish intervenes it is permitted, as Mordechai and Levush write regarding Chassan Bereishis and Chassan Torah.
