Among them. Meaning, one calls a kohen, levi, and Yisrael; but after the levi it is forbidden. Knesses HaGedolah, Mishpat Tzedek, Rabbi Yosef Halevi, and Peri Chadash. If no levi is present, the kohen reads twice, then a Yisrael, and afterward another kohen may be called. See there. (See also Eliyah Rabbah for his resolution of the Beit Yosef’s question on the Rashba.)
A kohen. Nevertheless, without an interruption it is forbidden to call a Yisrael immediately afterward, because we are concerned that not everyone heard the entire matter. In a place where it was instituted that the city’s sage reads the Thirteen Attributes, if he is a kohen he may be called third. Mishpat Tzedek, Rabbi Yosef Halevi, Knesses HaGedolah, Olas Tamid, and Magen Avraham.
Seven. The same applies on a fast day: he should not be called for maftir, since maftir counts toward the required number of aliyos. If no Yisrael is present who knows how to read maftir, they prompt him. Magen Avraham. See siman 284, seif 4. Likewise, he should not be called seventh, nor on Yom Tov fifth. If a kohen was called fifth, and it is possible to exchange him with the maftir, they should do so: the person called fifth should remain there and read the maftir portion, while the final aliyah is given to another person. If this is impossible, then once he has already been called he does not step down. Eliyah Zuta. As for the practice of calling one kohen after another, one for the final aliyah and one for maftir, it may be explained that since Kaddish intervenes, it is permitted. See Magen Avraham. An incident occurred in Prague on Yom Tov where they mistakenly called a kohen for the fifth aliyah. The Gaon, author of Eliyah Rabbah, ruled that if it was possible to exchange him with the maftir, he should remain there and read maftir while another person reads the final aliyah; but if this was impossible, once he had already been called he should not step down. See there for his reasoning.
