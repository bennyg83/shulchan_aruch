They practiced to call Kohen, etc. Beit Yosef challenged: behold in such a case there is concern for defect of the last that they will say he is a challal and they called him after Israel; and he answered: since they called after him Levi and Israel, there is no concern — for if so they would need to say also the Levi who read after him is unfit, and for all this they are not concerned; and it is difficult on this: behold there is no Levi in the middle, only Israel alone; and further he wrote he holds we do not say "Kohen shall not read after Kohen" except when there is no Israelite interruption between them. And his words are puzzling — the matter itself, why are they not concerned with Israelite interruption for defect of the last; and Mateh Yehonatan z"l wrote it deals with one established regarding his father that he is a Kohen and not son of a divorcee, as we say nearby we are not concerned for defect of the second above; and he did not precision that above it does not deal with established that he is not son of a divorcee, but established regarding his father that he is a Kohen, and we say there is no concern perhaps he is son of a divorcee — for if so they would not have called him in place of Levi; and if so certainly here there is concern for defect in the second; and it appears to me the Sages were not concerned for defect of the second except where the public sees a change from what the Sages ordered: Kohen reads first and after him Levi, and if there is no Levi then after him Israel — and they did not order Kohen after Kohen; if so this mishnah has concern for defect as above; unlike if Kohen and after him Israel — behold he already did per order of the Sages, and afterward no change appears; therefore they were not concerned for defect — why should they be concerned in vain; and what is our practice to call Kohen or Levi for the last and we are not concerned for defect — it is because there is no change here, as above, simple and clear; and see siman 138 there is proof for what I said.
