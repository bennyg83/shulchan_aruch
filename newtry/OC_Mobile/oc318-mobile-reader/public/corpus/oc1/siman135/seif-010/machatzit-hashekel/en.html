Seif katan 10: But first, etc. Even according to the interpretation, etc. He comes to resolve the question of Bach and Taz. The Gemara in Gittin states: Abaye said, we have a tradition that if no kohen is present, the bundle has come apart. Rashi explains: the connection is broken; the levi has lost his honor and so forth, and does not read at all, and so forth. But, and so forth, I heard that there is no fixed order, and whichever one wishes may go first. End quote. Kesef Mishneh writes that Rambam, whose wording is like that of the Shulchan Aruch, “a levi should not go up after him,” evidently follows Rashi’s first interpretation. Beit Yosef likewise writes so. How then could Rama write upon the Mechaber’s wording, “but he may go first,” which follows Rashi’s second interpretation? The Mechaber follows the first interpretation, and Rama should have presented it as a dispute. Magen Avraham therefore cites Darchei Moshe to explain that even Rashi’s first interpretation agrees that a levi may go first. The dispute between the two interpretations concerns a levi and an Israelite who are equal in stature. According to the first interpretation, which says that the levi lost his honor, the two are like equal Israelites, and either one may be called first. According to the second interpretation, the levi did not lose all his honor, only part of it. When a kohen is present, the levi reads after him and before an Israelite even if the Israelite is greater than the levi. When there is no kohen, the levi loses that right and either may be called first, since each has an advantage: the Israelite is greater than the levi, while the levi has the advantage of being a levi. They are therefore equal. But if the levi is equal in learning to the Israelite, it is proper to give the levi precedence, because even without a kohen some measure of the levi’s right remains relative to an Israelite. The Rosh writes this explicitly according to the second interpretation, as cited by Beit Yosef. In any event, when they are equal, even according to Rashi’s first interpretation the levi may go first. Rama’s words are therefore correct. The phrase “specifically when the Israelite is not greater,” etc., follows Rashi’s first interpretation, which Rambam and Beit Yosef rule in accordance with, as explained.
