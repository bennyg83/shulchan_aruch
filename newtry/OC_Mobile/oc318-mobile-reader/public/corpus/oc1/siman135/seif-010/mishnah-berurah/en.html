(31) With an Israelite interruption — meaning that he read from the beginning the entire order as the sages instituted: priest, Levi, and Israel; then it is permitted to return and call a priest [and similarly if there is no Levi and the priest read twice and then Israel, it is also permitted to return and call a priest]. And similarly it is permitted to call by name Levi for any aliyot they wish, and all the more so for the last or for maftir; and the reason is that here there is no concern for the flaw of the first — for if he were flawed they would murmur after him immediately and inform the chazzan and call another in his place, and they would not wait until after Israel's reading; and for the flaw of the second too there is no concern that they will say about the priest or Levi that they are disqualified, for they say "even though he is a priest," and similarly for Levi "even though he is a Levi."

(32) And similarly we practice, etc. — meaning: although we say in Shenei Luchot HaBrit that Levi after Levi should not be called, here since an Israelite interrupted between them, and also he says "even though he is a Levi," there is no concern at all, as above.

(33) For maftir in such a case — meaning that he should also mention that he is a priest or Levi.

(34) Priest or Levi, etc. — and similarly even if he wants to make a new order, namely priest, Levi, and Israel, it is forbidden per this view, since he is within the seven readers. The poskim wrote that on a fast day a priest or Levi should not ascend for maftir, who is from the number of readers — for maftir is the third; and if there is no Israelite who knows how to read haftarah, they call others for him.

(35) For the count of seven — and similarly on Yom Tov for the fifth [aliyah]. And b'dieved, if they erred and called a priest or Levi in the middle of the readers — if it is possible to switch at maftir or at the last, he waits at the ark and they call another in his place and he ascends for the last or maftir; and if impossible, since he ascended he does not descend but blesses and reads, as Rama concludes: in a place of need and urgency one may rely on the first view [later authorities].

(36) And similarly we practice, etc. — and in Levush he wrote that today even after seven readers we do not practice calling them except for the last among them, and similarly for maftir; and other later authorities copied thus. The reason appears in Levush: we are not concerned for flaw on the last, for on the contrary in these generations the last is considered the most important, since he concludes the portion; and for the greatest in the congregation they call the last, and even though they read haftarah after him, they interrupt between him and maftir with Kaddish; and also for maftir they are not particular if he is a priest [since it is like the beginning of reading, as below]. And even if the last was also a priest, they are not concerned for priest after priest, since Kaddish interrupts between them, end of Levush's words. Nevertheless, in a place of need, such as two grooms and attendants, and each has priests and Levites — they divide the portion in two: at first they read seven men for one, and then for the second groom and his attendants. The later authorities wrote that on Simchat Torah, when they take out three Torah scrolls, there can be three priests — one bridegroom of the Torah, one bridegroom of Bereishit, and one maftir — since each reads in a different book. But on Rosh Chodesh Tevet that falls on Shabbat, when there are also three scrolls — then in the first scroll in which they read the weekly portion, one should not call a priest at the end even if they added and read more than seven in it, since they have not yet completed the reading in this scroll and the completion is in the second scroll in which they read the Rosh Chodesh portion; only in the second scroll in which the last concludes, and in the scroll after it in which they read for Chanukah maftir, may they call a priest to be last and also priest for maftir. And similarly on the Shabbat of Parashat Shekalim or Parashat HaChodesh that falls on Rosh Chodesh when there are three scrolls — one does not call a priest to complete the reading in the first scroll, only in the last two scrolls may they call priests for last and maftir, as I wrote [Shaarei Ephraim; and see there in Pitchei Shearim what he brought further in the name of Peri Megadim regarding Chol HaMoed of Sukkot, and he wondered at it].
