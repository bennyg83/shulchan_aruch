(31) With a Yisrael interrupting. This means that they first completed the full order instituted by Chazal, kohen, levi, and Yisrael. It is then permitted to call another kohen. Likewise, if no levi is present and the kohen read twice, followed by a Yisrael, another kohen may then be called. A levi may similarly be called by name for any later aliyah, and certainly for the final aliyah or maftir. There is no concern that this casts doubt on the first person, because if he were disqualified people would have objected immediately, informed the chazzan, and called another person in his place rather than waiting until after the Yisrael’s aliyah. Nor is there concern that the second kohen or levi will be thought disqualified, because the announcement specifies, “although he is a kohen,” or, regarding a levi, “although he is a levi.”
(32) And similarly is the practice, etc. Although seif 9 states that a levi should not be called after another levi, here a Yisrael intervened and the announcement also says, “although he is a levi,” so there is no concern, as explained above.
(33) For maftir in such a case. Meaning, the announcement should likewise mention that he is a kohen or levi.
(34) A kohen or levi, etc. According to this view, even if one wishes to begin a new order of kohen, levi, and Yisrael, it remains forbidden while still within the seven required aliyos. The poskim write that on a fast day a kohen or levi should not receive maftir, since maftir is included among the required readers and is the third aliyah. If no Yisrael knows how to read the Haftarah, another person should prompt him.
(35) Within the count of seven. The same applies on Yom Tov within the five required aliyos. After the fact, if a kohen or levi was mistakenly called in the middle of the required aliyos, then if he can be switched to maftir or the final aliyah, he should wait by the bimah while another person is called in his place, and afterward he should receive the final aliyah or maftir. If this is impossible, once he has ascended he does not descend, but recites the blessing and reads, as Rama concludes that in a case of need and pressing circumstances one may rely on the first view. Later authorities.
(36) And similarly is the practice, etc. Levush writes that nowadays, even after the seven required aliyos, the practice is to call a kohen or levi only for the final aliyah or for maftir, and the other later authorities rule likewise. Levush explains that there is no concern about the final aliyah casting doubt on him. On the contrary, in these generations the final aliyah is considered the most distinguished because it completes the parashah, and the greatest person in the congregation is called for it. Although maftir follows, Kaddish intervenes between them. There is also no objection to a kohen receiving maftir, because it is treated like the beginning of a new reading. Even if the final aliyah was also given to a kohen, there is no concern about kohen after kohen because Kaddish intervenes. Nevertheless, where necessary, such as when there are two chasanim with attendants and each group includes kohanim and leviim, the parashah may be divided into two: seven men are first called for one chasan, and afterward the second chasan and his attendants are called. The later authorities write that on Simchas Torah, when three Sifrei Torah are taken out, three kohanim may receive Chasan Torah, Chasan Bereishis, and maftir, because each reads from a different Sefer Torah. On Rosh Chodesh Teves that falls on Shabbos, however, although three Sifrei Torah are also used, a kohen should not be called for the final aliyah in the first Sefer Torah containing the weekly parashah, even if more than seven aliyos were added there, because the reading has not yet been completed in that Sefer Torah. The completion occurs in the second Sefer Torah, from which the Rosh Chodesh reading is read. In the second Sefer Torah, where the final aliyah completes the reading, and in the third, from which the Chanukah maftir is read, a kohen may be called for the final aliyah and another kohen for maftir. The same applies when Parashas Shekalim or Parashas HaChodesh falls on Rosh Chodesh and three Sifrei Torah are used: a kohen should not complete the reading in the first Sefer Torah, but kohanim may be called for the final aliyah and maftir in the last two Sifrei Torah. Shaarei Ephraim. See there in Pischei Shearim regarding what he further cites from Peri Megadim concerning Chol HaMoed Sukkos and his objection to it.
(37) In a case of need and pressing circumstances, etc. For example, on Simchas Torah and similar occasions, one may rely on the view that after the first three readers, kohen, levi, and Yisrael, another kohen or levi may be called, as explained above according to the Mechaber. Even if we rely on the first view, however, many Rishonim and later authorities hold that one should not ordinarily call a kohen or levi unless a new order is formed, namely kohen, levi, and Yisrael. In that arrangement there is no concern that people will say he is not a kohen, since a levi is called after him. Without such an order, a kohen or levi should not be called because of possible doubt concerning his status. According to this view, even the final aliyah or maftir should not be given to a kohen or levi, but the universal practice is lenient in those cases, as the later authorities rule and as Levush explains. It is therefore proper to be careful to call them specifically in the regular order, even when they are called after the seven required aliyos.
