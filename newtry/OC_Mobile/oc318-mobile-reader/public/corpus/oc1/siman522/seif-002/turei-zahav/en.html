(א) There is no exception in the chair. P. sits in the chair and carries it:

(ב) When many needed him. Tur wrote "and he cannot walk without it" — implies if he can walk without it it is forbidden; but from Ran's words it implies even if he can walk without it they permitted him because of community distress or so he should not soil his garments, all the more if he was old or walking is burdensome; and Tur's view is astounding, for if he cannot walk without it even any person is permitted like one whose sinews contracted — and one could say there he himself has permission to walk with a stick, unlike here where others carry him — those others were not permitted except when many need him and he cannot walk without it — such is Tur's view; and since these are rabbinic matters it appears one can rely on Ran to be lenient, and likewise Rashal:

(ג) On the shoulder. Meaning even on the shoulder where each places his hands on his fellow's shoulder and the chair on their arms, and all the more if they carry between their hands. In Rambam it is written they go out in a chair after him; and Beit Yosef that it implies he was explaining they carried a chair after important people to sit there where they wished. And Rashal challenged this, for it implies in the Gemara it refers to what they take the people out in a chair; also he did not know what prohibition there is to take out a chair after a man or woman accustomed to sit in chairs even when the public does not need him, for any need of the day is somewhat permitted in hotza'ah, etc. — therefore it appears this "after him" is a scribal error in Rambam — end quote. In my humble opinion it appears there is certainly more prohibition in taking out a chair and benches than a stick a blind person goes out with, to resolve the sugya — that he can walk well and nevertheless it is forbidden since he can walk otherwise because of degradation of Yom Tov; all the more there is degradation of Yom Tov in taking out chairs, and it resembles weekday activity more, and they did not permit hotza'ah for partial need except where there is no degradation of Yom Tov, like taking out a child as above — so it appears to me:
