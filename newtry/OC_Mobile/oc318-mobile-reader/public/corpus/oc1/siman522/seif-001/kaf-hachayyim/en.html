1) [Se'if 1] A blind person may not go out with his cane. This is a weekday manner of conduct and degrades Yom Tov. Rashi, Beitzah 25b; Beis Yosef; Levush. This applies specifically in a public domain or a karmelis resembling a public domain. Yam Shel Shlomo, chapter 3 of Beitzah, siman 14; Taz, subsection 1; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1; Chemed Moshe, subsection 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1; Chayei Adam, rule 96, subsection 5; Mikraei Kodesh, siman 12, subsection 14; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 2. He may, however, go from a house to a courtyard without an eruv, because the restrictions of eruv and carrying do not apply on Yom Tov. Eliyah Rabbah there; Chayei Adam there. It is likewise permitted in a city enclosed by an eruv. Mikraei Kodesh there. Nevertheless, Responsa Radbaz, manuscript siman 2163, writes that some people who use a cane when the roads are muddy may not go out with it on Shabbos or Yom Tov, even in a courtyard enclosed by an eruv, because this is a weekday activity. I cited this above, siman 301, subsection 105. See there. Machazik Berachah in this siman, subsection 1, requires further study for expressing uncertainty whether Radbaz's ruling applies likewise on Yom Tov, for Radbaz explicitly writes 'on Shabbos and Yom Tov.' Nevertheless, it appears that in a case of need one may be lenient and go with a cane on Yom Tov from a house to a courtyard or within a city enclosed by an eruv. Even on Shabbos some permit this in an enclosed place, as written in our words there. See there.
2) There. A blind person may not go out with his cane. The same applies to an elderly person. Derishah, subsection 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav there; Mikraei Kodesh there; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 1. Only if he has become so old that he cannot walk without a cane is it permitted, as written above, siman 301, subsection 103. See there.
3) There. A blind person may not go out with his cane. If he cannot walk without a cane, it is permitted. Rokeach, siman 299; Levush; Ateres Zekeinim; Mishbetzos Zahav, subsection 1. Levush writes there that even on Shabbos it is permitted if he cannot walk without a cane, because the cane is like his shoe. This implies that even in a public domain he may go out with it on Shabbos. Rokeach, siman 104, however, writes, 'but not on Shabbos into a public domain.' Erech HaShulchan, subsection 1, cites him. See above, siman 301, se'if 18, and our words there, with Heaven's help.
4) There. Nor may a shepherd go out with his satchel. This too refers to a public domain or a karmelis and is forbidden because it degrades Yom Tov, as written above, subsection 1. See there.
