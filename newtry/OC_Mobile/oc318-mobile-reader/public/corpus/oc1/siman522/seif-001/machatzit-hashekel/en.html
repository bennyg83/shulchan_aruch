(s.k. 1) On, etc. — Beit Yosef: meaning he wrote that from Tur's words it implies it was not permitted to him except when he cannot walk there except with great exertion because he is old or sick and the way is far; and he wrote Ran disagrees and permits even if he can walk without exertion but so he should not soil, etc.; and since Shulchan Aruch is stam it implies Ran's view is primary. And know Taz wrote in s.k. 1 in name of Maharshal: nevertheless it appears it is forbidden on Yom Tov only in reshut harabbim or karmelit similar to reshut harabbim as explained in the first chapter Ein Eruv VeHotza'ah on Yom Tov:

(ב) (And see what Taz wrote per Maharshal in siman 518 subsection 2) to exclude Shabbat — even outside the eruv it is forbidden, etc. — end of his words; it appears from his words that where there is eruv, where there is no prohibition of carrying, it is permitted even on Shabbat. But Magen Avraham wrote above siman 301 subsection 27, and this is his language: what important people practice to walk with a cane in hand — they are forbidden to go out on Shabbat, etc.; but in a place where there is eruv it is permitted to go out, for he does not carry it except for honor. But an ordinary person who does not carry it for honor — even where there is eruv it is forbidden, for there is degradation of Shabbat, as written siman 522 — end of his words; meaning here on Yom Tov where there is no eruv and carrying for Yom Tov, and nevertheless it is forbidden — therefore on account of degradation of Yom Tov. If so, the same on Shabbat even where there is eruv — behold he wrote unlike Maharshal; nevertheless for an important person Magen Avraham concedes there is no concern of degradation. And Taz also wrote there subsection 12: that which it is forbidden to go out with a cane — nevertheless where there is concern lest one fall, such as a sloped place and rain fell or on ice called eis — it is permitted, see there.
