Se'if 1: Permitted, etc. There, s.v. "the halacha follows R' Yosi on the Moed." And even though they said there "and with ginosteri it is forbidden," the Rif wrote that it refers to a mourner; and therefore we say there "I was standing," etc., meaning that he did not have a knife there; and therefore there is no difficulty from the inference that in a case of doubt it is forbidden. And this is what [the Mechaber] wrote: "even," etc. And a proof from what is stated there: "they sought [regarding] the mustache"; and just as where mustache [trimming] is permitted in every case, so too for his nails. And see Nimukei Yosef.
Whether, etc. There.
But, etc. For they interpret [it] also regarding Chol HaMoed; and see Tosafos there, s.v. "and with ginostri," etc.
Whether with a knife, etc. See Rashi, s.v. "ginostri," etc., and Tosafos, s.v. "and with ginostera," who explained in the Aruch, etc.; and the same applies to a knife nowadays, since the usual way is to cut them with a knife. And this is the language of Hagahos Semak: and so is the main [ruling], that we require a change, as is written "and with ginostera it is forbidden," meaning with scissors; and R' Baruch explained that the same applies to a knife, for such is the usual way to cut them with a knife.
Unless, etc. There, in the name of Tosafos: that it is no worse than removing hair of the armpit and of the ervah, which is permitted; and see the aforementioned Tosafos; and I heard in the name of the Rashba, etc., that it is taught in the Yerushalmi, etc., meaning hair of the armpit and ervah; and Maharam disagrees, and explained that specifically a non-Jewish woman should cut them for her with a knife or with scissors, but she herself may not; and so he ruled in Yoreh De'ah, siman 390, se'if 6. But here one may rely on those who permit, since there are those who permit in every case; and see Darkei Moshe.
