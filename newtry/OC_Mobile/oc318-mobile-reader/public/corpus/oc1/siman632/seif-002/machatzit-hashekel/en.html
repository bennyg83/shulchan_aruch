(א) (b) This is what the Omnipresent does not do. R. is not far away from the width:

(ב) Since the height, etc. — we do not say chavit and lavud to be stringent; meaning first it must be said chavit rami as above siman 531 seif 5. And thereby it is as if the roof is placed below. And afterward it must be said lavud of the roof with the beam; and to be stringent we do not say both apply — lavud to be stringent is Magen Avraham's view in siman 502 and similarly in this case that we say not like Rama; but chavit and lavud for each other to be stringent he admits we do not say. And see what Ran wrote on the dispute on daf 11a that R' Eliezer and Ravina disagree and everyone holds if there was air of three tefachim in the sukkah and he reduced it with invalid sechach it is valid, for invalid sechach has no measure to invalidate, and likewise air has no measure to invalidate it for no three tefachim of air remained and we say lavud. All this if it is such from the side. But if such in the middle of sechach they dispute whether lavud applies in the middle or not. Ran asked why the dispute was brought in such a case where air became less than three tefachim by reducing with skewers — it should have been brought plainly: air from the start in the middle of sechach less than three tefachim — lavud does not apply in the middle. And answer: the novelty comes to teach us for the view that there is no lavud in the middle even if there was three tefachim air from the side and he reduced via invalid sechach — he admits we say lavud, meaning only when he placed invalid sechach next to valid sechach and air remained next to the wall — in such case we say lavud from the side. But if he placed invalid sechach next to the wall we do not say lavud as it is lavud in the middle. And even though one could say that invalid sechach placed next to the wall is dofen akumah and then the air is from the side and one could say lavud, nevertheless we do not say both dofen akumah and lavud together for now. And certainly in this the amoraim do not dispute, for even per the view that we say lavud in the middle and so is the halachah, nevertheless presumably he admits we do not say lavud and dofen akumah for each other. And although it seems from Magen Avraham's words that chavit and lavud apply to each other if not for stringency — one must distinguish between dofen akumah and lavud versus chavit and lavud; nevertheless it is a novelty in my eyes that Beit Yosef did not bring Ran's words to law, for we do not find poskim who disagree with Ran. Also one could derive proof from the gemara as mentioned. And even if Beit Yosef found someone who disagrees with Ran — nevertheless there should be a dispute here. And perhaps Ran's intent is specifically for the view that there is no lavud in the middle — one can only say lavud if we first say dofen akumah and thereby lavud is from the side; in such case we do not say both since each needs the other. But for us who say lavud in the middle we say in one sukkah dofen akumah and also lavud if that sukkah needs dofen akumah and also lavud since they do not need each other — therefore Beit Yosef did not bring Ran for us. However this is not so — even if this reasoning, there is nevertheless a practical difference for us too, for sometimes one needs dofen akumah and also lavud and they need each other per Magen Avraham in this siman citing Ran that we only say dofen akumah when walls reach the sechach — so too when walls are high to the sechach but sechach is distant across the width and there is air — one cannot say dofen akumah per Ran's view; but if that air is not three tefachim wide one could say first lavud and it is as if walls reach the sechach and then say dofen akumah — they need each other, for one cannot say dofen akumah unless lavud is said first. And what difference whether one must say lavud first then dofen akumah or the reverse — and we find such when walls are distant from sechach less than three tefachim with air there — must say lavud first then dofen akumah — so for us too there is a difference in Ran's words: in such case we do not say both, and Beit Yosef should have brought Ran.
