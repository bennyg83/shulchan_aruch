(There) "But it appears to me that Rashi's intent..." Note: this is unnecessary, for according to us, even when there is no wall in such a case it is valid. I also do not know what he repaired by this to answer the difficulty of Maharsha. If the intent is that when there is a wall interrupting, the wall divides the board into two pieces, one handbreadth outside and three inside, then why did Rashi explain that there is no wall, since inside it could be excluded by means of bent wall? And if the intent is that only through bent wall is the board considered two pieces, then it is forbidden to sit beneath it, for it is invalid s'chach, since from the wall and inward we consider it such according to the view that one does not sleep beneath it unless it is less than three, as the Beis Yosef brought in his name in this siman, and as the Rosh wrote in Sukkah at the end of chapter 3: "Rashi did not validate the entire sukkah, for it has no s'chach, since we view the s'chach as though it is bent." One can say that Rashi holds that in a large sukkah, invalid s'chach that is less than four may be slept under. This is the view of the Razah, as the Ran brought in his name in the third chapter of Sukkah. According to those poskim who do not validate sleeping beneath it in a large sukkah except when it is less than three, they hold that the wall divides the board into two pieces without bent wall, and the board does not have the name of invalid s'chach, so it is permitted to sleep beneath it.
