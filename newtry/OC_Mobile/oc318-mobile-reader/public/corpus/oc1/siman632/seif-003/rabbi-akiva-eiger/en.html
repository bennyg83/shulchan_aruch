Magen Avraham, s.k. 3: As is explained in Shulchan Aruch. This means that it initially states that the invalid s’chach exceeds the valid s’chach. In my humble opinion, one can say that if the invalid s’chach is located at the side of the fourth wall, then if the sukkah as a whole measures more than seven by seven tefachim it is valid, because we regard the invalid s’chach as part of the sukkah up to slightly less than three tefachim. What difference does it make if still more invalid s’chach lies beyond it? The same applies even if the invalid s’chach lies beside one of the middle walls: we apply the principle of a curved wall to part of the invalid s’chach, while the remainder is invalid s’chach measuring less than three tefachim, which combines to complete the minimum measure of a sukkah. If, however, three tefachim of invalid s’chach lie in the middle of the sukkah, one can say that it is invalid when the valid s’chach alone does not contain the minimum measure of a sukkah, since the entire area occupied by the invalid s’chach (three tefachim) is necessarily treated as outside the sukkah. If so, the implications in the wording of Shulchan Aruch do not conflict. Consider this carefully. The wording of Maharsha cited in Magen Avraham, s.k. 1, implies that even when the sukkah is open on the fourth side, the valid s’chach alone must contain the minimum measure of a sukkah.
