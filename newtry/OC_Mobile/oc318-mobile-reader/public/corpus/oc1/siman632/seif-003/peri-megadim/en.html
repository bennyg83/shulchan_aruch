It is valid. See Turei Zahav: it is as though it is not present, as he wrote at the end. This is not because of nullification and not because of lavud, for where an actual substance interrupts, lavud is not relevant; lavud applies specifically to open air [see Tosafos Shabbos in the laws of Eruvin regarding a side-post]. The words of the Levush, the light of Israel, require much analysis. For if so, you have nullified every prohibition: if we say that one may nullify by Torah law, let one place a neveilah next to two slaughtered animals and the like. Whenever it is recognizable, nullification certainly does not apply. [Regarding Sanhedrin, one can say that since it is not known which view is correct, it is like they are not recognizable; or as the Turei Zahav wrote, that the minority is as though it is not present, and there this is not relevant.] The minority is transformed; unlike prohibitions, where the minority is considered as though it is not present, as derived from Sanhedrin, as Rashi wrote at the beginning of Beitzah, and automatically the same is true in reverse regarding permissibility. One need not throw away one except rabbinically, as in Yoreh De'ah, siman 109. See Tosafos in Sanhedrin regarding those liable for stoning among those liable for burning; according to one answer, one can say that by Torah law one must throw away one. In Pri Megadim there, in the introduction to the laws of mixtures, we wrote about this. Tosafos in Chullin regarding a fixed item wrote that although he does not know from whom he bought, we should have said to follow the majority, for for now it is not recognizable; the law of fixed is a novelty. But where it is recognizable, it is obvious without proof that it is not nullified. The Turei Zahav, of blessed memory, wrote similarly with a fortiori reasoning.
