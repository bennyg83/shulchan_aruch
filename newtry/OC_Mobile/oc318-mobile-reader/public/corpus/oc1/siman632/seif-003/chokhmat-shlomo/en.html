But in a small sukkah that has only seven by seven, etc. Note: This implies that if it did not have nine handbreadths, with two of invalid s'chach and two of open air, it does not invalidate. In my humble opinion, it appears that there is a distinction in the matter. If there are two handbreadths of invalid s'chach adjacent to the valid s'chach that completes the seven, and afterward there are two handbreadths of open air, this is valid, since one does not need to join the open air to the invalid s'chach and to the body of the sukkah; let it be as though it is not present. But if the reverse is true, namely five handbreadths of valid s'chach, then two handbreadths of open air, and then two handbreadths of invalid s'chach, the sukkah is invalid. My reason is this: although we rule that we say lavud, nevertheless that means only that the thing adjacent to the open air is considered as though it extends further, also opposite the open air, and it is as though closed. If so, this is fine when on the two sides there is something valid for s'chach, for then we say lavud. But if on one side invalid s'chach is placed, what have you seen that we should say lavud so that it is as though the valid s'chach extends further? Perhaps the opposite: the invalid s'chach extends further. Since without the invalid material there would be no relevance to lavud, and only the invalid material opposite it causes one to say lavud, if so it is as though the invalid material extends further, and there are two more handbreadths of invalid s'chach. Either way: if we do not say lavud, there is no sukkah measure; and if we consider lavud, it is as though there was additional invalid s'chach. A proof to our words is from what Tosafos asked: why do we need "for yourself" to exclude a stolen sukkah? Let it be derived because it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. What did they ask? One can say that the practical difference is this: if the sukkah was nine handbreadths and had two handbreadths of invalid s'chach, then without the reason of the exclusion from "for yourself," but only because of mitzvah through transgression, it would be valid here. For were it not for the transgression, if there were only two handbreadths of stolen s'chach here and two were open air, it too would be valid because of lavud. If so, he does not fulfill the mitzvah through the transgression. By contrast, now that the Torah excluded stolen s'chach and it has an intrinsic disqualification, now it is four handbreadths together and is invalid s'chach that invalidates with four. But if Scripture had not revealed that stolen s'chach is invalid, and it were invalid only because of mitzvah through transgression, then we would require that he fulfill the mitzvah through the transgression, and here, if there were not four, it would also be valid through two handbreadths of open air, for it would help through the law of lavud. Since Tosafos did not answer this way, it is proven that in such a case too it is invalid and lavud does not help, for the reason above. Examine carefully. However, one can reject this proof, for one can say that in any case, were it not that the s'chach was invalid, lavud would not be relevant, since it is more than three handbreadths distant from the wall; if so, in the end he fulfills it through a transgression and it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. Nevertheless, even if there is no proof, the law is correct as I wrote. Now, while on this matter, it appears to explain the language of the Yerushalmi in the first chapter of Sukkah, halachah 1, and this is its language: Rabbi Yosei and the Rabbis in the name of Chizkiyah: why did they say that invalid s'chach invalidates only with four amos? Because it did not come to the permissibility of the sukkah. These are its words. See Korban HaEidah, who pressed himself in explaining that its intent is bent wall. This is astonishing, for what does "the permissibility of the sukkah" have to do with this? Even without this, there are walls. Rather, it appears that this works well according to one who examines the Talmud in Sukkah 17b, at the beginning of the sugya there on 17a: "And I said to them: what if there is invalid s'chach less than four and open air less than three, etc.," until the end. There it is not because their measures are equal to one another, but because there is no measure of a sukkah. If so, it works very well: Chizkiyah will hold like Rav, who holds in our Talmud that even in the middle it invalidates with four amos, and therefore he comes to give a reason for his words, why we require four amos and depend on measure; perhaps it should be with four handbreadths because of separation. But if it is because of separation, it is difficult: why, with open air less than three and invalid s'chach less than four, is it not invalid? Yet if so, it is difficult: according to your reasoning, why do we not say with less, etc.? Rather, perforce the reason is that since their measures are not equal, they do not combine. But it is difficult: they are equal in their measures in a small sukkah. Therefore he said that from there there is no proof, for there it is not because of combination, but because they came only to the permissibility of the sukkah, and there is no measure. Nevertheless, they do not combine. Therefore, when it is less than four amos it is valid, because the matter depends on the measure, and therefore they do not combine; but according to those who hold that it is because of separation, they should combine. From this it is proven that its measure is four amos. In my opinion, I have aimed at the truth in the view of the Yerushalmi, thank Hashem. Examine very carefully.
