Magen Avraham, s.k. 1: They reach the s’chach. Ran and Bach. Why do we not first apply gud asik, making it as though the walls reach the s’chach, and then apply the principle of a curved wall? In my humble opinion, Ran wrote this in accordance with his own position in chapter 2 concerning a gap of three tefachim that was reduced by placing spits beside the wall. According to the opinion that lavud does not apply in the middle of a sukkah, it is invalid; we do not first apply the principle of a curved wall and then apply lavud from the side, because we do not combine two halachic constructs; see there. This is because lavud can apply only by means of the halachic principle of a curved wall. The same applies here: the principle of a curved wall can apply only by means of the halachic construct of gud asik. Consider this carefully. I am therefore surprised that Magen Avraham cited these words of Ran as practical law, for it appears that we do not rule like Ran on this point. The Tur and Shulchan Aruch above, siman 630, seif 9, validate the sukkah even if the wall is not actually aligned beneath the s’chach. In that case, where the walls do not reach the height of the s’chach, lavud can be invoked only if we first apply the principle of a curved wall, yet the sukkah is nevertheless validated in this manner. Consider this carefully. I discussed this at length in a responsum, with the help of Heaven.
