1) [Seif 1] Invalid s'chach, etc. This applies even if the s'chach is invalid only by rabbinic law. A proof is found in Sukkah 14b, in the case where he placed a board four tefachim wide at the edge of the roofing, etc.; see there. So too wrote Mishna Berurah in Shaar HaTziyun, os 1. See below, os 9. Also see above, siman 629, regarding what is called invalid s'chach.
2) Ibid. It invalidates in the middle with four tefachim. For an area of four tefachim is significant enough to constitute a separation, and it therefore separates the valid s'chach on one side of the sukkah from the valid s'chach on the other. Rashi, Sukkah 17a. This applies when the invalid s'chach extends across the entire sukkah and divides the sukkah into two, and no area with the minimum valid dimensions of a sukkah together with walls remains in one place, as written below, seif 2, in the Rama's gloss; see there.
3) There: But less than four, it is valid, and it is permitted to sleep under it. And the same law applies to eating under it. Beit Yosef; Levush. And see below, ot 17.
4) Ibid. And one may sleep beneath it. The Ran writes that the Ra'avad disagrees, maintaining that the statement permitting one to sleep beneath it was said only when it is less than three tefachim, but if it is three tefachim wide, one may not sleep beneath it even in a large sukkah. Bach. So too wrote the Ritva in his novellae to Sukkah 19, stating that his teacher agreed with this view. So too wrote Shiltei HaGibborim in the name of the Riaz. Their words were cited by Chayei Adam, klal 146, os 21, who writes that one should therefore be stringent. So too wrote Mishna Berurah, os 3, that one should ideally be stringent.
