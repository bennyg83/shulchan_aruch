For we say. See Turei Zahav and the Mishnah in Sukkah 17a; see there. His entire intent, of blessed memory, is that the halachah follows the Tur: even where the wall is three handbreadths distant from the s'chach, and invalid s'chach of up to four amos is at the side, we say two principles, either "extend the partition upward" and afterward bent wall, even though the Ran was stringent in this, as was the Bach; see Magen Avraham, letter 1. The Mechaber omitted it and said nothing about it. The Darkei Moshe brought the Ran, and the Turei Zahav, of blessed memory, decided like the Tur. In siman 630:4, where we say two principles, extend upward and lavud, the same applies here: extend upward and bent wall. See Bach. He discussed the explanation of bent wall at length, for one can give three interpretations. First, invalid s'chach of up to four amos is considered a wall, as though it bent and folded upward; Rashi and the Ran, of blessed memory, agreed to this, and as the Mechaber wrote, it emerges that at the edge of the wall near the s'chach we view it as though some of the invalid s'chach was given there to the wall and bent. This is unlike where there is open air of three handbreadths across, for this does not apply and it is invalid. One could say that part of the wall is given to the s'chach, but this is not so, for what does it have to give? [And this is what he wrote, "as I wrote before this," meaning: just as previously the s'chach gives to the wall, so too let us say the reverse, that the wall gives to the s'chach; this is impossible, for what does it have to give?] Perforce "bent" means that the wall goes at a slant to the valid s'chach. If so, there is no difference at all between invalid s'chach at the side and open air of three handbreadths, for all is one: the wall goes at a slant. Why, then, does the Mishnah teach that if he distanced the wall from the s'chach three handbreadths it is invalid? The Tur holds this from reasoning, that we say two principles, as in siman 630:9. What the Turei Zahav wrote as an a fortiori argument requires some analysis. What he wrote about the Beis Yosef is similar to what the Bach asked; see there. Read the words "daled-ayin" as "bent wall," meaning: there is no proof from the fact that Rashi did not write simply that with an open space of three handbreadths across one cannot say bent wall. Since we do not say bent wall with open air in height, one could say: extend upward, and invalid s'chach gives a part, etc.; all the more so across, that we do not say it. This is astonishing. Was Rashi trying to prove that with open air across three handbreadths we do not say bent wall? That is a Mishnah: if he distanced it, it is invalid. Rather, Rashi means that bent wall means it bends and folds upward; according to this, in every law where it runs horizontally and we do not say bent wall, the same applies with height: in such a case we do not say bent wall. If we would say bent wall, etc. Against the reasoning of the Ran, of blessed memory, he asked: in siman 630:9 we say two principles, extend upward and lavud; the same should apply to extend upward and bent wall, since bent wall and lavud are laws given to Moshe at Sinai. Why should we not equate them and say two principles here as there? From the Mishnah, "if he distanced, etc.," one can answer that it speaks of a small sukkah of seven by seven, where bent wall by slant and diagonal does not leave a height of ten handbreadths and a length of seven by seven, as in siman 631 in Magen Avraham, letter 10. What Rashi, of blessed memory, wrote regarding bent wall can be explained like the Ran, of blessed memory: that therefore we require specifically that the wall reach the s'chach, for otherwise it is invalid. If the explanation of bent wall were by diagonal, one could say it is valid even when the wall does not reach the s'chach. This is what Rashi, of blessed memory, was precise about, and this is what he wrote: if so, regarding open air too let us say this. The Beis Yosef, of blessed memory, understood this as meaning "as in the Mishnah," that is, Rashi should have said briefly: if so, why is it invalid when he distanced it, as in the language of the Mishnah? The phrase "let us say this too" is extra. Rather, Rashi means that if we say bent wall by slant and diagonal, the wall goes, and the Mishnah is in a small sukkah; if so, in a large sukkah, with open air running across as in the Mishnah, it would be valid, and we have not heard this. The Beis Yosef therefore inferred well: if so, he should have said simply that this cannot validate, for it is all the more so from open air itself, regarding which we do not say bent wall; certainly in such a case, and he should have stated the greater novelty. Understand this and see Bach.
Know that it is implied from the Ran, of blessed memory, that it is specifically when the wall reaches the invalid s'chach; otherwise, even with less than three handbreadths, we do not say bent wall. See Magen Avraham. This requires analysis, for it is impossible that there not be some crack and space between wall and s'chach. Is it as though he attached them to one another with nails and boards? It implies that any gap less than three handbreadths is like lavud, and we say bent wall. Specifically when there are three handbreadths between the wall and the invalid s'chach in height, we do not say bent wall according to the Ran. With Hashem's help, this will be explained in Magen Avraham.
