Is bent. It implies specifically when the walls reach the s'chach. Ran and Bach; see Magen Avraham. The Turei Zahav disagrees with this and permits even when the walls do not reach the s'chach; see there. See Responsa Panim Me'iros, siman 61: when one makes a sukkah inside a house, and above is the roof covered with shingles, he must remove them until the place where there will be less than three opposite the walls, since the walls do not reach the s'chach. Likewise, one who makes his sukkah outside and combines walls built under a shingled roof, and the walls do not reach the roof even within less than three, and the roof projects four handbreadths: according to the Ran, this sukkah has no partitions, and one must be careful about this, for it is common. These are his words.
To the walls. It appears to me that if the roof is slanted like our roofs, even though there are four in its slope, it does not invalidate unless there are four in its horizontal projection, for then it occupies four in the sukkah. And if the roof is bent above, and below near it there is a beam, even though it does not extend from it three handbreadths, since in height it is distant from it three handbreadths, they do not join, and we do not say chavot and lavud to be stringent. Magen Avraham; see there.
