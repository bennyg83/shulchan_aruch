The wall is bent. It implies specifically when the walls reach the s'chach [Ran and Bach]. Know that if one placed a board four handbreadths wide at the mouth of the sukkah, even though he inserted only three handbreadths into the sukkah, it is invalid, for it is like s'chach extending from the sukkah, which is judged like the sukkah. The Ba'al HaMa'or explains this specifically in a small sukkah; examine carefully [Bach]. This is Rashi's language: "At the mouth of the sukkah, near the fourth side, where there is no wall from which it could be excluded through bent wall, for here there is no wall but only an opening; and even though he did not insert all of it into the interior of the sukkah, it is invalid, for that handbreadth outside is also considered sukkah, since if it were valid s'chach it would be permitted to sleep there and fulfill the obligation of sukkah," as written in siman 631:7. The Rashal wrote that this refers to a small sukkah, for if the measure of a sukkah remained toward the middle, it is obvious that it is valid. If so, it requires analysis why Rashi wrote "where there is no wall, etc.," for even if there were a wall there it would be invalid, since the measure of a sukkah did not remain. These are his words. If so, according to him, even in a large sukkah that has a wall, if the board is fixed partly outside the wall and partly inside, it is forbidden to sit beneath it. For even though we say bent wall, it is forbidden to sit there, since it is invalid s'chach. If so, even if it is covered with small boards called shingles, if there is a width of three handbreadths inside it is forbidden to sit beneath them; and if there is another handbreadth outside, all of it is considered one piece, since they are connected with nails and there is no space between them [see what I wrote in siman 627:3 in the name of the Rashba]. But it appears to me that Rashi's intent is that if there were a wall there, we would say bent wall, and that handbreadth projecting outward would not be considered part of the sukkah at all, and it would be as though the board did not contain four, since part of it is outside. If so, all of it would be valid s'chach, for now the initial assumption was that if boards are turned on their sides, they are valid; examine carefully. And if you say: although we rule that if they were turned on their sides they are invalid, nevertheless here it is invalid s'chach of less than four, since the wall is between them and it does not join, and it is permitted to sit beneath it. So it appears to me.
The invalid s'chach, etc. It appears to me that if the roof is slanted like our roofs, even though there are four in its slope, it is not invalid unless there are four in its horizontal projection, for then it occupies four within the sukkah. And if the roof is bent above, and below near it there is a beam, even though it does not extend from it three handbreadths, since in height it is distant from it three handbreadths, they do not join, and we do not say chavot and lavud to be stringent.
Only seven by seven. This is not precise, for even if it has nine and a half, and invalid s'chach of three handbreadths, it is invalid, since if you remove the invalid part there is no valid sukkah measure, as I wrote above in Shulchan Aruch; and so it is implied from Rashi's language.
