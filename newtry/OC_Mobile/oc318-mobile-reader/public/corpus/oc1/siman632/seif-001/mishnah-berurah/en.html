(1) Invalid s'chach. See above in siman 629 regarding what is called invalid s'chach.
(2) In the middle, with four handbreadths. This means that a measure of four handbreadths runs across the entire sukkah [which has three walls] along its length. A measure of four handbreadths is a significant place to separate and divide between the walls, until the walls no longer help one another and they appear like two sukkos, each having only two walls.
(3) And it is permitted to sleep beneath it. Some poskim hold that although less than four handbreadths leaves the sukkah valid, nevertheless under that place it is forbidden to sleep and eat unless that place is less than three handbreadths; therefore one should be stringent initially.
(4) As though the wall is bent, etc. At first glance this language implies that this is specifically when the walls reach the s'chach. But if the walls do not reach the s'chach, although generally we are not particular about this, as above in siman 630:9, for we consider them as though they reach upward until the s'chach, nevertheless in our case, where there is invalid s'chach over the sukkah and we wish to validate it because of bent wall, we do not say bent wall in this case. Some say that even if the walls do not reach the s'chach, we also say that we view the wall as though it reaches upward and bends. Possibly, where from the walls to the s'chach is less than three handbreadths, everyone agrees that one may be lenient, for we say lavud and bent wall.
(5) The invalid s'chach, etc. If the s'chach above is not laid evenly but slants like our roofs, even though there are four amos in its slope, it does not invalidate unless there are four amos in its horizontal projection. And if it is bent above, and below near it there is a beam, so that with the beam there will be four amos, even though the beam does not extend from it three handbreadths, since in height it is distant from it three handbreadths, they do not join.
(6) However, one does not sleep beneath it as long as, etc. Meaning that although the whole sukkah is valid, nevertheless that place, since it is four handbreadths, is a significant place of its own and is not nullified to the sukkah.
(7) That has four handbreadths. Possibly even if inside the sukkah it has only three handbreadths and one handbreadth projects outward, nevertheless it is forbidden to sit beneath it.
(8) That has only seven by seven. This is not precise, for even if the sukkah holds nine and a half and it has invalid s'chach of three handbreadths, it invalidates the whole sukkah, since if you remove the invalid part there is no valid sukkah measure [Magen Avraham]. See Eliyah Rabbah.
(9) It is valid, and one sleeps beneath it. Since it is a small amount, it is considered as though it is not present [Turei Zahav].
