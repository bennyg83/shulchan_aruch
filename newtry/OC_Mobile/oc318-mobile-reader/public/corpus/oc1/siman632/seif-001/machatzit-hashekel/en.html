(Small paragraph 1) The wall, etc. Reaches the s'chach. For if open air interrupts between the wall and the s'chach, how can one say bent wall, meaning that the s'chach is considered a wall and will need the wall? It cannot be joined, for open air interrupts.
And know, etc. And it is judged as a sukkah. This is Rashi's language there on this matter, page 14b at the end: "That handbreadth outside is also considered like a sukkah, for if it were valid s'chach it would be permitted to sleep there and he would fulfill the obligation of sukkah, for we say later that s'chach extending out from the sukkah in such a case is judged as a sukkah." These are Rashi's words. This is what is mentioned in siman 631:6. Meaning: just as regarding validation, even though the middle wall was not made for the outer partitions, nevertheless it is valid, for perforce the inner and outer parts are one s'chach; the same applies regarding disqualification: we consider it as though the fourth handbreadth too is inside.
If so, according to him. Meaning, according to Maharsha, for it implies that he holds that even if there is a wall there, nevertheless we consider that handbreadth outside as though it is inside, and it is a board of four handbreadths inside, and there is invalid s'chach of four handbreadths, which invalidates a sukkah. It is only that we would validate it because of bent wall, as in every place where there is invalid s'chach of less than four amos. About this Maharsha asked: what does bent wall help? In any event the sukkah is invalid, for these three handbreadths of the board are lacking from the measure of the sukkah, since we are dealing with a small sukkah. If so, according to Maharsha's view, even in a large sukkah, although if we say bent wall a sukkah measure remains and it is valid, nevertheless it is forbidden to sit and sleep beneath those three handbreadths, like the law of bent wall.
And there is no space between them. See what I wrote in siman 627, etc. He wrote there that we do not consider them as one even when they are fixed with nails unless he connected them together before placing them on the roof. This applies specifically there because there is space between them, even if the space is not three handbreadths. This is unlike here, where there is no space between them at all.
It is all valid s'chach. "For now, etc." Meaning: there the amoraim disagree about boards four handbreadths wide, which are invalid for s'chach. If he turned them on their sides, and the sides are not four handbreadths wide, may one use them for s'chach? Do we say that since none of the board within the sukkah is four handbreadths wide it is permitted, or perhaps a board that is four handbreadths wide has the name of invalid s'chach upon it, and even though no four handbreadths of it are in the sukkah, nevertheless it is forbidden, just like metal spits, which are invalid even though they are not four handbreadths wide. The Talmud sought to challenge the one who says that one may use them for s'chach if he turned them on their sides, from the Baraisa: if he placed on it a board four handbreadths wide, even though he inserted only three handbreadths into it, it is invalid. What are the circumstances? Is it not such a case, where he turned them on their sides and the sides are only three handbreadths wide? Evidently in such a case it is invalid. The Talmud answers: really, if he turned them on their sides it is valid; here we are dealing with a case where he placed a board four handbreadths wide at the mouth of the sukkah and inserted three handbreadths into the sukkah, while one handbreadth projects outside the sukkah. Through bent wall, those three handbreadths have the status of wall upon them and do not join with the handbreadth that projects outside, according to the view of the Magen Avraham. If so, those three handbreadths also have the law of valid s'chach, since only three handbreadths of the board are in the sukkah. Although the status of wall is upon them, nevertheless it is valid s'chach, for we say bent wall only so that the handbreadth projecting outside should not join with it. If so, it joins to the measure of a valid sukkah, since there are only three handbreadths in the sukkah; for one may cover the whole sukkah with boards four handbreadths wide if he inserts into the sukkah only three handbreadths from each board, namely where he turned them on their sides. With this Maharsha's question is resolved, for he had to say that there is no wall there, since if there were a wall it would be valid, even though we are speaking of a small sukkah, as above.
And although we rule, etc. Meaning: what of it? Granted, Maharsha's question is resolved according to the initial assumption, like the one who says that if he turned them on their sides it is valid, for if there were a wall there, the three handbreadths too would have the name of valid s'chach. But in practice there is no practical difference, since we rule that if he turned them on their sides it is invalid. If so, although when there is a wall we say through bent wall that the handbreadth projecting outside does not join with the three handbreadths inside, nevertheless the three handbreadths inside have the law of invalid s'chach, as when they are turned on their sides. If so, the same would apply to shingles in such a case. Nevertheless, it is invalid s'chach of less than four handbreadths. Even according to the one who says that if he turned them on their sides it is invalid, it invalidates only when he covered the entire sukkah with the sides of boards next to one another. But because of one board that he turned on its side, the sukkah does not become invalid. For even if we say that it has the law of iron spits, even with spits and with all invalid s'chach, less than four handbreadths in a large sukkah may be slept beneath.
