Turei Zahav, small paragraph 2. "Since this is a significant place of its own, etc." This is astonishing, for Rashi wrote this reason about the case where invalid s'chach in the middle invalidates with four handbreadths. But here the reason is that its validation is only through bent wall, and therefore although the sukkah is valid, one should not sleep beneath it. So too the Levush wrote the reason, and it is clear from the words of Rashi and the other poskim. In truth, this language that the Turei Zahav wrote here is Rashi's language in Sukkah 17b, s.v. "rather, according to you, etc.": "for this is a significant place of its own and it separates in this sukkah, etc." See there. [What is written in the Turei Zahav, "through separating," the words "through" are a scribal error, and it should read "and it separates."] There it is speaking about invalid s'chach in the middle. Therefore it appears to be a scribal error, and this notation should be moved earlier, at the beginning of the siman, on what he wrote "in the middle, with four handbreadths." So wrote the glosses of Mekor Chayim. It is also possible that the Turei Zahav intended to explain that which he says, "as long as it has four handbreadths," but with less than four one sleeps beneath it. Seemingly, one might say that this law applies specifically in the middle, where less than four is nullified by the valid s'chach on this side and that side; therefore he wrote above that with less than four handbreadths one sleeps beneath it. But here, with less than four from the side, although the sukkah is valid because of bent wall, nevertheless one should not sleep beneath it, since valid s'chach is only on one side. See the Ran, who says that the Ra'avad holds that even in the middle, with less than four, one does not sleep beneath it, only with less than three; but this is not the view of the Razah, and so is the view of the Tur. Therefore the Turei Zahav wrote here that the primary reason for four handbreadths is that it is considered a significant place of its own. Automatically, with less than four, since it is not significant, it is nullified to the rest of the valid sukkah, whether in the middle or from the side, and one does not need bent wall at all with less than four handbreadths. Perhaps the end of the language is missing, and it should read: this is the reason for the invalidity of four handbreadths in the middle, and the same applies here, specifically when there are four handbreadths. So it appears in my humble opinion.
