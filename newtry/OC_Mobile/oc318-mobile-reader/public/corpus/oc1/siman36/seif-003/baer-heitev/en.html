Shatnez Getz. The taggin must touch the bodies of the letters, and each tag must be separate from the others. Responsa Rema MiPano, siman 35. They should not be attached at the end of the letter, but in its middle. If the taggin do not touch the bodies of the letters, or if they touch one another, the writing is invalid. Nevertheless, if we know that an expert scribe wrote them, we assume that they separated only after they were written and we validate them. Rema MiPano; Magen Avraham. See there, where he concludes that if he made a ches according to Rashi's form it is valid even if he lengthened it. Although it is not square, it is valid. See above, siman 32, seif katan 40.
It does not invalidate. Bach invalidates the writing if the letters Shatnez Getz were not crowned. It appears to me that it is proper to repair them afterward. Even in tefillin the repair is effective, and this does not violate the requirement that the letters be written in order. Olas Tamid and Knesses HaGedolah write likewise. See Taz. Sefer Baruch She'amar cites many authorities who invalidate the writing when these letters were not crowned. Responsa Radach writes that the same applies if one added more than the three zayins mentioned in the Gemara: the writing is invalid. Sefer Iggeres HaTiyul writes that the letters of Shatnez form the words Satan and idolatry, which are two great accusers. This is also the secret of Shatnez Getz, for Getz is likewise the name of an accuser. The taggin upon these letters are like a sword and spear through which one is protected from them.
