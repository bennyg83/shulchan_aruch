(Seif katan 3) It is necessary, etc. “If they do not, etc., they are invalid.” He must necessarily hold like Magen Avraham's later citation of Bach, who invalidates when the letters Shatnez Getz were not crowned, unlike Shulchan Aruch, which validates even where they were not crowned at all. All the more so, according to that view, they are invalid when the taggin touch one another. Olas Tamid comments upon the conclusion that where we know an expert wrote them we validate them: “We learn from his words that if the taggin were originally made properly and afterward became damaged, one should not invalidate the writing. This requires study as practical halachah.”
A ches according to Rashi's view. Menachos states that Rabbi Elazar said: I saw an expert scribe who made the roof of the ches with a choter. Rashi understands choter as a staff, and therefore requires the ches to be square with a staff projecting upward on the left side. Rabbeinu Tam understands the term as a camel's hump and therefore requires two zayins with a hump above them. The passage discusses a case where the hump remains in its proper place. If the letter was made with the hump above the end of the dalet, one may erase part of the dalet, leaving only the end of its head, and add a line so that it becomes a zayin. The hump then remains in its proper place. If, however, the hump ends in the middle of the dalet, then erasing part of the dalet and turning it into a zayin would leave the hump extending beyond the zayin, not in its proper place. Why does erasing only part of the dalet help? Ordinarily one must erase everything written in invalid form, as explained above in siman 32, seifim 17 and 18, and the whole dalet was written while the ches was invalid. The answer is that there was no invalidity in forming the dalet. At the moment it was written, the scribe could have completed the ches according to Rashi by adding the staff. Although he now makes the left side into a zayin according to Rabbeinu Tam, the dalet itself was written validly, because even completing it as Rashi's ches would have been valid. Responsa Avodas HaGershuni, siman 82, writes likewise.
