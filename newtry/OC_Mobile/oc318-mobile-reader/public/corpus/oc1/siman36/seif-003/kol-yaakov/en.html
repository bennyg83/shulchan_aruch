3. Tefillin written in Ashkenazic script. Since the Ashkenazic and Sephardic communities differ in certain forms of Ashuris letters, Maharam ibn Chaviv, in manuscript responsum 4, rules that tefillin written in Ashkenazic script are invalid for Sephardim. Rabbi Yaakov Molcho likewise writes this in manuscript responsum 110. Birkei Yosef, letter 2, cites both rulings, and Beis Oved, in the laws of tefillin and straps, letter 26, follows them. He writes, however: “In pressing circumstances, when tefillin written in Sephardic script are unavailable, he should put on Ashkenazic tefillin so as not to neglect the mitzvah, but without a blessing.” In his later work Beis Menuchah, in the laws of letter forms, letter 76, he retracted this ruling. He cites Responsa Sedeh HaAretz, part 3, Yoreh Deah, siman 18, and Noda BiYehudah, second edition, Yoreh Deah, end of siman 171, that daily practice shows an Ashkenazi in a Sephardic synagogue is called to the Torah, recites the blessing, and reads from their scroll, and likewise a Sephardi in an Ashkenazic synagogue. He therefore writes that the same applies to tefillin: Ashkenazic-script tefillin are valid for a Sephardi, contrary to Maharam ibn Chaviv and Rabbi Yaakov Molcho, who invalidated each community's script for the other. Their view is not accepted. The tefillin are valid and may be worn with a blessing. What he wrote earlier in Beis Oved is therefore not the halachah. This is the primary ruling both for tefillin and for a Sefer Torah. See there.
