It is necessary to add taggin. The taggin must touch the bodies of the letters, and each one must be separate from the others. Responsa Rema MiPano, siman 35. They should not be attached at the end of the letter, but in its middle. Sefer HaZichronos. If they do not touch the bodies of the letters, or if they touch one another, the writing is invalid. Nevertheless, if we know that an expert scribe wrote them, we assume they separated only after being written and validate them. Rema MiPano there. See what I wrote in siman 32, seif katan 3. It appears to me that if he formed a ches according to Rashi, in the form illustrated in the source, it is valid, as Beit Yosef writes that he found recorded. He further cites Rabbi Yitzchak Aboab that ches is among the square letters, and therefore should not be lengthened at the end of a line, because then it would no longer appear as two zayins. It also should not be made like a dalet and zayin. If he made it in that form, he should scrape away part of the dalet until it becomes a zayin. Olas Tamid in the name of Maharam Mottal. This applies where the hump remains in its proper place. Sefer Yere'im writes that one should combine both views, Rashi and Rabbeinu Tam. This addition does not damage the letter. Although the letters of Rabbi Akiva say that no crown is attached to the ches, the meaning is that one is not obligated to make it so. If one wishes, he may do so, provided he places it at the end of the roof on the left side so that it appears like the head of the left leg, and not as a separate crown above the roof. He should not lengthen the roof at all. If he lengthened it, the letter is invalid and cannot be repaired in tefillin. It appears to me that this refers to a case where he made two zayins with a wide roof rather than a hump. If he made the ches according to Rashi, however, then even if he lengthened it, it remains valid after the fact although it is not square. Bach invalidates if the letters Shatnez Getz were not crowned. It appears to me that it is proper to repair them, and even in tefillin the repair is effective.
