If it does not touch, they are invalid. The reading of a child does not help here. Since the intended letter is known and there is no possibility of confusing it with another letter, the reading of a child is useful only where the doubt arises because it may resemble another letter, as explained in siman 32, seif 16. Beit Yosef cites Agur that the two projections made upon a yod must be formed before beginning the next letter, because according to Rabbeinu Tam the yod is invalid without them. If one wrote another letter and only afterward added the projections, the writing would be out of order and must be placed in genizah. Beit Yosef afterward cites Ri'as that only the right leg is indispensable regarding writing in order, but not the point, because even without the point it is still a yod. It appears from Beit Yosef that he did not accept Agur's stringency as practical halachah, and therefore omitted it from Shulchan Aruch. On the contrary, siman 32 implies that where some of the yods upon the shins or similar components do not touch, but a child recognizes the letter, the child's reading helps and the repair is not considered out of order, since the letter's form remains recognizable. It is obvious that the same applies here, all the more so, because disconnected yods are a more serious defect and nevertheless are not considered out of order. Levush, however, writes that one should be stringent concerning the point of the yod, because without it the letter was not a yod. This does not appear correct to me. Repair helps even for the disconnected yods. Hagahos Maimoniyos, chapter 1 of Hilchos Tefillin, explicitly implies that there is even greater room for leniency concerning the point of the yod than concerning disconnected yods of shins. See there concerning the form of the letter kof.
