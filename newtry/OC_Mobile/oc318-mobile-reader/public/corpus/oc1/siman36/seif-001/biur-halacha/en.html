**And the lower dot should be lekhatchila, etc.** — One who examines this volume should know that in many places I did not explain in the text, and also in Sha'ar HaTziyun, whether the law is only lekhatchila or b'dieved; and this is for one of three reasons: either it is obvious that it is lekhatchila or b'dieved, or there are varying details — sometimes lekhatchila and sometimes b'dieved, such as where it is written that a letter must be made thus so that one may place a letter next to it — obviously if one did not do so and thereby a break in a word appears as two words, it is invalid; but if that letter is at the end of a word it is valid b'dieved; and many similar cases — I did not wish to elaborate, therefore I obscured the matter; or I also had doubt and left it as is. Therefore a person should not hurry to be lenient or stringent and cause others loss unless he finds clear proof.

**And one may not be lenient** — so Pri Megadim. However, from the words of Rabbenu Yonah [whom I brought in letter kaf] it is proven that the main matter depends on the bottom; and therefore it seems that if a child reads it correctly, one may at least be lenient to add ink for repair in a Torah scroll, and there is no issue of "not in order."

**And the right leg thin** — from the words of Gra it seems this is only lekhatchila; and according to what Beit Yosef wrote in letter zayin, the reason — see there — apparently it is for delay, for the baraita concludes on all of them "yiganez," see there in Shabbat in the Gemara. And perhaps one may answer that only when the right thigh descends lower than the left is there recognition between gimel and tzadi unlike Beit Shmuel; and therefore descending lower than the left thigh is for delay, and therefore Gra did not mention lekhatchila there; nevertheless for practical law all is uncertain — according to Rosh's explanation there in Hilchot Sefer Torah on this baraita [and see Rashi there on this baraita] there is no proof one must be precise in this; but lekhatchila one should certainly be careful because of other authorities, as Beit Yosef wrote similarly in letter alef.

**And also it should not be crooked** — and b'dieved if it was crooked, uncertain; see Beit Yosef for the reason from what is written in the Gemara: why does the leg of gimel extend toward dalet — in such a case what is written in the Gemara, why does the leg of dalet extend toward gimel, is only lekhatchila [as will be explained in Biur Halacha below]; and in any case correction helps here even in a Torah scroll; and likewise if the left thigh equals the right leg, similar to what Pri Megadim was lenient in letter kaf regarding a simple kaf made square — see there.

**And lekhatchila the leg should be straight, etc.** — the source is in Beit Yosef from Shabbat 104: why does the leg of dalet extend toward gimel, etc.; and it seems this is only lekhatchila, from that which is stated there: "he corrected one letter, he is liable," such as if he removed the tag of dalet and made it resh; and in Eruvin 13a: perhaps a fly sat, etc., see there. And perhaps one may reject: resh is valid b'dieved even if its leg was straight — if so there is no proof; nevertheless for practical law it seems one need not be stringent b'dieved from the above in siman 32 s.k. 96 in Taz — see there; and in brief the general rule of "not in order" that I copied from Pri Megadim — see there.

**And if** — know the doubt is only according to what we clarified in siman 32 s.k. 205 in Biur Halacha: the halachah follows the Gaon Ra'avad that correction helps in a letter that does not appear as two letters even without obvious separation [for Pri Megadim correction does not help in a Torah scroll in this case], such as a yod of alef that does not touch the alef — even though the yod is a separate letter above it, since the other letter is this one, there is no other name upon it — this also helps for the yod: one should not read over it the name yod but the name alef; as Pri Megadim also noted this very reasoning [and only according to his view this helps only with obvious separation]. And we clarified there too: if the chatoteret on top of the head do not touch each other and separation was obvious, correction does not help because it appears as two zayins — we do not say the chatoteret saves the letter so that through the chatoteret it is called chet; still, perhaps even here there is no image of chet because there is no adhesion on one side to the zayin; or perhaps here, chatoteret on top, chet is not severed in its middle — the name chatoteret is upon it and saves the zayin beneath from being called zayin — and similarly in yod-alef; uncertain.

**And it should not be bent much** — what is called "bent much" appears from Baruch SheAmar in letter alef (1): if it touches the ground of the tet; and so from Sefer HaAgur; but uncertain — if so, why did Beit Yosef write the reason here that it completely resembles pei alef, brought from Terumat HaDeshen and Hagahot Maimoniyot [i.e., on the right side, for on the left, the zayin, it is evident it is not pei alef — their view: if the letter partly resembles another letter, it is not ketivah temah; and such is the entire view of Sefer HaYir'im on this baraita regarding alefs and ayins, etc., see there; and an act of Rekach in the name of Maharanach in siman 1, and many disagree with this reasoning — not to elaborate]. Behold in pei alef Beit Shulchan Aruch and Agur wrote, and the halachah was ruled below: if the dot touched on any inner side it is invalid — if so, conversely, if one does not bend it much it would resemble pei alef more; but certainly even if it does not touch the ground of the tet, any great bending is forbidden, and likewise if it touches — at least it partly resembles pei alef; with this I am satisfied why Beit Yosef and Levush and all Shulchan Aruch commentators omitted explaining this — if Beit Shulchan Aruch held thus they would not be silent from explaining and would not write simply "bent much." I also saw to explain part of the law b'dieved: I saw in Gra it is written "and it should not be bent much" lekhatchila — implying b'dieved permitted; and in Lechem David Emet I saw he wrote: one should not bend the letter tet much inward lest it completely resemble pei alef — if the point touches its body inside it is invalid. If we say Gra explains "bent much" like Beit Shulchan Aruch above, this is disputed — Gra validates and others disqualify. However, the main law b'dieved — whether per our explanation or Beit Shulchan Aruch and Agur — uncertain per Sefer HaYir'im and Terumat HaDeshen and Hagahot Maimoniyot that here it completely resembles pei alef; and Sefer HaYir'im and Terumat HaDeshen explicitly wrote this is the baraita's intent: one should not make tet like pei, etc.; and the baraita concluded on all of them "yiganez" [as proven in the Gemara there]. Indeed per Rosh in Hilchot Sefer Torah in the name of Rabbeinu Tam: tet and pei — though they do not resemble each other in writing or reading, Rabbeinu Tam wrote the right side of tet should be bent, and if turned to the left it appears pei — there is no proof here; the baraita intends to teach that though tet resembles pei on one side, still one should not make it pei; but regarding slight vs. great bending the baraita did not teach — whence to forbid? And further proof: Rosh does not hold this distinction of "bent much," for there in Hilchot Sefer Torah he copied almost all of Terumat HaDeshen and omitted this, for he did not hold it; and Tur and Rabbenu Yonah did not mention this, only wrote it should be bent. Conclusion: "bent much" depends on the dispute — per Terumat HaDeshen, Yir'im, and Hagahot Maimoniyot, invalid even b'dieved; per Rosh, Tur, and Riyyono, permitted; and since it is a Torah matter, one should be stringent; and per our explanation above, "bent much" applies even without touching below — forbidden in all respects. However, it is very hard to be stringent b'dieved against Beit Shulchan Aruch and Agur, who imply permitted without touching; but lekhatchila it seems one should correct where possible — i.e., in a Torah scroll; if found during reading, do not take out another scroll for this — rely on validators; but afterward erase the tet until its letter-form is nullified and correct it, otherwise invalid as chet; and in tefillin where "not in order" disqualifies, rely on the validators above. If the bend touches the ground of the tet, even in tefillin invalid — per Lechem David Emet above; do not be lenient against Terumat HaDeshen and Hagahot Maimoniyot; and perhaps Rosh agrees invalid because the form changed; even if shown to a child who reads tet — it is not surrounded by parchment there, as below regarding shin at the end — but for this alone scraping might help in that place alone, unlike Terumat HaDeshen and Hagahot Maimoniyot: this is included in tet-pei in all forms — one must nullify the letter's form, which is not conceivable in tefillin; and so above.

**Also the yod must have a small tag on top, etc.** — b'dieved it seems from Levush one may be lenient; but in my view uncertain: in Beit Shulchan Aruch in the first alef-bet it is explicitly proven for Rabbeinu Tam that the lower point and upper tag are equal regarding invalidation; and that the Mishnah states "even the point of a yod hinders" and did not state the tag — perhaps for emphasis, even though the tag is smaller than the tag, as in Beit Yosef; and Rosh in Hilchot Sefer Torah and Hagahot Maimoniyot and Sefer Torah did not decide; they wrote the point of a yod may mean the upper tag — see there; and in any case it seems even if not hindering b'dieved for reading, lekhatchila one must certainly correct even if found in old passages; and all the more in the left point one must correct and not leave as is in the batim.

**He will drag the entire point, etc.** — I wonder: since it appears as chet, its form changed and it is invalid — and so there is a law of scraping; and one cannot say that since the point is still missing it is not a letter and becomes valid by completing through writing — this is only Rabbeinu Tam, for whom the point hinders; but for Rashi, valid without a point, the letter is made by dragging alone; and perhaps the halachah follows Rashi, for Rabbeinu Tam's law is not clear to all poskim [see Noda BiYehudah Mahadura Tinyana siman 113, who validates b'dieved without a left point]; and to be stringent one should consider his words, not be lenient. And if they say it does not appear as a true small chet but they doubt a small chet and a child reads it yod — then this alone without dragging should help, as Tur ruled regarding any letter in doubt; why did they write a child's reading does not help [one must examine the responsum] — therefore the correct view is that of the Yesh Omrim brought inside.

**If the child, etc., it is possible, etc.** — what I wrote in the language "possible," even though in simple kaf Pri Megadim wrote one may distinguish, for there its extension proves its form, unlike here. Nevertheless, in my humble opinion one may be lenient in pressing need: per the language of Ra'avan brought in Beit Yosef in letter nun, that all bent letters should be round below, etc. — implying here too bent kaf; and if so the main recognition is by the roundness below; and so from Darkhei Moshe brought in letter zayin at the end; and especially Beit Yosef wrote in letter peh that in Assyrian writing they are accustomed lekhatchila to make this kaf angular from above — how can we say that for us, even if a child reads it as a letter, correction does not help because it lacks its essential form? And further proof: the main recognition depends below — Rabbenu Yerucham interpreted on Shabbat that one should not write beitin like kafin — i.e., the scribe should be careful with the point behind the bet below on the right that it not appear as kaf; and automatically one must interpret kafin of beitin similarly, on the lower side; and all Riyyono's words are in Rosh Hilchot Ketanot — see there — except he explained it refers to the lower side; and in any case, by a child and adding ink one may be lenient.

**If he extended the roof, etc.** — this is the language of Darkhei Chayim in Hilchot Ketivat Sefer Torah; he did not mention the measure of doublings because he held even in kaf it is only for the mitzvah [to this he pointed in Darkhei Moshe; see Mordechai on all letters that it is only lekhatchila]; and so proven in Levush siman 36; see Chiddushei Re'ah in siman 32 s.k. 205, unlike Taz on yod in siman 473 and Sha'arei Teshuvah who held his words to be stringent without doublings of leg from roof even b'dieved. And Dagul MeRevavah in siman 32 s.k. 226 in Magen Avraham was stringent in this even b'dieved, because it is proven in Beit Yosef siman 36 — wondrous to me: the examiner there in letter kaf in the second alef-bet will see explicitly he mentioned invalidity only if the roof is longer than its leg; but here he wrote only that there must be doublings, meaning for the mitzvah. Later I found in Berachat Mayim to the Gaon in Even HaEzer order of gittin he also wrote explicitly they were stringent only if the roof is longer than the leg, not half over half — see there; if so, all the more doublings were mentioned only for the mitzvah.

**And scribes practiced according to this view** — see also Pri Megadim who wrote: what they said regarding the right thigh of hei — if it has the fullness of a small letter, namely yod, it is valid — it seems to me the same for other letters, such as dalet for lamed, etc.: if the right thigh remains like a yod, that suffices. And it seems in lamed his intent is regarding this lower line: even if not extended below thin like a yod, that suffices — for his intent is to be lenient even in the middle round line on the right; if so, there is no image of lamed at all.

**Like kaf and vav** — I wrote all per Beit Yosef and later poskim, unlike Pri Megadim who wrote its image is like nun and vav, for there is no source; perhaps their reason from seif 18 where the Mechaber wrote: drag the trunk until it remains like a bent nun — that too is no proof, for perhaps there the Mechaber in laws of letter-writing did not specify lekhatchila custom for the mitzvah, since even nun-vav is valid for all; or he relied on Sephardic custom not extending the upper line until opposite the lower base, as in Lechem David Emet — if so it resembles nun-vav, as written there in Lechem David Emet: for them mem resembles tet, and in tet it is known what Rabbenu Chananel wrote — resembles nun-vav, as Beit Yosef in letter nun; but per our practice, dragging until kaf-form suffices. Know: in Levush and in Even HaEzer at the end, order of gittin arranged by Maharamerari, it is written: drag until bent kaf-form; and what Taz wrote there that mem is in two writings like nun-vav — Beit Aharon already caught him on this.

**And it should reach opposite the lower base** — b'dieved if it did not reach below and a child read it mem, uncertain — perhaps this is not from the essential letter-form, only lekhatchila.

**One may drag the remainder** — nevertheless if he extended so much that a child did not recognize the letter thereby — in my view uncertain whether dragging helps; see Rambam chapter 1 of Hilchot Tefillin halachah 19; and even if one says in hei in this manner it is also valid — for its essential image is so for many rishonim, as above.

**Pri Megadim left it uncertain** — and a sage of the time brought proof for the validators from Shabbat 104b: one intended to write one letter and two came out on his hand — he is liable; and per Rabbenu Chananel [brought in Hagahot Maimoniyot and Beit Yosef in this letter] this refers to tet: he intended to write tet and did not attach the thigh to the left [so in Hagahot Maimoniyot and Turei Zahav in Beit Yosef it says right], and it became nun-zayin — impossible that it is for delay even b'dieved, for no nun-name is upon it — why liable for two on Shabbat? The nun in tet he presumably made like other tet where they do not extend the nun's neck except from its side, not its middle; and in my view one may reject: otherwise per the Gemara's conclusion there that it requires ziyunin, etc., we must say this baraita deals where he made the tet's nun upright above and also tagged, unlike other tet that require the nun without ziyun and slightly bent inward, as poskim conclude — we may also say it deals where he made the tet's nun pulled from the middle of its head like a real nun, not like other tet, therefore liable also for the nun in it. Regarding correction to bent nun whose head is vav-form, see inside what I wrote: correction helps even in tefillin, for scraping does not apply since not validated by dragging alone, nor "not in order" — their form is still called upon them, for all are accustomed to such nun; and I would prove from Pri Megadim in simple kaf made square above: even poskim who disqualify and do not distinguish simple from bent, even b'dieved agree adding ink to round it helps even in tefillin, no "not in order" — its form is still called upon it, for all are accustomed to bent kaf; if so, all the more bent nun — correction helps since even without correction all read it nun. And truly a fortiori from there: there bent kaf must be round — Gemara explicit Shabbat 103b, otherwise no image upon it; and for poskim who disqualify even simple kaf made square b'dieved, not distinguishing image of bent from simple, since both are called kaf upon them — it would seem that by squaring the form is lost and no kaf is upon it; nevertheless Pri Megadim wrote correction helps, still called upon it since all are accustomed to bent kaf; all the more in our case — even in simple nun Ran was stringent — we have no Gemara source that correction does not help if made above vav-form; all the more we need not be stringent on bent nun made above vav-form that correction does not help, as above. What he wrote: if he drags much he loses its image, and when correcting afterward there is "not in order" — and what he wrote: afterward add ink — if done in reverse and very wide, perhaps thereby the essential image is lost, for all are not accustomed thus, and later dragging from the left will not help. And know further: per Pri Megadim who erases both in one erasure — even b'dieved he holds one may not distinguish simple from bent since both are called nun upon them [and know in simple kaf made square he sides with poskim who disqualify even b'dieved]; one may also say similarly in open mem if made round below — disqualify even b'dieved for this reason since both are called mem upon them; and closed mem is surely invalid, included in "memim samkhin"; if so, likewise in open mem; uncertain for practical law. Correction helps here even in tefillin, for otherwise the essential form is still upon it.

**And its right thigh, etc., bent kaf** — b'dieved it seems even slightly bent suffices; see Beit Yosef, and so Rashi's view Shabbat 104a s.v. leya'al behakh — see there; and so Rabbenu Yerucham and Agur wrote to extend the kuf's leg so it not resemble hei — from all this it seems open below, and in any case slightly bent suffices. See Beit Aharon who brought from Radbaz that its essential form is resh-nun, and he elaborated; and concluded: its form is like resh, only the end of the resh curves left like lamed; and so from Magen David of Radbaz; to satisfy all, make it bent below like kaf, as scribes practice — not like some scribes who make its form like lamed's curve — that is not unified and cuts plantings, only as stated — end quote.
