What Rama writes in his gloss, that if one altered the form of the script, means that he did not write the letters in the forms mentioned in the works of the scribes. Nevertheless, the essential form of each letter must remain upon it. All the more so, if he wrote reishin like daletin, beisin like kafin, or the reverse, it is invalid. What Taz writes, that the reading of a child does not help, means that it does not help without repair. Through repair it does help, because it is not considered writing out of order, as explained in siman 32, seif 25, and as will be explained shortly. What is written concerning the point of the yod, that it may be repaired, is implied by Hagahos Maimoniyos, chapter 1 of Hilchos Tefillin. There it appears that a greater defect exists when the yods of the alefs do not touch than when the point of the yod is missing, yet repair helps for the yods of the alefs. All the more so it helps for the point of the yod. Magen Avraham writes similarly in siman 32, letter 36, in the name of Beit Yosef here. See Magen Avraham, letter 3. Taggin may likewise be repaired, and this is not considered writing out of order. Although taggin are of lesser status than the point of the yod, as Hagahos Maimoniyos writes there in the name of Rabbeinu Tam in Sefer HaYashar, they are certainly no worse than the yods of the alefs, for which repair helps. With Hashem's help, this will be explained further there.
I have one difficulty, which I shall record here. Shabbos 105a concludes that if one intended to write a ches and wrote two zayins, he is exempt. The implication is that if he intended to write two zayins he would be liable. This seems to show that the letters Shatnez Getz without their zayins, meaning their taggin, are not letters at all. Derishah already noted this. Yet Rav Ashi states on Shabbos 104b, concerning one who corrected a single letter and is liable, that he removed the staff from a ches and thereby made two zayins. Rambam rules likewise in chapter 11 of Hilchos Shabbos, halachah 13. This implies that even without the zayin-like crowns the letters are considered complete, for “corrected” implies that the missing element was supplied without a separate act of writing. Rambam omits the ruling that one is liable for adding the zayins. See Tosafos there, 104b, s.v. “intended,” and 105a, s.v. “but was it not taught.” It therefore appears that according to Rav Sheshes the baraisa is not accepted as halachah. Nevertheless, the matter requires study. Still, making a mark is also liable on Shabbos. See Shabbos 103a and Rashi there. Some authorities likewise hold that even one who writes in ordinary script is liable for two letters. All the more so one should be liable for writing these letters without their crowns. The case in Shabbos may be explained as referring to one writing sifrei Torah, tefillin, or mezuzos, where the crowns are required initially. Since his intention was not fulfilled, he is exempt. Understand this. Accordingly, if one writes two broken letter forms on Shabbos and intended to make precisely those forms, he is liable, like one who makes a mark. See Rambam, chapter 11 of Hilchos Shabbos, halachah 17. According to Rabbeinu Tam he may be exempt because such writing is invalid in a Sefer Torah. With Hashem's help, this will be explained further.
