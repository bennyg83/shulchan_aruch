(א) Seals on vessels — this is the language of closing — meaning that their cover is closed with rope knots:
(ב) One may untie it — for it is not a permanent knot, for behold it is always made to be untied:
(ג) Or to cut it with a knife, etc. — and it is not called demolishing, because this is not complete demolition; and it is not forbidden in vessels according to all — unlike breaking a wooden or metal cover, which is complete demolition and applies even in vessels:
(ד) Or to untie its braiding — see in Biur Halacha, that per the Rambam there is prohibition in this:
(ה) Forbidden to remove the hinges — for breaking a cover is similar, and in this there is concern of demolition; and likewise one who returns the door of the vessel onto its hinge — there is concern of building:
(ו) With this — he holds that it is not comparable to breaking a cover, and it is inferior demolition, since one need not break anything; and know that according to all it is forbidden to fix the hinges or a lock with nails to the vessel, for it is metaken mana; and see in Chayei Adam, who wrote that even through a non-Jew it is forbidden, for it is a Torah melacha:
(ז) Of chests — and the same applies to all other vessels:
(ח) There are those who permit — he holds that demolition does not apply there at all in vessels; and as I explained above in s.k. 1 in Mishna Berurah — there are early authorities who hold thus; and even though the Mechaber ruled anonymously at the beginning of this seif itself regarding breaking a cover — forbidden — nevertheless he brought here the view of "there are those who permit" in order to rely on them to be lenient at least through a non-Jew, as he concludes afterward, as is in Beit Yosef:
(ט) Through a non-Jew — and there are those who wrote: specifically with great loss or when he is greatly pressed for need of a mitzvah; and all this regarding breaking the cover; but to open it with a knife or large needle — permitted, even a house door [later authorities]:
