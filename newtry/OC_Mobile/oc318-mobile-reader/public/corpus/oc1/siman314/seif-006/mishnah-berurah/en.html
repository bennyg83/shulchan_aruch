(א) Permitted to throw off — for there is no building and demolition in vessels; and it deals with an inferior barrel whose broken pieces were glued with pitch and not a whole one, as is all explained above in s.k. 1; and if he wishes to throw off only the head of the stopper alone — even in a whole barrel it is permitted, for it is not called breaking, since it is not attached to the barrel:
(ב) With a sword — a kli she'melachto l'issur may be moved for need of its body:
(ג) For he does not intend for an opening — but to widen its mouth so he can easily take what is inside; and specifically when he does this because of guests [Eliyah Rabbah in the name of Rashi Lanz]; and permitted also to tear the hide from the mouth of a wine barrel, provided he does not intend to make a spout [meaning like a gutter] (Tosefta):
(ד) But to its hole on its side, etc. — for a hole is like making an opening for the barrel; and even a small hole that is not fit for inserting and removing is also forbidden rabbinically, both in the stopper and in the barrel; except there is a distinction between them: regarding the stopper it is forbidden with such a hole only when he makes the hole on the side of the stopper; but when he pierces above in the head of the stopper to draw wine from there — permitted, as below; but in a barrel it is forbidden in every place he makes a hole in it; and that which Shulchan Aruch cited "on its side" — because of the stopper he cited it:
(ה) Certainly he intends for an opening — for if he does not intend for an opening but to widen the wine's outlet, he should open the stopper; but when he pierces in the stopper above — perforce he does not intend for an opening, for it is not the way to make an opening there lest dust and pebbles fall:
(ו) Permitted — even in a whole barrel it is permitted, for the stopper is not attached to the barrel, as stated in s.k. 23; and it is reasonable that in this according to all it is permitted, even not because of guests:
