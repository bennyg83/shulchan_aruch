(ג) With pitch — one may. Because of its inferiority they do not care about it and there is no concern lest he intend to make a vessel (Tosafos and the Rosh): The Rambam wrote chapter 23: One who makes a hole that is fit for inserting and removing — such as the hole in a rooster coop that is fit for inserting light and removing smoke — is liable for makeh b'patish; therefore they decreed upon every hole, even if it is only fit for inserting or removing alone, lest he come to make a hole for which one is liable. The Kesef Mishnah challenged in chapter 10: behold, he wrote that one is liable for building; and it appears to me that here it deals with a coop that is not attached to the ground and there is no building in it; and he wrote further: One who makes a hole — all the more so, whether in wood, in building, in metal, or in vessels — is liable for makeh b'patish, and this is when it is fit for inserting and removing. One who makes a hole in the floor of the house so that water will exit is liable for building (Rashdam siman 5):

(ד) That he intend for this. We do not know what Rama novelly taught:

(ה) And specifically that he also removed, etc. Thus Terumat HaDeshen: It appears that a knife stuck in a barrel deals with when they removed it and inserted it on a weekday, and it is not psik reisha that he will widen the hole; he brings proof from the knife between the bricks of the oven that was pulled, etc., and that case is certainly not psik reisha that he will widen the hole; and nevertheless he agrees that if it were psik reisha to widen the hole it would be forbidden, for he makes a hole and need not cover with earth — liable by Torah law for building, as is stated in the first chapter of Beitza, end of his words. Behold, it is clear that Terumat HaDeshen also holds that even per the conclusion that Shmuel is established, nevertheless it deals with a matter that is not psik reisha at all; and if so, the case of the barrel also deals with a matter that is not psik reisha at all, for it is a Torah melacha of making an opening for the barrel; but psik reisha in a rabbinic melacha Terumat HaDeshen holds is permitted, and he brings proof from Mordechai chapter HaZorek that permits pouring water even though it descends to the karmelis, since he does not intend that it go outside, and even though it is psik reisha — see there (see Tosafos Berachos daf 24b and siman 318 seif 12, and see Tosafos Pesachim daf 47). And for me it requires further study regarding the Gaon who said daf 113: pillars stuck in the ground — it is forbidden to remove them; what is the reason? If we say because he makes holes automatically — we learned one who buries a turnip, etc., and there it is certainly psik reisha that he makes holes, and nevertheless it is permitted; and so too Tosafos, that even if part of it is not exposed it is permitted to stick in, etc., see there daf 91, and the reason is medakdeik, as is stated daf 73, and exempt even according to R' Yehuda; and the words of Tosafos daf 39 in s.v. mipnei shemaziz, etc., require further study — see there; and it is possible that it deals with fixing, such as in a field, as the Maggid Mishnah wrote in seif 1; and in the first chapter what he wrote regarding digging a hole and not needing earth — liable — this applies only when he needs the hole; but when he needs neither hole nor earth at all, it is obvious that he is exempt, for it is a melacha she'einah tzerichah l'gufah and davar she'eino mitkavein and medakdeik; and also digging kil'achar yad, as Rashi stated daf 46: digging for which one is liable is with a shovel and mattock — see there; therefore it appears to me that by the law of the land there is no prohibition here, even in a wall and in a large barrel, since it is davar she'eino mitkavein and medakdeik; except that Terumat HaDeshen was forced to uphold the custom that they practiced, as he himself stated — see there; and so too Mahariv siman 131, that they practiced a prohibition to remove it, for it appears like making a hole in a wall, end of his words. Also what he wrote to permit psik reisha in rabbinic matters — and so too siman 66 — requires further study, for in siman 316 seif 4 it is explicit that it is forbidden, and it is Talmud; and so too Tosafos in Shabbos daf 103 end of s.v. lo tzricha — see there; and so too siman 337 seif 1 and seif 2 — see there; and even though Tosafos Pesachim daf 25 wrote that it is permitted — see there — this is in the manner of "one can say," but in truth it is forbidden; and so too siman 277 seif 1 and seif 3; and even though the Rosh in Pesachim wrote that psik reisha is forbidden in every Torah matter — it appears to me that he stated this because of other prohibitions — see there; but according to all, psik reisha is forbidden even in rabbinic matters; and what he brought as proof from Mordechai is not proof, for there if he does nothing and even if they go out to the public domain there is no prohibition, as is written siman 357; rather the Rabbis decreed in the public domain, and in the karmelis they did not decree, since when he does not intend to remove it, since he does not intend he does not come to remove it directly — see there; and see siman 253, that with amirah to a non-Jew in psik reisha there is room to permit; and examine his words, for I have abbreviated:
