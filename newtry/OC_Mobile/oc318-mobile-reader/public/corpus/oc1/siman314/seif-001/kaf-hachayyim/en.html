1) [Seif 1] The prohibitions of building and dismantling do not apply to utensils. This refers to construction and dismantling that are not complete. Complete construction and dismantling apply even to utensils. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 1. Therefore, one may break specifically a vessel whose broken pieces were joined with pitch, since this is not complete dismantling. Levush Serad.
2) There, gloss: That does not hold forty se'ah. The authority who permits breaking a vessel joined from pieces does so even when it is large, while the authority who permits a small vessel does so even when it is whole, as stated in Beis Yosef. The Shulchan Aruch's view is therefore to permit even a large one. Nevertheless, the gloss adopts the stringent position that both favorable conditions are required. Likkutei Pri Chadash. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 2, writes the same, although he adds that since the matter is rabbinic one certainly need not be so stringent; see there.
3) There, gloss: That does not hold forty se'ah. If it holds forty se'ah, it is considered a structure, and building and dismantling apply to it. Levush; Magen Avraham, paragraph 1. This means that if it holds forty se'ah, it is forbidden to break it because of dismantling even when its broken pieces were joined with pitch. All this follows Rema's position, which is stringent in accordance with both views, as stated in the preceding paragraph.
4) There, gloss: That does not hold forty se'ah. This means one amah by one amah by three amos high, including the walls but excluding the thickness of the rim, which is like a molding surrounding its edge, and the legs. Magen Avraham, paragraph 2; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 2. Responsa Me'il Tzedakah, at the end of siman 27, writes regarding stains found on an infant's cradle that its legs join toward the measure of forty se'ah, since the legs are essential to the cradle because they enable the cradle and child to be rocked; see there. The same applies regarding Shabbos. Machatzis HaShekel. See Yoreh De'ah at the beginning of siman 201 regarding the measure of forty se'ah. Even if it is not made in this particular shape, meaning one amah by one amah by three amos high, anything holding forty se'ah is considered a structure. It nevertheless appears that its roof must be one tefach wide. Otherwise, even if it is long and narrow, it is not a structure. Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 9.
5) There: Such as a barrel that broke, etc. Korban Nesanel, on Rosh, chapter 22 of Shabbos, paragraph 5, questions why Beis Yosef did not cite Rif and Rambam, who permit even initially when the vessel is not assembled from broken pieces, and he rules permissively; see there. This is also the view of Rashba and Ran in the chapter Chavis, Ritva in his Chiddushim to Eruvin 35, and Rashbetz in his Chiddushim to Rosh Hashanah, who writes that if a shofar is inside a barrel one may break the barrel, as in the law concerning dried figs. Rashbash, siman 160, writes the same; see there. Radbaz, in Leshonos HaRambam, siman 130, likewise permits breaking a chest to remove something needed for Shabbos when the key was lost; see there. Erekh HaShulchan, paragraph 1. Eliyah Rabbah, siman 307, at the end of paragraph 31, writes the same; see there. Although we follow the Shulchan Aruch's ruling that this is permitted only for a barrel that broke and was reassembled, while a whole barrel is forbidden, it is nevertheless possible that in pressing circumstances, when a person suffers because he has nothing to eat, one may rely upon the lenient authorities, provided the vessel does not hold forty se'ah, as stated above in paragraph 2. It must also be broken in a manner that does not thereby fashion it into a utensil, for otherwise this constitutes completing a utensil and all opinions hold that he is liable.
6) There: And he joined its broken pieces with pitch, etc. Because the vessel is inferior, he is not protective of it, and there is no concern that he may intend to fashion a utensil. Tosafos and Rosh; Magen Avraham, paragraph 3.
7) There: He may break it, etc. He may do so either by hand or with a utensil. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1. Levush writes that he should break the joined area. Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 1, however, writes that it is also permitted to break a place where the earthenware itself is whole; see there.
8) There: Provided that he does not intend to make a neat opening, etc. Similarly, he must not intend to make the break neat so that it forms an opening and the vessel remains usable. Rashi, Beitzah 33b, beginning with the words 'But did we not learn.'
9) There: Provided that he does not intend to perforate it, etc. Rambam writes in chapter 10, halachah 16, that one who makes any hole, whether in wood, metal, a building, or utensils, performs a derivative of completing a utensil and is liable, provided the hole is made to bring substances in and take them out. Magen Avraham cites him there. One who makes a hole in the floor of a house so that water can drain out is liable for building. Responsa Maharashdam, siman 5; Magen Avraham there; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 2. It is forbidden even through a non-Jew. Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 6. This is also evident from what was stated above at the end of siman 313; see there.
10) There: It is considered fashioning a utensil. In the chapter Chavis, Shabbos 146, the Gemara states that any opening not made both to bring substances in and take them out is not an opening. The Sages nevertheless prohibited it because of a chicken coop, where an opening is made to bring in air and remove foul heat so that the chickens will not die. This implies that fashioning a utensil here is not forbidden by the Torah except in the case of a chicken coop. Taz, paragraph 1. Rambam writes the same at the beginning of chapter 23, and Magen Avraham cites him there. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 3, writes similarly. The rule that liability for a hole applies only when it is made both to bring in and take out refers to a detached object not connected to the ground. For something attached to the ground, liability for building applies even when the hole is not made both to bring in and take out. Shulchan Aruch HaRav there; Eshel Avraham, paragraph 3.
11) There: Even in a manner that does not fashion a utensil. This means that he does not make an opening but breaks the vessel. The Shulchan Aruch then states an even greater novelty: 'even a mere hole,' meaning that he does not intend it as an opening. It has two grounds for leniency, since he does not break the vessel at all and the hole is not an opening. Nevertheless, it is forbidden because we are concerned that he may intend to make an opening. When the vessel's broken pieces were joined with pitch, however, we do not have this concern for the reason stated by Magen Avraham, paragraph 3. Levush Serad. See above, paragraph 6, for Magen Avraham's reasoning.
12) One who drives a peg into a wall in order to hang utensils or the like is liable for building. Even if he does not drive in the peg but only drills a hole in the wall in which to insert it, he is liable for building on account of that hole, since the hole contributes to the structure by receiving the peg. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 2. This is based on the Gemara, Shabbos 103a, and Rashi's commentary there.
