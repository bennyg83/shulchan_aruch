(א) That does not hold forty se'ah, etc. — see in Biur Halacha that when it holds forty se'ah it is forbidden even in a shaky barrel such as this; and see in Eliyah Rabbah who brought that according to Rashba in a shaky barrel such as this — even if it holds forty se'ah — demolishing does not apply; and it appears one may be lenient through a non-Jew — for otherwise it is a d'rabbanan prohibition since he is destructively ruining:

(ב) Forbidden to break it, etc. — see in Mishna Berurah that even though he is destructively ruining d'rabbanan — nevertheless forbidden — as explained from the words of Magen Avraham and other later authorities. And know that even though according to Rashi it appears that in destructive ruining where it is for Shabbat need it is permitted l'chatchila — Tosafos, Rosh, and Ran disagree with him [Magen Avraham] — and there is practical difference from this also regarding other melachot; and it is possible Rashi also agrees regarding other melachot; and you will know that regarding demolishing a large tower which is included in a tent — it is proven from Rashi Eruvin 34 and likewise proven from the Gemara there, in the conclusion that it is forbidden for everyone to break it and take from it the eruv to eat it — even though it is also for Shabbat need; and even for those who hold that even a whole utensil is permitted to break and take food from it — their reason is only because they hold demolishing does not apply at all in utensils — but not because destructive ruining applies; and likewise proven from Rambam chapter 1 of Hilchot Shabbat laws 3 and 17 that destructive ruining is exempt but forbidden in all melachot — and they would prepare him nevertheless; and we do not find any shevut that would be permitted through a Jew because it is for Shabbat need [and I found this reasoning also among later authorities] — and perforce it is simple one may not be lenient in this at all:
