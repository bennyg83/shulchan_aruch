(א) That is not complete building — but complete building and complete demolition apply also in vessels; and therefore specifically when he glued the broken pieces with pitch may he break it, for this is not complete demolition; but if it were whole it would be complete demolition and forbidden, even in a small vessel; and the later authorities wrote below in s.k. 9 that these small barrels of spices — it is forbidden to remove the hoops [called rings] in order to take what is inside, and it is not comparable to a barrel whose broken pieces were glued with pitch — for there its way from the outset is to do so and it is like a whole vessel; but through a non-Jew there is certainly room to permit, as below in s.k. 37; and with tying by ropes, even through a Jew, there is room to permit:
(ב) That does not hold — for if it holds 40 se'ah it is considered like an ohel, and there is concern of building and demolition in it, even minimal building and demolition:
(ג) Forty se'ah — that is, an amah by an amah in height of 3 amos with the thickness of the walls, without the thickness of the decorative bands [which is like a rim surrounding its lip] and the legs [Magen Avraham in the name of the Rambam]:
(ד) One may break it — see in Peri Megadim, who sides that the permission to break applies even when not in a place glued with pitch:
(ה) To its hole a proper hole — and the same if he throws off its head from above and intends to beautify the break so that it remain a vessel:
(ו) Metaken mana — see in Taz, that it is implied that even if the hole is a small hole that is not fit for inserting and removing, it is nevertheless forbidden rabbinically; and if it is a large hole fit for inserting and removing there is a Torah prohibition; and so it is implied in Biur HaGra:
(ז) Forbidden to break it, etc. — because of demolition; and even though in general we rule that a demolisher is liable only when he intends to build — otherwise he is medakdeik rabbinically — nevertheless it is forbidden; and in Biur HaGra he agreed with the poskim who hold that there is no demolition in vessels, even when he makes a break in a whole vessel, unless he makes it a vessel through the breaking — then he is liable according to all because of makeh b'patish:
(ח) That he does not make a vessel — meaning that he does not make a hole for an opening but breaks it; and afterward he wrote a further novelty that even a mere hole — meaning a hole that is not fit for an opening — there are two improvements in it: he does not break the barrel and also it is not an opening — nevertheless it is forbidden. The Rambam wrote chapter 23: One who makes a hole that is fit for inserting and removing — such as the hole in a rooster coop that is fit for inserting light and removing smoke — is liable for makeh b'patish [which is there the general term for every completion of melacha that is a toldah of this av melacha, that the artisan strikes with a hammer on the vessel at its completion to straighten its crookedness]; therefore they decreed upon every hole, even if it is only fit for inserting or removing alone, lest he come to make a hole for which one is liable; and see in Magen Avraham, who explained that even if it is a coop that is not attached to the ground, where there is no concern of building in making the hole — nevertheless he is liable for makeh b'patish, which is completion of the coop's melacha; and he wrote further in chapter 10: Whoever does a thing that is completion of melacha — this is a toldah of makeh b'patish and he is liable. One who makes any hole, whether in wood, in metal, in building, or in vessels — this is a toldah of makeh b'patish and he is liable; and every opening that is not fit for inserting and removing — one is not liable for making it, end of his words. It is in the Gemara: if one drives a nail into a wall [to hang upon it] he is liable for makeh b'patish. One who makes a hole in the floor of the house so that water will exit is liable for building [later authorities]:
(ט) If to widen it — forbidden — meaning, even if he does not widen it so much that it will be fit for inserting and removing through it — nevertheless it is forbidden at least rabbinically [Gemara]:
(י) Provided that he intend — comes to explain here only the Mechaber's view, that specifically when he intends to widen; but if he does not intend, even though it is psik reisha that it will widen through his action — it is permitted, as the Mechaber himself concluded afterward; but know that Rama himself afterward in the gloss — that even if he does not intend to widen it is forbidden if it is psik reisha — and he should have written this there, in the name of "some say"; but he wanted to seal thus for halacha; and see what we wrote there:
(כ) Stuck — meaning that it was stuck forcefully, and through removing and inserting it is psik reisha that the hole will widen; nevertheless, since its primary prohibition is only rabbinic, he holds it is permitted since he does not intend for this; and Rama's view is to disagree with him — that specifically when he removed it once from erev Shabbat, through this it widened somewhat and thereafter it is not psik reisha; but otherwise it is forbidden, for it is psik reisha; and even though it is an opening that is not fit for inserting and removing, whose prohibition is only rabbinic — as stated — he holds that psik reisha is forbidden even in a rabbinic matter; and see below in s.k. 12, that in a wall that is attached even the Mechaber agrees that it is forbidden to remove the knife that was stuck in it from erev Shabbat [and the same applies to a barrel that holds forty se'ah, which also leaves the category of vessel and is like an ohel] — for there, when he intends to widen the hole, he is liable because of chisul, which is a toldah of building; and therefore even if he does not intend, nevertheless it is almost psik reisha; and behold regarding the essence of the law, Magen Avraham and Eliyah Rabbah and Biur HaGra and Gra Zal agreed that psik reisha is forbidden even in a rabbinic matter; and nevertheless here Magen Avraham and Biur HaGra side that by the law of the land even in a wall it is permitted where he does not intend, since he does not need the hole and it is melacha she'einah tzerichah l'gufah and is inferior to other rabbinic prohibitions that are forbidden where it is psik reisha, since it is also medakdeik and also kil'achar yad — and automatically a hole is made in the wall through removing the knife that widens the hole — and as above in siman 311 s.k. 8 regarding a radish — see there; except that the world practiced a prohibition to remove it, for it appears like making a hole in a wall; and also Taz sides thus in the essence of the law, even in a wall; and nevertheless he concludes that it is not in his power to be lenient against Terumat HaDeshen and Shulchan Aruch and Rama, who all ruled at the end of the siman to be stringent regarding a wall [and so too is the view of Eliyah Rabbah like Shulchan Aruch and Rama — it is possible that he is satisfied that the hole be wide to return and stick the knife in it, and in such a manner it is certainly forbidden according to all]; except regarding a barrel, whose primary prohibition is only rabbinic — one may rely where there is great need on the Mechaber's view, who permits removing the knife on Shabbat since he does not intend to add to the hole, even where he never removed it from the beginning:
(ל) For he makes a hole and opening — meaning that even though it is clear in s.k. 12 that where the knife was stuck in a detached matter it is permitted to remove it on Shabbat in every manner, for building does not apply there — here it is different, for there is concern of metaken petachah; and even if it is not fit for inserting and removing, it is nevertheless forbidden rabbinically:
