(א) Fixing a utensil. In chapter Chavit (Shabbos daf 146) we say: Any opening that is not made for inserting and removing is not an opening; and it is the rabbis who decreed due to a chicken coop — that one makes it for letting in air and letting out heat — meaning the foul odor so the chickens do not die from it. From this it is implied that this fixing a utensil is not a d'oraisa prohibition except for a chicken coop:

(ב) If to widen it, etc. It is implied from this language that even in psik reisha it is permitted without intent for that — for he said "if to widen it," which implies he certainly widens; and so in the language of Tur "and he comes to widen it" — which implies this is certain; and likewise per the explanation of the Mordechai chapter 6 Chavit who wrote: The rabbis taught — one does not pierce a new hole; and if he comes to add — he adds; and some say one does not add; R' Yehuda Ish Bilbao — the halacha is like the one who says one does not add. And it appears to me that here we deal when he intended to add; but if a knife was stuck from erev Shabbat in a barrel — permitted to remove it and insert it, since he does not intend to add, as we say at the end of chapter Bameh Tomnin: Shmuel said — these knives between the boards — if he removed it and returned it — permitted — end of his words. It appears at first glance that also for a knife in a barrel we require that he removed it from erev Shabbat; and it is difficult — for there in chapter Bameh Tomnin they challenge Shmuel's statement that we require removed and returned; and likewise R' Huna asks there regarding silixos that is made to emit odor — they insert it in a pot of earth — from the Mishnah "one who insulates a turnip" — if part of it was exposed it may be taken on Shabbat, and we do not require removed and returned — and we conclude with a refutation. If so, how does the Mordechai bring proof from Shmuel and R' Huna? And Beit Yosef answered and brought proof from the conclusion that we do not require removed and returned — and it is permitted due to not intending; and the halacha does not imply thus; and furthermore if so the Mordechai should have brought proof from the Mishnah of insulating a turnip mentioned in the explanation of the permission — why did he need Shmuel's words which only stringently add to the Mishnah? And it appears to me that this is the Mordechai's intent: The reason of R' Huna and Shmuel — the Mordechai holds like Tosafos chapter Kirah (Shabbos daf 44) — that it is because he removes earth and widens the cavity; and the Mordechai explains that this is Tosafos' intent: When the turnip was buried there was no cavity there, for the turnip seals it; and its hollow is very narrow; but when he removed it from erev Shabbat and there was a proper cavity and he returned it on erev Shabbat — even though it is impossible that by taking it on Shabbat there will not be somewhat more widening and it will be exactly like adding to the hole in chapter Chavit — nevertheless permitted since he does not intend to add; and on this we challenge from the Mishnah that we do not require removed and returned; and the Mishnah's reason appears from what R' Abba said in chapter 1 of Beitza daf 8: One who digs a cavity on Shabbat and needs only the earth is exempt — the reason being it is melacha she'eina tzricha l'gufa; and Tosafos wrote in their name that nevertheless it is forbidden l'chatchila except that due to rejoicing on Yom Tov they permitted this even l'chatchila; regarding a peg stuck by itself on Shabbat there is prohibition l'chatchila — namely where the act in any case is agreeable to him — even though he does not want the pit, nevertheless he wants the earth; unlike here in insulating a turnip where he does not want earth at all but only to take the turnip — perforce permitted entirely here even without removed and returned; and with this we challenge Shmuel and R' Huna who hold there is also prohibition in making a cavity and require removed and returned specifically — and they remain with a refutation; and the Mordechai brings proof that for those rabbis there is prohibition in making a cavity and nevertheless permitted for them with removed and returned — namely like adding to the hole which cannot be otherwise — likewise in removing the knife from the barrel which certainly adds to the hole when he moves the knife — nevertheless permitted since it is only adding and he does not intend; and nevertheless he does not bring proof from the Mishnah per the conclusion there — for there was no prohibition at all even in making the cavity; and here in a barrel there is prohibition to make a new hole — for here he certainly wants the opening so wine flows from there. If so one could say even adding is forbidden; nevertheless he brings from Shmuel that there is prohibition and nevertheless permitted when adding since he does not intend — namely in something that has no d'oraisa prohibition even when intentional — namely in the case of making a hole as stated above; and likewise in the case of insulating a turnip according to Shmuel and R' Huna. All this — it appears to me correct and clear with Hashem's help — except that in Terumat HaDeshen siman 64 he did not lean to this; and thus he wrote what we need there: He asked — a knife stuck in a wooden wall from erev Shabbat, or in a bench, or in a detached board — permitted to remove the knife on Shabbat or not? Answer — it appears where it was stuck in an attached wall there is concern to forbid if he removes it due to what we learned in chapter HaBoneh regarding the one who drills — whoever does so is liable; and they explain according to Rav that he is liable due to building and liable before the hole is filled as stated there, in the Gemara and Rashi — if so in removing the knife where it is stuck somewhat forcefully it is almost psik reisha that he will add to the hole — and he is like one who drills all the more; and it does not appear we can bring proof from what the Mordechai wrote in chapter Chavit that if a knife was stuck in a barrel — permitted to remove and insert since he does not intend to add — and he brings proof from chapter Beitza — these knives between boards — if he removed and returned — permitted. One can say since a barrel is a utensil and there is no building and demolishing in utensils in something that has no reinforcement and craftsmanship — for everyone — therefore there is no concern of drilling even if it cannot be otherwise but he will add to the hole. And one could ask — for they conclude there in chapter Chavit that he is liable due to fixing an opening; from the Torah any opening not made for inserting and removing is not called an opening — only the rabbis decreed on all openings and holes due to a chicken coop; and therefore when he intends to add it is forbidden; but when he does not intend we can say even if it is psik reisha it is permitted as a d'rabbanan prohibition as stated in responsa of Maharam in Mordechai chapter HaZorek. However it appears this knife stuck in a barrel deals where they already removed and inserted it on a weekday — and it is not psik reisha that he will add to the hole — since he brings proof from chapter Beitza regarding knives between boards removed and returned — and that certainly deals where it is not psik reisha that he will widen the cavity. R' Yehuda chapter Kirah — therefore requires removed and returned so he not move earth from its place and due to muktzeh (it appears to me there is a textual error and it should read: not like Rashi due to moving earth from its place and muktzeh, but rather that he will widen the cavity). And nevertheless he necessarily agrees that if it were psik reisha to widen the cavity it would be forbidden — for he makes a cavity and does not need earth only — liable from the Torah due to building as stated explicitly in chapter 1 of Beitza regarding a peg stuck (it appears there is a textual error and it should read: and even though he makes a cavity and needs only earth — he is not liable from the Torah due to building; nevertheless he necessarily agrees that if it were psik reisha to widen the cavity it is forbidden as stated above — to emend what is stated explicitly in chapter 1 of Beitza daf 8). And one could ask — if it deals with a knife in a barrel that is not psik reisha — what does it teach us? It is obvious that anything one does not intend — even in d'oraisa melacha — is permitted, for we hold like R' Shimon. One can say it was necessary to teach us that they did not decree lest he come to intend to add to the hole since we know he wants widening of the hole — as we find they decreed lest one make cavities, etc.; and from this he brings proof from removed and returned that they did not decree lest he widen cavities intentionally in order to insert the knife easily. And from now that a knife in a barrel deals without psik reisha — it is resolved even in a barrel larger than forty se'ah — for Or Zarua wrote on this of slicing the head with a sword — in such a large barrel building applies like in the ground; and if it were psik reisha he would be liable due to drilling like when stuck in a wall as explained above; but knives between boards — it does not appear to hold him liable due to drilling even without removed and returned — for there it is only separating boards from each other since it is not one body; but due to widening a cavity it applies there; and therefore R' Yehuda used this language and not the language of drilling; and since the world already practiced prohibition to remove a knife stuck in a wall and we find a reason in it — we have to uphold the custom; but if the knife is stuck in something detached like a bench and similar — we know no reason at all for prohibition; and one who is stringent exceeds his measure — end of his words. And Beit Yosef challenged that his words contradict each other — for he wrote that a knife in a barrel is permitted only if they stuck and removed it on a weekday; and at the end he wrote if stuck in something detached there is no prohibition — and it implies even without removed and returned — for otherwise even in something attached it would be permitted; and if so why not a detached barrel? And perhaps he speaks specifically of a forty-se'ah barrel whose law is like the ground; but this is not in the implication of his language who wrote even in a large barrel, etc. — it implies in every barrel he speaks — end of his words. And Maharachal strained greatly to establish the language "even" and establishes the words of Terumat HaDeshen specifically in a large barrel — and this is not mentioned there at all. And in my humble opinion — to explain the words of Tosafos simply — Terumat HaDeshen did not need per the Mordechai removed and returned in a knife in a barrel — but specifically in this case where the barrel has wine inside and it is a peg stuck there from erev Shabbat to remove wine — he wants this opening and there is properly an opening there even though from the Torah it is not called an opening since it is not made also for inserting like a chicken coop — nevertheless it was an opening and the rabbis decreed on it due to a coop as above; therefore removed and returned is required; unlike in a detached bench where there is no need at all to make an opening there — also there is no concern of drilling since it is not attached — perforce permitted without removed and returned. This — it appears to me simple and clear in the view of Terumat HaDeshen. However Beit Yosef challenged further — from Terumat HaDeshen's words it appears that we follow Shmuel that it is permitted only specifically with removed and returned; and in the Gemara Shmuel was refuted; and one can explain that Terumat HaDeshen explains that the Gemara only challenges according to R' Huna in the law of silixos — for that case of turnip is similar where in both there is concern of moving earth; but in a knife between boards there is also concern due to building — and it is impossible at all to say thus — for Rashi explicitly explained that he challenges on both; and likewise Tosafos chapter Kirah (Shabbos daf 44) that Shmuel was also refuted; and furthermore if so Terumat HaDeshen's own words contradict each other — for afterward he wrote that in a knife between boards there is no concern of drilling since they are not one body — perforce the Beit Yosef's challenge on Terumat HaDeshen is a strong challenge — for how can he build a foundation on Shmuel's words to require removed and returned when he was refuted? And furthermore it is difficult — how can one explain the Mordechai's words regarding a knife in a barrel on removed and returned and it is not psik reisha — for the Mordechai relies on the Gemara that said "and some say one does not add" — and this is specifically in psik reisha — for otherwise even in a new hole it is permitted without intent, for we hold like R' Shimon; and furthermore the language "adding" implies they certainly add — and on this the Mordechai permits without intent. And furthermore I am greatly astonished at what he forbade in a knife in an attached wall due to building however small and compares it to drilling all the more in the Gemara — but in truth it is not comparable — for there he needs that hole as Rashi explained in chapter HaBoneh (Shabbos daf 103) — drilling is in any case to fill and drive in a peg; and piercing is not to fill; and the entire passage deals where there is need — both for drilling and for piercing; but when not for need there is no d'oraisa prohibition at all — it is melacha she'eina tzricha l'gufa; and if so anyone who sticks a knife in a wall to store it there has no Torah liability; and why should it not be permitted when he adds without intent even in psik reisha — this is exactly like the case of a knife between boards where we do not require to the conclusion removed and returned. And according to what I said to explain the Mordechai's words — it deals even in psik reisha and is permitted since it is only adding and not intentional — if so also one who sticks a knife on erev Shabbat in a wall and did not remove it until Shabbat is permitted; except I have no authority to be lenient against Terumat HaDeshen; and Shulchan Aruch and Rama ruled thus at the end of this siman; but in removing a knife from a barrel — Beit Yosef and Shulchan Aruch disagree and permit even if he did not remove it from erev Shabbat; but Rama rules like Terumat HaDeshen that specifically he must have removed it from erev Shabbat — it appears for a great need when there is no bolt one may rely to permit even if he did not remove them on erev Shabbat — in my humble opinion:
