[1] [Levush] Forty se'ah, etc. This means a cubit by a cubit with a height of three cubits, including the walls but excluding the thickness of the rim and legs (Magen Avraham in the name of Rambam; see Yoreh Deah at the beginning of siman 201). Rashba in Avodat HaKodesh, p. 42, holds that even a vessel holding more than forty se'ah may be handled if its broken pieces were reattached. This requires study.
[2] [Levush] And some say specifically, etc. Levush correctly presents this as "some say," although Rama did not phrase it that way (Malbushei Yom Tov). Nachalat Tzvi rules that even if the stopper was not removed before Shabbos it is permitted, and Taz and Magen Avraham are also lenient in a case of great need. In my humble opinion Rama's view is primary. Were I to analyze the entire dispute at length the discussion would be excessive, for all the authorities wrote extensively. Taz's proposed emendation of Terumat HaDeshen is unnecessary, as Magen Avraham shows. I maintain, however, that Rama's reason is that the hole is intended as an opening and is desired so that the wine can flow out, and therefore is not destructive as Magen Avraham thought. Nevertheless, it is only rabbinic, as Shabbos 146a explicitly states that they decreed because of making a vent. Magen Avraham's claim that Terumat HaDeshen, siman 64, treats it as biblical is surprising in light of that Gemara, which Terumat HaDeshen himself cites. Rather, Terumat HaDeshen says it is biblical only regarding a cavity in the ground. His proof is that since the ground case necessarily concerns a cavity prepared before Shabbos, the same applies to a barrel according to Mordechai, who compares them. In truth, making the barrel opening is rabbinic. This proves that Terumat HaDeshen himself holds that an inevitable result is prohibited even in a rabbinic prohibition. His earlier language was not stated according to the final conclusion. Magen Avraham's questions therefore fall away. His additional proof from Beitzah 35 that an inevitable result is prohibited even rabbinically is also surprising, since he himself in siman 316, seif katan 9, cites Maharsha that the Gemara there should not be read with the phrase "and do you think so?" One can also say that Terumat HaDeshen holds that bees are considered normally trapped when confined, as will be explained below. Rambam, chapter 10, writes that if one person places a stone and another the mortar, the one placing the mortar is liable. This applies to a middle row; on the upper row, even merely lifting and placing the stone upon mortar creates liability, while on the bottom row positioning it is sufficient, etc. One who builds upon utensils is exempt. The placement of the Mishkan boards upon their sockets does not prove building applies to utensils because the sockets were treated as ground. Rashdam writes that one who makes a hole in the floor of a house to drain water through it is liable for building.
