(א) (s.k. 1) That does not hold — it is considered like an ohel, meaning it left the category of vessel and has the law of an ohel; and in an ohel there is certainly building and demolition — Ran chapter 22 and Rashi and Tosafos chapter 3 of Eruvin daf 34, as should be read. Know that which we learned in Shabbos chapter 22 daf 146a: a person breaks barrels to eat dried figs from them, provided he does not intend to make a vessel — Rashi explained: one breaks, etc., for in medakdeik there is no prohibition on Shabbat; and Ran wrote: and it is not clear to them that all medakdekim are exempt from prohibition; nevertheless, the reason is that since in general medakdeik is exempt, here for Shabbat need it is permitted l'chatchila; however, when it is permitted it is specifically in a small vessel, for building and demolition do not apply to it; but in a large vessel — no; and proof: for we say in Eruvin daf 34: they placed the eruv in a tower and the key was lost — his eruv is not an eruv; and we establish it in a wooden tower tied with a rope and a knife is needed to cut it; R' Eliezer holds like R' Nechemiah, that a vessel is moved only for the use specific to it, and therefore it is forbidden to move a knife to cut the rope (and if so, according to Beit Hillel, then he acquires the eruv, for we rule that it must be in the place of his eruv where he can eat it, and now he is in one place and his eruv in another place); and therefore he holds that his eruv is not an eruv; and the Sages hold that all vessels are moved for need of their body; but if not so, even the Rabbis agree that his eruv is not an eruv, and we do not permit breaking the tower; and the reason is that there we deal with a large tower (meaning that holds 40 se'ah, for it has the law of an ohel) that is like an ohel and building and demolition apply to it; but in a small tower building and demolition do not apply, and therefore it is permitted here with barrels, end of Ran's words. If so, according to Ran there is no distinction whether the vessel is whole or not — everything depends on whether it is small or large. However, Tosafos in Eruvin daf 34b and likewise the Rosh proved that even in a small vessel building and demolition apply, for we say in Shabbos daf 102b: one inserts a peg into the handle of a hoe (Rashi: a small peg they stick into the handle of the hoe so it will not come out) — Rav said: liable for building; behold, also in a small vessel building and demolition apply; therefore they explain that this of "a person breaks barrels to eat" deals with moustaki — meaning, that it broke and he glued the broken pieces with pitch; and Beit Yosef wrote to resolve Ran's difficulty from this of inserting a peg into the handle of a hoe: for when we say there is no building, etc., this applies to a vessel that came apart and does not need a artisan to restore it; but one who makes a vessel from the beginning, or a vessel that came apart and needs a artisan to restore it, etc. — building in vessels does not apply; rather, "one who makes a vessel" is what it is called, and he is liable for building; and so too he himself wrote at the beginning of chapter HaBoneh and at the beginning of chapter Kol HaKelim, end of Beit Yosef's words. If so, according to Tosafos and the Rosh there is no distinction between a large and small vessel, but between a whole vessel and one that broke and was glued with pitch; and in Shulchan Aruch he cited both stringencies — that it was not permitted to break except in a small one and also that it broke; and it must be that he holds as primary like Tosafos, that even in a small one building and demolition apply, and therefore we require specifically that it broke; and nevertheless by reasoning he holds that when it holds 40 se'ah it left the category of vessel and has the law of an ohel, as is in Eruvin daf 35a, and therefore even if glued with pitch there is prohibition:

(ב) And Ran disagrees with Rashi, etc. — he comes to resolve the difficulty of Shulchan Aruch on Ran, who wrote and concluded: and the reason it is permitted here with barrels — meaning, that it deals with small barrels; and he wondered at him: even if it is a large vessel where building and demolition apply, there is room to permit, for behold medakdeik is exempt; and so Magen Avraham wrote: Ran disagrees, etc. — meaning, that Ran first brought Rashi's explanation and needed to say according to Rashi's view that for Shabbat need the prohibition of medakdeik was permitted; but Ran according to his own view it is not reasonable thus, and therefore his challenge: but when it is permitted it is specifically in a small vessel, etc., is his own reasoning, that the permission here is not because of medakdeik but because it is a small vessel:
