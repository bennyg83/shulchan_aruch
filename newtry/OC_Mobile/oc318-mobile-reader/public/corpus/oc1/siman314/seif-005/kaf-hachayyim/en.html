This is also the view of Rashba and Ran in the chapter Chavis, Ritva in his Chiddushim to Eruvin 35, and Rashbetz in his Chiddushim to Rosh Hashanah, who writes that if a shofar is inside a barrel one may break the barrel, as in the law concerning dried figs. Rashbash, siman 160, writes the same; see there. Radbaz, in Leshonos HaRambam, siman 130, likewise permits breaking a chest to remove something needed for Shabbos when the key was lost; see there. Erekh HaShulchan, paragraph 1. Eliyah Rabbah, siman 307, at the end of paragraph 31, writes the same; see there. Although we follow the Shulchan Aruch's ruling that this is permitted only for a barrel that broke and was reassembled, while a whole barrel is forbidden, it is nevertheless possible that in pressing circumstances, when a person suffers because he has nothing to eat, one may rely upon the lenient authorities, provided the vessel does not hold forty se'ah, as stated above in paragraph 2. It must also be broken in a manner that does not thereby fashion it into a utensil, for otherwise this constitutes completing a utensil and all opinions hold that he is liable.
