[4] And some say, etc. Kol Bo cites this as an alternative opinion, but all the other authorities imply that even a wooden barrel is permitted. Shibbolei HaLeket cited by Beit Yosef likewise appears to discuss wood, and Nachalat Tzvi rules accordingly. There is also proof from Piskei Tosafot in chapter Bakol Me'arvin, which explains that the Mishnah permitting breaking a barrel concerns a wooden barrel, and the law of making a hole in an old barrel refers back to that Mishnah. Shabbos 147 also distinguishes between a hole for preserving and one for strengthening, without distinguishing wood from earthenware. This also rejects Kol Bo's distinction concerning summer.
[5] [Levush] And some say, etc. Shabbos 146a states that inserting a myrtle leaf as a spout is prohibited. Rav Yemar says because it forms a gutter, while Rav Ashi says because one may cut it. The practical difference is when it is already cut and lying ready. Perishah asks where Rav Ashi's prohibition applies, since if it is not cut and one intends to cut it on Shabbos the prohibition is obvious and Shmuel need not teach it. It appears to me that the prohibition applies when one inserts the leaf into the barrel while still attached to its branch, because we decree lest he cut it from the branch. This appears to be the view of the Remezim, which writes that a myrtle leaf already cut from its branch may be inserted, and Rabbenu Yerucham, netiv 13, part 8, writes similarly. Yet this implies that even a single ready leaf is permitted, contradicting Tur's cited view. Perishah explains that Tur understood the prohibition to apply when there is only one leaf and reads the Gemara's "cut and lying" in the plural. Beit Yosef implies that even a previously cut leaf is prohibited if it was not prepared, because when one must search for it we decree lest he cut one; if it is already lying ready it is permitted. Rashi explains "lest he cut" as lest he cut the leaf from the branch, and because the branch is hard this would constitute making a utensil. Chochmat Shlomo emends Rashi to read "and since it is cut, etc.," apparently because a myrtle leaf is soft, as Beit Yosef cites Ran in chapter HaMevi. Yet Ran on Shabbos explains like Rashi, and it is strained to emend him too. Perishah explains that Rashi holds Rav Ashi considers a leaf cut from a hard branch itself significant like a hard object, whereas in chapter HaMevi Ran explains Rav Yemar's view, which does not fear cutting because the leaf itself is soft. I can also explain "cut and lying" as meaning that the spout was already fashioned before Shabbos, so Rav Yemar permits while Rav Ashi prohibits. More plausibly, compare Shabbos 125a concerning a vine branch tied to a pitcher: if tied, one may draw with it on Shabbos; if not tied, not. Rav Ashi explains the reason as a decree lest one cut it, meaning lest the next day it be too long and one cut it, thereby making a utensil. Here too perhaps we fear that on Shabbos it may be too long and he will trim it. Thus "cut and lying" means prepared before Shabbos, like the tied branch. The authorities, however, do not appear to understand it this way.
[6] [Levush] Only two, etc. Beit Yosef writes this, but Bach and Perishah prohibit even with three, permitting only when there are many. In my humble opinion there is proof from the beginning of Yoma, where another Kohen is prepared for the Kohen Gadol lest he become impure. Although impurity is common, only one substitute is prepared. Otherwise there would be no limit. Beit Yosef writes that one may tear the leather covering the mouth of a wine barrel provided he does not intend to fashion a spout, which would be making a utensil. Malbushei Yom Tov rules likewise.
[7] The top of the barrel, etc. This implies that one may even cut off part of the barrel itself together with the stopper (Tosafot 48a). The case concerns a barrel made by joining fragments with pitch, as stated in seif 1 (Shiltei Gibborim; Magen Avraham). Perishah, however, writes that even an intact barrel is permitted because one does not create a separate opening; the opening is formed in the barrel together with removal of the stopper. Maharshal writes that this is permitted specifically for guests, and the Gemara likewise implies so.
[8] In the stopper above, etc. This implies that making the opening in the top of the barrel itself is prohibited, as all the later authorities write. The Remezim and Rashba's Avodat HaKodesh also imply this, contrary to Taz's lengthy attempt to permit because dirt falls into it and it therefore is not a proper opening. That reason does not permit a hole in the barrel itself. Tosafot on Eruvin 35 writes that even a barrel joined from fragments with pitch may not be pierced with a spear point.
[9] [Levush] If it is large, etc. This requires study, because a stopper is not considered attached to the barrel, as stated on 48a. Nevertheless, cutting off the entire top as above may be prohibited.
[10] [Levush] Because there is no building, etc. According to this, a vessel holding more than forty se'ah would be prohibited, as Olat Shabbat writes. Yet the example of a large cabinet apparently does not imply so, and Shiltei Gibborim, chapter Chaviyot, appears to permit even a large vessel. See Shiyurei Knesset HaGedolah. This requires study.
[11] And some permit, etc. Because one need not demolish anything. Bach decides this way. The same applies to removing pegs inserted jointly through the lid and walls of a chest to fasten them together (Shiltei Gibborim). Magen Avraham writes that fixing hinges in place with nails is prohibited according to all opinions because it repairs a utensil. Perhaps the same applies to inserting the pegs just mentioned.
