(א) (s.k. 5) And specifically, etc. — thus Terumat HaDeshen, etc. Know that the words of Beit Yosef and Rama derive from the words of Mordechai and Terumat HaDeshen; and in truth Rama disagrees with Beit Yosef, for according to Beit Yosef one need not have removed it from erev Shabbat seif 1; and nevertheless Rama did not write the language of "some say," since it is not explicit thus in Beit Yosef's words and the like — this is Rama's way, to write his words in the language of explanation per the Mechaber's view and not in the language of "some say," as is known; and as will be explained. However, Magen Avraham disagrees with them; therefore Biur Halacha cites Terumat HaDeshen's language and first I will cite the language of the question, and through this Magen Avraham's words will be understood more (also Taz brought it): Question: a knife stuck in a wooden wall from erev Shabbat, or in a bench that is not attached — is it permitted to remove it on Shabbat? Answer: when it is stuck in a wall, which is attached — forbidden, for it is psik reisha in removing it that he will certainly widen the hole and be liable for building, since it is stuck somewhat forcefully — it is almost psik reisha; and one cannot bring proof to permit from that which Mordechai wrote in chapter Chaviyot, that if a knife was stuck in barrels it is permitted to remove and insert it, since he does not intend to add; and he brings proof from chapter Bameh Tomnin (daf 50b, as Shmuel said): this knife between the bricks (Rashi: a brick wall, and they stick the knife between row and row) that was pulled from erev Shabbat is permitted; and one can say: since a barrel is a vessel and there is no building and demolition in vessels like Rav — therefore there is no concern of building even if it is impossible that he not add to the hole; and do not challenge to liable for metaken petachah — behold we conclude, etc., that by Torah law every opening that is not fit for inserting and removing, etc., except that the Sages decreed, etc., because of a rooster coop, etc., and therefore when he intends it is forbidden; but when he does not intend we can say that even if psik reisha it is permitted in a rabbinic prohibition — one can say both in intending and in psik reisha, as is in the responsum of Maharam in Mordechai chapter HaZorek, etc. However, it appears that this knife stuck in barrels, as Magen Avraham cited at the end of his words and the conclusion of his words there, in the responsum — both with a knife stuck in a wooden wall and with these barrels in Mordechai it is forbidden; but in a detached matter like a bench and the like — we do not know a reason for prohibition. R' Yehuda explained chapter Kirah daf 44, etc., lest he move earth from its place; and he concluded there in Terumat HaDeshen: and because of muktzeh, end of his words. And in truth this is Rashi's explanation; but according to R' Yehuda's explanation, who rejected this reason — behold R' Huna also holds thus, and R' Huna holds moving is permitted — if so there is only moving from the side; therefore R' Yehuda explained the reason is because of widening the hole; and therefore perforce there is a textual error in Terumat HaDeshen; and see Taz what he emended. But Magen Avraham holds that only two words "and because of muktzeh" are a textual error; and his intent is "lest he move earth" — meaning, and he will widen the hole. Behold Beit Yosef wondered at Terumat HaDeshen with two wonderings: one, that he concludes that in a bench and the like, detached, there is no prohibition — if so, why did he need the barrels in Mordechai "that was pulled" — behold that is a detached matter; and see Bach to resolve: it deals with a large barrel that holds 40 se'ah; and the examiner there, in the responsum will see that he holds that Mordechai deals with ordinary small barrels — except that Terumat HaDeshen wrote: if "that was pulled," even a large barrel is permitted — see there. He further challenged Beit Yosef on what Terumat HaDeshen wrote, that Mordechai perforce deals with "that was pulled" — behold he brings proof from the knife between the oven, and there it deals with "that was pulled"; and behold Shmuel is established there thus: R' Huna said: this silikusta (Rashi: stakes in the ground that they stick in soft earth so they remain moist) that was pulled is permitted; and if not — forbidden; Shmuel said: this knife between the oven that was pulled, etc.; Rav Ketina challenged: but we learned one who buries a turnip and radishes under the vine — if part of it was exposed he is not concerned, neither for kilayim nor for seventh year nor tithe, and they are moved on Shabbat; it refutes; and Rashi wrote: Rav Ketina challenged these Rabbis who said that if not "that was pulled" it is forbidden, end of his words; and therefore Rif and the Rosh ruled that we do not require "that was pulled"; and if so, what Mordechai wrote, etc., as we say in chapter Beitza: Shmuel said these knives, etc. — not to say that we rule like Shmuel, for behold he is refuted; rather from the end of the matter that we concluded that even if not "that was pulled" it is permitted — he brings and is not concerned with the end of the matter that is explicit that Shmuel is refuted and we do not rule like him:

(ב) And if so, with a knife stuck in barrels, even if not "that was pulled" it is permitted, etc.; and it is possible that he explains that Rav Ketina challenged only R' Huna, who said this silikusta, etc., for burying a turnip is more relevant to this water than to both of them — he told him lest he move earth; but the knife between the oven — he also told him because of widening the hole, which is building, end of Beit Yosef's words; and therefore Beit Yosef did not cite in barrels that we require "that was pulled"; but Rama, who holds like Terumat HaDeshen, wrote that we require "that was pulled":

(ג) Behold it is clear, etc. — he comes to reject Beit Yosef's explanation, that Terumat HaDeshen holds that Rav Ketina's refutation does not apply to Shmuel; and one cannot say thus, for Terumat HaDeshen brought the words of Tosafos on daf 44, where R' Yehuda explained there that the reason is because of a hole; and there in another matter they wrote Tosafos explicitly that Rav Ketina's refutation applies also to Shmuel; and if so, how could Terumat HaDeshen write not like Tosafos' words, which are before his eyes and in his hands, and not mention them at all? Also, from where for Terumat HaDeshen — even if Terumat HaDeshen according to his own view holds that the refutation does not apply to Shmuel — nevertheless, from where does Mordechai hold thus, since Terumat HaDeshen knows that Tosafos hold that also for Shmuel he challenged; and perhaps Mordechai also holds thus; or that Terumat HaDeshen also holds that the refutation applies also to Shmuel regarding Beit Yosef's difficulty, since Shmuel is refuted that we do not require "that was pulled," and how did Terumat HaDeshen write that Mordechai means that we require "that was pulled" — thus Magen Avraham to resolve what Magen Avraham wrote; and it is implied that he holds it is obvious to Terumat HaDeshen, etc.; and his intent is: for R' Yehuda wrote: therefore Shmuel requires "that was pulled" lest he widen the hole; and if so, it turns out that Shmuel is refuted that we do not require "that was pulled"; but nevertheless perforce we require that there not be psik reisha regarding widening the hole; and Rav Ketina challenged Shmuel only regarding what requires specifically "that was pulled," and if not we do not find permission, for it is certainly psik reisha; and we learned one who buries a turnip, etc.; and if so, perforce it must be that even if not "that was pulled" we find that it is not psik reisha; but if it is psik reisha Rav Ketina agrees that it is forbidden:
