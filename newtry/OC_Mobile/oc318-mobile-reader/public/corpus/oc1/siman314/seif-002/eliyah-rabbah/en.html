[3] And it is permitted to open it. But enlarging the opening appears governed by the previous law, as Nachalat Tzvi and Olat Shabbat write. Although Beit Yosef permitted it, Shulchan Aruch's unqualified wording implies he retracted. Nachalat Tzvi's inference from Mordechai's phrase "in a place made for it" that only an already existing hole may be enlarged is surprising, because Mordechai concludes by referencing Rif. He plainly means Rif's statement that the rule applies in a place made for preserving, and the word "in it" must be understood accordingly. This is clear, and Mordechai with Maharam and Gedulat Mordechai likewise imply so.
