(2) [Levushei Serad] And some say specifically, etc. Well directed that he wrote in the name of "some say" even though Rama did not write thus (Tosafos Yom Tov); and likewise in Nachalat Tzvi he decided that even if he did not remove it while it was still daytime it is permitted; and likewise Taz and Magen Avraham are lenient for a great need; and in my humble opinion the essence is like Rama; and if I begin to debate this matter the scope will be shortened — for all expanded greatly on this; and what Taz emended in Terumat HaDeshen — the reader in Magen Avraham will see there is no need to emend; but I say that Rama's reason is that it is an opening and he wants it so wine exits — and it is not destructive ruining as arose in Magen Avraham's view; and nevertheless it is only d'rabbanan as explicit in Shabbos daf 146 that they decreed due to a coop; and what Magen Avraham wrote that Terumat HaDeshen's view siman 64 is that it is d'oraisa — one can wonder at him from the Talmud cited above; and Terumat HaDeshen himself wrote there; rather it appears in my humble opinion Terumat HaDeshen did not write it is d'oraisa except regarding a cavity in the ground — and not (in my humble opinion a proof) since in the cavity perforce we deal with removing it while it was still daytime; if so the same law applies to barrels per the Mordechai who compares them — truly for barrels it is d'rabbanan; and now it is proven Terumat HaDeshen himself holds that in a d'rabbanan prohibition psik reisha is forbidden; and at first they did not write according to truth — and it is clear; and if so all the challenges and astonishments Magen Avraham wondered at in this are for naught — end of discussion. And in any case what he challenged from Shulchan Aruch Beitza daf 35 that psik reisha is forbidden even d'rabbanan — wondrous — behold he himself wrote siman 316 section 29 in the name of Maharsha that we do not have this text in the Talmud — and it is reasonable — see there; also one can say Terumat HaDeshen holds that words in their category are trapped — as will be explained below. Rambam wrote chapter 10: One placed the stone and one placed the mortar — one who places mortar is liable; and this is in the middle row; but in the upper row even if he raised a stone and placed it on mortar he is liable; and in the lower row if he merely aligned it, etc. One who builds on utensils is exempt — for what they placed in the Temple boards on sockets was not building in utensils — for sockets are like the ground.
