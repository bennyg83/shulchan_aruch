Water. Since these vessels are designated for this purpose, they are like a beis hakisei and are prohibited even when they have no foul odor. This is the view of the Rosh and Rambam. The Magen Avraham writes: It appears to me that if the vessel is also designated for another use, such as pouring wastewater into it, it is permitted when it contains neither excrement nor a foul odor. The same applies if it is designated for urination, but each time one urinates into it he adds a reviis of water. The Yad Aharon disagrees, explaining that according to the Beit Yosef, a reviis of water is effective for urine because urine is clear, mixes with the water, and is nullified. What is absorbed into the vessel, however, does not mix with the water so as to become nullified. Here too, it is impossible that while one urinates the vessel does not absorb some small amount. Adding water after absorption is therefore ineffective. See Taz and Baer Heitev.
Glass. Because it does not absorb. The Taz writes that according to the Rama in siman 451, who cites an opinion that even hagalah is ineffective for glass utensils, one should likewise be stringent here, since the underlying reason is the same; see there. The Yad Aharon disagrees and rules that one need not be stringent here; see there.
Coated. This means what is called glazed.
Thoroughly. Provided that it has no odor. Tosafos at the end of the chapter Mi Shemeiso permits it if water was placed in them.
