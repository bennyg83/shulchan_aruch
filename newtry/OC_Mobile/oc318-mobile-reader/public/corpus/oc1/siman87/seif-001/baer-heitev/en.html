Water. Since they are designated for this purpose, it is considered like a lavatory and is forbidden even if they have no bad odor — the Rosh and the Rambam. And Magen Avraham wrote, and it seems to me that if the vessel is also designated for another use, such as to pour into it wash-water, if there is no feces and no bad odor it is permitted; and the same applies if it is designated for urination and each time he urinates he puts a revi'it of water into it, it is permitted — so far his words. And Yad Aharon disputes him and wrote: since the reason is as Beit Yosef wrote, that a revi'it of water helps for urine because with clear water it mixes in the water and is nullified, but what is absorbed in the vessel does not mix with the water so as to be nullified — if so, the same applies here, for it is impossible that while he is urinating the vessel does not absorb something slight; therefore pouring water after absorption does not help. And see Taz and Eshel Avraham.

Glass, which does not absorb. And Taz wrote: it appears that according to what Rama wrote in Siman 491, that some say hag'alah does not help for a glass vessel — the same applies that one should be stringent here, for it is one reason; see there. And Yad Aharon disputes him that one need not be stringent here; see there.

Coated — that is, what we call glazed.

Well — provided there is no odor in it. And in Tosafos at the beginning of Mi Shemesu they wrote to permit if one put water in them.
