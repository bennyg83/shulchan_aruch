Seif katan 1, even, etc. “It is like a beis hakisei,” etc. Meaning, since it is like a beis hakisei, it is prohibited on a Torah level, and adding water is therefore ineffective. Water helps only for urine, because Torah law prohibits urine only while it is being discharged, whereas afterward its prohibition is merely Rabbinic. The Sages therefore permitted adding water, as Beit Yosef writes in their name. Beit Yosef himself, however, gives another reason: urine is clear, mixes with the water, and becomes nullified. By contrast, what is absorbed in a chamber pot or urinal vessel does not mix with the water and is not nullified; therefore adding water is ineffective. See there.
It appears to me that if the vessel is also designated for another use, etc. It is permitted. This may apply specifically according to the Rosh and Rambam’s reason stated above, namely that we treat these vessels stringently as having the law of a beis hakisei and excrement. One can then say that this applies only when the vessel is designated for urine or excrement, but not when it is also designated for another use. According to Beit Yosef’s reason, however, that adding water is ineffective because the absorbed material does not mix with the water, perhaps it remains prohibited even if designated for another use. Its additional designation changes nothing, for it still contains absorbed urine or excrement that is not nullified by adding water.
The same applies, etc. “And each time,” etc. In my humble opinion, this is sufficient even according to Beit Yosef’s reason, because the vessel was never saturated with urine alone, but only with urine that had already become nullified in water before being absorbed.
