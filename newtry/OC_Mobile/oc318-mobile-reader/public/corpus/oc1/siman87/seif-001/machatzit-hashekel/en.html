Siman katan 1, "Even if..."—it is considered like a lavatory, etc., and he means that since it is like a lavatory, therefore it is forbidden from the Torah; and therefore pouring water does not help. For specifically regarding urine, pouring water helps, since from the Torah it is only forbidden opposite the column [of filth], and afterward it is only rabbinically forbidden; and therefore they were lenient regarding pouring water. So too Rabbeinu Yonah in his name; but according to his own view he wrote another reason—that specifically urine, which is clear and mixes with water and is nullified, is therefore permitted; whereas not so with the urine receptacle and chamberpot, for what is absorbed in them does not mix with water and is not nullified; therefore pouring water does not help. See there.

And it seems to me that if the vessel is also designated for another use, etc., it is permitted—perhaps specifically per the reason of the Rosh and Rambam cited above, who said we are stringent with them because their law is like a lavatory and excrement; and therefore one could say specifically when designated for urine or excrement. But when also designated for another use, not. But according to Rabbeinu Yonah's reason that pouring water does not help because what is absorbed does not mix with water—if so, perhaps even when also designated for another use it is forbidden, for what of it—at any rate there is absorbed urine and excrement in it, and it is not nullified by pouring water.

And similarly, etc., and every time, etc.—this is according to my humble opinion sufficient also for Rabbeinu Yonah's reason, for there was never absorbed urine alone, but urine that had already been nullified in water before being absorbed in it.
