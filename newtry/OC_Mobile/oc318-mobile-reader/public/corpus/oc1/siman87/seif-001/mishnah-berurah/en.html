(1) A chamberpot, etc.—and even if now there is nothing in them, and even if they are washed well inside and outside and completely clean so that there is not even a bad smell in them.

(2) For urine—and if the vessel is also designated for another use, such as to pour beverages into it, if there is no excrement and no bad smell it is permitted; and similarly if every time he urinates he puts a revi'it of water into it, it is permitted if no bad smell reaches from it; and it seems to me that in this case, if a bad smell reaches from the vessel, its law is like an actual urine receptacle and one must distance four amot from where the smell ends. See Biur Halachah.

(3) Like excrement—before him as far as he can see, and behind him four amot from where the smell ends. And it is implied from the words of many later authorities that they agree with Rabbeinu Yonah that from the Torah a chamberpot and urine receptacle are forbidden, although urine itself is only rabbinically forbidden; the urine receptacle is more stringent, for since it is designated for this it is like a lavatory, and therefore pouring water into them also does not help, even if he filled them to their rims. And if he read or prayed in a place of a urine receptacle that was within his four amot, he returns if he finds it in a place where he could have relieved himself initially upon it. For other blessings, see later siman 185 seif 5 and in Mishna Berurah there, and apply the law here. And if he found the chamberpot and urine receptacle within the measure of as far as he can see, he does not return after the fact, as above in siman 81 seif 2 in Mishna Berurah—see there.

(4) Of glass—the Taz raises doubts about this, but most of the later authorities and almost all of them agree to be lenient; and the reason is that in all of these they hold that they do not absorb, and even if they absorb, their absorption is not great; therefore there is no law of a lavatory upon them.

(5) Coated—some say this means what we call glazed; and some say specifically if it is coated with lead, not glazed. Also some say we require that it be coated inside and outside; and there are those who are lenient that when it says coated, only inside. And here in practice one should be stringent that we require coating also outside; but when it is overturned on its mouth one may rely to be lenient per the view of those who permit, so too Eliyah Rabbah. And the vessels we call porcelain—for other prohibitions their law is like earthenware vessels that hag'alah does not help; and it appears one should be stringent also for this matter. See Biur Halachah.

(6) Well—from inside and outside, and also that there should be no bad smell from it; and if a bad smell reaches from it, its law is like a bad smell that has no [fixed] source, since the vessel no longer has the name of chamberpot and urine receptacle and it is clean, and one need not distance except until where the smell ends. And if the vessel is not washed, one must distance from it four amot, lest there be substance of filth upon it.
