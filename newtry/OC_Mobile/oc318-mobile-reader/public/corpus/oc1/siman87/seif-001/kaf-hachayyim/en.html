1) [Seif 1] A chamber pot for feces and a urinal, etc. So it is taught in the chapter Mi Shemeiso, page 25b: "The Sages taught: It is forbidden to recite Krias Shema opposite a chamber pot for feces or a urinal, even when they contain nothing." Rashi explains that a chamber pot and a urinal are both earthenware vessels; one intended for feces is called a chamber pot, while one intended for urine is called a urinal. End quote. Rosh writes that it is reasonable that a wooden vessel is forbidden because the filth is absorbed within it and it is full of contamination, whereas a glass vessel is permitted. Tur rules accordingly, and this is also the ruling of Maran. Although Rashba permits a wooden vessel, Rosh and Tur forbid it, treating it like earthenware because it absorbs and is full of contamination, and Maran rules accordingly. We therefore follow this ruling. The later authorities write likewise.
2) There: One must distance himself from them as from feces: in front of him, as far as he can see; behind him, four amos from the point at which the odor ends. Tur, Beis Yosef, and Olas Tamid. The later authorities write likewise.
3) There: Even if one poured water into them. Since they are designated for this purpose, they are regarded like a privy, even when they have no foul odor. Rosh, chapter Mi Shemeiso, siman 54. Rambam likewise writes this in chapter 3 of Hilchos Krias Shema, halachah 12, and Beis Yosef cites his ruling; Magen Avraham, seif katan 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1. Rabbeinu Yonah writes that the reason adding water does not help for these vessels as it does for urine is that we treat them like feces, which is forbidden by Torah law under the verse, "Your camp shall be holy." Urine, however, is forbidden only by rabbinic law, for the Torah forbade only reciting opposite the stream itself; adding water is therefore sufficient for urine. End of his words, cited by Beis Yosef. Beis Yosef gives another reason: urine is permitted when water is added because it is clear, mixes with the water, and becomes nullified. In contrast, with a chamber pot or urinal, that which has been absorbed into the vessel does not mix with the water so as to become nullified; adding water therefore does not help. End of his words. Taz, seif katan 1, cites this explanation, and Levush and Shulchan Aruch HaRav there write likewise. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1, writes that according to Beis Yosef's reason, a urinal is forbidden only by rabbinic law, like urine itself, whereas according to Rabbeinu Yonah's reason it is forbidden by Torah law. The practical difference concerns a case of doubt; see there. Olas Tamid, paragraph 2, writes similarly, though he concludes that the practical ruling requires study; see there. See what we wrote above in siman 73, paragraph 8, and siman 74, paragraph 7, beginning with the words "You should know." Based on the reason given by Rosh and Rambam (that these vessels are designated for this purpose) Magen Avraham writes there that if the vessel is also designated for another use, such as receiving wastewater, then it is permitted when it contains neither feces nor an odor. Solet Belulah, paragraph 1; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2; Halachah Berurah, paragraph 1; and Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 2, cite his ruling. Machatzis HaShekel on Magen Avraham, seif katan 1, writes, however, that this applies only according to the reason of Rosh and Rambam. According to Beis Yosef's reason (that the substance absorbed in the vessel does not mix with the water) it is possible that the vessel is forbidden even when also designated for another use; see there. Birkei Yosef, paragraph 1, likewise writes that one should be stringent, and Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 8, cites his ruling.
3) Further: Magen Avraham writes there that the same rule applies if the vessel is designated for urination and each time one urinates, one puts a revi'is of water into it: it is permitted. Solet Belulah, Eliyah Rabbah, and Shulchan Aruch HaRav there cite his ruling. Yad Aharon, in his glosses to Beis Yosef, writes concerning these words of Magen Avraham that one should be stringent. Since the reason is as Maran writes; that a revi'is of water helps only for a clear liquid, which mixes with the water and becomes nullified, whereas that which is absorbed into the vessel does not mix with the water so as to become nullified; the same reasoning applies here. It is impossible that the vessel does not absorb some small amount while the person is urinating, so what does adding a revi'is of water help after the absorption has already occurred? End of his words. Machatzis HaShekel there, however, understood the aforementioned words of Magen Avraham as referring to a case in which the water was placed in the vessel before urination. He writes that this is sufficient even according to Beis Yosef's reason, because the vessel never absorbed undiluted urine; rather, it absorbed urine that had already become nullified in the water before being absorbed. End of his words. Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 3, likewise explains Magen Avraham's words in this manner; see there.
