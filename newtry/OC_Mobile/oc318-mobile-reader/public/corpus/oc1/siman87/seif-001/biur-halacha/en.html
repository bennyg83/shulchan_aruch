Chamberpot, etc. — See in Mishna Berurah what he wrote in note (2), that if a bad odor reaches, etc., for it is apparently very difficult on Magen Avraham, who permits if one is always careful to pour immediately a revi'it of water — is it not according to Rabbeinu Yonah that the receptacle is forbidden by Torah law, even though urine itself is only rabbinic? And therefore, either because it putrefies more we judge it like feces, as Chayei Adam wrote; or because since it is designated for this it is like a lavatory, and accordingly its prohibition is by Torah law, as implied in the novelae of Re'em — if so, what does it help that one always pours a revi'it of water? For that which a revi'it of water helps for urine, even when it is abundant, is because urine is only rabbinic, as Rashi explained (Berachos 25b): if its prohibition were by Torah law it would not help at all. And automatically, what does this help for the receptacle, whose prohibition is by Torah law because of the filth absorbed in it — will a little water that one pours into it prevent the filth of urine from entering it? And this is like the question of Yeshuos Yaakov brought in Be'er Heitev, and preferable to it. And perhaps one could say: since we see there is no bad odor in it, automatically it is proven that filth did not absorb in it so much, and its prohibition is only because it is designated for urine and is considered like a lavatory; and therefore, since he always pours water into it immediately after urinating, he did not allow it to become associated with a lavatory — and with this the above question of Yeshuos Yaakov is also resolved. And accordingly, if a bad odor reaches from it, its law is exactly like the receptacle, as is my humble opinion.

But if they are of metals or of coated earthenware — See in Mishna Berurah regarding porcelain vessels. And behold, even though regarding hag'alah Peri Megadim wrote in Siman 491 that they are earthenware vessels, nevertheless regarding this matter it requires investigation. For regarding what Taz wrote, that according to what was explained in Siman 491 that a glass vessel is also like earthenware — the same applies here — Yeshuot Yaakov wrote: I am astonished, for regarding glazed earthenware the opinion of the above poskim is that it is not in the category of a chamberpot for excrement, and this is certainly considered like earthenware regarding absorption of prohibition [and similarly glazed earthenware coated on one side — Chakham Tzvi wrote there in note 64 that the opinion of most poskim is that it is like an earthenware vessel, and with this all would agree it helps, as above]. Rather, certainly regarding a chamberpot for excrement, since one can rinse it and it is smooth, there is no concern of a chamberpot — so far his words. And if so, the same applies in our matter too: perhaps since they are smooth and do not absorb as much as an earthenware vessel, perhaps their law is not like an earthenware vessel. And nevertheless it seems that in practice it is correct to be stringent, for even regarding a glass vessel itself Peri Chadash is stringent; and although there we follow the later authorities to be lenient, nevertheless here one should be stringent.
