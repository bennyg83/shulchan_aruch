[1] Even if one poured, etc. And there is no bad odor — for the receptacle is inferior to urine itself, which is clear and nullified in water, which is not so with the receptacle (Beit Yosef). According to this, in any case it is only rabbinic like urine; but Rabbeinu Yonah wrote that the receptacle is forbidden by Torah law like feces, and a practical difference emerges regarding a doubt. Taz wrote: according to what Rama wrote in Siman 491, that some say hag'alah does not help for a glass vessel — the same applies that one should be stringent here, since some say glass is inferior to earthenware — up to here. And he was not precise, for certainly it is not inferior to an earthenware vessel; rather, it does not seem plausible to him that "some say" compare it to an earthenware vessel — and Taz himself wrote so there. And that hag'alah does not help for it, even when its use is cold, is when it is customary to leave crumbs there and it is pressed in, as Levush wrote there. Also it seems to me that being compared to an earthenware vessel is regarding that hag'alah does not help because it does not leave the status of its mouth since it is made from sand; but in any case it does not absorb as much as an earthenware vessel, and therefore it is permitted here. And you should know that the Mordechai and Agudah in the chapter Mi Shemesu permit, and in the chapter Kol Sha'ah forbid hag'alah except as we say. And furthermore, Taz and Magen Avraham wrote that after the fact it is permitted even without hag'alah.
