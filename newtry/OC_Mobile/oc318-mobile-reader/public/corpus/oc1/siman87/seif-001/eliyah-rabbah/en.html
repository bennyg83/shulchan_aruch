[1] Even if he poured, etc. This applies where it has no foul odor. A urinal vessel is worse than urine itself, because urine is clear and becomes nullified in water, unlike what is absorbed in the vessel, as the Beit Yosef explains. According to this, its prohibition is nevertheless only Rabbinic, like urine. Rabbeinu Yonah, however, writes that a urinal vessel is prohibited on a Torah level like excrement, which affects cases of doubt. The Taz writes that according to the Rama in siman 451, who cites an opinion that hagalah is ineffective for glass utensils, one should likewise be stringent here, because some say glass is worse than earthenware. This is imprecise. Glass is certainly not worse than earthenware; rather, that opinion does not accept that it is unlike earthenware. The Taz himself writes this there. Hagalah is ineffective even when its use was cold only where crumbs are ordinarily left in it, creating soaking, as Levush writes there. It also appears that its similarity to earthenware is only that hagalah is ineffective, because that which leaves through its manufacture does not leave through hagalah, since it is formed from sand. Nevertheless, it does not absorb as much as earthenware and is therefore permitted here. Proof is that Mordechai and Agudah permit it in the chapter Mi Shemeiso, although they prohibit hagalah in the chapter Kol Shaah. The distinction must therefore be as stated. Furthermore, Taz and Magen Avraham write that after the fact it is permitted even without hagalah.
[2] Of metal, etc. The Rashba’s wording is, “of types of metal.” Shelah writes that a person should accustom himself to have a urinal vessel of metal or glass, keep it washed and clean, and place a reviis of water in it when urinating. He may then recite Asher Yatzar and study Torah near it. It appears to me that if it is clean and no foul odor emanates from it, washing it from one Erev Shabbos to the next is sufficient. Thus writes Tahor Yadayim Yosef Ometz. Magen Avraham writes that if an earthenware or wooden vessel is also designated for another use, such as pouring wastewater into it, it is permitted when it contains no excrement or odor. The same applies if it is designated for urination and a reviis of water is added each time. Taz writes that according to the Rama in siman 451, who holds that hagalah is ineffective for glass, one should likewise be stringent here because glass is worse than earthenware. This is unconvincing, for the Taz himself writes that after the fact it is permitted and is stringent only initially, specifically regarding the stringency of chametz. His statement that glass is worse than earthenware is also astonishing. Even those who are stringent there merely treat it like earthenware, as Beit Yosef writes, but certainly not worse.
