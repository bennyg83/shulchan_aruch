A urinal vessel is worse than urine itself, because urine is clear and becomes nullified in water, unlike what is absorbed in the vessel, as the Beit Yosef explains. According to this, its prohibition is nevertheless only Rabbinic, like urine. Rabbeinu Yonah, however, writes that a urinal vessel is prohibited on a Torah level like excrement, which affects cases of doubt. The Taz writes that according to the Rama in siman 451, who cites an opinion that hagalah is ineffective for glass utensils, one should likewise be stringent here, because some say glass is worse than earthenware. This is imprecise. Glass is certainly not worse than earthenware; rather, that opinion does not accept that it is unlike earthenware. The Taz himself writes this there. Hagalah is ineffective even when its use was cold only where crumbs are ordinarily left in it, creating soaking, as Levush writes there. It also appears that its similarity to earthenware is only that hagalah is ineffective, because that which leaves through its manufacture does not leave through hagalah, since it is formed from sand. Nevertheless, it does not absorb as much as earthenware and is therefore permitted here. Proof is that Mordechai and Agudah permit it in the chapter Mi Shemeiso, although they prohibit hagalah in the chapter Kol Shaah. The distinction must therefore be as stated. Furthermore, Taz and Magen Avraham write that after the fact it is permitted even without hagalah.
