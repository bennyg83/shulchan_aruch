Even. See Taz. Rabbeinu Yonah’s reason is that a urinal vessel is prohibited on a Torah level, whereas urine itself is only Rabbinic. Therefore, a reviis of water helps for urine but not for a urinal vessel, which is more repulsive and prohibited on a Torah level. In the Berlin edition of Beit Yosef, the words “Rabbeinu Yonah” are missing; see there. According to this, a reviis of water would not help for urine having a foul odor, because such urine is prohibited on a Torah level, as explained in simanim 74 and 79 in Taz’s introduction; see there. The plain implication of the poskim does not support this, and the Gemara’s question, “how much water is required,” also implies that the prohibition is Torah-level. Beit Yosef therefore chose the reason that the absorbed matter does not mix with the water. For the same reason, adding water does not help for excrement, because it does not mix. Thick saliva helps temporarily because it covers, not because it nullifies, and murky water likewise helps as a covering. The expression “and cover it” can bear two meanings: first, a proper covering, such as excrement enclosed in glass; second, concealment from sight. Thick saliva and murky water conceal it from sight and are therefore also considered a covering. See siman 76, Magen Avraham seif katan 9. What I wrote here appears more correct. See also siman 79, Magen Avraham seif katan 1.
