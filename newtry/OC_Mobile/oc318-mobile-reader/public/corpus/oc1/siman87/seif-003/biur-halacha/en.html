Permitted to recite, etc., since one distanced, etc. — See in Mishna Berurah: if the bed is ten [tefachim] high, etc., one may recite [Shema] before the bed. And it is obvious that the same applies when one lies on the bed; and proof from above, Siman 79, seif 2; see there in Mishna Berurah. But one must be careful in this not to extend a single limb outside the bed toward the place of the chamberpot; see above, Siman 76, seif 4. Chayei Adam wrote: a chest [and wardrobe] and the like standing in a house, ten tefachim high and four tefachim wide along its length, that separates between a person and feces — one should be stringent according to Magen Avraham, for even so it is forbidden unless it separates from wall to wall. But if there was urine on the ground or in a vessel that is not a chamberpot, in a matter where there is only a rabbinic prohibition, it seems to me that one may stand opposite the partition and recite and bless; but not opposite the air-gap of the partition. A partition ten [tefachim] wide is considered a partition to permit opposite it. And if the partition is more than the air-gap, such that the standing portion is greater than the breach, it seems to me that even regarding feces one may permit in a time of pressure opposite the partition, provided the feces are not visible. And similarly a groove that separates, ten [tefachim] deep along four [tefachim], and its width from lip to lip is also four tefachim wide — behold, this is like two domains; see there. He also wrote: specifically when the partition is ten [tefachim] high and four [tefachim] wide from the ground until the end of the height of ten, or even if less than three [tefachim] near the ground it does not have a width of four, such as when it stands on thin legs — we say lavud; but our table and the like, which stands on legs and does not have a width of four even though they are ten [tefachim] high — is not a partition to separate.

And similarly if one inverted, etc. — Levush wrote: if one placed the chamberpot and receptacle inside a vessel whose partitions are higher than the chamberpot and receptacle until they are not visible, it is as if covered and permitted if there is no bad odor. But Nekudas Hakesef and Levushei Churah dispute and hold that it does not help unless its partitions are higher than ten tefachim — so Rabbi Akiva Eiger. Peri Megadim wrote: a wide roof that protrudes over the house [called "pir libin"] is not considered a separate domain for itself regarding Rosh Hashanah; therefore if there is feces outside it, it is forbidden to say [Shema] beneath it four amot, even if one does not see it. And see in Siman 76, seif 8, that even if it is only a place where one might be concerned, one must be careful in this.
