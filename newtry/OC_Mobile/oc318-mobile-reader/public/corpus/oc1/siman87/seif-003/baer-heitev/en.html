Vessels. Bach wrote: a bed ten [tefachim] high whose partitions reach the ground, and the chamberpot behind the bed is called a partition and one may recite [Shema] — so far his words. And Magen Avraham wrote, and it seems to me, specifically when the bed partitions from wall to wall; but otherwise it is not considered a partition; see there. If one placed them under the bed and the bed's partitions reach within less than three [tefachim] of the ground, it is permitted — Beit Yosef 38, Bach. And see Siman 402, seif 66, whether we say lavud regarding vessels; but here all agree it is considered sealed, and from this reason the same applies if one covered it with a vessel and there is a little air between the vessel and the ground, such as if it protrudes a little from one side — we say lavud; but if the vessel is open on top, even less than three [tefachim], it is forbidden, for we require that it be covered. Magen Avraham.
