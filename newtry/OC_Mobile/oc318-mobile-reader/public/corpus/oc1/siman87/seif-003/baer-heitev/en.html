Vessel. The Bach writes that when a bed is ten tefachim high, its partitions reach the floor, and the chamber pot is behind the bed, the bed is considered a partition and one may recite Krias Shema. The Magen Avraham writes that this applies specifically when the bed separates from wall to wall; otherwise it is not considered a partition. See there. If he placed them beneath the bed and the bed’s partitions reach to within less than three tefachim of the ground, it is permitted. Beit Yosef, Lechem Chamudos, and Bach. See siman 502, end of seif 1, regarding whether the rule of lavud applies to utensils. Here, however, all agree that it is considered sealed. For the same reason, it appears to me that if he covered it with a vessel and there is a small gap between that vessel and the ground, such as where one side protrudes slightly, we apply lavud. But if the covering vessel is open above, even if the opening is less than three tefachim, it is prohibited, because it must be covered. Magen Avraham.
