9) “That he distanced himself.” If there was a bed in the house that was ten handbreadths high and its partitions reached the ground on every side; or at least its legs were short and the gap below them was less than three handbreadths, so that lavud applies; and excrement, a chamber pot, or a urinal vessel stood behind the bed, the bed is considered a partition. It is permitted to recite Keriat Shema in front of the bed without distancing oneself, provided that he cannot see the chamber pot and no foul odor reaches him. The Magen Avraham writes that this applies specifically when the bed extends from wall to wall; otherwise it is not considered a partition. Many authorities question this ruling, and Nishmat Adam also expresses astonishment. Chayei Adam therefore concludes that regarding urine on the ground or urine in a vessel that is not a designated urinal vessel, whose prohibition is only rabbinic, one may be lenient and recite Keriat Shema or a blessing opposite the partition even when it does not extend from wall to wall, though not opposite the open space alongside it. See Biur Halachah, where we copied his entire formulation. Fixed boards attached to a wall, called a shelf or bench, do not constitute a separate domain even if they are ten handbreadths high and perhaps even four handbreadths wide, because they have no partitions reaching the ground. Likewise, our tables that stand on legs and are less than four handbreadths wide do not constitute a partition even if ten handbreadths high. Chayei Adam. He further writes that if a chamber pot, or even urine, stands between an oven and the wall, it appears to me that one may not stand alongside the oven and recite Keriat Shema even though the oven intervenes. Since the oven is one of the fixtures used by the house, it is subordinate to the house. The same applies in every similar case. Other household furniture, however, which is not specially designated for the house, appears to intervene when it meets the conditions explained above regarding a bed. If he placed the chamber pot or urinal vessel beneath a bed and the bed’s partitions reach to within less than three handbreadths of the ground, it is permitted to recite Keriat Shema in front of or upon the bed even if the bed itself is not ten handbreadths high, because lavud applies and the vessel is considered covered. All this applies only when no foul odor reaches him. If odor reaches him, neither covering nor a partition helps. Our beds, whose legs are more than three handbreadths high, require the regular distancing from the chamber pot.
10) “A vessel.” Even if there is a small airspace between the vessel and the ground (for example, if it protrudes slightly on one side) as long as the gap is less than three handbreadths, we apply lavud and it is considered covered. If the vessel is open on top, however, it is prohibited even when the gap beneath it is less than three handbreadths.
