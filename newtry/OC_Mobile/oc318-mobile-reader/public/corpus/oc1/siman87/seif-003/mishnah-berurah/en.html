(9) That he distanced—and if there was in the house a bed ten [handbreadths] high and the partitions reach the ground on all sides, or at least its legs are short and are not three [tefachim] high so that it is considered lavud; and excrement or a chamberpot and urine receptacle standing behind the bed—the bed is considered a partition and permitted to recite before the bed, and he need not distance, provided he does not see the chamberpot and also no bad smell reaches him. And the Magen Avraham wrote specifically when the bed interposes from wall to wall; but otherwise it is not considered a partition, and many raise doubts about this law; also in Sefer Neshamah Adam he wondered about it; therefore Chayei Adam concludes that for urine that was on the ground, or in a vessel that is not a chamberpot—for they are only rabbinically forbidden—one may be lenient to recite and bless opposite the partition even if it does not interpose, but not opposite the open air; and see in Biur Halachah where we copied all his wording on this. Fixed shelves in the wall [called palitza or bank]—although they are ten [handbreadths] high, and perhaps even if their width is four tefachim, it is not a domain of its own, since it has no partitions reaching the ground. And similarly our tables standing on legs that are not four tefachim wide—even though they are ten [handbreadths] high—they are not a partition to interrupt (Chayei Adam). He also wrote there: a chamberpot or even urine standing between an oven and the wall—it seems to me forbidden to stand at the side of the oven and recite, even though the oven interrupts, for since it is among the household utensils it is nullified relative to the house; and similarly anything like this. But other household vessels that are not designated for the house—it seems to me they interrupt, and as explained above regarding a bed. If he placed the chamberpot and urine receptacle under the bed and the bed's partitions reach to less than three [tefachim] near the ground—even if the bed itself is not ten [handbreadths] high—it is permitted to recite before the bed and on top of it, lavud, and it is as covered; and all this when no bad smell reaches; but when it reaches, neither covering nor partition interruption helps. And our beds whose legs are three [tefachim] high require distancing from the chamberpot per the law.

(10) A vessel—even if there is a bit of air between the vessel and the ground, such as if it protrudes a bit from one side, as long as it is less than three [tefachim] we say lavud and it is as covered; but if the vessel is open on top, even less than three [tefachim] it is forbidden.
