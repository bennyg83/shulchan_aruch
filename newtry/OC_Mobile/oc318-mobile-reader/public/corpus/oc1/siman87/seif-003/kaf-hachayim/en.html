3) There — even if one poured water into them, since they are designated for this purpose they become like a lavatory, even if they have no bad odor — Rosh, chapter Mi Shemesu, Siman 24; and so the Rambam, chapter 3 of Hilchos Kerias Shema, law 12; and Beis David, Beit Yosef; Magen Avraham, note 1. Razah, letter 1; and Rabbeinu Yonah wrote that the reason pouring water does not help for them as it helps for urine is because we judge them like feces and they are forbidden by Torah law from "and you shall be holy"; but urine is forbidden only rabbinically, for the Torah forbade only opposite a column — therefore water suffices for them — so far. And Beit Yosef brought this. And Beit Yosef gave another reason for urine: that is the reason it is permitted with pouring water, because they are clear and mix in water and are nullified; not so with a chamberpot and receptacle, for what is absorbed in them does not mix in water so as to be nullified — therefore pouring water does not help for them — so far. And Taz brought this, note 1; and so Levush; Razah there. And Rabbi Akiva Eiger wrote, letter 1: according to Beit Yosef's reason, the prohibition of the receptacle is only rabbinic like urine; but according to Rabbeinu Yonah it is forbidden by Torah law, and there is a practical difference regarding a doubt — see there. And so Eshel Avraham, letter 2, except he wrote "it requires investigation for the law" — see there. And see in our words above, Siman 73, letter 8, and Siman 74, letter 7, in the words "and know"; and per the reason that the Rosh and Rambam, may their memory be a blessing, wrote that they are designated for this, etc., Magen Avraham wrote there: if the vessel is also designated for another use, such as to pour wash-water into it, if there is no feces and no odor it is permitted. And Sha'ar HaTziyun brought this, letter 1; Rabbi Akiva Eiger, letter 2; Chayei Adam, letter 1; Razah, letter 2. However, Machatzis Hashekel on Magen Avraham, note 1, wrote that this is specifically per the reason of the Rosh and Rambam; but per the reason of Beit Yosef, who wrote that what is absorbed does not mix with water, perhaps even if it is also designated for another use it is forbidden — see there. And so Birkei Yosef, letter 1, that one should be stringent. And Chida brought this, letter 8.

3) Furthermore Magen Avraham wrote there that the same applies if it is designated for urination and each time he urinates he puts a revi'it of water into it — it is permitted. And Sha'ar HaTziyun brought this there; Rabbi Akiva Eiger there; Razah there. But Yeshuot Yaakov in Hagahos Be'er Yitzchak wrote on these words of Magen Avraham that one should be stringent, for since the reason is as our master, may his memory be a blessing, wrote — a revi'it of water only helps for clear [urine] that mixes in water and is nullified, but what is absorbed in the vessel does not mix in water so as to be nullified — if so, the same applies here; and this is the reason here, that it is impossible that while he urinates the vessel does not absorb something slight — if so, what does pouring a revi'it of water after absorption help? — so far. However, Machatzis Hashekel there understood the words of the aforementioned Magen Avraham that it discusses a case where one put the water before urinating, for he wrote that this suffices even according to Beit Yosef's reason, that never was urine alone absorbed, but only urine that was already nullified in water before being absorbed in it — so far. And so to explain Maamar Mordechai, letter 3, on the words of Magen Avraham — see there.
