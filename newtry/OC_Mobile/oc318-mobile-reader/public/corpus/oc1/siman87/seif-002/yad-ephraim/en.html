In Magen Avraham, se'if katan 4: “And so it is implied by Rabbeinu Yonah, whose principal difficulty concerns the inquiry,” etc. The explanation of his words appears to be as follows. In the Talmud there is a dispute: the Rabbis hold that being behind the bed is ineffective, while Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel holds that it is effective. It is stated there that Rav Yosef asked Rav Huna about a bed three handbreadths high, etc.; regarding one ten handbreadths high, he did not ask. Abaye said: You acted correctly by not asking. Rashi writes that the Gaon explains the inquiry as concerning a closed bed. He challenges that, if so, why did Rav Yosef need to say that he did not ask about a height of ten handbreadths? Abaye likewise did not need to say, “You acted correctly,” because it is obvious that a height of ten constitutes a partition, as the baraita teaches: “If it was lower than ten,” etc. Tur writes that the Gaon holds the inquiry follows the view of Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, and the statement “I did not ask about ten” means that regarding that height Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel certainly holds it separates; see Bach. According to this, Rabbeinu Yonah should have challenged: since the discussion concerns a closed bed, what is the Rabbis' reason for disagreeing with Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel and holding that even at a height of ten it does not separate? Why should it not separate, when the closed side forms a complete partition at a height of ten? Rather, it must discuss a bed whose side does not extend from wall to wall. The Rabbis therefore properly hold that being behind the bed is ineffective, while Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel holds that even in such a case it constitutes a partition. Nevertheless, his difficulty with the statement “I did not ask about ten,” etc., remains sound. It is obvious that according to Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel it is a partition: since in his view even one that does not extend from wall to wall is considered a partition, what difference is there between a height of ten and a much greater height? Clearly a height of ten is a partition just as a greater height is, in accordance with the rule concerning a place ten handbreadths high. However, Magen Avraham's statement, “and it is known that the halakhah follows the Rabbis,” requires study. If the inquiry follows Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, we should rule in accordance with Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel because the inquiry was formulated according to his view. Tur explains that for this very reason the aforementioned Gaon ruled in accordance with Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel; Turei Zahav also cites this, and Rabbeinu Yonah rules in accordance with Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel. If one does not rule in accordance with Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, one must necessarily explain that the inquiry follows Rashba's view. In that case, it provides no proof at all regarding whether an obstruction extending from wall to wall is considered a partition, because the inquiry does not discuss such a case.
