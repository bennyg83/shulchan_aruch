In the Tur. “The Gaon explains,” etc. The Tur gives the reason why the Gaon ruled like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel: the Gemara’s unresolved question proceeds according to his view. One might object that according to Rashi’s explanation, the question proceeds according to Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar, yet the halachah nevertheless does not follow him, for Rava ruled otherwise. This is no difficulty. Whenever the Talmud contains no explicit ruling by an Amora, we follow the Tanna according to whose view the Gemara frames its question. When an explicit Amoraic ruling is before us, however, an Amora has authority to rule as appears correct to him, even like an individual against the majority. We find this many times among the poskim. There. The Tur’s phrase, “as though it were in another domain,” means as though the bed were in another domain. The excrement is therefore with him in his own domain and is uncovered. Beit Yosef’s emendation here to “in one domain” was unnecessary.
