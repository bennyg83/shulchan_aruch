[2] Of metal, etc. The Rashba’s wording is, “of types of metal.” Shelah writes that a person should accustom himself to have a urinal vessel of metal or glass, keep it washed and clean, and place a reviis of water in it when urinating. He may then recite Asher Yatzar and study Torah near it. It appears to me that if it is clean and no foul odor emanates from it, washing it from one Erev Shabbos to the next is sufficient. Thus writes Tahor Yadayim Yosef Ometz. Magen Avraham writes that if an earthenware or wooden vessel is also designated for another use, such as pouring wastewater into it, it is permitted when it contains no excrement or odor. The same applies if it is designated for urination and a reviis of water is added each time. Taz writes that according to the Rama in siman 451, who holds that hagalah is ineffective for glass, one should likewise be stringent here because glass is worse than earthenware. This is unconvincing, for the Taz himself writes that after the fact it is permitted and is stringent only initially, specifically regarding the stringency of chametz. His statement that glass is worse than earthenware is also astonishing. Even those who are stringent there merely treat it like earthenware, as Beit Yosef writes, but certainly not worse.
