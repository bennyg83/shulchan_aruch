[2] Of metals, etc. The wording of Rashba: of types of metal. Shelah wrote (folio 66): a person should accustom himself to have a receptacle of metal or glass, and it should be washed and clean, and he should put into it a revi'it of water in which he urinates — and then he may bless "Who formed" and study Torah beside it. And it seems to me that if it is clean and no bad odor emerges from the vessel, then it suffices if he washes it from erev Shabbos to erev Shabbos and has pure hands — Yosef Ometz — up to here. Magen Avraham wrote: if the vessel of earthenware or wood is also designated for another use, such as to pour wash-water into it, if there is no feces and no odor it is permitted; and the same applies if it is designated for urination and each time he gives a revi'it of water into it, it is permitted. Taz wrote: according to what Rama wrote in Siman 491 that hag'alah does not help for a glass vessel — the same applies that one should be stringent here, since glass is inferior to earthenware — up to here. And it is not clear, for he himself wrote that after the fact it is permitted; only initially he is stringent — and that is specifically regarding the stringency of chametz. Also what he wrote that it is inferior to earthenware is astonishing, for even for those who are stringent, only there we consider it like an earthenware vessel, as Beit Yosef wrote there — but certainly it is not inferior.
