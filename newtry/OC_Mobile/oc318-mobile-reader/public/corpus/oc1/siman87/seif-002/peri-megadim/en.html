Of—in Eshel Avraham he wrote that it does not absorb; and behold, regarding metals it is established that they absorb through pressure, as explained in Yoreh De'ah siman 68 in Shach, siman 33, and in Perishah there and Korban HaEdah in Magen Avraham, letter 4, I expanded. But since they do not absorb much, we do not judge them like excrement and a lavatory; and if so, the same applies to glass, although in siman 451 hag'alah does not help—here in our case he ruled leniently; see Eliyah Rabbah. And see Levush: so too pottery and wood are accustomed to absorb much, whereas not so metals and glass—they do not absorb much. However, one who examines the Beit Yosef wrote that glass does not absorb at all; and so too regarding metals under pressure, as the Magen Avraham wrote, that if it absorbs a bit it is like a lavatory when designated, as the Rosh and Rambam (and what the Rosh wrote; however, chamberpot and urine receptacle of pottery is not a repetition to say not because of lavatory but because of absorption, as the Perishah wrote; rather this is the explanation: however, even though because of lavatory, nevertheless one may permit metals and glass, for in Talmudic language chamberpot and urine receptacle are pottery; whereas not so if because of excrement that is very filthy—it is obvious no proof is needed, for certainly glass does not absorb, so too [metals]). And if so, the Taz properly challenged the Beit Yosef, for he was silent to him here—he conceded to him; and in siman 451 seif 27 he wrote that hag'alah does not help for them; and regarding metals one could say there sometimes they pour boiling [water], Magen Avraham there, letter 50; and see in Perishah Yoreh De'ah 105 in Taz.
