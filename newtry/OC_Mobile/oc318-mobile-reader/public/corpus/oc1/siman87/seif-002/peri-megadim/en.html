Of. See Taz, who writes that metal does not absorb. We rule, however, that metal absorbs through soaking, as explained in Yoreh Deah siman 68, Shach seif katan 33, and in my Peri Chadash there; I discussed this at length in Mishbetzos Zahav to siman 105, seif katan 4. Rather, because it does not absorb much, we do not treat it like excrement or a beis hakisei. The same should therefore apply to glass. Although hagalah is ineffective for glass in siman 451, regarding gentile wine the ruling is lenient; see Eliyah Rabbah. Levush likewise writes that earthenware and wood ordinarily absorb much, unlike metal and glass. Nevertheless, one who studies Beit Yosef sees that he writes that glass does not absorb at all, and one must say the same regarding metal soaked in liquid, as Magen Avraham writes. For if it absorbed even slightly, its designation would make it like a beis hakisei according to the Rosh and Rambam. The Rosh’s statement, “however, an earthenware chamber pot or urinal vessel,” is not a retraction indicating that the reason is absorption rather than likeness to a beis hakisei, as Perishah explains. Rather, he means that although the reason is likeness to a beis hakisei, metal and glass are nevertheless permitted because the Talmud specifically speaks of an earthenware chamber pot and urinal vessel. Were the reason simply that it is heavily soiled like excrement, no proof would be needed, for glass plainly does not absorb. Accordingly, Taz properly challenges the Rama: by remaining silent here, Rama accepted the lenient ruling, yet in siman 451:27 he writes that hagalah is ineffective for glass. Regarding metal, one can answer that there people sometimes pour boiling liquid into it. See Magen Avraham there, seif katan 50, and what I wrote in Peri Chadash to Yoreh Deah 105 on Taz.
