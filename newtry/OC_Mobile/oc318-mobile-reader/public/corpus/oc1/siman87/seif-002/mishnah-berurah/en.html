(7) There are those who permit—for it is like a covering.

(8) And there are those who forbid—for since the filth is absorbed within it, the vessel itself is like excrement and forbidden until he covers it. But of metal, even if not washed inside, overturning helps, for its prohibition is only from within, and overturning is considered a covering. And if he covered the chamberpot and urine receptacle and a bad smell wafts from them—whether of pottery and washed or of metal and not washed—the Chida wrote that one must distance four amot from where the smell ends, for this is called a bad smell that has a [fixed] source; and see what is written in Biur Halachah in siman 79 in the introduction, letter 7. And if it is of metal and washed, it suffices to distance until where the bad smell ends, for this is a bad smell that has no [fixed] source. The ShLAH wrote that a person should accustom himself to have a urine receptacle of metal or glass, and it should be washed and clean, and he should pour a revi'it of water into it when he urinates in it; and then it is permitted for him to bless "Who formed" and to study Torah beside it; and it seems to me that if it is clean and no bad smell comes out from it, then it suffices to wash it from one erev [Shabbos] to the next, end quote; and this is brought in Eliyah Rabbah.
