(8) Some prohibit. Since the filth is absorbed within it, the vessel itself is like excrement and remains prohibited until covered. A metal vessel, however, even if not washed inside, may be inverted, because its prohibition is only from its interior and inversion is considered covering. If one covered a chamber pot or urinal vessel and a foul odor emanates from it, whether it is earthenware and washed or metal and unwashed, Derech HaChaim writes that he must distance himself four amos from where the odor ceases, because this is a foul odor with a tangible source. See Biur Halachah, introduction to siman 79, paragraph 7. If it is metal and washed, it is enough to distance himself until the odor ceases, because this is an odor without a tangible source. Shelah writes that a person should accustom himself to have a metal or glass urinal vessel, keep it washed and clean, and add a reviis of water when urinating into it. He may then recite Asher Yatzar and study Torah near it. If it is clean and emits no foul odor, washing it from one Erev Shabbos to the next is sufficient. This is cited by Eliyah Rabbah.
