The Bach wrote: a bed ten [handbreadths] high with partitions reaching the ground, and a chamberpot behind the bed is considered a partition and permitted to recite, end quote; and it appears to me specifically when the bed interposes from wall to wall; but otherwise it is not considered a partition, for it is no better than a small and large courtyard in siman 79 seif 3, and so too in Turei Zahav that his main difficulty is on the question—see there—for he does not ask why the Rabbis disagree with Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel but necessarily that their reason is because it does not interpose from wall to wall it is not considered an interruption; and it is known that we rule like the Rabbis. If he placed them under the bed and the bed's partitions reach to less than three [tefachim] near the ground, it is permitted (Levush, Bach, Beit Yosef); and the matter is in siman 76 seif 2, and see what I wrote in siman 582 seif 61 whether we say lavud regarding a vessel—but here all agree it is as sealed; and therefore it seems to me similarly if he covered it with a vessel and there is a bit of air between the vessel and the ground, such as if it protrudes a bit from one side—we say lavud; but if the vessel is open on top, even less than three [tefachim] it is forbidden, for we require that it be covered.
