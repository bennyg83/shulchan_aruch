Bach writes that when a bed is ten tefachim high, its partitions reach the ground, and the chamber pot is behind it, the bed is considered a partition and one may recite. It appears to me that this applies only when the bed separates from wall to wall. Otherwise it is not a significant partition, no better than the smaller and larger courtyard discussed in siman 79:3. This is also implied by Rabbeinu Yonah, whose principal objection concerns the Gemara’s unresolved question; see there. He does not ask why the Sages disagree with Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel. It must be that their reason is that since the bed does not separate from wall to wall, it is not a significant division, and we rule like the Sages. If he placed them beneath the bed and its partitions reach to within less than three tefachim of the ground, it is permitted, as stated by Lechem Chamudos, Bach, and Beit Yosef. See siman 76:2 and what I wrote at the end of siman 502:1 regarding whether lavud applies to utensils. Here all agree that it is considered sealed. For the same reason, it appears to me that if he covered it with a vessel and there is a small gap between the vessel and the ground, such as where one side protrudes slightly, we apply lavud. If the vessel is open above, however, even when the opening is less than three tefachim, it is prohibited, because it must be covered.
