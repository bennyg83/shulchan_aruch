Taz: It appears these are the words of Bach — one who reads the obligation of the day may not read what is not relevant to the day; therefore on the fourth Rosh Chodesh he reads for the obligation of the day "uv'roshei chodsheichem." But one may say this is not so; rather his intent, may his memory be blessed, is that on Rosh Chodesh the Prophets' enactment is that they must read from the Torah — so it is that three begin from parashat HaTamid; and see Kesef Mishneh chapter 12 Laws of Temple Service. Unlike the enactment of a second scroll for Mussaf — a Geonic enactment — they did not wish to enact reading what is not relevant to the day. And Rosh Chodesh that falls on Shabbat — we read, and on Shabbat day also for Mussaf the maftir is relevant, as in siman 325; and see Levush brought by Nachalat Tzvi — also on Rosh Chodesh when there is in the parashah something fit to read, one person takes out and they begin from above, for we require four; unlike Shabbat when its matter has no three verses to take out a Torah scroll for the parashah, etc., see there.
