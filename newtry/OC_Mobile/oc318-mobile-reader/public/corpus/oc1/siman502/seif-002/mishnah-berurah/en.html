{19} A bundle, etc.; every piece of wood that the fire has not taken hold of, etc. But if the fire has taken hold of it, it is forbidden to remove it, for thereby one diminishes the fire of the entire bundle, since they are bound together; but if they are not bound together, even those that the fire has taken hold of, it is permitted to remove them. And Rama's view is not to distinguish between bound and unbound: for whenever they are in one mortar [hearth], if one takes one burning piece of wood from them there is a concern of extinguishing, for by separating it from the hearth its light may diminish; but this is not a psik reisha, and therefore it depends on his intent: if he draws it off in order that it go out, it is forbidden; and if he intends to place it on another side or in another hearth, it is permitted.
{20} And to place it on another side. And the same applies if he draws it off and takes it from there in order to give light before him: that too is permitted, for he does not intend to extinguish; and there are those who are stringent in this, and it is proper to be stringent. And one must warn the members of the household when they cook fish that after the cooking is finished they should not take the brands, but should leave them to burn out [Chayei Adam].
