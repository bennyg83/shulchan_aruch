Since one does not intend to extinguish. See what I wrote in siman 514, se'if 2, that this requires further study from that which appears here: that even if one intends to extinguish, sometimes it is permitted, such as when there is much fire and it harms the cooked dish and one needs to remove some from there; and this is what we find in siman 514, that it is permitted to extinguish for the need of food preparation. And Beis Yosef wrote in the name of Mordechai: if a person takes a brand from the hearth to give light at night, it is not comparable to drawing oil from a lamp; however, people are accustomed to be careful, and that is proper; end quote. And according to the reason here, that one does not intend to extinguish, it implies that the same applies if one places it on another hearth, that it is permitted.
