One may not take out, etc. See Mishnah Berurah regarding after the fact, that it is permitted to use them; so it is implied from Maggid Mishneh, and so wrote Radbaz in the name of Rashba, and so agreed the later authorities Bach, Magen Avraham, Eliyah Rabbah, and other later authorities, unlike Taz who is stringent in this; and all the more so one should not forbid the cooked dish that was cooked on this fire. And regarding the essence of the prohibition, whether it is according to the Torah or rabbinic: Taz holds that it is according to the Torah; and from the words of R' Ovadiah of Bartenura it is implied that it is rabbinic, for he wrote that it resembles a melacha; and see Responsa Ksav Sofer, siman 68, who elaborates on this.
