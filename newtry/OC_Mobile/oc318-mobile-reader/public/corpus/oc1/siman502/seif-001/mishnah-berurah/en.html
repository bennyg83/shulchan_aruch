{1} One may not take out, etc. Because in all of these one creates fire on Yom Tov; and since it is possible to produce fire from erev Yom Tov so that it be ready for him on Yom Tov, creating it on Yom Tov was not permitted; for it is no better than other preparatory acts that are possible to do while it is still day, which are forbidden.
{2} Not from wood, etc. For example, that they rub them against each other or strike them against each other until the fire comes out.
{3} And not from the earth. Hard ground, when one digs it, produces fire; and likewise in such a manner, when one rubs clods of earth against each other, they will produce fire.
{4} And not from water. That is, that they put water in a vessel of white glass and place it in the sun, and when the sun is very hot they bring near to it flax tow and it burns. And after the fact, if one transgressed and took [fire] out, it is permitted to use them. And see Responsa Ksav Sofer, siman 67, who forbids lighting a match (what they call Schwefelhölzchen) by thrusting it into hot stove ash that has no fire in it, only the heat of fire, for one is producing something new; and likewise, to rub the match on hot whitened iron, as in an iron oven, he also wrote that it does not appear to permit; but if there is a burning coal there, it is permitted to touch the match to it so that it kindle, even though there is no flame attached to the coal; and also from smoldering coals he tends there to permit.
{5} And one may not make charcoal. The reason is that it is a utensil for goldsmiths, and also that one is extinguishing.
{6} And one may not blow with a bellows. Because it resembles a craftsman's melacha; and this does not include blowing on the fire with a cloth or with a vessel made for that purpose from feathers, for that does not resemble a bellows at all [Chayei Adam].
{7} With a householder's bellows, etc. But with a large craftsman's bellows, even by means of a change it is forbidden.
{8} To cover the fire. That is, that one covers it at night so that the fire be preserved for him for tomorrow.
{9} With prepared earth. To exclude if it was heated with [fire] that day and it is cold, for it is muktzeh.
{10} If it does not extinguish it. That is to say, in a manner that it will not be a psik reisha, for then it is forbidden; and even though his intent in the extinguishing is for the need of food preparation for tomorrow, and extinguishing for the actual need of food preparation one should not forbid, for one may roast meat on coals: that case is different, for the extinguishing is at the time of preparing the food, which is not so here, where it is long beforehand. And there are later authorities who are lenient in every case, since in any event one does not intend to extinguish, but only that thereby one find fire tomorrow for the need of that day.
{11} For the need of the second day of Yom Tov. For example, on the first day of Yom Tov in the morning one covers so that it be ready for the evening at night: it is forbidden, because one may not trouble oneself from one Yom Tov to the other.
{12} One does not place this upon that, etc. That is, when one makes two rows on the two sides and places wood upon them from above, for then it looks like an ohel; but one row, to place this upon that, is permitted.
{13} Because it looks like building. Specifically if one also arranges the rows that are on the sides on Yom Tov; but if they were arranged while it was still day, it is permitted to place wood upon them from above.
{14} Upon the stones. That is, when one arranged them today; but if they were lying there from yesterday, it is permitted to place the pot upon them, as above.
{15} A row upon a row. And from the words of the later authorities it is implied that according to what Rama wrote later, that it is forbidden only when one needs the air beneath it, here too regarding eggs one should forbid only when one places one upon two and there is air enough to place fire between them in order to roast them all together.
{16} That has walls, etc. For otherwise there is no name of an ohel upon this without partitions.
{17} That reach the ground. And when it reaches less than three tefachim near the ground, Beis Yosef's view is that it is like lavud; and so Magen Avraham concludes to be stringent in this.
{18} And some say even if it reaches the ground, etc. See above in siman 315, se'if 3 and se'if 7, and in Mishnah Berurah there.
