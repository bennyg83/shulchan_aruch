Laws of fire on Yom Tov. 4 sections: One may not produce fire from wood, stones, earth, or water; one may not make charcoal; one may not blow with
bellows but only with a tube — and the custom has been to permit using household bellows with a change by inverting it from top to bottom. One who makes a bonfire on Yom Tov — when arranging the wood he does not place one piece on top of another until the arrangement is complete, since it appears like building; rather he either throws the wood in a jumbled manner or arranges it with a change. How so? He places a piece on top and then puts another beneath it and another beneath that until he reaches the ground. Similarly with a pot — he holds it and places the stones beneath it, but does not place it on top of the stones. Similarly with a bed — he holds the boards above and inserts the legs beneath them. Even eggs one should not stack row upon row like a tower, but should change and start from top to bottom. Similarly all such things require a change.Rema: It is permitted to cover a fire with a vessel or with prepared earth if one does not extinguish it, and specifically for the needs of the first day of Yom Tov — but for the needs of the second day it is forbidden. (Maharil) Rema: Similarly a table with sides that reach the ground (Tosafot) requires a change. But it is permitted to set up our ordinary table on its legs and there is no concern of building. Some say that even if it reaches the ground, as long as one does not need the airspace beneath it, it is permitted.
