One may not produce - For it was possible to produce it on the eve of Yom Tov. Raavad wrote that the reason is because he creates something new, and there is no preparation here, as stated in the Gemara. Maggid Mishneh wrote: this is not like an egg that was laid, which is forbidden, because this is not something fit in itself, but only to bake or cook with. It appears that if one did produce fire, it is permitted to use it, end quote.
From the earth - The morning vapors, as Ran explains.
From the water - They place a glass filled with water opposite the sun, put flax tow in it, and it catches fire, as Ran and Rashi explain.
Charcoal - Because he makes a tool for goldsmiths, as Rashi explains; Ran explained because he extinguishes.
If he does not extinguish it - Yam Shel Shlomo wrote that even if he extinguishes it, it is an unintended act and also a melachah she'einah tzrichah legufah, end quote. See Tosafot 22 and siman 507 seif 4, and what I wrote at the end of siman 509.
For the need of the second Yom Tov - For example, in the morning he covers it so it will be prepared for the evening; see the beginning of siman 503.
For it appears like building - Specifically when he arranges four rows of wood like the four walls of a box and afterward places wood on top of them. But otherwise no change is required, as Tur and Rosh write, and so is Rambam's view according to Beit Yosef and Kesef Mishneh. Nevertheless, when placing two stones and wood upon them, he must hold the wood in his hand and place the stones beneath it, as stated regarding a pot.
Row upon row - Kesef Mishneh asked: he does not need the air beneath it, and he did not mention what was written.
That reach the ground - Beit Yosef wrote that when it reaches within less than three tefachim of the ground, it is considered lavud. This does not appear correct, for we do not say lavud to be stringent, as Tur wrote at the end of siman 632 and Rosh there, and so appears in the Gemara in the first chapter of Sukkah; this is Darkei Moshe's language. So too it is written in Yoreh De'ah siman 371 at the end of seif 4 in Hagahot Ashiri, taken from Mahariv, but that reason, that we do not say lavud to be stringent, is not mentioned there; Rama wrote it on his own. To me this requires study. On the contrary, in Sukkah 18 we say the laws of impurity are different because they are a received halachah, and therefore we do not say lavud. So wrote Tosafot in Eruvin 10, and they wrote there that nevertheless the empty space joins to the width of a tefach. We see that there is lavud to be stringent. However, what they wrote there, that this is why nearby it requires two fingerbreadths on one side, two fingerbreadths on the other, and a fingerbreadth of empty space in the middle, which makes a tefach, and if we said lavud then any amount on one side and any amount on the other would suffice, end quote; and on 102 they wrote: it does not seem reasonable that in such a case it be considered making a tent so as to forbid doing it on Shabbat, for would it be forbidden to stretch two strings within three tefachim on Shabbat because of making a tent? end quote. Thus even where we say lavud, we do not say it becomes a tent. One can say that in truth it is a tent regarding Shabbat, since afterward one may spread a sheet over them, as stated there, but nevertheless it is permitted to stretch it on Shabbat because he himself does nothing. Proof: two fingerbreadths on each side, etc., is a tent even regarding impurity, and nevertheless it is permitted to make it on Shabbat, as written regarding a bridal canopy. But a partition seven tefachim and something high within less than three tefachim of the ground is a proper partition, and it is forbidden to place the table upon it. It also appears to me that it is forbidden to set up a partition seven tefachim and something high within less than three of the ground if he comes to permit something thereby, as written in siman 315; for according to Rabbi Yehudah it is forbidden to set up a suspended partition, as stated in Sukkah 16b, and it also appears in Eruvin 9b that we say lavud to be stringent regarding lechis, see there. It appears to me that our case of a suspended table depends on great authorities who disagree whether we say lavud with utensils. In Shabbat 8, Rashi explained that even for a utensil not four by four wide we say lavud, except for something whose partitions are made to place things inside it, such as a beehive; and so wrote Ba'al HaMaor. Tosafot wrote that lavud applies only where the partitions are seven tefachim and something high without the base and four wide; otherwise it is like other objects, and we do not say lavud regarding them. Ramban in Milchamot wrote that we do not say lavud to make a utensil into a domain, for we do not find in the Talmud lavud and gud with utensils; rather the reason with a beehive is that when it reaches within less than three of the ground, it is considered as resting, end quote. This requires study, for Shabbat 101 implies that if one inserted a reed with a basket at its top and there are not three tefachim from it to the ground, we say lavud; and so it appears in Eruvin 33 regarding a basket, and so Tur wrote in siman 87 that we say lavud with a bed. Therefore it appears to me to be stringent. What Tur wrote in siman 632 should be explained as written there.
