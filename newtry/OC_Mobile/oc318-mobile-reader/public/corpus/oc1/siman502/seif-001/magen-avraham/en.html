(א) One may not produce. For it is possible to generate from erev, and Ra'avad wrote the reason is because he generates and there is no preparation here as in the Gemara; Maggid Mishneh wrote it is not like an egg that was born which is forbidden because this is not a thing fit by itself but only to bake or cook in it; it appears if he generated fire it is permitted to use — end quote.

(ב) From the dust. The cattle (R)

(ג) From the water. A glass of water is filled with water against the sun and gives it a range of flaws and fuels

(ד) Charcoal — because one makes a vessel for goldsmiths (Rashi), and Ran explained because it extinguishes.

(ה) If he does not extinguish it. Yeshuos Shabbos wrote even if he extinguishes it is davar she'eino mitkaven and also melacha she'einah tzricha l'gufah — end quote; see Tosafot 22 and siman 507 seif 4 and what is written siman 509.

(ז) It appears like building. Specifically when he arranges four rows of wood like the four walls of the ark and then places wood on them; otherwise no change is needed (Tur, Rosh, and so wrote Rambam, Beit Yosef, Kesef Mishneh). Nevertheless when placing two stones and wood on them he must hold the wood in his hand and place the stones beneath, as stated regarding a pot.

(ח) Row upon row. Kesef Mishneh challenged: behold air beneath is not needed; he did not mention what was written.

(ט) That reach the ground. Beit Yosef wrote: when less than three handbreadths reach the ground it is considered lavud and it does not appear we say lavud to be stringent, as Tur wrote siman 372 and Rosh there, and so in the Gemara first chapter of Sukkah — end Darkei Moshe. And similarly Yoreh De'ah siman 371 seif 4 in Hagahot Eshkol, taken from Mahariv, but the reason is not mentioned there that lavud is only to be lenient — Rama wrote it from his own reasoning, and I have a question, for on the contrary in Sukkah 18 they say this case is different in laws of impurity that the halachah was learned and therefore we do not say lavud, and so Tosafot in Eruvin 10 wrote, and they wrote there that nevertheless the hollow combines to a handbreadth width — from which we see lavud applies to be stringent; however what they wrote there, and similarly nearby, requires fingers from here and fingers from here and a finger hollow in the middle making a handbreadth, and if we say lavud, a slight amount from here and a slight amount from here suffices — end quote.
