(Magen Avraham, s.k. 9) And to me this requires further study, for on the contrary, etc. Marginal note: As to why he does not ask on the Rosh: because he explained later in siman 632 that the Rosh's intent is that certainly we say lavud even to be stringent, for it is considered as if the two are adjacent to each other; nevertheless we do not say lavud to be stringent so that invalid sechach mixed in would block [valid sechach]; see there. And I find this very difficult, for before that the Rosh is uncertain whether if there is invalid sechach of two tefachim on this side and on that side, with open space of less than three intervening, the invalid sechach combines or not; and his uncertainty is difficult, for obviously they combine, for we say lavud; rather it must be that the Rosh and the Tur hold that even to consider the two as adjacent to each other by means of lavud, we do not say lavud to be stringent. But it appears to resolve Maggid Mishneh's difficulty: that therefore the Talmud did not reject [the comparison] by saying that case is different because we do not say lavud to be stringent, because even so it would be proved from the latter clause there that we do not say lavud in the middle to be lenient; for it was taught there: if part of the tumah is in the house and part opposite the skylight, the house is tamei and opposite the tumah it is tamei; why should we not say lavud, so that what is above would be tahor opposite the tumah? Therefore it rejects [by saying] tumah is different. And further it appears that certainly from the side we say lavud even to be stringent; only in the middle the Rosh ruled that we do not say lavud to be stringent; for when we say lavud in the middle, that is because we say the law follows Ravina to be lenient, which is not so if the lavud is to be stringent, [then] the law follows Rav Acha, who is lenient in such a case. And by chance I found so in Sefer Me'iras Einayim, siman 350; see there. And all this is according to us; but according to the one who holds that we say lavud in the middle, according to him we say lavud whether to be lenient or to be stringent; and the one who holds that we do not say lavud likewise does not distinguish between leniency and stringency; and therefore the Talmud rejects [by saying] tumah is different, for it is a transmitted law. And with this Magen Avraham's difficulty from what is proved in Eruvin, folio 9, regarding side-posts, that we come to the dispute of Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel and the Rabbis, [is removed:] that we say lavud to be stringent according to Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, that is lavud from the side. And from this is proved like the words of Beis Yosef: when the table reaches near the ground; and Darkei Moshe was not correct in challenging him, for from the side it is proved that we say lavud even to be stringent; and with this the complaint of Responsa Chacham Tzvi, siman 59, is removed. And this is his language: and not because I am concerned with what Rama wrote in Yoreh Deah, siman 371, that we do not say lavud to be stringent; for with apology to his honor, a clear Talmud escaped him, in Eruvin, folio 25: if one distanced [the partition] from the wall by less than three, he has accomplished nothing; and Rashi explained that it is like a partition upon a partition; thus we say lavud even to be stringent; see there. And I say: why did he leave aside that of Eruvin, folio 9, regarding side-posts, and not challenge from it? Because one may say as I wrote, that from the side we say lavud even to be stringent, but in the middle we do not say so. If so, also regarding making panels, since it is from the side, we say lavud even to be stringent; and this is easy.
