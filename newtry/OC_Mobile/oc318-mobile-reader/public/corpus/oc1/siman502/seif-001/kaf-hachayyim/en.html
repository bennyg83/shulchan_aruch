1) [Se'if 1] Fire may not be produced, etc. The reason is that one thereby creates something new on Yom Tov. Beitzah 33b. Rambam writes at the beginning of chapter 4: 'Only kindling from an existing flame was permitted on Yom Tov. Producing fire is forbidden, since it could have been produced before Yom Tov.' These are his words. Maggid Mishneh writes there that if one produced fire, he may use it. Beis Yosef and Bach cite him, as do Olas Shabbos, subsection 1, and Magen Avraham, subsection 1. Taz, however, disagrees. He writes that initially it is certainly forbidden to cook with that fire, and even if one transgressed and cooked, the cooked food is forbidden whenever a Jew produced the fire in violation of the law. Its status is like food cooked on Shabbos. See there. Pri Chadash likewise writes regarding the aforementioned statement of Maggid Mishneh that it is not conclusive. Levush, however, follows Maggid Mishneh and writes that if one transgressed and produced fire, he may use it. Derishah, subsection 1, and Ateres Zekeinim write likewise. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 3, rejects all the aforementioned arguments of Taz, and Birkei Yosef, subsection 2, and Bigdei Yesha agree with him. Maamar Mordechai, Nehar Shalom, subsection 1, and Chemed Moshe, subsection 2, also reject Taz's words. See there. See Radbaz, volume 2, in Leshonos HaRambam, at the end of responsum 150. He writes that Rashba explicitly permits it and that the reason of Maggid Mishneh is correct. See there. Nehar Shalom there cites him. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1; Chayei Adam, rule 95, subsection 12; Mikraei Kodesh, siman 2, subsection 1; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 4, rule likewise.
2) There. Fire may not be produced, etc. The reason is that fire could have been produced before Yom Tov. One authority infers from this that if a person could not produce fire before Yom Tov, such as where he was imprisoned or in a wilderness and did not obtain the means to produce it until Yom Tov, he may produce it on Yom Tov. This also appears to be the view of the author of Tosafos. Sefer Beis Moed. Rabbi Avraham Azulai cites it in his handwritten glosses. Birkei Yosef, subsection 1; Zechor LeAvraham, subsection 10; Mikraei Kodesh there, Likutei Rima, subsection 2, cite it. Pri HaShulchan, however, writes regarding the aforementioned words of Birkei Yosef that the unqualified rulings of the halachic authorities do not imply this. Rather, creating something new resembles a melachah and was not permitted on Yom Tov. These are his words. Since the matter is disputed, it is possible that in such a case, when one cannot obtain fire from others on Yom Tov, one may be lenient and produce fire from the small sulfur-tipped sticks discussed below, subsection 6. See there.
3) There. Neither from wood nor from stones. For example, one rubs them together or strikes them against one another until fire emerges. Rambam, at the beginning of chapter 4; Tosafos Yom Tov, chapter 4 of Beitzah, mishnah 7. This means that cotton or flax tow is placed upon them. Striking produces sparks of fire, which ignite that material.
4) There. Nor from earth. This refers to hard ground that emits fire when dug, similar to our gargishta from the place where it is quarried. Rashi, Beitzah 33a. Ran explains: 'Dung of cattle or sheep is buried in the earth, and when it decomposes, fire emerges from it.' These are his words. This refers to material that emits sparks, beside which some soft substance is placed so that it catches fire from them.
