1) [Se'if 1] Fire may not be produced, etc. The reason is that one thereby creates something new on Yom Tov. Beitzah 33b. Rambam writes at the beginning of chapter 4: 'Only kindling from an existing flame was permitted on Yom Tov. Producing fire is forbidden, since it could have been produced before Yom Tov.' These are his words. Maggid Mishneh writes there that if one produced fire, he may use it. Beis Yosef and Bach cite him, as do Olas Shabbos, subsection 1, and Magen Avraham, subsection 1. Taz, however, disagrees. He writes that initially it is certainly forbidden to cook with that fire, and even if one transgressed and cooked, the cooked food is forbidden whenever a Jew produced the fire in violation of the law. Its status is like food cooked on Shabbos. See there. Pri Chadash likewise writes regarding the aforementioned statement of Maggid Mishneh that it is not conclusive. Levush, however, follows Maggid Mishneh and writes that if one transgressed and produced fire, he may use it. Derishah, subsection 1, and Ateres Zekeinim write likewise. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 3, rejects all the aforementioned arguments of Taz, and Birkei Yosef, subsection 2, and Bigdei Yesha agree with him. Maamar Mordechai, Nehar Shalom, subsection 1, and Chemed Moshe, subsection 2, also reject Taz's words. See there. See Radbaz, volume 2, in Leshonos HaRambam, at the end of responsum 150. He writes that Rashba explicitly permits it and that the reason of Maggid Mishneh is correct. See there. Nehar Shalom there cites him. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1; Chayei Adam, rule 95, subsection 12; Mikraei Kodesh, siman 2, subsection 1; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 4, rule likewise.
