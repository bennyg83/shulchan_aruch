One may not take out, etc. The Gemara explains the reason: because one creates [fire] on Yom Tov. And in Rambam, chapter 4, it is written that on Yom Tov only kindling from an existing fire was permitted, but to produce fire is forbidden, for it is possible to produce it from the eve [of Yom Tov]; end quote. And Raavad wrote that he should have said the reason is because one creates [something newly], and there is no preparation here, and that is the reason explained in the Gemara. And Maggid Mishneh wrote upon him: I do not see that he wished to challenge, for this is not like an egg that was born, which is forbidden, because this is not something fit in itself, but only to cook or bake with it; and it appears that if one took out [fire], it is permitted to use it, and they forbade it only because one should not have created it since it is possible from the eve; end quote. His intent appears to be that Rambam came only to instruct that this is not food preparation itself but preparatory acts for food needs, and one should have created this on the eve, and all preparatory acts are forbidden if they are possible on the eve; and accordingly it is easy: one who takes out fire in order to warm himself has the law of food needs, as Ran wrote, as written in siman 501: whatever one benefits from its body itself, such as fire to warm oneself, has the law of food needs and not of preparatory acts. And if you say, here too: but we learned a nonspecific mishnah, "One may not take out fire, etc."; and it appears to me that Rambam's intent is that even though it resembles food needs, there is no permission, for the Torah permitted food needs only to prepare what already exists in the world, only that it was not fit to eat without preparation, such as cooking and baking; but to produce and create so that there be food needs, that is, something from nothing, this the Torah did not permit. But then it is difficult: how then did the Torah permit food needs, since it is impossible without fire? To this Rambam answered: for it is possible from the eve; and specifically with that did they permit food needs. And regarding what Maggid Mishneh wrote, that if one took out fire it is permitted to use it, I did not reach the depth of his mind; for in any case it is a preparatory act for food needs and does not override Yom Tov according to the Torah, as they expound "it" and not its preparatory acts, as stated in siman 509; if so, its law is like one who tithes his produce on Shabbos intentionally, or cooks, as stated in chapter 2 of Beitza, folio 17: with a Shabbos prohibition, even after the fact it is forbidden, except regarding tevel, which is rabbinic, where they were lenient after the fact; and even so it teaches that something according to the Torah is forbidden even after the fact; and there is no difference between Yom Tov and Shabbos except food needs, not their preparatory acts that are forbidden; and it is exactly like Shabbos. Also from the words of Rambam it appears to me toward prohibition, for he wrote that producing fire was not permitted at all; implying that it is like Shabbos, since it was not permitted, and he means according to the Torah. And if from the language "One may not take out" you wish to learn permission if it is after the fact, as my master my father-in-law of blessed memory wrote, there is no proof from this; for we say "One may not bake from Yom Tov for Shabbos," and nevertheless it is a question for us in chapter 2 of Beitza there: if one transgressed and baked, what [is the law]? Therefore it appears to me that not only is it forbidden l'chatchila to cook with that fire, but even if one transgressed and cooked, the cooked dish is forbidden whenever a Jew took out fire in prohibition; and its law is like one who cooks on Shabbos.
