(א) One may not produce, etc. The Gemara explains the reason is because he generates on Yom Tov; Rambam chapter 4 wrote only kindling from existing fire was permitted on Yom Tov but generating fire is forbidden since possible from erev — end quote. Ra'avad wrote the reason should be because he generates and there is no preparation, and that is the reason explicit in the Gemara; Maggid Mishneh wrote on this: I do not see he wished to grasp, for this is not like an egg that was born which is forbidden because this is not fit by itself but to cook or bake in it; it appears if he generated fire it is permitted to use and they forbade only because he should not generate since possible from erev — end quote; his intention appears Rambam comes only to teach this is not ochel nefesh but machshir ochel nefesh and he should have generated this erev; all machshirim are forbidden if possible erev — resolve clearly. Therefore for one who generates fire to warm in it it would be ochel nefesh law, as Ran wrote as in siman 501: anything one benefits from its body such as fire to warm in it is ochel nefesh not machshirim; you could say so here too, yet we learned stam one may not produce fire, etc.; it appears Rambam's intention: although similar to ochel nefesh there is no permission because Torah permitted ochel nefesh only to fix what already exists in the world, not fit to eat without fixing like cooking and baking; but to generate and produce ochel nefesh from nothing Torah did not permit; therefore he challenged: how did Torah permit ochel nefesh since impossible without fire? Rambam answered: behold possible from erev, and only thereby they permitted ochel nefesh. In what Maggid Mishneh wrote that if he generated fire it is permitted to use — I did not grasp his end mind, for in any case it is machshir ochel nefesh and does not override Yom Tov from Torah as we expound, and not machshirim as in siman 509; if so its law is like one who tithed produce intentionally on Shabbat or cooked — Beitzah 17: for Shabbat prohibition even b'dieved forbidden except for a Torah matter which is d'rabbanan they were lenient b'dieved; nevertheless we learn that d'oraisa matters are forbidden even b'dieved; Yom Tov differs from Shabbat only in ochel nefesh not machshirim forbidden — it would be literally like Shabbat; also from Rambam's words it appears forbidden, for he wrote generating fire was not permitted at all — implies like Shabbat since not permitted it is removed from Torah; if from words one may not produce you wish to learn permission b'dieved as my teacher zl wrote — there is no proof, for we say one may not bake from Yom Tov to Shabbat and nevertheless Beitzah chapter 2 asks if he transgressed and baked what — end quote; it appears not required l'chatchila forbidden to cook in that fire, but even if he transgressed and cooked the food is forbidden whenever Israel generated fire in prohibition, law like cooking on Shabbat.
