{21} With a log. For wood stands for kindling and not for anything else; therefore even with a dry log that is prepared for kindling it is forbidden to move it for the need of something else, and for that [other use] it is included in muktzeh. And the view of several later authorities is that the Mechaber follows his ruling in siman 495, se'if 4, that muktzeh is forbidden on Yom Tov; but according to the poskim who permit muktzeh on Yom Tov, it is permitted to support with a dry log that is fit for kindling; but with a moist [log], since it is not fit for kindling, it is forbidden, for its law is like stones. And Beis Meir wrote that in constrained circumstances one may rely to be lenient with a dry log.
{22} To roast on it. That is, that one thrusts the meat upon it for roasting; and specifically with dry wood that is fit for kindling, and for the reason that we say, what difference is it to me to roast on it versus to roast on coals; but with moist wood it is forbidden to roast meat. And specifically if it is fit to roast upon as is, without fixing; but to fix it on Yom Tov so that it be fit to roast upon is forbidden, as below in siman 509.
{23} Even though it is not fit, etc. That is to say, and for this reason it is forbidden to use it for any purpose, as above; nevertheless, to kindle it together with other dry wood is permitted. And although this law deals with moist [wood], and the law of roasting deals specifically with dry, as above, nevertheless an a fortiori applies here: it is more reasonable to permit moist [wood] in a large fire of other dry wood than to permit dry [wood] for roasting upon it.
