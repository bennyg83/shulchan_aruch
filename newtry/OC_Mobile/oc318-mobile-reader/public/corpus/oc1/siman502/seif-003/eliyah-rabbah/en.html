[3] [Levush] It is permitted to use it, etc. The Taz wondered at Maggid Mishneh and Shulchan Aruch: since this is a preparatory act for food needs that does not override Yom Tov, its law should be like one who intentionally tithes his produce on Shabbat or cooks, where even after the fact it is forbidden, for the only difference between Yom Tov and Shabbat is food preparation. End quote. In my humble opinion this is not difficult at all, for "the only difference..." refers to what is permitted initially, not to one who transgressed, as Tosafot and Rashi wrote in Megillah 9. He also overlooked what Beit Yosef wrote at the end of siman 503 in the name of Rashba, that only with Shabbat prohibitions do we forbid the result for intentional transgressors, namely tithing and immersing even though they are rabbinic, but not on Yom Tov at all. And what he asked about the Bach's inference, that the Tur wrote generally "one may not remove" and did not write "it is forbidden," implying that it is permitted; but we say "one may not bake from Yom Tov for Shabbat," and nevertheless the Talmud asks: if one transgressed and baked, what is the law? End quote. This is not difficult at all, for Yam Shel Shlomo in siman 503 resolves that it is permitted; and in any case, here the Tur should have explained that it is forbidden.
