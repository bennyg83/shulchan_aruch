[3] [Levush] it is permitted to use, etc. Taz wondered on the Maggid and Shulchan Aruch, since he is a machshir ochel nefesh that does not override Yom Tov — its law is like one who tithed his produce intentionally on Shabbat or cooked, for even b'dieved it is forbidden; there is no difference between Yom Tov and Shabbat except ochel nefesh alone — until here. In my humble opinion this is not difficult at all, for "there is no difference" refers to l'chatchila not to transgression, as Tosafot and Rashi wrote in Megillah 9; also it escaped him what Beit Yosef wrote at end of siman 503 in name of Rashba that specifically for a Shabbat prohibition they forbade intentional action (namely tithe and immerse though d'rabbanan), but on Yom Tov not at all; also what he challenged on what Bach emphasized that Tur wrote stam one may not produce and did not write it is forbidden — implying permitted — we say one may not bake from Yom Tov to Shabbat and nevertheless the Gemara asks if he transgressed and baked what — until here, not difficult at all, for it is simple that it is permitted in Yam Shel Shlomo siman 503; in any case Tur should have explained here that it is forbidden.
