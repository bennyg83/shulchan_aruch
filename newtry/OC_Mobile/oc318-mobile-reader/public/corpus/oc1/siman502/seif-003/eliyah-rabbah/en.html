[3] [Levush] It is permitted to use, etc. Taz wondered at Maggid Mishneh and Shulchan Aruch: since this is a preparatory act for food needs that does not override Yom Tov, its law should be like one who intentionally tithes his produce on Shabbos or cooks, where even after the fact it is forbidden; for there is no difference between Yom Tov and Shabbos except food preparation alone; end quote. In my humble opinion this is not difficult at all, for "there is no difference..." refers to what is [addressed] l'chatchila, not to one who already transgressed, as Tosafos and Rashi wrote in Megillah, folio 9. He also overlooked what Beis Yosef wrote at the end of siman 503 in the name of Rashba, that specifically with a Shabbos prohibition we forbid [the benefit] for their intentional transgressor (namely, one who tithes or immerses, even though it is rabbinic), but on Yom Tov not at all. And what he asked about Bach's inference, that the Tur wrote generally "one may not take out" and did not write that it is forbidden, implying that it is permitted: but we say "one may not bake from Yom Tov for Shabbos," and nevertheless the Gemara asks: if one transgressed and baked, what [is the law]? End quote. This is not difficult at all, for Yam Shel Shlomo in siman 503 resolves that it is permitted; and in any case, here the Tur should have explained that it is forbidden.
