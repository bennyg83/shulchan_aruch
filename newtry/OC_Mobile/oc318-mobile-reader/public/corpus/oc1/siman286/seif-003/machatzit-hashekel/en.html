S.k. 3: Of, etc. As stated there, ‘if he wishes to eat.’ This means as ruled above in siman 233: if he wishes to eat a meal that is prohibited before the time of Mincha Gedolah, he may pray Mincha Gedolah; see Magen Avraham there. The same applies here, even according to the first opinion, which maintains that he must first pray Mincha even if he does not wish to eat.
Nevertheless, since he must pray Mussaf and may not pray Mussaf until he first prays Mincha, he is therefore permitted to pray Mincha Gedolah.
And if so, nowadays, etc. This begins a new seif katan and refers to the Mechaber’s words, ‘This applies specifically, etc., when he wishes to eat and is prohibited, etc.’ Magen Avraham comments on this: If so, according to our practice of relying on a public announcement, etc., as stated above in siman 292; and one is therefore permitted to eat before Mincha; see Magen Avraham there, s.k. 15; then according to the second opinion here, even if he wishes to eat, he nevertheless prays Mussaf first, before Mincha.
And it must be explained, etc. For Mincha can be made up, etc. By chance I saw in Tzelach on Berachos, by our master Rabbi Yechezkel, of blessed memory, Av Beis Din of the holy community of Prague, that he wrote that Magen Avraham overlooked the words of the Jerusalem Talmud, where the opposite of his position is explained: when there is insufficient time, it is even more evident that Mincha takes precedence; see there. The Jerusalem Talmud is not available to me for examination.
