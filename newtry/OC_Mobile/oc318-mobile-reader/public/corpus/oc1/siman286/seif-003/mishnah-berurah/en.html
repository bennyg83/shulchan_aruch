(7) Eating fruit - Although after Shacharit he has already become obligated in kiddush and may not eat or drink beforehand, and kiddush must be in the place of a meal, one may say that it suffices for him to drink a cup of wine after kiddush, for this too is called a meal, as above in siman 273; and afterward he may eat the fruit. Or, when he does not have so much wine, one may rely on the poskim who hold that it suffices for him to drink the entire kiddush cup, which holds a revi'it, for this is also called kiddush in the place of a meal.
(8) A little bread - Meaning a kebeitzah, for all this is included in incidental eating; all the more so if he eats types of targima from the five species, which is permitted.
(9) To sustain the heart - Meaning that he eats many fruits, for eating fruit is not called fixed eating. It appears that if his heart is weak, he may also eat bread until his mind is settled, even if it is more than a kebeitzah. Eliyah Rabbah wrote, and it is brought in Shaarei Teshuvah, that if his heart is weak and he has no wine or other item on which to make kiddush, he may eat fruit, and the same applies to types of targima from the five species, before Mussaf even without kiddush; but otherwise one should not be lenient.
