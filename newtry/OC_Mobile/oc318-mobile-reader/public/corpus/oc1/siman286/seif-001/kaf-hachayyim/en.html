1) [Seif 1] The time for the Mussaf prayer, etc. In Sephardic communities the custom was to say LeMoshe Tzivisa, while in Ashkenazic communities the custom is to say Tikanta Shabbos, arranged according to the reverse alphabet from tav to alef. This alludes to redemption, as the verse states, 'I will whistle to them and gather them.' From Tikanta through meSinai it contains twenty-one words, corresponding to the passages VeVayom HaShabbos and Olas Shabbos, which contain twenty-one words. Tur. Shaar HaKavanos, folio 73d, likewise writes that one must follow the Ashkenazic custom and recite Tikanta Shabbos, etc. At the end, the five final letters are added in the words meSinai nitztavu tzivui pe'aleha kara'ui, followed by, 'May it be Your will, Hashem my God and God of my forefathers, that You bring us up,' etc. These embody the mystical illumination of the twenty-two letters in the supernal mind, from below upward, according to the mystical meaning of the statement in Idra Naso: 'The letter tav inscribes a mark for the Ancient One that has no equal,' as explained by us in its place. These are his words. Sephardic communities likewise now recite Tikanta, because the Arizal instructed this and gave an esoteric reason. Although Pri Etz Chaim, gate 18, chapter 20, writes that one should say Tikanta with the letter kuf, we possess only what Shaar HaKavanos records, arranged by Rabbi Shmuel Vital in the name of his father, Rabbi Chaim Vital. There it is written Tikanta with the letter kaf, as stated above, and this is the common practice.
