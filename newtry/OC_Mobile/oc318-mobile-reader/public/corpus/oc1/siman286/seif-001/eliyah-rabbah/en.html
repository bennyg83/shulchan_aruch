[1] [Levush] And the sanctity of the day, etc. If he did not recite the order of the offerings in the Mussaf prayer, he must repeat the prayer, even if he did not say "as stated," because this is required for the day, as stated in the responsa of Maharam of Rothenburg (Malbushei Yom Tov). This requires study in light of siman 268, seif 4.
[2] [Levush] The expression "arranged," etc. In Tanya it is explained as an expression of clarification, as in, "and He weighs the hearts." One should say "and the Mussaf offering, etc.," and likewise in every Mussaf prayer (Maharshal, siman 64). In Shacharit we say "You did not give it and You did not bequeath it" in the masculine form because it refers back to "and on the seventh day," and "day" is masculine. In Mussaf, however, it refers to the arrangement of Shabbos, and "Shabbos" is feminine, so we say "its offerings" and "its explanations." I heard this from my teacher, Rabbi Helman of blessed memory, who said it in the name of his father in law. See siman 292, seif katan 40, where Shabbos is also treated as masculine.
[3] [Levush] Companions, etc. Meaning that in the future the Holy One, blessed is He, will make a feast for the righteous from it.
[4] [Levush] Shema, etc. "Hashem is One" follows "He is our God," and one should not say "One" twice. Our custom, however, is to say "One" twice (Magen Avraham). See Sefer HaManhig, siman 44. Abudraham writes that for this reason we explain, "He is our God." See siman 61, seif 9.
[5] [Levush] And the reason, etc. Sefer HaManhig writes: Perhaps on Shabbos and Yom Tov, when they prolonged the prayer, they became aware of this, analogous to the shofar blasts in Mussaf. See siman 621 regarding the reason. In Maharil, Hilchot Yom Kippur, the initial letters of "ein mi nodeh" spell amen, followed by "Baruch Atah," so it is as though one said "Baruch Atah El Melech Ne'eman."
