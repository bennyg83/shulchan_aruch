Se'if 1 A sukkah beneath an upper sukkah: the upper one is valid and the lower one is invalid, etc. Note: The source of this law is in the first chapter of Sukkah, in the Mishnah. We read in the Yerushalmi, first chapter, halacha 3: Rabbi Yehudah says, if there are no residents in the upper one, the lower one is valid. Does residents mean actual residents, or even fit for residents? One Tanna recited before Rabbi Zeira: and even the lower one is valid. No person says even unless he agrees about the first case. This teaches that it means actual residents. This is the Yerushalmi's wording. The Korban HaEdah explained that if the upper one is not fit for dwelling, how is the upper one valid? See there. This is difficult for me. What proof is there? We rule that if one supported the s'chach on a tree, one may not ascend to it on Yom Tov because he is using the tree, and nevertheless if he transgressed and ascended, he fulfilled his obligation, as written in the Ateret Zekenim here in this siman. Rabbi Yehudah follows his own view, for he holds that if one made it on top of a camel it is invalid because it is not fit for seven days; he follows the rabbinic restriction. If so, here too let us say that Rabbi Yehudah's intent is that when it is not fit for dwelling, the lower one is valid. And what is implied, that even the upper one is valid, can be found in a case where it is not fit for dwelling, such as where the s'chach of the lower sukkah is supported on a tree and is the floor of the upper one. If so, on Shabbos it is not fit for the upper one's dwelling, since one may not ascend to it on Yom Tov or Shabbos. Therefore the lower one is valid, for rabbinically the upper one is not fit for dwelling. Nevertheless, it is still possible to say that the upper one too is valid, for if he transgressed and ascended he fulfilled his obligation. Therefore the word even is used properly. What proof is there? This requires great examination. I have another difficulty: what proof is there? We rule that a sukkah invalid because of make it, and not from what was already made, such as one who hollows out a stack to make a sukkah, is invalid; and likewise with one who placed s'chach over a roof and removed that roof, as the Magen Avraham writes in siman 626. But s'chach higher than twenty is not considered make it and not from what was already made, as the poskim write. Necessarily the reason for the distinction is that where it was not called s'chach at all, such as hollowing out a stack, it is considered make it and not from what was already made. But if it was called s'chach, make it and not from what was already made does not apply, since it was called s'chach from the start, and also the invalidity is not in the s'chach itself but because of another factor, excluding s'chach made of attached material, etc. If so, let us say that Rabbi Yehudah's intent is as follows: certainly as long as both layers of s'chach remain, one can say it is invalid even if there are residents in the upper one, because in the end there are two layers of s'chach. Rabbi Yehudah speaks where the upper one is removed. Then, if there are no residents in the upper one, the lower layer had the name of s'chach upon it, and therefore, although there had been two layers of s'chach, if the upper one is removed the lower one is valid, with no concern of make it and not from what was already made. But if there are residents in the upper one, the lower one is like one who placed s'chach under a roof, like one who hollows out a stack, and like one who placed s'chach inside a house; therefore even if the upper one is removed, the lower one is invalid because of make it and not from what was already made. According to this, if this is the intent, one can still say that Rabbi Yehudah refers to not fit for dwelling. And when he says even the lower one, it means not only that if the lower one were removed it would certainly be permitted to sit below in the lower area by means of the upper s'chach, and this would not be considered make it and not from what was already made, but even in the reverse case, where the upper one was removed and the lower one remained, it too is valid and has no concern of make it and not from what was already made. What proof is this? This requires examination according to the view of Mahari Weil cited by the Magen Avraham in siman 627. But according to the Rema's view it works well, for necessarily Rabbi Yehudah speaks where the upper one is not removed; if it were removed, then even where there are residents in the upper one, the lower one would be valid, no worse than one who placed s'chach under a roof. Consider this carefully. I returned and saw that the same difficulty applies to our Talmud as well, on page 10, where it says: what is not fit for dwelling, etc. Why not say that it speaks where he made it on top of a tree and the like, so that it is not fit for dwelling, and Rabbi Yehudah follows his view while the first Tanna follows his view, for the first Tanna follows the Torah-level status? The second question is likewise difficult; consider it. Now Tosafot's view too is that what is above twenty is not called invalid s'chach. But Rashi's view is that it is invalid s'chach so as to invalidate what is below it opposite it. In my humble opinion there is no dispute here. Rabbeinu Tam proved his view from Eruvin, where part of the s'chach is within twenty, etc. It appears to me to reconcile Rashi's text and view, and to reconcile the sugyot so they do not contradict one another. The reason that what is above twenty does not invalidate what is opposite it and is not called invalid s'chach is because he has the ability to reduce it and lower it, and it itself would become valid. We rule in Kiddushin that anything in one's power is not considered lacking time. It is not comparable to attached material, even though he also has power to cut it, because one can say this resembles what the Talmud answers in Kiddushin chapter 3 regarding one who says to his maidservant, etc.: there, at first she was like an animal, and now another person's consent transfers her. This proves that where there is a change in the body of the thing, the fact that it is in his power does not help. So too here: with attached material, if he detaches it, there will be a change in its body, for at first it was attached and now it is detached, so being in his power does not help. But with s'chach higher than twenty, if he lowers it there is no change in its body, so the fact that he can reduce it helps. This is fine on a weekday, but on Yom Tov he cannot lower it, for that would be dismantling an ohel. See Tosafot in the third chapter of Sukkah, page 33, s.v. niktam me'erev Yom Tov, where it is proven that the Talmud holds that anything requiring a prohibited act is not considered in one's power. If so, since on Shabbos and Yom Tov it is forbidden to reduce it because it is dismantling an ohel, it should invalidate what is below it opposite it. One can say that in truth on Shabbos and Yom Tov it is invalid, and it is valid only for the other days of the festival. But one can ask: it is not a sukkah fit for seven days, since on Shabbos it becomes invalid because of invalid s'chach. Yet according to us, we rule like the Sages, that if one made it on top of an animal it is valid and is not considered a sukkah unfit for seven days, because by Torah law it is fit. If so, here too dismantling a temporary ohel on Shabbos and Yom Tov is only rabbinic; by Torah law it is in his power to reduce it even on Shabbos and Yom Tov, and only rabbinically it is not fit, so it is still fit for seven days. But this is only according to the Sages. According to Rabbi Yehudah there, who holds it is invalid because we follow the rabbinic status, here too it is invalid because it is not fit for seven days. Therefore it works well: in Eruvin the Talmud proceeds according to the halacha, for we rule like the Sages, and therefore what is higher than twenty is not invalid s'chach to invalidate what is opposite it. But here one can say either that Rabbi Yirmeyah holds like Rabbi Yehudah, who follows the rabbinic status, or that he did not want to enter a dispute, and therefore he chose a case where the upper one stands within twenty, in which case it is valid according to everyone. But if it were higher than twenty, it would be invalid according to Rabbi Yehudah, and he does not speak in a disputed case. Therefore Rashi explained as he did. If so, in practical law Rashi too agrees with Rabbeinu Tam, except that on Yom Tov and Shabbos it is invalid. Consider this carefully.
