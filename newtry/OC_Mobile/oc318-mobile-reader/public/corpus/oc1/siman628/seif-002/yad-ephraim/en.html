(Se'if katan 6) And perhaps nowadays, when it is not customary, etc.; for even if he places it there, it is not called using attached material, as written in siman 336. This is not understood, for the Rosh forbids for this very reason, lest he place something there, and he must necessarily hold that it is called using what is attached. The Magen Avraham should have said that we do not decree lest he place something there, since nowadays it is not customary to place things there, and in such a case we do not decree. The intent of his words appears according to what he wrote in siman 336, that according to the Rosh, if one removes something from a tree it is not called using attached material. Rather, the reason it is forbidden to place a candle on a tree on Yom Tov is lest he ascend the tree to take it. The Magen Avraham challenged this from the Rosh's words here, where he holds that it is forbidden to take an object from atop the tree, etc. At first glance this is not understood, for perhaps here too the prohibition is lest he ascend the tree. One must say that there it works well: it is forbidden to place something on the tree lest he ascend it to remove it, and that is one decree. But here, if we came to forbid because perhaps he will ascend, it would be a decree upon a decree, for in truth he is not placing anything there; we would have to decree lest he place something, and lest he ascend. That would be a decree upon a decree. From this the Magen Avraham proves that even if we do not decree lest he ascend, placing it there is forbidden because it is use of attached material, and he proves it by way of difficulty. The view of the Maggid Mishneh is indeed that it is forbidden because of using attached material. In order to avoid difficulty from what the Rosh wrote to prove from the law that one may tie a rope to a cow, the Magen Avraham wrote a distinction: specifically a tree, whose normal use is to take from it and place on it, is considered use in this manner; but an animal is not normally used in this way. See there. According to this, even according to the proof that the Magen Avraham derives from the Rosh here, that taking from it is considered using attached material, one must still distinguish between a tree, whose normal use, etc., and an animal. Otherwise one could challenge the Rosh here from the law that one may tie a rope to a cow. Necessarily one must distinguish that with a tree, since this is its manner of use, it is called using attached material. Now in the Rosh's wording, that he wrote a decree lest he place his object on the s'chach, perhaps the matter does not depend on regular practice. Rather, they decreed that once you allow him to ascend, if it should happen that he needs to place his object down and he places it there, he will be using attached material. However, in the Maggid Mishneh's wording it is explicit that they were accustomed to place items there. If so, one can say that if the Rosh's intent is that it is a decree lest he place, etc., it is because that was the regular practice; but if it is not the practice, why should we be concerned for this? According to this, the Magen Avraham's words are well understood, though the word moreover is missing. The text should read: and perhaps nowadays, etc., and we do not decree lest he place something on the s'chach; moreover, even if he does place, etc. His intent is that he first brings the Rosh's words, which could be explained as not depending on regular practice but as a decree lest such a case occur. Yet it is somewhat difficult to say so, for why should we invent a decree that he will do this, when we see that it is not customary to do so? We do not decree because of an uncommon possibility. Therefore he brings the Maggid Mishneh's words, that they were accustomed, etc. Since in their times they were accustomed to this, it is understandable that they decreed not to ascend lest he act as usual and place something there. On this he wrote: since the core prohibition is because he may act as usual, it follows that nowadays, when we are not accustomed to this, it is permitted, and we do not decree lest he place something on the s'chach. What the Magen Avraham wrote, that it is not forbidden because of something enacted by a formal count, etc., in siman 117, is like a parenthetical remark, as is found many times in the Magen Avraham. Then he wrote further: moreover, even if he places something there, it is presumably not called using attached material, as written in siman 336. That means that there, from the case of tying a rope to a cow, where it is permitted, it is necessary to distinguish: placing something is not called using attached material unless it is the normal way of using it, to take from it and place upon it. Since nowadays people are not accustomed to use the s'chach that way, it is like tying a rope to a cow, which is permitted. And since there is no concern of using attached material, there is no concern lest he ascend the tree, for that is the reason it is forbidden to place a candle on a tree on Yom Tov. There, the decree lest he ascend applies because he actually placed the candle there. But here, where in truth he is not placing anything, to decree lest he place and lest he ascend would be a decree upon a decree, as I wrote above regarding the Magen Avraham's words in siman 336. This is why the Magen Avraham was precise in his wording and wrote that even if he places it there, it is not called using attached material; he did not say that one should not decree lest he place, because it is permitted to place. His intent is as I wrote: in truth, placing is certainly forbidden, for even if there is no issue of using attached material, it is still forbidden to place it there because of a decree lest he ascend the tree. Therefore he wrote only that in any event it is not called using attached material. If it were forbidden because of use, it would be one decree. But since there is no prohibition on that account, here, where he is not actually placing anything, it is permitted to enter the sukkah, for decreeing lest he place and lest he ascend would be a decree upon a decree. So it appears to me to be correct. In truth, even without emending the Magen Avraham's words by adding moreover, his words can be aligned according to what I wrote; but it is closer to add the word moreover, for with this the words are properly arranged and correct, with God's help.
