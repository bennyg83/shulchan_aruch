The tree, it is valid. This is where the s'chach can stand without the tree, because he supported it on poles. Otherwise it is forbidden, for we decree lest he use the tree as s'chach, as written in siman 629:7, in the name of the Ran.
And one may not ascend to it. Even if the sukkah is on the ground and the reeds of the s'chach are supported by the tree, it is forbidden, as a decree lest he place his objects on the s'chach, as the Rosh and Rabbeinu Yerucham write. Nevertheless, the Perishah wrote: they were accustomed to place and hang their objects on the s'chach and use them. Perhaps nowadays, when it is not customary to use it that way, it is permitted. It is not forbidden as a decree that required a formal count, since it is only because of a concern, as written in Yoreh De'ah 116. We also do not decree lest he place something on the s'chach, for even if he does place it there, it is not called using attached material, as written in siman 336:1.
