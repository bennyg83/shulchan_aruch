Seif 1, if one was standing in reshut ha-yachid etc. Note: behold Rambam wrote in chapter 13 of Hilchot Shabbat: if one threw within 4 amot and it rolled outside 4 amot he is exempt; if one threw outside 4 amot and it rolled within 4 amot — if it rested on something outside 4 amot and afterward rolled and entered within 4 amot he is liable etc.; and see in Maggid Mishneh what he wrote in the name of Rashba, and these are his words: that which the Mishnah teaches "threw within 4 amot and it rolled outside 4 amot" — it is reasonable that it deals with one who throws to its opposite place, meaning it may rest wherever he wishes; for if not, but he intended to throw specifically within 4, it is obvious — and why teach "and it rolled"? It is obvious it should say "he intended to throw 2 and threw 4" — exempt; and since so, the first case too — since in such a case it is specifically when it rested on something within 4 and returned and rolled — otherwise why exempt? For it rested from its force outside 4 etc. And accordingly it would also be possible to ask on the first case — why, it did not rest? And establish when it rested on something but it is pleasant for it to return after — exempt, end of his words. But Rav Barceloni wrote: exempt — why? Because his intent was not outside 4 amot, end of his words in Maggid Mishneh. And behold I was asked on this by a certain scholar who understood Rav Barceloni's intent that he also explains like Rashba that it deals with one who says "wherever you wish it may rest," and nevertheless he exempts since his intent was also on within 4 amot, and even if he also intended outside 4 amot, since his intent was not on a place of liability alone but combined with a makom patur he is exempt; and on this the aforementioned scholar challenged from the sugya in chapter 1 of Shabbos from chapter HaZorek that it says there that throwing from reshut ha-yachid to reshut ha-yachid with reshut ha-rabbim in between — R' Akiva makes liable, and his reason is from "captured is as placed" applies; and behold in chapter HaZorek in R' Yehuda's sugya the derivative is liable in place of the primary explicitly there that it is impossible to say "captured is as placed" applies, or only when one says "wherever you wish it may rest"; and if so since he threw to reshut ha-yachid behold his intent includes if it falls in the other reshut ha-yachid — and if so included in his intent is also a makom patur — why is he liable? Such is the content of his question. And this is what I answered him: behold even if we explain per his understanding of Rav Barceloni's words, it also appears there is a distinction between whether his intent for a makom patur is in a place that is first before the place of liability, versus whether the makom patur is after the place of liability or the place of liability is before it comes to the makom patur — in this we say since he came to the place of liability and behold he also intended this place, he is already liable and is not exempt from his liability even if he passes from his place of liability to a makom patur and it is pleasant for it in its fall also there; since he is already liable it does not cease; and especially per the view that "captured is as placed" applies — if so he knows it will pass through the place of liability and be as placed — if so it is as if he intended it fall here and also in reshut ha-yachid, and therefore since he is liable it does not cease; but if the makom patur is first — if so immediately upon reaching the makom patur it is already pleasant for it to rest there and not pass further; therefore what passed afterward is as if it passed without his will and he is not liable for that. And thereby Rav Barceloni's words are properly understood. However in truth this is not Rav Barceloni's path; for if his intent is as he understood it, and Rav Barceloni also deals with one who says "wherever you wish it may rest" etc., and his intent — since he also intended a makom patur he is exempt — it should have said "not outside 4 amot alone"; then it would be properly guarded — whoever did not intend on his place of liability alone but combined with a makom patur; but since he uses the wording "his intent was not outside 4 amot" it implies it deals where his intent was not at all on outside 4 amot but only on within 4 amot; if his intent were also on outside 4 amot even though also on a makom patur he would be liable; and Rav Barceloni's intent is thus that he does not deal with one who says "wherever you wish it may rest," rather he deals like Rashba's second answer that Maggid Mishneh brought, that the reason the Mishnah exempts in the first case is because he intended only on a makom patur — meaning to resolve Rashba's question why he does not also ask on the first case — behold it did not rest. Therefore he explains plainly it deals where he intended only within, and what it teaches "and it rolled outside 4" is agav the last case that it needs to teach "rolled" to include that even in such a case he is liable — it also teaches in the first case "rolled"; and for this he explains plainly he intended only on within 4 amot, therefore exempt; and for this he asks only from the last case and as answered when it rested on something, and perforce it deals with one who says "wherever you wish it may rest" — if so the first case too deals with this; nevertheless per the conclusion exempt because it deals when it rested on something as in the last case; and the essence of Rav Barceloni's intent per the second answer in the sugya is to resolve Rashba's question, and it is properly understood plainly; but this is certainly true — whoever intended that it be placed in a place of liability even if he also intended a makom patur he is certainly liable, and so reason dictates, and so is the law for all without doubt; and this is clear — understand well:
