(א) Within 10 — see Mishna Berurah what he wrote regarding above 10; and behold in Tosafos Shabbos 3b s.v. here they wrote that even l'chatchila it is permitted to extend above 10; but Rashba in his chiddushim rejected their words and concluded that l'chatchila it is forbidden lest he come to extend below 10; and likewise in the name of his teacher for prohibition; and likewise Meiri; and see in Peri Megadim who also sides in the name of Rashi to be stringent in this; and it is surprising he did not bring Rashba's words:

(ב) To extend it to another courtyard — and even if that courtyard is his or they made an eruv together [later authorities]; and see in later authorities that it is not specifically extending, for sometimes there is a d'oraisa prohibition here, such as in one row of balconies where throwing is likewise forbidden:

(ג) And some say this applies only when he extended it while it was still day etc. — behold the first view is Rashi's view, and Beit Yosef brought in the name of Ran that Rif's view is also like Rashi's view, and consequently Rambam who was silent in his words is also explained like Rif's words like Rif's view and like Rashi's view, unlike Rosh who sides to say Rif holds like Tosafos' view, and therefore Shulchan Aruch stated the first view in general terms; and in truth it is also proven in Rashba's chiddushim that he explains Rambam's view as Beit Yosef understood, and likewise Meiri's view is like the first view; nevertheless the view of "some say" is also the view of many early authorities as Acharonim wrote. And one should consider for the first view: if he extended it while it was still day intentionally, do we penalize him not to return it? For it appears reasonable to say that for them too, per the sugya's conclusion, they penalized only when he extended intentionally from twilight, for he did a prohibition at the start of his action; and as the sugya initially distinguishes between while it was still day and twilight; nevertheless Ran wrote for Rashi's view [which is the first view] there is no distinction between while it was still day and twilight, and so is proven from Avodat HaKodesh for Rashba according to Rashi's view, and so is somewhat implied from Gra, and the reason appears that they hold at the beginning of the sugya it deals only regarding unintentional action, but intentionally even from while it was still day it is forbidden; and in Meiri I found his view is as we wrote, and these are his words: if he extended it while it was still day even intentionally he may return it to himself — there is no intentional action from while it was still day that is more severe than unintentional violation of Shabbat," end of his words — meaning since per the conclusion the Gemara is lenient regarding unintentional action even from twilight, it is fitting to be lenient from while it was still day even intentionally:
