(א) Within 10 — for above 10 in reshut ha-rabbim it is a makom patur as above in siman 345 seif 12, and even if he extended intentionally he may return it:

(ב) Unintentionally permitted etc. — even though he did a prohibition, for he uprooted in reshut ha-yachid in order to extend into reshut ha-rabbim, nevertheless the rabbis did not penalize him since he did it unintentionally:

(ג) To that same courtyard — so that his intent not be fulfilled, for he intended before to remove them from there:

(ד) To another courtyard — and even if he originally intended to extend them into reshut ha-rabbim, it is likewise forbidden to extend them to another courtyard, for in any case his intent was fulfilled that he intended to remove them from his courtyard, and we are concerned lest another time he cast them into reshut ha-rabbim. And some say specifically when his original intent was to remove them to that courtyard is it forbidden, for his intent was completely fulfilled; but if his intent was to extend them into reshut ha-rabbim it is permitted to turn to a courtyard. And all this when that courtyard was not in one row of balconies with this courtyard but one opposite the other; but if it was in one row and reshut ha-rabbim extends also between the courtyards — behold there is a d'oraisa prohibition to extend from one to the other, even if they are above 10, as stated above at the end of siman 347:

(ה) Intentionally forbidden — for the rabbis penalized him because he did a prohibition; rather he should hold it in his hand until nightfall:

(ו) When he extended it while it was still day — then the rabbis penalized him for holding his hand intentionally in the air of reshut ha-rabbim until twilight:

(ז) To return it — meaning to that same courtyard:

(ח) Liability for chatat — this wording is not precise, for here it deals with intentional action; rather meaning lest he forget and cast from his hand and come to a melacha that carries liability for chatat for those who do it unintentionally; unlike when he extended it while it was still day, even if he casts at the end it will only be a mere prohibition, for he did not uproot on Shabbat; and see in Acharonim who sides to be lenient like "some say," for so is the view of many early authorities:

(ט) But to karmelis — which is only rabbinic shevut, even if he would cast them there directly they did not forbid, for it is like a kind of decree upon a decree:

(י) In any case — meaning both unintentionally and intentionally; and specifically to that same courtyard he may return, but not to another courtyard. And Gra in his Biur concludes to forbid in karmelis as in reshut ha-rabbim. One who was clearing objects from corner to corner and changed his mind and took them out is exempt from chatat, for there was no uprooting from the first moment for that purpose, and it turns out he only did placing and there is only a mere prohibition. If a reed was in his hand and he lifted one end while the other end rested on the ground, and then lifted the other end — even the entire day — he is exempt since he did not uproot the entire reed from the ground.
