1) [Seif 1] A person should always set his table after Shabbos, etc. This is compared to accompanying the King when He departs, just as one accompanies Him when He arrives. The siddurim explain that a person has a limb called nisko'i, which derives nourishment from eating only after Shabbos. Beis Yosef in the name of Shibbolei HaLeket. It appears to me that the reason is as follows. It is known that this bone is called luz. It is the person's essential core and root, and from it the person developed from the original drop. When a person dies, this bone neither dissolves nor diminishes. If placed in fire it does not burn, in a mill it is not ground, and under a hammer it does not shatter. It is the bone that endures eternally, from which the person will live at the resurrection. It receives punishment and delight after a person's death, and its root and essence derive from the substance of Heaven. Mateh Moshe, siman 513. See there for what he writes further concerning this. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2, cites it. Since it derives nourishment from eating only after Shabbos, it did not benefit from the Tree of Knowledge and therefore does not decay. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 3. See the sacred Zohar, parashas Pinchas, folio 222a, and Midrash HaNe'elam, parashas Toldos, folio 137a, which states that they call that spinal bone 'the deceitful Besuel,' etc., and that it does not tolerate the flavor of human food like the other bones, etc.; see there. The statement that it does not tolerate the flavor of food can be explained to mean that it does not derive nourishment from food at all times like the other bones, but only after Shabbos, as Shibbolei HaLeket writes. Note this well.
