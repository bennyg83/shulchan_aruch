Paragraph 12: They stipulated, etc. Megillah 28 says, 'The synagogues of Babylonia are made conditionally,' implying that by default they are built subject to a condition.
Rabbi Yaakov bei Rav, cited by Magen Avraham in paragraph 11, says that even in the Diaspora synagogues retain their sanctity when ruined. But they are built conditionally and should therefore at least have less sanctity than while inhabited. Evidently, in the Diaspora an explicit stipulation is required, and Rabbi Yaakov bei Rav discusses an unspecified case.
And it is astonishing, etc. See what I wrote on se'if 1, paragraph 2.
In the name of Hagahos Ashri, etc. Meaning: according to Magen Avraham's explanation, Hagahos Ashri permitted anyone to eat and drink even while synagogues are inhabited because he holds that even then they were built conditionally. If he required an explicit stipulation in the Diaspora, how could he permit an unspecified synagogue? How do we know an explicit stipulation was made when it was built? Evidently, he holds that no explicit stipulation is required.
Nevertheless, this is not conclusive. Hagahos Ashri may refer to the Gemara's case of Babylonian synagogues, with the practical implication for us only where we know an explicit stipulation was made.
