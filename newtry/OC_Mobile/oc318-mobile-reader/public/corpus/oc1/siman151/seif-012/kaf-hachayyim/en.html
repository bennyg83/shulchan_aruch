The Beit Yosef also cites this in this siman in the name of the Ramban. Birkei Yosef, paragraph 3, similarly writes in the name of R' Yeshayah the First, in Responsa Beit Yosef, siman 88, that even in a synagogue in Eretz Yisrael, guests may eat and drink there although they are not Torah scholars, for the prohibition was stated only regarding the residents of the city. Erech HaShulchan, paragraph 1, cites him and writes that this is also the view of the Sefer Mitzvos Gadol, positive commandment 29, and Orchos Chaim, folio 63, because it is considered a seudas mitzvah, which is permitted; see there. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2, however, questions the Taz's words, for Tosafos on Megillah 28a write that the case there concerns a room adjoining the synagogue, while eating in the synagogue itself is forbidden. Tosafos and the Rosh write the same at the beginning of Bava Basra. Furthermore, the Taz's statement that the Beit Yosef wrote this in the name of the Ramban is no proof, for that source concerns synagogues in Bavel that were built conditionally, where such use is permitted just as a condition is effective for an aron and sacred accessories in siman 154, seif 8. Magen Avraham in siman 269, paragraph 2, likewise writes that the case there concerns a house adjoining the synagogue, but eating in the synagogue itself is forbidden; see there. Birkei Yosef in Shiyurei Berachah to this siman, paragraph 3, also writes regarding the Taz's ruling that this does not appear to be the view of the poskim; see there. This is likewise the view of the Ran at the beginning of the last chapter of Pesachim, the Maggid Mishneh in chapter 29 of Hilchos Shabbos, the Meiri at the end of Megillah, and Tosafos and the Mordechai there. They maintain that even in a synagogue in Bavel, a condition is ineffective for this purpose until after it is destroyed; see there. See Responsa Rashbash, siman 274, where he discusses this at length and writes that a Torah scholar requires no condition, but for guests who are not Torah scholars one must explicitly stipulate this; see there. Erech HaShulchan cites him there. See also Shiltei HaGibborim on the Rif, chapter Bnei HaIr, paragraph 3, who writes in the name of Riaz that the Gemara's statement that the synagogues of Bavel are built conditionally refers only to guests, since travelers passing from place to place are common in Bavel; see there. This matter is therefore subject to a dispute. Some say that even in a synagogue in Bavel a condition is ineffective for this purpose until after its destruction; some say that it is effective even while the building stands; and some say that even in a synagogue in Eretz Yisrael, where a condition is ineffective, guests may eat there. Therefore, since the matter is disputed, guests should initially take care, whenever possible, not to eat in a synagogue, even outside Eretz Yisrael. In pressing circumstances they may rely upon the permissive opinions. See below, seif 11.
