And if the road passed through it, etc. Nevertheless, it is not proper to use it as a shortcut. Rabbi Eliezer said, 'I never made it a shortcut,' which implies that his statement concerns even cases where doing so is technically permitted; otherwise there would be no novelty. Kesef Mishneh. So too Piskei Tosafot, chapter Bnei HaIr.
It is permitted, etc. The Levush writes that some say it is a mitzvah. So the Beit Yosef writes in the name of the Rosh and Tur. The Shulchan Aruch, however, simply says that it is permitted, because the Beit Yosef explains that the Rif and Rambam do not have the reading 'it is a mitzvah,' only 'it is permitted,' and the Shulchan Aruch generally follows the Rif and Rambam. The Levush's reason seems to be that we generally follow the Rosh. Magen Avraham rules likewise. This also seems to me the principal view, for the Ran in Bnei HaIr rules so, and I found the same in Baal Halachot Gedolot, folio 12, whose words are received tradition. I am further surprised at the Beit Yosef, because the Rif at the end of Berachot, as explained by the Ran there, clearly has this intent. Nachalat Tzvi's proof for the Rambam from Rabbi Eliezer's statement that he never used a synagogue as a shortcut overlooks the Kesef Mishneh there. Rabbi Eliezer refers to a case where the road already passed through it, or to avoiding use as a shortcut where otherwise technically permitted. The Gemara's verse concerning one who enters to pray does not refer to that situation even according to the Rambam. Piskei Tosafot likewise implies this, and Maadanei Melech at the end of Berachot leans to the view that the verse is phrased as a command. Therefore it seems to me that this is the practical ruling.
