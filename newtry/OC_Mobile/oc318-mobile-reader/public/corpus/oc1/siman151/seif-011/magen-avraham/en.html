An explicit condition. Only in Babylonia is a synagogue presumed to have been built conditionally; elsewhere an explicit condition is required. This follows Tur and Mordechai. Rambam omits this law. See se'if 10 and Rabbi Yaakov Beirav. Shiltei Gibborim, page 265, cites Riaz as permitting it only for guests. It is surprising that Responsa Masas Binyamin applies the presumption to our synagogues. Hagahos Ashri cited earlier gives slight support to that view, but it is not conclusive.
When it is ruined. This means specifically when it has been ruined and will not be rebuilt. Agudah.
While it is standing, no condition helps. This is difficult in light of siman 194, se'if 8, where a condition helps even for an Ark and other sacred accessories. Here, however, the condition concerns frivolity, eating, drinking, or entering because of sun; for these, it does not help while the synagogue stands. This follows the Rosh. Sowing the site is different because it involves excessive degradation and causes anguish.
Outside Eretz Yisrael. Tosafos explains that at the Redemption their sanctity will lapse. This is difficult, because the Gemara says that all synagogues in Babylonia will be relocated to Eretz Yisrael. Perhaps Tosafos refers to ruins existing before the Redemption, which will not be relocated. One cannot say that only the building, but not its ground, will be relocated, for the ground of Tabor and Carmel will certainly be moved, and the same should follow all the more here.
