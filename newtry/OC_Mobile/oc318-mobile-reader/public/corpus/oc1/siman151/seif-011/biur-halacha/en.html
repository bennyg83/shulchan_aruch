To use it. Eliyah Rabbah cites Bach, siman 153: if they stipulated to pray there only until a set time and never afterward, they may use it freely once that time ends. This requires examination, for such an explicit temporary designation is no designation at all, so even during that time it lacks synagogue sanctity; Mordechai cited by Bach proves so. Nevertheless, actual levity should be avoided during the designated period. The Gemara concludes, according to Mordechai, that even so one should not act frivolously there; meaning calculations. Although a temporary designation has no legal force, calculations, and certainly laughter and joking, remain improper. Eating, drinking, and other uses appear not to require stringency. See Mishnah Berurah, siman 153, paragraph 51.
But while it stands, etc. There are several explanations of 'they were made conditionally.' Tosafos and Rosh say a condition does not help while standing but does after ruin. Rashi and Or Zarua say it helps even while standing. Ramban, Ran, and Rashba, responsa IV, say it does not permit explicit unpressured use even after ruin, but under pressure it helps even while standing; pressure includes temporarily feeding or lodging poor people. Although the Mechaber rules that a condition does not help while standing, Taz at the beginning of the siman implies that under such pressure one may rely on Ramban. This is justified because Ran and Rashba agree, and Rashi and Or Zarua permit even more broadly. Even Rashi and Or Zarua plainly do not permit actual levity such as laughter and joking; the Gemara expressly forbids even calculations as levity.
A condition does not help. Magen Avraham limits this to levity-like uses (eating, drinking, or entering from sun) but says a condition helps for other use. Yet Rosh proves that while standing it does not even permit calling someone from there; why should that be worse than other ordinary use? All use must therefore be forbidden. His question from siman 154, where a condition helps for holy accessories such as a Torah-scroll wrapping, can be answered: every Jewish community must have a miniature Sanctuary, as siman 150 says they compel one another to build a synagogue. The Sages therefore ruled that a fixed synagogue cannot, while standing, be conditionally used for mundane purposes, for then it would be an ordinary house rather than a synagogue. If a wrapping loses the status of a holy accessory, by contrast, no communal institution is undermined. Magen Avraham's question exists only under Rashi and the other poskim who understand the condition as permitting immediate use, while Tosafos and Rosh still deny it during occupancy. Under the surviving text of Rabbeinu Chananel there is no question: he explains that whenever the public wishes, the building returns to its former status. Public consent removes its sanctity and permits non-degrading uses; when they resume prayer, it becomes sanctified again. Public calculations remain forbidden as levity. Rainwater use occurred while the public had not consented, so sanctity remained. Siman 154 differs because there the condition permits use while the object still retains sanctity.
But synagogues in Eretz Yisrael, etc. The Mechaber's categorical rule that a condition never helps in Eretz Yisrael requires examination. The Gemara only says Babylonian synagogues were built conditionally. If this means an implicit condition, it merely excludes an implicit condition in Eretz Yisrael; an explicit condition could still help, as the Yerushalmi expressly permits for holy accessories and as Magen Avraham asks. Even if, as Magen Avraham proves from several poskim, Babylonian congregations explicitly stipulated, this proves only that Babylonian custom was to stipulate for local reasons while Eretz Yisrael and other Diaspora congregations did not. It does not prove an explicit condition ineffective elsewhere; even modern Babylonian synagogues are not certainly conditional. No Rishon other than Tosafos states that a condition fails in Eretz Yisrael. Tosafos follows its own view that a condition helps only after ruin: when Mashiach comes, Diaspora Jews will gather to Eretz Yisrael, no longer need these miniature Sanctuaries, and their sanctity will necessarily lapse. A condition therefore works once ruined. Eretz Yisrael synagogues never necessarily lose sanctity, so a condition cannot help even after ruin. According to authorities permitting a condition under pressure even while standing, such as feeding poor guests, there may be no distinction; Riaz implies the Babylonian condition arose because guests were common, suggesting legal effectiveness everywhere. Taz permits reliance on Ramban and those authorities under pressure, so this should apply even in Eretz Yisrael. Even Rosh and Tur, who agree with Tosafos about standing versus ruined, may reject the geographic distinction and permit an explicit condition after ruin in Eretz Yisrael. A condition fundamentally helps a synagogue as it does holy accessories; the Sages only fenced use while standing. This may explain why Rosh and Tur omit Tosafos's geographic novelty. In sum, there are three categories. Serious levity (calculations, laughter, joking, and the like) is forbidden everywhere even with a condition, standing or ruined. Entirely non-frivolous mundane use may, according to Magen Avraham, be conditionally permitted everywhere, standing or ruined. Eating, drinking, and similar mildly frivolous use is conditionally permitted in Babylonia at least after ruin under Shulchan Aruch; several poskim permit it even while standing to feed poor people under pressure. According to the analysis here, one may perhaps be equally lenient in Eretz Yisrael.
