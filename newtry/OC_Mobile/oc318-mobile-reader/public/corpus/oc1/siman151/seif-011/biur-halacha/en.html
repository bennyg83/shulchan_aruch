But regarding synagogues in Eretz Yisrael, etc. — see Mishna Berurah. And behold regarding the essence of the law that Shulchan Aruch rules plainly that in Eretz Yisrael a condition does not help — one must examine, for at first glance this law is not clear to all [only per Tosafot's view, as below]: for in the Gemara this is not mentioned at all as "a condition does not help," but rather we say there that synagogues of Babylon were made on condition — and not necessarily: if we explain that by default they are made on condition, one cannot infer from this except to exclude Eretz Yisrael, where by default they do not stand on condition; but if they stipulated explicitly, why should it not help? — and like all uses of sanctity, where it is stated explicitly in Yerushalmi that a condition helps, as below in siman 153, and as Magen Avraham asked in note 14. However even per what Magen Avraham proved from several poskim that the intent of "made on condition" is that in Babylon they stipulated on them and not by default — nevertheless we have no proof that in Eretz Yisrael a condition does not help, but rather in Babylon their way was to stipulate for whatever reasons they had, and in Eretz Yisrael they did not stipulate; and the same in all chutz la'aretz — there was also no condition on their synagogues; and even in Babylon itself nowadays it is not clear that their synagogues are made on condition; but if they stipulated explicitly, it helps well in every place, like all uses of sanctity. And in truth this is not mentioned in all the rishonim that in Eretz Yisrael a condition does not help; and this novel law is mentioned only in Tosafot; and it appears they wrote this only for their view that in its settled state the condition does not help, only in its ruin; and therefore they wrote the reason that the condition helps in ruin — because when the redeemer comes, meaning that ultimately when the redeemer comes and Israel gathers from all the places where they were scattered among the nations to Eretz Yisrael and they will not need this small sanctuary, and they are destined to be destroyed — perforce sanctity will be nullified then; and therefore the condition also helps for all the time until it is destroyed; not so Eretz Yisrael, whose sanctity will not be nullified ever — therefore also in ruin a condition does not help, like in its settled state. And therefore for poskim who hold that also in settled state a condition helps for a pressing need, such as feeding poor guests — one can say there is no difference between Babylon and Eretz Yisrael, and even in Eretz Yisrael a condition helps if they stipulated, even for settled state for this matter [and so is Ra'avad, whose view is also this method, and he wrote the reason that therefore there was a condition in synagogues of Babylon because guests are common — and it implies that by law a condition helps in every place, even in settled state, except that there their way was to stipulate because guests are common]. And even though Shulchan Aruch rules plainly like Tosafot that only for ruin a condition helps — nevertheless it is evident from Taz at the start of the siman that we can rely on the view of Ramban and his supporters that even in settled state a condition helps for poor people in a pressing place, as above; and if so, this law will be so also in Eretz Yisrael. And more — we can say that even per the view of Rosh and Tur, who hold like Tosafot that in settled state a condition does not help and only in ruin — nevertheless in this they do not hold like Tosafot, but rather that if they stipulated explicitly it helps even in Eretz Yisrael in ruin [for by essence of the law a condition should help for a synagogue like uses of sanctity — only Chazal fenced it in settled state, as in Beiur Halacha; but why it helps in Babylon, the same in Eretz Yisrael — what is the difference?]. And that which the Gemara mentions synagogues of Babylon were made on condition — there their way was to stipulate, as above; and therefore Rosh and Tur omitted this law that Tosafot wrote, and only the Mechaber brought it plainly. The conclusion from this seif and from all our words is that there are three distinctions: (1) great levity such as accounts, joking, and the like — even in Babylon, and if they stipulated, it is forbidden, as stated explicitly in the Gemara, whether in settled state or ruin; (2) mundane uses that have no levity at all — evident from Magen Avraham that a condition helps in every place, as written in Mishna Berurah — and this too whether in settled state or ruin; (3) the matter under discussion — eating, drinking, and the like, which have slight levity — in Babylon a condition helps at least for ruin, per Shulchan Aruch and many poskim, and even in settled state to feed poor people there when the place is cramped; and per our words, perhaps one may be lenient in all this in Eretz Yisrael as well.
