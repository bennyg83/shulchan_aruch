And idle speech — and he wrote in Petach Einayim and these are his words: one who speaks mundane conversation in the synagogue — better for him not to come at all, for he sins and causes others to sin; and the Satan prosecutes and says today was short for me to speak until waiting for prayer time; and included in this are Torah scholars who chatter at prayer time and confuse the congregation with their shouting — not fitting, servants — even though Torah equals them all, the sages said time for Torah alone, etc.; and also lest gentiles take from them kal v'chomer and also not be silent to hear prayer from the shatz — end of his words; and Solet L'ov Yesharim brought note 2, and see there in Solet what he elaborated — see there. And so Aruch HaShulchan note 1 that one should rebuke one who speaks mundane conversation in synagogue even though it is not idle speech — for without need it is forbidden — all idle speech and he transgresses a positive rabbinic commandment; at least for livelihood need outside is permitted, in synagogue forbidden — end of his words; and see Rabbeinu Yosef Chemed responsa part 2 siman 31 who explained severity of speech prohibition in synagogue and especially at prayer time and its punishment and judgment and responsum; and they brought Birkei Yosef note 4 and these are his words Zohar parashat Terumah 131b: one who speaks in synagogue mundane words — woe to him for he appears separate; woe to him he diminishes faith; woe to him he has no share in God of Israel for he appears he has no God and is not found there and has no share in Him and does not fear Him, and I will conduct contempt in supernal array — end of his words; and he wrote in Sha'ar HaKavvanot 4a and these are his words: I saw my teacher extremely careful not to speak at all in synagogue even not at prayer time, and almost that even words of mussar and reproof and repentance he would not want to speak lest it draw some mundane speech — end of his words; and they brought Magen Avraham note 3; and see further in our words above siman 32 note 7.

(8) And on Shabbat kodesh there is additional prohibition — not only mundane speech even for need is forbidden, but even for synagogue need is forbidden as written in Zohar parashat Vayakhel 205b and these are his words: since holy people entered synagogue it is forbidden to engage even in synagogue needs except matters of praise and prayer and Torah. And one who engages in other matters and worldly matters — he is like a gentile who desecrates Shabbat and has no share among Israel; two angels escort him on this on Shabbat day and place their hands on his head and say woe to so-and-so who has no share in Holy One blessed be He; and therefore one must engage in prayer and songs and praises of their Master and in Torah — end of his words. And the reason is that the indwelling of Shekhinah resting in synagogue on Shabbat is not comparable to weekday indwelling and one must be stricter and it is forbidden to engage even in synagogue need — shatz dalet yud gimmel avei gimmel, 361, note 1.
