Paragraph 7: It is permitted, etc. This is Rambam's wording, but he did not write, etc. Meaning: Rambam presents no difficulty. Since the middle clause permits passage when one did not initially enter to take a shortcut, the final clause (if he entered to pray) would follow a fortiori and seem unnecessary. But Rambam did not quote the middle clause, so no question arises.
And Tur, etc. Tur writes that for one who entered to pray, taking the shortcut is a mitzvah. The final clause is therefore necessary: the middle clause merely permits it, while the final clause makes it a mitzvah.
And it is possible that this is a scribal error, etc. If so, it is not established that one who entered to pray may pass through when he simultaneously intended to take a shortcut.
Regarding the statement that it is a mitzvah to use it as a shortcut, Ran in Megillah explains that he thereby shows the synagogue is dear to him, since he walks through its entire length.
