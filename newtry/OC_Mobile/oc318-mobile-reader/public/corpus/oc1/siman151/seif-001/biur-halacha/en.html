Levity. Even if grave transgressions were committed in a synagogue, it remains permissible to pray and place a Torah scroll there [Responsa Re'em I 79].
One may not eat or drink in them. Rashbash 274 forbids a non-scholar to drink water in a synagogue or study hall. The present custom is for the public to drink water in synagogues [Birkei Yosef], perhaps relying on those who hold that Diaspora synagogues are built conditionally and the condition helps even while standing. This gives no reason for leniency in Eretz Yisrael. One learning in a study hall, even not regularly, may apparently drink when thirsty, since preventing interruption of learning is a mitzvah need, as in se'if 4. Shulchan Aruch does not specify whether eating is forbidden only as a fixed meal or even casually. Se'if 3 expressly forbids even temporary sleep in a synagogue but does not say so here, perhaps implying only fixed eating; alternatively, casual eating is at least no better than drinking. The matter requires consideration.

Scholars and their students. There is no distinction between synagogues and study halls in Eretz Yisrael and those in the Diaspora.
Some say that in a study hall, etc. Ran permits a scholar to use a study hall entirely like his home (even entering from sun or rain) apart from actual levity. Since Rema does not mention this leniency, he apparently adopts Ran only regarding eating and drinking, which are permitted even without pressure. It is nevertheless reasonable to be lenient with other uses as well for those learning there all day. Ran supplies no basis to permit even pressured use in a synagogue, except in Babylonia (or likewise elsewhere in the Diaspora) because they were built conditionally. Yet Rema's omission of Ran implies that on this point he follows the Mechaber's lenient ruling based on Rambam and other poskim.
Nor may calculations be made there. This applies even when the townspeople need those calculations and have no other meeting place, because it is levity toward the synagogue's honor [Ramban, Likkutim]. Thus even public calculations are forbidden, and certainly an individual's.
