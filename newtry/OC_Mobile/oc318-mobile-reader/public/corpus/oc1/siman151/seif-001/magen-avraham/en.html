Frivolity. Through such conduct they turn the synagogue into a house of idolatry. Semak. It is also forbidden to slaughter there. Mabit, volume 3, siman 4.
For a necessity. The Gemara relates that Rav Adda and Ravina were studying when rain began, and they entered the synagogue, saying they had not entered because of the rain but because study requires clarity. Beis Yosef explains that rain alone is not a great necessity, since people ordinarily walk in the rain. It appears that eating and drinking are permitted only to Torah scholars who are studying there, so that they will not have to interrupt their learning; if they are not learning there, it is forbidden. Ran also requires conduct reflecting reverence. Rambam writes that eating and drinking are permitted there when necessary. See se'if 4. The Rosh's abridged rulings state that eating and the like are forbidden, but sages and their students are permitted. Entering merely to escape sun or rain is forbidden even for scholars. The Yerushalmi permits a Torah scholar to lodge there. Tur and Rabbeinu Yerucham agree. Hagahos Ashri also agrees, but synagogues in Babylonia are built conditionally and all people may use them, even while they stand. Thus there is no dispute, unlike Bach's understanding.
One of the communal leaders. For example, when a relative of a communal leader died, as when Rafram eulogized his daughter-in-law in the synagogue. The Arizal would permit only prayer in a synagogue, not even words of mussar and repentance, lest mundane matters be introduced.
