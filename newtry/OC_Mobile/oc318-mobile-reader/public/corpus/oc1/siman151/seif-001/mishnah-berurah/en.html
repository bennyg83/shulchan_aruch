(1) Lightheaded conduct - For they are called a minor sanctuary, as it is written, "I was for them a minor sanctuary." Regarding the Temple it is written, "You shall fear My sanctuary," meaning that the awe of the One who dwells there should be upon him. Semak wrote that because of lightheadedness in a synagogue, they are turned into houses of idolatry, Heaven forbid.
(2) And idle conversation - This means even mundane conversation needed for livelihood, which is permitted outside, is forbidden in a synagogue; all the more so entirely idle conversation, from which one should certainly always refrain, as Peri Megadim writes. In the holy Zohar, parashat Vayakhel, it speaks very strongly about the greatness of this sin. All the more must one be careful in a synagogue and beit midrash against forbidden speech such as lashon hara, gossip, dispute, and quarrels, for besides being very severe sins, the sin is greater in a holy place, since he degrades the honor of the Shekhinah. One who sins privately is not comparable to one who sins in the king's palace before the king. The evil is further increased because he also causes the public to stumble in these severe sins; such conflict is like a breach in water: at first the sin begins with a few people, and in the end groups gather and join to quarrel with one another until the whole synagogue becomes like a great fire. Because of our many sins this often leads to insults, reviling, and publicly shaming others, sometimes even before the Sefer Torah, which is also a severe sin in itself. For even one who disgraces his fellow before a Torah scholar, Chazal said in Sanhedrin, chapter Chelek, is an apikores and has no share in the World to Come; all the more one who disgraces his fellow before the Sefer Torah and the honor of the Shekhinah, as similarly written in responsa Mahari Weil siman 152, see there. It also leads to blows, informing, and great desecration of Heaven's name among the nations. Who caused all this if not the first one who began the sin? Certainly in the future he will receive punishment corresponding to them all. Therefore one who fears and trembles at the word of Hashem should always set his eyes and heart to this: not to speak any idle matters in a synagogue or beit midrash, and this place should be designated for him only for Torah and prayer.
(3) And one may not eat, etc. - The same applies that one may not perform any work in them.
(4) Because of the sun, etc. - It does not help that he read or study a little upon entering, as below, since he can enter an ordinary house to be saved from the sun or rain, unless he was previously engaged outside in some matter of halachah and rain began to fall, in which case he may enter the beit midrash so the rain will not disturb him.
(5) Are permitted, etc. - The same applies if the people of the city temporarily needed to feed poor guests there or lodge them there, it is permitted. But this is specifically in synagogues outside the Land of Israel. Some forbid even this, and it appears that in pressing circumstances one should not be stringent.
(6) To eat and drink - The same applies to sleeping, which is permitted. But using them as a shortcut, entering them in sun because of the sun or in rain because of the rain, and all the cases below, and certainly joking, frivolity, and idle conversation, are forbidden; for is a Torah scholar not warned regarding the awe of the sanctuary?
(7) Because of pressing need - That is, the place was cramped for the students and they needed to eat there. Magen Avraham wrote that for people who study there regularly, it is always considered pressing need, for if he would need to go home to eat and drink he would certainly lose from his learning. But if they do not study in the synagogue or beit midrash, they are forbidden to eat and drink there; and so several Acharonim agreed.
(8) And some say that in a beit midrash, etc. - Although the sanctity of a beit midrash is greater than that of a synagogue, as below in siman 153 seif 1, they hold that regarding sages and their students who study there regularly, it was permitted to them even without pressing need, because the beit midrash is their home. This is not limited to eating and drinking; the same applies to all their uses, which are permitted.
(9) It is permitted - See Levush and Bach, who incline this way in practical law; and so it appears, that one should not be stringent for people who study there all day.
(10) Of the city - Where a relative of the prominent person died, and because of this many come for the eulogy; all the more when the eulogy is for a Torah scholar, as the Acharonim write. The Ari was very careful not to speak in the synagogue except his prayer; he did not even speak words of mussar, lest mundane words be drawn from it.
(11) A teaching - Meaning a halachah or Mishnah.
(12) And afterward read him - Meaning, he should not first call him before saying some words of Torah, for then it would appear that the words of Torah are merely secondary, as the Acharonim write.
(13) The time to walk - Meaning he should wait the time needed to walk two entrances, namely eight tefachim.
