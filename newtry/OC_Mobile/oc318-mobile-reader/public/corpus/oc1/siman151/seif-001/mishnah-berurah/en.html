(1) Levity. For synagogues and study halls are called a miniature Sanctuary, as it says, 'I have been for them a miniature Sanctuary.' Semak writes that if one behaves frivolously there, it is transformed into a house of idolatry.
(2) And idle conversation. This includes even mundane talk needed for one's livelihood, which is permitted outside but forbidden in a synagogue; completely idle talk should certainly always be avoided [Pri Megadim]. The Zohar, Vayakhel, speaks very severely of this sin. One must be especially careful in a synagogue or study hall to avoid forbidden speech such as lashon hara, gossip, disputes, and quarrels. Besides their intrinsic severity, committing them in a sacred place disgraces the Divine Presence; sinning privately cannot be compared to sinning in the King's palace before the King. It also causes the public to sin: a quarrel spreads like a breach in a dam, beginning with a few people until groups gather against one another and the entire synagogue becomes a great fire. This often leads, Heaven help us, to insults, curses, and public humiliation, sometimes before the Torah scroll. The Sages call one who disgraces another before a Torah scholar an apikores with no share in the World to Come; how much more so before the Torah scroll and the Divine Presence [Responsa Mahari Weil 152]. It can also lead to blows, informing, and grave desecration of God's Name among the nations. The first person who began the transgression caused all this and will bear responsibility corresponding to them all. One who fears the word of Hashem must therefore resolve never to speak idle words in a synagogue or study hall, reserving that place solely for Torah and prayer.
(3) One may not perform calculations. Nor any other work.
(4) Because of the rain. One may not enter merely for shelter and then learn or recite a verse to make the entry appear purposeful. If he had already been learning outside and rain began, he may enter and continue learning.
(5) To eat and lodge there. This refers to poor guests whom the townspeople temporarily feed and lodge there. It applies only to synagogues in the Diaspora. There is a dispute, but under pressure one may be lenient.
(6) For scholars. They may eat, drink, and sleep in a study hall. Shortening one's route through it, taking shelter there, and levity remain forbidden.
(7) Under pressure. This allowance is for students, since interrupting them would cause neglect of Torah. Those who regularly learn there are always considered under pressure. Where there would be no neglect of learning, the allowance does not apply.
(8) There are those who say that in a study hall, etc. Although a study hall has greater sanctity than a synagogue, as below in siman 153:1, scholars and students who learn there regularly were permitted even when not under pressure, because the study hall is their home. This is not limited to eating and drinking; all their needs are permitted.
(9) And so is the custom. Levush and Bach are lenient for those who learn there all day.
(10) For a great person. Such as when a close relative of a great person died, and certainly for the death of a Torah scholar. The Arizal was careful to speak in a synagogue only words of prayer, not even words of mussar.
(11) To teach there. Meaning words of halachah or Mishnah.
(12) He should read or learn a little. He should learn before calling the person he needs, not call him first and then learn.
(13) He should wait a little. The time needed to walk two entrances, namely eight tefachim.
