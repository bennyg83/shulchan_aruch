1) [Se'if 1] Open into one another. The houses also open into the courtyards adjacent to them.
2) There. And each places his eruv in the middle house. Tosafos write that even if the eruv was placed in one of the houses adjacent to the courtyards, or in one of the houses of the courtyard, these three houses cannot receive the eruv, since they all become gatehouses for the courtyard whose residents convey their eruv through them to the other courtyard. Magen Avraham, subsection 1; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1; Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1. Ma'orei Or, section Be'er Sheva, folio 115a, writes that this interpretation is reasonable. The failure of the authors of the Shulchan Aruch to mention these words of Tosafos in Beis Yosef and Darkei Moshe was an oversight. See there. Atzei Almogim, siman 370, subsection 19, however, writes that according to Rabbeinu Yerucham, it appears that he disagrees with this ruling and maintains that only one house is deemed a gatehouse for each house. See there. Kerem Shlomo cites his words.
