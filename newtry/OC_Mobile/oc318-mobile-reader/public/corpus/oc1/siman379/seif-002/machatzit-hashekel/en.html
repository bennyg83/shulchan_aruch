(Siman kof bet) In the house etc. And if we consider it as a full house etc., so it should read — meaning that each house adjacent to a courtyard is considered a gatehouse for the courtyard adjacent to it, since this house did not give to the eruv and the adjacent courtyard placed its eruv in the second house. If so, the house that did not give to the eruv interrupts between it and its eruv, and it cannot bring its eruv to its courtyard. Rather, it is certainly considered a gatehouse for it — so how can it be considered a full house regarding the second courtyard? They are two contradictory matters:

And even though in siman 393 etc. — meaning that we rule that the house acquires the eruv. Therefore if the eruv was valid in the house and afterward was eaten, it acquired. And he wrote there: if an agent from two houses placed an eruv on behalf of one of the owners and it was eaten in the middle of the night, and at that same time when the first eruv was eaten he placed an eruv on behalf of the other — both acquired the eruv. For regarding the first that was eaten we say that night had already come and it was valid all night, and regarding the other who placed at that time we say that night had not yet come. And even though they are two contradictory matters, since this matter is d'rabbanan:

And in Yoreh De'ah siman 111 — meaning thus, that we should not ask why both are forbidden, for it is impossible to permit both since they are two contradictory matters; at any rate, one of them should be permitted, and say that the house in which the eruv was placed is a full house, and regarding the other it is truly not an eruv since the house interrupts, as Magen Avraham wrote above. On this see Yoreh De'ah siman 111, where he wrote there: if there are two pots, one of permitted and one of forbidden food, and before them two pieces, one of permitted and one of forbidden food, and one piece fell into each pot and it is not known into which pot the forbidden or permitted piece fell — if it was a d'rabbanan prohibition we say: the forbidden fell into forbidden — double prohibition — and the permitted into permitted. And he wrote there seif 5: but here there are two pots both of permitted food, and before them two pieces, one of permitted and one of forbidden, and one piece fell into each pot — both are forbidden, for either way one became forbidden, and whichever we remove, specifically regarding one of them that was forbidden we say the forbidden fell into forbidden and we do not add prohibition to it; which is not so when both are permitted, that at any rate one must be forbidden — what is the reason from this to that. Therefore both are forbidden. The same applies here. And see there Shach in the small seif 11:
