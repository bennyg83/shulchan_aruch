(In Shulchan Aruch se'if 2) A non-Jew who accepted, etc. And if he was pressed to build, see what we wrote above in siman 244, s.k. 3, and from Responsa Noda BiYehuda, part 1, siman 12; and see in Noda BiYehuda, Mahadura Tinyana, siman 103, regarding building a synagogue where they settled with the builders by contract and stipulated that they rest on Shabbos and Yom Tov, but regarding Chol HaMoed they did not stipulate, and if they wish to halt the craftsman from his laborers' work on Chol HaMoed they would need to pay their wages; and he concluded to permit, for among the public there is no suspicion, as Magen Avraham wrote in siman 244, only because of chillul Hashem before non-Jews; and on Chol HaMoed there is no chillul Hashem, since they see Jews occupied in commerce and other permitted melachos, and they know that this is forbidden to a Jew on Yom Tov, therefore they suppose that on Chol HaMoed there is no prohibition of melacha, etc.; and see there that he resolves the words of Magen Avraham from what Eliyah Rabbah and Tosafos Shabbos challenged against him. And see in Responsa Maharam [Rothenburg] in Bava Batra HaKetanimon, siman 142: that even if the non-Jews began to build before Yom Tov and do not want to stop, and if one does not let them finish they will not be finished, it is forbidden to let them finish on Chol HaMoed: see there; and Machatzit HaShekel and Toras Chaim brought it, for so wrote Shibolei HaLeket in the name of the Geonim.
