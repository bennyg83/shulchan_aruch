And we copied the language of the Gemara: Mar Zutra the son — Ravina built for him a stone house with builders from outside the techum. Rav Safra and Rav Huna bar Chinena happened by and did not go up to him, and some say he too did not enter inside. And Shmuel said: builders from outside the techum are permitted — an important person is different. And some say: he assisted — he gave straw alongside them. Rashi explained that he gave straw to the work, for since he assisted alongside them it is forbidden. And the Tur wrote: and even if the house was built improperly it is fitting to be stringent not to enter it. Beit Yosef wrote: since our Rabbi wrote anonymously "it is fitting to be stringent" and did not distinguish between an important person and one who is not important, it appears he holds that the language "assisted with straw" is the latter version, and if so it was built improperly. And since the Talmud did not say it is forbidden to enter it but only that these rabbis or Mar Zutra himself did not enter inside, it implies it was not a matter of law but a matter of piety, and this is what our Rabbi said "it is fitting to be stringent" — end of his words. Explanation of his words: the Tur holds that there is certainly a full prohibition if one assists by giving straw on Shabbat, and it was not necessary to write it entirely, but the Gemara teaches us regarding if b'dieved it was built improperly that one should forbid entering there; and on this Beit Yosef wrote that the Tur holds that the first language deals with when it was built permissibly and nevertheless one should be stringent with an important person. And accordingly, if it was built improperly the Gemara is not dealing with this — from this it is possible that it is certainly forbidden to enter by law, for from what source would the Talmud permit? And in the Gemara that says these rabbis did not enter, it implies that for others there is no prohibition — it deals with when it was built permissibly, and the Tur did not rule like this language from two proofs: one, that otherwise he should have ruled that if built improperly it is forbidden by law; second, that he should have distinguished between important and non-important even if built permissibly. Rather certainly he rules like the latter language of the Gemara that deals with when it was built improperly since he assisted with straw, and even so they said in the Gemara that specifically the rabbis were careful — it implies there is no full prohibition in entering from the side of law, and therefore the Tur ruled it is fitting to be stringent — this is Beit Yosef's intent, and for no reason did Magen Avraham write on Beit Yosef that his mishnah overpowered him in this, and in siman 244 I wrote more on this — see there.
