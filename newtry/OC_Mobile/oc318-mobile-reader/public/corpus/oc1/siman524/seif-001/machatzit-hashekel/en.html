(1) Magen Avraham wrote thus, etc. What the Pri Megadim wrote here are the words of Tosafot, etc., and they wrote: we do not bring to the chuppah. It seems the same applies that we do not bring to the chuppah, for entering the chuppah forbidden on Shabbat we learn from the law of yibum:

(2) And as Ran wrote, etc. But it is difficult, for explicitly in the chapter Naarah she'nis'patetah it permits bringing to the chuppah on Shabbat and Yom Tov, for they say there the handiwork of a minor girl belongs to her father since the Merciful One granted the father to give her to the chuppah, even though it is annulled from melacha. And they rejected there perhaps it speaks of bringing to the chuppah on Shabbat and Yom Tov — evidently permitted to bring on Shabbat and Yom Tov. And it seems we must say there it speaks of bringing on the nights of Shabbat and Yom Tov, for melacha is forbidden but chuppah is permitted, for even on Yom Tov it is permitted to make a chuppah since it is only derabbanan — a decree lest he write. And every derabbanan matter does not apply on Yom Tov, as written at the end of siman 542 — so it seems to me to resolve with difficulty. And see Tosafot per the second explanation on "on Shabbos and Yom Tov" — it appears it speaks on Shabbat itself, and from their words it seems permitted to make a chuppah on Shabbat, see there, and it requires study — up to here Pri Megadim. On this Magen Avraham wrote his first difficulty and the explanation he wrote is forced — they are the words of Tosafot there (but it is astonishing, for Tosafot's words were before his eyes since he cited them), and they wrote Tosafot on "when he gives her," etc.: even though it says in Moed Katan and "you shall rejoice on your festival" not with your wife — for we do not marry on the intermediate days because one does not mix joy with joy, and it is a solid derashah as said in Chagigah that it is d'oraisa. One may say here it speaks one hour before Yom Tov which is like Yom Tov for melacha purposes — addition of d'oraisa, etc., or marriage without a feast, etc., is not considered such joy that it is forbidden to marry on Yom Tov for this reason — up to here Tosafot:

(3) And it is simple it speaks, etc. With this he comes to reject Pri Megadim's words that from the second explanation of Tosafot it seems permitted to make chuppah on Shabbat and Yom Tov, and it requires study — meaning it is proven from the mishnah that bringing to chuppah is also forbidden like yibum appears. And therefore Magen Avraham wrote Tosafot speak of Torah law, for they address what the Talmud rejected the proof from the daughter's handiwork to her father since she has merit for him to give her to chuppah — if so the Talmud wanted to bring proof from a verse. On this the Talmud rejected perhaps he gave her on Shabbos and Yom Tov — even though forbidden derabbanan lest he write, nevertheless there is no proof from what the Torah granted him to give her to chuppah, for from the Torah it is permitted to bring to chuppah on Shabbat and Yom Tov. And furthermore Magen Avraham brought proof that Tosafot's words are Torah law, for otherwise why did they not simply challenge how he gave her on Shabbat — and it is an explicit mishnah in Beitzah 36b: everything for which one is liable on account of shevut on Shabbat one is liable on Yom Tov, etc., and these are for optional matters: we do not judge, perform kiddushin, chalitzah, yibum, etc. Rather they challenged from Moed Katan and wrote it is a solid derashah, etc., or that it addresses Torah law, for the Talmud's give-and-take is also Torah law. Therefore they could not challenge from mishnah Beitzah 36, for there it speaks of shevut. Therefore they needed to say Moed Katan is a solid derashah — i.e., d'oraisa and not mere support and derabbanan. Therefore Tosafot's explanation speaks of chuppah without a feast — for though derabbanan they agree it is forbidden lest he write (or acquisition as Yerushalmi) — nevertheless from Torah it is permitted and not diminished from the verse "rejoice on your festival," for marriage without feast has no such joy:
