(א) One does not — Taz. (I said to record here a wondrous matter although it is not relevant here: Psalms 104. "The wild animals break their thirst" — in some editions with sin and an error specifically in the right shin; and in Targum "yitberun tzachtehon," see there; see Radak. Efaim — branches or leaves; and Radak: aleph with chirik and yod with chirik; and in many manuscripts the aleph is erroneous with chirik, and the reason is that it is on the pattern of peraim; see Minchat Shai on the root ef'a. However, he showed me an innovation here — K"K Papda in the anthology of R' Shmuel ben Melekh z"l on Psalms 104: aleph with chirik and the yod changed to mem of the verb, and in Aramaic efiah fits well, see there. But per Concordance ef'a aleph root mem of verb is difficult; and in Aramaic Daniel 3 wrote efah hei mem of verb; if so there are versions aleph with chirik as Minchat Shai and mem yod with chirik as said — and it is wondrous in my eyes. And if we say yod in place of aleph as ayin tzvi'im II Samuel 2 — the distinction is the yod; and in Aramaic efah with hei per Mar Talmu. In Tzevaot "he" the aleph was changed to yod; however there its root is beth-hei; see from the above anthology; and see in Tanakh hollow letters. Since I have no knowledge in this wisdom except a little of the first beginnings, I did not see fit to extend on this.)

(2) One does not ride — lest one cut a vine branch; siman 339 and 305 seif 18. In my Maggid book I wrote the word "rider" and "leader," and it goes from its heat: riding in a wagon too — he sits in the wagon and another leads; Rashi Chullin 79a in Rif — a calf he was riding in, he does not sit; it may be explained Bava Metzia 10b "one who sits in a wagon" — that the law is exempt and derabbanan forbidden; if so, whence do we have signs of Torah? (see from this); therefore he explained rider and leader are certainly liable; and in this "and they mounted the ark" II Samuel 6 — meaning the bearers bear it; and therefore Uzza was struck, see there and understand.
