One does not climb on a tree — a decree lest one detach, and it is a Torah prohibition. One does not ride — lest one cut a vine branch to strike it. One does not clap the hand. One does not slap on the thigh. One does not dance with the foot — all of these are for joy; a decree lest one repair a musical instrument. All of these the mishnah calls shevut for which there is no mitzvah aspect. And these are "for optional matters" — meaning they are close to mitzvah and the Sages forbade them on Yom Tov. We do not judge. We do not perform kiddushin. We do not perform chalitzah. We do not perform yibum. All of them have the reason lest he write a court ruling, or a betrothal document, or a chalitzah document, or a ketubah for yibum. And these are for mitzvah: We do not dedicate to the Temple. We do not impose cherem, etc. The reason is acquisition and sale — similar to sale that transfers from one's domain to Temple domain; and sale and purchase are forbidden from the Torah as written "from your pleasures." Or, sale and purchase leads to writing a bill of sale. And if you ask — let it be decree upon decree? It is all one decree. And they challenged: we do not judge — but that is a mitzvah! Rashi explained: why does it call it optional? Because some are more pressing than others. We do not perform kiddushin — but he performs a mitzvah, marrying a woman to be fruitful and multiply! Because he has a wife and children. We do not perform chalitzah and yibum — but he performs a mitzvah! Because there is an older brother and the mitzvah is on the elder to perform yibum. Tosafot wrote per Rashi's explanation that they only challenged what is not counted with the conclusion that they are mitzvah. And they answered that it is not so much a mitzvah as in the conclusion. And accordingly, also where there is someone more pressing than him, one does not judge. And likewise with "we do not perform kiddushin" — it implies that even if he has no wife and children it is forbidden. Rabbenu Tam explained that this is the meaning: why did they decree on these? And they answered: when he has a wife and children; but if he has no wife and children it is permitted to betroth, for he performs a mitzvah. The Tur brought the dispute of Rashi and Rabbenu Tam regarding "we do not judge" and "we do not perform kiddushin," but regarding chalitzah and yibum he wrote anonymously and did not write that for Rabbenu Tam it is specifically when there is an older brother there. This is astonishing — why did he not also write regarding those where there is dispute? Beit Yosef left it requiring further study. It appears to me regarding chalitzah and yibum that Rabbenu Tam agrees with Rashi that there is prohibition in all cases, for they are similar to sale and purchase — the yevamah is acquired through her handiwork; and likewise in chalitzah she acquires herself and leaves the harm of yibum. And regarding those of sale and purchase we said in the conclusion forbidden in all cases. Whereas regarding "we do not perform kiddushin" one may say they did not decree when he has no wife and children because perhaps another will precede him — not so in chalitzah and yibum. With this I reconcile what Tosafot wrote on Yerushalmi Yoma: R' Mona said these who bring in widows must dress them beforehand so it not be like acquiring acquisition on Shabbat — and it implies forbidden in all cases even when he has no wife and children. If so it is difficult for Rabbenu Tam who permits betrothal in this case. Per what I wrote it is fine: kiddushin is different because of "lest another precede him" — not so entering, which is marriage and comes after kiddushin. And one may say regarding chalitzah and yibum there is also sale and purchase — why did the Gemara say the reason is lest he write and not sale and purchase as in the conclusion? One may say he holds like the latter explanation of Rashi above that sale and purchase itself is forbidden due to a decree lest he write; and the mishnah included in this sugya only "lest he write." If so, the Tur properly wrote anonymously chalitzah and yibum — for the Gemara did not establish it regarding an older brother there, only so one not say why the mishnah called them optional and not mitzvah; and in this Rabbenu Tam agrees with Rashi that it must be explained thus — very correct. The Tur wrote: and all of them, whether done inadvertently or intentionally, what was done was done. And so Rif and Rosh brought in the name of Yerushalmi; and they wrote explicitly: one who transgressed and acquired land or movable property on Shabbat — his acquisition is an acquisition. From this is proof for what is written in Choshen Mishpat siman 203 that we do not hold like what Rama wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 230 regarding one who swore not to sell and sold — it is not a sale; see there Yoreh De'ah siman 230 and siman 228 seif 35 what is written on this. Again I saw in Rashal in chapter Hamashilin siman 8 that he wrote in the name of Hagahat Semak: one who swore not to sell and sold — it does not help, as we say in chapter Ha'ishah she'nafelu — once the Sages said not to sell, if he sells it does not help. And presumably rabbinic statement is not stricter than his oath; and he wrote there one must distinguish between that case of selling on Shabbat where his act helps, versus prohibition of oath that lost its power and right. And further Shabbat was given to be overridden for a sick person — not so with an oath — up to here what he wrote initially in the name of Hagahat Semak. Proof from chapter Ha'ishah she'nafelu — there is no proof at all, for it is difficult from here where there is rabbinic prohibition in what they forbade here in this siman and nevertheless it helps if one transgressed. Rather necessarily there is a distinction: there the Sages enacted their repair that he should not sell the property for the woman's benefit; and you might say if he sells it helps — what did their enactment accomplish? Certainly each would sell since he has no violation on him; rather the Sages gave the woman enhanced power over this property and he nullifies the power. Not so regarding a matter that is a violation like here — certainly the Sages' enactment helps that every person will beware of doing a rabbinic prohibition; but if one transgressed, the violation hangs on his head and his acts stand. What Rashal wrote to distinguish Shabbat from oath — I do not understand the distinction here; what difference is there between this and that? On the contrary we find greater stringency in inclusive prohibition — e.g. Shabbat which includes every person, versus oath which applies only to this one who swore. Many times we find prohibition does not apply upon prohibition; and on an inclusive prohibition it applies properly. Also what he wrote that Shabbat may be overridden — I do not know what relevance this has here. Furthermore sale made in the prohibition mentioned in prohibition of interest Yoreh De'ah siman 175 seif 8 is its refutation.
