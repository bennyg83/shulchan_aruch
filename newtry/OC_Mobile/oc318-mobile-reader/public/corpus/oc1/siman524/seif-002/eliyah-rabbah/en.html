[2] And we do not perform kiddushin, etc. Beit Yosef wrote: Rambam appears to have ruled like Rashi that even if he has no wife and children it is forbidden, since it writes anonymously "we do not perform kiddushin" — up to here; and Darkei Moshe and Rashal followed him. One may say he did not see Rambam's Mishnah commentary where Rambam explained explicitly like Rashi. And on Rama's responsum siman 125 this is more astounding. Also what they want to explain — that Rashi too agrees he permits when he has no children — is not so from the Rambam cited above; see there and investigate. Also what he challenged on Rashi's explanation, that he explained on "we do not perform kiddushin" and did not explain earlier on "we do not judge" — I was astonished; he meant to say Rashi explained on "we do not judge," etc., as explained on Beitzah 36, where Rashi afterward marked on "we do not perform kiddushin"; and so Tosafot there, and Ran explains: for Rabbenu Tam, who permits when he has no wife and children, the same law permits chalitzah and yibum when there is no older brother; and so in the sugya in the Talmud both are paired; not like Taz who divides that only for kiddushin they permitted lest another precede him — not so. Also, if so, Tosafot would not need to establish the Yerushalmi "those who bring in widows need to dress them beforehand" applies when he has wife and children — even without, it is permitted, since "lest he precede" does not apply once he has already married; and Semag and other poskim wrote thus. What Beit Yosef wondered on the Tur, that he did not also write yibum and chalitzah — it seems to me no difficulty, since he already agreed it is forbidden even for kiddushin when he has no wife and children; the same applies to yibum and chalitzah — one reason: acquisition. "We do not judge" he wrote for another reason — lest he write — investigate.
