The fruits. This means the beginning of eating the fruits, not the beginning of the meal. The case is where they were in the middle of the meal, and the meal had initially been established on meat or the like. In the middle of the meal, fruit was brought for eating together with bread, and they ate some with bread, intending also to eat more later with bread. They need not recite a blessing, because the fruits were initially brought to accompany the bread and are eaten with bread both at the beginning and at the end. They therefore join the rest of the meal, since they were initially brought for the needs of the meal and accompany the bread at both the beginning and the end. However, if they were not initially brought to accompany the bread, but only for dessert, then even if he eats them with bread at the beginning and end, if he eats them without bread in the middle he must recite a blessing, since they were not initially brought to accompany the bread. This is Rabbeinu Yonah's view. There are, however, other opinions. Therefore Hagahos Maimoniyos writes that a God-fearing person who is served fruit in the middle of the meal to accompany the bread should first eat a little without bread and recite a blessing, and afterward eat it either with or without bread. Bach writes likewise, and Magen Avraham rules accordingly, as does Perach Shoshan, principle 1, siman 6. Taz also discusses this at length and concludes that it is better to eat the fruit first without bread and recite a blessing. All this applies where the meal was established on other foods, so that the fruit is not considered to have come because of the meal. But one who establishes his meal on fruit is governed by seif 3: he should first eat it with bread, and he will then not need to recite a blessing on what he later eats without bread. See Be'er Esek, siman 36.
