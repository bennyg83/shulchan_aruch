And even if he eats, etc. This is Tur's view, see there in his language who wrote and wrote in name of Rosh, and there is room to doubt, etc., but when they bring him fruit in middle of meal, etc., as Beit Yosef wrote; and although Rosh did not write this explicitly there, it appears to our teacher that since he wrote one cannot prove from there, etc., evidently he holds they require blessing before them, end quote. His words are not understood—for since he wrote one cannot prove, who proves his view and how; it appears the words are founded and we still scrutinize what Rosh wrote; however one cannot prove from here that if they brought fruit to eat his bread with it, etc., and he should have said however one cannot prove that if he ate fruit in middle of meal one need not bless before them—for this discusses what he wrote before regarding without bread, etc., meaning without bread at all he should have said also here one cannot prove eating fruit without bread at all needs no blessing before, from that he did not tell him eat without bread within meal. It appears Rosh's suggestion is: initially he wrote there is room to doubt, etc., his intent when eating fruit without bread when establishing meal on other species—it is plain to me he must bless before them as Rabbenu Peretz, and even if he eats some with bread nevertheless what he will eat without bread is not helped except when establishing meal on fruit he doubts if what he eats with bread helps for what without bread; on this he wrote the reasoning is it helps well since main establishment was on this—in this he has no proof only reasoning; but there is seemingly difficulty on these words from Yerushalmi—for this he wrote there is no difficulty from Yerushalmi, for Yerushalmi said like Rabbenu Peretz that if eating fruit without bread after meal must bless before and after; he wrote that seemingly one could say on what I doubt when establishing on fruit, but when establishing on other species but they brought to eat with bread in middle of meal and he ate little without bread—it is plain he must bless before as Rabbenu Peretz—and therefore you must distinguish this from Rabbenu Peretz—for here he should have said eat them within meal and bless before, and therefore no need to bless before, and difficulty on Rabbenu Peretz who requires blessing before—and therefore you must say here is different for he ate some with bread, therefore must bless also on what he eats without bread, and this contradicts what he wrote no need to bless—therefore he said one cannot prove so and not challenge him, for here possibly he must bless and Yerushalmi did not challenge also on birkat acharonah that he would be obligated, namely after meal; now Tur's words are clear: but when they bring, etc., this is plain as Rosh's words. Difficulty: why Rosh wrote there is room to doubt and he resolves from his own reasoning—yet it is proven from Yerushalmi that Rav Huna did not bless at all on dates he ate because this was his main relish, and if thought he needs blessing before he should at least eat within meal and bless before; rather certainly no blessing needed; and he told him that initially Rosh thought here is only reasoning, and after bringing Yerushalmi he said to his words in truth from Yerushalmi, and retracted from what he said from reasoning side—this is not one, for Rosh's language does not imply so, and moreover Tur wrote there is room to doubt and brought reasoning—he should have resolved from Yerushalmi; it appears Rosh also doubted Yerushalmi how the incident was—possible to say Rav Huna blessed truly before but not after, and on this he was challenged that he should have eaten after meal and gain also birkat acharonah, and he answered main relish is on fruit—how can I leave fruit after meal to gain one blessing when I need this for primary eating; therefore also when establishing on fruit needs blessing before within meal, or he did not bless at all even before and was challenged on both blessings and answered on both: main relish is fruit, no blessing needed even before; therefore Rosh was forced to reasoning; and why did Rosh not reject cannot prove, etc., from this that we said perhaps Rav Huna blessed truly before—as one can say here he says cannot prove so for to bless also at end he said quench after your food meaning first blessing possibly was truly but on second he challenges—all clear in seif 4; and rises from Tur's view per Rosh: when establishing on fruit and eats some with bread and some without, exempt also what without bread whether beginning or end.

And there are those who disagree. This is Rabbenu Yonah's view: Beit Yosef wrote in his name that when establishing on fruit and eats at first specifically with bread, then exempt if he eats afterward without bread, but not reverse—if he eats first without bread he must bless; Rabbeinu Yitzchak learned this from what R' Yonah wrote at first: however if at first he had nothing else to dip bread except these fruits alone and begins to dip bread in them and afterward eats without bread—even if at end he does not eat with bread at all he does not bless on them since from this he made them primary for his dipping from start and began eating from them with bread, exempt afterward, end quote—Beit Yosef deduced that at first which R' Yonah mentioned is specifically; therefore wrote in his name if he began without bread and afterward with bread he must bless first—and great difficulty, for afterward R' Yonah brings Yerushalmi cited above that explains Rabbi Chiya ate dates after he ate bread and was not blessing even before them and concludes his reason: main relish is fruit, meaning main eating is they that from start I established on them although now I eat without bread no blessing needed, for when we say require blessing before them it is when they did not bring them to dip bread at all, end quote—R' Yonah did not require blessing before in this but without considering them with bread—implies if he eats afterward with bread but started somewhat without bread no blessing needed since from start he thought to eat with bread and they are primary of meal; also difficulty: why didn't Rabbi Chiya bless at all—he should have eaten little first without bread and be obligated in blessing before; also difficulty from what R' Yonah wrote afterward reconciling what world asks on Passover night why we do not bless on second dipping borei pri ha'adamah like first—the reason that initially intent was to eat it knowing he is obligated in eating, it was things coming because of meal, etc., and from this reason they blessed on matzah to eat matzah and not shehakol because secondary to bread he ate first, etc.—in maror he does not eat at all with his bread on Passover; possible he eats bread afterward; nevertheless start was without bread for he wrote secondary to bread he ate first, namely before his blessing on matzah, and nevertheless it helps—all the more here that what he eats afterward with bread helps and initially intent to eat and this is primary of meal he need not bless on what he eats little first without bread; and we should have said even if he did not eat bread at all with fruits in this he is exempt since this is primary of meal, that what he ate bread first drags after like maror and matzah; regarding wine also explicit there that properly should not bless on it dragged after bread already eaten but wine is important and establishes blessing for itself, but R' Yonah holds all these differ for evident establishment since Torah obligation on them such as maror and matzah, and regarding wine all know must drink after eating, unlike here establishing on fruit—on contrary most of world do not establish meal on it, we find some poskim hold batel da'at with everyone—therefore must clarify here to eat little specifically with fruits; Rav Huna also ate afterward with bread though first without bread and relied on bread eaten first. And what he wrote from his name and began to eat with bread, etc.—not precise that beginning required, rather he states extra that beginning alone suffices for what he eats afterward without bread, for possible to say afterward he thought not to eat with bread at all—nevertheless helps, all the more if eats afterward with bread we see intent still on bread and what he ate from it first without bread drags after this; clear Rabbenu Yonah does not disagree with Tur. General rule in this law: all fruit eaten with bread—plain no blessing at all; fruit without bread within meal for mere dessert—plain needs blessing before, not because of meal; but if eats some with bread there is distinction: if eats first with bread then without—must bless before unless he thought to eat afterward more with bread, and even if chance he won't eat afterward with bread we follow his thought; but if eats first without bread he must bless—this establishes meal on other things automatically fruits are not because of meal. Beit Yosef wrote: proper to be concerned when bringing fruit in meal that beginning of eating be with bread, end quote—meaning he should think to eat also afterward with bread, otherwise obligated to bless when eating afterward without bread, which is unacceptable: first perhaps he won't think and eat afterward without blessing improperly; second certainly he will eat afterward without bread for he does not establish meal on them—in my humble opinion better eat first without bread and bless on them, for per this view obligated in blessing—why prevent himself from blessing; Yerushalmi challenged Rabbi Chiya he should have acted to obligate in blessing—and this specifically when establishing on other things as we usually do most times; but one who establishes on fruit should eat first with bread and then need not bless on what eats afterward without bread—as above, clear and bright in seif 4.
