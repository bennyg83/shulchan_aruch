Bread. See Baer Heitev. Regarding what he writes, “unless it is Shabbos or Yom Tov,” see Yad Ephraim. See Even HaOzer, siman 174, who concludes that bread exempts it, since in most cases bread is primary and there is no dispute. See Birkei Yosef, who writes that the ruling applies specifically to cooked vegetables. Lettuce, squash, and raw cucumbers, however, are not eaten to accompany bread, and one recites a blessing over them. Responsa Beit David writes likewise and records that this was the practice there. According to this, even if one ate a cooked dish made from these vegetables to accompany bread, and afterward the same kinds were brought before him as fruit, he must recite a blessing over them. One cannot reject this because he already filled his stomach with them; see there. In our countries, however, there are poor people who eat bread in the morning together with radish, onions, and raw cucumbers. It appears that if one first eats them to accompany the bread, then even if he later eats them without bread he need not recite a blessing, as with other fruit and as explained in the following seif katan.
