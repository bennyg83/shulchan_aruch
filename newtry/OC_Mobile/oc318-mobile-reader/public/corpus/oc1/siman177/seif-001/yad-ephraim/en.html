In Magen Avraham, seif katan 1: This requires study; therefore he should not recite a blessing, etc. Some emend the text to read, "Therefore he should recite a blessing," etc., except on Shabbos and Yom Tov. The reason is that on Shabbos and Yom Tov one is obligated to eat bread, and the bread is then primary. This is also implied in siman 174, seif katan 12, where Magen Avraham appears to hold that if one eats only a little bread, he should not recite a blessing over the other foods. See there, where he refers to siman 212, meaning that there it is explained that whenever bread is secondary, no blessing is recited over it, while if one eats it first, one recites Shehakol over it. This also appears from Even HaOzer at the beginning of siman 174, who writes that Magen Avraham ruled accordingly. It likewise appears from what Magen Avraham writes in siman 168, seif katan 22: "And that which the students of Rabbeinu Yonah wrote," etc., "for the fruit." This necessarily refers to such a case, for otherwise bread would not exempt the fruit, as stated in siman 177. End quote. This too implies that in such a case one should recite a blessing over the fruit. Yet the arrangement of Magen Avraham's words requires study, for he writes considerations in favor of saying that the bread exempts the fruit and leaves the matter requiring study; how, then, does he conclude, "Therefore," etc.? In my humble opinion, perhaps the text should read: "Therefore he should not recite a blessing over them, but should eat them with the bread," meaning that he should make them secondary to the bread. His words "except on Shabbos and Yom Tov" mean that siman 215 states that one may leave fruit until after the meal in order to recite blessings over it and thereby complete one hundred blessings. In that case one may rely on the first opinion, recite a blessing over the fruit independently, and not eat it with bread. Another possibility is that in the phrase "and not over the bread," the words "and not over" are a scribal error and the text should read "and to eat the bread." This is a common copying error. It would mean that he should not act in this way, reciting a blessing over the fruit and then eating the bread, for although the amount of bread is small, it may nevertheless exempt the fruit. On Shabbos and Yom Tov, however, when he is obligated to eat bread, he may act this way and rely on the first opinion. This too requires study.
