Without bread they require no blessing. This is because they are secondary to the bread, as stated in siman 212. It therefore appears to me that if one does not desire to eat bread and eats only a little bread, reciting Hamotzi over it, the bread does not exempt the other foods. We do not rule like Rabbeinu Chananel, who holds that bread exempts every type of food. Rather, only when one establishes his meal on bread do we say that all the foods are brought because of the bread. When he does not desire to eat bread, it does not exempt them, and all the more so if he eats less than a kezayis. It is possible, however, that since it is customary to establish a meal on bread, the bread exempts them. This requires study. Therefore, he should not bless over them or over the bread, except on Shabbos and Yom Tov.
Such as figs. The same applies to anything brought for dessert, even if it was before him when he recited Hamotzi, and even to cooked fruits. Bread does not exempt them because they are unrelated to the bread. Shelah writes likewise.
And if at the beginning. This law is from the students of Rabbeinu Yonah. They explain that the case is where people were in the middle of a meal whose initial establishment was on meat or the like. During the meal, fruit was brought to accompany the bread, and they ate some with bread. They now eat some without bread, while intending still to eat more with bread afterward. No blessing is required, because the fruit was initially brought to accompany the bread and is eaten both initially and finally with bread. It therefore joins the rest of the meal, since it was brought from the outset for the meal and is used to accompany the bread both initially and finally. If, at the outset, he had no other foods with which to accompany the bread, and afterward he eats the fruit without bread, even if he does not eat it with bread at the end, he does not bless over it, because he made this fruit the principal accompaniment to his bread. If it was not initially brought to accompany the bread and he eats it independently, he must bless. This means that when the fruit is brought for dessert and not to accompany the bread, even if he ate it initially and finally with bread, if he eats it in the middle without bread he must bless, because it was not brought to accompany the bread. Most authorities agree with this. There are, however, other opinions. Hagahos Maimoniyos therefore writes that a God-fearing person who is served fruit during the meal to accompany the bread should first eat a little without bread and bless over it, and may afterward eat it either with bread or without bread. This implies that even where he eats it initially and finally with bread, doubt remains. Bach writes likewise, and Agudah rules that where one eats fruit with bread during the meal, or eats an apple pastry, it is proper first to eat a little fruit without bread and bless over it, since the fruit is not the primary component of his meal. Hagahos Tashbetz states that according to the text inside, one must remove a little fruit from the filling and bless over it. Maharam of Rothenburg says that one need not bless over the fruit in the filling even if one recited Hamotzi over the pastry, because the flour is primary. Meaning, not only when the blessing is Borei Minei Mezonos, as stated in siman 168, is no blessing required over the fruit, but even if one recited Hamotzi over the pastry, no separate blessing is required over the fruit, because the flour is primary and the bread exempts it. So it appears in Tashbetz; see what I wrote in siman 168. Regarding cooked fruits by themselves, even when cooked with meat, if the meal was not initially established on them, there is doubt whether a blessing is required. Some say that even when they are eaten with bread, a blessing is required, as Agudah and Kol Bo write; see Beit Yosef. It is therefore proper first to eat them without bread and bless over them. Semak and Agur, siman 232, write that Agur’s father practiced this way, and Knesses HaGedolah writes likewise in the name of Rabbi Shimshon in the name of Rashbetz. The abridged rulings of the Rosh, however, remain uncertain whether a blessing is required, since they are brought for the needs of the meal and not for dessert. It therefore states that one should sit before washing and bless over the fruit. He must eat less than the minimum amount so as not to incur an obligation of a final blessing, as stated in siman 174, seif 7. The Tur in siman 473 likewise writes that according to the view that no blessing is required during the meal, a final blessing must be recited over what was eaten before the meal. Nevertheless, the Tur writes as a certainty that if one eats the fruit without bread, a blessing is required because the meal was not initially established upon it. See seif 3.
