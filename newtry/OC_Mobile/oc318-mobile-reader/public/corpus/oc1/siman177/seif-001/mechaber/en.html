Things that come during the meal and after the meal — what is their law. Containing 5 sections: Things that come during the meal — if they are things that come on account of the meal, meaning things that are typically eaten as part of a fixed meal to accompany the bread, such as meat, fish, eggs, vegetables, cheese, porridge, and various salted foods — even if one eats them without bread they do not require a blessing beforehand, as the HaMotzi exempts them, nor afterward, as Grace After Meals exempts them. If they are things that do not come on account of the meal, meaning things that are not typically eaten as part of a fixed meal to accompany the bread, such as figs, grapes, and all kinds of fruit (see above 168:8) — if one eats them without bread they require a blessing beforehand, as HaMotzi does not exempt them since they are not part of the main meal; but they do not require a blessing afterward, since having come during the meal, Grace After Meals exempts them. If at the beginning of his eating he ate the fruits with bread, and at the end he ate bread with them, then even if in the interim he ate some without bread, they do not require a blessing even beforehand.
