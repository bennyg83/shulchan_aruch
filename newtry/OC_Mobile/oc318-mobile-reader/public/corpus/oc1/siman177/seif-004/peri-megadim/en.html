Since See Taz. Anything eaten with bread requires no blessing beforehand, even where the meal was not established on it. When eaten without bread, there is a distinction: if the meal was established on it, there is no need to eat it with bread at the end; if the meal was not established on it, it must be eaten with bread both at the beginning and at the end, as in seif 1, letters 1 and 3. What he writes regarding spirits before eating means after Hamotzi. Before Hamotzi, one must recite blessings before and after them. See Beit Yosef, citing Chiddushei HaRosh in rule 4, and the words of the Rosh and Darchei Moshe, where the Rosh discusses a case after Hamotzi. It follows that when spirits are drunk after the meal, an after-blessing is recited. See siman 474, Magen Avraham, letter 11. Even according to our practice, under which we do not withdraw from bread as in seif 1, nevertheless spirits after eating are consumed only for digestion, etc. See Eliyah Rabbah, letter 11, that if one drank wine, it exempts even spirits, since wine exempts all beverages. Regarding dipping one's bread in spirits, he expresses doubt, for if so, one would need to eat it with bread both at the beginning and at the end, as in seif 1. Radish, salad, horseradish, and the like are subordinate to the meat, and the meat is subordinate to the bread. See Magen Avraham, siman 174, letter 9, and siman 212.
