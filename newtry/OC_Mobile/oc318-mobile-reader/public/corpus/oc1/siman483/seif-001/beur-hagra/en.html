Se'if 1, One who etc. sanctifies. As it says there on folio 106b, "at times that it is beloved," etc. And even though he changes the order, that is of no concern to us; and see Tosafos folio 101, end of s.v. "R' Yehuda."
That he recites the blessing, etc. There on folio 114a, and according to the first explanation he recites the blessing, etc.; and the same law applies to bread, for it stands in place of wine; and there in the Gemara: "because," etc., another explanation, etc.; and these reasons apply to bread as well.
And he breaks. And this is specifically here, where he recites the blessing over the broken piece; but on other Shabbasos and Yamim Tovim, not, as Rashbam wrote on folio 106b, s.v. "that it is beloved," etc.; also he should not break etc.; and see Magen Avraham.
And he places. Like with the cup, as is written in siman 271, se'if 10, and it requires etc.
And he recites the blessing, etc. For these reasons do not apply; and on the contrary, here the reasons of Beis Shammai apply; and Kiddush is also more frequent than matzah.
And afterwards he eats. For before the matzah it is impossible, since it is forbidden to taste before Kiddush.
And he removes etc. Go'al Yisrael. This is to exclude the view of the Ra'ah, who wrote that one does not recite Go'al Yisrael, because it was instituted only over the cup. But that is not so, as it says there on folio 117b: it is learned from this regarding Birkas HaMazon etc., the four cups etc., each one etc.
And he recites the blessing etc., and afterwards. For it was instituted only after the Haggadah, just as if he had wine; and as it says, "at the time when matzah and maror" etc. And even the blessing over the matzah would properly be recited here, except as it says there on folio 115a: after he has filled his belly etc.
Without a blessing. That is to say, even though he interrupted between the blessing with the Haggadah; and see siman 475.
And in places etc., and some say etc., and it seems to me. As it says there: some say etc., and some say etc., and according to the Rosh etc., since it is impossible otherwise; and that is what is written here: "and it seems to me, since it is impossible to fulfill the obligation of four cups."
