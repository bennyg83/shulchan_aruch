One who has no wine. See above, siman 472, s.k. 21: if he has no wine, or he swore not to drink wine, he fulfills his obligation with raisin water, if there is moisture in them when one presses them, even without soaking them in water; see there. And see the Tur, that one must trouble oneself and spend much money in order to buy wine, just as one is obligated to pursue a lulav and esrog; and see above, siman 656.
He sanctifies over bread. And Magen Avraham wrote: one who has only one cup should take it for Kiddush; if he has two, he should take them for Kiddush and Birkas HaMazon; and if he has three, he should use them for the first three cups; end quote. And it seems to me that if it is the first night and he has only two cups, he should fulfill one cup for the second night, as above in siman 482 regarding matzah.
And he breaks. Meaning that he first breaks the middle one and takes half of it for the afikoman, as above in siman 473 and siman 475; and so wrote Magen Avraham.
Until Go'al Yisrael. But he should not say "Yehalelucha" as the closing of Hallel, because it was instituted over the cup; and that we say Go'al Yisrael is because it stands in place of "Who performed miracles," as is written in siman 473, s.k. 2; and see Magen Avraham.
Where they are accustomed to drink. This implies specifically in a place where they are accustomed to drink it, for then it is like chamar medinah; and as the Rav concludes explicitly: if it is chamar medinah. And the Bach's intention as well is like what the Rav ruled in the Shulchan Aruch, and this is easily understood; and unlike Magen Avraham, who wrote that even if all year their custom is not to drink [it], nevertheless, since they drink of them on Pesach, it is called chamar medinah; and the essence of his foundation is from the words of the Bach; but in truth the Bach agrees explicitly with the Rav's view here; and if so, there is no foundation for his words at all, and one should not be more lenient than the Rav was lenient here; end quote.
