(1) The custom. This is based upon a tradition transmitted among scribes from one person to another. Tur.
(2) In the number of lines. They likewise possess a received tradition concerning which words begin the lines in the arm tefillin and head tefillin, as explained in Tur and Beit Yosef. Nowadays scribes are not exacting about the words that begin the lines, and alter them as circumstances require. One who wishes to be exact should not greatly stretch or compress letters merely to align the beginnings of the lines, because this is not beautiful for the tefillin. A small adjustment is permitted. Peri Megadim.
(3) If he changed it. This applies both to the arm tefillin and to the head tefillin. If the only parchment available for the arm tefillin is too short and narrow to write seven lines unless the script is made extremely fine, it appears preferable to alter the number of lines. See Mordechai in Hilchos Ketanos, at the end of Hilchos Tefillin, cited above in siman 32. Included in “This is my God and I will beautify Him” is writing somewhat substantial letters so that they will not quickly become erased. Indeed, we plainly observe that the very fine script used by some scribes nowadays, due to our many sins, frequently contains many defects even from the outset, because the letters could not be formed according to law on account of their smallness. In most such passages many of the crowns required upon the letters of Shatnez Getz by the law of the Gemara are missing. Some early authorities invalidate such writing even after the fact. One who guards his soul should not purchase such passages for his tefillin unless he first has them examined with exceptional care and knows who wrote them. Passages of this kind are very often written by youths who do not yet know the laws of writing sifrei Torah, tefillin, and mezuzos.
