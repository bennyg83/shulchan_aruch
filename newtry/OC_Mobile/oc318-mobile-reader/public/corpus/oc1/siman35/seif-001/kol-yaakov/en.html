1. The custom concerning the lines, etc. This follows the received teaching of the Arizal in Shaar HaKavanos, discourse 1 concerning tefillin. Regarding the division of the line-openings, see Tur and Beis Yosef. We shall record the division written by the Arizal there, for this is our custom. In the head tefillin: The four lines of Kadesh begin Vayedaber, Mizeh, Bachodesh, and Betzeisi. The four lines of Vehayah Ki Yeviacha begin Vehayah, Vechol Peter, Hashem MiMitzrayim, and Peter Rechem. The four lines of Shema begin Shema, Nafshecha, Levanecha, and Le'os. The four lines of Vehayah Im Shamoa begin Vehayah, Venasati, Yevulah, and Beshivtecha. In the arm tefillin: The seven lines of Kadesh begin Vayedaber, Es Hayom, Bachodesh, Zavas, Es Shivas, Hashem Li, and Hashem MiMitzrayim. The seven lines of Vehayah Ki Yeviacha are listed as Vehayah, Rechem, Ve'im Lo, Zos Leshalchenu, LaHashem Kol, and Einecha. The seven lines of Shema begin Shema, Es, Me'odecha, Hayom, Beveisecha, Le'os, and Al Mezuzos. The seven lines of Vehayah Im Shamoa begin Vehayah, Uvechol Nafshechem, Ve'achalta, Hashem Bachem, Asher Hashem, Bein Eineichem, and Beisecha.
We shall now explain the intentions of the passages and lines, and the writing of the Divine Names, from the Arizal, according to the order arranged by the Rashash.
In the generality of the four letters of the Name Havayah, he should intend toward the light of Ein Sof, which extends from level to level and builds and rectifies these aspects through the power of the Botzina DeKardenusa.
In the upper point of the yod he should intend toward the unfolding of the light of the concealments, Adam Kadmon, Atik, the keter of Arich Anpin, and the keter of Abba.
In the yod as a whole he should intend toward Atik, Arich Anpin, and Mocha Setima'ah.
In the three points of the yod , regarding the upper point he should intend toward the keter and the daas, tiferes, and yesod of Abba, formed from the keter and the daas, tiferes, and yesod of the chochmah of the aspect of Ban within MaH; toward the keter of Imma, formed from the keter of the binah of MaH; and toward the keter and the daas, tiferes, and yesod of Yisrael Saba, formed from the tiferes and yesod of the aspect of Ban within MaH.
In the second, middle point he should intend toward the chochmah, chesed, and netzach of Abba, formed from the chochmah, chesed, and netzach of the chochmah of the aspect of Ban within MaH.
He should also intend toward the chochmah, chesed, and netzach of Yisrael Saba, formed from the chochmah and netzach of the aspect of Ban within MaH.
In the third point he should intend toward the binah, gevurah, and hod of Abba, formed from the binah, gevurah, and hod of the chochmah of the binah of MaH. He should also intend toward the binah, gevurah, and hod of Yisrael Saba, formed from the gevurah and hod of the binah of MaH. In the two letters vav and dalet that fill yod, he should intend toward the ten sefirot of Imma. The letter vav represents the upper nine sefirot, formed from the chesed, gevurah, tiferes, netzach, hod, and yesod of the chochmah of Ban. The letter dalet is malchus, formed from the malchus of the chochmah of Ban. All this is the right line of Arich Anpin and is the Name Av, spelled Yod-Vav-Dalet, Heh-Yod, Vav-Yod-Vav, Heh-Yod .
In the first heh of Havayah he should intend toward the generality of the first and second partzufim of Yisrael Saba and Tevunah. They are formed from the binah and malchus of the binah of MaH and from the six extremities and malchus of the binah of Ban, and they clothe the left side of Arich Anpin from the throat to the navel. This is the Name SaG, spelled Yod-Vav-Dalet, Heh-Yod, Vav-Alef-Vav, Heh-Yod .
In the vav he should intend toward the six extremities of MaH and Ban, which are Zeir Anpin. This is the Name MaH, spelled Yod-Vav-Dalet, Heh-Alef, Vav-Alef-Vav, Heh-Alef .
In the final heh he should intend toward the malchus of MaH and Ban, which is Nukva. This is the Name Ban, spelled Yod-Vav-Dalet, Heh-Heh, Vav-Vav, Heh-Heh .
He should also intend that the yod is the generality of Abba, while the lower point of the yod alludes to and includes Imma.
The two letters vav and dalet are Zeir Anpin and Nukva, still concealed within the drop of Abba. He should also intend that the letter yod includes keter, chochmah, and binah; the letter vav represents the seven lower sefirot; and the dalet is malchus. He should also intend that the letter yod represents the ten sefirot of direct light, while vav and dalet represent the ten sefirot of reflected light. He should also intend toward the five chasadim and five gevuros within the daas of Zeir Anpin. See Shemen Sasson, part 3, on discourse 1 concerning tefillin; Nahar Shalom, page 5, column 3 in the newer edition; and what the Rashash writes in Shaar Anach, chapter 6. One should also study what is written below in siman 276, letter 6, regarding the order of drawing down the letters of the Name during writing and the intention recorded there. Both teachings are the words of the living God. The intention recorded here is more detailed, and I have written it for one who possesses broader knowledge of the teachings of the Arizal.
A person must be careful when writing the Divine Name not to interrupt, whether in thought, speech, action, or deed. Such an interruption causes a blemish above and below, in accordance with the intentions mentioned in the laws of tefillin, separating and cutting the plantings, Heaven forbid, for everything is connected like a flame bound to a coal. See Matzas Shmurim in the laws of writing, and also my sacred work Kaf HaChayim, siman 98, letter 3. Examine this carefully.
Before beginning to write, he should say:
Vihi Noam from beginning to end. He should afterward recite Ana Bechoach with great concentration. He should then recite the following verse three times: “They shall neither harm nor destroy throughout My holy mountain, for the earth shall be filled with knowledge of Hashem as the waters cover the sea.” Afterward he should recite these four verses, whose initial letters form the Divine Name Peh Samech Tes Mem: “Turn to me and be gracious to me; grant Your strength to Your servant and save the son of Your maidservant.” “I hate divided thoughts, but I love Your Torah.” “Let me hear joy and gladness; let the bones You crushed rejoice.” “How precious is Your kindness, God; mankind takes refuge in the shadow of Your wings.”
May it be Your will, Hashem, our God and God of our fathers, that You act for the sake of Your great Name Peh Samech Tes Mem, whose numerical value equals tefillin, and guide me in Your truth and wondrous secrets, revealing Your hidden matters through the glory of Your strength in which Israel glories. Open for Your servant, the son of Your maidservant, the gates of forgiveness from Your goodness. Cause Your Shechinah to rest upon the work of my hands and grant me success in this writing, in which I write tefillin for the sake of the sanctity of the mitzvah of tefillin that You, Hashem our God, commanded us. Save me from errors of intention and errors of writing, for the sake of Your Name and the sanctity of the four passages of the tefillin, their verses, words, letters, vowels, cantillation notes, Divine Names, and the holy, pure, and awesome Names that emerge from them. May this be a favorable hour, an hour of mercy, attention, and listening. I shall call You and You shall answer me; I shall entreat You and You shall accept my prayer. Nullify from me every evil thought from the Other Side that may arise in my mind while writing and preparing the tefillin. Act for the sake of Your great Name Kera Satan, followed by Baruch Shem Kevod Malchuso Le'olam Va'ed, and the Name Alef Nun Samech Vav. May it likewise be Your will, Hashem, our God and God of our fathers, that You act for the sake of Your great and holy Name in the world of Asiyah, Yod Heh Vav Heh with the filling of the Name Ban; for the sake of Your great Name in the world of Yetzirah, with the filling of MaH; for the sake of Your great Name in the world of Beriah, with the filling of SaG; and for the sake of Your great and exalted Name in the world of Atzilus, with the filling of Av. May my prayer be accepted before You like an ascending and descending offering. Let no cause or obstruction interfere with my labor in preparing these tefillin, whether through error of intention or error of writing. Let no kelipah, Other Side, or any of their forces mislead me, accuse me, or obstruct and confuse my thoughts and ideas. Bestow upon me abundant influence from the upper pool, from the world of Atzilus to Beriah, from Beriah to Yetzirah, from Yetzirah to Asiyah, from Asiyah to the world of the spheres, and from there send a spark from Your awesome and wondrous luminaries upon Your servant, the son of so-and-so. May he be blessed from Your abundant goodness to unify Your Name and magnify the greatness of Your commandments that You commanded Your people Israel in Your holiness. Blessed are You, Who hears prayer.” See Matzas Shmurim, Hilchos Tefillin. Mishnas Chassidim, Tikkun Tefillin, chapter 1, letters 3 and 4, writes likewise.
He should further intend toward the Name Dalet Vav Dalet Tet Yod Reish Vav Nun, whose numerical value, together with its eight letters, equals yetzer hatov. Its vowels are as follows: the two dalets with patach; mem-vav and reish-vav with cholam; and tet-yod with chirik. Mishnas Chassidim there, letter 4.
Afterward, before writing, he should intend that all the verses of the four passages of the head tefillin have the numerical value of the Divine Name El that emerges from the Name SaG, namely its three yods and one alef. He should likewise intend toward the Name El that emerges from the Name Ehyeh when spelled with yods, which also contains three yods and one alef. In the four passages of the arm tefillin, he should intend toward the Name El emerging from Ehyeh with the filling of yods, as above, and also toward the Name El corresponding to chesed, as the verse says, “The kindness of God is all day.”
He should also intend that all the Names Havayah appearing in the four passages of tefillin number twenty-one, corresponding to the numerical value of the Name Ehyeh, as written in Shaar HaKavanos, discourse 2 concerning tefillin, and Pri Etz Chayim, Shaar HaTefillin, chapter 14. See there.

Study carefully Shaar HaKavanos, the discourse concerning tefillin, and Shemen Sasson there, regarding the order of writing tefillin in the name of the Rashash. Examine the matter closely and you will understand the entire order of intentions we have written. We have only set them out in greater detail and placed the intention for each line in its proper location, in order to make matters easier for the scribe who uses these intentions and prevent confusion. I found them arranged in this manner in the manuscript of our friend, the divine kabbalist Rabbi Shaul Chaim Dweck HaKohen, author of Eifah Sheleimah on Otzros Chayim.
Know that although Shemen Sasson records the full spelling of Adnus three times before the passage Vehayah Im Shamoa, one need intend it only once. This is clear because in the subsequent intentions for the lines, the Rav wrote it only once. The same applies before the passage: one should intend it only once.
Know further that what Shaar HaKavanos writes concerning the yod within the expanded spelling of the Name SaG, that its vav is filled with alef, is a scribal error. It should say that the vav is filled with yod, as written in the Rashash's siddur throughout the intentions concerning the expanded spelling of SaG. There, the vav within the spelling yod is filled with yod. See there.
You must also know one fundamental principle. Do not imagine that above there exists, Heaven forbid, any literal letter or point. All these expressions are only to make the matter comprehensible to the ears of people living in this physical world, who cannot grasp the supernal lights. They therefore depicted these matters through Names and letters. In truth, every Name, letter, and point alludes to supernal and refined lights whose essence we cannot comprehend. Each one points to a particular light. Rabbi Chaim Vital discusses this at great length and depth in his introduction to Shaar HaHakdamos. See there.

Know that there is no difference between Rashi tefillin and Rabbeinu Tam tefillin except regarding the order of the Divine Name. According to Rashi, the order follows the ordinary scriptural sequence, while according to Rabbeinu Tam it follows the secret of the permutation Yod Heh Heh Vav, with the two Havayah formations interwoven. In all other details there is no difference between them. Shaar HaKavanos, discourse 10, states this, and Mishnas Chassidim, Tikkun Tefillin, chapter 15, letter 1, writes likewise: “Afterward he should write Rabbeinu Tam tefillin in all the details described for Rashi tefillin,” etc. See there. The same order of intentions applies to Shimusha Rabba tefillin.
