(1) "They have a custom regarding the lines," etc. This follows the received teaching of the Arizal in Shaar HaKavanos, discourse 1 concerning tefillin. Regarding the division of the beginnings of the lines, see Tur and Beit Yosef. We shall record the division of the line openings written by the Arizal there, for this is our custom. In the head tefillin, the 4 lines of Kadesh begin: "Vayedaber," "Mizeh," "Bachodesh," and "Betzeisi." The 4 lines of Vehayah Ki Yeviacha begin: "Vehayah," "Vechol Peter," "Hashem MiMitzrayim," and "Peter Rechem." The 4 lines of Shema begin: "Shema," "Nafshecha," "Levanecha," and "Le'os." The 4 lines of Vehayah Im Shamoa begin: "Vehayah," "Venasati," "Yevulah," and "Beshivtecha." In the arm tefillin, the 7 lines of Kadesh begin: "Vayedaber," "Et Hayom," "Bachodesh," "Zavat," "Et Shiv'at," "Hashem Li," and "Hashem MiMitzrayim." The 7 lines of Vehayah Ki Yeviacha begin: "Vehayah," "Rechem," "Ve'im Lo," "Zot Leshalchenu," "LaHashem Kol," "Einecha," and the concluding line. The 7 lines of Shema begin: "Shema," "Et," "Me'odecha," "Hayom," "Beveitecha," "Le'ot," and "Al Mezuzot." The 7 lines of Vehayah Im Shamoa begin: "Vehayah," "Uvechol Nafshechem," "Ve'achalta," "Hashem Bachem," "Asher Hashem," "Bein Eineichem," and "Beitecha." We shall now explain the intentions of the passages and lines and the writing of the Divine Names according to the Arizal, following the order arranged by the Rashash. In the generality of the 4 letters of the Name Havayah, one should intend toward the light of Ein Sof extending from level to level and building and rectifying these aspects through the power of the Botzina DeKardenusa. In the upper point of the yod, one should intend toward the unfolding of the light of the hidden worlds, Adam Kadmon, Atik, the keter of Arich Anpin, and the keter of Abba. In the yod as a whole one should intend toward Atik, Arich Anpin, and Mocha Setima'ah. In the 3 points of the yod: in the upper point, one should intend toward the keter and the daat, tiferet, and yesod of Abba, formed from the keter and the daat, tiferet, and yesod of the chochmah of the aspect of Ban within MaH; toward the keter of Imma, formed from the keter of the binah of MaH; and toward the keter and the daat, tiferet, and yesod of Yisrael Saba, formed from the tiferet and yesod of the aspect of Ban within MaH. In the second, middle point, one should intend toward the chochmah, chesed, and netzach of Abba, formed from the chochmah, chesed, and netzach of the chochmah of the aspect of Ban within MaH, and also toward the chochmah, chesed, and netzach of Yisrael Saba, formed from the relevant aspects of Ban within MaH. In the third point, one should intend toward the binah, gevurah, and hod of Abba, formed from the binah, gevurah, and hod of the chochmah of binah of MaH, and also toward the binah, gevurah, and hod of Yisrael Saba, formed from the gevurah and hod of binah of MaH. In the 2 letters vav and dalet of the spelling Yod, one should intend toward the 10 sefirot of Imma. The vav represents the upper 9 sefirot, formed from the chesed, gevurah, tiferet, netzach, hod, and yesod of the chochmah of Ban, and the dalet is malchut, formed from the malchut of the chochmah of Ban. All this is the right line of Arich Anpin and is the Name Av: Yod-Vav-Dalet, Heh-Yod, Vav-Yod-Vav, Heh-Yod. In the first heh of Havayah one should intend toward the generality of the first and second partzufim of Yisrael Saba and Tevunah, formed from the binah and malchut of the binah of MaH and from the six extremities and malchut of the binah of Ban, clothing the left side of Arich Anpin from the throat to the navel. This is the Name SaG: Yod-Vav-Dalet, Heh-Yod, Vav-Alef-Vav, Heh-Yod. In the vav one should intend toward the six extremities of MaH and Ban, which are Zeir Anpin. This is the Name MaH: Yod-Vav-Dalet, Heh-Alef, Vav-Alef-Vav, Heh-Alef. In the final heh one should intend toward the malchut of MaH and Ban, which is Nukva. This is the Name Ban: Yod-Vav-Dalet, Heh-Heh, Vav-Vav, Heh-Heh. One should also intend that the yod is the generality of Abba, while the lower point of the yod alludes to and includes Imma. The 2 letters vav and dalet are Zeir Anpin and Nukva still concealed within the drop of Abba. The yod includes keter, chochmah, and binah; the vav represents the 7 lower sefirot; and the dalet is malchut. The yod represents the 10 sefirot of direct light and the vav-dalet the 10 sefirot of reflected light. One should also intend toward the 5 chasadim and 5 gevuros within the daat of Zeir Anpin. See Shemen Sasson, part 3, on discourse 1 concerning tefillin; Nahar Shalom, page 5, column 3 in the newer edition; and what the Rashash writes in Shaar Anach, chapter 6. One should also study what is written below in siman 276, letter 6, regarding the order of drawing down the letters of the Name during writing. Both are the words of the living God; the intention written here is more detailed and is intended for one who has greater knowledge of the teachings of the Arizal. A person must be careful when writing a Divine Name not to interrupt, whether in thought, speech, action, or deed. Such an interruption causes a blemish above and below in accordance with the intentions mentioned in the laws of tefillin, separating and cutting the plantings, Heaven forbid, because everything is connected like a flame bound to a coal. See Matzat Shmurim in the laws of writing, and also Kaf HaChayyim, siman 98, letter 3. Before beginning to write, he should recite Vihi Noam from beginning to end, then Ana Bechoach with great concentration, then 3 times the verse: "They shall neither harm nor destroy throughout My holy mountain, for the earth shall be filled with knowledge of Hashem as the waters cover the sea." He should then recite these 4 verses, whose initial letters form the Name Peh Samech Tet Mem: "Turn to me and be gracious to me; grant Your strength to Your servant and save the son of Your maidservant." "I hate divided thoughts, but I love Your Torah." "Let me hear joy and gladness; let the bones You crushed rejoice." "How precious is Your kindness, God; mankind takes refuge in the shadow of Your wings." He should then say: May it be Your will, Hashem, our God and God of our fathers, that You act for the sake of Your great Name Peh Samech Tet Mem, whose numerical value equals tefillin, and guide me in Your truth and wondrous secrets, revealing Your hidden matters through the glory of Your strength in which Israel glories. Open for Your servant, the son of Your maidservant, the gates of forgiveness from Your goodness. Cause Your Shechinah to rest upon the work of my hands and grant me success in this writing, in which I write tefillin for the sake of the sanctity of the mitzvah of tefillin that You, Hashem our God, commanded us. Save me from errors of intention and errors of writing, for the sake of Your Name and the sanctity of the 4 passages of the tefillin, their verses, words, letters, vowels, cantillation notes, Divine Names, and the holy, pure, and awesome Names that emerge from them. May this be a favorable hour, an hour of mercy, attention, and listening. I shall call You and You shall answer me; I shall entreat You and You shall accept my prayer. Nullify from me every evil thought from the Other Side that may arise in my mind while writing and preparing the tefillin. Act for the sake of Your great Name Kera Satan, followed by Baruch Shem Kevod Malchuto Le'olam Va'ed, and the Name Alef Nun Samech Vav. May it likewise be Your will, Hashem, our God and God of our fathers, that You act for the sake of Your great and holy Name in the world of Asiyah, Yod Heh Vav Heh with the filling of Ban; for the sake of Your great Name in the world of Yetzirah, with the filling of MaH; for the sake of Your great Name in the world of Beriah, with the filling of SaG; and for the sake of Your great and exalted Name in the world of Atzilut, with the filling of Av. May my prayer be accepted before You like an ascending and descending offering. Let no cause or obstruction interfere with my labor in preparing these tefillin, whether through error of intention or error of writing. Let no kelipah, Other Side, or any of their forces mislead me, accuse me, obstruct me, or confuse my thoughts and ideas. Bestow upon me abundant influence from the upper pool, from Atzilut to Beriah, from Beriah to Yetzirah, from Yetzirah to Asiyah, and from Asiyah to the realm of the spheres, and from there send a spark from Your awesome and wondrous luminaries upon Your servant, the son of so-and-so. May he be blessed from Your abundant goodness to unify Your Name and magnify the greatness of Your commandments that You commanded Your people Israel in Your holiness. Blessed are You, Who hears prayer. See Matzat Shmurim, Hilchot Tefillin; Mishnat Chassidim, Tikkun Tefillin, chapter 1, letters 3 and 4. He should further intend toward the Name Dalet Vav Dalet Tet Yod Reish Vav Nun, whose numerical value, together with its 8 letters, equals yetzer hatov. Its vowels are: the 2 dalets with patach; mem-vav and reish-vav with cholam; and tet-yod with chirik. Mishnat Chassidim there, letter 4. Before writing, he should intend that all the verses of the 4 passages of the head tefillin correspond numerically to the Name El emerging from the Name SaG, namely 3 yods and 1 alef, and also to the Name El emerging from Ehyeh when spelled with yods, likewise containing 3 yods and 1 alef. In the 4 passages of the arm tefillin he should intend toward the Name El emerging from Ehyeh spelled with yods and also toward the Name El associated with chesed, as the verse says, "The kindness of God is all day." He should also intend that all the Havayah Names appearing in the 4 passages of tefillin number 21, corresponding to the numerical value of the Name Ehyeh, as written in Shaar HaKavanos, discourse 2 concerning tefillin, and Pri Etz Chayim, Shaar HaTefillin, chapter 14. Order of writing the head tefillin. Initially he should intend toward the simple Name Havayah, which includes all 4 passages of the head tefillin, and toward the Names Av, SaG, MaH, and Ban extending from it, as well as the Names 161, 143, 151, and the fully spelled Name Adnus. The principal spellings are: Havayah; Av, Yod-Vav-Dalet Heh-Yod Vav-Yod-Vav Heh-Yod; SaG, Yod-Vav-Dalet Heh-Yod Vav-Alef-Vav Heh-Yod; MaH, Yod-Vav-Dalet Heh-Alef Vav-Alef-Vav Heh-Alef; Ban, Yod-Vav-Dalet Heh-Heh Vav-Vav Heh-Heh; and fully spelled Adnus, Alef-Lamed-Peh Dalet-Lamed-Tav Nun-Vav-Nun Yod-Vav-Dalet. Before Kadesh he should intend toward the simple yod, the Name Av, and the Name 161. In every line he should intend toward the simple yod, 1 letter from Av, and 1 letter from 161. The 10 verses of Kadesh correspond to the 10 sefirot and the simple yod of Havayah. As he writes the Divine Names within the passage, he should intend successively toward the parts of the Name Ehyeh specified by the Arizal: the first Havayah corresponds to the alef of Ehyeh; the later Havayah Names correspond to the successive portions of the first heh and then to the beginning of the yod of Ehyeh, in the order indicated in the source. For Vehayah Ki Yeviacha as a whole he should intend toward the first heh of the simple Name Havayah, the Name SaG, and the Name 143. In every line he should intend toward the first heh of Havayah, 1 letter from SaG, and 1 letter from 143. This passage contains 6 verses, corresponding to the heh spelled with alef. The Havayah Names within it correspond in sequence to the further parts of the yod of Ehyeh, through the eighth portion. For Shema as a whole he should intend toward the simple vav of Havayah, the Name MaH, and the Name 151. In every line he should intend toward the simple vav, 1 letter from MaH, and 1 letter from 151. The passage contains 6 verses corresponding to the simple vav. He should further intend to draw the Havayah Names of Kadesh into the 3 Havayah Names of Shema in the secret of chochmah and chasadim. The 2 Havayah Names in the first verse correspond to the ninth and tenth portions of the yod of Ehyeh, and the Havayah in Ve'ahavta begins the portions of the final heh. For Vehayah Im Shamoa as a whole he should intend toward the final heh of the simple Name Havayah, the Name Ban, and the fully spelled Name Adnus. In every line he should intend toward the final heh, 1 letter from Ban, and 1 letter from Adnus. The passage contains 7 verses, alluding to malchut, which receives from the 9 sefirot above it. He should further intend to draw the 7 Havayah Names of Vehayah Ki Yeviacha into the 4 Havayah Names of this passage in the secret of binah and gevuros. The Divine Names in the passage correspond successively to the stated portions of the final heh of Ehyeh. Order of the arm tefillin. One should intend that these 4 passages contain 31 verses, corresponding to the Name El emerging from Ehyeh spelled with yods, and also to the Name El associated with chesed, as above. One should further intend that these 4 passages contain 21 mentions of the Divine Name, corresponding to Ehyeh. He should then intend toward the simple Name Havayah that includes the 4 passages and toward the 4 Names emerging from its 4 letters: SaG, MaH, Ban, and the fully spelled Name Adnus, together with the Names 161, 143, and 151. Before Kadesh of the arm tefillin he should intend toward the simple yod of Havayah, the Name SaG, the Name 161, and the interweaving of 161 and SaG. In each line he should intend toward the 42 letters of Av, the simple yod, 6 letters from the 42-letter expansion of Av, and 3 letters from the interweaving of 161 and SaG, first joining the simple yod of Havayah with them. The 10 verses correspond to the 10 sefirot and to the yod of Havayah. The Divine Names in the passage correspond to the alef, the successive portions of the first heh, and the beginning of the yod of Ehyeh in the detailed order given above for the head tefillin. Before Vehayah Ki Yeviacha he should intend toward the first heh of the simple Name Havayah, the Name MaH, the Name 143, and the interweaving of 143 and MaH. In every line he should intend toward the 42-letter expansion of SaG, the simple heh, 6 letters from that expansion, and 3 letters from the interweaving of 143 and MaH, first joining the first heh of Havayah with them. This passage has 6 verses corresponding to the heh spelled with alef. The Divine Names correspond successively to the second through eighth portions of the yod of Ehyeh. Before Shema he should intend toward the simple vav of Havayah, the Name Ban, the Name 151, and the interweaving of 151 and Ban. In every line he should intend toward the 42-letter expansion of MaH, the simple vav, 6 letters from that expansion, and 3 letters from the interweaving of 151 and Ban, first joining the simple vav with them. This passage has 7 lines corresponding to the simple vav. He should further intend to draw the 7 Havayah Names of Kadesh into the 3 Havayah Names of Shema in the secret of chochmah and chasadim. Before Vehayah Im Shamoa he should intend toward the simple final heh of Havayah and the Name Adnus. In every line he should intend toward the Names SaG, 161, MaH, and Adnus, toward the simple final heh, 6 letters from these Names, and 2 letters from the fully spelled Name Adnus, first joining the simple final heh of Havayah with them. This passage contains 9 verses, alluding to malchut, which receives from keter, chochmah, binah, chesed, gevurah, tiferet, netzach, hod, and yesod above it. He should further intend to draw the 6 Havayah Names of Vehayah Ki Yeviacha into the 4 Havayah Names of this passage in the secret of binah and gevuros. Study carefully Shaar HaKavanos, the discourse concerning tefillin, and Shemen Sasson there regarding the order of writing tefillin in the name of the Rashash. By examining them one will understand the entire order of intentions written here. We have written them in greater detail and placed the intention of each line in its proper location in order to make matters easier for the scribe who uses these intentions and to prevent confusion. I found them arranged in this manner in the manuscript of our friend, the divine kabbalist Rabbi Shaul Chaim Dweck HaKohen, author of Eifah Sheleimah on Otzrot Chayim. Know that although Shemen Sasson records the full spelling of Adnus 3 times before Vehayah Im Shamoa, one need intend it only once, because afterward, in the intentions for the lines, the Rav wrote it only once. The same therefore applies before the passage. Know further that what Shaar HaKavanos writes concerning the yod within the expanded spelling of SaG, that its vav is filled with alef, is a scribal error. It should be filled with yod, as written throughout the Rashash's siddur concerning the expanded spelling of SaG. One must also know a fundamental principle: do not imagine that above there exists, Heaven forbid, any literal letter or point. All of these expressions are only to make the matter comprehensible to the ears of human beings living in this physical world, who cannot grasp the supernal lights. These matters were therefore depicted through Names and letters. In truth, every Name, letter, and point alludes to supernal and refined lights whose essence we cannot comprehend, each alluding to a particular light. Rabbi Chaim Vital explains this at great length and depth in his introduction to Shaar HaHakdamos. Afterward he should add the crownlets to the letters Shatnez Getz and Bedek Chayah, and have in mind the intentions of the crownlets recorded in siman 36, letter 15. He should make the batim, straps, knot, and stitching in accordance with the intentions written in siman 32, letter 175. Know that there is no difference between Rashi tefillin and Rabbeinu Tam tefillin except regarding the order of the Havayah. According to Rashi it follows the ordinary order, while according to Rabbeinu Tam it follows the secret of the permutation Yod Heh Heh Vav. In all other details there is no difference between them. Shaar HaKavanos, discourse 10, states this, and Mishnat Chassidim, Tikkun Tefillin, chapter 15, letter 1, writes likewise: afterward he should write Rabbeinu Tam tefillin with all the details written regarding Rashi tefillin. The same order of intentions applies to Shimusha Rabba tefillin.
