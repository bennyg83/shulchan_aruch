In order. See Taz. In my humble opinion, the master of blessed memory intended one point here. In the Beit Yosef he wrote the reason for giving precedence to tzitzis over tefillin: first, that tzitzis is equivalent, etc.; second, that it is more frequent on Shabbos and Yom Tov. In the Shulchan Aruch he wrote that we ascend in holiness. At first glance, he omitted the rule that when one item is frequent and another is not, the frequent one precedes according to the Torah. One may say that according to the Rambam in siman 18, a daytime garment at night has no positive commandment, while for us, night is the time for tefillin, only we do not rule so publicly, and if one is a pious person he need not remove them at night; perhaps tefillin are therefore more frequent, see siman 30. This is unlike the Rosh. Now, not passing over mitzvos is according to the Torah, as the Levush wrote here from the verse, and you shall guard the mitzvos, see there. If so, frequency is also according to the Torah; why should the frequent, which is explicit in the verse besides the morning burnt offering, see Zevachim at the beginning of Kol HaTadir, be pushed aside because of not passing over, etc.? Therefore he said that even this reason is only an asmachta, namely that tefillin are more sanctified, for this is a mitzvah and this is sanctity. The Rambam, of blessed memory, in Hilchos Temidin UMusafin, chapter 9, halachah 2, rules that when one item is frequent and the other is more sanctified, whichever he wishes he may precede. Since so, not passing over the mitzvos overrides the consideration that tzitzis is equivalent and the consideration of ascending, etc. Ascending means that a person is elevated from mitzvah to sanctity. What is written as seif 10 is a textual error and should read seif 6.
