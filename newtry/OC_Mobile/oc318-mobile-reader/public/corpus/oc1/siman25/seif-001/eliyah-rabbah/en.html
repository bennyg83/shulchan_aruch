[1] After he has donned, etc. Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef, writes: I heard a Scriptural support from the verse, 'The wrapped ones were Lavan's, and the bound ones Yaakov's.' Levush and Beis Yosef explain that the mitzvah of tzitzis is equivalent to all the mitzvos; moreover, it is more frequent, since it applies also on Shabbos and Yom Tov, and a frequent mitzvah takes precedence. For this reason, Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur 1, writes that one who cannot afford both tzitzis and tefillin should give precedence to tzitzis. Olas Tamid 1 objects that this is incorrect and that he should buy tefillin, for our Sages said, 'The sinners of Yisrael with their bodies' refers to a skull that did not put on tefillin. I say that this reason is insufficient, for Tosafos, s.v. Karkafsa, write that this applies specifically when one does not put on tefillin out of contempt; when he refrains because the mitzvah is debased in his eyes. Beis Yosef likewise writes in siman 37, in the name of Mordechai, siman 707, and Ran, 4a, s.v. Beinonim. Even according to Rosh, Hilchos Tefillin 28, cited there by Beis Yosef, it appears that one is not called a 'skull that did not put on tefillin' in this case, since he is purchasing another mitzvah. Nevertheless, since we do not find the designation 'skull' concerning tzitzis, but only concerning tefillin, there is evidence that tefillin are more stringent. Yet Beis Yosef there cites one interpretation under which tefillin is not meant specifically, and the same applies to all mitzvos. In any event, it appears to me that, as a matter of law, he should buy tefillin, because tzitzis is not an absolute obligation, as stated above in siman 24:1. Moreover, the poskim call tefillin an obligation upon one's person, whereas they do not call tzitzis such an obligation, as Abudraham writes on page 30. We rule below, at the end of siman 38, that tefillin precede mezuzah because an obligation upon one's person is superior. After Beis Hillel was printed, I found that in Yoreh Deah 285:2 he also rules this way, for a different reason: tefillin are sacred articles, while tzitzis are not sacred articles. The Yerushalmi, Peah 1:1, teaches that one is not obligated to go begging from door to door to purchase tefillin and tzitzis. Bach 1 writes that certain people in our time who own no tefillin and rely on friends to lend them tefillin after those friends have fulfilled their own obligation incur a great punishment.
