[1] After he has donned, etc. Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef, writes: I heard a Scriptural support from the verse, 'The wrapped ones were Lavan's, and the bound ones Yaakov's.' Levush and Beis Yosef explain that the mitzvah of tzitzis is equivalent to all the mitzvos; moreover, it is more frequent, since it applies also on Shabbos and Yom Tov, and a frequent mitzvah takes precedence. For this reason, Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur 1, writes that one who cannot afford both tzitzis and tefillin should give precedence to tzitzis. Olas Tamid 1 objects that this is incorrect and that he should buy tefillin, for our Sages said, 'The sinners of Yisrael with their bodies' refers to a skull that did not put on tefillin. I say that this reason is insufficient, for Tosafos, s.v. Karkafsa, write that this applies specifically when one does not put on tefillin out of contempt; when he refrains because the mitzvah is debased in his eyes. Beis Yosef likewise writes in siman 37, in the name of Mordechai, siman 707, and Ran, 4a, s.v. Beinonim. Even according to Rosh, Hilchos Tefillin 28, cited there by Beis Yosef, it appears that one is not called a 'skull that did not put on tefillin' in this case, since he is purchasing another mitzvah. Nevertheless, since we do not find the designation 'skull' concerning tzitzis, but only concerning tefillin, there is evidence that tefillin are more stringent. Yet Beis Yosef there cites one interpretation under which tefillin is not meant specifically, and the same applies to all mitzvos. In any event, it appears to me that, as a matter of law, he should buy tefillin, because tzitzis is not an absolute obligation, as stated above in siman 24:1. Moreover, the poskim call tefillin an obligation upon one's person, whereas they do not call tzitzis such an obligation, as Abudraham writes on page 30. We rule below, at the end of siman 38, that tefillin precede mezuzah because an obligation upon one's person is superior. After Beis Hillel was printed, I found that in Yoreh Deah 285:2 he also rules this way, for a different reason: tefillin are sacred articles, while tzitzis are not sacred articles. The Yerushalmi, Peah 1:1, teaches that one is not obligated to go begging from door to door to purchase tefillin and tzitzis. Bach 1 writes that certain people in our time who own no tefillin and rely on friends to lend them tefillin after those friends have fulfilled their own obligation incur a great punishment.
[2] He should put on tefillin, etc. Sefer HaChinuch, Vaeschanan, mitzvah 421, writes: Because man is a material being, he is necessarily drawn after desire, for the nature of matter seeks every pleasure, like a horse or mule without understanding. Were it not for the soul with which God graced him, restraining him from sin to the extent of its strength, he would succumb. Because the soul dwells within his boundary (the earth) and is distant from its own boundary (the heavens) it cannot always overcome him, while his material force overcomes it. It therefore requires many guards: tefillin, tzitzis, mezuzah, and that words of Torah not cease from our mouths day or night. These serve to remind us so that we cease the oppression of our hands. Our Sages further said: Why is entry into the Land juxtaposed to the passage of tefillin? The Holy One, blessed is He, said: Perform this mitzvah, through whose merit you will enter the Land and merit life in the World to Come. They also said that if a person's transgressions and merits are evenly balanced and he bears the sin of neglecting tefillin, it tips the scale toward guilt. Baruch She'amar 26 writes: In the three verses containing the word 'letotafos' (one in Vehayah Ki Yeviacha, one in Shema, and one in Vehayah Im Shamoa) and the word 'lezikaron' written in Kadesh Li Chol Bechor, all the letters of the alef-beis appear except gimel and samech. This teaches that a gas ruach, an arrogant person, should not wear tefillin; rather, they are for one whose spirit is humble and who takes no credit for himself when he studies or performs a mitzvah. Sefer Chassidim 273 writes that if the tefillin are in their case, one should not shake them out but grasp them with his hand and remove them. I found it written in Emek Berachah 15 that it is proper to wash the place where tefillin are put before putting them on.
[2*] We learn in Berachos 14b: Anyone who recites Krias Shema without tefillin is as though he bears false testimony against himself. Levush writes that some explain this as false testimony concerning God. That is, he says in Krias Shema, 'If you will diligently heed,' and, 'I shall give rain,' yet he does not put on tefillin, while the Holy One, blessed is He, does give rain. Rashi indeed explains it as false testimony concerning God, but Levush's explanation of Rashi (that this is because he says, 'If you will diligently heed,' etc.) is strained. Moreover, the verse does not say 'all My mitzvos'; it says 'My mitzvos.' Would rain not be given merely because he fails to fulfill the mitzvah of tefillin? I saw that Maadanei Melech, page 13, chapter 2, siman 10, note 9, explains Rashi to mean that, had he truthfully testified that 'Hashem is our God, Hashem is One,' he would have fulfilled His mitzvos. Bach 2 inclines to this, but adds that by binding tefillin one testifies to His unity; if he does not put on tefillin, he appears to testify that He is not One. I wonder why they do not mention Levush's words. Tosafos, Berachos 14b, s.v. UManach, write: This means false testimony concerning the Master Who commanded him, for he recites, 'They shall be frontlets' and 'a sign upon your arm,' yet they are absent. This appears to mean that he testifies falsely concerning God by implying that He did not command him to put on tefillin. I found that Abudraham, page 30, explains Rashi this way as well. Apart from this, it is evident to me (with due respect) that Levush did not carefully examine Tosafos. What he writes, 'It appears to me that this is no difficulty,' etc., was already stated explicitly by Tosafos, and Abudraham there asks and answers the same question. Perhaps Levush found their position difficult because, according to it, the Gemara should have said that anyone who wears a four-cornered garment without tzitzis is as though he bears false testimony. To answer this, he may have concluded, 'People are not accustomed to scrutinize,' etc. That is, even if one wears a four-cornered garment without tzitzis, people do not ordinarily scrutinize it, so no falsehood is apparent. This is strained and requires further study. I presented these words to my master and father, the Rav, may his light shine. He said that there is further reason to wonder at Levush's understanding that the false testimony arises because rain comes: Sanhedrin 113a, in the incident involving Achav, implies that withholding rain applies specifically to idolatry. Maharsha there also explains that 'there will be no rain' relates to 'you will swiftly perish,' following 'when you will have grown old.' Moreover, Vehayah Im Shamoa was addressed to the community, as Rashi explains in Parashas Eikev. The condition concerning rain would therefore apply only when the community does not put on tefillin. I replied that this could perhaps be resolved by saying that people at large will err and will not derive these interpretations, just as Achav did not derive them; it therefore appears like false testimony. Nevertheless, I have already clarified Rashi's true meaning.
[3] For one ascends in matters of sanctity, etc. At the end of this se'if, Levush writes that some say tefillin precede because they possess greater sanctity. One must say that the two positions dispute this very rationale: whether the item of greater sanctity should be put on first, or later, because one ascends in sanctity. In truth, I do not know who the 'some say' whom he cites below are; Beis Yosef does not mention them. The rationale of ascending in sanctity appears in Likkutei Mahariv, Dinim VeHalachos 20. It appears to follow the mystical teaching that a person proceeds from a lesser sanctity to a greater sanctity, as stated in the Shulchan Aruch of Mahari Luria, Hilchos Tzitzis 20, as well as in Zohar and Makor Chaim 1. Megillah 21b similarly states that when the last reader reads four verses he is praiseworthy, because one ascends in sanctity and does not descend. Moreover, the principal reason for the 'some say' below is, as Levush concludes, that the mitzvah of tzitzis was deferred when he dressed because his hands were not clean. One cannot object that Levush himself writes in siman 8:11 that the garment can be donned without a berachah. Nevertheless, at the beginning of that siman he implies that, ideally, it is better to don it after washing. Levush asks why Shulchan Aruch needed to give a reason for tzitzis preceding tefillin: the obligation of tzitzis takes effect immediately when one dresses, while tefillin are not put on when naked; for this reason, he says, the authorities did not need to mention it. In my humble opinion, this poses no difficulty. We rule that tzitzis is not an obligation upon one's person; one may, if he wishes, make all his garments without four corners and tzitzis. Only during Krias Shema and prayer must even one who does not remain wrapped in tzitzis all day take care to wear them, as stated above in siman 24:1. Shulchan Aruch therefore needed to give these reasons. Regarding Levush's statement that the authorities did not need to mention it for this reason, see Eliyah Zuta 1.
So that he should not pass over, etc. Magen Avraham wrote that this requires study, for it appears to him that there is no concern here, since the tefillin are still inside their pouch; and so it appears from seif 11. In my humble opinion, seif 11 is different, for there both are inside the pouch, and by removing one first he would be passing over the mitzvos more directly. Here, however, even though the tefillin are inside the pouch, it is still considered passing over, since the tefillin are in his hand by means of the pouch, whereas the tallis is not in his hand at all.
