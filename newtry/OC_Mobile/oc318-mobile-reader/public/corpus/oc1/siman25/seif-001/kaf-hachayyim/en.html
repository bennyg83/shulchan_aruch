1) [Se'if 1] After donning a fringed tallis, he should put on tefillin, for one ascends in matters of sanctity. Nimukei Yosef, in Hilchos Tzitzis, writes: Since the mitzvah of tzitzis is equivalent to all the mitzvos, it is fitting to give it precedence. Moreover, it is more frequent, for it applies both on weekdays and on Shabbos and Yom Tov, and when one matter is frequent and the other is not, the frequent one takes precedence. Beis Yosef and Levush cite him; see there. The Arizal gave a kabbalistic reason why one must specifically don the tallis before the tefillin (both the tallis katan and the tallis gadol) and the tallis katan is donned before the tallis gadol. Shaar HaKavanos, Derushei HaTzitzis, derush 2, states: Since tzitzis derives from the mochin of katnus, which precede the mochin of gadlus, one must therefore first don tzitzis and only afterward tefillin. This applies even to the tallis gadol used for wrapping, for the foregoing reason, contrary to the view of certain kabbalists who put on tefillin before the tallis gadol after having donned the tallis katan. All the more so should one not follow those who say that tefillin precede every form of tzitzis. Whoever examines the Zohar will find that the truth accords with our words. He writes similarly in Derushei HaTzitzis, derush 5, and Derushei Tefillas HaShachar, derush 3; see there for the mystical meaning. See also Pri Etz Chaim, Shaar HaTzitzis, chapter 3, and Shaar HaTefillah, chapter 7, s.v. Seder Aliyos HaTefillos Bechol; Nagid U'Metzaveh at the end of Derushei Tzitzis; Olas Tamid 1; Birkei Yosef 4 and his Kesher Gudal 3:1; Shalmei Tzibbur 37b; Siddur Beis Oved, laws of the place where tefillin are put on, 1; Chesed LaAlafim 1; and Ben Ish Chai, Vayeira 3. See also below, paragraph 12.
2) There: After donning a fringed tallis, he should put on tefillin. A mnemonic is: 'From a thread to a shoelace'; first the threads of tzitzis and afterward the straps of tefillin. Another mnemonic for wrapping in a tallis before putting on tefillin is: 'The wrapped ones were Lavan's, and the bound ones Yaakov's.' This means that first comes the wrapping in a white tallis, and afterward the bindings of the tefillin are Yaakov's; that is, they come last. Ateres Zekeinim 1; this also appears in Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef; Kaf HaChaim by Rav Chaim Palagi 10:1; and Ben Ish Chai, ibid.
3) There: After donning a fringed tallis, he should put on tefillin. Knesses HaGedolah writes in Hagahos HaTur that one who cannot afford to buy both should buy a tallis. Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur 1, rules likewise. Bnei Chayei, Olas Tamid 1, and Halachos Ketanos 1:54 disagree and write that tefillin take precedence. Yad Aharon in Hagahos HaTur and Eliyah Rabbah 1 rule likewise. See Maamar Mordechai 2; Yoreh Deah 10; Beis Hillel on Yoreh Deah 285; Erech HaShulchan 2; and Divrei Menachem, Hagahos HaTur 1, in the name of Maharch Nachum. Although Responsa Shemesh Tzedakah, Orach Chaim 15-16, defends Knesses HaGedolah, Birkei Yosef 1 writes that the later authorities' view (that tefillin precede) is primary. He rules likewise in Machazik Berachah 2, as do the glossator to Shalmei Tzibbur 26b; Siddur Beis Yaakov, laws of the obligation of the mitzvah of tefillin, 3; Zechirah LeChaim, letter tzadi; Shesilei Zeisim 1; and Yafeh LaLev 1. If, however, he can obtain tefillin on loan, he should buy tzitzis. Birkei Yosef, ibid., in the name of the later authorities; Shalmei Tzibbur, ibid.; Beis Yaakov, ibid.; Zechirah LeChaim, ibid.; Shaarei Teshuvah 1; and Yafeh LaLev, ibid. It appears that this applies only if he is confident that he will be able to borrow tefillin every day and will never miss the mitzvah of tefillin. Otherwise, he would be neglecting the mitzvah of tefillin, which, according to the aforementioned poskim whose view is primary in practice, precedes the mitzvah of tzitzis. Even if he can afford only one tefillah (either the hand-tefillin or the head-tefillin) it takes precedence over tzitzis. Beis Yaakov, ibid., 4. See below, paragraph 5.
4) If one has Rashi tefillin but wishes to put on Rabbeinu Tam tefillin, and he has no tzitzis, the tzitzis take precedence, since most people do not put on Rabbeinu Tam tefillin at all. The reverse is not true: even if he has Rabbeinu Tam tefillin but lacks Rashi tefillin, the Rashi tefillin precede tzitzis, because they are primary with respect to the berachah, as stated in siman 34. Although the sanctity of Rabbeinu Tam tefillin is exceedingly great, for fulfilling one's obligation Rashi tefillin take precedence. Indeed, it is precisely because each is a distinct spiritual operation, as stated in the Zohar Chadash and in siman 34, that most people refrained from putting on Rabbeinu Tam tefillin. It is therefore obvious that one who cannot purchase both should first buy Rashi tefillin and later, when he can afford them, buy Rabbeinu Tam tefillin. This is universal practice, and the same principle applies in relation to tzitzis. Shaarei Teshuvah, ibid. This also appears from the Arizal's words in Shaar HaKavanos, Derushei Tefillin, derush 6, which I cited in my sacred work Kol Yaakov 34:4. He writes that Rashi tefillin are put on before Rabbeinu Tam tefillin because the mochin of Imma, represented by Rashi tefillin, enter first, and afterward the mochin of Abba, represented by Rabbeinu Tam tefillin, enter. Since the former enter first, they must be put on first. Accordingly, one who cannot buy both should first buy Rashi tefillin and, when he is able, buy Rabbeinu Tam tefillin. The same appears from our practice in the silent Amidah and its repetition: the silent prayer draws down the mochin of Binah, while the repetition draws down the mochin of Chochmah. When both cannot be recited, only the silent prayer, corresponding to the mochin of Binah, is recited. So too here, when one cannot buy both, he should first buy Rashi tefillin. Although tzitzis also precedes tefillin in the spiritual order, as stated above in paragraph 1, whereas we wrote that one unable to buy both tzitzis and tefillin should give precedence to tefillin, that case is different because of the loss of the mitzvah: neglecting tefillin is more severe, as stated at the beginning of siman 37, whereas one who has no four-cornered garment does not thereby neglect the mitzvah of tzitzis, as stated at the beginning of siman 24. When the choice is between neglecting Rashi tefillin and Rabbeinu Tam tefillin, Rashi tefillin are more stringent, as stated at the beginning of siman 34, for the universal practice follows Rashi. Regarding Shaarei Teshuvah's ruling that tzitzis precede Rabbeinu Tam tefillin, however, see Yafeh LaLev 2, who challenges it and writes that Rabbeinu Tam tefillin also precede tzitzis. In light of the Arizal's words, arguments can be made in either direction. He should therefore buy Rabbeinu Tam tefillin and borrow tzitzis, or do the reverse, as circumstances permit, so that he neglects neither, as stated in the next paragraph.
5) One who cannot afford both head-tefillin and hand-tefillin should first buy head-tefillin. Divrei Moshe 3:1; see there; Shalmei Tzibbur 36a; and Shaarei Teshuvah at the end of paragraph 1. Yafeh LaLev 26:1, however, writes that he should first buy hand-tefillin; see there. If one cannot afford either tefillin or tzitzis, he is not obligated to go begging from door to door in order to buy them. Olas Tamid 1 in the name of Yerushalmi Peah; Eliyah Rabbah 1; and Shesilei Zeisim at the end of paragraph 1. Nevertheless, he should endeavor to borrow tzitzis and tefillin so that he can recite Krias Shema and pray while wearing them. Likewise, regarding what we wrote above; that one who has neither tzitzis nor tefillin and cannot afford both should first buy tefillin; he should endeavor to borrow tzitzis. Similarly, one who has only one of the two tefillin should endeavor to borrow tzitzis and the other tefillah, so as not to reverse the rectification and order prescribed by the Arizal: one must first don tzitzis, then tefillin, and afterward pray, as stated above in paragraph 1. He should at least endeavor to don the tzitzis and tefillin before reciting the passage of the daily offering, for that is the order according to the Arizal. See below, paragraph 27.
6) There: Those who place the tefillin and the tallis in a single bag must take care not to place the tefillin bag on top, etc. Magen Avraham 1 writes: It appears to me that there is no concern here, since the tefillin are still inside their bag; this is also implied by se'if 11. His proof from se'if 11, however, is no proof. Maran derived the law in se'if 11 from the responsum of Rabbi Yosef Gikatilla, based on Kabbalah, that the head-tefillin should not be removed from their case until the hand-tefillin have been put on, as explained in Beis Yosef. Shaar HaKavanos, Derushei Tefillin, derush 5, likewise states that the Arizal was careful not to remove the head-tefillin from their bag until he had put on the hand-tefillin and fastened it above the biceps. That prohibition is not based on the rule that one may not pass over mitzvos; rather, this is the prescribed kabbalistic order. A practical difference is that even if another person wishes to take out the head-tefillin for him before he has put on and fastened the hand-tefillin, doing so is forbidden. This is unlike Machatzis HaShekel, who suggests that the reason could have been the prohibition against passing over mitzvos, and that this reason was not given because, while the tefillin remain bound in their cases, that prohibition does not apply. Rather, the reason the prohibition against passing over mitzvos was not given is the practical difference just stated: even another person may not take out the head-tefillin for him before he fastens the hand-tefillin. Had the reason been passing over mitzvos, another person would have been permitted to remove them, as we shall explain further in se'if 11, with G-d's help. Maamar Mordechai 3 likewise writes regarding Magen Avraham's words that there is no proof at all from se'if 11, even by a strained inference. Nehar Shalom 1 also writes that se'if 11 affords no proof; see there. Nishmas Adam 13:2 rules likewise. Levush writes that even when the tefillin are inside a bag, passing them over entails the prohibition against passing over mitzvos. Taz 10, Eliyah Rabbah 4, and Chayei Adam 13:1 rule likewise. See Nishmas Adam 2, who adduces proofs, particularly because this is Maran's view. So too rule the later authorities: Shalmei Tzibbur 37a; Siddur Beis Yaakov, laws of the place where tefillin are put on, 1; Chesed LaAlafim 1; and Kitzur Shulchan Aruch 10:2, unlike Shulchan Aruch HaRav 3. See the next paragraph.
7) There: He will have to put them on before the tallis so that he does not pass over the mitzvah. Although, if one encounters the head-tefillin first, he sets it aside and first puts on the hand-tefillin, as stated in se'if 6, that case is different. Scripture says, 'It shall be a sign upon your arm,' and only afterward, 'and as frontlets between your eyes.' We also expound the verse to mean that whenever the head-tefillin are between your eyes, there shall be two tefillin upon you. It is therefore better to pass over that mitzvah than to contravene what is written in the Torah. Here, however, we know that tzitzis precedes only from a mere Scriptural allusion; it is better that this allusion be set aside than that one pass over a mitzvah. Beis Yosef; Levush; and Taz 1. All this follows the plain meaning. According to the Arizal, however, who gives a kabbalistic reason why tzitzis must be donned before tefillin and the hand-tefillin before the head-tefillin; see above, paragraph 1 and the sources cited there concerning the Arizal, as well as the sources cited in the preceding paragraph; there is no distinction. Just as one who encounters the head-tefillin first sets it aside and first puts on the hand-tefillin, so too one who happens to encounter tefillin first should set them aside and first don tzitzis. Although the prohibition against passing over mitzvos is derived from the verse, 'You shall guard the matzos,' which teaches that when a mitzvah comes to your hand you should not let it become delayed, as Levush writes, that applies to other mitzvos that have no prescribed order. Mitzvos that possess a kabbalistically prescribed order may be passed over, as in the case of tefillin. I subsequently saw that Solel Boneh 1, Yafeh LaLev 6, and Ben Ish Chai, Vayeira 4, also write this.
8) There: So that he does not pass over the mitzvah. If one's Shabbos tallis was in the bag in which he keeps his weekday tallis and tefillin, and the Shabbos tallis was on top of everything, he may set aside the Shabbos tallis and take the weekday tallis. This does not violate the rule against passing over mitzvos, since he does not intend to wear it now, on a weekday, but only on Shabbos. Birkei Yosef 3; see there, where he adduces proof from Magen Avraham 4. See also Siddur Beis Yaakov, laws of the place where tefillin are put on, 2; Shalmei Tzibbur 37a; Zechirah LeChaim 9; Shaarei Teshuvah 2; and Chesed LaAlafim 2.
9) There: So that he does not pass over the mitzvah. The prohibition against passing over mitzvos is rabbinic. Radbaz 2:589; Yad Malachi 3:3. He did not mention that Tosafos disagree with him. Ayin Zocher, maareches alef, siman 17, cited in Pischei Olam 3. See Tosafos, Megillah 6b, s.v. Mistaber.
10) There, in the gloss: Nevertheless, if tefillin are available to him and he has no tzitzis, he need not wait, etc. According to what was stated above in the Arizal's name; that the proper order and rectification specifically requires donning tzitzis before tefillin; and as Shalmei Tzibbur 37b writes, that the Zohar, Bamidbar 120b, indicates that putting on tefillin before the tallis is a grave prohibition, it follows that if he is assured that tzitzis will be brought to him before the time for Krias Shema and prayer passes, he must wait until they are brought. He should then wrap himself in tzitzis, put on tefillin, and pray. See Erech HaShulchan 4.
11) There, in the gloss: Rather, he puts on the tefillin, and when the tallis is brought he wraps himself in it. See what we shall write concerning this below in siman 26, paragraph 1, with G-d's help.
