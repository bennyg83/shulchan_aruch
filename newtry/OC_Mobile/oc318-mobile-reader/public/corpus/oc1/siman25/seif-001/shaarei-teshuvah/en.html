He should put on. Tefillin. See Baer Heitev. The Birkei Yosef wrote in the name of the later authorities that if it is possible for him to have tefillin on loan from someone else, tzitzis comes first; see there. See below, siman 38, seif katan 14, regarding tefillin and mezuzah. It seems that this is only if his heart is certain and confident that he will be able to borrow tefillin, so that at least he will not have to return them whenever he wants, if he does not happen to be able to buy them. Otherwise there is still concern that perhaps he will have to return them before other tefillin become available to him, and he will be found idle from the mitzvah of tefillin, which precedes the mitzvah of tzitzis according to these poskim, who are primary for the law. See Yad Ephraim, Responsa Shaagas Aryeh, siman 28, and Responsa Shemesh Tzedakah, siman 15, in the Taz. It seems that if he has Rashi tefillin and does not have Rabbeinu Tam tefillin, and he also does not have tzitzis, tzitzis comes first, since most people do not put on Rabbeinu Tam tefillin at all. But not the reverse. Even if he has Rabbeinu Tam tefillin, if he does not have Rashi tefillin, Rashi tefillin comes before tzitzis, for they are primary regarding the blessing, as written in siman 34. Although the sanctity of Rabbeinu Tam tefillin is very great, nevertheless regarding fulfilling his obligation, Rashi tefillin come first. On the contrary, because of this, most of the world refrained from putting them on, since each one is a distinct action, as written in Zohar Chadash and as written in siman 34. Therefore it is obvious that one who cannot buy both should buy Rashi tefillin first, and afterward, when he has means to buy Rabbeinu Tam tefillin, he should buy them, and this is the common practice. If so, the same applies regarding tzitzis. In Shalmei Tzibbur he wrote in the name of Responsa Devar Moshe that one whose means do not suffice to buy tefillin of the hand and tefillin of the head should buy the tefillin of the head first; see there.
