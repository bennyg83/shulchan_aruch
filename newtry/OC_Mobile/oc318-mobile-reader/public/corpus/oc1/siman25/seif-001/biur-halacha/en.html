For one ascends in matters of sanctity; this refers to the person, who must proceed from one level to another and ascend in sanctity. At first he merely covers himself with the covering of a mitzvah; through tefillin he binds himself with the bond of Divine unity and sanctity. Eliyah Rabbah and Gra. This resolves the objections of Shaagas Aryeh and Dagul MeRevavah.
That they should not place; see Magen Avraham, who writes that it appears there is no concern, since the tefillin are still inside their bag. Levush, Bach, and Taz 10 imply that no such distinction exists, and Nishmas Adam rules likewise. His proof against Magen Avraham; from Tosafos, Yoma 33b, s.v. Avurei, and Rabbeinu Eliyahu's explanation that one must first touch the hand-tefillin, although it is generally covered and the rule against passing over mitzvos nevertheless applies; is very weak. Magen Avraham's reasoning is that while the tefillin remain in their bag, the mitzvah of fastening them is not yet available to him, for he must first remove them from the bag. In the case there, however, touching the tefillin is itself the mitzvah; even if the shirt sleeve covers the tefillin, that mitzvah still applies. The rule against passing over mitzvos therefore applies there. Nevertheless, it is very difficult to rule leniently like Magen Avraham when so many authorities disagree. Even if this occurred while one was sitting among the public in the synagogue and he would be embarrassed as a result, I am uncertain whether one may be lenient. Nishmas Adam 68 concludes (and Pri Megadim, Mishbetzos Zahav 5, likewise implies) that the rule against passing over mitzvos is a Torah law. Shulchan Aruch already rules above, at the end of siman 13 in the gloss, that a Torah law is never set aside for human dignity unless there is great disgrace. Rama considers sitting without a tallis, even for a long time, only a minor disgrace, as Magen Avraham writes there. All the more so here, where it lasts only a few moments until he dons the tallis gadol. The matter nevertheless requires further study.
So that he does not encounter, etc. See Mishnah Berurah, where it explains, 'even,' etc. Its source is Yoma 33b: 'When he enters the Sanctuary,' etc., 'drawn outward,' etc. One might distinguish our case: because the tefillin remain in the tallis bag and he has not yet removed them, it is more lenient. Even if his hand touches them, he may withdraw it and take the tallis. Pri Megadim writes this according to Magen Avraham in Eshel Avraham 1 and Mishbetzos Zahav 10: according to Magen Avraham, the head- and hand-tefillin may ideally lie level with one another inside the case. Even if he happens to encounter the head-tefillin first, he may set it aside and first take the hand-tefillin, without concern for passing over mitzvos. None of this, however, accords with the practical law. Shulchan Aruch, whose view we follow, and almost all the later authorities (Levush, Bach, Taz, Nishmas Adam, and Artzos HaChaim, unlike Magen Avraham) make no such distinction. They hold that the rule against passing over mitzvos applies even when the tefillin are inside their bag. In my humble opinion, it is in fact obvious that Magen Avraham also agrees in our case, unlike Pri Megadim. Magen Avraham was lenient only when the tefillin lie in a separate bag and one has not touched them directly at all, but only holds the bag, through which the tefillin cannot be put on. Here, the tefillin themselves are in his hand, or are the first item available when he reaches in; what does it help that they are still inside the bag? Proof comes from Magen Avraham himself, siman 28:4. He explains Shulchan Aruch's statement that the hand-tefillin should be placed above the other as meaning that it should be somewhat higher, so that one encounters it first. According to Pri Megadim, this is unnecessary: it would suffice to make a marker in the winding of the straps so that, upon touching them, he can distinguish hand-tefillin from head-tefillin and avoid removing the head-tefillin first, as Pri Megadim writes in Mishbetzos Zahav 10. One could answer on his behalf that Shulchan Aruch's intention is to prevent one from forgetting and removing the head-tefillin from the case, but this is strained. Perhaps this is what Pri Megadim himself intended when he remained in difficulty concerning Magen Avraham below, siman 28:4. In any event, this makes no practical difference for us, since we rule unlike Magen Avraham even when they are inside their bag. The conclusion is that if one reaches into the tallis bag and first encounters the tefillin, he must remove and put them on first, because one may not pass over mitzvos. Regarding the tefillin themselves, he must ideally take care that the hand-tefillin be positioned somewhat higher, not exactly level with the head-tefillin, so that he does not encounter the head-tefillin first inside the bag and violate the rule against passing over mitzvos.
And he will have to put them on; at first glance, the following requires consideration. If one removed his tallis and tefillin only briefly, intending to put them back on immediately, so that, as ruled below, he need not repeat the berachah, and when redressing he happens to encounter the tefillin first, must he put them on first? Perhaps, since he did not divert his attention from the mitzvah, he is considered still engaged in it. With God's help, I resolve that no distinction should be made. This follows from Tosafos, Yoma 33b, s.v. Avurei, citing Rabbeinu Eliyahu's explanation of Rava's statement as referring to touching the tefillin: one must first touch the hand-tefillin because one may not pass over mitzvos. This is so although we rule that no berachah is recited over touching them, because one has not yet withdrawn from the mitzvah. All the more so does this apply in our case. One cannot say that the passage follows the law in Talmudic times, when a berachah was recited over touching them, for the author of that statement is Rava, and Rava himself holds in Sukkah 46a that no berachah is recited over touching them. Nor can one suggest that Rabbeinu Eliyahu follows the position Beis Yosef wishes to attribute to Rambam at the end of the siman: Rambam omitted the rule about touching because it applies only when the tefillin moved from their place and one wishes to replace them, which is an actual new placement, so Rava and the scholars of Rav Ashi do not disagree at all. Rabbeinu Eliyahu cannot hold this way, for then Tosafos' objection to Rashi remains: why did Rava need to derive the rule from Reish Lakish, when Scripture itself says first, 'You shall bind them,' and afterward, 'as frontlets'? Rather, according to Rabbeinu Eliyahu, Rava must hold that this is not called a new placement, or else Rabbeinu Eliyahu considers 'touching' to apply even when the tefillin did not move, like the other poskim. Yet Rava still holds that the rule against passing over mitzvos applies. Our conclusion (that no distinction exists) is therefore certain.
