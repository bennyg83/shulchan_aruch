Every time. Even if when he removed them his intention was to return them immediately, and even if he did not change his place at all in the meantime. The Rama nearby disagrees with this. If at the time of the blessing his intention was that he would later remove them and put them on again, it is clear from the words of the Magen Avraham above in siman 8, seif katan 16, that according to all opinions he need not bless again.
They slipped. The Shelah wrote that the reason the practice nowadays is not to bless when they slipped from their place is because during prayer one presumably does not divert his mind from them, and it is like removing them with intent to return them, where the gloss below rules that he does not bless again. If so, those who sometimes go until midday wearing tefillin, if they slipped, it is proper to bless. The Chayei Adam wrote that even during prayer, if they slipped, one who blesses has not lost. Nevertheless, it appears that minimizing blessings is preferable.
From their place. Specifically all of them or most of them. But if only part of them moved, although he must return them to their place, he need not bless again. Taz in siman 8, seif 15; see there in Peri Megadim.
He must bless. Since he moved them from their place, it is like he removed them entirely.
Not to bless. Since he moved them with intent to return them. This differs from when they slipped by themselves, for we say that immediately when they slipped the mitzvah departed, and therefore the Rav did not object to the Mechaber there. Know further that the same applies if he removed them entirely with intent to return them immediately; the “some say” also disagree, even if he changed his place in the meantime, and the Acharonim ruled so. But if he did not intend to return them immediately, only after some time, even if afterward he put them on at once, or if he intended to return them immediately but afterward the matter was delayed and he diverted his mind meanwhile, according to all opinions he must bless again. So too, if he removed them to enter the privy, even if he intended to put them on immediately, he must bless according to all opinions, for he may not enter the privy with them and they were pushed away from him, as written in siman 65. The Chayei Adam wrote that the same applies when he removes them because he needs to pass gas; since it is forbidden to pass gas while wearing them, he must bless again.
Of the arm. The same law applies to the head tefillin.
He did not divert his mind. Even if he spoke in between for a matter of need, it is not an interruption after the fact, as below in siman 167, seif 6. See above, siman 8, Mishnah Berurah, seif katan 28.
And bless. Since the mitzvah of placing them had not yet been done, the blessing had not yet taken effect on any mitzvah, and making the knot does not interrupt because it is part of the matter of the mitzvah; therefore the blessing takes effect on the second placement. From this one may learn regarding one who has tefillin without a knot in his hand, blessed, made the knot, and put them on, that after the fact the blessing he made properly counts, for making the knot is not an interruption. It implies from the words of the Shulchan Aruch that if after he tightened the tefillin on the arm the knot broke, he must bless again, for the act of the mitzvah had already been completed, and it is like they slipped from their place, where he must bless when returning them, as above. The Acharonim disagree and wrote that since the blessing lehaniach that he recited before putting on the arm tefillin also applies to the head tefillin, as stated above, as long as he has not yet put on the head tefillin he is still engaged in the mitzvah that belongs to that blessing, and the mitzvah over which he blessed is not yet completed until he puts on the head tefillin as well. Therefore, just as if the knot of the arm tefillin came loose before placing the head tefillin the Shulchan Aruch ruled that he need not bless again, the same applies if the knot broke, that he need not bless again. If the knot of the head tefillin or of the arm tefillin broke after he already put on and tightened the head tefillin, he must bless again. If no one there knows how to fix the knot and he needed to take other tefillin, in every case he must bless again, for it is comparable to the law in siman 206, seif 6.
Came loose. The knot. The same applies if they slipped from their place. What it says “he put on the head tefillin” means that he tightened it, for that is the essence of the mitzvah of placement, as above.
He tightens and blesses. For it is like they slipped from their place. So too, if the head tefillin came loose after being tightened, he must bless again when tightening it on his head. According to what we wrote above in the name of the Shelah, he should not bless if the tefillin came loose during the time of prayer in any case.
Borrowed. Even borrowing without the owner’s knowledge is permitted, for a person is pleased that a mitzvah be performed with his property. Only he must fold them as they were originally and not remove them from their original place, as with a tallis above in siman 14; see there. Acharonim.
Stolen. For it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. Even after despair of the owner, and even after the fact, he has not fulfilled the mitzvah of tefillin. However, if he sold the tefillin after despair, according to the view of the Magen Avraham in siman 649 and the Machatzit HaShekel above in siman 11, the second person may bless, for for him it is despair and transfer of domain. However, the Taz and the Gra in this siman wrote explicitly that in every case it is forbidden to bless. Although regarding fulfilling the mitzvah it implies from the words of the Gra that he fulfills it, nevertheless regarding the blessing it is forbidden because of “a robber blesses,” for through his taking it the stolen object left the owner’s domain. But one who took it from this second person may apparently bless; so I found in Damesek Eliezer above in siman 11; see there.
