Seif 12, they slipped out of place. There; and so the Rosh wrote in Hilchos Ketanos in the name of the Ri, and this is his wording in seif 5: “One must recite a blessing when he handles them after putting them on, for we rule like the Rabbis against Rabbi Eliezer,” etc. “From my teacher, Rabbi Shmuel bar Shneor, I heard that this is necessary only when they slipped out of place,” etc. But this is forced, and all the poskim wrote plainly like the first view.
He moved them, etc., and already, etc. See what I wrote there.
One who places, etc. Even according to the opinion that rules that if he moved them, etc., he must recite a blessing, and all the more so when they slipped out of place, where according to all opinions he must recite a blessing, and here it is like they slipped out of place. Nevertheless, since the strap broke at the beginning of tightening, the first blessing had not yet taken effect on any mitzvah. Now this is the first placement, as the Beit Yosef writes.
If it became untied, etc., but, etc. For it is like they slipped out of place, as above. But before placing the tefillin shel rosh, even if they slipped out of place, he need not recite the blessing again, for the first blessing still applies to the tefillin shel rosh. That is why if he spoke, etc. If so, the same applies where the knot broke, even after tightening, before placing the shel rosh; he need not recite the blessing again. This is unlike what was written initially, “and at the beginning of tightening,” etc. So too the Taz wrote. What the Magen Avraham wrote in seif katan 24, that making the knot is an interruption, is not so. If so, all the more at the beginning of tightening, as stated in Berachos 40a. The interruption between the blessing and the beginning of the mitzvah is greater than the interruption between one mitzvah and its companion or in the middle of the mitzvah. See above, siman 8, seif 14. Rather, certainly making the knot is not an interruption, for only speech or distraction is considered an interruption. If so, its law is like one who moved them.
It is permitted, etc. As with sukkah and lulav from the second day onward, even in the Mikdash, and with other mitzvos.
And not, etc. Even after despair and change of possession, as written above in siman 649, seif 1, unlike what is written in Beit Yosef in the name of Orchos Chayim; therefore he omitted it in Shulchan Aruch. The prohibition of stolen items is because it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, as written at the beginning of the third chapter of Sukkah, even after despair. Regarding the blessing, this applies even after a physical change, as written above in siman 649, seif 1, and all the more so after despair and change of possession, as Tosafot wrote there.
