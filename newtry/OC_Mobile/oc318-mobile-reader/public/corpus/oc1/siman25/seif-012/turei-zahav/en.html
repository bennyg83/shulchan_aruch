He moved them from their place, etc. Above in siman 8, seif 14, these matters were clarified, and that we must rule like the first anonymous opinion brought by the Rama there. If so, regarding tefillin one should rule that if he removed them in order to enter a privy, he must go back and bless. But if he did not enter a privy, he need not bless.
He need not go back and bless. Since the mitzvah of putting on has not yet been performed, the blessing has not taken effect upon any mitzvah. Since he did not divert his mind from it, it properly takes effect upon the second putting on. So wrote the Beit Yosef. From his words one can learn regarding one who has tefillin in his hand without a knot and blesses, then makes the knot and puts them on, that the blessing he recited first properly counts for him, for making the knot is not an interruption but a repair for the mitzvah, and it is similar to bringing salt between the blessing of HaMotzi and eating, below in siman 167, seif 6. The Beit Yosef further wrote that if he had already tightened it and afterward it broke and he repaired it, he must bless, for the mitzvah had already been performed once by tightening it. At first glance there is a proof from a tallis that fell entirely from him, where he must bless when he wraps himself again, as written in siman 8, seif 15. But this is difficult to me, for the blessing of lehaniach that he recites before putting on the tefillin of the hand also applies to the tefillin of the head, as above. If so, as long as he has not put on the tefillin of the head, he is still occupied with the mitzvah that belongs to that blessing. What he did in making a knot for the tefillin of the hand is also a repair for the mitzvah, for he cannot put on the tefillin of the head before the tefillin of the hand, and therefore he must fix the tefillin of the hand and afterward put them on properly. Thus the fulfillment of the mitzvah belonging to the blessing still continues until it is completed, namely after he has put on the tefillin of the head. It appears that this is the reason for what the Shulchan Aruch wrote afterward, which is from the words of the Raavad in the Beit Yosef, that if it became untied before putting on the tefillin of the head, etc., because the effect of the blessing is still continuing, as we wrote. Although the Raavad mentioned only the case of the loosening of the tightening, it appears that the same applies if it broke, for it is one reason, since if he moved them from their place he must bless. We will explain further nearby, with the help of Heaven.
If the tefillin of the hand became untied, etc. The intent of the Shulchan Aruch requires study, for being untied and breaking are equal, as written nearby. The order of the wording of the Beit Yosef also indicates so, for first he wrote that the knot broke, and afterward he concludes in this wording: afterward I found in Sefer Orchos Chaim in this wording, the Raavad wrote, etc., which implies that the very law the Beit Yosef mentioned first is also explained by the words of the Raavad. If so, it is difficult, for the Shulchan Aruch first wrote regarding breaking that specifically at the beginning of the tightening he need not bless, implying that after the tightening he must bless, as we wrote in the name of the Beit Yosef. Yet here he wrote regarding becoming untied that as long as he has not put on the tefillin of the head, he need not bless. It seems to me that in the Beit Yosef he was uncertain in explaining the words of the Raavad, whether there is a distinction between becoming untied and breaking. Perhaps regarding becoming untied it is more reasonable that he need not bless again, because only a minor act is lacking, namely the tightening alone. Since the tefillin of the head is still before him and belongs to the blessing of the tefillin of the hand, as written nearby, therefore he need not bless. This is unlike when the knot broke, which is a new act. Therefore in the Beit Yosef, after writing his distinction regarding breaking, he simply copied the wording of the Raavad and relied on the reader to understand the source of the matters. But in the Shulchan Aruch he wrote only the matter that is clear. Therefore he wrote only the case that has no doubt, namely that if it broke at the beginning of the tightening he need not bless. But after the tightening he wrote nothing, only copied the words of the Raavad as they are, from which one can learn that the same applies to breaking, that he need not bless even if it broke after the tightening, as appears clear to me. And so it appears to me to rule in practice, that even if it broke after the tightening he need not bless as long as he has not yet put on the tefillin of the head.
And not over stolen ones. For one who blesses over stolen bread has blasphemed Hashem, until there has been despair and change of possession. So wrote the Beit Yosef in the name of Orchos Chaim in the name of the Itur. But it seems to me, according to what we proved in siman 11, seif 6, that regarding tzitzis one should not bless over them even after despair and change of possession. So too here regarding tefillin. Therefore the Shulchan Aruch was concise here and wrote simply that they are invalid, which implies in all cases. So too he inferred in the Beit Yosef from the words of the Rambam in siman 11, from the fact that he wrote simply that they are invalid.
