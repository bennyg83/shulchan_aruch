To bless. The Shelah wrote that nowadays the practice is not to bless when they slipped from their place, because during prayer he presumably has not diverted his mind from them, and it is like removing them with intent to put them back. Magen Avraham. In Eliyah Rabbah he ruled like the view of the Shulchan Aruch and brought proof for his words; see there.
To bless. But if he removes them in order to enter the privy, he must bless according to all opinions, since he is not permitted to go into the privy with them and they were pushed away from him. The same applies if he did not intend to return immediately, that he must bless. Bach, Olas Tamid, Taz. The Magen Avraham wrote that perhaps when the privy is not far away, unlike their privies which were in the field, he need not bless. If so, there is no distinction between a privy and another place, and therefore the Rama wrote here without qualification; see there. Know that what the Rama wrote, “and there are those who say,” also applies to the case where he removes them entirely, for regarding this matter moving them from their place and removing them entirely are the same. Olas Tamid.
The tightening. Meaning, before the mitzvah was performed in its proper manner; therefore he need not bless again, for making the knot is not an interruption since it is part of the matter of the mitzvah. The Taz wrote that from this one may learn regarding one who has tefillin without a knot in his hand, blesses, makes the knot, and puts them on, that the blessing made first properly counts, for making the knot is not an interruption. But if after tightening the tefillin on the arm and the mitzvah has been done properly the knot then breaks, it implies that he must bless. The Taz disagrees and rules that as long as he has not yet put on the head tefillin he is still engaged in the mitzvah of the arm tefillin, for he cannot put on the head tefillin before the arm tefillin, and the mitzvah of the arm tefillin is not yet completed. It is no worse than what the Shulchan Aruch wrote, that if the arm tefillin came loose before putting on the head tefillin he need not bless; the same applies if it broke, that he need not bless, for coming loose and breaking are equal; see there. So ruled the Magen Avraham, who wrote that according to what he wrote in seif katan 15 in the name of the Shelah, it implies that even if it broke after tightening he need not bless. So ruled Responsa Halachos Ketanos, part 2, siman 120; see there. See Olas Tamid, Nachalas Tzvi, and what Yad Aharon wrote.
Stolen. For it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. The Taz wrote that even despair and transfer of domain do not help. Borrowing without the owner’s knowledge is permitted also with tefillin, as with a tallis, but he should fold them as they were originally. Responsa Binyamin Ze’ev, siman 299, cited in Knesses HaGedolah, Bnei Chaya, and Olas Tamid.
