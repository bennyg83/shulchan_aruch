If one puts on tefillin, etc. See Mishnah Berurah regarding the case in which, at the time of the berachah, he intended, etc. Although Magen Avraham discusses tzitzis there, it is obvious that the same applies to tefillin, for Menachos 43 compares the two. See there.
Some say that he should not recite a berachah; see Mishnah Berurah, where I cited the ruling that one who removes his tefillin to enter the lavatory must repeat the berachah. Although Magen Avraham remains uncertain regarding our lavatories, which are not distant, Chayei Adam has already written that we do not abandon the certainty of all these great authorities (Bach, Olas Tamid, Taz, and Eliyah Rabbah) because of Magen Avraham's uncertainty. Moreover, his underlying reasoning has been rejected; see Rabbi Akiva Eiger's novellae and Artzos HaChaim. I have also seen a host of authorities all write in the same manner, that the berachah must be repeated. Therefore, although Pri Megadim and Derech HaChaim cite Magen Avraham, one who recites the berachah certainly loses nothing, particularly since Gra agrees with Shulchan Aruch that in every circumstance he must repeat it.
And this is the practice; Artzos HaChaim writes that if one removed the tefillin without any particular intention, then if he removed both the hand- and head-tefillin he must repeat the berachah. If either the hand- or head-tefillin remained upon him, he need not repeat it, because he has not yet diverted his attention from the mitzvah. If, however, he intended not to put it back on immediately but only later, then even if one tefillah remained upon him, he must repeat the berachah. He derives all this from the law above in siman 8 regarding a tallis katan remaining upon him; the same applies here when one tefillah remains. See there. It appears obvious that one who removes his tefillin during prayer, even without specific intent, has the same law as one who removes them intending to replace them. Therefore, if he removed them to go outdoors to urinate, he need not afterward repeat the berachah.
The knot broke; if the strap of the head- or hand-tefillin broke after the mitzvah of putting them on had already been completed, and he removed and repaired the tefillin, the following appears at first glance. If the remaining strap retains the required measure explained below in siman 27:8, and some say it is therefore valid by law without any repair, this is no longer comparable to tefillin that slipped off by themselves. It is like moving them intentionally in order to replace them, for which we rule that one need not repeat the berachah. If they were completely invalid, he must repeat the berachah, for this is comparable to their slipping off. Perhaps, however, since when the strap broke the tefillin remained tightly fastened upon his head or arm, it is afterward regarded in every circumstance as removing them in order to replace them. See Beis Yosef.
Another knot; it appears to me that there is no distinction whether the knot is made from the same strap or he must use a new strap, because the berachah primarily applies to the tefillin themselves.
He need not repeat the berachah; see Mishnah Berurah, where we wrote that if the knot of the head- or hand-tefillin broke after he had already tightened the head-tefillin, he must repeat the berachah. Magen Avraham writes that, according to Shelah's ruling that no berachah is recited when tefillin slip, the same applies when the knot breaks. First, Shelah's law is based on treating the case as one who removes the tefillin intending to put them back on; regarding removal itself, many Rishonim and Acharonim, including Beis Yosef and Gra, hold that a berachah is required. Even according to Rama, who holds that one who removes them intending to put them back on need not recite a berachah, many authorities disagree with Shelah; see Eliyah Rabbah and Chayei Adam. Particularly in our case, many disagree with Magen Avraham because a distinction exists between this and Shelah's case. There, tefillin commonly slip during prayer, so at the time of the berachah he certainly intended to adjust them if they slipped. A broken knot, however, is uncommon. Pri Megadim and Derech HaChaim also appear to rule this way; see there. It therefore seems obvious to me that one who recites the berachah loses nothing.
He tightens it and recites the berachah; this is not literal, for the berachah must be recited before tightening it, as stated above in se'if 8. Know that the later authorities imply that this ruling applies specifically when the knot came undone by itself through loosening. If he untied it by hand and then retied it, according to Rama's ruling above concerning one who intentionally moved the tefillin, he need not repeat the berachah in any circumstance. I am uncertain about one who opened the tefillin to inspect the passages, found them valid, replaced them inside, and sewed them properly. Perhaps everyone agrees that he must repeat the berachah when putting them on, even if, when removing them, he intended to replace them if they proved valid. In the interim, their status as tefillin was nullified; they were merely passages and compartments. This differs from a loosened or broken knot, because tefillin retain their status even without straps. A similar uncertainty arises regarding tzitzis. It is obvious that if one discovers, while wearing his tallis, that one set of tzitzis is invalid by law and then repairs it, he must recite a berachah when he puts it on again. This precisely resembles a knot breaking after completion of the mitzvah; I found this ruling in Derech HaChaim. If, however, the tzitzis are valid by law but he wishes to remove the tallis, replace them with more beautiful tzitzis, and immediately put it back on, then at first glance this is called intentionally removing it in order to put it back on, and he need not repeat the berachah, as stated above in siman 8:14 in the gloss. One can reject this comparison: there the tallis and tzitzis remained intact and the mitzvah was never nullified, whereas here the mitzvah of tzitzis was temporarily removed from the garment. Perhaps everyone therefore agrees that he must repeat the berachah. This also differs from Artzos HaChaim's ruling above in siman 8 that if the tzitzis broke after the berachah but before donning the tallis, and he attaches other tzitzis, he need not repeat the berachah. There, fulfillment of the mitzvah had not yet begun, as Taz 12 (Artzos HaChaim's source) explains; our case occurs afterward. All this requires further study.
