If he heard Kaddish, etc. See Mishnah Berurah 36: if one interrupted and answered, he must repeat the berachah. Chayei Adam writes as obvious that, according to our practice, he must recite two berachos over the head-tefillin, while according to Shulchan Aruch he recites only 'concerning the mitzvah.' Artzos HaChaim concludes that in this case, even according to our practice following Rabbeinu Tam, he recites only one berachah; 'concerning the mitzvah.' See there for his reasoning. Be'er Heitev writes: It appears to me that if one's fellow intends to discharge him with the berachah over tefillin and he also intends to fulfill his obligation, he may answer Kaddish or Kedushah. This does not appear correct. Fulfillment through another's berachah is no better than reciting the berachah oneself. If one interrupted after his own berachah, even to answer Yehei Shmei Rabbah, he must repeat the berachah and is not discharged by the first one. He would therefore be causing an unnecessary berachah. See Eliyah Rabbah, which supports our words, and siman 167:6, Magen Avraham 19 and Taz 8, who writes there, among other things, 'Rokeach's ruling is additionally astonishing'; see there. Even Rokeach himself agrees regarding tefillin, as Pri Megadim writes there. All this indicates that even after the fact one should be stringent and repeat the berachah, even if his fellow had already put on his own tefillin at the time of the interruption. At the very least, it is certainly forbidden initially to interrupt in this manner when his fellow discharges him through the berachah.
