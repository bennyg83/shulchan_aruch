For the need of tefillin. Initially it is forbidden. However, if it cannot be done otherwise, it is permitted to speak. Magen Avraham.
With them. Even to answer amen to the blessing over tefillin that another recited for himself is forbidden. Devar Shmuel, siman 142. If he interrupted and answered, there is a dispute among the poskim whether he must bless again; the Taz ruled that he need not bless again, and so wrote Devar Shmuel there. See Responsa Panim Meiros, Orach Chaim part, siman 10. The Magen Avraham wrote that nevertheless it implies that one who puts them on during Chol HaMoed without a blessing may answer, for the prohibition is only because he causes a blessing, and here there is no blessing; see there. According to this, one may answer Kaddish, Kedushah, and amen between one tefillin and the other with Rabbeinu Tam tefillin, since no blessing is made on them. So too is written explicitly in Responsa Darkei Noam, Orach Chaim part, siman 10. It also seems to me that if his fellow blesses over tefillin and he intends to fulfill through his fellow’s blessing, and his fellow also intends to discharge him, he may answer Kaddish and Kedushah; this is clear. But mundane conversation is always forbidden, and so wrote Darkei Noam there. See Olas Tamid, seif katan 12, and see siman 31, seif katan 3, what I wrote there.
