[10] It is proper always to say, 'Blessed is the name of the glory of His kingdom forever and ever,' etc. Levush's omission of this practice appears to follow Beis Yosef, who writes: It does not seem proper to me for a person to bring himself into a possible berachah in vain and rely upon saying, 'Blessed is the name of the glory of His kingdom forever and ever.' Lechem Chamudos writes that Rama was not concerned with Beis Yosef's words: since our practice is to recite both berachos, it is proper, merely as an added precaution, to say 'Blessed is the name,' not because we place ourselves in any doubt of a berachah. Malbushei Yom Tov 2 likewise writes in the name of Derashos Maharash 171 that we are not uncertain; we consider Rabbeinu Tam's view definite. Nevertheless, Levush appears to hold that one should not say 'Blessed is the name,' lest a harmful error result and people mistakenly recite another doubtful berachah in reliance on saying 'Blessed is the name.' The general practice, however, follows Rama. I saw Olas Tamid 7 go even further, writing: It is better to intend not to fulfill the obligation through the berachah 'to put on tefillin'; the berachah recited over the head-tefillin will then not be in vain, as stated in Responsa Menachem Azaryah 102. This is astonishing. We rule that if one spoke between the two tefillin, or if he has only the head-tefillin, he recites two berachos. If he intends not to discharge the head-tefillin through 'to put on tefillin,' he will have to recite that berachah again over the head-tefillin. To the contrary, he must intend that 'to put on tefillin' apply also to the head-tefillin, for it was instituted for the head-tefillin as well, as Levush explains. Responsa Menachem Azaryah affords no proof, because he holds that only one berachah is recited over both tefillin, and that if one spoke between them he recites only 'concerning the mitzvah of tefillin.' For us, who recite two berachos, his reasoning is inapplicable. Rama's words are therefore primary. Lechem Chamudos explains that Rama's phrase, 'It is proper always to say it after the second berachah,' includes even one who spoke between the two tefillin. According to Rashi, he does not repeat 'to put on tefillin,' while according to our practice he repeats that berachah as well. Because one may not interrupt between the two berachos by saying 'Blessed is the name,' Rama says that he always says it after the second berachah, even in this case. When he spoke between them, however, he must intend when saying 'Blessed is the name' that it relate to the repeated berachah 'to put on tefillin.' It appears that the same law applies when he has only the head-tefillin.
