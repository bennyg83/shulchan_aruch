And it is good always to say “Blessed be the name of the glory of His kingdom forever and ever,” etc. The Levush omitted this, apparently because the Beit Yosef wrote: This matter does not appear correct to me, that a person should bring himself into a possible blessing in vain and rely on saying “Blessed be the name of the glory of His kingdom forever and ever.” Although Lechem Chamudos wrote that the Rama was not concerned for the words of the Beit Yosef, for since our practice is to recite both blessings it is good to say it merely for added caution, not because we enter into any doubt of a blessing at all, and so Malbushei Yom Tov wrote in the name of the derashos of Maharshal that we are not in doubt, for we are certain like Rabbeinu Tam, nevertheless it appears that the Levush holds that one should not say “Blessed be the name,” lest it appear as if there is a doubt of a blessing.
