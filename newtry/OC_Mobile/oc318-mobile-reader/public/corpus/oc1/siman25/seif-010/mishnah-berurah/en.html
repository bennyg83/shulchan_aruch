If he spoke. Even if he spoke between the blessing of lehaniach and putting on the tefillin of the hand, the law is also so.
He does not, etc. But initially it is forbidden to speak between the blessing of lehaniach and after putting on the tefillin of the head, even about matters needed for the tefillin, unless it is impossible otherwise. On Chol HaMoed, however, when no blessing is recited, perhaps one may be lenient initially regarding matters needed for the tefillin.
He should not interrupt. Even to answer amen to this very blessing, such as answering amen to the blessing over tefillin that another person recites, is forbidden, unless his fellow intends to discharge him through this blessing. This is from the responsum Divrei Shmuel and Rabbi Akiva Eiger in his novellae, unlike the Peri Megadim; see there.
To answer with them. If he stopped and answered yehei shemei rabbah, Kedushah, Barchu, or answered amen to some blessing that he heard, he goes back and blesses. See Biur Halacha. On Chol HaMoed, or one putting on Rabbeinu Tam tefillin and the like, where he puts them on without a blessing, one may be lenient and interrupt to answer yehei shemei rabbah, Kedushah, Barchu, and amen. But afterward he should move the tefillin of the hand from its place before putting on the tefillin of the head, so that there should be one being for both of them.
He is silent and listens. For silence is like answering with respect to fulfilling the obligation, but not that it is considered an interruption.
