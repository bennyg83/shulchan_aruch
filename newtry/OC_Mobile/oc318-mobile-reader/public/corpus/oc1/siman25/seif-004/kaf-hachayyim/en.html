4) If one has Rashi tefillin but wishes to put on Rabbeinu Tam tefillin, and he has no tzitzis, the tzitzis take precedence, since most people do not put on Rabbeinu Tam tefillin at all. The reverse is not true: even if he has Rabbeinu Tam tefillin but lacks Rashi tefillin, the Rashi tefillin precede tzitzis, because they are primary with respect to the berachah, as stated in siman 34. Although the sanctity of Rabbeinu Tam tefillin is exceedingly great, for fulfilling one's obligation Rashi tefillin take precedence. Indeed, it is precisely because each is a distinct spiritual operation, as stated in the Zohar Chadash and in siman 34, that most people refrained from putting on Rabbeinu Tam tefillin. It is therefore obvious that one who cannot purchase both should first buy Rashi tefillin and later, when he can afford them, buy Rabbeinu Tam tefillin. This is universal practice, and the same principle applies in relation to tzitzis. Shaarei Teshuvah, ibid. This also appears from the Arizal's words in Shaar HaKavanos, Derushei Tefillin, derush 6, which I cited in my sacred work Kol Yaakov 34:4. He writes that Rashi tefillin are put on before Rabbeinu Tam tefillin because the mochin of Imma, represented by Rashi tefillin, enter first, and afterward the mochin of Abba, represented by Rabbeinu Tam tefillin, enter. Since the former enter first, they must be put on first. Accordingly, one who cannot buy both should first buy Rashi tefillin and, when he is able, buy Rabbeinu Tam tefillin. The same appears from our practice in the silent Amidah and its repetition: the silent prayer draws down the mochin of Binah, while the repetition draws down the mochin of Chochmah. When both cannot be recited, only the silent prayer, corresponding to the mochin of Binah, is recited. So too here, when one cannot buy both, he should first buy Rashi tefillin. Although tzitzis also precedes tefillin in the spiritual order, as stated above in paragraph 1, whereas we wrote that one unable to buy both tzitzis and tefillin should give precedence to tefillin, that case is different because of the loss of the mitzvah: neglecting tefillin is more severe, as stated at the beginning of siman 37, whereas one who has no four-cornered garment does not thereby neglect the mitzvah of tzitzis, as stated at the beginning of siman 24. When the choice is between neglecting Rashi tefillin and Rabbeinu Tam tefillin, Rashi tefillin are more stringent, as stated at the beginning of siman 34, for the universal practice follows Rashi. Regarding Shaarei Teshuvah's ruling that tzitzis precede Rabbeinu Tam tefillin, however, see Yafeh LaLev 2, who challenges it and writes that Rabbeinu Tam tefillin also precede tzitzis. In light of the Arizal's words, arguments can be made in either direction. He should therefore buy Rabbeinu Tam tefillin and borrow tzitzis, or do the reverse, as circumstances permit, so that he neglects neither, as stated in the next paragraph.
