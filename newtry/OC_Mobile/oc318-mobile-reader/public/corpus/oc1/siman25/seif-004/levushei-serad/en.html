Ibid. “Mistaken vows,” end quote. He had thought that there was more of a mitzvah in doing this than in going from his house with tzitzis and tefillin, but the truth is not so. In truth, this is somewhat difficult, for mistaken vows apply specifically when at the time of the vow he explicitly mentioned the mistake, such as: “Wine is forbidden to me because it is bad for the intestines,” and it became known that it is beneficial. But if he did not say this explicitly, he requires release, as explained in Yoreh Deah, siman 232, seif 6 and 7; see there. However, in seif 10 there it is written that although he did not mention it explicitly, nevertheless we judge it as a mistaken vow. This is why the Magen Avraham concluded, “and see Yoreh Deah, siman 232, seif 10”; his intent is that from there Maharam derived his law. Even so, the Magen Avraham disagrees with him and concludes, “it appears to me that release is required.” The Taz there also disagrees with that ruling in seif 10; see there, and this is clear.
