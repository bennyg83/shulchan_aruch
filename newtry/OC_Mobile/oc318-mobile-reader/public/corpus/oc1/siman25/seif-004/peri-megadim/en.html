See Taz, who brought the words of the Razah at the end of Rosh Hashanah; the Beit Yosef brought them through the Ran in the name of the Razah, and the Magen Avraham also brought them in letter 15. This requires consideration, for one who examines the Razah there sees that he concludes that it is like one blessing two blessings over the head tefillin, and this is a third view. The Rif explains that if one spoke, etc.; Rabbeinu Tam explains that one recites two blessings over the head tefillin, because the blessing of lehaniach also refers to the head tefillin. But the Razah, of blessed memory, holds that they are certainly two mitzvos according to all, and lehaniach refers only to the arm tefillin. Rather, if one spoke, a transgression is in his hand because he interrupted between them, for the verse says that they shall be as a sign upon your arm and as frontlets, etc., one being for both of them, so that one not interrupt between them. It appears that even an interruption of silence is forbidden if he delays much without need. Therefore whenever he interrupted, he must necessarily recite lehaniach anew over the arm tefillin, touch the arm tefillin, and join the head tefillin to the blessing of al mitzvas. When he spoke and recites two blessings, it does not mean two blessings over the head tefillin; rather, it appears as if he blesses two over the head tefillin, but in truth lehaniach refers to the arm tefillin. According to this, even if he recited lehaniach anew and did not touch the arm tefillin, he should still touch it before the blessing of al mitzvas, so that the arm tefillin and head tefillin are joined, as the verse says, and they shall be, etc. But for us, since we rule that if he spoke he recites two blessings over the head tefillin itself, see siman 26, for one who puts on only the head tefillin recites two blessings over the head tefillin like Rabbeinu Tam and unlike the Razah, seemingly there is no need to touch the arm tefillin. Nevertheless, the Magen Avraham wrote in letter 15 that he should touch it in order to satisfy the doubt in the best way, for some say that as long as one touches tefillin he may bless, etc. The Magen Avraham's proof from the Taz is that the Beit Yosef brought the words of the Ran in the name of the Razah, but did not conclude that one blesses two over the head tefillin, except according to Rabbeinu Tam, meaning that one who follows Rabbeinu Tam should touch, etc.; therefore the Magen Avraham wrote so. In any event, as practical halachah, if one spoke and blessed anew with lehaniach but did not touch, he should still touch the arm tefillin, so that the two mitzvos are joined together. Also, even by silence, it is forbidden to interrupt more than necessary. Understand this together with what I wrote on Magen Avraham letter 15.
