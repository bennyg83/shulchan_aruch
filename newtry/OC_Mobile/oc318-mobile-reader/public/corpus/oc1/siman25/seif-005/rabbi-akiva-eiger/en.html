There, in the gloss. 'And so the custom has spread.' At first glance, there is a way to avoid the concern of a blessing in vain. When reciting lehaniach, one should have the following conditional intent: If the law follows Rashi, he does not intend with this blessing to exempt the head tefillin, and he may consequently recite al mitzvat tefillin over the head tefillin. If the law follows Tosafot, according to whom the head tefillin require two blessings, he intends that the blessing lehaniach should also cover the head tefillin. Afterward I was shown that Eliyah Rabbah cites Olat Tamid as saying that one should intend with the blessing lehaniach not to exempt the head tefillin. Eliyah Rabbah responds that according to Tosafot he would then have to recite two blessings over the head tefillin. But these authorities too accept the fundamental premise that one can intend not to exempt the head tefillin. As it appears to me, this may be compared to the law in siman 174, seif 4. Our proposal is therefore correct: He should have conditional intent, as in siman 489, Magen Avraham, seif katan 7.
