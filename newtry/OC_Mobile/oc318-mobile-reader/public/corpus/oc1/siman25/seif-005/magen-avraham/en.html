He should have intent. The Bach wrote in the name of the gaon Maharer Feivush, of blessed memory, that one must also read these two sections, Kadesh and Vehayah Ki Yeviacha. So too wrote the Shelah. But in Sifrei, Parashas Vaeschanan, it says that Shema and Vehayah Im Shamoa are subject to recitation, while Kadesh and Vehayah Ki Yeviacha are not subject to recitation, and the Yalkut brings this, meaning that one need not say them. Nevertheless, one who says them has not lost, for they sought to establish Parashas Balak in Kerias Shema because it contains the Exodus from Egypt, but they were concerned for burdening the congregation, as stated at the end of the first chapter of Berachos, and these sections also contain the Exodus from Egypt.
And he should put on, etc. The Olas Tamid did not examine siman 28.
Tefillin. The letter lamed is pronounced with a dagesh, as written in Lechem Chamudos.
Al mitzvas. With a patach beneath the vav, for it refers only to the head tefillin alone, as stated in siman 26. Even according to what the Tur wrote, that it also refers to the arm tefillin, nevertheless I practice saying it with a patach, because the two are considered as one. These are the words of Lechem Chamudos. So it seems to me as well, for we rule in Sukkah 46 like Rabbi Yehudah, that it is forbidden to recite one blessing over mitzvos for two items as one; rather, one must say that the two are considered as one. Furthermore, we find in the verse, the commandment of Hashem is pure, and it refers to all the mitzvos. By contrast, if one says mitzvos with a cholam, it does not fit well according to the other explanations.
