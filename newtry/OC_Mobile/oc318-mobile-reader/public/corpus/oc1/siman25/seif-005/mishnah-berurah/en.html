He should intend when putting them on. The Bach wrote in siman 8 that the reason for this is from the verse, “and it shall be for you a sign on your hand,” etc., “so that the Torah of Hashem shall be in your mouth, for with a strong hand He took you out,” etc. This teaches that the essence of the mitzvah and its fulfillment depend on intent, that he should intend at the time he performs the mitzvah. The Peri Megadim wrote that nevertheless, after the fact, even if he intended only for the sake of the mitzvah alone, he fulfilled.
To place four passages. Some practice, because of this, to read the four passages after putting on tefillin, namely Kadesh and Vehayah ki yeviacha, for Shema and Vehayah im shamoa are in any case read by all Israel at the time of Shema. With Rabbeinu Tam tefillin, he should say all four passages. This is a proper custom. Artzos HaChaim.

And bless. Meaning before tightening, as below.
Tefillin. The lamed has a dagesh.
Al mitzvas. With a patach under the vav, which is singular, and one should not say it with a cholam, which is plural, for this blessing was established only for the head tefillin alone, as will be explained in siman 26. So most Acharonim ruled, unlike the Taz.
And it is good to say. Because of the concern of a possible blessing in vain, as written in siman 206. This is not a complete doubt, for if so, how could one bless in a doubtful case of a blessing in vain and rely on saying Baruch shem kevod malchuso? Rather, we Ashkenazim hold as halacha like Rabbeinu Tam; only as an added measure, to remove ourselves from any possible question, we are accustomed to say Baruch shem kevod malchuso. One must be very careful not to say Baruch shem until after he has tightened the head tefillin properly on his head, for otherwise it will be an interruption between the blessing and the placement, and this blessing will certainly be in vain, and he will have to bless again. People stumble in this.
