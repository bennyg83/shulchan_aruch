He should first put on the arm tefillin and recite the blessing “to place tefillin,” not “to tie tefillin,” although the Torah states, “You shall tie them.” The reason is that the primary mitzvah of tefillin is for them to remain upon him throughout the day. Had the Sages instituted the blessing “to tie,” it would imply that by tying tefillin on his arm and head for a single moment he fulfills his obligation. They therefore instituted “to place,” which implies that they should remain upon him for at least some period. Rabbi Yitzchak Abuhav; Mahari Trani; Orach Chayim; Bach. In any event, it is proper to put on tefillin at Minchah. Responsa Menachem Azariah, siman 39. One should not remove them until reciting “as the days of heaven upon the earth” in the evening. The word tefillin denotes argument, as in “Pinchas stood and contended”: he conducted a dispute with his Creator. They are so called because they are testimony and proof that the name of the Omnipresent is called upon us and the nations fear us. Tosafos in the chapter HaKometz. Immediately after tying the arm tefillin, one should place the arm strap over its titora so that it does not move from its place. If it moved and he did not know whether this occurred before he put on the head tefillin or afterward, he would enter a doubt whether he must recite the blessing again. He should make three windings on the biceps and four windings on the forearm, and afterward put on the head tefillin. This is the implication of Maharil in Shulchan Aruch according to the esoteric teaching. In these lands, the practice is to put on both tefillin while standing. Since we do not know how to apprehend the esoteric teaching correctly and one can easily, Heaven forbid, “cut the shoots,” it is proper to put on both while standing, like a servant who stands before his master and accepts his seal. Maharshal, Responsa, siman 98; ot 13. One who recites Musaf while wearing tefillin is liable to death. Zohar, parashat Pinchas. Perhaps this also discusses a place where the Kedushah of Keter is recited during Musaf, as Rama writes. It is proper to remove them when Musaf is recited, at the point where one says, “May it be Your will that we observe Your statutes in this world,” etc., because the people of the West would recite the blessing upon removing tefillin, “Who sanctified us with His commandments and commanded us to observe His statutes.” Although one should not recite a blessing upon removing them, since no blessing is recited here, removing them at this point is permissible. Some do not put on tefillin on Chol HaMoed, and all the more so on Chol HaMoed Sukkot, when the four species constitute a sign corresponding to the four passages in the tefillin. So it appears to me.
