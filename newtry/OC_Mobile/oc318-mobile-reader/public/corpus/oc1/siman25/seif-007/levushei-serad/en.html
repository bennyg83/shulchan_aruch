Taz seif katan 6 Behold, we see that they consider them one mitzvah. Even without this, there is a difficulty against the Divrei Chamudos: how could he write that tefillin are different because they are one mitzvah? On the contrary, regarding one mitzvah it is more reasonable that speech should not interrupt, as with shofar blasts. See Magen Avraham, seif katan 11, siman 8. According to my answer, this difficulty is also resolved. The principle of his answer is that wherever one wrote regarding tefillin that they are one mitzvah, the intent is that they are two obligatory mitzvos that one is obligated to perform together, so that the two blessings should not apply to both of them. In truth, where it is entirely one mitzvah, speech has no power to interrupt, as with shofar blasts; and where they are entirely two mitzvos, such as slaughtering, his speech does nothing, for even without this, at the time of the blessing he had intent for the second as well.
