[12] With a kamatz, etc. Matzas Shimurim, page 64, in the name of HaMaarich in Sefer HaAruch, writes that Shulchan Aruch erred in ruling that 'lehaniach' is pronounced with a kamatz, for that form denotes rest. Here it should be pronounced with a patach and a dagesh, meaning to place tefillin, like the statement concerning the Chanukah lamp: 'It is a mitzvah to place it at the entrance of his house.' It is astonishing that he had the temerity to dispute Shulchan Aruch, whose ruling comes from Agur 37. He also failed to see Levush's words, which reject this position from Scriptural verses. The Talmudic wording affords no proof; perhaps the Talmud's text concerning Chanukah should also be vocalized with a kamatz. Tashbetz Katan 271 likewise accords with Shulchan Aruch. He also overlooked Radak's words in Sefer HaShorashim, where these appear under two separate roots: the form with a kamatz under the root nuach, and the form with a patach under the root yanach; see there. Further proof comes from the verses, 'Efrayim is joined to idols; leave him,' 'Now leave Me,' 'I shall leave him upon his land,' and 'to leave them in the wilderness,' all of which denote leaving. I also saw that Olas Tamid 9 writes: Levush and Lechem Chamudos disagree with Shulchan Aruch, and their words appear correct; that the berachah should be pronounced with a patach. This is exceedingly astonishing, for Levush, to the contrary, upholds the pronunciation with a kamatz, as does Lechem Chamudos, page 94, and Malbushei Yom Tov 4. Their objection concerns only Beis Yosef's statement that there is no difference in meaning; on that point they write that there is a great difference, and that it must specifically be pronounced with a kamatz. This is the primary ruling. I found in Piskei Tosafos, Menachos 131: 'He should recite lehaniach softly, and not lehaniach with a dagesh.' This evidently means that he should pronounce it with a kamatz, for the patach form has a dagesh. Lechem Chamudos 55 writes that in the berachah 'al mitzvas tefillin,' the vav of 'mitzvas' may be pronounced with a cholam or with a patach; one may choose. I am accustomed to pronounce it with a patach, because the two tefillin are considered a single unit. Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur 2, writes that this is the universal practice; Or Chadash, Berachos HaTefillin, rules likewise, unlike Taz 10. If one has only the head-tefillin, all agree that the vav is pronounced with a patach.
