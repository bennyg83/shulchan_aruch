Although, if one encounters the head-tefillin first, he sets it aside and first puts on the hand-tefillin, as stated in se'if 6, that case is different. Scripture says, 'It shall be a sign upon your arm,' and only afterward, 'and as frontlets between your eyes.' We also expound the verse to mean that whenever the head-tefillin are between your eyes, there shall be two tefillin upon you. It is therefore better to pass over that mitzvah than to contravene what is written in the Torah. Here, however, we know that tzitzis precedes only from a mere Scriptural allusion; it is better that this allusion be set aside than that one pass over a mitzvah. Beis Yosef; Levush; and Taz 1. All this follows the plain meaning. According to the Arizal, however, who gives a kabbalistic reason why tzitzis must be donned before tefillin and the hand-tefillin before the head-tefillin; see above, paragraph 1 and the sources cited there concerning the Arizal, as well as the sources cited in the preceding paragraph; there is no distinction. Just as one who encounters the head-tefillin first sets it aside and first puts on the hand-tefillin, so too one who happens to encounter tefillin first should set them aside and first don tzitzis. Although the prohibition against passing over mitzvos is derived from the verse, 'You shall guard the matzos,' which teaches that when a mitzvah comes to your hand you should not let it become delayed, as Levush writes, that applies to other mitzvos that have no prescribed order. Mitzvos that possess a kabbalistically prescribed order may be passed over, as in the case of tefillin. I subsequently saw that Solel Boneh 1, Yafeh LaLev 6, and Ben Ish Chai, Vayeira 4, also write this.
