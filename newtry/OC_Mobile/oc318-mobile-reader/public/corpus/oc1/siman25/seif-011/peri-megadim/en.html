Move them. See Taz, and in siman 8, letter 12, that regarding a bathroom, since it is impossible to enter there with tefillin, it is considered an interruption. Although in siman 21, letter 3, it says that with a large tallis it is not proper to enter a bathroom, nevertheless this is not strictly according to the law, and therefore for tzitzis it is not considered an interruption. As the Magen Avraham wrote in letter 22, in our bathrooms, which are not distant, one need not bless, and so is the practice. The Mechaber first brought the law of Rabbi from Sukkah 46, afterward the view of the Sages of the academy of Rav Ashi, and afterward his own explanation of the Sages of the academy of Rav Ashi, that they would touch them in order to return them to their place, even if they slipped knowingly. The Rav agreed with him in his explanation of the Rosh that if they slipped unknowingly, it is like intending not to return them immediately, but he disagreed with him where he moved them with intent to return them immediately; even if he removed them entirely with intent to return them immediately, he disagreed with him, and explained this in siman 8, seif 14. At first glance, according to the Mechaber, if they slipped from their place it is considered without need. Moreover, if unknowingly he put back the arm tefillin and blessed lehaniach, and afterward returned the head tefillin, even according to the Mechaber he blesses again where he did not know at the time of the first blessing.
