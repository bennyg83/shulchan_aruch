Before he wraps. The reason is that the blessing of lehaniach also returns upon the tefillin of the head, as above in seif katan 31. Certainly according to the Mechaber above in seif 5, who holds that he recites only one blessing, he should therefore minimize interruption as much as he can. The wrapping around the arm is not of the essence of the law, therefore it is good to delay it until he puts on the tefillin of the head. In the book of the kavanot of the Arizal it is written that he would first wrap the seven wrappings around his arm, but not the three wrappings on the finger, because he holds that initially the wrappings too are a mitzvah, and therefore this is not an interruption. Such is the practice in many places. See Responsa Mishkenos Yaakov, siman 28, whose view is also like our practice, to wrap first on the arm for tightening, for without this it does not remain and is not called tying; see there. The book Maamar Mordechai wrote that if he heard Kaddish or Kedushah after blessing over the tefillin of the hand, and if he wraps he will not have time to put on the tefillin of the head and answer, he should put on the tefillin of the head before the wrappings, if he can tighten somewhat so that the tefillin of the hand will not move from its place.
That it is forbidden, etc. This means even if he wants to take the tefillin of the head and the tefillin of the hand out of the case equally, for in such a case he does not pass over the mitzvos, nevertheless one must be careful, for there is a reason for this according to Kabbalah, as written in the Beit Yosef. Certainly to set aside the tefillin of the hand from his hand and occupy himself with taking out the tefillin of the head is forbidden according to the law.
Placed. Meaning that it should be tightened well upon the arm, and then it is permitted even before he has made the seven wrappings. If another person can take out and prepare the tefillin of the head while his fellow is putting on the tefillin of the hand, the Peri Megadim is stringent and the Artzos HaChaim is lenient; see there.
He should not prepare. Meaning that even if, while preparing the tefillin of the head, he will also not set the tefillin of the hand down from his hand, nevertheless, when afterward he sets down the tefillin of the head in order to put on the tefillin of the hand, it is like passing over the mitzvos.
Both while standing. See the Magen Avraham, who wants to decide that putting on the tefillin of the hand should be while sitting and the blessing should be while standing. But the Eliyah Rabbah, in the name of the Maharshal in Responsa siman 98, wrote: who is greater for us than Rabbi Shimon of Kinon, who after studying Kabbalah would pray like a one-day-old child, for one who cannot properly grasp its secret may come to cut down the plantings. Therefore the blessing and the putting on should be while standing. In Beur HaGra he proved that even according to the Zohar it is permitted to put on the tefillin of the hand while standing, therefore one should not move from the custom. The Knesses HaGedolah wrote in the rules of the poskim: any matter in which the masters of Kabbalah and the Zohar disagree with the Gemara and the poskim, follow the Gemara and the poskim. However, if the masters of Kabbalah are stringent, one should also be stringent. If it is not mentioned in the Gemara and the poskim, even though it is mentioned in Kabbalah, we cannot compel people to practice so. But a law that is not mentioned oppositely in the Talmud and poskim, one should follow the words of Kabbalah. Also, where there is a dispute among the poskim, the words of Kabbalah decide.
