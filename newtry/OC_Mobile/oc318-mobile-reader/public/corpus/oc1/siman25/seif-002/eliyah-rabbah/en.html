[2] He should put on tefillin, etc. Sefer HaChinuch, Vaeschanan, mitzvah 421, writes: Because man is a material being, he is necessarily drawn after desire, for the nature of matter seeks every pleasure, like a horse or mule without understanding. Were it not for the soul with which God graced him, restraining him from sin to the extent of its strength, he would succumb. Because the soul dwells within his boundary (the earth) and is distant from its own boundary (the heavens) it cannot always overcome him, while his material force overcomes it. It therefore requires many guards: tefillin, tzitzis, mezuzah, and that words of Torah not cease from our mouths day or night. These serve to remind us so that we cease the oppression of our hands. Our Sages further said: Why is entry into the Land juxtaposed to the passage of tefillin? The Holy One, blessed is He, said: Perform this mitzvah, through whose merit you will enter the Land and merit life in the World to Come. They also said that if a person's transgressions and merits are evenly balanced and he bears the sin of neglecting tefillin, it tips the scale toward guilt. Baruch She'amar 26 writes: In the three verses containing the word 'letotafos' (one in Vehayah Ki Yeviacha, one in Shema, and one in Vehayah Im Shamoa) and the word 'lezikaron' written in Kadesh Li Chol Bechor, all the letters of the alef-beis appear except gimel and samech. This teaches that a gas ruach, an arrogant person, should not wear tefillin; rather, they are for one whose spirit is humble and who takes no credit for himself when he studies or performs a mitzvah. Sefer Chassidim 273 writes that if the tefillin are in their case, one should not shake them out but grasp them with his hand and remove them. I found it written in Emek Berachah 15 that it is proper to wash the place where tefillin are put before putting them on.
[2*] We learn in Berachos 14b: Anyone who recites Krias Shema without tefillin is as though he bears false testimony against himself. Levush writes that some explain this as false testimony concerning God. That is, he says in Krias Shema, 'If you will diligently heed,' and, 'I shall give rain,' yet he does not put on tefillin, while the Holy One, blessed is He, does give rain. Rashi indeed explains it as false testimony concerning God, but Levush's explanation of Rashi (that this is because he says, 'If you will diligently heed,' etc.) is strained. Moreover, the verse does not say 'all My mitzvos'; it says 'My mitzvos.' Would rain not be given merely because he fails to fulfill the mitzvah of tefillin? I saw that Maadanei Melech, page 13, chapter 2, siman 10, note 9, explains Rashi to mean that, had he truthfully testified that 'Hashem is our God, Hashem is One,' he would have fulfilled His mitzvos. Bach 2 inclines to this, but adds that by binding tefillin one testifies to His unity; if he does not put on tefillin, he appears to testify that He is not One. I wonder why they do not mention Levush's words. Tosafos, Berachos 14b, s.v. UManach, write: This means false testimony concerning the Master Who commanded him, for he recites, 'They shall be frontlets' and 'a sign upon your arm,' yet they are absent. This appears to mean that he testifies falsely concerning God by implying that He did not command him to put on tefillin. I found that Abudraham, page 30, explains Rashi this way as well. Apart from this, it is evident to me (with due respect) that Levush did not carefully examine Tosafos. What he writes, 'It appears to me that this is no difficulty,' etc., was already stated explicitly by Tosafos, and Abudraham there asks and answers the same question. Perhaps Levush found their position difficult because, according to it, the Gemara should have said that anyone who wears a four-cornered garment without tzitzis is as though he bears false testimony. To answer this, he may have concluded, 'People are not accustomed to scrutinize,' etc. That is, even if one wears a four-cornered garment without tzitzis, people do not ordinarily scrutinize it, so no falsehood is apparent. This is strained and requires further study. I presented these words to my master and father, the Rav, may his light shine. He said that there is further reason to wonder at Levush's understanding that the false testimony arises because rain comes: Sanhedrin 113a, in the incident involving Achav, implies that withholding rain applies specifically to idolatry. Maharsha there also explains that 'there will be no rain' relates to 'you will swiftly perish,' following 'when you will have grown old.' Moreover, Vehayah Im Shamoa was addressed to the community, as Rashi explains in Parashas Eikev. The condition concerning rain would therefore apply only when the community does not put on tefillin. I replied that this could perhaps be resolved by saying that people at large will err and will not derive these interpretations, just as Achav did not derive them; it therefore appears like false testimony. Nevertheless, I have already clarified Rashi's true meaning.
