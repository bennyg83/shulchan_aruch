One goes crowned with tefillin and touches the mezuzah before leaving his doorway; then four angels accompany him to the synagogue (Zohar, Tikkunei Zohar, Tefillin). One who touches the mezuzah places his hand on it and says: "In Your name, Tal Atal, O the Omnipresent my shade on my right hand." Whenever leaving his doorway he places his hand on the mezuzah and says likewise; also say "May the the Omnipresent guard my going out and coming in from now and forever." In the morning, recite Rabbeinu Yehuda HaNasi's Shema — the verse Shema Yisrael, etc. If there are filthy alleys between his house and synagogue, he places his hand on them until passing, or puts tefillin under his hat. As one enters the synagogue crowned with tefillin, so one leaves crowned with tefillin (Zohar). When donning them, remember their kavanah; also look at two shin letters with one having three tagin and two having four tagin — kavanah alluding to 613 mitzvos: two shin = 300, both are one word pronounced "shesh" = 600, seven tagin = 613. Alternatively, shin hints Shechinah, four tagin against four heavenly beings of the chariot, against "the Omnipresent is exalted above all exalted ones"; three tagin against three patriarchs — the patriarchs are the chariot. For all kavanot in tefillin, see Zohar p. 32, Tikkunei Zohar, Tikkunei Tefillin, Rabbeinu Yonah chapter "Hayeh Korei," Beit David siman 72 — "and see the Omnipresent going out from all of them"; but without kavanah, tefillin are on mind and arm like stones, heaven forbid. One who recites Shema without tefillin, etc. — but tallis is not as obligatory as siman 24 (Maharshal). If one relieved himself, recited Shema, and donned tefillin — it is as if he built an altar and offered all sacrifices: "I will wash my hands in innocence and encircle Your altar, O the Omnipresent" — kavanah: innocence = relieving oneself; wash = netilat yadayim; hands = tefillin, as "let us lift our hearts to our palms"; if he prayed, he already recited Shema, for one must adjoin redemption to prayer. Likewise no Shema without tefillin, as Ulla and R' Yehudah — and the verse is not literal, for David was not a kohen ("the foreigner who approaches shall die") — how could he say "I will encircle Your altar"?
