He should walk crowned with tefillin and touch the mezuzah before leaving the entrance of his house. Four angels will then accompany him to the synagogue. Zohar, Tikkun 10, Tefillin. One who touches the mezuzah and places his hand upon it should say, “In Your name, Tal Ataleh; the Omnipresent is my shade at my right hand.” Whenever he leaves the entrance of his house, he should place his hand on the mezuzah and say this. He should also say, “The Omnipresent shall guard my going out and my coming in, from now and forever.” In the morning he should also recite the Keriat Shema of Rabbi Yehudah HaNasi, namely the verse “Hear, O Israel,” etc. If filthy alleyways lie between his house and the synagogue, he should place his hand over the tefillin until he has passed beyond them, or put the tefillin beneath his hat. Just as one must enter the synagogue crowned with tefillin, so must one leave the synagogue crowned with tefillin. Zohar. When putting them on, he should recall their intended meaning. He must also look at the two letter shins, one with three heads and the other with four heads, intending that they allude to the 613 mitzvot. How so? The numerical value of the two shins is 600; together they form one word, shesh, “six,” bringing the total to 606; their seven heads bring the total to 613. Alternatively, the letter shin alludes to the Shechinah. Its four heads correspond to the four creatures of the Chariot, against the four proud beings, while “the Omnipresent is exalted above all the proud.” Its three heads correspond to the three patriarchs, in accordance with the teaching that the patriarchs themselves are the Chariot. If you wish to master all the intentions a person should have regarding tefillin, see Zohar, page 332; page 506; page 419; page 482; and page 500; Rabbeinu Yonah in the chapter Hayah Koreh; and Beit Ze'ev, siman 72, page 73. One who fears God fulfills all of them. If he does not know the intended meaning, however, the tefillin rest upon his brain and arm like stones, Heaven forbid. One who recites Keriat Shema without tefillin, etc. A tallit, however, is not as absolute an obligation, as explained above in siman 24. Maharshal. If one relieves himself, recites Keriat Shema, and puts on tefillin, it is as though he built an altar and offered all the sacrifices upon it, as it is stated: “I shall wash my hands in cleanliness and circle Your altar, O Omnipresent.” This is the verse's intent: “cleanliness” alludes to relieving himself; “I shall wash” alludes to washing his hands; and “my hands” alludes to prayer, as it is written, “Let us lift our hearts to our hands.” If he prayed, it is obvious that he already recited Keriat Shema, because one must adjoin redemption to prayer, etc. Likewise, there is no proper Keriat Shema without tefillin, in accordance with the statements of Ulla and Rabbi Yochanan. The verse cannot be understood literally, because King David, peace be upon him, was not a kohen, and “the outsider who draws near shall die”; how then could he say, “and circle Your altar, O Omnipresent”?
