(Seif katan 3) He need not, etc. This is a scribal error, and this is how it should read: “and he should put on tefillin specifically in his house,” etc. It refers to what the Beit Yosef wrote in seif 2. So it is in the Zohar, and in Darkei Moshe he brought in the name of Recanati, who wrote in the name of the Zohar, that if one does this the Shechinah becomes present upon him, and two angels that stand over him accompany him to the synagogue and bless him, and one accuser who stands before a person’s doorway follows them and answers amen against his will. The Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Zohar: one who comes to the synagogue without tefillin on his head and tzitzis on his garment and says, “I will bow toward Your holy Sanctuary in Your awe,” the Holy One, blessed is He, says: where is My awe? Certainly he bears false testimony. End quote.
And see Yoreh Deah, siman 232, seif 10. Meaning, from this Maharam, in the responsum mentioned above, brought proof for his words. There it states in seif 7: if one saw from afar people eating his fruit and forbade it to them, and when he came near he saw that they were his father or his brothers, his vow is not a vow, even though he did not first explain at the time he prohibited it that this was his reason. Since they were far away and he thought they were not his father or brothers, nevertheless the matter itself proves that a person does not prohibit his fruit to his father and brothers. In seif 10 it states: if one swore not to do something because he thought it was a grave prohibition, and afterward it became known to him that it was not so severe a prohibition, the oath does not take effect at all, for it was in error. End quote. Maharam compared the case of his question to these two laws. Therefore the Magen Avraham concluded: it seems to me that release is required, since Maharam compared it also to seif 10, and there in seif 10 the Taz wrote that it implies from the Shulchan Aruch that even if he did not explain at the time of the oath that he swore because he thought it was a grave prohibition, nevertheless he does not need release. The Taz wondered why this differs from what is written there in seif 6, that if one said, “konam, my wife shall not benefit from me because she stole my purse or struck my son,” and it was found that she did not steal and did not strike, he does not need release. There we require specifically that he explain at the time of the vow that she stole, etc.; but if he vowed without specifying, even if he says that in his heart it was because she stole, etc., nevertheless he needs release. The same should apply in seif 10: if he did not explain that it was because he thought it was a grave prohibition, he should afterward need release. It is not comparable to seif 7, where even though he did not explain that he forbade them because they were not his father or brothers, nevertheless he needs no release, because that case is different, as the Shulchan Aruch wrote, since the matter itself proves that a person does not prohibit his fruit to his father and brothers, and it is as if he had explained. But in seif 6 and seif 10 we require specifically that he explain at the time of the vow, for the matter does not prove that one who vows against his wife does so because he suspected she stole; perhaps it was for another reason. So too in seif 10, perhaps even had he known it was only a light prohibition, he would still have sworn, and since he did not explain, he needs release. These are his words. Since Maharam compared the present law also to seif 10 there, according to the Taz here too release is required, since here also the matter does not prove. Although the Magen Avraham agrees that regarding the penalty money we do not collect from him, nevertheless regarding the prohibition he is concerned for the view of the Taz and requires release. In Responsa Nachalas Shivah, siman 6, he disagrees with Maharam’s law, for the intent of the Zohar that the Holy One, blessed is He, asks “where is My awe” applies only when one leaves the entrance of his house after day has already dawned and the mitzvah of tefillin and tzitzis already applies to him. But when he leaves before dawn, when it is still impossible to put on tzitzis and tefillin, there is no concern. He brings proof from the words of the author of Reishis Chochmah in his book Totzaos Chaim, and the verse “I will come to Your house... in Your awe” can be explained another way, as referring to the merit of the three Avos; see there. These are his words. One must say that the fact that the Ari of blessed memory was not among the first ten, his main reason was as the Magen Avraham wrote below in siman 90, seif katan 27: because he needed to relieve himself due to illness, and also so that he should go wrapped in tallis and tefillin. Thus one may say that his primary reason was that he needed to relieve himself, and the reason of going wrapped, etc., he made only as an additional support. So too wrote Eliyah Rabbah.
