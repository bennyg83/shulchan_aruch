Ibid. Two; in any case. It is difficult: what does “in any case” mean? In truth, if tefillin are one mitzvah there is no proof according to the approach of Rabbi Yehudah HaLevi, for with one mitzvah it is more reasonable that speech is an interruption. One may answer that his intent by “in any case” is as follows: whether according to Rabbi Yehudah HaLevi, who holds that tefillin are always two mitzvos, or according to the words of Reish Lakish, that they are not two mitzvos except when he spoke, at any rate the proof of Rabbi Yehudah HaLevi is from the case where he spoke, and then they are certainly two mitzvos. Yet if so, what he introduced at the beginning from “although,” etc., is not understandable, since without this introduction his question would have been stronger. It appears to me that the words “if so, what is he saying” as printed before us in the Taz are a scribal error. The text should read: “and there is a further difficulty: what is he saying,” etc. The flow of his words follows accordingly.
