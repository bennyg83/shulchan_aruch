The world practiced. Some do not leave them on beyond the obligation, for they require a clean body. Everything depends on the person: if he does not have a clean body and fears that he may pass gas, he should remove them immediately. See siman 37, seif 2, for this is why most people do not practice wearing them all day. In the name of the Ari of blessed memory they wrote that he would not remove them until after Al kein nekaveh lach. On a day that has a circumcision, it is proper not to remove them until after the circumcision, for circumcision is a sign and tefillin are a sign.
Three sanctifications. This is a scribal error, and in truth it should say three Kaddishim and four matters of sanctity. For Barchu es Hashem counts as one, since it is a matter of sanctity; the Kedushah of Sefah Berurah is second; the Kedushah of the Amidah; and the Kedushah of Uva leTziyon. The three Kaddishim are the half-Kaddish before Barchu, the half-Kaddish after the Shemoneh Esrei, and the full Kaddish after Uva leTziyon. Nevertheless, it implies from Peri Megadim and other Acharonim that in places where they are accustomed to say mourner’s Kaddish every day after Aleinu, it is good not to remove them until after mourner’s Kaddish. People who fold the tallis and tefillin and put them in their cases while Kaddish is being said do not act properly, for one must concentrate greatly when answering Yehei shemei rabbah, as explained below in siman 56 in the Tur and Shulchan Aruch. Yehei shemei rabbah is on an even higher level than Kedushah, as explained there in Magen Avraham, seif katan 1, and it is certainly no worse than other D’Rabbanan blessings, where it is forbidden to do even a light task while he is blessing, as explained below in siman 191, Magen Avraham, seif katan 2.
Until they return. A hint to the matter is “their king passed before them,” meaning the Torah scroll, “and Hashem was at their head,” meaning tefillin. Beit Yosef.
And place it in the ark. One who removes them earlier should at least be careful not to remove the head tefillin in front of the Torah scroll, so that he not uncover his head before it, but should move to the sides. With the arm tefillin, where there is no uncovering, or with head tefillin under the tallis gadol, it is permitted. Peri Megadim.
Mussaf. After they return the Torah scroll to the ark. In our places, where they bring it in immediately after the reading, he should remove them after the Kaddish before the Mussaf prayer. Eliyah Rabbah wrote that on Rosh Chodesh he should remove them after the Kedushah of Uva leTziyon, before saying “May it be Your will before You,” etc., “that we observe Your statutes,” and he should not wait to remove them until after Kaddish, so as not to interrupt between Kaddish and the prayer.
The same applies on Chol HaMoed. The Acharonim wrote that since there are those who say not to put them on at all on Chol HaMoed, as below in siman 31, one should hurry to remove them before Hallel, and the shaliach tzibbur after Hallel. On Chol HaMoed of Sukkos, when there is time while waiting for the esrog, even the shaliach tzibbur should remove them before Hallel. Those accustomed to wear them all day should put them on again on Rosh Chodesh after Mussaf, and need not bless again if he had this in mind at the time he removed them, according to the Rama’s ruling above in seif 12. On Chol HaMoed he should not put them on again at all until evening. Those who put on Rabbeinu Tam tefillin on Chol HaMoed should not put them on at all; on Rosh Chodesh they may put them on after the shaliach tzibbur finishes Mussaf, or remove Rashi tefillin before Uva leTziyon and wear Rabbeinu Tam tefillin while saying Uva leTziyon. Peri Megadim.
The Kedushah of Kesser. Meaning, in places where we say Nakdish at Mussaf, some places say Kesser yitenu lach; therefore it is not proper then for the crown of tefillin to be upon him, even during the silent prayer. It is obvious that if he forgot and began praying with them, he should not remove them in the middle, for this is only a custom.
In every place. The Taz wrote that one who practices not to remove them for Mussaf has no complaint against him, since we do not say the Kedushah of Kesser yitenu lach. I heard about one great person of the generation who would not remove them for Mussaf. However, one who prays with the congregation should certainly not deviate from the custom of the congregation.
