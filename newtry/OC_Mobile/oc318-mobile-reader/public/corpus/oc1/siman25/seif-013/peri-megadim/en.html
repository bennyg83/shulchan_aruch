If. See Taz. See Beit Yosef. He first wrote that if the knot tore before tightening, one does not bless, and after tightening one blesses. Afterward he wrote: I found written in the name of the Raavad that if the tightening became loosened, if it was before the head tefillin, one does not bless, etc. This implies that the words of the Raavad contradict what he wrote initially, and how did he write both here as halachah? Therefore he concluded that in his longer work he was uncertain whether the Raavad disagrees with what was written above, or whether one can say that making the knot is a greater interruption, as the Magen Avraham wrote in letter 23. For this reason he did not write anything about whether they contradict each other. In the Shulchan Aruch he wrote only what was clear to him, namely that if it tore before tightening, one does not bless, while if it tore after tightening he wrote nothing. Had his view been to distinguish between a knot and loosened tightening, as the Magen Avraham wrote, he would have explained this explicitly. Rather, he wrote the law of the Raavad, and automatically, in a doubt regarding blessings we are lenient. Therefore even if the knot tore after tightening, as long as he has not yet put on the head tefillin, he does not return and bless, unlike Magen Avraham 23. In a doubt regarding blessings, we are lenient.
