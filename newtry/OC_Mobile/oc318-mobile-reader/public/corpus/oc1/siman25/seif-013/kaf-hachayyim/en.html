13) There: He should put on tefillin in his home (specifically in his home) so that he leaves the entrance of his house wearing tzitzis and tefillin. Magen Avraham 3 in the names of the Zohar and Shaar HaKavanos. Levush rules likewise, as does Chida in Moreh BeEtzba 3:65 and Shesilei Zeisim 4. The Zohar, Vaeschanan 265a, states: Rabbi Shimon said: When a person rises at midnight and engages in Torah until dawn shines, then in the morning places tefillin upon his head and tefillin bearing the holy imprint upon his arm; see what I wrote in the preceding paragraph in the names of Mahari Tzemach, Ramaz, and Imrei Noam, who reconcile this wording of the Zohar, which mentions tefillin first; and wraps himself in the wrapping of a mitzvah, and then sets out from the entrance of his house, he encounters the mezuzah, the imprint of the holy Name, at his doorway. Four holy angels join him, leave the entrance of his house with him, and escort him to the synagogue. They proclaim before him: 'Give honor to the image of the holy King! Give honor to the King's son, to the precious countenance of the King!' The holy spirit rests upon him and proclaims, 'Yisrael, in whom I glory.' Then that holy spirit ascends above and testifies concerning him before the holy King. The supreme King then commands that he be inscribed before Him among all the members of His palace and all those known before Him. This is the meaning of, 'And a book of remembrance was written before Him,' etc. Not only that, but the Holy One, blessed is He, takes pride in him and proclaims concerning him throughout all the worlds: 'See the child I have made in My world!' But concerning one who enters the synagogue after leaving his doorway without tefillin upon his head and tzitzis upon his garment, and says, 'I will bow toward Your holy Sanctuary in awe of You,' the Holy One, blessed is He, says: 'Where is My awe? Behold, he bears false testimony.' Eliyah Rabbah 6 and Chesed LaAlafim 2 cite this in abridged form. See below, paragraph 20.
