(Seif katan 9) Al mitzvas with a patach. For if it is with a patach it is singular, and with a cholam it is plural, as stated in siman 26, where the Rama wrote that according to us, who bless two blessings on the head tefillin and arm tefillin, if one puts on only the head tefillin alone, he blesses two blessings: lehaniach and al mitzvas. When he puts on both, then the blessing lehaniach that he recited on the arm tefillin also applies to the head tefillin, and it is enough that he blesses al mitzvas on the head tefillin. But when he puts on only the head tefillin, since he has not yet blessed, he must bless on the head tefillin also the blessing lehaniach. There he further wrote: but if one puts on only the arm tefillin alone, he blesses only the blessing lehaniach. If one could say that the blessing al mitzvas also applies to the arm tefillin, then when putting on only the arm tefillin he should also have to bless both, lehaniach and al mitzvas. Rather, it must be that al mitzvas does not apply to the arm tefillin.
Even according to what the Tur wrote, etc. See Taz, seif katan 9, that it also applies to the arm tefillin. If so, he blesses on two mitzvos, and it would be fitting to say mitzvos with a cholam, which is plural.
For it is forbidden, etc. Over two at once, as it is written, “Blessed is Hashem day by day,” meaning each day give Him blessings appropriate to it. The same law applies with every mitzvah.
The commandment of Hashem is clear, etc. It uses mitzvas with a patach, and it refers to all mitzvos. This must be because all the mitzvos are bound and joined to one another, and therefore even many mitzvos are called by the singular form. As written in parshas Eikev, “the entire commandment that I command you today you shall observe,” which according to the simple meaning refers to all the mitzvos in the singular form. If so, when he says here mitzvas with a patach he fulfills according to both possibilities. If it refers only to the head tefillin, which is one mitzvah, it is certainly proper to say mitzvas in the singular. And if it also refers to the arm tefillin, which is like two mitzvos, it is still proper to say mitzvas with a patach in the singular, for even many mitzvos are called by a singular expression.
It does not fit the other explanation, meaning according to the one who holds that it does not apply to the arm tefillin and is only one mitzvah, while he says mitzvos with a cholam, which is plural.
