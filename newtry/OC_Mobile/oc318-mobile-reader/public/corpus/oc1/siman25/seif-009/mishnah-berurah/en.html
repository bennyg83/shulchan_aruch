It is forbidden to interrupt. Even with speech for a mitzvah, such as returning greeting to his rebbe and the like, because he thereby causes an unnecessary blessing. Even those who put on tefillin on Chol HaMoed without a blessing, or who put on the tefillin of Rabbeinu Tam after removing Rashi tefillin, or who removed tefillin with intent to put them back, where the Rama in seif 12 ruled that one need not recite another blessing when he puts them on afterward, nevertheless it is a transgression to speak between them. Initially we require them to be adjacent and joined to one another, as it is written, and it shall be for you as a sign upon your hand and as a remembrance between your eyes, meaning that there should be one being for both of them.
With speech. Even in the Holy Tongue. Initially it is forbidden even to interrupt with silence if he waits a long time without need, even if he did not divert his mind. Even to signal with his eyes or gesture with his fingers between the tefillin of the hand and the tefillin of the head, one should also be careful initially. If he put on the tefillin of the hand in one house and the tefillin of the head in another house, and his intent was for this, see above, siman 8, seif 13, and the Mishnah Berurah there regarding a tallis, and the same applies in our matter.
And if he interrupted. Even unintentionally.
And according to our practice, etc. The reason we recite two blessings over tefillin, and do not suffice with the blessing of lehaniach alone, is that we hold that the original institution of these blessings was this way. At the beginning of putting them on they instituted reciting lehaniach, and it also refers to the tefillin of the head. When he places the tefillin of the head and tightens it, he also recites al mitzvas, for this is the completion of the mitzvah. Therefore, automatically, if he spoke and diverted his mind, he must also repeat the blessing of lehaniach over the tefillin of the head. This is from the Rosh.
To go back and bless. It is proper then to handle the tefillin of the hand, to move it from its place and tighten the knot, and through this the blessing of lehaniach will return also upon the tefillin of the hand. It is then as though he has now put on the tefillin of the hand and the tefillin of the head close to one another. If he recited the blessing without handling it, he should handle it after the blessing. See Biur Halacha, where we wrote in the name of Rabbi Akiva Eiger on this matter.
