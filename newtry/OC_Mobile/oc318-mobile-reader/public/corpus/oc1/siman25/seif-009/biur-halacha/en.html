If he interrupted; it is obvious that this applies whether the interruption was through speech or merely through diversion of attention; the latter follows all the more so. See Magen Avraham 8:14. An interruption through speech consists even of a single word. Rosh writes in Hilchos Tefillin that if one answered Amen between the tefillin to a berachah he heard, he must repeat the berachah. Chayei Adam 5 is uncertain whether one or two words between a berachah and performance of the act constitutes an interruption. It is astonishing that he did not notice these words of Rosh. Be'er Heitev writes: If one put on the hand-tefillin and, while putting on the head-tefillin, interrupted with speech after it entered the airspace of his head but before the bayis touched his flesh, do we say that an object within an airspace is regarded as though it had come to rest? See Halachos Ketanos 2:42. Artzos HaChaim disputes this with clear proof that the mitzvah is not complete until the tefillin is tightened. Ikrei HaDinim writes likewise in the name of Tefillah LeDavid. Be'er Heitev further writes in the name of Halachos Ketanos that one who put on the hand-tefillin and made the three upper windings, but had not recited the berachah and then spoke, recites two berachos. Since berachos do not prevent fulfillment, this is properly regarded as speaking between the hand- and head-tefillin, requiring two berachos. Artzos HaChaim disputes this as well. If one donned a tallis katan without a berachah and afterward donned a tallis gadol, he may recite one berachah over both, as stated in Shulchan Aruch 8:10. The same applies here: the berachah 'to put on tefillin' applies retroactively also to the preceding hand-tefillin, and according to Shulchan Aruch he therefore needs to recite only one berachah. See there.
And according to our practice, etc. See Mishnah Berurah 32 regarding touching the tefillin. Know that Rabbi Akiva Eiger concludes in his novellae that if one spoke between them, he should first recite 'concerning the mitzvah of tefillin' over the head-tefillin. He should then touch the hand-tefillin, tighten its knot, and recite 'to put on tefillin.' He thereby satisfies both Rashi's and Rabbeinu Tam's positions. See there.
