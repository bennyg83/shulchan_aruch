Seif 9. 'It is prohibited to interrupt with speech, etc.' Responsa Panim Meirot, volume 1, siman 59, writes that if one hears another person reciting the blessing over the arm tefillin in the interim, he may answer Amen, because this affirmation concerning the placement of tefillin is not an interruption. Responsa Devar Shmuel, siman 142, writes that he should not answer Amen. It appears to me that there is proof from the Tur, Orach Chayim, siman 61, in the name of Ramah, who states that one answers Amen to the blessing HaBocher BeAmo Yisrael BeAhavah because there is no interruption, as it is no worse than offering a greeting. This implies that without that reason it would be an interruption even though one answers Amen to that very blessing. The same follows from the author's ruling in siman 66, seif 7, that one should not answer Amen after Ga'al Yisrael because it is an interruption, even though Amen affirms the blessing Ga'al Yisrael. According to the view of Ra'ah there, who maintains that one does answer Amen, the reason is that the affirmation is included within redemption and is therefore considered juxtaposing redemption to prayer. Nevertheless, regarding interruption between a blessing and its mitzvah, answering Amen constitutes an interruption.
Magen Avraham, seif katan 17. 'He may answer, since there is no prohibition.' See Eliyah Rabbah, who cites Mahari as saying that even on Chol HaMoed interruption is prohibited. The reason is not a concern about an unnecessary blessing, but the verse's use of the singular vehayah, teaching that the two tefillin must constitute one continuous placement; see there. Bekhor Shor in Gittin 34 likewise cites this. He writes there that for this reason the same applies to us when we put on Rabbeinu Tam tefillin because of uncertainty and without a blessing: One should not interrupt.
