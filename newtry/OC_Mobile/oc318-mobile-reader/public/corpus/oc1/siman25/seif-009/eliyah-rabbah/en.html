To put on tefillin, etc. The reason we do not bless “to bind,” as it is written, “and you shall bind them,” is that the essence of the mitzvah of tefillin is that they be resting upon him the whole day. If he blessed “to bind,” it would imply that he fulfilled his obligation by tying for one moment and then removing them. Therefore they instituted “to put on,” which implies that they should rest upon him for at least some time. So wrote the Bach. See below, seif 12. In Makor Chayim he wrote that since the blessing “to put on” also helps exempt the tefillin shel rosh, they instituted “to put on,” which has the sense of beginning, for he still must place the tefillin shel rosh. Lechem Chamudos wrote that according to the opinion that tefillin are two mitzvos, one who spoke between them must recite the blessing over the shel rosh.
