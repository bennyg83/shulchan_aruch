[9] To put on tefillin, etc. We do not recite the berachah 'to bind,' although the verse says, 'You shall bind them,' because the essence of the mitzvah of tefillin is that they remain upon him all day. Were one to say 'to bind,' it would imply that tying them for a single moment and then removing them suffices. The Sages therefore instituted the wording 'to put on,' which implies that they remain upon him for at least some period of time. Thus writes Bach 5; see below, se'if 12. Makor Chaim, page 46b, writes that because the berachah 'to put on tefillin' also exempts the head-tefillin, they instituted the wording 'to put on,' which connotes a beginning, since he must still put on the head-tefillin. Lechem Chamudos writes that the word 'tefillin' should be pronounced emphatically, indicating its doubled form, for 'tefillin' is related to an expression of judgment. If one transgressed and put on the head-tefillin first, see siman 684, Eliyah Rabbah 8.
