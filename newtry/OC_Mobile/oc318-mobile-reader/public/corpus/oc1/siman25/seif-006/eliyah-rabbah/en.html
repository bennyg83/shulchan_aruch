[6] He puts on tefillin and goes, etc. Olas Tamid 3 writes that in places where Jews live among non-Jews, or where the alleyways are filthy so that one cannot wear a tallis and tefillin from home, he should put them on in the synagogue's entrance hall and should not enter the synagogue without a tallis and tefillin. Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur, however, indicates that even where the alleyways are filthy, one should put them on at home and cover the tefillin when passing through the filthy area. This also appears to me from Responsa Menachem Azaryah 59, and from Magen Avraham 43, at the end of subparagraph 11. The Zohar, Vaeschanan, cited by Levush, begins: 'When he leaves the entrance of his house wearing tzitzis and tefillin, four holy angels join him, leave the entrance of his house with him, and escort him' (meaning that they escort him to the synagogue) 'and proclaim before him: Give honor to the image of the King!' It seems to me that Knesses HaGedolah did not examine the beginning of this Zohar passage; see there, and there is no need to elaborate. Zohar, Shelach 300b, likewise makes clear that they should be put on at home. Makor Chaim 2 writes: One who regularly goes to the synagogue before dawn in order to be counted among the first ten is nevertheless better off doing so, for he receives reward equivalent to them all and fulfills the verse, 'In a multitude of people is the glory of the King.' He can afterward sanctify himself further by wrapping in tzitzis and putting on tefillin. Although he does not fulfill the practice of putting on tefillin at home, he loses none of the perfection attained through donning the tallis and tefillin, each according to its aspect; doing so in the synagogue is preferable, as explained. The Shulchan Aruch of Mahari Luria, Hilchos Netilas Yadayim Shacharis 21, however, writes that it is better to go to the synagogue wrapped in tallis and tefillin than to go before the time for tallis and tefillin in order to be among the first ten. Responsa Maharam of Lublin 34 likewise exempted a person from a penalty requiring him to go recite Tehillim, for this reason; see there. Magen Avraham 3 rules similarly in the name of Kavanas HaAri. In truth, Makor Chaim's view is reasonable, since before the time for tzitzis arrives no obligation rests upon him at all. After Responsa Nachalas Shivah 6 was printed, I found that it accords with my words and states this in the name of Totzaos Chaim, paragraph 154.
