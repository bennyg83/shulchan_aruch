Encountered, etc. See Mishnah Berurah. Taz below, siman 684:4, rules this way, unlike Abudraham; Rabbi Akiva Eiger cites him in his novellae, and Pri Megadim rules likewise. This also appears from Gra's words there, at the end of siman 684:3. He explains Rama's ruling there by saying that we do not consider Chanukah, and does not give Abudraham's own reason, which Magen Avraham cites there. This implies that if one transgressed and put on the head-tefillin first, he need not remove it. I saw Artzos HaChaim incline toward Abudraham's ruling. Since the person initially committed a transgression and violated the Sages' statement that whenever the head-tefillin are between your eyes there must be two tefillin upon you, he must now correct this and put them on in the prescribed manner. This does not appear correct. We rule that absence of the hand-tefillin does not prevent fulfillment of the head-tefillin, demonstrating that he has intrinsically fulfilled the mitzvah of head-tefillin; he merely failed to perform the mitzvah in its ideal manner. One cannot say that he should remove the head-tefillin and put it on again, thereby now fulfilling the mitzvah ideally. Every moment that he wears tefillin, he fulfills the Holy One's mitzvah. We certainly should not instruct him to remove the mitzvah from himself now so that he can later fulfill it in a more choice manner. Yevamos 39a states an even stronger rule: a younger brother should perform yibbum, and we do not wait for the oldest brother to arrive from overseas, although Scripture says, 'The firstborn.' We do not delay a mitzvah at all, even though waiting would not actively nullify the positive commandment but would merely entail passive inaction. All the more so here should we not actively remove a mitzvah from one who is performing it so that he may later perform it more ideally. There is no distinction between a long and short delay, as Magen Avraham 2 proves. Although I subsequently found contrary evidence in siman 426:2 in the gloss; see Gra there in the name of Terumas HaDeshen, who distinguishes the Yevamos case because delay there could result in the mitzvah's complete rejection; the poskim have already challenged even that distinction in their responsa: Olas HaGereshuni 12; Pnei Yehoshua, Even HaEzer 15; and the addendum to Responsa Tzemach Tzedek 126. Moreover, that case entails passive inaction, whereas here one would actively uproot a positive commandment; it appears that everyone agrees in this case. It is also known that Gra himself instituted the practice of reciting Kiddush Levanah promptly rather than waiting, as Maaseh Rav records. It therefore appears obvious that the law follows Taz. As Eliyah Rabbah writes, what has been done is done; he should quickly put on the hand-tefillin so as to fulfill 'whenever the head-tefillin are between your eyes, there shall be two,' and that suffices.
