Lehaniach. With a kamatz. See Baer Heitev. What he wrote in the name of the Levush and Lechem Chamudos is not so, for on the contrary, they too decided to say it with a kamatz, and he wrote so based on the Olas Tamid. The Eliyah Rabbah already challenged him, and the Eliyah Rabbah and Mor Uketziyah agreed with the Beit Yosef to say it with a kamatz. So too wrote Rabbi Avraham Zacuto in a manuscript critique of the book Mitzvos Shemurim, and he wrote that with a kamatz, from the expression to cause a blessing to rest, it works well, meaning that it should rest there with permanence and fixedness through the knot, not moving here and there. This is not so with a patach and dagesh, whose root is to leave, which is also an expression of abandoning. Even when it is an expression of placing, such as and you shall place it before Hashem, it is only mere placing for a moment and temporarily; see there. Some of this is also written in Eliyah Rabbah; see there.
