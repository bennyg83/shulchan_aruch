Lehaniach. See Taz. See Levush, who challenged the Beit Yosef and said: in truth, lehaniach with a kamatz specifically has its root in rest, from the class of verbs with a weak middle letter, and there is no dagesh in the nun; but lehaniach with a patach under the hei and a dagesh in the nun has its root in leaving or placing aside. I examined the Meir Nesiv, which is called a concordance, under the root yanach, and it means placement, not abandonment. At first glance, placement is more fitting, to place them on the arm and on the head, unlike rest, which implies that they rest in their place and are not worn. The verse in Yechezkel 44:30 says to bring a blessing, see there. See the copied responsum there. According to the Mechaber there is no doubt that when one spoke, he blesses over the head tefillin al mitzvas with a patach, for it refers only to the head tefillin. The Rif is at the end of Rosh Hashanah regarding not interrupting with speech between the shofar blasts while seated and those while standing. See Rambam, chapter 4, halachah 4. What he wrote that one does not make mitzvos into bundles, I did not understand, for there the Gemara says this regarding not concluding with two blessings. I later saw that he intended the statement in Pesachim 102 that one does not recite two sanctifications over one cup; see Rashi there, Berachos, under the heading one does not conclude. He concludes that the two blessings, lehaniach and mitzvas, both refer to the arm tefillin and the head tefillin, and this is not called bundles, because in each of the tefillin there are three mitzvos. First, seeing the tefillin, and what is written in them about the Exodus from Egypt, which is a sign of the renewal of the world, since Hashem changed nature and watches over the lower beings, as the commentators wrote; and as the Rama, of blessed memory, explained, I am Hashem your G-d Who brought you out of Egypt, not Who created you, because they saw miracles resembling renewal of the world, such as fire within hail, opposites together, showing that He brings everything into being. Pharaoh said, who is Hashem, recognizing only the name Elokim, which indicates nature, as if once He created it He does not change it; therefore in the splitting of the sea and in the other plagues Pharaoh said, Hashem is righteous, the name of being. Second, placing them on the arm and head to subjugate the mind and the soul together with the body. For this one blesses over the arm tefillin two blessings: one, lehaniach; two, mitzvas tefillin, to remember what is written in them about the Exodus from Egypt, etc. Therefore, properly, even over the arm tefillin alone one should bless two blessings, and one should not interrupt between them, because the two blessings refer to both of them. At the end of his words he says that one should say mitzvos with a cholam, since they are two mitzvos and mitzvas also refers to the arm tefillin, as the Tur wrote here in Bayis Chadash; if so, he should say it in the plural. Even for the head tefillin alone, where he blesses two when he spoke, if he said it with a cholam, there is no difficulty according to the Razah, etc. But one who examines the Razah and Ran at the end of Rosh Hashanah, which I brought in letter 4, sees that he holds lehaniach applies only to the arm tefillin and mitzvas only to the head tefillin; the touching of the arm tefillin is required so that they shall be together, and it is like blessing two over the head tefillin, but the essence of the blessing lehaniach is over the arm tefillin, for as long as one touches tefillin he may bless, as I wrote on Magen Avraham letter 15. Only according to our ruling is it so; understand this. If he has only the arm tefillin, he blesses two blessings, and if only the head tefillin, two blessings, and mitzvas with a patach, because it refers only to one mitzvah, namely seeing the tefillin in order to remember what is written in them. We have only the words of the Rav here, that over the head tefillin one blesses two blessings, while over the arm tefillin only lehaniach and also al mitzvas. Whether with a cholam or a patach, permission is given, either like the Taz and his brother the gaon, or like the Magen Avraham letter 9, see there. From now, if one erred and said al mitzvas over the arm tefillin and lehaniach over the head tefillin, at first glance according to this responsum it is acceptable, but it requires study. See also what I wrote about this in Magen Avraham, letter 11. See Shaarei Tefillah, section 318, by the great grammarian Rav Zalman, regarding this.
