From the time. See the Taz, that some say shemirah is required from the time of harvest; and even an individual who practices thus, it is a vow; and anyone who did not eat during the meal from [matzah] guarded from the time of harvest returns and eats; Levush; see Chok Yaakov s.k. 6. And see Peri Chadash: what the Mechaber wrote, that one may rely on matzah that he ate during the meal, is specifically where [that is so]; but if he did not eat shemurah during the meal, even though at first he ate a kezayis of shemurah, he returns and eats afikoman; for he necessarily must have had shemurah matzah at first, as in siman 482; see there. And at first glance I did not understand his words; and afterward I saw that his words are true and "its fruit for food and its leaves for healing": for what the Mechaber wrote regarding shemurah matzah is from a responsum of the Rosh, klal 24; see Beis Yosef and Beer HaGolah. And he holds that one who has only a kezayis of shemurah matzah eats it at first; see Pesachim 120a in the Rosh, which the Peri Chadash cited in siman 482. And how then did he write in the responsum "shemurah matzah," etc.? It must be either way; rather, he has not fulfilled with what he ate as a kezayis at first of shemurah, for the afikoman is a remembrance of the Pesach offering and requires specifically eating when satisfied; and only when he ate during the meal a kezayis of shemurah has he fulfilled, for it was eaten when satisfied. And with God's help, in Magen Avraham s.k. 6 more will be explained.
