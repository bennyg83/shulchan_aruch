And the Maharshal wrote in a responsum, siman 86: and he should take out the afikoman as it is, wrapped in a cloth, and sling it behind him, and walk about four amos in the house, and say, "And thus our ancestors would walk with their kneading-troughs bound up in their garments"; end quote. And in Pesach Meubin, siman 315, he wrote, and these are his words: And my custom is to take a staff in my hand and my shoes on my feet, and to say, "And so shall you eat it: your loins girded, and your shoes on your feet, and your staff in your hand; and you shall eat it in haste; it is a Pesach unto Hashem"; and therefore I am accustomed not to remove the belt on Pesach night until after the afikoman; end quote. And in these lands they do not practice all of this.
A kezayis for each one. And the Maharil wrote, and the consensus of the Acharonim is, that l'chatchilah one should eat the measure of two olives' worth: one as a remembrance of the Pesach offering, and one as a remembrance of the matzah eaten with it; and at the very least he should not have less than a kezayis.
He should recite it before midnight. So it is written in the chapter Arvei Pesachim, and at the end of chapter 2 of Megillah, and the Rashba in a responsum, siman 445, in the name of Tosafos. And in Tosafos at the end of chapter 2 of Megillah they wrote, and these are their words: One must hurry to eat matzah before midnight, even the matzah of the afikoman, for matzah nowadays is a Torah obligation; but regarding the Hallel after the afikoman one need not be so stringent, for it is rabbinic; until here the language of Tosafos. And they have the custom to break a piece from the afikoman and to pierce them and hang it; and there is no concern of prohibition in piercing them on Yom Tov; and so wrote the Magen Avraham, siman 500 s.k. 7.
