And he should be careful, etc. — see Mishna Berurah what he wrote that one must be very careful, etc., for in the Gemara it is stated: per Ra'avad Pesach from the Torah is eaten only until midnight — so too matzah, as it is equated to Pesach; and R' Akiva disagrees and holds Pesach's eating time is the whole night, and consequently matzah's time from the Torah is the whole night; and there is dispute among Rishonim whether the halacha follows Ra'avad because many stam mishnayot agree with him, or R' Akiva because halacha follows R' Akiva from his colleague; and Rambam, Maggid Mishnah, Ittur, and Or Zarua in the name of R' Yitzchak of Corbeil rule matzah's time is the whole night; and some say Rif too, since he omitted Rava's statement that eating matzah after midnight does not fulfill per Ra'avad; yet many Gedolei Rishonim hold after midnight one does not fulfill — R' Chananel in chapter Afikoman, Semag in the name of R' Yitzchak of Corbeil [and so Tosafot in Zevachim 57b and Megillah 21b, only in Pesachim 120b Tosafot are uncertain], Mordechai end of Pesachim, Rabbeinu Yerucham netiv 5 channel 4; Or Zarua also sides thus — see there — that one does not fulfill from the Torah; Rokeach, Rosh, Rashba, Ran are uncertain and wrote one should be careful not to eat after more than midnight; and some Rishonim wrote even per R' Akiva it is only d'oraisa, but due to distancing he agrees it is forbidden l'chatchila after more than midnight — so Gra in his explanation; and therefore many Acharonim ruled that although certainly one is obligated to eat matzah even if delayed after midnight — perhaps the halacha is its time is the whole night from the Torah — nevertheless one should not bless on eating matzah, for doubtful blessings are lenient.
