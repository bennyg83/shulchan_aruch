A kezayis. See the Taz; and in Magen Avraham s.k. 1 in the name of the Maharil: two olives' worth, one as a remembrance of the Pesach offering and one as a remembrance of the matzah eaten with it. And see Pesachim 119b; and see the Bach regarding the two versions: whether we do not conclude after matzah with afikoman, or after the Pesach offering; [according to one,] a kezayis would have been enough. And with God's help, in Magen Avraham more will be explained. And Eliyah Rabbah s.k. 1 wrote like the Taz, because the mitzvah of matzah is dear; therefore one should eat two olives' worth; and this requires further study. It is permitted to make a hole in the afikoman and hang it on the wall; see Eliyah Rabbah; see Magen Avraham, siman 500 s.k. 7. And nevertheless, to aim for a triangular or square hole is forbidden on Yom Tov; only such a hole [as needed] to hang it involves no contempt of foods. And see Chok Yaakov s.k. 3; and see there in the name of Pesach Meubin; and some have the custom thus to take a staff and shoes on the feet and to say a remembrance, etc.; and see the Maharshal [who says] one should sling [it] over his shoulder, etc.
What is written. The Mechaber wrote that one should eat before midnight, but after the fact he should eat after midnight without a blessing. And see Peri Chadash what he wrote on this. And see Pesachim 83a, where it says "we learn from this that use of leftover [sacrificial meat] is a significant matter," and it rejects [that], for if so it should have been a case of leftover; and why did it not challenge: and according to your reason, let them remove them after midnight, since it is invalid according to R' Elazar ben Azaryah, and they have not yet served leftover? And also, how did Rav Mari prove thus: we learn from this that both of them hold like R' Akiva in Pesachim 120, and like the anonymous Mishnah in Megillah 28a like R' Akiva, that its mitzvah is the whole night. And with God's help, in Magen Avraham s.k. 1 more will be explained.
One who has no matzah within the techum: he may not go outside the techum. See siman 447, Magen Avraham s.k. 2: chametz is not a communal positive commandment, and the same applies to matzah: it is not a communal positive commandment. And see Chok Yaakov, siman 586, se'if 21. And the same applies to a woman in such a case, for she is obligated in matzah from the parallel [to the Pesach offering] in any event; she is forbidden to go outside the techum by rabbinic law, as a rabbinic negative commandment. And through instructing a non-Jew it is permitted; possibly even if he will eat only after midnight.
