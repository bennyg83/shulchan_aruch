A kezayis. And the consensus of the Acharonim is that l'chatchilah one should eat the measure of two olives' worth: one as a remembrance of the Pesach offering, and one as a remembrance of the matzah eaten with it; and at the very least he should not have less than a kezayis. And the Rokeach wrote: let the eating of the afikoman not be a burden to you. And see siman 476 in the gloss, that he should not eat it in a state of achilah gasah; for even though the Pesach offering is eaten when satisfied, nevertheless it is required that he desire to eat. The Bach wrote [citing] the Shelah: I have seen among the elevated ones that they would kiss the matzos and the maror, and likewise the sukkah upon entering and upon leaving, and likewise the Four Species of the lulav; and all of this is to endear the mitzvah; and fortunate is he who serves Hashem with joy. And see above, siman 473 s.k. 19, what is written there in the name of the Maharshal. And in Pesach Meubin, siman 315, he wrote further, and these are his words: And my custom is to take a staff in my hand and my shoes on my feet, and to say, "So shall you eat it: your loins girded, your shoes on your feet, and your staff in your hand; and you shall eat it in haste; it is a Pesach unto Hashem"; and therefore I am accustomed not to remove the belt on Pesach night until after the afikoman; end quote. And in these lands they do not practice all of this.
