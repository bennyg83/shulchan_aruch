In a cistern (susternah). This is what they call in Talmudic language a pit — water collected in a building in the ground where the building separates it from the ground and it is also covered above, so it is as if they are drawn; one should not compare this to what we call in foreign language ar'ar kastin, for although it is covered above, one may not draw from it to use close to drawing because every hour and moment flows into it from the river. However, if one wishes to leave them overnight like other water and draw at its time, it is permitted to draw under the pipe or what they call in foreign language ar'ar, like from a river or a well drawn from it; and likewise from the kastin itself if it is covered. Kaf HaChayim.

Pressure. If he has drawn mayim shelanu in his house he should take them even though there is concern they were drawn by day or at night — Kaf HaChayim, Ateret Zekenim. And in Halakhot Ketanot part 1 siman 97 he is lenient regarding rainwater collected from the air at night.
