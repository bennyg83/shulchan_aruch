It is customary, etc., and this is correct — from this language it implies only l'chatchila but b'dieved one does not forbid; and therefore they did not decide that a little salt put in matzah for taste would heat the dough [for although we rule salted is like boiling, that is specifically when it is not eaten because of its salt], and he was not concerned for the stringent authorities only regarding l'chatchila; and Rama in the gloss who added to forbid even b'dieved follows his reasoning, for Darkei Moshe wrote that according to what Tur wrote later siman 462, salt water is fruit juice — if so it is fruit juice with water, and we rule they hasten to become chametz unless baked immediately; therefore he wrote here that even b'dieved it is forbidden — meaning if he was not careful to bake immediately [and the dough in his hands does not save from becoming chametz]; and so Kaf HaChayim ruled [and so implies Magen Avraham]: if he was careful to bake immediately, one does not forbid b'dieved the matzah for a little salt put in; and so Eliyah Rabbah. Know that according to what Rama wrote later siman 462 in Darkei Moshe, Tur deals only with salt dug from the ground, but salt made from sea water like mayim shelanu is water for everyone — if so one does not forbid b'dieved in our salt in any case (unless he put much salt), and even per Magen Avraham's objection there that Tur deals with our salt — nevertheless for the law Beit Yosef concludes in the passage name of Ran and Hagahot Maimoniyot unlike Tur, and so ruled in Shulchan Aruch that it is in the category of water; if so Rama's ruling to forbid even b'dieved applies only to salt dug from the ground if not baked immediately. Know further that even if we explain Rama in Shulchan Aruch who ruled anonymously to be stringent b'dieved, it is not because it is fruit juice but because he was concerned for the stringent view due to heating the dough — nevertheless for the law one should study, for many poskim are lenient as Gra wrote; and so is proven in Ritva's novellae on Pesachim that b'dieved one certainly should not be stringent with a little salt; and so Maharam Chalavah in his novellae to be lenient with little salt. Especially if he was shogeg, one certainly should not be stringent, for even kneading in hot water some poskim are lenient b'shogeg b'dieved as above seif 3 — all the more so here where many are certainly lenient one may rely — in my humble opinion. See Peri Chadash who disagrees with Rama and agrees with Mechaber to be lenient b'dieved.
