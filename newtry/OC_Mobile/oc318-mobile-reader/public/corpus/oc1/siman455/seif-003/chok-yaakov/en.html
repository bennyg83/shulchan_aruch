One may not knead with hot water. The Levush wrote: even if they rested the whole night and were heated only by the sun; therefore his intent is: even if they rested, as long as they are still hot it is forbidden to knead with them; which is not so if they are cool before us; for for that reason we require overnight resting of water to dispel their heat from the sun, as is explained in the Gemara and poskim; and the same applies to fire-heated water if they cooled, as implied by the poskim and the Mechaber who wrote "and the water in it is lukewarm," implying specifically when they are still lukewarm; however, in Responsa of the Rama, siman 35, he wrote, and this is his language: that fire-heated water even after cooling one should forbid, for it is not even a need to say according to R' Eliezer of Metz that even water that did not rest overnight cooling does not help, and all the more so hot water; and even according to Rashi's view, nevertheless fire-heated water has no remedy; and therefore we find no posek who wrote that fire-heated water has a remedy; end quote. And in truth the Mechaber's implication is not so, as Olas Shabbas wrote; and so wrote my brother-in-law the Rav in Sefer Ateres Zekenim from the work cited as mem-yud-tet; and the poskim did not need to write a remedy, for it says fire-heated, and whenever they cooled they are no longer called fire-heated; and once they are cool before us, what concern of prohibition is there in this? And on the contrary, I have heard that sometimes water that was cooked and cooled is cooler than other water; also what he wrote that according to R' Eliezer of Metz cooling does not help for water that did not rest overnight is not compelling; and see Beis Yosef. Even though the work mem-yud-tet that my brother-in-law the Rav cited in Sefer Ateres Zekenim concluded, and this is his language: but Heaven forfend to overturn their words without proof; end quote; it appears from his words that he understood that the Levush's intent too in writing "even if they rested" was even if they cooled, therefore he did not come to overturn his words as well; and in truth the Levush's intent is simple as I wrote; and the proof is also clear from the language of the Gemara and the poskim and the Mechaber, who stated specifically hot or lukewarm; and one who wishes to be stringent should at least permit in a case of great loss.
Whose copper is thick. And Magen Avraham wrote: and the same applies to earthenware.
Lukewarm. That is, like the warmth of saliva; and it is in the Yerushalmi, chapter Kol Sha'ah, halacha 7: even though the meal-offering is kneaded lukewarm and they watch that it not become chametz, because the kohanim are diligent; but for matzah even the kohanim themselves may not knead lukewarm, so as not to distinguish between matzah and matzah; thus far; and see Menachos folio 55a; and it is explained in the Tur and Beis Yosef that regarding benefit all agree it is permitted even if one kneaded with hot water.
If one transgressed unknowingly and kneaded. Some later authorities are somewhat stringent in this, and one should not budge from the Rav's ruling; and nevertheless one should not take them to fulfill the obligation of the mitzvah for the first nights; see the Tur.
