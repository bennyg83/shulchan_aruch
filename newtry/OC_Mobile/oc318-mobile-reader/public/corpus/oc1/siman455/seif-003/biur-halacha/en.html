See Mishnah Berurah for the reason, and see Pri Megadim, who writes that this concern applies only when the kettle is still somewhat lukewarm because it was removed from the fire only a short time earlier. If it cooled for a long time, there is plainly no concern. And the water in it is lukewarm. If it cooled completely, everyone permits it, even according to those who are stringent regarding water that had been heated by fire and then cooled, because here it was not originally fire-heated water, as stated in seif katan 29. [Later authorities] One may rely on them. Bach is stringent in every case, but several later authorities rule like the Shulchan Aruch and Rama. Even according to Bach, if the water was drawn from rivers, one may rely upon the lenient view after the fact when the mistake was inadvertent, because according to Rashi the essential law of mayim shelanu applies specifically to spring water. Likewise, if mayim shelanu became hot and was cooled and dough was kneaded with it inadvertently, there is room for leniency even according to Bach. Eliyah Rabbah writes likewise. Water that did not remain overnight and was nullified 1 in 2, etc. If hot or lukewarm water became mixed with a large quantity of cold water until it cooled, Olat Shabbos, Chok Yaakov, and Eliyah Rabbah, as also cited by Shulchan Aruch HaRav, are lenient because it became nullified in the cold water. Chok Yosef and Bigdei Yesha are stringent, and Magen HaElef likewise implies stringency. Pri Megadim also questions the leniency, because we find it stated only regarding mayim shelanu. Therefore, initially one must certainly be careful not to knead with such water. After the fact, however, where there is a major financial loss or the loss of Yom Tov rejoicing, one may be lenient. Bigdei Yesha writes likewise.
