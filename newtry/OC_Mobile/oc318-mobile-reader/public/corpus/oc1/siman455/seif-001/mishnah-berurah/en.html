(1) One may not knead, etc.: whether matzas mitzvah or other matzos.
(2) But only with mayim shelanu: that is, overnight resting in a vessel after they were drawn; and Rashi wrote the reason in the Gemara: that the springs in the days of Nisan are warm, because the sun at that time travels at the lower edge of the firmament near the earth and warms the springs; and some explain that at night the sun travels below the ground and warms the springs; and therefore the Sages forbade using them for kneading immediately upon taking them from the ground (for they are like hot water, with which it is forbidden to knead, as below in se'if 3) until they stand in vessels and cool from their heat; and the measure of their standing in vessels and the time of drawing them from the well will be explained below.
(3) Whether they are river waters: even though they do not flow from springs and the reasons we wrote do not apply, nevertheless one should be stringent about this as well to cool them, because the heat of the sun beat upon them by day and they became somewhat warm.
(4) Near bein hashemashot: for ab initio it is proper and correct to draw them at bein hashemashot itself, so that the water cool while attached for twelve hours of the day, when the springs are cool by day, and twelve hours in the air at night; but because it is impossible to aim exactly at bein hashemashot, one may draw near bein hashemashot; and one should not be lenient ab initio to draw much while it is still day, and all the more so one should not delay drawing until after nightfall; and nevertheless, after the fact, if they were drawn much while it was still day, one need not be stringent, and one may knead with them ab initio after overnight resting; and see Biur Halacha. And likewise if one drew them at night before midnight, it is permitted to knead with them the next day ab initio, but one must wait twelve hours from the time one drew them.
(5) Or at bein hashemashot: so that a complete night pass over the water from when it was detached from what was attached. And bein hashemashot is after the sun sets until the stars appear; and on a cloudy day it is better to draw earlier rather than later.
(6) The whole night: and in a time of pressure one may be lenient to knead with them after dawn even though they did not stand twelve hours.
(7) One should leave them in a cellar that is cold: and if one has no cellar, leave them inside the house, and specifically in a room that is not heated.
(8) For the cellar is warm: and if one set them in a cellar or in a house that is heated, it will be explained below in the gloss.
(9) Before the sun rises: so that they not be heated by the heat of the sun; and this refers to where the water stands outdoors in a place from which the sun rises; but if they stand in a place where the face of the sun cannot be seen, there is no concern, as below in siman 459, se'if 1; see there. However, on a cloudy day in every case one must be careful to bring them in before the time of sunrise, for a cloudy day is all sun.
(10) Until they became heated: and if they became lukewarm, it is like becoming heated, as below in se'if 3. And lukewarm means when they became like the warmth of saliva; and see Shaarei Teshuvah, who wrote that whenever they became even somewhat hot, even though not like the warmth of saliva, one should not knead with them; see there.
(11) It does not harm: and even if they stood in the sun, so long as they did not become heated, it is permitted to knead with them.
(12) And when one carries the water, etc.: that is to say, when one carries them in the morning from the cellar to the kneading house, so that they not be heated by the sun. And the later authorities wrote that it is good to cover them also when one carries them from the river to one's house even though the sun has already set, so that nothing that causes chimutz fall into them; and for this reason the custom is to filter the water at the time of drawing through a clean white cloth.
(13) One should cover them: and if one carries the water in a glass vessel, covering with a cloth from above does not help, for the sun can heat through the thickness of the glass, unless one covers with a cloth over the walls.
(14) One should draw, etc.: according to what the Mechaber wrote in siman 458, that when the fourteenth falls on Shabbos one kneads on Friday, there is no novelty at all; and the words of the author of the gloss are said for those who customarily bake on the night of Pesach at Motzei Shabbos, as is explained there in the Tur.
(15) That is, on Thursday night: for on Friday at bein hashemashot one cannot draw, for Shabbos does not prepare for Yom Tov, and bein hashemashot is doubtful Shabbos; and while it is still day near bein hashemashot it is also not proper, lest one delay and do this at bein hashemashot (and the same law applies even to those who bake on the night of the second day of Yom Tov: they are forbidden to draw at bein hashemashot of the first day of Yom Tov, for one Yom Tov does not prepare for its fellow, and they must draw on the night of the fourteenth). And if one forgot to draw on Thursday night, one may draw on Friday while it is still day, even while the day is still great; and all this likewise applies to people who bake many days before Pesach: when they bake on Sunday they should take care ab initio to prepare water on Thursday night; after the fact, on Friday while it is still day. However, if one forgot to prepare while it was still day, let him prepare at bein hashemashot through a gentile; and some permit even through oneself.
(16) From the rivers: because the sun travels in the days of Nisan beneath the ground, and therefore the wells boil somewhat, which is not so of rivers; granted that at the place of their springing they boil, nevertheless when they flowed far they cool.
(17) From the melting of snows, etc.: for then well waters cool more than them, for in Nisan the sun travels in the mountains in order to melt the snows, and they become lukewarm.
(18) And one should not spill, etc.: for "he who guards a mitzvah will know no evil"; and not specifically matzas mitzvah, for the same applies to other matzos needed for Pesach; and nevertheless, if one can easily obtain other mayim shelanu, it is proper to do so for other matzos; but water prepared for matzas mitzvah it is not proper to spill even if one can easily obtain other water, for it appears like belittling what is written, "he who guards a mitzvah will know no evil" (Taz). And all this also applies regarding the tekufah.
(19) It is good ab initio: that is, before the tekufah falls.
(20) Iron: and one should take new or clean iron and hang it by a string inside the water, but one should not put one's hand into the water, for the hand warms the water; and on other days of the year one should place salt or a seal (Darkei Moshe; Magen Avraham).
(21) It is also forbidden to draw, etc.: for it is among the preparations for a mitzvah; and this is only ab initio.
(22) And even for other matzos, etc.: so that one not put chametz into it; and nevertheless there is no strict insistence on this, because the custom is to filter.
(23) For matzas mitzvah: and one should say at the time of drawing, "for the sake of matzas mitzvah" (later authorities).
(24) New earthenware vessels: but not old ones, even if they were of Pesach; and the reason is that old earthenware vessels are repulsive, and this is not hiddur.
(25) Of wood, etc.: and the same applies to stone or glazed earthenware vessels (called glazed); and all this is specifically when they were used only with water alone; but if there were fruit juices in them (meaning: all liquids other than water are called fruit juices for this matter), it is good to take care not to draw with them unless one first kashered them by hagalah, because fruit juices, when a little of them mixes into water, hasten the water to cause chimutz in the dough kneaded with them, as will be explained in siman 462; and it is obvious that one should forbid what some take large barrels that held honey and rinse them and put mayim shelanu for matzos in the bakeries: it is a complete prohibition, for they emit the fruit juices. And ab initio it is good to take care not to draw with a copper vessel, even if it is new, because copper warms; but after the fact there is no concern, and even if the water stood many days in it, it is permitted to knead with them; and even ab initio the custom is to permit kneading in a copper vessel, and they are careful only not to draw water with them, for the water needs cooling for one night, and when they are in a copper vessel they do not cool in a copper vessel as in other vessels.
