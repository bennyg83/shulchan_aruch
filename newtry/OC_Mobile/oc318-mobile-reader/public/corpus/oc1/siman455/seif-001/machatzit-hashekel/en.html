A. A. A. A. A. A. A. A.D. is a woman who is not hanged but in our water:

And Rashi — we rule like R' Nachman there 94b: in the rainy season the sun travels in the lower sky; therefore the whole world is cold and springs boil. Rashi there: in the lower part of the sky, close to the ground, at the edges of the sphere; therefore the whole world is cold, for it travels only at the sides of the world; and springs boil — meaning near the water at the sides of the world from which springs come — end quote. Nissan is still considered rainy season. Therefore there is no difference between night and day — always in rainy season, day and night, it travels in the lower sky; only at night it travels in the vault of the sky and the sky separates between sun and earth, hence darkness on the earth; by day it travels in the hollow of the sky toward the earth, hence the earth is lit. Therefore no difference whether drawn by day or night — water is hot and needs 12 hours cooling; if drawn in the morning, permitted to knead with them in the evening after 12 hours. R' Yehuda uses mayim shelanu implying they draw in the evening and knead in the morning — common practice: people bake by day, therefore must leave all night and may bake immediately at dawn.

And R' Eliezer of Metz — we rule like the admission of the sages of Israel to the sages of the nations there: no difference between summer and rainy season; always by day it travels below the sky (therefore it lights for us), and at night below the ground (therefore earth's thickness separates between sun and us, hence night darkens); therefore by day springs cold, at night boiling — at night the sun travels near the place springs issue; by day when the sun is far they cool. According to him, if drawn at bein ha shemashot, permitted to knead immediately, for they already cooled 12 hours attached by day. In this there is no dispute per R' Eliezer of Metz — only if drawn by day or night they disagree: R' Eliyahu Mizrachi and Rabbeinu Yehuda — per R' Eliyahu Mizrachi whether drawn day or night they have no remedy forever. Rabbeinu Yehuda challenged: if so why call them mayim shelanu? In any case if drawn at bein ha shemashot no overnight needed and permitted immediately; if drawn by day or night, overnight does not help — therefore Rabbeinu Yehuda: per R' Eliezer of Metz, if drawn by day they need overnight of the following night and it helps; if drawn at night they need the full night they were drawn and the day after and the night after. His reason and reasoning — see Beit Yosef who elaborated greatly. R' Yitzchak Saba's view is also like R' Eliezer of Metz here, for we rule like admission of sages of Israel; except where R' Eliezer of Metz holds if drawn at bein ha shemashot no overnight needed because they cooled 12 hours attached — R' Yitzchak Saba disagrees and requires 24 hours cooling; therefore draw at bein ha shemashot; then they cool 12 hours attached by day (for by day springs are cold) and need overnight all night and cool 12 hours detached (for at night the world is cold). Maharil leans like R' Yitzchak Saba. One should not be lenient to draw by day (meaning before close to bein ha shemashot, about half hour before) — per R' Eliyahu Mizrachi it is not correct; meaning per R' Eliezer of Metz not correct per R' Eliyahu Mizrachi even if left all night, for they have no remedy; but per Rabbeinu Yehuda overnight of the night helps as above.

In this all agree. Meaning R' Eliyahu Mizrachi and Rabbeinu Yehuda agree it is not correct per R' Eliezer of Metz to draw at night; they disagree: per R' Eliyahu Mizrachi they have no remedy forever; per Rabbeinu Yehuda overnight of the night drawn does not help; nevertheless overnight of the following night helps as above.

And the water is forbidden to the world of the east

One may be lenient: first, one may rely on Rashi that 12 hours waiting suffices whether drawn by day or night as above; second, even R' Eliezer of Metz was stringent only l'chatchila when possible; when impossible, perhaps R' Eliyahu Mizrachi agrees to be lenient.

Know Bach wrote further leniency per Raavan who holds for Rashi one does not need 12 hours at night but suffices until after dawn if the night was short. Authors ruled 12 hours for Rashi — fixed time by day if drawn in morning; in Nissan day and night are equal and night is 12 hours; therefore its measure by day equals places where in Nissan night is 12 hours — because we do not distinguish. But if drawn at evening bein ha shemashot, leaving the night as it is, even if short, permitted immediately at dawn — I copied his words to be a shield for people of the land who do not wait 12 hours and knead at dawn — they have whom to rely on; but most poskim did not write so and one should not be lenient. See siman 261 measure of bein ha shemashot; Magen Avraham wrote there it is about a quarter hour — see there; see here Taz s.v. regarding law, etc.; see Kaf HaChayim agreed with Magen Avraham that better to draw a little before bein ha shemashot than after — unlike Taz.
