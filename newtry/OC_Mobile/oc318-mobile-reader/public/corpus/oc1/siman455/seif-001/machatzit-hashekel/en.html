(s.k. 1) Near, etc. For by law, etc.: for it is stated on folio 42a: R' Yehuda said, a woman may not knead except with mayim shelanu.
And Rashi explained that we hold like Rav Nachman there, folio 94b: in the rainy season the sun travels at the lower edge of the firmament; therefore the whole world is cold and the springs are boiling. And Rashi explained there: at the lower edge of the firmament, near the ground, at the bottom of the dome; therefore the whole world is cold, for it travels only at the sides of the world; and the springs boil, that is, near the waters at the sides of the world from which the springs come; end quote. And Nisan is still considered among the rainy season. And according to this there is no difference between night and day: at all times the springs boil; for always in the rainy season, whether at night or by day, it travels at the lower edge of the firmament; only that at night it travels on the convex side of the firmament and the firmament intervenes between the sun and the earth, and therefore it is dark upon the earth; and by day it travels in the concave of the firmament toward the earth, and therefore the earth is illuminated by its light; and therefore there is no difference whether one drew them by day or by night: the waters are warm and need cooling for twelve hours. And therefore if one drew them in the morning, it is permitted to knead with them in the evening after twelve hours have passed; and when R' Yehuda used the term mayim shelanu, which implies that one draws them toward evening and kneads in the morning, he stated the usual practice: that the practice is to bake by day, therefore one must leave them overnight the whole night, and then one may bake immediately at dawn.
And the view of R' Eliezer of Metz is that we hold like the admission of the Sages of Israel to the Sages of the nations of the world there. That there is no difference between the sunny season and the rainy season; rather at all times by day it travels below the firmament (and therefore it illuminates for us), and at night it goes below the ground (therefore the ground of the world intervenes between the sun and us, and therefore the night is dark); and therefore by day the springs are cool and at night they boil, for at night the sun travels near the place where the springs issue, and by day when the sun is distant from them they cool. And according to his view, if one drew them at bein hashemashot it is permitted to knead with them immediately, for they already cooled while attached for the twelve hours of the day. And in this there is no dispute according to R' Eliezer of Metz. Rather, if one drew them by day or at night: in this R' Eliyahu Mizrachi disagreed with the Beis Yosef; for according to R' Eliyahu Mizrachi, whether drawn by day or by night, they never have a remedy. And Beis Yosef asked: if so, why call them mayim shelanu? Whichever way you turn: if one drew them at bein hashemashot, they need no overnight resting and are permitted immediately; and if one drew them by day or at night, overnight resting does not help, for they have no remedy; therefore Beis Yosef wrote that the view of R' Eliezer of Metz is: if one drew them by day they need overnight resting of the following night, and overnight resting helps; and if one drew them at night they need a complete overnight resting, that is, that night when they were drawn and the following day and the following night. And his reason and its explanation: see Beis Yosef, for he lengthened much. And the view of Ritzba is also like the view of R' Eliezer of Metz in this, that we hold like the admissions of the Sages of Israel; only regarding what R' Eliezer of Metz holds, that if one drew them at bein hashemashot one needs no overnight resting because they cooled twelve hours while attached, in this Ritzba disagrees: that we require that they cool twenty-four hours; and therefore one must draw them at bein hashemashot, and thus they cooled by day twelve hours while attached, for by day the springs are cool, and they need overnight resting the whole night and cool twelve hours while detached, for at night the world is cool. And Maharil holds like the view of Ritzba. And one should not be lenient to draw by day (that is, before near bein hashemashot, which is about half an hour before bein hashemashot), for according to Ra'em it is not proper; that is to say, according to R' Eliyahu Mizrachi it is not proper according to R' Eliezer of Metz even if they rest the whole night, for they have no remedy; but according to Beis Yosef, overnight resting of the night helps, as above.
For in this all agree. That is to say, both R' Eliyahu Mizrachi and Beis Yosef agree that according to R' Eliezer of Metz it is not proper to draw at night; only they disagree in this: according to R' Eliyahu Mizrachi they never have a remedy; and according to Beis Yosef, granted that overnight resting of that night when they were drawn does not help, nevertheless overnight resting of the following night helps, as above.
And the waters are forever forbidden according to R' Eliyahu Mizrachi, as above.
One may be lenient: first, one may rely on Rashi's view that waiting twelve hours suffices whether one drew them by day or by night, as above; and further, even R' Eliezer of Metz was stringent only ab initio where it is possible; but where it is not possible, perhaps Ra'em also agrees to be lenient.
And know that the Bach wrote a further leniency according to the view of Ravan, who holds regarding Rashi that we do not require twelve hours at night, but rather it suffices until after dawn rose if the night is short. And the authors stated twelve hours according to Rashi's explanation: that is a fixed measure by day if one drew them in the morning, for generally in Nisan day and night are equal and the night is twelve hours; therefore they measured by day according to the measure of places where in Nisan the night is twelve hours, because of "lo plug"; but if one drew them toward evening at bein hashemashot, overnight resting as the night is suffices even if short, and it is permitted immediately when dawn rises; and I copied his words to be a shield for the people of the land who do not wait twelve hours and knead early in the morning, for they have whom to rely upon; but most poskim did not write thus, and one should not be lenient. And see siman 265 for the measure of bein hashemashot, and it is a walk of 1,500 amos before nightfall. And Magen Avraham wrote there that it is about a quarter of an hour; see there. And see here in Taz, s.v. "And regarding the practical law," etc.; and see Chok Yaakov, who agreed with Magen Avraham that it is better to draw a bit before bein hashemashot than after bein hashemashot, unlike Taz.
