THE LAWS OF LEAVING WATER OVERNIGHT One may not knead except with water that was left overnight, whether they are waters of a pool or of a spring or whether they are waters of a river and they should be drawn while it is still day (close to the twilight period) or during the twilight period. One should not knead with them until the entire night has passed. One is able to draw large quantities of water in a single day. If it is hot at the time [of drawing], they[the waters] should be placed in the wine cellar which is cooler and if it is cold at the time, they should be placed out in the open, for the wine cellar is warmer, and one must rise early and bring them into the house prior to the sun rising, even if it's a cloudy day. REMA: and [regarding] if they were not brought in early, if they didn't stand [outside] to the extent that they became warm, they are not tainted. Ideally, the water should be left standing under the ceiling in case one forgets to bring them in early . When transporting the water under the open sky, one should cover it . When Pesach occurs on the first of the week, one should draw the waters on the outset of the 13th which is [solar] Thursday night . Initially, one should draw from the rivers and not from the wells . However, when the rivers are expanded from the thawing snow and rainfall, ideally one should draw from the wells . One should not spill water left overnight because of a death or the falling of a period [tekoopha] . Nevertheless, initially, its good to place iron in them [the water containers]. Also, it is forbidden for a gentile to draw the water for Mitzvah Matzos and even regarding other Matzot, a Jew should draw the water if possible. It is customary to gather water specifically for the Matzah[s] of the Mitzvah and to take [the water in] new earthenware vessels and one should not deviate from the custom.
