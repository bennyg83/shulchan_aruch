(1) One may not knead. Rashi wrote the reason: because in the rainy season the sun travels in the lower parts of the sky and the springs boil, as written 94b. And R' Eliyahu Mizrachi wrote the reason: because at night the sun travels below the ground — see there; and their words appear from our words that by day springs are cold and at night they boil. And Rif explained what is written at the end of chapter Kol Sha'ah: the drawn water that was drawn on that day — and it is implied from his words that drawing from the attached source heats them and requires overnight standing; therefore he calls them drawn water because drawing heats them — see R' Eliyahu Mizrachi. According to Rashi, whatever stood 12 hours detached, even from morning until evening, is sufficient; and according to R' Eliyahu Mizrachi, specifically when it was not attached at night; and according to his words, even after the beginning of the night it is permitted to knead with them provided it was not attached at the beginning of the night. But R' Yehuda bar Natan wrote, and likewise Semak in the name of R' Yitzchak and Hagahot Maimoniyot chapter 5, that the end of the night causes cooling because the north wind blows from midnight onward until dawn, as written at the beginning of Berachot; and it is cold, as written in Bava Batra 25b: from adulterers comes this north wind; therefore one must leave them the entire night — and so Rashba that one must leave them the entire night.

Between, etc. For Rashi, specifically cistern and spring water; but in rivers there is no concern, for specifically springs boil, as written in the Gemara: springs boil — and the reason is they are close to their exit from the source; and likewise according to R' Eliyahu Mizrachi's explanation. And Rashi wrote: nevertheless one should not breach the fence of the early authorities; but according to Rif's explanation it is forbidden by law.

While it is still day, etc. For R' Eliyahu Mizrachi wrote that according to R' Eliezer of Metz one must draw them specifically at bein ha shemashot so they remain attached all day, for by day springs are cold as written — and so Semak; and some are stringent to draw between day and night; and some forbid when drawn while it is still day even if left overnight afterward. Semak there rejected this because he mentioned only overnight standing — implying one need not be concerned when they were drawn, only that they stood overnight; and Beit Yosef rejected R' Eliyahu Mizrachi's words and explained "while it is still day or bein ha shemashot" means only that one should not draw after the beginning of the night; but Beit Yosef was concerned for the stringent view and wrote close to bein ha shemashot and not while it is still day. However, from the conclusion of all poskim and from the language of the Gemara it is implied like Shulchan Aruch's view; and likewise from Rif's language that he wrote they were drawn on that day.

And one may not knead, etc. To be concerned for R' Yehuda bar Natan's view as above.
