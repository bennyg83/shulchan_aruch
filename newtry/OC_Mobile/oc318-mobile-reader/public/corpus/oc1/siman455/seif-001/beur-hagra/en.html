(1) One may not knead. Rashi wrote the reason: because in the rainy season the sun travels at the lower edge of the firmament and the springs are boiling, as is written there, folio 94b. And Ra'em wrote the reason: because at night the sun travels below the ground; and Rashi said there: and their words appear better than ours, for by day the springs are cool and at night they are boiling. And the Rif explained what is written at the end of chapter Kol Sha'ah: "the scooped waters that were drawn that same day," and it is implied from his words that drawing them from what is still attached warms them, and they need overnight resting; and therefore they are called "scooped," for the drawing warms them; and see the Ran. According to Rashi's view, whatever stood twelve hours while detached, even from morning until evening, is fine; and according to Ra'em, specifically that it was not attached at night; and according to his words, even after the beginning of the night it is permitted to knead with them, so long as they were not attached at the beginning of the night. But R' Yitzchak bar Natan wrote, and likewise the Semak in the name of R' Yitzchak, and Hagahot Maimoniyot chapter 5, that the end of the night causes cooling, for the north wind blows from midnight onward until dawn, as is written at the beginning of Berachos; and it is cold, as is written in Bava Batra 25b: "and from the north cold," this is the north wind; and therefore one must leave them the whole night; and so wrote the Rashba, that one must leave them the whole night.
Whether etc. For according to Rashi, specifically water of cisterns and springs; but regarding rivers there is no concern, for specifically springs boil, as is written in the Gemara, "springs boil," and the reason is that they are near their exit from the source; and likewise according to Ra'em's explanation; and Rashi wrote: and nevertheless one should not breach the fence of the early authorities; but according to the Rif's explanation, by law it is forbidden.
While still daytime, etc. For R' Eliyahu Mizrachi wrote that according to the explanation of R' Eliezer of Metz one must specifically draw them at bein hashemashot so that they were attached all day, for by day the springs are cool, as is written there; and so wrote the Semak; and some are stringent to draw both by day and by night; and some are stringent to forbid when they were drawn while still daytime even if they afterward rested overnight; and the Semak rejected this there, for since it mentioned only overnight resting, it is implied that one need not care when they were drawn, only that they rested the whole night; and likewise Beis Yosef rejected the words of Ra'em; and that is what [the Mechaber] wrote, "while still daytime or at bein hashemashot," meaning only that one not draw after the beginning of the night; but the Bach was concerned for the stringent opinions and wrote near bein hashemashot and not while still daytime; but from the terse wording of all the poskim, and likewise from the language of the Gemara, it is implied like the view of the Shulchan Aruch; and likewise from the language of the Rif, who wrote "that were drawn that same day."
And one may not knead, etc. To be concerned for the view of R' Yitzchak bar Natan, as above.
And they may. According to all the explanations mentioned above.
And if time. To cool them, especially according to the Rif's explanation that the reason is that they were warmed by the drawing and one must cool them.
And one must, etc. As is written at the end of Kol Sha'ah: one may not knead in the sun.
And even. As is written at the beginning of chapter 3 of Yoma: a cloudy day is all sun; of what practical difference is this? For Rava's teaching: a woman may not knead, etc.
And if not. As Rambam and Shulchan Aruch wrote, siman 459, se'if 8; and see what I wrote there.
And when one carries, etc. See Magen Avraham.
When it falls. For Shabbos does not prepare for Yom Tov, as is written at the beginning of [tractate] Beitzah; and he follows his own reason, that he ruled that one must draw them specifically at bein hashemashot or near it; and see Magen Avraham.
Ab initio, etc. As above, that the springs boil, which is not so of rivers.
But when the rivers. As is written there: that the sun travels in Nisan in the mountains in order to melt the snows, and they become lukewarm.
One may not spill. As is written, "He who guards a mitzvah will know no evil"; and see Taz.
And it is also forbidden. For it is among the preparations for a mitzvah; and see above, siman 460, what is written there.
And even. So that one not put chametz into it.
And the custom is to take. As is written in Masseches Soferim regarding this matter, and specifically with earthenware vessels; and see what I wrote in siman 53, se'if 3.
