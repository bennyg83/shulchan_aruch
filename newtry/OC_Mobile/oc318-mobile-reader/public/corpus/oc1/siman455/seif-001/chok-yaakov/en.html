Or at bein hashemashot. For the main time to draw is that which is between sunset and the appearance of the stars, before the moon shines upon the earth; and so wrote Maharil; and see above, siman 261, for the measure of bein hashemashot; and because not everyone knows how to aim the measure of bein hashemashot, therefore they draw near bein hashemashot after sunset; and after the fact, if they draw while it is still day, or if they drew after the beginning of the night, it appears from the words of the later authorities that one may be lenient to knead with them after twelve hours. And in Responsa Meir Einei Chachamim, siman 32, he wrote that one should not be lenient except in a time of pressure or great loss; see above, se'if 3; and see Taz, who wrote that it is better to delay the drawing than to advance it. However, Maharil wrote the opposite, and this is his language: it is better to advance a bit than to delay, for overnight resting is not mentioned explicitly in the Talmud; end quote; and his words appear correct; and likewise is the implication of Magen Avraham.
Under the ceiling. And the reason he did not state to cover it with a cloth as he wrote nearby is because they must be in an open place where air has sway, as the Mechaber wrote in the name of Orchos Chaim; and if one covers it with a cloth they become heated; and according to this, what the world is careful specifically to cover the water even in cold time is not correct; and one must say that therefore covering at night does not help: we are concerned lest one forget to bring them in until long into the day and they become heated through the cloth or the cover if one does not cover them well; which is not so nearby, when one only carries the water mere transit, any covering helps.
And when one carries the water under the open sky. And it refers to what was written above: it is good to set it under the ceiling so that if one forgets to bring them in at dawn the sun not shine upon it. And for that reason he also warned that when one carries the water by day under the open sky to the place of the kneading house one cover it with a cloth so that they not become heated, as Semag and Semak wrote, and Terumat HaDeshen, siman 115, explicitly; and so wrote the Levush in explanation that it refers to daytime; for toward evening at the hour when they draw, when the sun has already set, one need not cover them at all, and it is only a custom and extra carefulness lest anything that warms fall into it, as Terumat HaDeshen wrote there; and all this is simple and clear, unlike Magen Avraham who explained that it refers to the time of drawing.
One should cover them. And specifically with earthenware and wood vessels and the like does covering help; but if they are in a glass vessel, covering does not help, for since the sun shines through the glass it heats more than when they are uncovered, as is in chapter 2 of Beitzah, folio 33a; see there in Rashi's explanation; and see above, siman 502; and this is easy.
The night of the thirteenth, that is, Thursday night. For if on Friday night we are concerned lest one advance before sunset or delay and desecrate Shabbos; so is the language of the Mordechai at the end of the first chapter of Pesachim; it is implied from this that after the fact, if one forgot to draw on the night of the thirteenth, it is permitted to draw on Friday while it is still day near bein hashemashot, or even at bein hashemashot, for we are not concerned for desecration of Shabbos, for anything that is only a shevut they did not decree upon at bein hashemashot in a place of mitzvah, as is explained above in siman 261 and 307; see there. However, from the words of the Rokeach, siman 265, brought in the words of the later authorities, who wrote, and this is his language: when the fourteenth falls on Shabbos, one draws on the night of the thirteenth from the river or well, and one does not draw from the cistern in his courtyard on Shabbos for kneading, for one needs much and it appears like filling his garden and his ruin, as is in Eruvin folio 104; end quote; it is implied that even after the fact drawing is forbidden; and in truth at first glance his words are astonishing: what concern of prohibition is there in this of appearing like filling, for there it is explained that specifically by means of a wheel it is forbidden, because one draws much and draws easily; and even with our wheel some permit, as is explained above in siman 338, se'if 6; and all the more so what they draw nowadays for an individual in his house, one or two jugs, which is a small matter, and it is also at bein hashemashot, when they did not decree upon a shevut; if so, what concern of prohibition is there in this? And in truth one may perhaps force a reconciliation of the Rokeach's words: that his intent is if one wants to draw on Shabbos itself, and so is slightly implied by his language, and he is not discussing bein hashemashot; and he also discusses when many courtyard residents want to draw and the like, but not where an individual forgot, when it is permitted to draw one or two jugs in his courtyard at bein hashemashot, or even from the river in a place where carrying is permitted (however, from the words of the later authorities who cited the Rokeach this is not implied). And it seemed more to me that there is a concern of prohibition of preparing from Shabbos for Yom Tov: (so what is written on Friday at the end of this siman permitting through small vessels and many people does not help for this reason.) And this concern is also mentioned in Beis Yosef and in Magen Avraham, and he concludes that one who forgot to draw on Thursday night should draw on Friday while it is still day, or command a gentile to draw at bein hashemashot, or take water that was drawn in his house even though it was not drawn for the sake of the mitzvah, since it is impossible otherwise; end quote. And the best is to draw oneself or through one's agent that same day near bein hashemashot, for there is no concern of prohibition as in the other ways; and see above, s.k. 1; and in Responsa Meir Einei Chachamim, siman 32, he wrote, and this is his language: the custom spread not to knead matzah after Shabbos except with water drawn on the night toward Friday, for then one can aim to draw them between sunsets and stars, which is not so with the two bein hashemashot of Friday and Motzei Shabbos; and in a place where they customarily practiced, one should not be lenient except in a time of pressure or great loss; end quote. And nevertheless, where there is no custom, one should not be stringent and practice as he wrote.
To draw from the wells. And Maharil wrote: it is a fine custom first to clean and purify the spring; and if it is impossible to clean all of it because of the abundance of its water source, nevertheless one should draw from it much water before the [actual] drawing; and all the wells in houses were presumptively drawn from with much water to clean the vessels for the days of Pesach; end quote. And Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah wrote that our custom is to filter the water through a white cloth so that nothing fall into them; end quote. And all this is only a stringency ab initio; and see above, siman 447, s.k. 12, and siman 467, se'if 12.
And nevertheless it is good ab initio to put iron in them. And one should take new iron and hang it by a string (Maharil and later authorities). And Magen Avraham wrote: Darkei Moshe wrote in the name of Hagahot Maimoniyot that on all other days of the year one should place salt or a seal; end quote. And Taz wrote, and this is his language: it appears that nevertheless on other nights, if one did not put iron in it to guard against the tekufah, one may spill them if one can easily do without that water; which is not so of these waters of matzas mitzvah, which it is forbidden to spill, for it appears like belittling the verse's statement, "he who guards a mitzvah will know no evil"; end quote. And similarly wrote Maharshal in his commentary on Semag; and my brother-in-law the Rav in Sefer Ateres Zekenim challenged him regarding what is implied from his words that specifically for matzas mitzvah one may permit the waters, but rather even for waters for other matzos one need not take care on account of the tekufah if it dies; and nevertheless he concluded in the name of Agudah that even for matzas mitzvah one may permit only when it is impossible to borrow mayim shelanu from him in that city; end quote. And it appears simple to me that even Agudah who is stringent said so only in a small city with few people; but in a large city we are certainly not obligated to go door to door asking for water, only among one's neighbors and acquaintances and the like; and so is implied from Taz's language, that even on all other days of the year one should not be stringent if one cannot easily do without that water; and all the more so of these waters; and the custom is to be lenient in every case.
It is also forbidden to draw. That is, ab initio; and so is explained explicitly in Maharil, that it is an extra stringency ab initio; and so wrote the Shlah.
And the custom is to take special water. And one should say at the time of drawing, for the sake of matzas mitzvah (Maharil; and Mahariv, siman 193). And Maharil further wrote that some customarily take special water for each night by itself.
New earthenware vessels, etc. In all printings of the Shulchan Aruch he wrote thus in the name of Kol Bo; and I did not find this in his words; and Magen Avraham wrote, and this is his language: Mateh Moshe wrote that one should not take a vessel that had fruit juices in it, for fruit juices with water hasten chimutz. (And it appears to me that through hagalah it is permitted.) Also one should not take in a copper vessel, which becomes heated; end quote. However, I have seen that the custom is to knead in copper troughs; end quote of Magen Avraham. And in truth one may distinguish: mayim shelanu that we must cool, and in a copper vessel perhaps it does not cool, which is not so regarding kneading; and see Hagahos Minhagim, who also forbids drawing with a copper vessel, and so is the custom; and after the fact one should not forbid; and so wrote Olas Shabbas at the end of siman 456; and so it appears; see what I wrote in se'if 3 regarding thick copper.
