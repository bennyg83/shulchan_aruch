1) [Se'if 1] One may knead only with water that rested overnight. This applies even to matzah not intended for the mitzvah, because the concern is that the dough might become chametz. Tashbetz, Ma'amar Chametz, page 30, and Responsa Tashbetz, volume 2, siman 87; Birkei Yosef, subsection 1; Kisei Eliyahu, subsection 1; Sha'arei Teshuvah, at the beginning of this siman. Rosh at the end of the chapter Kol Sha'ah and Maggid Mishneh, chapter 5, halachah 11, write likewise, as cited by Beis Yosef. Kol Bo, page 38a, writes that the Geonim and most Sages agreed to this, contrary to Rabbi Yitzchak ibn Ghayyat. See there. Erech HaShulchan, subsection 1, cites this. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1, writes likewise.
2) There. One may knead only with water that rested overnight. I found it written that the word lanu in this expression should be pronounced with a kamatz beneath the lamed, not with a patach. With a patach it would refer to the speaker, as in the phrase meaning 'we lodged in Gibeah.' Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1. In other words, with a kamatz the consonant is soft and the word describes the water as having rested overnight, like the phrase meaning 'we lodged in an inn.' With a patach the consonant is stressed and the word speaks about oneself.
3) There. One may knead only with water that rested overnight. When it is uncertain whether water rested overnight, one should not knead with it. If one did knead with it, the matzah is permitted. Mahari Bueno, cited by Mizbach Adamah. See Rema's gloss at the end of se'if 3; Machazik Berachah, Kuntres Acharon, subsection 1.
4) One who collected rainwater may knead matzah with it. According to all opinions, it need not rest overnight, provided that it is not placed where it will lose its chill. Radbaz, volume 5, siman 2,172; Birkei Yosef, subsection 4; Sha'arei Teshuvah, subsection 9. Halachos Ketanos, volume 1, siman 97, however, distinguishes: water collected during the day must rest overnight, while water collected at night need not. See there. Yad Aharon in Hagahos HaTur and Birkei Yosef, there, cite his ruling. See also below, subsection 64, regarding this matter.
5) It is permitted to knead with water when a non-Jew makes an incidental statement that it rested overnight in the non-Jew's house. No prohibition has been established, and the matter is rabbinic. One who is stringent will be blessed. The pious Rabbi Yosef Molcho in manuscript responsum, siman 103; Birkei Yosef, subsection 5. It appears that one certainly may not fulfill the mitzvah obligation with these matzos, since the water was not guarded for the mitzvah. Even for other matzos there is reason for concern, since perhaps a substance that causes leavening or something similar fell into the water. In pressing circumstances, one may permit it after filtering. Sha'arei Teshuvah, there.
6) There. One may knead only with water that rested overnight. This means water left overnight in a utensil after being drawn. Rashi, Pesachim 42a, explains that during Nissan the springs are warm because the rainy season has not yet ended. During the rainy season the sun travels low in the sky, so the world is cold and the springs are warm. The water is therefore left overnight to cool. These are his words. Others explain that the sun travels beneath the earth at night and warms the springs. The only remedy is therefore for the water not to remain connected to the ground at night, as explained by Beis Yosef. See there. Derashos Maharil writes that the water must cool for twenty-four hours before kneading: twelve daytime hours while still in the well, when the sun is distant from it, and twelve nighttime hours after it is drawn. See there.
