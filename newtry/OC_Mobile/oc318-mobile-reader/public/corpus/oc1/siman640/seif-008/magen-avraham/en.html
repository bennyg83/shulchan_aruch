And are obligated at night. This is when he lodges at night in an inhabited place; this is from Tosafos and the Ran. It implies that he must make a sukkah for himself there. So too it appears from Orchot Chaim, who wrote: since those who travel by day are obligated at night, one can say that those who travel in villages should be stringent to return home if there is no sukkah in that city; although there is room for a litigant to dispute, one who is stringent will be blessed. These are his words. In the Beis Yosef he explains that by law they are obligated to make a sukkah there, and if they do not make one they must return to their homes. Although one could say that if they have no sukkah at eating time they are exempt, just as if rain fell and one need not wait, nevertheless one who is stringent will be blessed, for this is called negligence, since he should have made a sukkah there and did not. All this applies when they travel from village to village to collect debts. But when one stays in one village for three or four days, he must make a sukkah there, as written at the end of this siman. The Levush wrote that he is not obligated to make a sukkah among the non-Jews, and that what it says, 'they are obligated at night,' means when there is a sukkah in the place where he lodges. He was not precise, for Tosafos wrote that when he reaches a settlement he is obligated, and a place of non-Jews is also called a settlement. Furthermore, according to him, what it says that they are exempt by day means that they need not wait until they reach a sukkah; if so, day and night are equal, and it should have stated plainly that if one wants to eat and there is no sukkah there, he need not wait. What difference is there whether he is traveling on the road or sitting at home? Rather, perforce this is what it means: travelers by day are exempt by day; even if they reach an inn, they are not obligated to exert themselves and make a sukkah there, since they intend to leave immediately. But at night they are obligated to make a sukkah there. This also appears from the language of Rashi, the Ran, and Tosafos; examine and you will find it. Therefore one should be stringent. So wrote the Beis Yosef in the name of Orchot Chaim, that they are exempt only when lodging on the road or in a place without settlement; see there. However, if he comes to a village close to eating time, he need not wait for a sukkah to be made, just as with rain. If he is in the field, even all the days of the festival, he need not exert himself and make a sukkah there, as stated in the Gemara at the beginning of page 26, for there is no settlement there.
