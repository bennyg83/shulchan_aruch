(39) Travelers, etc. For the Merciful One said, 'you shall dwell,' meaning in the manner of dwelling. It is known that when a person lives in his house during the rest of the year, he does not refrain from traveling for some matter and leaving his house. The same applies to sukkah.
(40) Are exempt from the sukkah by day. Meaning that he need not wait to eat until he reaches a sukkah, unless a sukkah becomes available before him at eating time without exertion.
(41) And are obligated at night. This is when he lodges at night in an inhabited place. Even if his inn does not have a sukkah, for example because the innkeeper is a non-Jew, he must make an effort in case he can find near his lodging some Jew who has a sukkah.
(42) But if they do not find a sukkah, etc. Meaning that he made an effort and did not find a sukkah nearby, he may eat and lodge in that place without a sukkah and is not obligated to make a sukkah. What he concludes, that they may go on their way, means that even if he knows at home that on the route he is taking he will not find a sukkah, nevertheless he may leave his house, for we have a rule that 'you shall dwell' means in the manner of dwelling, as above in subparagraph 39.
(43) For he need not make a dwelling there for himself. Meaning that where he does not find a sukkah, he is not obligated to make a new one.
(44) And although there is room to be lenient. Meaning that what they said, that those who travel by day are obligated at night, refers to where he finds a sukkah in that place, unlike this case.
(45) Nevertheless, one who is stringent, etc. All this is when he travels from village to village; by law he need not wait to eat, and only one who is stringent does so. But if he remains in one village for three or four days, he is obligated by law to make a sukkah there; this is from the Acharonim.
