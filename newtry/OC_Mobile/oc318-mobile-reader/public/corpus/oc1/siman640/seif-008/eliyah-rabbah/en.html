[8] Levush: Nevertheless, on the first night, etc. My teacher and master Rabbi Helman, of blessed memory, asked: if so, what does the Gemara ask on Rava, who says that one who is distressed is exempt from sukkah, from the Mishnah that sick people and their attendants are exempt? It says: a sick person yes, but one who is distressed no. Perhaps it taught a sick person because even on the first night he is exempt. It appears to me that this is the explanation: it should also teach one who is distressed, with regard to the other nights, just as it teaches in the final clause that casual eating is permitted outside the sukkah, which perforce does not apply on the first night, for then one is obligated even in an olive-volume. It further appears to me that perhaps in truth a sick person is exempt on the first night, and 'one who is distressed' was stated imprecisely: follow the reason learned from the festival of matzos. Even with a dangerously ill person, the danger is from his own condition and not from the sukkah, and if it is because of the sukkah, nothing stands before saving life. However, it is difficult for me regarding the poskim brought by the Beis Yosef who say that one who is distressed is exempt in eating only regarding sleeping. If so, what does the Gemara mentioned above ask? Perhaps it taught a sick person because he is exempt even from eating.
