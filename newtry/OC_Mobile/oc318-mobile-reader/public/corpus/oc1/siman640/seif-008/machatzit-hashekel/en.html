(Subparagraph 8) Not, etc. Sechach, etc., because of ohel. But on top of the sechach, even on a weekday, it is forbidden. The Rokeach's view is like Rabbeinu Tam, who permits at the end of siman 629 that if one spread a sheet under the sechach because of falling leaves, so they should not fall into the food, it is permitted; but on top of the sechach it is forbidden because of maris ayin, as explained there. The Rokeach holds that maris ayin applies specifically on top of the sechach, for someone passing in front of the sukkah will think that it was not covered with anything other than the sheet, which is invalid. But under the sechach there is no maris ayin, because one passing outside knows nothing at all about the spreading of the sheet, and one who enters will see that what was spread is only under valid sechach and is spread only to protect from the wind, for he already saw from outside before entering that the sechach is valid. This is not comparable to the rule that anything the Sages forbade because of suspicion is forbidden even in the most private room, for there we are concerned that perhaps someone will nevertheless see him, or because of a general no-distinction rule. Here, by contrast, inside there is no maris ayin at all, as explained. If so, under the sechach there is only the prohibition of ohel; therefore on Chol HaMoed it is permitted.
That one may rely, etc. Meaning: although the Rokeach wrote this simply because he rules like those who permit at the end of siman 629, as above, for us, since above at the end of siman 629 two opinions were brought as to whether such a case is invalid because of invalid sechach, one might have thought that it is forbidden on the sechach. Therefore the Magen Avraham teaches that in such a case, because of compulsion from the wind, one may rely on those who permit.
Since there is no empty space. The Rokeach forbade because of ohel only under the sechach, because then the usual way is that one does not climb all the way up to spread it directly under the sechach, but spreads it above a person's height, and presumably there is empty space between the sechach and the sheet. Although he does not need the airspace and empty space between them, nevertheless the Turei Zahav above in siman 315 concluded that where there are four walls, even if one does not need the airspace, it is still forbidden; see there. This is unlike where he spreads it on top of the sechach, where the usual way is to spread it directly on the sechach, because that is easier to do and spread. There is then no airspace at all.
And so it is explicit in the Gemara, page 27b; meaning that the Magen Avraham holds that spreading on top of the sechach is adding to an ohel. So it appears there in the Gemara, that such a case is called adding to an ohel. According to Rabbi Eliezer, who holds that adding to an ohel is forbidden, the same applies here and it is forbidden. According to the Rabbis, who hold that adding to an ohel is permitted, the same applies here and it is permitted. However, regarding a sheet it is explained there that even according to Rabbi Eliezer there is a reason to permit; see there.
