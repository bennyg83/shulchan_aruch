(Subparagraph 3) Obligated, but some say that the beis din, etc. This depends on the two answers of Tosafos that Terumas HaDeshen brought. Tosafos asked that in siman 616 it is explained, and it is an explicit Mishnah, that we educate minors by hours to fast on Yom Kippur, while generally we say that if a minor eats neveilos, the beis din is not commanded to separate him. In one answer they explained that the obligation of education is only on the father, not on the mother and not on the beis din. Therefore, if the father is not here, one may feed him outside the sukkah. He then brought a second answer, that there is a distinction between positive commandments and negative commandments. Even if you say that a negative commandment is more severe than a positive commandment, nevertheless education applies only to positive commandments. Therefore, regarding eating neveilos, which is a negative commandment, the beis din need not separate him. But educating for affliction on Yom Kippur is education to actively perform a positive matter. If so, the obligation of education applies also to the beis din. Since this latter answer appeared primary to Terumas HaDeshen, as he inferred there, he wrote that although the father is not here, one should not be lenient, etc.
See siman 616, meaning that this law derives from there, as mentioned above.
Nevertheless it implies that another person, etc. Meaning: although this matter is disputed in the two answers of Tosafos mentioned above, that is specifically if one sees the minor eating outside the sukkah, whether one must protest against him; or even to give the minor food outside the sukkah, which the minor will then eat outside the sukkah on his own. In such a case, according to the one who says that the mitzvah of education is not on the beis din, even if the minor eats it outside the sukkah, we do not care, because in any event he gave him a permitted item, since the minor could eat it inside the sukkah. What the minor did, eating it outside the sukkah, he did on his own understanding. So wrote the Magen Avraham above in siman 616; see there. But it is forbidden to feed him directly outside the sukkah, meaning to place it inside the child's mouth, or even to command him to eat outside the sukkah. This is forbidden according to all opinions, for it is like directly feeding him neveilah, which is forbidden.
