In Shulchan Aruch. Sick people and their attendants. Shevus Yaakov, part 3, siman 47, wrote about one hired by the community to attend to the sick, and attendants of the sick are exempt: is he nevertheless obligated to eat an olive-volume in the sukkah and recite the berachah of sukkah at every meal, since he sometimes goes for his own needs? He answered that he is exempt, since he is hired to serve the sick person for all his needs and to supervise him so that his work is complete. If he goes to the sukkah, he is distressed by the sick person's distress, because his mind is constantly upon him; therefore he is exempt. However, on the first night, when even one who is distressed must eat an olive-volume, he too is obligated, etc.; see there. It appears that everything depends on the circumstances. If during the week his way is to go periodically and stay away for a little while, since he is accustomed to do so for his own needs, he should not be lenient for a mitzvah need. But he should eat only an olive-volume and return to his work. If there are other attendants who serve in shifts, they are not exempt, since his watch is not constant; this one should eat while the other is serving.
