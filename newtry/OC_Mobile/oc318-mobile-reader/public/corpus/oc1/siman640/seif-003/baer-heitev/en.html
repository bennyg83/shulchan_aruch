According to this reason, one who drinks a laxative beverage, although he is greatly distressed, is nevertheless obligated in sukkah, since he should have done it before the festival or afterward. Therefore he should not do it on the festival. But after the fact, it appears to me that he is exempt from sukkah, because it is dangerous for him to sit in the sukkah; this is from the Magen Avraham. The Turei Zahav wrote that when one who bloodlets is obligated, that refers to one who bloodlets merely to preserve his health and does not feel ill. But if he feels ill and has some chill, he is exempt from sitting; see there. One who could enter the sukkah only by crossing a river by boat on Yom Tov is forbidden to cross. If he did cross after the fact, in a mitzvah case, under compulsion and pressing circumstances, one may rely on the Rashba, etc.; Kenesses HaGedolah in the name of Maharam Yonah, see there. The Magen Avraham in siman 639 wondered about him, for he is not obligated to go to his friend's sukkah, as written here in the gloss, that if the candles went out on a Sukkos night that falls on Shabbos, he is not obligated to go to his friend's sukkah to eat by the lamp, since there is exertion. If so, here too, where there is exertion, he is not obligated to cross the river; see there. It appears that there is no proof from there, for he already ate in the sukkah and fulfilled his obligation with an olive-volume of bread, which he was obligated to eat in the sukkah. And if you say he had not yet eaten, he can eat tomorrow. Therefore we say that if there is great exertion to go to his friend's sukkah, he is not obligated. But here, where he has no sukkah at all, if he crossed after the fact, one may rely on the Rashba. This is from Yad Aharon; see there.
