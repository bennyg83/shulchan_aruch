And a bris milah meal, etc. See Mishnah Berurah, where I wrote in the name of the Magen Avraham that ten people suffice for them. From this it implies that if the sukkah is too small to hold even ten people, it is permitted to eat outside the sukkah. But according to the reason the Gra copied, that it is not a Torah-level mitzvah that would override sukkah like marriage, even in such a case it is forbidden to eat outside the sukkah. The Pri Megadim also inclines that there is no room to be lenient in any case. Likewise with a betrothal meal, pidyon haben, bar mitzvah, siyum, and the like, it is obvious that they require a sukkah; this is from Bikkurei Yaakov. He also wrote that one who has a small sukkah may build a large sukkah on Chol HaMoed for the sake of a bris milah meal, because it is for the need of the festival. Although even for the need of the festival, work is permitted only privately, nevertheless here it is recognizable that it is for the need of the festival, and where it is recognizable it is permitted to do it publicly, as explained in siman 533, se'if 4. These are his words.
