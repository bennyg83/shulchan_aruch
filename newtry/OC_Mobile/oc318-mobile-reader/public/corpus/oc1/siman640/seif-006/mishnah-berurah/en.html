(32) A groom, etc. For simchah exists only in the chuppah, and chuppah means the place prepared for the sitting of the groom and bride; this is from the Rosh. They cannot eat in the sukkah and rejoice in the chuppah, because simchah is only in the place of the meal. It speaks where he married on the eve of the festival or earlier, since one does not marry women on the festival, as above in siman 546.
(33) Are exempt from the sukkah, etc. Some obligate him, for they hold that it is possible to eat in the sukkah and rejoice in the chuppah. Therefore the Acharonim agreed that it is proper for the groom to be stringent upon himself and sit in the sukkah, but not recite a berachah. So too the attendants, when they eat with the groom and gladden him during the seven days of feasting and sit there in the sukkah, need not recite a berachah. But when they eat at home, they are obligated in sukkah and must recite a berachah.
(34) And a bris milah meal, etc. This means even if the place is too narrow for them to sit there and it is impossible for them to rejoice there so much, nevertheless they are obligated in sukkah. Although it is a mitzvah meal, as written in Yoreh De'ah, siman 265, se'if 12, nevertheless it is not comparable to a wedding meal, where it is a great mitzvah to gladden groom and bride and it is called a mitzvah meal everywhere. This is not so much of a mitzvah, and therefore ten people suffice for them; this is from the Magen Avraham. Nevertheless, on Shemini Atzeres, if they want, they may eat in the house at a bris milah meal; this is from Eliyah Rabbah and Derech HaChaim.
