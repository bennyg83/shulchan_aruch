(Subparagraph 9) For he must, etc. For his head and most of his body we require at least ten tefachim less a fifth. A man's height is three cubits, which are eighteen tefachim. According to what the Magen Avraham initially assumed, this includes the head; subtract one tefach for the head, and seventeen tefachim remain for the body. If so, for his head and most of his body we require nine and a half and a little more, and therefore he used ten tefachim, a whole number. This is ten tefachim less a fifth according to the rule that every cubit by a cubit square has one cubit and two fifths diagonally. If so, the diagonal adds to seven tefachim fourteen fifths, which are three tefachim less a fifth. Together with the seven tefachim, this is nine tefachim and four fifths.
And his feet outside the sukkah. Although the beginning of the diagonal at the corner, and likewise the end, are very narrow, and wherever the diagonal does not extend a half-tefach, its width is not a tefach, so he cannot place his head there, even part of it, and likewise at the end part of his body, nevertheless he can bend himself somewhat, and this is not called distress. This is the way people sometimes sleep within their bed in a somewhat spacious place while bent. But I examined Terumas HaDeshen itself, and he did not write in the language that the Magen Avraham brought in his name, but like the end of the Magen Avraham's words, meaning specifically Rav Yosef, etc. Since Rashi needed to bring proof to Rabbi Yosef's statement that 'I am delicate,' perforce he brought proof only that this was known to all.
It implies from this, etc. Meaning, from the fact that we say on page 26 that Rav permitted Rav Acha to sleep in a canopy inside the sukkah because of baki, meaning flies, and did not simply tell him that it is permitted to sleep outside the sukkah because of baki, as Rava earlier permitted Rav Acha to sleep outside the sukkah because of the foul smell of the ground. Rather, certainly, in such a case, where one can save himself from the distress by sleeping under the canopy, this is preferable to sleeping outside the sukkah.
For there is one who says, etc. Namely on page 11a, Rabbah son of Rav Huna holds like Rabbi Yehudah, who holds in Perek HaYashen that one who sleeps under the bed in the sukkah fulfills his obligation.
Know that according to what the Magen Avraham wrote, were I not afraid to say a new matter, I would say as follows. Although the Magen Avraham wrote in siman 627, subparagraph 2, in the name of Agudah, who wrote in the name of Tosafos, that one should not cover with boards fixed by nails even if they are less than four tefachim wide, because they constitute a permanent dwelling, and so too in Tosafos, Sukkah 2a, s.v. ki avid, where they wrote the reason: although with walls we do not care if he makes them permanent, nevertheless the sechach specifically must be temporary; see there.
However, in Hagahos Maimoniyos, chapter 5 of Hilchos Sukkah, it is written as follows: there was an incident where Rabbi Shimon, Rabbeinu Tam's brother-in-law, covered with boards that did not have four tefachim, and made a very beautiful sukkah like a dome, fixed with nails, and Rabbeinu Tam invalidated it because it protects from rain. But if he laid them side by side, it is valid, because it does not protect from rain. These are his words. Thus Rabbi Shimon validated it, and even Rabbeinu Tam, who invalidated it, did so not because of the nails but because it protects from rain. In this is explained the dispute when the sechach is made in a way that protects from rain, at the beginning of siman 631, and according to several opinions it is valid. According to this, when rain falls and they close the roof doors called shutters, it is nevertheless preferable to sleep or eat inside the sukkah, even though the doors are closed, than to sleep or eat outside the sukkah. Under the doors, according to several poskim, one fulfills by law. This is the case of the canopy that the Magen Avraham wrote here. Although the Magen Avraham wrote at the end of siman 627: that he should not sit, for one might have thought that since he lifted them and then placed them down again, it is like loosening boards and the boards themselves should be valid; he teaches that this is not called an act, because he did not intend to place them there for shade. These are the words of the Magen Avraham. But where one did not place them for shade, everyone agrees it is invalid; see siman 635. Nevertheless, one can say that when he closes the doors, he should intend with the closing for the sake of shade. Also, the Levush at the end of siman 626 wrote that those doors were originally made for shade, but are invalid because rain cannot descend into the sukkah; see there.
If so, although there are two reasons to invalidate such a case. One is as the Agudah wrote in the name of Tosafos, that the sechach must be temporary. The second reason is as Rabbeinu Tam and the Levush wrote, that rain cannot descend into it. Nevertheless, for each reason there is one opinion that validates such a case, as explained above. If so, sitting under the doors is preferable to sitting entirely outside the sukkah. So it appears to me.
