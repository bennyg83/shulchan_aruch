See Ginat Veradim, klal 4, siman 13, that nowadays the shoshvinim and all members of the wedding party are obligated, since it is not the practice to celebrate the groom and bride inside the sukkah, but these eat by themselves, etc.; see there. All the more so, since the Rosh obligates even the groom himself, one may say that regarding invitees the authorities agree with the Rosh; see there. See Chaim Shaal, vol. 2, siman 35, who also wrote that guests who are merely invited to the day's meal are not called members of the wedding party. He brought the words of Ginat Veradim and wrote that the main ruling follows him. It also seems nearly certain that the custom of Jerusalem, may it be rebuilt and established, is that the groom and wedding party establish their meal in the sukkah; see there.
(*) (In Shulchan Aruch:) and a circumcision meal. See Magen Avraham, that although it is a seudat mitzvah, nevertheless it is not as significant as a wedding meal, and ten people suffice. See there, that the meal before the circumcision at night is not a seudat mitzvah, unlike Nachalat Shivah. See above siman 552. See Eliyah Rabbah, who wrote that Maharik, from whom the source of the law that one is obligated in the sukkah derives, was not discussing a circumcision meal; one may say that it is permitted, since it is mentioned in the Talmud, Perek Rabbi Eliezer DeMilah, and Tosafot there. Rather, he discusses the "week of the son," meaning the meal held by the woman who gave birth, which is not so much a mitzvah. However, one may say the opposite: perhaps a circumcision meal is forbidden outside the sukkah, since it need not be near the mother, while regarding the "week of the son," where they are accustomed to be specifically near the mother, he was uncertain. It appears that on Shemini Atzeret one may be lenient in both, and this is obvious. See there in Maharik, that regarding eating fruit alone, as people customarily do, one who is lenient loses nothing; see there. According to what I wrote in siman 639, that wine and fruit are permitted even if one makes a fixed meal of them, this has no practical difference except for one who is stringent with himself, and in this there is no need to be stringent. See Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 20, that perhaps nowadays they eat cooked dishes, and therefore he wrote in the name of Maharik that even the participants in the meal who eat near the mother do not require a sukkah then; see there.
