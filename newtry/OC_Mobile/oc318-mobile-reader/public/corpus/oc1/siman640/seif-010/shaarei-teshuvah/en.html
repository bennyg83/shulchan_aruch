See Ginas Veradim, rule 4, siman 13, that in our times the attendants and all members of the wedding party are obligated, for the way is not to feast the groom and bride inside the sukkah; rather, these people eat by themselves, etc.; see there. All the more so, since the Rosh obligates even the groom himself, regarding invited guests one can say that they agree with the Rosh. See Chaim Shaal, part 2, siman 35, who also wrote that invited guests who are merely summoned for the day's meal are not called members of the chuppah. He brought the words of Ginas Veradim and wrote that the main ruling is like him. It also seems close to certain that the custom of Yerushalayim is that the groom and members of the chuppah establish their meal in the sukkah; see there.
(In Shulchan Aruch) And a bris meal. See the Magen Avraham, that although it is a mitzvah meal, nevertheless it is not like a wedding meal, and ten people suffice; see there. He also wrote that the meal on the night before the circumcision is not a mitzvah meal, unlike Nachalas Shivah. See above, siman 552. See Eliyah Rabbah, who wrote that the view of Maharik, from whom the law that they are obligated in sukkah originates, does not speak of a bris milah meal, for one can say it is permitted since it is mentioned in the Talmud in Perek Rabbi Eliezer DeMilah and Tosafos there. Rather, he discusses shavua haben, meaning the meal near the birthing mother, which is not so much of a mitzvah. However, one can say the reverse: perhaps a bris milah meal is forbidden outside the sukkah, since it need not be at the birthing mother, while regarding shavua haben, which is customarily specifically near the birthing mother, he is uncertain. It appears that on Shemini Atzeres one may be lenient with both, which is obvious. See Maharik, that regarding merely eating fruit, as people customarily do, one who is lenient loses nothing; see there. According to what I wrote in siman 639, that wine and fruit are permitted even if one establishes himself over them, there is no practical difference in this except for one who is stringent upon himself, and in this one need not be stringent. See Eliyah Rabbah, subparagraph 20, that perhaps nowadays they eat dessert foods, and therefore he wrote in the name of Maharik that even the meal participants who eat near the birthing mother would not require a sukkah; see there.
