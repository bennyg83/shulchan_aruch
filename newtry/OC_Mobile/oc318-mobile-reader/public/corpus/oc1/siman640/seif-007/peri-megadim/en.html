See the Bayis Chadash, what he wrote in the name of the Rosh, and see Magen Avraham, letter 10. See Chok HaRei'ah, Sukkah 28b: why do I need 'native-born' and the extra heh of 'the native-born'? He answered in the name of the Ramban that 'native-born' excludes one who is distressed. If so, at first glance according to Rava, one who is distressed is exempt even when not because of the sukkah. For if the distress is from the sukkah, even without this, Rava derives in many places here 'you shall dwell' in the manner of dwelling. Rather, according to Abaye, who says sukkah is a halachah, and the verse 'native-born' is for converts and the word itself for one who is distressed, he is exempt even when not because of the sukkah. But according to Rava, one can say sukkah is from a verse and Yom Kippur is the halachah, as we wrote above in siman 639, and only on the first night do we need the verse of the fifteenth from the festival of matzos, and 'native-born' excludes women, and the extra heh includes converts. It works well that specifically one distressed because of the sukkah is exempt. See the Levush, who wrote that they attached it to the verse of 'native-born,' while in Chok HaRei'ah it implies an actual derivation. See Tosafos there; one can say it is from 'in Israel'; see there. There is no need to elaborate. See letter 8, and with Hashem's help it will be explained there further.
