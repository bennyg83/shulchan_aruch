Because of the wind. In all these cases he is exempt even from eating; this is from the Beis Yosef in the name of most poskim.
Or sleeping. This means in a place where there is no cold.
He does not fulfill. Therefore one should protest against those who make sukkos in the streets where it is impossible to sleep because of fear of thieves; this is from the Levush. But if he does not fear for his life, and fears only that his utensils will be stolen, he fulfills, for he can bring them into his house at night; Responsa Rema, siman 29, cited in Darchei Moshe.
Not under the sechach. Because of ohel. But on top of the sechach, even on a weekday, it is forbidden; this is from the Rokeach. However, if it is evident to everyone that he intends only to protect, it is permitted, for one may rely on those who permit at the end of siman 629. There is also no issue of ohel, since there is no empty space between it and the sukkah, as written in siman 315, se'if 2. So wrote the Rokeach, and this is explicit in the Gemara, page 24, that specifically according to Rabbi Chanina it is forbidden, but according to the Rabbis it is permitted.
For he must bend. The length of a small sukkah is seven tefachim, enough for his head and most of his body. For his head and most of his body we require at least ten tefachim; therefore, perforce he bends his height; this is from Terumas HaDeshen. This requires analysis, for one can say that at the time of sleeping he removes the table and lies diagonally in the sukkah, which is ten tefachim less a fifth, with his feet outside the sukkah in the house; see Rashi at the beginning of Sukkah. According to what Tosafos wrote in Eruvin 48, that a person's body is three cubits excluding his head, then for his head and most of his body more than ten tefachim is required, and the proof is sound. The Gemara says that the wind would drop splinters of sechach onto the food, and Rav Yosef left. Abaye said to him: but we learned, until the dish spoils. He answered: for me, since I am delicate, it is like the dish spoiling. Rashi explains that Rav Yosef was among the delicate, as stated in Perek Arvei Pesachim, where it is taught, etc. This means specifically Rav Yosef, who was known to be delicate, but ordinary people cannot say, 'I am delicate'; rather, we require until the dish spoils, unless he is known to be delicate. See siman 629, se'if 14, and the end of siman 614. It is stated on page 26 that if there are mosquitoes in the sukkah, one may sleep in a canopy even though it has a roof ten tefachim high; see siman 627. This implies that this is preferable to sleeping outside the sukkah, for there is one opinion that he fulfills in this.
