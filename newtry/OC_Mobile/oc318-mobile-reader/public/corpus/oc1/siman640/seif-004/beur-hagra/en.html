Se'if 4: But at night, etc. As written in siman 639, se'if 5, in the gloss.
Because of the wind. The Gemara there.
Or because of the flies, etc. The Yerushalmi there teaches: just as one leaves because of rain, so one leaves because of heat and because of mosquitoes.
Specifically where it came, etc.; but, etc. As written in the first chapter: in a shvutzeri one may not roof; what is the reason, etc.
And if, etc.; for it is not, etc. Just like a sukkah that does not hold his head, most of his body, and his table, or is less than ten tefachim high, or whose leaves descend into ten tefachim, or that cannot hold pillows and cushions, and similar cases.
One whose candles went out, etc. As we wrote above in siman 639, se'if 5, in the gloss: one who is not, etc., if he would leave, etc. Regarding a lamp, it is normal to leave in order to eat in a place with a lamp, as written in Perek Arvei Pesachim: Rav Huna recited Kiddush, etc., and his lamp was uprooted, etc. It also appears there that it is not normal to eat without a lamp, as it says there: taste something, lest by the time you go the lamp be uprooted, etc. See Tosafos there. This is also as written in the last chapter of Yoma, 'like daylight,' and at the beginning of Berachos, 'from the time the poor person,' and see Rashi there, s.v. from the time the poor person, etc.
And if it came, etc. See the beginning of siman 315.
One who cannot, etc. From what is written that the measure of a small sukkah is seven tefachim, and at least we require his head and most of his body inside the sukkah, as written there at the end of chapter 2. A person's head and most of his body is more than this measure, even diagonally, which is ten tefachim minus a fifth. Tosafos in Perek HaMotzi proved that his body without his head is eighteen tefachim. The Mordechai and the gloss already wrote above, 'or if it is impossible to do one of them, etc.' Perforce, one is not called distressed by this, for this is the normal manner.
And he cannot, etc. From what is written, 'from when is it permitted to clear out, etc.,' it implies that before then it is not permitted, even if he is distressed by this. So too the incident of Rav Yosef there. See Turei Zahav and Magen Avraham.
And one who is distressed, etc. The reason for one who is distressed is as Tosafos wrote, as above, from 'you shall dwell,' etc. Although it says this regarding a mourner, it teaches us, etc.; otherwise he would already be exempt. The Rosh already resolved this. See Magen Avraham, subparagraph 10, and see Turei Zahav.
