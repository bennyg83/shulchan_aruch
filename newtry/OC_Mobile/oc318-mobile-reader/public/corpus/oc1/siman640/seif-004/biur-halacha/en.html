Because of the wind. The same applies because of cold in cold places, as above in siman 639, se'if 2, in the gloss. See the Levush, who says that even if he has pillows and cushions to save himself from the cold, there is also room to be lenient, because not every person can exert himself every night to bring them there and the next day remove them, unless he has a place in the sukkah to leave them there for all the days of the festival. This is also proven in the Magen Avraham there, subparagraph 7, that in such a case we cannot exempt him because of the reason of cold; see there. Know that the agreement of most poskim is that one who is distressed is exempt even from eating, as the Magen Avraham wrote in the name of the Beis Yosef. Therefore it appears that one should be careful in cold weather to wear warm clothing when eating in the sukkah, so that he will not be distressed because of the cold and there will not be a concern of a berachah in vain.
Because he fears robbers, etc. See the Magen Avraham in the name of the Levush, that one should protest against those who make sukkos in the streets where it is impossible to sleep because of fear of thieves who may harm his body. According to how the Yad Ephraim in subparagraph 6 explains the words of the Magen Avraham, it implies that the Magen Avraham himself is lenient about this, unlike the Levush. We can also join the view of the Chacham Tzvi, who is lenient regarding the basic law of the Rema, as I copied in Mishnah Berurah. Therefore it appears that one who is lenient in this matter has whom to rely upon. Nevertheless, if he can make the sukkah elsewhere, that is certainly better.
Except in a matter about which people normally feel distress [Tur]. In my humble opinion, the Ran's view is not so. This is his language regarding the foul smell of gargishta: it does not mean that it is repulsive to everyone, for if so the sukkah would be invalid, as we say regarding shootsi and shatsari, that one may not roof with them because their smell is foul and one will leave them and go out. Rather, here we speak where he alone is distressed. These are his words. In truth, I do not understand the Ran's words, for there, after the fact, the sukkah is not invalid, as the Rambam and Rosh wrote, and so is proven above in siman 629, se'if 14; see there. If so, his proof falls away. Afterward I found that the Darchei Moshe already noted this about the Ran's words; see there. However, if he is delicate, even the Tur agrees that he is exempt, as the Bayis Chadash, Turei Zahav, Shaarei Ephraim, and others wrote, and as I wrote in Mishnah Berurah.
