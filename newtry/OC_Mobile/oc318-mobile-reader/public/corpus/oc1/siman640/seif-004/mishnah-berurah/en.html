(13) One who is distressed is exempt, etc. For we require it to be like dwelling, and even during the rest of the year a person does not live in a place where he is distressed.
(14) He but not his attendants. Meaning that if they want to eat or sleep, they must enter the sukkah. At first glance it appears that if one is hired by him to serve him, and during the rest of the year too he eats and sleeps only at his employer's place and not at his own house, he may do so today as well. This is similar to the guards of gardens and orchards explained below in this siman.
(15) But on the first night, etc. This follows his view in siman 639, se'if 5, in the gloss, where he wrote without qualification to be stringent regarding rain on the first night. The same applies here. But according to the other poskim who exempt there, the same applies here. Therefore, in practice, one should conduct oneself regarding one who is distressed as with the law of rain, as we wrote there in Mishnah Berurah, subparagraph 35; see there.
(16) One who cannot sleep, etc. The same applies that one who is distressed is exempt from all of them, even eating; this is from the Acharonim. That means that if wind, smell, and the like distress him while eating, he is exempt even from eating. But if the distress is only with sleeping, he remains obligated in eating.
(17) Or because of the smell. See above, siman 629, se'if 14, and Mishnah Berurah there; siman 630, se'if 1, and Mishnah Berurah there; and above in siman 79, Mishnah Berurah subparagraph 23.
(18) Or sleeping. This means in a place where there is no cold, and the distress is because of wind and the like, as above. But in cold places, one fulfills his obligation through eating even though he cannot sleep there, because it is impossible otherwise; automatically it is called like dwelling and also called fit for sleeping, if he had proper pillows and cushions.
(19) Because he fears robbers or thieves. Meaning that they could harm his body. Even in a place where he is not afraid by day, but at night he is afraid to sleep there because of them, this applies. However, if he is not afraid of bodily harm but only of theft of his utensils, he fulfills his obligation, for he can bring them into his house.
(20) He does not fulfill, etc. Meaning, even his obligation of eating. Some Acharonim disagree with this. Their view is that although initially one certainly should not make a sukkah in which he cannot fulfill all the matters according to law, nevertheless after the fact he fulfills the obligation of eating there, since regarding eating he is not distressed.
(21) He may leave, etc. Because it is normal for people to be distressed by this.
(22) And he need not go to his friend's sukkah. For this too is considered distress, since a person's food is pleasant to him only in his own place. On the first night he must exert himself and go to his friend's sukkah, but he should not recite a berachah there unless he is not distressed by this; this is from Eliyah Rabbah. The Chayei Adam wrote that with such distress a person may obligate himself, do it joyfully, and recite the berachah.
(23) If there is great exertion. Since this reasoning, that going to his friend's sukkah involves distress, was not so definite to him, he made it depend on whether there is great exertion. If there is not great exertion, he must go to his friend's sukkah that has a candle. Similarly, if rain fell and stopped, but it still drips from the trees and he cannot eat in his sukkah, while his friend has a sukkah with a roof made for closing and opening, he must go to his friend's sukkah if there is no great exertion in the matter.
(24) In the matter. This means at the time of eating. But it is obvious that even one who has no place in his courtyard to build a sukkah must find a place for himself to build a sukkah, and then that is called his home, just as all year people commonly move from house to house and then that is their home. This is from the gloss of the Chasam Sofer, and the Bikkurei Yaakov also wrote this as obvious.
(25) But not under the sechach. For then the sheet nullifies the sechach and invalidates the sukkah, as above in siman 629, se'if 19, according to the first view, which the Mechaber stated there without qualification. However, according to what we explained there, in a pressing circumstance, when spreading from the side does not help and he would have to eat outside the sukkah, one may rely on the second view, which permits hanging a sheet in order to protect. This should be forbidden only when he distances the sheet four tefachim from the sechach, for then the sheet is not nullified to the sechach. On Shabbos and Yom Tov it is proper to be careful not to distance it by an empty space of three tefachim from the sechach because of the concern of ohel. The same applies that in a pressing circumstance it is permitted to spread a sheet above the sechach to protect, whether on a weekday or on Shabbos and Yom Tov. However, because of the concern of observers, who will say that he is covering his sukkah with a sheet, one should be lenient when covering above only if it is recognizable to all that he intends only to protect from the wind. See the Magen Avraham above in siman 629, that nevertheless in all this one should not recite the berachah of leishev ba-sukkah.
(26) He is not called distressed. For a person sometimes sleeps bent in this way. The proof is from what we rule, that the measure of a sukkah is seven by seven tefachim, where certainly a person cannot sleep unless he bends his body and limbs. According to this, even if he initially made it in a way fit for sleeping stretched out, and afterward some matter occurred that made it cramped and narrow to lie down, he is still obligated to sleep there. The Nahar Shalom wrote that nevertheless, one who is delicate and knows about himself that for him sleeping bent there is distress is indeed exempt from sleeping there. Even so, he fulfills the obligation of eating, since at the time it was made it was fit for him to eat and sleep there.
(27) Even though he must bend his hands and feet. This means even if he has no way to stretch his legs outside the sukkah. If he can stretch his legs outside the sukkah, everyone agrees that he is not called distressed by this and is obligated to sleep there. He must only be careful that the table also be inside the sukkah; otherwise we are concerned that he may be drawn after his table, as above in siman 634.
(28) Except for a matter, etc. Otherwise, even if he is distressed, we say that his opinion is nullified relative to all people.
(29) About which people normally feel distress. Unless he is among the delicate, and all delicate people are distressed by this.
(30) Only if he will save, etc. The reason one who is distressed is exempt is that we require something like dwelling, and a person does not live in a place of distress whenever he can save himself from it in his house. The same applies with sukkah.
