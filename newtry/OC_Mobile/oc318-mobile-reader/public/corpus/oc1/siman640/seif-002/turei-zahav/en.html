Like a five-year-old, like a six-year-old. Meaning, each according to his sharpness. In Perek Keitzad Mishtatfin, Rav Asi said: a six-year-old minor goes out with his mother's eruv. Meaning, if she made an eruv for herself and did not acquire a share in the eruv for him, she brings him along with her, for he is like her body, since presumably her mind was on the minor, who cannot manage without his mother. They challenged: a minor who needs his mother goes out with his mother's eruv, and one who does not need his mother does not go out with his mother's eruv. We also learned similarly regarding sukkah: a minor who does not need his mother is obligated in sukkah. The school of Rabbi Yannai and Reish Lakish said: how much is this? Like a four-year-old, like a five-year-old. Rav Yehoshua tried to answer this and remained refuted. Afterward the Gemara asks against Rav Asi from what was taught: a minor who needs his mother goes out with his mother's eruv until age six. It answers for Rav Asi: until, and including. Shall we say this refutes Rabbi Yannai and Reish Lakish? It is not difficult: that case is where his father is in town, and the child goes in and out with him, so from four and above he is not called one who needs his mother. The baraisa is where his father is not in town, and the measure is until age six. The Rif and Rambam wrote only the words of Rav Asi, because we do not rely on that answer of whether his father is present, for the Gemara did not answer this way for Rav Asi, but instead answered that until includes the endpoint. It is Rabbi Yannai and Reish Lakish who answer this way, because they do not hold that until includes the endpoint. We hold like Rav Asi, since the Gemara brings him at the end of Perek Af Al Pi, and so ruled Tosafos and the Rosh. The Tur ruled in eruvin, above in siman 414, like that distinction whether his father is in town or not, but here he did not mention this distinction; rather, the Tur ruled 'like a six-year-old, like a seven-year-old,' which is like Rav Asi. Rav Asi's 'six-year-old' means a full six, and the Tur's 'seven-year-old' means the beginning of the seventh. It is certainly difficult why he did not rule here as he wrote in Hilchos Eruvin, end of siman 414. The Beis Yosef explained the Tur's words here, that 'like a six-year-old, like a seven-year-old' means that if his father is in town, the measure is like age six, and if his father is not in town, the measure is like age seven. In my humble opinion, it is unlikely that the Tur would write in such a hinting way. Rather, it appears to me that the Rosh and Tur also hold that Rav Asi, Rabbi Yannai, and Reish Lakish disagree: according to Rav Asi, both in eruv and in sukkah the measure is a full six-year-old, with no distinction whether his father is present; according to Rabbi Yannai and Reish Lakish there is a distinction. The Rosh and Tur apparently ruled like Rav Asi regarding eruv, namely stringently, so his mother should not perform a prohibited act of bringing her son along when he has a father in town, since according to Rabbi Yannai and Reish Lakish this is prohibited. But here, regarding sukkah, where the obligation of education is rabbinic and passive, for according to Rabbi Yannai and Reish Lakish there is no obligation on him even rabbinically, while according to Rav Asi there is an obligation, since it is rabbinic we follow leniently and do not obligate one to exert himself to educate him unless he is a full six and at the beginning of seven. This is what our teacher the Tur intended here when he wrote that one is obligated rabbinically to educate him. Why did he need to mention that the obligation is rabbinic? It was for the intent we mentioned: since this is only rabbinic, they did not impose the obligation until he is age seven; rather, he remains passive. This is unlike eruv, where his mother wants to perform an action by taking him along, and she should not do so until it is permitted according to all opinions, namely at five years. This appears to me correct and clear in the Tur's words. What the Shulchan Aruch wrote here, 'like a five-year-old, like a six-year-old,' means complete years, according to the Tur's view that we mentioned.
