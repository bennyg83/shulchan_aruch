Like a five-year-old. See Turei Zahav. See the Rambam, chapter 6 of Sukkah, halachah 1, and in the Maggid Mishneh and Lechem Mishneh it implies that the text is like a five-year-old and like a six-year-old, and so the Shulchan Aruch wrote here. See Eruvin 82b, and Tosafos and the Rosh there. What the Turei Zahav wrote, to distinguish that with eruv the mother performs an act by hand, and therefore he ruled stringently that when the father is not in the city, a full six-year-old goes out with his mother's eruv, while when the father is in the city, only until a full five years, this follows Rav Achi, who explained the eruv baraisa's 'until six' as meaning until and including six, not like Reish Lakish. Since in Perek Af Al Pi the Gemara brings Rav Achi, nevertheless in this matter the Turei Zahav was concerned to be stringent like Reish Lakish's distinction, perhaps specifically when the father is not in the city. But regarding sukkah, which is passive, he ruled entirely like Rav Achi and was not concerned to be stringent because of the distinction whether the father is in the city or not, since Rav Ashi does not distinguish and it is passive. What he wrote that according to Rabbi Yannai and Reish Lakish does not mean exactly like them, for they said already four and five, but only the distinction they made between whether the father is in the city or not. However, what he wrote that according to Rabbi Yannai there is no obligation and according to Rav Ashi there is obligation, I did not understand at first glance; it should be the reverse, unless the text is to be emended the other way. What he wrote at the end, that it is permitted according to all opinions, means regarding the distinction between whether the father is in the city or not; there is another way to explain this, and it requires analysis. Also with sukkah, for the mother to feed him directly is forbidden, unless the minor takes it himself. See Magen Avraham, letter 3, and with Hashem's help it will be explained there further.
