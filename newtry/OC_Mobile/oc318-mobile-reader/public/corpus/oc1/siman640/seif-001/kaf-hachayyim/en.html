1) [Seif 1] Women and servants, etc. are exempt from sukkah, because the Torah wrote an extra definite article, 'Every native in Israel shall dwell in sukkos.' We expound from the fact that it is written 'the native,' and not merely 'a native,' to exclude women, since 'the native' implies those distinguished among the natives, namely men and not women. Furthermore, it is a positive time-bound commandment, and women are exempt from every positive time-bound commandment. Should you ask: If so, why do I need the word 'the native'? It is necessary so that you should not say that we derive the identical word 'fifteenth' from Pesach: Just as women are obligated in the mitzvos of Pesach, so too they are obligated in sukkah. Therefore, it teaches us otherwise. Levush. This is from the Gemara, Sukkah 28; see there.
2) Ibid. Women and servants, etc. For this reason, servants are also exempt, since servants are obligated in mitzvos only to the extent that women are obligated, as we derive through the verbal analogy of 'to her, to her' from a woman. Levush. This is from the Gemara, Chagigah 4a; see there. This refers to a servant who immersed for the sake of servitude. If he did not immerse, in any event he is like a non-Jew and is not obligated in mitzvos, as stated in Yoreh De'ah, siman 267, seif 17; see there.
3) Ibid. And minors, etc. Minors are exempt, for they are not subject to performing mitzvos. Levush.
4) Ibid. Are exempt from sukkah. Nevertheless, women receive reward when they fulfill a positive time-bound commandment, as stated above, siman 589, sec. 23. Regarding whether women may recite a blessing over a positive time-bound commandment, see above, siman 589, seif 6, and our words there, sec. 23. Others, however, should not recite the blessing on their behalf unless the one reciting it is also blessing for himself, as stated there, seif 6, in the Rama's gloss. So too wrote the Magen Avraham in this siman, s.k. 1.
5) Ibid. Are exempt from sukkah. Nevertheless, it is a mitzvah for every Jewish man to have his wife and household sit with him in the sukkah, so that they may shelter in the shade of faith, purify their souls, and make known to them Hashem's kindnesses toward Israel, His people and inheritance, throughout all the days, so that His fear and love will be upon their faces and they will not sin. Yafeh LaLev, vol. 2, sec. 1.
6) Ibid. Are exempt from sukkah. Converts, however, are obligated, as stated in the Gemara there, Sukkah 28b. See Tosafos there, s.v. 'to include converts.'
7) Ibid. A tumtum and an androgynos are obligated because of doubt. Regarding the blessing, there is a dispute among the great authorities. The Rambam, Laws of Sukkah 6:13, ruled that they do not recite a blessing over the sukkah, while the Raavad there wrote that they do recite a blessing. See above, siman 17, seif 2, where the Shulchan Aruch ruled according to the Rambam not to recite a blessing, while the Rama there holds that they do recite a blessing; see there and our words there. See also above, siman 589, seif 4, and our words there, with G-d's help.
8) Ibid. Likewise, one who is half servant and half free is obligated. This is because of the aspect of freedom within him. Levush; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, sec. 1.
