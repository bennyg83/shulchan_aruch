(Turei Zahav, subparagraph 2) 'And the Rif and Rambam did not write, etc.; it is Rabbi Yannai and Reish Lakish who answer this way.' So wrote the Beis Yosef as well in siman 414, and this is astonishing to one who examines it. First, from where do Rabbi Yannai and Reish Lakish know that 'until' does not include the endpoint? If they explained that 'until' includes the endpoint, this answer, that his father is present, would also work. Rather, according to them the answer that 'until' includes the endpoint does not suffice the way it does for Rav Asi, and on the contrary, the difficulty against them would be greater: they hold that a five-year-old no longer needs his mother, while the baraisa says until six. Therefore one must distinguish between whether his father is present, etc. In order that the words of the Beis Yosef and Turei Zahav not be so astonishing, I will write briefly what appears to me to resolve them. First I will explain that the main intent is to bring proof that the halachah follows Rav Asi from the fact that the Talmud cites him in Perek Af Al Pi. At first glance this is not such proof, for although the Talmud there cites Rav Asi's statement, it brings it only to learn from it that until age six a child is drawn after his mother. If so, according to Rabbi Yannai and Reish Lakish too this is true where his father is not in town, and there it is not relevant to make that distinction. Thus one could well say that in law one should distinguish whether his father is present, as we distinguish according to Rabbi Yannai and Reish Lakish, and that the Talmud merely cited Rav Asi's wording for brevity, as we find in many places. In truth, however, this is not so. According to Rabbi Yannai and Reish Lakish, who hold that from four and above he does not need his mother, it is impossible to explain that 'until' includes the endpoint. Although we establish it where his father is not in town, it is not reasonable that there should be a two-year difference between when his father is in town and when his father is not in town. Even though Tosafos implies that such a distinction can indeed be made, the Rambam does not hold so. Perforce, according to them the baraisa means 'until' but not including the endpoint. If so, according to Rabbi Yannai and Reish Lakish, a six-year-old is not drawn after his mother in any case, for even if his father is not there, the measure is until six but not including six. There in Kesubos, where the Talmud cites Rav Asi regarding a full six-year-old, this is because the halachah is so, unlike Rabbi Yannai and Reish Lakish. If so, automatically there is no distinction whether his father is present, for the Talmud did not want to make such a distinction according to Rav Asi, but rather said that 'until' includes the endpoint. The reason appears to me to be that specifically according to the answer of Rabbi Yannai and Reish Lakish is it comfortable to distinguish this way and establish the baraisa, which teaches 'a minor who needs his mother,' where his father is not present, whereas Rabbi Yannai and Reish Lakish speak of a minor who does not need his mother, which we explain as where his father is present. But there is no hint of this at all in the baraisa; on the contrary, the phrase 'a minor who needs his mother' is more naturally explained where his father is not present. Therefore they hold that according to Rav Asi there is no difference in this, and in every case he is called needing his mother. But according to Rabbi Yannai and Reish Lakish this distinction works well, for according to them the baraisa speaks where his father is not present, but according to them one must say that 'until' does not include the endpoint, so as not to create a difference of more than one year. According to this, the flow of the Beis Yosef and Turei Zahav is that we do not rely on that answer, since according to Rav Asi we could have explained the baraisa like the questioner, that 'until' does not include the endpoint, but it speaks where his father is present, while Rav Asi speaks where his father is absent, and according to Rav Asi there would be only a one-year difference. Since the Talmud did not answer this way but forced the answer that 'until' includes the endpoint, this proves that it holds that according to Rav Asi one cannot establish it this way, as we wrote above. Only according to Rabbi Yannai and Reish Lakish does it answer this way, distinguishing whether his father is present or not, because according to them it means 'until' but not including the endpoint, and the difference between present and not present is only one year. If so, the passage in Perek Af Al Pi proves that the halachah follows Rav Asi, for according to Rabbi Yannai and Reish Lakish, even if his father is not present, a six-year-old is called one who does not need his mother. Nevertheless, what the Turei Zahav wrote requires analysis: he distinguishes and answers that regarding eruvin he ruled stringently like Rabbi Yannai and Reish Lakish, and he concluded at the end that it is permitted according to all opinions, namely at five years. This is astonishing, for according to Rabbi Yannai and Reish Lakish a six-year-old is called one who does not need his mother, and it is permitted only at a full five years where his father is present, according to Rabbi Yannai and Reish Lakish. This requires analysis.
