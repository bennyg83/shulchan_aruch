At all. See Be'er Heitev. See Responsa Chacham Tzvi there, who wrote to wonder about the Mordechai brought in the gloss, that if one is distressed in sleeping he does not fulfill even regarding eating. This is not so, for 'you shall dwell in the manner of dwelling' applies only to the manner of sitting, how it should be, and about this it was said that it should be like dwelling. But if one made two sukkos, one for eating and one for sleeping, even though the one for eating is not fit for sleeping, nevertheless he fulfills the obligation of eating. Therefore, what the gloss wrote in the name of Terumas HaDeshen, that if it is too narrow to stretch out his hands and feet he is nevertheless obligated to sleep in it, and this is proven from a seven-tefach sukkah and from the words of the Mordechai, is not so. Sense proves that there is no greater distress than this, and he should not initially make such a sukkah. But if it is Yom Tov and it has already been made, or if he has no place for another sukkah, he certainly need not sleep in it, and nevertheless he fulfills the obligation of eating there. See there, where he brought proof from Rabbi Akiva, who made a sukkah on the top of a ship, where the wind is common and it is not usual to sleep, and nevertheless he made it for eating; see there. See Responsa Mayim Rabbim, siman 53, Responsa Mahari Dayan, who upholds the words of the Mordechai and Terumas HaDeshen, and see there siman 54. See Machazik Berachah in the name of Zera Emes, who discussed at length that even one who does not sleep in the sukkah because of cold, or because it is impossible for him and his wife to sleep there, nevertheless fulfills the obligation of eating. See Birkei Yosef, who is uncertain about a non-dangerously ill person, whether he is exempt on the first night, and wrote that from the words of Orchot Chaim and Kol Bo it implies that a sick person is exempt even on the first night. So too it appears from the words of the Eliyah Rabbah, and specifically one who is distressed is obligated on the first night; see there.
