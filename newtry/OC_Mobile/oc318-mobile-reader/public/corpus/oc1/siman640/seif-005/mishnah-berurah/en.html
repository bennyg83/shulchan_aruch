(31) But he is obligated in sukkah. Although one who is distressed is exempt from sukkah, that applies only to distress that arises on its own because of the sukkah. But here he is distressing himself; he should settle his mind in order to fulfill the mitzvah. The Sefer Tanya wrote that if the deceased was so beloved to him that he cannot remove him from his heart, he is exempt. This speaks where he is distressed by sitting in the sukkah; this is from the Magen Avraham. See Yeshuos Yaakov, who disagrees with the Tanya about this. The Pri Megadim is uncertain regarding an onen on Chol HaMoed, or even on Yom Tov when he wants to bury through a non-Jew on the first day of Yom Tov or through a Jew on the second day of Yom Tov, when the laws of aninus apply, whether he is obligated in sukkah; see there. In Bikkurei Yaakov he inclines that he is exempt; see there.
