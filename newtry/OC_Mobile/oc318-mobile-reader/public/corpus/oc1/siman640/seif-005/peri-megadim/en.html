One who. See Turei Zahav. In Terumas HaDeshen, siman 92, he proved that this is not called distress, because a person sometimes sleeps bent this way. The meaning is that if it were distress, it would not help that he accepted upon himself to bear the distress and sleep this way, for since he is exempt, it would be as if he made the sukkah in a place where he cannot sleep and he would not fulfill at all. See Eliyah Rabbah, letter 10; that is his intent. If so, one can say that one hired to live that way is similar. Nevertheless one can say that this applies only as a leniency, namely where his normal way is so. But someone delicate, whose way is not to sleep this way, may not fulfill if he made it so small from the beginning. Perhaps the same applies if a new issue arose afterward, that he would not fulfill. But if he can sleep there with his head and most of his body inside, he fulfills both for eating and sleeping. It is specifically when his table is inside the house that he does not fulfill, because he may be drawn after it, as in siman 634, se'if 4; see there. See Terumas HaDeshen about this. What the Magen Avraham wrote in letter 9 is explained there in Terumas HaDeshen according to the opinion that a person is three cubits excluding his head, as Tosafos wrote in Eruvin. See Eliyah Rabbah, letter 10, and with Hashem's help it will be explained in Magen Avraham there.
