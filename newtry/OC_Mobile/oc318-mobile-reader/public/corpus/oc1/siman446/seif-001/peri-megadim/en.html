He should remove it. See the Taz. In the Tur he wrote that on Chol HaMoed he should remove it immediately, etc., and the Mechaber added, he should destroy it. There he wrote that removal is of no effect at all, for even in its place he must destroy it by burning; see there. What the Beis Yosef wrote according to Rashi's explanation, etc., means that Rabbi Yehuda and the Sages disagree after the sixth hour: according to Rabbi Yehuda, burning, and according to the Sages, crumbling, specifically destruction from the world, similar to nosar; but every law, etc. However, at the time of its destruction, after the sixth hour, by any means, even removing it from his domain is sufficient. See siman 440 in Taz, s.k. 4, that some say wherever it is not actually in his domain, he does not transgress; see there. If so, there is not such a great wonder here, for one can say this. As for the case of Rabbah and Rav Yosef, folio 28a, chametz to other rivers, etc., it implies that in any event we are concerned lest someone find it and take it; this can be answered somewhat. However, as practical law we hold that after it becomes forbidden, if he did not nullify it, when he removes it to the public domain he transgresses bal yera'eh. See Darchei Moshe here. And if he nullified it, even so it requires crumbling rabbinically, lest a person find it and eat it, as in siman 445.
