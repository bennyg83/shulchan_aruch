In his house. The Rivash wrote in siman 401: if one found chametz in the public domain, it is forbidden for him to lift it, for from the moment he lifts it he acquires it and transgresses bal yera'eh. Even if he does not intend to acquire it, but only lifts it in order to remove it from a place where many people pass so that they should not stumble over it, it is also forbidden; see there for his reason. It is not comparable to what we permit above in siman 308 se'if 18, to remove a thorn on Shabbos in a karmelis because of public harm, for a thorn is something unseen, unlike here, where harm is not common as it is there; see there. The halachic decisors copied his words.
For he cannot move it. See Mishnah Berurah. Even chametz prohibited only rabbinically is also forbidden to move, and so wrote the later authorities. If one is moving a cooked dish and bringing it to a halachic authority to ask about some chametz question, and the authority rules that it is forbidden, the later authorities discuss whether he may bring it home in order to cover it with a utensil, or whether he must immediately throw it from his hand; see Chok Yaakov, Eliyah Rabbah, and Beis Meir. It appears that even if we say it is permitted for him to carry it back, since it is already in his hand and initially he moved it permissibly and with authorization, like the case above in siman 266 and siman 308 se'if 3, nevertheless he should not bring it home, but to the privy. They permitted covering with a utensil only because it is impossible to move it, unlike here, where in any case he is moving it. So too it is implied from the end of Eliyah Rabbah's words.
