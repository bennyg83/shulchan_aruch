And he should destroy it immediately. He recites the blessing over destroying chametz; so wrote the Magen Avraham, and so he wrote above at the end of siman 435. Although according to Torah law he has already fulfilled his obligation through nullification, nevertheless he must recite a blessing, as with other rabbinic mitzvos. Even if it became chametz during Pesach, he should recite a blessing. It is possible that even over a mixture involving a mashehu, which is also rabbinic, one should recite a blessing over destroying it. However, the Magen Avraham wrote that one should not recite a blessing over a mixture involving a mashehu; see what I wrote above in siman 435, s.k. 5.
And if it is Yom Tov. At first glance it implies that specifically on Yom Tov it is forbidden to burn it, but on the eve of Yom Tov it is permitted to light the fire with it close to dark, and it continues burning on Yom Tov. This is not comparable to above in siman 255, where it is forbidden to light until most of the fire has taken hold, for there he lights it in order to benefit from it, to warm himself and the like, and therefore we are concerned and decree lest he stoke the coals; unlike here. However, in Responsa Rashba, siman 71, he wrote that according to the one who forbids burning chametz on Yom Tov, it is also forbidden to light the fire with it from the eve of Yom Tov so that it continues burning on Yom Tov, as is found in the Yerushalmi regarding oil of terumah that became tamei and a fire of consecrated items. He should cover it with a utensil. The Tur's view is that even if he did not nullify it, it is also forbidden to destroy it; so too it appears from the implication of the Rambam in chapter 3 of Hilchos Chametz. We do not say that since kindling was permitted for a need, it was also permitted not for a need. Many reasons for this have been explained; see Kesef Mishneh there, Maggid Mishneh, and Responsa Maharam of Lublin, siman 20. They did not permit even moving it, such as throwing it into the sea or scattering it to the wind. In truth, however, the view of the other great halachic decisors is explained as follows: specifically where there was nullification, so that he does not transgress bal yera'eh, covering with a utensil is enough; but if he did not nullify it, the prohibition of bal yera'eh comes and at least overrides rabbinic moving, and even burning is permitted, for we also say that since kindling was permitted for a need, it was permitted not for a need, as the Ran, Rabbeinu Yechiel in the Tur, Rashi, Hagahos Maimoniyos, the Rivash in responsum siman 401, Semak, and Agudah in the first chapter of Pesachim wrote. The Kol Bo concluded: and it speaks where he nullified before its time; the Rambam, of blessed memory, established it where he did not nullify, and not all agree with him. End quote. So too it is somewhat implied in the sugya of the Talmud, Pesachim, first chapter, folio 5b, regarding derive from this that we do not say, and the sugya of Tosafos there, unlike the Kesef Mishneh's explanation according to the Rambam; see there. So too, at first glance, one can infer from Rav's own words, for he said one should cover it with a utensil. If it were so that he speaks even where he did not nullify it, then when the Talmud says there that Rabbi Yehuda said in the name of Rav, one who checks must nullify, what is the reason? A decree lest he find a fine cake and his mind be upon it, etc.; why press so much in explaining Rav's reason? It could have said simply that Rav's reason follows his teaching, lest he find it on Yom Tov, when he cannot burn it, and then there would be no need for the forced reason of Rashi and Tosafos; see there. Rather, certainly even on Yom Tov it is permitted to burn it if he did not nullify it. So wrote the Bach. But the consensus of most later authorities follows the implication of the ruling in Shulchan Aruch, that he has no remedy at all except covering with a utensil. So my teacher and father-in-law, the Gaon, of blessed memory, practiced. They did not permit even telling a non-Jew to throw it into the sea, and so concludes the Magen Avraham. Nevertheless, where the permissive practice is followed, it stands, and they have on whom to rely, as I wrote that this is the view of great halachic decisors.
For he cannot move them. It is proper for every halachic authority to be careful to designate a room or closed chest, and similarly it is written in Responsa Mahariv, siman 193, regarding challah; see there. Then, when some item becomes chametz and they ask him about it, he can throw it there immediately and not move it further. In truth, I have seen the custom that after he declares it chametz for them, they move it from the house of the person who asked to the house of the one who asked the authority, and this is a complete prohibition. Perhaps for this reason they are not careful about the prohibition of moving it: since most rulings are only a mere stringency, therefore they are accustomed to be lenient and move it, since according to the law it is permitted for eating or benefit with a prohibition of mashehu according to some halachic decisors, and therefore they move that chametz. Nevertheless, this is not correct, for if so they should also move it in order to destroy it immediately by throwing it into the sea. In the Bach too it implies the opposite reasoning, for he wrote as follows: a cooked dish in which a wheat kernel was found, even on the second day of Yom Tov, should be kept until Chol HaMoed, since it is not actual chametz.
