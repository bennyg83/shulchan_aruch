(א) Take out burn immediately. Question muktzeh — wants burn Sages forbade — not muktzeh (Pri Megadim E.A. 2).

(ב) That he cannot move it — it is muktzeh since forbidden in benefit (Tosafot first chapter of Ketubot) and even though it was not in the category of nullification, e.g. it leavened on Yom Tov and he violates bal yera'eh d'oraisa — it is muktzeh rabbinically since sit and do not act applies; and since his intent was to burn it but the Sages forbade him, he does not violate — it is coercion; and Rambam wrote they did not permit moving in order to burn because at the time he moves it he has not yet fulfilled the positive commandment of teshibutu'se-or until burn time (as Magen Avraham chapter 3). Maharam of Lublin siman 20 and likewise Ran regarding shofar: we learn at end of Rosh HaShanah we do not roll away the mound — why? shofar is aseh and Yom Tov lav and aseh, and aseh does not override lav and aseh; and since d'oraisa it does not override, likewise rabbinic muktzeh where they strengthened their words like Torah law (end of his words). Shelah questioned there: why say Yom Tov is aseh and lav — even lav alone does not override since at uproaching time he does not fulfill the aseh; R' Tam answered aseh of the public overrides lav even though at uproaching time he does not fulfill the aseh (end of his words). It appears chametz is not called aseh of the public — only shofar that the individual brings and the public hears is aseh of the public; otherwise chametz too would be aseh and lav and override Yom Tov's aseh-lav; however one could say as Rashi and Rif Bava Metzia chapter 2: for overriding they do not combine lavs (see there). And Shelah wrote that removing chametz through a gentile comes aseh rabbinic of teshibutu'se-or and overrides rabbinic lav of telling a gentile, although regarding shofar telling a gentile is not permitted — it is different there since at uproaching time he does not fulfill the aseh, unlike here (end of his words) — requires study, for likewise even when giving to a gentile he does not fulfill the aseh until the gentile destroys it from the world, as it discusses after prohibition time; and I proved above regarding shofar the reason is not uproaching time, etc.; and per Shelah even through Israel one may cast to sea since there is only rabbinic prohibition — therefore it appears cover with a vessel as gemara and poskim imply; the reason appears as Tosafot page 29: one who delays his chametz intending to burn does not violate in that delay, etc., because bal yera'eh was attached to the aseh and therefore he does not violate when he burns at the end (Pesachim 95 likewise). It appears permitted to cast to sea through a gentile as shevus-shevus permitted in place of mitzva (simanim 521:3, 494:4, 499:4, 587:21, 517). But winnowing to wind may be d'oraisa melacha as siman 519; nevertheless all poskim wrote to cover with a vessel, and Maharam of Lublin's custom likewise; perhaps they fear the gentile will not crumble properly and the essence of biur is burning; also the gentile may say he does sorcery as siman 533:7. Bach ruled full chametz like flour on which rain fell must be burned on Yom Tov since not in nullification; so practiced; a dish with grain — wait until dark; if a wheat grain found in a dish on seventh day of Pesach burn immediately, for if he waits until last day it becomes permitted and the prohibition returns (end of his words). See siman 467 what he wrote to burn full chametz on Yom Tov — dispute among poskim, see Tur; where custom, custom; where no custom, sit and do not act is preferable; also what he wrote on seventh day burn — distant reasoning that one would make a prohibition and burn on Yom Tov lest it return to permitted; rather another reason: as I wrote in the name of Tosafot, one who delays to burn does not violate, but here he delays to preserve and will not burn afterward — therefore burn on seventh through a gentile; and as Mishnah Berurah siman 467 and if found on eighth permitted to delay implies on seventh forbidden to delay.
