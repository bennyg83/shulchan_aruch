He should remove it and destroy it, etc. In the Tur it is written only, he should remove it. The Beis Yosef explained in one explanation, according to Rashi's explanation, that after the time it becomes forbidden, its destruction may be by any means; the Tur understood that the same applies that removing it from his domain is sufficient. I say: Heaven forbid that we explain the Tur's words this way, for chametz, even when it is not in his domain, is considered as if it were in his domain, as we say in the Gemara in the first chapter: Rabbi Yishmael said, there are two things that are not in a person's domain, but Scripture made them as if they were in his domain, and they are chametz on Pesach and a pit in the public domain. If so, what benefit is there in removing it from his domain? Rather, he is obligated to destroy it from the world, whether according to Rabbi Yehuda or according to the Rabbis; they disagree only about how he destroys it. The explanation of the Tur's words is that he did not need to write that he should destroy it, for that is obvious; rather, he teaches us that as soon as he finds chametz in his dwelling place, he should immediately take it outside, so that it will not be before him in the place where he is even for a short delay until he burns it, lest he come to stumble by eating it. Therefore he wrote in any case that he should remove it from the place near him until he comes to destroy it. This appears simple to me.
And if it is Yom Tov, etc. In the Tur it is written: and the same law applies to chametz that remained in his house on the fourteenth that fell on Shabbos, after five hours.
Because he cannot move it. In the Tur it is written: it is not necessary to say that if he nullified it, so that there is no Torah prohibition in keeping it, it is obvious that he cannot move it; but even if he did not nullify it, so that there is a Torah prohibition, nevertheless he should not remove it by any means, since he cannot destroy it according to its law, namely by burning according to Rabbi Yehuda. End quote. According to this, for the Rabbis, who hold that its destruction is by any means, he should have been able to move it in order to destroy it by throwing it into water. So too it appears from Perek Kol Shaah, regarding the eve of Pesach that fell on Shabbos, where Zunin, the official of Rabban Gamliel, came and said: the time has arrived to destroy the chametz. If so, there is a difficulty for the Rambam and the Rosh, who ruled in siman 445 like the Rabbis: why did they require a utensil on Yom Tov to cover it, and not destroy it immediately? Therefore it appears to me that according to the Rambam the prohibition is only because of moving it, since it is not fit for him for benefit and is like ordinary muktzeh. As for what the Rema wrote, that even burning it in its place is forbidden, that is because we do not say that since kindling was permitted for a Yom Tov need, kindling not for a need was also permitted, unless there is at least some need of the day. This is unlike here, where there is no need of the day, since he can wait until evening and cover it with a utensil during the day. So it appears to me.
