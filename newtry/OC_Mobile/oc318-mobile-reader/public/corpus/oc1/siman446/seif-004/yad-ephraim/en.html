(s.k. 4) There — Rivash forbade, etc. — it is not applicable to say his intent was upon it, or 'since in truth,' etc. — thus must be emended: meaning since the chametz is not his, one cannot say from yesterday his intent was upon it — if so forbidden per those who hold nolad is forbidden on Shabbat; or meaning: even per those who hold nolad is permitted, nevertheless since Rivash in truth deals with forbidden in benefit, if so forbidden to move; but at hour four it is truly permitted and Rivish did not bring proof from tevel and milk but because it is proven that what is not fit is forbidden to move — all the more chametz of a gentile forbidden in benefit. More correct to emend: Rivash deals with chametz found after prohibition time — one cannot say his intent was upon it since it is in truth forbidden in benefit — meaning one cannot say he intended to cast to dogs since it is in truth forbidden in benefit; and further one may say: solely because forbidden in benefit there was no place to forbid moving, since it is still fit to give to that gentile himself whose chametz it is — if so even if that gentile is not here he may move since it is fit for him in such a case; therefore he said: since it is a gentile's chametz found now, one cannot say from yesterday he had intent, and it is nolad and forbidden — with this reason alone it does not suffice if it were now permitted in benefit, for even without yesterday's intent, since he had intent on all chametz to cast to dogs, this too is included and it is like casting to dogs; therefore Magen Avraham said: and since in truth it is forbidden in benefit, he had no intent from yesterday on this chametz and therefore forbidden. But it is not comfortable that Magen Avraham explains Rivash per those who hold nolad forbidden on Shabbat; see siman 499 s.k. 9 Magen Avraham who brought Tosafot Shabbat 129 and siman 510.
