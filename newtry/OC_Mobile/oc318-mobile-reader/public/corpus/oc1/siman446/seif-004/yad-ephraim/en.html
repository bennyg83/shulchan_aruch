There. And that which the Rivash forbade, etc.: it is not relevant to say that his mind was upon it, or since in truth, etc. This is how it must be emended. That is to say, since the chametz is not his, it is not relevant to say that his mind was upon it from yesterday; if so, it is forbidden according to the one who says nolad is forbidden on Shabbos. Or, since, etc., that is to say, even according to the one who says nolad is permitted, nevertheless, since the Rivash in truth speaks where it is forbidden for benefit, it is therefore forbidden to move it. But during the fourth hour it is truly permitted. The Rivash brought proof from tevel and forbidden fats only because it is proven that something unfit is forbidden to move; if so, all the more so a non-Jew's chametz, since it is forbidden for benefit. It seems more correct to emend that it should read: the Rivash speaks of chametz found after the time it became forbidden, where it is not relevant to say that his mind was upon it, since in truth it is forbidden for benefit. That is to say, it is not relevant here to say that his mind was to throw it to dogs, since in truth it is forbidden for benefit. One can further say that because of this reason alone, that it is forbidden for benefit, there would have been no basis to forbid moving it, for it is still fit to give to that same non-Jew whose chametz it is; if so, even if that non-Jew is not here, he could properly move it, since it is fit for him in that way. Therefore he said that since it is the chametz of a non-Jew that was found now, it is not relevant to say that from yesterday his mind was on this, and it is nolad and forbidden. Yet this reason alone would not suffice if it were now permitted for benefit; for then, although his mind was not on it from yesterday, since his mind was on all chametz that he would have to throw to dogs, this too would be included and it would be proper to throw it to dogs. Therefore the Magen Avraham said: and since in truth it is forbidden for benefit, if so, his mind was not on this chametz at all from yesterday, and therefore it is forbidden. However, it is not comfortable to me that the Magen Avraham would explain the words of the Rivash as speaking according to the one who says nolad is forbidden on Shabbos. See siman 499, s.k. 9, the words of the Magen Avraham, who brought the words of Tosafos in Shabbos, folio 29; and see siman 310.
