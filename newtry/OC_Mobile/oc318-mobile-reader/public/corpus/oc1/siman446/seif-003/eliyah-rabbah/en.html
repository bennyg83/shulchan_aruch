Of the second day of Yom Tov, etc. This is specifically to throw it into the river or sea, but not to burn it. Magen Avraham, Taz, and Olas Shabbos. And specifically where there was no nullification. Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah and Chok Yaakov. I wrote in Eliyah Zuta that my elder, the Gaon, of blessed memory, was stringent in every case, as on the first day; so too it implies in Responsa Maharam of Lublin and in the Bach there, and so it implies in Responsa Bach siman 124. So too it implies from the Shulchan Aruch and the halachic decisors, who wrote without qualification in siman 444 se'if 5: until after Yom Tov. Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah also discusses this at length and brings many halachic decisors who forbid it. Therefore it appears that the Shulchan Aruch and Levush wrote the permissive view as there is one who says, but the first view they wrote as, if it is on Chol HaMoed, etc., which implies that the second day of Yom Tov is like the first. The Bach wrote: a wheat kernel found in a cooked dish on the seventh day of Pesach should be thrown into the river without crumbling, for if he keeps it until the final day, it is permitted, and it is found that the prohibition returns to being permitted. End quote. I asked my elder, the Gaon, of blessed memory: is it not muktzeh? What difference does it make that it will return to being permitted, since its law is so in siman 447 se'if 11? He answered me that he too was not accustomed to rule like him in this matter. Later the Magen Avraham was printed, and I saw that he too objected to the Bach and wrote another reason: one who keeps chametz in order to destroy it does not transgress, but on the seventh day, when he is not doing so in order to destroy it, he transgresses it; therefore he ruled that he should destroy it on the seventh day through a non-Jew. So too it implies in Responsa Bach there. This is not clear, for with a mashehu he certainly does not transgress; rather, the practice is to burn it so as not to come to mishap, and since it is muktzeh, one should not do so. With this the reason written in Chok Yaakov is also rejected, that he wants the preservation of a prohibition, for this is not so with something that absorbed a mashehu of prohibition. Furthermore, he is doing nothing, and it is not comparable to the end of siman 450, where he performs guarding or selling, which is an action. I later found in the abridged rulings of the Rosh on parchment: if it is during the final days of Pesach, it is permitted to keep it.
