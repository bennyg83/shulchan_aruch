(א) Per Taz — from Rambam chapter 5 law 8 it implies it refers to what precedes it 'without nullification,' and Ran too implies in all cases even without nullification cover with a vessel — and he violates bal yera'eh; rather the reason is even without nullification they established their word, all the more Magen Avraham 2. And what Mishnah Berurah wrote to burn in place is obvious forbidden; rather it teaches that burning in place is a lav of burning from within, and here there is somewhat need of the day as Rava Pesachim 5b proves we do not say from permitted — therefore for us it is permitted; bal yera'eh was attached to a positive commandment and when his intent is to burn there is no Torah prohibition as Tosafot Pesachim 29b s.v. Rava Ashi and Magen Avraham 2; and Rava holds it was not attached to a positive commandment; the Tosefta Magen Avraham brought chapter 1 law 3 implies there is dispute; in Hagahot Gra we wrote on this that it depends on the dispute whether a lav preceded by a positive commandment, etc.; but from Tosafot Ketubot 3a s.v. from within it implies even where there is somewhat need of the day rabbinically we say from within; therefore he said on day one they were not concerned lest he come to eat and covering suffices; see Rashi Pesachim 6a (see there). Magen Avraham letter 3 and Eliyah Rabba there will be explained.

(ב) (s.k. 15) Forbidden move — reed 511:8.

(ג) Initially it was difficult for me what Rashba wrote in responsum siman 71: one who finds chametz on Yom Tov without nullification may burn it; and even from Rashi (5b) that if he stopped it in a vessel he may feed dogs — implies burning is forbidden, and let us say it is permitted; and I found in Yerushalmi Bava Metzia: one who derives from notar — forbidden; one who does not derive — permitted (end of his words). Difficult: R' Akiva has no from within — if so Rashi explained well 'feed dogs'; and Rashi 6a on bal yera'eh: if he nullified it implies he did not nullify it from burning on Yom Tov — likewise in Hagahot Beit Yosef. It is resolved: Rashba explains Rava that R' Akiva has no from within as Temim De'im page 25a beginning: R' Akiva has no from within in chametz alone because he derives from notar that one does not burn on Yom Tov (Rashi 5b derives from notar) — therefore Rashba: from Rashi 'feed dogs' means on the first day part is permitted in benefit — strained — and he did not explain whether if he does not burn it is permitted to burn; we say from within as Ketubot 7a concludes and implies R' Akiva agrees; per Rashi thus forbidden since there is no need of the day at all — cast to sea or to dogs; or per Pri Chadash part is permitted. What he wrote 6:8 without nullification — cast to sea or crumble — not at burn time; per him on seventh; and Rashba holds nevertheless there was somewhat need of the day or to move it — it was not at its time, Magen Avraham 2. 'Burn in place' he means; and what Beit Yosef wrote 'from Rashi' means take out and burn in sea, etc., but not that he must burn; Pri Megadim 1; see Pnei Yehoshua 5b commentary on Rosh's difficulty from Ramban; and implies regarding notar too there is a positive commandment every moment since a verse is needed that Yom Tov does not override; and Yerushalmi Bava Metzia he brought nearby.

(ד) Question: may one kindle a chametz bonfire from erev Yom Tov so it burns through Yom Tov? Answer: it appears permitted even on Yom Tov; what Yerushalmi Bava Metzia halacha 1 'what of kindling a chametz bonfire' concludes: one who derives from notar — forbidden — the explanation is as Rashba responsum 71: even from erev Yom Tov forbidden; if so we are in doubt whether like R' Yehuda or Chachamim and we follow stringency; and Avnei Nezer and Chayei Adam Even HaEzer 28 — nevertheless in our Talmud Shabbat 23 and not burning oil lest one tilt, or Yom Tov in a vessel — likewise Rambam in Mishnah commentary: this is ordinary Shabbat permitted — he disagrees with Yerushalmi here and halacha follows our Talmud; Pri Chadash 2; and what seems permitted will be explained in Magen Avraham and Eliyah Rabba there.
