He pushes it with a reed. It is forbidden to touch it with one's hand, lest he eat from it, since the chametz is not his and he is not destroying it. This is unlike chametz that he wants to destroy, which he is permitted to touch. In Responsa Rivash, siman 401, he wrote that if chametz is found in the public domain, it is obvious that he should not move it, for by lifting it he would be found acquiring it, and it would become his chametz, and he would transgress bal yera'eh and bal yimatzei. Even in order that the public not be harmed by it, he should not move it; see his words at length. According to this, even on Chol HaMoed he should not move it, but should burn it in its place. Yet perhaps, since he moves it in order to burn it immediately, it is proper. See Tosafos in Pesachim, folio 29.
For it is forbidden to move it. Even by pushing with a reed, as explained above in siman 311 se'if 8.
On Yom Tov and Shabbos. For this reason, when the eve of Pesach falls on Shabbos, what remains after five hours is covered with a utensil; but during the fifth hour, when it is permitted for benefit and one may feed it to a wild animal, domestic animal, or bird, it is also permitted to move it. So is the implication of the Tur, and so conclude the Bach and the Magen Avraham.
