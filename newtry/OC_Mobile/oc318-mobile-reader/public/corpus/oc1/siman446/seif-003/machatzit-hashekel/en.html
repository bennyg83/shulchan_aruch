And in Tosafos, first chapter of Kesubos, this does not imply so, for they wrote on folio 7a, s.v. since, etc. Their language is as follows: one who finds chametz in his house, etc., covers it with a utensil, and we do not permit burning it because of the principle of since, etc.; this is because of muktzeh, for it is forbidden to move it. But one cannot say that it speaks where he nullified it, for nevertheless there is a rabbinic mitzvah to burn it, and it would be a need of the day, etc. End quote. One must say that Tosafos hold that burning kodshim is different, because it is a Scriptural decree, as stated in Shabbos.
And Maharam of Lublin wrote the reason, etc. According to the words of Tosafos above, who forbade it because of muktzeh, in any case it should have been proper to burn it in its place.
See siman 502. That is to say, there in se'if 3 he wrote that one may not support a pot or a door with a piece of wood, for we hold that wood, even on Yom Tov, is muktzeh and was given for use only for the need of kindling. Since one is forbidden to benefit from chametz at the time of burning, the kindling and burning of chametz is therefore comparable to supporting a pot, and it is forbidden to move the wood for its sake on Yom Tov. There is no need for the answer of Maharam of Lublin.
However, in Tosafos, Pesachim folio 5, etc. This refers to what Rabbi Akiva said regarding the verse, only on the first day you shall destroy it. Perforce he means on the eve of Yom Tov, for if it meant on Yom Tov, Scripture already states that you shall not do any labor, and we find that kindling is a primary labor. Rava said: derive from Rabbi Akiva that the destruction of chametz is only by burning, and derive from this that we do not say the principle of since it was permitted, etc. Tosafos wrote as follows: if we say the principle of since, it is permitted, although we require some need of the day; this is Tosafos following their view, for they hold that according to Torah law one requires some need, as they wrote in Beitzah folio 12. Here, what he destroys is considered a need. End quote. If so, according to the Kesef Mishneh, who holds that destruction is not considered even some need, the above difficulty of Tosafos remains.
And it appears to me, etc. This means on Yom Tov. That is to say, since according to Torah law its fixed time is on Yom Tov, it is therefore called a need; unlike according to the truth, that its fixed time is from the eve of Yom Tov. If he did not destroy it on the eve of Yom Tov, the destruction on Yom Tov is not a Yom Tov need.
See Responsa Rashba, siman 71. The responsum of the Rashba is not in my possession, but according to what I saw in Pri Chadash, who cited it, he wrote in the name of the Yerushalmi: what is the law of lighting a fire in a pile of chametz? The one who derives it from nosar forbids it, and the one who does not derive it from nosar permits it.
And the Rashba understood that it speaks of whether to burn the chametz on Yom Tov. To this he answered that according to Rabbi Yehuda, who holds that the destruction of chametz is only by burning and derives it from nosar, as stated on folio 27, its law is therefore like nosar, namely disqualified kodshim, which we do not burn on Yom Tov. But according to the Rabbis, who hold that its destruction is by any means and we do not derive it from nosar, it is permitted to burn it on Yom Tov. According to this, for us, who rule like the Rabbis, as written at the beginning of siman 445, it is permitted to burn it on Yom Tov. But the Pri Chadash disagreed: the intent of the Yerushalmi is to light the fire in a pile of chametz on the eve of Yom Tov so that it will burn on Yom Tov. About this it says that the one who derives from nosar forbids it, for in such a case nosar too is forbidden; see there for the reason. But the one who does not derive from nosar permits it in such a case. However, regarding burning it on Yom Tov itself, the Yerushalmi also agrees that even according to the Rabbis it is forbidden; see there. See Chok Yaakov at the beginning of the siman.
The Beis Yosef wrote in the name of the Rivash, etc. A thing that is forbidden for eating, etc.; see Magen Avraham at the beginning of siman 515.
Therefore it appears to me that specifically tevel fruits, etc. That his mind is not, etc. For with anything that can possibly be fit for a person, his mind is not to give it to dogs; therefore, as long as it is not fit for a person, it is forbidden to move, because it is not fit for anything. But with chametz, since he knew from yesterday that at this time, namely tomorrow in the fifth hour, it would not be fit for him, his mind was on it from yesterday for dogs. In Shabbos folio 143, Rava permitted throwing the innards of a goose to dogs on Yom Tov, since he knew from yesterday that they would spoil and would not be fit for a person, and therefore his mind was on them for dogs. As for the Rivash forbidding it, he speaks of a non-Jew's chametz, as explained in that responsum and as written in the Beis Yosef; see there.
And the Rambam. For it implies that he speaks of a Jew's chametz, since he wrote his words without qualification.
He speaks where there is none, etc. For in such a case the fact that it is fit for dogs does not help, as the Magen Avraham wrote above in siman 308, s.k. 54.
And the Chok Yaakov wrote as follows. It is proper for every halachic authority to be careful to designate a room or box, so that when an item becomes chametz for the one asking, he may throw it there immediately and not move it; and in truth I have seen people move it, etc., and it is a complete prohibition. A similar case is found in Shabbos folio 156b: when a treifah would be brought before Levi on Yom Tov, he would not examine it except when he sat by the refuse heap, for he said: perhaps it will not be found kosher, and it will not be fit even for dogs. Perhaps for this reason they are not careful, etc.; nevertheless, this is not correct, etc. End quote. See Sefer Eliyah Rabbah.
