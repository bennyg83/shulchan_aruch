And there is one who says that the second day of Yom Tov. So wrote the Beis Yosef in the name of Orchos Chaim. In the Taz he wrote that this is also the view of the Rambam in chapter 3, who wrote: and he covers it with a utensil until after the first day of Yom Tov and destroys it, which implies that it is permitted to destroy it on the second day of Yom Tov. In truth, his words are not compelling, for one can say that what he wrote, after the first day of Yom Tov, comes to exclude after the final day of Pesach, meaning that one should not say it is forbidden to burn it on Chol HaMoed; and so it is heard from this view, the Tur and the other halachic decisors. So too the implication of the Tur is that there is no distinction between the first and second day of Yom Tov, for he wrote as follows: one who finds chametz in his house, if it is on Chol HaMoed, should remove it immediately. This implies specifically on Chol HaMoed, unlike on the second day of Yom Tov, when he should cover it with a utensil. So wrote Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah. This also appears to me to be the Tur's intent in what he wrote: and if it is Yom Tov, some say that he should cover it with a utensil until night, when he removes it. End quote. The Beis Yosef wondered why the Tur wrote in the language of some say, for the law is explicit in the Gemara; see the Beis Yosef, who presses to answer and inserts a dispute into the Tur's view, which the Tur's wording cannot bear at all, for afterward the Tur explicitly states this dispute, and why would he need both? According to what I wrote, however, it is understandable: he used the language some say because of the second day of Yom Tov; the some say holds that he waits only until night and may burn it on Yom Tov, like the view of Orchos Chaim, excluding the Tur's own view, which he first wrote without qualification to forbid. This too may be the view of the Shulchan Aruch, which first states the law without qualification like the Tur's language, and afterward writes in the name of there is one who says, in the singular. So too my brother-in-law the Rav wrote in Sefer Eliyah Zuta, that so practiced my teacher and father-in-law, the Gaon, of blessed memory. This is also reasonable from the plain wording of the Talmud and the other halachic decisors, who used the term Yom Tov without qualification, with everything included, for there is no difference between the first and second day of Yom Tov, as below in siman 496. However, where there was no nullification, or where it became chametz during Pesach and was not included in the nullification, in which case, according to many halachic decisors, he destroys it even on the first day of Yom Tov, as I wrote above in s.k. 3, at least one may rely on them on the second day of Yom Tov, by moving it to the sea, or to the wind, or to the privy. If possible he should do this through a non-Jew. It is possible that Orchos Chaim himself speaks only where he did not nullify it, and so wrote Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah.
Of the second day of Yom Tov. The Bach wrote in this siman and in his responsa at the end of siman 124: if a chametz mixture is found on the seventh day of Pesach, he should destroy it immediately, for if he keeps it until the final day begins, it will be permitted to keep it, and the prohibition will be found returning to permission. End quote. My brother-in-law the Rav, in Sefer Eliyah Zuta, and the Magen Avraham reject the Bach's reason; see the Magen Avraham, who wrote another reason. To me the reason for the matter appears simple: since it will return to being permitted, as explained in siman 467, it is like one who wants the preservation of a prohibition on the seventh day, which is forbidden regarding chametz, as explained below at the end of siman 450. Therefore they should destroy it by moving it to scatter it to the wind or to the sea, and it is good to do so through a non-Jew if possible. So it appears to me.
