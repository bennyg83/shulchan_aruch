This means that as soon as one finds chametz in one's residence, one should take it outside so that it is no longer before one, without leaving it there even briefly until it can be burned, lest one stumble and eat it. Taz, subsection 1; Chemed Moshe, subsection 1. If wheat is found to contain kernels that became chametz, one should sort out everything that can be sorted and burn the sprouted kernels. The remainder may be retained. Whatever cannot be sorted is considered unidentifiable and is nullified in a majority as a dry substance mixed with another dry substance; it may be retained where a substantial loss is involved. Responsa of Rabbi Chaim Kohen, Orach Chaim, siman 9; Pis'chei Olam, subsection 2.
