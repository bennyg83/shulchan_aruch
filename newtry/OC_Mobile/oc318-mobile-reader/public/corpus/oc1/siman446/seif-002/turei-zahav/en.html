Of the second day of Yom Tov. The Beis Yosef wrote regarding this: but the second day, which is rabbinic, it is permitted to move it and throw it away or burn it. End quote. This is astonishing to me: since moving and throwing are possible, why should we permit burning on the second day of Yom Tov, and why should we treat the second day of Yom Tov lightly? The Rambam in chapter 3 wrote: if chametz remained on the fourteenth that fell on Shabbos, after four hours, he nullifies it and covers it with a utensil until after the first day of Yom Tov, and then destroys it. It appears that he comes to permit destroying it on the second day of Yom Tov. The Mordechai too wrote that it is said in the name of Ri that a wheat kernel found in a chicken requires covering with a utensil in order to burn it on Chol HaMoed. End quote. This shows that on the second day of Yom Tov it is forbidden to burn it. In my humble opinion, this means that on the second day of Yom Tov he should destroy it by throwing it into the sea, according to the Rabbis above, for the Rambam ruled like them. Even according to Rabbi Yehuda, whose law is that it requires burning, nevertheless, since according to Rashi after six hours its destruction is by any means, we may properly rely on this to be lenient on the second day of Yom Tov and permit only the prohibition of moving it, but not to burn it, which is a primary category of labor. The Mordechai holds like Rabbeinu Tam.
