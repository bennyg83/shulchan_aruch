(ט) And further, in my view, even through an Israelite, etc. — it is not that Shelah permitted only through a gentile as shevus-shevus, unlike through an Israelite which is a single shevus. That is, per the reason Shelah gave that it comes, etc., and overrides a rabbinic lav, it applies even to a single shevus. And further, therefore what Shelah wrote: and although regarding shofar there is no telling, etc. — meaning per Tosafot and as Shelah wrote to reconcile: 'That regarding shofar on Rosh HaShanah on Shabbat shofar does not override even shevus-shevus, as Tur Orach Chayim siman 586 in the name of Tosafot: it is different there at uprooting time,' etc. — but we do not rule thus in siman 586 end, and shevus-shevus is permitted in place of a mitzva like shofar (see there); rather Shelah's intent was to permit chametz for all, even per Tosafot who are stringent on shofar — and per Tosafot there is no distinction between one shevus and shevus-shevus; if so, one should permit even through an Israelite although there is only one shevus. Still one could say perhaps Shelah permitted only through a gentile because in his view at uproaching time what the gentile removes counts — unlike an Israelite who is not at uproaching time, etc. — meaning certainly Shelah was not unaware that through gentile removal he has not yet fulfilled biur until the gentile casts to sea, etc.; nevertheless he held it is at uproaching time because removal is the start of biur — unlike rolling away the mound for shofar-blowing where rolling is not the start of the mitzva of blowing itself. But Magen Avraham disputes this reasoning above: one is not called fulfilling the aseh until burn time, but removal is not part of biur at all; here too it is not at uproaching time like shofar. Therefore he questioned Shelah well: if removal counts as start of biur, the same should permit through Israel casting to sea as start of biur at uproaching time.

(מ) And it appears to me another reason, etc. — but here he delays it in order to preserve it; and in responsum Chavot Yair siman 86 part 7: what of it, since there is only a slight mixture and no d'oraisa bal yera'eh prohibition — meaning in any case there is rabbinic bal yera'eh since he delays to preserve it; and on this the responsum distinguishes: a slight mixture — we rule even to preserve until after Pesach it is forbidden due to concern for mishap lest one come to eat it; but bal yera'eh even rabbinically does not exist, so what is wrong with delaying to preserve. For mishap one need not be concerned here since he covers with a vessel as one who finds on first Yom Tov. And I bring proof there is no bal yera'eh even rabbinically: one, from Rashi's reason they were stringent on chametz even in any amount because of karet and people are not careful — that is only for eating; for bal yera'eh where there is no karet they need not be stringent in any amount; and per Rambam it is not nullified because it is davar sheyeish lo matirin — therefore one may delay it even rabbinically, otherwise he has no remedy except to violate bal yera'eh rabbinically. See Avodat Avodah; one may refine his proofs: one proof from Rashi — since delay without karet prohibition they did not forbid any amount in benefit, and there is no karet in benefit either (see Tosafot Pesachim 23b s.v. what is the difference) and no lashes either, yet in mixtures any amount they forbade even in benefit; therefore since chametz has karet they were stringent in any amount to give chametz the law of existing substance, and the same may be said for bal yera'eh. That they were lenient on moldy chametz which does not forbid any amount since there is no karet there, as Magen Avraham siman 467 wrote. Second proof from Rambam who calls it davar sheyeish lo matirin — one may question per Ran in chapter Kol Sha'ah on the reason of those who say davar sheyeish lo matirin, etc.: since we rule like R' Shimon that although R' Shimon forbids after Pesach rabbinically, he agrees through mixture it is permitted — this davar sheyeish lo matirin mixture, etc., and I sharpen this reason, etc. — and further it is close to say since the stringency of davar sheyeish lo matirin is rabbinic they were stringent only when the type itself will eventually become permitted, but here the chametz itself is forbidden although its mixture is permitted — perhaps they equated measures not to judge it as davar sheyeish lo matirin although those who hold so say: since d'oraisa for R' Shimon davar sheyeish lo matirin is when R' Shimon forbade as penalty it does not come to be lenient, etc., and it was not clear to him (end of his words) — if so those who hold thus and Rambam among them hold: since d'oraisa it is called davar sheyeish lo matirin, therefore even rabbinic bal yera'eh exists nevertheless it is davar sheyeish lo matirin since d'oraisa one may delay it; therefore the proof from Chavot Yair is rejected. Therefore this law requires study. See Magen Avraham siman 467 s.k. 43 and what he wrote there.
