Se'if katan 2: For he cannot, etc. For it is muktzeh, etc.; therefore even throwing it into the privy is forbidden, as the Magen Avraham wrote in siman 445, s.k. 2.
For granted, there is no prohibition in destroying it; nevertheless, moving it is forbidden. Since this is by way of sit and do not act, etc.; that is to say, the Sages have power to uproot a Torah matter through sit and do not act.
And since his desire was, etc. That is to say, nevertheless he does not transgress bal yera'eh. So too Tosafos wrote in Shabbos, folio 4a, s.v. before he comes, etc.; see there.
And Maharam, etc. And so wrote the Ran, etc. The wording is not exact, for Maharam and the Ran did not prophesy in one style. Maharam wrote as follows: the Sages upheld their words through sit and do not act, even to uproot a Torah matter, as we find regarding shofar-blowing on Rosh Hashanah that falls on Shabbos, etc. End quote; he elaborated there. But he did not give the reason that Yom Tov is a positive commandment and a negative commandment. From Rosh Hashanah that falls on Shabbos he brought proof only that the Sages have power, but there the reason is lest one carry, etc., whereas here there is another reason. The Ran wrote nothing regarding chametz, only in tractate Rosh Hashanah, chapter 4, folio 32, on that which we learned in the Mishnah: a shofar of Rosh Hashanah, one may not clear rubble from over it and one may not carry it beyond the techum. The Gemara concludes that the reason is that shofar is a positive commandment, and Yom Tov is a positive commandment and a negative commandment. About this the Ran wrote that although this is only a shevus, nevertheless they strengthened, etc. It appears to me that with chametz, etc.; for if not, chametz too has a positive commandment and a negative commandment, the positive commandment of tashbisu se'or and the negative commandment of bal yera'eh, etc. If so, the positive and negative commandments of chametz should override the positive and negative commandments of Yom Tov. Rather, perforce with chametz the reason is that at the time he uproots the prohibition he does not fulfill the positive commandment. According to this, perforce chametz is not considered a positive commandment of the public, for with a positive commandment of the public we do not require that it be at the time he uproots the prohibition, etc. See what I wrote below regarding the words of the Shelah.
As Rashi and the Rif wrote in chapter 2 of Bava Metzia. On folio 30, where the Sages taught: and you shall hide yourself, meaning there are times when you may hide yourself and need not return the lost object, such as a kohen where the lost object is in a cemetery, etc., or an elderly person for whom it is beneath his dignity to return the lost object. The Gemara concludes: for what case was the verse necessary? If you say for a kohen where it is in a cemetery, that is obvious: returning a lost object is a positive commandment, and the tumah of a kohen has a positive commandment and a negative commandment, and a positive commandment does not override, etc. Rashi wrote: although with returning a lost object there is a positive commandment and a negative commandment, nevertheless the negative commandment does not help to override a negative commandment; rather, only the positive commandment is what overrides it. End quote.
And in the Shelah, etc. That a rabbinic positive commandment of tashbisu comes, etc. That is to say, even if he already nullified it, nevertheless telling a non-Jew is permitted, since in any event he is obligated rabbinically to destroy it.
For regarding shofar, telling, etc., does not apply. To clear the rubble, etc., and the like.
And this requires examination, for here too, etc. For he speaks after its forbidden time. That is to say, it is impossible to give it as a gift to a non-Jew, for if so that would have been enough; since it is after its forbidden time, it is forbidden to give it as a gift, for it is forbidden for benefit. Perforce the intent is that the non-Jew remove it and destroy it. If so, with chametz too it is not at the time he uproots the prohibition, etc. There is a further difficulty from what I proved, etc.; that is to say, as the Shelah wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Tam, that with a positive commandment of the public we do not require that it be at the time he uproots the prohibition, etc. However, from this there is no proof, for the Ran himself wrote there about the Mishnah, where the Talmud gave the reason for shofar because Yom Tov is a positive commandment and a negative commandment, as follows: it stated the true reason; however, I do not need this, for even if Yom Tov were only a negative commandment, the preparations for shofar would not come and override it, because at the time he uproots the negative commandment he does not fulfill the positive commandment. End quote. Thus, perforce the Ran does not hold like Rabbeinu Tam, and even with a positive commandment of the public we specifically require that it be at the time he uproots the prohibition. Rather, the difficulty is from the words of the Talmud itself, for it gave the reason that Yom Tov is a positive commandment and a negative commandment, etc.; if so, even where it is at the time he uproots, etc., it nevertheless does not override the negative commandment of Yom Tov.
Furthermore, according to him, even through a Jew, etc. One cannot say that the Shelah permitted only through a non-Jew because it is a shevus of a shevus, unlike through a Jew, where it is one shevus. This is not so, for according to the reason the Shelah gave, that it comes, etc., and overrides a rabbinic negative commandment, this applies even to one shevus. Furthermore, perforce what the Shelah wrote, although regarding shofar telling, etc., does not apply, means according to Tosafos, as the Shelah wrote there explicitly, as follows: regarding shofar on Rosh Hashanah that falls on Shabbos, shofar does not override even a shevus of a shevus, as the Tur, Orach Chayim, at the end of siman 586, wrote in the name of Tosafos, because there it is different, since at the time, etc. End quote. But we do not rule this way at the end of siman 586, and a shevus of a shevus is permitted in a place of the mitzvah of shofar; see there. Rather, the Shelah's intent is to permit chametz according to everyone, even according to Tosafos, who are stringent regarding shofar. According to Tosafos there is no distinction between one shevus and a shevus of a shevus; if so, the same should be true that it is permitted even through a Jew, although there is only one shevus. Still, one might say that perhaps the Shelah permitted specifically through a non-Jew because in his view, when the non-Jew removes it, it is considered at the time he uproots the prohibition, unlike a Jew, where it is not at the time he uproots, etc. This is not so, for certainly it did not escape the Shelah that through the non-Jew's removal he has not yet fulfilled destruction, but only afterward, when the non-Jew throws it into the sea or the like. Nevertheless, he holds that it is considered at the time he uproots, etc., because the removal is the beginning of the destruction, unlike clearing the rubble for shofar-blowing, where one cannot say that the clearing is the beginning of the mitzvah of blowing. But the Magen Avraham disputes this reasoning above: it is not called fulfilling the positive commandment until the time of destruction, and removal is not part of the destruction at all. Here too it is not at the time, etc., just as with shofar. According to this, he properly challenged the Shelah here: according to the Shelah, who holds that in such a case it is at the time he uproots, etc., because removal is the beginning of destruction, the same should be true that through a Jew it should be permitted, for even with a Jew one can say that removing it in order to throw it into the sea is the beginning of destruction and is at that time, etc.
Therefore it appears to me, etc. And the reason appears to me, etc. That is to say, what he wrote that the Sages upheld their words in the face of the prohibition of bal yera'eh, etc., does not require the reasons above. Rather, as Tosafos wrote, in such a case there is no bal yera'eh. Although the Rambam disagrees and holds that it is not a negative commandment rectified by a positive commandment, as Mishneh LaMelech wrote in chapter 1, law 3, of Hilchos Chametz, and he wrote that the Rambam relied on the Tosefta; see there. If so, according to the words of Magen Avraham one must say that the Rambam in truth holds like the view that the law here speaks specifically where he nullified it, but if he did not nullify it, it is permitted to burn it on Yom Tov, unlike what the Maggid Mishneh wrote in chapter 3, law 5; see there.
But to scatter it to the wind, etc. As he wrote at the end of siman 319: one who spits into the wind on Shabbos and the wind scatters the spittle is liable for winnowing. End quote. But as for the fact that one must crumble it before scattering it, the Magen Avraham was not concerned, since in any event most chametz is bread and the like, and we hold that there is no grinding after grinding. Although Mishneh LaMelech, chapter 1, law 3, wrote as follows: it further appears that in such a case, where one must crumble it until it is fine in order to scatter it to the wind, everyone agrees that it is a derivative of grinding and he is liable to bring a chatas, as is proven from the words of Tosafos, Shabbos folio 114b. End quote. He was already challenged in Sefer Tosafos Shabbos above in siman 321: what proof is there from the words of Tosafos? They speak there of beets and vegetables, but regarding bread we have not heard this, for one can say that even if it is very fine, nevertheless there is no grinding after grinding; see there.
And the Bach ruled, etc. And at the end of siman 467, in Magen Avraham s.k. 43, he wrote that after Pesach, in such a case it is permitted even for eating.
And another reason appears to me, etc. But here he keeps it in order to preserve it. In Responsa Chacham Tzvi, siman 86, he disagreed with this: what of it, since there is only a mixture involving a mashehu, and there is no Torah prohibition of bal yera'eh? Perforce the intent is that in any case there is a rabbinic bal yera'eh prohibition, since he keeps it in order to preserve it. About this he disagreed in that responsum: granted, regarding a mixture involving a mashehu, we rule that even preserving it until after Pesach is forbidden, but that is because of concern for mishap, lest one come to eat it; but there is no bal yera'eh prohibition even rabbinically. If so, what is the problem with keeping it in order to preserve it? And for mishap there is no concern in such a case, since he covers it with a utensil, like one who found it on the first days of Yom Tov. He brought proof that there is no bal yera'eh even rabbinically. First, from Rashi's reason that they were stringent with chametz even by a mashehu because there is kares and people do not keep away from it; this is specifically regarding eating, but regarding bal yera'eh, where there is no kares, one should not be stringent by a mashehu. According to what the Rambam wrote, that it is not nullified because it is a davar sheyesh lo matirin, one must say that it is permitted to keep it even rabbinically; for if not, it has no permitting outcome unless he transgresses bal yera'eh rabbinically. This is the substance of his words. See Sefer Eliyah Rabbah. One may examine his proofs somewhat. Regarding the first proof from Rashi's words, that since keeping has no kares prohibition they were not stringent by a mashehu: benefit proves otherwise, for benefit also has no kares, and see Tosafos, Pesachim folio 23b, s.v. what is the difference; there is not even lashes, and nevertheless in a mixture involving a mashehu they forbade it even for benefit. Perforce one must say that since chametz has a law of kares, they were stringent by a mashehu to give it the law of actual chametz. The same can be said regarding bal yera'eh. As for their leniency regarding noksheh, which does not forbid by a mashehu since it has no kares, as written at the beginning of siman 447, that is because in that chametz there is no kares at all. His second proof from the Rambam's words, that he called it davar sheyesh lo matirin, can also be questioned based on the words of the Ran in Perek Kol Shaah regarding the reason of those who say it is because of davar sheyesh lo matirin. He wrote as follows: since we hold like Rabbi Shimon, although Rabbi Shimon forbade it after Pesach rabbinically, nevertheless he agrees that through a mixture it is permitted, and this mixture is therefore a davar sheyesh lo matirin, etc. I am uncertain about this reason, etc. Furthermore, it is close to say that since the stringency of davar sheyesh lo matirin is rabbinic, they were stringent only when the type itself is destined to become permitted; but here, where the chametz itself is forbidden, although its mixture is permitted, perhaps they made a uniform rule not to judge it as a davar sheyesh lo matirin. Although the proponents of this reasoning said that since according to Torah law, according to Rabbi Shimon, it is a davar sheyesh lo matirin, when Rabbi Shimon forbade it as a penalty he did not come to be lenient with it, etc., this was not clear to him. End quote. If so, those who hold this reasoning, and the Rambam among them, hold that since according to Torah law it is a davar sheyesh lo matirin, it is called a davar sheyesh lo matirin. Therefore the proof of Responsa Chacham Tzvi is also rejected, for even if you say that rabbinically there is bal yera'eh, nevertheless it is a davar sheyesh lo matirin, since according to Torah law it is permitted to keep it. Therefore this law requires examination. See Magen Avraham, siman 467, s.k. 43, and what I wrote there.
