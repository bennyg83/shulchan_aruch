Like Chol HaMoed. Nevertheless, it is proper not to burn it, but only to throw it into the sea or into the privy; if possible, he should do it through a non-Jew. Later authorities. Chok Yaakov wrote that this applies specifically where he did not nullify it, or where it became chametz during Pesach, for then, according to many halachic decisors, even on the first day of Yom Tov he destroys it, as I wrote in the previous s.k.; at least one may rely on them on the second day of Yom Tov, by moving it to the sea or through a non-Jew. But where he nullified it, no, for there is no difference between the first and second day of Yom Tov; see there. So wrote Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah. So too the Bach wrote that there is no distinction between the first and second day of Yom Tov if it is not actual chametz. If a chametz mixture is found on the seventh day of Pesach, they should destroy it by moving it to the sea or to the wind, or through a non-Jew; for if he keeps it until the final day of Pesach begins, it will be permitted to keep it, and the prohibition will revert to being permitted. Bach, Magen Avraham, and Chok Yaakov. See siman 467 se'if 10. The Gaon Rabbi Shimon of Prague did not rule like the Bach in this matter.
