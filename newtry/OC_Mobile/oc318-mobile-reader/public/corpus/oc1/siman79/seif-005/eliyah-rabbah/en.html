His law is like behind him. He wrote in Kesef Mishnah in the name of Rabbeinu Menachem: specifically when it is not possible to move it behind him; but when it is possible, it is considered like before him—until here. From the conclusion of all the poskim it does not seem so; also from the wording "as far as his eyes can see" it implies that specifically before him, where he sees it, we require so much. It seems the poskim learned from this, and likewise it seems in the commentary of the Mishnah to the Rambam; and even though below in siman 81 seif 2 we hold that specifically when it is certainly possible one goes to the sides—it seems that there refers specifically to when he stands and the feces is opposite him; but when he stands and the feces is at his sides, he need not distance four amot but only to the sides—so it seems to me. Magen Avraham wrote: and at the sides before him it is considered like before him, as in raising the hands siman 128 seif 28—until here. To me it seems it is not comparable to raising the hands, for there the reason that behind the priests is not included in the blessing is because they appear as though the blessing is not important in their eyes, as Tosafos Sotah 38a s.v. mechitzah wrote; therefore the sides are considered like before him, since the blessing is still important in his eyes, since he is not standing behind them. This is not so here, where the reason is because of "nothing indecent shall be seen by you"—it seems that whatever he does not see in his direct line of sight without intent is not considered before him. You know that the sides of the priests themselves are considered before him, whereas here it is considered like behind him—except as I explained.
