Moist donkey feces. It seems to me that these usually have a bad smell; therefore even if it happens once that one does not smell, it is forbidden, for it is like human feces.

If the smell does not reach him. It implies that even if they have a smell, as long as the smell does not reach him it is permitted. And it is a doubt—for in Beit Yosef he wrote that if they have a smell their law is like human feces; it implies one must distance four amot from where the smell ceased. And indeed in Tur he wrote above likewise: if it has a smell one distances until where the smell ceases. But Rabbeinu Yonah wrote that he did not specifically teach this; rather he means four amot from where the smell ceased—until here. And it seems to me that he specifically taught this, for since their law is not like feces one need not distance except to where the smell ceases; and likewise in Tur regarding chicken feces. And according to Rabbeinu Yonah's view, it must be that the case "if the smell does not reach him" means he need not check whether it has a bad smell, for usually they do not have a bad smell. And what he wrote—this is the main point according to my humble opinion.
