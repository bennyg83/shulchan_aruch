Soft donkey dung. It appears to me that these generally have a foul odor. Therefore, even if on one occasion they happen not to smell, they are forbidden, for they are treated like human excrement.
If the odor does not reach him. This implies that even if they have an odor, it is permitted so long as the odor does not reach him. This requires study, for in seif 4 the Mechaber writes that if they have an odor, their law is like that of human excrement, which implies that one must distance himself four amos from the place where the odor ceases. The Tur indeed writes above that if it has an odor, one must distance himself until the odor ceases. The Beit Yosef, however, writes that this is not exact, and that it means four amos from the place where the odor ceases. In my view, the Tur’s wording is exact. Since their law is not fully like excrement, one need distance himself only until the odor ceases. This is also implied by Rabbeinu Yonah regarding chicken droppings. According to the Beit Yosef, the meaning must be that if the odor does not reach him, he need not investigate whether they have a foul odor, since ordinarily they do not. What I have written appears primary.
