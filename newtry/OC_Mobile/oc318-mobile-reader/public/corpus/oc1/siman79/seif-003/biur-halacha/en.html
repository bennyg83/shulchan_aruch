That he should distance himself the required amount. See Mishnah Berurah, where I wrote in the name of the Beit Yosef. It is astonishing regarding the great ruler, the Beit Yosef, how this was so obvious to him, and he did not mention that according to Rashi's words in Eiruvin 92a, s.v. it is forbidden to sow, where he explains that the entire small area is considered like the opening of the large area, see there, automatically the same law would apply regarding excrement. If the excrement is in the large area and there are not four amos from the opening, it would automatically be forbidden throughout the small area, for the Gemara there places them in one category; see there. So too the Ritva there writes this explicitly. Tosafot there, 92b, s.v. she'ilmalei, although at first they wanted to reject Rashi's explanation, nevertheless in the end they proved that Rashi's explanation is primary. If so, we have three pillars for this law, yet he did not mention their view at all, neither here nor in Yoreh Deah, siman 296, seif 49, regarding kilayim, and it requires study. Afterward I found in the sefer Yevaser Tov that he also asked this question.
If no bad odor reaches him. See Mishnah Berurah, and see Peri Megadim, who explained the words of the Mechaber that the intent is that no bad odor reaches the large area at all. For if it did reach there, it would be forbidden even if the odor did not reach him, according to what the Levush ruled above, that in this case also one specifically requires four amos from the place where the odor ceases.
