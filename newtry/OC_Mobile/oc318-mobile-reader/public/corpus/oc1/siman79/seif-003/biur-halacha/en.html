(א) That he should distance per the required measure — see in the Mishna Berurah what he wrote in the name of the Beit Yosef; and it is astonishing regarding the great master the Beit Yosef how this is so plain to him and he did not mention that per Rashi's words in Eruvin 92a1, s.v. "it is forbidden to sow," who explained that the entire small [courtyard] is considered the opening place of the large one — see there; automatically the law will be the same regarding feces, that if the feces is in the large one and one is not distant four amot from the opening, automatically it is forbidden in the entire small one, for in one enclosure they lower them in the Gemara — see there; and so too the Ritva there explicitly; and Tosafos there 92b, s.v. "that if," etc. — although at first they wanted to reject Rashi's explanation, nevertheless in the end they were compelled that Rashi's explanation is primary. If so, we have three pillars for this law, and he did not mention their method at all, neither here nor in siman 296, seif 69, regarding kilayim — it is difficult. Afterward I found in Sefer Yevaser Tov that he also raised this difficulty.

If the bad smell does not reach him — see in the Mishna Berurah; and see in the Peri Megadim, who explained the words of the author: the intent is that the bad smell does not reach at all in the large [courtyard]; for if it would reach, it would be forbidden even if the smell did not reach him — according to what the Levush ruled above, that in this too we require specifically four amot from where the smell ceases.
