(s.k. 1) Even, etc.—end of siman 75; that is to say, regarding ervah in such a case it is a dispute of Rabbeinu Yehuda with the Bach—for Rabbeinu Yehuda it is permitted, but regarding feces even Rabbeinu Yehuda concedes. (s.k. 2) At his side, etc.—regarding a crooked lulav below end siman 645: crooked before him is invalid and behind him valid; and he wrote there that if it bent to the sides it is invalid like before him. And the Rambam, etc., also wrote that the sides have the law like behind him—and end siman 81. This too is the proof of R' Menachem (except there is a printing error there) who wrote in siman 81: he was reading and saw feces opposite him—he goes so the feces remains behind him four amot; and if impossible to go before him, he moves it to the sides and distances four amot—behold, where it is possible for feces to be behind him, that is preferable, and it does not help that he moves it to the sides. The sides before him—that is to say, not his face directly opposite, but the sides before him—as written siman 128, etc., that they say there regarding raising the hands that behind the priests is not included in the blessing, but those standing before the priests—but drawn to the sides before the priests are included in the blessing, as though standing directly opposite the faces of the priests—except that therefore, since this compares the shaliach tzibur to what is written in siman 128, if so what we rule there that the sides of the priest himself are also considered before him—if so, why did he write in the name of R' Menachem that if possible it is preferable to place it behind him four amot, but without possibility the sides have the law like behind him? (s.k. 4) Voice, etc.—intent of the listener, etc., and it is like answering amen; and here, since feces interrupts, he cannot be a listener like one answering amen, for it is forbidden to answer. (s.k. 5) In a place, etc., four handbreadths wide by four handbreadths—and the Taz disagrees and holds even if it is not four handbreadths wide. (s.k. 8) When he does not, etc.—it is permitted in this according to everyone, for when closing his eyes the prohibition is only because of "and your camp shall be holy"; and here, since a partition interrupts, it is called "and your camp shall be holy," and the reason of those who forbid is because he sees it; and since he closes his eyes, and likewise one who does not see it, it is permitted. (s.k. 9) And some say, etc.—in doubt, etc.; however one may say it is different here, etc., and as written in seif 9—that is to say, we rule a bad smell without substance, namely flatulence, is forbidden to read until the smell ceases; if so, it would be difficult for one who says a partition interrupts even regarding smell—and is it worse than a smell without substance? Therefore one must say: specifically if the feces is in another room—even though the smell comes to this room—nevertheless the partition interrupts, since the feces from which the smell came was never in this room, and we judge the smell following the feces in the other room; unlike a smell without substance, namely flatulence—that is, the person from whom flatulence came was initially, when flatulence left him, also in this room; it is worse and forbidden even though now it is as though the feces passed—for now, after flatulence left, the person is not called feces; and this deals even when flatulence came from another. And he wrote in seif 9: stam implies even if another left the room but the smell of flatulence remained—it is nevertheless forbidden to read; and the same here when feces was removed and smell remained. And also the Rashba, etc.—it seems to me a printing error and it was reversed, and this "and also the Rashba," etc., must be earlier; nevertheless one may say it is different here, etc., and he brings proof from the Rashba to permit—that is, the Rashba explains a smell without substance not only from flatulence but even from feces when the feces is covered; and in such a case it is forbidden to read, but regarding flatulence it is permitted according to him, since there is no feces here; and the same here, even though we do not rule like the Rashba and forbid flatulence—perhaps here it is preferable to flatulence; and in the end Magen Avraham retracted and holds here it is similar to flatulence.
