From the place where the odor ceases, etc. This is the dispute of Rav Huna and Rav Chisda. The Semag wrote that the halacha follows Rav Chisda, for a baraisa supports him. This is difficult: why is it necessary to say that a baraisa supports him? Let him derive it from the fact that it is the stringent view, as he himself ruled in the law of excrement on one's flesh above, siman 76, seif 4, which is also a dispute of Rav Huna and Rav Chisda.
Four amos, etc. See the Levush, who gives the reason that four amos acquire for a person in every place, and it is considered his camp, and it is written, Your camp shall be holy. In Ma'adanei Melech and Nachalas Tzvi they understood that the Levush wrote this reason on his own; see there. I found it explicitly in the Chiddushei Rashba, page 20, that anything outside four amos is not his camp.
And in front of him, etc. The Levush writes that the Torah made it depend on covering. This is somewhat difficult: regarding this, the Torah made it depend on covering, so that if he covered it, even within four amos it is permitted. But from where do we know that outside four amos in front of him it is forbidden if he did not cover it? I then saw in the Chiddushim there that he wrote that because of There shall not be seen among you an indecent matter it is forbidden in front of him as far as his eyes can see. Although the verse refers to actual nakedness, nevertheless, since the Torah combined them and wrote them together, There shall not be seen also applies to excrement. I will explain the words of the Levush himself in seif 2 this way as well, and it is possible to force this explanation here too, that this was also his intent.
At night or a blind person, etc. The Olas Tamid wrote as follows: One should not ask from above, at the end of siman 75, where they ruled that if one closes his eyes it is permitted to read, for there the Torah made it depend on sight, but here it made it depend on a holy camp, and the camp is not holy since there is no partition there. Thus far. So it also appears from the Beit Yosef at the end of siman 75. I am astonished, for according to the words of the Rashba that I wrote, it is clear that in front of him and outside four amos there is no prohibition at all because of Your camp shall be holy, but because of There shall not be seen among you. This supports the ruling of the Bach that I cited there, that even regarding nakedness it is forbidden at night or when closing the eyes. The Taz wrote that even other words of Torah are forbidden, even in a place where there is doubt whether there is excrement, such as in front of houses and courtyards. The Bach wrote in siman 86 that the distancing from urine has the same law as excrement.
Its law is like behind him. The Kesef Mishneh wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Manoach that this is specifically where it is impossible to go behind him, but where it is possible, it is like in front of him. Thus far. From the plain wording of all the poskim it does not appear so. Also, from the expression as far as his eyes can see, it implies that specifically in front of him, where he sees it, do we require that much. It appears that the poskim learned from this, and so it appears in the Rambam's commentary to the Mishnah. Although below, siman 81, seif 2, we rule that specifically where it is impossible he goes to the sides, it appears that this applies specifically there, where he is standing and the excrement is opposite him. But when he is standing and the excrement is to his sides, he need distance only four amos to the sides. So it appears to me. The Magen Avraham wrote: the sides that are in front of him are like in front of him, as with Nesias Kapayim in siman 128, seif 28. Thus far. To me it appears that this is not comparable to Nesias Kapayim. There the reason is that those behind the kohanim are not included in the blessing because they show themselves as though the blessing is not important to them, as Tosafot in Sotah 38, s.v. mechitzah, wrote. Therefore the sides are like in front of him, for the blessing is still important in his eyes since he is not standing behind him. This is not so here, where the reason is because of sight. It appears that anything he does not see by looking naturally, without deliberately turning, is not considered in front of him. Know this, for the actual sides of the kohanim are like in front of them, whereas here they are like behind him; rather, as I explained.
Since he discharges, etc. Although nowadays each individual prays for himself, nevertheless he is obligated to answer Amen to the blessing of the shaliach tzibbur. So wrote the Olas Tamid. According to this, during Pesukei D'Zimrah until Yishtabach the shaliach tzibbur may continue saying it.
