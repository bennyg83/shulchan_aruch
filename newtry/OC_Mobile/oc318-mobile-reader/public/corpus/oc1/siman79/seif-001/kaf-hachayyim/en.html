1) [Se'if 1] If human excrement is behind him, he must distance himself four amos, etc. Tur writes that a foul odor with a source, such as the odor of human excrement, requires a distance of four amos when behind him. Rashba explains that "a foul odor with a source" means exposed excrement. Beis Yosef cites him and writes that this is obvious. Prishah, paragraph 1, explains that when it is covered, even an odor that has a source requires for Krias Shema only that one distance himself until the odor ceases. Once it is covered, it is called an odor without a source. His proof is flatulence from the body, which likewise originates in excrement but is termed without a source because the excrement is covered within the body. Beis Yosef similarly writes at the end of this siman that Orchos Chaim, citing Rashba, defines a foul odor without a source as covered excrement. Rokeach, siman 323, however, writes: For a foul odor with a source, such as covered excrement emitting an odor, one moves four amos from the place where the odor ceases and prays. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 10, cites him and writes that this is also implied by Kesef Mishneh, Hilchos Krias Shema, chapter 3, halachah 12, citing Rabbeinu Manoach; by Rambam himself there; and by Rashi, Tur, and the other poskim, who define an odor without a source specifically as flatulence from the body. Shulchan Aruch also appears to agree, since it does not state that covered excrement is termed without a source and mentions at the end of the siman only flatulence. Eliyah Rabbah writes likewise in siman 76, paragraph 1. Chayei Adam, rule 3, paragraph 22, concurs. Therefore, when possible, one should be stringent and not recite while facing covered excrement that emits an odor unless he is four amos beyond the place where the odor ceases. If that is impossible, one may rely upon Rashba and Prishah, who require only that he move beyond the odor. See also below, paragraph 19.
