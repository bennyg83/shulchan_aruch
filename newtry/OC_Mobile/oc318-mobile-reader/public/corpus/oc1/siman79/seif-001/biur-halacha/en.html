(א) I will copy here an introduction to this siman; its core is from the language of the Peri Megadim in this siman and in other places, sometimes with slight changes of wording so that it may be explained well. In some places I have clarified the matter so that it should be agreed upon for practical halachah per the other later authorities as well — and this is his language. Before we come to explain here in this siman and in other places the matters that were raised on these topics, I will present here first a comprehensive introduction that is very necessary to know, and it is: Know that the law of feces is divided into ten laws: (1) Feces behind him and at his sides; (2) Before him; (3) In a place ten tefachim high or ten low; (4) Feces in another room with an open doorway; (5) Feces in a skylight; (6) Feces in water; (7) A bad smell that has a substance; (8) Feces in its place (passing); (9) The law of urine, a chamber pot, and a cesspit; (10) A garbage heap and animal excrement. 1. Feces behind him or at his sides within four amot — it is forbidden by Torah law to recite the Shema, pray, or speak words of Torah [or recite any blessing — Ramban in his Likutim], because it is said, "and your camp shall be holy," and all within four amot is a person's encampment. If he recited in a place where one should be concerned and afterward found [feces], he returns and recites, as explained at the end of siman 76 — see there. 2. Feces before him — one distances as far as his eyes can see. The Rosh and the Rashba disagree on this. The Rashba holds that the verse "that He not see in you any unseemly thing" applies to feces as well; therefore there is a stringency per the Rashba that even in one room, wherever one sees it, it is forbidden by Torah law as with ervah; and a leniency per the Rashba that outside four amot, even if it is within as far as his eyes can see, thought is permitted as with ervah. The Rosh holds that "that He not see" applies only to ervah and not to feces; with feces there is only a prohibition because of "your camp shall be holy." Therefore there is a leniency per the Rosh that in another room, even if one sees it, it is permitted because the verse reveals that in another domain it is not one's encampment. What is forbidden in another room before him as far as his eyes can see is either because of a rabbinic prohibition or also from the Torah, since wherever one sees it before him as far as his eyes can see it is included in encampment and is forbidden. Therefore there is a stringency per the Rosh that as far as his eyes can see it is forbidden even in thought, whether rabbinically or from the Torah, since as far as his eyes can see is a place of encampment. Per what is written in the Prishah, brought in the Mishbetzot Zahav there, there should not be this stringency per the Rosh. The Mishbetzot Zahav wrote there another distinction between the Rosh and the Rashba, and he retracted it in siman 81, letter 4, in Eshel Avraham; therefore I did not copy it. 3. Feces in a place ten tefachim high or ten low — this is another domain per the Rosh; even if one sees it, it is permitted, since "that He not see" does not apply to it. Per the Rashba it is forbidden, as above. Per all opinions, squinting the eyes helps here, since he is in another domain — so wrote the Mishbetzot Zahav; and the author here rules plainly like the Magen Avraham, seif kaf-chet, and there he retracted from this and it seems his view is to be stringent. In a pressing situation one may be lenient [Darkhei Chayyim]. If four tefachim of width are required, it will be explained within, be'ezrat Hashem. 4. Feces in another room with an open doorway, or feces before the doorway even if one sees it — per the Rosh it is permitted; even where there is no door, since "that He not see" does not apply in such a case, and it is no worse than what we wrote in letter 3. Per the Rashba it is forbidden; it is possible that where there is a door, even per the Rashba it is permitted because an open doorway is like a closed one — see there. But from the words of the Shulchan Aruch it does not seem so, as the Mishbetzot Zahav himself wrote, and all the later authorities ruled plainly on this and did not distinguish. 5. Feces in a skylight is permitted, for the Merciful One made it depend on covering. It seems from Rashi and the Rambam that even if the feces is not covered at all, only a glass partition separates between them — this is the covering, and not necessarily that one must cover the feces from all sides. Even for the Bach, the Taz brought in seif kaf-gimmel that for a high place we require ten tefachim by four tefachim by four tefachim in the partition — we do not require this; whatever separation there is between him and the feces is permitted, and this is the covering [and his words are very plain; it is difficult regarding the Eliyah Rabbah in siman 87, who wrote there regarding a bed ten high that separates between him and the feces that we require its partitions to reach the ground on all sides — per the words of the Peri Megadim above it is plain that we require only on the side that separates between him and the feces; it is difficult]. The Peri Megadim did not mention the measure of the partition's width; see siman 87 in the Magen Avraham, seif kaf-dalet, that if the partition is in a house we require it to divide from wall to wall — see there; and if in a field he did not mention its measure — see in the Nekudas HaKesef, general rule 3, who elaborated on this; his view is that whatever is the width of four tefachim is considered a partition to permit opposite it, but not opposite the air — see there. The Peri Megadim also wrote: if the feces is outside and one sees through a glass window, it is permitted according to all opinions, since a glass partition separates between them; and even the Rashba in seif 2, who forbids in another domain when one sees it, nevertheless when there is a glass partition it is permitted. 6. The sixth: feces in water is not considered covering, although for ervah it is considered covering — as in siman 74, seif kaf-chet in the Magen Avraham; for feces, not as in siman 76, Magen Avraham seif kaf-tet, for it is written "and you shall cover," and this is not called covering, unlike regarding exposure of ervah. 7. The seventh: a bad smell that has a substance is like feces from the Torah; therefore one must distance behind it four amot from where the smell ceases. If he recited, he returns and recites. But regarding this matter it is more lenient than feces: if it is in another room, even though the separation does not help for a bad smell, nevertheless the distancing of four amot from where the smell ceases is not required — only that the bad smell not reach him is sufficient. Even if he is in the room where the bad smell reached, nevertheless since it did not reach him, it is permitted. If the bad smell reaches him, it is forbidden only rabbinically, for it is considered only a bad smell without substance, whose prohibition is only rabbinic, as below [he ruled like the Eliyah Rabbah and the Prishah, not like the Levush; and so the Darkhei Chayyim copied for halachah. However, from the words of the Gra in his Beur on seif 2, s.v. "regarding the Rosh," etc., it seems he holds like the Levush that even with a separation of domain we require four amot from where the smell ceases; and so the Chayei Adam copied, general rule 3, law 22. Afterward I found in the Peri Megadim itself, in Eshel Avraham, letters 11 and 15 and 16, that he tends to say that even when covered and with a separation of domain, nevertheless we require four amot from where the smell ceases; and in the end it remains in doubt. Also regarding covered, the Peri Megadim in Eshel Avraham contradicts himself and it seems he retracted: in siman 76, letter 1, in Eshel Avraham it seems he concludes like the Rashba; and at the end of this siman in the Mishbetzot Zahav he retracted and it remains in doubt. So too in the Beur HaGra mentioned above it seems this is considered a bad smell with substance [but I do not understand what he wrote, for the Rashba wrote that covered is called a bad smell without substance]. So too in the Chayei Adam in the aforementioned law, and in Darkhei Chayyim, law 29, it also seems he rules like the Rokeach that covered is called a bad smell with substance; and so too in Sefer HaEshkol his view is thus]. 8. The eighth: passing feces that does not stand in its place is also words of Torah — so it seems in the Gemara; and the Shulchan Aruch ruled in siman 76, seif 63: before him as far as his eyes can see, and behind him four amot. From the Rambam it seems, and the Bach ruled thus, that even from before him four amot suffice. 9. The ninth: urine from the road is forbidden only opposite the column that drips, and all the more so from the ground rabbinically; nevertheless one must distance from urine as far as his eyes can see before him and four amot behind him — all this when they are not putrid; but when they are putrid, it is forbidden by Torah law. 10. The tenth: a garbage heap with a bad smell, a stinking carcass, and anything similar — its law is like feces and forbidden by Torah law [although in the Peri Megadim only a plain garbage heap and carcass and anything putrid were mentioned; nevertheless it is known to one who examines this halachah well that a plain garbage heap is not included in this except when it is known that it has a bad smell, for its prohibition is only because usually there is feces and urine in it; but when it is known that there is none, one need not distance. So too anything putrid applies only when it is like these, whose bad smell is strong and not accustomed to cease quickly; and per the agreement of the Magen Avraham in seif kaf-gimmel, in my humble opinion one need not distance except until the smell ceases]. The same applies to a chamber pot of urine and a cesspit — also forbidden by Torah law. Regarding animal and wild beast excrement it will be explained at the end of the siman. A bad smell without substance is forbidden only rabbinically — so it is explained in Likutei HaRamban and its Beur; its laws — see at the end of the siman. Covered with its smell wafting — see above in letter 7.

(ב) Four amot from where the smell ceases — see in the Nekudas HaKesef, general rule 3, who wrote that distancing from where the smell ceases is only rabbinic; therefore after the fact one need not return and pray — see there. From the Levush and Peri Megadim it seems it is from the Torah.

(ג) And from before him one must distance, etc. — see in the Chayei Adam, who wrote that per the Rashba it is from the Torah, for "that He not see in you any unseemly thing" applies to feces as well according to him, and the verse depends on sight; but per the Rosh it is only rabbinic. In the Peri Megadim he is uncertain about this for the Rosh, and from the Magen Avraham in seif kaf-alef, and likewise from the Prishah and other later authorities brought in Eshel Avraham seif kaf-alef, it seems that even per the Rosh it is from the Torah — see there. Nevertheless, even if we say it is from the Torah, one is not obligated to distance as far as his eyes can see except if he knows there is feces there, even though he does not see it with his eyes — unlike in a case of doubt; and some are stringent if it is a place where one should be concerned. See on this below, siman 81, and in the Mishna Berurah there.

(ד) As far as his eyes can see — with this one need not [distance] from where the smell ceases, but since he cannot see it, behold his camp is holy [Rav Zalman].

(ה) Or if he is blind — it is plain that a blind person must estimate as far as his eyes can see like an average person. Other people at night who do not see — ostensibly each one must estimate per his daytime vision, whether he has great or small vision — see there. Know further that regarding feces, per all opinions turning the face alone without moving his body does not help, so that this should be considered like feces behind him — needless to say per the Taz and other later authorities above at the end of siman 75, that even for ervah this does not help, and all the more so for feces. Even per the Beit Yosef, for whom turning the face alone helps there, as the Peri Megadim wrote that I am different there, for squinting the eyes also helps per him — unlike in our matter, where squinting the eyes and a blind person are forbidden; both are forbidden, as explained to one who examines there, that for the Beit Yosef squinting the eyes and turning the face are equivalent.

(ו) Until it is removed — see in the Mishna Berurah what he wrote: if one is now engaged in verses of song, ostensibly the same applies if the prayer leader is engaged in the Shema and its blessings — whether to silence him, for we are not now accustomed to fulfill one's obligation through this; and even if it happened that one person is within his four amot and because of this he cannot finish the blessings before the cantor and answer amen to the cantor's blessing — what does it matter to the cantor? He must say the blessings and the Shema for himself, and it is not similar to the cantor's repetition in our time, whose main purpose was fixed for the congregation, as is proven in siman 124, seif 4; and there is no proof from the Magen Avraham to the contrary from what he cited specifically verses of song, for he refers to places where they are accustomed to fulfill obligation through the blessing of the Shema; therefore he says verses of song, that in this there is no way to fulfill obligation, as is proven in his wording — see there in his words, for this is his main reason; and if so, in our time the same applies to the Shema and its blessings — it is difficult. Regarding urine: if it happened at the time of the Shema and its blessings outside the four amot of the prayer leader, certainly one may be lenient not to inform him — as below.

(ז) Within four amot of the feces — see in the Mishna Berurah what he wrote: the same applies if a child urinated water, etc.; and in Sefer Mor VeKetziah he wrote that if a child urinated water while the prayer leader is praying aloud and the prayer leader does not know, one need not silence him. His main reason is: since the prohibition of urine is only rabbinic because of honor of the congregation, it is fitting to uphold Torah law. It is really difficult to reject all the later authorities who did not mention this reasoning — it seems they do not hold thus, unless the urine is from some sick person who drips involuntarily, for certainly one must be careful not to reveal it so as not to embarrass him. Nevertheless, if it is outside the four amot of the prayer leader, according to what we are not accustomed today to leave during the prayer leader's prayer, it is possible to rely on being lenient regarding urine not to inform the prayer leader to silence him. If the urine was within the four amot of the prayer leader and until water was brought to pour on it he waited in order to finish the entire prayer — whether he must return to the beginning because of this, see above in siman 78 in Biur Halachah.
