I will copy here an introduction to this siman. Its essence is from the language of the Peri Megadim in this siman and in other places, sometimes with a slight change of wording so that it will be well clarified. In a few places, however, I clarified the matter so that it should be agreed upon as practical halacha also according to the other later poskim. This is his language: Before we come to explain here in this siman and in other places the points that arise regarding these matters, I will first give an introduction here that includes what is very necessary to know. Know that the law of excrement is divided into ten laws:
(1) excrement behind him or to the sides; (2) in front of him; (3) in a place ten tefachim higher or ten tefachim lower; (4) excrement in another room with an open doorway; (5) excrement behind glass; (6) excrement in water; (7) a bad odor that has a source; (8) excrement in its place; (9) the law of urine, a chamber pot, and a pan for excrement; (10) a refuse heap and animal excrement.
1. Excrement behind him or to the sides: within four amos it is forbidden according to the Torah to read Krias Shema, pray, speak words of Torah, or recite any blessing, as the Ramban writes in his Likkutim, because it says, Your camp shall be holy, and anything within four amos is considered a person's camp. If one read in a place where there was reason to be uncertain and afterward found excrement, he must go back and read, as was explained at the end of siman 76, see there.
2. Excrement in front of him: he must distance himself as far as his eyes can see. The Rosh and Rashba disagree about this. The Rashba holds that the prohibition, There shall not be seen among you an indecent matter, also applies to excrement. According to this, there is a stringency, that even in one room, whenever he sees it, it is forbidden according to the Torah, as with nakedness. There is also a leniency according to the Rashba, that outside four amos, even though it is within the range of his sight, thinking Torah thoughts is permitted, as with nakedness. The Rosh holds that the prohibition, There shall not be seen, applies only to nakedness and not to excrement; with excrement there is only a prohibition because of Your camp shall be holy. If so, there is a leniency according to the Rosh, that in another room, even though he sees it, it is permitted, because Scripture revealed that another domain is not considered his camp. What is forbidden in another room in front of him as far as his eyes can see is either because of a D'Rabbanan prohibition, or even according to the Torah, because anything he sees in front of him within the range of sight is considered part of his camp and is forbidden. According to this, there is a stringency according to the Rosh, that within the range of sight even thought is forbidden, either D'Rabbanan or according to the Torah, because the range of sight is a place of encampment. According to what is written in Peri Chadash, brought in Mishbetzos Zahav there, this will not be a stringency according to the Rosh. The Mishbetzos Zahav wrote there another practical difference between the Rosh and the Rashba, but retracted it in siman 81, letter 4 in Eshel Avraham, and therefore I did not copy it.
3. Excrement in a place ten high or ten low, which is another domain: according to the Rosh, even if he sees it, it is permitted, for There shall not be seen does not apply to it; according to the Rashba it is forbidden, as above. According to everyone, closing one's eyes helps for this, since it is in another domain. So wrote the Mishbetzos Zahav, and he ruled here simply like the Magen Avraham, seif katan 8, but there he retracted this, and it appears that his view is to be stringent. In pressing circumstances one may be lenient, as Derech HaChaim writes. Whether it must have a width of four will be explained inside, with the help of Heaven.
4. Excrement in another room with an open doorway, or excrement in front of the doorway: even though he sees it, according to the Rosh it is permitted, even when there is no door, for There shall not be seen does not apply in this case, and it is no worse than what we wrote in letter 3. According to the Rashba it is forbidden. It is possible that when there is a door, even the Rashba permits it because an open doorway is considered as though closed; see there. However, the words of the Shulchan Aruch do not imply this, as the Mishbetzos Zahav himself wrote, and all the later authorities wrote simply in this matter and did not distinguish.
5. Excrement behind glass is permitted, for the Torah made the matter depend on covering. It appears from Rashi and the Rambam that even if the excrement is not covered at all, but only a glass partition separates between them, this is the covering; it is not necessary specifically that he cover the excrement from all sides. Even according to the Bach, cited by the Taz, seif katan 3, that a high place requires ten tefachim in height and a width of four by four tefachim, regarding a partition we do not require this, and whenever there is a separation between him and the excrement, it is permitted; this is the covering. His words are very clear, and the Eliyah Rabbah in siman 87 requires study, for he wrote there regarding a bed ten high that separates between him and excrement that its partitions must reach the ground on all sides. According to the words of the Peri Megadim above, it is clear that we require only the side that separates between him and the excrement, and it requires study. The Peri Megadim did not mention at all the required width of the partition. See siman 87 in Magen Avraham, seif katan 4, that if the partition is in a house it must separate from wall to wall, see there. If it is in a field, he did not mention its measure. See Nishmas Adam, klal 3, who discusses this at length, and his view is that any width of four tefachim is considered a partition to permit facing it, but not facing the open space; see there. The Peri Megadim further wrote that if the excrement is outside and he sees it through a glass window, it is permitted according to everyone, because a glass partition separates between them. Even according to the Rashba in seif 2, who forbids in another domain when he sees it, nevertheless when there is a glass partition it is permitted.
6. The sixth: excrement in water is not considered covered. Although regarding nakedness it is considered covered, as in Magen Avraham, siman 74, seif katan 8, regarding excrement it is not, as in Magen Avraham, siman 76, seif katan 9, for it is written, and you shall cover, and this is not called covering, unlike with regard to exposed nakedness.
7. The seventh: a bad odor that has a source is like excrement according to the Torah. Therefore one must distance behind himself four amos from the place where the odor ceases, and if he read, he must go back and read. However, in this respect it is lighter than excrement: if it is in another room, although separation does not help for a bad odor, nevertheless he need not distance four amos from the place where the odor ceases; as long as the bad odor does not reach him, it is permitted. Even if the bad odor reached the room in which he is, nevertheless, since it did not reach him, it is permitted. If the bad odor reaches him, this is forbidden only D'Rabbanan, for it is considered only like a bad odor that has no source, whose prohibition is only D'Rabbanan, as below. He ruled like the Eliyah Rabbah and Perishah, and not like the Levush, and Derech HaChaim also copied this as halacha. However, from the words of the Gra in his commentary on seif 2, s.v. according to the Rosh, etc., it appears that he holds like the Levush, that even where a separate domain intervenes one requires four amos from the place where the odor ceases, and Chayei Adam, klal 3, din 22, also copied this. Afterward I found in the Peri Megadim himself, in Eshel Avraham, letters 11, 15, and 16, that he inclines to say that when it is covered or there is a separation of domain, nevertheless one requires four amos from the place where the odor ceases, and in the end he leaves it requiring study. Also regarding covered excrement, the Peri Megadim contradicts himself, and it appears that he retracted, for in siman 76, letter 1, in Eshel Avraham, it appears that he rules simply like the Rashba, but at the end of this siman in Mishbetzos Zahav he retracts and leaves it requiring study. So too from the commentary of the Gra above, it appears that this is considered a bad odor that has a source, though I do not understand what he wrote, as the Rashba wrote, for the Rashba wrote that when it is covered it is called a bad odor that has no source. So too in Chayei Adam there and in Derech HaChaim, din 29, it appears that he rules like the Rokeach, that covered excrement is called a bad odor that has a source. The Sefer HaEshkol also holds this way.
8. The eighth: excrement passing by and not standing in its place is also a Torah matter. So it appears from the Gemara, and the Shulchan Aruch ruled in siman 76, seif 3, that in front of him the distance is as far as his eyes can see, and behind him four amos. From the Rambam it appears, and the Bach ruled this way, that even in front of him four amos suffices.
9. The ninth: urine. According to the Torah it is forbidden only opposite the stream itself. When it is trickling, and certainly when it is on the ground, the prohibition is D'Rabbanan. Nevertheless one must distance himself from urine as far as his eyes can see in front of him and four amos behind him. All this is when it does not stink, but when it stinks it is forbidden according to the Torah.
10. The tenth: a refuse heap whose odor is bad, a carcass that stinks, and anything similar have the law of excrement and are forbidden according to the Torah. Although the Peri Megadim mentions only a refuse heap, a carcass, and anything that stinks, nevertheless one who examines this halacha well knows that a regular refuse heap is not included in this unless it is known that its bad odor is of this sort, for otherwise its prohibition is only because presumably it contains excrement and urine. But when it is known that it does not contain them, one need not distance himself. So too, anything that stinks applies only when it is similar to these, whose bad odor is strong and not normally quickly removed, according to the agreement of the Magen Avraham in seif katan 13. Otherwise one need distance himself only until the odor ceases, as it appears to my humble opinion. The same applies to a chamber pot for urine and a pan for excrement; they too are forbidden according to the Torah. Regarding the excrement of a domesticated or wild animal, it will be explained at the end of the siman. A bad odor that has no source is forbidden only D'Rabbanan. So it is explained in the Likkutim of the Ramban; for its explanation and laws, see the end of the siman. As to excrement that is covered and its odor emerges, see above in letter 7.
Four amos from the place where the odor ceases. See Nishmas Adam, klal 3, who wrote that the requirement from the place where the odor ceases is only D'Rabbanan, and therefore after the fact he does not go back and pray; see there. But from the Levush and Peri Megadim it appears that it is according to the Torah.
And in front of him he must distance himself, etc. See Chayei Adam, who wrote that according to the Rashba this is according to the Torah, for according to him the prohibition, There shall not be seen among you an indecent matter, also applies to excrement, and Scripture made it depend on sight. But according to the Rosh it is only D'Rabbanan. The Peri Megadim is uncertain about this according to the Rosh. From the Magen Avraham in seif katan 1, and from the Peri Chadash and the other later authorities cited in Eshel Avraham, seif katan 1, it appears that even according to the Rosh it is according to the Torah; see there. Nevertheless, even if we say that it is according to the Torah, he is required to distance himself as far as his eyes can see only if he knows there is excrement there, even though he does not see it with his eyes, unlike in a doubtful case. There are those who are stringent if it is a place where there is reason to be doubtful. See below, siman 81, and the Mishnah Berurah there.
As far as his eyes can see. In this case he need not distance himself from the place where the odor ceases; rather, since he cannot see it, his camp is holy. GraZ.
Or he is blind. It is obvious that a blind person must estimate according to the sight range of an average person. As for other people at night, when one cannot see, it would appear that each person must estimate according to his daytime vision, whether he has stronger or weaker sight; see further. Know also that regarding excrement, according to everyone merely turning one's face without turning his body does not help to consider it as excrement behind him. This is not only according to the Taz and the other later authorities above, at the end of siman 75, who hold that even regarding nakedness this does not help, and certainly so regarding excrement. Even according to the Beit Yosef, for whom merely turning one's face helps there, as the Peri Megadim wrote, that case is different, for closing the eyes also helps according to him there. But in our matter, where closing the eyes is forbidden and a blind person is forbidden, this too is forbidden, as is clear to one who examines there; for according to the Beit Yosef, closing the eyes and turning the face are equivalent.
Until he removes it. See Mishnah Berurah regarding what I wrote, that if he is presently occupied with Pesukei D'Zimrah, then seemingly the same applies if the shaliach tzibbur is occupied with Krias Shema and its blessings, whether he should be silenced. For nowadays we are not accustomed to discharge others' obligation with this. Even if it happens that the excrement is within the four amos of one person, and because of it he will not be able to finish the blessings before the chazzan and answer Amen to the chazzan's blessing, why should this matter to the chazzan? He must say the blessings and Krias Shema for himself. This is not comparable to the repetition of the shaliach tzibbur nowadays, whose essence was instituted for the congregation, as is evident in siman 124, seif 4. There is no proof to the contrary from the Magen Avraham, from the fact that he mentioned specifically Pesukei D'Zimrah, for he is referring to places where they are accustomed to discharge others' obligation with the blessings of Krias Shema. Therefore he said Pesukei D'Zimrah, for there the manner is not to discharge others, as is evident from his wording; see his words there, for this is his essential reason. If so, nowadays the same applies to Krias Shema and its blessings, and it requires study. Regarding urine, if it occurs during Krias Shema and its blessings outside the four amos of the shaliach tzibbur, certainly one may be lenient not to inform him, as follows.
Within four amos of the excrement. See Mishnah Berurah, where I wrote that the same applies if the child urinated, etc. In Mor U'Ketziah he wrote that if a child urinated while the shaliach tzibbur is praying aloud and the shaliach tzibbur does not know, one should not silence him. His main reason is that since the prohibition of urine is only D'Rabbanan, for the honor of the congregation it is proper to leave the matter at the Torah-level law. In truth, it is difficult to reject all the later authorities because of this, since they did not mention this reasoning, which implies that they do not hold of it, unless the urine is from some ill person who drips involuntarily, in which case one should certainly be careful not to reveal it so as not to shame him. Nevertheless, if it is outside the four amos of the shaliach tzibbur, according to our current practice not to fulfill our prayer obligation through the shaliach tzibbur, it is possible to rely on leniency regarding urine and not inform the shaliach tzibbur to silence him. If the urine was within the four amos of the shaliach tzibbur, and while they brought water to pour into it enough time passed to finish the entire prayer, whether he must return to the beginning because of this, see above in siman 78 in Biur Halacha.
