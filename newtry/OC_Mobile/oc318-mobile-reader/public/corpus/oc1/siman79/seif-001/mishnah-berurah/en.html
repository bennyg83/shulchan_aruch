(1) Human feces—even if a bad smell does not reach one from it; and see below siman 82.

(2) Four amot, etc.—for it is written "and your camp shall be holy," and Chazal received that a person's camp is four amot; and if a bad smell reaches from the feces within his four amot, behold his entire encampment place is not in holiness.

(3) As far as his eyes can see—see in Peri Megadim, who wrote that per the Rashba speech alone is forbidden within as far as his eyes can see, but to ponder words of Torah is permitted if he is outside four amot from where the smell ceased; and per the Rosh there is room to consider this, for perhaps it is forbidden from the Torah or rabbinically—see there.

(4) Even at night—therefore it is forbidden to say even at night Kerias Shema or any other matter of sanctity before houses, because it is customary for feces to be there, and all the more so in a courtyard.

(5) Its law is like behind him—nevertheless ab initio he should be careful to walk so that the feces is behind him, and then four amot suffice; and if it is only possible with difficulty, one may be lenient also regarding the sides within four amot as behind him. And this is when it is truly at his side; but at the sides before him [such as urine pipes that smell extending along the street, and the like] Magen Avraham wrote that its law is like before him, and Eliyah Rabbah wrote that its law is like at the sides and four amot suffice. Chayei Adam wrote that what one sees without tilting his head to the sides—all agree its law is like before him. He also wrote that one must be very careful at the time of Kiddush Levanah in streets where foul urine pipes extend along the street from the sides, for even if they are beyond four amot they extend also before him and it is forbidden as far as his eyes can see; and all the more so one must distance four amot from where the smell ceased, and it is better not to sanctify [the moon] at all. And one who is careful not to mention words of sanctity in a place that is not clean—upon him Scripture says, "and by this matter you shall prolong your days."

(6) Until they remove it—and if because of this the shaliach tzibur waited long enough to finish all of it and he was not within his four amot, he should not return to the beginning.

(7) Fulfill their obligation—that is to say, it is as if each one is now praying for himself; and although our practice is that each individual prays for himself, nevertheless each individual must answer amen to the blessing of the shaliach tzibur, and feces may not be before him. And if the shaliach tzibur is now engaged in Pesukei deZimra, which does not fulfill others' obligation and there is no answering amen there, he is permitted to speak as usual if he is outside his four amot from where the smell ceased; and if he is within his four amot he must be silent, and even if he waited long enough to finish all of it he returns only to the place where he stopped; and likewise regarding the blessing of Kerias Shema. But in Kerias Shema and prayer recited quietly, if he tarried long enough to finish all of it [from the beginning of the prayer until its end] and the feces was within his four amot, he returns to the beginning; and we estimate per the reader.

(8) And it is impossible—and if it happened that no person sits within four amot of the feces, it is permitted to pray, even though feces separates between them and the shaliach tzibur; they may turn their faces so that it is not before them, for it is forbidden as far as his eyes can see. However in siman 55 s.k. 11 some say one should not answer amen when feces separates between them; and see there what we wrote on this.

(9) Four amot, etc.—that is to say, from where the smell ceased. And if a child urinated in the synagogue, the shaliach tzibur should also be silent and wait until they pour water on it or cover it or distance the entire congregation four amot from it [Taz, Peri Megadim, and Divrei Chamudos]. See what we wrote in Biur Halacha: if the urine was outside the four amot of the shaliach tzibur, perhaps one may rely on the lenient view that he need not be informed. Nevertheless the congregation should be careful to distance or cover or pour water, for otherwise they cannot answer amen.
