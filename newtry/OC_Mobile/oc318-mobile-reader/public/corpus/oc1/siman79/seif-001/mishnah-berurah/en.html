Four amos, etc. For it is written, "Your camp shall be holy," and our Sages received that a person's camp extends four amos. If a foul odor from the excrement reaches within his four amos, his entire encampment is not holy.
As far as he can see. See Peri Megadim, who writes that according to the Rashba only speech is forbidden within visual range, but contemplating Torah matters is permitted if one is more than four amos from the point where the odor ceases. According to the Rosh, this requires consideration, for it is possible that even contemplation is forbidden either by Torah law or Rabbinically. See there.
Even at night. Therefore, even at night it is forbidden to recite Krias Shema or any other sacred matter in front of houses, since excrement is commonly found there, and certainly in a courtyard.
Its law is like that which is behind him. Nevertheless, initially he should take care to walk so that the excrement is directly behind him, in which case four amos suffice. If this is possible only with excessive difficulty, four amos to the side also suffice, as when it is behind him. This applies only to the actual sides. Regarding the forward sides, such as foul drainage channels extending along the street and similar cases, the Magen Avraham writes that their law is like that which is in front of him. Eliyah Rabbah writes that their law is like the sides, and four amos suffice. Chayei Adam writes that anything he can see without turning his head to the side is, according to all opinions, treated as being in front of him. He further writes that one must be especially careful when reciting Kiddush Levanah in streets where foul drainage channels extend along the sides of the street. Even if they are four amos away, they also extend in front of him and are therefore forbidden within visual range. Certainly, he must be four amos from the point where the odor ceases. It is preferable not to recite Kiddush Levanah at all. Concerning one who is careful not to mention sacred matters in an unclean place, Scripture says, "Through this matter you shall prolong your days."

Fulfill their obligation. Meaning, it is as though each person is now praying for himself. Although our practice is that each individual prays for himself, every individual must nevertheless answer amen to the chazzan's blessing, and it is forbidden for excrement to be before him. If the chazzan is now reciting Pesukei DeZimrah, in which he does not discharge anyone's obligation and there is no answering of amen, he may continue normally if he is more than four amos from the point where the odor ceases. If he is within four amos, he must remain silent. Even if he paused long enough to complete the entire section, he returns only to where he stopped. The same applies to Birchos Krias Shema. Regarding Krias Shema and the silent Amidah, however, if he paused long enough to complete the entire section, measured from the beginning of the prayer to its end, and the excrement was within his four amos, he returns to the beginning. The required duration is assessed according to that particular reader.
And it is impossible. If it happened that no person is sitting within four amos of the excrement, it is permitted to pray. Although the excrement separates between them and the chazzan, they may turn their faces so that it is not before them, since it is forbidden within visual range. Nevertheless, in siman 55, seif 20, the Mechaber cites an opinion that one should not answer amen when excrement intervenes between them. See there and what we wrote on this matter.
Four amos, etc. Meaning, from the point where the odor ceases. If a child urinated in the synagogue, the chazzan must likewise remain silent and wait until water is poured over it, it is covered, or the entire congregation moves four amos away from it. See what we wrote in Biur Halachah, that if the urine was outside the chazzan's four amos, it may be possible to rely on the lenient opinion that there is no need to inform him. Nevertheless, the congregation should move away, cover it, or pour water over it, for otherwise they will be unable to answer amen.
