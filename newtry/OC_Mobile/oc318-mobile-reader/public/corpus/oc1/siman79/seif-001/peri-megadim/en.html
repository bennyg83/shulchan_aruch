Even at night. Taz: even doubtful feces — its law is like certain [feces] in a place where doubt is appropriate; and before it, as far as one can see. And I saw fit to precede here one introduction, and there is need for it for these simanim until siman 87.

Know that this law of feces is divided into ten laws: (1) feces behind him (and at the sides); (2) before him; (3) in a place ten handbreadths high or ten low; (4) feces in one room and opening the door; (5) feces in a lantern; (6) feces in water; (7) bad smell that has a substantial source and that has no substantial source; (8) passing feces; (9) I also included the law of urine, a chamber pot, and a potty of filth; (10) garbage heap and animal and beast feces.

The first: feces behind him within four amos — forbidden miderabbanan to recite Kerias Shema and speak words of Torah, as it says "and your camp shall be holy" (Devarim 23:14–15), and this applies to feces; and all within four amos of a person's encampment is likewise Chayei Adam and other poskim. And if he read even in a place where doubt is appropriate, he returns and reads as in siman 76 seif 8 — and we have spoken many times of this: if the reason is that the All-Merciful said "you shall not do," it does not help, like Rava in Temurah 4; and you might say specifically an action — no, for one who separates terumah before bikkurim challenges from it that speech and thought count — see there. However one who examines Shulchan Aruch Choshen Mishpat siman 28 and Beit Din 11 infers that "you shall not do" etc. applies specifically when it cannot be conceived as speech; not so here, where place causes [the prohibition], and it appears rabbinic, as Rava said in Berachos 22, "sacrifice of the wicked is abomination" — see there; and see siman 185, and it will be explained below regarding urine in this matter (im yirtzeh the Omnipresent).

The second: feces before him — one distances as far as one can see. And you already know the dispute of the Rosh and Rashba; as it appears to the Rosh, "you shall not see" applies only to ervah, and as the plain meaning of the passage in Shabbos 150a, and regarding feces there is only "and your camp shall be holy"; if so, outside four amos why is it forbidden before him? We have several approaches: either rabbinically it is forbidden before him whenever he sees it; or miderabbanan, whatever he sees before him is included in "your camp." And as a proof, encampment is included even outside four amos. In our novellae we expanded this; now I saw in Pri Chadash that since it is written "and you shall not see" with a vav, it applies also to feces — meaning whatever is outside his camp; nevertheless not in another domain — for in another domain the verse reveals "your camp," and in such a case it is permitted; therefore it is permitted in another room even if he sees — see there. For Rashba it is clear that he holds "you shall not see" applies also to feces, and even in another room, whatever he sees is forbidden miderabbanan. Accordingly for the Rosh, if we say as far as one can see is rabbinic, there is no difficulty in Berachos 22b, "he found feces in its place" and likewise what the Sages taught, "he moves it behind him and does not return and pray" — for as far as one can see is rabbinic, doubt is for leniency, like Magen Avraham in siman 76 seif 8. However even for Rashba, who [holds] as far as one can see is miderabbanan, he rules as far as one can see one does not return and read — in siman 76 Magen Avraham 4, that regarding "your camp shall be holy" there is only "you shall not see," and since he did not know and did not see, there is no Torah prohibition here. Accordingly if we say to begin with that before him as far as one can see is a place of encampment, one could say this here; however in any case it appears from the Mechaber's ruling here seif 2 that he ruled like Rashba, and likewise in siman 76. There is a difference for the Rosh: one could say as far as one can see, even mental contemplation is forbidden rabbinically; or before him is a place of encampment as far as one can see. But for Rashba, mental contemplation outside four amos as far as one can see is permitted — for regarding ervah too mental contemplation is permitted in Shabbos 150a; likewise feces, where there is no "your camp" but only "you shall not see."

And behold, it is simple to understand from this Jerusalem Talmud of Rabbi Ilai and his colleagues, brought in Chayei Adam and Taz and Pri Chadash, that certainly within four amos there was no feces — for otherwise "your camp" would apply only as far as one can see; if so, how did he rule there that as far as one can see mental contemplation is permitted, and the ruling is that mental contemplation [is permitted] as in siman 85 seif 2 and siman 47 seif 4? Rather that Borka there ruled when giving the reason, since it was daytime and he should not have said only "forbidden," and there it was to distinguish from prohibition that it was permitted as in siman 85; and necessarily Rabbi Ilai said only plainly "forbidden," and afterward when he left there he stated the reason. Nevertheless the law is true for Rashba that as far as one can see mental contemplation is permitted, as I wrote. Also from what the Sages stated there, "a Torah scholar should not walk in filthy alleys" — we infer that outside four amos mental contemplation is permitted. And those who see — Rabbi Ilai and his colleagues knew certainly that there was feces as far as one can see, and it is forbidden even at night; for if from doubt, the Mechaber ruled in siman 76 like the first view in plain [text] that one does not return to the beginning. And what the Taz wrote, "even with doubtful feces" — possibly he speaks within four amos, and the matter stands on its own. And what was difficult regarding passing feces for Rashba will be explained below there.

The third: in a place ten [tefachim] high or ten low, which is another domain — for the Rosh, even if he sees it is permitted, for the verse "you shall not see" does not apply to it; and for Rashba it is forbidden as explained here seif 2. And if we require height ten and width four — Taz, letter 3, and see there regarding feces in a glass partition from this. And what Magen Avraham wrote, letter 8, regarding squinting one's eyes — for all it is permitted; to me the poor man in Israel, his source is now; and if passing feces in Berachos 25a, Choshen Mishpat permits, for it is not "your camp," as Chayei Adam wrote; and still, not "you shall not see" for Rashba — we infer that squinting is permitted; and it requires study from the Jerusalem Talmud and from seif 1 here that as far as one can see is also not "your camp" but only "you shall not see," and nevertheless at night it is forbidden; and that [case] of passing feces — one could say it deals with behind him. Indeed it is true that Rambam explained to us regarding before him in chapter 3 of Hilchos Kerias Shema 13, a row on the water opposite him, and as Rashi explained Berachos 25a, passing feces before him — see there. And I saw that the words of our master are built on foundations of truth and reason, as written in letter 1 regarding night, that we require "your camp shall be holy," and he showed to the common people that for the Mechaber it is certainly difficult: in siman 67 regarding ervah, words of Torah — squinting helps; regarding feces, some say only "you shall not see," not ervah; and for the Rosh one could say as far as one can see is rabbinic, it should be stricter rabbinically than Torah law; therefore the Mechaber holds before him as far as one can see, and one who sees feces — it is not "your camp shall be holy," and therefore squinting does not help; not so regarding ervah, where there is only "you shall not see," whatever squinting helps — see there in siman 67 Taz letter 2. Accordingly it is certain that in another room where there is only "you shall not see," squinting helps like ervah as stated; and for Beit Yosef and other authorities, it is forbidden in such a case in another room like ervah; and the Mechaber learned this from Rashi and Rambam regarding passing feces — how is Abaye permitted before him, behold he sees? Therefore through squinting and passing, he holds we do not call it "your camp," and it is fine; understand this, and see Magen Avraham 1 in the name of Prisha and Levush.

The fourth: feces in another room with the door open — the commentator on the Rosh Berachos 25a regarding bad smell that has a substantial source wrote: a high place, even if he sees, is permitted, for "your camp shall be holy"; accordingly feces before the opening is permitted, for the opening is like closed, and like feces in a glass partition. Behold you will see there is a difference: even if you say a high place ten [tefachim], although it is another domain, one who sees is forbidden to Rashba; nevertheless before the opening, like closed, the opening is considered, and like feces in a glass partition — it applies even if he sees, since it is covered the verse is revealed that covering helps even for Rashba, as we need to say there regarding feces in a glass partition; likewise before the opening. And behold we do not find that Rashba disagrees on this, only regarding high and low place, which is not like closed; and one could say regarding a bad door Rashba agrees that closed is considered. However the Mechaber in seif 2 according to his view at length questioned from small and large courtyard; and Pri Chadash explained behind him — one could say through squinting, as Magen Avraham wrote letter 8, that for the Mechaber it is certainly so.

The fifth: feces in a glass partition is permitted, for regarding covering the All-Merciful relies, and in Rif the reading is "your camp shall be holy" applies — see our novellae; and even for Rashba, that "you shall not see" does not apply to it, the verse is revealed that covering helps. (Primarily, covering is not called another domain but like buried in the ground, and likewise Rambam chapter 3 Kerias Shema 9.) And know that the glass partition mentioned in the Gemara Berachos 25b — Rashi rendered it as a glass partition that interrupts between him and [the feces]; and Rambam explained there 10, and his words: there was between him and the feces a glass partition — permitted (and ervah in a glass partition is likewise). Accordingly, even though the feces is not covered at all, only a glass partition interrupts between them — that is the covering; and some say the feces must be covered on all sides; and even Beit Yosef brought Taz 3 that for a high place we require ten [tefachim] and four by four [tefachim] — for a partition we do not require this, and whatever interruption exists between him and the feces is permitted, and that is the covering. Accordingly if feces is outside and he sees through a glass window — permitted for all, for a glass runner interrupts between them; and even for Rashba in seif 2 who forbids in another domain — when there is a glass partition it is permitted.

The sixth: feces in water is not considered covering, although regarding uncovering ervah it is covering, as in siman 74 letter 8 in Magen Avraham; regarding feces, not as in siman 76 Magen Avraham 9 — "and you covered," and this is not called covering, unlike regarding uncovering ervah; and see there at length.

The seventh: bad smell that has a substantial source is like feces itself and forbidden miderabbanan; and like Rav Chisda, one distances four amos from where the smell ceased; and it is obvious he returns and reads (even if we say rabbinically one does not return and read) — Pri Chadash, siman 76. And if feces is in another room, he brought the first view in plain [text] that interruption does not help for bad smell; and likewise plainly in seif 3 as Pri Chadash wrote; nevertheless, whatever bad smell does not reach him, even if there is bad smell in the room, he need not [distance] four amos from the place of smell — likewise in Beit Yosef and Prisha from what Rabbenu Yerucham wrote in the name of the rabbis of France, and Rashba forbids; and for Rambam, even in another domain he rules like Rav Huna that when it does not reach him — bad smell; nevertheless since Rabbenu Yonah wrote thus, we infer for us too in another domain interruption helps at least when it does not reach him — bad smell, as stated. And one must say he considers it like bad smell that has no substantial source — namely, when covered in a vessel it is considered bad smell without substantial source; Magen Avraham here letter 9 — likewise in another room. Accordingly if bad smell reaches him, it is forbidden only rabbinically, like bad smell without substantial source that is permitted miderabbanan; for in seif 9 words of Torah are permitted without substantial source; and if it were miderabbanan, certainly even for words of Torah it would be forbidden, as Taz letter 1 wrote, and from Prisha who wrote "and you covered" — the All-Merciful said without bad smell implies miderabbanan it is forbidden; and it requires study, for it appears rabbinically, as written; if so, reading — all say miderabbanan it is permitted, as stated. And the words of Levush in seif 2 and seif 3 appear contradictory: in seif 2 he wrote four amos from where the smell ceased, and in seif 3 he wrote if the smell does not reach him it is permitted — and it will be explained.

The eighth: passing feces when one does not stand in its place — behold in siman 76 the Shulchan Aruch ruled before him as far as one can see and behind him four amos; and Magen Avraham and Bach ruled likewise, that even before him four amos suffice. And if we were to say before him as far as one can see is rabbinic, outside four amos is not his camp — one could say regarding passing, in such a case they did not decree, and four amos suffice; but the Mechaber according to his view in siman 67 and here seif 1 necessarily holds as far as one can see is Torah law, "your camp shall be holy," as Magen Avraham wrote here letter 1; if so, he ruled well in siman 76 seif 3 before him as far as one can see, and Bach according to his view in siman 67; understand this, and see at length.

The ninth: urine miderabbanan opposite the pillar is forbidden, for it drips; and all the more so from the ground — rabbinically; nevertheless one must distance from urine as far as one can see before him and behind him four amos; and all this when they do not putrefy. When they putrefy, by Torah law it is forbidden. Garbage dump, carcass, and anything that putrefies — its law is like feces and forbidden miderabbanan; thus I raised in my novellae in the name of Rabbenu Yerucham; and likewise abscess matter, urine, and chamber pot of feces are also forbidden miderabbanan; and see below siman 87 on this. See Rambam chapter 3 Kerias Shema 8.

The tenth: garbage dump, bad smell, and anything with bad smell — forbidden miderabbanan. Feces of domestic animal and wild beast — all whose food is grain is forbidden; Pri Chadash; and it will be explained at the end of the siman; here there is no need to expand further. Guard this introduction, and when I need it in the coming simanim I will allude to it (im yirtzeh the Omnipresent).
