In Magen Avraham, se'if katan 2: The sides are treated like the front, as stated regarding a bent lulav. His intent is that there Rava says: 'They taught this only when it bends forward, but if it bends backward, that is its natural manner.' Rav Nachman says that a bend toward the sides is like one toward the front, while another version treats it like the back. The poskim rule stringently, following the view that the sides are like the front. We thus see that although Rava said only 'forward,' his wording included the sides as grounds for disqualification. Here too, when the required distance is stated as 'before him, as far as his eyes can see,' the term 'before him' includes the sides for that same distance. Rambam discusses a case where no other arrangement is possible; it is then preferable for him to turn toward the side.
