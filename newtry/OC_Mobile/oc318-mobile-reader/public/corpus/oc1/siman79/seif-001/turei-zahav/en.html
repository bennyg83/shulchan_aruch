Even at night. The Rosh, in the chapter Mi Shemeiso, brings the following Yerushalmi: Rabbi Ila and his colleagues were sitting before an inn at night. He asked, “May words of Torah be spoken here?” They answered him, “Since, were it daytime, we would see what is before us, it is presently forbidden.” We learn from this that even other words of Torah are forbidden, not only Krias Shema, and even in a place where it is doubtful whether excrement is present. This refers to the area before houses, where excrement is commonly found, and certainly to a courtyard.
And it is impossible that there should not be, and so forth, of the excrement. This means from the point where the odor ends. I do not know why he did not write that one must distance as far as he can see. Concerning urine, however, it is proper to permit it once the entire congregation distances four amos from it.
