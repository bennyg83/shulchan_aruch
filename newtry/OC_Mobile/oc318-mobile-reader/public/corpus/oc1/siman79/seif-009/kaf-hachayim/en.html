(9) There—and if it is at his side, its law is like behind him. Maran, z"l, in Beit Yosef wrote this in the name of the Rambam, z"l; and in Kesef Mishnah chapter 3 of Kerias Shema din 8 he wrote in the name of R' Menachem that ostensibly it seems considered before him, as we say regarding a crooked lulav; and one may say that what the Rav wrote deals with when he cannot move it behind him—for the Gemara said where it is possible he moves it behind him four amot; if so, where possible it is considered before him, and where not possible it is considered like behind him—until here. And likewise Elya Rabba in Hagahos Beit Yosef; and so from the words of Maran, z"l, below siman 81 seif 2; and likewise Peri Megadim in Eshel Avraham letter 2. However, Eliyah Rabbah letter 5 wrote that the conclusion of all the poskim implies thus—see there what he wrote to distinguish between here and siman 86; and Perishah letter 1 wrote that lechatchila one should be concerned, but if he read Kerias Shema and feces was at his side, it is simple he need not return, and even for prayer the law is likewise—and this is simple, until here—see there. Magen Avraham s.k. 2 distinguishes: at the sides before him it is considered before him—see there. Rebbe Zalman brought it letter 1. Ben Ish Chai, z"l, parashas Bo letter 14. Therefore, since there is a dispute in this, lechatchila one should be stringent and consider the sides like before him; and bedieved, if impossible, we consider it like behind him, as written below siman 81 seif 2; and likewise Kitzur Shulchan Aruch siman 5 letter 8. What he sees without tilting his head to the sides—according to all, its law is like before him. Chayei Adam klal 3 letter 14. Ben Ish Chai brought it there.
