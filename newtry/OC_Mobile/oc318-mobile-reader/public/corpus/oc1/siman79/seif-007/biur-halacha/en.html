But their chicken coop, etc.—see in the Mishna Berurah what he wrote in the name of Chayei Adam regarding an animal pen, and in Kitzur Shulchan Aruch he wrote that his words are not clear, and the poskim also did not mention it. But a chicken coop may be distinguished, and that is to say: because apart from the stench it is also repugnant, as the Rosh and Tur hold—and who would say that this reasoning also applies to an animal pen? One cannot bring proof to the Shulchan Aruch from what we rule in Yoreh De'ah siman 286, that a cowshed is obligated in a mezuzah—even though there we rule that it is exempt, evidently it is not considered like a synagogue, whereas coops prove that they are obligated; therefore the reason is that even though for a blessing it is forbidden to bless there as in a synagogue, nevertheless for a mezuzah it is not in that category, since it is not made for dignified dwelling so that it would be exempt for that reason—see there [and see Chayei Adam klal 15 din 12, that this remains in doubt]. But there is another proof to bring for the Shulchan Aruch: according to Chayei Adam, it is not specifically chicken feces—likewise animal feces, because of their abundance, is considered like human feces; if so, likewise dog and pig feces would have the same law in their pen, and it does not seem so in the Gemara according to what Tosafos explained there s.v. velo hilkhesa, that the baraisa forbidding chicken feces refers to a coop where there is much of their feces, as the Rosh wrote; if so, we can say that the first clause of the baraisa forbidding dog and pig feces also refers in this manner—and why does Rava say there "there is no halacha," etc., to establish it thus? Rather, certainly in all cases it is permitted regarding other things, except chicken feces alone, and the reason is as above. Nevertheless it seems clear for halacha that the law follows Chayei Adam, and not for his reason that a cowshed is in the category of a garbage heap; they already said that an unspecified garbage heap, even if it has no bad smell, is forbidden because of the chazakah of a garbage heap that it contains feces until one checks—and likewise in siman 76 seif 7, and Kitzur Shulchan Aruch also concedes to this. And when the cowshed stinks, such that people are accustomed to suffer from it—in that case we do not need all this, for it is in the category of a garbage heap with a bad smell. And if we cleared the cowshed of animals and the bad smell left the pen, it seems that afterward it is permitted to read there—but on condition that the walls are also checked for feces, because by majority filth is found on walls; therefore one who guards his soul should distance himself from this. And they said in Berachos 24b: one who is careful to remember the word of Hashem in holiness merits long life—see the Gemara there.
