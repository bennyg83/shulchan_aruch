2) There: He must distance himself four amos, etc. Just as one distances himself from excrement, so must he distance himself from a foul odor even when it does not come from excrement. Any odor that ordinarily causes people distress is called foul. The prohibition applies even to one who has an illness and cannot smell, and to a blind person who cannot see, since other people can see and smell it. Night has the same law as day. Something whose natural odor is unpleasant is permitted; only something that became putrid through decay is treated as excrement. Chayei Adam there, paragraph 12; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, siman 5, paragraph 6; Ben Ish Chai, Parashas Bo, paragraphs 17 and 27. We write likewise below in siman 86, paragraph 2. Shaar Ruach HaKodesh, folio 8, s.v. "Know," writes that when a person smells a carcass or foul odor, it greatly damages his soul and causes it to distance itself from him; the proof is the Motzaei Shabbos spices, which restore the soul. Obviously, only something that became putrid through decay harms the soul. Something naturally possessing an unpleasant odor, such as petroleum and the like, does not harm it, just as recitation while facing it is not forbidden, as stated above and below in siman 86, paragraph 2.
