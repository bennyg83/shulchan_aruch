After the fact, if it was not four by four handbreadths, he need not repeat the prayer except as a voluntary prayer, because the later authorities dispute this matter.

Beside it. Meaning, near the doorway. There is no distinction whether or not there is a door. If he inserts his head into the place containing the excrement, all agree that it is forbidden, even if he closes his eyes. Conversely, if the excrement and the person are in the house but he puts his head outside the house or through the window, it is likewise forbidden to recite, because his head is associated with the majority of his body.
Specifically when he does not, etc. The reason is that this opinion maintains that the verse, "No unseemly thing shall be seen," also refers to the prohibition concerning excrement stated at the beginning of the verse. If he sees it through a glass window, all agree that it is permitted, as stated above at the beginning of siman 76. If he closes his eyes, or at night, some say that all agree it is permitted because it is in another domain, while others prohibit it. In pressing circumstances, when he cannot turn his face away, one may rely on the lenient opinion. If he cannot see the excrement because of its height or depth, however, all agree it is permitted.
He sees it. Therefore, it is forbidden to speak sacred matters while looking through an open window if he sees refuse heaps or drainage channels in the streets, or if a pig stands before his window, even though no foul odor reaches him. One must be careful about this when reciting Kiddush Levanah through an open window or on balconies. At minimum, he must close his eyes, and then, if no foul odor reaches him, it is permitted. Where he is uncertain whether the object is refuse, it is permitted because there is a double doubt, perhaps it is not refuse and perhaps the halachah follows the Rosh. Nevertheless, initially it is preferable that he close his eyes, in keeping with, "Do not withhold good," etc. See Biur Halachah.
