(10) If it was, etc.—the same applies in reverse if he stood on the high place and the feces lay below on the ground; and see below siman 87 in Mishna Berurah regarding if he lay on a bed ten handbreadths high and beneath it or before it stood a potty of filth, and other matters belonging to this seif.

(11) In a high place, etc.—and also that it is four by four handbreadths wide, so that it is an important place; and post factum if it was not four by four, he need not return and pray again, only as a voluntary prayer, for there is dispute among the later authorities on this [Divrei Chamudos].

(12) In another house—that is, in another room.

(13) At its side—that is, next to the doorway; and there is no difference whether there is a door there or not. And if he put his head into a place where feces is, all agree it is forbidden, even while squinting his eyes [Magen Avraham siman 74 and Divrei Chamudos]. And likewise the reverse: if the feces and the person are in the house and he put his head outside the house or outside the window, it is likewise forbidden to recite, for we follow the head after the majority of the body [there].

(14) Specifically when he does not see it, etc.—the reason is that he holds the verse "that He not see" applies also to the prohibition of feces stated at the beginning of the verse; and if he sees it through a glass window, all agree it is permitted, as above at the beginning of siman 76—see there. And if he squints his eyes or at night, some say it is permitted in this according to all, since he is in another domain; and some forbid; and at a time of distress when he cannot turn his face away, one may rely on the lenient authorities. However when he cannot see the feces because of its height or depth, all agree it is permitted in this.

(15) He sees it—therefore it is forbidden to speak words of sanctity if he looks through an open window and sees garbage heaps (urine pipes) in the streets, or a pig stands before his window, even if a bad smell does not reach him; and one must be careful about this at the time of Kiddush Levanah through an open window (or on gangways), but he must at least squint his eyes; and then if no bad smell reaches him it is permitted. And where there is doubt whether it is a garbage heap, it is permitted because it is a double doubt—perhaps the halachah is like the Rosh; nevertheless ab initio it is more proper that he squint his eyes rather than rely on being lenient [Chayei Adam]; and see in Biur Halacha.

(16) And if it has, etc.—and according to this view the later authorities ruled.

(17) It does not help—that is to say, we require four amot from where the smell ceased; and there are lenient authorities who hold that here it suffices up to the place where the smell alone ceases; and see in Biur Halacha.

(18) And not a change of domain—therefore one who prays in a house and a bad smell reaches him from another place should stop and wait until the smell passes. And one who has an illness so that he does not smell—perhaps one may be lenient in this at a time of distress.

(19) Its smell—if they passed feces through the house and a bad smell still remains in the house, one should be stringent; however it seems to me that one who does not smell is permitted to recite in this [Magen Avraham].
