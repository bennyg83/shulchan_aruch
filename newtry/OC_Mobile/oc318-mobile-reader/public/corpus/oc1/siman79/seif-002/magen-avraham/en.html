And some say that just as, and so forth. It requires study whether, if excrement was removed from a house but a foul odor still remains there, one may recite. At first glance, it would appear permitted according to this opinion, by a kal vachomer. Here, although the excrement still lies in its place, one may recite even though its odor reaches him. All the more so when the excrement has been removed. Furthermore, the Amoraim dispute passing excrement, and although we rule stringently, that is when he stands in the street opposite it. Here, however, it is outside the room, and all would permit it. Nevertheless, one can say that this case is different, because the excrement was in this room, and it remains forbidden to recite until the odor ceases, as stated in seif 9. The Rashba also explains that a foul odor without a source means covered excrement, as I wrote in siman 76, seif 2. One should therefore be stringent. Still, it appears to me that one who cannot smell it may recite in this situation.
