(2) There—one must distance four amot, etc., as they distance from feces, so they distance from a bad smell even if it is not feces. Whatever people are accustomed to suffer from because of that smell is called a bad smell; and even if he has an illness so that he does not smell, and likewise a blind person who does not see—since other people can see and smell, it is forbidden, and even at night its law is like by day. However, something whose nature is a bad smell is permitted and is not called feces, except what putrefies because of mold. Chayei Adam there letter 12; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch siman 5 letter 6; Ben Ish Chai parashas Bo letter 17 and letter 27; and likewise below siman 86 letter 2—see there. See Sha'ar Ruach HaKodesh page 8 s.v. da, that he wrote it is forbidden to smell a person in any carcass, or a bad smell greatly harms his soul and causes one to distance from it; proof of this from spices of Moshe to restore the soul—until here. It is simple that this too—that it does not harm the soul—except something that putrefied because of mold; but something whose nature is a bad smell, like naphtha and the like, does not harm, just as it is not forbidden to read facing it, as explained, and as written below siman 86 letter 2.
