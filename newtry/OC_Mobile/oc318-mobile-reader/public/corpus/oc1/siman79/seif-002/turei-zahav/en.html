Ten handbreadths high. My father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote that it must also be four by four handbreadths wide, as Rashbam explains at the beginning of the chapter Beis Kor, because only then is it a domain unto itself. In truth, however, we find at the beginning of HaZorek, Gittin 77, concerning a get, that a height of ten handbreadths is considered a separate domain even when it is not four handbreadths wide. It appears that the law of excrement here should be compared to that case. In Bava Basra 56 it is stated that for Shabbos, to divide domains, even a narrow barrier is effective, just as it is effective concerning a get, namely a height of ten handbreadths without a width of four, as Rashbam explains there. It is further clear there that anything regarded as a domain for dividing areas on Shabbos certainly serves as a separation regarding tumah. Surely tumah is no less stringent than excrement, and it should likewise create a separation here. Only in the case of Beis Kor do we require a height or depth of ten handbreadths together with a width of four, because there the separation must have the status of an independent field. As the Gemara states there, a person does not wish to pay his money at one time and then see the land divided into two or three places. Concerning division of domains, by contrast, it is certainly a division even without a width of four handbreadths, as with Shabbos. Although liability for transferring between domains applies only from a private domain to a public domain, for the purpose of dividing domains the intervening area need not possess the full measure of four by four handbreadths and a height of ten. The same is true regarding a get. Therefore, concerning excrement as well, if it is ten handbreadths deep or ten handbreadths high, even though it is not four handbreadths wide, it properly constitutes a separation. This appears correct to me.
