(א) But per the Rashba, etc. — in the Beit Yosef it seems that for the essential halachah we adopt the Rosh; so too the Eser Tikkunim. From the words of the Shulchan Aruch it seems he leans more toward the Rashba; so too the Peri Megadim in the name of the Nekudas HaKesef; so too the Eliyah Rabbah; and in the Chayei Adam it seems it is a doubt in the law — and so it is correct.

(ב) Only when he does not see it — and thought is permitted in all cases, since we derive it only from "that He not see," it is not stringent like opposite ervah, for we rule that thought is permitted. [Peri Megadim and Chayei Adam]

(ג) It — see in the Mishna Berurah what we wrote: if he squints his eyes or at night, it is permitted in this according to all opinions — so too the Magen Avraham; his reason is that since he is in a domain by itself and does not see it directly, there is no [violation] because of "your camp shall be holy" nor because of "that He not see in you any unseemly thing." In the Peri Megadim he challenged his ruling according to what we wrote above at the end of siman 75 — the conclusion of the later authorities regarding the prohibition of ervah, that even when squinting his eyes there is also [violation] because of "that He not see in you," etc.; the same applies that even in our matter one should be stringent on this, and it is not permitted except when he cannot see the feces because of its height or depth. However, according to what the Peri Megadim leans in siman 75, that even for ervah itself, if it is in another domain it is permitted according to all opinions when squinting his eyes — there is no difficulty here; therefore one may be lenient in a pressing situation. Nevertheless, many adopt the Rosh, and it is a doubt — so too the Darkhei Chayyim. When he is praying and a pig passes before the open window, it seems that in this according to all opinions one may be lenient when squinting his eyes, for one may combine with this also the view of the Bach above in siman 76, who holds that for passing feces four amot suffice even before him — and all the more so when he is in another domain. Without squinting the eyes, it is proper to be stringent — and such is the custom.

The separation does not help — meaning what was mentioned at the beginning, when it was on a place ten tefachim high. For the Taz it is certainly satisfactory, for he holds that we do not require it to be four wide, and nevertheless it is considered a separation; but for the Bach, who holds that it must also be four by four — if so, why is this called by the name of separation? Is it not also a domain by itself? It requires investigation.

Nor a change of domain — see in the Mishna Berurah, seif kaf-zayin, what he wrote that we require four amot, etc. — this is the view of the Levush, the Gra, and the Chayei Adam; and those who are lenient are the Prishah, the Eliyah Rabbah, the Darkhei Chayyim, and the Shaarei Teshuvah. The Peri Megadim in the Mishbetzot Zahav, letter 5, remains in doubt; and likewise regarding covered with its smell wafting there are also opinions among the authorities, as we wrote above in the introduction, letter 7 — see there.
