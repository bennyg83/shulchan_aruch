It. See Mishnah Berurah, where we wrote that if he closes his eyes, or it is night, it is permitted in this according to everyone. So wrote the Magen Avraham. His reason is that since it is in a domain of its own and he does not see it directly, there is neither a problem of Your camp shall be holy nor of There shall not be seen among you an indecent matter. The Peri Megadim questioned his ruling according to what we wrote above at the end of siman 75, that the conclusion of the later authorities regarding forbidden nakedness is that even when he closes his eyes, there is still a prohibition of There shall not be seen among you, etc. So too in our matter, one should be stringent, and it is permitted only when he cannot see the excrement because of its height or depth. However, according to what the Peri Megadim inclines to in siman 75, that even regarding nakedness itself, if it is in another domain it is permitted according to everyone when he closes his eyes, there is no difficulty here. Therefore one may be lenient in pressing circumstances. Aside from this, many hold like the Rosh, making it a double doubt, and so wrote the Derech HaChaim. When one is praying and a pig passes before an open window, it appears to me that in this case, according to everyone, one may be lenient if he closes his eyes, for one may combine with this the view of the Bach above in siman 76, who holds that for passing excrement four amos suffices even in front of him, and certainly when it is in another domain. Without closing the eyes it is proper to be stringent, and this is the practice.
