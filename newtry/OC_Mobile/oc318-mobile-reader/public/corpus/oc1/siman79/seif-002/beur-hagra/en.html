Seif 2, or if it was, and so forth. As stated in the ninth chapter of Eruvin, and as stated in seif 3. This is the meaning of, “even if the doorway,” and so forth.
And he sits beside it, and so forth. As stated concerning a place that was ten handbreadths high, and so forth.
And he sees, and so forth. As stated above, siman 76, seif 1.
If there is no, and so forth. As stated below.
According to the Rosh. This follows from his statement, “If it was ten handbreadths high,” and so forth, “but otherwise he must distance himself as far as he can see,” and so forth. It is therefore evident that the case concerns one who sees it, yet when it is elevated, and so forth, it is permitted. Furthermore, the Torah did not make the law of excrement dependent upon sight, as stated there. The Rashba, however, maintains that the case concerns one in which there is some cause preventing him from seeing it, while if it is not elevated it is nevertheless forbidden, as stated in seif 1.
And if there is, and so forth. This is derived from the statement there concerning a foul odor that has a source, and so forth. Yet the first clause of the baraisa already lists excrement and things like it, from which one must distance himself. At first glance, this would be identical to the first clause. Moreover, there it says, “as far as he can see,” whereas here it requires only four amos. Also, the phrase, “a foul odor, and so forth, one must distance himself from it,” implies distance from the odor, and it does not say that one must distance himself from the substance itself until the odor ceases. It must therefore refer to a case in which he need not distance himself from the substance itself, such as when it is in another house or is covered, as the Rashba writes, and he must distance himself only because of the odor. The Mechaber likewise rules without qualification in seif 3 and in siman 83, seifim 1 and 4.
And some say, and so forth. Tosafos there, s.v. “a foul odor,” and so forth. Accordingly, siman 83, seif 4, does not follow Tosafos.
