And he sees it, etc.—Berachos 25a: there was a place ten handbreadths high or ten low; he sits at its side and reads Kerias Shema, and if not he distances as far as his eyes can see. The Rosh explained that it deals even when he sees it; and Nachalas Tzvi wrote that his reason is: if not so, it would be difficult—why was it necessary to teach "and if not," etc., for behold when he sees it, even at ten high he distances as far as his eyes can see. To the Rashba it seems, in my humble opinion, that he holds it was necessary to teach "and if not," etc., in order to inform us that at ten high when he does not see it, he need not distance four amot—until here. His words are wondrous and very strained to reconcile—how can this be heard from "and if not," etc.; moreover, it already taught him "he sits at its side and reads." Also the words of the Rashba in his chiddushim were omitted: he explains the Gemara that here it speaks when if ten high he sits at its side and reads, since he is not in its place literally and does not see it because the height of the place or its depth caused him not to see it; but if it is opposite him, even though there is another reason he does not see it—as long as it is in a place where he could see it, he may not read until he distances from it as far as his eyes can see, since it is not another domain—and this is explained more in the responsum of the Rashba siman 473. Also Beit Yosef, in waiving his honor, did not go into the depth of the Rashba's words here, who rejected his words from that which in seif 3 it is permitted regarding small feces—for perforce it deals with one who sees; if not so, even when he is with the feces in one house it would be permitted; perforce it is never difficult, and it always deals with one who does not see; and that which he permits specifically in another courtyard is because in that courtyard, even if he does not see it, it is forbidden as far as his eyes can see; therefore there is no permission except in another domain, as I explained. Therefore it seems to me for halacha per the Rashba, and so Nachalas Tzvi ruled. Magen Avraham wrote: it seems to me that if he closes his eyes or at night it is permitted in this according to everyone—until here. From the Rashba's words that I brought, it implies it is forbidden—specifically when the height of the place or its depth causes that he does not see, in that case it is permitted.
