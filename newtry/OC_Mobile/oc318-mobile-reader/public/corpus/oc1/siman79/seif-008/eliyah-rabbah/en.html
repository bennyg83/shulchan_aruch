8) And he sees it, etc. Berachos 25 states: If there was a place ten tefachim high or ten tefachim low, he sits beside it and recites Keriat Shema; otherwise he moves as far away as his eyes can see. Rosh explains that this applies even when he sees the excrement. Nachalat Tzvi writes that his reason is that otherwise, why would the text need to teach, 'otherwise, etc.'? If seeing it required him to move as far as his eyes can see even when it was ten tefachim high, the clause would be necessary. Regarding Rashba, Nachalat Tzvi writes that in his humble opinion Rashba holds the clause is necessary to teach that when it is ten tefachim high and he does not see it, he need not move four amos away. His words are astonishing and extremely strained. How can this be inferred from 'otherwise, etc.'? Moreover, the text has already taught that he sits beside it and recites. He also overlooked Rashba's explanation in his Chiddushim: when the place is ten tefachim high, he sits beside it and recites because it is not actually in his place and he does not see it; the height or depth of the place caused it to be hidden. If it is opposite him, however, even if some other cause prevents him from seeing it, as long as it is in a place where he could see it he may not recite until he moves as far away as his eyes can see, because it is not a separate domain. This is explained still more clearly in Responsa Rashba, siman 473. Beit Yosef too, with due respect, did not penetrate Rashba's intent when he rejected his view from seif 3, which permits excrement in a smaller room. Beit Yosef argued that this must discuss a case in which he sees it, because otherwise it would be permitted even if he and the excrement were in one room. There is no difficulty: it can always refer to a case in which he does not see it. The reason permission is given specifically when it is in another courtyard is that in the same courtyard it is forbidden as far as his eyes could see even if he does not actually see it. Permission therefore exists only in a separate domain, as I explained. It consequently appears to me that the halacha follows Rashba; Nachalat Tzvi also rules this way. Magen Avraham writes: 'It appears to me that if he closes his eyes, or at night, it is permitted here according to all opinions.' Rashba's words cited above imply that it is forbidden; he permits only when the height or depth of the place itself causes the excrement not to be seen.
