At night or a blind person, etc.—Olas Tamid wrote this is his wording. One should not question: for above at the end of siman 75 they ruled that if one closes his eyes it is permitted to read—for there the Merciful One made it dependent on seeing, but here it depends on a holy camp, and behold the camp is not holy since there is no partition—until here. And likewise it seems in Beit Yosef at the end of siman 75. I wonder: per the Rashba's words that I wrote, it is clear that before him and outside four amot there is no prohibition because of a holy camp at all, but only because of "nothing indecent shall be seen by you"; and this supports the ruling of the Bach that I wrote there, that even regarding ervah it is forbidden at night and when closing one's eyes. The Taz wrote: even other words of Torah are forbidden, even in a place where there is doubt whether there is feces there—such as before houses and a courtyard. The Bach wrote in siman 86 that distancing from urine has the same law as feces.
