If hides were placed in them — from this wording it seems that even if afterward the hides were removed from them and they dried and the bad smell was removed, nevertheless the name of feces applies to them; and from the wording of R. Tanchum on this matter it does not seem so ostensibly — see there; and see what we wrote nearby on this matter.

And if they have a bad smell, etc. — this does not refer to feces of a dog and pig, but only to other domesticated animals, wild beasts, and birds; for regarding dog and pig the sages established that their smell is not bad when hides were not placed in them — so it is proven in the Gemara there 25a1: R. Yehuda said, there is no halachah, etc. — see there.

Their law is like human feces — see in the Mishna Berurah what he wrote: even if the smell ceased, it is also forbidden. I wrote this because since the author holds that its law is literally like human feces, there it is forbidden in all cases, as in siman 82 — see there; if so, all the more so for cat feces and all these things mentioned from seif 5 onward are forbidden even if the smell ceased afterward — so too in Sefer Halachah Berurah. Nevertheless, it is not so clear to me on this matter, for perhaps for this reason it is more lenient than actual feces; and it also seems ostensibly from the author's wording who wrote "moist donkey excrement," that it means specifically while it is still soft, but if it dried afterward it is permitted, even though initially it was soft. One may reject that the author's intent is only to exclude if initially it was dry, that the name of feces does not apply at all. Afterward I found before me in the Yerushalmi that he wrote: while it is still soft — and it requires investigation for practice.
