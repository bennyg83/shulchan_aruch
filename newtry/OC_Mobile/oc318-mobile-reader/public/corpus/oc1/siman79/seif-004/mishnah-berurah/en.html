(22) And if they have, etc.—that is to say, if we know they have a bad smell; but usually one need not check this, for usually they do not have a bad smell.

(23) Bad smell—that is, anything people are accustomed to suffer from because of that smell. Chayei Adam wrote that something whose nature is a bad smell, such as asafoetida and the like, is permitted and is not called feces, only what putrefies because of mold; and likewise in Minchat Shmuel.

(24) Like human feces—that is to say, we require distancing four amot from where the smell ceased behind him and before him as far as his eyes can see; and it is possible that even if the smell later ceased, nevertheless feces already fell upon it and it is forbidden like human feces. Magen Avraham disagrees with all this and ruled that plain animal, beast, and bird feces alone—except those known to smell very badly, as below—even if they have a bad smell when they emerge, the bad smell is not so strong and its way is to cease; therefore their law is not like feces and one need not distance except to where the smell ceases; and so ruled in Magen Giborim. And in Divrei Chamudos he holds it is a doubt in the law; therefore in Kerias Shema if he read within four amot he returns and reads again, and in prayer he returns and prays and makes a condition that it be as a voluntary prayer—see there. And it seems to me one need not be stringent at all regarding prayer, for many later authorities hold like Magen Avraham.
