(18) All that he can catch: and it implies that even if it is proven with certainty that it is more than is needed for the moed, it is also permitted; and it is not comparable to flour in se'if 1 and the like, where it is permitted only according to his need with expansive measure. This case is different, for not every fish is equal in its taste, and regarding every catch one can say perhaps he will find ones that are more choice, and that is the honor of Yom Tov.
(19) And he salts everything on the moed: that is to say, it is not comparable to se'if 3, where it is not permitted to gather them in order to pickle them; there it is different, for pickled [fruits] are not fit to eat for some days, and if so it is only preparation for the weekday days; which is not so regarding fish, for it is possible under constraint to squeeze and eat, and if so it is possible that he will benefit from this also on Yom Tov, and he did not toil only for weekday need alone.
(20) And it is permitted to catch them in public when it is noticeable etc.: and as is permitted regarding spices in siman 539, se'if 10. And it implies according to this that it is not only one who hunts for himself, but even a hunter whose craft and livelihood is from this is also permitted in public; and see what we will write below, that there are those who disagree about this. Flies and mosquitoes: if they distress a person, it is permitted to kill them on Chol HaMoed, for there is no greater human need than this; and even though he can drive them away, they immediately return upon him (Radbaz, part 2, siman 726).
