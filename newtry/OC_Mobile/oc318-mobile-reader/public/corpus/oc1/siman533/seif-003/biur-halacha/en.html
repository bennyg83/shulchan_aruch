And if he gathered them to eat them. So the text should read; and so it is in the Tur and in the Shulchan Aruch of the first printing.
It is forbidden to gather them at the outset in order to pickle them. See Magen Avraham, who wrote that the intent of the Shulchan Aruch is even if they will not be found after the moed; for gathering is a complete melacha and is permitted only for a davar ha'aved, and forgoing of profit is not called a loss. And according to him, the Shulchan Aruch deals where the fruits were ownerless or where he wishes to buy from a non-Jew (Peri Megadim); that is to say, for if it is a tree of a Jew and they would spoil after the moed, it is certainly a davar ha'aved and permitted. And see below what I wrote about this. And it is astonishing regarding the Chayei Adam in klal 106, se'if 6, and in klal 108, se'if 2, that he copied the words of the Magen Avraham and did not copy that it deals with fruits that are ownerless, or at least with one who buys from a non-Jew.
It is permitted to buy it and pickle it. See Beur HaGra that this is only according to the first answer of the Raavad that is brought in the Rosh at the end of the first chapter, that we do not require that it be fit specifically for the moed, but rather [we distinguish] by a find; and according to this distinction, regarding other matters it is permitted even to toil with a melacha, for example where one must salt and pickle and the like. But according to the latter answer of the Raavad, merchandise is different, for it is not a melacha at all, but only a mere weekday act; but a melacha they did not permit on account of profit unless there is a loss; and watering is a complete melacha, and likewise regarding salting, and in the melacha there is also extra exertion (see the Ran); and according to this it is certainly forbidden even to buy in order to pickle, even if it will not be found after the moed. And so wrote Chiddushei Rabbi Akiva Eiger, and he wonders at the Shulchan Aruch that it did not adopt the law according to the latter answer, while the Ran brought only the latter answer. And according to this, if it is not available after the moed, it is permitted even to buy it before gathering, and he gathers and pickles; and that which the Mechaber did not mention [this], perhaps is to exclude that if it is available to buy after the moed, it is forbidden even to buy that which is already gathered; and that which he forbids at the outset to gather them deals where it is available after the moed. And we can also say that the beginning deals with ownerless fruits and compares it to a find; and so it is implied in Damesek Eliezer; see there. And behold, from the Peri Megadim above it implies that even to buy it from a non-Jew before gathering and to gather them afterwards is forbidden; and he wrote this according to the opinion of the Magen Avraham that a complete melacha such as gathering is forbidden even if it will not be found after the moed, so much so that there is no loss. And behold, according to this the Magen Avraham held the latter answer of the Raavad; and according to this it is difficult what the Mechaber permitted to pickle it; and already Rabbi Akiva Eiger raised this difficulty in his novellae and remained with the difficulty. And it is possible to force and say that the Mechaber holds that salting is lighter, for the primary law is that there is no tanning in foods, as the Magen Avraham wrote in s.k. 7; and therefore even though he adopted the Raavad in the second answer, nevertheless regarding salting he is more lenient than the Raavad.
