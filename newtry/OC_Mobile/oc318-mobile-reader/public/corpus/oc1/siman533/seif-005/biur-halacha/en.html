Of wild animals and birds etc. See what I wrote in the Mishnah Berurah, that several poskim disagree, etc. And we will explain briefly: this is the wording of the Mordechai: hunters who hunt birds, and grinders of groats and coarse meal, sell in private for the need of the moed; but fish hunters, where the matter is noticeable that it is for the need of the moed, it would appear to permit even in public, from that which we say above at the end of the first chapter, "it is permitted to go and hunt," which implies in public; and one may compare it to spices, where it is noticeable that it is for the need of the moed, and we permit in the Gemara; end quote. And behold, even though he began his words regarding sale, nevertheless from the end of his words, where he brought from the end of the first chapter, it implies that he permits even regarding hunting by craftsmen. And therefore the Rama hinted briefly that this law of the Mechaber does not deal with fish, for regarding them it is permitted to hunt even by craftsmen. And see Beur HaGra: that according to this, the Mordechai did not have the Gemara reading "fish," but only wild animals and birds; but for us, that the Gemara reading is wild animals and birds and fish, they hunt in private, and likewise in the Yerushalmi also it is proven thus: if so, it is simple that fish too have the same law as wild animals and birds, that craftsmen are forbidden to hunt them on the moed in public. And in truth that is also the reading in Hilchos Pesukos and in Shibbolei HaLeket and in the Ran and in the Ramban in his novellae on Moed Katan; and so Magen Avraham also leans that with craftsmen it is forbidden even regarding fish in public; and his intent is that even if we do not explicitly read "fish" in the Gemara, one still should not permit regarding fish; for according to the opinion of the Rambam, hunting in public for the need of the moed is forbidden only with craftsmen; if so there is no proof from the end of the first chapter, for there it deals without craftsmen; see there. However, according to his words I find the words of the Beis Yosef very difficult: for at first he copied the words of the Rambam and wrote in the name of the Maggid Mishneh that so also wrote the Ramban, and that is primary; and afterwards he copied the words of the Mordechai above, which implies that he also holds like him. And further it is difficult: for it is known that sellers of fruit, clothing, and vessels mentioned in the Gemara also deal with those whose craft is such all year; and therefore they require privacy; for a householder who sells for the need of the moed does not require privacy, as Magen Avraham brought in siman 539, s.k. 11, in the name of poskim. And certainly in such a case sellers of spices mentioned in the Gemara also deal with those whose craft is such all year; and even so we adopt there at the end of the siman that it is permitted to sell them in public, because it is something that does not keep, and everyone knows that it is for the need of the moed. If so, in our matter regarding sale of fish as well, in such a case everyone knows that one who buys fish in the market on Chol HaMoed is not buying to leave them for after the moed, but to eat them on Chol HaMoed or on Yom Tov; so why should we forbid it? And perhaps someone will say that one must distinguish between spice sellers whose craft is such all year and craftsman hunters regarding sale. I will copy what I found, with Hashem's help, in Likkutei HaRamban on Moed Katan, and this is his wording: bird and fish hunters need privacy because they appear as if they are hunting even for after the moed; and further because their work is such, so as not to permit a craft on the moed, for they appear as if they are engaged in their work to earn as is their way on other days of the year, etc.; and this is the privacy that we learned regarding hunters and grinders and regarding a shop open to a stoa, and it is of sellers of fruit, clothing, and vessels: for anyone who becomes a seller to the public appears as if he is engaged in his work on other days of the year; end quote. Behold, it is one reasoning regarding hunters and fruit sellers; and even so he wrote that therefore regarding sale of spices they were lenient to sell in the market, because the matter is noticeable that they are doing it for the need of Yom Tov, for ordinary taking of spices is for the pot of the day; and if so, likewise in our matter regarding sale of fish, it is simple that one should not be stringent. What emerges from our words: that even though regarding hunting it is certainly forbidden even regarding fish with craftsman hunters in public, because it appears that he is engaged in his work as on other days of the year, and according to our Gemara reading of wild animals and birds and fish; but regarding selling them in the market, even the hunters are permitted to sell them in public, for everyone knows that one who buys for the need of the moed is buying; and with this is resolved why the Beis Yosef brought the words of the Mordechai, for there is a practical difference regarding sale; and also in Piskei Tosafos he brings explicitly regarding sale that it is permitted even in public with craftsmen, as regarding spices. And we can also say that even Rashi, who mentioned in the Mishnah regarding sale, likewise there is no proof that he disagrees with the Mordechai regarding fish, for a practical difference from his commentary is only regarding wild animals and birds; and that which is mentioned in the Gemara also fish was mentioned there only regarding hunting.
