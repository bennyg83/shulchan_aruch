(21) All melachos that are for the need of the moed: that is to say, even though in truth it is for the need of the moed, nevertheless when they do etc.; and it deals with a type of melacha that is not proven to the onlooker that it is for the need of the moed, such as those that he concludes; and see below siman 539, se'if 10.
(22) Their craftsmen: for with a private person who does for himself, he does not do in a large measure, and everyone knows that it is for the need of the day; which is not so regarding a craftsman who does a great deal together: they say that he is engaged in his craft as on other days of the year, and he is not preparing for Yom Tov.
(23) Of wild animals and birds: the Rama omitted fish, for he holds that regarding fish, which do not keep for long, even with a hunter everyone knows that he is hunting for the day and not for after Yom Tov, and therefore it is permitted in public, as I wrote above in se'if 4. However, several poskim disagree about this and forbade a hunter whose work is such to hunt fish in public. The poskim wrote: if hunters customarily do not hunt on Chol HaMoed, they may retract from their custom, and this is not like one who practiced a custom of a mitzvah; on the contrary, they diminish the joy of the festival. And it is clarified in the Yerushalmi that hunters of wild animals, birds, and fish are permitted to hunt even with large snares and the like, in order to increase joy on the festival; for if you permit them only with a hook and small nets, they catch only a little and diminish the joy. One who has a large pond and digs beside it so that the water will flow and he will catch the fish: Magen Avraham leans that one can say it is forbidden, for the matter is more proven that it is for weekday need; and it appears to me that one should be lenient in this through a non-Jew if he wants to eat the fish on the moed.
(24) Behold these do in private: that is, both the hunting and the grinding and the grape-harvesting, and the selling, as is clarified below in siman 539, se'if 11. And see Biur Halacha: regarding selling fish, one who practices to be lenient in public, one should not protest against him, for he has upon whom to rely.
