(11) Because etc. the foods: for example figs and dates that they dry in the sun and that must be covered at night because of the dew. And all this if he does it for this need; and even in this he is not permitted to uproot except according to what he needs. And the same law applies if he needs flax seed for hair-washing, for they soak it and the women wash their hair with it to smooth their flesh and brighten their appearance: it is permitted to uproot the flax for this; and even though for this he would have needed to pluck only the head of the stalk, the Sages did not impose on him to change from the way he does on a weekday, and it is permitted to uproot with the root. And also in this it is permitted only according to his need, as above.
(12) That is fit to put into beer: the text should read "to put into beer," for they mix it into beer to give it sharpness. And also in this, [it is permitted] if he does it for this need and also only according to what he needs for the moed; and likewise regarding sesame seeds below.
(13) And to uproot sesame: it is not our sesame which is complete food; rather it is a species of seed from whose kernels they make oil, or they fry them in honey, and they are fit to eat; and they are fit for all this only when they are dry; but the moist ones must be left to wither and dry, and they are not fit until after the moed.
(14) That the dry ones among them etc.: and it implies that it is permitted for him to uproot without sorting, and afterwards he sorts those that are fit to eat and for oil.
