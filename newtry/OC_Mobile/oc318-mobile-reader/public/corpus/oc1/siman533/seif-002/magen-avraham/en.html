"It is permitted to uproot." Rabbeinu Yerucham writes that he may uproot only as much as he needs. Maggid Mishneh explains that flax is needed for women's adornment, to rub upon the face, and since flax is not ordinarily uprooted in portions they permitted uprooting the entire plant when some of it is needed. Beit Yosef cites this. In my view, this explanation has been misunderstood. Maggid Mishneh likewise agrees that one may uproot only what is needed, for this is no more lenient than something required for food. His intent is that the women do not need the entire stalk, only the flaxseed. One might therefore have thought that he should not uproot the plant at all, but merely cut off its upper portion. He teaches that since the plant must be uprooted in order to obtain what is needed in the ordinary manner, the entire stalk may be uprooted. Ran likewise implies this. This appears clear to me.
