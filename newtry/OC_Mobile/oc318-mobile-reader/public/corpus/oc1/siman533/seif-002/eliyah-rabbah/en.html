[2] But one may not use a device etc. And the reason in the Beis Yosef is that the Rambam is a solitary opinion in permitting this ha'aramah, for the Rif did not mention at all the law of ha'aramah, because he holds that it is forbidden; end quote. And I wonder: on the contrary, from the fact that the Rif did not conclude "provided that one not use a device," and in the wording of the beraisa it implies that he holds it is permitted. And it is even more astonishing how the Beis Yosef did not notice what the Rif wrote in chapter "Tolinin," a beraisa that permits with ha'aramah; and it appears that for this reason the Rif did not need to mention it again in chapter "Mi Shehafach." And it also appears that from this sugya the Rambam learned to permit; and in truth this is difficult against the Raavad and those who join him who forbid. And it appears to me that this sugya disagrees with the sugya of chapter "Mi Shehafach." One may also say that the questioner did not know of the beraisa that forbids, and the answerer answers him even according to his words. Nevertheless it is clear that the Rif, who brings the sugya of "Tolinin," holds like the Rambam; and so ruled the Raavan. Also one must examine the Beis Yosef in siman 335, who wrote that the Rambam permits specifically after the fact; yet here he permits ab initio; and here and there it is a dispute of the Sages and Rabbi Yehudah. Afterwards I found that Magen Avraham there also explained the words of the Rambam thus.
