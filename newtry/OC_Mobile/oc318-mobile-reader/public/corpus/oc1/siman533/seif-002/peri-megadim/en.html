See Taz: this is the wording of the Rambam of blessed memory, Hilchos Yom Tov, chapter 7, halacha 9: specifically their craftsmen; and as the Maggid Mishneh wrote in the name of the Ramban of blessed memory: but a private person may hunt in public. And see Magen Avraham 15; and God willing it will be clarified there. And regarding milking cows, the Levush wrote that it is a davar ha'aved, lest their milk flow away; see Eliyah Rabbah on Terumas HaDeshen 153, where it implies that the reason is that it is a layman's act and involves no exertion. And I looked there, and one can say this is the meaning: one must distinguish between melacha of Chol HaMoed, where they did not forbid a layman's act, and Tishah B'Av, where they forbade it; hence one cannot compare one to the other. However, milking cows is not for this reason, but because it is a davar ha'aved, as the Beis Yosef wrote: one can say there is exertion and a craftsman's act to milk well and the like. And see siman 554 in Magen Avraham 25, who brought that of Terumas HaDeshen, and it implies as I wrote; and a davar ha'aved is permitted on Tishah B'Av. Nevertheless, anything that is possible through a non-Jew is not called a loss, and it is forbidden to do it oneself; and from this the same law applies to Chol HaMoed: in such a case of a loss, if it is possible through a non-Jew, indeed it is forbidden through a Jew. And in siman 538, God willing, it will be clarified. And according to this, if he wants the milk for crumbled bread and the like, one can say it is forbidden to milk thus; see siman 505; and this requires further study for now.
