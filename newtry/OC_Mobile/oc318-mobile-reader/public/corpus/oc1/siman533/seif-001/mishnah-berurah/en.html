(1) For the need of the moed: that he has no flour in his house; and even if he can buy or borrow from his fellow, as below in siman 537, se'if 15. And the same law applies that he may also do other melachos in such a case, namely harvesting and sorting, etc., as is clarified there in the aforementioned se'if.
(2) Even if he intended his work: that is to say, he could have prepared it from before Yom Tov, and on his own initiative he left this matter to do on Chol HaMoed; for in several places we are stringent about this, but regarding needs of food they were not stringent about this.
(3) And to chop wood: to heat his oven, as he concludes at the end. And see above in siman 501, s.k. 8 in the Mishnah Berurah, what pertains to our matter.
(4) Whether of barley: for there is much exertion.
(5) But one may not use a device to grind etc.: and if the owner of the mill does not want to grind a small measure, he may grind even a large amount, and it is not like one who intends to leave over; for he is grinding the excess for the sake of that small amount that he needs.
(6) More intentionally: and if he used a device, it is permitted after the fact, since in any case he eats some of the flour or of the beer, as the Mechaber ruled above in siman 503, se'if 1, in such a case. However, according to the commentators who explained there that this is specifically where he acted wantonly, but where he used a device it is forbidden even after the fact, it is possible that the same law applies here that it is forbidden.
(7) And he says "from this I want": and even though he eats from this that he grinds, nevertheless it is ha'aramah, for he does not need this.
(8) Regarding beer: and likewise regarding wheat and barley and the like: if he has flour from them that is not so fine, it is permitted to grind others that are finer than them.
(9) It is better without ha'aramah: that is to say, that in truth the new is superior and he does not intend ha'aramah. And know that there are poskim who rely to be lenient with ha'aramah where he has old and says "I want new beer" and drinks also from the new; and one should not protest against one who wishes to rely on this reasoning. Nevertheless some wrote that even these poskim are lenient only in this, where the ha'aramah is not noticeable, for not everyone knows that he has old beer; but not one who grinds wheat or makes beer and uses a device to make more for after Yom Tov, for everyone sees that he is grinding a large amount as he grinds on a weekday.
(10) It is permitted: the Chayei Adam wrote: it appears to me that even if he has fine clean bread, it is permitted to bake coarser bread for the need of Chol HaMoed; even though on Yom Tov it is forbidden, on Chol HaMoed it is permitted; provided that in any case he also needs some on the moed and does so with expansive measure, and if he has leftovers, they remain.
