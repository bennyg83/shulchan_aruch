It is better without ha'aramah. See in the Mishnah Berurah what we wrote; and know that there are poskim etc.; and one should not protest against one who wishes to rely on this reasoning; and that is the opinion of the Magen Avraham and Eliyah Rabbah in the name of the Rambam and the Raavan. And therefore one should be lenient to join [the case of making] spirits on Chol HaMoed: that is, they put the spirits in a vat and fire beneath it, and the spirits boil and rise through pipes, and the pipes are cooled in water that they draw with a bucket from the well, and several other melachos are involved in this; even so it is permitted when the Jews will drink some of them on Chol HaMoed, since the Magen Avraham holds that even regarding beer it is permitted to use a device when he has old beer; and even though with beer it is not so good to drink so quickly from the new, and need of the moed does not apply so strongly, and with spirits one can drink immediately. And if we say that melacha of Chol HaMoed is rabbinic, certainly it is permitted to use a device; and even if we say it is forbidden according to the Torah, it is also possible that they forbade only with noticeable ha'aramah, and here it is not noticeable (Responsa Shoel U'Meishiv, third edition, part 1, siman 19). However, in the Peri Megadim it implies that according to the opinion that melacha of Chol HaMoed is forbidden according to the Torah, one should not be lenient even with ha'aramah that is not noticeable; and see above in siman 530 in the Biur Halacha. And therefore the proper practice is to conduct oneself as Baer Heitev wrote in the name of Responsa Beis Yaakov; see there.
It is permitted. See in the Mishnah Berurah what we wrote: that even according to the opinion of the poskim who permit with ha'aramah, it is forbidden regarding one who grinds wheat and uses a device to grind more for after Yom Tov, etc. See in Chayei Adam who wrote, and this is his wording: in a time of pressure, for example where he will not be able to bake immediately after the moed, one may rely to permit baking by needing a little of one loaf, even though he uses a device and intentionally makes more, since this ha'aramah is not so noticeable. (And according to what we wrote inside, this is not a permission even according to the lenient opinion; however in Nishmas Adam he clarified the words further: that one may rely in this on that opinion in siman 507, se'if 6, that even with our ovens a person may bake a full oven of bread even though he needs only one loaf.) And all the more so when he makes one large loaf [or one large cake] that involves only one exertion: it is permitted according to all opinions.
