Se'if 1. Even if he intended etc. From what is stated in chapter 2 of Beitzah: "now, fine flour..." And if it were so, vows and freewill offerings would be [cases of] intending, and regarding fine flour we say in chapter 7 of Shabbos that one is liable because of grinding. And see the Rosh, who wrote: specifically regarding a davar ha'aved, intending is forbidden; but something that is a need, even intending is permitted.
Whether of etc. There in the other beraisa.
However, one need not etc. From the fact that it says "provided that one not use a device," specifically ha'aramah is forbidden; but anything not done with ha'aramah is permitted. And see Rashi there, s.v. "provided that..." And as is written in chapter 2 of Beitzah 17b: "but one who cooks..." "provided that..."
Or if etc. Not like the other beraisa, which comes like a solitary opinion; and see what I wrote in siman 335, se'if 3.
But if etc. And the same law applies etc. For what is written: one uses a device and drinks from the new: they called it ha'aramah because new beer is inferior to old; and that is what it means "uses a device"; which is not so regarding warm bread, which is superior. And see at the beginning of chapter 3 of Pesachim and in the first chapter of Chullin; and that is what is written "and the same law applies..." And so wrote the Raavad in the Hasagos.
