Even. See Taz; the Rosh, Moed Katan 12b; Mordechai; and see Beis Yosef. And it implies that something that is not food for the need of the moed is forbidden if he intended his work for the moed. And it also implies that anything for the need of the moed, even from one day to the next, is permitted; only after the moed is it forbidden. See Rashi, Moed Katan 12b; and see siman 530: according to the opinion that melacha of Chol HaMoed is forbidden according to the Torah, then food for the person on Yom Tov for another day, such as from the first [day] for the seventh, is forbidden; yet Chol HaMoed is permitted; and a davar ha'aved is permitted; all are descending ranks. Nevertheless, something that is not food: whether from one day to the next [is permitted] requires further study. Also, where he intended his work for the moed regarding something that is not eating: if they are losing [value], or not: is it comparable to a davar ha'aved? Since he intended his work for the moed he is negligent, and it was not lost from the outset. And see siman 538 in Magen Avraham, letter 2; and God willing it will be clarified there.
