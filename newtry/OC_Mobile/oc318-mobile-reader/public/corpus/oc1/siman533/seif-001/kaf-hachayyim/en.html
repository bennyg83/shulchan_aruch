1) [Seif 1] It is permitted to grind flour for the needs of the festival. Even if he can buy it or borrow it from his fellow, it is permitted to grind, as stated below in siman 537, seif 15. The same applies to other labors that he can perform in this manner, namely harvesting, gathering, and the like, as explained there.
2) There: Even if he intended to perform his labor during the festival. This means that he could have performed it before the festival but intended to perform it during the festival. They did not prohibit it, so that people should not come to diminish the joy of Yom Tov. Levush.
3) There: Even if he intended to perform his labor during the festival. Regarding food needs, they did not prohibit one who intended to perform his labor during the festival. Beis Yosef in the name of Rabbeinu Asher and the Mordechai; Turei Zahav, s.k. 1; Magen Avraham, s.k. 1. Regarding other matters, however, they decreed against one who intended to perform his labor during the festival, as stated below in siman 536, seif 1; see there.
4) There: And to cut wood from that which is attached to the ground. This applies when he needs it for cooking, heating the house, or the like. Chayei Adam, klal 108, paragraph 2. In this case too, even if he intended to perform the labor during the festival, it is permitted. Biur HaGra.
5) There: And to cut wood, etc. However, the consensus of the poskim is to prohibit cutting down a palm tree because he needs its sawdust. Beis Yosef; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1; Bigdei Yesha; Magen Avraham there; Maamar Mordechai, siman 54, in Likkutei Rima, paragraph 2. Regarding chopping detached wood, meticulous people are accustomed to prohibit it even on Chol HaMoed, and they prepare everything before Yom Tov, as stated above in siman 501, paragraph 9; see there.
