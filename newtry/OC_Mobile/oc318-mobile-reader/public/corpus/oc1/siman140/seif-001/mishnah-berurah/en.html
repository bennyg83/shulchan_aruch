1) The one reading from the Torah, etc. All the more so, if he became unable to speak immediately after the blessing, before he began to read, the second person does not fulfill his obligation through the first person’s blessing.
2) From the place where the first person began. This means that he should not begin from the place where the first person stopped and then recite a blessing before and after the remaining verses. Were he to do so, the verses read by the first person would have a blessing before them but none afterward, while the later verses would have a blessing afterward but none before them. He therefore begins where the first person began and recites a blessing before and after the reading.
3) And he recites the opening blessing. He cannot rely on the opening blessing recited by the person who became unable to speak, because one called to the Torah does not recite the blessing to discharge others. Rather, each person called to the Torah recites his own blessing. See the Acharonim, who write that when the replacement repeats the blessing, he does not repeat Barechu; he repeats only the blessing itself, which is the essential blessing over the Torah.
4) According to Rambam, etc. Rambam maintains that the replacement relies on the first person’s blessing. The Acharonim write that the first opinion is primary.
5) Even nowadays, etc. The reason is that we regard what the prayer leader reads as though the person called to the Torah himself were reading.
