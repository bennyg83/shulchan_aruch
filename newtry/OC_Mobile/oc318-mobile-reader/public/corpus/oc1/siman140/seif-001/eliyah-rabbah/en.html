[1] And according to Rambam, etc. In Eliyahu Zuta I concluded that the primary halachah follows the first opinion, contrary to several later authorities; see there at length. I also found this stated explicitly in Sefer Adam VeChavah, folio 19, Shibolei HaLeket, and Tanyah, and this is the accepted practice. It appears to me at first glance that the second reader should begin from “Blessed are You,” which is the essential beginning of Bircas HaTorah, but he should not say Barchu, since the congregation already fulfilled that obligation. Likewise, above in siman 138, where one read only two verses and must return and recite the blessing, this appears to me to be the law. This is also somewhat implied by the incident involving Maharil that I will cite in this siman. Nevertheless, I hesitate to rule this way because the poskim wrote without qualification, and one may also distinguish between the cases.
