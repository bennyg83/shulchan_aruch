And some say that he must. Here the Mechaber does not decide the matter, but in Beit Yosef he rules that another blessing is required, as he writes in siman 206. This also appears to be the primary halachah, based on what we proved in Yoreh Deah, siman 279, concerning a Torah scroll found to be invalid. That case is not comparable to a lupine that fell from one’s hand, because the matter depends on intent. With a Torah scroll, one is obligated to read, and therefore he certainly intended that if the first scroll were found invalid he would read from a second, since he cannot exempt himself from the obligation. With a lupine, however, he has no obligation to eat, and therefore his intent extended only to what he planned at that moment. Here too, since at the time of the blessing he thought he would read this passage and another never entered his mind, he must recite a second blessing afterward. Nevertheless, one may still distinguish. This applies specifically where he was shown an entirely different passage, as in Abudraham’s incident, where he was supposed to read the Rosh Chodesh passage but was shown the Chanukah passage, and therefore had to roll the Torah scroll from one place to another. In that case he must recite another blessing. If, however, the error occurred within the same passage, and the verses actually appropriate for him were also visible without rolling the scroll elsewhere, he need not recite another blessing, because the Torah scroll is open before him and his intent encompasses everything visible before him. This appears correct to me, with Hashem’s help. See what I wrote in siman 489.
