Not. See Taz. Nowadays, certainly each person recites the blessing for himself, and no one fulfills the obligation through another person’s blessing. See Bach and the Yerushalmi, and what I wrote in letter 1 concerning this. At first glance, whether the listener must intend to fulfill his obligation through the blessing depends on whether mitzvos require intent; see Magen Avraham, siman 60, seif katan 3, and siman 213, seif 3, and Be’er HaGolah there, letter 9. One could say that Rabbeinu Yoel follows the opinion that mitzvos do not require intent. Alternatively, in a case where one performs no act, all agree that intent is required, even for a D’Rabbanan obligation. Even if speech generally does not require intent, this cannot be proven from the case of hearing the Megillah. With Hashem’s help, this will be explained elsewhere.
