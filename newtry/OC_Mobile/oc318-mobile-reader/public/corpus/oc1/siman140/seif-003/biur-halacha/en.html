(1) And he blessed on the Torah — and if he began to say Baruch Atah and remembered — then if it is a nearby place that does not require a great interruption until he rolls to the proper place, such as the nearby column before him or after him — then they show him immediately the proper place and he finishes his blessing; but if he must roll and because of the delay of rolling there will be a noticeable interruption between mention of the Name and the blessing — he should finish immediately "lamdeini chukecha" so the mention of the Name not be in vain, and roll to the proper place and begin the blessing again; and if he already also said Elokeinu — then he cannot finish again "lamdeini chukecha" — then he should rely that he finishes the blessing after rolling to the proper place, even if there is some delay, since interruption of silence is not considered much of an interruption [Shaarei Efrayim].

(2) And he began to read, etc. — see Magen Avraham who questions whether since he began reading his blessing is interrupted, perhaps he need not return and bless; the Gra and Avraham Zuckerman and Shakh agreed the law is with the Mechaber.

(3) And they reminded him, etc. — see Peri Megadim who holds specifically if at the time of blessing he knew the portion he blesses on and intended only for it, but after the blessing it became known he must read another portion — therefore one returns and blesses with intent, for at blessing he did not intend on it; not so if the matter itself was in error, such as they opened the sefer Torah and did not check to see and thought the day's obligation portion was written there and blessed on it, and afterward it became known it is another portion and they had to roll to the proper place — this is not an interruption, since his intent was initially for this portion. Baal Dvar Chaim brought proof from siman 209 seif 2 in hagahah that one need not distinguish — see there.
