(8) Some say he need not, etc. — their reason is that presumptively his intent is on all portions of the Torah laid before him.

(9) And some say he must — bless when reading the portion fit to read; their reason is he certainly did not intend in his blessing only for the portion shown him beforehand, and therefore he must bless anew on this portion. Later authorities wrote that in practice they conduct according to this view. There is no practical difference whether the place shown him initially was an entirely different portion or even in the same order but he must roll from the first place to this place — in all cases he must return and bless, for certainly his intent in his blessing was not on what is not unrolled before him. [Chayei Adam and other later authorities — one who is lenient not to return and bless if the place shown him was in the same order of this week, even though he must roll from place to place, none protest his hand, for he has whom to rely upon.] If this portion fit for him to read was also unrolled before him when they showed him the first place — the Taz and Shakh agreed he need not return and bless, for his intent was on all that is unrolled before him. Even if the place shown him initially was from the past week's order, since this week's order is also unrolled before him, his intent is on all that is unrolled; one need not distinguish whether the fit portion was on this column or the second column, for presumptively his intent is on all that is open before him. Also there is no distinction whether the place shown him was above and the fit place to read is below, or they showed him below and the fit place was above — in every case he need not return and bless, since he need not roll; but if they showed him below and the fit place is above and he returns to the fit place, it is good that he read until the place they showed him initially and a few verses below.
