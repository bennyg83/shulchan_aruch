8) Some say that he need not, etc. Their reason is that his intention presumably encompasses all the Torah portions in the scroll lying before him.
9) And some say that he must. He must recite another blessing when he reads the proper portion. Their reason is that he certainly intended through his original blessing only the portion initially shown to him, and he must therefore recite a new blessing over this portion. The Acharonim write that the practical custom follows this latter opinion. It makes no difference whether the place initially shown to him belonged to an entirely different portion or was in the same weekly sidrah but required rolling from the first place to the correct one; in every such case he must repeat the blessing, because he certainly did not intend what was not open before him. Chayei Adam and other Acharonim. If one is lenient and does not repeat the blessing when the place shown was within that week’s sidrah, even though the scroll must be rolled from one place to another, he should not be protested, for he has authorities upon whom to rely. If the proper portion was also open before him when the first place was shown, Turei Zahav and Shaarei Ephraim agree that he need not repeat the blessing, because his intention encompassed everything open before him. This applies even if the place initially shown was from the previous week’s sidrah, provided that the correct portion was also open before him. There is no distinction whether the correct portion was in the same column or the other column, nor whether the initially indicated place was above and the proper place below, or the reverse; whenever no rolling is required, he need not repeat the blessing. If the place shown was below while the proper place was above and he returns to the proper place, it is preferable that he continue reading until the place originally shown to him and then read several additional verses beyond it.
