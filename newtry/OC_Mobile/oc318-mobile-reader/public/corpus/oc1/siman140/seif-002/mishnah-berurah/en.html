6) And he read some verses. There is no difference whether he read one verse or several. If, however, he had not yet read anything and interrupted (even for a matter of Torah) then, if it was unrelated to the reading, he must repeat the blessing. This applies even if he spoke only one word, just as with all blessings over mitzvos or benefits: one must repeat the blessing if he interrupted immediately afterward, even when he knows with certainty that he did not divert his attention, because the mitzvah had not yet begun and the blessing therefore had nothing upon which to take effect. If he spoke for the needs of the reading, he need not repeat the blessing, though even this is initially prohibited. There is no distinction between Lashon HaKodesh and a secular language.
7) It is not an interruption. That is, it does not require him to recite a new blessing when he resumes reading, because the opening blessing has not yet ceased to apply. Even if he diverted his attention from reading further and afterward changed his mind and resumed, he need not repeat the blessing. Some authorities disagree regarding a case in which he diverted his attention, and one is lenient in a case of doubt concerning blessings. Nevertheless, it is proper for the person reciting the blessing to intend initially that even if he diverts his attention, he will resume reading on the strength of this blessing. Even so, initially it is prohibited to interrupt in the middle of the reading, even without diverting his attention at all.
