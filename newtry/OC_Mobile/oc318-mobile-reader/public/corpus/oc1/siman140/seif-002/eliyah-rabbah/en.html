[2] And he read part, etc. This implies that if he interrupted before reading anything at all, he must return and recite the blessing. This is likewise implied by Bedek HaBayis, which compares this case to the blessing over a meal; see there. This is difficult, however, because above in siman 47, seif 9, regarding Bircas HaTorah, we rule that even if one interrupted before learning, he need not return and recite the blessing. It is unlike the blessing over bread or fruit because one is constantly obligated to learn Torah. Perhaps one may distinguish between the morning Bircas HaTorah, which applies to the continual obligation of Torah study, and the blessing of one called to the Torah, whose primary intent is directed to this particular reading. Since he did not begin, he must return and recite the blessing. Furthermore, I wrote there that Bach concludes that he must return and recite it; see there.
