And even. See Taz and Beit Yosef, who require the person called to the Torah to read together with the chazzan, in accordance with their view in siman 141, seif 2, and Taz there, seif katan 3. According to our practice, however, a blind person may be called to the Torah, as stated in siman 139, seif 3, because hearing is equivalent to reading. At first glance, the principle that hearing is equivalent to reading should not apply to a blind person, because anything unfit for the required act may be disqualified from fulfilling it through another. Yet we rule in Yoreh Deah, siman 1, that a mute person may slaughter initially if another person recites the blessing, contrary to Hagahos Ashiri in the name of Or Zarua, who remains uncertain because of this principle. See also siman 213, seif 3, where a mute person may fulfill the blessing through another person in connection with slaughter. According to this, if a distinguished person and leading Torah scholar is mute, perhaps another person may recite the blessing, while he is called to the Torah and the chazzan reads. This requires study.
