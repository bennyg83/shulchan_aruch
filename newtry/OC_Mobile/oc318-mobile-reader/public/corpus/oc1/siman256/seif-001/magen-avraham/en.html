Close to Shabbat. Bach wrote that half an hour close to minchah they announce, and it is in place of the first tekiyah; and barechu is in place of the sixth tekiyah — therefore this requires further study, for the second tekiyah was to stop the people from melacha, and it was at least at the time of minchah ketanah, which is two and a half hours before night, as siman 251. So too it is appropriate to practice sending a man to stop those engaged in melacha from their work; also shopkeepers should close their shops about an hour before Shabbat, for I have seen the great stumbling-block that sometimes one official comes and bargaining continues until actual nightfall — "their guilt is upon your heads" is written. Also most of the world have a leniency that as long as they have not said barechu, even though it is actually night, they do all they need; this I have seen with my own eyes. Therefore barechu should be said early, and one should not wait for a great man who has not yet come if it is close to nightfall, for it is a merit for him that Shabbat not be desecrated through him — "the wise man has his eyes in his head."
