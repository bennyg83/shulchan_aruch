And they also practice sending a man to stop those engaged in melacha from their work, and shopkeepers should also close about an hour before Shabbat, for I have seen a great stumbling-block: sometimes an official comes and the bargaining continues until night, and "their guilt is upon your heads" is written. Also, most of the world have a mistaken leniency that as long as they have not said barechu, even when it is actually night, they do all they need for Shabbat; this I have seen with my own eyes. Therefore barechu should be said early, and one should not wait for an important and great person who has not yet come, for it is a merit for him that Shabbat not be desecrated through him — "the wise man has his eyes in his head" (Magen Avraham). I found one who had not prepared his food: one may wait with barechu; but if it is close to accepting Shabbat, they should not wait. Shaarei Chaim siman 262, and in Magen Avraham siman 263 seif kaf, see there.
