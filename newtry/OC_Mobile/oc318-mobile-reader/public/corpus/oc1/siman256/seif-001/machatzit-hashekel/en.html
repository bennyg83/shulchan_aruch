(א) (ס"ק א') Close, etc. — and it was at least at the time of minchah ketanah. He means to say: for he wrote above siman 251 that from minchah and above melacha is forbidden, but the poskim there disagree whether he means minchah gedolah or minchah ketanah; nevertheless, even for one who is lenient there, from minchah ketanah and above melacha is forbidden — if so, necessarily what we say in the Gemara that the second tekiyah was to stop the people from melacha must mean the second tekiyah was two and a half hours before night, when the time of minchah ketanah begins, as written siman 251. The first tekiyah was much before that, as Magen Avraham wrote in siman 251 — and how did Bach write that the first tekiyah was half an hour close to minchah? So too we should announce half an hour close to minchah — but our way is not to advance praying Minchah on Erev Shabbat two and a half hours before night.

(ב) Like an hour before Shabbat — even though he wrote that in any case we need two and a half hours before night, why did he write that an hour before Shabbat suffices? It must be that his intent in what he wrote "before Shabbat" means before accepting Shabbat; and so is our way, to advance praying Maariv on Erev Shabbat more than on weekdays, as siman 267. Perhaps Magen Avraham's measure is an hour and a half before night, the time of our Maariv prayer on Erev Shabbat; therefore it suffices if they advance stopping melacha one hour before accepting Shabbat, which combined is two and a half hours. Or one may say he chose the lesser evil, for it is impossible to lead the public to stop their way to which they accustomed themselves; therefore at least the leaders of the generation should stop about an hour so they not stumble, as he wrote afterward — not to do melacha and to close shops, for both have the same law, and it was at the time of the second tekiyah as explained in the Gemara.

(ג) And also most of the world, etc. — Now he speaks of the matter of announcement, and now he wished to warn that they advance saying barechu, which is accepting Shabbat.
