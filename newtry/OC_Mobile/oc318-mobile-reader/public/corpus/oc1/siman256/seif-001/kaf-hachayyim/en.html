1) [Seif 1] When the Jewish people lived securely in their settlements, they would sound blasts on Friday, etc. It appears that if someone now wishes to institute the sounding of blasts in his city in order to cause the people to cease labor, he may do so. The practice was discontinued only because of fear of the non-Jews. Now that circumstances are unlike those of previous years, every city can restore the ancient enactment. This practice existed even outside Eretz Yisrael, as Maaseh Rokeach writes in chapter 5 of Hilchos Shabbos, halachah 18; see there. Regrettably, other nations have a large suspended bell, struck by a clapper called a campana, whose sound is heard from afar. Why should the Jewish people be at a disadvantage and not establish a signal to cause the people to cease labor on Shabbos and Yom Tov, to announce the times of the three prayers, and for similar purposes, as was done in the Beis HaMikdash? Ruach Chaim, paragraph 1.
