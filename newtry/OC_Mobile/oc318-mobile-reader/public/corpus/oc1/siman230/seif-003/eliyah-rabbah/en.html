(3) When he wishes to leave etc. — Aruchot Chaim wrote in Sulat Belulah the text is l'shalom on entry and b'shalom on exit, and this is correct; and Aruchot Chaim concluded that one should say that You took me out b'shalom and as You took me out b'shalom so lead us b'shalom and support us b'shalom, until here. And I wonder — I checked that in the nusach written in Shulchan Aruch and Levush it is so in the gemara, Ba'al Halachot Gedolot, Rif, Rambam, Rosh, Semak, Rabbeinu Chananel, Rokeach — in all of them l'shalom; also there is support from the gemara end of Berachos: one who takes leave of his fellow should not say to you b'shalom but rather lecha l'shalom, for Yitro said to Moshe lech l'shalom and succeeded, and David who said to Avshalom lech b'shalom went and was hanged. But I found in Perishah that he wrote on the above gemara that when he said lecha b'shalom the meaning of his speech is that in the place he goes there will be peace — but not all the time he is occupied in going with this matter; and what Yaakov wrote 'And I will return in peace to my father's house' and Yitro said 'All this people upon its place will come b'shalom' — there b'shalom refers to coming and returning and that was the end of their intent, which is not so lecha b'shalom — one who goes to some place for some need and purpose needs that there also be peace for him, until here. And according to this reason one can say b'shalom in some matters — but who will stick his head against the nusach of the gemara and the great ones mentioned above.
