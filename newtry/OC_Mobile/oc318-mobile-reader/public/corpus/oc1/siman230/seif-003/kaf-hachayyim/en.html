3) Even after forty days, he may pray that this child be viable offspring who engages in Torah, mitzvos, and good deeds. Eliyah Rabbah there in the name of Shulchan Gavoah. See Shaar HaGilgulim, introduction 10, which writes that everything depends upon the thoughts of the man and woman at the time of marital relations. The surrounding light is drawn from the man and the inner light from the woman. If both direct their thoughts toward good, the child will be completely righteous in both the inner and surrounding lights. If both direct their thoughts toward evil, the child will be wicked in both the inner and surrounding lights. If the father's thought is good and the mother's evil, the surrounding light will be good and the inner light evil. Over time, the surrounding light will compel the inner light and transform it to merit; see there. Therefore, every person must instruct his wife as well to maintain a good thought at that time, meaning that both should intend for the sake of a mitzvah, so that the child will be righteous in both the inner and surrounding lights.
