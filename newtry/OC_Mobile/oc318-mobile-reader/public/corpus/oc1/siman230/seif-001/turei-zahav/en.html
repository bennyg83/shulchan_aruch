When he wishes to leave etc. Like Beit Yosef, and these are his words: from explanation of the tradition it appears they required him to pray for peace of the road once when leaving the walled city and he does not sign it — and this is the matter here; otherwise when he signs it as said in siman 110. And one should wonder — why two are needed; it would have been fitting to fix the closing in this prayer when praying upon leaving in peace and that would suffice, as in Kol Bo siman 87 — one entering a walled city prays twice: on entry says yehi ratzon etc., entering in peace etc., leaving in peace says modah ani etc. and as You took me out in peace so lead me in peace and support me in peace etc. Blessed are You Who hears prayer — and so here in Shulchan Aruch; and this is tefillat haderech etc. This deals with one entering a walled city and lodging there — we require tefillat haderech a second time, or he dwells in a walled city and leaves at the beginning of his journey — then he needs two prayers, one for leaving the walled city and one for the road; therefore he said to make one prayer from both and sign it, as written here. But if he dwells in a city that is not a walled city and there is no fear at all in leaving the city, only tefillat haderech because of road fear — on a day when he goes through a walled city and does not lodge there, when leaving from it he need not say tefillat haderech since it is the same day, only the prayer stated here for leaving the walled city — then he says as written here without closing. All this appears the simple intent of Beit Yosef and Shulchan Aruch and Kol Bo; and Mahari Veil wrote on it in vain and the rest leave to its Master for he is an important man with supporters.
