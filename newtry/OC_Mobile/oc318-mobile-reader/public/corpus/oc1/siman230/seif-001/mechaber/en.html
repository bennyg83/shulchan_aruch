The law of several particular blessings. It contains 5 seifim: One who prays concerning something that has already occurred, such as one who enters a city, hears a cry in the city, and says, 'May it be Your will that this cry is not from my household,' or whose wife is pregnant and, after 40 days of pregnancy, says, 'May it be Your will that my wife give birth to a male,' has uttered a prayer in vain. Rather, a person should pray concerning what is yet to come and give thanks for what has passed. Thus, one who enters a city says, 'May it be Your will, Hashem our God, that You bring me into this city in peace.' If he entered in peace he says, 'I thank You, Hashem our God, that You brought me into this city in peace.' When he wishes to leave, he says, 'May it be Your will, Hashem our God, that You take me out of this city in peace.' If he left in peace he says, 'I thank You, Hashem my God, that You took me out of this city in peace; just as You took me out in peace, so may You lead me in peace,' etc., through 'Blessed are You, Hashem, who hears prayer.' This is Tefillat HaDerech as recorded, and all its laws are in siman 110.
