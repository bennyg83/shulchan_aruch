1) [Seif 1] And he heard the sound of a cry, etc. A person should not accustom himself to fear, as it is stated, 'For the thing I greatly feared came upon me,' and, 'Sinners in Tziyon were afraid.' The verse, 'Fortunate is the person who always fears,' refers only to words of Torah, meaning that he should always fear for them and take care not to forget them. Riaz in Piskei Halachos on the chapter HaRoeh in Berachos; Yafeh LaLev, paragraph 1. This is from Berachos 60a; see there.
1) A person should not request something contrary to nature, such as praying that his wife, who miscarried in the eighth month, should deliver a surviving child. Likewise, it is forbidden to pray that the Holy One, blessed is He, perform a miracle for him by changing the world's order, such as that his tree produce fruit before its proper time. Sefer Chassidim, siman 794. Kisei Shlomo on Shabbos 21b writes the same; see there.
2) There: After forty days, etc. Within forty days, however, his prayer can help, because the fetus's formation is not complete until forty days. Levush. Prayer within forty days helps if both produced seed simultaneously. If the man produced seed first, she bears a female and his prayer is ineffective. If the woman produced seed first, she bears a male without his prayer. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1. This is from the Gemara there. The unqualified wording of the halachic authorities implies that throughout the first forty days he may pray, because perhaps they both produced seed simultaneously. Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 1.
3) Even after forty days, he may pray that this child be viable offspring who engages in Torah, mitzvos, and good deeds. Eliyah Rabbah there in the name of Shulchan Gavoah. See Shaar HaGilgulim, introduction 10, which writes that everything depends upon the thoughts of the man and woman at the time of marital relations. The surrounding light is drawn from the man and the inner light from the woman. If both direct their thoughts toward good, the child will be completely righteous in both the inner and surrounding lights. If both direct their thoughts toward evil, the child will be wicked in both the inner and surrounding lights. If the father's thought is good and the mother's evil, the surrounding light will be good and the inner light evil. Over time, the surrounding light will compel the inner light and transform it to merit; see there. Therefore, every person must instruct his wife as well to maintain a good thought at that time, meaning that both should intend for the sake of a mitzvah, so that the child will be righteous in both the inner and surrounding lights.
4) There: One who enters a city should recite a blessing, etc. Wicked rulers are present there and seek pretexts against people. Rashi on Berachos 54a; Magen Avraham, paragraph 2.
5) Although the Gemara's first version establishes this as referring to a city where people are not judged and executed, its second version says that it applies even to a city where people are judged and executed. The halachah follows the second version, especially since the blessing contains no mention of the Name and kingship and is merely prayer and thanksgiving. Raavad, of blessed memory, writes the same in his glosses on Baal HaMaor, of blessed memory, at the end of Berachos, cited in Temim Deim, siman 240; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2. The Gemara there gives the reason that sometimes no person happens to be present to argue in his favor. Therefore, he must pray before the Omnipresent. The Yerushalmi there says that this applies even in Jewish encampments. Eliyah Rabbah there. Chayei Adam, rule 64, paragraph 4, writes that the same applies when there is concern for financial loss: one must pray.
