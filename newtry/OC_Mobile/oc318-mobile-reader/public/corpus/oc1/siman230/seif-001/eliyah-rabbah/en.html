[Levush] But within, etc. The reason is that perhaps both husband and wife emitted seed at the same time. If he emitted first, she bears a female and his prayer cannot help; if she emitted first, she bears a male even without his prayer. Solet Belulah writes that even after forty days one may still pray that this child be living and enduring offspring, occupied in Torah, mitzvot, and good deeds.
One who enters a city, etc. Rashi explains that such places contain corrupt rulers who seek accusations. The Gemara's second version says that even in a place with judges who execute only according to law, one must still be concerned, because as a foreigner he may have no one available to argue merit on his behalf. Knesset HaGedolah rules according to this version, as do Tamim Deim and Abudarham. The Yerushalmi applies the prayer even among settlements of Jews.
When he wishes to leave, etc. Orchot Chaim cites Solet Belulah as reading "in peace" upon entering and "to peace" upon leaving, and says this is correct. Orchot Chaim concludes that one should say: "You brought me out in peace; just as You brought me out in peace, so may You lead us to peace and support us in peace." I am surprised, because I checked the text used by Shulchan Aruch and Levush and found that the Gemara, Baal Halachot Gedolot, Rif, Rambam, Rosh, Semag, Semak, Raavan, and Rokeach all say "to peace." There is also support from the Gemara at the end of Berachot: when parting from a friend one should not say "go in peace" but "go to peace." Yitro told Moshe "go to peace" and he succeeded, whereas David told Avshalom "go in peace" and he went and was hanged. Perishah explains that "go in peace" implies peace only at the destination and not throughout the journey, whereas phrases like Yaakov's "I return in peace to my father's house" and Yitro's "all this people will come to their place in peace" attach peace to the arrival itself, which was their final objective. According to this reasoning, some contexts may allow "in peace," but who would lightly disregard the text of the Gemara and the great authorities cited above?
[Levush] And all the prayer, etc. When one lives in a walled or dangerous city and leaves it at the beginning of his journey, or lodged there overnight, he says this prayer with its conclusion, because he is also obligated in Tefilat HaDerech and includes it here. But if he lives in an ordinary city where there is no special fear upon leaving, and during the day passes through such a dangerous city without lodging there, then he says the petition and thanksgiving only until the words concerning an ambush on the road, without a concluding blessing. So Bach and Taz make clear, and Rabbenu Yerucham, Abudarham, and the poskim say explicitly, unlike Magen Avraham, who inaccurately cites Bach as saying to continue until "to peace." His further comments there are also imprecise. Magen Avraham also writes, unlike Olat Tamid, that even after leaving the city one should wait to say the prayer until he has actually begun traveling. This is incorrect. The prayer upon leaving a city is said immediately upon leaving, as I explained in siman 110 seif katan 16. Since he is obligated in that prayer, he may conclude with the blessing that simultaneously fulfills Tefilat HaDerech, for after the fact everyone agrees it is effective, as I explained above. Bach also implies this to one who examines his words.
