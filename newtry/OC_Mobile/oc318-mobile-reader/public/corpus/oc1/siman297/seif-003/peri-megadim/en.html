At a gathering. See Beit Yosef, who notes an apparent contradiction in Tur. The Mechaber rules here as in siman 217, that the prohibition applies specifically at a gathering. In siman 298, seif katan 5, I explained at length that according to Rif and Rosh as well, it applies specifically at a gathering. See there. The reference to siman 239 is a scribal error. If one non Jew and a majority of Jews are reclining there, either the spices belong to the non Jew, in which case this is an ordinary non Jewish gathering, or the spices belong to a Jew, in which case even a majority of non Jews cannot prohibit what is not theirs. See siman 217. With Hashem’s help, this will be explained further in siman 298.
A question. Concerning a valuable, fragrant esrog over which one wishes to recite the blessing on Motzaei Shabbos when no other spices are available, and fruit of orlah became mixed with it in a ratio of less than one to two hundred or more, I discussed this at length in siman 217 in the Magen Avraham section. See Shach, Yoreh Deah, siman 108, seif katan 26, and siman 119, seif katan 27. I also discussed it at length in the laws of festivals. One who is prohibited by a vow from benefiting from another person may not smell his spices, and he may not recite the blessing over that person’s lamp, because this is not a mitzvah for which we say that mitzvos were not given for benefit. See siman 653. It is permitted to smell an esrog designated for the mitzvah, but it is forbidden to smell a hadas designated for the mitzvah, even for Havdalah, because Havdalah is not the mitzvah for which the hadas was designated. A stipulation cannot be made nowadays. See Beit Yosef there. Similarly here, if one recited Havdalah from plag haminchah and then blessed over spices, as in siman 293, one may say that if spices become available on Motzaei Shabbos he should bless again in order to restore his spirit, and this is not a blessing in vain. With Hashem’s help, this will be explained further in Magen Avraham.
