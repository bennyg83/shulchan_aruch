5) There: He recites a blessing over spices, etc. Taz writes in Yoreh De'ah, siman 108, paragraph 10, that pepper may be used for Havdalah, contrary to a text he found stating that one should not use pepper for Havdalah after Shabbos; see there. He writes the same in this siman, paragraph 5. This also appears to be Magen Avraham's view at the beginning of the siman. Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur, paragraph 1, likewise writes that a blessing is recited over crushed pepper; see there. Taz's aforementioned words, however, appear to permit even uncrushed pepper. Olas Tamid, paragraph 1, writes that Knesses HaGedolah's view is correct: specifically crushed pepper may be used because its fragrance is stronger. The same applies to ginger, called imber in the vernacular; a blessing may be recited over it if it is crushed; see there. Nekudos HaKesef there in Yoreh De'ah, however, challenges Taz and writes that Borei Minei Besamim should not be recited over pepper. Furthermore, in cases of doubt regarding blessings we rule leniently; see there. Eliyah Rabbah in this siman, paragraph 1, similarly writes that no blessing should be recited over pepper or ginger, because they are called seasonings rather than spices. He afterward writes that as a matter of practical halachah, perhaps they may be used for Havdalah and a blessing recited over them, but concludes that he lacks authority to be lenient in a case of doubt regarding blessings; see there. Eshel Avraham writes that according to the opinion that pepper is not called a spice, one should recite Hanosein Rei'ach Tov BaPeiros over it, and perhaps there is also no objection if he recites Borei Minei Besamim; see there. See, however, Machazik Berachah, paragraph 5, who discusses the matter and concludes that as a matter of practical halachah, one may recite a blessing over freshly crushed pepper for Havdalah. This is demonstrated and explicitly stated by Responsa Rashba and Maran in the Shulchan Aruch, Yoreh De'ah, siman 108, and by Kol Bo, Orach Chaim, Mateh Moshe, and those who follow them. Nevertheless, because of the dispute, one should recite Borei Minei Besamim, as Orach Chaim writes. Mahari Zayin in Responsa Perach Shoshan, klal 1, siman 13, writes the same; see there. Machazik Berachah writes in Kuntres Acharon there that in pressing circumstances, when one cannot crush the pepper, one may rely upon all the aforementioned great authorities and recite a blessing over whole pepper, but should recite Borei Minei Besamim; see there. Shaarei Teshuvah, paragraph 1, cites this. See Sedei Chemed, Asifas Dinim, system of heh, paragraph 13, who appears to agree with Machazik Berachah that in pressing circumstances it may be used for Havdalah; see there.
