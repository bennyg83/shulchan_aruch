1) [Seif 1] He recites a blessing over spices, etc. Tosafos in Beitzah 33b writes that the reason for reciting a blessing over spices after Shabbos is the departure of the additional soul, whereas there is no additional soul on Yom Tov. When Yom Tov occurs immediately after Shabbos, no blessing is recited over spices because the important and fine foods settle his mind without the fragrance of spices. These are their words. Bach writes that the blessing over spices was instituted only when two factors coincide: the soul's loss and the beginning of the fire of Gehinnom, which burns and emits a foul odor. Since a person's mind is weakened by the soul's departure, the foul odor of Gehinnom's fire can easily cause him weakness. When there is no loss of soul, however, the foul odor need not concern us, just as it does not concern us during the weekdays. Therefore, no blessing is recited after Yom Tov, although there is a foul odor, because there is no loss of soul. Similarly, no blessing is recited when Yom Tov begins after Shabbos, although the soul departs, because there is no foul odor. This appears correct. These are his words.
2) There: He recites a blessing over spices, etc. Regarding fragrant wood, one should make the esoteric intention for fragrance explained by us concerning the fragrance of myrtle on Shabbos night itself; see there. Of the two myrtle bundles prepared for Shabbos, as is known, take care when purchasing them that they contain three valid three-leafed myrtle branches. After Shabbos, when reciting Borei Atzei Besamim, take only those three branches in your hand and smell them. These three myrtles should be tied in one knot as a single bundle. Intend that the three myrtles correspond to nefesh, ruach, and neshamah, and intend to retain some of the additional nefesh, ruach, and neshamah added to you on Shabbos. This is accomplished now through these three myrtles and smelling them. It is already known that all actions performed after Shabbos are intended to retain part of the additional Shabbos sanctity for the coming weekdays. Shaar HaKavanos, folio 60b. One should smell the myrtle three times, corresponding to nefesh, ruach, and neshamah. Pri Etz Chaim, gate 18, chapter 24. It appears that even where myrtle is unavailable and the blessing is recited over other spices, one should take three branches of those spices, intending that they correspond to nefesh, ruach, and neshamah, as stated above. See above, siman 216, paragraph 16, for what we wrote regarding the intention for fragrance; see there.
3) One who fasts on Shabbos must recite a blessing over spices, unlike on Yom Kippur. Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur, paragraph 2; Magen Avraham at the beginning of the siman; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 2. Ginas Veradim, klal 1, siman 8, also agrees; see there. Machazik Berachah, paragraph 1.
4) There: He recites a blessing over spices, etc. When reciting Borei Atzei Besamim after Shabbos, he should hold them in their natural direction of growth. Magen Avraham there in the name of Mahari Weil. If he recites Borei Isvei Besamim or Borei Minei Besamim, which does not mention trees, he need not hold them in their natural direction of growth. Machatzis HaShekel. Nevertheless, the correct practice is to hold even a species that is not woody in its natural direction of growth while reciting the blessing.
5) There: He recites a blessing over spices, etc. Taz writes in Yoreh De'ah, siman 108, paragraph 10, that pepper may be used for Havdalah, contrary to a text he found stating that one should not use pepper for Havdalah after Shabbos; see there. He writes the same in this siman, paragraph 5. This also appears to be Magen Avraham's view at the beginning of the siman. Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur, paragraph 1, likewise writes that a blessing is recited over crushed pepper; see there. Taz's aforementioned words, however, appear to permit even uncrushed pepper. Olas Tamid, paragraph 1, writes that Knesses HaGedolah's view is correct: specifically crushed pepper may be used because its fragrance is stronger. The same applies to ginger, called imber in the vernacular; a blessing may be recited over it if it is crushed; see there. Nekudos HaKesef there in Yoreh De'ah, however, challenges Taz and writes that Borei Minei Besamim should not be recited over pepper. Furthermore, in cases of doubt regarding blessings we rule leniently; see there. Eliyah Rabbah in this siman, paragraph 1, similarly writes that no blessing should be recited over pepper or ginger, because they are called seasonings rather than spices. He afterward writes that as a matter of practical halachah, perhaps they may be used for Havdalah and a blessing recited over them, but concludes that he lacks authority to be lenient in a case of doubt regarding blessings; see there. Eshel Avraham writes that according to the opinion that pepper is not called a spice, one should recite Hanosein Rei'ach Tov BaPeiros over it, and perhaps there is also no objection if he recites Borei Minei Besamim; see there. See, however, Machazik Berachah, paragraph 5, who discusses the matter and concludes that as a matter of practical halachah, one may recite a blessing over freshly crushed pepper for Havdalah. This is demonstrated and explicitly stated by Responsa Rashba and Maran in the Shulchan Aruch, Yoreh De'ah, siman 108, and by Kol Bo, Orach Chaim, Mateh Moshe, and those who follow them. Nevertheless, because of the dispute, one should recite Borei Minei Besamim, as Orach Chaim writes. Mahari Zayin in Responsa Perach Shoshan, klal 1, siman 13, writes the same; see there. Machazik Berachah writes in Kuntres Acharon there that in pressing circumstances, when one cannot crush the pepper, one may rely upon all the aforementioned great authorities and recite a blessing over whole pepper, but should recite Borei Minei Besamim; see there. Shaarei Teshuvah, paragraph 1, cites this. See Sedei Chemed, Asifas Dinim, system of heh, paragraph 13, who appears to agree with Machazik Berachah that in pressing circumstances it may be used for Havdalah; see there.
