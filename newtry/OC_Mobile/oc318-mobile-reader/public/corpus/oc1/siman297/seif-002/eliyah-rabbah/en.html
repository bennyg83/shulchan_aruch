[3] One does not recite a blessing, etc. Although this was already written in siman 217, it may teach here that even when he has nothing else, the mitzvah is omitted, and that if he blessed he did not fulfill the obligation, as the conclusion indicates. This also answers Magen Avraham's objection to Olat Tamid for omitting fragrances associated with forbidden relations, even though the law was already written earlier.
[4] Of a gathering, etc. This implies specifically a gathering, but otherwise it is permitted, as Nachalat Tzvi rules. Taz in siman 298 concludes that in practice one should be lenient only regarding a merchant. See siman 217, seif katan 9.
[5] He did not fulfill the obligation, etc. It is surprising to me how this differs from shofar and lulav (Malbushei Yom Tov). Lechem Chamudot likewise leaves the question unresolved. In my humble opinion there is no difficulty, because there the reason is that mitzvot were not given for physical benefit, as explained below in siman 586, whereas here the purpose of the mitzvah itself is enjoyment.
