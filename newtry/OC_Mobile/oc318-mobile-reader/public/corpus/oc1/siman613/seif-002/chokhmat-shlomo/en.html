Se'if 2 In the gloss: "And he should not intend the pleasure of washing, but only to remove the evil spirit from the hands." Note: This is difficult for me. What is the concern if he intends it? It is a case where it is impossible and he intends it, for he is compelled to wash because of the evil spirit. What does it matter if he also intends his pleasure? Granted, we rule that where it is impossible and he intends it, it is forbidden; that is only with prohibitions of benefit. But on Yom Kippur, are the waters items from which benefit is forbidden? They are permitted for benefit, and only the act is forbidden. Since he is compelled to perform the act, what does it matter if he also intends his pleasure? There is an even greater difficulty from the Talmud, which says in chapter 8 of Yoma: when they disagree in a case where it is impossible, in chullin, one master holds that tevel is more severe, and one master holds that terumah is more severe, etc. This is difficult: from where do we know that they disagree about this? Perhaps according to everyone terumah is more severe, and they disagree about the following: the Kol Bo wrote that one who is forbidden by vow to benefit from a shofar, another may blow it for him, but he himself should not blow it, because many people derive benefit when they blow, and one must be concerned lest he also intend his own benefit. If so, here too one can say that if we feed him tevel, there is also bodily benefit, and one must be concerned lest he intend his benefit and not preservation of life, and it would be forbidden. But if one separates the tevel, this separation contains no bodily benefit and he does not intend his benefit through it. Also, since if he does not separate it, he will be permitted to eat it as it is, he has no reason to intend benefit in this separation. Consequently, since they made a reminder for him by separating first, it will be a reminder that it is forbidden to intend his own benefit, and therefore even with the terumah he will not come to intend his benefit, but only preservation of life. The other opinion holds like Rabbi Yehudah, that where it is impossible and he intends it, it is permitted according to him, as stated in chapter 2 of Pesachim. Therefore he holds that we feed him tevel, and they disagree about a case where it is impossible and he intends it. Or perhaps he holds that we are not concerned that he will also intend his benefit. From where do we know that he holds tevel is more severe? There is another difficulty from the view of the Raavad and the Ran, who discuss whether we slaughter for a sick person or feed him neveilah. Why not say this reason: that we slaughter for him because if we feed him neveilah perhaps he will also intend bodily benefit, whereas slaughtering contains no bodily benefit, and he also knows that if they do not slaughter for him, he will be permitted to eat as is. If so, there is no reason in slaughtering to intend bodily benefit; therefore we slaughter for him. Why did they not write this reason? Perforce, they do not hold like the Hagahos Maimoniyos in this, but rather that if he intends it, there is no prohibition. Perhaps one can distinguish: specifically with washing, where he comes to remove the evil spirit and not for bodily pleasure at all, it is forbidden for him to intend bodily pleasure. This is unlike a sick person, where in the end the eating is the health of his body, to keep his life, and since in the end it is bodily benefit, it is permitted even if he does not intend preservation of life but generally his benefit. One can also say that preservation of life is different. This is forced, and still requires study.
