Soiled with mud or excrement, etc. However, washing to remove perspiration is forbidden, as stated in the following siman regarding anointing, that it is forbidden in order to remove grime; the same applies to washing. Taz, s.k. 1. This is also the view of the Bach and Pri Chadash. However, the words of the Levush at the beginning of siman 614 below imply that washing is permitted to remove grime. This is also the view of the Magen Avraham there, s.k. 1. So too wrote Eliyah Rabbah there, sec. 1, in the name of Shibolei HaLeket, and Maamar Mordechai there, sec. 1, in the name of Rabbeinu Manoach on the Rambam, chapter 3, see there. Mateh Ephraim, sec. 2, distinguished between the cases: whenever the perspiration causes him distress because he is delicate, it may be permitted in accordance with the Levush and Magen Avraham. One who is not delicate and does not have such a great need for this washing should be stringent not to wash merely to remove perspiration, just as it is forbidden to anoint with oil to remove grime, as explained in the following siman. So too wrote the Mishna Berurah, sec. 2. See above siman 4, seif 21 in the gloss, and our words there, sec. 95.
