By boat - The Kenesses ha-Gedolah wrote that for a Torah scholar it is permitted, as is stated in the chapter Tolin. But he was not precise, for there it is permitted only by way of a stratagem, as I wrote at the end of siman 339; see there in the Beis Yosef.
There is one who forbids - The Tur wrote: if they permitted full washing, all the more so swimming, which is lighter. One can say that since he does not intend washing, there is not so much prohibition; but swimming is subject to the decree because of making a swimmer's barrel, and is forbidden (Bach). It is surprising regarding the Tur, for even to guard produce it is permitted to pass through water; if so, let us say that it is permitted by boat. Yet in the Gemara it is evident that it is forbidden; see Beis Yosef, siman 339, and see Rabbi Yosef Halevi, siman 33. There is another difficulty, for we say explicitly in the Gemara, and it is brought in siman 248, that for a mitzvah matter one may sail on a ship even on Friday, and he stipulates with him that he will rest. Rabbenu Yitzchak explained that it is because of swimming, and the Tur brought this. If so, the words of the Tur contradict each other. Perhaps there it speaks when the non-Jew performs labor for him, while here it speaks in a place where no labor is performed. Something like this appears in the responsum of R. Moshe Alashkar and in Sefer Binyamin Ze'ev. Nevertheless, one should not be lenient at all. So too it appears in the Gemara in the incident of the ferry of Chatzdad. An ordinary ferry means a place where people pass by boats, and even so they did not want to go by boat; see there.
