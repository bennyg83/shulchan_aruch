(18) But to pass, etc. - Washing, which is forbidden only on Yom Kippur, they permitted. But swimming, which is forbidden on every Shabbos of the year, they did not permit. Also, washing is different because he can do it with a change, namely by not removing his hand from beneath his garment, and he has a recognizable sign, as above. This is unlike swimming, where he cannot make such a sign, and there is a decree lest he make a swimmer's barrel to swim on it. Even if the non-Jew who transports it performs no labor for the Jew through this, one should still not be lenient.
(19) In a small boat - And all the more so actual swimming is forbidden.
