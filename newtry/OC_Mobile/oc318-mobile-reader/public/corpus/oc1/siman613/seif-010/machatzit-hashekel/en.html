(Seif katan 10) To minimize, etc. “In practice it appears to me that one should be stringent,” etc.; “I do not know what stringency there is,” etc.; and see siman 498, etc. Meaning, Magen Avraham certainly agrees with Bach as a matter of law that if one can go around, it is preferable to do so rather than cross through water, even though this increases his walking. Magen Avraham’s difficulty concerns Bach’s wording, “It appears to me that one should be stringent,” calling the requirement to go around rather than cross a stringency. Had Rama’s ruling left him a choice to go around or cross, it would be appropriate to call the requirement to go around a stringency. Since according to Rama the law is the reverse, that he should specifically cross and not go around, Bach’s ruling cannot properly be described as a stringency. Nevertheless, Magen Avraham brings support for Bach’s actual ruling, that it is preferable to go around, from simanim 499 and 510.
And a decree lest, etc. He may thereby come to wringing, as stated in the Gemara. This refers to Shabbos, chapter VeEilu Kesharim, 113b: Rabbi Zeira said in the name of Rav Huna that if a person was walking on Shabbos and encountered a water channel, if he can place his first foot on the opposite side before lifting the second, meaning that it is no wider than one stride, he may cross. Otherwise it is forbidden. Rava challenged: What should he do? If he goes around, he increases his walking. If he crosses through the water, his garments may become soaked and he may come to wring them. Rather, since there is no alternative, it is permitted for him to leap across. This is explained above in siman 301, seif 3. Here too, when he crosses through water, one should be concerned that his garments may become soaked, etc. Rosh raises this objection. He answers that here he was permitted to cross only if he does not remove his hand from beneath the edge of his garment. This serves as a reminder, and therefore no decree was made; see Beis Yosef. In any event, going around is preferable, because it entails no concern of wringing at all.
