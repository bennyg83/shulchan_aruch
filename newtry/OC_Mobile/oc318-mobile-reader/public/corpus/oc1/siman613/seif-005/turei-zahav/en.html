Provided that he does not take out, etc. So that he remembers and does not come to wringing. The Tur wrote: Behag wrote that this is specifically up to his neck, but to cross by swimming is forbidden. This is surprising: if they permitted the more severe washing, since they supported it on a verse, all the more so swimming, which is lighter, end quote. The Beis Yosef answered that they permitted washing, which is forbidden only on Yom Kippur, but they did not permit swimming, which is forbidden on every Shabbos of the year. My teacher and father-in-law wrote similarly. This is difficult to me, for the Tur holds that in truth it is permitted to swim on any Shabbos in order to go to his teacher, and he derives it by a kal vachomer from washing on Yom Kippur, which is more severe: all the more so swimming, which is lighter, should be permitted on Yom Kippur and on every Shabbos for the sake of one's teacher, just as they permitted passing through water on every Shabbos for a mitzvah matter, as written in siman 301, se'if 5. It seems to me to answer the Tur's question that washing is different, because he can make a change there, namely that he does not take his hand out from under his garment, and there is a recognizable sign. This is unlike swimming, where no recognizable sign is relevant, and there is a decree lest he make a swimmer's barrel, as above.

In the Gemara, chapter Yom ha-Kippurim, 77b, it says: "and it shall pass up to the loins," from here that it is permitted to pass until the loins; from there onward it says, "which I cannot pass." Why then did he write, "even if it is only until the loins"? Perhaps he was not exact, and wrote stringently.
