(Seif katan 8) Some say, and the Tur, etc. “This is surprising regarding the Tur,” etc. Meaning, according to the Tur, traveling by boat is more lenient than washing. He therefore derives that since one may cross water for a mitzvah, all the more so may one travel by boat. If so, regarding guarding produce, for which crossing water was likewise permitted, as stated in seif 8, all the more so should traveling by boat be permitted. Yet the Gemara proves that one may not travel by boat to guard produce. This is the Gemara in Shabbos 139b cited above, where traveling by boat to guard produce was permitted only for a Torah scholar and specifically through subterfuge, as explained above in seif katan 7.
Rabbeinu Yitzchak explains that the reason is swimming. Meaning, this excludes the other authorities who give different reasons, as explained in siman 248. Although it appears somewhat there that Baal Halachos Gedolos disagrees with Rabbeinu Yitzchak and maintains that the reason is techum, and that the “some say that it is forbidden” cited here refers to Baal Halachos Gedolos, Magen Avraham nevertheless understands that the Tur does not intend merely to challenge Baal Halachos Gedolos according to his own position. Rather, the Tur also disputes him as a matter of practical law, and the Tur’s rulings consequently contradict one another. This likewise appears from Magen Avraham’s wording. It also appears from Magen Avraham in siman 248, seif katan 10, that he understands that Baal Halachos Gedolos also considers the reason to be swimming, as Rabbeinu Yitzchak writes; see Responsa Shav Yaakov. It is therefore evident that even for a mitzvah one must specifically stipulate that the ship will rest on Shabbos. How, then, did the Tur permit one initially to cross on Shabbos for a mitzvah? The same appears from Yoma 77b: Rabbi Yitzchak and Rav Shmuel bar Rav Yitzchak were standing at the bank of the River Pappa, at the ferry of Chatzdad. Rami bar Pappa stood on the opposite side and called to them: “May I cross to come to you and ask a halachic teaching?” Rabbi Yitzchak answered that Rav and Shmuel both said that he may cross, provided that he does not remove his hand from beneath the edge of his garment. Nevertheless, they did not wish him to travel by boat. Otherwise, Rabbi Yitzchak should have told him to cross by boat, first because according to the Tur that is a more lenient prohibition than crossing through water, and second because it was certainly easier for them to travel by boat than through water. One cannot answer that Rabbi Yitzchak wished to teach them the novelty that even crossing through water is permitted, for their question was not whether crossing water was permitted as an abstract law. They wished to ask a halachic teaching and did not know by what route they could reach the others. They asked whether they could cross through the water. Moreover, why did they not ask whether they could travel by boat to ask the halachic teaching, which would have involved the more lenient prohibition?
