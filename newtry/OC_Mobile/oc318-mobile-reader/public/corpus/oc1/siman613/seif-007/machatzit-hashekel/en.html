(Seif katan 7) In a boat, etc. Specifically through subterfuge, etc. It is stated in Shabbos, chapter Toling, 139b, that if a non-Jew’s boat is standing in the water and is not moving, a Torah scholar may enter it to sleep, even though he knows that the non-Jew will take him to the other side, where the Torah scholar has produce that he wishes to guard. The Torah scholar says that his intention is to sleep, not to cross. The Gemara explains that even if he intended to cross in the boat, the prohibition would be only D’Rabbanan. It was therefore permitted for a Torah scholar through subterfuge, and we do not decree lest he do so without subterfuge, because he is a Torah scholar; see there.
See there in Beis Yosef. Meaning, Beis Yosef writes that for this reason Rambam omitted this law, because he maintains that nowadays there is no one with the status of a Torah scholar for this purpose, and it was therefore not permitted even through subterfuge. Shulchan Aruch likewise omitted it there for this reason.
