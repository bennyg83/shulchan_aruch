[7] Until the loins, etc. This is difficult, for in Yoma 77, "and He made me pass until the loins," from here that it is permitted to pass until the loins, implies that until it reaches the loins it is permitted, and only above that is forbidden, as Rashi explained there. Later the Taz and Magen Avraham were printed, and they too raised this difficulty; see there. Perhaps the Shulchan Aruch was stringent because of danger to life, since it is impossible to measure so precisely. But it is difficult, for in the Beis Yosef he wrote that this is what is in the Talmud. Therefore it seems to me that in the Talmud it refers to what it said, "until the ankles, until the knees," and it counts from below upward, while in the Beis Yosef and Shulchan Aruch it refers to the law of up to his neck and counts from above downward. Examine this carefully. Regarding the essence of this law, I require study about the Beis Yosef, the Shulchan Aruch, and the Acharonim, for what the Talmud says, "a river is different, for the water rushes," speaks specifically of the stream that emerges from the Holy of Holies, as the Talmud says there that even ships cannot go there. So too it implies from Rashi's explanation there. A proof for this is from the Rif, Rambam, and all the authorities, who wrote simply that it is permitted until his neck and did not distinguish in this way. So too it is clear from the plain reading of the Talmud there. This requires study.
