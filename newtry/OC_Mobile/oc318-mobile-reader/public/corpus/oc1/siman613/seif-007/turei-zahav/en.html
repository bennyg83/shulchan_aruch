The teacher is forbidden, etc. This is a question in the Gemara that was not resolved, and they ruled stringently. This is difficult to me: it is a rabbinic matter, and we rule everywhere that a rabbinic doubt is treated leniently. Another difficulty arises about the question itself: why should one who goes to guard his produce be better than going to one's student to study with him? The mitzvah of learning with others is a great mitzvah, as stated at the end of the first chapter of Bava Kamma, that learning is greater than fulfillment of the commandments, and in many places. It appears that the reason here is that the student can come to him, so why should the teacher go to the student? Therefore it seems that if it is impossible for the student to go, his teacher may go to him. The Levush wrote that even in this case it is forbidden, because the Sages did not distinguish. I do not know the source for this, especially in this matter, which is a rabbinic doubt.
