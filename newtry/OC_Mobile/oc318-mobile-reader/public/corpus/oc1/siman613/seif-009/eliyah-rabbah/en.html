[9] The teacher, etc. This is difficult: it is obvious, for even regarding one equal to him it is forbidden if he is not greater than him. One can say that I might have thought that if it is impossible for the student to go, it would be permitted for the teacher to go so that the student can fulfill the mitzvah of greeting his teacher, or so that he can learn something from him. Now what the Kenesses ha-Gedolah asked about the Talmud's question, "what is the law of a student going to his teacher," is resolved, for it seems to me that the question was where it was impossible to go. It seems to me that from this the Levush derived what he wrote, "and even if the student cannot, etc." The Taz challenged this and wrote, "I do not know a source for this." In my humble opinion, its source is from the Gemara, as I explained. Also, regarding what the Taz wondered, that since it is an unresolved question and a rabbinic doubt, the Tur should have ruled leniently: in my humble opinion, this is not difficult at all, for washing is stringent, as I wrote above in the words of the Tur, because they supported it on a verse.
