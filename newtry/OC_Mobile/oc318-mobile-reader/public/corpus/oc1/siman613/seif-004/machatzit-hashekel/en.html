(Seif katan 4) Some medicinal benefit. For complete medical treatment, however, it is permitted, etc. This likewise appears from Beis Yosef in the name of Semak. Beis Yosef writes: “Semak writes: Maharam says that a person may wash his eyes with water on Yom Kippur because it is medicinal for the eyes, as stated in the chapter Shemonah Sheratzim, meaning Shabbos 108b, where Shmuel says that a drop of cold water upon the eyes in the morning is better than all eye salves; and medical treatment is permitted on Yom Kippur.” End quote. I do not understand this, for Semak did not write that medical treatment is permitted on Yom Kippur, but only that there is no prohibition of washing when it is not for pleasure and his intent is solely medicinal. In my humble opinion, this applies specifically to a healthy person who does not require actual treatment. If he requires actual treatment because his eyes hurt, it is forbidden even on Yom Tov and Shabbos because of the decree against grinding medicinal ingredients. Siman 328, seifim 19 and 20, permits it only where the act does not appear medicinal but merely resembles washing. On Yom Kippur, when washing itself is forbidden, it should therefore be prohibited. We must consequently say that the case is one in which his entire body became ill, for whom a rabbinic medical act is permitted, as stated in siman 328, seif 17. One might have thought that on Yom Kippur it should nevertheless be forbidden because washing is according to the Torah; therefore he teaches that when done for treatment there is no prohibition of washing.
See the end of seif 9. Meaning, Rama permits washing for an ill person even when he is not dangerously ill.
