1) [Seif 1] It is forbidden to wash on Yom Kippur, etc. Any washing for pleasure is forbidden on Yom Kippur because of, "that you shall not be afflicted," etc. Levush; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, sec. 1. See above siman 611, sec. 3.
2) Ibid. Soiled with mud or excrement, etc. However, washing to remove perspiration is forbidden, as stated in the following siman regarding anointing, that it is forbidden in order to remove grime; the same applies to washing. Taz, s.k. 1. This is also the view of the Bach and Pri Chadash. However, the words of the Levush at the beginning of siman 614 below imply that washing is permitted to remove grime. This is also the view of the Magen Avraham there, s.k. 1. So too wrote Eliyah Rabbah there, sec. 1, in the name of Shibolei HaLeket, and Maamar Mordechai there, sec. 1, in the name of Rabbeinu Manoach on the Rambam, chapter 3, see there. Mateh Ephraim, sec. 2, distinguished between the cases: whenever the perspiration causes him distress because he is delicate, it may be permitted in accordance with the Levush and Magen Avraham. One who is not delicate and does not have such a great need for this washing should be stringent not to wash merely to remove perspiration, just as it is forbidden to anoint with oil to remove grime, as explained in the following siman. So too wrote the Mishna Berurah, sec. 2. See above siman 4, seif 21 in the gloss, and our words there, sec. 95.
3) Ibid. He may wash them. Even when he is not praying, he may wash them. Bach; Ateres Zekeinim.
4) Ibid. He may wash them. He must take care to wash only the soiled place. Magen Avraham, s.k. 1; Eliyah Rabbah, sec. 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, sec. 1; Chayei Adam, rule 145, sec. 9; Mateh Ephraim, sec. 1; Mishna Berurah, sec. 1.
5) And if his body is soiled in several places and it is burdensome for him to wash each soiled place separately, he may enter the water and wash his entire body at one time in order to remove the dirt upon him. When he washes because of dirt, he should intend that it is not for the pleasure of washing, but only to remove the dirt. Mateh Ephraim, ibid.; Mishna Berurah, ibid.; Tosafos Chaim on Chayei Adam there, secs. 11 and 12.
6) And if his hands were soiled with excrement, or he touched covered areas of his body, even if he touched only with one finger, he must wash both hands up to the finger joints. But if his hand touched mud or mire, he may wash only the soiled place. Derech HaChaim, sec. 1; Tosafos Chaim there, sec. 12; Mishna Berurah, sec. 6. This applies even for prayer. Mishna Berurah, ibid.
7) Ibid. For they prohibited only washing for pleasure. Keep this principle in your hand: whenever one does not intend to derive pleasure from washing, even if its only purpose is, "Prepare to meet your God, O Israel," it is permitted. Radbaz, vol. 1, siman 37; Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur; Shulchan Gavoah, sec. 8.
