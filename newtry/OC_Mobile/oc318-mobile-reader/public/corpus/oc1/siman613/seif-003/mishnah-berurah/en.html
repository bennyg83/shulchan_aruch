(4) And he rubbed with his hand, or relieved himself and wiped, etc. - From this language it implies that if he did not wipe after a bowel movement and did not rub after urinating, it is forbidden for him to wash. In truth, the authorities disagree about this, for some say that even if he did not wipe or rub, he should wash his hands because of "prepare to meet your God, O Israel," since he must recite Asher Yatzar. Therefore the Acharonim agreed that it is proper at the outset to wipe and rub, in order to remove himself from dispute, for in that case according to all views it is permitted to wash. When does this apply? On the night of Yom Kippur after the prayer. But during the day, when they pray all day, if he urinated, even though he did not rub, or relieved himself, even though he did not wipe, he may wash his hands until the end of the finger joints, as follows.
(5) And if he wants to pray, even if he did not wipe, etc. - It implies from this that if he did not relieve himself at all, he should not wash his hands for prayer alone. Although it is explained above in siman 233, se'if 2, that for prayer alone it is a mitzvah to wash the hands, here, because of the stringency of Yom Kippur, he was concerned for those authorities who hold that for prayer alone they did not institute washing hands except in the morning, because he has become like a new creation.
(6) Until the end of the finger joints - And no farther, even if he relieved himself and wiped. See Derech ha-Chayim, that the same applies if he touched his body in covered places, which are places of sweat: even if he touched with only one finger, he must wash his whole hand until the finger joints. But if he touched mud or mire with his hand, he needs to wash only the soiled place. This applies even for prayer.
(7) Washes his hands - The washing is until the wrist, as explained above in siman 128, se'if 6 (Chayei Adam in the name of Eliyah Rabbah).
(8) For most washing, etc. - See Ma'amar Mordechai, who had difficulty with the word "most." Also in Hagahos Maimoniyos the word "most" is not present; see there.
