12) [Seif 3] If he urinated and rubbed with his hand, etc. This implies that without rubbing, he should not wash his hands unless he wishes to pray, as stated afterward. However, the Beit Yosef cited several great authorities who maintain that even without rubbing, he may wash his hands because of preparation, meaning in order to recite Asher Yatzar in holiness, see there. The Bach wrote that in practice he should bring himself into an obligation to wash by wiping and rubbing, for then he is obligated to wash his hands according to all opinions, see there. The Pri Chadash wrote that this is unnecessary, because any washing that is not intended to remove grime or the like, and that involves preparation, is permitted, see there. Nevertheless, the Magen Avraham, s.k. 2, wrote in accordance with the Bach, that he should wipe and rub in order to remove himself from the dispute. So too wrote Shulchan Aruch HaRav, sec. 4; Chayei Adam, rule 145, sec. 11; Mateh Ephraim, sec. 4; Mishna Berurah, sec. 4. This applies specifically at night. During the day, however, if he urinated or relieved himself, since prayers are recited throughout the day, he may wash his hands even if he did not wipe and rub. Magen Avraham, ibid.; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, sec. 5; Mateh Ephraim, ibid.; Mishna Berurah, ibid. All this concerns one who relieved himself or urinated in a field or the like. But if he entered a fixed privy, in any case he must wash his hands because of the harmful spirit that rests upon the hands in a privy, as stated above siman 4, sec. 65, see there. See Mishbetzos Zahav, sec. 2.
