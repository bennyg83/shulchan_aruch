(1) Until the end - The one who reads this section is called Chasan Torah. The custom is to sell this section and the section of Bereishis, and people buy them for large sums. It is proper to beautify the matter, if possible, that the buyers should be crowned with Torah, or at least from the leaders of the congregation.
(2) And in the second, Bereishis - The one who reads this section is called Chasan Bereishis. Even one who already received an aliyah in the section Vezos HaBerachah may go up as Chasan Torah or Chasan Bereishis. But Chasan Torah should not read the section of Chasan Bereishis. The reason is that since there is no interruption between them, this would imply a defect in the first Sefer Torah, for people would say it was invalid; therefore he reads immediately in the second. All this is in the same synagogue, but one who read Chasan Torah in this synagogue may certainly read Chasan Bereishis in another synagogue. Maharil wrote that a kohen or levi may be Chasan Torah or Chasan Bereishis.
(3) To do - They place the third Sefer Torah next to it and say half Kaddish.
(4) Simchas Torah - The Eliyah Rabbah wrote that on Simchas Torah, night and day, one should increase lamps in the synagogue in honor of the Torah scrolls that are taken out. The poskim wrote that on the night of Simchas Torah one should read the parashah twice with the text and once with Targum, for now is its time.
(5) That we rejoice - In honor of the Torah it is permitted to dance on Yom Tov, but to burn incense, meaning to scatter types of spices on coals, is forbidden. All the more so it is forbidden to ignite powder in order to make a sound for joy. Nevertheless, it is permitted to fasten powder into a lamp on erev Yom Tov before lighting the lamp, even though this ultimately causes the lamp to go out through it.
(6) For the completion of the Torah - As appears in Midrash Shir HaShirim, and the same applies to the completion of a mitzvah. The Eliyah Rabbah wrote: It is explained from the poskim that one should gladden the public in every possible way with the joy of a mitzvah, unlike those who strike and push one another until the joy is turned into grief, Heaven forbid; through this they also prevent joy of a mitzvah, and therefore one should rebuke them. End quote. Maharik too, in shoresh 9, greatly elaborated that one should not abolish any custom practiced in honor of the joy of the Torah; see there. Therefore, people act badly in many places by recently abolishing the making of feasting and joy on Simchas Torah, even though they rejoice on other days and all their days are like festivals. Because of our many sins, this was caused by degradation of the honor of the Torah, that the Torah lies in a corner and no one seeks or asks for it. Would that He return and have mercy on the breach of Bnei Yisrael speedily in our days; Bikkurei Yaakov.
(7) And the custom is, etc., that they pledge donations - In our days the custom is that all who receive aliyos pledge donations for the needs of the beis midrash and to support those who learn Torah.
(8) And they call others, etc. - Even a mourner within twelve months may eat at this festive meal.
(9) All the Sifrei Torah in the ark - If there are only a few Sifrei Torah in this synagogue, one should not bring a Sefer Torah from another synagogue. There is one who permits it, because although throughout the year one should not bring a Sefer Torah temporarily, but only for a fixed use, on Simchas Torah even temporary use is permitted; LeDavid Emes.
(10) The custom is to circle - Some circle three times, and some seven times, as on Hoshana Rabbah; each place according to its custom, as the later authorities wrote.
(11) Because of joy - The Maharik wrote in the name of Rav Hai Gaon in shoresh 9: on this day our custom is to dance on it, even several elders, at the time when they say praises to the Torah, etc. Therefore one should strengthen himself in this, to dance and sing in honor of the Torah, as written regarding David HaMelech, who leaped and danced with all his strength before Hashem. So too is written in the name of the Arizal, and they testified about the Arizal that he said the upper level he attained came to him because he would rejoice with all his strength in the joy of a mitzvah. They also wrote about the Gra, of blessed memory, that he would dance before the Sefer Torah with all his strength.
(12) To increase those called - Because of the honor of the Torah, and also so that everyone should merit the joy of the Torah. The custom is to call two at one time, or a kohen with all the kohanim and a levi with all the levi'im. Some question this, and the Eliyah Rabbah resolves the custom. Nevertheless, ab initio the correct thing is that one should recite the blessing and the rest should intend to fulfill their obligation through his blessing, or that one should recite the blessing before and discharge his fellow, and one should recite the blessing afterward and discharge his fellow. The same applies when an adult is called with all the youths: the adult should recite the blessing and the youths should listen and fulfill their obligation through his blessing. The Chayei Adam wrote that it is proper to choose ten people who will listen to the Torah blessings and answer amen, because people practice great laxity and there are not ten listening to the Torah reading.
(13) Many times - Until 'the eternal God is a dwelling place,' but from there onward we do not repeat, for there they begin to read for Chasan Torah; Levush. The custom is that before reading Chasan Torah they read for all the youths in order to train them in the mitzvah of Torah reading; Moldan said until 'a dwelling place,' and so wrote Maharil.
(14) And they read for them the passage of 'the angel who redeems' - In the Levush he wrote that they bless them with the verse 'the angel who redeems.' The Eliyah Rabbah wrote that they bless them before their final blessing, and so is the custom.
(15) The pledge-sections in the Torah - These are the sections that they customarily sell separately throughout the year, and for which people pledge donations, such as the passage of Veyiten Lecha, the passage of the angel, the passage of Vayechulu, the passage of Yevarechecha, and the passage of Mah Tovu. These are called nedarim, and so it is implied in Magen Avraham, siman 282. In our days the custom is to call three men in the section of Vezos HaBerachah, and they must say Kaddish after the reading; they do not rely on the Kaddish after Aleinu, because that is an interruption; Derech HaChaim.
(16) Even with a minor - Although we wrote above in s.k. 1 that it is proper for the one who completes to be crowned with Torah, or at least one of the important members of the congregation, according to the essential law even a minor may complete it. See above in siman 282, se'if 3, and in Birkei Yosef there.
(17) Two - The custom is not to ascend to the duchan, because drunkenness is common; Levush. The Eliyah Rabbah wrote that in Prague they have the custom to ascend at Mussaf, and some ascend at Shacharis; each place follows its custom, and they should be careful not to drink.
