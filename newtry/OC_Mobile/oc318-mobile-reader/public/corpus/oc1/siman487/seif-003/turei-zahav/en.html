He must go back and mention it in Ya'aleh Veyavo. Even though regarding Rosh Chodesh that falls on Shabbos he wrote in siman 425 that one does not mention [Shabbos] in Ya'aleh Veyavo of Shabbos: that case is different, for the Rosh in chapter Bameh Madlikin gave a reason that he already concluded with [the formula] of Shabbos when he says "Who sanctifies the Shabbos," and the Mordechai gave a reason there that if there is no Rosh Chodesh there is no Shabbos in the Avodah [blessing]. And Beis Yosef wrote: and according to this one should mention [the formula] of Shabbos on Yom Tov that falls on Shabbos in Ya'aleh Veyavo, for those reasons do not apply there; and also in Birkas HaMazon on Rosh Chodesh that falls on Shabbos one does not mention Shabbos in Ya'aleh Veyavo, because he already said Retzeh VeHachalitzenu, which is for Shabbos; which is not so here, where it is reasonable to mention it, for what difference is there between Ya'aleh Veyavo and Ateh Bechartanu, in which he mentions both. And in Responsa Maharil, siman 3, and likewise in his derashos, he wrote that there is a great dispute among the authorities about this, and he brings these proofs that we mentioned, except that he decided not to mention Shabbos in it; and his proof is from chapter 2 of Beitzah, folio 17: on Yom Tov that falls on Shabbos he begins with [the formula] of Shabbos and concludes with [the formula] of Shabbos and says the sanctity of the day in the middle; and Rashi explained that he says "and You gave us, Hashem our God, this day of rest and this day of such-and-such festival," which implies that one should not mention the sanctity of Shabbos except at the beginning and the end, meaning not in the middle; and even though we say "Shabbosos for rest," that is because it is adjacent to "and You gave us etc.," which is included in the beginning; and for this reason they say "Shabbos and the festivals of Your holiness," to say something that parallels the conclusion; end quote. And there is no proof at all in my humble opinion, for is it to be interpreted that what he said "the sanctity of the day in the middle" comes to exclude the sanctity of Shabbos so that it not be in the middle but only at the beginning? And that is certainly not so, for there is further a beraisa there: on Shabbos that falls on Rosh Chodesh, in Musaf he begins with [the formula] of Shabbos and concludes with [the formula] of Shabbos and the sanctity of the day is in the middle; and there certainly they mention the sanctity of Shabbos also in the middle, for they mention verses of the Shabbos korban and "and they will rejoice in Your kingship" in the middle; rather, certainly what he said "the sanctity of the day in the middle" comes only to exclude that he not say at the end [the formula] of Yom Tov, but rather conclude with [the formula] of Shabbos alone, for it is primary and in it alone are the beginning and the end, and Yom Tov has no distinction like it; but regarding the distinction of Yom Tov that is in the middle, there is a fortiori that Shabbos should have a distinction together with it, for they place verses of Shabbos before the Musaf korban of Yom Tov. And this is clear in the intent of this beraisa also regarding Yom Tov on Shabbos, for Rabbi disagrees and says that he concludes also with [the formula] of Yom Tov, and we hold like him, as is clear that they came to disagree only about the conclusion; and if so, just as in Musaf he mentions [the formula] of Shabbos even in the middle, why should Shacharis of Yom Tov on Shabbos be less as to mentioning it in Ya'aleh Veyavo? And if so, on the contrary, we have a clear proof from here to mention [the formula] of Shabbos in Ya'aleh Veyavo on Yom Tov that falls on Shabbos; and so wrote Beis Yosef that the world's custom is to mention it, as Rama wrote here. And Maharshal in chapter 2 of Beitzah, siman 13, brought Maharil's words and wrote that the essence is that Ya'aleh Veyavo was not instituted for Shabbos, and there is nothing sufficient in this to prove or decide from it; for certainly it was instituted on every Yom Tov to say it according to what it is: if on Yom Tov he mentions Yom Tov, and if on Rosh Chodesh even on Shabbos they do not mention Shabbos, only for its reason, because he already said a special prayer for Shabbos, Yismach Moshe, and likewise in Birkas HaMazon in Retzeh; therefore afterward he says a special prayer for Rosh Chodesh (and Yom Tov); which is not so regarding Shabbos (and Yom Tov), where there is no law of division between them, and whoever mentions both is mentioning [properly]. And in the Levush, and likewise my late father-in-law of blessed memory, agreed not to mention Shabbos in Ya'aleh Veyavo on Yom Tov on Shabbos, like Maharil. And as for the law, it certainly seems reasonable to us to mention it, even though on Rosh Chodesh they do not mention, and likewise in Birkas HaMazon there is nothing [wrong] in this. And greater than this we find regarding the text "for You are a gracious and compassionate King," which Rama brings in siman 181, se'if 3, that one should not say "King" in Birkas HaMazon, and in prayer they say it, because there is a distinction between them, as is stated there, as I wrote on this that one can distinguish. And even for those great authorities who do not agree to mention it, one should not protest against any person if he mentions it, for there is no wasted blessing here, only a mention alone, and there is no loss in that; on the contrary, it is reasonable to mention it so that the merit of Shabbos joined with Yom Tov assist to fulfill our request in what we pray in Ya'aleh Veyavo, as it appears to me according to my humble opinion.
