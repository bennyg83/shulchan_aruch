3) If Pesach falls on Shabbos, there is no reason to refrain from reciting the piyyut Lecha Dodi, etc., including ‘Let us welcome Shabbos,’ etc. Beis David, siman 264; Birkei Yosef, paragraph 6. Some communities do not recite Lecha Dodi when Yom Tov falls on Shabbos or on a Shabbos of Chol HaMoed, but recite only ‘A psalm, a song for the Shabbos day.’ Some communities recite it but omit Hisnaari. In any event, all recite Lechu Neranenah. Elya Zuta, siman 488, paragraph 1. The custom of Beis El (the community whose practices follow the teachings of the Arizal) is not to recite it even on ordinary Shabbosos of the year. Instead, they begin the evening service with Leshem Yichud, etc., ‘Come, let us go out to greet Shabbos the Queen, to the field of holy apples,’ followed by ‘A psalm of David: Render to Hashem,’ etc., in accordance with the order written above in siman 261, paragraph 8; see there. When Yom Tov falls on Shabbos, after that order they recite the psalm relevant to that Yom Tov, and then Leshem Yichud, etc.: ‘I hereby come to pray the evening service of Shabbos and the particular Yom Tov, which Yaakov, peace be upon him, instituted, to rectify its root in the supernal place.’ They then recite ‘May the pleasantness,’ etc., Kaddish, Barechu, and begin the blessings of Kerias Shema, etc.
