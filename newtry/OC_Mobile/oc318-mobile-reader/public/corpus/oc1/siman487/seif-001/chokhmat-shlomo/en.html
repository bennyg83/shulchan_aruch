Seif 1: And if he said "Who sanctifies Shabbat" and retracted within [time of] speech he fulfilled, etc. Note: see Magen Avraham here who wrote that if he concluded only with Shabbat but mentioned Yom Tov in the middle he need not return and pray; and see Eshel Avraham who disagrees and holds he returns — so ruled the Gaon Baal Chavat Daat in his siddur. Even per Magen Avraham it appears this is only if he mentioned Yom Tov in the middle within an intermediate berachah — then since he at least prayed properly and also prayed that of Yom Tov in the middle, he holds he does not return; but if he prayed only that of Shabbat in Avodah and mentioned Yaaleh VeYavo, since he did not mention Yom Tov in the middle at all, certainly he must return and pray, even though if he prayed weekday and mentioned Yom Tov in Avodah he does not return — there it is correct that even on Yom Tov one must pray eighteen. Mahariw in truth disagrees with Magen Avraham and holds if he did not pray eighteen, even if he mentioned Yom Tov in the middle he returns, etc.; therefore even per Magen Avraham this is only Yom Tov; but if he mentioned in Avodah, if he prayed eighteen he at least fulfilled the Sages' enactment in that he prayed eighteen, and it is correct; but if he did not pray eighteen and did not mention in the middle he did not fulfill the Sages' enactment at all, therefore certainly he returns — and so implies Magen Avraham's wording; in this case he returns without doubt; if he also mentioned in the middle he may return but conditionally: if obligated to pray it is to his obligation, if exempt it is voluntary; though one does not pray voluntary on Shabbat and Yom Tov — since some hold one may pray voluntary on Shabbat, see Beit Yosef siman 108 — therefore it is a safeik perhaps like Eshel Avraham, or if like Magen Avraham perhaps voluntary prayer on Shabbat — therefore pray conditionally — end quote; correct for halacha. (There) seif 4: on the first Passover night they complete Hallel in congregation with melody with berachah beginning and end. Note: yes, so implies in Tractate Soferim; however in my opinion there is clear proof to the contrary from our Gemara that one does not bless at all on Hallel on Passover nights — from what we learn in Megillah 21: anything whose mitzvah is at night is valid all night — to include what? To include eating matzah, not like Raavad. In Tosafot there it implies even per Raavad Hallel after afikoman certainly is valid all night — see there; if so, it is difficult from where the Gemara learns that the sippaya "whose mitzvah is at night is valid all night" excludes matzah eating and not like Raavad — perhaps also like Raavad and includes Hallel reading valid all night, which Raavad admits; and at the beginning it taught Hallel for a daytime mitzvah is valid all day — it should also include Hallel reading; if so, one should also say at night we include Hallel reading. We do not say rabbinic matters are not taught — as Taz explained his way; nevertheless one may say Hallel is considered in Mishnah as obligatory since prophets enacted it, not considered rabbinic as written there — if so, since it wrote in Gemara Hallel on Rosh Chodesh is only rabbinic, it implies other days are d'oraisa; if so, also Passover night is d'oraisa, for the Gemara brought only Rosh Chodesh alone — if so, also Passover night is d'oraisa. Reason supports: if the obligation is to read Hallel, what difference between night and day — if so, also at night it is d'oraisa — if so, why not answer it teaches this and it comes also like Raavad. Perforce the reason what it teaches valid all night is only b'dieved — l'chatchila one should hurry when possible, zealous precede mitzvot — perforce it does not teach valid all night except b'dieved if they did not read before; then matzah eating fits — then it is not its time, it is notar and forbidden to eat; likewise where berachah applies and there is concern for berachah lebatalah — therefore it needed to teach it is still in time, valid all day and all night; but Hallel on Passover night, since one does not bless on it, it is obvious if they did not read before it is valid all night as one reading Torah. It fits if one does not bless on Hallel at night; but if we say one blesses on Hallel at night, it is very difficult to resolve what it teaches at night regarding Hallel, and it comes also like Raavad — perforce at night there is no Hallel with berachah; clear proof for Rama's custom and ours, and truly required per Rama in the name of Tosafot and Rashba that l'chatchila one must also read Hallel before midnight as Chayei Adam wrote there — if l'chatchila permitted after midnight, say this distinction — examine well. A scholar asked me on the Shulchan Aruch's words from Yerushalmi Shekalim ch. 83: what of drinking them in one city, since R. Mani said R. Yochanan: Hallel — if they hear in synagogue he fulfilled; this teaches if they drink in one city he fulfilled. He asked: per Beit Yosef they say Hallel in synagogue — perforce it is from law and they fulfill thereby though on custom one does not bless; if so, if they fulfill thereby, why did Shulchan Aruch rule if they drank out of order they did not fulfill — end the scholar's question. I answered: certainly the Shulchan Aruch holds even though he wrote they say Hallel on Passover with berachah, nevertheless he does not fulfill thereby since he did not write one fulfills thereby — perforce he holds one does not fulfill thereby; and that they bless on it — though on custom one does not bless, this is different, for custom has no relevance this day; the essence is law, like Hoshanot — there one does not bless; but Hallel this night its essence is law, and since Hallel obligation applies this night, they properly bless even when only custom, and also to exempt the Hallel said afterward which is from law; and I later found in Tur siman 473 that the custom of reading Hallel Passover night in synagogue is custom — see there; nevertheless he wrote they bless on it; accordingly the scholar's question is resolved: from R. Yochanan's words that if one said Hallel in synagogue he fulfilled — it implies he holds one need not say Hallel afterward at all — from this it is clear he holds if they drank out of order they fulfilled; but we do not hold so — even if he said in synagogue he is obligated to say afterward in order; therefore if they drank out of order they did not fulfill — and it fits well — end quote.
