This Yom Tov, a holy convocation. And our custom is to say "this day of the Festival of Matzos, the time of our freedom, a holy convocation, a remembrance of the Exodus from Egypt," and we do not say "be'ahavah" from "a holy convocation," for they already said "and You gave us, Hashem our God, with love festivals for joy." However, when it falls on Shabbos, some say they say "be'ahavah" twice; and see in the Levush the reason at length; and see above, siman 490.

The day of rest. So is the view of the Talmud in Masseches Beitzah, folio 17a, and so is the language of the Tur; and Maharil wrote in the order of Yom Tov prayer that such is the custom and text of the people of Austria; and it is a wonder that they leave the language of Scripture, "Remember the Shabbos day"; and if you say Yom Tov too is called Shabbos, nevertheless it is not called "the Shabbos day"; end quote. And in truth, in my humble opinion there is no wonder here at all, since it is called Shabbos, therefore it is also called "the Shabbos day," as it is written "a day of Shabbason"; and a clear proof for this is from what is explained at the beginning of siman 488, that they say zemiros of Shabbos and say "A chapter of Tehillim, a song for the Shabbos day," for the reason that Yom Tov too is called Shabbos, as the Magen Avraham wrote in the name of Darkei Moshe; so it is also called "the Shabbos day." And see in Pnei Moshe, siman 132, who wrote that one should not mock this custom, and that so say all the great authorities in Constantinople, and not like those who say the mnemonic "menuchah [is for] an am ha'aretz," meaning that one who says "day of rest" is an am ha'aretz. However, the Rambam wrote in the text of the blessing "this Shabbos day," and so is our text and our custom; each river follows its own course, and one should not change.
And he concludes "Who sanctifies the Shabbos." And the Magen Avraham wrote that if on Yom Tov or on Shabbos he concluded "Who sanctifies Yisrael," he has fulfilled in prayer but not in Kiddush, and so it is implied in Pesachim folio 117; end quote. And his words are not compelling at all to one who studies the sugya there, for even in prayer it is possible that he has not fulfilled, for it is like changing the order of the blessing itself; and the Talmud there used abbreviated language, and it is obvious. And see in Kenesses HaGedolah in his glosses to the Tur, who wrote that if it fell on Shabbos and he did not mention [the formula] of Shabbos and concluded "Who sanctifies Yisrael and the seasons," he has fulfilled; end quote. And one must say that this is not like changing the very text of the blessing, since they say so on every Yom Tov, only that he forgot [to include] Shabbos, and its law is like in se'if 3.
Who sanctifies the Shabbos and Yisrael and the seasons. So too is the text in the Rambam, the word "veYisrael" with a vav; but in the Talmud's text it is "Yisrael" without a vav, and so wrote the Tur; and Maharshal in Masseches Beitzah, chapter 2, siman 11, also wrote two views on this, and they wrote that it is proper to say "Yisrael" without a vav; see there. And in all such matters one should not change the custom, and all the more so one should not be particular about one who says this way or that way, for they have what to rely on. See also siman 236.
