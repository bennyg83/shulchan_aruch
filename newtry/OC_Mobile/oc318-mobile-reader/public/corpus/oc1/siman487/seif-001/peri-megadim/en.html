There is no. See Taz and see Magen Avraham s.k. 4 not to recite Kiddush; and see Rama and Eliyah Rabbah 6 and 9. They agreed with the Magen Avraham; and it is implied that even if there is a house adjacent to the synagogue and guests ate there, it is possible that one does not recite Kiddush on the night of Pesach; and this requires further study. And they do not recite Kiddush on both nights in the synagogue. And see siman 269 in Beis Yosef and Bach; and according to the reason of Rabbeinu Yonah and the Bach there, nevertheless on the night of Pesach one can say each person takes the trouble and hears Kiddush from an expert; see there. And see the Levush who asked that the poor of Yisrael anyway cannot [fulfill thereby], for there is no Kiddush except in the place of the meal; see there. And one can say "the poor of Yisrael" also refers to guests, and there was never an institution to recite Kiddush in the synagogue on Pesach nights.
What the Rov wrote. And see above, siman 490, se'if 3, "a holy convocation"; see there in Taz and Magen Avraham 2. We say "and You gave us with love festivals for joy etc. this day of the Festival of Matzos, a holy convocation etc."; and when it falls on Shabbos he should say afterward again "be'ahavah, a holy convocation," and one "be'ahavah" applies to Yom Tov in general, which they received with love etc., and on Shabbos they received it at Marah also with love; see Levush. The word Vehasienu: see Eliyah Rabbah in the name of R' David Abudarham: (1) from the language of exaltation and greatness; (2) from the language of raising signal-fires, illuminate Your countenance; (3) from "Pharaoh will lift up," that our remembrance rise; (4) "and I carried [you] on eagles' wings"; and the correct is from the language of a gift, "and he brought gifts"; see there. And one should say "who sanctify Your Name," paralleling the conclusion, not "who love Your Name"; and likewise on Shabbos; and it is a fortiori; see siman 267. In the Levush: they light two candles and bless "to light the Yom Tov candle"; and see the laws of Shabbos, that is in his home; and see Meir Nesiv on "carry" etc., and Vehasienu lacking the nun of the root; see there. See siman 270: Bameh Madlikin one does not say on Yom Tov when it falls on Shabbos, nor on Shabbos of Chol HaMoed; see there. See Levush: and one does not say Ma'arivim when it falls on Shabbos, lest he tilt the lamp; see siman 275; and it is permitted to say the Haggadah on Shabbos by the lamp; see there. Nevertheless it is fine to light a good candle-lamp rather than oil for saying the Haggadah; see there on this.
