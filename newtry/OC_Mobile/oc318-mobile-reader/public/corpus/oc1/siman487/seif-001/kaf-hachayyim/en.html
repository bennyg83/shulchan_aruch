1) [Seif 1] The order of the evening service, etc.: They recite Kerias Shema, etc., conclude the blessing, spread over the congregation, and recite the verse ‘Moshe declared the appointed festivals,’ etc. This does not constitute an interruption between redemption and prayer, for it is considered an extension of the blessing of redemption. Levush in this siman and in siman 582. Knesses HaGedolah similarly writes in Hagahos HaTur that the custom is to recite after Hashkiveinu, on Yom Tov nights, ‘These are the appointed festivals of Hashem, holy convocations’; on Rosh Hashanah nights, ‘On the day of your rejoicing and on your appointed festivals’; and on Yom Kippur night, ‘For on this day He shall atone for you,’ etc. Peri Chadash writes likewise. Chok Yosef, paragraph 1; Shalmei Tzibbur, page 194b. Although Levush writes that on Yom Tov nights they recite the verse ‘Moshe declared,’ each author wrote according to the custom of his locality; in some places the custom is to recite both verses.
2) Knesses HaGedolah further wrote there: When Yom Tov, Rosh Hashanah, or Yom Kippur falls on Friday night, they first recite ‘The Children of Israel shall observe Shabbos,’ and afterward recite the passage reflecting the sanctity of that day. This concludes his words. See further regarding this above in siman 267, seif 3, and our comments there, with the help of Heaven.
3) If Pesach falls on Shabbos, there is no reason to refrain from reciting the piyyut Lecha Dodi, etc., including ‘Let us welcome Shabbos,’ etc. Beis David, siman 264; Birkei Yosef, paragraph 6. Some communities do not recite Lecha Dodi when Yom Tov falls on Shabbos or on a Shabbos of Chol HaMoed, but recite only ‘A psalm, a song for the Shabbos day.’ Some communities recite it but omit Hisnaari. In any event, all recite Lechu Neranenah. Elya Zuta, siman 488, paragraph 1. The custom of Beis El (the community whose practices follow the teachings of the Arizal) is not to recite it even on ordinary Shabbosos of the year. Instead, they begin the evening service with Leshem Yichud, etc., ‘Come, let us go out to greet Shabbos the Queen, to the field of holy apples,’ followed by ‘A psalm of David: Render to Hashem,’ etc., in accordance with the order written above in siman 261, paragraph 8; see there. When Yom Tov falls on Shabbos, after that order they recite the psalm relevant to that Yom Tov, and then Leshem Yichud, etc.: ‘I hereby come to pray the evening service of Shabbos and the particular Yom Tov, which Yaakov, peace be upon him, instituted, to rectify its root in the supernal place.’ They then recite ‘May the pleasantness,’ etc., Kaddish, Barechu, and begin the blessings of Kerias Shema, etc.
4) There: ‘The Yom Tov, a holy convocation,’ etc. Our custom is to say, ‘this day of the Festival of Matzos, the season of our freedom, a holy convocation, in remembrance of the Exodus from Egypt.’ One should not say ‘with love, a holy convocation,’ since one has already said, ‘Hashem our God, You gave us with love festivals for rejoicing.’ When it falls on Shabbos, however, some say the word ‘with love’ twice. See Levush for the reason. Chok Yaakov, paragraph 1; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1. He writes that this is unlike Pesach Meubin, paragraph 131, who says not to recite ‘with love’ in the phrase ‘You gave us’; see there. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 6, and Mishna Berurah, paragraph 1, write likewise. Nevertheless, the Mechaber writes: ‘Our custom is to say, “Hashem our God, You gave us with love festivals,” etc., “with love, a holy convocation,” etc.’ He explains that the first ‘with love’ refers to the blessed Creator, Who in His love and compassion gave us the festivals, etc.; the second refers to us, for we lovingly regard the holy convocation and do not, G-d forbid, consider it a burden. Yafeh LaLev, volume 2, paragraph 1, likewise writes that ‘with love’ is recited twice; ‘Hashem our God, You gave us with love festivals,’ etc., and ‘with love, a holy convocation,’ etc.; as printed in the siddurim, whether the festival falls on Shabbos or on a weekday, in accordance with the Mechaber and Sheva Binyamin, siman 304; see there. This is the known Sephardic custom.
