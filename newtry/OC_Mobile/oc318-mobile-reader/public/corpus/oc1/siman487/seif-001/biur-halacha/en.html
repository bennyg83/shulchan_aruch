Within toch kedei dibbur - See Mishnah Berurah; and see in Peri Megadim who wrote that even if he began Retzeh, so long as he has not said four words he can go back and correct, that is, say "Who sanctifies Yisrael and the seasons"; and in my humble opinion this is a great difficulty, for once he began another blessing, even one word, it is reasonable that his correction does not help; and similar to what he ruled above in siman 114, that if he forgot to mention rain he can correct even after finishing Mechayeh HaMeisim, for the reason that all this belongs to this blessing, but once he has moved to another blessing and said even one word of the third blessing, he can no longer correct and he returns; and likewise of this sort, if he forgot Ya'aleh Veyavo and remembered after he began one word of Modim, he must return to Retzeh; and the same applies here. And even if he did not begin another blessing, only that he interrupted with some speech before he went back and said "Who sanctifies Yisrael and the seasons," there is also much to study whether his correction still helps, for it is possible that the Shulchan Aruch deals here only with silence alone. And see in Responsa Rashba, siman 35, regarding the Rif's reason, from which it is implied that therefore it helps when he said "Who creates the fruit of the vine" [then] "through Whose word everything came to be," because it is like adding praise in the middle; and likewise regarding HaKel HaKadosh when he afterward finished HaMelech HaKadosh, that therefore his retracting within toch kedei dibbur also helps because it is like adding praise; and according to this, when he interrupted with other speech in the middle, where that reason does not apply, one must return. And even though from the words of Tosafos, Berachos 12a, s.v. "lo," it is somewhat implied that they do not distinguish in this, nevertheless at any rate this law requires further study for practice; and especially in the aforementioned law that he began another blessing, it is certainly reasonable that his correction afterward does not help. I found in siman 582 in Shaarei Teshuvah in the name of the book Mateh Yehudah regarding the blessing HaMelech HaKadosh, that there too toch kedei dibbur helps as here; and there he ruled explicitly that if he began another blessing, even though he is still within toch kedei dibbur, he returns; and it is obvious that the same applies in our matter.
Who sanctifies the Shabbos and Yisrael and the seasons - See Mishnah Berurah what we wrote that we decided etc.; and that is, that in the Gemara there is a dispute of Beis Shammai and Beis Hillel regarding Shabbos and Yom Tov: according to Beis Shammai he prays eight, and according to Beis Hillel seven, and he opens with [the formula] of Shabbos and concludes with [the formula] of Shabbos and mentions Yom Tov in the middle; Rabbi says he even concludes with it "Who sanctifies the Shabbos and Yisrael and the seasons"; and we conclude there that the law follows Rabbi. And Kenesses HaGedolah wrote that after the fact, if he concluded only with [the formula] of Yom Tov, he has fulfilled since he mentioned Shabbos in the middle; and Peri Chadash and several other poskim disagree with him, that whether he mentioned Shabbos alone or Yom Tov alone he has not fulfilled, since he changed the conclusion from the coinage that the Sages coined. And in my humble opinion it would seem to distinguish: if he omitted [the formula] of Shabbos he has not fulfilled, and if he omitted [the formula] of Yom Tov he has fulfilled; for one can say that even according to Rabbi who added to conclude also with [the formula] of Yom Tov, that is only ideally, but after the fact if he concluded only with [the formula] of Shabbos he admits that he has fulfilled, for certainly he does not disagree with Beis Hillel that the essence of the blessing's text is only to conclude with [the formula] of Shabbos, except that ideally he added also with [the formula] of Yom Tov. And there is a proof for this from the Yerushalmi, where it is stated in the name of Rabbi himself like Beis Hillel of here; and so it is stated there: there is a Tanna who teaches he opens with [the formula] of Shabbos and concludes with [the formula] of Yom Tov, and there is a Tanna who teaches he opens with [the formula] of Yom Tov and concludes with [the formula] of Shabbos; Rabbi says he opens with [the formula] of Shabbos and concludes with [the formula] of Shabbos and mentions [the formula] of Yom Tov in between; R' Shimon ben Pazi said in the name of R' Yehoshua ben Levi: the law follows Rabbi; end quote of the Yerushalmi. So Rabbi there stated exactly like Beis Hillel of here, and we rule also there that the law follows Rabbi; and so that Rabbi's words not contradict each other, and also the halachic rulings not contradict each other, one must say that there it refers to the essence of the blessing's text according to Beis Hillel, and that Rabbi admits that the essence of the blessing's text [even regarding after the fact, that he has not fulfilled if he changed] is to finish with [the formula] of Shabbos, for he does not disagree with Beis Hillel in this; and about that it was stated there "the law follows Rabbi"; and here in our Gemara we say in his name that he added from himself to finish also with [the formula] of Yom Tov as well, but that is only ideally, for this is not of the essence of the blessing's text; and the Talmud ruled also in this that the law follows Rabbi, that is, that ideally he must finish with both of them, but after the fact [concluding] with Shabbos alone suffices, like that of the Yerushalmi. And so I saw in Netivos HaMishpat who also leaned that Rabbi refers only to ideally; and according to this there is a distinction between Shabbos and Yom Tov: if he omitted [the formula] of Yom Tov he has fulfilled, but if he omitted [the formula] of Shabbos he has not fulfilled, for the essence of the blessing's text is of Shabbos, and what he finished with [the formula] of Yom Tov accomplished nothing, and it is as if he did not conclude at all, for certainly he has not fulfilled. However, Chayei Adam wrote that in every case he has fulfilled, and he supported this from the Yerushalmi: where a blessing includes two things and he finished with one of them, he has fulfilled; for so it is stated there: R' Abahu in the name of R' Lazar: in every place if he went and mentioned "Adir HaMeluchah" he has not fulfilled, except for HaKel HaKadosh of Rosh Hashanah, and only in Musaf; and it accords with R' Yochanan ben Nuri; and he explained because according to R' Yochanan ben Nuri one must include Malchuyos in the sanctification of the Name, and therefore even if at the end he concluded only "Blessed are You, Hashem, Adir HaMeluchah," he has fulfilled even though he did not mention the sanctification of the Name in the conclusion; so a blessing that includes two things and he concluded with one, even with what is not of the essence of the blessing [as here, where the essence of the blessing is sanctification of the Name and Malchuyos is included in it], he has fulfilled after the fact. However, in my humble opinion this interpretation is very difficult for me, for if so what does it teach us that in every place if he went and mentioned "Blessed are You, Hashem, Adir HaMeluchah" he has not fulfilled? It is obvious, for he did not conclude with sanctification of the Name at all; and therefore it appears to me that it deals with a case where he concluded with sanctification of the Name, only that he also mentioned Adir HaMeluchah; and this is its meaning: throughout the year if he mentioned "Blessed are You, Hashem, Adir HaMeluchah, HaKel HaKadosh" he has not fulfilled, for he changed from the coinage; except in Musaf of Rosh Hashanah according to R' Yochanan ben Nuri, that even though ideally he must finish with sanctification of the Name alone [see there in the Alfasi in the view of R' Yochanan ben Beroka what he wrote regarding R' Yochanan ben Nuri's view], nevertheless if he went and said "Blessed are You, Hashem, Adir HaMeluchah, HaKel HaKadosh" he has fulfilled, for this too is of the matter of the blessing according to him. And even though I afterward found in the Milchamos, chapter 4 of Rosh Hashanah, that from the flow of its language it seems it too interprets the Yerushalmi like Chayei Adam, nevertheless for the law even according to their interpretation it is not compelling that we hold for the law like R' Eliezer there, that if he changed the essence of the text and finished with another [formula] that is included in it he has fulfilled; and according to what we wrote above regarding R' Yehoshua ben Levi who ruled like Rabbi, it appears that that is also regarding after the fact, that if he changed and finished only with [the formula] of Yom Tov he has not fulfilled, so as not to contradict our Talmud, as above; and this requires further study for practice. However, if he finished with [the formula] of Shabbos alone and began Retzeh, he certainly need not return.
