4) There: ‘The Yom Tov, a holy convocation,’ etc. Our custom is to say, ‘this day of the Festival of Matzos, the season of our freedom, a holy convocation, in remembrance of the Exodus from Egypt.’ One should not say ‘with love, a holy convocation,’ since one has already said, ‘Hashem our God, You gave us with love festivals for rejoicing.’ When it falls on Shabbos, however, some say the word ‘with love’ twice. See Levush for the reason. Chok Yaakov, paragraph 1; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1. He writes that this is unlike Pesach Meubin, paragraph 131, who says not to recite ‘with love’ in the phrase ‘You gave us’; see there. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 6, and Mishna Berurah, paragraph 1, write likewise. Nevertheless, the Mechaber writes: ‘Our custom is to say, “Hashem our God, You gave us with love festivals,” etc., “with love, a holy convocation,” etc.’ He explains that the first ‘with love’ refers to the blessed Creator, Who in His love and compassion gave us the festivals, etc.; the second refers to us, for we lovingly regard the holy convocation and do not, God forbid, consider it a burden. Yafeh LaLev, volume 2, paragraph 1, likewise writes that ‘with love’ is recited twice; ‘Hashem our God, You gave us with love festivals,’ etc., and ‘with love, a holy convocation,’ etc.; as printed in the siddurim, whether the festival falls on Shabbos or on a weekday, in accordance with the Mechaber and Sheva Binyamin, siman 304; see there. This is the known Sephardic custom.
