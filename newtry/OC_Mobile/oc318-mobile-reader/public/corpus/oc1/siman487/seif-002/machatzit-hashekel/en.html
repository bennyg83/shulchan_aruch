(s.k. 2) After etc. But most of the poskim etc.: Specifically the Rambam, and according to how Kesef Mishnah understood the Rambam's words, we follow the intention; but most of the poskim, and so one can say of the Rambam's words, that we do not follow his intention, but only according to what he uttered with his lips.
Specifically when he did not mention [the formula] of Yom Tov etc. For he already wrote in siman 268, se'if 4, even if he prayed a weekday Amidah etc., and all the more so here etc., for Yom Tov too is called Shabbos. Eliyah Rabbah and likewise Tosefes Shabbos understood that the Magen Avraham's view for the law is: if he mentioned [the formula] of Yom Tov in the middle, even if he concluded with [the formula] of Shabbos, he has fulfilled. Therefore in Eliyah Rabbah in siman 268 he wrote that the Magen Avraham's words here contradict what he wrote in siman 268; and in Tosefes Shabbos, siman 268, on what the Magen Avraham wrote there s.k. 5, and this is his language on what the Shulchan Aruch wrote there, that if he prayed an Amidah of eighteen on Shabbos and mentioned [the formula] of Shabbos in the middle he has fulfilled, and this is his language: this law requires further study, for in siman 487, se'if 1, he wrote that if on Yom Tov he concluded "Who sanctifies the Shabbos," he has not fulfilled even though he mentioned Yom Tov in the middle, since he concluded improperly etc.; and all the more so where he fixed no blessing at all for Shabbos. And one must say that it is worse when he concluded with [the formula] of another day; and this requires further study; end quote. And Tosefes Shabbos wrote, and this is his language: and his resolution is forced, for we hold in siman 108 that one who errs and mentions an occasion of other days in a prayer not in its time, it is not an interruption, and the same applies here too etc.; but in siman 487 the Magen Avraham resolved that there it deals with when he did not mention [the formula] of Yom Tov in the middle; end quote of Tosefes Shabbos. What he asked from siman 108, in my humble opinion is not a difficulty, for what connection is there between mention in the middle and conclusion? Granted that interruption it is not, nevertheless in the conclusion it is worse, for he changed from the coinage of the Sages, for everything follows the conclusion, as is written here. But regarding the essence of the law, in my humble opinion the intentions of the Magen Avraham in siman 268 and here are twins together and have one intention. And here too he holds that even if he mentioned [the formula] of Yom Tov in the middle, since he concluded improperly [he has not fulfilled]. For granted that here he first wrote "specifically when he did not mention [the formula] of Yom Tov in the middle," nevertheless in his conclusion here he concludes as he wrote in siman 268, for afterward he also asked on the law of 268 that everything follows the conclusion, and he resolved: since by law one should pray eighteen on Shabbos, only that they did not impose the burden on him. And if so, when he prays weekday [Amidah] on Shabbos, it is not a change in the conclusion from what the Sages coined, for on the contrary even the Sages' intent is to pray weekday [Amidah], except that they were lenient for him that a short prayer of seven blessings of Shabbos suffices. And if so, if he is stringent upon himself to pray eighteen blessings, there is no change from the coinage of the Sages; and if so this works well for that of siman 268. But here, where he prayed and concluded with [the formula] of Shabbos even though he mentioned Yom Tov in the middle, what of it? Behold everything follows the conclusion, and he changed in the coinage of the conclusion. And even if he prayed the entire Yom Tov Amidah, only that the conclusion was of Shabbos, and the conclusion was improper, he has not fulfilled, for he did not conclude as the Sages fixed.
And even though he first wrote "and all the more so here that he concluded Who sanctifies the Shabbos, for Yom Tov too is called Shabbos." It is implied that here is better than if he prayed an Amidah of eighteen; he wrote this before he concluded this reasoning, since by law one should pray eighteen on Shabbos, and if so certainly here that he concluded with [the formula] of Shabbos is better; but after he concluded this, "since by law one should pray eighteen on Shabbos etc.," on the contrary that of siman 268 is better. Therefore here too his conclusion is that if he concluded with [the formula] of Shabbos, even if he mentioned Yom Tov in the middle, he has not fulfilled; even though if he prayed eighteen and mentioned [the formula] of Shabbos in the middle he has fulfilled, here it is worse, since he concluded with [the formula] of another day it is worse, for he changed from the coinage of the Sages, as the Magen Avraham wrote in siman 268; which is not so when he prayed a weekday Amidah, which is not called a change of coinage, as above. And so somewhat in se'if 3 etc., even if he did not conclude with [the formula] of Yom Tov. That is to say, it is somewhat implied so from his writing "he forgot Ateh Bechartanu and said Ya'aleh Veyavo," and he did not write "and said Ya'aleh Veyavo and Vehasienu," implying that he also forgot Vehasienu. If so, since he forgot Vehasienu, there is evidence that he did not conclude with [the formula] of Yom Tov, for why would a different blessing not of Yom Tov be fluent on his tongue? For Vehasienu is a Yom Tov blessing; and since he did not say Vehasienu, necessarily he said a different blessing; and why would he finish the other blessing "Who sanctifies Yisrael and the seasons"? Must it not be that even if he did not conclude with [the formula] of Yom Tov, since he mentioned in the middle he has fulfilled. And according to his conclusion as written above, one must say this is no proof, and in truth it is not a complete necessity.
And if so, if he concluded etc. For here it deals with a case where he did not mention [the formula] of Yom Tov and also erred in the conclusion and finished "Who sanctifies the Shabbos"; nevertheless, since he retracted within toch kedei dibbur and said "Who sanctifies Yisrael etc.," he has fulfilled; all the more so if he finished according to its law "Who sanctifies Yisrael and the seasons."

And it is implied in Pesachim folio 117b: Rava said, I found the elders of Pumbedisa. Who say: on Shabbos, both in prayer and in Kiddush, he concludes "Who sanctifies the Shabbos"; and on Yom Tov, both in prayer and in Kiddush, he concludes "Who sanctifies Yisrael and the seasons." For Yom Tov, since Yisrael established it through sanctification of the month and the years, therefore they attribute the sanctity to Yisrael, and they conclude "Who sanctifies Yisrael," who sanctify the seasons; but Shabbos is fixed from the six days of Creation and does not depend on Yisrael, therefore he does not mention Yisrael in the conclusion. And Rava said to them: granted that in Kiddush the law is with you, but in prayer it is not as you say; rather, prayer, whether on Shabbos or on Yom Tov, is "Who sanctifies Yisrael"; and my reason is that since prayer is in public, therefore out of honor of the congregation one should mention Yisrael even on Shabbos in the conclusion. And the conclusion of the Talmud there is like the elders of Pumbedisa, and so we hold. And the Magen Avraham holds that when Rava said that in prayer, whether on Shabbos or on Yom Tov, "Who sanctifies Yisrael," that is to say he says no more, and even on Yom Tov one need not finish "and the seasons"; therefore, granted that ideally we hold like the elders of Pumbedisa, nevertheless after the fact if he acted like Rava we do not make him repeat the act. And therefore the Magen Avraham finished: but in Kiddush he has not fulfilled, for in Kiddush Rava agrees; so it appears to me. But Chok Yaakov wrote on the Magen Avraham's words that his words are not compelling, for Rava abbreviated his words and mentioned only wherein he disagrees with the elders of Pumbedisa, who hold that on Shabbos one should not mention Yisrael in the conclusion; and Rava said that in prayer Shabbos is equal to Yom Tov in that one must mention Yisrael.
And automatically we know that each must finish according to its law: on Shabbos he must add the word "the Shabbos" in the conclusion, and on Yom Tov he must add the word "and the seasons." And so Eliyah Rabbah agreed with him. And perhaps the Magen Avraham's intent is that it is impossible that Rava disagreed only that also on Shabbos one must mention Yisrael in the conclusion; if so, why lengthen "prayer, whether on Shabbos or on Yom Tov, Who sanctifies Yisrael," why mention Yom Tov, for on Yom Tov he agrees, and he should have said only that prayer even on Shabbos is "Who sanctifies Yisrael"; rather, necessarily he also disagrees on Yom Tov that one need not finish "and the seasons," and on Shabbos one need not mention Shabbos. But this is not a necessity, for he mentioned also Kiddush; see there in the Gemara; and in Kiddush he does not disagree at all. And see in Chok Yaakov who brought in the name of Kenesses HaGedolah that if on Yom Tov that falls on Shabbos he said "Who sanctifies Yisrael and the seasons" and did not mention Shabbos in the conclusion, he has fulfilled. And Peri Chadash disagrees and holds that he must return and pray, for it is a change of coinage. And the same applies if he concluded with [the formula] of Shabbos alone and said "Who sanctifies the Shabbos" and nothing more: it also is a change; and likewise if he concluded "Who sanctifies Yisrael and the Shabbos and the seasons," it too is called a change and he returns; as is stated in Masseches Beitzah, folio 17a, that one should not precede "Who sanctifies Yisrael" to Shabbos, for Yisrael do not sanctify Shabbos, but it is fixed from the six days of Creation; which is not so of Yom Tov, whose sanctity depends on the sanctity of Yisrael, for through Yisrael being sanctified they sanctify months and years, and thereby the Yamim Tovim are sanctified; see there.
