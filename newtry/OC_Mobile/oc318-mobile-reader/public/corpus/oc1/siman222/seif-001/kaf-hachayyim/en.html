1) [Seif 1] Over reports, etc. This applies specifically if he heard the report from a trustworthy person who saw the event. Sefer Chassidim, siman 841; Mateh Moshe, siman 369; Magen Avraham, paragraph 1. Even if he heard it from a trustworthy person, such as Reuven who was robbed and heard that the robber was caught and that everything would be returned, he should not recite the blessing until it is actually returned to him. Sefer Chassidim there; Mateh Moshe there; Olas Tamid at the end of siman 221; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1.
2) One who was compelled by poverty to marry a wealthy woman against his desire recites HaTov VeHaMeitiv and Dayan HaEmes. Sefer Chassidim, siman 382; Magen Avraham there; Eliyah Rabbah there. See, however, Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1, who writes that nowadays these blessings are recited less frequently, as stated in the gloss to siman 223, seif 1; see there. Therefore, it appears that he should recite them without the Name and kingship.
3) There: Over reports, etc. The use of the plural phrases 'good reports' and 'tidings' may indicate that even if many arrived simultaneously or one after another, a single blessing suffices. Halachos Ketanos, volume 1, siman 199; Yad Aharon, Hagahos HaTur. We wrote the same above at the beginning of siman 219; see there.
4) There: If they are good for him and for others, etc. This applies specifically when they share that same good, such as when his wife bore a son, as in siman 223, or when he inherited his father together with brothers, as explained there. If they do not share that same good, although the reports are good for him and for others, he recites only Shehecheyanu. Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 1.
