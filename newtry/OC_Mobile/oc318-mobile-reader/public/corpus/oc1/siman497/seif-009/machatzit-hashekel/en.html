Seif katan 9, that they still require, etc. And to me this requires study, etc. Meaning, what the Baal Magen Avraham permitted regarding birds in a roofed enclosure, even in a large enclosure, except for a deror bird, which is forbidden even in a roofed enclosure. With a wild animal, which cannot fly, there is no reason to distinguish between roofed and unroofed. Furthermore, Tosafot wrote, etc. On 24 it says there: regarding what Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel said, this is the rule, anything still lacking trapping is forbidden, what is considered lacking trapping? Rav Yosef said in Shmuel's name: anything about which one says, bring a trap and let us catch it. Rashi asked that just above it gave another measure of what is considered not trapped, namely anything he cannot reach in one lunge. Rashi answered that these two measures, reaching it in one lunge and not needing a trap, are the same. Tosafot wrote that this is forced, and therefore answered that above speaks of a wild animal and here of a bird. Although above it said regarding birds, where the Mishnayos were contradicted, one says one may trap a wild animal and bird from enclosures, while there it says one may not trap wild animals and birds from enclosures on Yom Tov, and it answers regarding birds that this speaks of a roofed enclosure and that of an unroofed one, nevertheless it did not explain the measure of the enclosure, how small it must be not to be considered lacking trapping. Thus Tosafot explicitly wrote that even in a roofed enclosure, with a bird, one requires the measure of an enclosure; whenever one would say bring a trap, it is still forbidden. However, in the responsum of Baal Magen Avraham there he cited the language of the Ran, and the Beis Yosef also cited it here, which implies as he wrote. The Ran states: what is considered lacking trapping? Such as where we say, bring a trap, etc. He wrote in his novellae: regarding birds that are not domesticated, it is clear in the Gemara that whenever they are in a roofed place and have no way to exit from it, they are considered trapped, except for a deror bird, etc. This implies like the Baal Magen Avraham. Regarding Tosafot's question, one can say that the Ran holds like Rashi's answer above, and that the measure of bring a trap applies to a wild animal, but for a bird a roofed place suffices.
So it also appears in the Rambam, chapter 10 of Hilchos Shabbos.
That is, it does not need a trap, and from this we automatically know that each species follows what it requires. This is also the view of the Shulchan Aruch at the beginning of siman 316. In truth, this is the language of the Shulchan Aruch there: one who traps, etc., and other birds and a deer into a house or enclosure where they are trapped is liable. The Magen Avraham understood that what is written, where they are trapped, refers also to a house; although a house is roofed, he nevertheless conditioned it on their being trapped there. This implies that the matter does not depend only on being roofed, but specifically requires that they be trapped there, meaning that it is a small house. Nevertheless, there is room for a litigant to disagree and say that where they are trapped refers only to the adjacent enclosure, not to the house; but in a roofed house, even if it is large, they are considered trapped. Perhaps the Magen Avraham holds that the enclosure is similar to a house, meaning that the enclosure is roofed, and even so he conditioned it on their being trapped in it.
So it also appears in the Rambam. This is the proof he brought above from the Rambam, except that here he strengthens the proof somewhat more.
Even roofed, as written above, for if it is not roofed, even a small enclosure is not considered trapped.
So it may appear in the Gemara. Perhaps he understood this from the fact that when the Gemara resolved the contradiction between the Mishnah that one may trap wild animals and birds from enclosures and the other Mishnah that one may not trap from enclosures, it answered: bird against bird is not difficult; this is in a roofed enclosure and that in an unroofed one. Wild animal against wild animal is not difficult; this is in a large enclosure and that in a small enclosure. Since it included wild animal and bird in one clause, it implies that just as the wild animal case speaks of a small enclosure, so too the bird case speaks of a small enclosure, even though perforce it also speaks of a roofed one. This implies that even when roofed, it must be a small enclosure. But Maharsha there wrote that according to Tosafot, once it answers regarding wild animals that this is a small enclosure, etc., it no longer needs to establish the bird case as roofed or unroofed; rather, it too can be explained as a small enclosure where one does not say bring a trap, etc. Thus the proof I wrote is gone. However, the Magen Avraham follows his own view, for he wrote above that without a roof, even a small house is not considered trapped. Therefore even according to the conclusion one must say that the Mishnah permitting birds to be trapped speaks of a roofed enclosure; only the Mishnah saying they may not be trapped need not speak of an unroofed enclosure, but even if roofed, it is a large enclosure. According to this, the Magen Avraham's proof above is sound, that even when roofed one requires a small enclosure, as above.
