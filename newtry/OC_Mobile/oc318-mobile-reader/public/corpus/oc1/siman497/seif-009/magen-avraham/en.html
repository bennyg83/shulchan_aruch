It is forbidden to catch them. One who does so is liable to bring a chatas. The reason is that although they return to their coop in the evening, their sustenance is not incumbent upon you and they are not considered prepared. Alternatively, they tend to slip away and flee from people, and are not easily caught. Gemara. Rosh's discussion of geese implies the first reason, while Ran implies the second. Both reasons should be taken into account. See the end of siman 316.
That do not fly. Meaning, beyond the techum. Yam Shel Shlomo, chapter 1, siman 28, and Beit Yosef, siman 517, in the name of a responsum of Rosh. Maggid Mishneh and Ran, however, write that the permission applies specifically when they hop, because they do not hop more than fifty amos, or when they fly only a little. Tur in siman 517 and Rosh in his rulings also omit the words “beyond the techum.” It therefore appears to me that Rosh in his responsum follows his own view that since pigeons return to their coop in the evening, they do not still require trapping. They are considered unprepared only because their sustenance is not incumbent upon you. Therefore, if they do not fly beyond the techum, they are considered prepared. In his rulings, however, Rosh retracted and held that the reason is nevertheless trapping. Otherwise, designation should be effective. Therefore, if they fly in a manner requiring a net to catch them, they are forbidden. See Maggid Mishneh.
