And he found three. In that case, one unprepared bird is mixed among them. We cannot say that one is nullified in two, because it is an item that will later become permitted. Ran. According to this, if one of them was eaten or fell into the sea, all the others are permitted, as stated in Yoreh Deah, siman 110, seifim 7 and 8. See there. The Gemara, however, raises an additional concern that perhaps all of the original birds left and these are different birds. One must say that Ran understands that according to the Gemara's conclusion, which states that fledglings are different because they tend to hop, we no longer suspect that all the original birds left. Rather, we say that one bird came here from elsewhere. Only regarding money do we entertain the other concern. If one left one hundred coins and found two hundred, we say that these are different coins, because people do not ordinarily place their money beside another person's money. Regarding pigeons, however, we say that one came here from elsewhere. See seif 13 in the gloss and what is written in siman 439.
With a partition. In the manner of separate nests in dovecotes. In a single nest, however, we say that these are certainly the same birds and that they merely exchanged places. Hagahos Ashri; Rashi.
