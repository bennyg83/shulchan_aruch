He designated two and found three. From the view of the Taz, s.k. 9, it is implied that if he designated two that were tied and afterward found three that were tied, it is permitted; for even though the third came from elsewhere, still presumably a person tied the third to them, and presumably he did not tie them on Yom Tov but before Yom Tov; and if so, they are all designated through this tying; see there, that his words are somewhat garbled, but so his intent appears. And so the Shulchan Aruch HaRav copied for practical law. However, in the Peri Chadash this matter is set out as forbidden; and likewise in Chemed Moshe he also wrote that one should not rely on this for practical law.
