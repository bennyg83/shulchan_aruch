{36} He designated two, etc. At least one pigeon came from elsewhere and was not designated. It is not nullified, because living creatures are important and are not nullified, and it is also an item that will later become permitted. The later authorities write that this applies where he left them tied or untied and found the third in the same condition. If he left the two tied and found them as he had left them, while a third pigeon beside them was untied, the two are not forbidden. We assume with certainty that they are the ones he prepared, and that the untied pigeon came from elsewhere. They further write that the one forbidden pigeon prohibits them all only while all are present. If one was eaten or fell into the sea and no longer exists, the others are permitted, for we say that the forbidden one was eaten or fell, in accordance with the conditions explained in Yoreh Deah 110:7-8; see the Rama there.
{37} In separate compartments, etc. Dovecotes are generally built in many tiers, with numerous partitions in each tier. If, however, he designated black and white pigeons in one compartment and found them in exchanged positions, they are permitted, for they certainly changed places with one another.
{38} And he found black pigeons in the place, etc. If he found them in the places where he had left them, then even if many other white or black pigeons that he had not designated were in the dovecote, we are not concerned that they exchanged places.
{39} They are forbidden. Where he designated two and found three, at least one certainly came from elsewhere. Where he designated black pigeons and found white ones, we are likewise concerned that those he designated flew away and these are others that came from elsewhere. This concerns a case where other dovecotes are within fifty cubits, or where the pigeons can fly somewhat. Otherwise, it is preferable to assume that they exchanged places rather than that they came from elsewhere, as the Mechaber writes in seif 14.
