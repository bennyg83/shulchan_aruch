And even if they were tied. In the Tur this "even if tied" is not written. And we will present the sugya of folio 10, and it will be clarified with God's help that the law is with him. If he designated three and found two, they are permitted. What is the reason? These are they, and one of them went out into the world. Let us say the Mishnah is Rabbi and not the Sages, for it was taught: if he left two hundred (meaning a sela of maaser sheni) and found a maneh: a maneh is set down and a maneh is cast about, [these are] the words of Rabbi; and the Sages say all are chullin (for certainly he took both and placed them elsewhere, and this is chullin and he forgot). Even if you say [it is] the Sages: it was stated upon it that R' Yochanan and R' Elazar both say fledglings are different since they are prone to walk about (one by one; therefore we attribute that one went and its fellows remained; but regarding a maneh it did not go by itself unless he took it, and when he took he took both). And we ask: and why does he need to answer upon it "since they are prone to walk about"? Behold it was stated upon it regarding R' Yochanan and R' Elazar: one said the dispute is about two pouches; but regarding one pouch, all agree [they are] chullin; and one said the dispute is about one pouch (that both counts are placed in one pouch: in that the Sages say that when he took the whole pouch he took [all]); but regarding two pouches they agree that he took one and left the second. It is well according to the one who said the dispute is about two pouches: that is why he needed to answer (for it is similar to our Mishnah of two fledglings; he needs to answer so that our Mishnah stand like the Sages because of "since they are prone to walk about"; but according to the one who said that regarding two pouches the Sages agree, now why does he need to answer "prone to walk about": behold regarding two pouches they also say so). Rav Ashi said: here we are dealing with tied fledglings and tied pouches (the one who said the dispute is about two pouches: he means two pouches like one pouch, that is, tied; and the one who said but regarding two pouches all agree "a maneh is set down," etc.: regarding ones that are not tied; and our answer implies even regarding tied fledglings that the remaining are permitted; therefore if not that fledglings are different since they are prone to walk about, it would not come like the Sages, for regarding tied pouches the Sages say all agree [they are] chullin). But pouches do not come apart from each other (and since they are tied, he took both). And Rabbi would say to you: pouches too sometimes their knots decay. And I have reason to scrutinize why Rav Ashi needed to explain that of pouches as also dealing with tied ones: let him answer that specifically regarding one pouch they disagree, and regarding two pouches that are not tied the Sages agree with Rabbi that a maneh is set down, etc.; and that is regarding untied ones; but regarding tied ones Rabbi agrees with the Sages that all are chullin; and here regarding tied ones in designation, everyone agrees they are forbidden, for all were taken, except that because they are prone to walk about it is permitted according to everyone. And it appears to me that Rav Ashi wants to explain the Mishnah of designation and the Mishnah of maaser sheni in one style, since they are taught in one wording; and there is more to answer on this, except that this is not its place. And Beis Yosef asked why the Tur did not write "permitted even when tied," since Rav Ashi establishes it so. It is well according to the Rambam: there is nothing to ask, for he rules that the dispute is about two pouches, so we have no compulsion to establish the Mishnah of designation regarding tied ones; and if so we can say that regarding tied ones they are forbidden, and "prone to walk about" does not help, for Rav Ashi needed to establish regarding tied ones only according to the one who said the dispute is about one pouch. But the Tur, who ruled in siman 439 like the one who said the dispute is about one pouch, and distinguished between tied or not: he should have said here "even when tied." And he answered that it is possible that since he wrote here simply "permitted," even when tied is implied; until here. And this is puzzling: why did the Tur not teach us the novelty explicitly "even when tied," since there is a distinction in siman 439 in this. And it appears to me that there is nothing to ask, for the Tur has no decisive ruling whose words [prevail] in the dispute of one pouch or two pouches; and therefore here regarding a doubtful case of something prepared, where we hold that we follow the stringent view, as above in se'if 3: therefore we should say maybe the law follows the one who said the dispute is about two pouches, and here regarding designation it is permitted even according to the Sages because they are prone to walk about; but regarding tied ones they agree that the reasoning of being prone to walk about does not help, and they are forbidden, for certainly all were taken and these are others. Therefore he did not write here to permit when tied; and to forbid he also did not want to write, for maybe the reasoning of walking about helps even when tied; and all the more so according to the one who said the dispute is about one pouch, for it is proven clearly that even when tied they are permitted by force of the reasoning of walking about. But above in siman 439 regarding chametz: there is a doubt whether one needs to search after all the ten: it is a rabbinic doubt, for according to the Torah mere bitul suffices; we rely in a doubt on the lenient view like the one who said the dispute is about one pouch, that is, two that are tied; therefore regarding tied ones it is forbidden like the Sages who disagree with Rabbi; and regarding ones that are not tied we follow the lenient view even according to the Sages, who agree regarding two pouches that are not tied; so the view of the Tur appears to me correctly; and one should not permit here when tied for the reason I wrote; and so it is proper for practical law. And there is further to scrutinize regarding Rav Ashi's establishment that it deals with tied ones: and if they are still tied before us, those that remain, it is obvious they are permitted, for here there is no concern except maybe these went out into the world and these are others: who tied them after they came here? And even if you want to say that another person tied them: certainly on Yom Tov he did not tie them, but before Yom Tov, and it turns out they are prepared in any case. It is well regarding maaser sheni that there is a doubt maybe he himself placed maaser sheni coins elsewhere and placed others that are chullin here; which is not so regarding this designation. And further, even if he designated two and found three, and the two are tied and the added one is not tied (for if it is tied, they are certainly others): rather certainly the third is not tied, only these two: if so these two that are tied should be permitted for the reason we said. And it appears that here it deals with [a case where] now they are not tied, but at the time of designation they were tied; and therefore if he designated two that were tied and found three that are not tied, they are forbidden because of the mixture of the added one: for if he had found only two that are not tied, they would be permitted, for we say these are they, except that their knot came undone; but if he designated three that were tied and found two that are not tied, we say one of them had its knot undone and went out into the world, and these are those that remained, except that their knot came undone. And my master, my father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote regarding one who left two that were tied and found them not tied: they are forbidden; and what appears according to my humble opinion correctly, I have written.
