13) There: Doubtful catching is forbidden, etc. All laws of muktzeh and nolad are included, because the item will become permitted later or because the Sages were stringent with this doubt. Rashba disagrees: since muktzeh and nolad are rabbinic, their doubts are lenient; only a matter close to a Torah prohibition is forbidden, such as uncertainty whether it was caught on Yom Tov or beforehand. Maggid Mishneh, chapter 2, law 6, questions Rashba: why then is an egg of uncertain Yom Tov origin forbidden? He favors Rambam, who draws no distinction. Beis Yosef, Levush 5, and Pri Chadash agree, as do Chemed Moshe 5, Shulchan Aruch HaRav 8, and Mikra Kodesh 37:18. Bach distinguishes where a prohibition was previously established, but Pri Chadash and Maamar Mordechai 4 reject this. The unqualified rulings below in siman 513:2 and 517:1 and the later authorities imply all doubtful muktzeh and nolad is forbidden, whether or not prohibition was established. The lenient statement below in siman 498:3 will be explained there, with God's help.
