{40} He designated three and found two, etc. We do not presume an irregularity. We say that the two are the ones he prepared, and only one went elsewhere.
{41} They separate from one another. Living creatures commonly loosen and separate from their bindings, and we say that the third detached itself and went elsewhere. The same applies if he also found the remaining two untied; they are permitted for this reason. Taz disagrees with this ruling. According to him, whenever they were not found as they had been left, we say that those he designated went elsewhere and these are different pigeons. He concedes, however, that if one designated two and found two, then even if he left them tied and found them untied, we do not presume an irregularity but say that their bindings became loosened. Several later authorities agree with the Rama in all cases.
