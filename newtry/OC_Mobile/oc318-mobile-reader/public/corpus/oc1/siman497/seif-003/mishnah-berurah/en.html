{7} A doubt whether it was trapped. Although according to several authorities the primary prohibition of trapping on Yom Tov is not according to the Torah, as the Mechaber also rules without qualification in siman 495, seif 2, they nevertheless ruled stringently even in a case of doubt because it is an item that will later become permitted, since it can be eaten after Yom Tov. Regarding an item that will later become permitted, the Sages were stringent even in a doubt concerning a D'Rabbanan prohibition.
{8} They are forbidden. They are forbidden even to move. In the evening, however, they are permitted for eating, even if they were certainly trapped that day, and one need not wait the time required to perform the act, because they were not trapped by a person but were caught on their own. The poskim agree that this does not apply only to a doubt concerning trapping, but likewise to every doubt concerning muktzeh, and certainly to a doubt concerning nolad, regarding which we are stringent as with a Torah prohibition. There is a dispute among the poskim regarding a doubt whether something came from outside the techum; this will be explained below in siman 498, seifim 3 and 4.
{9} If he found the traps damaged. The traps are spread over a great length. When an animal falls into one end and struggles and pulls away in order to escape, all the traps are damaged, and the other end is also dislodged from the place where it had been fixed. Therefore, although he saw nothing trapped at this location, this is an indication that an animal or bird was trapped at the other end.
