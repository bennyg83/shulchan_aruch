12) [Se'if 3] Even doubtful catching is forbidden, etc. Although a rabbinic doubt is generally lenient, this is forbidden because it is something that will become permitted later, or because the Sages chose stringency here. Mordechai in Yevamos notes many rabbinic doubts treated stringently, so cases cannot be analogized. Magen Avraham 3. Calling the matter rabbinic follows Rambam and those who hold these Yom Tov labors rabbinically forbidden; others hold they are Torah-prohibited, as above siman 495, paragraph 24. The reasons differ practically: if the prohibition is because the item will later become permitted, fish that will spoil by evening may be eaten that day, as in Yoreh De'ah 102:4. Under the second reason they remain forbidden. Beis Meir extensively proves the second reason and forbids every case. Nevertheless, in great loss and for Yom Tov rejoicing one may perhaps rely on the first.
13) There: Doubtful catching is forbidden, etc. All laws of muktzeh and nolad are included, because the item will become permitted later or because the Sages were stringent with this doubt. Rashba disagrees: since muktzeh and nolad are rabbinic, their doubts are lenient; only a matter close to a Torah prohibition is forbidden, such as uncertainty whether it was caught on Yom Tov or beforehand. Maggid Mishneh, chapter 2, law 6, questions Rashba: why then is an egg of uncertain Yom Tov origin forbidden? He favors Rambam, who draws no distinction. Beis Yosef, Levush 5, and Pri Chadash agree, as do Chemed Moshe 5, Shulchan Aruch HaRav 8, and Mikra Kodesh 37:18. Bach distinguishes where a prohibition was previously established, but Pri Chadash and Maamar Mordechai 4 reject this. The unqualified rulings below in siman 513:2 and 517:1 and the later authorities imply all doubtful muktzeh and nolad is forbidden, whether or not prohibition was established. The lenient statement below in siman 498:3 will be explained there, with G-d's help.
14) There: Even doubtful catching is forbidden, etc. It is likewise forbidden to move the item, as in the preceding se'if and siman 515:1. Pri Chadash; Shulchan Gavoah 4; Shulchan Aruch HaRav 8. On Rosh Hashanah it appears forbidden both days where doubt exists whether it was caught before Yom Tov or on the first day. Eshel Avraham 3. See below, paragraph 19.
15) There: Even doubtful catching is forbidden, etc. By evening they are permitted even if certainly caught by themselves that day. Magen Avraham 3. One need not wait the time needed to perform the act, since they were caught by themselves, as above siman 322:3 and our words there. Likewise, if a gentile caught them for himself and later gave them to a Jew, no waiting is required, as above siman 325:5. See also below, siman 515:1.
16) There: If he found the traps damaged, etc. The traps extend a mil or half a mil. When an animal falls into one end, its struggling and pulling to escape damages the entire line and dislodges the other end from its fastening, signaling an animal at the far end. Rashi, Beitzah 24b; Taz 4.
17) There: If he found the traps damaged, etc. Bach rules stringently, but Eliyah Rabbah 6 challenges him from several Rishonim and the Yerushalmi, all of whom rule like Shulchan Aruch. Pri Chadash agrees, as do the later authorities.
