Seif katan 3, even, etc. But in the evening they are permitted, meaning one need not wait at night for the time it would take to do it. Although when a non-Jew brings a gift on Yom Tov, even after Yom Tov one must wait enough time to have done it, as explained in siman 325 and siman 515, the reason there is so that he not benefit from labor of Yom Tov. Once he waits that amount of time, he no longer benefits from the labor of Yom Tov.
For at night he could have done it as well. According to Rabbeinu Tam, the reason is lest he command the non-Jew to do labor for him on Yom Tov; once he waits enough time to have done it, this concern no longer applies, since he gains nothing from the non-Jew doing it on Yom Tov. This applies specifically to labor done by a person. But labor that happens on its own does not require waiting enough time to have done it, for the above reasons do not apply. So too it is written in siman 322, seif 3, regarding fruit that fell from a tree on Shabbos: although it is forbidden on Shabbos, after Shabbos it is permitted and one need not wait enough time to have done it.
