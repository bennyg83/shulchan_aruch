Even a doubtful case of trapping. Even according to the one who holds that the prohibition of trapping is rabbinic, nevertheless regarding its doubt we follow the stringent view, for it is a matter that has a [later] permit. And nevertheless regarding a double doubt, as is written afterward, it is permitted. And it requires further study regarding the Rema, who wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 110, se'if 8, that a double doubt does not help for a matter that has a [later] permit, and one should be stringent unless needed; and here he wrote nothing.
The traps are disordered. Rashi explained: that they are spread over the length of a mil, and when a wild animal falls at one end, because it loosens and tears to get out, the traps all become disordered, and their other end is pulled from where it was stuck, and it is a sign for him that there is a wild animal at the other end. And the Maggid Mishneh wrote: even though the Rif did not mention this, and likewise the Rambam, nevertheless the law is so, and they did not need to bring it, for it is obvious; and in the Gemara they brought it only incidentally beside the rest.
