Even a doubtful case of trapping, etc. And a gift concerning which there is a concern of trapping, or something attached [to the ground] that was brought to a Jew on the first day of Yom Tov, is permitted to another Jew on the second day of Yom Tov, that is, after enough time has elapsed for them [to have done the work] (Responsa Maharshal, siman 39). And the same applies to every case of doubtful muktzeh and doubtful something that came into being [on Yom Tov]: it is forbidden (the Ran, chapter "Ein Tzadin," and the Maggid Mishneh, chapter 2 of Hilchos Yom Tov). And their law is the same whether on the first day of Yom Tov or on the second day of Yom Tov (Beis Yosef). And some say that on the second day of Yom Tov a doubtful case of something prepared [in advance] is permitted (so wrote the Ran).
