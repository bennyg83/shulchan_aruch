Magen Avraham, s.k. 18. "But the simple meaning is that it is according to the Torah" (Bach). And for this reason it is fine that it is permitted to flay before the inspection, and we are not concerned lest it be a treifah, as it is forbidden to eat before the inspection: that is, the flaying now is only rabbinic, for in a Torah matter we follow the majority of animals that are kosher, and we permit it by reason of its end because of its beginning. And according to this it is possible that if it was slaughtered before Yom Tov not for the need of Yom Tov, or that he had leisure to flay while it was still day, it is forbidden to flay on Yom Tov before inspection. And according to the Maharshal, who holds that one would not refrain from slaughtering because of a concern of treifos, as the Magen Avraham wrote in his name in s.k. 19, it is apparently difficult why it is permitted to flay before the inspection.
