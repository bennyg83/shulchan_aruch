It is narrow. Even if it is very long, it is permitted. Maggid Mishneh. Any box wider than six tefachim is forbidden to take from on Yom Tov, as stated in seif 1. Bach. Maharil writes that if one can catch them by hand, trapping does not apply; if they can be caught only with a utensil, one may not take them on Yom Tov. Therefore, fish kept in a well are considered still requiring trapping. According to him, if one can catch them by hand but does not wish to descend into the water and instead takes them with a utensil, it is permitted. This follows Rambam and Rashi. According to Rama, however, who follows Raavad and Rashba, whenever the box is wide it is forbidden even if trapping is not lacking. The common practice is lenient, but at minimum one should act through a non-Jew when the box is wide. Maharshal adopts a different approach. He writes that one who catches fish is not liable for trapping, because it does not resemble the trapping in the Mishkan, but is liable for reaping, since he detaches the fish from its place of growth, as stated in the Yerushalmi, chapter 7 of Shabbos. For this reason it is forbidden even on Yom Tov. This applies specifically in a pond where the fish swim in water and feed from the earth and vegetation. A perforated box within the water, however, involves no trapping at all. Even if one needs a small net to reach into the box, it is not regarded as trapping and is permitted on Yom Tov. A water channel is the fish's normal place of growth as it travels toward the box, and when it is blocked off the fish is considered trapped because it can no longer swim in its place of growth as before. I do not understand this. The Yerushalmi's statement that one who separates any creature from its life-source is liable for reaping concerns a fish already trapped and standing in a bowl of water, which one removes from the bowl on Shabbos, thereby causing it to die. The same appears in our Gemara at the end of page 107, in Rashi and Tosafot. Although Rashi there explains the liability as taking a life, one may say that the Yerushalmi views it as uprooting the creature from its place of growth. It is nevertheless obvious that trapping applies to fish, as all the poskim write. Maharshal's further statement that reaping is forbidden on Yom Tov proves nothing, because trapping is also forbidden on Yom Tov.
