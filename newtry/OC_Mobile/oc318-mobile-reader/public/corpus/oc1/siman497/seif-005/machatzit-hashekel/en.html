Seif katan 5, it is permitted specifically to take all of them. But if he does not want to take all of them, he must tie, before Yom Tov, a thread or cord to the fish he wants to take.
As written in seif 10 regarding doves. One must say, this one and that one I will take, and it is not enough to say, from here I will take. The reason is as the Magen Avraham wrote in seif katan 13, and these are the words of the Gemara: sometimes he leaves the lean ones, etc., and moves something unfit, or sometimes they are all lean and he may refrain from the joy of Yom Tov. Because of these reasons, the Yam Shel Shlomo holds that with fish too there is concern, and therefore he must take them all. It is possible, however, that this is comparable to geese, etc., where it is only a stringency to say before Yom Tov, this one and that one I will take, or to take them all, as the Magen Avraham wrote in seif katan 8. The reason to distinguish between doves and geese is written in Yam Shel Shlomo, chapter Ein Tzadin, siman 2. The first reason, lest he leave the lean ones, etc., and move something unfit for him, applies specifically to doves, which require designation because they are muktzeh. If after moving them he leaves them because they are lean, it is clarified retroactively that he had not designated the lean ones, and he therefore moved muktzeh. This is unlike geese and chickens, which do not require designation because they are not muktzeh, and that concern disappears. Even if there is still concern that it becomes clear retroactively that he exerted himself unnecessarily for Yom Tov by moving the lean ones, most poskim were not concerned for such extra exertion, only Raavyah and Rashbat. According to the law one may rely on most poskim; only a G-d-fearing person should choose before Yom Tov to satisfy Raavyah and Rashbat. The second reason, lest they all be found lean and he refrain from the joy of Yom Tov, does not apply to geese, since they walk inside the house and he handles them constantly, so he knows whether they are fat or lean.
Although he is not exacting about each one. Nevertheless, generally he knows whether they are all fat or lean, and even so he relied upon them. The Magen Avraham holds that perhaps with fish too these concerns do not apply, as with geese. The first reason, lest he leave the lean ones, etc., does not apply, for when one dams the water he designates them all, since by damming it all of them are prepared, and this is better than designating the whole dovecote in seif 11, which is ineffective; see the Taz there. Therefore the Magen Avraham used the language perhaps, for perhaps it is comparable to the case of seif 11, as above. Regarding the second reason, lest they all be lean and he refrain from the joy of Yom Tov, Maharshal himself wrote in the above siman 2 that this concern does not apply to fish, because before opening the fish one does not know which is lean and which is fat; also, people are not so particular with fish whether they are fat or lean; furthermore, if he refrains and does not eat fish at all because they are lean, this is not a withholding of the joy of Yom Tov, for joy is only with meat. According to this, in the Magen Avraham's view, even one who is G-d-fearing need not be stringent with fish to take them all, even to satisfy Raavyah and Rashbat. For in the ways he rejected the second reason regarding fish, two of them also reject the first reason: we need not be concerned that he may leave the lean ones, etc., and exert himself unnecessarily, because before the fish is opened he does not know whether it is lean such that one might worry he will leave it, and he is not so particular about a fish if it is lean for the same reason. Therefore he will not come to move muktzeh or exert himself unnecessarily.
