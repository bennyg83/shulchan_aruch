{12} At the entrance and exit. This is so that no new fish enter and none of the existing fish leave.
{13} He may take, etc. There is no muktzeh concern, because blocking the channel is considered equivalent to designating known fish. Whether he may select what he needs and return the remainder to the water depends upon his intention when he blocked it. If he thought that he might need all the fish found in the channel on Yom Tov, all the fish were prepared. He may then lift as many as he wishes, select the large and choice fish, and return the rest to the water. If, however, when he blocked the channel he knew with certainty that he would need only some fish, nothing was prepared specifically. When he selects one and leaves a smaller one, it becomes evident that the latter was not prepared, and he has moved it unnecessarily, as discussed below in seif 10. He should therefore not select and leave the remainder, but must take and eat whatever comes into his hand, whether good or inferior, and may not return what he does not prefer.
{14} It is narrow. See Bach and the other later authorities, who write that if it is more than six tefachim wide it is forbidden. Regarding its length, many later authorities are lenient even if it is very long, because when it is narrow the fish can be caught easily. Even where it is permitted, some are stringent not to trap them with a net in the ordinary weekday manner, but only by hand. One should initially take this opinion into account. This does not apply only to a water channel. The same laws apply if he placed fish in a perforated box and set it in the water. One authority maintains that these laws apply specifically to a pond or channel, where the fish remain upon the ground in their natural habitat. When they are contained in a utensil, however, trapping does not apply at all, and it is permitted even if one must bring a net. It therefore appears that one need not be stringent regarding a box, and may take the fish by hand even when the box is much wider than six tefachim. The later authorities write that if one placed fish before Yom Tov into a well and they can be caught only with a utensil, it is forbidden to catch them on Yom Tov. Nevertheless, if the pit is deep, he need not descend in order to catch them by hand, but may stand above and catch them with a utensil.
