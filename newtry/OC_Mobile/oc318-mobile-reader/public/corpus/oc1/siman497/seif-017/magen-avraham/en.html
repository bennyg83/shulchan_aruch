It is forbidden to move it. This follows those who prohibit muktzeh on Yom Tov, at the end of siman 495. Why did the Sages not permit moving it from the sun into the shade, allowing the end because of the beginning, lest one refrain from slaughtering? Because one can initially slaughter it in the shade. Maharshal writes that one will not refrain from slaughtering for this reason, because tereifos are uncommon. Therefore, even selling it to a non-Jew is forbidden. Nowadays, however, when tereifos are common, one may conceal it, because otherwise people would refrain from slaughtering. This permission does not apply to geese and chickens, since tereifos are uncommon among them. See siman 498, seifim 5 and 6. It follows that selling it to a non-Jew is also forbidden. Levush writes in siman 500 that in a place where the hindquarters are not deveined, one may sell them to a non-Jew in this manner, because otherwise people would not slaughter animals for Yom Tov. It appears to me that if the meat can be kept until after Yom Tov without spoiling, one may not sell a tereifah or the hindquarters to a non-Jew. If it was slaughtered before Yom Tov, it is forbidden in every case, as written in siman 499, unless it was slaughtered immediately before Yom Tov and there was no opportunity to sell it beforehand.
