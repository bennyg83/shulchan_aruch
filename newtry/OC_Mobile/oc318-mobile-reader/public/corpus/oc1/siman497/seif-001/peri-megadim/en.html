See Taz: he elaborated, and the content of his intent is to permit fish that are in a square kastin, and to explain the words of the Raavad and the Rashba, of blessed memory, and the Rema in the gloss, as is written in letter 2; and see letter 6, which implies he somewhat contradicts here, and God willing it will be clarified. And there is also a typographical error in print in what is written "which is not so regarding these," etc.: the word "which is not so" needs to be emended and one should write "and if so, regarding these that do not need a trap, they are forbidden (a small fishpond); [regarding] these, why are they permitted?" And that is regarding damming an irrigation channel. And behold, the Rema, of blessed memory, who wrote that the fish slip this way and that way, implies he understood the Mechaber as ruling like the Raavad and Rashba, of blessed memory, to distinguish between a small fishpond, which is forbidden regarding fish, for they are covered from the eye and lack trapping, and the law of an irrigation channel in se'if 5: an amah of six tefachim, they are trapped of themselves. And however, what is noted from the Maggid Mishneh in chapter 2 of Hilchos Yom Tov: that [source] is not there at all; it is only to support the Rambam, of blessed memory, who permits a small fishpond like an irrigation channel. And however, Beis Yosef distinguishes for the Raavad and Rashba between an irrigation channel that is very narrow and a small fishpond that is forbidden. And however, in the Maggid Mishneh there: an irrigation channel even if it is very long, since it is not so wide, is permitted; that is for the Rambam, of blessed memory, who does not distinguish between a small fishpond and an amah, so he was forced [to say so]; which is not so for the Raavad and Rashba: this is not compelled, and he takes on folio 25a specifically a narrow irrigation channel of six tefachim, as the Bach, of blessed memory, wrote, and as Beis Yosef, of blessed memory, wrote; and see Magen Avraham letter 6. And perhaps for that reason the Rema omitted this in se'if 5; and see Beis Yosef on this. And however, regarding what the Taz brought from the words of Rashi, one can say that even a small fishpond without trapping is still forbidden for fish, for exertion not for a need is also forbidden on Yom Tov even in food preparation itself if it is possible before Yom Tov without any loss at all; and see siman 495 in Magen Avraham letter 23. Except that it is difficult from damming an irrigation channel, etc. And the wording of the Rambam, of blessed memory, chapter 2, law 7, "behold they are muktzeh," requires further study, for muktzeh because of trapping: he should have said plainly "one may not trap from them" (and see Magen Avraham letter 2 and understand). And it is possible that if his slave trapped for his own need, it is forbidden for a Jew to eat from it because of muktzeh; and it still requires further study. And the view of the Raavad, of blessed memory: since fish are covered from the eye, even in a very small fishpond, and likewise an irrigation channel, if much time passed and his mind is not to designate them, they are muktzeh on Yom Tov; but when one dams before Yom Tov it is designation and permitted on Yom Tov, and one need not say "this one and that one I am taking" as with pigeons in se'if 10, for the reason that here there is no muktzeh at all. And nevertheless it requires further study. And in letter 7 he wrote that here [the concern] "perhaps he moves something that is not fit" does not apply, and God willing it will be clarified there. And from this he ruled regarding a kastin to permit even a square one that is not narrow like an amah: nevertheless one needs no trap or contrivance, and it does not lack trapping, only muktzeh according to the Raavad and Rashba; and since he took them yesterday and placed them there, it is a proper designation and permitted. But a kastin in water with many fish placed there to multiply and increase, and sometimes he traps from them: one may say that certainly on Yom Tov they are muktzeh, and even if he designated them verbally before Yom Tov it requires further study according to what is written here; and it is not comparable to damming an irrigation channel, which is a significant act and needs no speech; and according to what is written in letter 7 one may say that here regarding fish, verbal designation suffices. And according to this, what is written in the Gemara 25a, "an irrigation channel," specifically: according to the Raavad as well there is no distinction according to this view; and therefore an irrigation channel that is very long needs to be of six tefachim; but if it is wider, since it slips a bit to the width, it then floats in the length and lacks trapping and contrivance; which is not so regarding a small fishpond and a square kastin: even if it is a bit wide, there is no contrivance and trapping; and understand this. And God willing in letters 2 and 7 it will be clarified further.
