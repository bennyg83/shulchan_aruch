1) [Se'if 1] One may not catch fish from enclosures. Fish enclosures are water pools. Animal enclosures are fenced yards into which wild animals are gathered, give birth, and are raised. Rashi, Beitzah 23b. Rambam writes similarly in his Commentary to the Mishnah there.
2) There: One may not catch fish, etc. Although food preparation is permitted, catching is forbidden because it could be done before Yom Tov: the fish can remain in its trap in the water without dying and be raised the next day. Rashi there. Tosafos say it resembles harvesting. See above, siman 495, paragraph 20.
3) There: From enclosures. Certainly from a river as well, as stated above in siman 495:2.
4) There, gloss: Even where they do not require catching, etc. Even in a small enclosure where one need not say, 'Bring a net and let us catch it.' For animals and birds this is considered not requiring catching, as below in se'if 7, but fish are forbidden because they evade capture. This differs from the channel in se'if 5, which is extremely narrow, so the fish cannot evade as much. Maamar Mordechai 1; Mishnah Berurah 2. See below, paragraph 24. Rambam, chapter 2, law 7, permits a small enclosure whenever the fish do not require catching, as does Pri Chadash in se'if 5. Bach rules unlike Rambam and says Raavad and Rashba agree with him. The later authorities are stringent like Rema. See below, paragraph 25.
5) There, gloss: And the fish evade, etc. Because they are hidden from view. Raavad on Rambam, chapter 2, law 7. Thus, if the water is clear and the fish visible, it is permitted when they do not require catching. Magen Avraham 1; Eliyah Rabbah 3; Eshel Avraham 1. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, however, forbids even clear water in Kuntres Acharon 1. Since Rambam permits a small enclosure whenever no catching is needed, if the fish were designated before Yom Tov, the enclosure is closed on all sides, and the water is clear enough to seize them quickly without evasion, one may be lenient. More is explained below in se'if 5.
