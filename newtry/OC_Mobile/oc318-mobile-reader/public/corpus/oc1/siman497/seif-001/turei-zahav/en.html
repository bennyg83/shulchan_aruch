One may not trap, etc. I will present briefly the Gemara needed for this and the view of the poskim. For it is stated in the Mishnah: one may not trap fish from the pens on Yom Tov (Rashi explained: because it was possible to trap yesterday and leave it in a trap in the water, and tomorrow take it out; and Tosafos gave the reason that trapping resembles reaping; but there is no distinction in food preparation if it was possible yesterday). But one may trap a wild animal and a bird from the pens (Rashi explained: that a fish pen is pools of water, and a pen of wild animals is enclosed carpefs with a fence, and they bring wild animals into them and they give birth and raise [them] there). Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel says: not all pens are equal; this is the rule: whatever lacks trapping (that one needs to trap it) is forbidden, and whatever does not lack trapping is permitted. And the law follows Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel. And we establish that which is permitted regarding a wild animal and a bird as specifically in small pens. And we explain in the Gemara that which Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel took regarding lacking trapping: that is, whenever one says, "bring a trap and we will trap it" (meaning one needs to seek contrivances to catch it). And afterward it is stated in the Gemara: Rav said, an irrigation channel that they dammed (so that fish not enter it from outside after dark, and these he prepared and designated through this act) are permitted. And behold, the Rambam held that Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel refers also to fish; therefore he ruled in chapter 2 of Hilchos Yom Tov: fish in large pens, and likewise a wild animal and a bird in large pens: whatever lacks trapping, etc., behold it is muktzeh, and one may not trap them on Yom Tov. And the Raavad wrote upon him: This is not correct, for a wild animal and a bird that they permitted is only in small pens, and even so fish one may not trap; and the reason is because they are covered by water from the eye; end quote. And the Maggid Mishneh explained that the Rambam holds that Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel refers also to fish, and there too it depends on lacking trapping; and the Raavad and the Rashba hold that Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel refers only to wild animals and birds. And the Rambam relied on that which we permit when damming the water, as is written in this siman: and what is the difference between this and a small fishpond? And if a small fishpond that needs no trap: its fish are forbidden; these, why are they permitted? Rather, certainly whatever does not lack trapping is permitted; and an irrigation channel, even if it is very long, since it is not so wide, the fish inside it are as if trapped; and so is the law regarding small pens; end quote. And if so, it is certainly difficult on the Raavad and the Rashba from that of damming an irrigation channel, as the Maggid Mishneh wrote this as support for the Rambam. And Beis Yosef answered, and so it appears: that pens are different, for they are very wide; and even though one needs no trap to catch the fish, nevertheless since they are covered from the eye they slip this way and that way, and therefore they are not considered trapped; but an irrigation channel, since it is not wide, cannot slip; therefore they are as if trapped; end quote. And I cannot reconcile, according to my humble opinion, Beis Yosef's answer in this at all: for since according to the Raavad it is permitted regarding a wild animal and a bird when not lacking trapping, automatically regarding fish it is forbidden even when not lacking trapping; even though they are as if trapped, still it is forbidden since they are covered from the eye; if so, what does sealing the water help: even though it is narrow and does not lack trapping, nevertheless it should be forbidden because of the water covering, and their being trapped and standing does not help; and I have no place to distinguish between them. And what appears to me according to my humble opinion to answer the difficulty of the Maggid Mishneh on the Raavad and the Rashba in this way: that when one needs trapping it is forbidden because of muktzeh, as is written nearby in the name of the Rambam. And even though in chapter 1 of Hilchos Yom Tov the Rambam wrote that Yom Tov is more severe than Shabbos, for muktzeh on Shabbos is permitted and on Yom Tov forbidden, and the Raavad disagrees and equates Yom Tov to Shabbos: nevertheless it is obvious that regarding muktzeh of this sort it is obvious that even on Shabbos it is forbidden, and even R' Shimon agrees. And that is the reason the Raavad wrote "because they are covered from the eye": for it is apparently difficult: is it gaze of the eye that permits the prohibition of trapping? Behold there is already no trapping there, since one needs no trap; rather certainly he said it because of muktzeh. Except that according to the Rambam the muktzeh is specifically because of lack of trapping, and according to the Raavad the muktzeh is specifically because of covering from the eye. And if so, there is nothing to ask from damming an irrigation channel, which is permitted: for the main reason of muktzeh is that from yesterday he set his mind away from it and did not prepare it; and this one who dammed the irrigation channel from yesterday: certainly there is no muktzeh here. And this is clear from the wording of Rashi that we mentioned, who wrote "and these he prepared, and from outside they will not enter": behold, exactly as we wrote. And this is also proven from the wording of Rav, who said "an irrigation channel that he dammed before Yom Tov," and did not say briefly "an irrigation channel that is dammed is permitted"; rather it is clear that a person must perform an act before Yom Tov or earlier to remove it from muktzeh. And this is very correct according to my humble opinion. And the words of the Tur imply that he holds like the Raavad, for he wrote regarding fish simply to forbid, except that when damming the water he permits, and regarding a wild animal and a bird he distinguishes between lacking trapping or not; rather certainly regarding fish it is forbidden even when not lacking trapping because of muktzeh, like the Raavad we mentioned. And so ruled the Maharshal in chapter "Ein Tzadin": the law follows the Raavad and Rashba. And according to my words there is a complete permit for fish that one prepares before Yom Tov and places in a large perforated box in the water: since all of them do not lack trapping and there are no muktzeh among them, it is exactly like damming an irrigation channel that we mentioned. And the Maharshal there in chapter "Ein Tzadin" also wrote to permit with this box, even if one needs a small net to reach in there: there is no trapping in that. Except that he wrote on this that it appears to him to distinguish: specifically if one wants to take all of them and cook them, it is permitted; but if one wants to take one or two, it is forbidden unless he ties before Yom Tov with a string, for it is no better than dovecote pigeons and attic pigeons, which even if they do not fly are forbidden; and [verbal designation] does not help because of the reason lest they be lean and he leave them and move them for nothing, as is in this siman. And afterward he rejected this and said that there regarding pigeons one should be concerned lest he move something that is muktzeh; but here, that he took them by hand and placed them in a box before Yom Tov, there is no muktzeh at all, for all of them are fit for him even without designation; until here. And I wondered: why did he need to elaborate on this, for it is obvious that it is permitted to take what he wants; what difference is there from damming an irrigation channel that we mentioned? And further, why was it not difficult for him from the Mishnah that one may trap wild animals and birds from the pens, and we are not concerned lest they not be good in his eyes and he move them for nothing? Rather certainly specifically regarding pigeons there is a prohibition of muktzeh, and the main permit is through designation; therefore he must designate properly, as is written in this siman; which is not so regarding these that need no designation and there is no muktzeh here since they are in small pens; and likewise regarding fish, if he dams an irrigation channel from yesterday it is as if he takes each fish separately before Yom Tov and leaves it there; and tying a string is not needed, according to my humble opinion; and see what I wrote in se'if 10 on this. Afterward I saw my master, my father-in-law, of blessed memory, who also followed Beis Yosef's distinction according to the view of the Raavad and Rashba regarding an irrigation channel, and he concluded on this that since the kastin is somewhat wide, the fish are forbidden because of trapping according to the view of the Raavad and Rashba; and so it appears from the words of Rashi, who found no permit in a pen for fish except to trap them before Yom Tov and leave them in his trap; and it is not comparable to an irrigation channel that is not wide; and whenever it is wider than six tefachim, the measure of an irrigation channel, the fish are forbidden; until here. And it appears to me that they were not particular that an irrigation channel be short; rather, since it is long, if it is wide the fish can slip along the length, which is very great; which is not so regarding a box that is square: even if it slips a bit from his hand, it has no place to flee. And they gave this general rule: whenever one does not say "bring a trap," Rashi explained: that he would need contrivances to trap it, that is, to spread food there or to spread it to every side from end to end; which is not so when one reaches with a net into the box to take it: even if one reaches several times until one removes it from there, there is no insistence in this, as it appears to me according to my humble opinion.
Even in a place where they do not lack trapping. This wording is not settled, since he concluded upon this "because the fishpond is very wide," etc.; and if so it certainly lacks trapping, and for this reason the Gemara requires specifically a small fishpond so that it not lack trapping. Except that according to the Raavad and Rashba a small fishpond does not help except regarding a wild animal and a bird, and not regarding fish, as is written nearby; unless he dams water, etc., as is written nearby, for the reason that there is no muktzeh here. And since the Shulchan Aruch ruled here regarding fish to forbid, and did not include a wild animal and a bird with them, and as the Tur wrote, it implies that he rules like the Raavad and Rashba. Therefore the Rema should have written in this wording: even in a place where they do not lack trapping, because they are muktzeh since they are covered.
