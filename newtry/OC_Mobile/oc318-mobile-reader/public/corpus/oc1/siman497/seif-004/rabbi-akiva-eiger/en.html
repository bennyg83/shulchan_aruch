Taz, s.k. 9. "For we hold that we follow the stringent view, as is written in se'if 9." This is not so sufficient, for it appears that regarding a doubt that is a matter of dispute, even with muktzeh we follow the lenient view, as the Rosh wrote at the end of Beitzah regarding wilderness [animals]: since there are two versions, we act leniently, for muktzeh is rabbinic. And so wrote the Rosh, chapter 3 of Beitzah, regarding muktzeh for half of Shabbos. And it is possible to distinguish: specifically there, where the doubt is whether they are forbidden because of muktzeh or not; but here, where if they are others', even though the doubt is from the law whether we are concerned for others' or not, nevertheless it is like a doubt in a deed, since the essence of the doubt is whether they are others' or not.
