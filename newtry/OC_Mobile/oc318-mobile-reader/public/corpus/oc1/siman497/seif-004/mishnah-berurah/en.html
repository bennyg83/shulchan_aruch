{10} On the second day of Yom Tov. On the second day of Rosh Hashanah it is forbidden, because the two days are treated as one extended day. The same applies to a second day of Yom Tov adjacent to Shabbos.
{11} For it is a double doubt. Perhaps today is not Yom Tov, and perhaps it was trapped yesterday and had already been prepared. We are not stringent because it will become permitted tomorrow, since where there is a complete double doubt permitting it, no prohibition is established at all. Others disagree. They maintain that for us the second day of Yom Tov is not observed because of doubt, since we are expert in fixing the calendar and know that it is a weekday. Rather, we preserve the custom of our ancestors, and it is treated as a definite Yom Tov D'Rabbanan. There is therefore only one doubt, and it is forbidden, as in seif 3. One should be stringent in accordance with this opinion unless there is a major loss.
