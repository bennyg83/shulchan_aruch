A doubtful case of prepared status, etc. This requires study, for Rashi and Tosafot there did not disagree about Rav Pappa's statement because it was picked on the first day of Yom Tov, but according to everyone a doubt is forbidden on the second day of Yom Tov. See siman 513, seif 6 in the gloss, and see Magen Avraham. Similarly, in Maharil, Hilchos Yom Tov, he wrote that on Simchas Torah they took eggs from a non-Jew, filled a hen with them, and also made other foods with them, and Maharil forbade the hen and everything made with the eggs until after Yom Tov, after enough time to have done it, because of a doubtful case of nolad.
For it is a double doubt. It is permitted even regarding something that has a permitted alternative, in accordance with the view of the Rosh in a responsum, klal 2, seif 1. He wrote that what we say at the beginning of Beitzah, that its doubt is forbidden, and if it became mixed, etc., implies that it refers to the doubtful item; and such a case is called a double doubt in Zevachim 74a. He wrote that this is specifically when one doubt is through a mixture, but a double doubt in the object itself is permitted, and he permitted new grain there because of a double doubt. See Yoreh De'ah, siman 110, seif 9, and siman 293, seif 3 in the gloss. Nevertheless, every ordinary case, etc., is not like what is written in siman 110, seif 8 in the gloss, that some say that something that has a permitted alternative, etc.; that is the view of Maharam cited there by the Rosh and by Mordechai at the end of Kol HaTzelamim, from the beginning of Beitzah, as above.
