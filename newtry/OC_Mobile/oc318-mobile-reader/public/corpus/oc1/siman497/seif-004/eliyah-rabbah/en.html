Lest one come, etc. In Nachalas Tzvi he challenged the Levush, for he ruled in siman 495, se'if 2, that trapping is rabbinic like the Rambam and the Ran; if so it is a decree upon a decree, as Tosafos wrote s.v. "ve'tanya"; until here. And it appears to me that this is no difficulty at all, for the Levush was precise in "to trap and to take," meaning that since it is forbidden to trap, it is muktzeh, and as he wrote in se'if 7: a decree lest one take; and as the Rambam and the Ran themselves wrote. And if one asks, [regarding] that which the Beis Yosef in siman 498 explained in the Rambam as a decree lest one trap, which the Magen Avraham brought: therefore the main reason of the Rambam appears to be because of muktzeh, and so wrote the Bach; and if so, even regarding muktzeh animals one does not water them and does not place food. And the Magen Avraham wrote: specifically right before them it is forbidden to give, but at some distance from them it is permitted to give; and see siman 324, se'if 13; until here. Impure animals and birds that are forbidden to eat: it is permitted to place [food] right before them (Ran); and see siman 324, se'if 11. And it is written at the beginning of chapter "Ein Tzadin" that they distinguish between Shabbos, which is more severe: it is not appropriate to decree lest one take; if so, even regarding pure [animals] it is permitted.
