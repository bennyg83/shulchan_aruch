Seif katan 4, for it is a double doubt, etc. In the object itself, etc., meaning both doubts concern whether there is any prohibition at all.
For if it was not trapped today, there is no prohibition at all. Likewise, if today is weekday, there is no prohibition at all. But with a double doubt through a mixture, meaning a third mixture according to the Beis Yosef, and according to the Shach even a second mixture, it is forbidden, for this is how we rule in Yoreh De'ah regarding important items that are not nullified if they became mixed even in a thousand. If afterward one of the mixture fell into two other permitted items, and from those three one fell into two other permitted items, the third mixture is permitted because of a double doubt. But in such a case regarding something that has a permitted alternative, the Rama wrote that some say it is forbidden, and he concludes that it is good to be stringent unless there is need or significant loss. Therefore the Taz asked: if so, why did the Rama state here plainly that a doubtful case of prepared status is permitted on the second day of Yom Tov? The Shach there answered with his main resolution, distinguishing between a double doubt generated by a mixture, as there, where the Rama was stringent because there is a definite prohibition present, and here, where both doubts concern whether there is any prohibition at all, in which case even something with a permitted alternative is permitted. Know that the Ran wrote: although for us the second day of Yom Tov is not observed because of doubt, since we are expert in the calendar and know that the first day is primary, nevertheless because of the custom of our fathers we treat it as holy; even so, it has the status of a doubt, for our fathers, who were not expert, had a doubt, and we too do not treat it more stringently than our fathers, since it is only because of their custom.
But the main law requires study. Meaning, although a double doubt in the object itself is permitted even for something that has a permitted alternative, nevertheless such a second day of Yom Tov may not enter the category of doubt at all, for the Sages treated it as definite for every matter.

For a non-Jew speaking incidentally, etc. Although a non-Jew speaking incidentally is not believed regarding a Torah prohibition, nevertheless the second day of Yom Tov is D'Rabbanan, and he is believed.
And this is his language: although, etc. He wrote there that when Yom Tov falls after Shabbos, an egg laid that day is forbidden according to the Torah because of preparation. Nevertheless, if the second day of Yom Tov falls on Sunday, a non-Jew is believed when speaking incidentally that it was not laid that day. Although there is a concern of preparation, a Torah prohibition, he is nevertheless believed, since the second day of Yom Tov itself is D'Rabbanan. On this he asked: perforce, the second day of Yom Tov is like the first for all matters, as though it were according to the Torah. The proof is that if a non-Jew brought a gift, even on the second day of Yom Tov, it is forbidden to everyone, even to one for whom it was not brought, since it contains a concern of a Torah prohibition, namely perhaps it was trapped or detached today. Although he brought it on the second day of Yom Tov, which is D'Rabbanan, and regarding a D'Rabbanan prohibition we are lenient for another, as where he brought a gift without a concern of attached produce or trapping, but only the concern that it was brought from outside the techum, it is permitted to one for whom it was not brought, since techumin are D'Rabbanan. Rather, one must say that the Sages made the second day like the first for every matter, as though it were according to the Torah. If so, why is a non-Jew believed when speaking incidentally on the second day of Yom Tov? This is that decree, meaning a non-Jew who brought a gift, etc.

In Kenesses HaGedolah he wrote about the language of the Rosh of Lunel, who is the source of this law cited by the Ran in his name, that doubtful prepared status on the second day of Yom Tov is permitted because of a double doubt.
For on the second day of Rosh Hashanah it is forbidden, since there is no double doubt, for the two days are one sanctity and are like one long day.
The same applies to the second day of Yom Tov adjacent to Shabbos, namely where the first day of Yom Tov fell on Shabbos and the next day, Sunday, is the second day of Yom Tov; it is forbidden, for there is no double doubt. The doubt that perhaps it was trapped yesterday does not exist, since even if it was trapped yesterday, that was Shabbos and it is forbidden, for Shabbos and Yom Tov are one sanctity, and something born on one is forbidden on the other, as written in siman 513. So it appears to me. If, however, there is room to doubt that it may have been detached or trapped before Shabbos, this is a double doubt.
