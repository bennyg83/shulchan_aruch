If. See Taz: at first glance he somewhat contradicts here what he wrote in letter 1 regarding damming an irrigation channel, that it is like taking each fish separately. And nevertheless one may say that this and that are one matter, and it is regarding designation of the whole dovecote when one needs only one pair: both reasons apply, muktzeh and preventing the joy of Yom Tov; see letter 6. For nevertheless his mind is only on what he needs, and when he returns those that are not fit, they become muktzeh retroactively; and all the more so there is a concern of preventing the joy of Yom Tov. And the same applies when he designates two pairs and needs only one; see Peri Chadash. And what Rashi wrote, "since he did not designate all of them," that is that he does not need them. And regarding geese and chickens the two reasons do not apply; and likewise a wild animal and bird in small pens; which is not so regarding fish when damming: he designated for all, and the two reasons do not apply, for it is not the way to return to the water at all; rather he traps more and will eat everything he trapped. And what is written at the end, "and he returns to the water": it appears to me to emend, and he should not say he returns to the water what was not good. And according to this, the same applies in small fishponds regarding fish that have no trapping, and he designated all of them from yesterday: he is permitted; and all the more so in a kastin in such a case; see there; and as stated.
