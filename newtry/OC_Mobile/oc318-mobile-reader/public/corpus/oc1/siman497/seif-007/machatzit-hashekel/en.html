Seif katan 7, geese, etc. The same applies to other birds, but he mentioned geese because it is the usual case that they are domesticated and their sustenance is upon him. So wrote the Magen Avraham.
If so, even outside the courtyard it is permitted, for there is no reason to distinguish. Since it was permitted in a courtyard even where a trap is needed, so too outside the courtyard it should be permitted.
Since the Rosh wrote in a courtyard, as he wrote: geese and chickens that walk in a courtyard. The Baal Magen Avraham understood that this is a condition, specifically when they walk in a courtyard. Although we do not know a reason to distinguish between inside the courtyard and outside it, he did not have the confidence to disagree with the Rosh based on reasoning alone.
If so, why did the Tur mention in a house? Meaning, the Tur wrote: geese and chickens that are in a house. It cannot be that this is a condition, that the Rosh holds only in a courtyard is it permitted and the Tur means that only in a house is it permitted; according to the Baal Magen Avraham he should have said that even in a courtyard it is permitted, as the Rosh wrote. Therefore it appears that what is written in a courtyard or in a house means: the courtyard mentioned by the Rosh or the house mentioned by the Tur, meaning that they were raised there, etc. This is not a legal condition, but the normal case, for this reason, that they come to their coop because they were raised there. There is also a legal difference: those newly bought are forbidden, since they do not come to their coop in the evening. According to this, there is no distinction between house and courtyard, and therefore although the Rosh wrote courtyard, the Tur wrote house, since the legal point is only that through this they come to their coop, so both are the same.
So too appears to be the view of the Shulchan Aruch, which wrote both, in the house or in the courtyard. If it came to teach a law, namely that only in a house or courtyard it is permitted but outside them it is forbidden, it should have said only courtyard and the house would follow all the more so. Rather, it mentioned only the usual case, that they were raised in a house or courtyard and through this come to their coop in the evening, and no greater novelty of house or courtyard applies.
For whatever is forbidden according to the Torah, etc. Even if they come to their coop and their sustenance is upon him, nevertheless on Shabbos it is forbidden D'Rabbanan, as written in siman 316 according to the Rama, with whom the Magen Avraham agreed. Only on Yom Tov was it permitted because of the joy of Yom Tov. But what is forbidden according to the Torah on Shabbos was not permitted on Yom Tov because of the joy of Yom Tov, as written above in siman 495, that trapping on Yom Tov, even for food preparation, is forbidden.
