{31} He need not shake them. This excludes the opinion of Beis Shammai, who hold that designation is ineffective unless one performs an act, namely shaking the nest, and certainly if he takes hold of the selected pigeons by hand. We do not rule this way. Even if he stands at a distance and selects them by sight, that is sufficient. Nevertheless, he must identify them clearly in his mind or recognize them with certainty, so that on the following day he will not exchange them for others.
{32} It is insufficient. He did not state explicitly which one he would take, and we do not say that the one he takes on the following day is retroactively determined as the one he intended when designating. The Sages were concerned that when he comes to take one, he may feel it, find it thin, leave it, and take a fatter one. He will then have moved the first unnecessarily, and it was muktzeh, since he designated only the one he would ultimately take. For this reason, even if he later decides to take whichever one first comes into his hand, it remains forbidden. If, however, he explicitly designates a particular bird, then even if he did not feel or inspect it at all, we are not concerned that he will find it thin on the following day and leave it. Since before Yom Tov he could have examined it carefully to determine whether it was fat and did not do so, he evidently accepted it in whatever condition it might be.
