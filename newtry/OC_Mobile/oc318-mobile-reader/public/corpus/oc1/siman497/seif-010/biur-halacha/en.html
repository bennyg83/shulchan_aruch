But when one says, "From here I am taking." From the words of the Kol Bo brought in Beis Yosef, siman 118, it is clear that the law is not specifically regarding pigeons; rather the same applies to every muktzeh of a living creature, such as a hen that stands to raise eggs, according to the one who holds there is muktzeh on Yom Tov; see there. And so the view of the Rema is implied, who copied his words in siman 495 at its end, that every muktzeh that is not a living creature; see there. And it is implied that regarding any living creature, "from here," etc., does not suffice. And so it is proven from the Or Zarua in our sugya, who wrote: and likewise one who has a hen that stands to raise eggs, etc.; see there. And it is also brought in Hagahos Asheri. And it is proven that even regarding a hen there is the reason that he moves [one] and leaves [others]. And I wrote this to exclude the reasoning of the author of Shittah Mekubetzes, who meant to say that our sugya is specifically regarding pigeons, for it is not the way to inspect them whether they are fat or lean, which is not so regarding a hen, which one inspects constantly and knows what they are, and there is no room for concern. And it is possible further to say that even he said [this] only regarding a hen that stands for eating, which one always inspects [for he is dealing there with the latter answer of the sugya, "perhaps he found them all lean," etc.; and also earlier the Shittah Mekubetzes there deals with one that stands for eating; see there], which is not so regarding one that stands to raise eggs: it is possible that he does not know their fatness more than with pigeons.
