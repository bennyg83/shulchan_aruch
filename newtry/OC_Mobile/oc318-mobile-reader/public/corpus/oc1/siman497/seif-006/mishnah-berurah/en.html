{15} That are in the house, etc. Meaning, they were raised in the house.
{16} That are intended for eating. This excludes birds kept for producing eggs, which are forbidden according to the opinion that muktzeh applies on Yom Tov. If one purchased these birds and did not decide at all for what purpose to keep them, they are permitted, because birds are ordinarily intended for eating.
{17} It is permitted to trap them. This applies even from the public domain and even if one must bring a net, because they return to their coop in the evening and are constantly fed in the house, so they are considered already trapped. This applies only when he catches them in order to slaughter them, and it was permitted for the sake of rejoicing on Yom Tov. Otherwise, according to several poskim, it is forbidden, as their opinion is cited above in siman 316, seif 12, in the Rama. The later authorities write that a bird newly purchased, which has not yet become accustomed to the house and does not return to its coop in the evening, is subject to the prohibition of trapping on Shabbos, as stated above in siman 316. It is likewise forbidden to trap it on Yom Tov even while it is in the house, unless it entered a confined place. Chayei Adam writes that at night, when birds are sitting upon the beam, it is permitted to catch them because their eyes are dim at that time and they do not slip away from the person catching them.
{18} They do not require designation. They are already prepared. The poskim write that a person who fears Heaven should nevertheless look at the birds beforehand and select the ones he intends to take, so that on the following day he will not pick one up and then put it down. Although they are all prepared, this would involve unnecessary exertion on Yom Tov.
