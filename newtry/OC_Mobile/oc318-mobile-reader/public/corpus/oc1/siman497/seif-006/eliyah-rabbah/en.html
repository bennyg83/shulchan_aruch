The traps are disordered, etc. The Bach wrote: It is astonishing regarding the Maggid who permits when one finds them disordered, like R' Shimon ben Elazar, and so ruled the Shulchan Aruch; and it is not clear, for the law follows the first Tanna who disagrees with R' Shimon ben Elazar (Beitzah folio 24), that it is always forbidden until they see a wild animal in the trap before Yom Tov; and so wrote all the authors; until here his wording. And [this is] astonishing regarding his astonishment, for I checked in all the authors and I found only that they wrote in the wording of the Shulchan Aruch: unless it is known that they were trapped today; and the Maggid already explained that when they became disordered it is as if known; and therefore the Rif omitted [the opposing view]. And it is all the more astonishing that I found in Avodas HaKodesh, folio 39, siman 296, that they ruled explicitly like the Maggid and the Shulchan Aruch. Also what he wrote in Yam shel Shlomo, siman 4, escaped him: that the first Tanna also agrees with R' Shimon ben Elazar, and on the contrary he holds that even a doubtful case of something prepared is permitted; and that which he forbids from nets is where it is not known whether the trap was spread before Yom Tov; and on this R' Shimon ben Elazar disagrees: that even when it is known that it was spread before Yom Tov it is forbidden; but when they became disordered before Yom Tov, everyone agrees that it is permitted. And R' Shimon ben Elazar said it only incidentally beside the prohibition. And I found a proof for this in the Tosefta: R' Shimon ben Elazar says: whatever they spread before Yom Tov, and one came and found them disordered, etc.; for it is difficult: why does he need "whatever they spread before Yom Tov," if not to exclude the first Tanna who permits, and he says it is forbidden unless they became disordered before Yom Tov. And likewise in the Yerushalmi, law 20, he ruled simply without dispute that it is permitted when they became disordered. And now it is fine that Rashi explained that the law follows R' Shimon ben Elazar, that a doubtful case of something prepared is forbidden, etc.; and examine carefully. And Maharsha did not delve into all this. But it is somewhat difficult that the first Tanna should have distinguished within his own [category] regarding nets between known that it was spread before Yom Tov and not known, and not between anigron; and one can answer. And with this I merited to understand the words of the great HaMaor, of blessed memory: that which R' Shimon ben Elazar said, one may slaughter from anigron on Yom Tov but not from the nets: that is law, even though the Rif did not write it; until here his wording. The explanation appears to me regarding the law above, that he found them disordered, which R' Shimon ben Elazar said in the law of anigron: that this the Rif omitted, as I wrote above; but the law of anigron itself R' Shimon ben Elazar did not say. And Milchamos Hashem challenged HaMaor, of blessed memory: is the Rif a hunter, etc.? But the main law he already wrote, that a doubtful case of something prepared is forbidden; until here his wording. And that is because he understood that it refers to anigron, and that is not so. And in order not to equate him with one who errs, it appears to me to reconcile that he too understood so, except that he wrote a reason for the Rif's omission of "hunter": that this is obvious, that if they became disordered while it was still day, presumably they were trapped, as the Maggid wrote; and that which he wrote "is he a hunter" is to learn the manner of trapping; and that is what he wrote, "but the main law he already wrote," etc., meaning that R' Shimon ben Elazar said this only incidentally beside [the rule that] a doubtful case of something prepared is forbidden. The general principle of the matter is that the great Geonim among the authors ruled explicitly like the Shulchan Aruch, and the words of the Bach are rejected in this. And in passing I will explain the Yerushalmi mentioned above; this is its wording: Who informs [us] if the trap became disordered: something certain that they were trapped while it was still day; and even if it became disordered, and one is concerned to say maybe they were not trapped while it was still day; until here. And at first glance it is puzzling, and in the gloss on the Yerushalmi there he emends "but if it did not become disordered," etc., and he fixed nothing, for it is still difficult: what is "and even if it became disordered," etc.? And according to my humble opinion, to explain it without any emendation: that which he wrote "if it became disordered," etc., deals with while it was still day; and that which he wrote "if it became disordered," etc., means on Yom Tov; and that which he wrote "even if it became disordered," etc., means even though there is a doubt maybe it became disordered while it was still day, it is as if trapped on Yom Tov; and see in our Talmud and examine carefully.
