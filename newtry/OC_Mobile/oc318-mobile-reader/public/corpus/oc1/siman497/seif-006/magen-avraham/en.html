Geese, etc. The same applies to other domesticated birds that return to their coop in the evening and whose sustenance is incumbent upon you. They are regarded as already trapped, and one may catch them even outside their coop. Rashi. One may even say, “Bring a net and let us catch it.” Rabbeinu Yerucham. It follows that one may catch them even outside the courtyard. Mishnah Berurah writes likewise, but concludes that since Rosh writes “in the courtyard,” one should not permit it outside the courtyard. This is difficult. If it applies specifically in a courtyard, why does Tur write “in the house,” and similarly in his abridged rulings? It therefore appears to me that “in the courtyard or in the house” means that they were raised there, and consequently return to their coop in the evening. They may therefore be caught even outside the courtyard. This also appears to be Shulchan Aruch's view, and is clear in my opinion. Birds newly purchased, however, that are not yet accustomed to his home and do not return to their coop in the evening have the same law as other wild animals and birds. Regarding anything forbidden according to the Torah, there is no distinction between Shabbos and Yom Tov. Mishnah Berurah writes likewise.
To catch them. This is permitted for the sake of rejoicing on Yom Tov. When he does not need them for food, however, it is forbidden to catch them, as written at the end of siman 316 in the gloss. Mishnah Berurah. See what I wrote there, that the principal ruling follows Rama. Maharshal writes that a God-fearing person should select them before Yom Tov and say, “I shall take this one and this one.” See seif 8.
