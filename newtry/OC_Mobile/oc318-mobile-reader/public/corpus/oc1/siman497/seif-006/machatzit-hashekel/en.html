Seif katan 6, it is narrow, etc. More than six handbreadths, etc. This is similar to a water channel, which was permitted because it is narrow. It is only one cubit wide, for it is called a water channel because of its measure, being one cubit deep and one cubit wide, which is six handbreadths.
And Maharil, etc. Meaning, he was lenient even if it is wider than six handbreadths. The Taz was even more lenient, for in a square box even if it is wide it is permitted, since only a water channel, which is long, must be narrow. But a box that is not long is permitted even if it is wide, even when trapping is needed, as long as no tactic is needed to trap it. See there, seif katan 1, and it implies that he is lenient even according to the Raavad.
And this is according to the Rambam. Meaning, Maharil's leniency follows the Rambam. But according to what the Rama wrote in seif 1 like the Raavad, it must be only six handbreadths wide.
And one should be stringent. Nevertheless, if the water is clear, the Magen Avraham wrote in seif katan 1 that the Raavad agrees.
The Rashal adopted another approach. He was not satisfied with the Beis Yosef's answer to the Maggid Mishneh's challenge to the Raavad from the law of one who dams a water channel, that since it is narrow it is permitted, for nevertheless it is no better than a small enclosure, which according to the Raavad is forbidden for fish. Therefore he wrote to resolve that with fish there is no liability because of trapping, for it is not similar to the Mishkan. Meaning, all Shabbos labors are learned from the Mishkan, as stated in Shabbos 49, and only what existed in the Mishkan is forbidden. Trapping fish was not needed in the Mishkan, only uprooting something from its place of growth, since its life and food are earth and grasses.
But a box, etc. Even if it requires a trap. Meaning, since one is not liable for trapping fish but because he uproots something from its place of growth, then in a box, as soon as he took it from the water and placed it in the box, he uprooted it from its place of growth, since in the box it does not eat from the earth or grasses. Therefore when he takes it from the box, even by means of a trap, he has done nothing.
And a water channel, etc. Now the Rashal comes to answer the Maggid Mishneh's challenge to the Raavad from a water channel: in a water channel, as soon as he dammed it before Yom Tov, he uprooted it from its place of growth. Although it still eats from the earth and grasses, nevertheless since its main growth is through its movement along the length of the channel, once he dammed it before Yom Tov and it can no longer travel along its length, it was already uprooted before Yom Tov.
And I do not know, meaning, from where did he derive this new law, that fish have no prohibition of trapping, without a compelling proof. The proof from the Yerushalmi is not valid. The Yerushalmi says: one who catches a fish, etc., and anything that you separate from its life source, is liable because of reaping. From here Maharshal proved that since it did not obligate him because of trapping, there is no trapping by fish. To this the Magen Avraham says that the Yerushalmi speaks where the fish is already in a bowl of water and already trapped, so it is impossible to obligate him except because of reaping. So too in the Gemara, even one who takes a fish from a bowl of water is liable, except that the Gemara gives the reason of taking life, while the Yerushalmi gives the reason of uprooting from its place of growth. The Yerushalmi preferred the reason of uprooting from its growth, because according to the reason of taking life he is not liable until he leaves it outside the water until the area between its fins dries like a sela; but because of uprooting from its growth he is liable immediately when he removes it from the water. Our Gemara says because of taking life, for it holds that liability for kneading and reaping applies only to things that grow from the ground, as the Rashba wrote in his novellae in the name of the Ramban.
And what he wrote above, and for this reason one is liable, etc. Meaning, Maharshal wrote earlier within his words: for this reason one is liable even on Yom Tov. Meaning, Maharshal wanted to prove from the fact that trapping fish is forbidden even on Yom Tov that the reason must be uprooting from its growth, for reaping is also forbidden on Yom Tov, as above in siman 495. On this the Magen Avraham wrote: what proof is there? Even if you say the prohibition is because of trapping, it is also understandable that it is forbidden on Yom Tov, for trapping too is forbidden on Yom Tov, as written in siman 495. According to this, since the Magen Avraham rejected Maharshal's words, the law of a box and water channel depends on the dispute of the Rambam, Raavad, Bach, and Maharil, as above.
