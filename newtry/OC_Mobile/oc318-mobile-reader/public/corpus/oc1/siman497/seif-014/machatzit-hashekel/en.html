(ז) And see in the gloss that even when tied they say they are made to fly apart. And what is written siman 499 — perhaps he intended to settle what requires slight examination on Bach's explanation: for it is stated regarding the word "young pigeons that fly" — it was stated about them: R' Yehuda and R' Eliezer — one said in two pouches there is a dispute but in one pouch it is certainly non-sacred; and one said in one pouch there is a dispute but in two pouches it is certainly one set down and one suspended; and if so per the one who says between them is the view of R' Yehuda or R' Eliezer — in two pouches the Sages agree the set one remains; and why did R' Yehuda and R' Eliezer need to change "young pigeons are different since they are made to fly apart" — behold young pigeons are similar to two pouches. R' Eliezer said: here with tied young pigeons and tied pouches we are dealing — meaning that which we say in one pouch there is a dispute is not literally one pouch but two pouches similar to one pouch, namely that they are tied, and the Sages hold in such a case (their view) that all are non-sacred; and they wanted to establish the mishnah here with tied young pigeons; and therefore the Sages must say young pigeons are different since they are made to fly apart and detach from each other — thus far the Gemara. And if so the Gemara's words on the first clause "he left two and found three" that says in any case if others etc. — according to Bach's explanation, meaning for the Sages: since it did not enter their mind "young pigeons are different since they are made to fly apart," all the more it did not enter their mind the words of R' Eliezer that we deal with tied young pigeons; and if so per the one who says in two pouches the Sages agree — if so with young pigeons that are not tied the Sages agree to Rabbi that we do not say they are others; if so how did he assess whether others etc. — meaning for the Sages: behold young pigeons that are not tied are similar to two pouches — the Sages agree we do not say they are others; it should have been that the Gemara goes specifically per the one who says in two pouches there is a dispute. And for this he hinted to examine what Magen Avraham wrote in siman 499 seif katan 7: that which we say in one pouch there is a dispute — meaning two pouches similar to one pouch, namely two tied pouches; but in two actual pouches that are not tied the Sages agree; meaning specifically in the latter clause "he left two hundred and found one hundred," but in the first clause "he left one hundred and found two hundred" the Sages disagree even in two pouches that are not tied. And with this one can explain what is written per the previous view "if others etc." for the Sages, even per the one who says in two pouches the Sages agree. And see Avodat HaGashmi there in siman 499; and Bach challenged: since R' Eliezer establishes the latter clause with tied ones, if so the first clause too "he left two and found three" also deals with tied ones; and if so it should be difficult in any case — if he found two tied and the third not tied, why are the two forbidden — behold we know that the untied one came from elsewhere and the two tied ones are what he designated; and if he found all three tied, if so it is obvious they are forbidden, for it is seen that all three are what he did not designate; and if he found them all untied, why specifically "left two and found three" — even if he found only two untied they are forbidden, for in any case they are others since he designated tied ones; and the words that in truth even if he found two they are forbidden since they are untied, and it mentioned "found three" because it deals with all cases, whether he designated tied or untied, and when he designated untied it is forbidden only when he found three — therefore it mentioned "found three." But Taz and Peri Chadash who agreed with him disagree and hold: when he designated two tied and found two untied they are permitted, for they detach from each other and they are the ones.
