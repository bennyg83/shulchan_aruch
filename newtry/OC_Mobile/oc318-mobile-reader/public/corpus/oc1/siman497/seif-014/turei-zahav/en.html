Those found in front of the nest are forbidden. We say that the designated birds went elsewhere and these birds came from elsewhere, because when majority and proximity conflict, we follow the majority. All the more so, if this dovecote contains other compartments with birds that were not designated, we assume that the birds came from those compartments whenever such a concern is possible, since this combines proximity and majority. Rava needed to mention this only according to the opinion that when majority conflicts with proximity we do not follow the majority, but we rule that the majority is followed. The Beit Yosef writes here: 'Therefore, one can say that the case involves two nests, one above the other, etc., for since he found the birds he designated in their place, we are not concerned at all.' With due respect, these words were written without proper consideration. If he found them in their place, everyone permits them, and if he did not find them in their place, no authority permits them.
But if they can flutter, etc. The Tur writes, 'provided that it cannot fly,' etc. His intent is not that when it is inside the nest it is permitted even if it can fly, for a bird that can truly fly is certainly forbidden because of trapping. Rather, his intent is to distinguish whether there is another nest within fifty amos, as he writes afterward.
