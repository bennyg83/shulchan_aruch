Fish and birds, etc. These are Rambam's words. Beit Yosef writes in siman 498 that Rambam maintains that specifically fish, wild animals, and birds may not be given drink, because we are concerned that one may come to trap them. An animal, however, to which trapping does not apply, may be given drink even if it is muktzeh. Maggid Mishneh implies that anything fit for human consumption may not be fed, lest one come to eat it, and that the permission applies only to non-kosher animals and non-kosher birds. See there. Bach likewise explains Rambam this way. Ran writes that something prepared for human use from the previous day was in one's mind, and since it is in his possession he may feed it, as stated in siman 324, seif 11. We rule that arum may be moved because it is food for ravens. This differs from fish, wild animals, and birds, which were not prepared for human use, and therefore one may not place food before them. Yet this is difficult, because Ran himself writes at the end of Beitzah that regarding giving drink, both categories are alike because their sustenance is incumbent upon you. This implies that he permits giving drink even to animals of the wilderness although they are muktzeh, as written in siman 498. One must therefore say that Ran holds that since an animal's sustenance is incumbent upon you, one may feed it even if it is muktzeh and was not in one's mind. See Tosafot at the end of chapter HaOreg, who distinguish differently. In any event, it appears to me that according to all opinions it is forbidden only to place the food directly before them, but one may place it at some distance from them. See siman 324, seif 13.
