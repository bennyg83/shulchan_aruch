Fish and birds, etc. See the Mishnah Berurah, that the same applies to an animal; and that is from the words of the Magen Avraham and the other later authorities that I cited in Shaar HaTziyun. And the truth appears so in the words of the Mechaber, which are the wording of the Rambam, since he hung this on muktzeh and not on trapping; hence even in a place where there is no trapping it is also forbidden. However, if this is the intent of the Rambam, it requires further study whether one should be stringent like his words in this, for most poskim disagree with this. Not only according to the view of Rashi at the beginning of chapter "Ein Tzadin," that where their food is upon him, even where there is an issue of trapping it is also permitted; but even according to Tosafos who disagree with him, nevertheless that is specifically where there is trapping, but without trapping there is no one who holds [it is forbidden]; see Tosafos at the beginning of chapter "Ein Tzadin" and Shabbos 106. And so is the view of Rashi there (not like what he wrote in chapter "Ein Tzadin"). And so is the view of the Rashba in his work Avodas HaKodesh, and so is the view of the Ra'ah and the Ran, that whatever is in his domain is permitted even where there is muktzeh (see their words at the beginning of chapter "Ein Tzadin" and at the end of "Mashilin"). And so also wrote in the novellae of the Ran, Shabbos 106, that whatever is in his domain, even though it is muktzeh, its food is upon you and it is permitted. And in truth there is no reason to say that the Sages forbade an owner to feed his animal on Yom Tov where it stands for milking, even though its food is upon him, and not to be concerned for the suffering of living creatures, and all this because of a concern lest he forget and come to slaughter it and transgress a prohibition of muktzeh that is at most rabbinic; and the Torah said "in order that your ox and your donkey may rest," and what rest is there if he does not give it to eat and it dies of hunger? [See Shabbos folio 122 in Tosafos s.v. "ma'amid"; and even if we are stringent regarding a wilderness animal, that is because it is accustomed to eat there, which is not so regarding an animal that stands for milking.] And therefore it appears to me according to my humble opinion that one should not be stringent where there is no trapping. And see Magen Avraham in the name of Beis Yosef, who wanted to say that the intent of the Rambam as well is specifically where there is trapping. And even if we hold like the view of the later authorities that the view of the Rambam is to forbid in every case, still most poskim disagree with him, as above. And likewise the Mechaber in se'if 7 ruled simply to be lenient like them. Afterward I found in the Peri Chadash, siman 498, se'if 3, that he wrote that everyday practice is that one gives food on Yom Tov to a hen that stands to raise eggs, even though it is in the category of muktzeh; rather the main point depends on trapping; see there.
