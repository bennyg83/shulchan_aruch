Seif katan 2, fish, etc. Specifically fish, etc. The Beis Yosef derives this precisely from the fact that he did not include muktzeh animals as well, such as wilderness animals, as written in siman 498, seif 3. Rather, necessarily, we are concerned only where one might tell him to perform a Torah prohibition, namely with something still requiring trapping, lest he trap them; but an animal that does not require trapping and has only a prohibition of muktzeh, we are not concerned.
But from the Maggid Mishneh it implies, etc. For the Maggid Mishneh was not troubled by the Rambam's failure to mention an animal as well.
For we decree lest he come to eat them, etc. If so, even wilderness animals, although they do not require trapping, are nevertheless forbidden because of the concern that he may eat muktzeh. This is unlike non-kosher animals, regarding which there is no concern that he may eat them. What the Ran wrote appears to be a third view: he does not distinguish between non-kosher and kosher; rather, since they are in his possession, his mind is upon them and they are permitted, and if not, they are forbidden. In any event, a kosher muktzeh animal is certainly forbidden, because his mind is not upon it, not because of a concern that he might eat from it.
For regarding watering, etc. We learned: one may not water and slaughter wilderness animals, but one may water and slaughter domestic animals. The Gemara asks: why say both watering and slaughtering? It answers: it teaches incidentally that a person should water his animal and then slaughter it because of the adhesion of the hide. The Ran wrote on the Gemara's question, why did it need to say, etc., as follows: with respect to watering, certainly both these and those have their sustenance upon you, meaning both wilderness and domestic animals; it mentioned it only because of slaughtering, and for slaughtering why is watering needed? The Gemara answers, etc. Thus he holds that even wilderness animals may be watered and fed.
Specifically directly before them, etc. See siman 324, meaning, as we find there that it is permitted to stand one's animal over attached produce so it will eat, and we are not concerned that a person may detach it, since that is a severe prohibition. But it is forbidden to stand one's animal so that it will go over muktzeh, for since that is a lighter prohibition we are concerned lest he take the muktzeh by hand and feed it. It is permitted, however, to stand before it until it goes on its own to the muktzeh. Thus, since there is a noticeable distinction for him, we do not decree lest he take the muktzeh by hand. The same applies here in a similar case: we are not concerned for the concerns present here, since he has a noticeable distinction.
