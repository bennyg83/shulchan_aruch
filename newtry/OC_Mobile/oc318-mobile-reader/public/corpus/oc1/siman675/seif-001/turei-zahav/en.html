Kindling performs the mitzvah. Rashi explained: like the Menorah. If so, this does not apply to a Shabbos lamp. See siman 263.
For one who sees says it is for his own need. In the Gemara we ask against the one who says kindling performs the mitzvah from what Rava said: if he was holding the Chanukah lamp, he has done nothing. Learn from this that placement performs the mitzvah. We answer: there, one who sees says that he is holding it for his own need. Come and hear: Rava said, if he kindled it inside and took it outside, he has done nothing. Granted, if kindling performs the mitzvah, it is well, for its mitzvah must be performed in its place; since this is its mitzvah, it must be done in the place of obligation. But if placement performs the mitzvah, why has he done nothing? It answers: there too, one who sees says that he kindled it for his own need. If so, it implies that according to the view that kindling performs the mitzvah, one does not need the reason that one who sees says, etc., but rather the reason is that we require kindling in its place, as the questioner understood. Why then did the Tur and Shulchan Aruch write the reason that one who sees says, etc.? It appears that the Tur was precise from the language of the Gemara, which says there too, implying that this is not as obvious in this case as in Rava's first case. Therefore the questioner initially thought that specifically in Rava's first case, where he was holding it in his hand, it is certainly logical to the observer that it is for his own need, for that is the way people hold something in their hand when they want to use it. But regarding one who kindles inside, there is not as much reason to say so. Therefore he labored to find, according to the view that kindling performs the mitzvah, an answer that the mitzvah must be outside, for there is the place of the Chanukah lamp according to the talmudic law. But according to the view that placement performs the mitzvah, it is difficult. The Gemara answers that here too, nevertheless, the observer can err, because he does not kindle in the place of obligation, which is outside. This is unnecessary effort, so surely he has some need inside, in the place where he is kindling. Since this is so, even according to the view that kindling performs the mitzvah, one no longer needs to say that kindling must be in its place; rather, we say that he too holds this reason of the observer, since in any event you must say that he accepts the reason of the observer because of Rava's first case, where he was holding the Chanukah lamp. Therefore our teacher mentioned only this reason of the observer in both cases. This is simple in my humble opinion.
And likewise if he kindles and holds it in his hand. Rashi explained: from when he kindled it until it went out. Until here. This implies that if he holds it in his hand for a short time and afterward places it while it is burning, he has fulfilled. The language of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch can also be read this way, for they wrote and holds it in his hand, meaning for the entire time of its mitzvah.
