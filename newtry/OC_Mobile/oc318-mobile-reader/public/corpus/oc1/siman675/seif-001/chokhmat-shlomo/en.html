Seif 1: Kindling accomplishes the mitzvah and not placement, etc.; therefore a lamp that was burning all day, etc. NB: The Rambam's wording here: a lamp that was burning all day — to say one extinguishes, blesses, and kindles, for kindling is the mitzvah and not placement — end. I saw in Peri Chadash who questioned him: from his wording it implies if placement accomplished the mitzvah there would be no need for extinguishing, and this contradicts the Gemara that challenges there — if you would think placement accomplishes the mitzvah, why extinguish, raise, kindle, and place? If so, even for the view that placement accomplishes the mitzvah, extinguishing is required. What Peri Chadash answered with two answers does not appear correct. The second answer — the Rambam speaks of kindling for Hanukkah — according to Tosafot there this certainly does not appear correct, for then he should have explained, especially since the Gemara challenges from extinguishing and raising — it implies he holds that anonymously "a lamp burning all day" does not speak of Hanukkah; if so, since anonymously it implies Shabbat lights, the Rambam should have explained; also why abandon the explicit Gemara law speaking of Shabbat and cite another law not explicit in the Gemara? The first answer Peri Chadash wrote — his intent and for precision: placement is not required; if placement accomplished the mitzvah, placement would also be required — this too does not appear correct, for the Rambam should have explained and not relied on precision. However it appears there is no difficulty: Tosafot there truly questioned: if placement accomplishes the mitzvah, why extinguish? — it is no worse than kindling accidentally; in my humble opinion it fits well. One may say this is similar to slaughter: we hold intention is not required for slaughter, and even if the knife fell without intent it is valid; however if it fell by itself it is invalid, for an act is in any case required; if slaughtered accidentally it is valid — it proves accidental act counts as act, only intention they lack; but they have act, and it is preferable to falling by itself. So too in Hanukkah: even for the view placement accomplishes the mitzvah — that is only that kindling l'shem mitzvah is not required, and accidental kindling helps, for even though they lack intention, and even for those who hold mitzvot require intention, they agree in rabbinic matters intention is not required — only if fit for intention; since fit for mixing, mixing does not block; since accidental they lack intention, their kindling does not help if kindling accomplishes the mitzvah; also since they are not obligated, they cannot discharge others; nevertheless if placement accomplishes the mitzvah, accidental kindling helps. However if it kindled by itself it certainly does not help, for in any case human act is required in kindling, and kindled by itself does not help; therefore likewise if it burned from erev Shabbat, since the first act is gone, as we say in Berachot regarding kindling from my brands the first pillar is gone; and likewise water turning a wheel and slaughtering — the first force is gone and afterward it is by itself; so too here the first force is gone and afterward it is as kindled by itself; therefore it is worse than accidental kindling, for in any case act is required, and this is by itself. Tosafot's question is settled — examine well. Therefore there is no difficulty: there is a dispute in the Gemara if fire is because of its substance or because of monetary value; we hold because of substance; the Navi in Bava Kamma chapter 2 questioned: if fire is because of substance, it should be forbidden to kindle a lamp on Shabbat even if kindled on erev Shabbat; they answered it is as if he did everything at permitted time on erev Shabbat; if so, at least his acts exist and all is considered his act, and again it is human act and extinguishing is not required — it is no worse than accidental kindling. Therefore the Gemara that challenges from extinguishing and raising — it is that otherwise one could not challenge the view requiring extinguishing — one could say R' Yehuda bar Nachmani holds like the view fire is because of monetary value, and then there is no human act and it is as kindled by itself, worse than accidental kindling; therefore it challenges him: even so raising should be required; but for us who hold fire is because of substance, all kindling is as his act, and therefore not worse than accidental kindling; therefore the Rambam wrote only because kindling accomplishes the mitzvah therefore one extinguishes — it fits well — examine well. (Same seif) If he kindled inside and took it outside he has not fulfilled, etc. NB: See in my works on Orach Chayim year 5566 what I wrote resolving the Tur's words; see Bach and Taz who wondered why this reason is needed since we hold kindling accomplishes the mitzvah; see in my works Orach Chayim year 5549 siman 673 to resolve this. It also appears there is a practical difference if a courtyard has two entrances — must kindle at both; Ran wrote since it is only because of suspicion, blessing is not required; in my humble opinion that does not appear correct, for the baraita taught: if he kindled inside and took outside he did nothing; from "he did nothing" it is proven blessing is required; the Gemara concludes the reason from the viewer saying for his need — how did he do nothing? It is proven that because of suspicion blessing is required; therefore it fits that Tur and Shulchan Aruch cited this reason to prove: if for suspicion blessing is required, from "he did nothing" likewise with two entrances blessing is required even though only suspicion. However one may distinguish: this suspicion "for his need" means he kindled for need — then he did not publicize the miracle at all, therefore must bless; but with two entrances, although they suspect he did not kindle Hanukkah, nevertheless he truly publicized the miracle — therefore one could say no blessing is required — such one could say; however the Tur may hold they are equal — examine. I was uncertain: if everyone used only wax lights and their custom was not olive oil, and now one kindled olive oil, or he was mehader and held many lights in hand for the days — do we say the viewer saying for his need does not apply, for such a lamp is not normally for need, and it is not normal to use many lights — he will be compelled to say it is for Hanukkah; or even then do we decree the Rabbis did not distinguish in their enactment? — requires study for law — examine.
