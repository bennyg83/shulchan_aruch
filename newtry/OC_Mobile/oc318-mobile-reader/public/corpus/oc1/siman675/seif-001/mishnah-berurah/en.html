Kindling performs the mitzvah. This is from the fact that we bless: Who commanded us to kindle a lamp, etc. According to this, the same law applies to Shabbos and Yom Tov, as we wrote above in siman 263, section 11, in the gloss.
Therefore, etc. Meaning, if we held that placement performs the mitzvah, he would need to extinguish it, lift it, place it for the sake of the mitzvah, and kindle it. But since kindling performs the mitzvah, he kindles it as it is placed.
He extinguishes and kindles it for the sake of the mitzvah. It is not effective that he kindled it yesterday for the sake of the mitzvah, because each day is a matter unto itself.
Nevertheless, one must, etc. Meaning: do not say that placement does not matter to us at all and that he fulfills no matter how he kindles. Therefore he teaches this.
That he should kindle it in the place where it is placed, for if, etc. In the time of the Gemara, when one needed to place the Chanukah lamp outside at the entrance of his house, this is literal: he must kindle it also in the place of placement, meaning outside, for there is the place of obligation for publicizing the miracle. Even today, when we are lenient and kindle inside the house, nevertheless one does not fulfill in this manner, for the kindling of the lamps must be in the place where he will finally place them, because one who sees will say that he kindles it for his own need. According to this, it is obvious that the same applies if he kindled it outside and placed it inside; he also does not fulfill for this reason.
And took it outside, etc. The authorities wrote that even if the kindling and placement were in one place, whether inside or outside, one should also be careful not to move the Chanukah lamps from their place until the required measure of lighting is completed, namely half an hour. Some later authorities are lenient about this, and Pri Megadim wrote that ab initio one should certainly be careful about this. Even with Chanukah lamps kindled in the synagogue, it is also proper to be careful ab initio not to move them from their place until the measure of half an hour.
And holds it in his hand, etc. The view of Turei Zahav is that this is specifically when he holds it throughout the time of its mitzvah. But if he holds it in his hand for a short time and afterward places it while it is burning, he has fulfilled. Several later authorities question this, and their view is that it should not be lit until it is first placed in its location.
