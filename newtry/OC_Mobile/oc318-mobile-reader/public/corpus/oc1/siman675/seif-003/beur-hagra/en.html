Seif 3: And there are those who say, etc. So too in Orach Mishpat, and as written in Yerushalmi they instituted that a minor reads the Megillah, etc. This is according to R' Yehuda in Megillah 19b, as the Gemara there speaks of a minor in such a case; see Tosafot there d.h. v'Rabbi Yehuda, etc. It appears his reason for ruling like R' Yehuda is from Tosafot's difficulty there, and he holds the sugya in Berachot chapter 3 that a son blesses for his father, and the sugya in Pesachim that R' Shimon and R' Yehuda said aggadah — all follow R' Yehuda; and that he does not discharge with Hallel — he holds like the Geonim that Hallel is d'oraisa; but Tosafot there answer it speaks where the minor ate enough to be satiated, which is also one of the rabbinic matters; so too Mordechai there; and so the second answer in Berachot 15a d.h. v'Rabbi Yehuda; and another answer there that in the evening prayer it is different — see there; and from Pesachim they answer a blind person is different since he was already obligated; and as Ran wrote in Kiddushin end of chapter 1 in the name of aggadah they were not blind from birth; but in Tosafot Rosh HaShanah 34a d.h. ha, etc., not so — it implies he holds like the Orach Mishpat mentioned who wrote there; however one may reject that a minor can discharge an obligated rabbinic person like a son blessing for his father, and he counts toward seven and discharges others — 18 in Megillah for R' Yehuda, etc. — see there he wrote therefore they said aggadah; and therefore R' Yehuda says in chapter 3 of Megillah whoever did not see lights, etc. — it implies if he saw he discharges even though exempt from all mitzvot; rather according to what Tosafot wrote in Megillah that a blind person is different since he was already obligated, apparently there was no difficulty from Megillah; but it is not so — it implies from R' Yehuda's words that he may not recite because he never benefited, and the Gemara challenges the Rabbanan from this — why? — and answers because of R' Yehuda; it implies in other rabbinic matters he agrees one can discharge even if he never saw; therefore it must be as Tosafot wrote in Rosh HaShanah there. However for a blind person there is no practical difference, for we hold not like R' Yehuda — anonymous Mishnah in chapter HaChovel is not like him, as written; and likewise the Pesachim sugya is not like him, as written, that a blind person is exempt only from saying the Hagadah — they deduced there: who said aggadah in Beit R' Yehuda? — they did not deduce on Kiddush and all year; furthermore they concluded there that even in Hagadah they are obligated; so Mordechai in chapter 2 of Megillah — see there; the difference is only for a minor.
