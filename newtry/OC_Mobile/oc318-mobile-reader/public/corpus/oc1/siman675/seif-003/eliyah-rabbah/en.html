For one who sees, etc. Bach asked that in the Talmud, Shabbos 22, it gives the reason that we require kindling in its place, and so wrote Rambam. He answered that this teaches even according to us, who kindle indoors, that if he took them outside afterward he did not fulfill, because one who sees, etc. He objected to this answer, for in any event it should also have written the reason that kindling must be in its place, which is also halachah. It seems to me that he follows his own view, which he wrote earlier, that there is a practical difference: according to the reason that kindling must be in its place, this is required also on Shabbos and Yom Tov; but according to the reason that one who sees, etc., it is not required for a Shabbos or Yom Tov lamp, where there is no publicizing of the miracle. This is understandable. I am astonished, for Mahariv in the laws of Pesach wrote that even according to the reason of one who sees, it is also required on Shabbos and Yom Tov. I saw in Responsa Beis Yaakov, siman 53, that he wrote the aforementioned practical difference in the name of Mordechai, but he was not precise, for Mordechai in chapter Bameh Madlikin did not write it at all. Rather, he wrote the law of Shabbos and Yom Tov regarding the reason that kindling must be in its place, because we hold that kindling performs the mitzvah and there is no need for the reason of one who sees, as in our question. In truth, the same law follows even from the reason of one who sees, as Mahariv wrote. Now Tosafos, beginning with the words for he should have asked, is well understood, for they did not write this practical difference. With this the first answer of Bach is also rejected, and this is what Turei Zahav intended in his lengthy discussion: he holds that according to the Tur, in the conclusion the reason of kindling in its place was rejected even according to the view that kindling performs the mitzvah, since if there is no practical difference, why should we say a reason that was rejected? Furthermore, according to Bach, who says there is a practical difference, why did Rema and Levush rule in siman 263, section 10, that on Shabbos and Yom Tov too one requires kindling in its place? Aside from this, it is forced, and Responsa Beis Yaakov also wrote many forced answers. It is also difficult regarding his explanation of Rashi's view, that according to the view that placement performs the mitzvah, he agrees that kindling is also a mitzvah, only placement is primary, while according to the view that kindling performs the mitzvah, placement is not a mitzvah at all. Therefore the Talmud says that according to the view that kindling performs, etc., for that reason he has done nothing. This is difficult: if so, what does the Talmud say about Rava's statement that one who held the Chanukah lamp has done nothing: learn from this that placement performs the mitzvah. But kindling is still also a mitzvah, so how does it say he has done nothing? Also, what he answers to Tosafos' question beginning with he extinguishes, on page 23, that it says he kindles because this too is a mitzvah, is not precise, for Tosafos conclude: is it worse when it is already lit and standing than when a deaf-mute, mentally incompetent person, or minor kindled it? They mean that with a deaf-mute, mentally incompetent person, or minor, the one seeing it need not kindle, for this is the practical difference, as Tosafos wrote just before. It is also difficult for what he later explains, that the Tur rules that according to the view that kindling performs the mitzvah, placement is also a mitzvah. If so, what does the Talmud say, unless kindling performs the mitzvah it is well; the question still remains that he should have said he places it, since placement too is a mitzvah. He himself later explained this way, contradicting himself.
