And there is one who says that a minor who has reached the age of education is permitted, etc. Note: see Magen Avraham below, siman 689, and in the laws of Megillah, where he asked that the words of Shulchan Aruch contradict one another. There he ruled plainly that a minor never discharges an adult in any way, and did not bring the view of the Itur; here he brought the view of the Itur, who holds that a minor who has reached education discharges an adult. He wrote that it is forced to distinguish between Chanukah lamp and Megillah, because in Ran it implies that they are equal; see there. It seems in my humble opinion that this can be properly resolved. The reason that one person discharges another is disputed: some hold it is because of mutual responsibility, and some hold it is because of agency. In our novellae to the laws of Megillah, siman 589, chapter 4, we asked that the words of Tosafos contradict one another. In Berachos they wrote that with Birkat Hamazon a minor cannot discharge an adult because two rabbinic levels do not come and discharge one rabbinic level. We wrote that this is understandable according to mutual responsibility: since they are not alike and their obligations are not equal, this one cannot discharge that one. But according to agency, it is as though he himself blesses; why should two rabbinic levels not be effective to discharge one rabbinic level? Tosafos in Gittin hold that in a matter to which the minor himself is connected, a minor can have agency. If so, since the minor is at least obligated in Birkat Hamazon, he is connected to it, and is considered the agent of the adult. Why then should the minor not discharge him? See there for what I wrote about this. But now I saw that it is well understood according to the words of Maharit in a responsum, who holds that an agent is not effective for an oath because words are not handed over to an agent; see there. In truth, elsewhere I wondered about him, for one may say that words are not handed to an agent means that the agent cannot make another agent, but he himself can be an agent even for words. Nevertheless, Maharit at least holds that words are not handed to an agent and agency is not relevant there. If so, it is well understood. There, with Birkat Hamazon, which is only words, agency is not relevant, only mutual responsibility; therefore two rabbinic levels do not discharge one rabbinic level. But where there is an act, such that one can appoint an agent, one may properly say that two rabbinic levels discharge one rabbinic level because of agency. If so, this is fine regarding Chanukah, which is an act and agency applies. But Megillah is words, and agency does not apply; therefore two rabbinic levels do not discharge one rabbinic level. It is well understood. The view of Ran and Kol Bo is that an agent can nullify another person's chametz. But Ran brings there other views that nullification through an agent is not effective; they disagree about the aforementioned reasoning of Maharit, whether agency applies to words. According to this, Ran follows his own view: since he holds that agency applies to words, Megillah is indeed equal to Chanukah. But for us as practical law, although in the laws of Pesach the ruling follows those who say an agent can nullify, nevertheless regarding Megillah he properly ruled that no minor discharges, because there is a double doubt for stringency. Perhaps the halachah follows those who hold that even regarding Chanukah, which is an act, two rabbinic levels do not discharge one rabbinic level. And if you say the halachah follows Baal HaItur, who holds that in Chanukah two rabbinic levels discharge one rabbinic level, one may say that his main reason is agency; if so, perhaps the halachah follows those who hold that words cannot be made subject to agency, and therefore in Megillah the only reason is mutual responsibility, and with that certainly two rabbinic levels do not discharge one rabbinic level. Therefore, since there is a double doubt for stringency, he did not take the view of Baal HaItur into account at all. But regarding Chanukah, which is an act and agency is relevant, he properly brought the view of Baal HaItur, for here one can say that since the reason is agency, two rabbinic levels can properly discharge one rabbinic level. This reasoning seems correct in my humble opinion, and it is well understood; examine carefully.
