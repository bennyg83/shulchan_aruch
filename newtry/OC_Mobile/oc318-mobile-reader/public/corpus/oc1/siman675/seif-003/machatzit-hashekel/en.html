Paragraph 3: For one who sees, etc. We say the reason, etc. The Gemara asks against the one who says placement performs the mitzvah from what Rava said: if he kindled it inside and took it outside, he has done nothing. Granted, if kindling performs the mitzvah, it is well, because we require kindling in its place, meaning the place of its mitzvah. But if you say placement performs the mitzvah, why has he done nothing? It answers: one who sees says that he kindled it for his own need. Thus, according to us, who hold that kindling performs the mitzvah, we do not need the reason of one who sees, etc. He answered that it comes to say, and this is how one should read it:
That even according to us, etc. If so, when he first kindled inside, it was kindling in its place. Nevertheless, when he took it outside, he did not fulfill because one who sees says, etc. In truth, Bach was not satisfied with this answer, for if so, at any rate it should have written also the reason that kindling must be in its place. There is a practical difference regarding a Shabbos lamp, where the reason that one who sees says that he kindled it for his own need does not apply, because in truth it was kindled for his own need, for domestic peace. But according to the reason that kindling must be in its place, even a Shabbos lamp must be kindled in its place. Therefore he concluded that the authorities who copied only the reason of one who sees, etc., hold that once the conclusion gives the reason of one who sees, even if you say kindling performs the mitzvah, the reason is not that kindling must be in its place, but rather because of one who sees. For in any event this reason must be said if you say kindling performs the mitzvah in the next law, where he holds it in his hand and does not fulfill, because of one who sees. See there.
Magen Avraham was not satisfied with this answer, for Rema wrote above in siman 263 that a Shabbos lamp also requires kindling in its place. Therefore he considered the first answer of Bach primary. Regarding Bach's difficulty, that it should at least have written also the reason that kindling must be in its place, perhaps Magen Avraham holds that here, regarding the Chanukah lamp, there is no practical difference, so Beis Yosef was not concerned to give the reason that kindling must be in its place. Or, regarding Beis Yosef, Magen Avraham may agree to Bach's answer, that he holds that according to the conclusion one does not require kindling in its place; Magen Avraham copied Bach's first answer only for us, according to what Rema wrote in siman 263 that even a Shabbos lamp requires kindling in its place. One must say that Rema, who did not gloss here also with the reason that kindling must be in its place, relied on what he wrote in siman 263.
