It is not an eruv even if he intended. Explanation: he himself enables for them and for himself, but not through another — for through another it helps as written afterward; and in Tur it is written 'and enabled' — Beit Yosef wrote: if it is not a textual corruption it must be that this 'and enabled' also deals through himself; and Tur's reason is that he did not intend to enable him as a free gift but on account of the perutah he gives him, and money does not acquire — therefore he has no share in the eruv, end of his words.

But if he said to him 'make an eruv for me.' Tur's reason is that behold he made him an agent to make an eruv for him in a matter that helps, and since he cannot have an eruv unless he enables him like the others, it is as if he said he should enable him like the others, end of his words. And it is puzzling from what Rosh wrote in chapter 1 of Kiddushin that I brought above siman 14, and these are his words: and likewise one who lends a ring to his friend to betroth a woman with it — we are witnesses that his intent is to give it with any effective wording, for with this intent he gave it to him; and if the wording of loan would not help, he would adopt another wording that helps for the name of kiddushin, for everyone is expert in law and they thought the wording of loan would help; we estimate his intent that if the wording of loan would not help, he would give it with another effective wording, and at minimum as a gift on condition to return, end of his words. Behold before us that this one says the wording of loan and nevertheless we explain based on estimation of intent that he did not know the law and thought this would be in place of effective wording according to law — namely a gift; so too let us say if he said 'enable me' he thinks on account of money — nevertheless since according to law it does not help with money, let us say he intended effective wording such as 'make an eruv for me' — why do we not estimate his intent here as in kiddushin? What is different there that he wants kiddushin — here too he wants eruv which is a mitzvah? And one can say that here it is different, for a person wants to perform a mitzvah specifically with his own and not with his friend's; therefore perhaps he was particular and did not make him an agent to enable for free but specifically said on account of the money he should enable him, not for free; but as will be explained shortly, Rosh does not hold this view — requires study.

If he gave him a vessel. For specifically money does not acquire until he pulls the object; but if he transfers a vessel to him it is chalipin, and since this one acquired the vessel, this one acquired the bread that pertains to the eruv, as written regarding chalipin in many places.

Through another, etc. Tosafos daf 81 wrote, and these are their words: and R' Yitzchak explained that even if he intends to transfer to him for free, and even if he enabled him through another or the shopkeeper took the money and bought for him from the market — it is not an eruv, since the giver intended to buy for him from the shopkeeper and the money is not acquired by him, and all the enablements the shopkeeper makes are done without the homeowner's knowledge, end of their words. And Rosh was puzzled on this: since this one revealed his intent that he wants an eruv by giving — behold he gave a perutah to the shopkeeper, and since the money is not acquired and the shopkeeper enables him — why did he not acquire? And therefore he ruled that enabling through another helps, and likewise Tur and Shulchan Aruch; requires study — why did he reject Tosafos' view? For certainly he can say: I do not want one person to give me a gift, but let the mitzvah be done with my money; and on this he revealed his intent by giving money; and even regarding something that is not a mitzvah we find in chapter 1 of Bava Batra that one can say to one tenant 'I do not want to receive a gift,' as written 'and he who hates gifts shall live' — a fortiori here regarding a mitzvah, and he gives money — certainly he specifically does not want it for free; as I explained, Tosafos' words contain no puzzlement in this.

A perutah to the homeowner. Because it is not the way of a homeowner to sell his own and intend that he acquire with his money — he said to him as if he said 'make an eruv for me' that he made him an agent; and the presumption is that an agent performs his agency.
