Seif katan 2: "three men," etc., "for there is nothing here to interrupt," etc. After U'va LeTzion they recite Kaddish, and after the Torah reading, before the Amidah, they likewise recite Kaddish. If Kaddish were said at the bimah after the reading, how could another Kaddish be recited before the Amidah when nothing intervenes between the two Kaddishes? At Shacharis there is an interruption between the two Kaddishes, namely Ashrei, which is recited after the Kaddish following the Torah reading. At Minchah, however, Ashrei was already recited before the Torah reading, and nothing is said after the reading. Even at Minchah on a fast day, one might have thought to recite Kaddish after the three readers complete the Torah reading and before the haftarah, so that the haftarah would intervene between the two Kaddishes. Therefore Magen Avraham writes that the haftarah belongs to the Torah reading and is discharged by the Kaddish recited before the haftarah; if so, what would the Kaddish before the Amidah be recited for?
"And the Rivash wrote," etc. In a place that has no Sefer Torah, one cannot recite the Kaddish before the Amidah, because Kaddish was already recited after U'va LeTzion; what basis is there for a second Kaddish? Eliyah Rabbah asked, based on Magen Avraham at the end of siman 143, that even where there is no Sefer Torah the chazzan should read aloud from a Chumash; if so, that reading should serve as an interruption. Tevuos Shor likewise writes: "It is also wondrous in my eyes why this should be inferior to verses of Tehillim. One must distinguish between the chazzan's Kaddish and a mourner's Kaddish." In my humble opinion, one can say that we do not find either a mourner's Kaddish or the chazzan's Kaddish recited except after supplication and prayer, or song and praise. For Torah study one says Kaddish d'Rabbanan. Only when the Torah is read from a Sefer Torah in fulfillment of Ezra's enactment do we recite Kaddish for fulfillment of that rabbinic enactment. Where there is no Sefer Torah and the chazzan reads from a Chumash merely so that the practice of Torah reading not be forgotten, this is not an enactment of the Sages but only a custom. Such a reading does not warrant an ordinary Kaddish; only Kaddish d'Rabbanan for the study, which was instituted for a congregation, would apply.
That they should likewise not say "Va'ani Sefilasi," as stated above in the preceding seif katan according to the reason given there.
If so, they should say "Va'ani Sefilasi" and recite Kaddish over it. Meaning, according to the second reason, that one should not create an interruption, there is a remedy: after "Va'ani Sefilasi" they can recite a second Kaddish.
"The one who says Yehalelu." Meaning, regarding what was written above, that for this reason Kaddish is not recited at Minchah after the reading because nothing intervenes between the Kaddish after the reading and the Kaddish before the Amidah: but there is the interruption of saying Yehalelu while returning the Sefer Torah to the aron. Necessarily, a single verse is not considered an interruption.
"Or they should say it," etc. Meaning, if despite this they wish to say "Va'ani Sefilasi," they should say it immediately after completing U'va LeTzion and then recite Kaddish, so that there will be no interruption. Nevertheless, according to the reason of the Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah cited in seif katan 1, even immediately after completing U'va LeTzion it should not be said.
