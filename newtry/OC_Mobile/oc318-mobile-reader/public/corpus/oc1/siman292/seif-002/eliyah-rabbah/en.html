[2] [Levush] "Even though we drank," etc. Abudarham explains: "I come to pray, and I ask of You a favorable time for universal atonement." Kol Bo, however, writes: "I hasten to pray to You at the fixed time of Minchah, which is a favorable time." According to Kol Bo, "a favorable time" is not a request; rather, Minchah itself is called a favorable time because Eliyahu was answered then. According to Abudarham there is an additional reason. Since one who is intoxicated may not pray, we ask that this prayer be accepted as a favorable time. Tanya gives another reason for saying, "As for me, may my prayer to You be at a favorable time," at Shabbos Minchah. The final tribulation, unlike the earlier exiles, will last 9 months. Its final day will be Shabbos, and the distress will intensify beyond all that preceded it until Minchah, when Israel will be answered and the redeemer will come.
