[2] [Levush] Even though we have drunk, etc. I come to pray, and I ask of You a favorable time to grant atonement to the world (Abudraham). Kol Bo, however, explains: "I hasten to pray to You at the fixed time of Minchah, which is a favorable time." According to this, "a favorable time" is not itself a request, but Minchah is called a favorable time because Eliyahu was answered then. According to Abudraham there is an additional reason: since one who is intoxicated may not pray, we ask that this prayer be accepted as a favorable time. Tanya gives another reason for saying "As for me, may my prayer to You be at a favorable time" at Shabbos Minchah. The final tribulation, unlike any before it, will last nine months; its last day will be Shabbos, and the distress will intensify beyond all that preceded it until Minchah, when Israel will be answered and the redeemer will come.
