[1] [Levush:] They prolong it, etc. This is because of the burden upon the congregation, so that it not be difficult for the elderly, pregnant women, and nursing women, as Kol Bo, siman 48, writes. In siman 15 he writes that this resembles the practice of studying Pirkei Avos at Minchah because of the public discourse. Mateh Moshe writes that he found in the name of Rokeach that since sinners return to Gehinnom toward evening, the Order of Kedushah was left for Minchah in order to cool the fire of Gehinnom. I found the following wording in Rokeach, siman 363: “The Order of Kedushah is recited every day to protect from the judgment of Gehinnom, as it is said, ‘A land of gloom, without order,’ implying that where there is order there is no gloom. They deferred the Order of Kedushah from the morning because of the abundance of prayers.” This appears somewhat contradictory and requires study. See siman 132, seif katan 4. Sefer HaManhig writes that it is recited at Shabbos Minchah, unlike other Minchah services, because the Order of Kedushah speaks of redemption, and through the merit of Shabbos they will be redeemed.
