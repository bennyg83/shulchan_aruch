[1] [Levush] That they prolong, etc. This is because of the burden on the congregation, so that it not be difficult for elderly people, pregnant women, and nursing women (Kol Bo, siman 48). In siman 15 it writes that this is analogous to Pirkei Avot, which is recited at Minchah because of the sermon. Mateh Moshe writes that he found in the name of Rokeach that toward evening the wicked return to Gehinnom, and therefore the recitation was left for Minchah in order to cool the fire of Gehinnom. I saw in Rokeach, siman 363: "The order of Kedushah is recited each day to protect from the judgment of Gehinnom, as it says, 'a land of gloom and the shadow of death, without order.' This implies that where there is order there is no shadow of death. They deferred the order of Kedushah from the morning service because of the abundance of prayers." This is somewhat contradictory and requires study. See siman 132, seif katan 4. Sefer HaManhig writes that it is recited at Shabbos Minchah, unlike other Minchah prayers, because the order of Kedushah speaks of redemption, and through the merit of Shabbos Israel will be redeemed.
