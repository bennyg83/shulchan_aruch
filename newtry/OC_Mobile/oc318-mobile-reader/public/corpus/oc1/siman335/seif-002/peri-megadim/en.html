(א) One may save. He challenged Mishna Berurah who brought "some say" plainly, while Maharai himself was stringent for Beit Yosef; and in siman 334 he brought two "some say" and did not decide — it appears at first glance that the last one he brought is to forbid; and Mishna Berurah omitted to permit to another courtyard — this is more reasonable, as Taz wrote there note 8 — see there; and see Beit Yosef and Perishah; and see how our master Taz explained this, as Mishna Berurah wrote in note 1: he explained lest he repair — when a barrel broke below on the ground, "one may save from it" — if so, Tur's question in siman 334 applies here too; without Mishna Berurah's words I would have said according to Perishah's words: extinguishing challenges well — for we fear extinguishing even a friend's house; unlike concern for public domain; but for Mishna Berurah it is difficult; and in Beit Yosef's words: also Ramban disagrees, since according to Perishah on the top of the roof there is only concern for public domain; understand this; end of his words note 3.

(ב) Eliyah Rabbah wrote note 3: wine that was brought outside the techum — forbidden; if the barrel broke — forbidden to place a vessel beneath it, for he nullifies the vessel from its primary use; and even what was brought outside the techum — permitted in moving, as in siman 320 seif 8, even twelve mil; nevertheless one may say nullifying a vessel from its primary use has many reasons: (1) building, which establishes a place for it; (2) breaking a vessel, for it is impossible to use it further — here too he nullifies temporarily from its primary use, for it is impossible to use it for permitted liquid while forbidden wine is in it; see siman 426 in Magen Avraham note 14 and siman 310 seif 6 from this; and in Peri Megadim Eshel Avraham and in our chiddushim Shabbos 43 and Maharal Derush we elaborated; and Sefer Teshuvah on this.
