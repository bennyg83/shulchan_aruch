1) [Seif 1] Enough for three meals. The reason is that if the Sages permitted saving more, his panic might cause him to repair the barrel, as with a fire in siman 334. Taz, paragraph 1. This is also the reason for the gloss. Chemed Moshe, paragraph 1, however, questions this explanation because repairing applies only to a wooden barrel, and leaves the matter requiring examination; see there. Bach explains instead that one might forget and bring a utensil through a public domain; see there. See below, siman 345, seif 7, where it is disputed whether a public domain exists today. According to Shulchan Aruch there, it does exist today; see there. See also above, siman 307, seif 19.
2) There: Enough for three meals. This means before eating the Friday-night meal, as stated at the beginning of siman 334. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1.
3) There: Enough for three meals. This discusses a place where wine is uncommon and is ordinarily drunk only during a meal. In a place where wine is common and is ordinarily drunk throughout the day, one may save more, because we maintain that drinking has no fixed measure, as stated in siman 323. Tosefes Shabbos there. At the beginning of siman 334, however, Tosefes Shabbos writes that it is possible that even where wine is very common, no more than the amount needed for three meals may be saved because we follow the majority of the world. Other beverages, however, may plainly be saved in the amount needed to drink throughout the day; see there.
4) There: Enough for three meals. Piskei Terumas HaDeshen, siman 196, writes that if a barrel broke and its wine spilled upon the ground, one may scoop up as much as he wishes with small cups and troughs. This does not constitute weekday conduct and is certainly permitted, because once the wine has completely spilled upon the ground, what panic remains? See there. Beis Yosef, however, challenges him and writes that one should not rely on this responsum. Whether or not the wine spilled, one may save no more than the amount needed for three meals; see there. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1, likewise writes that Terumas HaDeshen's argument is not compelling; see there. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 1, writes the same. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1, nevertheless inclines to be lenient like Terumas HaDeshen. Shulchan Aruch HaRav rules stringently like Beis Yosef, and this appears to be the practical law.
5) There: Enough for three meals. This restriction applies specifically on Shabbos because of the decree concerning carrying from a public domain. On Yom Tov, however, one may save everything he wishes. Nevertheless, whenever possible, changing the ordinary method out of respect for Yom Tov is a worthy custom and practice, unless the change causes greater exertion or attracts more attention. Divrei Ben Shlomo, siman 141.
