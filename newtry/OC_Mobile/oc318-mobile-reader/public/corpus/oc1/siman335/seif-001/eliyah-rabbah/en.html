[1] [Levush] Rather, it cracked, etc. Likewise, if the contents already spilled completely onto the ground, there is no concern that he will panic, since the loss already occurred. He may therefore scoop and pour with small ladles, called shaf, as much as he wishes, as Maharai ruled and the Peri Chadash agreed. The Beit Yosef's question from Tosafot can be answered by saying that Tosafot discusses a case where not all the wine had spilled.
[2] Some say specifically, etc. The citation points to the Mordechai, Tosafot, and Hagahot Maimoniyot. This requires study, because the Mordechai in Perek Chaviyot explicitly prohibits even when the barrel merely cracked, and the Beit Yosef did not cite Tosafot or Hagahot Maimoniyot for this opinion.
[3] And some say that every, etc. This is surprising, for in siman 334, seif 11, the Shulchan Aruch did not decide between the opinions, whereas here he simply permits, as stated by the Taz. In my humble opinion, the present case is different because the concern is lest he bring a vessel through the public domain, as Maharai writes, whereas above the concern is lest he save too much. Moreover, in our circumstances we do not have a true public domain. The Rama and Levush write that we are concerned he may repair something, implying repair of the barrel. This is surprising because it contradicts Maharai, and it is difficult to understand the Gemara's question in Shabbos 114, 'But if a barrel broke, what decree is there?' There would seemingly be a decree lest he repair it. Rather, that is not the concern. The Gemara answers that he may bring a vessel through the public domain, as Tosafot on page 5 and the explicit baraita indicate.
