(א) (s.k. 1) Food, etc. — see in Maharai in Piskei U'Kesavim siman 196: it happened that barrels spilled entirely on the ground, and he leaned to permit drawing into small tubs called "sha'af," for such is not weekday practice (that is, it is not his way thus on a weekday); and he wrote that so it appears from Rambam: where one deviates from weekday practice we are not concerned lest he come to a Torah melacha — end of his words; and he did not explain from which wording it is implied. And it appears for this matter that he wrote in chapter 23: a barrel broke, etc., provided he does not sponge in wine or dab with oil — for if he acts as he does on a weekday, perhaps he will come to squeezing — end of his words; it implies specifically when the doer acts as on a weekday without change there is concern lest he forget that it is Shabbat and come to squeezing; unlike when he changes and has recognition that today is Shabbat — we do not decree lest he perform the melacha of squeezing; so too this that it is forbidden to save more than two meals that they forbade in the Gemara — out of panic lest he bring vessels via the public domain — if he changes we do not decree; and on this Magen Avraham wrote that it is not necessary, for one should not compare rabbinic decrees: even though where he changes there is no concern lest he squeeze, nevertheless perhaps lest he bring vessels via the public domain we decree even when he changes:

(ב) And also Rabbenu Yerucham disagrees with him, etc. — furthermore Maharai leaned to leniency for another reason: in Hagahot Maimoni in the name of Rabbeinu Tam — specifically when it broke, but when it cracked and drips drop by drop — permitted, for he is not panicked; if so, when it spilled entirely onto the ground — in what would he be panicked; and one could ask: panicked he is, lest it be absorbed in the ground — who says to us that this panic is like broken, lest it be like cracked; end of his words. Rabbenu Yerucham challenged him: perforce even if it spilled entirely onto the ground he is still very panicked, like broken, from the words of Tosafos daf 117b, where the Gemara concludes there: the reason one may not save on account of fire more than three meals, as is written in siman 334, is because a person is panicked over his property, and if you permit him more he will come to extinguish; and it asks from that which was taught: a barrel broke for him on the top of his roof, etc. — and the same law is written here in seif 2 — why was he not permitted more than three meals — what should we fear; and it answers: lest out of his panic he bring vessels via the public domain to save; and Tosafos the questioner challenged: he should not have concluded lest he bring vessels via the public domain when he asked from the braita; rather he should have asked from the mishnah of the chapter Barrels — which is the law mentioned here in seif 1; and explain for one version: in the mishnah "a barrel broke for him" it concluded in his mind that it may be spoken when it spilled entirely onto the ground, and it answered him: lest he sponge (and come to squeezing); but in the braita where it spilled on the top of the roof, where it is not relevant that it speaks thus — it asks well that it did not conclude concern lest he bring a vessel via public domain, etc. Behold it is explained that even when it spilled entirely onto the ground he is very panicked, until even the questioner concluded lest he sponge — not like Maharai; and since Maharai already wrote that where one deviates from weekday practice we are not concerned for squeezing, and he inferred so from Rambam's wording as above — only that Magen Avraham wrote "perhaps" and it is not necessary; and I wrote above that Magen Avraham's intent is that one should not compare rabbinic decrees of the Sages: for squeezing there is no concern, nevertheless lest he bring vessels via the public domain even when he changes; therefore Magen Avraham concludes here: we are concerned that out of panic he will bring vessels via the public domain — until here Tosafos did not write the decree lest he sponge except per the questioner, who had not yet concluded lest he bring vessels via the public domain; but per the conclusion, also when it spilled entirely onto the ground, since he is very panicked — lest he bring vessels via the public domain — change does not help:

(ג) And it is possible that nowadays, etc. — also this side of leniency Maharai wrote; and so too Magen Avraham; and it is possible that for this reason Magen Avraham wrote, etc.; and that which the Gemara cited the decree lest he bring vessels, etc., and did not say the decree lest he repair — Chayei Adam and Sefer Teshuvah [wrote]: one reason is that in the time of the Gemara, when public domain was common, they cited one concern; and similarly the Maggid Mishneh: lest he repair; and furthermore in the time of the Gemara their barrels were of earthenware, and in earthenware repair is difficult — why need "lest he repair"; unlike for us, whose barrels are of wood — one may well say lest he repair:
