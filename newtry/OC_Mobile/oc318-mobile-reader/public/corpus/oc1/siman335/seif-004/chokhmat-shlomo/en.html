Seif 4. If the majority of wine from a vat of grapes, etc. — see in Magen Avraham s.k. 4 what he wrote on Darkei Moshe; and it requires study — for one does not nullify a prohibition l'chatchila; behold according to what I wrote in Chiddushei Orach Chayim in my composition Mishna 559 daf 85 — to divide that in something that has permitted form afterward and does not have the status of something that has a permitted form — then permitted to nullify it l'chatchila; and we explained this as R' Meir of Rotenburg's view — end of his words; and Darkei Moshe's words are great support for our words, for he holds that what has permitted form is permitted to nullify; and although Magen Avraham challenges him — it appears he does not disagree on the law we wrote, for Magen Avraham speaks of something that in itself has something that has a permitted form, and it is not nullified — only that now it became mixed in a non-type; therefore it is resolved for b'dieved if it already mixed — then in a non-type, since it is not in existence and the permitted is not called upon it, it does not have the status of something that has a permitted form; if so, resolved b'dieved when it is no longer in existence; but to mix l'chatchila — behold then it is still in existence and the permitted is called upon it and it has a permitted form; therefore since it is stricter than all prohibitions — how can we be more lenient to permit mixing l'chatchila; but in its own law of new wine, it completely lacks the status of something that has a permitted form — even when in existence it is properly lenient even for nullifying; so it is explained. And in R' Meir of Rotenburg's law in what he wrote: we nullify the prohibition lightly — although there it speaks of something that has a permitted form in itself — in truth Darkei Moshe and Magen Avraham dispute: Darkei Moshe holds like R' Meir — even in this, we follow afterward; and since after nullification it will not have the status of something that has a permitted form — therefore permitted to mix l'chatchila and its prohibition is light; and Magen Avraham holds we follow now — and now it is in existence and has the status of something that has a permitted form; and all the more so one may not be lenient in it more than all prohibitions; and in the law where the thing itself lacks the status of something that has a permitted form — it appears all agree permitted to nullify l'chatchila; so it is explained in my humble opinion; and see there in siman 486 and you will understand more. And do not reject our words from chametz and sukkah in s'chach — for there it appears the law of not nullifying a prohibition l'chatchila applies even when it has permitted form; there it is a Torah prohibition, and I spoke only of a rabbinic prohibition, as is written in siman 486 above — that the Rav explains what the Gemara distinguishes between d'oraisa and d'rabbanan is only regarding something that has permitted form afterward; but when it lacks permitted form — even from rabbinic law one does not nullify; and in Torah law, even when it has permitted form one does not nullify; and here it is only rabbinic prohibition; and so there regarding new wine I relied on those who hold new wine nowadays is rabbinic — see there; and understand well:
