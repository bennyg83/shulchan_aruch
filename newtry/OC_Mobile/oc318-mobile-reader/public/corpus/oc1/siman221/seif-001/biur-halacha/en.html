(א) If they were in distress, etc. — Rambam chapter 10 of laws of blessings wrote anonymously and did not mention if they were in distress; nevertheless it appears that he too agrees it is specifically in this manner, for in the Mishnah it states on rains and on good tidings, etc., and in the Gemara at the beginning it deals with one who sees — if one hears it implies rains fell, meaning good tidings; and certainly good tidings apply only when they were in distress at first; if so what the Mishnah said on rains also deals only in this manner; therefore Rambam agrees to the gloss nearby; and why Rambam did not mention explicitly if they were in distress — because it is not his way to write what is not explicit in the Talmud, and also in Eretz Yisrael and its surroundings there is great dryness and much withholding until they need mercy for rain and rain comes only in seasons — certainly there is great joy when rain falls and they must give thanks; and one may also say the Mechaber agrees in Eretz Yisrael and used this wording because he wished to hint that in lands frequent in rain one does not bless the rain blessing except when they were in distress from withholding, as in the gloss; but in Eretz Yisrael even anonymously it is included, for there everyone awaits the time of rains and grieves its absence and rejoices greatly at its fall. I saw in Peri Megadim he wrote it is obvious that even in Eretz Yisrael when years are in order one does not bless on rain — in my humble opinion this requires study for practical law [and what Beit Yosef wrote implying when years are in order no need to bless — see there, it refers to chutz laAretz; Beit Yosef explains Kol Bo that it refers only when years are in order, not that Beit Yosef refers to Eretz Yisrael — see Kol Bo's words carefully and you will find as we say] — this is not found in any later posek; afterward I found in Avot deRabbi Natan that he wrote in Eretz Yisrael it requires study for practical law — therefore it appears one blesses without Heaven's name and kingship.

(ב) They bless on them from when they fell, etc. — this is the measure of when the groom goes out toward the bride stated in the Gemara. From the Mechaber's words it is proven that all said in seif 2 applies even if only this measure fell; and so Tur proves; but in the Gemara it implies haTov vehaMetiv is blessed only when much fell — more than this measure explained in Shulchan Aruch, as Rashi there s.v. deAta porta proves; and it is difficult on Rif and Rosh who omitted this answer — though for Modim one may bless even deAta porta, meaning from when the groom goes out, etc. [and what the Gemara answers both when much fell Modim also applies — end quote]; nevertheless haTov vehaMetiv is blessed only when much fell, whether in the first or last answer. Later I found in Tzelach who also asked this on Rif and Rosh [and what is written that Rambam intended this in law 5 many rains fell — to teach haTov vehaMetiv requires more than groom measure — unlike Shulchan Aruch is not clear at all; truly Rambam's intent is only on what he wrote afterward in law 16 when water multiplied on earth — see there well, all blessings in one context — and Rambam calls this many rains fell]. In Sefer Berakhat Rosh he also asked this and innovated: truly Rif and Rosh explain the sugya opposite Rashi — Rashi in first answer explains deAta porta is Modim anachnu lach on groom measure; if so deAta tova is more than this measure; they explain deAta tova is groom measure on which he blesses rov hahodot, abundance of thanks on such great rain thanking each drop; deAta porta is less than this measure on which he blesses haTov vehaMetiv — this is first answer; in last answer both only when much fell, and less than this measure one does not bless at all — so we rule; therefore well done omitting all this and Rif wrote as in Taanit where groom measure is explained; Rif and Rosh equate all blessings in one manner — now Shulchan Aruch's ruling is sound. Careful analysis shows Rambam's view too: first in law 5 he wrote many rains fell, meaning deAta tova in Gemara; then in law 16 he specified groom measure — for him that is deAta tova; if so Gemara's deAta porta is less than this measure; we rule like Gemara's last answer as Beit Yosef wrote. Therefore Shulchan Aruch stands. Later I found in Shitah Mekubetzet on Berakhot this sugya explains explicitly in the Talmud and also that Rambam implies as we wrote — see there. This is summary.
