Seif 2. “If he has a field in partnership.” Note: See Magen Avraham, who cites Shach as ruling that if a person has a wife and children, he also recites HaTov VeHaMeitiv. Magen Avraham wonders at this, since he may not give them any of those fruits. He further asks why the Gemara in Berachos did not offer this distinction. His words are surprising. First, why should the Gemara have needed to state this explicitly? It merely distinguishes generally between one who has a partner and one who does not. It is entirely possible that having a wife and children is itself considered a partnership. Regarding the substance of Magen Avraham’s objection, that the husband may not give them any of the fruit, one may answer as follows. Kesubos 80 states, and this is the practical halachah in Even HaEzer, siman 85, that a husband may not sell the produce of his wife’s nichsei melog for many years because of the principle of household benefit. This proves that even if he supports her from another source, the produce of nichsei melog nevertheless belongs to her in the sense of household benefit. The same applies here. The success of his field’s produce benefits his wife and children through the increased welfare of the household. Although with nichsei tzon barzel we rule that the husband may sell and his wife cannot object on the basis of household benefit, that is because where the property is not hers she cannot compel him to provide increased household comfort, and basic support is sufficient. Yet since increased household welfare is itself a genuine benefit, when his field produces successfully, his wife and children also benefit through the improvement of the household. Shach therefore correctly wrote that a man with a wife and children is considered to have partners. This also resolves Magen Avraham’s question why the Gemara did not answer this way. In Kesubos 80 there is a dispute. Abaye does not recognize household benefit as the reason, but is concerned that she may become embittered; Rava gives the reason of household benefit. According to Abaye, household benefit is not itself considered a benefit, and therefore one’s wife and children would not be regarded as partners. The Gemara therefore answers in a manner acceptable to all opinions, including Abaye. We, however, rule like Rava that household benefit is a genuine benefit. Shach therefore correctly wrote that a wife and children are considered partners. This is clear; examine it carefully.
