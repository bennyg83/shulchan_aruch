In the Tur he cites the language of the Gemara. "The fast of the seventh": this is the third of Tishrei on which Gedaliah was killed; and why was it written here? To teach you that the death of the righteous is equivalent to the burning of the House of our God. This requires explanation: What difficulty does he have with what is stated here? For he concludes here that he lists them in the order of the months, even though regarding this period of time the fast of the tenth comes earlier than [the others], because he lists the calamities first; and if so, regarding the fast of the seventh we could say the same. And it appears that the fast of the tenth is in any case a memorial of the destruction of the Temple, for on that day the enemy force began [its siege]; therefore he lists everything that alludes to the destruction according to the order of the months; which is not so for Tzom Gedaliah, which is a matter of the death of the righteous and has in it no allusion to destruction. The difficulty is why he listed it among those that allude to the destruction; and he answered: to teach you, etc. The Beis Yosef cited the words of Rabbeinu Yerucham, as follows: The third of Tishrei on which Gedaliah was killed; they said in Rosh Hashana that he was killed and his fast was postponed to a weekday (end quote). And according to this it would appear that if there is a milah on Tzom Gedaliah, the baal bris need not fast until night, as is written in siman 559: a baal bris does not complete [the fast] on Tisha B'Av that falls on Shabbos and is postponed to Sunday, because it is his Yom Tov; and we could say the same of Tzom Gedaliah. And one may say that that case is different, for the postponement is apparent to all in our times, for we see that the primary [calendar] date of the Tisha B'Av fast falls on Shabbos and is merely postponed; therefore its law was changed; which is not so for Tzom Gedaliah, for the first time they postponed it and decreed to fast forever on the third day; the decree always falls equally, and the postponement is not apparent to us.
