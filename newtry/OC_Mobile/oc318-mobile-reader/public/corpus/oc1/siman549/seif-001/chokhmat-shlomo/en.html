Se'if 1. They are obligated to fast on the ninth of Av and on the seventeenth of Tammuz, etc. Note: In the Tur and in the Taz they cited what the Sages said in Tractate Rosh Hashana, folio 18: "the fast of the seventh," on which Gedaliah ben Achikam was killed; and see there what I wrote in my work on the haftarah of Parashas Devarim, "Chazon Yeshayahu," in the year 5597 to the abbreviated reckoning; see there and examine carefully. And I saw in Baer Heitev that he wished to bring a proof for the Taz that there is a distinction between whether the postponement is recognizable or not, from what is stated in the Gemara in the first chapter of Megillah, that Rebbi wished to abolish Tisha B'Av, and why did he not wish to abolish Tzom Gedaliah; see there. And with all due respect, he erred, and the words of Tosafos on that place escaped his notice; they raised a difficulty that a beis din cannot annul the words of a fellow beis din unless it is greater than it in wisdom, etc., and they answered that they did not wish to abolish [it] entirely, but only [to remove] the stringency that is in it beyond that of the other fasts; and if so, that works well for Tisha B'Av, but regarding Tzom Gedaliah there is no stringency beyond that of the other fasts, and what would he abolish? To abolish it entirely, a beis din cannot annul the words of a fellow beis din, etc. And if so, even according to the view that he only wished to abolish it when postponed, then that works well for Tisha B'Av that was postponed, for that is not a complete abolition, since it will be observed in other years; but regarding Tzom Gedaliah, since it is always postponed, if he abolishes it, it would be abolished entirely; and if so, a beis din cannot annul the words of a fellow beis din. And what proof is there from this regarding being lenient for a bris milah, where there is no complete abolition, for he will [still] fast if there is no bris milah? One may properly say that there is no distinction between Tzom Gedaliah and postponed Tisha B'Av; and this is clear and correct; examine carefully.
