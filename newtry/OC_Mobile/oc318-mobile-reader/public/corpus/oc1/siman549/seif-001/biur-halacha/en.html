They are obligated to fast, etc. Ritva wrote at the end of Taanis: A chasan on whose [wedding days] one of the four fasts falls during the days of the rejoicing of his heart; it appears to me that he is obligated to fast on them; for even though they are days of festival and joy for him, and new mourning does not take effect upon them, and there is no joy except through eating, as we say regarding the festivals and regarding Purim; nevertheless, since his festival is an individual's festival, of rabbinic force, and these fasts are of the public, public mourning comes and overrides a rabbinic individual's festival; and furthermore, regarding an individual the verse spoke fully, "If I do not raise Yerushalayim above my chiefest joy" (end quote). And there is no contradiction from this to what is clarified in siman 559, that a baal bris fasts but does not complete [the fast], even though that too is only an individual's Yom Tov; there the reason is because [the fast] is postponed (but nevertheless there is a proof from these words of the Ritva to the conclusion of the Beis Yosef, that where it is not postponed he is obligated to complete [the fast]). And we derive from these words of the Ritva that he holds that Tzom Gedaliah is not always called "postponed," in accordance with the reasoning of the Taz, and not like the Rosh Yosef cited in Shaarei Teshuvah.
