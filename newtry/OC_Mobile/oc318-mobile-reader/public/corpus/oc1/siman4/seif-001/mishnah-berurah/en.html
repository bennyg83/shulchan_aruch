He shall wash his hands, etc. There are two reasons for this. The Rosh wrote that since a person's hands are active, it is impossible that he did not touch filthy flesh at night. Therefore Chazal instituted a blessing over washing for Krias Shema and prayer. The Rashba wrote that in the morning, after sleep, we become like a new creation, as it is written, 'They are new every morning; great is Your faithfulness.' We must thank Him, may He be blessed, for creating us for His honor, to serve Him and bless His Name. For this matter they instituted in the morning all those blessings that we recite every morning. Therefore they also instituted this matter in the morning, to sanctify ourselves with His sanctity and wash our hands from a vessel, like a kohen who sanctifies his hands from the laver before his service. For practical halacha, we adopt both reasons stringently regarding washing, as below in this siman. If one did not recite the blessing of al netilas yadayim before prayer, he should no longer recite it after prayer, according to all opinions; Chayei Adam.
And he should bless. Immediately after washing, he should bless, and he need not wait until drying; drying also does not prevent fulfillment. Some are stringent not to bless until after he dries his hands, for they hold that the ruach ra'ah does not leave the hands until after drying, and they hold that it is forbidden to bless with hands upon which ruach ra'ah rests. But the view of the poskim is not so; Artzos HaChaim. The Seder HaYom wrote that one should not touch his garments until he washes, but the Gemara does not imply this; Magen Avraham. It is stated in Berachos, page 51: do not take your shirt in the morning from the attendant's hand and put it on, because of danger from damaging forces; rather, he should take it himself from the place where it is. This applies even if that attendant already washed his hands. We say in the Gemara that a person washes his face, hands, and feet every day in honor of his Creator. In our countries, where we do not walk barefoot, one need not wash his feet in the morning; Peri Megadim.
Also Asher Yatzar. The reason is that in the morning one becomes like a new creation, and we must thank Him. The blessing of Elokai Neshamah was instituted for the soul, and Asher Yatzar for the formation of the body. There are also other reasons; see the later authorities. The Peri Megadim wrote that according to the reason of becoming a new creation, if one was awake the entire night, in which case he did not become a new creation in the morning, he cannot recite Asher Yatzar unless he relieved himself. Some say that if he urinated at night, he may rely, regarding the blessing of Asher Yatzar, on the Asher Yatzar of the morning; this requires study for practical halacha. Also, it often happens that in the morning he again feels a need to urinate, and the morning blessing of Asher Yatzar can no longer apply to the urination of the night, as below in siman 7 in the Mishnah Berurah, seif katan 1. A God-fearing person should be stringent with himself: he should wash his hands, put on pants or gird himself with another item to separate between his heart and his ervah, and recite Asher Yatzar. However, at the time of the blessing he should be careful to distance himself four amos from the chamber pot of urine if it is standing in his room. If the chamber pot was not designated for this, it is enough to pour a revi'is of water into the urine each time he urinates.
And even, etc. One who wishes to remove himself from doubt should be careful to relieve himself immediately after pouring water over his hands three times. When he leaves the privy, he should wash his hands a second time and recite Asher Yatzar, thereby fulfilling his obligation according to all opinions. It is even better not to recite even the blessing of netilas yadayim until after the second washing, which he does after leaving the privy, especially if he needed to relieve himself at the time of the first washing, for then he certainly may not bless. The Chayei Adam wrote that it is proper to wait to recite al netilas yadayim until after he cleans himself and wants to pray; see there for his reason. Afterwards I found in Maaseh Rav that this was the practice of the Gra, of blessed memory; see Biur Halacha.
