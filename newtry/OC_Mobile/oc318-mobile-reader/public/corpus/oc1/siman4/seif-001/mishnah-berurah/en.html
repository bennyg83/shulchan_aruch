(1) He shall wash his hands, etc. — There are two reasons for this. The Rosh wrote: because a person's hands are busy, and it is impossible that he did not touch unclean flesh at night; therefore the Sages enacted a blessing on netilat yadayim for Keriat Shema and for prayer. The Rashba wrote: because in the morning, after sleep, we become like a new creature, as it is written, "They are new every morning; great is Your faithfulness" — we need to give thanks to the Blessed One who created us for His glory, to serve Him, and to bless His name; and for this reason they enacted in the morning all those blessings that we recite every morning; therefore they also enacted this matter in the morning — to sanctify ourselves in His holiness and to take our hands from a vessel, like a kohen who sanctifies his hands from the basin before his service. For halachah we adopt both reasons stringently regarding netilat yadayim, as below in this siman. And if he did not bless al netilat yadayim before prayer, he does not bless again after prayer, in the view of all [Chayei Adam]:

(2) And he blesses — Immediately after netilat yadayim he blesses; he need not wait for drying, and drying does not prevent [the blessing]. Some are stringent not to bless until after he dries his hands, for they hold that ruach ra'ah does not depart from the hands until after drying; and some hold that it is forbidden to bless with hands upon which ruach ra'ah rests — but the view of the poskim is not so [Eliyah Rabbah, Orach Chayyim]. Seder HaYom wrote that one should not touch his garments until he washes; the Gemara does not imply this [Magen Avraham]. It is stated in Berachos 51a: Do not take your cloak in the morning from the hand of your attendant and put it on, because of the danger of harmful spirits; rather, he should take it himself from the place where it lies. And even if that attendant already washed his hands — we say in the Gemara: A person washes his face, hands, and feet every day out of respect for his Creator. In our countries, where we do not go barefoot, one need not wash his feet in the morning [Peri Megadim]:

(3) Also Asher Yatzar — The reason is because in the morning one becomes a new creature and we must give thanks; therefore the blessings Elokei Neshamah and Asher Yatzar were enacted regarding the creation of the body. There are other reasons as well — see the Acharonim. The Peri Megadim wrote: per the reason of a new creature, if one was awake all night — for then he does not become a new creature in the morning — he cannot recite Asher Yatzar unless he attended to his needs. Some say that if he urinated at night he may rely regarding Asher Yatzar on the Asher Yatzar of the morning; and the law is uncertain. Also, it often happens that in the morning he again feels the urge to urinate and can no longer apply the morning blessing of Asher Yatzar on the urination of the night — as below in siman 7 in Mishna Berurah, note 1. And some are stringent with themselves: they wash their hands, put on trousers or gird themselves with something else to separate between the heart and the nakedness, and recite Asher Yatzar. But at the time of the blessing he should keep four amot away from the urine receptacle if he is standing in his room; and if the receptacle was not designated for this, it suffices to pour a revi'is of water into the urine each time he urinates:

(4) And even, etc. — One who wishes to remove himself from doubt should be careful to attend to his needs immediately after pouring water on his hands three times; and when he exits the privy he should wash his hands a second time and recite Asher Yatzar, and thereby fulfill his obligation according to all. It is better still not to recite even the blessing of netilat yadayim until after the second washing that he performs upon exiting the privy — especially if he needed to relieve himself at the first washing, for then he certainly may not bless. Chayei Adam wrote that it is proper to wait from reciting al netilat yadayim until after he has cleaned himself and is ready to pray — see there for his reason. Later I found in Maaseh Rav that thus was the practice of the Gra, z"l — see Biur Halacha:

(5) Siman 160 — Namely, water whose appearance has changed, or work was done with it, or it is salty or putrid so that a dog cannot drink from it; and from this it is fit for prayer, for here one needs only ordinary cleanliness:

(6) He does not bless — Meaning, he does not bless on netilat yadayim but only on cleanliness of the hands:
