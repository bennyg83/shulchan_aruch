Al Netilas Yadayim. The hands are active and it is likely that during the night they touched soiled parts of the body; therefore the Sages instituted washing before Krias Shema and prayer. This is the explanation of the Rosh. Rashba explains that in the morning a person is like a new creation, as it is written, "They are new every morning," and one therefore washes his hands. Although the expression netilah derives from the vessel called a natla, and here a vessel is not indispensable as it is for washing before a meal, Beis Yosef writes that the wording of the blessing should not be changed. See se'if 6 regarding whether to recite the blessing immediately or in the synagogue.
