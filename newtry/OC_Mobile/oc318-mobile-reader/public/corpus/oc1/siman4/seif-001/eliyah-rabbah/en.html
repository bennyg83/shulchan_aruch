Even if he did not relieve himself, etc. The Levush explains that it is impossible that he did not urinate or relieve himself a little during the night or before lying down, and he did not recite the blessing; therefore we recite it, etc. See below, siman 441, s.k. 4. In Sefer Mitzvos Shemurah, in the name of Adei Zahav, he challenges the Levush, arguing that one should not recite Asher Yatzar in the morning if he has not now relieved himself; see there. In Eliyah Zuta I defended the words of the Levush at length. He also did not see the words of the Bach, who wrote that the reason is that it is like the other morning blessings in siman 46: even if one did not become obligated, one blesses because they are blessings of praise over the practice and order of the world. So too wrote the Perishah. He added another reason: the mouth is included among the openings; if his mouth were not open, he could not speak and bless. Therefore one must bless Asher Yatzar even if he did not relieve himself. In Darchei Moshe he wrote the reason that Asher Yatzar too is connected with the evil spirit, for since one may not touch his mouth or nose with his hands, this washing removes the spirit, and then he may put his hand to the openings or close them when necessary. According to this explanation one should recite Asher Yatzar immediately after the blessing of washing the hands. But if he needs to relieve himself, it seems that he should relieve himself before washing; see Magen Avraham. Magen Avraham wrote in the name of the Rambam, chapter 4 of Hilchos Tefillah, that one washes his face, hands, and feet. The Raavad objected: why his feet? In the Shulchan Aruch of Mahari Luria of blessed memory he wrote only his face, and so is the custom. Emek Berachah wrote that in the blessing of washing the hands one should have in mind that it has thirteen words corresponding to the thirteen attributes.
