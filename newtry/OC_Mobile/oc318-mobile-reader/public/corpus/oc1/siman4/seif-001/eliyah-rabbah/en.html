[1] Even if he did not relieve himself, etc. The reason in Levush is that perhaps he did not pass water or relieved himself a little at night or before lying down and did not bless — therefore they bless, etc. See below siman 441 sk 4. Here in the book Mitzvos Shomrim in the name of Edi Zahav he challenged Levush that one should not bless in the morning "Who formed" when he has not now relieved himself — see there. In Eliyahu Zuta I settled Levush's words at length. He also did not see Bach's words, who wrote the reason: like the other morning blessings in siman 46 — although one is not obligated, they bless because they are blessings of praise for the world's custom and order — so too Prisha wrote. He added another reason: the mouth is included among the orifices, and if his mouth were not open he could not speak and bless — therefore one must bless "Who formed" even if he did not relieve himself. In Darkei Moshe he wrote the reason for "Who formed" is tied to evil spirit: since it is forbidden to touch with his hands neither mouth nor nose, and through this netilah the spirit is removed, then it is permitted to send his hand to the orifices or to cover them when he needs — according to this interpretation one should bless "Who formed" immediately after the blessing of netilat yadayim. But if he needs his orifices, it seems he should relieve himself before netilat yadayim — see Magen Avraham. Magen Avraham wrote in the name of Rambam, chapter 4 of Laws of Prayer: he washes his face, hands, and feet — end quote. But Raavad challenged: his feet — why? Likewise in Shulchan Aruch, Mahari Luria wrote only his face, and so is the practice. Emek Berachah wrote: one should have intent in the blessing of netilat yadayim that it has thirteen words corresponding to the thirteen attributes.
