“And he blesses netilas yadayim.” According to what we hold—even if he relieved himself and is about to pray—he does not bless netilas yadayim; yet in the morning we do bless, so a reason is needed: as the Rashba explains, in the morning one becomes like a new creature and we must give thanks; accordingly they instituted all the morning berachos; therefore we must sanctify ourselves in His holiness and wash the hands from a vessel, like a kohen who sanctifies his hands from the laver before his service. Bach cites Tolaas Yaakov: anyone who goes four cubits without morning netilas yadayim is liable to death. Seder Hayom writes not to touch garments until he washes; siman 46 does not read that way. Berachos 51a: “Do not take your robe in the morning from the sun-heated pot and put it on,” which implies in some writings that he should bless netilas yadayim immediately, then relieve himself, then say Asher Yatzar. It appears to us better to relieve himself first so he may say Asher Yatzar right away and avoid a doubt—some say one may bless Asher Yatzar without relieving himself, and all the more so if he must use the bathroom it is forbidden for him to bless. The Gemara says a person washes face, hands, and feet every day for the honor of his Maker; so too Rambam, Hilchos Tefillah ch. 4, and Hagahos Maimoniyos.
“And even.” The reason is that before netilas yadayim it is forbidden to touch the openings to close or open them, and now it is permitted to touch once he has blessed Asher Yatzar (Darkei Moshe). It appears to us that this is because he has become like a new creature, as stated above.
“Siman 160.” Rema’s intent is specifically water whose appearance changed, or on which the labors mentioned in siman 160 were performed.
“Valid.” Even an unfit vessel and weak water pressure do not prevent washing, and one blesses, as stated in siman 67.
“That one does not bless.” Levush writes to bless on cleanliness of the hands, as explained in siman 22; perhaps the case here is that he does not wish to pray now. Bach ruled that one always blesses netilas yadayim.
