Even if he did not relieve himself. See the end of Mishnah Berurah. Chayei Adam 7:6 writes: 'It is proper to wait to recite Al Netilas Yadayim until after cleaning oneself and preparing to pray, thereby satisfying everyone. If he blessed first and later relieves himself, Rosh requires another blessing. Although we do not rule so, it is proper to take care. Magen Avraham 1.' Chayei Adam apparently had a different text in Magen Avraham; our text is unintelligible on this point. See the later authorities. Rabbi Chaim Tzanzer writes likewise in his glosses. Chayei Adam implies that one who rises long before prayer, even if he urinates (which is called relieving oneself) should nevertheless wait to recite Al Netilas Yadayim until morning, since he will presumably use the privy again before prayer, thereby satisfying Rosh, who holds the blessing was principally instituted for prayer. This advice is apparently difficult: according to Rashba, the first washing upon arising fulfilled the obligation; how can he later say 'Who commanded us concerning washing the hands' over the second washing? The blessing cannot refer back to the first washing after a long interruption, which the later authorities in siman 6 say should initially be avoided. One may answer that Beis Yosef in siman 6 writes that according to Rashba's reason of becoming a new creation, the blessing may be delayed and arranged with the others despite the long interruption; but then he should immediately recite all the morning blessings too. Shaarei Teshuvah 6:5 implies he disagrees with Chayei Adam and has one recite Al Netilas Yadayim and Asher Yatzar immediately after relieving himself. Derech HaChaim makes no distinction, perhaps because of our question; Shaarei Teshuvah 4 clearly follows this approach. I later found Chayei Adam's view in Maaseh Rav: one should adjoin Al Netilas Yadayim to prayer and not recite it immediately upon arising when he does not intend to pray at once, provided he does not forget. Although this may follow his ruling like Rosh that every prayer requires a blessing, unlike our ruling, it is still better to follow Chayei Adam. One certainly satisfies Rosh, and perhaps Rashba too according to Beis Yosef; if one does not, Rosh is certainly not satisfied, as Responsa Rosh cited by Beis Yosef proves. Rambam also appears to agree with Rosh that washing was principally instituted for prayer, not becoming a new creation, since Hilchos Tefillah 4:3 requires a blessing before every prayer, as Beis Yosef cites below in siman 92.
Below, siman 160. See Mishnah Berurah, Magen Avraham, Shaarei Teshuvah, and Pri Megadim. They imply that Rema means we consider the view that no blessing is recited specifically in this case; if a vessel, human force, or a revi'is of water is lacking, he may certainly recite Al Netilas Yadayim. Rema's conclusion in se'if 7, gloss, 'and the other invalidating factors,' refers only to lacking a revi'is. Biur HaGra on se'if 7 and Prishah imply that according to Raah, no blessing is recited without a vessel and human force. I omitted all this because it makes no practical difference: the later authorities; Maharshal, Damesek Eliezer, Bach, Eliyah Rabbah, Derech HaChaim, and Artzos HaChaim; rule that even over entirely invalid water one may recite Al Netilas Yadayim. Biur HaGra also appears to compare this law to se'if 22, where he inclines toward reciting the blessing, as Magen Avraham challenges there. This is the law after the fact; initially, it is proper to ensure a vessel and human force as well, as explained in se'if 7.
Over them. See Mishnah Berurah. Shaarei Teshuvah writes that one who follows Shulchan Aruch and washed without a blessing, then obtained valid water, should wash again without a blessing. According to Chayei Adam cited above, he can avoid losing the blessing: before prayer he should urinate and wipe the droplets, or relieve himself, then wash with valid water and bless. Pri Megadim 5 likewise implies that if valid water becomes available an hour or two later, he may bless over washing. It is best to follow our advice.
