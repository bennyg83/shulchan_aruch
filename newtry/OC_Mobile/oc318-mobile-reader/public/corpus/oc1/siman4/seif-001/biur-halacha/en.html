And even if he did not relieve himself — see Mishna Berurah at the end. This is the language of the Chayei Adam, klal 7 seif 6: it is proper to wait to recite al netilas yadayim until after he cleans himself and intends to pray, for then he satisfies all views. But if he blessed beforehand and afterward goes to relieve himself — per the Rosh he must return and bless again; and although we do not rule thus, nevertheless it is proper to be careful about this. There in Magen Avraham subparagraph 21. End quote. (It implies that he had a different textual reading in Magen Avraham; but in truth according to what is written before us he has no explanation — see the later authorities.) So too the Gaon R' Chaim of Sanz in his glosses. It appears from the Chayei Adam that one who rose a long time before prayer — even if he passed water, which is already called by the name of relieving oneself — nevertheless, since presumably when morning prayer time arrives he will go to the privy to clean himself, he should now wait for the blessing of al netilas yadayim until the morning when he cleans himself before prayer, in order to satisfy the Rosh's view, who holds that the main [reason] was instituted for prayer. At first glance this is difficult on his advice: how can he now discharge his blessing per the Rashba? Did he not already fulfill the obligation of netilah when he washed immediately upon rising? How can he now say on the second washing "and commanded us regarding netilas yadayim"? And to say that this blessing refers to the first washing — is there not a great interruption? And lekhatchilah one must be very careful about this, as explained in many later authorities in siman 6 — see there. However, in truth it can be reconciled: for the Beis Yosef wrote in siman 6 that per the Rashba, because of "new creature," we may delay it and arrange it with the other morning blessings, although there is a great interruption; but if so, one would then need to bless immediately afterward all the other morning blessings as well. From the conclusion of Shaarei Teshuvah in siman 6 sk 5 it appears that he does not hold like the Chayei Adam, but rather immediately after relieving oneself he blesses al netilas yadayim and Asher Yatzar; and so too in Darkhei Chaim no distinction is mentioned in this matter — perhaps the reason is our difficulty above; and so it is proven in Shaarei Teshuvah sk 4 that this is his view. Later I found in Maaseh Rav like the Chayei Adam, who wrote that one should attach the blessing of al netilas yadayim to prayer and not say it immediately upon rising when he does not intend to pray immediately — provided he does not forget. End quote. Although one can reject this, for there he addresses his own view which he ruled there like the Rosh that one must bless for each prayer — not so for us. Nevertheless, it is better to do as the Chayei Adam, for per the Rosh this certainly satisfies all views, and it is also possible for the Rashba per what the Beis Yosef wrote as above — not so if he does not act per the Rosh, for he certainly does not satisfy all views, as proven from the Rosh's responsum cited in Beis Yosef. Also from the Rambam it appears he holds like the Rosh, that the main [institution] of netilah was for prayer and not for "new creature," since he holds one must bless for each prayer — chapter 4 of Laws of Prayer halacha 3, cited in Beis Yosef later in siman 92.

Below, siman 105 — see Mishna Berurah, and see Magen Avraham and Shaarei Teshuvah and Peri Megadim. It appears from them that Rama's intent is to say that only regarding this we are concerned for the view "some say" that one does not bless on them; but regarding a vessel and human force, or if he lacks a revi'it of water, he can certainly recite al netilas yadayim. What Rama concluded in seif 7 in the gloss and the other disqualifying factors — that applies only if he lacks a revi'it of water, per the Gra's explanation in seif 7. So too the Prisha implies: per Raah he should not bless if there is no vessel and human force. I did not copy all this because it has no practical difference in the ruling, for otherwise the later authorities ruled (Rashal, Damesek Eliezer, Bach, Eliyah Rabbah, Darkhei Chaim, Aruch HaChaim) that even on completely invalid water one may recite al netilas yadayim; and so the Gra's explanation implies by comparing this law to that in seif 22, where he leans to rule that one blesses al netilas yadayim, per Magen Avraham's question there. All this for actual law; but lekhatchilah it is proper to be concerned and stringent even about vessel and human force, as explained in seif 7.

On them — see Mishna Berurah; and see Shaarei Teshuvah, who wrote that one who follows the Shulchan Aruch and washed without a blessing, and afterward kosher water became available for him — he should return and wash without a blessing. According to what we wrote above in the name of Chayei Adam, one can give advice so he does not lose the blessing, namely that before prayer he passes water and rubs, or relieves himself, and afterward washes his hands in kosher water and can bless. From Peri Megadim sk 5 it also appears that when kosher water becomes available for him after an hour or two, he can bless on netilas yadayim. It is good to act as we wrote.
