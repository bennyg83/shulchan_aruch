And he should bless. See Taz, who brought the reasons of the Rosh and Rashba regarding the blessing. The Zohar and Orchos Chayim do not relate to the blessing, as written in Magen Avraham s.k. 1. He takes the words of the Rosh as primary, for according to the law no vessel is needed at all, and waters invalid for washing hands for a meal are valid here lechatchilah. So too appears to be the view of the Mechaber, who in seif 1 wrote without qualification that they are valid, and in seif 7 wrote that it is good to be concerned for the Rashba. According to Magen Avraham s.k. 1, however, that the primary view is like the Rashba, one certainly must be careful lechatchilah according to the law in all matters, like the laver; for this reason one blesses over washing the hands, using a vessel, etc.; see the Beis Yosef. The Taz follows his view in siman 7, s.k. 1, where he answers Magen Avraham's question: specifically in the morning they instituted the blessing because most times one's hands are soiled, and they instituted the blessing based on the majority. This differs from Minchah, where most times one is not soiled, and the uncommon case that he touched a soiled place and immediately goes to pray was not included in the enactment. For ordinary hands that were merely unattended, one certainly does not need a blessing. According to this, a new law emerges: if one slept in gloves and knows certainly that he did not touch any soiled place, according to the Rosh he does not bless, while according to the Rashba he blesses, as Magen Avraham wrote from this in s.k. 1. See what I wrote on Taz s.k. 9 and Magen Avraham s.k. 13 about this further; here is not the place to elaborate. See also what I wrote in seif 23 on Magen Avraham s.k. 26, 7, and 8 about this. Know that one can say that in the morning the obligation of washing hands according to the Rambam in chapter 1 of Hilchos Tefillah, and the view of the Mechaber in siman 106, seif 2, is according to the Torah, and one time is enough. Therefore, in the morning, when one prayer is according to the Torah, the Sages instituted washing hands; but for Minchah, which is D'Rabbanan, the Sages did not institute washing hands with a blessing. Although one prays Maariv at night and the day follows the night, and should you say that specifically by day the mitzvah according to the Torah is to serve Him as the service of the day, this is mistaken, for if so women would be exempt like tzitzis, yet the Rambam in chapter 1 of Hilchos Tefillah and the Mechaber in siman 107 wrote that women are obligated in prayer according to the Torah. Rather, Maariv was optional in the time of the Gemara, and one prayer in the morning is according to the Torah. Even nowadays, when they made Maariv obligatory, the original enactment did not move from its place, like kiddush in the synagogue and the like. However, the Taz in siman 106, s.k. 2, wrote that prayer is D'Rabbanan. And what the Taz wrote regarding whether one blesses immediately, see siman 6 in Taz s.k. 4. Although drying does not prevent validity here, as Magen Avraham wrote here in s.k. 6, so after washing and before drying it is already after the act, this will be explained in Magen Avraham s.k. 6.
