And he shall bless — Eshel Avraham brought the reason of Rosh and Rashba regarding the blessing (Zohar and Orach Chayyim are not relevant to the blessing, as Magen Avraham (1) wrote) and adopts Rosh's words as primary: per the law no vessel at all is required, and water disqualified for netilat yadayim for a meal is valid here lechatchila; and so appears the Mechaber's view in se'if 1 — stam kosher — and in se'if 7 to take account of Rashba he wrote "good," etc.; and for Magen Avraham (1) the main point is like Rashba — certainly lechatchila he should be careful per the law in all matters as for a basin; and why bless al netilat yadayim — he took the vessel, etc. — see Beit Yosef. He follows his approach in siman 7, letter 1, answering Magen Avraham's question: they instituted it specifically for morning because usually his hands are soiled and for the majority they instituted a blessing; unlike mincha, where usually they are not soiled, and the minority who touched soiled matter and immediately goes to pray — they did not institute; and for stam yadayim with mental distraction — certainly no blessing is required; therefore we derive the law of "if he slept": if he slept resting his hands and knows certainly he did not touch soiled matter — for Rosh he does not bless, for Rashba he blesses as Magen Avraham (1) from this; see Taz 9 and Magen Avraham 13 from this further; here we will not expand more; see also se'if 23, Magen Avraham 26, 7, 8 from this. And know: one can say in the morning they obligated al netilat yadayim per Rambam, chapter 1 of prayer, and Mechaber 106:2 that it is Torah; and once a day suffices — therefore morning prayer, once Torah — Chazal instituted netilat yadayim; but for mincha derabanan Chazal did not institute netilat yadayim with a blessing; even though he prayed maariv at night and goes after the day — some say specifically by day the mitzvah is Torah and "to serve Him" was daytime work — women would be exempt like tzitzit; yet Rambam, once prayer, and Mechaber 107 wrote women are obligated in prayer twice — rather meaning maariv was reshut in Gemara times and prayer once in the morning is deoraita; nowadays too they made it an obligation — it did not move from its place like Kiddush in the synagogue, etc.; but Taz 106:2 wrote prayer is derabanan.
