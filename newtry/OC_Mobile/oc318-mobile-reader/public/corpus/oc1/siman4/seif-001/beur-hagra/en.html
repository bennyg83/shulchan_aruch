Seif 1, "he should wash his hands." For prayer, Berakhot 15a. And so he arranged at the end of siman 2: "and he should check himself," etc. In siman 3 he explained its laws; here: he should wash, and afterward come the laws of Shema and prayer.

"And he should recite the blessing," etc. There, siman 2, and because of prayer.

"And some say." This follows the Rashba in siman 191, who wrote that morning differs from Minchah and Maariv because one has become like a new creature, as stated in the Midrash on "new every morning," etc.; therefore one must thank Him and serve Him. They instituted "Elokai Neshamah" for the soul and "Asher Yatzar" for the body. Thus the wording of all the early authorities places "Elokai Neshamah" immediately after "Asher Yatzar," and so is the order of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch in siman 6; the blessing on Torah follows all the morning blessings before the passage of the Tamid, and so is the order of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch, simanim 46, 47, and 48. The Rav, however, changed the order at the end of siman 46 and wrote, "the custom is," etc. The reason is that in the synagogue they begin from "Elokai Neshamah"; see Magen Avraham, siman 6, small paragraph 8. At home they recite only "al netilas yadayim" and "Asher Yatzar," as the Tur wrote there; at times one recites verses and supplications before going to the synagogue. This is what he wrote at the end of siman 46: "one should not read," etc.; "but the custom," etc.; "and they are accustomed," etc., meaning to satisfy the first view. That accords with the second opinion in the Tur, siman 6, that one need not juxtapose "Asher Yatzar" to "Elokai Neshamah"; but the first opinion there requires juxtaposition, as the Rosh wrote at the beginning of rule 4.

"Water invalid," etc. It is no worse than a pebble, chip of wood, or anything that cleans. There.

"However," etc. Meaning, he should say "for the cleanliness of the hands," as written in seif 22. But the Raah does not accept the rule of seif 22, as written there, for he holds the blessing was instituted only over washing.
