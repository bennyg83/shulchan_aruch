Seif 1: "He should wash his hands." For prayer — Berachos 15a. And so he arranged at the end of siman 2: "and he should examine himself," etc. And he explained its laws in siman 3; here he should wash, and afterward the laws of Shema and prayer.

"And he should recite the blessing," etc. There, siman 2, and for the sake of prayer.

"And some say." Per the Rashba in siman 191, who wrote that morning differs from Minchah and Maariv because one has become like a new creature, as stated in the Midrash on the verse "new every morning," etc.; therefore one must thank Him and serve Him. They instituted Elokai Neshamah for the soul and Asher Yatzar for the body. Therefore the wording of all the early authorities places Elokai Neshamah immediately after Asher Yatzar, and so is the order of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch in siman 6; the blessing on the Torah follows all the morning blessings before the passage of the Tamid, and so is the order of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch in simanim 46, 47, and 48. But the Rabbi changed the order at the end of siman 46 and wrote, "the custom is," etc. The reason is that in the synagogue they begin from Elokai Neshamah; see Magen Avraham, siman 6, s.k. 8. At home one recites only al netilas yadayim and Asher Yatzar, as the Tur wrote there; at times one recites verses and supplications before going to the synagogue. This is what he wrote at the end of siman 46: "one should not read," etc.; "but the custom," etc.; "and they are accustomed," etc. — meaning, to satisfy the first view. That follows the second view in the Tur, siman 6, that one need not juxtapose Asher Yatzar to Elokai Neshamah; but the first view there requires juxtaposition, as the Rosh wrote at the beginning of rule 4.

"Unfit water," etc. For it is no worse than a pebble, a chip of wood, and anything that cleans. There.

"However," etc. Meaning, he should say [the blessing] for cleanliness of the hands, as stated in seif 22. But the Raah does not hold the law of seif 22, as stated above, for he holds they did not institute the blessing only for netilah; and see what is written below.
