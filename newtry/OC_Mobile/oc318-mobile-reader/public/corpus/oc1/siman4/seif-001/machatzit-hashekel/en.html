Note 1, and he blesses al netilat yadayim. As we hold, etc. — and this is the Rosh, who gave a reason that for this reason one needs netilat yadayim and a blessing: it is impossible that at night he did not touch sleep in an unclean place; this is the Rosh in his view, who holds that even for mincha prayer, if he attended to his needs and wishes to pray, he needs washing and to bless al netilat yadayim. But as we hold, even if he attended to his needs, etc., the reason must be as Rashba stated, etc.

Therefore we must sanctify ourselves, etc. Namely, the morning prayer is greater than the mincha prayer, because we have become a new creature — Hashem restored souls to dead corpses; therefore one must do more for the morning prayer, by analogy to sacrificial service, where the kohen needed sanctification. Although the kohen also needed to sanctify his feet, nevertheless they made this only by way of analogy, but it is not equal in all respects to sanctification.

The Bach wrote in the name of Tur Yoreh De'ah, etc. He is liable for death, and the Bach wondered at Rabbi Yosef Karo that he omitted it — perhaps so that they would be shogeg, etc. In Eliyah Rabbah, siman 1, he brought in the name of his brother-in-law, the Maharari, author of Minchat Yaakov, who said to settle the custom of the world that relies on Mordechai, whom Shach brought in Yoreh De'ah, siman 344, on the matter where we say one does not speak before the dead except the words of the dead. Rabbi Hai Gaon wrote that this is specifically within four amot of the dead; Mordechai wrote that everything beneath him is considered as four amot; if so, the same applies here — one may walk through the whole room before netilat yadayim. The author of Eliyah Rabbah rejected this, for we hold everywhere, also regarding excrement in a house, that the whole room is not considered four amot; if so, Mordechai's words are difficult. At present, Mordechai spoke only regarding the dead, for four amot were not mentioned in the Gemara's explanation — only Rabbi Hai; and since the reason is that it is a disgrace to the dead, as though one despairs of burying him since one speaks other things, therefore for this reason it is reasonable for Mordechai that the whole room is considered for this matter as four amot. But where four amot were explicitly mentioned, Mordechai concedes that it is specifically that measure, and we do not say the whole room is considered four amot; if so, there is never anything to rely on — and he elaborated. Afterward he wrote that he found in Sefer Damascus Eliezer that it is possible in this era the power of ruach raah is not so strong as to harm one who walks four amot without netilat yadayim, as Shivta and other matters that have changed in this era. Nevertheless, he concluded in Eliyah Rabbah that one who fears Heaven should be stringent with himself not to walk four amot without netilat yadayim, and should prepare near his bed one vessel with water and one empty vessel into which to pour.

And in Seder HaYom he wrote, etc. And in the Gemara it does not imply so, and the matter is miltha 46. Namely, there the order is the morning blessings, and so it is in the Talmud. He wrote: when he dresses he blesses "Who clothes the naked," etc.; when he puts on his shoes he blesses, etc.; when he girds himself, etc.; when he places a hat on his head, etc.; when he washes his hands he blesses, etc. — behold, netilat yadayim is ordered at the end, after putting on all his garments. Some say he blesses, etc., as Rama wrote, that it is forbidden for him to bless, as written in siman 92 — we say in the Gemara, Shabbat 49b. Thus wrote the Rambam; but the Ra'avad challenged him: what need is there to wash his feet for prayer? The author of Maaseh Rokeach wrote that the Ra'avad overlooked the Talmud's words in tractate Shabbat cited above. Kesef Mishneh defended the Ra'avad, and this is his language: he explains that this baraita was not stated for prayer, but whether one may wash his face, hands, and feet in order to beautify himself, as Rashi explained — end quote.
