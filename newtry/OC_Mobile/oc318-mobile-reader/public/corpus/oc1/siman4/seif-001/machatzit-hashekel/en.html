S.k. 1, and he should bless over washing the hands. According to what we hold, etc. The Rosh gave the reason that therefore washing and a blessing are required, because it is impossible that during the night, while sleeping, he did not touch a soiled place. This follows the Rosh's own view, for he holds that even for Minchah, if one relieved himself and wishes to pray, he must wash and bless over washing the hands. But according to what we hold, that even if he relieved himself, etc., one must say the reason is as the Rashba wrote, etc.
Therefore we must sanctify ourselves, etc. That is, Shacharis is greater than Minchah because we have become like a new creation, since He, blessed be He, returned souls to dead bodies. Therefore for Shacharis more than for Minchah one must make it resemble the Temple service, where the kohen required sanctification. Although the kohen also had to sanctify his feet, nevertheless they made it only a resemblance and it is not equal to sanctification in every respect.
The Bach wrote in the name of Tosafos Yeshanim, etc. That he is liable to death. The Bach wondered at the Beis Yosef for omitting it, perhaps because it is better that they be inadvertent, etc. In Sefer Eliyah Rabbah, siman 1, he brought in the name of his brother-in-law, Rabbi Yaakov, author of Minchas Yaakov, who sought to justify the common custom by relying on the Mordechai brought by the Shach in Yoreh Deah siman 344 regarding the rule that one may not say matters before the dead except matters of the dead. Rav Hai Gaon wrote that this is only within four amos of the dead, and the Mordechai wrote that the whole area under the same roof is considered four amos. If so, here too it would be permitted to walk anywhere in the room before washing. The author of Eliyah Rabbah rejected this, for we hold everywhere, even regarding excrement in a house, that the entire room is not considered four amos. Therefore the words of the Mordechai must be limited to a dead person, where the measure of four amos is not stated explicitly in the Gemara but only by Rav Hai. Since the reason is disgrace to the dead, for it appears as though he despairs of burying him when he speaks of other matters, the Mordechai reasonably held that for this purpose the whole room is like four amos. But where four amos is stated explicitly, the Mordechai agrees that it is specific and we do not say the whole room is like four amos. Thus the world has nothing to rely on, and he wrote at length. Afterward he wrote that he found in Sefer Damesek Eliezer that perhaps nowadays the power of the evil spirit is not so strong as to harm one who walks four amos without washing, just as with shivta and other matters that have changed nowadays. Nevertheless, Eliyah Rabbah concludes that one who fears Heaven should be stringent and not walk four amos without washing, and should prepare next to his bed one vessel with water and one empty vessel into which to wash.
And in Seder HaYom he wrote, etc. But the Gemara does not imply so; see siman 46. That is, there the order of the morning blessings is arranged, and so it is in the Gemara: when he dresses, he blesses Who clothes the naked; when he puts on his shoes, he blesses; when he girds himself, he blesses; when he places a cap on his head, he blesses; when he washes his hands, he blesses. Thus washing is arranged at the end, after putting on all his garments. Some say that he blesses, etc., as the Rama wrote, that it is forbidden for him to bless, as written in siman 92. We say in Shabbos 49b: so wrote the Rambam. The Raavad objected: why does he need washing the feet for prayer? The author of Migdal Oz wrote that the Raavad overlooked the words of that Gemara in Shabbos. The Kesef Mishneh defended the Raavad, writing that he explains the beraisa not as taught regarding prayer, but regarding whether it is permitted to wash one's face, hands, and feet to beautify oneself, as Rashi explained.
