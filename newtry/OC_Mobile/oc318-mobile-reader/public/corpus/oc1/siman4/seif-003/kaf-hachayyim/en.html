18) [Se'if 3] Before washing his hands, he should not touch his mouth or nose, etc. Mor U'Ketziah writes: 'It appears to me that this precaution against touching one's mouth and eyes before washing applies specifically to touching inside these organs, but there is no concern outside.' Perishah, siman 46:5, appears to agree, as does Eliyah Rabbah there. Chida, however, writes in Machazik Berachah 3 here that their words are unconvincing, for the inside and outside are certainly alike. He writes that this is implied by Zohar; Alfasi Zuta at the end of Berachos by Rama MiPano; Maadanei Melech there; and Pri Chadash, siman 46; see there. Maamar Mordechai, siman 46:3, agrees, as do Shalmei Tzibbur, page 18a, and Shaarei Teshuvah, siman 46:3, in the aforementioned authorities' name.
18) If any of the aforementioned areas itches during sleep, he should scratch it through a garment. He should also not inhale snuff before washing his hands. If he strongly desires it, he should rise, wash his hands, and then inhale the powder. After daytime sleep or sleep after midnight, however, one need not take all the aforementioned precautions, even though we wrote that he should wash three times. Ben Ish Chai, ibid. 15.
19) There: Nor his nose, etc. The Gemara there, page 29, states: A hand blinds; a hand deafens; a hand causes polyps. Rashi explains: A hand touching the eye before washing blinds; touching the ear deafens; touching the mouth or nose causes polyps - foul odor of the nose and mouth. Solel Boneh 3 writes: 'Regarding the statement that the evil spirit blinds and deafens, etc., although this is not perceptible to the physical eye, it concerns Torah. One who touches one of these areas becomes blind or deaf to Torah that day: he will not understand what he sees in Torah and will not understand what he hears, as it says, "Who is blind but My servant, or deaf as the messenger I send?" See Shaar HaAvodah, chapter 12, page 49c.'
20) There: He should not touch before washing his hands, etc. One must take great care not to touch any food or drink before washing, so as not to defile it. Women must be especially warned about this, since most food preparation is done by them. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, second edition, 2; Pri Megadim, Mishbetzos Zahav 7; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch 2:5; and Amudei HaShulchan there, paragraph 7. The same precaution applies before washing after using the lavatory; see above, paragraph 8. After the fact, food or drink that was touched is permitted. Chayei Adam 2:2. A G-d-fearing person should be stringent for himself. Amudei HaShulchan, ibid. According to Malbim, food touched before handwashing can be remedied by rinsing it three times. Amudei HaShulchan, ibid.
21) There: Nor his ears, etc. He should likewise not touch the male organ below the crown, or even above it for purposes of marital relations. Nor should he touch water in a basin prepared for the entire household's handwashing, lest his hands defile it. When washing from the vessel, he must take great care not to touch the water inside and defile it. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ibid.
22) Pri Megadim, Mishbetzos Zahav, writes that it is proper to take care to wash young children's hands every morning, for danger is treated more stringently than prohibition. The public is not careful about this, and I do not know why. Chida likewise writes in Moreh BeEtzba 2:60: 'He should warn that his young children's hands be washed properly, so that they do not defile everything they touch and, Heaven forbid, cause the Other Side to rest upon it all.' Shulchan Aruch HaRav explains that people customarily treat the touch of children who have not reached the age of chinuch leniently because the holy soul's principal and completed entry into a person occurs at thirteen years and one day for a male and twelve years for a female. They then become obligated in Torah mitzvos and liable for punishment. This holy soul begins entering through education in Torah and mitzvos, concerning which the Sages obligated parents to educate children, and also through the mitzvah of bris milah. Therefore, one who guards against a child's touch from the day of his bris onward may be called holy. Ben Ish Chai, ibid. 10, writes in Chesed LaAlafim's name that this custom is a beneficial segulah for children, enabling them to grow in purity and become sacred growths.
23) One need not be concerned, however, about a non-Jew's touch. This spirit of impurity desires to rest only in a sacred vessel, at a place from which holiness has departed. This refers to Jewish bodies while they sleep, when their holy souls depart from their bodies and the spirit of impurity then rests upon them. When the soul returns to the body, the spirit of impurity departs from the entire body and remains only upon the hands. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ibid.; Pri Megadim, Mishbetzos Zahav, ibid.; Amudei HaShulchan, ibid.
