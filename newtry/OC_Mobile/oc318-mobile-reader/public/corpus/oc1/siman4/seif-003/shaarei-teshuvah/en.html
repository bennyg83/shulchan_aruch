Baer Hetev; and see Yad Ephraim — it seems Baer Hetev emended Magen Avraham's words in seif kaf gimel and seif kaf dalet [of Magen Avraham they are] one seif — and he aimed well, unlike some proofreaders on another matter. See there. And he wrote in Shlemei Tzibbur — water in barrels in which beer was drawn, or rosewater flowing through a pipe from the boiler; and in water in barrels they cool the pipe inside (and it is like in our countries in wine-making — distillation) — as Rama wrote, even at dawn one should not bless on water whose appearance changed or melacha was done with it; and this water had melacha done with it, as explained in siman 160. Therefore one may wash with them if he has no others and not bless on netilat yadayim. See there; and see below seif kaf he that later authorities [hold] on water with melacha done — he washes and also blesses. If so, in a time of need when he has no others except this flowing water — he may wash and bless on them as well. See below seif kaf zayin; and see Shulchan Lechem that water remaining in household utensils one may wash his hands with them in time of need, but not rinse his mouth because it is repulsive — see there. And see siman 160 — water in which one rinsed vessels that need not be rinsed are invalid for netilat yadayim. If so, per Rama he washes and does not bless; and there in siman 160 it is explained Bach and other later authorities are stringent for netilat yadayim for a meal with water a dog or pig drank from — that is like poured-out water; and one should be stringent also not to wash from them at dawn, only in time of need; and regarding blessing, one who practices like Rama should not bless here either.
