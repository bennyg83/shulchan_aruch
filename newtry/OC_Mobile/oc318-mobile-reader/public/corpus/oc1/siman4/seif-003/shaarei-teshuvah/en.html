Shalmei Tzibbur discusses water in a barrel used while producing beer or rosewater: vapor flows through a pipe from the boiler, and water in the barrel cools the pipe, as occurs in our countries when distilling liquor. Rama writes that even in the morning one does not bless over water whose appearance changed or with which work was performed. Since this water performed work, as explained in siman 160, one should wash with it when no other water is available but not recite Al Netilat Yadayim; see there. Below, se'if katan 5, however, the later authorities hold that one both washes and blesses with water used for work. Accordingly, in pressing circumstances, when only this runoff water is available, one may wash and bless with it. See below, se'if katan 7. Shalmei Tzibbur further writes that water remaining from household use may be used to wash the hands in pressing circumstances, but one should not rinse his mouth with it because it is repulsive; see there. Siman 160 states that water used to rinse unwashed utensils is invalid for netilat yadayim. According to Rama, then, one should wash with it but not bless. Siman 160 also explains that Bach and other later authorities are stringent regarding washing for a meal with water from which a dog or pig drank, because it resembles wastewater. One should likewise be stringent not to use it for morning washing except in pressing circumstances; one who follows Rama should not bless over it. See there for additional legal distinctions that can be applied from that context to ours.
