[8] Without a blessing. Bach concludes he blesses and his proof is not compelling — see there; so Taz and Magen Avraham imply they ruled like Shulchan Aruch. So I found in Berachas Avraham page 182: if he soiled his hand at night, even Levush and Rama agree he blesses; also if he slept even incidental sleep — Nachalas Tzvi wrote they agree he blesses, because it applies to him "He entrusted His deposit" and returns it. Beis Yosef indeed wrote so. It appears to me: Beis Yosef wrote this only for Rashba, who holds reason of netilat yadayim blessing is because He entrusted, etc.; but for Rosh one does not bless because hands are not soiled — and Beis Yosef holds Rosh's words are primary. Only the doubt is whether evil spirit dwells on hands when he awoke all night. Know: if not so, even awakening all night he should bless, as Beis Yosef wrote for Rashba's reason — rabbis did not differentiate. Moreover Shulchan Aruch and Levush should have considered this doubt whether even awakening all night the reason of entrusting soul applies, as they considered doubt of evil spirit. Rather perforce they hold Rosh is primary here — so Levush end of this siman implies. If we rule he blesses when he slept incidental sleep, it seems we come for another reason — evil spirit — but then we require specifically sixty breaths, for with less evil spirit does not dwell, as Zohar Beis Yosef brought implies. One can also say: if the reason is evil spirit, even certainly one does not bless, for we do not find blessing on matters of danger, as Rashal chapter Kol HaBasar siman 47 and Magen Avraham wrote; so Berachas Avraham page 49:15; so Rama page 165 implies. One should align Levush in the seif after where he wrote there is no main blessing, etc.; but in this seif it seems he understood: because of doubt of evil spirit one does not bless. See below.
