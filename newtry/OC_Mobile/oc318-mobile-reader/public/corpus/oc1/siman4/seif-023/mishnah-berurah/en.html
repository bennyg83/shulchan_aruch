(59) Before netilah — For stam yadayim — meaning, when he does not know whether they are soiled — they are not invalid for a blessing, and all the more so for words of Torah, for we bless on fruits without netilat yadayim:

(60) Naked — Then presumably his hands are soiled from touching the private parts. With our garments, where the neck opening is exposed, even if he slept clothed his hands are presumably soiled, for it is his way to rub his body where he sweats. This applies specifically to substantial sleep; but for casual sleep there is no presumption that he touched, and the law is as stam yadayim — fit for Torah study and for blessings, only not for prayer:

(61) He cleaned them — in bundles and with anything that cleans. The same applies to Torah: it is forbidden without cleanliness. See Peri Megadim, who wrote that even if he has water, nevertheless for blessings and Torah ordinary cleanliness suffices, as implied in the Gra's explanations; water is required only to remove the evil spirit. In Sha'at, in the name of the Zohar, he wrote that one should not bless without netilat yadayim, and it is fitting to be stringent when he has water.
