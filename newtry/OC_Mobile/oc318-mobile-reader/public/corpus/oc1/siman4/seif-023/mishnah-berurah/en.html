Before washing. For ordinary hands, meaning when he does not know whether they are dirty, are not invalid for blessing, and certainly not for words of Torah, for one recites blessings over fruits and does not need netilas yadayim.
Naked. For then presumably his hands are filthy from touching covered parts. With our clothing, where the neck opening is open, even if one slept in his clothes, presumably his hands are filthy, for his way is to scratch his body in places of sweat. This is specifically if he slept a fixed sleep, but with a temporary sleep there is no presumption that he touched, and their law is like ordinary hands, which are valid for Torah study and blessing, but not for prayer.
That he should clean them. With pebbles or anything that cleans. The same applies to Torah: it is forbidden without cleaning. See the Peri Megadim, who wrote that even if one has water, nevertheless for blessings and Torah ordinary cleaning is sufficient, and so it also implies in the Biurei HaGra. Only in order to remove ruach ra'ah do we require water. In Shaarei Teshuvah in the name of the Zohar, he wrote that one should not bless without netilas yadayim, and it is proper to be stringent when one has water. See below in siman 62, seif katan 8, in Mishnah Berurah.
