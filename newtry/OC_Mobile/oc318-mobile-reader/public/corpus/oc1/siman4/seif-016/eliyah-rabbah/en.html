Sixty breaths, etc. Lechem Chamudos wrote that the Shulchan Aruch followed the Zohar in Parashas Vayigash brought in the Beis Yosef, that sixty breaths are forbidden, but in his view we leave the words of the Zohar and follow our Gemara in chapter HaYashen, that only more than sixty breaths is forbidden. In my humble opinion this is very difficult. First, below at the beginning of siman 231 the Shulchan Aruch himself ruled like the Gemara. Further, in Divrei Chachamim he wrote in the name of Chessed LeAvraham that if one slept sixty breaths or less, he does not taste the taste of death until he sleeps more than that, and concluded that this is explicit from the Zohar in Parashas Vayigash. This is the opposite of what Lechem Chamudos testified. In truth, the Zohar brought by the Beis Yosef appears self-contradictory: it first says David did not sleep except sixty breaths, implying sixty itself is permitted; afterward it says that until sixty breaths less one, he is alive, because sixty breaths less one is the mystery of supernal life, implying sixty is forbidden. Perhaps the Shulchan Aruch and Levush hold that even this one breath is included in life, but as a safeguard they were concerned not to sleep more than sixty. Olas Tamid wondered why the Shulchan Aruch omitted the Gemara in chapter HaYashen in the name of Rav that it is forbidden to sleep by day, etc., while the Shulchan Aruch implies only a pious practice. He missed the words of the Shulchan Aruch in siman 231, where he ruled like the Gemara. Nor is there a contradiction in the Shulchan Aruch, for one may say that more than sixty is forbidden, but here he speaks up to sixty. According to this, the Gemara does not disagree with the Zohar, unlike Lechem Chamudos. One may also say that the Shulchan Aruch proved that the conclusion of the Gemara is only a pious practice from the statement about Abaye, that the master's sleep was like Rav's, etc., and from Abaye sleeping from Pumbedisa to Bei Kuvei, which Rashi explains as six parsahs. Rav Yosef's verse, how long will you lie, lazy one, was because he did not act with piety. Also, the word forbidden is no proof, as above in siman 2 regarding a married man, where the Beis Yosef explains that it is pious conduct, and as I wrote in the name of Tosafos in siman 240 regarding the prohibition of marital relations in famine years. One may further explain the Shulchan Aruch that here he speaks of sleeping at night, because of tasting the taste of death, whereas the Gemara and below siman 231 speak by day, where the prohibition is for another reason, because of neglect of Torah, as the Levush wrote there. Although the Beis Yosef implies that he understood the reason by day as by night, in Shulchan Aruch he holds as I wrote. Alternatively, he speaks because of doubt, as he ruled in the preceding seif, and I found this reason in the Agudah there as well. The Levush, however, is difficult, for here he writes the reason as tasting the taste of death, which contradicts himself. Since the Agudah wrote the reason of neglect of Torah, it appears that he holds that by day the evil spirit does not rest. This explains the Rama's gloss that in the Gemara in chapter HaYashen it implies that he was careful specifically by day. The Levush followed him and ruled to be careful specifically by day and not by night. In my humble opinion one may say that the Gemara does not disagree with the Zohar: when it says it is forbidden to sleep by day, that is the novelty, for even by day, where there is no evil spirit, it is forbidden. Alternatively, by day there is a prohibition, while by night it is only pious conduct, for the main time of Torah is by day, as below in siman 238 and Yoreh Deah siman 246. There are further ways to explain and develop this, but my way in this work is to be brief. What Magen Avraham asked against Rama, that it is not found in the Gemara that David was careful by day but only the amoraim, is not difficult, for it says the sleep of Rav, etc., was like David, and Rav speaks of day, as Rashi explains. What he further asked, that according to everyone it is forbidden by day, I have already brought that this is not according to everyone. See Eliyah Zuta, where I asked on the Levush.
