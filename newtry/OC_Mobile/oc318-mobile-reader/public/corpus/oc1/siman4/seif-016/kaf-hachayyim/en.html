16) After rubbing, do not leave your fingers bent downward. Immediately straighten your hands and fingers upward and raise them to your head, or at least opposite your face, for the face is also part of the head. Recite Al Netilas Yadayim immediately upon raising and straightening your hands, so that raising them not be purposeless. Zohar states that it is forbidden to raise one's hands empty. Keep your hands raised and straight until completing the berachah. This is why the wording is Al Netilas Yadayim, denoting elevation, as in, 'He lifted them and carried them,' rather than Al Rechitzas Yadayim, 'over washing the hands.' When raising both hands opposite the head, take care to turn the arm joints - the elbows, called cobdo in the vernacular and aks in Arabic - inward toward the body, in the mystical sense of, 'All their backs turned inward.' During the berachah, one must also open both palms like one seeking to receive something in them, alluding to receiving purity through them, as our master writes in Shaar HaKavanos and Olas Tamid. Ben Ish Chai, ibid. 4.
16) Matsas Shimurim, page 3d, writes that one who does not raise his hands after washing is included among those who disparage handwashing, etc.; see there. Siddur Beis Yaakov, laws of handwashing 4, writes that while raising them one should recite the verse, 'Raise your hands in holiness,' etc. Ruach Chaim, siman 5:2, writes that this verse should not be recited, since he has not yet recited Birchos HaTorah. This is clear; see there.
