“Not to sleep.” Some writings say not to sleep by day; see siman 231:1. Emek HaMelech cites that on Shabbos one may sleep by day—see siman 290.

“Parashas HaYashan.” This requires study: we do not find that formulation there except regarding the Amoraim, and for all opinions it is forbidden to sleep excessively by day, as stated in siman 231; but David was careful even at night, as Rabbeinu Yerucham cites the Zohar. Berachos 3a states explicitly: until midnight at night he dozed like a horse—that is sixty breaths; and what they say that he had a harp to wake him from sleep means if midnight arrived within those sixty breaths, for in truth he slept several times sixty breaths before midnight of the first night. We must say that Rema’s ruling “what difference does it make—Rabbeinu Yerucham wrote that David was careful,” etc., means only that an especially pious person may impose a stringency on himself, and that is what he means; and in the Gemara, Parashas HaYashan, etc., means that an especially pious person should not be stringent except by day.
