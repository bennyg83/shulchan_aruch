[11] Sixty breaths, etc. Lechem Chamudos wrote, and this is his language: Shulchan Aruch follows the Zohar in Parashat Vayigash that Beis Yosef brought — sixty breaths are forbidden. It seems to me we abandon the Zohar's words and follow our Gemara in chapter HaYashen, that specifically more than sixty breaths is forbidden — end quote. It appears to me this is very difficult: first, below at the beginning of siman 231 Shulchan Aruch itself ruled like the Gemara. Second, it is astonishing: in Devarim Chachamim he wrote in the name of Chesed LeAvraham that if one sleeps sixty breaths or less he does not taste the taste of death until he sleeps more than that — and he concluded so is explained from Zohar Vayigash — end quote. Did he rule in Zohar to the contrary, as Lechem Chamudos testified? In truth from the Zohar Beis Yosef brought it seems to contradict itself: it opens "David did not sleep except sixty breaths" — implying sixty itself is permitted; then it writes until sixty breaths minus one he lives, etc., because sixty breaths minus one is the secret of upper life until sixty breaths — behold sixty is forbidden. Perhaps Shulchan Aruch and Levush hold that even one breath is included in "life," but for avoidance they were concerned about this not to sleep more than sixty. In Olas Tamid he wrote: I wonder why Shulchan Aruch omitted the Gemara's words in chapter HaYashen in Rav's name that sleeping by day is forbidden, etc., and from Shulchan Aruch it implies there is only piety — end quote. You see he omitted Shulchan Aruch's words in siman 231 where he ruled like the Gemara — see there. One need not make Shulchan Aruch contradict itself: one can say more than sixty is forbidden but here it speaks until sixty — and according to this Gemara does not argue with Zohar, unlike Lechem Chamudos. One can also say Shulchan Aruch proved the Gemara's conclusion is only piety, from Abaye's words on it: Rav's sleep was like his master's, etc.; also from Abaye sleeping when entering from Pumbedita to Bei Kubi — in Rashi's explanation "six parsa" implies so. Rav Yosef called to him "Until when, lazy one, will you lie?" — because he did not practice piety. Furthermore it seems to me from the language "forbidden" there is no proof, for above in siman 2 regarding if he was married, Beis Yosef explained it is piety; and I wrote in the name of Tosafos in siman 240 regarding forbidden relations in years of famine — end quote. One can further explain Shulchan Aruch's view: here it deals with sleep at night and because one tastes the taste of death; but Gemara and below siman 231 deal with day, forbidden for another reason — bitul Torah, as Levush wrote there. Although in Beis Yosef it seems he understood the day reason like night — nevertheless Shulchan Aruch holds as I wrote. Or he speaks from doubt, as Shulchan Aruch ruled in the previous seif; and in Agudah there I found this reason. On Levush there is astonishment here: he wrote the reason because of tasting death and contradicts himself. Since Agudah wrote reason of bitul Torah, it seems he holds by day evil spirit does not dwell. Now Rama's words on Shulchan Aruch are satisfactory, and in Gemara chapter HaYashen it implies specifically by day he was careful — end quote. Levush followed him and ruled to be careful specifically by day and not at night. It appears to me one can say Gemara does not argue with Zohar in this. That which says "forbidden to sleep by day" — he states an extra matter: even by day when there is no evil spirit it is forbidden; or by day there is prohibition and at night it is only piety, since main Torah time is by day, as below siman 238 and Yoreh Deah siman 246. There is more to explain and innovate here, but my way is to shorten in this book. What Magen Avraham challenged on Rama — that in Gemara David was not found careful by day except regarding Amoraim — is no difficulty, for it says Rav's sleep, etc., like David; and Rav by day is the case, as Rashi explained. Also what he challenged "for all agree forbidden by day" — I already brought it is not all agree. See in Eliyahu Zuta what I challenged on Levush.
