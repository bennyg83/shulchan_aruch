And he should wash them without a blessing. See Mishnah Berurah 33, where I write that the proper course is to bring himself into definite obligation. This does not contradict paragraph 32, from Magen Avraham, requiring concern for Rashba's view after regular sleep, which would seemingly extend to relieving oneself, as Eliyah Rabbah 9 proves. Nor is it like the later authorities' consensus in paragraph 30, which concerns one who had not washed at all. In our case, he did not bless the first time; one may say that even according to Rashba he can bless now, as Beis Yosef writes in siman 7, with the blessing referring to the first washing. We discussed this at length above in Biur Halachah at the siman's beginning. Also know that the gloss's ruling here and in se'if 15, 'wash without a blessing,' differs from se'if 13: there uncertainty alone causes omission; here everyone agrees no blessing is required [later authorities].
