When day becomes light, etc. For one may say that the evil spirit rests until daylight. The Shelah, folio 66, wrote that one must warn the masses about this when they rise for Selichos before Rosh Hashanah and after Rosh Hashanah, that they should wash their hands when day becomes light. The Levush wrote that even if he lay down again before day and slept a temporary sleep, he need not bless when he rises and washes if he did not relieve himself, because there is no presumption that he touched soiled flesh, since it was only a temporary sleep. This implies that if he slept a fixed sleep or relieved himself, he should bless, for then presumably his hands are soiled. With this I understand the difficulty raised by Malbushei Yom Tov, that the Levush should also have said here the reason that we do not bless over a doubt, rather than because the main blessing, etc. According to what I wrote, it is clear: he teaches that the main blessing is because of soiled hands, meaning that even where the other reasons do not apply, as here where he already washed, nevertheless if he again slept a fixed sleep or relieved himself he blesses again. Still, as a matter of practice there is room for doubt, since he already washed and blessed, and it is like one who did so by day; so it implies somewhat from Nachalas Tzvi and Magen Avraham. The Levush seems to have derived it from what the Beis Yosef wrote in the name of the Rosh, which implies this. But in my humble opinion the Rosh follows his own view, for even for washing hands before Minchah or Maariv he blesses when his hands are soiled. For us, however, who do not hold so regarding other prayers, the same applies in the morning once he has already blessed.
