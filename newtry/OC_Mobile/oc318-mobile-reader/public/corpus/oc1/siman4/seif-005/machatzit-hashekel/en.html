S.k. 5, unlike, etc. As written in seif 22; see what I wrote there. That is, the Levush asked and brought proof that he blesses, since in seif 22 even where he cleans with a pebble he blesses, and this water is no worse than a pebble.
But the Levush wrote that one could say over cleanliness of the hands, as in seif 22. Magen Avraham alluded to looking at what he wrote there, that it is proper to bless over washing the hands, and all the more so here according to the Levush, where one could say over washing the hands.
And perhaps it speaks, etc. His intention is to justify the opinion that one should not bless, against the Levush's difficulty that it is no worse than a pebble. He writes that it speaks where he does not wish to pray now and is washing only to remove the evil spirit. Since they are uncertain whether these waters help remove the evil spirit, and even the pebble in seif 22 helps only for prayer, if he does not wish to pray now he should not bless. It implies that if it would help remove the evil spirit, he would bless even though one need not bless merely for removing evil spirit, as Magen Avraham wrote in s.k. 13, because he will pray later and could bless now, since some purpose is achieved by the washing, namely removing the evil spirit. But if it does not help remove the evil spirit and he does not intend to pray now, there is no present purpose in the washing, so he should not bless.
