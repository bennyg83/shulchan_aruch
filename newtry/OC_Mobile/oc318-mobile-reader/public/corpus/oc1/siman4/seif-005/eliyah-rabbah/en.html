[5] With his right hand, etc. So Bach answered the difficulty on Zohar contradicting itself: for taking the vessel one takes with right first, and pouring water is from left to right. But in Migdal David he challenged this answer and agreed with Beis Yosef's first answer: for netilat yadayim for eating one pours immediately right over left, since they are already pure and he washes only for added sanctity — and he concluded he saw great rabbis practice so; and so he practiced — end quote. It needs study: in Mahari Luria's Shulchan Aruch for eating he wrote one must pour twice on right hand and then twice on left and then rub both hands together — end quote — and it is known he follows Zohar. More astonishing: in Zohar Chadash page 40 in Megillas Ruth he wrote explicitly for eating one pours first left to right. Needs study; see responsum Be'er Eshek siman 104. Rama wrote page 165: if after pouring on his hands he pours again, from right to right — end quote — reason: right is mercy. He also wrote: immediately when the pillar of dawn rises the evil spirit departs — not until actual morning light or sunrise — end quote. I recall: it seems to me from Gemara Zevachim 19: one who sanctified hands and feet from the basin at night must sanctify by day, for overnight stay invalidates sanctification — Rashi explained: pillar of dawn causes overnight stay. Accordingly it is also possible if he washed at keri he need not wash again, as we say there 20: keri until morning does not invalidate overnight stay — see there, but one can distinguish. Mahari Luria wrote: one must spread the palms at the blessing, hinting acceptance of purity, and wash them to the joint — end quote. So Rambam wrote chapter 4 Laws of Prayer. He also wrote he practiced at dawn: take right in left hand, then left in right, three times — not three consecutive right and three consecutive left — end quote. But Knesset HaGedolah wrote in Seder HaYom: pour left to right three times, then give vessel to right hand and pour on left three times — end quote — implying three consecutive pours; this is more reasonable, for if alternating there are concerns: holding vessel where wet from water already on wet hand; and when taking vessel to pour second time water beyond the joint returns down because then he must lower hand to pour on the other. Seder HaYom wrote: all ten fingers must be upward, not as one who says to lower and pour from above at the arm side. Knesset HaGedolah in Seder HaYom: bless netilat yadayim before drying; if when he washed he saw he needs his orifices, he should not bless netilat yadayim until after leaving privy, not to multiply blessings — end quote. Apparently difficult: in any case one should not bless, as below siman 92 in prayer. One can distinguish prayer and blessing, unlike Magen Avraham's view. It seems Seder HaYom wrote this for one who says one blesses every time he relieved himself on netilat yadayim; but for us who hold in siman 7 one blesses only "Who formed" — this law applies to "Who formed." But blessing of netilat yadayim one blesses immediately, for he need not bless afterward.
