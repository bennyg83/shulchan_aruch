5) There: He should wash his hands. Although strict law requires only washing the hands, it is nevertheless a mitzvah to wash one's face as well. Shalmei Tzibbur, page 19a; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch 2:3; Ben Ish Chai, ibid. 11. Shaar HaKavanos, first discourse on the night, likewise says to wash one's face, as does Pri Etz Chaim, Shaar Tikkun Chatzos, chapter 3. Although Shaar HaKavanos there also says to wash one's feet, Pri Etz Chaim does not, nor does Shaar HaKavanos in its discourses on the morning berachos. Yafeh Shaah 2 explains that we lack the power to drive the external forces from the feet, for their hold is strongest there. The kohanim alone, by virtue of the Temple service, are told, 'Aharon and his sons shall wash their hands and their feet.' This also explains washing the feet on Erev Shabbos, for the merit of Shabbos can protect. Ben Ish Chai, end of paragraph 1, cites this. Solel Boneh 2 and Shalmei Tzibbur, page 4c, write likewise.
