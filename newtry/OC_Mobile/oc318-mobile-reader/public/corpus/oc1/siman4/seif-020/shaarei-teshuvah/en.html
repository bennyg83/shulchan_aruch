From the privy. See Baer Heitev. Machazik Berachah writes that the listed cases do not all share one reason. Rising from bed, leaving a privy, bathing, and cutting one's nails require washing because of the ruach ra'ah. Nevertheless, only one who rises from bed must wash three times into a vessel; the others do not, because the ruach ra'ah has less control in those cases. Walking among the dead, touching a corpse, and marital relations require three washings because of impurity. Removing one's shoes and touching one's head require washing for cleanliness. Maharchah Kohen writes likewise in Mekor Chayyim; see there at length. Regarding leaving the privy, we have seen elderly sages wash three times; they would also wash once after removing leather footwear. See there. I saw someone write that one who touches a flea need not wash his hands because it does not arise from human filth; see siman 316. It nevertheless appears that he should clean his hands with something that cleans, as stated below in seif 22; see there.
