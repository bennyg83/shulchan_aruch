Without a blessing. The Bach concludes that one should bless, but his proof is not compelling; see there. From the Taz and Magen Avraham as well it implies that they ruled like the Shulchan Aruch. I also found so in Sefer Birkat Avraham, folio 182. It implies that if he soiled his hand at night, even the Levush and Rama agree that he should bless. Likewise, if he slept even a temporary sleep, Nachalas Tzvi wrote that they agree he should bless, because it applies that he deposited his pledge with the Holy One, blessed be He, and it was returned to him. Indeed the Beis Yosef wrote so. But in my humble opinion one may say that the Beis Yosef wrote this only according to the Rashba, who holds that the reason for the blessing over washing the hands is because he deposited, etc.; but according to the Rosh he does not bless, since his hands are not soiled, and the Beis Yosef holds the words of the Rosh primary. The only doubt is whether the evil spirit rests on the hands when one remained awake all night. You may know this, for otherwise even one who remained awake all night should bless, as the Beis Yosef wrote that according to the Rashba's reason one blesses because the Sages did not differentiate. Further, the Shulchan Aruch and Levush should at least have counted this doubt as well, whether even when one remained awake all night the reason of having deposited his soul applies, just as they counted the doubt of evil spirit. Rather, they necessarily hold the words of the Rosh primary in this matter, and so it also implies in the Levush at the end of this siman. If we say that one should rule to bless after a temporary sleep, it seems that we come to it for another reason, because of evil spirit; but according to this it must be specifically sixty breaths, for less than this the evil spirit does not rest, as implied in the Zohar brought by the Beis Yosef. One may further say that if the reason is evil spirit, even where it is certain one does not bless, for we do not find a blessing over matters of danger, as Maharshal wrote in chapter Kol HaBasar, siman 47, and Magen Avraham. So too wrote Sefer Birkat Avraham, folios 49 and 55, and the words of Rama, folio 326, incline this way. The words of the Levush in the next seif can also be understood this way, when he wrote that the main blessing is not, etc.; but in this seif it implies that he understood that because of doubt about evil spirit one does not bless.
