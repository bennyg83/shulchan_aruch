[13] And one who removes his shoes, etc. Meaning with his hands; but Olas Tamid wrote, and this is his language: it implies even if he did not touch them — touching suffices with mere cleaning — end quote. It is difficult from what he himself wrote in siman 198 seif 4. For shoes without laces one removes after netilah for raising hands, since he need not touch them — so wrote Sheyarei Knesset HaGedolah there. Perhaps there it deals with filthy place, forbidden by law afterward to raise hands, not from evil spirit. See below siman 92 seif 7. Again I found in Makor Chaim: one removing shoes and touching his feet and rubbing his head — this is not because of evil spirit but cleanliness — end quote. One must say when removing he presumably touches the shoes. What Shulchan Aruch wrote "wonder his vessels" — meaning even if he did not find a louse. What he wrote "leaves his mind" — I heard it comes to sin, for a man sins only if a spirit of folly enters him.
