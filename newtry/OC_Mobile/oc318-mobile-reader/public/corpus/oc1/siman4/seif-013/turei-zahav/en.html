According to Rashba's reason, the aspect of becoming a new creation is absent, though perhaps he should nevertheless wash because the enactment was made without distinction. According to the Rosh's reason, the concern that he touched a soiled place is absent, and the enactment does not apply. Why, then, should the Rosh not also accept an enactment without distinction? Rashba regards the blessing as praise and thanks that the Holy One, blessed is He, returned people's souls, like the blessing over the rooster, which follows the order of the world. The Rosh, however, regards washing as necessary to prevent transgression and prayer with soiled hands; when that concern does not exist, there is no washing obligation. Because of the doubt, Shulchan Aruch and Rema rule that he should wash without a blessing.
