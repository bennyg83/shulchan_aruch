If he was awake etc. In Beit Yosef he wrote the reason of Rashba for morning netilah — that it is because one becomes like a new creature; and if one was awake all night he does not become like a new creature. And it is possible that even when awake one must wash his hands in the morning, since they enacted to wash hands in the morning — the Rabbis did not distinguish. But per the Rosh, one need not wash except because he touched an unclean place; when he did not touch, it implies they did not enact [washing], end quote. And one must say that his words — why did he not also say for the Rosh's reason "since the Rabbis did not distinguish"? And it seems specifically for Rashba, whose reason is because one must give praise and thanks for the kindness of the Holy One blessed be He who restores the souls of others who were sleeping — for example, in the blessing "who gives understanding to the rooster," as is written below regarding the custom of the world to bless; unlike the reason of the Rosh, that it is not a statute to give thanks but to save a person from transgression, that he not pray with defiled hands — and since this does not apply here, netilah is not required. This is the place of doubt; therefore Shulchan Aruch and Rama ruled here that they wash without a blessing, as above:
