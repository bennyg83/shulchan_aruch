If he remained awake, etc. See Mishnah Berurah 28. Likewise, if he slept at night wearing gloves and certainly did not touch a dirty place, according to Rosh he need not wash in the morning except to remove the evil spirit, without a blessing [Pri Megadim; Artzos HaChaim].
All night. See Mishnah Berurah 27. If he slept at the beginning of the night, arose, and washed to remove the evil spirit, it appears that according to Rashba he must wash again in the morning for prayer with a blessing. He cannot be considered to have become a new creation when he arose early at night, for it says, 'They are new every morning.' To satisfy Rosh, who would not require another morning blessing, he should relieve himself before prayer and thereby become obligated to wash and bless according to everyone.
And he should wash them without a blessing. See Mishnah Berurah 30 citing the later authorities' consensus. Rosh requires a morning blessing because the hands are unclean. Although before Minchah, siman 7:1 rules no blessing is recited even when washing unclean hands, the Sages specifically instituted obligatory morning washing with a blessing. Thus, if he relieved himself or touched a dirty place, Rosh requires washing. Rashba, who bases morning washing on becoming a new creation, also requires a blessing even from one who remained awake, under the uniform enactment; moreover, one blesses over the world's renewal even without personal benefit, as Rashba writes in Responsum 153.
