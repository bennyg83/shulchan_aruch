Seif 13, "if he was awake," etc., "to pray." The Rosh at the beginning of rule 4 wrote that the reason for morning washing is that during sleep one touches unclean places, for hands are busy, as stated in Sukkah chapter 2 and in Berakhot there: "all," etc., as it is written, "I shall wash in cleanliness," etc. Thus it is for cleanliness, and according to this here it is not needed. According to the Rashba's explanation too, at first glance it is also not needed, for "a new creature" does not apply to one who remained awake, as the Midrash says: flesh and blood gives a deposit, etc. But one may say the Rabbis did not differentiate, since according to the Rashba it is a fixed ordinance.
"And to remove," etc. Orchos Chaim wrote: if one rose before daylight, some say that when day becomes light he must wash again, for a harmful spirit rests on the hands the whole night until day. But in Zohar 184 it states explicitly that it is because of sleep, for the soul departs, etc., and so too there, 169b. This is what he wrote, "if one rose early," etc., "and to remove"; but for prayer certainly he need not, and see Magen Avraham.
