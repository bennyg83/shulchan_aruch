(27) The entire night — and if he slept part of it, according to all he must wash his hands in the morning as required — and that is when he slept a substantial sleep on his bed — because ordinary hands are busy hands and he surely touched an unclean place; but if he slept a casual sleep, per the Rosh its law in this is as if he did not sleep at all:

(28) To pray and to remove — that is, for prayer there is doubt whether the reason for morning netilat yadayim is as the Rashba [said] above in Mishna Berurah siman katan 1 — and if so, even though now he has not become a new creature, the Rabbis did not distinguish and he requires netilah — or perhaps the main reason is as the Rosh there, and upon waking if he did not touch an unclean place he does not require netilah. And to remove ruach ra'ah there is likewise doubt whether in general sleep causes ruach ra'ah — and if so, even if he sleeps by day ruach ra'ah rests upon him and he must wash his hands, and awakening at night he need not wash his hands; or the night causes ruach ra'ah and the law would be the opposite:

(29) And he shall wash them — meaning, three times out of doubt; and likewise in the two seifim that follow this:

(30) Without a blessing — some latter authorities disagree with the Rama's ruling on this and hold that one must recite a blessing, and some agree with him — and all this [applies] when he did not relieve himself; but if he relieved himself before prayer, the latter authorities agree that in this case he must wash his hands and recite a blessing. And likewise it is proper to act lekhatchilah for one who was awake all night — such as on the night of Shavuot — he should relieve himself or pour water and rub, for then according to all he becomes obligated to recite a blessing and he recites "Al Netilat Yadayim" and "Asher Yatzar":
