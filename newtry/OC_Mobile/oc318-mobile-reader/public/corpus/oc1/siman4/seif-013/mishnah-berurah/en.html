The entire night. If he slept part of it, according to all opinions he must wash his hands in the morning according to the law, provided that he slept a fixed sleep on his bed, because hands are ordinarily active and he certainly touched a filthy place. But if he slept a temporary sleep, according to the Rosh his law in this respect is as though he did not sleep at all.
For prayer and to remove. That is, regarding prayer there is a doubt whether the reason for morning netilas yadayim is like the Rashba above in Mishnah Berurah, seif katan 1, in which case, even though now he did not become a new creation, the Sages did not differentiate and he needs washing; or perhaps the primary reason is like the Rosh there, and when one was awake and did not touch a filthy place, he does not need washing. Regarding removal of ruach ra'ah there is also a doubt: perhaps generally sleep causes ruach ra'ah, and if so even one who slept during the day has ruach ra'ah rest upon him and must wash his hands, while one who was awake at night need not wash his hands; or perhaps the night causes ruach ra'ah, and then the law would be the opposite.
And he should wash them. Meaning, three times because of the doubt; and the same applies in the two following seifim.
Without a blessing. Some of the later authorities disagree with the Rama's ruling in this and hold that he must bless, while others agree with him. All this is when he did not relieve himself. But if he relieved himself before prayer, the agreement of the later authorities is that in this case he must wash his hands and bless. It is therefore proper to do so initially for one who was awake the entire night, such as on Shavuos night: he should relieve himself, or urinate and rub, for then according to all opinions he will become obligated to bless, and he should recite al netilas yadayim and Asher Yatzar.
