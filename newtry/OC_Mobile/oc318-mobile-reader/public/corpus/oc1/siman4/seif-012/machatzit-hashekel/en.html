Note 12, without a blessing. Bach wrote that he blesses because of lo plug. Namely, Rabbi Yosef Karo wrote that per Rashba, since one became a new creature when he dozed, even though he has not yet become a new creature, nevertheless perhaps because of lo plug he needs netilat yadayim and a blessing. Not so per the Rosh: where one is awake and did not touch, certainly they did not enact this. The reason, in my humble opinion, to distinguish between the Rosh's reason and Rashba's reason: granted, per Rashba, morning netilat yadayim is a new enactment because one became a new creature; if so, perhaps even if he did not become a new creature, because of lo plug he needs netilat yadayim and a blessing. Not so per the Rosh — lest he touched an unclean place; this is not an enactment in morning netilat yadayim, for even for mincha prayer, if he attended to his needs, he needs netilat yadayim and a blessing. If so, why is morning [different]? Even if he is awake and did not touch, he would still need netilat yadayim and a blessing because of lo plug. If so, all day and at mincha prayer, even if he did not attend to his needs, he would need netilat yadayim and a blessing — and this certainly does not exist (and see what Taz wrote). Bach divided that therefore even for the Rosh, because of lo plug, netilat yadayim and a blessing are required, for they asked the Rosh: one who rises before daybreak to study — does he need netilat yadayim and a blessing? He answered that he needs netilat yadayim and a blessing. If so, the Rosh's words contradict one another, for the Rosh holds that if he attended to his needs and afterward wishes to study, he need not bless — except that "one who rises early to study" is not precise, but the action was thus; the same applies if he stood to do his work — the Rosh also holds he needs netilat yadayim and a blessing. The reason is that since if he stands and wishes to pray immediately he needs netilat yadayim and a blessing because of prayer, therefore even if he stands and does not yet wish to pray, he nevertheless needs netilat yadayim and a blessing because of lo plug. And since we say lo plug, therefore even if he is awake — he certainly did not touch — nevertheless he needs netilat yadayim and a blessing because of lo plug. Nevertheless, this is not so strong a proof for the Rosh, etc. — namely, for the Rosh one never says lo plug; and that he ruled regarding one who rises early to study that he blesses — the reason is not because of study, as Bach wrote, not precisely, but because he will pray and then need to bless; therefore he blesses immediately now. But for one who is awake, even if he prayed he does not bless, for he certainly did not touch and there is no doubt here. That Rabbi Yosef Karo was uncertain whether to bless is because for us the main reason is Rashba, as he wrote in note 1 — that one becomes a new creature; and although this is only while sleeping, nevertheless netilat yadayim and a blessing are required because of lo plug.
