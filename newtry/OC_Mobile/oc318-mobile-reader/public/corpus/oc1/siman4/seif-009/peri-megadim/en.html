If — Taz — the place of doubt to remove ruach ra'ah is whether the reason is the Zohar or the reason of Orach Chayyim. And there is doubt regarding prayer — whether the reason is the Rashba and "we do not distinguish," or the reason of the Rosh. And the Levush [held] lefi peshuto in this case (see Be'er HaGolah, letter gimel; also as the Rosh holds) that he did not touch [an unclean place] and he holds that for the Rashba the Beit Yosef's view applies — even though he wrote "and possibly thus it is" that lefi peshuto [it applies] — and there is doubt perhaps the halacha follows the Rosh, and he follows his view in note 1 and siman 7 note 1; even for us the reason of the Rosh applies. And what was written for the Rashba — lefi peshuto it is not for removing [ruach ra'ah] — even for the Rashba the reason is also that one touched an unclean place or [has] ordinary hands; and that they did not enact [a blessing] in Minchah prayer — for here, because one becomes a new creature, he needs [washing] like a kohen [needs] a vessel like a kiyyor — therefore they enacted a blessing; whereas every day, cleansing with dirt suffices. And see Magen Avraham below note 25 — he holds one can say they enacted to bless also for the reason of new creature alone: and you see that therefore even one who slept casually, where we are not concerned he touched an unclean place — for the Rashba, [with] a new creature, even so he should not bless, lest the halacha follows the Rosh. However if he relieved himself and slept casually, according to all he blesses; and even though for us in seif 7 [it applies] the entire day — nevertheless you find such a case, and certainly lefi peshuto, or that most times [hands] are not clean, as the Taz in siman 7 note 1 (and likewise on Shavuot night, if he slept casually he should relieve himself and bless Al Netilat Yadayim — see siman 494). But for Magen Avraham notes 1 and 2 — he rejected the reason of the Rosh for us: even one who slept casually and did not relieve himself blesses, and certainly Al Netilat Yadayim.
