[9] When day breaks, etc. One can say evil spirit dwells until daybreak. Shelah page 66 wrote they must warn the multitude when standing for selichos before and after Rosh Hashanah to wash hands when day breaks. Levush wrote: even if he returned and lay down before day and slept incidental sleep, he need not bless when he rises and washes if he did not relieve himself — no presumption he touched soiled flesh, since it was incidental sleep — end quote. It implies: if he slept fixed sleep or relieved himself he blesses, for then presumably hands are soiled. This satisfies what Levush on Yom Tov clothing challenged: Levush should have said here too the reason is one does not bless on doubt, not because there is no main blessing, etc. — end quote. According to what I wrote it is satisfactory: it implies to us the main blessing is because of soiled hands — meaning even if other reasons do not apply, as here he already washed. Nevertheless when he returned and slept fixed sleep or relieved himself he returns and blesses. For actual law there is room to doubt: since he already washed and blessed it is as if he did so by day — so Nachalas Tzvi and Magen Avraham imply somewhat. Levush seems derived from Beis Yosef in Rosh's name implying so. It appears to me Rosh has his reason: even for netilat yadayim for Minchah or Maariv he blesses when hands are soiled; but for us who do not hold thus for other prayers, the same applies at dawn since he already blessed.
