Paragraph 12. And he proved this from Responsa Rosh. Beis Yosef's doubt applies only according to Rashba's reason, as Taz 9 writes. Bach proves that even Rosh accepts a uniform enactment: Responsa Rosh says one who rises early to study blesses Al Netilas Yadayim, whereas one who studies by day and needs to wash does not bless even according to Rosh; thus morning has a uniform rule. Magen Avraham rejects the proof: the early riser will eventually pray when day arrives, so he blesses. Although before Minchah one blesses only when praying immediately, Beis Yosef agrees that in the morning the rule is uniform and one blesses even when not praying immediately, as the Mechaber in se'if 1 implies according to Magen Avraham 5. But we have not heard that a uniform rule applies where washing is never intrinsically required; Beis Yosef's doubt therefore remains for one who stayed awake. Magen Avraham's conclusion, 'but according to us,' is not superfluous: although Rosh has some basis for uniformity, according to our principal reason of becoming a new creation there is greater room to say the rule is always uniform.
