{Rama: Note 12.} And he proved this from the responsum of the Rosh — namely, that Beit Yosef is in doubt only for Rashba's reason, as written in Taz, note 9. Bach proved that even per the Rosh one does not disagree, since the responsum of the Rosh states: one who rises early to study blesses al netilat yadayim; but one who studies during the day and needs to wash his hands does not bless, even per the Rosh, except in the morning because of lo plug. Magen Avraham rejected this: there it is different, since he will pray when the day arrives; therefore he blesses. And although at mincha he does not bless unless he prays immediately, nevertheless for this matter Beit Yosef also concedes that we say in the morning lo plug — even if he does not pray immediately he blesses, as is evident in the author, seif 1, according to Magen Avraham, note 5 — see there. But that we should say lo plug where by law one never needs any netilat yadayim at any time — we never heard of this; therefore in one who is awake, Beit Yosef's doubt remains. From this you will see that what Magen Avraham concludes, "but for us," etc., is seemingly superfluous, for even for the Rosh's reason Bach's proof is refuted, as stated. Rather, Magen Avraham teaches that although for the Rosh there is reason to say lo plug, as stated, nevertheless for us, whose reason is beriah chadashah, there is room to say that one never says lo plug — delve into it.
