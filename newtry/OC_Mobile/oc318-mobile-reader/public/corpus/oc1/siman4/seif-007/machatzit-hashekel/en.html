S.k. 7, a vessel, etc. But on chips of wood it is forbidden. See siman 181, seif 2: final waters, although they are not poured on the ground because an evil spirit rests on them, may nevertheless be poured on chips of wood. There the reason is that the washing is because of Sodomite salt or another reason, and thus the water itself does not yet have an evil spirit upon it through the washing; only when it reaches the ground does the evil spirit rest on it. Since something separates between the water and the ground, the evil spirit has no permission to rest upon it, as Magen Avraham writes below, and therefore it is permitted on chips. Here, however, the evil spirit rests on the hands, and through washing it is removed from the hands and rests on the washing water. If so, what help are chips of wood? Although there is a separation between them and the ground, the evil spirit already rests on the water. Therefore a vessel is needed into which to wash, and it should be poured where people do not pass. For this reason the Beis Yosef wrote in the name of the Zohar that one should not give those waters to sorceresses, and the Bach wrote that even to any person asking for those waters one should not give them, for certainly his intent is for sorcery.
