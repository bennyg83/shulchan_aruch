From someone. See Taz. Regarding a non-Jew, one may say that he does not receive impurity; according to the reason of the Zohar, whoever sleeps, etc., the proof is from non-Jewish graves according to the opinion that even by touch they do not convey impurity. In truth, the world is not careful about this. But women should be very careful, since they prepare foods, lest they make them impure with their hands; see Taz. I also do not know why people are not careful with minors, and it is proper to wash their hands every morning, for danger is more severe than prohibition. Regarding what he wrote about washing hands for a meal, Eliyah Rabbah s.k. 7 challenged it, and so wrote Shelah and Seder HaYom, that for washing hands for a meal, which is only for added sanctity, one may wash from someone who has not washed. So it also implies in siman 159, seif 11 and seif 12; see there in the Taz, though there is not such a strong proof from there.
