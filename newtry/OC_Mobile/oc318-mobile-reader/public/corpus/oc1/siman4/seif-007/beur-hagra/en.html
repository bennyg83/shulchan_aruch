Seif 7, "it is good to be particular," etc. From what is said in chapter 25: one may wash his hands in the morning and stipulate about them for the whole day. Since it says "in the morning," it implies that with that washing taken for prayer he stipulates; thus he washes according to the laws of washing. And in Berakhot there: "he should wash his hands," etc.; similarly they said there: whoever relieves himself and washes his hands. It does not say "rinses," as it says, "a person rinses his face," etc., and in many places; thus proper netilah is required. The reason is like a priest sanctifying himself to serve; this is the correct text.
"However," etc. Meaning after the fact; but initially one must be particular, as above. So wrote the Beis Yosef in the name of the Rada, that human force is required, and in the name of Zohar 1:184b, "and one vessel from above," etc. But all the decisors hold that this is not required, as above in seif 1; only the Rashba requires it. Nevertheless it does not invalidate after the fact, for it is no worse than a pebble, etc., as above. This is what "however," etc. means: although initially one should be careful, as the Zohar and Rada wrote, and as the Shulchan Aruch wrote, "it is good to be particular," according to the Raah in seif 1 he should not recite the blessing, and initially it is invalidating; for the case of the West refers to one without water, and Rabbenu Chananel cursed at the time of prayer, and all the more according to the reading, "but for prayer he must seek it."
