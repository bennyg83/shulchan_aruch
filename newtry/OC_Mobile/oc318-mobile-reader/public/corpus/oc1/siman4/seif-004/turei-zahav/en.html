The place of bloodletting. Shulchan Aruch implies that even after one has washed in the morning, these restrictions apply throughout the day. This follows Beis Yosef. The Gemara warns against bringing the hand to the eye and the other listed organs. Rashi explains that these warnings apply before the morning washing, except for touching the genital organ and the anus, which is forbidden throughout the day because it may cause an emission or harm the hemorrhoids. Beis Yosef infers that the other warnings involve injury, whereas those two do not, and accordingly extends the warnings concerning a beer cask and bloodletting throughout the day. This is difficult: why did Rashi omit the beer-related matters, which the Gemara mentions before the genital organ? It appears that all the listed dangers apply only before the morning washing. Rashi singled out the genital organ and anus because their all-day prohibition is known elsewhere, in Shabbos 81. A hand inserted into a beer cask may cause blindness or deafness because the beer will later be drunk and reach the mouth and nose. Beis Yosef and Shulchan Aruch's statement that it merely spoils the beer is implausible in light of the severity of "let his hand be cut off." The Tur omitted these cases because he listed the common daily concerns, not every danger. Bach agrees with this explanation.
