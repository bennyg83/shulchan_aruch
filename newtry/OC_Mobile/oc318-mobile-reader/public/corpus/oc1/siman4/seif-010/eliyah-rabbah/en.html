But Migdal David challenged this answer and agreed to the first answer of the Beis Yosef, that for washing hands for eating one pours from the right hand onto the left, since they are already pure and he washes only for added sanctity; he concluded that he saw great teachers practice so, and he himself practiced so. This requires study, for in the Shulchan Aruch of Mahari Luria he wrote regarding washing hands for eating that one must pour twice on the right hand and afterward twice on the left, and afterward rub both hands together, and it is known that he follows the Zohar. I am even more surprised, for in Zohar Chadash, folio 40 in Megillas Rus, it says explicitly regarding washing hands for eating that he should pour first from left to right. This requires study; see Responsa Be'er Esek, siman 104. Rama wrote on folio 325 that if another person pours on his hands, he should pour from right to right, for the right is mercy. He also wrote that immediately when dawn rises the evil spirit departs, not only at full daylight or sunrise. A support for this seems to me from Zevachim 19: if one sanctified his hands and feet from the laver at night he must sanctify again by day, for staying overnight invalidates sanctification; Rashi explains that dawn creates the overnight status. According to this, perhaps if one washed at cockcrow he need not wash again, as stated there, folio 20, that from cockcrow until morning overnight status does not invalidate; see there, though one may distinguish. Mahari Luria wrote that one must open the palms at the time of the blessing, as a hint to receiving purity, and wash them until the joint. So wrote the Rambam, chapter 4 of Hilchos Tefillah. He also wrote that in the morning he would take the right hand with his left hand and then the left hand with his right hand, doing this three times, and did not wash the right three consecutive times and the left three consecutive times. But Knesses HaGedolah wrote in the name of Seder HaYom that one pours from the left onto the right three times and then gives the vessel to the right hand and pours onto the left three times. It is clear that they wash three consecutive times, and this is more reasonable, for if one alternates there are several concerns: he may grasp the vessel at a wet place that he already held with his wet hand, and when taking the vessel to pour on the other hand a second time, the water that went beyond the joint may return and descend, since he must lower his hand to pour on the other. Seder HaYom wrote that all ten fingers should point upward, unlike one who says they should be lowered and water poured over them from above from the arm side. Knesses HaGedolah wrote in the name of Seder HaYom that one blesses over washing the hands before drying. If after washing he sees that he needs to relieve himself, he should not bless over washing the hands until after leaving the privy, so as not to multiply blessings. At first glance this is difficult, for in any event one should not bless then, as below in siman 92 regarding prayer. Perhaps one can distinguish between prayer and a blessing, unlike the reasoning of Magen Avraham. It seems that Seder HaYom wrote this according to the opinion that one blesses over washing the hands every time he relieves himself. But for us, who hold in siman 7 that one blesses only Asher Yatzar, this ruling applies to Asher Yatzar. The blessing over washing the hands, however, should be recited immediately, for he will not need to bless it afterward.
