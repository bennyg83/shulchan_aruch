{Rama: Magen Avraham, note 6.} He explains be'di'eved. He wanted this because Rama's wording can be mistaken: since he wrote "however it does not prevent — not a vessel," etc., it seems the author is not dealing with a vessel; if so, the explanation in Rama is as follows — because the author wrote "it is good to be stringent," namely lekhatchilah; on this Rama wrote "however, a vessel and human force do not prevent even lekhatchilah," and the author deals only with water invalid on their own account, for regarding that one must be stringent lekhatchilah — so it should be interpreted. But some say that if so, how did he bring in seif 1 an opinion not to bless — where do we find this, since even regarding invalidity in the water itself there is only "it is good to be stringent"? Therefore Magen Avraham explains Rama's words as if he wrote "however be'di'eved it does not prevent"; and indeed the author also deals with vessel and human force, where the invalidity is not in the water itself — there is only "it is good to be stringent"; but regarding invalidity in the water themselves, there is an opinion that one does not bless — and this is what Magen Avraham concludes, "see above seif 1":
