Magen Avraham, s.k. 6 Meaning after the fact. He intends this because the language of the Rama could be misunderstood: since he wrote, however a vessel, etc., is not indispensable, it might imply that the Mechaber did not speak of a vessel, and that the Rama means it is not indispensable even lechatchilah. Since the Mechaber wrote that it is good to be careful, the Rama would be saying that a vessel and human force do not prevent even lechatchilah, and that the Mechaber spoke only of water invalid in itself, where one must be careful lechatchilah. This is not correct, for then how did he bring in seif 1 an opinion not to bless? Where do we find such a thing if even water invalid in itself is only something good to be careful about? Therefore Magen Avraham explained the Rama as though he wrote that after the fact it does not invalidate. In truth the Mechaber too speaks of a vessel and human force, where the invalidity is not in the water itself; there it is only good to be careful. But invalidity in the water itself is where there is an opinion that one does not bless. This is why Magen Avraham concludes by referring to what he wrote in seif 1.
