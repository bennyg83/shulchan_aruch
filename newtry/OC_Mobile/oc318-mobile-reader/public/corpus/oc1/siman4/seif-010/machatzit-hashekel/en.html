S.k. 10, three waters, and the same applies in a river, etc. The reason for three changed waters is that if one shakes his hands in one vessel three times it certainly does not help, because at the first shaking the water already became impure, and the later shaking does not help. This is not so with three changed waters in three vessels, where that concern does not apply. Nevertheless, he is uncertain lest the evil spirit is removed only when the water comes by pouring and not by shaking. If so, the same applies in a flowing river: even if he shakes his hands in one place in the river, it has the law of three changed waters, because the first water passes away. In any event, one could also have said that a river, being attached to the ground, is not subject to the water becoming impure through the first shaking, for attached water does not receive impurity.
It is obvious, etc. For it is no worse than a pebble; this is specifically for prayer, since a pebble too helps only regarding prayer.
And see siman 159, as it should read, unlike Olas Tamid, and siman 160, seif 12. That is, Olas Tamid wrote that one who immerses his hands in water gathered in the ground, if he immersed three times, it helps even to remove the evil spirit; however, if it does not have a mikveh measure, meaning forty se'ah, there is room for doubt. If he inserted his hands into snow in three different places, and it is spread on the ground, this helps in pressing circumstances. Eliyah Rabbah understood that Magen Avraham meant to challenge Olas Tamid by saying first that it obviously does not require forty se'ah because it is no worse than a pebble, while Olas Tamid was uncertain without forty se'ah. Eliyah Rabbah wrote that this is no challenge, for a pebble helps only for prayer, whereas Olas Tamid speaks of removing evil spirit. In my humble opinion this is not Magen Avraham's intent. Rather, his words, see siman 159, unlike Olas Tamid, are a separate matter and do not refer to what preceded. Magen Avraham means that according to Olas Tamid, who holds that immersion helps remove evil spirit like pouring, for immersion is superior to washing in every place and so we hold also regarding washing hands for eating, there is no room for doubt with water gathered in the ground that is less than forty se'ah. This is subject to a dispute of the poskim below in siman 159, seif 14, and the Beis Yosef there ruled leniently; only where possible did the Rama write to be stringent. He also marked to see siman 160, seif 12, referring to what Olas Tamid wrote that snow spread on the ground helps. There he alluded to what Magen Avraham wrote in s.k. 16, whether the snow must be melted and crushed. But on the core law of Olas Tamid, who was certain that immersion helps remove evil spirit, Eliyah Rabbah disagrees, for perhaps pouring specifically is required, as the Beis Yosef wrote, and immersion does not help.
