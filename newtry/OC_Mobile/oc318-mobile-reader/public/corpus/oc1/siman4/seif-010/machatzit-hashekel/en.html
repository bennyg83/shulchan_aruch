(Note 10) Three waters, and the same applies in a river, etc. For the reason for three exchanged waters is that if one rinses in one vessel three times, it certainly does not help, for with the first rinsing the water is already defiled, and then the later rinsing does not help — not so with three exchanged waters in three vessels, where this is not so. Nevertheless he was uncertain whether perhaps ruach raah departs only when the water comes by pouring and not by rinsing; if so, the same applies in a flowing river: even if one rinses in one place in the river, it has the law of three exchanged waters, for the first passes and the next comes. In any case, the Maggid Mishneh wrote that in a river connected to the ground, the water need not become defiled by one rinsing, for connected water does not accept tumah.

Plainly, etc. That it does not detract from a bundle — and this is specifically for prayer, for a bundle too is effective only for prayer.

And the matter is siman 159 (as it should read), not like Eshel Tov, and siman 160, seif 12 — namely, Eshel Tov wrote, and this is his language: "However, one who immerses his hands in water collected in the ground — if he immerses three times it helps also to remove ruach raah, etc.; however, if they do not contain a mikveh measure (namely, forty seah), one should be uncertain, etc.; and if he inserts his hands into snow in three separate places while it lies flat on the ground, it helps in a time of need," etc., end quote. In Eliyah Rabbah he understood that Magen Avraham's intention was to challenge Eshel Tov in what he wrote first, and plainly there is no forty seah that does not detract from a bundle, and Eshel Tov was uncertain when they lack forty seah; on this Eliyah Rabbah wrote that there is no challenge from this, for a bundle is effective only for prayer, and Eshel Tov deals with removing ruach raah. But in my humble opinion this is not Magen Avraham's intention. Rather, what he wrote, "and the matter is siman 159, not like Eshel Tov," is a separate matter and does not refer to the above; Magen Avraham's intention is that according to Eshel Tov, immersion helps to remove ruach raah like pouring, for immersion is preferable in every respect to netilat yadayim, and as we hold, also for netilat yadayim for eating. If so, one need not be uncertain about water collected in the ground when they are collected in the ground and are less than forty seah, for there is a dispute among the poskim below, siman 159, seif 14, and Rabbi Yosef Karo ruled there leniently — except where possible, Rama wrote there that one should be stringent, and he also directed one to look at siman 160, seif 12 — namely, what Eshel Tov wrote, that snow spread on the ground helps; on this he hinted to look at what Magen Avraham wrote there, note 16, whether one requires the snow to be melted and crushed. But regarding the body of Eshel Tov's law, where it is clear to him that immersion helps to remove ruach raah, Eliyah Rabbah disagrees, for perhaps we require specifically pouring, as Rabbi Yosef Karo wrote, and immersion does not help.
