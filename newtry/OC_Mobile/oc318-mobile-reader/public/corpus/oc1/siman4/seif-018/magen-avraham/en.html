“With water.” Not with something that merely cleans (Mordechai), and three pourings are not required (Seder Hayom).
“From the privy.” He should wash immediately as in the morning [there, same siman].
“From his shoes.” Meaning with his hands.
“Among the dead.” That is, the cemetery (Maharil responsum 23). He wrote that one should wash before praying at graves; and they wash again when returning to the synagogue courtyard, for demons accompany those who return, and one should wash the face as well—see there.
“Who touched a corpse.” This implies that if he merely went to one dead person he need not wash; nevertheless the custom is to wash even upon entering where one dead lies, and the same for those who escort him.
“Who removes his garments.” Even if he did not touch the louse he did not find.
