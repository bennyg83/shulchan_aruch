(38) They require netilah — to remove ruach ra'ah that rests thereby; therefore he should hasten to wash immediately. And netilah should be until the joint, and at least until the end of the finger joints; but if his hand became soiled with mud and mire, for which there is no concern of ruach ra'ah, he need only wipe the soiled place alone and that suffices. And regarding prayer it will be explained below in siman 92 — see Biur Halacha there:

(39) In water — even though for prayer, and all the more so for Torah, after all these [cases] ordinary cleanliness suffices, as below in seif 22 which speaks of rising from bed, and all the more so in the others; nevertheless to remove ruach ra'ah one specifically requires water — see below in siman katan 41. And one does not need three times except upon rising from bed; and some are stringent regarding one who walks among the dead and attends to a sickbed, for they require likewise three times. And in Sefer Heichal HaKodesh they are stringent regarding one who exits the privy three times; and in Magen Avraham siman 7 he contradicted his words:

(40) And one who exits the privy — even if he did not relieve himself; and likewise from the bathhouse even if he did not bathe there — because of ruach ra'ah that dwells in these places:

(41) From his shoes — because he touches his hands when removing them; but if he did not touch them, he does not require netilah; and one who removes his shoes and touches his feet and covers his head — this is not because of ruach ra'ah but only because of cleanliness; therefore he need not hasten to wash his hands immediately, unlike in the others which are because of ruach ra'ah, as above in siman katan 38:

(42) Among the dead — in the cemetery; and Maharil siman 23 wrote that one should wash before praying at the graves, and they wash a second time upon returning to the synagogue courtyard, for evil spirits accompany those who return. And some are accustomed then to wash the face as well:

(43) At a corpse — and even one who entered [a room] with a single dead person, or who went to watch over him, practice netilah. And they were accustomed to be careful if a person entered another house before he bathed; and the custom of our fathers is Torah [Rama Yoreh De'ah 376]:

(44) His garments — even if he did not touch a louse he did not find [on himself]:

(45) On a louse — and on a flea perhaps it suffices with ordinary cleanliness:

(46) On his hand — in unclean places where there are traces of sweat:

(47) When he loses his presence of mind — see in Eliyah Rabbah who explains meaning that a spirit of folly clothed him, and thereby he may later come to transgression; and as they said: a person does not commit a transgression unless a spirit of folly enters him:
