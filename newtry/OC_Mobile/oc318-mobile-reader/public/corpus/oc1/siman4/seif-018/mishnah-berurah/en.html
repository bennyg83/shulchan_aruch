Require washing. To remove the ruach ra'ah that rests because of this; therefore he should hurry to wash immediately. The washing should be until the joint, and at least until the end of the finger joints. But if his hand became dirty with mud or mire, where there is no issue of ruach ra'ah, he need only wipe the dirty place, and that is enough. Regarding prayer, this will be explained below in siman 92; see there in Biur Halacha.
With water. Although for prayer, and certainly for Torah, after all these things ordinary cleaning is enough, as below in seif 22, which discusses one who rose from bed, and certainly regarding the others, nevertheless to remove ruach ra'ah one specifically needs water. See below, seif katan 41. Three times is necessary only when one rises from bed. Some are stringent regarding one who walks among the dead and one who had marital relations, requiring three times there as well. In Sefer Heichal HaKodesh he is stringent that one who leaves the privy should wash three times, but in Magen Avraham, siman 7, he contradicted his words.
And one who leaves the privy. Even if he did not relieve himself; and likewise from the bathhouse, even if he did not bathe there, because of ruach ra'ah that rests in these places.
His shoes. Because he touches them with his hands when removing them. But if he did not touch them, he does not need washing. One who removes his shoes and touches his feet, and one who rubs his head, is not because of ruach ra'ah but only because of cleanliness; therefore he does not need to hurry to wash his hands immediately, unlike the others, which are because of ruach ra'ah, as above in seif katan 38.
Among the dead. In a cemetery. The Maharil, siman 23, wrote that one should wash before praying at the graves, and they wash a second time upon returning to the courtyard of the cemetery, because harmful spirits accompany those who return. Some then also wash the face.
With a corpse. Even one who entered where there was a corpse, or who went to escort it, has the practice to wash. They were accustomed to be particular that a person not enter another house before he washes, and the custom of our fathers is Torah; Rama, Yoreh Deah 376.
His garments. Even if he did not touch a louse, for he did not find one.
With a louse. With a flea, it is possible that ordinary cleaning is sufficient.
With his hand. In the filthy places that have pellets of sweat.
He loses his mind. See Eliyah Rabbah, that this means a spirit of foolishness clothes him, and through this he can later come to sin, as they said: a person does not commit a transgression unless a spirit of foolishness enters him.
