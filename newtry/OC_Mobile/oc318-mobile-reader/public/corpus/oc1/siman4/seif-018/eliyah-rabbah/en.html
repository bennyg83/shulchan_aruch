With water, etc. Specifically water, not mere cleaning. Seder HaYom wrote that one need not pour three times except when rising from bed. Rama wrote that one should be careful lechatchilah in all the stringencies practiced for washing hands for eating in all these matters, and not like those who wash little by little; however, after the fact it does not invalidate. But Sefer Mekor Chayim wrote that one who leaves the privy, although an evil spirit rests on him, nevertheless it is not a princess and does not require a vessel; likewise one who walks among the dead needs three times but not a vessel, and similarly one who had marital relations. But one who leaves a bathhouse or cuts his nails need not wash three times. In Magen Avraham, siman 7, he inclines that even after the privy three times is not required, and so it implies in siman 165. Seder HaYom wrote that one must wash his hands as quickly as possible, in order to remove from himself the power of that impurity and withdraw from it. See below, siman 240, concerning these laws.
And one who removes his shoes, etc. This means with his hands. But Olas Tamid wrote that it implies even if he did not touch them, and that if he did touch them ordinary cleaning is sufficient. This is difficult from what he himself wrote in siman 198, seif 4. With shoes that have no tying, they may be removed after washing for duchening, since one need not touch them; so wrote Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah there. Perhaps there he speaks of the law of a soiled place, which according to the law forbids him afterward to raise his hands, and not of evil spirit. See below, siman 92, seif 6. I later found in Sefer Mekor Chayim that one who removes his shoes, touches his feet, or rubs his head, this is not because of evil spirit but because of cleanliness. One must say that when removing shoes he presumably touches them. What the Shulchan Aruch wrote regarding searching his garments means even if he did not find a louse. What he wrote that he loses his mind, I heard that it means he comes to sin, for a person does not sin unless a spirit of folly enters him.
