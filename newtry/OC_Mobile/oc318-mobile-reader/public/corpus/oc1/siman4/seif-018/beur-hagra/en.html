Seif 18, "with water." The Mordechai is uncertain about this and wrote that one should act stringently because of possible danger, as stated in Chullin chapter 1; and in Avodah Zarah chapter 2 they said, "answers are not given for danger."
"One who rises." Shabbos there, and the Zohar there and there.
"And one who leaves the privy." Gemara, Bava Kamma chapter 1, and Sukkah chapter 4, 46a; and Zohar there, 10b. There it implies that one who slept even by day must wash, for it says: if so, by day, when he does not sleep, etc.
"And one who cuts his nails." Pesachim, chapter Arvei Pesachim.
"And one who removes his shoes." They learned it from the priestly blessing, in accordance with the Rambam, who wrote there that the washing is because of impurity of the hands. See Beis Yosef, siman 128. What the Gemara says, "as it is stated: lift your hands," etc., means that they must be clean at the time of the blessing.
"And some say also," etc. As in the morning, because of evil spirit, as stated in Chagigah chapter 1 and in Sanhedrin, chapter Four Deaths: "one who consults the dead" means one who sleeps in a cemetery in order, etc. See Magen Avraham and Maharil, section 23.
"And one who has marital relations." In Sukkah chapter 2: he forgot and had relations, etc.
"And one who touches a louse, and one who touches," etc. According to the Rosh, for this reason they instituted washing in the morning, and also for terumah in Shabbos chapter 1. So too all those listed are places of filth and are included in what was said: hands are busy. See Rashi, Nazir 59a, gloss beginning "i de'amar" and gloss beginning "amar lei," etc. This is what he wrote in seif 21: "one must," etc.
"And one who did one," etc. In the baraisa of Maaseh Torah of Rabbi: eight things, etc. There are three categories there; see Kol Bo and Rabbenu Chananel.
