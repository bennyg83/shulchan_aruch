Particles of sweat. See Taz. This refers specifically to covered places of the body. The humble writer says: because the ways of wisdom are hidden from me, I will briefly investigate several questions relevant to this siman and other simanim. There are six washings for added sanctity: meal, morning, Minchah, Shema, Torah study, and dipping food into liquid. Washing for a meal is explained in simanim 159 and 160, including what invalidates and what is proper lechatchilah; see there. For morning washing, lechatchilah one should be careful about all matters that invalidate washing for a meal, as the Mechaber wrote here in seif 7. When I say matters that invalidate, it may be that what is only lechatchilah there, such as in siman 159, seif 8 and seif 13, is not needed here for prayer; for example, shaking in a vessel, which according to the Rashba himself is lechatchilah here, while there it is acceptable. Regarding intent, one may say that here intent is not required, as we will say below with the help of Heaven. Know that if rabbinic mitzvos also require intent, then for non-sacred slaughter, which in Yoreh Deah does not require intent; washing for non-sacred food in siman 159, seif 13, which does not require intent; and a niddah immersing for her husband, which does not require intent, although these are Torah laws, one must necessarily say that any mitzvah from which one cannot exempt himself requires the intent of the one performing it, whereas slaughter, immersion, and the like are different, for he need not slaughter and eat, or immerse and have relations. In those, intent is not needed. If so, morning washing, since one cannot be without washing and prayer, might require intent. However, Magen Avraham in siman 60, s.k. 3, brought several opinions that D'Rabbanan mitzvos do not require intent; if so, according to everyone morning washing does not require intent. But water is required for two reasons: first because of evil spirit, and second because for prayer we require water specifically when possible. Minchah is explained in siman 233; see there, that if he has no water he cleans with a pebble. See siman 7 there, s.k. 2, and siman 92, s.k. 1, where he holds that the rulings of the Mechaber contradict one another; with the help of Heaven this will be explained there. Regarding Shema it appears that according to everyone one need not go back four mil to find water; only for prayer must one go back. See Beis Yosef, simanim 92 and 98, from Berachos 15a: these words apply to Shema, etc. The reason for the distinction is that Shema is Torah law while prayer is D'Rabbanan; see Kesef Mishneh, chapter 3 of Hilchos Shema, halachah 1, and chapter 4 of Hilchos Tefillah, halachah 2. I do not know, for according to the Rambam prayer is according to the Torah at least once; how can he distinguish between Shema and prayer, and not within prayer itself between one prayer and a second? See chapter 1 of Hilchos Tefillah and the view of the Shulchan Aruch in siman 106, as Magen Avraham wrote there, that prayer is according to the Torah. With this one may say that Minchah is D'Rabbanan, Shacharis is according to the Torah, and Maariv is optional; therefore they were stricter regarding Shacharis. There is another reason to distinguish Shema: its time is brief, three hours, and afterward he is only like one reading in the Torah, whereas prayer has four hours and afterward at least there is reward for prayer; see Rashi, Berachos 15a. In our novellae we wrote that according to this, all the more so for evening Shema, whose time is all night according to the Torah, and even if he passed midnight he fulfills the mitzvah of Shema, it is superior to prayer, where there is only reward for prayer not in its time, and it is obvious that he must go back after water. See Kesef Mishneh, chapter 1 of Hilchos Shema, regarding what he wrote that according to the Torah its time is all day; see chapter 1 of Hilchos Shema, halachah 13. For all these, Shema, prayer, and a meal, even ordinary unattended hands invalidate, as explained in siman 93, and it appears the same for Shema. But for Torah study, ordinary hands are fit for Torah study, for a blessing, and for mentioning the Name, as Magen Avraham wrote in siman 92, s.k. 4. There is also a distinction: for Torah study and blessings, according to everyone cleaning with earth is enough lechatchilah and water is not needed. Dipping into liquid is explained in siman 158, seif 4, that it requires washing without a blessing. The Taz there, s.k. 6, implies that it is only for cleanliness and not because of a connection to terumah. According to this its law is like other blessings: with filth, cleaning with earth is enough lechatchilah, and the same here with ordinary hands. But one who examines the Beis Yosef and Levush there will see many reasons because of a connection to terumah or because liquids are prone to impurity; if so, certainly cleaning is not enough even for ordinary hands, but specifically water is required, and one does not bless. There are two kinds of dirt. One is grime in covered places and those matters listed here in seif 18; for this, specifically water is needed because of evil spirit, although for Shema and prayer, if he has none, earth is enough, and for Torah study and blessings earth is also enough lechatchilah. But dirt from mud or mire, which is not repulsive grime and has no evil spirit, for Torah study and blessings earth or anything that cleans is enough lechatchilah; for Shema and prayer, lechatchilah water is required and after the fact cleaning suffices. If one is standing in prayer and touched, it is explained in siman 92, seif 6, that lechatchilah cleaning with earth is enough. There is a further distinction: for dirt from mud or mire, for Torah study and blessings one needs to clean only that place; but regarding the matters listed here in seif 18, because of evil spirit, lechatchilah one certainly needs the entire joint until the arm, and at least until the finger joints; see siman 613 and Magen Avraham here s.k. 7 and what I wrote there. It appears to me that ordinary hands are invalid for prayer and one must clean even the back of the hand; do not say only the palm, which usually touches, but he cleans everything if he has water, and if not he cleans with earth until the joint. Whether to distinguish between ordinary hands and soiled hands regarding going back for water will be explained with the help of Heaven in siman 92. Know that any revi'is of water in the ground, if not drawn, is fit for vessels and has the law of a mikveh; needless to say a spring purifies with any amount. See siman 159, seif 14, Taz s.k. 18 and Magen Avraham s.k. 26. Such waters do not receive impurity at all; see Pesachim 17b, there is no difference when it is in the ground, etc. According to this, what Magen Avraham wrote in s.k. 10 against Olas Tamid is good, and we wrote there that now we see that the law is with him. The explanation is that a pebble is enough for prayer, and as for receiving impurity, they do not receive impurity at all; we are not concerned for evil spirit, as explained in siman 159, seif 14. He also referred to siman 160, seif 12, that snow too has the law of a mikveh. See Yoreh Deah siman 201, seif 30, where some say even if it has not melted, and some are stringent. For evil spirit, if it has the law of a mikveh the evil spirit does not rest in it; otherwise there is a concern of evil spirit even if snow is not capable of receiving impurity. See the Rambam, Hilchos Keilim 21. There is no need to elaborate, and with the help of Heaven this will be explained more in Magen Avraham.
