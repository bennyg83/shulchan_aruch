Seif 15, "one who slept," etc. According to Orchos Chaim he need not, as above, and so too Zohar 169b and 184b imply specifically at night. Yet one may say this is not precise, and it merely speaks of the usual case. See what I wrote in seif 18. The reason is sleep, as written there, 67a, that one who tastes more than sixty breaths tastes the taste of death. So it also appears from the Gemara in Sukkah chapter 2: it is forbidden to sleep by day, etc., for this reason.
