{Rama: (There)} That it does not detract from a bundle. It is puzzling, for a bundle is effective only for prayer — yet for prayer, even if one rinses into a small amount of seawater drawn in a vessel it helps, as explained in Shulchan Aruch; so what does forty seah have to do with prayer? Furthermore, what did he challenge against Eshel Tov? He certainly deals with removing ruach raah; then forty seah are required so that there be a mikveh measure that purifies a tamei from his tumah — then it helps even for ruach raah. Rava and Peri Megadim challenged this as well. It is clear that there is a printing error here, and these words "that it does not detract from a bundle" belong in seif 22, in their place in the previous note regarding rinsing his hands, where Magen Avraham wrote to bless on cleanliness; on this basis he wrote that it does not detract — since it helps for prayer, one blesses on cleanliness; the same applies to this rinsing. Here Magen Avraham wrote, and it is plain that he does not require forty seah — namely, rainwater collected in the ground: even if they do not contain forty seah, it helps even for ruach raah. His proof is from siman 159, seif 14, where we rule like the lenient view, and he holds that even less than forty seah purifies vessels; therefore it helps for netilat yadayim for eating. And although he wrote there that one should be stringent l'chatchila, here for morning netilat yadayim, which is more lenient, one need not be stringent. And since they can purify vessel tumah, hands are not defiled from ruach raah; therefore he challenges Eshel Tov properly. What he commands one to look at siman 105, seif 7, is not in order to challenge Eshel Tov — for otherwise he would have written it before the words "not like Eshel Tov." Rather, his intention is a novel law explained there: in the hot springs of Tiberias, in Beit HaGadol, where there are not forty seah, one does not immerse hands in them because of a gezeirah — see Magen Avraham, note 10; therefore morning netilat yadayim also does not help. All this is clear.
