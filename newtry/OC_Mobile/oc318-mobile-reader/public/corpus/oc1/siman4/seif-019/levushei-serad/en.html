Ibid. For it is no worse than a pebble. This is surprising, for a pebble helps only for prayer, and for prayer even shaking into a little drawn water in a vessel helps, as explained in Shulchan Aruch; what then has forty se'ah to do with prayer? Further, what was his challenge to Olas Tamid, who certainly spoke of removing evil spirit, for which forty se'ah are needed so that it has the measure of a mikveh that purifies an impure person? Eliyah Rabbah and Pri Megadim also asked this. The clear truth is that there is a scribal error: the words for it is no worse than a pebble in seif 22 belong in the previous s.k., regarding shaking his hands, where Magen Avraham wrote to bless over cleanliness. On that he wrote that it is no worse, for since it helps for prayer he blesses over cleanliness, and the same applies to this shaking. Here Magen Avraham wrote that it is obvious that forty se'ah are not required, meaning rainwater gathered in the ground, even without forty se'ah, helps even for evil spirit. His proof is from siman 159, seif 14, where we follow the lenient view that even less than forty se'ah purifies utensils; therefore it helps for washing hands for eating. Although there the Rama wrote that one should be stringent lechatchilah, here, in morning washing, which is lighter, one need not be stringent. Since such water can purify the impurity of utensils, hands do not become impure from the evil spirit, and therefore he properly challenges Olas Tamid. His direction to see siman 160, seif 12, is not meant as a challenge to Olas Tamid, for then he would have written it before the words unlike Olas Tamid; rather, he intends a new law, that with the hot springs of Tiberias, according to Baal Halachos Gedolos, if there are not forty se'ah one does not immerse the hands in them because of a decree, and therefore morning washing would also not help. All this is clear.
