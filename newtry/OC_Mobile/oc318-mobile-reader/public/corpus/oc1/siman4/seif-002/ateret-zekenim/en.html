He should be meticulous, etc. One who walks four amot in the morning before washing his hands is liable for death (Tikkunei Zohar in the name of the Zohar); therefore he should wash his hands somewhat before he goes. Alternatively, even before he puts on his shirt; and after he has dressed properly he should wash well with pouring:

Three times, as explained: he should wash, first pouring on his right hand from his left hand, then on his left hand from his right hand, and so he does three times — not three times consecutively on the right hand and likewise on the left — and there is reason for this per Kabbalah (so too R' Yitzchak in Shulchan Aruch per the true ruling: when he washes the third time he prays, in the writings of the Ari z"l) that the evil spirit is not driven away except by washing three times intermittently. In Sefer Mitzvos Shemurim he gave a reason for this per the secret; and this matter must be warned to women especially, for most preparation of our foods is by them, and if impurity is not driven from their hands they will defile the foods with their hands: He should rinse his mouth; outside of a public fast it is forbidden to rinse the mouth. He should raise his hands level with his head and spread the palms of his hands as one who wishes to receive something — that is acceptance of purity and sanctity — and he should bring his bent elbows inward per the secret, and all his back inward. He should bless Al Netilat Yadayim, and when he blesses he should intend to remove that filth and distance it from his hands and to imbue them with a spirit of purity, end of his words. When he pours water on his right hand he should say each time "the great hand"; when he pours on his left hand he should say each time "the strong hand"; when he rubs his hands together he should say three times "the exalted hand." When he fills his hands to wash his face he should say "His hands are rods of gold set with beryl." When he washes his face he should say "His head is finest gold, his locks are curled, black as a raven"; he should also intend that it indicates supreme sanctity, for "in the image of God He made man." He must also wash because of "Prepare to meet your God, O Israel." When he washes his eyes he should say "His eyes are like doves beside streams of water, bathing in milk, sitting by fullness." When he washes his cheeks he should say "His cheeks are beds of spices, yielding fragrance." When he washes his lips he should say "His lips are roses, dripping flowing myrrh." When he washes his mouth he should say "His mouth is most sweet, altogether desirable." When he dries his face he should say "As for their appearance, four had the face of a man and four had the face of a lion on the right" — and thereby his words will be heard and his awe will rest upon every creature, and he will find favor in the eyes of all who see him. When he puts his shoes on his feet he should say "His legs are pillars of marble," end of his words.
