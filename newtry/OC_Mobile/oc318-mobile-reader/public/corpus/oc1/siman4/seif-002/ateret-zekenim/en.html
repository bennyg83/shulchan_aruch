One must be precise, etc. One who walks four amos in the morning before washing his hands is liable to death. Tolaas Yaakov in the name of the Zohar. He should therefore wash his hands to some extent before walking four amos, even before putting on his shirt. After he has dressed properly, he should wash them thoroughly by pouring water over them.
The correct text is "three times." He should first take his right hand with his left hand and then his left hand with his right hand, and he should do this three times. He should not wash the right hand three consecutive times and then the left hand three consecutive times. There is a reason for this according to Kabbalah. Rabbi Yonah likewise writes this based on the mystical tradition. When washing the third time, he should pray, as stated in the writings of the Arizal. The evil spirit is driven away only through washing three times in alternating order. Matzas Shimurim gives a reason for this according to the mystical tradition. Women must be warned about this with particular care, because they prepare most of our foods. If the impurity is not driven from their hands, they will render the foods impure with their hands.
He should rinse his mouth, except that on a communal fast it is forbidden to rinse one's mouth. He should raise his hands opposite his head and open his palms like someone who wishes to receive something; this represents receiving purity and holiness. He should bring the joints of his arms inward, in accordance with the mystical meaning of "all of their backs were toward the house." He should recite Al Netilas Yadayim, and while reciting the blessing he should intend to remove that contamination and distance it from his hands, and to cause a spirit of purity to rest upon them. End quote. Each time he pours water over his right hand, he should say, "the great hand." Each time he pours water over his left hand, he should say, "the strong hand." When rubbing his hands together, he should say three times, "the uplifted hand." When filling his hands in order to wash his face, he should say, "His hands are cylinders of gold, set with beryl." When washing his face, he should say, "His head is finest gold; His locks are curled, black as a raven." He should also intend that the face indicates supernal holiness, for "God made man in His image." He must also wash because of the verse, "Prepare to meet your God, O Israel." When washing his eyes, he should say, "His eyes are like doves beside streams of water, bathed in milk, sitting by fullness." When washing his cheeks, he should say, "His cheeks are like a bed of spices, towers of perfume." When washing his lips, he should say, "His lips are roses, dripping flowing myrrh." When washing his mouth, he should say, "His abdomen is polished ivory, overlaid with sapphires." When drying his face, he should say, "Their faces were the face of a man, and the face of a lion toward the right of the four of them." Through this, his words will be heeded, fear of him will rest upon every creature, and he will find favor in the eyes of all who see him. When putting his shoes on his feet, he should say, "His legs are pillars of marble." End quote.
