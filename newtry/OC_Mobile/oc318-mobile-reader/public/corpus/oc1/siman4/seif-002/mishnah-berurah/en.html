He should be exact. For aside from the reasons mentioned in seif katan 1, that one must wash his hands for prayer, he must also wash because of the ruach ra'ah that rests upon the hands. However, where the washing is only because of ruach ra'ah, they would not have instituted a blessing over it. Therefore we also need the reasons mentioned above.
To pour over them. Initially he should wash them until the joint of the arm; if he has no water, up to the joints of his fingers is enough. He must spread his palms like one who wishes to receive something, and raise them opposite his head. It is also good to rinse his mouth, but on a public fast he should not rinse.
Three times. Even if he pours much at one time, it does not help, for the ruach does not depart except in three times, namely once on the right hand and then on the left hand, up to three times alternately. In Maaseh Rav it is written: four times; three times to remove the ruach ra'ah and the fourth to remove the water that became impure. Women must be warned to be careful with netilas yadayim three times alternately, like men, and also because they prepare foods, so that they should not make them impure with their hands. One must also be careful that children wash their hands in the morning, for they touch food. Regarding a non-Jew touching foods without netilas yadayim, one need not be concerned, for they do not receive impurity.
