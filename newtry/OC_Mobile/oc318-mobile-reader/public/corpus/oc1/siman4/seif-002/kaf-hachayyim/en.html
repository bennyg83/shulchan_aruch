12) [Se'if 2] He should take care to pour water upon them three times, etc. The Arizal writes in Shaar HaKavanos: 'The order of morning handwashing is as follows. First, he takes the vessel of water in his right hand and transfers it to his left. He then pours water from his left hand upon his right, and afterward from his right hand upon his left. This is one time. He repeats this order a second and third time, so that each hand is washed three times. He should not wash one hand three consecutive times and then the other, but once upon this hand and once upon the other, alternately. Through this, the spirit of impurity called Shivta, the king's daughter, which is fastidious, skips and leaps until it is entirely uprooted. Otherwise, it is not uprooted. This is also found in a manuscript of Zohar.' Pri Etz Chaim there, chapter 3; Matsas Shimurim, page 3; Olas Tamid, page 17; Siddur Beis Yaakov, laws of handwashing 4; and Ben Ish Chai, ibid. 1, write likewise. This is unlike Seder HaYom, cited by Knesses HaGedolah in Hagahos HaTur and other later authorities, who writes in Zohar's name that one should pour three consecutive times, etc.; see there. One who wishes to be stringent should first pour three consecutive times and then pour two more times upon each hand alternately, as Zechor LeAvraham 1, entry nun, writes. Chesed LaAlafim and Artzos HaChaim agree, as cited by Yafeh LaLev 1:7. It is even better to pour three consecutive times and then three additional alternating times, as Yaavetz writes in Siddur Amudei Shamayim and as Pischei HaDevir rules. Rav Chaim Palagi agrees in Kaf HaChaim 18, end of paragraph 22, as does Yafeh LaLev there; see there. The primary practice, however, follows the Arizal: after one pours three alternating times upon each hand, the impurity is uprooted and gone. He may therefore recite the berachah before drying, as stated above in paragraph 8. Water carries away the impurity because impurity derives from the external forces, which are severe gevurot, as explained by the Arizal, whereas water represents chasadim. The chasadim therefore come and nullify the gevurot.
13) Some are meticulous that the right hand should never receive the vessel directly from the left. Rather, after the left hand pours upon the right, it places the vessel on the ground; the right hand then takes it from the ground and pours upon the left. The right hand, however, always gives the vessel directly to the left so that the left will pour upon it. This demonstrates the left hand's submission to the right, like a servant whom his master commands to serve him. Ben Ish Chai, ibid.
14) One must wash all the fingers and the palm through the wrist. Pri Etz Chaim, Shaar HaBerachos, chapter 3; Olas Tamid, page 18b; Ben Ish Chai, ibid. 2. Regarding why washing through the finger joints suffices on Yom Kippur and Tishah BeAv, Mahari Tzemach writes in Olas Tamid, page 17b, that the evil spirit does not exercise dominion on Yom Kippur, while on Tishah BeAv it is not particular about so small a matter, because, due to our sins, the entire day belongs to it. Ben Ish Chai cites this in the aforementioned paragraph.
15) After washing three alternating times, one must rub the hands together, as stated explicitly by the Arizal in Pri Etz Chaim there. Olas Tamid, page 19a. Although the plain meaning of his words indicates that rubbing once suffices, Rabbi Rashash requires rubbing three times and gives an order of kavanos for each of the three. One should therefore take care to rub three times. Ben Ish Chai, ibid. 3. See Nehar Shalom, page 80a. Ben Ish Chai writes there that during the rubbing one should keep his fingers pointing upward and not bent. Regarding the kavanos for washing and rubbing, see below, siman 162:2.
16) After rubbing, do not leave your fingers bent downward. Immediately straighten your hands and fingers upward and raise them to your head, or at least opposite your face, for the face is also part of the head. Recite Al Netilas Yadayim immediately upon raising and straightening your hands, so that raising them not be purposeless. Zohar states that it is forbidden to raise one's hands empty. Keep your hands raised and straight until completing the berachah. This is why the wording is Al Netilas Yadayim, denoting elevation, as in, 'He lifted them and carried them,' rather than Al Rechitzas Yadayim, 'over washing the hands.' When raising both hands opposite the head, take care to turn the arm joints - the elbows, called cobdo in the vernacular and aks in Arabic - inward toward the body, in the mystical sense of, 'All their backs turned inward.' During the berachah, one must also open both palms like one seeking to receive something in them, alluding to receiving purity through them, as our master writes in Shaar HaKavanos and Olas Tamid. Ben Ish Chai, ibid. 4.
16) Matsas Shimurim, page 3d, writes that one who does not raise his hands after washing is included among those who disparage handwashing, etc.; see there. Siddur Beis Yaakov, laws of handwashing 4, writes that while raising them one should recite the verse, 'Raise your hands in holiness,' etc. Ruach Chaim, siman 5:2, writes that this verse should not be recited, since he has not yet recited Birchos HaTorah. This is clear; see there.
17) One who washed his hands and then touched the hands of another person who had not washed must wash a second time. Ruach Chaim, ibid. 6. Ben Ish Chai, ibid. 12, writes likewise in Lev Chaim 65's name.
