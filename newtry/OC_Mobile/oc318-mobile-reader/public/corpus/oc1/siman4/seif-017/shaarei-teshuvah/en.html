One who sleeps by day. See Baer Heitev. Noda B'Yehuda, second edition, siman 12, records a question from the gaon R' Yeshayah Berlin, of blessed memory, about Rashi on Sukkah 26b. The Gemara says that Abaye slept as long as the distance from Bei Kuvei to Pumbedita, and Rashi explains that people commonly say this was six parasangs. No such fact is found, and Kiddushin 70a appears to imply the reverse. Noda B'Yehuda answers that perhaps it is found in a Midrash; Kiddushin supplies no proof, because there may have been another place named Bei Kuvei. See there. Tiferet Tzvi, siman 1, proposes emending the text to two parasangs, but this too is found nowhere. See Gittin 4a, where Rabbah bar bar Chanah says, 'I myself saw that place, and it was like the distance from Bei Kuvei to Pumbedita.' The passage refers to Kefar Ludim and Lod, which it treats as absorbed within the border. Rashi explains there that a border can protrude four or five parasangs and towns within the protrusion are called absorbed within the boundary. If Rashi in Sukkah says the distance was six parasangs, his statement here of four or five cannot be precise. This requires further study. Tiferet Tzvi writes that regarding sixty breaths there is no distinction between a weekday and Shabbos. If so, the reports that Arizal slept two or three hours show that three hours do not exceed sixty breaths; refraining from more than three hours of daytime sleep is an act of piety. R' Yosef therefore applied to Abaye the verse, 'How long will you lie down?' See there. According to him, one must say that Shabbos is nevertheless preferable: because sleep on Shabbos is a pleasure, there is not even an act of piety in avoiding more than two hours of sleep. In truth, however, the report about Arizal proves nothing regarding sixty breaths. Perhaps he awakened several times during his sleep and never slept sixty breaths continuously. Chida writes in the Kuntres Acharon at the end of Machazik Berachah: 'I heard from a Torah scholar who feared Hashem, from the inland western cities, who traveled for a long time and repeatedly measured a horse's sleep. He counted fifty-nine breaths before the horse awakened, and those fifty-nine breaths lasted more than half an hour. I have now seen, however, that the gaon Rema MiPano writes in Alfasi Zuta, chapter HaYashen: “Sixty breaths; the measure of one breath is one part in 1,080 of an hour.”' According to Rema MiPano, the total is much smaller: only one eighteenth of an hour, three minutes and one third. This does not appear to fit Sukkah there or Tur and Shulchan Aruch below in siman 231. Birkei Yosef writes that R' Chayyim Vital proved from the Zohar that the ruach ra'ah rests upon one who sleeps by day. Maharam di Lonzano writes likewise.
