Sleeping by day — Baer Hetev. And he wrote in responsa Noda B'Yehudah second edition siman 12, that he was asked by the gaon Mahari Berlin, may he rest in Eden, in what Rashi wrote in Sukkah 26a — Abbaye would sleep like the capacity of Rova Kovi to Pumbedita; and Rashi explained that they say in the world it was six parasangs — and we have not found it thus. And in Kiddushin 70a it implies the opposite; and he answered that perhaps it is found so in the Midrash; and from Kiddushin there is no proof, for there is also another Beit Kovi. See there. And in Tiferet Tzvi siman 1 he wanted to emend "two parasangs" — see there; and this too is not found anywhere. See Gittin 4b — Rabbah bar bar Chana said: To me this place appeared and was like the capacity of Rova Kovi to Pumbedita — see there — that is, it stands from Lod to the Lodim burial ground that there it is considered an enclave; and Rashi there explained that Lod projects four or five parasangs, and towns standing within the projection are called enclaves within the boundary. See there. And according to what Rashi wrote in Sukkah that it was six parasangs — it must be said that he did not specifically cite four and five parasangs; and it requires study. And see Tiferet Tzvi who wrote that for the matter of sixty breaths one should not distinguish between weekday and Shabbat. And if so, from that which they wrote the Ari would sleep two or three hours — it implies three hours is no more than sixty breaths; and more than three hours is the measure of piety not to sleep by day — therefore R' Yosef called to him, "Abbaye — when will you arise," etc. See there. And according to his words it must be said that nevertheless Shabbat is preferable — since sleep on Shabbat is pleasure, even the measure of piety does not exist if he sleeps more than two hours. But really what was written in the name of the Ari is not at all necessary for the matter of sixty breaths — perhaps he awakened several times in his sleep and did not sleep sixty breaths at one time. And in the last pamphlet at the end of Machzik Berachah he wrote: Thus I heard from a God-fearing talmid chacham, R' Uri of the Old Jewish Quarter, who was often on the road — he would inspect several times the horse's sleep and would count fifty-nine breaths; the horse would awaken and the fifty-ninth breath of the horse was more than half an hour. But now I saw in the gaon R' Menachem Azariah of Fano in Sefer Alphasi Zuta, chapter "HaYashen," who wrote: Thus — sixty breaths, and the measure of one breath is one of four hundred eighty in an hour — end of his words. And according to R' Menachem Azariah's words he is lenient in measures greatly — it is not only an eighteenth of an hour, which is the measure of three minutes and a third; and this is not implied in Sukkah there; and below siman 231 in Tur and Shulchan Aruch there.
