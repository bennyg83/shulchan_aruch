[1] Into it a revi'is, etc. The Malbushei Yom Tov wrote that for a child so much water is unnecessary, as explained below in siman 302, seif 10. I find this surprising, because the Beit Yosef there explains that the case is one in which the child urinated into his wrapping garment, and only a few drops of urine, a very small quantity, seeped onto the mother's garment. A small amount of water is then sufficient to nullify it. If, however, the child urinated directly into the garment or onto the ground, a revi'is is certainly required. Perhaps he inferred from the language of the Rama and Levush there, 'into which a child urinated,' that they do not accept the explanation of the Beit Yosef and Magen Avraham. Yet the distinction between an adult and a child is not persuasive, since the poskim there appear to mention a child only because that is the usual case, and the same law applies to an adult, as we shall explain in the responsa to siman 64. It is also possible to distinguish between urine that remains visibly present and urine absorbed into a garment. Furthermore, there the reference to a small quantity is explained in seif katan 2. The Lechem Chamudos, in his commentary to the Rambam's Mishnah, chapter Mi Shemeiso, wrote that one must pour the revi'is of water into it all at once. I have seen that none of the early or later authorities mentioned this, and perhaps they regarded the matter as self-evident. Rambam himself, in chapter 3 of Hilchos Krias Shema, also did not mention it, but wrote in its place that a revi'is of water is required for urine from one urination. The Bartenura wrote likewise. This requires study. Responsa Radbaz, part 2, siman 30, states that people who suffer from hemorrhoids and continuously discharge blood, together with foul, putrid mucus having a bad odor, may not recite Krias Shema, the Amidah, Torah, or blessings over enjoyment while the foul mucus is flowing from them. When there is no bad odor, if the blood or mucus emerges from the intestine, the matter can be remedied by washing the soiled place. If it emerges from the anal opening itself, there is no remedy. How can one determine whether it comes from the intestine or from the anal opening? If it emerges intermittently with force, it is from the intestines. If it continuously seeps and descends on its own, it is from the anal opening.
