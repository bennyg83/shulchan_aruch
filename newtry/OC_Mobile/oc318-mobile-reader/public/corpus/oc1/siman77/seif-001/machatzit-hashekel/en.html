It implies that even if the urine is less than a revi'it, a revi'it of water is required; and therefore Magen Avraham raised a difficulty below siman 82 s.k. 25, for Rama wrote that although it is forbidden to put water on a garment on Shabbos, for its permissibility is like its laundering — nevertheless, if a child put water on the garment, one may wipe his hands that were washed in that garment, and thereby the absorbed urine is nullified, and there is no prohibition in this, for it is the way of filth. And he raised a difficulty: here we wrote that even for a small amount of urine we require a revi'it of water, yet the water on his hands does not contain a revi'it; and one could answer that since the urine is not visible, we do not require a revi'it. But in the responsum of Chacham Tzvi siman 102 he wrote that even if they are visible, if there is not a revi'it of urine one does not need a revi'it of water to nullify them — a majority of water suffices, for urine, which is rabbinic, is not as stringent as a Torah prohibition that is nullified in a majority; and they said revi'it of water only to be lenient — that even if the urine is very abundant, a revi'it suffices.
