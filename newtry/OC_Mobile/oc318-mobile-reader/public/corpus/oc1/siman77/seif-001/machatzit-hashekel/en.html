Seif katan 1. One occasion, etc. Or a small amount. This implies that even when the urine is less than a revi'is, a revi'is of water is required. The Magen Avraham therefore asked below, in siman 302, seif katan 25, concerning the Rama's ruling that although it is forbidden on Shabbos to place water upon a garment because soaking it constitutes laundering, nevertheless, if a child urinated upon a garment, one may wipe his washed hands on that garment. The absorbed urine is thereby nullified, and there is no prohibition because this is done in a manner of soiling. He asks that it is written here that even for a small amount of urine a revi'is of water is required, whereas the water upon his hands does not amount to a revi'is. He answers that because the urine is no longer visibly present, a revi'is is unnecessary. Responsa Chacham Tzvi, siman 102, however, wrote that even when the urine remains visibly present, if it is less than a revi'is, a revi'is of water is unnecessary. A majority of water is sufficient, because urine, whose prohibition is Rabbinic, cannot be more stringent than a Torah prohibition that is nullified by a majority. The Sages prescribed a revi'is of water only as a leniency, so that even a very large quantity of urine may be nullified with one revi'is. See there.
