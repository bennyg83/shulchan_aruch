(1) Opposite, etc. All the distancing requirements stated below in siman 79, seif 1, regarding excrement apply here as well.
(2) Until he pours, etc. According to Torah law, recitation is forbidden only opposite the stream of urine itself. Once it has fallen upon the ground, the prohibition is only Rabbinic, as explained above at the end of siman 76. The Sages therefore permitted nullifying it by means of a revi'is of water, even when the quantity of urine is large. Accordingly, a revi'is of water is effective only when the urine does not emit a foul odor. If it is known to emit a foul odor, in which case recitation opposite it is forbidden by Torah law, a revi'is is ineffective. One must instead add enough water to eliminate the odor.
(3) A revi'is of water. Some authorities maintain that it must be poured all at once, for otherwise each small amount is nullified as it is poured.
(4) Upon the ground. He should ensure that the water reaches every place where the urine has spread and remains wet enough to moisten something else. See Biur Halachah.
(5) Designated for it. In that case, even when it contains no urine at all, recitation opposite it is forbidden. Adding water does not render the vessel itself fit, as will be explained in siman 87.
(6) The water was first in the vessel. Similarly, if a revi'is of water was first upon the ground and he subsequently urinated onto the water, one may recite Krias Shema, even if the urine has now caused the liquid to spread farther.
