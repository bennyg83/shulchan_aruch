In the holy tongue. And it is the way of piety to be stringent [Shulchan Aruch, siman 554]. It is stated in Shabbat page 150 that one may engage in hirhur in words of Torah opposite nakedness, as it is written: "and He shall not see in you anything unseemly" — speech is forbidden, hirhur is permitted; see in the Rosh, Terumat HaDeshen in the mishnah of one who had a seminal emission, etc. And one must say that that which is forbidden to engage in hirhur in the bathhouse is because its filth is abundant, as written in siman 45. However, in Yoreh Deah siman 1, seif 10, it implies that when he is naked and his fellow blesses, it is forbidden to intend to listen to the blessings to fulfill his obligation through his blessing — for one cannot say of him shomea ke'einah, since it is forbidden for him to answer Amen — as the Taz and Shach [wrote].

To restrain from a prohibited matter. The same applies if a woman entered his heart — he may engage in hirhur there in words of Torah, for this is to restrain from prohibition (Shulchan Aruch).

And regarding holy matters. The Rambam wrote regarding that case where Rabbi Meir said to him: there is no knife on Shabbat — and it would have been enough to tell him "do not comb" or "I do not want you to comb" — yet nevertheless he told him in language of instruction, etc. The Ran wrote: even without restraining from prohibition, it is permitted to tell one's fellow "do such-and-such for me," even though automatically it becomes instruction — for he told him "take in a kli sheni and give," even though we learn from this that warming is not his cooking.
