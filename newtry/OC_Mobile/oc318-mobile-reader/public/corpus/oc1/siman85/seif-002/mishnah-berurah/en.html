(4) Even to engage in hirhur — even when no person is there.

(5) In words of Torah — and likewise it is forbidden to examine in the bathroom the weights of the names and verbs of the holy tongue, for there is no way to reach knowledge except through the Scriptures, and one will come to hirhur in Scripture. And it is obvious that a person may reflect in the bathroom on the greatness of his lowliness, and that in the end he will return entirely to being dust, maggots, and worms — and pride is not fitting for him.

(6) Forbidden — and he should think there about his household accounts and expenses so that he does not come to hirhur; and on Shabbat he should think about buildings and pleasant drawings.

(7) In the bathroom — and even within the four cubits of the bathroom from the place where the odor ceased, hirhur is also forbidden; see above in siman 79 in Mishnah Berurah sk 3. It is written in Tosafot Yerushalayim in the name of the Yerushalmi of Burski that it is forbidden to pray and likewise to mention anything sacred — and this is when processing has begun that produces a bad smell; but if it has not yet begun, it is permitted. And it is obvious that when it is forbidden, this applies even to the craftsman himself who is accustomed to the bad smell and does not sense it — since people are distressed by it; see above in siman 79, seif 1.

(8) And in the bathhouse — for filth is abundant within it and it is repugnant, and it is like excrement in the bathroom; and the reason for the prohibition of hirhur in all these places is because we require "and your camp shall be holy," and it is lacking [Shabbat 150b]. Beit Yosef wrote: one whose study is fluent in his mouth and he engaged in hirhur in the bathroom or bathhouse under duress — it is permitted. Some say that since he is under duress in his hirhur, he may even express it with his lips — and so it implies from Zevachim 102b. But in Birkhei Yosef he concludes that speech is forbidden in every manner, and the proof from the Gemara can be rejected as written there by the rabbi in Berakhat HaZevach and Tzon Kedoshim — see there. And what he wrote, that if he engaged in hirhur under duress it is permitted — this means that if he engaged in hirhur he did not violate a prohibition, since he was under duress; however lekhatchilah it is a person's duty to push away hirhur, and all the more so speech is forbidden — and so too in Yeshuot Yaakov, that speech is forbidden.

(9) In the holy tongue — and it is the way of piety to be stringent.

(10) Ne'eman — the Raavad forbids Rachum; Beit Chadash wrote that one should be stringent, and so Peri Chadash. However, in a foreign language, all agree it is permitted, since this is not designated specifically for the Holy One, blessed be He, unlike other words of Torah; and all the more so the names that are not erased — it is forbidden to say them there even in a foreign language, such as Gott in Ashkenazic, or Boga in Polish and Russian, and the like — for although this name has no sanctity in the letters of its writing and it is permitted to erase it, nevertheless there is [prohibition] because of degradation in mentioning it in a filthy place, as with mentioning Shalom, which one may also erase; yet since the Holy One, blessed be He, is called by it, even though it is not designated for Him, it is forbidden to mention it when intending the matter of peace, as written in siman 84; and all the more so here, for regarding several matters its law is like names in the holy tongue — such as for an oath, for mentioning God's name in vain, and for cursing one's fellow with the Name.

(11) It is permitted to say them — for people too are sometimes called by them, as it says "chanun veRachum veTzadik"; and specifically when one says it thus. But when one says "HaRachum yerachem alekha," one should say it is forbidden, like asking about peace [Peri Megadim].

(12) That are not erased — I say that there is Shalom, which is forbidden to mention there, as above in siman 84.

(13) To restrain from a prohibited matter — and the same applies if a thought of sin entered his heart: he may engage in hirhur there in words of Torah, because this is like restraining him from prohibition, for the Torah saves from evil hirhurim.

(14) And regarding holy matters — meaning to tell him even in language of instruction that it is forbidden to do thus; and he need not be precise and say "do not do thus" without language of instruction. Some are stringent and hold that it is forbidden to speak in language that appears as Torah — for example, if he sees in the bathhouse that they shave after midnight, he should say "they do not shave," for this language is not language of instruction, even though automatically it is understood as forbidden; but he should not say "it is forbidden to shave." And it seems to me that if his words are not understood in brief, he may appraise before them the magnitude of the prohibition in all its parts in order to restrain them. Magen Avraham wrote in the name of the Ran: even without restraining from prohibition, it is permitted to tell one's fellow "do such-and-such for me," even if automatically it becomes instruction whereby he teaches him that he is permitted to do so — since he does not say them in language of instruction.

(15) And in a place where it is permitted — such as in the middle room of the bathhouse, as above; or such as when he himself is not entirely clean — for example, if he touched his hands in covered places — for if his hands or other places on his body are actually filthy, even hirhur is forbidden [Peri Megadim].

(16) To rule on a matter of law — for this is like hirhur, where one thinks the rationale in his heart; see above siman 47, seif 4, in Mishnah Berurah and Biur Halacha.
