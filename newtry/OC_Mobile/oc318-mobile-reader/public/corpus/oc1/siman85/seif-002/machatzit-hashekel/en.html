Seif katan 2: In the sacred tongue, etc. It is stated in Shabbos, etc., that thought is permitted. See the Rosh and Rabbeinu Yonah, etc. The reason is that we rule that thought is not equivalent to speech.
The prohibition against thinking Torah in a bathhouse means as follows. In a privy, thought is certainly forbidden, as stated there in Shabbos, because the verse concerning excrement says, 'Your camp shall be holy,' and the verse does not mention speech. This implies that even thought is forbidden, as Rashi explains there, for the verse means that your encampment must be holy because Jews constantly think words of Torah; see there. In a bathhouse, however, the prohibition would apparently be only because of exposed nakedness, since people stand there unclothed. For this reason, not all speech is forbidden there, even an inquiry about another's welfare, except in the inner room where everyone stands unclothed. Magen Avraham therefore asks that thinking should be permitted opposite nakedness.
It is because the bathhouse contains much filth through its use with hot water; see there. It is thus comparable to excrement and a privy. For this reason I was uncertain regarding our mikvaos, which are usually heated, whether they likewise have the law of a bathhouse because their filth is abundant. Nevertheless, Yoreh Deah 1:14 appears to imply otherwise, etc. The Mishnah teaches that a naked person may not slaughter because he cannot recite the blessing. The Shach and Taz write there that although another person could recite the blessing while he slaughters, and although that other person may not bless facing him while he is naked, the other person could turn his face away and bless. They answer that the slaughterer would nevertheless be forbidden to intend to fulfill his obligation through the other's blessing, because he is naked and hearing is equivalent to answering. Magen Avraham's question is that thought opposite nakedness is permitted, so why may the naked person not intend to fulfill his obligation through another's blessing? One must answer that hearing is equivalent to answering and is more substantial than thought, because it gives him the status of having said the words himself.
