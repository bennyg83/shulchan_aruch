Seif 2: Even to think, etc. Berachos there and in several other places.
And in a place, etc. Berachos there: A Torah scholar is forbidden, etc.
And even, etc. This follows from the Gemara's question in Shabbos 40b, 'How could he do so,' etc., and its answer that it was to prevent a prohibition, etc. These are the words of the Ran. In the Yerushalmi, chapter 3 of Shabbos, Rabbi Yosi bar Idi said in the name of Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi: One may ask questions concerning the laws of a bathhouse in a bathhouse, etc. Thus, Rabbi Shimon bar Abba entered to bathe with Rabbi Meir and asked him, 'May one rinse?' He answered, 'It is forbidden,' etc. But did Shmuel not ask Rav, 'May one answer Amen in a filthy place?' Rav answered, 'It is forbidden,' and even the word 'forbidden' that I told you is forbidden to say there. He found a Tanna who taught: One may ask questions concerning the laws of a bathhouse in a bathhouse and the laws of a privy in a privy. At first glance the halachah should follow Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi, but the Babylonian Gemara does not appear so, as stated above. In our version of the Yerushalmi the text reads, 'He found a Tanna who taught: One may not ask,' and that is a scribal error.
And concerning sacred matters, etc. Rambam. See Magen Avraham in the name of Kesef Mishneh. The Rashba wrote: What was the Gemara's question, could one imagine that he should allow him to commit a prohibition? Rather, he should not have spoken in the language of a ruling, but as one who had no need for that act. This is what is meant by, 'Know, etc.,' meaning that he actually spoke there in the language of a ruling.
And in a place, etc. See Tosafos to Avodah Zarah 44b, beginning 'The Tanna,' etc., and 'Rabbeinu Yitzchak says,' etc. The Ran likewise wrote this, except that he explained that it is not because of enmity, but because thought is permitted there, and whenever one does not state the reason for the matter it is considered like thought. He also wrote another explanation, following Rashbam.
