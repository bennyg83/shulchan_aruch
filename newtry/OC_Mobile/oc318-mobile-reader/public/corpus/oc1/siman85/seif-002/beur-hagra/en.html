Seif 2: "Even to contemplate..." — Berachos there and in several places.

"And in a place where..." — Berachos there, words of Torah are forbidden [etc.]

"And even..." — from what is challenged in Shabbos 40b, "and how did he do so..." and it answers "to separate..." — these are the words of Ran. And in Yerushalmi chapter 3 of Shabbos, R' Yonasan bar Idi in the name of R' Yehoshua ben Levi: they may ask laws of the bathhouse in the bathhouse, etc. As this case: Rashba entered to bathe with R' Meir; he asked him whether rinsing is permitted; he told him forbidden, etc. And not so Shmuel, who asked Rav whether answering amen in a filthy place is permitted; he told him forbidden, and forbidden — for I told you forbidden. A tanna was found who taught: they may ask laws of the bathhouse in the bathhouse, and laws of the bathroom in the bathroom. And it would appear the halacha is like R' Yehoshua ben Levi; but in our Gemara it is not stated so, as written above. And in our version of the Yerushalmi a tanna was found who taught: they may not ask — and this is a scribal error.

"And regarding holy matters..." — Rambam, and Magen Avraham in the name of Kesef Mishnah. Rashba wrote: what is the difficulty — is it conceivable to leave him to do a prohibition? Rather, he should not have needed to speak in language of instruction, but as one who does not need this; and this is what "you shall know" means — that even though he spoke there in actual language of instruction.

"And in a place where..." — see Tosafos Moed Katan 9b, s.v. tanna, etc., and Or Zarua, etc., and similarly Ran; except he wrote not because of hatred, but because there contemplation is permitted, and whoever does not state the rationale of the matter is like contemplation; and he wrote another explanation like Rashbam.
