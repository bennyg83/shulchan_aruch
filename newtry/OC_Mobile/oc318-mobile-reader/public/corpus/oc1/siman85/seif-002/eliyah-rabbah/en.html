[2] Even to contemplate words of Torah, etc. Kesef Mishnah wrote, chapter 3 of Hilchos Kri'as Shema — this is his wording: In chapter Kirah, R' Yochanan said: in every place it is permitted to contemplate words of Torah except the bathhouse and bathroom; and from that it lists generically bathroom and bathhouse, it implies that even though there is no feces in the bathroom and no person in the bathhouse, and even a new one, it is forbidden to contemplate there — similar to Kri'as Shema and prayer — until here is his wording. But in Nachalas Tzvi he wrote that in a new bathroom contemplation is permitted; for if not, what is Ravina's question in the first chapter of Shabbos, "is there invitation or not" — resolve it from this [statement] of R' Yochanan that even contemplation is forbidden, and all the more so reading; rather certainly contemplation is permitted, until here. I did not reach the end of his mind, for Ravina's question itself is the question; and since we establish "there is invitation" for prohibition, contemplation is also forbidden. But one may analyze: certainly Kesef Mishnah does not mean a new bathhouse, for even reading is permitted as stated in siman 84; rather perforce he means a new bathroom. And if so, from where does he learn from R' Yochanan from listing generically bathroom that even in a new one it is forbidden — for in bathhouse too he listed generically? On the contrary, one should say: just as bathhouse certainly refers to an old one, bathroom too refers to an old one. (And therefore) [and further it is difficult]: how did he write "similar to Kri'as Shema and prayer" — behold an intermediate bathhouse proves that Kri'as Shema and prayer are forbidden and contemplation is permitted? And one may answer with difficulty: if a person asked him a halachic matter, it is forbidden to tell him, because one does not respond in a bathhouse; but in an intermediate house it is permitted (Ran in Avodah Zarah). And in chapter Kirah he wrote it is permitted to tell him even without separating from prohibition, "do for me thus," even though automatically it is instruction. Beit Yosef wrote: one whose study is fluent on his lips in the bathroom or bathhouse and contemplated under duress — permitted, until here; and Derisha explained: since he is under duress in his contemplation, he may even express with his lips, and so is implied in Zevachim 102b. Sefer Chasidim wrote: if a thought of sin came to him in the bathroom, he may contemplate words of Torah in order to separate from prohibition. Shlah wrote, page 81: a person in the bathroom should think of his expenses and household expenses, or accustom himself to accounts; and on Shabbos he should think of pleasant buildings and designs. Magen Avraham wrote in the name of Sefer Chasidim, siman 554, that it is a measure of piety to be stringent not to speak secular matters in the holy tongue, until here — and I looked there and did not find it.
