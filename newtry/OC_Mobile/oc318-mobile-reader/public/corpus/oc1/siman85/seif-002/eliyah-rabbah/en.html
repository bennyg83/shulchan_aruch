2) Even to contemplate words of Torah, etc. Kesef Mishneh writes in chapter 3 of the laws of Keriat Shema: “In the chapter Kirah, Rabbi Yochanan said: In every place it is permitted to contemplate words of Torah except a bathhouse and a privy. Since he states ‘privy’ and ‘bathhouse’ without qualification, it implies that even if no feces is present in the privy and no person is present in the bathhouse, and even if it is new, it is forbidden to contemplate Torah there, just as with Keriat Shema and prayer.” These are his words. Nachalat Tzvi, however, writes that contemplation is permitted in a new privy. Otherwise, what is Ravina's question in the first chapter of Shabbat whether designation is effective or not? It could be resolved from Rabbi Yochanan's statement that even contemplation is forbidden, and all the more so recitation. Rather, contemplation must certainly be permitted. These are his words. I do not understand his reasoning, for that itself is Ravina's question; since we rule that designation is effective to prohibit, contemplation is also forbidden. Yet this requires analysis: Kesef Mishneh certainly cannot mean a new bathhouse, because even recitation is permitted there, as stated in siman 84. He must therefore refer to a new privy. If so, how does he infer from Rabbi Yochanan's unqualified mention of a privy that even a new one is forbidden? Rabbi Yochanan also mentions a bathhouse without qualification. On the contrary, just as the bathhouse certainly means an old one, we should say that the privy also means an old one. It is further difficult that he writes, “just as with Keriat Shema and prayer,” because the middle room of a bathhouse proves otherwise: Keriat Shema and prayer are forbidden there, while contemplation is permitted. One may answer with difficulty that if someone asked him a halakhic question, he may not answer him in the inner bathhouse, because halakhic questions are not answered in a bathhouse, whereas it is permitted in the middle room. Ran in Avodah Zarah. In the chapter Kirah it is written that even when there is no need to prevent a prohibition, one may tell him, “Do this for me,” although this implicitly conveys a halakhic ruling. Beit Yosef writes that if one's learning is fluent in his mouth while he is in a privy or bathhouse and he contemplates it involuntarily, this is permitted. Derishah explains that since his contemplation is involuntary, he may even articulate it with his lips; this is also implied in Zevachim, page 102b. Sefer Chasidim writes that if a sinful thought comes to a person in a privy, he may contemplate words of Torah in order to separate himself from the prohibition. Shelah writes on page 81 that in a privy a person should think about his expenses and household expenses, or accustom himself to calculations; on Shabbat he should think about attractive buildings and designs. Magen Avraham writes in the name of Sefer Chasidim, siman 954, that it is a pious practice to be stringent not to speak secular matters in the Holy Tongue. These are his words. I examined that passage and did not find this statement.
