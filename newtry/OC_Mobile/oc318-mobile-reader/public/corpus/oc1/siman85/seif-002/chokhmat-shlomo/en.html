(There) Seif 2. Even to contemplate words of Torah, etc. NB: I was uncertain whether it is permitted to contemplate Him, blessed be He, or not. And prima facie one should learn a fortiori that it is forbidden to contemplate His existence and His power. However, per the kabbalists' view that with tefillin of Rabbenu Tam one may converse in them mundane speech by force of their great sanctity and no impure thing cleaves to them — one could say all the more so here. And prima facie it seems to me a proof for this from the Gemara, chapter 1 of Yoma (7b), that brought there a dispute of R' Yehuda and R' Shimon regarding the tzitz, and said there that R' Shimon's reason is as written "and it shall be on his forehead always" — what is "always"? If literally always, does it not require a bit [of oil]? Does it not require entering the Holy [Temple]? Rather, "always" comes for appeasement. And R' Yehuda — that "always" is that he should not divert his mind from it, etc. And it is difficult — he should have challenged R' Shimon: what will he do with the verse "I have set Hashem before me always"? It is also difficult thus: what is "always" — does it not require a bit, does it not require entering the bathroom? Granted for R' Yehuda it works well that the intent is not to divert his mind from it; but for R' Shimon it is difficult what he explains regarding "always." And perforce it is proved that regarding His actual existence, blessed be He, it is permitted to contemplate Him, also in the bathroom; also "does it not require a bit" is not applicable to Him — one could say that all dreams are from daytime thoughts, and if one thinks of Him, blessed be He, always, also at night so are his thoughts, and "always" applies to him; and it works well as we thought — understand well.
