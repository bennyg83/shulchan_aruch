It is forbidden. See Baer Heitev; Megillah 28a, 'In all my days I did not think, etc.'; and Rashba's novellae there. This accords with Berachos 24b, where according to R' Yochanan, one who is walking may do so strictly speaking. See Eliyah Rabbah, Responsa She'eilas Yaavetz, siman 10, and below, se'if katan 5. See also Yoreh De'ah at the end of siman 246, which permits entering a privy while occupied with an unresolved halachic issue. See Radbaz, siman 342, cited by Birkei Yosef. Lechem Yehudah, Hilchos Krias Shema 3, writes that one may not think Torah thoughts within four cubits behind or beside a privy, or in front of it as far as one can see. She'eilas Yaavetz writes that in a privy one may not study the grammatical patterns of Hebrew nouns and verbs, because they can be understood only through Scriptural verses and he will come to think about Scripture; see siman 10. Birkei Yosef derives from this that one may not compose a literary text there in Talmudic language, since he will certainly come to think somewhat about a Talmudic passage.
