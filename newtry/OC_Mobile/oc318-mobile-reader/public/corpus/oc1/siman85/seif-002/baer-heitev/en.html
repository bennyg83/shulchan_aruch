And on Shabbos one should think of [Shabbos] buildings and designs, Shlah. And see below siman 62, seif kaf-gimmel, regarding the law of contemplating a blessing over one's bed.

In the holy tongue. And it is a measure of piety to be stringent. Sefer Chasidim; Magen Avraham.

Ne'eman. The Ra'avad forbids [the name] Rachum. Beit Chadash wrote one should be stringent, and similarly Peri Chadash. However, in a foreign language all permit. And specifically Shalom or Rachum and Chanun may be mentioned in a foreign language. But other names that are not erasable may not be mentioned even in a foreign language. And similarly other matters of the holy tongue may not be said in secular language, Ateret Zekenim, Avodah Zarah. And see above siman 84, seif bet, Sha'arei Teshuvah in the name of Peri Chadash.

Erasable. According to him, for there is [the name] Shalom that is forbidden to mention there, as written siman 84.

The prohibited matter. And the same if a thought of sin entered his heart — he may contemplate there words of Torah, for it is to separate from prohibition. Sefer Chasidim; Magen Avraham. (Beit Yosef wrote: one whose study is fluent on his lips in the bathroom or bathhouse and contemplated under duress — permitted. Prisha explained: since he is under duress in his contemplation, he may even express with his lips, and so is implied in Zevachim 102b.)

Holy matters. As that case where Rabbi Meir said there is no smearing on Shabbos — it would suffice to say to him "do not smear" or "I do not want you to smear" — yet he told him in language of instruction. Kesef Mishnah. Ran wrote: even without separating from prohibition it is permitted to tell his fellow "do for me such-and-such," even though automatically it is instruction, for he told him "take with a second vessel and place," even though we learn from it that warming is not its cooking. Magen Avraham.
