(There) In the continuation, 'because its filth is abundant.' See my responsum concerning a heated mikveh that contains steam and filth, Chasam Sofer, Orach Chayim, siman 18. There I was asked about one who has in his room an excavated pool of hot mikveh water for the purification of the women of his household, covered with wooden boards. May he study in that room, where there is no bathtub or other bathing fixture, but only the mikveh pit, which is heated as needed once or twice a month? In my responsum I concluded as follows: In the case before us, the room containing the pit is clean and pure. Although once or twice a month a woman washes there in hot water, and although the pit itself contains considerable filth, the room is a separate domain that is clean and pure and contains neither steam nor filth. Therefore, when no women are washing there and the pit is covered with boards, I know of no basis to forbid Torah study there. As an added measure of propriety, he should place a movable partition outside, between the person studying and the pit covered with boards. I personally knew that the renowned pious Rabbi Zalman Chasid, of blessed memory, in Frankfurt am Main, had a hot mikveh in his garden courtyard. It was covered, and he studied there during the summer, and no one questioned the practice.
