Regarding the word 'even,' see Taz in seif 2, who writes that at night, when one's hands are not clean because they touched the body, he may think the blessing if he wishes to drink. If they are actually soiled, however, it is forbidden even to think the blessing. Concerning excrement on one's body that is covered by clothing, there are differing opinions in siman 76:4. If the bed itself is not clean, it would seem that all agree that covering it permits thought. See Magen Avraham there, seifim katanim 6 and 7, and what I wrote there.
What the Mechaber wrote in seif 1, 'He shall not walk,' etc., is from Berachos 24b, which teaches that even while walking it is forbidden. Even if he does not see the excrement, the requirement that one's camp be holy applies, and he must repeat the recitation. See in the Gemara the verse, 'Through this matter you shall prolong your days.' See also Derishah, who expounds the verse, 'To guard, to perform all the words of this Torah, for it is not an empty matter,' etc. 'To guard' refers to negative commandments. The straightforward meaning is that 'to guard' refers to negative commandments and 'to perform' to positive commandments. Yet because the verse says 'to guard, to perform' without a connecting word before 'to perform,' and then says 'all the words of this Torah,' it implies that even positive commandments sometimes require restraint. At times one is forbidden to engage in Torah and must remain empty of it in a filthy place. This is not truly emptiness. On the contrary, by this silence you fulfill a mitzvah, for Torah is your life when you engage in it, and 'through this matter,' by interrupting your learning, 'you shall prolong your days.' See there. With Hashem's help, this will be explained in Sefer HaMaggid.
If his learning is fluent in his mouth and he thought it involuntarily, it is permitted. See Beit Yosef, Eliyah Rabbah, and Peri Chadash. See Derishah, who maintains that he may even articulate it with his lips, since he is compelled and thought is permitted, and this appears from Zevachim 102b, 'I learned this law,' etc. See there.
