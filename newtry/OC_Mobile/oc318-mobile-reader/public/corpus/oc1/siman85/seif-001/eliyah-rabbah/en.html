[1] He must pause, etc. And if he paused, Scripture says "and through this matter you shall prolong your days" (Tur). It is difficult, for in Yalkut he explained the verse as a warning that they should speak words of Torah; and behold Rashi explained in Berachos 24, "and through this matter" — because of this speech that you were careful about, you shall prolong your days, until here. And his explanation appears: we say before this in the Gemara it did not resolve what "for you have despised the word of the Omnipresent regarding this" means; and it is plausible to Rashi that we derive a matter from a matter, and it is easy to understand. And Prisha explained the words of Tur: he learned from the beginning of the verse that says "and he said to them, set your hearts, etc., that you afflict your children to observe and do all the words of this Torah, for it is not an empty matter from you, for it is your life, and through this matter you shall prolong, etc." — he questioned the many repetitions of language; rather this is what it says: "to observe and to do" — to fulfill words of Torah and engage in them, namely included in doing; and also "to observe" — to be careful not to engage in it in a place of filth, for wherever it says "guard" and "lest," it is only a negative command; and it says "for it is not an empty matter" — that you should not say it would be disgraceful for you to have one hour empty without Torah; therefore it said this emptiness is not considered emptiness at all. And afterwards it specifies two types of reward: "for it is your life" is on engaging in Torah and performing the mitzvah; and "through this matter" is on the empty matter mentioned, namely guarding against engaging in it.
