Seif 1. A person shall not go in alleyways, etc. NB: Behold, bedi'eved if he transgressed and read, it appears he must return and read, as written above in Shulchan Aruch siman 76 (seif 5), and in my composition Sefer HaChaim; and afterwards I found it in Peri Megadim — the reason is that it is mitzvah haba'ah be'aveirah. And behold, now I saw it can be said in this what is written in the Gemara, chapter 3 of Berachos (24b): it did not resolve what R' Mesharshia bar [son of the daughter] of R' Yehoshua ben Levi said — upon him Scripture says "Also I gave them statutes that are not good..." R' Eliezer said: they are "those who draw iniquity with cords of vanity." R' Ada... said from here "for you have despised the word of Hashem regarding this." And behold, to understand what they dispute and what is the halachic relevance, it appears to say: behold, regarding mitzvah haba'ah be'aveirah there is a dispute — some hold mitzvah haba'ah be'aveirah is fully valid, and some hold it is invalid. But this is regarding a Torah matter; however, for a rabbinic mitzvah it is valid as mitzvah haba'ah be'aveirah, and we already wrote in several places that according to this view, the same applies to a rabbinic transgression — also valid as mitzvah haba'ah be'aveirah. And some hold that even for a rabbinic mitzvah, mitzvah haba'ah be'aveirah is invalid. And it appears they dispute this: behold, the view that learns from the verse "I gave them statutes that are not good" holds mitzvah haba'ah be'aveirah is valid; and if so, it would be that there is transgression, and it is explained in tractate Sotah that transgression extinguishes mitzvah; therefore he does not receive reward for the mitzvah; nevertheless, conversely he is also not punished, since the mitzvah extinguishes the transgression and they cancel each other; therefore he said "Also I gave them statutes that are not good... in which they shall not live" — that they do not receive reward for them, but in any case they also do not receive punishment. And the other view holds mitzvah haba'ah be'aveirah is invalid; and since there is no mitzvah and only transgression remains, he receives punishment upon it. And behold it is known that the reward of transgression is transgression, and transgression draws transgression, and such is the way of the yetzer hara — every time it grows heavier, as stated in the Midrash; therefore from a mitzvah that comes through transgression one later comes to transgression alone. And behold transgressions are not compared to ropes, for they are like spider filaments and no rope is made from them; only mitzvos are strong and compared to ropes; therefore if one does a mitzvah without transgression combined, they are "cords of truth"; and if one does a mitzvah that in truth is here through transgression and invalid, they are "cords of vanity." Therefore he said "they draw iniquity with cords of vanity" — like mitzvah haba'ah be'aveirah, which appears as a mitzvah but is not a mitzvah; through this they truly draw iniquity afterwards. And if so, it is proved from this that mitzvah haba'ah be'aveirah is invalid, for otherwise at least punishment would not reach them to be drawn from this to actual transgression; and perforce mitzvah haba'ah be'aveirah is invalid, and even bedi'eved one does not fulfill through it. And this is the view: even if reading in a filthy place is forbidden only rabbinically, it is also invalid by force of mitzvah haba'ah be'aveirah; and the view holds that even rabbinically we are concerned for mitzvah haba'ah be'aveirah, and the same for a rabbinic mitzvah. But the other view holds mitzvah haba'ah be'aveirah is valid rabbinically; and if so, if reading in a filthy place were only rabbinically forbidden, he would fulfill through it, only that he transgressed "for you have despised the word of Hashem regarding this," which is explicit in the Torah; and for a Torah transgression we disqualify mitzvah haba'ah be'aveirah — therefore he does not fulfill through this; but if it were a rabbinic transgression he would fulfill; and similarly also a rabbinic mitzvah is valid as mitzvah haba'ah be'aveirah. And there are three distinct views in this; it comes out well — understand well.
