(א) (s.k. 1) And they made an eruv. And per the view that permits exchanging. Meaning in rabbinic domains — namely that the prohibition to move objects from one to the other is only d'rabbanan, such as karmelit to reshut ha-yachid if there is makom patur between them — whether permitted to move from karmelit via makom patur is disputed among poskim above siman 346 seif 1; and according to those who permit, here too it is permitted even if they did not make an eruv — behold no eruv is needed to move from house to fellow's house except d'rabbanan; and throwing is perforce above ten as Magen Avraham s.k. 2 wrote — for otherwise without eruv one could say rabbinic domains via makom patur:

(ב) See siman 355, etc. — he did not write to see siman 345, for there the dispute of the aforementioned poskim is explained — from there there is no proof, for one may say that even according to those who permit moving in rabbinic domains via makom patur, that is specifically when it rested in makom patur first before taking it to the other domain — namely first taking from karmelit to makom patur and it rested there and in this there is no prohibition, and afterward when bringing to reshut ha-yachid from makom patur likewise no prohibition, as taught there siman 345 that a person stands in makom patur and takes an object from the hand of one standing in reshut ha-yachid, etc.; if so when it reaches the hand of one standing in makom patur it is as if it rested there; therefore permitted; but in our case where one person conveys from domain to forbidden domain via makom patur and it did not rest in makom patur — one may say according to all it is forbidden; and so Tosafos explicitly Eruvin daf 90a s.v. "karmelit and reshut ha-yachid," etc. — see there; therefore Magen Avraham wrote to see siman 355 where Rama brought in Mordechai's name the law of a threshold above ten — from which it is proven he holds that even if it did not rest in makom patur it is permitted — and see there Magen Avraham s.k. 11:

(ג) And in my humble opinion Tosafos Shabbos daf 101a s.v. "R' Yehuda says," etc. — this is a dispute of R' Yehuda and the Rabbis: R' Yehuda permits in such a case even if it did not rest in makom patur, and the Rabbis forbid unless it rested in makom patur; and it appears to me Mordechai ruled like R' Yehuda (and the reason: even though the Sages disagree with R' Yehuda, it appears to me they rule like him, for on daf 100b in Shabbos we say one who travels on a ship — Rav Huna said: remove a ziz — all the more so fill and draw; and Rabbah and R' Chisda hold a wide ziz is required four tefachim; and they conclude they argue about this — Rav Huna holds since they are accustomed that a ship does not travel if the water is not at least ten high, if so the sea is in any case karmelit; and he holds those ten from the ground — we consider the sea ground; if so when drawing above the water which is at least ten high, for otherwise the ship does not travel as above — if so the air above ten is makom patur and properly even a ziz — all the more so not needed except because of concern; and see Rashi there; and therefore the water is higher than ten from the ground as Tosafos there s.v. "they are accustomed," etc. wrote — otherwise how does he draw with a vessel, behold the vessel sinks in water — see there; and from Tosafos' words it is proven they hold resting in makom patur is required, for if not what is their difficulty — let it be he submerges the vessel within the water below ten and it is karmelit in any case, conveying to the ship via air above ten; and perhaps Rav Huna holds permitted to exchange in rabbinic domains via makom patur; or Tosafos hold forbidden if it did not rest, as in Eruvin daf 90 as above; and perhaps this is Mordechai's proof to rule like R' Yehuda, for Tosafos' difficulty applies — behold the vessel sinks in water and Tosafos' answer is not satisfactory that what "they are accustomed" means the ship does not travel without ten is that the vessel need not sink — the Gemara's language cannot bear this; perforce the vessel sinks and it matters not, for Rav Huna holds permitted to exchange in rabbinic domains via makom patur even if it did not rest as above — end of discussion):

(ד) It requires further study, etc. opening between them — meaning as explained below siman 372 that two courtyards with one wall between them — if there is an opening in the wall that through making an eruv they can move objects via the opening, then their eruv helps; but if there is no opening, even if they make an eruv they cannot move from courtyard to fellow except via throwing above the wall or via holes — for them an eruv does not help; and if so here where reshus harabbim interrupts between the houses, they cannot move from one to the other except via throwing as Shulchan Aruch wrote — if so when they made an eruv it still does not help, nevertheless forbidden to throw:
