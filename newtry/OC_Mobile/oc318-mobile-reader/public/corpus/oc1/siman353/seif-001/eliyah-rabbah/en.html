[1] [Levush] For we have not found, etc. Therefore it is permitted even when both balconies are on the same level. This differs from handing an object across, where liability exists when both platforms are on one level, as in the Mishkan. If they directly face one another, however, even handing across is exempt. So too it appears from Avodat HaKodesh, page 15, and Rabbenu Yerucham.
[2] It is permitted to throw, etc. The Rambam and Gemara indicate that this applies specifically above ten handbreadths, where the object passes from one private domain to another through a makom petur. The Magen Avraham therefore prohibits below ten, and the Bach and Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah write this as obvious. I am astonished, however, because the Tur, Rashba, Shulchan Aruch, and other authorities state the law without qualification, implying that it applies even below ten. Rabbenu Yerucham, page 75, likewise writes that one who throws from one private domain to another with a public domain between them is exempt but prohibited, even below ten and certainly above ten. This implies that the prohibition and permission are alike in both cases. I examined Shabbos 97 and saw that there is no proof from there. The Gemara mentions above ten because according to Rabbi Akiva one is liable below ten, since an object caught in the air is considered as though it rested. According to the Sages, who exempt, it is possible that below ten is also permitted where the two domains made an eruv or belong to one person. The Rambam in chapter 15 may likewise mention above ten because everyone agrees there, or to teach that even above ten it is prohibited without an eruv when one domain is higher. Proof is that the Rambam begins, 'If he threw from one to the other above ten he is exempt,' and concludes, 'provided they made an eruv; even garments and metal utensils may then be thrown.' Examine carefully. See siman 354, seif katan 4. Rabbi Yonatan in Perek HaMotzi and Kol Bo, page 29, in the name of Baal HaHashlamah, imply somewhat that below ten is prohibited, but this can be rejected.
[3] [Levush] And they made an eruv, etc. The Magen Avraham writes that according to the opinion permitting transfer through a makom petur, it is permitted even without an eruv. This requires study, because in siman 346 the Beit Yosef says the Rambam permits such transfer, yet here the Rambam requires an eruv. Moreover, the requirement of an eruv is explicit in the baraita in Shabbos 97 and is accepted by everyone. Also, at the end of siman 355, seif 1, regarding boats, it appears that even when they are ten handbreadths high an eruv is required.
