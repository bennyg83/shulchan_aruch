3) There. Who established an eruv. According to the authority who permits transferring from one domain to another by way of an exempt area, it is permitted even if they did not establish an eruv. Magen Avraham, subsection 1. This means that according to the authority who permits transferring by way of an exempt area when the domains are Rabbinic, as stated in siman 346, se'if 1, it is permitted here. However, many later authorities question this ruling of the Magen Avraham. There, in siman 346, the discussion concerns an object that comes to rest, and for that reason some permit it; here, however, the discussion concerns throwing, in which the object does not come to rest, and it is forbidden. Some explain that his statement refers to the words of Mordechai cited in the gloss below, siman 355, se'if 3, which discusses a case in which the object does not come to rest. See there. See Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 3; Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 1; Ma'amar Mordechai, subsection 2; Nehar Shalom; Eshel Avraham, subsection 1; and Machatzis HaShekel, and examine their words carefully. Nevertheless, we already wrote above, siman 346, subsection 17, that when the object does not come to rest one should be stringent even when the domains are Rabbinic and the transfer is by way of an exempt area. See there. The same therefore applies here. See further below, siman 355, se'if 3, in the gloss.
