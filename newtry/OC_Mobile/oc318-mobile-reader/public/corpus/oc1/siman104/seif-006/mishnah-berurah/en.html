(22) it says "the reader" because this law derives from Kriat Shema, where the same law applies, as written above siman 65 seif 1 in the gloss.

(23) and the same if he mentioned the matter of other days in prayer — such as Shabbat and Yom Tov on a weekday and the like — his law is likewise as if he chatted [Acharonim]; and it will be explained below end of siman 108 in Mishna Berurah, see there.

(24) and if he chatted in Kriat Shema and its blessings, see above siman 65 seif 1 in Mishna Berurah.

(25) meaning, if he chatted in the middle of a blessing with slight speech, even if through this he did not delay long enough to finish that blessing, he returns to the beginning of the blessing; and if through the speech he delayed long enough to finish the entire prayer from beginning to end, he returns to the beginning of the prayer; and per what we explained above in note 16, this law applies only when he chatted due to ones — such as by robbers and the like as above; but if he chatted not due to ones but through mistake or he thought it was permitted, even if he delayed to finish all of it he does not return to the beginning but only to the beginning of the blessing; and if he chatted between blessing and blessing, although he transgressed thereby, nevertheless no correction applies according to all — rather immediately after the speech he completes his prayer; and if he chatted intentionally — requires study in this law: some say he returns immediately to the beginning of the prayer even with slight speech; and some say there is no difference between unwitting and intentional.
