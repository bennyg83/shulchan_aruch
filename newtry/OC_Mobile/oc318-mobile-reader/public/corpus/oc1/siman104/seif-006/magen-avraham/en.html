If he spoke. See siman 114, seif 7, that if he spoke deliberately he returns to the beginning, and see Bach. This requires study regarding one who deliberately spoke during Krias Shema. Perhaps he need not return to the beginning, as implied by the incident of Rabbi Abbahu, who was walking through filthy alleyways. See there. This may be rejected, because there he asked a Torah question, which is not as serious, and perhaps was permitted ab initio. Nowadays, when most people are not careful against speaking during the blessings of Krias Shema, perhaps they are treated as inadvertent, as the Taz writes in Yoreh Deah, siman 99, that one who believes an act permitted is considered inadvertent. See Makkos, chapter 2. The Chiddushei Rashba at the beginning of the second chapter of Berachos implies that even if one deliberately spoke he need not return to the beginning, though perhaps that applies only when he spoke words of Torah.
