“Its law, etc., is like the laws of interruptions.” See Mishnah Berurah, where I wrote that if he spoke briefly, this means even without compulsion, as implied by Derech HaChaim, and this is correct. Know further that whenever we have said in these matters that he returns to the beginning of the blessing, if after the fact he did not do so, he should not now return to the beginning on that account. After the fact we rely upon the Rashba, who holds that in all these cases he returns only to the place where he stopped, and not to the beginning of the blessing. Eliyah Rabbah rules this way, unlike Taz, and Chayei Adam and Nishmas Adam do likewise. This is especially so according to Biur HaGra, who agrees with the Rashba. What we further wrote concerning an intentional interruption, that some say he returns to the beginning, is the view of the Bach, Knesses HaGedolah, Magen Avraham, Eliyah Rabbah, Peri Chadash, Chayei Adam, and Magen Gibborim. It is also evident from Peri Chadash and Biur HaGra that they understand the views of Avi HaEzri, Abudraham in the name of Rav Gershom ben Shlomo, and Orchos Chaim in the name of the Raavad in this manner. The other view, that there is no distinction between inadvertent and intentional speech, is that of Shulchan Aruch, Levush, Olas Tamid, Taz, and Derech HaChaim. Biur HaGra states that the Rashba also follows this view, though he is even more lenient, holding that even after intentional speech one returns only to the place where he stopped and not to the beginning of the blessing, consistent with his view regarding inadvertent speech. Know further that it appears that those who require one to return to the beginning of the prayer after intentional speech apply this even if he spoke between one blessing and another. Taz, who is lenient after the fact in that case, does so only according to his own view that intentional speech in the middle of a blessing requires returning only to the beginning of that blessing, because only that blessing was impaired. According to the view that he returns to the beginning of the prayer, the same should apply between blessings. Shulchan Shlomo does not write this, and his reasoning requires study. I later found that Magen Avraham, siman 183, seif katan 11, rules regarding intentional speech during Bircas HaMazon that one returns to the beginning, as in prayer. There he is discussing speech between blessings. One might answer with difficulty that the three blessings of Bircas HaMazon are considered one unit, as explained in siman 188, Magen Avraham seif katan 8, unlike prayer. The wording of Rav Gershom ben Shlomo cited in Beit Yosef, however, does not imply this distinction.
