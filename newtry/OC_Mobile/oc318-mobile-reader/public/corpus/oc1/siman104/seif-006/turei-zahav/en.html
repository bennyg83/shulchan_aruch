If he spoke in prayer, etc. — in Beit Yosef he brings in the name of Abudraham, and these are his words: Rabbenu Gershom bar Shlomo was asked before us: if one spoke in prayer, must he return or not? And if he returns, does he return to the beginning or to that blessing in which he spoke? And we answered him that he must return to the beginning, for all eighteen blessings are like one blessing — end quote; and so in Eliyah Rabbah in the name of the Raavad; and Beit Yosef wrote on this, and from what he wrote it will be explained that the view of the poskim is not thus; and although one may distinguish that those interruptions were with permission and this without permission — nevertheless it appears this is not grounds to obligate him to return to the beginning; and what he said all eighteen blessings are considered as one — this is not so, but only the first three are considered as one, and likewise the last three, etc. — end quote; and therefore he ruled here that it is only like other interruptions. And I find it more difficult, for the words of the Raavad here contradict what Beit Yosef wrote in his name in siman 65, that he holds like the view of Tosafos, Rosh, and Tur, that if he waited without duress, even if he waited long enough to finish it all, he need not return to the beginning but only to the place he paused; and behold this case without duress is literally like this case of speech here, which is also without permission — so why did he write here that if he spoke in prayer he must return to the beginning? And Mahari Mintz wrote that these words of Rabbenu Gershom refer to intentional speech, and in this they said all eighteen blessings are as one; and he brought proof from what the Tur wrote in siman 114 in the name of Abudraham, that wherever they said he returns to the place he erred — this is when he erred unwittingly; but intentionally and deliberately he returns to the beginning; and therefore he wrote that an error escaped Beit Yosef, since he himself ruled in siman 114 like Abudraham, and he is like Rabbenu Gershom here and here, and disagrees with Rabbenu Gershom. And I wondered: if so, the Tur also erred, God forbid, for in siman 65 he rules that whether in prayer or Keriat Shema, whoever waited, even long enough to finish it all, not due to duress — he does not return except to the place he paused; and it appears to me that not due to duress there means intentional; if so, this contradicts the words of Abudraham, and he himself brings the words of Abudraham in siman 104. And I have a great question per Beit Yosef's understanding of the words of Rabbenu Gershom and the Raavad, who holds like him — the words of the Raavad contradict each other, for in siman 65 Beit Yosef wrote that the Rashba wrote in the name of the Raavad like the view of the Tur that we mentioned, that if he waited long enough for all of it without duress he returns to the place he paused — so why did he write here that if he spoke he returns to the beginning? One may answer that interruption of speech is worse than interruption of waiting — this is not so, for explicitly the Rashba wrote in responsum siman 244, whom Rama brings in siman 488 regarding Megillah: if he waited, even if he spoke, he returns to the place he paused; and Megillah and prayer are equal to that view of the Tur and Raavad who distinguish between waiting due to duress or not, as the Tur wrote explicitly in siman 85. And in my humble opinion, certainly per how our master the author of Beit Yosef understood the words of Rabbenu Gershom and the Raavad — it refers to speech in the middle of a blessing and immediately after the speech when he returns to pray — in this it is well ruled that he need not return except to that blessing in which he spoke, for speech therein is not worse than if he did not say it at all — certainly it suffices when he returns and says it; and this is both for unwitting and intentional speech, one law for them; and if he waited in that speech long enough to finish it all, the matter depends like other interruptions due to waiting mentioned above. And in this all agree, except Rabbenu Gershom does not refer to this — and this is forced from his language that he says 'or he returns to that blessing in which he spoke'; and if it referred to this, that he has not yet begun to pray after the speech, he should have said 'or he returns to the beginning of the blessing in which he spoke,' as the Tur's language here — that it also refers to this law, how he should conduct himself immediately after speech; but in truth Rabbenu Gershom does not refer to this, but when he spoke and finished his prayer and uprooted his feet, and he asked at the outset of this matter in what the Mishnah said: even a king who asks of his welfare he should not answer; and if he transgressed and answered, what is the law — whether unwitting or intentional, as we find regarding writing the Name in Yoreh Deah siman 276, that there too we say even a king of Israel who asks of his welfare he should not answer; and Beit Yosef elaborates there: if he transgressed and answered, whether he may read in that Torah scroll or not; and likewise the questioner asked Rabbenu Gershom: if he finished the entire prayer, who is to say speech causes the prayer to be void or not? And the place of doubt appears to be from what we find in siman 114 regarding words one must say in the eighteenth blessing, such as yaaleh veyavo on Rosh Chodesh — we say if he did not uproot his feet he returns to R'tzei, and if he uprooted he returns to the beginning; and in this Abudraham wrote that if he did so intentionally, even if he did not uproot, it is as uprooting and he needs better repair; and below in siman 114 the reason for this will be written — end quote. And he asked here: if we say the speech he made should be like the law of not saying yaaleh veyavo and he must return — as there he did not say the eighteen blessings properly, so here too one might say it is different here, for in any case he prayed all that was required, only he made the interruption; nevertheless, if he had been reminded after the interruption it would have been possible for him to repair by beginning the blessing, and then the interruption would not harm at all — unlike here, where he already finished his prayer, we have no [reason] to say he should return to pray a second time because of the interruption; whether the interruption is due to waiting or speech, all is asked of him. And if you say he should return — to where should he return to pray again? On this Rabbenu Gershom answered: the interruption is literally like that law of not saying yaaleh veyavo or other matters that return to the beginning — it is as if he did not say the blessing of R'tzei at all; and this is if he uprooted his feet — likewise here, for all blessings of the eighteen impede one another, for if he said only seventeen he did not fulfill; likewise here in that blessing in which one interrupted with speech, it is as if he did not say it at all, since he corrupted the wording, and it is as one who said seventeen blessings; therefore he returns to the beginning if he uprooted his feet. We find an answer to what Beit Yosef challenged on what Rabbenu Gershom said, that all eighteen blessings are one — for only the first three and last three are as one; and this matter does not apply if he finished the prayer and uprooted his feet, for then it is as if he said only seventeen blessings, for all are voided, for we require eighteen corresponding to one another. And therefore Rabbenu Gershom and the Raavad wrote well, and also Beit Yosef and Shulchan Aruch hold simply for the law as he wrote, only he did not explain the matters as we explained; and therefore it entered his mind to dispute and nullify their words; but in truth all their paths are peaceful per what we explained, with God's help. And it appears further: if he spoke between blessing and blessing, he needs no correction even immediately after the speech, but he completes his prayer; and nevertheless we say if it is possible to shorten, he should shorten; but if he waited long enough to finish it all, he returns to the beginning even in this, for speech does not interrupt except in the middle, where it corrupts the wording of the blessing; unlike between blessing and blessing — although it is forbidden to do so, nevertheless he does not corrupt the blessings and he completes his prayer, unless he waited long enough to finish it all, for then they do not join together; and this is similar to one who spoke in the middle of a meal, as the Rashba compared in siman 244 regarding Megillah; but if he waited long enough for all of it, it is similar to food digested in his intestines that he must bless again haMotzi; but if he spoke in the middle, then the blessing was corrupted.
