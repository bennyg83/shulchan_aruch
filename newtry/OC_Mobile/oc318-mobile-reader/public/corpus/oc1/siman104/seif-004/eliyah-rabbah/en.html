[4] “He should not interrupt, etc.” For example, he should not tell another person to remove it. He may, however, walk, because walking is not an interruption, as Beit Yosef writes. Although the preceding seif states that one may not leave his place, walking is not considered an interruption requiring him to begin again from the start of the blessing. Rather, he returns to the point where he stopped. If he interrupted by speaking, however, it is a complete interruption and he must begin again from the start of the blessing. This is also implied by Lechem Chamudos, page 40. This resolves Taz’s objection. See Responsa Beit Yaakov, siman 16.
