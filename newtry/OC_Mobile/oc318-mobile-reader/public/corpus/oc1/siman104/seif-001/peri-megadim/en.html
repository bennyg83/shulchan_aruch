But Taz — Ri holds like Tosafot Berachot 41a that R' Akiva, who would bow with his hands and feet extended and necessarily uproot his place, is permitted even within prayer, were it not that one does not bow, etc.; and the Mechaber in siman 113 seif 1 rules that in the middle one may chat; nevertheless he explains here in seif 2 like Rosh that within prayer one may not walk (Ri explained: after prayer, because one does not bow, etc.; and possibly Ri like Rosh — see there); and in siman 621 seif 4 and Taz 3, see there; and nevertheless one can say that not for a need, only to bow, is forbidden within prayer; and likewise the prayer leader on Yom Kippur is forbidden (and they set up a stand for him as customary and he need not uproot); but for a need, such as a snake, it is certainly permitted, as Magen Avraham wrote note 3; and that we do not find walking is called interruption like speech and he must return to the beginning of the blessing as in seif 5 — Ri, per his view, walking is not called interruption at all, unlike our view. And in siman 8 seif 13 regarding tallit — Taz note 11 and Magen Avraham 17 — specifically from house to house is interruption; here in one house, no; and here one can say from house to house nevertheless it is not interruption regarding a snake, like speech.
