Seif 1 — he should not interrupt, etc. Note: see in Magen Avraham note 1 what he wrote regarding the incident of that pious man — it should read that he was confident, etc., and so is explained in Taz (siman 66); but in truth this is not correct, for how can one say he was guaranteed in this, since free choice exists and one may not rely on miracles. But in my opinion one can say the intent per Ma'ayan HaBerachot: what the minister said to him — who will sue for your blood — meaning even by Heaven he would not be punished, for it is not considered suicide with intent; understand well. And per this one can say the pious man's answer was thus: behold, suicide with intent is forbidden and his sin is too great to bear; although in his own domain a person may do as he wishes — why can he not end his life? But the intent is: if man were created for himself, one could say if he wishes he may end his life; but man was created only to honor the Creator and serve Him — therefore like a king's servant he cannot say he does not wish to serve; so too man cannot waive Heaven's honor and service if Heaven chose him. But this works if in his death he does not honor the Creator; but if even in his death knowingly he honors the Creator, nevertheless l'chatchila it is forbidden, for he cannot exchange this service for that, and perhaps he was created for another purpose; but in any event if he did so there is no punishment, since even in this he honors the Creator. Therefore those who give their lives for kiddush Heaven — even in what they are not obligated to give their soul, even for those who hold it forbidden to give one's soul — nevertheless it is not suicide with intent; and this is what the pious man told him: the minister's main question was that it is like suicide with intent with a great sin — why was he permitted to do so; therefore he told him: since in any event he did not answer him, he intended for Heaven's honor — that is His honor, like a king's servant; therefore since even in this he intended Heaven's honor, in any event the law of suicide with intent does not apply; and if they killed him he would be punished by Heaven — the opposite of what the minister told him "who will sue for your blood"; and it fits well, there's help; understand well. Also one can say he answered him: in other mitzvot besides these three it is forbidden to give one's soul — for since one is not as standing before the king, there is no kiddush Heaven except in these three matters; but in prayer, which is as standing before the king, even in a light matter there is kiddush Heaven, even in private — although to conquer the queen with me in the house; and therefore in prayer, as standing before the king, even a light matter is like these three severe matters and is like in public, and one is obligated to give his soul for it. And this is correct to decide between the views, not like Magen Avraham who equated the matters to each other; only this is my view; understand well. And there is further proof for our words and to explain in his reason: we say above in Berachot regarding one praying behind the synagogue — Eliyahu came, appeared to him as an Arab, etc., took a sword and killed him — behold he was liable to death for this, for certainly Eliyahu did not kill him improperly, and even though this sin does not obligate death — necessarily during prayer, which is as standing before the king, even transgressing anything minor one is liable to death and it is like a trace of idolatry, since it appears as if speaking before his Master; and if so, the poskim wrote and Rama brought (siman 16) regarding one who has relations with a gentile woman — he should be killed and not transgress; and they learned from what zealots strike him — it is proven it is in the category of forbidden relations; so too here, since speaking with another before the king is rebellion, it is a trace of idolatry and he should be killed and not transgress. Through this fits very well what the minister added asking: and if you had answered him what would they have done to you — that is superfluous, and the minister's initial answer would suffice; if so, his a fortiori argument suffices — before the king they would not do so, why extend to say "and if you had answered what would they have done to you." But per what I wrote it is very reasonable, since the minister's main question was how he was permitted to place himself in mortal danger and it is suicide with intent; therefore he answered: if he had done so they would have cut off his head with a sword — behold this is a capital sin and like rebelling against the kingdom, therefore it is a trace of idolatry and he is obligated to give his soul; therefore we say above regarding kings of the nations of the world the pious man does not hold so, but holds he is obligated to give his soul; therefore in any event for us all agree one may be stringent on himself, not like Magen Avraham; and so appears to my humble opinion; understand well.
