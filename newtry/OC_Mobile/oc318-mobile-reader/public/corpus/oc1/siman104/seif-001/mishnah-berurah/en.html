(1) and even a mere gesture is forbidden; if not for a crying child, he may gesture with his hands so he be quiet and they not disturb his prayer; and if it does not help he should distance himself from him and not speak to him. And likewise a distinguished person standing in prayer while the prayer leader waits for him at Kaddish or Kedusha, and he is not pleased that they wait for him — this disturbs his prayer — he may gesture to the prayer leader to pray as usual [Shoel UMeishiv].

(2) and even for monetary loss he may not interrupt. Chayei Adam wrote [kelal 25 seif 9]: one standing in prayer who is uncertain about some law how he should pray — such as if he forgot something in the prayer — may go from his place to a particular place and look there in a book; and if it is permitted to ask the law — requires study; and it appears to me it is permitted.

(3) and the same law applies to a general robber, and he fears lest they kill him.

(4) and it is better to shorten when possible than to deviate from the path [Bach and Ateret Zekenim]; but Peri Megadim wrote one should settle this, for it implies walking for the need of prayer is not called interruption.

(5) meaning he may even ask after his welfare when he estimates that without this there could be danger; and certainly he may respond to him [Ateret Zekenim].
