(7) If he waited in order to finish, etc. — and in Eliyah Zuta I challenged: Bach disagrees with Shulchan Aruch here and wrote and ruled that specifically in duress due to his circumstances — such as a place unfit to read in it because there is feces or water dripping on his knees — for he is like a transgressor who should have been careful from the outset not to come to this, he returns to the beginning; but here, where the duress is general — oxen or a wagon — one need not return to the beginning, for it is not called duress; and so wrote Olas Tamid in his name. And one may question: above in siman 25 he ruled explicitly to distinguish between Keriat Shema and prayer — that in prayer even if duress did not prevent him he returns to the beginning; and so ruled Nachalat Tzvi there in his name — and this requires much study. Also the proof Bach brought here from Tosafos Berachos 33, who wrote one does not return to the beginning — in my humble opinion it is not a proof, for one may say Tosafos holds like the rabbis of France whom Beit Yosef brought in siman 65, that even in prayer with complete duress one does not return to the beginning. Again it was printed from the Taz, and I saw he wondered on Bach — Tosafos refers when he did not wait in order to finish it entirely, for there is a dispute there on 23 per the answer of Rav Ashi — end quote. And I did not reach the end of his view — that there the reason of the one who says he returns to the beginning is because of 'a man was visible' — when he began to pray he needed to relieve himself, as Rashi explained there; but here regarding a scorpion this does not apply, and Tosafos did not need to prove at all that one does not return to the beginning. And with this it appears to resolve what Mahari Mintz wondered on Rama and Levush, who ruled here anonymously and in siman 68 distinguish between duress or not; and so wondered Lechem Chamudos page 24 and 27 and page 40. And per what I wrote nothing is difficult — there is a distinction between prayer and Keriat Shema, and they relied on the poskim that in prayer even without duress [one returns]. Again I saw the Levush wrote in siman 85 on the law of Keriat Shema, and likewise the law in Hallel, prayer, and Megillah — thus it is clear to him the matter requires specifically duress. And per this it is simple Lechem Chamudos and Mahari Mintz have no difficulty on the Levush — it is obvious it refers here also to duress, and he relied on that which is above, as the Levush wrote in his hagahah in siman 65 and 85 explicitly; and so the words of Rama are resolved — for he wrote explicitly in siman 65, and likewise in siman 104 — end quote. And if so, there was no need to emend here that it refers specifically to duress; nevertheless one may say it refers to what we estimate regarding the reader. But a proof: in siman 23, seif 2, 'and see above siman 85' — it shows a place in the law of prayer on the law of Keriat Shema — implies specifically in duress one returns to the beginning; and per this one must say it refers here when there was duress in a matter due to his circumstances as above in siman 65; but oxen and a wagon are not duress, as Bach ruled here. But I saw in the Prisha that he wrote from Maharam of Coucy whom Beit Yosef brought in siman 65 — it is clear that whatever is not from his knowledge is called duress — unlike what Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Rashba, that whatever there is no 'a man was visible' is not called duress, but everything is called duress — end quote. And per this it is well explained that here Levush and Rama [apply] even regarding oxen and a wagon that one returns to the beginning; and in truth this requires study — for these are the words of Tosafos Berachos 22, and there it is explained the matter depends on 'a man was visible'; and so it appears in Piskei Tosafos there; and so it appears in the Tur and Rabbeinu Yerucham. Nevertheless in the language of the Rosh in the chapter 'One whose dead lies before him' it appears like the words of the Prisha; and so in Abudraham. And it further appears to me that even though we say the matter depends on 'a man was visible,' nevertheless in prayer one should be stringent in every duress, since in any case for the Rif and Rambam in prayer regarding the six [blessings] one must finish it entirely and returns to the beginning even without duress; and so I found Magen Avraham ruled in siman 65; and in particular per what I explained in siman 65, for so is the view of the Raavad.
