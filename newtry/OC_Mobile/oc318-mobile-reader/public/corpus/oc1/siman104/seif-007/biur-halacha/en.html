“And he shall be considered as answering.” See Mishnah Berurah. See also Beit Yosef and Darchei Moshe, who explain that the basic question whether one may remain silent and listen depends on a dispute. Some say that since hearing is equivalent to answering, he is considered to have answered in actuality and this is forbidden as an interruption. Nevertheless, the ruling is lenient because of the established custom. According to our custom, where we hold that although hearing is equivalent to answering it is not literally speech, there is room to consider whether a person who needed to relieve himself while another person discharged his obligation in prayer has fulfilled his obligation. Although a baal keri may not have another person discharge his obligation in prayer, as Tosafos, Berachos 20b, s.v. “As we find,” write at the end of their comment, perhaps a baal keri is more stringent. Regarding other blessings, see Mishnah Berurah above, siman 92, seif katan 6. See also Peri Megadim, who raises another similar question.
