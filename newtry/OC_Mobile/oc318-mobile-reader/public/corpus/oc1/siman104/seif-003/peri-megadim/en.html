If he chatted — Taz elaborated: what Ra'avad contradicted himself — here he ruled that intentional chatting returns to beginning, and in siman 65 he holds like Rosh that even if he delayed to finish all without ones he does not return to beginning; and this without ones is intentional; the examiner in Chiddushei HaRash 33a: Ra'avad holds "pushed away" returns — if not pushed away, no; nevertheless ones applies possibly. Also the simple distinction between speech and silence interruption — certainly what the Gemara Yevamot brought, Bach brought that silence is like speech — namely regarding delay to finish all; but without delay certainly there is distinction between speech and silence, for in speech even without delay to finish even that blessing he returns to beginning of blessing per Tosafot 33a and as the Mechaber wrote in seif 5; and in silence, even if he delayed to finish that blessing he does not return to beginning of blessing — and certainly without delay; so it is obvious in what measure you would give this; and certainly speech is not in all matters like silence. And from siman 488 seif 5 "he chatted" — meaning intentional — Rabbeinu Yonah wrote that even if he delayed to finish all he does not return to beginning; and here Rabbeinu Yonah ruled in siman 65 and 87 and 88 and 84 like Rosh and Ra'avad that one does not distinguish between Kriat Shema and prayer and Hallel and Megillah at all, only between anus or not; if so why did he rule in Megillah that intentional chatting does not return to beginning (even if we say the Mechaber here deals with unwitting — Magen Avraham note 6 on this); and Rashba there — one can say he distinguishes between Kriat Shema, etc., unlike our view as said; nevertheless Magen Avraham note 6 wrote to distinguish here between Kriat Shema and prayer; and likewise Megillah — intentional chatting does not return to beginning, unlike prayer; and it appears there is duplication in Taz's words on Ra'avad's difficulty mentioned above; and Taz raised that certainly if he chatted intentionally between blessing and blessing all agree he begins the second blessing and that suffices; and even if he chatted in the middle of a blessing, if he returns to beginning of blessing it suffices; and there is a dispute in siman 114 seif 7, where there intentionally he did not say R'tzei in Avodah and finished the blessing — thus they penalized him to return to beginning of prayer; unlike here where he chatted in the middle of a blessing — if he returns to beginning of blessing it suffices, for it is as if he did not say that blessing at all. And the question of Ri Graz was: he chatted in the middle of a blessing intentionally and finished that blessing and prayed more — they ruled he must return to beginning of prayer, for it is like siman 114 seif 7; and if unwitting: if he uprooted his feet he returns to beginning of prayer, and if not he returns to beginning of that blessing.
