[3] “In the supplications that follow, etc.” For example, during Elokai Netzor and the other supplications. Regarding Rabbi Akiva, the Gemara says that a person would leave him in one corner and find him in another because of his many bowings, and this occurred during the supplications after the prayer. Olas Tamid writes that if the chazzan is honored with opening the ark, which people regard to some degree as a mitzvah, there is no prohibition against leaving his place. He brings proof from the case of Rabbi Akiva, but nevertheless writes that the chazzan should first say, “May the words of my mouth, etc.” I do not know why proof is needed or what prohibition could apply, for every day we see the chazzan leave his place and recite Tachanun. On Monday and Thursday he recites VeHu Rachum, walks to the bimah, and reads from the Torah before Kaddish Tiskabel, in which he says Oseh Shalom. Rather, this prohibition applies specifically to an individual praying. The chazzan, since he already prayed silently, need not immediately take three steps back, as explained below in siman 123. See also the end of siman 621 regarding the chazzan leaving his place during the prayer.
