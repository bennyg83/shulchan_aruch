(13) If he paused. This applies even to mere silence without speech, and even between one blessing and another.
(14) All of it. Even if he is near the end of the prayer, the measure is calculated from the beginning of the prayer to its end.
(15) He returns, etc. If he did not return to the beginning but instead returned to the place where he stopped and completed the prayer, he must return to the beginning and pray the entire prayer again. This applies where there was complete duress. In a case of robbers or the like mentioned above, however, after the fact he has fulfilled his obligation, because perhaps the halachah follows the lenient authorities cited above, who maintain that this is not considered duress.
(16) To the beginning. According to our practice, following the Rama’s ruling above in siman 65 regarding Krias Shema, in accordance with the authorities who maintain that one returns to the beginning only when the interruption resulted from duress, the same rule applies to prayer. Some maintain, however, that our case, involving a scorpion or ox approaching him, or an interruption caused by robbers and the like, is also considered an interruption caused by duress, because at that time he was unable to pray, and he therefore returns to the beginning. Others maintain that this is not considered duress unless the person himself was unfit to pray or the place was unfit, as opposed to another type of duress. The Magen Avraham and many other Acharonim conclude that regarding prayer, other forms of duress are also considered duress, since some Rishonim maintain that during prayer, if one paused long enough to complete the whole prayer, he returns to the beginning even without any duress. Regarding Krias Shema and all other matters, however, duress exists only where the person or place was unfit.
(17) And if not. Meaning, he did not remain silent long enough to complete the entire prayer. Nevertheless, he paused for a significant period, because if he paused only long enough to complete that blessing, all authorities agree that he returns only to the place where he stopped. See Biur Halachah for what we wrote in the name of Derech HaChaim.
(18) He returns to the beginning. The Mechaber maintains that prayer is more stringent in this respect than Krias Shema. Therefore, during Krias Shema or its blessings, after an interruption it suffices to return to the place where one stopped, but here one must return to the beginning of the blessing, because a lengthy pause spoiled the blessing. This applies specifically where the pause occurred in the middle of a blessing. Between blessings, after the fact there is no concern so long as he did not pause long enough to complete the whole prayer. See Biur Halachah.
(19) The blessing. The Chayei Adam writes that after the fact, if one did not return to the beginning of the blessing and has already completed it, he may not go back. Magen Gibborim disagrees entirely with the Shulchan Aruch’s ruling and writes that according to the essential law, if one did not pause long enough to complete the whole prayer, it is not considered an interruption and he returns only to the place where he stopped. Tosafos’ ruling that he returns to the beginning of the blessing concerns a case where he paused long enough to complete the whole prayer, and Tosafos maintain that this type of duress is not considered duress even regarding prayer. Biur HaGra agrees with the Rashba’s view cited in Beit Yosef. It appears from his words that in every case one returns only to the place where he stopped, unless he paused long enough to complete the whole prayer because of duress, in which case he returns to the beginning of the prayer.
(20) In the first three, etc. All of these are considered one blessing. Therefore, even if he did not pause, he returns to the beginning of the prayer or to Retzei. According to what we wrote in the name of Biur HaGra and Magen Gibborim, here too he need return only to the place where he stopped.
(21) He returns to the beginning. The Chayei Adam writes that this applies specifically where he already completed the blessing. If he interrupted in the middle of a blessing, he returns only to the beginning of that blessing, because returning while still in the middle is not considered repetition, but merely correction of the wording.
