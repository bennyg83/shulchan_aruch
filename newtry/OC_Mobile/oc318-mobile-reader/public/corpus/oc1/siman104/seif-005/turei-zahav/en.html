He returns to the beginning. Although in siman 65 the ruling concerning Krias Shema is that he need return only to the point where he stopped, the Amidah is more stringent. The Rif proves this from the ruling in the chapter “Mi Shemeiso” concerning water flowing upon one’s knees, where if he paused, etc., he returns to the beginning, while concerning Krias Shema he returns to the point where he stopped. The Rif brings proof that this is the conclusion at the end of tractate Rosh Hashanah, and likewise regarding Hallel, shofar blasts, and the Megillah. Tosafos, the Rosh, and the Tur, however, do not distinguish between Krias Shema and the Amidah. Rather, they distinguish between a pause caused by coercion, which is more severe, and another pause. The Rama rules this way in siman 65. Here the Tur did not need to state that one who paused long enough to complete the entire prayer returns to the beginning, because he already wrote in siman 85, also regarding the Amidah, that there is a distinction between a pause caused by coercion and one not so caused. The Shulchan Aruch’s ruling here that if one paused he returns to the beginning is accepted by all. Even the Rosh and Tur agree, differing only in the reason. According to the Shulchan Aruch, the reason is the greater stringency of the Amidah. According to the Rosh and Tur, it is because the pause was caused by coercion. The Rama therefore had no need to comment on the Shulchan Aruch here. My father-in-law, the Bach, needlessly disputes the Shulchan Aruch and Rama here, maintaining that even if one paused long enough to complete the entire prayer he returns only to the point where he stopped. He relies on Tosafos in the chapter “Ein Omdin,” page 34, concerning one who interrupts the Amidah because of a scorpion. Tosafos write that they did not find that one who interrupted returns to the beginning, since the Gemara does not say that if he interrupted he returns to the beginning, but only that he returns to the beginning of the blessing. In truth, Tosafos discuss a case in which he interrupted without pausing long enough to complete the entire prayer. There is a dispute concerning such a case in the chapter “Mi Shemeiso,” page 24, according to Rav Ashi’s answer, and here everyone agrees that he returns to the point where he stopped. If, however, he paused long enough to complete the entire prayer, it is obvious that according to all opinions he returns to the beginning.
