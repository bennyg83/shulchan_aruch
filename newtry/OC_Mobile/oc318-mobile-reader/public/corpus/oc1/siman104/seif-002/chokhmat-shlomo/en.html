(66) If he chatted in prayer, his law regarding returning is like the law of interruptions, etc. Note: Beit Yosef understood Ra'avad's view that for chatting one returns to the beginning like all eighteen blessings; and wrote most poskim disagree; and Taz elaborated to explain Ra'avad's words. And I wonder on all of them how they did not see the gemara in Ta'anit 3b regarding what R' Chiya said in rainy season: if he did not say "Who makes the wind blow," they do not return him; if he did not say "Who makes the rain fall," they return him; and not only that, but even if he said "Who removes the wind" and "Who clears the dew," they do not return him; understand well. And it is difficult for him — it should be as if it were not, since he did not restrain; nevertheless since he mentioned what is irrelevant to prayer, it is interruption in the middle of prayer in what is irrelevant, and is no less than idle chatter which is interruption; and necessarily for chatting in the middle, even in the Amidah, he does not return to the beginning — how much more in other blessings he does not return; and this is clear proof. And in this I saw Rif cited this statement, but Rambam and Rosh omitted the end of the gemara's words regarding if he said "Who removes the wind and clears the dew" they do not return him — wondrous why; requires study. And what appeared in its explanation: they hold like Ra'avad that for chatting one returns to the beginning, but hold this depends on the dispute in Berachot whether if he waited long enough to finish all he returns to the beginning — then if due to ones he returns when waiting to finish all, then for chatting even without waiting he also returns to the beginning, for the law of chatting is actual interruption in speech without waiting, like waiting without speech to finish all; and they equate the measure of waiting to finish all and actual speech even without waiting to finish all. Granted for waiting without action, since he did nothing, only we say waiting is interruption — we require the measure of waiting, i.e., long enough to finish all; otherwise it is not considered waiting; but how is actual interruption in speech without measure interruption? Therefore for one who says even when waiting to finish all he does not return, although it is the measure of waiting, necessarily he holds interruption does not harm in the middle, and from this chatting in speech, although interruption, also does not return. But for one who says waiting long enough to finish returns, necessarily he holds interruption harms in the middle; only without waiting he does not return from the principle that without measure he did nothing and it is not considered interruption; and from this if he actually chatted, certainly it is interruption and he returns even without waiting. Therefore Rambam and Rosh hold R' Chiya would hold like one who says in Berachot even when waiting to finish all he does not return — if so, likewise when interrupting with speech he does not return; but for us who rule that when waiting to finish all he returns — if so, likewise when interrupting with speech without waiting he returns. Therefore they omitted this, for in any event from the reason of interruption they return him; and Ra'avad has support from Rambam and Rosh; and Rif would hold like the poskim that for chatting he does not return — therefore they cited it; understand well, for it is correct with Hashem's help. And although Rif rules in Keriat Shema even when waiting to finish he does not return, nevertheless he holds in prayer one returns to the beginning when waiting — and he decided the halacha between R' Eliezer and R' Yochanan that in prayer he returns and in Keriat Shema he does not; but they themselves only divide for one who says he does not return — even in prayer he does not return; and for one who says he returns — even in Keriat Shema he returns; only Rif decided halacha by compromise that in Keriat Shema he does not return and in prayer he returns; and it fits well; understand well. And see in my work Toldot Moshe Nachmanides in Yoreh De'ah Laws of Interest siman 160 in a responsum to the community of Tolchin, where we wrote incidentally on the custom in our time to speak mundane words in the middle of prayer — what is their law; see there; understand well.
