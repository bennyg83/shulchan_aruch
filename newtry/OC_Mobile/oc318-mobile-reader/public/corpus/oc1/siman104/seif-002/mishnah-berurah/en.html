(7) and siman 90 seif 27 is different, for there it is a need of prayer.

(8) and one should rebuke prayer leaders who uproot from their place on Yom Kippur when they reach "and we bow" in the order of the service, as brought siman 621 seif 4 in the gloss — rather they set up a stand for him as customary between him and the ark; and in the service they remove the stand and he need not uproot his feet.

(9) for then it is permitted if the uprooting is for some mitzvah — such as our custom that the prayer leader immediately after the repetition uproots his feet and sits for Tachanun in places where they are accustomed to say Tachanun sitting; and likewise he ascends the bimah for Torah reading even though he has not yet reached "may it be accepted," for with "may it be accepted" he takes the three steps; or as we say in the gemara regarding R' Akiva, that a person would place him in this corner and find him in another corner from abundant bowing and prostrations — and that was in supplications after prayer; but not for a need it is forbidden to move from his place until he takes the three steps [Magen Avraham]. Olat Tamid wrote: after he finished the prayer, before he said "Who makes peace," there is no prohibition if he uprooted his feet for some mitzvah — such as if they honored him to open the ark at Avinu Malkeinu, which the public considers somewhat a mitzvah; nevertheless it appears one should say beforehand "may they be acceptable," etc.
