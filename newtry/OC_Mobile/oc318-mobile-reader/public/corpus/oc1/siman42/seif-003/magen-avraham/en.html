A cloth that he designated. It appears to me that if a minor placed tefillin inside it, the cloth becomes forbidden. But where an item becomes forbidden through designation alone, a minor's designation is ineffective, as Chullin 13 teaches that minors possess legally effective action but not legally effective intent. It is obvious that one cannot forbid property that is not his, as explained at the end of Yoreh Deah, siman 349. If one stole cloth and made it into a tefillin pouch, he acquires it through physical alteration and renders it forbidden. See Bava Kama 104.
Permanently. If one made a pouch for tefillin, then even if he placed tefillin inside intending later to remove them, the pouch is forbidden. The same applies if he had an existing pouch, added something to it for the sake of tefillin, and then placed tefillin inside intending later to remove them. It remains forbidden until he removes the addition. If he placed tefillin inside once intending to use it permanently for them, it is forbidden even without prior designation. I derived all this from Tur, Yoreh Deah, siman 364, and Beit Yosef there. Nimukei Yosef in chapter 7 of Sanhedrin likewise writes this. Shiltei HaGibborim in chapter 3 of Berachos does not.
For the sacred object itself. This means the parchment or the batim. Tefillin straps, however, do not require processing for their own sake because they are accessories. Designation of accessories is ineffective. Nimukei Yosef explains the dispute of the Sages and Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel in Menachos this way. This implies that Nimukei Yosef follows Rashi's explanation: according to the one who says designation is ineffective, processing for its own sake is unnecessary. Therefore, for the sacred object itself, which requires processing for its own sake, designation is effective, unlike straps. See siman 32, seif 37, and siman 33, seif 3, where we rule like Tosafos. It requires study, because Nimukei Yosef himself in Hilchos Tefillin follows Tosafos. Nevertheless, Ramban in Milchamos, first chapter of Sukkah, writes at length concerning the principal law. He concludes that even though processing must be for the sake of the mitzvah, one may still change the item because designation is ineffective. Objects used for a mitzvah, such as tzitzis, shofar, lulav, and Chanukah lights, do not become forbidden merely because they were made for that purpose. They may be used for mundane matters. See siman 21 and siman 638, end of seif 1. Even regarding sacred objects, a stipulation permits mundane use, since some authorities permit it in any event. Levush and Bach write likewise. A get is not considered sacred. Bach, unlike Olas Tamid. If one already began writing sacred material upon parchment or paper, he may not afterward write mundane matters upon it. For accessories of sanctity, a stipulation is effective even to allow their use while they remain sacred, as explained in siman 154, seif 8. It does not permit degrading use, as explained in siman 151, seif 11.
