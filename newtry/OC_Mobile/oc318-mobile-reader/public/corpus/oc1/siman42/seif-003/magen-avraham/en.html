A sudar that he designated — it seems to me that a minor who wrapped tefillin in it is forbidden; but something forbidden by designation alone is not forbidden by a minor’s designation, as stated in Chullin, page 13: they have action but no thought. It is obvious that a person cannot forbid what is not his, as written in Yoreh Deah, end of siman 258. If they stole a garment and made a tefillin pouch from it, they acquire it through change of form and it is forbidden; see Bava Kamma, page 104.

Forever — and if he made a pouch for tefillin, even if he wrapped with intent to empty it, it is forbidden; and the same if there was a pouch and he added something for tefillin and wrapped with intent to empty it — forbidden until he removes what he added. If he wrapped once with intent always to wrap in it, even if he did not designate, it is forbidden — all this I learned from the words of Taz, Yoreh Deah, siman 364, see there in Beit Yosef; and so in Noda B'Yehuda, chapter 7 of Sanhedrin; and in Shevut Yaakov, chapter 3 of Berachot, he did not write thus.

For the body of kedushah — meaning the parchment or the titzah; but tefillin straps do not require processing leshemah, for they are used items, and designation of used items does not count — as stated there in Menachot in the dispute of the Rabbis and Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel — so far. Noda B'Yehuda implies that he holds like Rashi, who says designation does not count — therefore no processing leshemah is needed; therefore for the body of kedushah, which requires processing leshemah, designation counts — not so for straps. And see siman 32, seif 37, and siman 33, seif 3, that we rule like Tosafot — uncertain, for the later authority himself wrote in Laws of Tefillin like Tosafot — uncertain. Nevertheless, in the fundamental law the Ramban wrote in Milchamot, first chapter of Sukkah, and elaborated there, and concluded: “And in what the Meor wrote, Rabba does not hold designation does not count for the body of kedushah, because he said the reason — ‘used items, used items we learn’ — to exclude the heifer whose neck is broken, which is kedushah itself, he said nothing; for scrolls of writing too are sanctified by virtue of the writing — when the writing goes, their kedushah goes, as stated in chapter 16 of Shabbos; therefore they are not comparable to the heifer, which is kedushah itself,” etc. The essence of his words: even though it requires processing leshemah, nevertheless it is permitted to change them, for designation does not count; and Tosafot wrote that also for the heifer we rule like Rabbi Yochanan, that it is not removed until it is decapitated — if so, kal vachomer for tefillin. Know that all this is in kedushah such as tefillin; but tashmishei mitzvah such as tzitzit, shofar, lulav, Chanukah candle — even if he made them for this purpose, they are not forbidden, and it is permitted to use them for mundane purposes — as in the Meor; and see siman 21 and siman 638, seif 6. Even for something of kedushah, a condition helps to use it for mundane purposes, for otherwise some permit, as above; and so the Levush and Bach. A get is not called something of kedushah [Bach, not like some say]. However, if he began to write on the parchment or paper, it is forbidden afterward to write mundane words on it; but for tashmishei kedushah a condition helps to use them even while still in their kedushah [as written in siman 154, seif 8]; but for an indecent use a condition does not help, as written in siman 151, seif 11.

He wrapped it — meaning temporarily, and he did not designate it to remain there forever.
