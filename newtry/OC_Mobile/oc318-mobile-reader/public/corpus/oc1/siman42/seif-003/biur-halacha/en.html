One who designated, etc. — See Mishnah Berurah in what he wrote — even by speech alone; and this is from Darchei Halacha who wrote so, although Pri Megadim is uncertain whether even thought alone helps — I did not wish to conceal this, for it seems surprising to me why the poskim ignored Tosafot in Sanhedrin 48a s.v. "they placed," who conclude finally that even designation by speech does not help unless he took it and said "this shall be for a scroll" or for a dead person — as cited. And one should not push that Tosafot speak only for the view that designation is significant — unlike for us, who also require narrowing, which helps even weak designation; but the plain meaning of the passage does not imply this in the Gemara passage and Abbaye who said, etc. And likewise from "he added a partition," etc. — it implies that mere designation alone does not help even for Rava — as cited in Tosafot. And in any case, for mere thought one certainly should not be strict. And know: what we wrote that thought alone for designation is insufficient refers specifically when he did not narrow it immediately at the time of designation, but only afterward and did not think then at all — only placed tefillin in it without specification and emptied it afterward. But if this thought was at the time he narrowed it — behold, he narrowed it with intent of permanence and it is forbidden even if he emptied it afterward, for it is considered as if he narrowed and designated it, as below. Later I found in Ezrat HaKohen in the novelae of Ra'avad in Yoreh De'ah siman 364 in the name of the novelae of the Ran, who also holds as we suggested at the start of our words — that sometimes even designation by speech does not help. I will copy what he wrote there [regarding a dead person, and likewise regarding tefillin, as Magen Avraham wrote]: if when he placed tefillin in some object he designated explicitly that it should be set apart for this, or thought so in his heart — it is forbidden even without an act, even if he emptied it afterward for tefillin. But if the designation was beforehand and he did not then place tefillin in it, and afterward without other consent [for if he agreed afterward to place in it only with intent to empty it, the Ran holds it is permitted in all cases, as will be written later in his name] he placed tefillin in it — it depends whether the prior designation was by an act: it is forbidden by placing tefillin therein simply afterward, even if he emptied it afterward for tefillin; but if the designation was only by speech, it is not forbidden, since the designation was not at the time of placing the tefillin — end quote. And behold, his first law is simple and agreed by almost all poskim, as we ourselves wrote at the start; but in his second law something new arose. Later I also found in Eliyah Rabbah, who copied to halachah the words of Shulchan Gavoah that mere speech is not designation; and it is obvious that what Magen Avraham wrote from Shulchan Gavoah's words does not imply otherwise — he was not referring to designation by speech. Therefore practically — requires investigation regarding designation by speech alone. Nevertheless it seems the Ran agrees with Tosafot that if he took it and said "this shall be for tefillin" — it helps, for this is considered like an act, as Tosafot proved there from "they gave it to a scribe" — as cited.

To wrap tefillin in it. — It seems to me that only if at the time of designation he already had tefillin does the designation take effect; but if he made a pouch for tefillin he would acquire later — designation does not take effect in this, and afterward it is like he narrowed it and did not designate it — permitted if only temporarily. And I support this from Sanhedrin 48a, that one who built for the living — as cited in Rashi; and it is obvious.

Tefillin, etc. — See in Mishna Berurah seif katan 1 in what I wrote, that even though tefillin were placed inside it, etc. See in Chayei Adam, general rule 14, seif 21, in what he wrote, “and it seems to me,” etc. It is obvious that this is a scribal error, and one must say that even though tefillin were placed inside it, etc., for otherwise it would be only a tashmish of a tashmish; nevertheless his words are difficult, for he compares this to the law of Magen Avraham cited above in siman 40, seif katan 2, and from his words it seems that per Magen Avraham this is forbidden — and it is not clear, for behold it was made from the outset also for a tallit, which is for mundane use, and therefore it does not have the status of tashmishei kedushah, as if one stipulated explicitly that it would be permitted to use it for mundane purposes — and as Magen Avraham wrote similarly in siman 34, seif katan 6; see there. And that which Magen Avraham forbids in siman 40 cited above to use the bayit until one places it again inside a vessel with a name — that is different, for Magen Avraham’s reasoning is that since it is also designated for tefillin it becomes two vessels. What will you say — that since it is also designated for a tallit, there is no kedushah on this case at all? Behold, a name does not depend on kedushah at all, for even a hundred vessels, one atop the other, in general a tashmish of a tashmish has no kedushah, and nevertheless a name is forbidden there, for a name is not considered a cover except on something that is not always designated for it; therefore Magen Avraham forbids there, for in a tallit case one is accustomed always to cover the tefillin as well. Not so in our matter, for the prohibition to use applies only to something that bears the name of tashmishei kedushah, and this was not made from the outset also for mundane use. Perhaps Chayei Adam holds that even if one stipulated that it would be permitted to use it for some mundane matter, it is permitted only for that specific matter.

Forever — See in Mishna Berurah seif katan 12 in what he wrote: “some say,” etc. — that is the view of Eliyah Rabbah; see there in what he brought from the responsum of Chavot Yair, that it is implied thus; and in what he wrote from the words of the Gra, etc., that is from what he wrote as the source for this law of “forever,” as written in the gloss, and as above in seif 2 [for it is obvious that this is to prove that the Gemara does not deal with a stipulation that it be only temporarily, or another condition that diminishes the designation; and in what he wrote, and as the Rosh wrote in the name of the Yerushalmi, he leaned on this to resolve the difficulty of Peri Megadim in Magen Tzedek: from where do we know that a condition helps even to lower them to mundane use — for there in Menachot it deals with lowering to minor kedushah, and on this he hinted, and as the Rosh wrote, etc.; see there, that it deals even with lowering to mundane use] (and the author of Damascus Eliezer applied the words of the Gra to another matter without need). And apparently, per the words of Eliyah Rabbah cited above, the Gemara need not say in Berachot 23b, “that which he did not designate,” see there — and one may reject this.

And he wrapped it in it, etc. — See in Mishna Berurah in what he wrote regarding a minor who wrapped, etc.; and see in Peri Megadim, who wrote that a minor who wraps is not forbidden except when he wrapped it with intent of permanence, or his father designated it for him — for otherwise it is as “he wrapped it in it and did not designate,” for he has no thought; and from Magen Avraham it does not seem so, and likewise the Gra and Chayei Adam ruled like Magen Avraham. The difficulty of Peri Megadim can easily be resolved, apparently; and see below.

Tefillin one time — Even at the time he now wraps them for the first time after designation, if he wrapped them together with money as well, nevertheless kedushah takes effect, since he designated them at first for tefillin alone; and therefore it is forbidden to do so unless he retracts explicitly from the first designation — then a later statement comes and nullifies the earlier statement. Proof from Berachot 23b, and the other baraita: “one may not wrap,” see there. One should not reject that the baraita deals specifically where he wrapped the tefillin first; but at one time — if so, why does the Gemara need to resolve that the other baraita deals where he did not designate? It would be resolved simply: with his money at the very same time. Rather, certainly as we wrote. It is also possible to say that if he made a pouch for tefillin and afterward wrapped money in it, and afterward wrapped tefillin in it — it is forbidden afterward to wrap coins in it, since at the time he wrapped the money he did not annul explicitly the designation. Perhaps this is considered annulling explicitly — uncertain. Know further that which Magen Avraham wrote in the name of the Tur, that if he made the pouch for tefillin, even if afterward he wrapped with intent to empty it — it is forbidden: the Ran in his novellae, chapter “Nigmar haDin” [and brought in the novellae of Raavad in Yoreh Deah, siman 364, see there] does not hold thus. Nevertheless one should not be lenient in practice, for the words of Tosafot there derive from the Ramban in Sefer Toladot Adam. A hand-tefillin case — see in Peri Megadim in siman 154, who leans to say that it does not bear the name of tashmishei kedushah, for the bayit itself bears only the name of tashmishei kedushah, not the shin; and in Magen Avraham I explained with several proofs that this is not so, and Peri Megadim himself wrote there finally on this — uncertain; and also in this siman he himself wrote the opposite. Further, let no posek be omitted to inform us that this law of Rabbenu Chananel, “a sudar that he designated to wrap in it,” etc., deals specifically with head tefillin; and further, from its place it is decided in Berachot 23b, for the Gemara juxtaposes two baraitot on this matter — if so, let us interpret that this baraita, “a person may wrap his tefillin with his money,” deals with hand tefillin, and one could say that “his tefillin” implies both; for otherwise let it teach “tefillah.” If so, it is proven explicitly that hand tefillin are also forbidden, for presumably in the other baraita it also teaches, “a person may not wrap his tefillin with his money,” as the plain sense of the Gemara implies; see there. With difficulty one could say that the prohibition is because of the straps on them, which have the knot of the Divine Name, for per Rashi that is the body of the kedushah — but if so, how will the later authorities be resolved, and the other poskim who hold not like Rashi — which implies that this law of Rabbenu Chananel applies to hand tefillin as well.

It is forbidden to wrap in it, etc. — See in the responsum of Shaar Ephraim, siman 21, who raised that whatever exceeds the space needed for tefillin is not called a need of tefillin; therefore he permitted there a tefillin pouch that has two pockets, one under the other, that one may place the tefillin below and money in the pocket above it; see there [in Beit Yosef].

In such a case — See Mishna Berurah; and see in the responsum of Mishkenot Yaakov, siman 55, that ink made for writing STa"M is also designation for the body of kedushah, and it is superior to it, and it is forbidden to use it for mundane purposes unless he stipulated from the outset. I saw in Mishnat Avraham that he challenged this from Yoreh Deah, siman 274, in Shach and Taz there, seif katan 3, 4, 5; see there. In truth one must examine this greatly, for as his words imply in the Ramban in Milchamot, first chapter of Sukkah — even though the Ramban himself is lenient there — he disagrees on the fundamental law that designation for the body of kedushah counts; not so for us. His difficulty can be resolved: perhaps there it deals where it was not made with intent for STa"M, and the writing itself is not like wrapping with intent of permanence, for perhaps only the ink needed for writing was sanctified, and there extra ink spilled beyond what was needed onto the letter; see there — similar to what Shaar Ephraim wrote above — uncertain. Therefore it is better to stipulate from the outset at the time of making the ink.

For the body of kedushah — See in Mishna Berurah in what he wrote; or processing leather for straps is from the later authority cited in Magen Avraham, and Tzemach Tzedek and Eliyah Rabbah brought it for halachah. Nevertheless one must examine this somewhat, for Peri Megadim remained finally uncertain on this point of Tzemach Tzedek: per Rashi, who holds that the place of the knot of the dalet and yod is kedushah itself, it follows that processing is designation for the body of kedushah. However, I saw in Tzemach Tzedek that he hinted there to examine in Tosafot there in Megillah 26b, for in truth almost all poskim disagree with Rashi, and Shulchan Aruch too below in siman 154 rules plainly not like him. Further, it seems possible that even per Rashi, that the yod and dalet are the Divine Name — nevertheless, as long as the strap is not tied in the titzah, there is still no designation upon it through processing, for it is not comparable to processing parchment, where processing is preparation to write upon it, and therefore immediately when processing is done it is designation; not so here, where the main matter is donning on the head and arm — tying the strap in the titzah is designation for this mitzvah, not the processing. For even after processing there is still no name of “strap” on the leather, and even after cutting it into straps the strap is not yet designated for the mitzvah until he ties it in the titzah; then it is designated for the body of kedushah, namely the donning through which the yod and shin were sanctified — for it is obvious that through manufacture alone the letters are not yet sanctified. This reasoning is explicitly mentioned in the Meor in Sukkah: “The same applies to tefillin straps: if one comes to change them after processing, before joining them to the titzah, he has permission — they are not forbidden by designation, even though he processed them for their sake” — see there at length. Evidently the Meor holds that there is no designation for the mitzvah as long as he did not tie it in the titzah; and the same for us, who hold that designation for the body of kedushah applies — as long as he did not tie it in the titzah, there is no designation upon it. Nevertheless, where it is not necessary, it is better to be stringent to remove all concerns, for apparently from the later authority who is stringent about processed leather for the batim, this reasoning does not follow; and in a case of need one may be lenient in all respects, for Raavad is alone in being stringent about designation for a strap.

It counts — See in Magen Avraham, who leans to rule like the Ramban, who disagrees on this; and per the Gra in his commentary, who resolves well the view of Raavad and the later authority who hold this view; and he also brought that Ritva too holds that designation for the body of kedushah counts. It is very difficult to be lenient against all these great authorities. Again I found one of the later authorities who also wrote that in practice one should be stringent like the first view; and likewise in the responsum of Mishkenot Yaakov, siman 55, he also decides to rule like Rama and not like Magen Avraham. Know that what I wrote in Mishna Berurah — “however, if he began to write,” etc. — and even a condition does not help in this, is proven from Eliyah Rabbah and Peri Megadim and other later authorities; and in truth this is also proven from Magen Avraham for one who examines it. Chayei Adam is lenient about a condition even here — and I do not know from where.

He wrapped it — See in Mishna Berurah in what he wrote: “or that he wrapped it in it,” etc., that then he had in mind to wrap permanently, etc. Even though in Peri Megadim it seems that we require wrapping explicitly with intent to wrap forever — I omitted this, for all the later authorities did not mention this; they only wrote plainly with intent always to wrap in it. And on all of them, in the Ran that I copied above, it is written explicitly that thought also helps for this. Further, one may say that Peri Megadim also concedes to this, and did not use this wording only because he wished to conclude there that if he wrapped explicitly with intent to empty it, it is permitted even if he wrapped many times, as long as he did not sew a pouch for tefillin; see there — he used “explicitly” here too, with difficulty.

It is permitted to wrap, etc. — See in Mishna Berurah in what he wrote: “some say,” etc. — this is from Rashi, Berachot 23b, s.v. “with his money,” etc.; see there at length. And see in Noda B'Yehuda, chapter “Nigmar haDin,” who also wrote that this applies when the tefillin were removed; but as long as the tefillin are on them, it is forbidden — and Eliyah Rabbah and the Gra copied this, that it is permitted to wrap coins together with tefillin, and cited this in Berachot 23b. It is astonishing that he did not mention Rashi’s view at all on this.
Know that it seems to me that what Noda B'Yehuda wrote — “forbidden” — means only that it is not proper conduct, as we wrote inside, but not an actual prohibition; for otherwise the Gemara’s rhetoric is difficult: the baraita “one may not wrap” would mean with money specifically, and where he did not designate. Perhaps this is the Gra’s reason for omitting it. In Yoreh Deah, siman 364, seif 1: if they placed a corpse in it with intent to empty it and did not designate from the outset, it is permitted after emptying — which implies that while the corpse is there it is forbidden to benefit from the grave; and the Gemara compares them there [Sanhedrin 48] to each other. And apparently there is difficulty on Shulchan Aruch here, for since he omitted Noda B'Yehuda’s view, it seems he holds that by law the sudar is not forbidden for mundane use even while the tefillin are there — yet there he rules plainly that it is forbidden. Perhaps he cited there only the manner of the matter, or because of the latter clause he cited there; see there — forced, uncertain.

Coins — See in Nahar Shalom, who wrote that likewise it is permitted to use it for an indecent use, specifically after the tefillin were removed from there; and he brought proof for the matter; see there.
