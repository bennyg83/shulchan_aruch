A cloth that he designated, etc. Mishnah Berurah states that verbal designation suffices, following Derech HaChayim. Peri Megadim is uncertain whether even thought alone is sufficient, but I did not rule so because Tosafos, Sanhedrin 48a, concludes that even verbal designation is ineffective unless one takes the item and says, “This shall be for a book,” or “for a corpse.” The Gemara likewise appears to show that mere designation is ineffective, even according to Rava, unless accompanied by an act. At the very least, thought alone certainly should not be treated stringently. This applies where the thought occurred earlier and the tefillin were inserted later without any present intention. If, however, he thinks at the moment of insertion that the pouch will be permanently used for the tefillin, the insertion itself is permanent use and forbids it even if the tefillin are later removed. Rabbi Akiva Eiger cites Chiddushei HaRan in Yoreh Deah, siman 364, that if one expressly designated the item when inserting the tefillin, or formed that intention in his heart then, it becomes forbidden even without a separate act, even after the tefillin are removed. If the designation occurred earlier, then later silent insertion forbids it only where the earlier designation included a physical act. Verbal designation alone, made at a different time from the insertion, does not forbid it. Eliyah Rabbah likewise cites Shiltei HaGibborim that speech alone is not designation. Magen Avraham's contrary inference from Shiltei HaGibborim does not concern verbal designation. The practical ruling therefore requires study where there was speech alone. Nevertheless, HaRan would presumably agree with Tosafos that if one took the item and said, “This shall be for tefillin,” it is effective, because that counts as an act.
For wrapping in it. It appears that designation takes effect only if he already owns tefillin at that time. If he made a pouch for tefillin he plans to purchase later, the designation does not take effect, and later temporary insertion is like use without prior designation. This follows Sanhedrin 48a regarding a monument built for a living person. It is straightforward.
Tefillin, etc. See Mishnah Berurah, seif katan 11. Chayei Adam's wording should read that even though tefillin were placed inside it, rather than “if tefillin were not placed inside it.” Otherwise it would be merely an accessory to an accessory. His ruling is nevertheless very difficult. He compares this case to Magen Avraham in siman 40, seif katan 2, and implies that Magen Avraham would forbid it. That is not correct. The large bag was originally made also for a tallis, a mundane item, and therefore it does not have the status of an accessory of sanctity. It is as though one expressly stipulated that mundane use would be permitted, comparable to Magen Avraham in siman 34, seif katan 6. In siman 40, however, the question is not sanctity but whether the item is considered a covering. Even a hundred vessels, which ordinarily have no sanctity as accessories to accessories, are insufficient if they are all designated for the tefillin. Since the tallis bag regularly covers the tefillin, Magen Avraham treats it as their vessel. Here the prohibition of mundane use applies only to an object bearing the status of an accessory of sanctity. Since this bag was originally made also for mundane use, it should be permitted. Perhaps Chayei Adam holds that an express condition permitting one particular mundane use permits only that use and no other.
Permanently. See Mishnah Berurah 12. The first view cited there is Eliyah Rabbah's; see his citation from Responsa Chavos Yair, which implies this. What we cited from Gra comes from the source he gives for this law ('permanently,' as the gloss states) and from the law above in se'if 2. It is obvious that Gra intends to prove that the Gemara does not discuss a case where one stipulated that the use would be only temporary, or made another condition limiting the designation. When he adds, 'as Rosh writes in the name of the Yerushalmi,' he thereby resolves Pri Megadim's question in Mishbetzos Zahav: how can a condition permit lowering the item to mundane use when Menachos discusses only lowering it to a lesser sanctity? Gra points to the Yerushalmi, which discusses lowering it even to mundane use. See there. Damesek Eliezer unnecessarily redirected Gra's words to a different issue. At first glance, Eliyah Rabbah's aforementioned view is difficult: why, then, does Berachos 23b need to distinguish a pouch that was not designated? This can be answered.
And he wrapped, etc. See Mishnah Berurah concerning a minor who inserts tefillin. Peri Megadim writes that a minor's insertion forbids the pouch only if he uses it permanently or if his father designated it. Otherwise it is mere use without designation, since a minor has no legally effective intent. Magen Avraham does not imply this distinction, and Shulchan Aruch HaRav and Chayei Adam likewise rule without qualification like Magen Avraham. Peri Megadim's question can readily be answered, but this is not the place to elaborate.
Tefillin once. Even if, the first time after designating the pouch, one places money together with the tefillin, sanctity nevertheless takes effect because he originally designated it for tefillin alone. It is consequently forbidden to do this unless he expressly retracts the original designation, for speech can nullify speech. Proof comes from Berachos 23b and the other baraisa, 'He should not wrap'; see there. One cannot reject this by saying that the baraisa forbids only where the tefillin were placed first, but simultaneous placement is permitted. If so, why did the Gemara resolve the contradiction by saying that the other baraisa concerns a pouch that was not designated? It could simply have answered that it permits wrapping the tefillin together with his money at the same time. Our conclusion is therefore certain. One might further say that if he made a pouch for tefillin, then placed money inside it, and afterward placed tefillin inside it, he may no longer place money there later, because when he placed the money he did not expressly nullify the designation. Perhaps, however, this itself counts as express nullification. The matter requires study. Know also that Magen Avraham cites Tur as ruling that if one made the pouch for tefillin, even if he later inserted them intending to remove them, it becomes forbidden. Chiddushei HaRan, chapter Nigmar HaDin (cited by Rabbi Akiva Eiger in Yoreh Deah 364) disagrees. Nevertheless, one should not rule leniently in practice, because Tur and Shulchan Aruch there derive from Ramban in Toras HaAdam. Regarding a pouch for hand-tefillin, Pri Megadim in siman 154 inclines to say that it is not called an accessory of sanctity, because the bayis itself has only the status of an accessory of sanctity, as it lacks the letter shin. In Mishnah Berurah I explained through several proofs that this is incorrect. Pri Megadim himself ultimately writes that the matter requires further study, and in this siman, in Eshel Avraham, he himself rules the opposite. Moreover, no posek ever states that Rabbi Chiya's law concerning a cloth designated for wrapping tefillin applies specifically to head-tefillin. The point is also compelled by Berachos 23b, where the Gemara raises a contradiction between two baraisos on this subject. If the baraisa permitting a person to wrap his tefillin together with his money concerned hand-tefillin, the Gemara could have answered accordingly. One cannot say that the plural 'his tefillin' necessarily means both sets, because otherwise it would have said 'a tefillah'; the other baraisa also plainly states that a person may not wrap 'his tefillin' with money. Thus, hand-tefillin are explicitly forbidden as well. One could answer with difficulty that the prohibition arises from their straps, which contain the hand-tefillin knot that Rashi regards as essential sanctity. But how would Nimukei Yosef and the other poskim who disagree with Rashi explain it? Rabbi Chiya's law must therefore apply to hand-tefillin as well.
It is forbidden to wrap in it, etc. Responsa Shaar Ephraim, siman 21, rules that any part extending beyond the space needed for the tefillin is not considered necessary for them. He therefore permits placing tefillin in the lower compartment of a double pouch and money in the upper compartment.
Of this kind. Mishnah Berurah cites Responsa Mishkenos Yaakov, siman 55, that ink prepared for writing sifrei Torah, tefillin, and mezuzos is also designation for an object of essential sanctity and may not be used for mundane purposes unless one stipulated from the outset. Mishnas Avraham challenges this from Yoreh Deah, siman 274, Shach and Taz, seifim katanim 3 through 5. The matter indeed requires much study. Mishkenos Yaakov's view appears to follow Ramban in Milchamos, first chapter of Sukkah. Ramban himself is lenient only because he disputes the entire rule that designation of an object of essential sanctity is effective. The objection can be answered by saying that the cases in Yoreh Deah concern ink not deliberately made for sacred writing. The writing itself may sanctify only the ink needed for the letters, while excess ink spilled upon a letter remains outside that sanctification, comparable to Shaar Ephraim's ruling. The matter requires study. It is therefore preferable to stipulate at the outset when making the ink.
For the sacred object itself. See Mishnah Berurah, where we included hide processed for straps, following Nimukei Yosef, cited by Magen Avraham and adopted as law by Tzemach Tzedek and Eliyah Rabbah. This requires some consideration. Pri Megadim ultimately remains in difficulty concerning Tzemach Tzedek according to Rashi, who holds that the places of the dalet- and yod-knots possess essential sanctity; processing would then be designation for an object of essential sanctity. Tzemach Tzedek, however, directs the reader to Tosafos, Megillah 26b. In truth, almost all the poskim disagree with Rashi, and Shulchan Aruch below in siman 154 also rules without qualification unlike him. Furthermore, it appears to me that even according to Rashi (although the yod and dalet are halachah leMoshe miSinai) processing the hide does not designate it so long as the strap has not been attached to the bayis. This differs from processing parchment: processing prepares parchment to be written upon, and therefore immediately constitutes designation. Here, the principal purpose is wearing the tefillin upon one's head and arm. Attaching the strap to the bayis designates it for this mitzvah, not processing. Even after processing, the hide does not yet bear the name of a strap; even after it is cut into straps, it is not ready for the mitzvah until it is attached to the bayis. It is then designated for the sacred object; that is, for wearing, through which the yod and dalet become sanctified. Plainly, making them alone does not yet sanctify the letters. This precise reasoning appears in Baal HaMaor on Sukkah: 'The same applies to tefillin straps. If one wishes to change them after processing but before attaching them to the bayis, he may do so, for designation does not forbid them even though they were processed for their own sake.' See there at greater length. Thus, Baal HaMaor holds that they are not designated for the mitzvah until attached to the bayis. The same follows for us: although designation for an object of essential sanctity is effective, before attachment to the bayis no designation exists. Nevertheless, absent need, it is preferable to be stringent and satisfy every concern, because Nimukei Yosef's stringency regarding hide processed for batim may imply otherwise. In pressing circumstances one may be lenient in every respect, for Baal HaMaor is the lone authority who treats designation of straps stringently once their true designation has occurred.
It is legally effective. Magen Avraham inclines toward Ramban, who disagrees and holds that such designation is ineffective. Biur HaGra, however, strongly defends Baal HaMaor and Nimukei Yosef, and cites Ritva as likewise holding that designation for an object of essential sanctity is effective. It is very difficult to be lenient against all these authorities. I later found another later authority who likewise rules stringently in practice, and Responsa Mishkenos Yaakov, siman 55, also rules like Rama and unlike Magen Avraham. Mishnah Berurah states that if one began writing sacred text, even a condition does not permit later mundane writing. Eliyah Rabbah, Peri Megadim, and the other later authorities clearly support this. Magen Avraham himself also implies it upon careful reading. Chayei Adam is lenient even with a condition in this case, but I do not know his source.
He wrapped in it. Mishnah Berurah explains this as temporary use performed with an intention at that time to use it permanently. Although Peri Megadim appears to require an express statement of permanent intent, the other later authorities simply write that he used it with intent for permanent use. HaRan, cited above, explicitly holds that thought is sufficient. Peri Megadim may also agree and use the expression “expressly” only because he goes on to say that if one expressly inserted the tefillin intending to remove them later, it remains permitted even after repeated use, so long as the pouch was not made for tefillin.
It is permitted to wrap, etc. Mishnah Berurah cites two views. The stringent view is Rashi's in Berachos 23b, who permits money only after the tefillin have been removed. Nimukei Yosef in chapter Nigmar HaDin writes likewise, and Eliyah Rabbah cites him. Shulchan Aruch HaRav permits money together with the tefillin and cites Berachos 23b, but remarkably does not mention Rashi's view.
The stringent ruling appears to be a matter of respect rather than a complete prohibition. Otherwise the Gemara could resolve the contradictory baraisos by saying that one forbids money together with the tefillin even in a non-designated pouch. Yoreh Deah, siman 364, rules that a grave used temporarily without prior designation may be used after the corpse is removed, implying that benefit is forbidden while the corpse remains there. Sanhedrin 48 compares graves and tefillin pouches. This creates some difficulty with Shulchan Aruch's omission of Nimukei Yosef. Perhaps the wording in Yoreh Deah merely reflects the ordinary case, or was chosen for the continuation. The matter requires study.
Money. Nahar Shalom writes that the pouch may similarly be used for a degrading purpose after the tefillin have been removed. See there.
