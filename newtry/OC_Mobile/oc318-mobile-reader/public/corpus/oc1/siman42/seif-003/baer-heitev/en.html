Forever. And if he made a pouch for the sake of tefillin, even if he narrowed it with intent to empty it — it is forbidden. And likewise if there was a pouch and he added something in it for the sake of tefillin and narrowed it with intent to empty it — it is forbidden until he removes what he added. And if he narrowed it once with intent to narrow it permanently, even if he did not designate it — it is forbidden — Magen Avraham, as cited. Once. It seems to me that a minor who narrows it for tefillin is forbidden; but a thing forbidden by designation alone is not forbidden by a minor's designation, for a minor has action but no thought. And it is simple that a person cannot forbid something that is not his. And if a thief stole a garment and made a tefillin pouch from it, he acquires it through change of form and it is forbidden — Magen Avraham.

Permitted. And without stipulation it is forbidden; so those who use a tefillin sack when traveling do not act well — but it seems to teach merit: those people presumably have an explicit gift. And likewise in what Rama wrote here that parchment processed for tefillin is forbidden to write mundane matters on it — yet we see every day that scribes write ketubot and gittin and other secular matters on their parchment; rather certainly that when scribes prepare it at first, their intent is to use them for both holy and secular matters. Therefore it is obvious that one may write a get on parchment processed for a Torah scroll, as in Orach Chayyim siman 124 seif 2 — Magen Avraham, Taz; see Bach and Orach Tzedek. However, if he began to write a holy matter on parchment or paper, it is forbidden to write mundane matters on it afterward, and a stipulation does not help; and all this is regarding sanctity such as tefillin and a Torah scroll. But for tashmishei kedushah a stipulation helps to use them even while they remain in sanctity, as written in siman 154 seif 8; but for an indecent use it does not help, as in siman 154. And tashmishei mitzvah such as tzitzit, shofar, lulav, Hanukkah candle — even if made for that purpose they are not forbidden, and one may use them for mundane matters even without a stipulation — as cited.
