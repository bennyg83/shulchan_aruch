Seif 3, in Rama: Parchment processed for tefillin may not be used for mundane writing, because designation of this kind for the sacred object is legally effective. Magen Avraham explains that this refers to parchment used for the batim. This appears difficult. In seif 1, Shulchan Aruch rules that new head tefillin which have never been worn may be converted into arm tefillin, because designation alone is ineffective. Yet the batim themselves are part of the sacred object. The matter is resolved as follows. Designation of an item of essential sanctity is effective here because processing must be performed for the sake of the mitzvah. Therefore, when one wishes to divert it to wholly mundane use, the original processing prevents that. But changing from a higher sanctity to a lower sanctity is different. Since processing need not specify the higher level of sanctity, the designation for head tefillin is ineffective with respect to conversion into arm tefillin. Thus, Shulchan Aruch permits changing head tefillin to arm tefillin because processing does not require specific intent for head tefillin. Rama forbids changing the parchment to mundane use because processing for sacred use is required. I later found that Magen Avraham himself writes this above in siman 32, and I was pleased. From this I derived a ruling in a case presented to me. Rashi passages for head and arm tefillin had been inserted into Rashi batim, but the owner later wished to convert the batim into Rabbeinu Tam tefillin. I was asked whether this was permitted after Rashi passages had already been placed inside. At first I thought it should be permitted. Shulchan Aruch's ruling that new head tefillin may be changed into arm tefillin appears to include a case where passages were already inserted but the tefillin had not yet been worn. This must mean that inserting the passages is only designation and not a completed act. A tefillin pouch that was designated and then actually used becomes forbidden because placing the tefillin inside completes the purpose for which the pouch was made. In batim, however, the principal act is wearing them upon the head. Inserting the passages is not the final act and therefore remains mere designation. Accordingly, one might permit changing Rashi batim into Rabbeinu Tam batim. Upon reconsideration, however, I concluded that it is forbidden. As shown by Rama, changing an item processed for sanctity to wholly mundane use is forbidden because processing for its own sake is required. Changing head tefillin into arm tefillin is permitted because both are certainly sacred, though one has a lower degree. Specific processing for the higher sanctity is unnecessary. Rabbeinu Tam tefillin, however, are disputed. According to the authorities who rule like Rashi, Rabbeinu Tam tefillin are not tefillin at all and are regarded as mundane. For those authorities, changing Rashi batim into Rabbeinu Tam batim is a diversion to mundane use. We must therefore act stringently. Once the batim were designated for Rashi tefillin by inserting those passages, designation for essential sanctity is effective where the contemplated change would be considered mundane according to some poskim. Possibly, even mere mental designation for Rashi tefillin would prohibit changing them into Rabbeinu Tam tefillin. This appears correct.
