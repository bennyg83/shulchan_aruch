(Seif katan 3) New, etc. Designation alone is ineffective. So long as the tefillin have not been worn, there has been nothing beyond designation.
If the strap snapped, etc. As explained at the end of siman 33, a strap that remains long enough to encircle the arm, extend to the middle finger, wrap around the finger three times, and be tied cannot be repaired through tying or sewing at that point. Since the strap snapped despite retaining the required measure, he wishes to reverse it, make the knot at the end of the long section, and attach below the short piece that formerly held the knot.
And now it will be used for wrapping the fingers. This wording appears inconsistent with what I wrote at the end of siman 33. There I explained that if enough remains to extend to the finger and wrap around it, he may tie on an added piece. Nevertheless, initially he should first extend the intact strap to the finger, make the three finger wraps with the intact portion, then return toward the upper arm and make the seven arm wraps with the attached section. If Magen Avraham agreed, he should have said that the former knot section would now be used for the seven wraps around the arm, not the finger wraps. I nevertheless maintain my earlier ruling. In my humble opinion, the strap should first be extended to the finger and wrapped there. Magen Avraham's wording here is imprecise because the principal issue here is the lowering of sanctity, not the details of the wrapping order.
Beit Yosef writes in siman 33 in the name of Hagahos Maimoniyos that if a strap snapped, etc. Although we do not rule like that Hagahos Maimoniyos regarding the repair itself, we may nevertheless derive from it the law concerning lowering sanctity, regarding which no one disputes.
Shabbos 98b states: Rabbi Shimon ben Yochai said in the name of Rabbi Elazar in the name of Rav Huna, who said in the name of Rav: One who carries four amos in a covered public domain is exempt. The Gemara asks that the wagons used to carry the Mishkan boards were covered. Each wagon was five amos long and five amos wide. The boards were ten amos long. Five amos separated one wagon from the other. The boards were laid crosswise over the wagons, with their midpoint over each wagon. Each board therefore projected two and one-half amos beyond each side of the wagon, and the same was true of the neighboring wagon. Thus, both the space beneath the wagons and the space between them were covered by the boards. Yet Rav said in the name of Rabbi Chiya that beneath, between, and alongside the wagons was a public domain. The Gemara concludes that the boards were placed on their sides. Their thickness was only one amah. It then asks that the wagons were still covered. Since each wagon was five amos long, they presumably placed at least four boards upon each wagon. Four boards occupied four amos, leaving only one amah of open space divided among the three gaps. No gap would reach three tefachim, and the rule of lavud would treat the entire area as covered. The Gemara answers that according to the view that the boards were one amah thick below and tapered above to a fingerbreadth, there was ample open space between the upper portions of the boards. Lavud did not apply and the area remained a public domain. Tosafos cites Ri Poras, who asks that according to the tapering view they presumably arranged the thin edge of one board beside the thick edge of the next, which was the most orderly arrangement. They could then have placed six boards while occupying only three amos and three fingerbreadths. Nearly two amos would remain, divided among five gaps. Each gap would still be less than three tefachim, and lavud would again render the wagon covered. Tosafos answers that they were not permitted to arrange them this way, as Magen Avraham cites: when placing the boards upon the wagons, etc.
That was above. The curtains were spread over that side. This is the language of Tosafos there. If even there, where both sides of every board had equal sanctity because each board possessed both an upper and lower side, one could not turn one board so that its formerly upper side lay beside the ground-facing side of another board, all the more so here. Here the strap segment formerly held the knot, whereas in its proposed new position it would hold no knot at all.
