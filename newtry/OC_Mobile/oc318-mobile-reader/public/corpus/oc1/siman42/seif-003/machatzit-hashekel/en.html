(Seif katan 3) New ones, etc. — for designation does not count; and this applies when he had not donned them — only designation.

If it broke, etc. — as written in siman 33: so that the arm portion is strong enough to tie and stretch to the middle finger, and wrap from it three wrappings on the finger and tie — it has no remedy, neither by tying nor by sewing. Since it broke, it seems impossible to tie and sew there. However, since the strap that broke is of the measure explained at the end of siman 33, if he wishes to reverse the strap and make the knot at the end of the long strap, and at the other end tie the small piece of strap that originally had the knot —

And now he shall make on it the wrappings of the fingers. From this wording it is implied not as written in siman 33: that if enough remains to stretch to the finger and also wrap around the finger, he is permitted to tie with it — nevertheless he must first stretch it to the finger and wrap three wrappings on the whole piece of strap, and afterward return backward toward the kubbetz and wrap on the arm seven wrappings with the tied strap. For if Magen Avraham held thus, he should have said, “and now he shall make on it seven wrappings on the arm.” Nevertheless, in my place I stand, and in my humble opinion it must be as written in siman 33: stretch them first to the finger and wrap there, etc. Magen Avraham’s wording here is not precise, for here this is not the main law; his main intent is to discuss the law of lowering kedushah.

And Rabbenu Yonah wrote in siman 33 in the name of the Geonim: if it broke, etc. — even though we do not rule like this Geonic view, nevertheless regarding lowering kedushah one may derive from his words that in this there is no dispute.

On Shabbos, page 98: Rabbenu Shimon ben Lakish, Rabbenu Elazar, Rabbenu Chama — Rav said: one who carries four cubits in a public domain on Rosh Hashanah is exempt. The Gemara objects: but the wagons (on which the planks of the Mishkan were carried) were a public domain (for the wagons were square, five cubits long and five cubits wide, and the planks were ten cubits long; between wagon and wagon was an airspace of five cubits; they placed the length of the planks across the width of the wagon — namely the middle of the planks — and it occupied the width of the wagon, five cubits; from the plank outside the wagon remained five cubits — namely two and a half cubits on this side of the wagon and two and a half on the other side; and so on the wagon at the side there was airspace under the wagons, and the airspace between wagon and wagon was covered by planks). Rav said in the name of Rabbenu Chananel: the wagons beneath them, between them, and at their sides were a public domain; and he concludes that they placed the planks on their sides, and the thickness of the planks was only one cubit. The Gemara still objects: they were a public domain, since the length of the wagon was also five cubits — if so, presumably they did not place on one wagon more than seven planks, for five planks were five cubits, and five cubits in five cubits do not fit; at least four planks they placed — thus all the airspace was a public domain, and only one cubit remained. If we divide that cubit into three airspaces between four planks, it reaches one airspace of three fingerbreadths; and we say lavud, and it is as if all were a public domain. We say: granted, per the one who says the planks below — their thickness one cubit above — taper and diminish to a fingerbreadth, for they sloped from below upward — if so, above there was much airspace between plank and plank, for lavud does not apply, and it is a public domain. Tosafot challenged in the name of Rabbenu Yitzchak Porat: per the one who says they taper to a fingerbreadth, presumably they arranged the edge of this plank against the thickness of the other plank, for thus it is arranged more properly; if so, they would place six planks and still hold only three cubits and three fingerbreadths; airspace remained from the wagon two cubits less three fingerbreadths — and if you divide them into five airspaces, for also between the edge of one and the thickness of the other there was presumably airspace, it does not reach three fingerbreadths for each airspace; still it is lavud and it is a public domain on Rosh Hashanah. On this they answered that they were not permitted to do so — and as Magen Avraham wrote in their name when placing the planks on the wagons, etc.

That was above — for the curtains were spread over them; and it is comparable to that which he said, etc. — so Tosafot there. If so, all the more so here, etc. — for there the kedushah of two planks is equal, for in every plank there is an upper side and a lower side, except that one plank is reversed; all the more so here, where this strap has no knot at all, and at first it had a knot.
