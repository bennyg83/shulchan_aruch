Nevertheless, one may defend them by saying that they presumably act as though they explicitly stipulated this use. A proof comes from Rama in siman 154, seif 8, who writes that the custom is to derive various benefits from sacred objects because widespread practice is treated as though beis din stipulated concerning them from the outset. Likewise, when a person buys a new tefillin bag, he presumably intends to use it in the ordinary way people use such bags while traveling. The same principle applies to Rama's ruling here that parchment processed for tefillin may not be used for mundane writing. We see every day that scribes write kesubos, gittin, and other non-sacred documents upon their parchment. Presumably, when they prepare it, they intend it for both sacred and mundane uses, since that is the common practice. My father-in-law Bach writes that one may not write a get upon parchment prepared for a Sefer Torah unless he expressly stipulated during processing that he would write gittin upon it when necessary. According to what I have written, however, it is permitted without such an express stipulation. This is also implied by Rama in Even HaEzer, siman 124, who writes that initially a get should be written upon parchment prepared like that of a Sefer Torah. He does not mention any need for a stipulation during processing. Evidently, no express stipulation is required, as I have explained.
