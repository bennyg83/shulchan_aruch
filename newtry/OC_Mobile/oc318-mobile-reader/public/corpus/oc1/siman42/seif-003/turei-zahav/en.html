And if he stipulated — it implies that by default there is permission only with explicit stipulation; and therefore it is not proper what many people do who use a tefillin sack when traveling and store there other things they need on the road; but it seems to judge them favorably, for those people by default are as explicit stipulation, and proof from what Rama wrote in siman 154, seif 8 that people practice enjoying many benefits from sacred matters, etc., since they practice thus and it is impossible for the heart of beit din to stipulate on them from the outset, etc.; and likewise that by default when buying a new sack for tefillin his intent is to use it in the manner people use on the road. And likewise in what Rama wrote here that parchment processed for tefillin is forbidden to write mundane matters — yet we see every day scribes write on their parchments bills, gittin, and other discretionary matters; but surely by default when scribes prepare it initially their intent is to use it for sacred and discretionary matters, since such is their custom. And Rema z"l wrote that one does not write gittin on parchment prepared for a Sefer Torah unless he stipulated from the outset at the time of processing to write gittin on it when needed; but when he did not stipulate from the outset it is a complete prohibition to write gittin on it; end quote. And according to what Shach wrote "to do so" — and so from Rama's words in Even HaEzer siman 124 that wrote l'chatchila one should write it on parchment prepared for a Sefer Torah, etc., end quote — and it was not mentioned there that one must stipulate on the parchment initially at the time of processing; rather it is certain that it is not required, as stated.
