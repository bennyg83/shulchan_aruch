(9) Kerchief — and likewise if he made a pouch anew for the sake of tefillin, likewise it is not forbidden to use it for mundane matters until he wraps tefillin in it, for designation is not significant; and likewise with all sacred utensils, even a case for a Sefer Torah. And an utensil of an utensil has no sanctity at all.

(10) That he designated — even by speech alone, and all the more so if he took it and said "this shall be for tefillin," then it helps. And see in Biur Halacha what I wrote in the name of the Ran.

(11) Tefillin — and a tallit bag is not called a sacred utensil, only a mitzvah utensil; therefore it is permitted to place other mundane matters in it. For even though a tefillin bag is also placed in it, the tallit bag is only an utensil of a utensil. And even if one places his siddur in it, which has sanctity, or tefillin without a bag — nevertheless, in time of need it is permitted to place mundane matters in it, for behold it was made from the outset also for a tallit, which is a mundane matter, and it is as if he stipulated, as below in the gloss. And those people who travel on the road and use a tefillin sack for mundane matters — even though by law it is forbidden — nevertheless one should find merit for them, for since they are accustomed to this it is as if they stipulated from the outset. Nevertheless, l'chatchila it is not proper to do so.

(12) Permanently — some say only explicitly, but by default we do not say his designation is permanent, since a kerchief by default is not designated specifically for tefillin; but from the Gra's words in his commentary it appears that by default it is as permanent, and the Mechaber did not limit this except where he stipulated that it should be only temporarily and not permanently. And if he made a pouch to place tefillin in, or said to a craftsman "make me a tefillin pouch," according to all it is by default as permanent.

(13) And he wrapped — all this is with his own kerchief or pouch, but a person does not forbid something that is not his unless he stole a garment and cut it and made a pouch from it, for then he acquires it through change of form and it becomes forbidden. And a minor who wrapped tefillin in the pouch that was designated for this is also forbidden; but something that becomes forbidden through designation alone, as will be explained, is not forbidden through a minor's designation, for a minor has action but no thought, even if they revealed their thoughts in speech.

(14) Once — even though it was only temporarily, which is the manner of occasional use, since it was by default we combine the act with the designation. But if he specified at the time of placing that it is on condition to remove them later, it is permitted to use it for mundane matters afterward. And if he made the pouch for the sake of tefillin and wrapped tefillin in it once, even on condition to remove them afterward, it is forbidden to wrap money in it; and likewise if the pouch was already made and he added something to it for the sake of tefillin to beautify it, he is like one who made the pouch for the sake of tefillin until he removes what he added. And see in Biur Halacha.

(15) Forbidden — forever, even after it deteriorated and is no longer fit for tefillin and requires genizah.

(16) Money — and likewise any other thing, even something that has any sanctity, as long as it is below tefillin in sanctity, such as a mezuzah, as above in siman 32, seif 8; and he mentioned money only because of the latter clause. And therefore one should not place a siddur in a pouch that was originally designated for tefillin alone; nevertheless, one should not protest, for since people are accustomed to this it is as if they stipulated from the outset, as in seif kaf-alef (21) above.

(17) For any matter — even if he made the pouch for the sake of tefillin and also wrapped tefillin in it permanently, it is permitted, since he stipulated at the time of making that he could change when he wishes, and a condition helps to use them even while they are in their sanctity, as written in siman 154, seif 8; however, for an indecent use a condition does not help, and likewise if he wants to wrap something in the strap fixed to the tefillin on him, it does not help because of contempt for the mitzvah.

(18) For the sake of tefillin — that is, the parshiyot of tefillin; and likewise leather processed for the compartments of head or hand tefillin, and even though the shin is not engraved on it, nevertheless it is called a body of sanctity; and all the more so leather processed for a Sefer Torah or mezuzah.

(19) Mundane matters — but to lower its sanctity, such as to write on it a mezuzah whose sanctity is lighter than tefillin, or other sacred writings, is permitted even on hide that was processed for a Sefer Torah. And a bill of divorce is a mundane matter, and it is forbidden to write it on parchment processed for a Sefer Torah unless he stipulated, as below.

(20) In such a manner — that is, through a complete act, to exclude if there was only designation in mere speech, or if there was only repair of the parchment and ruling it for its sake.

(21) The body, etc. — to exclude designation for sacred utensils, such as making a pouch for tefillin or processing leather for straps, which is only sacred utensils — one may change it even to mundane use; and see in Biur Halacha that we explained that not in a time of pressing need one should be stringent regarding processing leather for straps.

(22) The sanctity — but designation for the body of a mitzvah, such as tzitzit, shofar, lulav, sukkah, Chanukah candle — even if he made them for this purpose, they are not forbidden, for designation is not significant, as below in siman 638, seif 1 in the gloss, and it is permitted to use them for mundane matters even without a condition. And see above in siman 21 and below in siman 154 that there is another practical difference between sanctity and mitzvah.

(23) Is significant — even though in seif 1 we rule that one may change head tefillin and lower them from sanctity because designation is not significant — even though there too it is the body of sanctity — that case is different, for in any event he makes some sanctity in them; but here he does not want to make something mundane in them, and therefore designation regarding the body of sanctity helps. And see in the Magen Avraham that he cited dissenting opinions on this and holds that even regarding the body of sanctity designation is nothing; nevertheless, regarding practical action one should be stringent like the first view, as we wrote in Biur Halacha; and in any event a condition helps in this. However, if he began to write on the parchment a Torah scroll or other sacred writings, even on paper — according to all it is forbidden to write mundane matters on it afterward, for this is an act regarding the body of sanctity, and even a condition does not help for this.

(24) He wrapped — meaning temporarily; and likewise by default, even if he did so many times, as long as each time it was temporarily in an occasional manner, it is not thereby sanctified. But if he wrapped permanently, or wrapped even once but with intent of permanence — meaning that then he intended to always wrap in it — even if he did not designate from the outset, it is likewise forbidden, for it is as designation and wrapping. And if he specified on condition to remove them — even if he wrapped many times and also designated from the outset — it is permitted, provided he did not make the pouch for the sake of tefillin, as in seif kaf-daled (24) above.

(25) And did not designate — ever for the sake of tefillin.

(26) Permitted — some say this means the kerchief is not sanctified by wrapping once without designation; but as long as the tefillin are there, it is not proper conduct to place money together there, but one should tie them in a knot by themselves.
