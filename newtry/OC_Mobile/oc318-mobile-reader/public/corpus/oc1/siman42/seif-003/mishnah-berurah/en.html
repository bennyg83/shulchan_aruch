(9) A cloth. The same law applies if he made a new pouch for tefillin. Mundane use is not forbidden until he actually places tefillin inside, because designation alone is ineffective. This applies to all accessories of sanctity, even a Torah-scroll case. An accessory to an accessory has no sanctity at all.
(10) That he designated. Verbal designation suffices, and certainly taking the item and saying, “This shall be for tefillin,” is effective. See Biur Halachah concerning HaRan's view.
(11) Tefillin. A tallis bag is only an accessory of a mitzvah and may be used for mundane items. Although it also contains the tefillin pouch, it is only an accessory to an accessory. Even if a siddur or uncovered tefillin are placed directly inside, mundane items may be placed there when needed, because the bag was made from the outset also for the tallis, which is a mundane item for this purpose. This is as though one stipulated. People who travel and use a tefillin bag for mundane items technically act improperly. One may nevertheless defend them because their established practice is equivalent to an original condition. Even so, one should not initially do this.
(12) Permanently. Some say that express permanent designation is required. Without it, an ordinary cloth is not presumed designated forever. Biur HaGra implies that ordinary designation is presumed permanent. The Mechaber excludes only a case where one expressly limited the use to a temporary period. If one made a pouch specifically for tefillin, or instructed a craftsman to make a tefillin pouch, everyone agrees that ordinary designation is permanent.
(13) And he wrapped in it. This applies to his own cloth or pouch. A person cannot forbid another's property. If one stole cloth, cut it, and made it into a pouch, he acquires it through the physical change and can render it forbidden. If a minor places tefillin into a pouch designated for them, the pouch also becomes forbidden. But an item forbidden by designation alone is not forbidden by a minor's designation, because a minor possesses legally effective action but not legally effective intent, even when he expresses that intent verbally.
(14) Once. Even if the use was temporary, ordinary use combines with the prior designation. If he expressly stated at the time of insertion that he intended later to remove the tefillin, the pouch may afterward be used for mundane purposes. If the pouch itself was made for tefillin and tefillin were placed inside even once, then even express intent to remove them later does not permit mundane use. The same applies where an existing pouch was improved for tefillin. It remains forbidden until the added feature is removed. See Biur Halachah.
(15) It is forbidden. Permanently, even after the pouch deteriorates and is no longer fit for tefillin. It requires genizah.
(16) Money. The same applies to any item of lower sanctity, including a mezuzah, as stated above in siman 32, seif 8. Accordingly, one should not place a siddur inside a pouch originally designated only for tefillin. Nevertheless, one need not protest, because established practice is equivalent to an original condition.
(17) In every circumstance. Even if he made the pouch for tefillin and used it permanently, it is permitted when he stipulated at the time it was made that he could change its use. A condition permits use even while the object retains its sanctity, as explained in siman 154, seif 8. It does not permit a degrading use. Likewise, one may not wrap another item with a tefillin strap while the strap remains attached to the tefillin, because that degrades the mitzvah.
(18) For the sake of tefillin. This means the tefillin passages and likewise hide processed for the batim of head or arm tefillin. Even if the shin has not yet been formed, the batim are objects of essential sanctity. All the more so, this applies to hide processed for a Sefer Torah or mezuzah.
(19) Mundane matters. It may be lowered to a lesser sanctity, such as using tefillin parchment for a mezuzah or other Torah writing. Even hide processed for a Sefer Torah may be used for this. A get is mundane and may not be written upon Sefer Torah parchment unless a condition was made.
(20) Of this kind. Meaning designation through a complete physical act, excluding mere verbal designation or merely preparing and scoring the parchment for the sake of the mitzvah.
(21) For the sacred, etc. This excludes designation for accessories of sanctity, such as making a tefillin pouch or processing hide for straps. Such designation does not forbid changing it even to mundane use. See Biur Halachah, where we explain that when not under pressure one should be stringent regarding hide processed for straps.
(22) Object. Designation for an object used in a mitzvah, such as tzitzis, shofar, lulav, sukkah, or Chanukah lights, does not forbid it. Mere designation is ineffective, as explained in siman 638, seif 1, in Rama. Such objects may be used for mundane purposes without a condition. See siman 21 and siman 154 for further distinctions between sanctity and mitzvah objects.
(23) It is legally effective. Although seif 1 permits changing new head tefillin to arm tefillin because designation is ineffective, there the item still receives some sanctity. Here he seeks to use it for wholly mundane purposes. Designation through processing for the essential sacred object is therefore effective. Magen Avraham cites authorities who disagree and hold that even such designation is ineffective. In practice, one should be stringent like the first view. A condition is effective before writing begins. If, however, one began writing a Sefer Torah, tefillin, mezuzah, or another sacred text upon parchment or even paper, everyone agrees that he may not afterward write mundane matters there. This is an act involving the sacred object itself, and even a condition does not help.
(24) He wrapped in it. Meaning temporary use without prior designation, even repeated many times, provided each use was temporary. If he used it permanently, or even once with an intention at that moment to use it permanently, it becomes forbidden even without prior designation. If he expressly inserted the tefillin intending to remove them later, it remains permitted even after repeated use, so long as the pouch itself was not made for tefillin, as explained in seif katan 14.
