Seif 3: A cloth, etc., permanently. See Rashi, Avodah Zarah 47b, s.v. “he brought in,” etc.
Permanently, etc. As stated in Rama: “And if, etc.” This follows what was explained above in seif 2, and Rosh in the final chapter of Megillah in the name of the Yerushalmi. See there.
And parchment, etc. This follows Tosafos in Menachos 42b, s.v. “until,” etc. Even though designation is generally ineffective, processing for a sacred object is effective, showing that in such a case designation is legally significant. See Nimukei Yosef there, unlike Magen Avraham. This is why Nimukei Yosef writes that processing straps is different. One might ask why processing differs from weaving a garment for a corpse. The answer follows Baal HaMaor in the first chapter of Sukkah: a garment for a corpse is merely an accessory to the sacred object, whereas processing concerns the sacred object itself. Baal HaMaor nevertheless writes that straps too are part of the essential sanctity, since the knot is made from them. See Sanhedrin 48a regarding excluding the heifer, etc., and 48b regarding the tannaitic dispute. Rashi explains that Rava follows the first tanna. Menachos 42b likewise implies that Abaye follows Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel. Tosafos challenges Rashi from Gittin 54b, where we rule that processing must be for the sake of the mitzvah. The hides of tefillin themselves are also part of the sacred object. Tosafos further asks from Rav and Shmuel in Menachos, where their dispute is likewise connected to a tannaitic disagreement, and from Sukkah, which treats Shmuel's view as normative. Ritva answers that the first tanna does not dispute designation regarding an object of essential sanctity, where designation is certainly effective. Rather, he holds that tefillin do not require being made for their own sake, even in their writing. In this respect tefillin are more lenient than a Sefer Torah and mezuzah, just as they are regarding scoring lines. Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel disagrees even regarding accessories, like Abaye. The tanna who permits telling a craftsman, “Make for me,” etc., agrees with Rava that designation of accessories is ineffective. Everyone nevertheless agrees that the sacred text itself requires being made for its own sake. We rule like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel regarding essential sanctity and like Shmuel, but not like him regarding accessories. These do not require being made for their own sake, because designation of accessories is ineffective, unlike Abaye. This still requires study, because Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel mentioned only the sacred object itself, regarding which Rava agrees. If so, where is the tannaitic dispute? Perhaps everyone follows Rava and they differ only over whether processing must be for the sake of the mitzvah. See Tosafos there and in Menachos. Their words imply that even regarding the sacred object itself designation is ineffective, as Rabbeinu Tam and the alternative explanation state.
