(2) And he placed inside it, etc. Lechem Chamudos, page 99, writes that Levush erred by requiring both designation for permanent tefillin use and actual use with that intent. Even if he placed tefillin inside without specification, it is forbidden. Nachalas Tzvi likewise challenges Levush at length, as I wrote in Eliyah Zuta. In my humble opinion, Levush does not mean that the owner expressly stated that he was placing them there permanently. In such a case the pouch is forbidden even without prior designation, as Beit Yosef and Levush himself write in Yoreh Deah, beginning of siman 364, regarding a grave. Rather, Levush's phrase means that he placed the tefillin there without qualification, which is presumed to follow his previous designation, unless he retracted his original condition before placing them there. This appears correct. Responsa Chavos Yair, siman 59, later reached the same conclusion. I further wrote in Eliyah Zuta that if he did not retract his original designation, even temporary use renders the pouch forbidden. This is evident from Nimukei Yosef at the end of the chapter Nigmar HaDin. Chavos Yair writes that Yoreh Deah, siman 364, proves that if he designated it permanently, it becomes forbidden even if he used it only for a limited time. He further writes that only a cloth normally used for mundane purposes requires express permanent designation. If one made a pouch to hold tefillin, or told a craftsman to make him a tefillin pouch, the physical form itself constitutes designation, without any need to think expressly that it is permanent. Shiltei HaGibborim in the chapter Mi SheMeiso writes that if one made any alteration in a tefillin cloth for the sake of tefillin, this constitutes designation. If he then tied tefillin in it temporarily, it is forbidden. If he made no alteration and merely designated it, then even if he tied tefillin in it temporarily, it remains permitted.
(3) It is permitted, etc. This applies only after the tefillin have been removed. While the tefillin remain inside, mundane use is forbidden. Nimukei Yosef there. Magen Avraham writes that if a minor placed tefillin inside, the pouch becomes forbidden. It is obvious that a person cannot forbid property that is not his. If one stole cloth and made it into a tefillin pouch, he acquires it through physical alteration. Responsa Shaar Ephraim writes that a pouch in which money was stored may not later be used for tefillin. If one adds a new lower lining at the place where the tefillin will rest, it is permitted.
