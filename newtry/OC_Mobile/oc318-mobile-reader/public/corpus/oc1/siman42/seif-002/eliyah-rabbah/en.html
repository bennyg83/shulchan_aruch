[2] And he wrapped it in it, etc. — This is the wording of Lechem Chamudos, page 99. The Levush erred here, writing that designation is required to wrap tefillin in it forever, and that he wrapped with this intent — and it is not so, for even if he wrapped plainly it is forbidden — so far. And likewise in Nachalat Tzvi he extensively challenged the Levush, and I wrote in Eliyahu Zuta. In my humble opinion, the Levush does not deal with one who explicitly wrapped with intent to wrap forever — for in that case it is forbidden even without any designation at all, as Beit Yosef and the Levush himself wrote in Yoreh Deah, beginning of siman 364, regarding a grave with a corpse. Rather, what the Levush meant by “with this intent” is that he wrapped plainly, and he is presumed to have this intent since he already designated it — unlike if before wrapping he retracts from the first condition; so it seems to me. Now the responsum of Chavot Yair was printed, and I saw in siman 59 that he inclined to these words. I also wrote in Eliyahu Zuta: what if he did not retract from the first condition — even if he wrapped temporarily? It is proven in Nimukey Yosef, end of chapter “Nigmar haDin,” that it is forbidden; and Chavot Yair wrote there that it is proven from Yoreh Deah, siman 364, that if he designated it forever, even if he only wrapped for some time, it is forbidden. He further wrote that specifically for a sudar that is ready to be used for any mundane matter do we require designation forever — not so if he made a pouch to place tefillin, or told a craftsman “make me a tefillin pouch”: that is designation without his speech, and one need not think with permanent intent. He wrote in Shiltei Gibborim, chapter “Mi sheMet”: a tefillin sudar — if he made any repair on it for tefillin, that is designation; and if he tied tefillin on it temporarily, it is forbidden. But if he made no repair, only designation alone, that is not designation, and even if he tied tefillin on it temporarily it is permitted — so far.
