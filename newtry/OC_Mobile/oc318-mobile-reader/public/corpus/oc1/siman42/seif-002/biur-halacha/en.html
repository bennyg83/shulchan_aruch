If he stipulated concerning them from the outset. Mishnah Berurah explains that the condition must be made when the head tefillin are made. Bach appears to hold that a condition made the first time they are tied is also effective. This is difficult. Making head tefillin is no less than making a pouch for tefillin, and Magen Avraham proves from Tur that if no condition was made at the time of designation, then even inserting the tefillin temporarily with intent to remove them does not permit later mundane use. The ruling in siman 154, seif 8, that a condition helps may also concern a condition made at the time of designation, or a case where designation and permanent use occurred together. Menachos 34 likewise implies that the condition is made at the time of designation. That proof can be rejected because it follows the view that designation itself is effective, whereas according to our ruling designation alone is ineffective and perhaps a condition made when the tefillin are first tied upon the head is sufficient. Bach may reject Olas Tamid's a fortiori reasoning. The matter requires study. The Rama's later ruling, “If he stipulated concerning it from the outset,” may refer even to a condition made only when he first inserted the tefillin, provided he stated that the use was temporary, because the pouch had not originally been made for tefillin. If Rama refers to every case, then where the pouch was made for tefillin the condition must have been made at the time it was made.
From head tefillin to arm tefillin. This includes the passages and certainly the straps. There is, however, an important distinction. A condition regarding passages or batim, which are themselves sacred objects, permits only lowering them to a lesser sanctity. It does not permit mundane use, such as writing mundane matters upon their margins, even while they are still new. A condition concerning straps, which are only accessories of sanctity, permits even mundane use, including after they were used, as seif 3 shows. This follows Shulchan Aruch in siman 154. Nevertheless, since Bach interprets Rashi as holding that the place of the knot and the yod possess essential sanctity, one should initially treat those parts like the passages once they have been used. Merely making the knot probably does not yet confer sanctity.
