Ba'er Hetev, and requires further study: why did the Magen Avraham cite turning the other end upward — it implies literally reversing the long strap, for even without reversal, merely that in that short part of the long strap where it broke he wants to make the knot — this too is forbidden, since he takes the short part that had the knot and ties it below, it is lowering from sanctity; and in any case from what he concludes "and if the strap is not..." it implies that if he does not tie the short part below, there is no concern with reversal alone. And see in Rabbi Akiva Eiger in the name of his grandfather the Gaon Maharash who permits reversing, and proof from "and he took from the stones..." that were all made one stone. Difficulty: there is still jealousy in some side resting on the stone — therefore there is no concern with this; see there who wrote that from siman yod does not imply this. And the intent is that there, note 14, he wrote in the name of Mahari Chayim and Shelah for the reason of the atara not to reverse, etc., and there wrote that therefore one should not be lenient with a strap to reverse; but he concludes there to be lenient also with a tallit if torn above, etc. See there. And also in Halakhot Ketanot part 2 siman 51 that wrote Ba'er Hetev in his name permitting — see there — he only permits by law. And wrote there that those meticulous to make a mark on a tallit — perhaps for these there is concern also in this; see there. And the proof that Rabbi Akiva Eiger brings in the name of his grandfather is not so strong a proof, for since they were miraculous acts, perhaps they were not in a manner of sticking one to another but became one mass blended from all. But in any case there is no proof from mere aggada and asmakhta. And it is reasonable to say that what the Magen Avraham wrote to turn its head, etc. — he does not mean literal reversal to turn their beginning that hangs toward the ground and give the upper inside the pass-through to make the knot with it; rather his intent is what he wrote "the other head," namely the head of the other strap where it broke — that were originally far from the pass-through — now they take it and raise it to place it inside the pass-through, and such too is called reversal. And as the Magen Avraham wrote afterward from the Beit Yosef in the name of Hagahot Maimoniyot that wrote "and he shall reverse the strap inside the pass-through" — and there it is obvious that it does not mean literal reversal, only that whatever was placed initially in this place and he raises or lowers it to another place is called reversal. And therefore one should not learn from these words of the Magen Avraham to reverse literally, for it seems one should not do so even not for the reason of lowering for the sake of the knot, as written above siman 1 regarding a mitzvah tallit. And it appears one should not reverse except in time of pressing need when the end where it broke is soft and weak and not fit to make the arm binding as required so it would be close to breaking and he has no other strap — then one may permit reversing, as Rabbi Akiva Eiger wrote; and see what Yad Ephraim wrote on the Magen Avraham's words. And one may say the Magen Avraham holds here there are two factors: there is no knot in this piece, and also he lowers from knot sanctity to finger wrappings; but in this case alone where there is no knot in it, there is no concern since it is not fit for hand tefillin. And a practical difference: if in the long strap alone there is enough also for finger wrappings, it is permitted to tie it since now no sanctity is used in it at all. And perhaps Rabbenu Yerucham forbids more to tie it and wrap on his hand, for again there is no sacred use here at all and it should be buried; requires further study.
