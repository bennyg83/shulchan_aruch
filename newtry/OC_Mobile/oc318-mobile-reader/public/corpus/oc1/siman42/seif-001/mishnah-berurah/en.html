(1) Arm. This applies even if he has no arm tefillin and possesses two sets of head tefillin.
(2) A strap. The same plainly applies to a pouch designated for head tefillin, which is also an accessory of sanctity like the strap. This is stated in Megillah 26b, implied by Biur HaGra, and written by Magen Avraham at the end of siman 28.
(3) From them. Magen Avraham writes that if the arm-tefillin strap snapped near the knot, one may not reverse the intact lower end upward and make the arm-tefillin knot there, then tie the piece that formerly held the knot below it. This lowers the former knot section from its sanctity, since it held the knot and yod and would now be used for the finger wraps. That piece requires genizah. If the remaining strap is too short, he should make fewer arm wraps. The same applies to head tefillin. If the encircling section snapped, one may not reverse the portion formerly inside the knot so that it now lies outside. If the strap snapped outside the knot and he wishes to pull the break downward so that the strap retains the required measure, some permit this. Although the place of the knot moves downward, the same section still holds the knot. Others hold that this too improperly lowers its sanctity. According to the latter view, even where an arm-tefillin strap snapped near the knot and one does not intend to reattach the broken piece below, he should not reverse the strap and make the knot from its lower end, because the formerly upper section would move farther from the knot. One should therefore be stringent and reverse the strap only in pressing circumstances, where no other strap is available and the broken section is soft and weak and liable to break if used for tying upon the arm. See Biur Halachah.
(4) In the head tefillin. The head tefillin contain the shin and dalet, whereas the arm tefillin contain only the yod knot.
(5) It is permitted. Meaning that he makes four compartments and places each passage into its own compartment. The same applies to taking passages and straps from arm tefillin for head tefillin.
(6) He did not wear them. Even though he designated and prepared them for the head, designation alone is ineffective.
(7) Like one compartment. Even though the passages are written upon four parchments and placed inside four internal compartments, this is valid after the fact, as explained in siman 32, seif 47. See Biur Halachah.
