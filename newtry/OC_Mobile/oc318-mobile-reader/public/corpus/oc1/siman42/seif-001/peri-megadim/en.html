Regarding Rama's ruling. Taz concludes that ordinary practice is equivalent to an express stipulation, since people customarily use such parchment for both sacred and mundane purposes. See Even HaEzer, siman 124, Chelkas Mechokek and Beis Shmuel, letters 5 and 8. Eliyah Rabbah explains that the phrase does not mean parchment fully prepared for a Sefer Torah and processed for that purpose, but parchment prepared in the same physical manner as Sefer Torah parchment. This explains why it says that processing for the sake of a get is unnecessary. Olas Tamid writes that a get possesses some sanctity, and therefore changing to a lower sanctity is permitted, whereas changing to wholly mundane use is forbidden. I have some difficulty with the Mechaber and Rama's ruling that a condition helps even after the tefillin were worn, and likewise permits mundane use. Their source is Menachos 34, according to the view that designation is effective, where a condition helps. Perhaps that condition helps only to permit changing to a lower sanctity, but not to mundane use. Likewise, a condition may help before actual use, but actual use may be more severe. This requires study. I later saw that Responsa Tzemach Tzedek, siman 81, noticed this issue.
Responsa Tzemach Tzedek, siman 81, discusses hide processed for tefillin straps and whether a condition permits mundane use. He writes first that even for the sacred object itself a condition is effective, unlike Levush, who remained uncertain. Second, even according to Levush, straps are accessories of sanctity. He brings proof from a pouch. Although a pouch is initially only verbally designated, once tefillin are placed inside it there is actual use, and nevertheless a prior condition helps. All the more so, it should help regarding processing. See what I shall write further concerning this in Magen Avraham. Tosafos in Megillah 26b, s.v. “and accessories,” according to Rashi's explanation of the dalet and yod, appears to indicate that straps themselves possess sanctity. The statement that straps are accessories of sanctity may refer only to the portions extending beyond the knot. This requires study.
Know that it requires consideration whether the rule that one may not lower an item from greater sanctity to lesser sanctity is a Torah law or Rabbinic. See Rashi on the Mishnah in Megillah 26a, who cites the Tosefta's phrase “when they consecrated them.” This appears to imply a Torah law, and reason also supports that conclusion. Even when an item is no longer fit for the higher sanctity, one may not lower it. Menachos 32 teaches, and Rambam rules in chapter 5 of Hilchos Tefillin, that a worn-out Sefer Torah may not be converted into a mezuzah. Thus, even though it is no longer fit to serve as a Sefer Torah, it may not be lowered to a lesser sanctity.
