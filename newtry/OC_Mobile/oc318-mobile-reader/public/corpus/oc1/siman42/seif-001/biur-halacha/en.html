It is forbidden, etc. Peri Megadim is uncertain whether the rule that one may not lower an object from a higher sanctity to a lower sanctity is D'Oraisa or D'Rabbanan, and ultimately inclines to say that it is D'Oraisa. Responsa Mishkenos Yaakov, siman 55, likewise brings proof from the Yerushalmi that this rule is D'Oraisa.
Even to take a strap, etc. All the more so, it is forbidden to take one passage from head tefillin and place it into arm tefillin. If one passage was found invalid, whether the earlier passages may be taken for arm tefillin depends upon the nature of the defect. If the passage was invalid from the outset, such as because a letter was not written properly or because of another original defect, the remaining passages and straps are still regarded as new. If the defect arose later through age or another subsequent deterioration, the higher sanctity has already taken effect upon them and they may not be changed.
From them. See Mishnah Berurah for our concluding ruling, which follows Shaarei Teshuvah. Chayei Adam is even more lenient for a poor person, and certainly where the mitzvah would otherwise be lost. He permits attaching below the upper piece of an arm-tefillin strap that snapped, relying upon Eliyah Rabbah. Eliyah Rabbah's proof appears to establish only that one may reverse a strap from one end to the other, as Magen Avraham himself permits, and even that proof is not decisive, as Shaarei Teshuvah writes. Nevertheless, one should not protest against a lenient ruling where the mitzvah would otherwise be lost. In truth, it is preferable to make fewer wraps so that the mitzvah is not lost, as Magen Avraham writes. Chayei Adam's ruling is relevant where the head-tefillin strap snapped within the section that encircles the head. A poor person, or one who has no other strap, may draw the broken place downward so that it will now lie beneath the knot. In my opinion, the better solution is to cut away and place in genizah a small section of the strap at the break, including the portion that formerly encircled the head and held the knot. He should afterward attach the remaining strap with sinews and make the knot upon an intact section, taking care that none of what formerly lay inside the knot should now project beyond it. This satisfies even Magen Avraham.
But from arm tefillin, etc. The same applies to taking the passages and straps, as stated in Mishnah Berurah. Although arm-tefillin passages are ordinarily written upon one parchment, it is possible to place them into four compartments. See Peri Megadim and siman 32, seif 45, seif katan 62, and seif 47 in Taz.
If they were, etc. This permission plainly applies only in pressing circumstances, where one lacks arm tefillin and has two sets of head tefillin, as the Gemara states. Otherwise, arm-tefillin passages should initially be written upon a single parchment, as explained above in siman 32, seif 47. The Mechaber did not need to repeat this. Peri Megadim's discussion here is astonishing to me.
New, which were not yet, etc. See Mishnah Berurah. A new strap placed into old tefillin would also appear to constitute only designation, and it may therefore be changed, because it is not called an accessory of sanctity until the tefillin have been placed upon the head once. Although Megillah 26b lists accessories of sanctity together (the tefillin pouch and their straps) and a pouch, once used after designation, becomes an accessory of sanctity, as stated nearby in se'if 3, one might infer that a strap similarly becomes an accessory as soon as it is tied to the tefillin. That is incorrect. A pouch is called an accessory of sanctity because it serves the sacred object placed inside it; once the tefillin have been inserted, it has served sanctity. Straps, however, are called accessories of sanctity because they serve the sacred tefillin by enabling a person to place them upon his head and arm. Until he has actually worn them, they have not yet acquired the status of accessories of sanctity and remain mere designation. The practical law requires further study.
It is permitted to change, etc. Peri Megadim derives from this that if one wrote part of a Sefer Torah upon parchment and the writing became invalid because of a Divine Name or a similar issue, he may write tefillin or mezuzos upon the unused portion. Writing itself is only a strong designation, not the combination of designation and actual permanent use. This is unlike Eliyah Rabbah, who is stringent. I later found that Noda BiYehudah, second edition, Yoreh Deah, siman 174, discusses this and concludes that one who permits writing tefillin upon it has not erred, but one should not be lenient for a mezuzah. See there for the reason.
From head tefillin to arm tefillin. This refers to the passages and batim. They may not be changed to wholly mundane use, such as writing mundane matters upon their margins, as stated in seif 3 and Mishnah Berurah, seif katan 18. Even a condition does not help for such mundane use. This remains true even according to the authorities who hold that designation is ineffective for an object of essential sanctity, because beginning to write the sacred text is stronger than ordinary designation. Straps, however, are only accessories of sanctity. When new, they may be changed even to mundane use. Tzemach Tzedek, cited by Eliyah Rabbah, rules that hide processed for straps may be changed to mundane use without a condition, because designation for an accessory of sanctity is ineffective. According to Baal HaMaor, however, designation of straps is effective at least to prohibit mundane use. Although most poskim disagree and make no distinction between straps and other accessories, one should be stringent when not under pressure.
