Forbidden, etc. — See Pri Megadim, who is uncertain whether the law of "we do not lower" is from the Torah or rabbinic, and finally inclined to say it is from the Torah. And see in responsa Mishkenot Yaakov siman 55, who also brought proof from Yerushalmi that "we do not lower" is from the Torah.

Even if he took a strap, etc. — And all the more so if he took from head tefillin some parashah to place in hand tefillin — it is forbidden. And if some invalidity is found in one parashah, whether it is permitted to take from the parashah before it to place them in hand tefillin depends on this: if the invalidity is because some letter was not written properly from the outset, or some other invalidity that was invalid from the beginning — then the other parashiyot and the straps are still considered new; but if the invalidity came about through their having been worn, or some other defect that occurred later — strict sanctity has already applied to them and it is forbidden to change them.

From them. — See Mishnah Berurah in what we wrote at the end — that is the ruling of Sha'arei Teshuvah. And Chayei Adam is even more lenient in this, and all the more so where there is nullification of a mitzvah — it is permitted to attach the hand piece below the upper piece that broke; and he wrote that one may rely on this from Eliyah Rabbah who permitted — as cited. Even though apparently Eliyah Rabbah's proof is not proof for this, only that it is permitted to turn one strap from its end to its beginning, as Magen Avraham himself permitted — even for this there is not so much proof, as Sha'arei Teshuvah wrote. Nevertheless one should not protest where there is nullification of a mitzvah to be lenient. And in truth it is better not to make so many wrappings, and the mitzvah will not be nullified, as Magen Avraham wrote. But there is a practical difference from his words: if the head strap broke within the portion that encircles the head, whether it is permitted to pull down the broken place so it is now under the knot where it is needed, or if he has no other strap — but the more correct way is to cut from one strap at the broken place a small piece, namely what used to encircle the head and the place of the knot, and hide it; and the rest of the strap he joins later with sinews and makes the knot in the whole place, but he shortens it so nothing extends beyond the knot from what was originally inside the knot — and in this way one satisfies even Magen Avraham's view.

But from hand (tefillin), etc. — See Mishnah Berurah in what he wrote; and likewise it is permitted to take the parashiyot, etc., even though hand tefillin are usually written on one parchment — nevertheless it can occur that they fit in four compartments. See Pri Megadim, and in siman 32 seif 45 in s.k. 62, and seif 47 in Taz there.

And if they were, etc. — And it is obvious that all this is specifically under duress when he has no hand tefillin and has two head tefillin, as they said in the Gemara — for otherwise, lechatchilah one must write four parashiyot of hand tefillin on one parchment. The author did not need to explain this, for he already explained it in siman 32 seif 47. And Pri Megadim's words on this are wondrous to me.

New ones that are still, etc. — See Mishnah Berurah. And a new strap placed inside old tefillin — apparently this too is not yet designation only, and it is permitted to change it, for it is not called by the name of tashmishei kedushah until he placed it once on the head. And although in Megillah 26b they taught in one passage tashmishei kedushah — a tefillin pouch and its straps — and in a pouch, once he narrowed it for the head after designation it again becomes tashmishei kedushah, as below seif 3; nevertheless apparently the same applies to a strap: once they are tied with the tefillin it again becomes among their accessories — for there it is different, because the pouch is called tashmishei kedushah since it serves the sanctity that enters it; therefore once tefillin are placed inside it, it has already served sanctity. Unlike straps, which are called tashmishei kedushah simply because they serve the sanctity by which a person places tefillin on his head and arm — as long as he has not yet placed them on himself they are not yet called by that name, and it is only designation; and practically — requires investigation.

Permitted to change, etc. — From this Pri Megadim inferred above in siman 32 s.k. 10: if he wrote on parchment for a Torah scroll and it was disqualified through a Divine Name and the like — it is permitted to write on the remainder, for the writing too is not yet designation from above, and it is not like "he narrowed it and designated it" — unlike Eliyah Rabbah who is strict in this. Later I found in Noda B'Yehuda Mahadura Tinyana Yoreh De'ah siman 174, who discussed this and inclined to be lenient to write tefillin on it afterward without error; but for a mezuzah one should not be lenient — as cited for the reason.

From head (tefillin) to hand (tefillin). — That is, the parashiyot and the compartments — for mundane matters it is forbidden to change them (such as writing on the sheets mundane matters and all such cases), as explained in seif 3 in the hagahah and in Mishnah Berurah in s.k. 18. And even a stipulation does not help in this, as below; and even for opinions that disagree with the hagahah and hold that designation even for the body of sanctity is not a significant matter — this is preferable, as Magen Avraham ruled: if he began to write on parchment or paper, it is forbidden to write mundane matters on it. The reason is that this is preferable to another designation: even though this too is not designation only — for otherwise it would be forbidden to lower even to a lighter sanctity — nevertheless it is preferable because it is in the body of sanctity itself. But straps, which are only tashmishei kedushah, as explained below in siman 152 — it is permitted to change them even for mundane matters since they are new, as Tzemach Tzedek ruled and Eliyah Rabbah brought to halachah: hide processed for straps may be changed even for mundane matters even without a stipulation, because it is not designation except for tashmishei kedushah and it is not a significant matter. See below what is written in the name of the Razah, that according to his words designation in straps is a significant matter not to change them at least for mundane matters; and although most poskim disagree with him and do not distinguish between straps and other tashmishei kedushah — nevertheless, not in a case of duress, one should be strict.
