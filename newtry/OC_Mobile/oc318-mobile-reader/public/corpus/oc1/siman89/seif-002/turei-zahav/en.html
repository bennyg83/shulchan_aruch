And if he began reciting the blessings, etc. This is difficult for me. The Gemara challenges the prohibition against extending a greeting before prayer from the Mishnah that states that between the sections of Krias Shema one may initiate and return a greeting. Rashi explains that Krias Shema precedes prayer, yet the Mishnah teaches that one may initiate a greeting. According to this, what is the Gemara’s question? In the Mishnah he has already begun the blessings of Krias Shema. On the strength of this difficulty, Terumas HaDeshen ruled regarding attending to one’s affairs that reciting some of the blessings before Baruch She’amar does not help, as Rama writes nearby. All the more so, then, should it not help here. Moreover, Shulchan Aruch itself writes nearby, regarding attending to one’s affairs, that it is forbidden specifically until after the Amidah. Why, then, did it rule leniently here regarding extending a greeting? One cannot answer that Shulchan Aruch distinguishes between extending a greeting and attending to one’s affairs, because the principal proof that reciting some blessings does not help regarding one’s affairs is the Gemara’s question from the law between the sections, as explained, and that question concerns extending a greeting. It therefore appears to me that reciting some blessings certainly does not help regarding extending a greeting either. Here, however, the discussion concerns bowing alone, without extending a greeting, as Beit Yosef explicitly writes in the name of Orchos Chaim. He concludes that if one recited some blessings, there is not as much concern. This means that bowing alone need not be forbidden, but extending a greeting is certainly not permitted by reciting some blessings, just as is written afterward regarding attending to one’s affairs.
