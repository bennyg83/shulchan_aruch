And if he began to bless, etc. It is difficult, for in the Gemara it challenges why they forbade giving greeting before prayer from the mishnah in “Between the chapters” one asks and responds — and Rashi explained: but Kriat Shema is before prayer, and it teaches “one asks” — and therefore what is the challenge? In the mishnah he already began with the blessings of Kriat Shema; and from this challenge Terumat HaDeshen ruled regarding doing one’s personal affairs that what he said of partial blessings before “Baruch” that he said does not help, as Rama stated shortly. If so, all the more so here it does not help. And further, Shulchan Aruch itself wrote shortly regarding doing personal affairs that it is forbidden until after eighteen specifically — so why was he lenient here regarding giving greeting? And one could answer that Shulchan Aruch distinguishes between giving greeting and doing personal affairs in this, for the main proof that partial blessings do not help regarding personal affairs is from what the Gemara challenges from “between the chapters,” as stated — and that is regarding giving greeting. And it seems to me that certainly regarding giving greeting too partial blessings do not help; only here the case is bowing alone without giving greeting, as Beit Yosef stated explicitly in the name of Orach Chayyim — he concluded that if he said partial blessings there is not so much concern, meaning that for bowing alone there is no prohibition; but for giving greeting it certainly does not help, as stated afterward regarding personal affairs:
