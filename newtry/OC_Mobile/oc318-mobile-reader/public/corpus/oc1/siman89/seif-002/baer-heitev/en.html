Prayer. This means from dawn.
Peace. One who has servants or attendants whom he needs to serve him should warn them the previous evening that upon rising from their beds they should wash their hands, recite the blessings, and read the first paragraph of Shema. They may then attend to his needs. This is stated in Halachos Ketanos, part 2, siman 4. Shevus Yaakov, part 2, siman 22, writes concerning this that it is all a matter of pious conduct, but strictly according to the law there is no concern. See there.
Business. While going for that purpose, he may go to his fellow's doorway and say, “May my master's morning be good.” However, even in such a case it is forbidden to greet him with the word shalom. Magen Avraham.
Concern. This means that it is then permitted to bow when one did not arise early to go to his fellow's doorway. However, when one arose early to go to his doorway, both bowing and greeting him with shalom are forbidden, even if he already began the blessings. Lechem Chamudos, Bach, Magen Avraham, Taz, and Peri Chadash.
Good. He should not greet him with shalom, even though he ordinarily greets him with shalom. Magen Avraham.
