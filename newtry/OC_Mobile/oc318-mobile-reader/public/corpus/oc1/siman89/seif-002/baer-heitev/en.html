Peace. One who has servants or attendants whom he needs to serve him should warn them from the evening that when they rise from their beds they wash their hands, bless the blessings, and recite the first paragraph of Shema, and afterward they may attend to their needs (Leket Yosher vol. 2, siman 4). And in responsum Shevut Yaakov vol. 2, siman 22, he wrote on this that all this is from the measure of piety, but by law there is no stringency (see above).

The matter. And incidentally to this matter he goes to his fellow's door to say "morning light to you, my master, good" (tzafra demarei tav), but to give him peace even in such a case is forbidden. Magen Avraham.

To be concerned. The explanation is that then one may bow when he is not going out to meet him at his door; but when he goes out to meet him at his door, whether to bow or to give peace, even if he began the blessing it is forbidden. Levush, Bach, Magen Avraham, Taz, and Peri Chadash.

Good. And he should not give him peace even though he is accustomed to give him peace. Magen Avraham.
