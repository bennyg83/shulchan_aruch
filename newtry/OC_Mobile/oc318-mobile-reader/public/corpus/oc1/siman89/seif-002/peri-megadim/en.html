And if — the Taz and Eshel Avraham: Shulchan Aruch contradicts itself, for here it wrote "some blessings" permitted — implying even greeting at his doorway; and so Levush explicitly; and if so — that in Berachos 14a it is forbidden to give greeting before praying — this means blessings of Shema; and all the more doing one's needs there, that he pray blessings too. And in Turei Zahav 9 he proved that the wording "prays Shacharit" — for otherwise what does it ask between the chapters; and if so the author in seif 2 did not heed this difficulty — why did he write in seif 3 Shacharit specifically; and I explained that bowing alone without greeting is permitted when saying blessings, even at his doorway; and all the more greeting alone is lighter than bowing alone — it is strained to say regarding blessings greeting is stricter than bowing alone, as Levush wrote; but greeting with bowing as was their law — forbidden even if he said blessings until he prays Shacharit; and similarly his needs are like greeting with bowing. And I am speaking of Levush's words. And in Eliyah Rabbah, note 7, it implies he explains these eleven — the essence of greeting is bowing alone; and forbidden on the road without blessings; and with blessings at his doorway forbidden in bowing alone — but greeting alone is permitted with blessings even at his doorway, for the essence of greeting in the Gemara is bowing; and therefore according to view one — greeting alone when going early is forbidden even with blessings (as placed between the chapters); and it should have been said according to view one — stringency; and these eleven — leniency is drawn; but from the words of Nachatz it appears blessings are permitted — meaning on the road; and bowing alone is the subject; and this too on the path of going to his doorway — forbidden with blessings; and even greeting alone forbidden at his doorway with blessings; and also leniency is not drawn from these eleven; and Magen Avraham, note 5. And behold eleven — two that greeting is forbidden on the road — apparently it is custom that they practiced so that one give his heart to prayer — not from law; and Peri Chadash and Levush; and nevertheless one could say from law, for they hold that on the road it is permitted when he said the blessings — for thus it deals between the chapters; and that which they do not distinguish forbidden without blessings — because "one who prays" implies Shacharit prayer specifically, as Turei Zahav wrote; so too the wording "until he prays" implies; and therefore they distinguish at his doorway; and see Darkei Moshe, chapter 3, and he returned here.

If he goes early to his doorway and gives greeting in a foreign language — one could say; and see siman 84. And it is reasonable — the Christians who say "the Merciful One have mercy on you," or "the Gracious One be gracious to you," and the like — even according to those who forbid in the bathhouse — see siman 84 — here it is permitted, for they bless in the name of the Holy One, blessed be He; and it is possible the same if one says "May He who is called Peace give you peace" — permitted; and on this requires investigation.
