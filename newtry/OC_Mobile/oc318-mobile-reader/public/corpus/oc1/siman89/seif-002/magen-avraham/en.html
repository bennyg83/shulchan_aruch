To see some business matter. And incidentally through this he goes to his friend's door to tell him "tzafra d'mara tav"; but to give him shalom—even in such a case—it is forbidden.

There is not so much concern. It is written in the Levush Chayyim—that is, when one does not rise early to his door, for it was not mentioned in the Gemara as forbidden; but when one rises early to his door it is forbidden, for the Gemara challenges from that in "between the chapters" one asks out of honor, and there he had already said the blessings—and likewise the Bach (and the Gra made himself as if he did not know). But what he challenged—that the words of the Shulchan Aruch contradict each other—I do not know what difficulty he had in mind.

And some say that even when one encounters [his friend], etc.—and he shall not give him shalom even though he is accustomed to give him shalom [so in the Rashba].
