To see to some business. And incidentally he goes to his friend's doorway to say to him, 'May the Master make your morning good.' But to give him shalom, even in such a case, is forbidden.
There is not so much concern. It is written in Lechem Chamudos that this means where he did not rise early to his doorway, for that was not mentioned in the Gemara as forbidden. But when he rises early to his doorway it is forbidden, for the Gemara challenges from the case that between the sections one may inquire because of honor, and there he had already said the blessings. So too the Bach wrote. But what the Olas Tamid made as if he did not know, and what he challenged that the words of the Shulchan Aruch contradict one another, I do not know what was difficult for him.
And some say that even when he finds, etc. He should not give him shalom, even though he is accustomed to giving him shalom. So it appears from the Rashba.
