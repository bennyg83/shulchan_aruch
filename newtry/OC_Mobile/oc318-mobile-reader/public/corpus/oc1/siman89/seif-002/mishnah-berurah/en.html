(8) time of prayer — That is, from amud hashachar; and see Biur Halacha in seif 1.

(9) to go early, etc. — The same applies if one goes in the beis haknesses from one's fixed place to a friend's place to give him shalom.

(10) to his friend's door — The same if it were his father or teacher. If there is concern for another man's enmity — if one can extend one's hand without saying shalom, it is fine [Peri Megadim]. When one meets him on the road, one may be lenient regarding another man's enmity even in saying shalom, for by law it is permitted, as below, similarly.

(11) His Name, etc. — It is not proper to mention His Name upon a person and honor him before honoring the Holy One, blessed be He. In a foreign tongue one should investigate. It is reasonable that if one says "the Merciful One will have mercy on you" or "the Gracious One will grant you grace" and the like, it is permitted, for one blesses him with the Name of the Holy One, blessed be He [Peri Megadim, see there further].

(12) when he needed — That is, when he needed to walk through his friend's courtyard to see some business matter, and on that account goes to his door to say tzafra d'mari tav — but to give him shalom even in such a case is forbidden; see Biur Halacha. And know that what the Shulchan Aruch wrote — to see some business matter — specifically to see, but to actually engage in the matter is forbidden before prayer, as below in seif 3.

(13) and some say, etc. — And likewise one should rule [Bach and Eretz Tzvi].

(14) no concern — Explanation: then it is permitted to bow when one is not going early to his door; but when going early to his door, whether to bow or to give shalom, even if one has begun the blessings — it is forbidden.

(15) in the marketplace, etc. — And one should not give shalom even if one is accustomed to give him shalom.

(16) so that he gives thought — That is, even though by law it is permitted, nevertheless our predecessors were stringent in this for this reason. If one has begun the blessings, one need not be so concerned, and it is permitted to give shalom. All this is to open [the door] to say shalom to one's friend; but when one's friend asks of his welfare, one may respond in any case. All the more so one who has already prayed may go early to the door of a friend who has not prayed to give him shalom, even though one will need to respond to him.
