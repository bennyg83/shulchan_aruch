The time of prayer. This means from dawn; see Biur Halacha on seif 1.
To go early, etc. The same applies if he goes in the synagogue from his fixed place to his friend's place to give him shalom.
To his friend's doorway. The same applies if it was his father or his rebbe. If there is concern for enmity from a powerful person, if he can give his hand without saying shalom, that is proper. Peri Megadim. When he meets him on the road, one may be lenient with a powerful person even in saying shalom, for otherwise it is permitted by strict law, as below; so it appears to me.
For His name, etc. It is not proper to mention His Name over a person and honor him before honoring the Holy One, blessed is He. In a foreign language, this requires examination. It stands to reason that if he says, 'May the Merciful One have mercy on you,' or 'May the Gracious One favor you,' and the like, it is permitted, for he blesses him with the Name of the Holy One, blessed is He. Peri Megadim; see there further.
When he needed. Meaning that he needed to pass through his friend's courtyard in order to see to some business, and incidentally he goes to his doorway to say to him, 'May the Master's morning be good.' But to give him shalom is forbidden even in such a case; see Biur Halacha. Know that what the Shulchan Aruch wrote, 'to see to some business,' is specifically to see; but actually engaging in business is forbidden before prayer, as below in seif 3.
And some say, etc. And so one should rule. Bach and Olas Tamid.
There is no concern. Meaning that then it is permitted to bow when he did not rise early to his doorway. But when he rises early to his doorway, whether to bow or to give shalom, it is forbidden even if he has begun the blessings.
When he finds, etc. He should not give shalom, even though he is accustomed to giving him shalom.
So that he gives. Meaning that although by law it is permitted, nevertheless our early authorities were accustomed to be stringent in this because of this reason. If he began the blessings, there is not so much concern and it is permitted to give shalom. All this is regarding beginning to say shalom to his friend; but when his friend asks about his welfare, it is permitted to answer in every case. All the more so, one who already prayed may rise early to the doorway of his friend who has not prayed in order to give him shalom, even though his friend will need to answer him.
