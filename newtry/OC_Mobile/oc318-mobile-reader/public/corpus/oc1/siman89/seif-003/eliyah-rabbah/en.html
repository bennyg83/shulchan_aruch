(3) And after noon, etc. — and Bach concludes that its time is until the time of mincha gedolah, which is half an hour after noon; and from the time of mincha and above one prays two Minchas; and Magen Avraham disagrees with him, because in truth the time of mincha begins after noon, as is found in siman 233, up to here. And in truth so it is implied in Yoma 28, and so Rashi explained in parashas Bo; and that which we establish the time of mincha as half an hour after noon — one must say because of concern lest they err; and study is required in Sanhedrin chapter hayu bodkin. However, I saw that Pri Megadim and Taz also ruled like Bach, and so it appears to me is the view of Kesef Mishnah chapter 3 of Hilchos Tefillah, and in Raavan siman 167, and in Sefer Tzida Laderech and Rekanati; and it appears reasonable to them, since it is forbidden to pray mincha until half an hour, as is implied in Rashi's explanation 26 — it is as if it is not the time of mincha, and he prays Shacharit. However, regarding intentional transgression it appears to me to rule like Shulchan Aruch and Levush, for per the sugya of the halachah it appears that intentionally, even after a third of the day, it is forbidden and his blessing is in vain; and so wrote in Sefer Raavan there; and if so, it is enough for us to be lenient until noon and no more. And what the Shulchan Aruch wrote, and he prayed, etc. — it appears that the same applies l'chatchila, that he can pray after four [hours] when he erred or transgressed; and all the more so when he was an onen — and see siman 71.
