And not to drink. See Be'er HaTov, who wrote in the name of Maharach: this question was asked in the responsum of Shevut Yaakov, siman 8 — whether one may eat or drink something before daybreak so that his mind settle upon it. He answered that for a weak person it is permitted to eat and drink something to strengthen his heart for the service of the Creator; and in the siddurim of the Arizal he wrote explicitly that such is the custom of Maharach — except that half an hour before day it is forbidden, as mentioned by the poskim, for then the time of prayer has arrived. He concluded there that all the stringent words of the Zohar refer only to one who acts out of gluttony for excessive pleasure, out of arrogance — and they relied on the Zohar that this is like one who divines and practices sorcery on Shabbos; but not for one who does so for healing, or so that his body be strong and healthy to pray with proper intent.
