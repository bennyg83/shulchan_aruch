And where healing is needed, it is permitted to drink water and strong liquor before praying (Radbaz vol. 4, siman 238). And for an old and weak person who cannot sustain himself until the time the congregation leaves the synagogue, especially on Shabbos and Yom Tov when sometimes they delay until chatzos — it is better to permit him to pray at home at ease and make Kiddush and eat something in the morning, and afterward go to synagogue and direct his heart with the congregation in Shacharis and pray afterward with them the day's Musaf. And not that he drink coffee or chicory without first accepting the yoke of the kingdom of heaven, for certainly on this idolatry is said "and Me you cast behind your back" (Leket HaKemach). And so Peri Chadash — that coffee one may drink, especially in Egypt where the mind does not settle without coffee; but coffee with cold or any kinds of condiments in order not to drink coffee on an empty stomach is forbidden, there. And Maharachav wrote that even if he rose at chatzos at night it is forbidden to taste anything until after prayer, as written in the Zohar. And I heard that one of the great ones permitted drinking coffee — for specifically foods that increase blood are forbidden (Yad Aharon).
