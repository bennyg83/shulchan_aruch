Permitted. This is because arrogance is not relevant to these beverages. With sugar, however, it is forbidden. Where it is needed medicinally, one may drink water with sugar before praying, as stated by Radbaz, part 4, siman 238. An elderly and weak person who cannot sustain himself until the congregation leaves the synagogue, especially on Shabbos and Yom Tov when they sometimes remain until after midday, is better permitted to pray calmly at home, recite Kiddush, and eat something in the morning. He should then go to the synagogue, direct his heart together with the congregation during Shacharis, and afterward pray the day's Musaf with them. He should not merely drink coffee or chocolate without first accepting the yoke of the Kingdom of Heaven, for this is certainly included in the verse, “You cast Me behind your body.” Leket HaKemach. Peri Chadash likewise writes that one may drink coffee, especially in Egypt, where one's mind does not settle without coffee. Coffee with sugar, however, or any type of food eaten so that one not drink the coffee on an empty stomach, is forbidden. See there. Rabbi Chaim Vital writes that even one who rises at midnight may not taste anything until after prayer, as stated in the Zohar.
