3) There: If he prayed from alos hashachar, etc. Pri Chadash, paragraph 1, writes: The Mechaber states the law without clarifying it. In truth there are three stages for the beginning of prayer time. The principal mitzvah is at sunrise, like the vasikin, as it is written, "They shall fear You with the sun." Initially one may pray once alos hashachar has risen and the eastern sky has become illuminated, like the morning tamid. If he prayed from alos hashachar, however, he has fulfilled his obligation, as the Mishnah in Megillah teaches that all daytime mitzvos performed from alos hashachar are valid. The tamid too is not invalid if slaughtered from alos hashachar. The principal initial time for all daytime mitzvos is sunrise; only the tamid was advanced slightly before sunrise because all offerings had to be brought before the afternoon tamid. If one performed them from alos hashachar, he plainly fulfilled his obligation, unlike the implication of Rabbeinu Yerucham cited by Beis Yosef and of the Mechaber. Ateres Zekenim, paragraph 1, writes that alos hashachar precedes the time at which one can recognize an acquaintance four amos away. From the latter time one may initially pray, though the preferred mitzvah is to pray immediately after sunrise. Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 1, writes the same in the name of Rabbeinu Yerucham. Under this understanding of Rabbeinu Yerucham, one should not protest so strongly against those who pray early during the Selichos period, because they do not pray so early as to precede the time at which one can recognize an acquaintance. Thus, all agree that the preferred mitzvah requires sunrise, and that after the fact one who prayed from alos hashachar has fulfilled his obligation even if the eastern sky was not yet illuminated. Magen Avraham, paragraph 3, writes likewise. Erekh HaShulchan, paragraph 1, similarly cites Ramban in Milchamos at the beginning of Berachos, unlike Eliyah Rabbah and Mateh Yehudah, paragraph 1, who require the eastern sky to be illuminated even after the fact. This also appears from the unqualified language of the poskim that one fulfills the obligation from alos hashachar. Pri Chadash holds that once the eastern sky is illuminated one may pray initially; Maran regards even that as only after the fact, as Shulchan Aruch states. Ateres Zekenim permits initial prayer from the time one can recognize an acquaintance four amos away, which also appears to be Rabbeinu Yerucham's view according to Maamar Mordechai. We do not, however, find that Maran makes this distinction in Beis Yosef. See what we wrote above in siman 25, paragraph 27: according to the mystical teaching one must wear tzitzis and tefillin before Krias Shema in the order of the offerings, or at least before the passage of the tamid. The time for putting on tefillin is when one can recognize an acquaintance, as explained in siman 30, se'if 1, and our discussion there. If one waits until that time, puts on tzitzis and tefillin, and begins from the passage of the Akeidah, praying deliberately and calmly, word by word as proper without omitting any letter and with particular concentration upon the Divine Names, his Shemoneh Esrei will occur near sunrise (the emergence of the sun's body above the earth) as explained above in siman 58, paragraph 10. Fortunate is one who serves his Master properly and does not begrudge a short amount of time for prayer, for these matters stand at the height of the world.
