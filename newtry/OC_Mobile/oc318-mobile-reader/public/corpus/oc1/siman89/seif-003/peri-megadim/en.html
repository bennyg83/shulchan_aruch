Or — the Taz, examining the Beit Yosef, appears to hold that his view too is that it is forbidden, because going on the road falls under doing one's personal affairs (there is no doing of personal affairs greater than this); only he was troubled how in siman 89 they permitted the Rashba (see in the Tur) to pray on the road. He answered that there one is under duress, for the caravan will not wait for him, and therefore it was permitted to set out before praying. But the Taz explained that there, from dawn onward, the main time of prayer is only from sunrise (and possibly even after daybreak is not its main time), and it is permitted to set out before praying — and this is not doing personal affairs, for only once its time has arrived is it forbidden, as stated in seif 2 regarding greeting. And Magen Avraham, siman 6, disagrees, that the time of prayer is called from dawn, since when under duress one may set out properly it is called the time of prayer in such a case; and in a case of duress they permitted the Rashba to go to pray on the road. And therefore when there is no duress it is forbidden regarding greeting and personal affairs from dawn. And to the Taz it appears that greeting is also permitted before sunrise — and what does the Gemara challenge in the chapter "One who asks" — that the early pious ones too were before sunrise? Unless that before daybreak is not called the time of prayer; but after daybreak, after the fact, it is properly called the time of prayer (and it will necessarily be a dispute whether daybreak and sunrise are equivalent; at least see siman 58, seif 1, on this).
