Or to set out on a journey. Beit Yosef, in the name of Mahari Abuhav, writes that this applies specifically according to the view of the Rosh cited by Tur at the end of the siman, that praying at home is preferable. Beit Yosef distinguishes that here the case is one in which he is able to delay but wishes to hurry, etc. It appears that none of this is necessary. Here the case is even one in which he will take a short journey and much of the day will remain, so that upon arriving he will still be able to pray standing. Nevertheless, it is forbidden because he is attending to his own affairs before prayer. The later case, by contrast, is one in which he would have to pray while traveling and not standing. The prohibition against attending to one’s affairs before prayer does not apply there, because he sets out early, before the principal time for prayer has arrived.
