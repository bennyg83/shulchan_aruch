A practical difference arising from this second reason is that eating remains forbidden even after one has accepted the yoke of the Kingdom of Heaven through Krias Shema, as long as he has not yet prayed the Amidah. It therefore appears to me that even one who is compelled to eat medicinally before prayer should at least recite Krias Shema first.
Food and drink taken medicinally are likewise permitted. See Peri Chadash and Peri Megadim, who write that even if one could consume them after prayer, they are nevertheless permitted because his intention is medicinal. Chayei Adam writes: It appears to me that one who must eat because of weakness of the heart may do so. The later authorities write that a weak person who cannot sustain himself until the congregation finishes praying in the synagogue should preferably be permitted to pray privately at home in the morning, eat a little, and afterward go to the synagogue to hear Kaddish, Barechu, and the other sacred responses, rather than eat before praying and then go to the synagogue to pray with the congregation. This is stated by Baer Heitev in the name of Leket HaKemach, and follows plainly from Berachos 27b, in the passage concerning weakness of the heart; see there.
