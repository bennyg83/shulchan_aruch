(א) (siman kaf gimel) and it became light, etc. — from when alos hashachar rises. That is, from the beginning of the rising of alos hashachar; but "the east became light" is afterward, when alos hashachar spreads over the entire face of the east.

(ב) For by law, with the rising of alos hashachar, etc. — Meaning: for this reason the time of Shacharis is until four hours — for prayers were instituted corresponding to the tamid offerings. And the morning tamid had its time until four hours; and since the end of prayer time is analogous to the tamid, likewise the beginning of prayer time equals the tamid.

(ג) As it is in Yoma chapter 28, halachah 34 a bit. And so it is there: they said to the appointee, "Go out and see if the time for slaughter [of the morning tamid] has arrived" (that is, as Rashi explains, he went up on the wall or on the roof) — if it has arrived, the seer says "Barkai" (that is, the beginning of alos hashachar); Matya ben Shmuel says, "The entire east has become light" — and we rule like Matya ben Shmuel. And afterward we learned: "And why did they need this?" (Rashi explains: to go up on the roof and see) — because once the light of the moon rose and they thought the east had become light, and they slaughtered the tamid and took it out to the house of burning; and Magen Avraham holds: just as they were concerned about error and needed to go up and see — likewise for this reason itself Matya ben Shmuel holds that the entire east must become light, and alos hashachar alone does not suffice.

(ד) And what is written, "And why did they need this — to go up on the roof" — and he did not explain why they needed to wait until the east became light. First, the wording "why did they need this" does not imply so. And also per Rashi it is more fitting, for the mishnah "why did they need" applies to all [authorities], even per the first tanna of Matya ben Shmuel.

(ה) And so too in the second chapter of Megillah, etc. — and Rashi explained there on page 80 folio 1, and these are his words: that alos hashachar is daytime.

(ו) But since not everyone is expert in it, they must wait until sunrise; so ends [his words].

(ז) And it seems to me regarding what is written in siman 233, etc. — Meaning: that by law mincha time begins immediately at midnight; but because we are not expert, they were concerned about error and added half an hour, and said mincha time begins from six and a half hours. And we rule in siman 233 that if one prayed during that half hour — that is, immediately after six hours — he has not fulfilled [his obligation].
