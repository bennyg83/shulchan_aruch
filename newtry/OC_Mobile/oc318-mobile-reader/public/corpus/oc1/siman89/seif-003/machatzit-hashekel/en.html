(Seif katan 3) And the eastern horizon became illuminated, etc. From the rising of dawn. This means from the beginning of dawn’s ascent. The illumination of the eastern horizon occurs afterward, when the light of dawn spreads across the entire eastern horizon.
For by law, from the rising of dawn, etc. Meaning, this is why the time for Shacharis extends until four hours, because the prayers were instituted corresponding to the daily offerings. The morning daily offering could be brought until four hours. Since the end time for prayer parallels that of the daily offering, the beginning time for prayer likewise corresponds to that offering.
As stated in Yoma at the beginning of the third chapter, although the wording is not entirely exact. This is what is stated there: The officer said to them, “Go out and see whether the time for slaughtering the morning daily offering has arrived.” This accords with Rashi’s explanation that the observer ascended the wall or the roof. If the time had arrived, the observer would say, “The light has flashed,” meaning the beginning of dawn. Mattisyah ben Shmuel says, “The entire eastern horizon has become illuminated,” and the halachah follows Mattisyah ben Shmuel. Afterward the Mishnah teaches: Why was this necessary? Rashi explains, why was it necessary to ascend the roof and look? Because on one occasion the light of the moon rose, they mistook it for illumination of the east, slaughtered the daily offering, and had to take it to the place of burning. Magen Avraham understands that just as they had to ascend and inspect because of the concern for error, for that same reason Mattisyah ben Shmuel holds that the entire eastern horizon must be illuminated and that the mere rising of dawn is insufficient.
And what he wrote, “Why was this necessary,” meaning to ascend the roof. He did not explain it as asking why they had to wait until the eastern horizon became illuminated. First, the phrase “Why was this necessary” does not imply that explanation. Moreover, Rashi’s explanation is preferable, because according to it the Mishnah’s question applies according to everyone, even according to the first Tanna who disagrees with Mattisyah ben Shmuel.
And so it is at the end of the second chapter of Megillah, etc. Rashi explains there, on page 20a: “For from dawn it is considered daytime.”
However, because not everyone is expert in identifying it, they must wait until sunrise. Thus far his words.
This is not comparable to what is written in siman 233, etc. Meaning, by strict law the time for Minchah begins immediately at midday. Because we are not expert and there is concern for error, they added half an hour and said that Minchah begins at six and one half hours. Nevertheless, it is established in siman 233 that if one prayed during that half hour, namely immediately after six hours, he did not fulfill his obligation.
