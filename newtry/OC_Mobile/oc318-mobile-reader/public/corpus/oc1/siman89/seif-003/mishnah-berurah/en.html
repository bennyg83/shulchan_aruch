To engage, etc. Before the light of morning it is permitted to do work. Shelah and Chok Shlomo. The Eliyah Rabbah wrote that nevertheless one should not do any matter until he blesses the order of blessings. It is obvious that his intent is within the half-hour close to the light of morning.
His needs. One who has Hebrew servants or attendants whom he needs to serve him should warn them from the evening that when they rise from their beds they should wash their hands, recite the blessings, and read one section of Shema, and afterward they may attend to his needs. All this is a measure of piety, but by strict law there is no concern.
To set out on the road. Even before Minchah or Maariv, if their time has arrived, their law is like before Shacharis.
Until he prays. Even if in the place where he will arrive he will also be able to pray with the congregation in its proper time, nevertheless it is forbidden, for traveling on the road is like being involved in his needs. However, all this is not in a pressing circumstance. In a pressing circumstance, when the caravan will not wait for him, he may first set out on the road and pray on the way, as below in seif 8. It appears from many poskim that even if the sun has already risen, this is also permitted in such a case.
And not to eat. Regarding one who violates this, the Sages said that the verse says about him, 'And Me you cast behind your body.' The Holy One, blessed is He, said: after he ate and drank and became arrogant, he accepted upon himself the kingdom of Heaven. They also attached their words to the verse, 'Do not eat upon the blood,' meaning: do not eat before you pray for your blood. Even mere tasting is forbidden.
Water is permitted. For arrogance does not apply to it, specifically without sugar. But with sugar it is forbidden, and all the more so beer, called bier, is certainly forbidden. Tea and coffee may be drunk before prayer so that he can focus his mind and pray, especially in places where people are accustomed to them and the mind is not settled without them. All this is without sugar and without milk, according to the later authorities. The world is accustomed to be lenient and drink with sugar. Perhaps the intent of the later authorities, and likewise the Radbaz who wrote that with sugar there is no greater arrogance before prayer, is only if he puts the sugar into the tea to sweeten it, for this is forbidden before prayer. But if he takes a little sugar in his mouth while drinking, and without this he cannot drink the tea, this is not included in arrogance. What emerges from our words is that putting sugar into the tea, or eating a little pastry while drinking so as not to drink tea or coffee on an empty stomach, has no side of leniency before prayer, unless one has weakness of the heart and needs this for healing. Nevertheless, it is good that he first say at least the passage of Shema Yisrael. The new Pischei Teshuvah wrote that one should prevent drinking tea in the morning before prayer in a gathering of friends; see his reason there. Aside from this, another prohibition commonly branches out from it, for the Rashba wrote, and he is cited in the Beis Yosef, that a person is forbidden to be involved in his affairs before he prays so that his heart not turn to anything until he prays. So too it is written in Shulchan Aruch at the end of seif 2, that it is forbidden to delay himself with any other matters at all until he prays. Those who are accustomed to gather with friends while drinking tea sometimes, through much conversation, come thereby to pass also the time for Krias Shema and prayer.
On Shabbos. Meaning, do not say that on Shabbos it is forbidden in any case because it is forbidden to taste before kiddush. It teaches us that it is forbidden specifically when the time of kiddush has arrived; here, since he has not prayed, the time of kiddush has not yet arrived. After Mussaf it is forbidden because of kiddush; and before Mussaf, see siman 286, seif 3.
It is permitted. Even fine foods and drinks, to which arrogance applies, since he is not doing it out of arrogance but for healing, even though he is not fully sick, it is permitted. All the more so it is permitted to drink medicinal drinks and potions before prayer.
