(17) to busy oneself, etc. — And before morning light it is permitted to do melacha [Shelah and Chavos Yair]. The Ateres Zekanim wrote that nevertheless one should not do any matter until one has blessed the order of blessings. It is obvious that his intent is in the half hour close to morning light.

(18) in his needs — One who has non-Jewish slaves or servants who must attend him should warn them from the evening that upon rising from their beds they wash their hands, bless the blessings, and recite one paragraph of Shema, and afterward may attend to his needs. All this is from the measure of piety; but by law there is no strictness.

(19) to set out on a journey — And even before Minchah and Maariv if their time has arrived, their law is like before Shacharit.

(20) until he prays — And even if in the place he will come to he could also pray with the congregation at its time, nevertheless it is forbidden, for walking on the road is like engaging in one's needs. All this is not at a time of pressing need; but at a time of pressing need, when the caravan will not wait for him, one may go on the road beforehand and pray on the road, as below in seif 8; and it is implied from many poskim that even after sunrise it is also permitted in this case.

(21) and not to eat — One who transgresses this — Chazal said that about him scripture said "and Me you cast behind your back." The Holy One, blessed be He, said: After he ate and drank and became proud, he accepted upon himself the yoke of Heaven. They also based their words on the verse "do not eat on the blood" — do not eat before you pray for your blood — and even a taste in general is forbidden.

(22) water is permitted — For pride does not apply to it. Specifically without sugar; but with sugar it is forbidden, and all the more so alcoholic beverage (as called in the vernacular) — certainly forbidden. Tea and coffee are permitted to drink before prayer so that one can direct one's mind and pray; in particular in places where people are accustomed to them and the mind does not settle without them — all this without sugar and without milk [Acharonim]. The world is accustomed to be lenient to drink with sugar; perhaps the intent of the Acharonim, and likewise Radbaz wrote, that with sugar there is no greater pride than this before prayer — only if one puts the sugar inside the tea to sweeten it, that is forbidden before prayer. But if one takes a little sugar in one's mouth at the time of drinking and without this one cannot drink the tea, that is not within the category of pride. The conclusion from our words: in putting sugar inside the tea, or eating a little kinds of preserves at the time of drinking so as not to drink the tea and coffee on an empty stomach — there is no side to be lenient in this before prayer, unless one who has a weak heart and needs it for medicine. Nevertheless it is good that one say at the outset at least the paragraph Shema Yisrael. The book Pe'ulas Chadash wrote one should prevent drinking tea in the morning before prayer in gathering with friends — see there his reason. Besides this, one is accustomed to branch out from this to another prohibition, for Rashba wrote and it is brought in Beit Yosef that therefore it is forbidden for a person to engage in his affairs before praying, so that he does not turn his heart to any matter until he prays; and likewise in Shulchan Aruch end of seif 2, that it is forbidden to delay in other matters altogether until one prays. Those accustomed to gather with friends at the time of drinking tea are sometimes accustomed through much conversation to pass thereby also the time of Keriat Shema and prayer.

(23) on Shabbat — That is to say, do not think that on Shabbat it is forbidden in general because it is forbidden to taste before Kiddush — let it inform you that it is forbidden only specifically when Kiddush time has arrived. Here, since one has not prayed, Kiddush time has not yet arrived. After Musaf prayer it is forbidden because of Kiddush; and before Musaf see siman 286 seif 3.

(24) permitted — Even good foods and drinks to which pride applies — since one is not doing it out of pride but for medicine — even if one is not completely ill it is permitted; and all the more so that it is permitted to drink before prayer thin foods and soaked foods for medicine.
