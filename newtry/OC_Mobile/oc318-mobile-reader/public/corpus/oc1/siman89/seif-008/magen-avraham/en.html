In a time of pressure, etc.—there is to question, for most of the world are not careful about this; and perhaps they practice like the other poskim who ruled like the Rashba that adjoining redemption to prayer is preferable. And further, Rashi explained that prayer standing is preferable so that one can concentrate—and nowadays in any case people do not concentrate so much; therefore it is better to adjoin redemption to prayer. And it is implied from Rashi's words that if he can pray sitting on the road, it is better that he adjoin redemption to prayer than that he pray in his house standing—for even sitting one can concentrate—and so in the Rosh for one of the versions. However, it is stated in the Gemara that one sitting in a wagon or on a traveling ship is considered as walking for this matter; and it is simple that if he can recognize his friend at a distance of four amot he may also recite Kriat Shema in his house. It is stated in the Gemara: Rav Ashi on Shabbos was accustomed to preach immediately at daybreak and pray during the time of preaching while sitting, and the people slipped away one by one and prayed; and he would not delay the preaching in order to pray before the preaching—see there. Nevertheless it seems to me specifically in their days, when all their actions were for Heaven's sake and they would teach and admonish the people in the sermon; but nowadays one should not cancel prayer standing because of the sermon. And further, it is stated there that when he arrived at his house he returned and prayed standing.

Thus it is even more preferable. And according to this view, if he is on the road and sees that the time of Kriat Shema will pass, he should recite Kriat Shema on the road with its three blessings, and afterward when he comes to the inn he should pray standing; nevertheless it seems to me he should recite it on the road without its blessings—see end of siman 46.
