(א) (siman kaf chet) There is no, etc. — for the Gemara poses a difficulty on this, that we say it is forbidden for a person to give greeting to his fellow before he prays.

(ב) But we learned between the chapters: one may ask out of respect. And they answer: that which is forbidden deals with one who arises early to his door. And it is difficult: what is the difficulty from the mishnah between the chapters? It is different there, for he already said part of the blessings — behold, he stands between the chapters; nevertheless, what is forbidden by law is forbidden until he prays the eighteen blessings; and here only what the poskim added to be stringent was permitted.

(ג) But what he posed as a difficulty — the words of Shulchan Aruch contradict each other. Meaning: here Rabbeinu Yosef permits by saying part of the blessings, and in section 3 regarding doing one's needs Rabbeinu Yosef forbids unless he already prayed the eighteen blessings — what he said, "part of the blessings," does not help. And in the Gemara it is implied that the law of both is equal — for in both laws the Gemara said "before he prays."

(ד) And just as regarding doing one's needs, the explanation of "before he prays" means the eighteen-blessing prayer — likewise "before he prays" stated regarding asking for peace means the eighteen-blessing prayer. And what is the difficulty? He himself wrote that giving greeting mentioned in the Gemara — that is, when arising early to his door — is in truth also forbidden until he prayed the eighteen-blessing prayer.

(ה) In Shulchan Aruch: and some say that even when meeting his fellow, etc., so that he will pay attention, etc. — these are the words of Raavad, and he wrote, and these are his words: so that he will pay attention, etc., and because it is a change in language he will remember; so ends [his words]. Therefore I was uncertain: in their days, when their custom was to give greeting — if so, when he said "tzafra demare tav" it is a change, and through this he will remember; unlike in this time, when everyone's way is to say "tzafra demare tav" and not give greeting — if so, there is no change here. And if so, it is possible that for Raavad in this time one should not say "tzafra demare tav."
