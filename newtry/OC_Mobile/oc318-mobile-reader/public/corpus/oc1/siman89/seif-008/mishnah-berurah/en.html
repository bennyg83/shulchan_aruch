Who must, etc. Meaning that the caravan will not wait for him, or there is some other pressure or compulsion.
He may pray. Meaning that on an ordinary road he cannot concentrate in prayer, whether he is walking on foot or sitting in a wagon or a ship. Therefore the Sages said it is better that he pray from dawn, even though it is not yet the main time for prayer and he will also be unable to join redemption to prayer, since he cannot read Krias Shema then, for its time has not yet arrived until he can recognize his friend at a distance of four cubits. Nevertheless, this is better than praying on the road in its time with joining redemption to prayer, for in his house he can pray standing.
From dawn. See Biur Halacha on seif 1, where we wrote in the name of many poskim that they hold this is specifically after the east has lit up, and before this the time for prayer has not yet arrived. This is especially true if he wants then to bless over the tefillin, for even according to the lenient opinions this is forbidden even after the east has lit up, until the time when he can recognize his friend at a distance of four cubits. If that time has arrived, according to all opinions he should read Krias Shema and pray in his house properly, even though sunrise has not yet occurred, since it is a pressing circumstance. If he is on the road at an inn and his caravan is standing to pray before this time, and he estimates that by Barchu the time will arrive, he too may begin the blessing Baruch She'amar with them for now without tefillin, or he may put on tefillin without a blessing. After the time arrives, between Yishtabach and Yotzer, he should see whether the time has arrived, touch them, and bless.
If possible, etc. Meaning that if he knows that he will not be able to concentrate on even the first verse of Krias Shema on the road, such as if he is in a place of bands of wild animals or robbers, he may read Krias Shema in his house immediately from dawn, even though the main time has not yet arrived, as above in siman 58, seif 3.
This is more preferable. Most people are not careful about this. Perhaps it is because they follow the other poskim, who hold that joining redemption to prayer is preferable, and therefore they pray on the road with redemption joined to prayer. Magen Avraham, see there further. He also wrote that if one is on the road and fears that the time for Krias Shema will pass, he should read Krias Shema without the blessings; and when he comes to an inn, he should read Krias Shema again with its blessings and join redemption to prayer. If he sees that the time for prayer will also pass, which is only until four hours, he should certainly pray Shemoneh Esrei as well on the road, walking or sitting. Peri Megadim. When one travels at night and can come to an inn to pray Maariv there, it is proper to delay praying until he arrives there and not pray while still on the road, unless he sleeps on the wagon and there is concern that in the meantime dawn will rise. See Shaarei Teshuvah in siman 235, seif katan 3. Or, if he fears that he will not have a resting place there to pray, because the people of the house will disturb him.
