(4) There — if he prayed from when the column of dawn rose, etc. And the column of dawn is the light sparkling at the eastern corner before sunrise — for about an hour and a fifth of an hour of the seasonal hours — Rambam in his commentary on the Mishnah at the beginning of the first chapter of Berachos; and so too in the Gemara Pesachim 94a, for thus it is stated there: Rabbi Yehuda says — the thickness of the firmament is one tenth of the day; know how far a moderate person walks in a day — ten parasangs; and from the rising of dawn until sunrise four mil; from sunset until the emergence of the stars four mil — you find the thickness of the firmament is one tenth of the day — end quote of the Gemara. And what may be asked on the words of the Rambam — that here it implies he ruled like Rabbi Yehuda, but in his commentary on the Mishnah, chapter 9 of Pesachim, mishnah 2, and in his composition Laws of Korban Pesach chapter 5, laws 8–9, it implies he ruled like Ula that a moderate person walks thirty mil — see above; see Magen Avraham, note 2, who asked this question and left it as requiring investigation; and what Chok Yaakov wrote in siman 459, note 10; and Ra'av — that here it implies the measure of the time of dawn-rise is one tenth of the day, and now we calculate close to one eighth of the day as known to those who calculate the calendar. One could say that here it deals with from when the column of dawn rose and the face of the east brightened, but what we calculate is from point one when the column of dawn begins to rise — for there are three times, as stated in the previous note in the name of Peri Chadash; and see further in the Gemara where it says "they hurried — this case is different," and examine carefully.
