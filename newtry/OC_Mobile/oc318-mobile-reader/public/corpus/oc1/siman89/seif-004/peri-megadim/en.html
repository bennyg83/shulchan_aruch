The prohibition against eating before prayer is supported by the verses concerning arrogance and “you shall not eat upon the blood.” The requirement to interrupt when dawn arrives may appear difficult, since attending to one's affairs is permitted before the eastern horizon becomes illuminated, as I wrote in the third note. It may be answered that the present ruling indeed rests on two separate grounds: the concern of arrogance and also the Rabbinic prohibition derived from “you shall not eat upon the blood.” Greeting and attending to one's affairs from dawn before the eastern horizon becomes illuminated, by contrast, although supported by the verse “he is regarded as a high place,” do not involve the separate Rabbinic prohibition derived from “you shall not eat,” as it appears to me.
