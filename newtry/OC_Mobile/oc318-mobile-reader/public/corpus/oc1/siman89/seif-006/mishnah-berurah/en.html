(30) to learn is forbidden — That is, to begin learning; but if he already began, even after the time arrived he does not stop if he will remain with time afterward to pray at the time of prayer. But Keriat Shema — some say he must stop and recite it, since he began after its time already arrived; and some are lenient in this too unless he fears the time will pass. And see below siman 106 in Biur Halacha.

(31) once the time arrives, etc. — That is, from when amud hashachar rises; but before that it is permitted even to begin, and when he has begun he does not stop unless he fears the time will pass. Some are lenient lechatchilah even after amud hashachar rises — all this when the time close to sunrise fit for praying lechatchilah has not yet arrived.

(32) in his beis midrash — That is, alone; if the congregation is accustomed to assemble to him there, this too is permitted, for certainly they will remind him when they come. And see siman 90 seif 18 in the Hagahah.

(33) absorbed in his learning — See Chayei Adam who wrote that even if he has a fixed study session of what he learns, there is not so much concern he will be drawn out in learning — nevertheless it is forbidden. Therefore one who is on the road or dwells in a settled place, even in summer — even though it is a mitzvah to pray at the hour when the congregation prays — nevertheless since it is forbidden to learn before praying, better that he pray immediately so that he not be nullified from his learning. And in this era travelers for the most part do not learn Torah on the road, and it would be fitting to wait for the time when the congregation prays; nevertheless since through this one passes the time of Keriat Shema and sometimes also the time of prayer, therefore they should be careful that immediately upon rising, before engaging in any matter, they pray; and all the more so in large congregations they pray immediately when day breaks — and thus always at the hour when the congregation prays — end quote. It appears one should limit when blessing on tefillin that one be able to recognize one's friend at a distance of four amot, as we wrote above.

(34) And he passes by, etc. — and if he said to his friend that he is not learning, that if he is disturbed in his study he will remember him so as not to pass the time — it is permitted to learn.

(35) Teaching others, etc. — and specifically if Keriat Shema will not pass because of this; for if he sees that the time will pass he is obligated to read it beforehand, since the time has already arrived — and as I proved below in siman 106 in Biur Halacha.
