It is forbidden to learn. Meaning, to begin learning. But if he already began, even after the time arrived, he does not interrupt again if he will have time afterward to pray within the time for prayer. However, regarding Krias Shema, some say that he must interrupt and read it, since he began after its time had already arrived; and some are lenient in this as well, unless he fears the time will pass. See below in siman 106 in Biur Halacha.
From when it arrives, etc. This means from dawn. But before this it is permitted even to begin; and once he began, he does not interrupt, unless he fears the time will pass. Some are lenient initially even after dawn has risen, as long as the time close to sunrise, when it is proper initially to pray, has not arrived.
In his beis midrash. Meaning, alone. If the public is accustomed to gather with him there, this too is permitted, for they will certainly remind him when they come. See siman 90, seif 18, in the gloss.
He is absorbed in his learning. See Chayei Adam, who wrote that even if he has a fixed measure that he learns, so that there is not so much concern that he will be drawn after his learning, nevertheless it is forbidden. Therefore, one who is on the road or lives in a settlement, even in summer, although it is a mitzvah to pray at the time when the congregation prays, nevertheless, since it is forbidden to learn before he prays, it is better that he pray immediately so that he not be prevented from his learning. In our time, since travelers generally do not learn Torah on the road, it would have been proper for them to wait for the time when the congregation prays. Nevertheless, since this leads to passing the time for Krias Shema, and sometimes also the time for prayer, they should be careful that immediately when they rise, before they engage in anything, they pray. All the more so in large communities, where people pray immediately when the day has lit up, in which case it is always at the time the congregation is praying, end quote. One should try to time the blessing on the tefillin so that he can recognize his friend at a distance of four cubits, as we wrote above.
And he will pass, etc. If he told his friend, who is not learning, that if he becomes absorbed in his studies his friend should remind him not to let the time pass, it is permitted to learn.
He teaches others, etc. Specifically if Krias Shema will not pass because of this. If he fears the time will pass, he is obligated to read it first, since the time has already arrived, as I proved below in siman 106 in Biur Halacha.
