This is also implied by the Shulchan Aruch and is the ruling of Bach. Lechem Chamudos and Nachalas Tzvi discuss at length the source for the Shulchan Aruch's ruling that bowing is forbidden even when one did not arise early to go to his fellow's doorway. In my humble opinion, it may be explained as follows. There are two interpretations of the Gemara's statement that it is forbidden to greet someone when one arises early to go to his doorway. According to one interpretation it means an actual greeting of shalom, while according to the other it refers specifically to bowing, as is evident from Beit Yosef. According to the interpretation that it means an actual greeting, it appears that bowing is always forbidden.
