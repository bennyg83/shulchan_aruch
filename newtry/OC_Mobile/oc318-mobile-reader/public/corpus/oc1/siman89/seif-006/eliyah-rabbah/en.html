[6] Even without, etc. But otherwise — crossing over there — Levush; it appears the same law applies if one encounters him on the road by chance: it is forbidden. And so it appears in the Shulchan Aruch, and so ruled the Bach; and see in Lechem Chamudos there and in Nachalat Tzvi — they elaborated from where the Shulchan Aruch derived that bowing is forbidden even without going early to his doorway. In my humble opinion it is possible to explain because there are two explanations in what the Gemara said: it is forbidden to give greeting when one goes early to his doorway — and according to one explanation, actual greeting; and according to another explanation, specifically bowing, as is evident in Beit Yosef — and if so, per the explanation of actual greeting, bowing would always be forbidden.
