(Seif katan 5) And afterward, etc. “For in truth the time for Minchah, etc.” Meaning, the time for Minchah is from six and one half hours. Accordingly, if one transgressed and did not pray Shacharis before four hours, it should have been permissible to pray until six and one half hours. Bach indeed holds this way, and writes that the Shulchan Aruch’s statement “until midday” is not precise. Magen Avraham, however, holds that it is precise, because in truth the time for Minchah begins at midday. They merely added half an hour because of possible error and ruled not to pray Minchah until six and one half hours. In any event, from midday onward the time is no longer suited for Shacharis. Although Bach disputes Beit Yosef’s reasoning for prohibiting Shacharis immediately after midday, it is still prohibited after midday for another reason, namely that the time for Minchah begins immediately after midday. See Taz.
