He must interrupt. This means when dawn rises, as the Rosh writes. See below, siman 235, where it appears from Peri Megadim that if one began eating during the half hour before nightfall, he must interrupt immediately. Seemingly the same should apply here. Even were one to say that the prohibition against eating before prayer should not be treated like a Torah prohibition, even according to the first opinion, nevertheless, because the morning Krias Shema is a Torah obligation, the law should be that if they began shortly before its time, they must interrupt. Perhaps here they were not as stringent because even after dawn arrives one should not ideally fulfill the obligation of Krias Shema at that time, as stated above in siman 58, seif 4. Alternatively, perhaps the Rosh formulated the ruling this way because it also includes a case in which one began permissibly.
Some say that he need not. See Mishnah Berurah, where I wrote that he recites Krias Shema without its blessings. It appears to me that he is also obligated to put on tefillin before eating, for this too is a Torah obligation, and their time likewise begins when the time for Krias Shema begins, as stated above in siman 30, seif 1.
