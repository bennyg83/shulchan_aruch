One must stop. And even though for Minchah prayer one need not stop, as written in siman 232—here it is different, since they relied on the verse "do not eat upon the blood," one must stop; and therefore it is forbidden to begin eating at night if he knows the meal will continue until alot hashachar, such as at a large feast—see siman 232 seif 6. Nevertheless it is not so compelled; and in Tov Ha'aretz he wrote in the name of the Ra'ah that even if one arose at midnight of the night, it is forbidden to taste before he prays—and likewise in the Zohar.

And some say he need not. Since prayer is rabbinic, even if he began after alot hashachar rose he need not stop; nevertheless, because of Kriat Shema he must stop if he began during the prohibition, as written at the end of siman 70; and it must be that he recites Kriat Shema without its blessings—see siman 70. And it seems to me that if he blessed Baruch Hu and is engaged in drinking, according to all he must stop, as written in siman 239.
