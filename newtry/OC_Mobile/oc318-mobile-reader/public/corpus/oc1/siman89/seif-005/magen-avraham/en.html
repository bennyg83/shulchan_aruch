He must interrupt. Although regarding Minchah one need not interrupt, as written in siman 232, here it is different, since they based it on the verse, 'Do not eat upon the blood'; therefore he must interrupt. If so, it is forbidden to begin eating at night if he knows that the meal will continue until dawn rises, such as at a large meal. See siman 232, seif 6. However, this is not so compelling. In the book Tov HaAretz it is written in the name of Rav Chaim Vital that even if one rises at midnight, it is forbidden to taste before he prays, and so it appears from the Zohar.
And some say that he need not. Since prayer is D'Rabbanan, even if he began after dawn rose, he need not interrupt. However, because of Krias Shema he must interrupt if he began in a forbidden manner, as written at the end of siman 70. One must say that he reads Krias Shema without its blessings; see siman 60. It appears to me that if he recited Birkas HaMazon and is engaged in drinking, according to all opinions he must interrupt, as written in siman 239.
