(27) if he began to eat — Even if he was in a permitted situation, meaning there was more than half an hour until amud hashachar — within this half hour it is forbidden to begin eating, as Rama wrote in siman 592 regarding lulav; and the same regarding Keriat Shema which is from the Torah. Specifically if one eats more than a kebeitzah which is derech keva, or drinks in this quantity; but in less than that it is permitted until amud hashachar.

(28) must stop — When amud hashachar rises. Even though in Minchah prayer one need not stop as stated siman 232, here it is different since they based it on the verse "do not eat on the blood — do not eat before you pray for your blood" — one must stop. In the name of the Zohar the Acharonim wrote that even one who rises at midnight may not taste before praying. Peri Megadim wrote that specifically when he was asleep; and possibly drinking is permitted. By law even if asleep it is permitted to eat and drink; nevertheless lechatchilah it is proper to be careful in this if one is not compelled. And see Machatzis HaShekel who wrote in responsa Shevut Yaakov siman 8, in the name of the siddur of the Ari z"l, that the Rekanati himself when his heart was weak ate or drank something to strengthen his body, and he only forbade eating to satisfy appetite — see there. And in Chayei Adam kelal 16 he also wrote that one who rises before daylight and his heart is weak and through this would be nullified from learning — permitted to eat.

(29) and some say he need not, etc. — That is, for prayer even if he began in a forbidden situation; but for Keriat Shema alone, which is from the Torah, he must stop if he began in a forbidden situation and recites Keriat Shema without its blessings — for its blessings are from rabbinic authority — and after he finishes he recites Keriat Shema with its blessings and prays. The Acharonim wrote the main ruling is like the first view. If he blessed in Baruch She'amar and is engaged in drinking, he must stop according to this view.
