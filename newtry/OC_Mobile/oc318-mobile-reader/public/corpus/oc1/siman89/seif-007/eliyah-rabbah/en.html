[7] There is no concern, etc., to go to a friend's doorway and give him greeting — Levush. Malbushei Yom Tov wrote: he was not precise, for we do not say 'do not be concerned' except regarding bowing without going to the doorway — end quote; and similarly in Olas Tamid he challenged the Levush, who permits bowing even when going early to his doorway; and in Eliyahu Zuta I wrote that they were not precise in the Levush, for he wrote 'and to give him greeting' — which implies bowing is forbidden; and that which permits giving greeting even when going early to his doorway — this is since he already blessed, he relied on the one who explained the Gemara [Berachos] regarding bowing; and if so, to give greeting even when going early is permitted. And with this is also resolved what Nachalat Tzvi and Olas Tamid wondered about on the Gemara, page 14 — see there; and there is no need to elaborate; and all this is not like the Taz. And from the reason I mentioned it appears one may also bow when encountering him, since he already blessed; and so ruled the Bach. Piskei Tosafos wrote: one should be concerned in practice to forbid all of these even once the time of Keriat Shema or the evening prayer has arrived — but before Musaf or Mincha it is permitted.
