The sun. Maharshal wrote: on the days of Selichos it is fitting to be careful about this, for they are accustomed to hurry before sunrise and it is not correct, end quote. Magen Avraham wrote: and perhaps they relied on that it is trouble for the congregation — that they go to their houses and assemble again; nevertheless it appears l'chatchila not to hurry so much.

Alos hashachar. That is the light that shines toward the east before sunrise — an hour and a fifth of an hour. Magen Avraham there. (And Eliyah Rabbah wrote that specifically when the entire east has lit up — and it does not suffice when the dawn gleams as a point alone; and not as Magen Avraham, and not as Minchas Kohen there, who brought several proofs for his words.)

The day. And if the day is long — 18 hours — it is 6 hours of the day; and when the day is 9 hours, it is 3 hours. For always they calculate these hours relative to the length of that day, and they are called seasonal hours. Although I wrote in siman 1, seif kaf vav in the name of the Zohar that for midnight we calculate the night as 12 hours — that is different here, for with regard to the Omnipresent, blessed be He, night is like day illuminates (Magen Avraham siman 233, seif 4). And see siman 124, seif 2 that one must finish prayer before a third of the day.

To pray. Bach disagrees on this and permits praying after chatzos until the time of Mincha gedolah arrives (see above); and Taz ruled so in practice, only that from the time of Mincha gedolah, which is 6 and a half hours, he prays Mincha first and afterward completes Shacharis (see above); and so too Knesset HaGedolah and Peri Chadash. Magen Avraham wrote that in truth the time of Mincha begins after chatzos; if so he would pray Mincha twice. (And in Eliyah Rabbah he decided like Bach and Taz. However, if he transgressed intentionally, it seems we should rule like Shulchan Aruch.)
