The sun. Maharshal writes that during the days of Selichos one should be careful about this, for people commonly begin before sunrise, and this is improper. Magen Avraham writes that perhaps they relied on the fact that it would burden the congregation to go home and then assemble again. Nevertheless, ideally one should see to it not to arise so early.
Dawn. This means the light that shines on the eastern horizon one hour and one fifth of an hour before sunrise. See what Magen Avraham writes. (Eliyah Rabbah writes that this applies specifically when the entire eastern horizon has become illuminated; it is insufficient for dawn merely to flash like a single point. This is unlike Magen Avraham and unlike Minchas Kohen. See there, where he brings several proofs for his position.)
The day. Thus, when the day is eighteen hours long, this time is six ordinary hours into the day, and when the day is nine hours long, it is three ordinary hours into the day. These hours are always calculated in proportion to the length of the day, and they are called seasonal hours. Although I wrote in siman 1, seif katan 6, in the name of the Zohar, that for purposes of midnight the night is calculated as twelve hours, this case is different, for regarding Hashem, night shines like day. See Magen Avraham, siman 233, seif katan 4. See also siman 124, seif 2, that one must finish the prayer before one third of the day.
To pray. Bach disputes this and permits one to pray after midday until the time of Minchah Gedolah. See there. Taz likewise rules this way in practice, except that once the time of Minchah Gedolah arrives, which is six and one half hours into the day, he should first pray Minchah and afterward make up Shacharis. See there. This is also written by Knesses HaGedolah and Peri Chadash. Magen Avraham writes that the time for Minchah actually begins immediately after midday, and therefore he should pray Minchah twice. (Eliyah Rabbah rules in accordance with Bach and Taz. Nevertheless, where one intentionally allowed the time to pass, it appears to me that one should rule in accordance with the Shulchan Aruch.)
