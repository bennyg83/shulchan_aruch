And if he prayed from the rise of amud hashachar, etc. — amud hashachar is called when the light begins to sparkle at the edge of the east before sunrise of the sun, for the measure of an hour and a fifth of an hour (Rambam chapter 1 of Berachos), and not as some people err that amud hashachar is the morning star, which is a time long before, as is found in the Yerushalmi chapter 1 of Berachos. And it is implied from the words of the Mechaber that before the east became light, even if amud hashachar rose, he does not fulfill [his obligation] even b'dieved; and so it is implied from the words of Rabbeinu Yerucham and the opinion of Magen Avraham and Peri Chadash that from the rise of amud hashachar he fulfills b'dieved, for it is day. And in Peri Megadim he questions this, even though in the essence of the law it is implied that he holds like their words that from the Torah it is day even before the face of the east became light for every matter, since the dawn rose; and because of this, in a time of pressure it is permitted l'chatchila immediately from the rise of amud hashachar; nevertheless, when it is not a time of pressure, he tends to prove from the words of the Rambam regarding prayer that even b'dieved he does not fulfill from the rise of amud hashachar. However, more than this we find in Sefer Eliyah Rabbah and in Sefer Mateh Yehudah and the Gra in his book Shenos Eliyahu — all alike agreed that until the east becomes light, even b'dieved he does not fulfill, because he is still in the category of night until the east became light; and all the poskim who wrote "from the rise of amud hashachar" in some matter — they meant [after] the east became light; and likewise what we learned in Megillah 20, "and all of them who did from the rise of amud hashachar" — kosher — they meant [after] the east became light. I also found afterward in Sefer Magen Giborim that he tends to decide thus for halacha like Eliyah Rabbah; and it is astonishing that he did not mention that the Gra also wrote thus, and he brought on this proof from the Yerushalmi — see there in siman 1; and there is a practical difference from this for several laws in the Torah; and likewise what is written below in seif 8 that in a time of pressure it is permitted l'chatchila to pray from the rise of amud hashachar — that is also when the face of the east became light; and the Mechaber relied on this on what is above at the beginning of the siman that specifically after it became light — for a time of pressure l'chatchila is not more lenient than when it is not a time of pressure b'dieved (and with this what Peri Megadim wondered about from above siman 58 seifim 3 and 4 is removed); and likewise above in siman 58 seifim 3 and 4 regarding Kerias Shema it will also be specifically after the east became light; and see above in siman 58 in Biur Halacha where I brought proof from the words of the Rashba that he also holds like Eliyah Rabbah and the Gra and Mateh Yehudah mentioned above. And for practical law, certainly one must be concerned for all these great authorities in every matter whose mitzvah is by day if he did it before the east became light — to return and do it after it became light; however, regarding prayer it is possible to distinguish — and that is, when it is not a time of pressure, it appears one should be stringent in this even b'dieved, like Peri Megadim mentioned above; and so it is implied from Darkhei Chaim who copied for halacha like Bach and Eliyah Rabbah that it does not suffice when the dawn sparkles like a point, but until the entire east becomes light; but in a time of pressure, if he prayed before the east became light, it is possible to rely b'dieved on Magen Avraham and Peri Chadash and Peri Megadim mentioned above not to return and pray because of concern for a blessing in vain, and it requires study. And l'chatchila certainly one must be very careful in this even in a time of pressure. And all this is because of the mitzvah of Kerias Shema and prayer; and at a time when he also needs to put on tefillin, he should wait a little more — that is, until he can recognize his friend at a distance of four amos; and if he puts them on earlier, he should put them on without a blessing, and when the time arrives he should feel them and bless, as was explained in siman 30 seif 3.

He fulfilled — that is, b'dieved, as I wrote in Mishna Berurah; and so many Acharonim copied; and Peri Chadash disagrees with the Shulchan Aruch and holds that from when it became light is its time l'chatchila; only for veterans is it before sunrise. However, according to what we wrote above that before it became light he does not fulfill even b'dieved — if so, it is proven from all the poskim that even after it became light one should not pray l'chatchila, for this is still within [the category of] amud hashachar. Also there is no practical difference in all this for our law, for his words are only regarding the essence of the mitzvah of prayer; but according to what everyone in Israel practices and is careful l'chatchila to adjoin redemption to prayer — how can he pray l'chatchila immediately when the day becomes light? Behold he must recite Kerias Shema and its blessings, and for them we require specifically until he can recognize his friend at a distance of four amos, as was explained in siman 58. However, after this time it is possible one should not protest against those who practice leniently — see Bach and Derishah what they wrote for the opinion of Rabbeinu Yerucham; and see above in siman 58 in Biur Halacha. However, for the mitzvah from the preferred [manner], for everyone one should not pray [the Amidah of] eighteen before sunrise.

And after noon, etc. — and see in Mishna Berurah regarding the half hour after noon. And after this half hour, for everyone he should no longer pray the Shacharit prayer, even if what he did not pray until now was under duress — and all the more so if intentional; and one who prays his blessings in vain according to several poskim — rather he should pray first Mincha and afterward [the Amidah of] eighteen for the sake of Shacharit to complete it; and this is when the nullification of the prayer was initially under duress and not intentional, as will be explained below in siman 108. And a partial remedy — see regarding this there in seif 7.
