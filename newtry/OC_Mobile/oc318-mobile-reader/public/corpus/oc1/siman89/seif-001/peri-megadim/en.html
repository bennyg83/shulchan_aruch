And after — the Taz. Here there are five views, as I shall explain with Hashem's help. (1) The first way of Beit Yosef: the questioner asks until midnight and no more; and Rav Yehuda prays Mincha — all the more so when another prayer has not arrived — in half an hour one can pray Shacharit; and I explained that the reward of prayer not at its time — and so in chapter 3 of Laws of Prayer, law 1 — therefore there is no difference between Rabbi Yehuda and the Rabbis: one who prays not at its time, even half an hour (and similarly from the splitting of Mincha for Rabbi Yehuda until night — the whole day one prays, as we need to say "if so" below); and if mistaken — yes; if intentional — no. (2) The second way: half an hour — no; and "the whole day" means for the Rabbis who hold Mincha until evening, and for Rabbi Yehuda according to Mincha — and we do not push the dispute so far between Rabbi Yehuda and the Sages; and for each of the ways there is leniency and stringency as stated. And if we combine both ways together — certainly we do not push the dispute; and for Rabbi Yehuda until midnight, if intentional he prays; nevertheless in half an hour, if mistaken he prays — and all the more so if the time of another prayer has arrived — and so in that place we resolved three difficulties: (1) the difficulty of Lechem Mishneh and Taz here, that they do not distinguish until midnight to exclude half an hour; (2) the difficulty of Maharsha, that they do not distinguish regarding intentional — for Rabbi Yehuda, intentional too until midnight — and they answer for the Rabbis and Rabbi Yehuda: reward at its time, etc.; and the wording of the Rambam — passed and erred until midnight — yes; after midnight passed — no; and the author omitted after midnight — perhaps both ways are true, but Be'er HaGolah showed to Kaf He, seif 3, and there the author brings the words of Rashba as he understood — in half an hour he does not fulfill — see there; and the Rambam wrote "and if he passed or erred" — one could say neither this nor that after midnight; and more should be said that nevertheless he has no reward at its time — the author wrote "erred or passed" — nevertheless there is reward of prayer; and the Taz was left with when it says "the whole day" alone, half an hour — but from when it says "the whole day" it implies until night; and for Rabbi Yehuda the Mincha prayer is according to Mincha, and there is no time of Mincha, and still the day is great — and nevertheless he prays Shacharit (and in truth it requires investigation for the law in Kaf He — it implies whenever the time of the prayer after it has passed there are no makeup payments for Shacharit — and "if so" will be explained in Kaf He in the wording of Levush). He further asked the difficulty of Lechem Mishneh; therefore the Taz explained like Bach that two leniencies are true, and even half an hour intentionally too one prays in that half hour (not so if we combine — at least intentionally no); and it appears to me that since they do not distinguish until midnight and half an hour intentionally no — it implies that even intentionally, since the time of Mincha has not arrived — when Mincha arrives too, specifically if mistaken there are makeup payments, not if intentional (the reverse of way one from extreme to extreme); and automatically the same for Rabbi Yehuda — the whole day one prays like the Rabbis, and the difference is only reward at its time: for the Rabbis until midnight, and half an hour is a measure of not at its time; and from Mincha specifically if mistaken — and three times it is the same for Rabbi Yehuda until four hours its time, and from four hours until the end of six and a half intentionally not at its time, and at Mincha specifically if mistaken (the wording of the Taz can be reconciled thus; and what he wrote for the Rabbis even after Mincha — meaning when the time of Mincha has arrived; and for Rabbi Yehuda after the time of Mincha ends). And what he wrote — explanation of Lechem Chamudos — that one should pray makeup payments immediately after praying Mincha, not to interrupt between them; nevertheless if he waited a long time, nevertheless he completes Shacharit — and the proof: "the whole day" for Rabbi Yehuda said after splitting Mincha. And what he wrote between Mincha and Maariv — no time interrupts — implies in Beit HaShulchan too the time of Mincha is for the Rabbis, siman 233, and in our innovations we wrote from this — and if so Rabbi Yehuda speaks. And what he also wrote — the time of Mincha is for the Rabbis, siman 233 — and in our innovations we wrote from this — and if so Rabbi Yehuda speaks. And what he wrote after the time of Maariv — that one not interrupt between them with waiting. Nevertheless what he wrote — for thus is the intent of Rama — from what he showed the place in Kaf He, note 3, there is explained the explanation the author understood in Rashba, and since he was silent there I concede to him. Behold you have four views as stated.

However the fifth view that the Perishah examines is thus: the whole day from midnight until evening — even intentionally — reward not at its time; and if mistaken from half seven — if he completes it, reward at its time they give him; therefore it is called completing. And therefore in half an hour intentional and mistaken are equal — not at its time — see there. And the proof: since I did not explain what he wrote — do not infer intentional too prays — and on this it said "the whole day" — even intentional not at its time they give him. Nevertheless for the law one should not rely on this, only as the author and Be'er HaGolah ruled here — after midnight no; and regarding intentional at the time of Mincha in Kaf He, note 7 — specifically as a voluntary prayer, not plain — and "if so" will be explained there further. And those until midnight — it appears one must pray; and so is the wording of the Rambam — he fulfilled the obligation of prayer (the author changed slightly — understand). And in the Tur are two versions: "and if he passed and did not pray — he prays until midnight — obligation"; and "and if he passed and prayed until midnight" — one could say permission; and you see that one could say obligation to pray until midnight, and not at its time he has; and at the time of prayer if he wants — voluntary, not obligation. And half an hour — it is forbidden for him to pray even if mistaken and voluntary — do not say, since if mistaken the Taz in Kaf He, note 5 — and "if so" will be explained there further; and the Perishah wrote there is no difference between the versions, as one could say in this. Say from now: three times and four laws — from what is written until midnight one must pray and fulfills Shacharit prayer as the Rambam wrote, and reward not at its time he has; and it is reasonable we do not push the dispute — and for the Rabbis one must, and certainly the same for Rabbi Yehuda one must; and why did the author change from the wording of the Rambam (and even if we combine — this is hard) — in half an hour even if mistaken it is forbidden to pray; and at the time of Mincha if mistaken he completes; and for the author reward not at its time; and for the Perishah reward at its time (and it is possible there is a distinction if a rabbinic mitzvah occurred at the time of completion — which to push off — understand); and if intentional, if he wants he prays as voluntary and there is no obligation on him — and "if so" in Kaf He will be explained further. And I saw Peri Chadash in siman 108, note 3, explained in Turei Zahav like the Taz here — and it implies there he stopped and went to his business — he does not pray; and here is Rabbeinu Yeruham according to Mincha and the whole day — it is hard; and if we say like the Perishah — going to his business — no makeup payments like prayer at its time; also in our innovations we noted from Tosafos — "until" and "until" are inclusive; and now I saw Peri Chadash raised this.

And behold Peri Chadash has a sixth view, and exactly like the first way of Beit Yosef — Rif and Rosh — specifically where he erred and prayed — yes; if intentional — no; nevertheless if mistaken the whole day, even in half an hour. And behold in half an hour we have the ruling of the author and the rabbi; and one should be concerned for the ruling of Peri Chadash in the afternoon until midnight — if intentional there is a remedy that he stipulate and say: if the halacha is like Peri Chadash it shall be voluntary and it is fine; and from this I say to him in Kaf He, note 7, and Taz, note 5 — all who erred pray; intentional as voluntary is fine; and no innovation is needed even for us.
