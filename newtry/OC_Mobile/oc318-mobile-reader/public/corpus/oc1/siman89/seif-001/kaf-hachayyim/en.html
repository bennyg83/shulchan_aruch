1) [Se'if 1] The proper time for Shacharis is to begin at sunrise, etc. We have already explained the time of sunrise above in siman 58, paragraph 10; consult that discussion.
2) There: The proper time is to begin at sunrise, etc. I found written in the name of Maharshal that during the Selichos period one should take care concerning this, for people customarily pray before sunrise, which is improper. Perhaps they rely upon the burden to the congregation if they must go home and gather again, as Tur writes in siman 235 concerning Maariv. Nevertheless, initially one should try not to rise quite so early. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2, cites him and objects that the cases are not comparable: for such a brief interval they can wait in the synagogue rather than go home, unlike the case of Maariv. Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1, cites this and writes that the same applies in winter: if they pray early, they should wait. See the following paragraph.
3) There: If he prayed from alos hashachar, etc. Pri Chadash, paragraph 1, writes: The Mechaber states the law without clarifying it. In truth there are three stages for the beginning of prayer time. The principal mitzvah is at sunrise, like the vasikin, as it is written, "They shall fear You with the sun." Initially one may pray once alos hashachar has risen and the eastern sky has become illuminated, like the morning tamid. If he prayed from alos hashachar, however, he has fulfilled his obligation, as the Mishnah in Megillah teaches that all daytime mitzvos performed from alos hashachar are valid. The tamid too is not invalid if slaughtered from alos hashachar. The principal initial time for all daytime mitzvos is sunrise; only the tamid was advanced slightly before sunrise because all offerings had to be brought before the afternoon tamid. If one performed them from alos hashachar, he plainly fulfilled his obligation, unlike the implication of Rabbeinu Yerucham cited by Beis Yosef and of the Mechaber. Ateres Zekenim, paragraph 1, writes that alos hashachar precedes the time at which one can recognize an acquaintance four amos away. From the latter time one may initially pray, though the preferred mitzvah is to pray immediately after sunrise. Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 1, writes the same in the name of Rabbeinu Yerucham. Under this understanding of Rabbeinu Yerucham, one should not protest so strongly against those who pray early during the Selichos period, because they do not pray so early as to precede the time at which one can recognize an acquaintance. Thus, all agree that the preferred mitzvah requires sunrise, and that after the fact one who prayed from alos hashachar has fulfilled his obligation even if the eastern sky was not yet illuminated. Magen Avraham, paragraph 3, writes likewise. Erekh HaShulchan, paragraph 1, similarly cites Ramban in Milchamos at the beginning of Berachos, unlike Eliyah Rabbah and Mateh Yehudah, paragraph 1, who require the eastern sky to be illuminated even after the fact. This also appears from the unqualified language of the poskim that one fulfills the obligation from alos hashachar. Pri Chadash holds that once the eastern sky is illuminated one may pray initially; Maran regards even that as only after the fact, as Shulchan Aruch states. Ateres Zekenim permits initial prayer from the time one can recognize an acquaintance four amos away, which also appears to be Rabbeinu Yerucham's view according to Maamar Mordechai. We do not, however, find that Maran makes this distinction in Beis Yosef. See what we wrote above in siman 25, paragraph 27: according to the mystical teaching one must wear tzitzis and tefillin before Krias Shema in the order of the offerings, or at least before the passage of the tamid. The time for putting on tefillin is when one can recognize an acquaintance, as explained in siman 30, se'if 1, and our discussion there. If one waits until that time, puts on tzitzis and tefillin, and begins from the passage of the Akeidah, praying deliberately and calmly, word by word as proper without omitting any letter and with particular concentration upon the Divine Names, his Shemoneh Esrei will occur near sunrise (the emergence of the sun's body above the earth) as explained above in siman 58, paragraph 10. Fortunate is one who serves his Master properly and does not begrudge a short amount of time for prayer, for these matters stand at the height of the world.
4) There: If he prayed from alos hashachar, etc. Alos hashachar is the light shining at the eastern horizon before sunrise by one hour and one-fifth of a seasonal hour. Rambam, commentary to the Mishnah, beginning of Berachos. This also follows from Pesachim 94a: Rabbi Yehudah says that the thickness of the firmament is one-tenth of a day. An average person walks ten parsah in a day; from alos hashachar to sunrise is four mil, and from sunset until the emergence of the stars is four mil. The thickness of the firmament is thus one-tenth of a day. Rambam appears here to rule like Rabbi Yehudah, whereas in his commentary to Pesachim, chapter 9, mishnah 2, and Hilchos Korban Pesach, chapter 5, halachos 8-9, he appears to rule like Ulla, that an average person walks thirty mil in a day. Magen Avraham, paragraph 2, raises this question and leaves it unresolved. See Chok Yaakov to siman 459, paragraph 10. Although the calculation here makes alos hashachar one-tenth of the day before sunrise, whereas we currently calculate close to one-eighth of the day, as calendar experts know, one can answer that the present discussion concerns when alos hashachar has risen and the eastern sky has become illuminated. Our calculation begins at the first point at which alos hashachar starts to rise. There are three stages, as explained in the preceding paragraph in the name of Pri Chadash. See also the Gemara there, which says that the haste of the Israelites was different. Consider this carefully.
5) There: If he prayed from alos hashachar, etc., he fulfilled his obligation. This implies that after the fact it is permitted even without pressing circumstances; in pressing circumstances it is permitted initially, as stated at the end of this siman. Maadanei Yom Tov, Netzach Yisrael, and Lechem Chamudos at the beginning of the chapter Tefillas HaShachar; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1, unlike Levush.
6) There: If he prayed from alos hashachar, etc., he fulfilled his obligation. One who prays before the prayer's proper time has not fulfilled his obligation and must repeat it at its time. Rambam, Hilchos Tefillah, chapter 3, halachah 7; Beis Yosef.
7) There: If he prayed from alos hashachar, etc., he fulfilled his obligation. Although the time for Birchos HaShachar begins from midnight, as we wrote above in siman 1, paragraph 7, and siman 46, paragraph 49, prayer is different because it corresponds to the tamid offering. Its time therefore begins specifically at alos hashachar, as explained in Beis Yosef and above, paragraph 3. All four sections of prayer, beginning with Krias Shema in the order of the offerings, must be recited only from alos hashachar onward. This is known to those versed in the hidden wisdom and explained in Shaar HaKavanos, discourse 5 concerning tefillin, the discourse on Kaddish, and many other places for one who understands the Rav's words. We already wrote above in paragraph 3 that tzitzis and tefillin must likewise be put on before all four sections of prayer; see there.
8) There: Its time extends through the end of four hours, which is one-third of the day. If the day is eighteen hours long, this is six ordinary hours into the day; if the day is nine hours long, this is three ordinary hours. Magen Avraham, paragraph 4; Soles Belulah, paragraph 4; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1; Chayei Adam, rule 27, paragraph 1. Shulchan Aruch HaRav writes there that these seasonal hours are calculated from alos hashachar until the emergence of the stars. Tiferes Adam writes likewise there.
