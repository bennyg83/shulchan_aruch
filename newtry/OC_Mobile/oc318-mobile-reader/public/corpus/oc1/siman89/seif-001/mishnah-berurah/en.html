At sunrise. This is the time when the sun begins to shine on the tops of the mountains. It is proper and correct to be careful about this initially, even during the days of Selichos, and likewise in winter when people rise early and come to the synagogue very early in the morning, they should wait to pray until sunrise, as above in siman 58, seif 1, see there. However, when one rises early to set out on the road, or in a similar pressing circumstance or compulsion, where it is permitted to pray earlier as below in seif 8, in any case they should wait to bless on the tefillin until one can recognize an acquaintance at a distance of four cubits, for before then it is forbidden to bless over them, as above in siman 30, seif 1; see there in the Mishnah Berurah. The Peri Megadim wrote in siman 494 that on Shavuos, when people are awake all night, it is permitted to pray before sunrise, because if they go home in the morning it is a burden to gather again. Nevertheless, one should be careful not to read Krias Shema so very early, meaning before he sees his friend at a distance of four cubits and recognizes him, for this is forbidden initially, as above in siman 58; see there.
Dawn. Dawn is when the morning begins to brighten somewhat, little by little. From the words of the Mechaber it implies that before it has lit up, even after the fact he has not fulfilled. Many later authorities agree, unlike the Magen Avraham; see Biur Halacha.
The eastern face. We require that the face of the entire east light up, and it is not enough that dawn has brightened like a mere point. Rabbi Akiva Eiger wrote in his novellae that even though stars are still visible in the sky, nevertheless it is day.
He fulfilled. After the fact, and even not in a pressing circumstance. In a pressing circumstance, such as when he rises early to set out on the road, or a similar other pressure or compulsion, it is permitted even initially, as below in seif 8; see there. Know that the words of the Shulchan Aruch were stated only regarding the obligation of prayer, that he fulfills it after the east has lit up, for by then its time has arrived, since prayer was enacted corresponding to the tamid, and the slaughter of the tamid was immediately when the east lit up. But regarding Krias Shema before prayer, the Mechaber is not speaking in this siman. See above in siman 58, in Mishnah Berurah seif katan 19, that he does not fulfill the obligation of Krias Shema even after the fact if he does this regularly, until he can recognize his acquaintance at a distance of four cubits; and this is later than the time when the east has lit up. This is especially true when he then wants to put on tefillin and bless over them, for certainly he should not bless until the time of recognizing his friend arrives.
The day. If the day is eighteen hours long, this is six hours into the day; and when the day is nine hours long, this is three hours, for we always measure these hours according to the nature of the day. They are called seasonal hours, for the day is divided into twelve parts. The matter of the third is that the morning prayer corresponds to the daily tamid offering, and they would never delay offering it until after a third of the day. One must finish Shemoneh Esrei within the third of the day.
Or transgressed. Meaning that he deliberately delayed the prayer until its time passed, and afterward prayed. Even so, he fulfilled the obligation of prayer, though it was not in its time. The same is true initially: if he did not pray earlier, he must pray after four hours until noon, only he does not receive reward like prayer in its time. It is good that he pray then as a voluntary prayer, because there are poskim who hold that if he deliberately delayed until four hours, he can no longer pray at all. Therefore he should pray and stipulate that if by law he is exempt, it should be a voluntary prayer. Peri Megadim.
And after noon. From the Rama's language it implies that even immediately after noon it is forbidden to pray Shacharis, and this refers to what was said above, whether he deliberately transgressed or erred inadvertently. Some are lenient during the half-hour after noon. In practice, it appears from the Peri Megadim and Derech HaChaim that one should not move from the ruling of the Rama; and so it appears from the Beur HaGra, for in truth the time of Minchah has already arrived, beginning from the start of the seventh hour. But because we are not expert and he may come to err, the Sages forbade it. Therefore, when he erred or was compelled and did not pray before noon, he should wait half an hour after noon, meaning until the time of Minchah arrives, and pray Minchah and afterward Shacharis as a makeup, and not the reverse. This applies specifically when he erred or was compelled; but deliberately, he has no remedy, as all this will be explained in siman 108. However, after the fact, if he prayed Shacharis during this half-hour after noon, he fulfilled and need not pray again. Derech HaChaim; see there further. See the novellae of Rabbi Akiva Eiger regarding what he wrote in the name of the Tzlach on this matter.
