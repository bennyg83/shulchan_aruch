(a) With the netz (sunrise)—it is the hour when the sun begins to shine on the mountaintops. And it is fitting and correct to be careful about this initially, even on Selichot days; and likewise in winter when people wake early and come to synagogue in the morning—they should wait from praying until sunrise, as above in siman 58 seif 1—see there. But when one wakes early to set out on a journey and similar cases of other pressure and compulsion, it is permitted to pray early as will be stated below in seif 8; in any case they should wait from blessing on tefillin until he recognizes his usual companion somewhat at a distance of four amot—for beforehand it is forbidden to bless on them, as above in siman 30 seif 1—see there in Mishna Berurah. The Peri Megadim wrote in siman 494 that on Shavuot when they stay awake all night, it is permitted to pray before sunrise—for when they go to their homes at dawn it is troublesome to assemble again. Nevertheless one should be careful not to recite Kriat Shema so early at dawn—that is, before he sees his friend at a distance of four amot and recognizes him—for this is forbidden lekhatchilah, as above in siman 58—see there.

(b) Amud hashachar (dawn)—that is [explained] in [siman 58], it is when dawn begins to shine in part of it little by little. And it is implied from the words of the Mechaber that before the east has brightened, even after the fact he did not fulfill; and many Acharonim agreed, not like the Magen Avraham—and see in Biur Halacha.

(c) The face of the east—and we require that the entire face of the east brighten, and it does not suffice with dawn having shone only as a point. The Rosh Aharon wrote in his chiddushim that even though the stars are still visible in the sky, nevertheless it is day.

(d) he fulfilled — bedi'eved, and even not at a time of pressing need; and at a time of pressing need, such as when one rises early to set out on a journey, and similarly other pressing need and duress — even lechatchilah, as below in seif 8, see there. And know that the words of the Shulchan Aruch apply only regarding the obligation of the mitzvah of prayer alone — that one fulfills it after the east has become light, for then its time has already arrived, as it was enacted corresponding to the tamid, and the slaughter of the tamid was immediately when the east became light. But regarding Keriat Shema that precedes prayer, the author does not deal with this in this siman; and see above in siman 58 in Mishna Berurah sk"19, that one does not fulfill the obligation of Keriat Shema thereby even bedi'eved. If one is accustomed to do so until he recognizes his regular companion somewhat at a distance of four amot — and this is later than the time when the east became light — and in particular when one then wishes to put on tefillin and bless over them, for certainly one may not bless until the time arrives of recognizing the above.

(e) the day — If the day is eighteen hours long, six hours are a third of the day. When the day is nine hours long, three hours are a third. For always these hours are calculated relative to the length of that day, and they are called seasonal hours, as the day is divided into twelve parts. The meaning of a third is that Shacharit corresponds to the tamid offering, and they never delayed bringing it after a third of the day, and one must finish Shacharit within the third of the day.

(f) or he transgressed — That is, intentionally: one delayed prayer until its time passed and then prayed — nevertheless one fulfills the obligation of prayer even though it was not at its time. The same applies lechatchilah: if one did not pray earlier, one must pray after the fourth hour until noon — only one does not receive reward like prayer at its time. It is good that one then pray as a nedavah, for there are poskim who hold that in intentional delay until the fourth hour one can no longer pray at all; therefore one should pray and stipulate that if according to law one is exempt, this is a nedavah [Peri Megadim].

(g) and after noon — It is implied from the language of Rabbeinu Yonah that even immediately after noon it is forbidden to pray Shacharit. And what was stated above applies whether one transgressed intentionally or erred unwittingly. Some are lenient in the half hour after noon. For practical law it is implied from Peri Megadim and Darkhei Chayim that one should not deviate from Rabbeinu Yonah's ruling; and similarly it is implied from Biur HaGra that in truth Minchah time has already arrived, beginning from the start of seven — but because we are not expert and perhaps one will come to err, therefore Chazal forbade it. Therefore when one erred unwittingly or was compelled and did not pray before noon, one waits after noon a half hour (that is, so that Minchah time will arrive), prays Minchah, and afterward Shacharit as tashlumin — not the reverse. And this is specifically in error or compulsion; but intentionally there is no remedy, as will be explained all of this in siman 108. However bedi'eved, if one prayed Shacharit in this half hour after noon, one has fulfilled and need not pray again [Darkhei Chayim, see there further]. And see in Chidushei R' Akiva Eiger what he wrote in the name of Tzelach on this matter.
