[1] When the eastern horizon became illuminated, etc. This applies specifically when the entire eastern horizon has become illuminated; it is insufficient for dawn merely to flash at a single point. Bach writes this. Magen Avraham, however, writes that even if the eastern horizon did not become illuminated at all, one has fulfilled his obligation, because according to the strict law the daily offering may be brought from dawn. The requirement that the eastern horizon become illuminated is only a safeguard lest one err, as stated in Yoma, chapter 3, 28b. In my humble opinion, the strict law itself requires the eastern horizon to become illuminated. This also appears from Rambam at the beginning of Hilchos Berachos. They went up to the roof to look because of the possibility of error, and this likewise appears from the Gemara there. Study it carefully. This is also unlike Minchas Kohen, page 3. All the poskim who mention dawn presumably mean that the eastern horizon has become illuminated, because that is what dawn signifies. Furthermore, when a safeguard is enacted lest one err, even after the fact the act is invalid, as explained in siman 233. Although Magen Avraham writes that here error is possible only when moonlight rises, this is incorrect, for he overlooked Bartenura there, who writes that even on Yom Kippur, when moonlight is not a concern, we fear that another similar error may occur. Rambam, in his commentary to the Mishnah, writes that an error in fact occurred on Yom Kippur. The Gemara there on page 29 requires study.
