At sunrise. I found written in the name of Rashal: during the days of Selichos one should be careful about this, for people are accustomed to pray early before sunrise, and this is not proper, end quote. Perhaps they relied on the fact that it is a burden on the congregation to go home and gather a second time, as the Tur wrote in siman 235 regarding Maariv. Nevertheless, initially it appears that one should not rise quite so early.
From when dawn has risen. This means the light that shines on the eastern edge before sunrise, an hour and a fifth of an hour before sunrise, according to the Rambam. Meaning, before sunrise, for we say in the ninth chapter of Pesachim that from dawn until sunrise is five mil. Nevertheless, the Rambam requires study, for he himself, in the third chapter of Pesachim, Mishnah 2, as the Tosafot Yom Tov wrote in his name, holds that the measure of a mil is two fifths of an hour. If so, five mil is two hours, so why did he write an hour and a fifth? Even according to what is written in siman 459, seif 2, that a mil is a quarter-hour and a twentieth, five mil would be an hour and a half. It appears to me that he ruled like the one who says that from dawn to sunrise is four mil, because the other view was rejected by a difficulty. Nevertheless, his words contradict what he wrote in Pesachim, chapter 3; see there.
And if the eastern face lit up, he fulfilled. So wrote Rabbeinu Yerucham. But the other poskim wrote simply that if one prayed from dawn, he fulfilled; and so it appears to me, for as a matter of law, from dawn it is valid for offering the tamid. It is only that we are concerned lest one come to err, and therefore we require that the eastern face have lit up, as is found at the beginning of the third chapter of Yoma. So too at the end of the second chapter of Megillah, in the Mishnah: all of them, if done from dawn, are valid. This is not comparable to what is written in siman 233, that because of a concern lest one come to err, even after the fact he has not fulfilled, for there it is a common error, since it is impossible to determine the hour so precisely, as is found in the first chapter of Pesachim. But at dawn there is no error except when the light of the moon rises, as is found at the beginning of the third chapter of Yoma.
A third of the day. If the day is eighteen hours long, this is six hours into the day; and when the day is nine hours long, it is three hours. See what I wrote in siman 233, and what I wrote in siman 124, seif 2, and siman 232, seif 1, that one must finish the prayer before one third of the day.
And after noon, etc. Until the time of Minchah arrives, and he prays Minchah twice, for in truth the time of Minchah begins after noon. See what I wrote in siman 233. The Bach disagrees, but his words are not exact.
