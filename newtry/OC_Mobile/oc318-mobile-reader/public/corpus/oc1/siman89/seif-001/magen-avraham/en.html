With sunrise (netz ha-chamah). Maharshal wrote: on Selichot days one should be careful about this, for they are accustomed to pray before sunrise and this is not correct—end quote. And perhaps they relied on the trouble to the community—that they would go to their homes and assemble again—as the Tur wrote in siman 235 regarding Maariv. Nevertheless, ideally one should not wake so early.

From when the morning [star] rises (alot hashachar). That is, the light sparkling at the edge of the east before sunrise—an hour and a fifth of an hour [Rambam]—meaning before sunrise (netz ha-chamah), for they say from alot hashachar until sunrise is five mil in chapter 9 of Pesachim. Nevertheless there is to question the Rambam, for he himself in chapter 3 of Pesachim mishnah 2—the Taz wrote in his name that the measure of a mil is two fifths of an hour, and therefore five mil is two hours; so why did he write an hour and a fifth? And even according to what is written in siman 459 seif 2 that a mil is a quarter and a part of an hour, therefore five mil would be an hour and a half. And it seems he ruled like the one who says from alot hashachar until sunrise is four mil, because he concluded with a refutation of the other opinion; nevertheless his words contradict what is written in Pesachim chapter 3—see there.

And the face of the east has brightened—he fulfilled. So wrote Rabbeinu Yeruham; but the other poskim wrote simply that if he prayed from alot hashachar he fulfilled, and so it seems to me—for from the law of alot hashachar it is valid for offering the tamid, only they are concerned lest one come to err; therefore we require that the face of the east has brightened, as stated in Yoma 28b, and likewise at the end of chapter 2 in Megillah in the mishnah—and all who acted from alot hashachar are valid. And one could say regarding what is written in siman 233 that because of concern lest one err, even after the fact he did not fulfill—there it is a common error, for it is impossible to pinpoint the hour so precisely, as stated in the first chapter of Pesachim; but regarding alot hashachar there is no concern to err except when the light of the moon rises, as stated in Yoma 28b.

A third of the day. And if the day is long—18 hours—there are six hours on the day; and when the day is long nine hours, there are three hours, as he wrote in siman 233, and as he wrote in siman 124 seif 2 and siman 232 seif 1 that one must finish the prayer before a third of the day.

And after midnight, etc.—until the time of Minchah arrives and he prays Minchah twice, for in truth the time of Minchah begins after midnight, as he wrote in siman 233; and the Bach disagrees, and his words are not correct.
