(1) He swallows them. This is because he cannot spit them out, as they would become repulsive and be wasted. Nor can he move them to one side of his mouth and recite a blessing, as in seif 2 regarding food, because one cannot speak while liquid is in his mouth. They therefore permitted him to swallow it. Levushei Serad writes that if he placed only a small amount in his mouth and can recite the blessing with difficulty, he should do so.
(2) And he does not recite a blessing over them, etc. Some say that this applies only when he has no more liquid to drink and is dependent upon this liquid. If he has more, he should spit it out rather than benefit without a blessing. This is the proper practice. Magen Avraham and Eliyah Rabbah.
(3) The initial blessing. The reason is that once he has already swallowed the liquid, the mitzvah of the initial blessing has passed. He must, however, recite an after-blessing if he drank the required quantity. See below, siman 210.
(4) And some say that he recites a blessing over them. Meaning, after swallowing he recites the initial blessing over them. This is unlike the ruling in siman 167, seif 8, that if one remembers after finishing his meal that he did not recite a blessing, he does not do so. Here, because he remembered while the liquid was still in his mouth, he was obligated to recite a blessing at that time. This resembles, to some degree, reciting a blessing before performance, except that he was unable to recite it then. He therefore does not lose the blessing.
(5) And this appears to be the primary view. Most poskim follow the first opinion, that he does not recite the initial blessing, but only an after-blessing if he drank the required quantity. Eliyah Rabbah likewise agrees.
