1) [Seif 1] If one forgot and put it into his mouth, etc., he swallows it and does not recite a blessing, etc. This is because the liquids have lost their status as beverages, since they are no longer fit for everyone to drink. Therefore, one need not be concerned even if he could recite the blessing with difficulty. Raavad writes that this applies specifically when he has no more liquid to drink and urgently needs these liquids. If he has more, however, he should spit them out and not derive benefit without a blessing merely to save a small amount of liquid. It is proper to conduct oneself this way and not enter into a dispute. Bach writes the same; Magen Avraham, paragraph 1; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1. Nevertheless, it appears to me that it is better to recite the blessing in his heart, because the Rambam, of blessed memory, in chapter 1 of Hilchos Berachos, law 7, holds that after the fact one fulfills his obligation if he recites a blessing in his heart. Although we do not rule accordingly, in any event, in such a case, where he has already placed the liquid in his mouth, it will go to waste if he spits it out, and there is a further dispute if he recites the blessing afterward, it is better to rely upon the Rambam's view that after the fact he fulfills his obligation. Only if, for some reason, he cannot bear to keep the liquid in his mouth until he recites the blessing in his heart, one may say that he should spit it out so as not to enter into a dispute and also not to derive benefit without a blessing.
