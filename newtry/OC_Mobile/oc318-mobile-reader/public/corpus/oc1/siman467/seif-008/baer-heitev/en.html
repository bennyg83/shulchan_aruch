In barrels, etc., that they make, etc. Explanation: two things are required — specifically from that which is in the barrel it is permitted to make a drink; for even if you say there is a little flour in it, it is nullified in sixty at the time of cooking before Pesach; but those that they bring in small vessels are forbidden even to make a drink from them, and many times it was clarified to me that they mix much flour in them. And to eat the honey in its own form in any case is forbidden, for it comes to be confused; and so too Maharil and Maharshal. And even to make the drink on Pesach — Bach; however honey that they bring with small hives whole as it lies in the hive, not yet crushed — they practice permissively according to all, for there is no way to mix anything in it when they shake them from the hive to the hive; and see Magen Avraham. And Taz wrote that the implication of the Rabbi's words implies those they bring in barrels from the hive — that even to eat it thus in its own form is permitted; therefore it appears to me complete permission to cook on chol ha-mo'ed a drink in honey that is recognizable as coming from a hive, as long as it is fit for drinking on Yom Tov; even though Mo'ach wrote to forbid making the drink on the festival from concern for mixture — nevertheless it appears to me that one who is lenient for honor of Yom Tov in these lands where we have no other drink on Pesach has not lost. And tsuker. Ateret Zekenim wrote: I saw that a question came before the elders, the Gaon Maharash, may his memory be blessed, on the eve of Pesach — hot tsuker had rested, and he ruled to sell to a gentile — end quote. And Taz and Magen Avraham wrote that there is no prohibition in hot tsuker or in what we call kandil tsuker to keep it in one's house; nevertheless they are accustomed to sell it to a gentile l'chatchila, but b'dieved it is permitted — and this is the intent of the Rabbi in the gloss: tsuker kandil. And not what we now call tsuker kandil — that one has more concern and is full chametz, for they cook white tsuker in sour vats and it becomes red, and even to keep it is forbidden because there is a mixture of chametz in it. And all the more so types of tsuker confections in which they coat types of fruits and spices — this has full leavening. And Chayei Adam wrote that even l'chatchila one should be careful regarding keeping these things; nevertheless if one transgressed and kept those mixtures and there is great loss or prevention of Yom Tov joy, one does not forbid b'dieved, for there is much uncertainty and concern — perhaps it was not mixed in it; and if you say it was mixed in it, perhaps it was nullified before Pesach; and if you say it was not nullified, perhaps they did not mix wheat flour in it for strawberries; and fruit juice does not leaven; and if you say for soaking — perhaps it did not leaven through this soaking. Nevertheless before the decisor rules he needs investigation by a sage how and what was done, for this matter varies according to place and time; and l'chatchila they are accustomed to be stringent in every matter. But what we now call tsuker kandil — many common people are lenient and this error is drawn for them from the language of the Rabbi in the gloss; and the later authorities already agreed that it is an error in their hands, as written. And see in Shachnahag that he wrote that also tsuker that is ground very fine has also concern for leavening; and also the drink made from tsuker that they call sharpi or lat and arig — they are accustomed to be stringent; and see in Sefer Bnei Chayya:
