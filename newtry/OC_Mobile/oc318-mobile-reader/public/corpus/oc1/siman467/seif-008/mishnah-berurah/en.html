(2) and eat it on Passover - some have written, that is, to eat the honey in its entirety as it is, but kneading it into a paste is prohibited because it is found that when removing the wax from the honey, mix water with it, and especially water with fruit juice and acidifiers:

(24) However there are those who are stringent — that they are accustomed to mix flour into it for sweetness:

(25) Not to eat honey only, etc. — explanation: in this honey that they bring in a barrel from the hive where wax is still mixed in, the custom is to eat it even in its own form, and likewise to make from it a medicinal drink; because there is no manner of forgery; and even to make from it a medicinal drink on Pesach is permitted because of Yom Tov rejoicing if he has no other drink. But what they bring in small vessels cleaned of wax — the manner of non-Jews is to mix flour in them; therefore it is forbidden to eat it, and even to make from it a drink on Pesach. And there are among the Acharonim who are stringent in this even before Pesach, because it is common that they mix much flour in them until there are not even in the water mixtures sixty against the flour to nullify them; and likewise they are stringent not to eat honey in its own form on Pesach even from those that come from large barrels, lest one come to be lenient with all honey [except those they bring with the small rooms whole as it lies in the hive that has not yet been crushed]. And regarding practical halachah in making a medicinal drink before Pesach, it is implied from the words of the Acharonim that they are now accustomed to be lenient even from those in small vessels, for we suspend that the flour will be nullified in sixty [unless it is known that in those places the manner is to mix much flour]; and nevertheless one who is stringent in this not to make a medicinal drink except from honey that comes in a barrel with wax — praise to him. And regarding eating on Pesach honey in its own form from the barrels — it depends on local custom, and in our country the custom is to be lenient; and nevertheless one must investigate and know the local custom, for there are places where they put bread in the hive at crushing time or other chametz matters; and in a place where there is concern for this, the honey is forbidden to eat in its own form on Pesach, but it is permitted to make from it a medicinal drink before Pesach [Chayei Adam]:

(26) And dried figs — for there is concern they sprinkle flour on them when they dry them; and grapes are accustomed to dry them in an oven near bread or after they removed the bread from the oven; and there are places where they do not sprinkle flour on them and dry them in the sun — and this is what he wrote that it depends on local custom:

(27) Not to eat any fruits, etc. — that is, for the above reason. And nevertheless the custom is lenient to make from them a drink on Pesach — that is, they soak them in water until their taste is absorbed in the water and they drink the water on Pesach; for since there is in them only a general concern, they are not so stringent to forbid even their soaking water; but they are not so lenient to cook them on Pesach in order to drink their cooking water on Pesach, because through cooking their taste is absorbed in the water more than through soaking; and in some places they are accustomed to be stringent with soaking water as with cooking water [unless they soaked and cooked them before Pesach]; and likewise on the eve of Pesach in the name of Noda B'Yehuda agrees that l'chatchila it is proper to be stringent, and therefore it is good to strain the liquid from the fruits on the eve before nightfall — then even if a little flour adhered to the fruits and became mixed in the liquid, it is nullified in sixty:

(28) In a manner where there is no concern for chametz — such as if it is known to him that it dried in the sun or in an oven after kashering it:

(29) And even to retain it is forbidden — for there is concern they mix flour into it; and the Acharonim agreed that that which we call good sugar, made like a hat, has been clarified through investigation of craftsmen that they do not mix flour into it; and nevertheless they were accustomed to sell it l'chatchila [and b'dieved it is permitted after Pesach, and also on the last Yom Tov they eat it]; and that which has a hechsher certificate from a rabbi from its place of manufacture — they are accustomed to eat it l'chatchila. However sugar made very fine has more concern for chametzing from flour mixtures, and it is forbidden to retain it; and nevertheless b'dieved if one transgressed and retained them, it is implied from Acharonim not to forbid this either; and see in responsa the manner of certification in this regarding eating l'chatchila. And know that in sugar mixtures where they coat types of fruits and spices, and all the more so those made like small balls from sugar mixtures — the Acharonim agreed that one must certainly be concerned lest there is a chametz mixture in them, and it is forbidden to retain by law, and all the more so to eat them even on the last Yom Tov of the diaspora:

(30) And on the last Yom Tov — of the diaspora; since it is rabbinic, we are not stringent regarding something that has only a general concern:

(31) And candy sugar that does not have, etc. — this is not the cooked sugar cut into pieces that we call candy sugar, for in those there is greater concern for chametzing and it is forbidden to retain them and forbidden to eat them even on the last Yom Tov; rather it is sugar that comes from the land of Canada, and it is like our good sugar, and it was clear to them that there is no concern for chametzing at all; and nevertheless on other days they may not eat them except through certification, so that one should not come to be lenient also in sugar mixtures. And the Acharonim wrote that even l'chatchila one must certainly be careful not to retain our Canadian sugar and sugar mixtures; nevertheless if one transgressed and retained them, one does not forbid after Pesach, for there are several uncertainties in this — see Kaf HaChayim:

(32) And the custom in these countries not to eat, etc. — but to retain them is permitted:

(33) Turmeric — for they forge them with flour to establish its appearance; and likewise they place se'or on it to restore its appearance; and the Acharonim wrote that even turmeric grown in one's garden and guarded from chametzing — the custom is to treat it as forbidden because of appearance, for turmeric is not common in these countries and they will think it is turmeric that comes from distant places; unlike other fruits that were dried by a Jew and guarded from chametzing, where we are not concerned because of appearance, since they are common and everyone knows that sometimes they dry them through a Jew. They further wrote that tobacco — the manner is to soak it in beer in some places, and one must seal it in a room or make a partition before it; and likewise turmeric in places where the custom is to treat it as forbidden. And Chayei Adam wrote that he investigated among craftsmen who make snuff tobacco (that is, they sniff it through the nose) — they moisten the tobacco package with wine, and it is known that yayin nesech is forbidden in benefit by law; and if so it is proper to be careful not to sniff that tobacco all year. And behold all this Chayei Adam wrote in his time; but now one must investigate among craftsmen whether they do so:

(34) Or negeliach — because they soak them in barley water before drying them [Darkei Moshe]:

(35) However they do not forbid their mixture — since the rule implies even in less than sixty; and this applies to all the above in this gloss; and the reason is that we are not so stringent because of a general concern; however regarding honey and sugar mixtures there are those who are stringent to forbid unless there were sixty in the dish against it; and nevertheless in a place of great loss or prevention of Yom Tov rejoicing one may be lenient:
