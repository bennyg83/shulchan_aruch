This implies that during the fifth hour on the eve of Pesach, when it cannot be consumed before Pesach, it is forbidden to sell even small amounts to a non-Jew lest they be sold to a Jew. This also appears from the Talmud, Rambam, and the other authorities. Nevertheless, if a large amount of a Jew's grain became chametz on the eve of Pesach, it cannot be sold to non-Jews in quantities that will be consumed before Pesach, and a substantial loss is involved, one may sell it to a known non-Jew who will not touch it but will guard it until after Pesach. One may tell the buyer, 'Instead of buying for one maneh,' and so forth, as explained above in siman 448, se'ifim 3 and 4. There is then no concern.
