And candy sugar. See the Taz regarding sour kettles: ordinary vessels are not ben yomo, and this is secondary taste from secondary taste. See siman 447 and the Chok Yaakov. What he wrote that candy sugar is eaten on the final Yom Tov refers to hot sugar that was not cooked in the usual way. In truth, hot sugar is cooked in special utensils known to the craftsmen, and the matter depends on the practice.
And if. If during Chol HaMoed Pesach there is a market day and much profit in selling the sugar, one may say that he is allowed to sell the hot sugar during Chol HaMoed. Some merchants testified that no flour is mixed into hot sugar. Nevertheless, everything depends on local custom. In a place where they practiced prohibition, one should not permit it in their presence; in a place where they practiced permission, it is permitted.
