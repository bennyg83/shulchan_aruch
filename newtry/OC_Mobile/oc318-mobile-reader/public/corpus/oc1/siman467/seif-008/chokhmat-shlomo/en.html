And the custom in these lands is not to eat honey, etc. Marginal note: Behold an incident came before me with a certain wealthy man from the holy city who brought with him a drink called rum similar to the drink arrack, which are sharper than spirits, and he brought this from the city of London, and he also brought a hechsher letter from the rabbi of the London community, and there it is stated how this drink is made in the lands of India in such a way that there is no concern of chametzing at all; and in the London community they have practiced for these forty years drinking this rum on Pesach; and the London rabbi and the aforementioned wealthy man asked me to give my agreement and the force of my ban to certify it also in our land for Pesach. And this is what I answered: "Let not my son go down with you"; Heaven forbid that my hand be in this to breach a fence herein; and in my opinion it is forbidden on Pesach. Not only according to Magen Avraham in this siman, who holds that honey intact is forbidden if already crushed, and necessarily they decreed here lest it be confused with other honey, even though regarding other honey too there is no severe prohibition but only a remote concern, and even so they decreed here lest it be confused: if so, the same applies to this drink: granted that for this drink there is a hechsher, nevertheless since from its body the difference between this and that is not recognizable, one should be concerned for confusion with other arrack which is chametz. Even according to Taz who permits honey intact and is not concerned for confusion here, nevertheless it appears that this drink differs from honey: this is similar to what Magen Avraham wrote in this siman s.k. 10 regarding saffron in the name of the Rashba, that even what grew in his house is forbidden because of a decree: that is, since saffron is not commonly grown in our land, they will think it is saffron coming from afar; but fruits that a Jew dried are permitted and we are not concerned for confusion (because they are common in our land, but this drink [is not]); and likewise regarding honey from the hive as above (end quote). And if so, this itself is Taz's reason who permits honey and is not concerned for confusion because it is common in our land; but this drink, which is not common in our land, is exactly like the saffron law: even though we know it was made with a hechsher it is forbidden by force of a decree of confusion; so too here. And for this reason it is not similar to Pesach spirits that they practice permission to drink and are not concerned for confusion with chametz spirits, because that permitted [drink] is common in our land; but these drinks that are not common in our land one should be concerned for confusion. And further it appears that even regarding spirits the early ones permitted only because of deprivation of Yom Tov joy, for in their time good wine was not common in our land, and people were accustomed to spirits, and [denying it] would be deprivation of Yom Tov joy; therefore for this they left it according to the [basic] law that it is permitted; but drinking the aforementioned rum is not equal for every soul and there is no deprivation of Yom Tov joy in this, and also now in our land wine is cheaper than these drinks; therefore it is fitting to be concerned here for confusion. And further it appears even more that this is similar to what the poskim wrote above in siman 463, that one should not plaster the pots themselves with baked matzah in a place where there are servants and breaches are common; and all the more so in our case, for through our many sins the generation is very breached and breaches are very common: one should not be lenient in such a case, and it is more severe than a decree regarding baked matzah; and also similar to what the poskim wrote in siman 460, that one should not be lenient regarding what the early ones were stringent about making panadish or pladish; see there; and that too is only by force of a decree and concern for confusion; and the same applies here; and also the very species of kitniyos that the Sages forbade is only by force of concern for confusion; and the same applies here. And further it appears that what they permitted Pesach spirits is according to the majority that the taste of Pesach spirits differs from the taste of chametz spirits, and one can easily feel the taste between them; therefore they were not concerned here for confusion; and behold even so, after they erred and deceived Jews with it, they themselves caused their own loss by not giving mind to distinguish between this and that; but at any rate the majority, since if they give mind they can easily distinguish, therefore they were not concerned here for confusion. But regarding the aforementioned drink, whose taste is not recognizable between those coming from chametz and those coming from other things, and also since it is not common in our land and they are not expert in its taste to distinguish between this and that, certainly one should be concerned here for confusion. And one should not say: since arrack ordinarily is made from rum according to what several people said, therefore one should not decree drink of rum because of arrack, for arrack itself is forbidden only by force of a kitniyos custom and it would be a decree upon a decree. This is not so: aside from what we find on Pesach that they piled stringency on stringency and decreed here a decree upon a decree, as evident from several places, and one of them is in siman 446 regarding [chametz that] gives a spoiled taste, that even spoiled-taste and any-amount are forbidden: behold each alone is forbidden only because of Pesach stringency, and even so they decreed even when both join together even though it is a decree upon a decree. And aside from this it appears it is one decree: both arrack and this drink, even if made from rum or another thing, we decree here because of that made from chametz: the whole is one decree. And further it appears that the main prohibition here is by force of concern of mixture: perhaps spirits are mixed into it, just as they forbade non-Jew's milk because of concern of mixture of something impure, and also forbade non-Jew's fish-brine because of mixture of yayin nesech: if so here too they forbade arrack because of concern of mixture of chametz spirits, and likewise the rum drink is forbidden because of mixture of spirits; and if so, since it has a drag of Torah prohibition, even if there is one known case where they did not mix spirits into it it is forbidden because of concern of confusion; even though regarding fish-brine they did not decree this, regarding chametz they were more stringent; and especially there stam yeinam is rabbinic, whereas chametz has a Torah prohibition; and this is exactly like the saffron law, which is forbidden because they sprinkle wine on it, and the Rashba forbade even what grew in his house because of concern of confusion; and the same applies here too; and it is not similar to Pesach spirits, on which they did not decree so for the reason above. And one can further say: on the contrary, that itself gives [the reason]: regarding chametz spirits, whose prohibition is severe and it is known that it itself is chametz, therefore if we are lenient with Pesach spirits they will not err in this to permit chametz spirits. And it is similar to what we say in general: these are words of Torah and need no strengthening; these are words of the Scribes and need strengthening; so too here regarding arrack, since it itself is of the kitniyos species, it is not severe in their eyes and is only by force of concern of spirits mixture in it and is only a rabbinic decree; therefore it needs more strengthening here even when known that nothing was mixed into it; and without this that would not stand. And especially through our many sins according to the breach of the generation, would that it were in our hands to forbid even Pesach spirits: how good that would be, and how many pitfalls already result from this; only that it is not in our hands to decree what has already breached. But since regarding the old ones we are already ashamed, Heaven forbid that we add the new ones and breach further a breach to permit also arrack; Heaven forbid; and in a little while it will result that they will be lenient to drink all arrack on Pesach. Therefore Heaven forbid that our hands be with this to uphold this hechsher and to permit practicing permission in it that our fathers did not envision; and behold the London rabbi who filled his hand with the hechsher did not support his hand except because he wrote that they already practiced so there in his land for these forty years, and he brought proof from a letter of some rabbis there who practiced such permission from of old; but if they had not practiced so from beforehand, he would not have dared to permit the matter; and all the more so we, what shall we answer after him, given that this drink is far from our land: certainly it is not fitting to breach a breach in it. Words of one who speaks in truth for the honor of the Torah without destroying its foundation to dust, Heaven forbid; and may the good God send us the teacher of righteousness and teach us according to law and halachah, as the soul of the holy Shlomo Kluger desires; Sunday, 28 Adar I, in the year "in order that You be justified in Your words" according to the abbreviated era, here in Brody.
