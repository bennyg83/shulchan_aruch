There are those who are stringent. For we are concerned lest flour was mixed in; and even though honey is fruit juice, nevertheless the flour is full chametz, for they are accustomed to wash wheat:

Only that, etc., that they make, etc. Explanation: two things are required — specifically from that in the barrel it is permitted to make a drink, for even if there is a little flour in it, it is nullified in sixty at the time of cooking before Pesach; but those they bring in small vessels are forbidden even to make a drink from them; and many times it was clarified to me they mix much flour in them. And to eat honey as is in any case is forbidden, for substitution will come; and so Maharil and Rashal; and forbidden to make the drink on Pesach (Bach, and so in Darkei Moshe); and the Levush wrote, and this is its language: some say specifically the drink called mead is permitted because it has much water and presumably the flour was nullified before Pesach; but honey as is that they cooked before Pesach — even though they were careful from leavening and strained it from the bees in it — forbidden to eat and to put into food; and it appears one who is lenient has not lost and one who is stringent — may he be stringent; and it requires further study. However honey they bring from the hive — all practice leniency, for there is no way to mix anything in it when they draw it from the hive — end of his words; he was precise and wrote they bring from the hive, and did not write they bring in a barrel from the hive, as written, because that is forbidden to eat as is, and only what they bring with small whole combs as they rest in the hive that was not yet crushed is permitted; and some practice to cook honey water until it returns and becomes thick like honey and eat it; and according to the Bach it is forbidden as stated; and all is according to custom:

And dried grapes. Maharil wrote in responsum siman 105 not to eat dried grapes; and if a person wishes to fulfill what is written in Shir HaShirim open the pomegranate, he can fulfill with our grapes — end of his words. And in Maharil it is written one should be careful with thin grapes, for they mix them with raisins. In the holy community of Kalish they practice leniency from old times to buy small dried pears from gentiles and make a drink from them — meaning they pour cold water on them and soak until it becomes a drink; but to cook with the pears they practice prohibition, and also the pears themselves they do not eat; and the vessels with which they draw the drink they practice not to use for anything else. It appears the reason is concern lest a little chametz adhered to them — therefore they do not eat them, but the drink they drink, for that little is nullified in sixty; and even though they soak also on Pesach, nevertheless they were not stringent from uncertainty; and what they practice prohibition to cook them is because through cooking it will impart more taste; and even though there are gentiles who dry pears in an oven after they baked bread in it, nevertheless it is taste imparted by taste of the permitted; and even though the Rama wrote in siman 447 to be stringent on this — here it is uncertainty, for many dried in heat or after the oven was lit — therefore they practiced leniency, as stated, to justify their custom; but in other places they practiced stringency:

Forbidden to keep it. Shach wrote, and this is its language: it requires study, for it appears to me from the Talmud Yerushalmi the reverse — specifically small combs made from kneading tsuker are forbidden, but stam tsuker is permitted to keep; and so I saw my teacher rule that tsuker that passed over Pesach he permitted — end of his words; and so in Hagahot Maimon: Maharil wrote in responsum siman 25: tsuker — they are accustomed to be careful if it is not great need for healing, for merchants mix many types in it — end of his words; and those made like small combs from tsuker kneading are forbidden to keep (in the name of Maharil and Moharash); and in Talmud Yerushalmi it is written one should be careful in all types of spices made from tsuker kneading, like legumes and coated seeds — end of his words; Hagahot Maimon implies he holds stam tsuker is permitted to keep; and so in the Levush — see there; and so I heard from the craftsman who prepares it that that made like a hat has no flour (and so the author of the Shulchan Aruch); however l'chatchila they practice to sell it to a gentile before Pesach, and b'dieved it is permitted; and Mahari Bruna wrote in a collection: I found a matter in taste that is the reverse — white tsuker has no concern for leavening, and tsuker kandiya has concern for leavening; and so I heard practiced in the holy community of Krakow — end of his words; and Maharil: Maharaz permitted his students to eat on the last Yom Tov tsuker he brought from Candia that was clear to him there is no concern for leavening — end of his words:

Saffron. In responsum of the Rashba siman 133 it is written they are concerned for flour with water and pour on it; and the Levush wrote: what grows in his house garden is forbidden because of a decree (Maharil) — meaning because saffron is not common to grow in these countries, and they will think it is saffron that comes from afar; but fruits a Jew dried we are not concerned for substitution because it is common; and so with honey from the hive, as stated above — therefore Mahari Cohen; and Tuv Ta'am: the way is to soak it in beer and one must seal it in a room or make a partition before it — end of his words; and if so, the same for saffron:

Their mixture does not forbid. It implies even if much mixed in a cooked dish it is permitted, since the concern is distant; however in honey Darkei Moshe wrote, and this is its language: however it appears that if a little of it mixed in a cooked dish on Pesach one does not forbid it, for it is fit to rely on the Rosh in such a case, as in siman 447; and regarding rabbinic prohibition in any amount we do not establish prohibition b'dieved — it implies if mixed in less than sixty it is forbidden, and so is essential; but in saffron and the like one should not be stringent even in less than sixty, for the taste of chametz already passed and went, as in siman 447 regarding dry meat, and Yoreh De'ah siman 69 regarding blood:
