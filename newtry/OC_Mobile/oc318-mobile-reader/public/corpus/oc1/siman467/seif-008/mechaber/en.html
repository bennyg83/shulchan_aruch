Honey of a gentile — we do not establish presumptive prohibition upon it, and we eat it on Pesach:However some are stringent (Mordechai chapter Kol Sha'ah and Hagahot Maimoni chapter 4) and the custom in these countries is not to eat honey except that which they bring in barrels from the hive from which they make a drink called mead, and dried figs and dried grapes called roziny (large or small) depending on local custom, for some are stringent not to eat them and some are lenient; therefore they were accustomed in these countries to be stringent not to eat any dried fruits unless it is known they were dried in a manner where there is no concern for chametzing. And tsuker is forbidden to eat it, even' to keep it is forbidden (Terumat HaDeshen) siman 111). And on the last Yom Tov they eat dried fruits and tsuker kandil that has no chametzing concern (Maharil).) And the custom in these countries is not to eat saffron called zafarin or negelach (Maharil}. However they do not) forbid its mixture, and so it appears to me::
