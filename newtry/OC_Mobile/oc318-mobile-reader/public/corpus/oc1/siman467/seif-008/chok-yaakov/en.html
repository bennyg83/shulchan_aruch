We do not presume it forbidden by saying that it is counterfeit. This is the language of the Tur. It implies that if we know that fine flour was mixed in, it must be destroyed, as is explicit in the Yerushalmi at the end of Perek Kol Sha'ah. One should not rely on the leniency written by the Rosh in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah regarding honey, for that refers only to suspicion, not to known admixture.
Only that which is brought in barrels. The Rama's wording implies that in such a case it is permitted even to eat it as is. The Taz proves this from the Rama's language, unlike the Magen Avraham, who wrote that the Rama agrees with some later authorities that even honey brought in barrels may not be eaten as is, but only used to make a drink before Pesach.
And dried grapes. The Magen Avraham wrote in the name of the Maharil that if one wishes to preserve unripe grape clusters, he may preserve them with our grapes.
No dried fruits. The practice, however, is to make a drink from them, namely by pouring cold water over them and soaking them until they become a drink. The Magen Avraham testified that this was the custom in the holy community of Kalisch. But one should not cook with them. See there, where he explains the reason for the custom at length.
In a manner where there is no concern for chametz. We do not decree one case because of another. Similarly, honey from hives is permitted and we do not decree, because it is common to bring honey from the hive, and with fruit that a Jew dries in his home he is careful from chametz. This is unlike saffron, which is not common to grow in one's garden, and therefore even that which grows in a garden is forbidden.
Even drinking it is forbidden. My brother-in-law in Eliyahu Zuta wrote that a question came before his grandfather, the Gaon Maharam Shmuel, after Pesach concerning sugar that had been kept, and he ruled to sell it to a non-Jew. The Taz and Magen Avraham wrote that there is no prohibition in the sugar called hot sugar, or what we call clear sugar, and it is permitted to keep it at home.
Called saffron. See Yoreh De'ah, seif katan 114, regarding saffron during the year. The Magen Avraham wrote that tobacco is commonly soaked in beer and therefore must be closed in a room or have a partition placed before it; the same applies to saffron. See the end of siman 451 regarding vessels dyed with saffron, that one should not use them on Pesach.
Nevertheless, their mixture is not forbidden. This implies that even in less than sixty it is permitted. See seif katan 16 regarding honey. With sugar too, one should not forbid the mixtures, even with kinds of sweetened mixtures and in cases of major loss and the like, where one may be lenient, as I wrote in seif katan 28.
