There are those who are stringent. It is the Mordechai that we are concerned lest flour or fine flour was mixed in. And the Beit Yosef wrote that there is no prohibition in this, for it is fruit juice and only fine flour leavens; and it is possible that they are concerned that it is full chametz since they knead wheat first. And the Rosh wrote in a responsum general 24 that he did not see a custom of prohibition in honey, for mixing flour is not common and also it was nullified before Pesach; and if we come to be concerned because of mixtures we should forbid it all year — some say they put nevelah meat into it and it turns into honey and is permitted, for after the fact we follow — end of his words. And nevertheless the Rama concludes that one may not eat it on Pesach except what they bring from the hive, even though in the Mordechai he forbids this too because of substitution with other honey — it is an extra stringency. And I saw in many places that they eat it, and also in the holy community of Krakow there is no custom to forbid — unlike the Rama's words here that one should not eat honey except what they bring from the hive that they make a drink from, etc. — meaning even to eat it as is it is permitted, for they make a drink from it l'chatchila; but one should not say that specifically the drink is permitted because it was made before Pesach, for he did not mention before Pesach; and further, he began with eating — therefore this too even in eating is permitted, only that he mentioned the drink as evidence that people practice leniency. And based on this it appears to me there is full permission to cook on chol ha-mo'ed a drink called mead from honey that is recognizable as coming from the hive to the sight of people — anything fit for drinking on Yom Tov; even though the author of the Or Zarua wrote to forbid making the drink on the festival because of concern for mixtures, and wrote that so they practiced — nevertheless it appears to me that one who is lenient for the honor of Yom Tov in these countries where we have no other drink on Pesach has not lost; and there is no applicable custom of prohibition here, for each one prepares for himself before Pesach; but if one comes to cook on the festival and has no drink other than that — one should not protest him, and honoring Yom Tov is in his hand, as appears to me. And based on the words of the Rosh's responsum that we mentioned in the name of R' Yonah, it appears to teach merit on what is written in Shulchan Aruch Yoreh De'ah siman 114 that the Rashba was careful from saffron because they mix dry meat in it; and according to R' Yonah's words there is permission since the saffron dissolves in food and the prohibition is nullified, and it is literally like honey here all year:

And dried figs. For there is concern they scatter flour on them; and even those that grew in his garden are forbidden because of what an observer sees — because of other figs; and the prohibition of grapes is that they are placed near figs:

And tsuker kandil. This is not what we call tsuker kandil — that has more concern and is full chametz, for they cook white tsuker in sour vats and it reddens; and even to keep it is forbidden because there is a mixture of chametz. Rather it is tsuker kandiya that Maharil wrote they ate on the last Yom Tov, and R' Nasan gave the students tsuker he brought from Candia that was clear to them there is no chametz in it. And it is difficult why they did not decree because of other tsuker like with figs; and it appears that tsuker is a distinct species and its form is recognizable, and we do not decree on it; and it appears this is what we call kaner or hot tsuker that certainly has no chametz and no cooking was done on it — only they bring it as its natural product; that is what they eat on the last Yom Tov, as stated:
