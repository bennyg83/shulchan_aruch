Se'if 8: Honey, etc. For we do not establish a presumption of prohibition, as above; and further, even if fine flour was mixed in there is no prohibition, as the Rosh wrote on the Yerushalmi cited above regarding Rabbeinu Chananel, etc.: forged honey with fine flour, etc.; and the concern that water was mixed into the honey is remote.
However, there are those who are stringent. For they are accustomed to mix fine flour into it for soaking; therefore the Yerushalmi said one must destroy because of the honey, unlike the Rosh; and as Tosafos wrote in chapter "Kol Sha'ah" and in Menachos regarding red vinegar, because they first soak the barley in water; and in such a case we establish a presumption of prohibition because they profit from forgery, as written in Avodah Zarah 34b regarding that boat of fish-brine, etc.: perhaps when it is pressed [it becomes like] wine, etc.; and see there Tosafos 32a s.v. "from the storehouse." But if he did not see him drawing, etc.; and so here, where they profit by it.
And figs, etc., depends, etc. For figs they are accustomed to mix fine flour into them for soaking. And grapes they are accustomed to dry in an oven near bread, or after they removed the bread from the oven; and in places where they do not mix fine flour and they dry in the sun it is permitted; and see Tosafos to Avodah Zarah there, s.v. "this vinegar," etc.: therefore, etc., for there are places, etc.; and so the Gemara implies, "this vinegar," etc., that they mix in it, etc., meaning they are accustomed to mix, implying specifically in places where they are accustomed.
And therefore they practiced. Like regarding grapes, as above.
And sugar. For they mix flour into it.
And on Yom Tov. But on other days, even those [sugars] that they do not mix, are forbidden because of [possible] confusion.
And the custom. For they forge it with fine flour to maintain its appearance, and likewise they place it on sourdough starter to restore its appearance.
Negelikh [cloves]. For they soak them in barley water.
However. For regarding a rabbinic "any amount" we do not establish a presumption of prohibition after the fact.
