And see in Sefer Beit Me'ir who wrote that all this deals with those ships where the wheat is placed on decks in the middle of the ship and the wheat does not come into the water that the bottom of the ship draws; and also above it is covered — unlike ships (called canal) where the bottom of the ship is known to always be full of water and the wheat is submerged in the water, and also above it is exposed; and it is likely that while it is on the way rain fell upon them, and also when the sailors constantly raise the oars from the water to move the ship, spray certainly splashes upon them from the river water on all sides of the ship — therefore l'chatchila it is certainly forbidden to buy that wheat for Pesach in every manner; and b'dieved in a place of great loss one could be lenient because of concern for rain from above or what splashed from the oars, lest it is only slight dripping; however, because it is known that the wheat placed at the bottom of the ship where there is always water was certainly steeped in them for a long time, only now they dried — they are forbidden even if they did not split open — therefore one must inquire from expert merchants whether they know that if there are sixty against the wheat above relative to what is at the bottom of the ship, it is permitted b'dieved to eat them on Pesach; and if not, they are forbidden even b'dieved.
