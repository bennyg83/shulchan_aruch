(In Magen Avraham s.k. 2) For there they sense the taste of wine, etc. — I did not understand, for behold it is explained there the concern lest he buy it for his workers — see Beit Yosef there; and in what he does not sell much together we rely that he will not sell it to an Israelite except for his own need as buyer, as Hagahot Maimoni wrote and in Rabbeinu Yerucham I looked — and his words require study, for there in Rabbeinu Yerucham it is also written regarding if stam wine of gentiles became mixed with others — that some wrote it is permitted to sell except for the value of yayin nesech that there is no concern an Israelite will buy from him — for it is not customary that an Israelite buys wine from a gentile; and Tosafot wrote that because of the stringency of idolatry we are concerned an Israelite will buy for his workers — and this is truly puzzling, for behold stam wine of gentiles is forbidden in benefit; and therefore one must sell except for the value of yayin nesech; and if so how do we say an Israelite will buy for his workers; and on this Magen Avraham did not correct anything in what he wrote to explain the intent of Riaz that he should not sell much together — the explanation of "much together" is not selling all of it except for the value of yayin nesech (and the explanation of the term "together" is like the term stated in the Gemara "he sells all of it" — because through transferring benefit he can sell afterward only to her alone; but if he comes to sell except for the value of yayin nesech he must sell everything together — for this he said that here he does not sell everything together except for the value of yayin nesech and he has no permission except through transferring benefit); and in truth this is the explanation — for behold in Riaz it is explicit that certainly it is permitted to sell except for the value of yayin nesech — if not we are concerned he will return and sell it to an Israelite for his workers; and however in stam wine of gentiles it is puzzling — since when wine comes to a gentile's hand how will an Israelite buy from him even for his workers; only Rama deals with wine mixed in water — this reasoning is possible. And however according to this one may say Rama wrote specifically much together; but a little we say he buys for his own need; and even though there is no necessity in Riaz's words — nevertheless there is room to say thus; but what Magen Avraham wrote to decide this is not Riaz's intent — for otherwise it is difficult, for behold they sense, etc. — requires study.
