A split wheat kernel. The Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah wrote that since the Rama did not add here too that even one that is not split is forbidden, it appears that he holds that if it is not split, roasting forbids only the thickness that must be removed. However, the other later authorities do not agree. See what I wrote above in seifim katan 26 and 48.
The place to which it spreads. This discusses a lean chicken, for if it is not lean, the taste spreads and the entire item is forbidden, as explained above at the beginning of siman 447. See there.
Because when they turn the spit. See my work Minchas Yaakov, klal 28, seif katan 2, where I challenged the Perishah and wrote that only regarding a prohibition of a minute amount do we have this concern, but not with other prohibitions. I saw that the Magen Avraham also intended precisely this distinction, as I wrote there.
When they turn the spit. My brother-in-law, the Rav, wrote in Eliyahu Zuta that if the spit is not turned, it appears from the Rama's view that the law of roasting is like the law of salting.
That touch one another. The Magen Avraham wrote that if the chicken is fatty, even if it does not touch the others it forbids everything. Nevertheless, where the kernel is not split, it appears to me that one should not be stringent, as I wrote above.
