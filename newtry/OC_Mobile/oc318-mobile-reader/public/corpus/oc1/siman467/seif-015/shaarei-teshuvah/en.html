And sugar. See Baer Heitev; and see responsa Noda BiYehuda part 1 siman 23, and Mahadura Tinyana siman 72, who wrote regarding powdered sugar brought in barrels, and some are concerned for a mixture of flour, and also perhaps the barrels are from barrels used to carry flour to that country where it grows; and he wrote that if the seller is among the great merchants one should not protest in a place whose custom is to be lenient; and in any case they should be careful to take that powder in the presence of the Jew immediately upon opening the barrels and to take from the middle of the barrels; see there. And see there in part 1 siman 24, who wrote that in the place of its growth one need not be concerned for forgery, for there flour is far more expensive than sugar; therefore the custom when it is brought by ship to Hamburg or Amsterdam is that the rabbi sends his agents to seal the barrels, and from there they bring them sealed with a hechsher from the rabbi; see there. And see She'elas Yaavetz part 2 siman 65, etc., that [regarding] chest-sugar from the note, its permission spread according to our master the Noda BiYehuda of blessed memory. And see responsa Tiferes Tzvi simanim 18 and 19, who also wrote to permit that brought in barrels from distant places from the place of the sugar's growth itself, as to merchants of Eretz Yisrael, and the sugar is bronze-colored, and one need not be concerned for a flour mixture, which is a remote concern and does not match in appearance; but if it is not bronze-colored one should be somewhat stringent even if it came from its place of growth where flour is [cheap]; nevertheless there is a decree lest they come to confuse and take also from other merchants who empty on that rabbi's route and pour from vessel to vessel, and there is concern that where flour is cheap they mix flour there; we are concerned even regarding a merchant; see there. And it appears that in a place of need or loss one may permit in this when it is known that all year barrels of sugar come to him from their place of growth as they are, even if not bronze, and they give him a hechsher letter on the barrels that come to him in this way, and there is no concern of confusion to buy from one who has no hechsher letter, for it is known that certainly no hechsher letter is given except after investigation that there is no concern; unlike one who has no hechsher letter: the buyer should be concerned for himself.
