Tur in the name of Rabbeinu Ephraim; Magen Avraham, subsection 3. Below in se'if 9, the Shulchan Aruch permits a cooked dish if a kernel did not actually split, even though the kernel rests undisturbed in the food. This may be because the cooked dish is hot and therefore resembles scalding, which does not cause leavening according to Tur, simanim 461 and 467. See there. We are concerned only that the kernel may have been in the water before it boiled, and therefore are not so stringent. Another and primary explanation is that a minute admixture in a cooked dish is only rabbinically prohibited, and we are not so stringent, as Magen Avraham writes in subsection 12. See there. Based upon Rambam, chapter 5, the reason appears straightforward: grain that was mixed with water or cooked but did not actually split is not chametz under Torah law. The prohibition of a minute admixture is also rabbinic, and we do not impose two rabbinic stringencies. Chok Yaakov, subsection 7. He writes there that all this explains the authorities and the Shulchan Aruch, but in practice Rema concludes in the gloss to se'if 9 that the custom is to prohibit under all circumstances.
