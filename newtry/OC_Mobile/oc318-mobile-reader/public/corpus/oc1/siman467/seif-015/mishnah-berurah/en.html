(79) That was found in it, etc. — Chayei Adam wrote: meaning specifically that it was found inside it, for one can explain that it became leavened in the water in which it was rinsed; but if found on the chicken, then if it had been at rinsing time it would have fallen — therefore it fell now; and chicken fat is like fruit and does not leaven:

(80) Split wheat kernel — and according to the Rama's words above in seif 9, the same applies even if it did not split open, only softened; but if it did not soften — nothing is required:

(81) One cuts the place of its spread, etc. — that is, as written above that one deepens all around it; for in roast, according to all, one must be stringent more; and specifically if the chicken was lean; but if it is fatty, at the place of contact of the wheat kernel the whole thing is forbidden, for the permitted part goes and seasons the wheat kernel, and afterward the wheat kernel releases something throughout the entire chicken:

(82) Because when they turn over, etc. — and specifically in chametz prohibition which is in any amount we are concerned for this, unlike other prohibitions:

(83) The spit — it implies that if it was roasted over coals, one does not forbid more than the amount of its spread; and according to the Rav in the gloss mentioned above, certainly one may not be lenient in roast more than in salting [Acharonim]:

(84) Touching one another — and if they do not touch one another they are not forbidden, and we do not say that absorption of any amount goes and spreads from one to another through the spit; and if the chicken is fatty it forbids everything even if they do not touch [Magen Avraham]; and Mekor Chaim is lenient in the matter; and even according to Magen Avraham one should not be stringent in this except with split open and not when softened:

(85) For through turning over, etc. — and without turning over the others are permitted even without peeling; and Peri Megadim wrote that according to the Rav above one should rule that roast is no worse than salting, and even when the chicken is entirely lean it is entirely forbidden even without turning the spit; but the others one may permit in great loss if known that he did not turn the spit:
