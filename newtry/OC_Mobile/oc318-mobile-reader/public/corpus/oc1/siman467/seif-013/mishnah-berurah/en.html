(65) Used that day. And it deals with a case where they koshered before the time of its prohibition; for otherwise one may not do hagalah to a vessel used that day, as explained above in siman 452. And the Mechaber mentioned "used that day" because if the cauldron was not used that day, even the hagalah waters themselves are permitted on Pesach, since they koshered before Pesach and they are not [vessels] used that day, as explained above in siman 451 and siman 447 se'if 5 in the gloss; see there. And know that if the hagalah was during Pesach, then even if the cauldron is not used that day the water in the cistern is forbidden according to the Rama above in siman 447 se'if 10 in the gloss.
(66) And they went down, etc., during Pesach. That is, the descent was during Pesach but the hagalah was before Pesach; and if the descent was also before Pesach the waters were nullified in sixty in the water of the cistern; and some are stringent that even if there is sixty against them one should not use those waters l'chatchila since one can easily find other water. It is forbidden to drink its waters; and if he transgressed and cooked a cooked dish from them, Peri Megadim wrote that one may permit if it is major financial loss, since the hagalah was before Pesach.
(67) Because they mixed. Meaning, even according to those who permit cold above because it imparts no taste at all, nevertheless here it is forbidden, for through mixing the body of the chametz is mixed into it. If grain kernels are found in a water well on Pesach, the later authorities wrote that it is fitting to be careful as much as possible not to use those waters on Pesach even according to those who permit in cold, lest it was pickled in it a full day and is pickled; and nevertheless if he has no other water, since his life depends on it, one may permit by force of a double doubt: perhaps it fell now, and even if it fell a full day beforehand, perhaps it did not crack open and soften until within a full day and did not become pickled from the time it became chametz; and we rely on the poskim that cold does not forbid without pickling. And a slice of bread found in a well: see above in s.k. 62. One who wishes to draw on Pesach from non-Jews' wells or from Jews' wells not guarded from chametz all year: it is proper that he strain the water with a clean cloth every time he draws.
