Into the pit during Pesach. In the Tur it is written stam, but the Beit Yosef explained it deals during Pesach, for if before Pesach behold it was nullified before Pesach and does not return and revive; and if so Shulchan Aruch holds like the first view in siman 447 seif 4; and the author of the Or Zarua wrote that nevertheless l'chatchila it is forbidden even before Pesach, since it is possible to draw water from another pit; and in my humble opinion here sometimes it is forbidden even b'dieved even before Pesach, for the leavened water remained above — even though a little mixed above, nevertheless they stood and endured and are as if in their own substance, and it resembles that law in R' Yehuda siman 92 regarding did not stir and covered, for it does not mix entirely except if they already drew from there several times before — then it is like stirring the pot and it is nullified before Pesach; but during Pesach there is no distinction, as appears to me. And the Mordechai wrote in chapter 20: this law of hag'alah of a pot also applies; and afterward wrote: but wheat that fell into a pit of water, etc., that it does not leaven while the wheat kernel is cold — end of his words. Once a servant brought a vessel with honey called mead to drink and a wheat kernel was found in it; I said it does not forbid in cold, even though it leavened, as the Tur wrote: since the water is cold the wheat kernel does not impart taste in them, and even full chametz that fell into a pit is permitted for this reason — end of his words. And even though it is written in Shulchan Aruch seif 12 that some forbid during Pesach — we already proved there is no distinction found except the Beit Yosef wrote thus as an answer to explain the Rosh's words, and we are not concerned lest it was in water, etc.; and we already mentioned the difficulty; and if so we do not find anyone who disagrees on this matter; and on the contrary, from the Mordechai we mentioned nearby it is proven that on Pesach itself the wheat was in the well, and nevertheless he wrote it does not impart taste in cold; and also there is no concern here lest the wheat kernel was when the honey was boiling, for then what was released from it was already nullified before Pesach and does not return and revive — even though the wheat kernel is still inside, nevertheless at that time it was cold and does not impart taste, as stated:
