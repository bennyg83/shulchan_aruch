Into a cistern during Pesach. In the Tur it is written without specification, only that Beis Yosef explained that it deals with during Pesach, for if before Pesach it was already nullified before Pesach and does not return and revive; and if so the Shulchan Aruch holds like the first view in siman 447 se'if 4. And my master and father-in-law of blessed memory wrote that nevertheless l'chatchila it is forbidden even before Pesach, since one can draw water from another cistern; and in my humble opinion here sometimes it is forbidden even after the fact even before Pesach, for the chametzed waters remained above: even though they mixed somewhat above, nevertheless they rest and stand and are as if intact, and it is similar to that of [Riva] Rid in siman 92 regarding [a pot] not stirred and covered, that it does not become mixed throughout, unless they already drew from there several times beforehand, which is like stirring the pot, and it is nullified before Pesach; but during Pesach there is no distinction, as appears to me. And the Mordechai in chapter "Kol Sha'ah" likewise wrote this law [regarding] that they did hagalah to a cauldron, and afterward wrote: but the wheat kernels that fell into the water cistern, etc., that it does not make chametz while the wheat kernel is cold (end quote). Once a servant brought a vessel with honey called mead to drink, and one wheat kernel was found in it; I said that it does not forbid in cold even though it became chametz, as the Tur wrote, and this is his language: Since the water is cold, the wheat kernel does not impart taste into them; and even full chametz that fell into a cistern is permitted for this reason (end quote). And even though Shulchan Aruch se'if 12 wrote "and there is one who forbids during Pesach," we already proved that we do not find this distinction; rather Beis Yosef wrote it by way of an answer to resolve what the Rosh wrote, "and we are not concerned lest it was in the water," etc.; and we already noted that there is no difficulty; and if so we do not find anyone who disputes this point; on the contrary, from the Mordechai we just mentioned it is evident that even on Pesach itself the wheat kernels were in the well, and even so he wrote that cold imparts no taste; and one also need not be concerned here lest the wheat kernel was [there] while the honey was boiling, for if so what was released from it was already nullified before Pesach and does not return and revive; even though the wheat kernel is still inside it, nevertheless it is cold at that time and does not impart taste, as appears to me.
