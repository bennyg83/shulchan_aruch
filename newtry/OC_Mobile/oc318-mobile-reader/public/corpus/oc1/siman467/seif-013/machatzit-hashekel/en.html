And in the Taz in Yoreh De'ah, etc., one cannot understand his words through purging — I must present his words briefly: in Chullin page 100 Rav taught: once taste was given in a piece, the piece itself becomes nevelah and forbids all the pieces because they are its species — Rav holds species in its species is not nullified, meaning such as when a pot was full of gravy and within the gravy were several pieces of permitted meat, and above the gravy was placed one permitted piece and on that piece a piece of nevelah; and one cannot combine the pieces within the gravy neither for prohibition nor for permission, since that piece on which the nevelah rests is above the gravy. Therefore if the nevelah piece gave taste in that permitted piece it becomes nevelah; and if it stirred again the whole pot until that upper piece too entered the gravy, it forbids all the pieces even though there are sixty against it because they are its species and for R' Yehuda species in its species is not nullified from the Torah. And the Gemara challenges: what is different — taste was given, etc., even if it did not give taste as well, since it stirred the nevelah piece itself forbids the other pieces; and Abbaye answers: when he preceded and removed the prohibition — meaning the nevelah piece before stirring the pot. And Tosafos challenged: still the difficulty remains — why taste was given in the piece; behold the nevelah piece with that upper piece is species in its species and should be forbidden in any amount and forbid the whole pot when stirred — because of any amount. Chametz within its time, etc., Magen Avraham explained below his intent in this. If it became mixed in its species and not its species — we say remove, etc. — if so the intent is not as the simple meaning that it fell immediately into its species and not its species; for even though for R' Yehuda species in its species is not nullified, nevertheless we say seif katan: its species is as if it is not its species and what is not its species is greater and nullifies it, as we say in Chullin page 106 that this law has no application here for practical differences. Rather it must be that it fell first into its species alone and forbade it in any amount, and afterward that piece forbidden in any amount fell into not its species — for if the piece had become nevelah it would require sixty against the whole piece, and now that through any amount it did not become nevelah it does not need sixty except against the any amount. And what he concluded remove its species as if it is not — the Gemara used concise language; its intent is that piece forbidden in any amount because it was its species — we say remove, for it does not forbid through purging since it did not become nevelah; therefore he did not conclude in the Gemara's language and what is not its species is greater and nullifies it; but still the difficulty remains why it fell afterward into not its species — even if it fell again into its species, since he certainly holds as stated in Sefer Nekudot that since another force is mixed in it, for R' Yehuda species in its species from the Torah is not nullified by Torah decree — in such a case it is called not its species; and it is a great stretch to say his intent in what he wrote is truly thus — that even its species is called not its species. Rather without that it is strained in my eyes that the practical difference should be as the simple meaning according to R' Yehuda who holds species in its species is not nullified — for we do not hold like him; therefore in my humble opinion it deals with davar sheyeish lo matirin, for we hold species in its species is not nullified and in not its species it is nullified; and accordingly one understands why the Rama follows his view that he wrote in Turei Zahav: the reason they distinguish for davar sheyeish lo matirin between its species and not its species — Shach brought in siman 102 seif katan 4, and this is its language: the importance is not from the prohibition itself but because afterward he will have permission, etc., and whatever became mixed not in its species the permission is not called by the name of the prohibited thing but by the name of what it was mixed into — and it is as if it has no permitters, end quote. And if so in davar sheyeish lo matirin even not its species was not fit to be nullified except because the prohibition is called by the name of the permitted; if so in such a case even the any amount of absorption with another force mixed in is called not its species for R' Yehuda that species in its species from the Torah is not nullified by Torah decree, as stated in Sefer Nekudot; but for davar sheyeish lo matirin, since in any case it is called by the name of the prohibition even if not its species it is not nullified — therefore the Rama needed to say it became mixed afterward in not its species literally; and this is a straight reasoning in my humble opinion.
