And in Taz in Yoreh De'ah, etc. It is impossible to understand the words of its release; I must briefly set out his words. It is stated in Chullin folio 100: Rav taught: Once it imparted taste into a piece, the piece itself becomes nevelah and forbids all the pieces because they are of its species: for Rav holds like R' Yehuda that same-species is not nullified. Meaning, for example there was a pot full of sauce, and in the sauce several pieces of kosher meat, and above the sauce resting on the pieces was one kosher piece, and on that piece was a piece of nevelah, and it is impossible to join the pieces in the sauce neither for prohibition nor for permission, since that piece with the nevelah on it is above the sauce; therefore if the nevelah pieces imparted taste into that kosher piece it becomes nevelah, and if afterward he stirred the whole pot until the upper piece also entered the sauce it forbids all the pieces even if they are sixty against it, because they are of its species and same-species is not nullified. And the Gemara asks: Why specifically where it imparted taste, etc.? Even if it did not impart taste too, once he stirred, the nevelah piece itself should forbid the other pieces. And Abaye answers: when he preceded and removed the prohibition, namely the nevelah piece, before he stirred the pot. And Tosafos asked: still it is difficult: why do I need that it imparted taste into the piece? The nevelah piece with that upper piece is same-species and should forbid in any amount. And Rabbeinu Tam answered: granted that we say also regarding other prohibitions "a piece becomes nevelah-in-essence"; nevertheless since that piece was forbidden only through any amount it would not forbid the other pieces in the pot through [any amount], and also it itself does not become nevelah-in-essence; and so the Tur copied in Yoreh De'ah. And from this Taz derived regarding chametz on Pesach: a piece forbidden through any amount, if that piece afterward fell elsewhere, does not forbid in any amount: a fortiori, for according to R' Yehuda same-species according to the Torah is not nullified, and even so in such a case it does not forbid; all the more so chametz on Pesach, where what forbids in any amount is rabbinic: all the more so such a case will not forbid. And they forced this into the Tur's language there, and Taz was precise in his language: "it does not forbid through its release," meaning as Taz concluded there: specifically if he recognizes that piece and casts it outside: if so it forbids only through its release, therefore it is permitted, as above. But if he does not recognize the piece, in any case he must nullify the body of the piece, for it was forbidden; and also regarding liquid with liquid; see there.
And so Kesef Mishneh wrote, etc. And even though the piece does not become nevelah-in-essence, nevertheless the prohibition forbids in any amount: meaning any amount of absorbed prohibition forbids. And that is the Derishah's reasoning that Taz brought there; only that Taz rejected his words: for if so Tosafos's difficulties return to their place: why do I need that it imparted taste into the piece? Granted the piece does not become nevelah-in-essence through any amount: nevertheless that absorbed any-amount should forbid the whole pot when stirred because they are of its species. And he answered in Sefer Nekudos HaKesef that according to R' Yehuda, since specifically same-species is not nullified: if so specifically when the prohibition is intact then it is called same-species and forbids in any amount; which is not so when that any-amount is absorbed in a piece and another force is mixed into that any-amount: it is not same-species with the pieces in the pot; therefore the any-amount does not forbid the pot when stirred. Which is not so for us regarding chametz, which even if not its species [forbids] in any amount: that absorbed any-amount forbids; see there.
And there is a practical difference, etc. Meaning for us what difference is there if we say the piece becomes nevelah-in-essence through any amount and the piece forbids the other pieces, or we say it does not become nevelah-in-essence and the any-amount inside it forbids? Certainly according to R' Yehuda there is a practical difference as above; but for us who do not hold like R' Yehuda there is also a practical difference.
Something made for its taste, etc. Meaning if first it fell into something made for its taste where sixty does not help and it was forbidden, and afterward that piece fell elsewhere where also the absorption is not made for taste there: if the piece had become nevelah-in-essence one would need sixty against the whole piece; but since through any amount it did not become nevelah-in-essence one needs sixty only against the any-amount.
Chametz within its time, etc. Magen Avraham explained below his intent herein. If it mixed with its species and not with its species, where we say "remove," etc.: therefore the intent is not as simple that it fell immediately into its species and not its species: for even though according to R' Yehuda same-species is not nullified, nevertheless we say remove its species as if it is not, and what is not its species outweighs it and nullifies it, as we say in Chullin folio 100; and this law has no connection here to a practical difference; therefore one must say it first fell into its species alone and forbade in any amount, and afterward the piece forbidden in any amount fell into not its species: if the piece had become nevelah one would need sixty against the whole piece, and now that through any amount it did not become nevelah one needs sixty only against the any-amount. And what he concluded "remove its species as if it is not" is Gemara rhetorical polish; and his intent is that that piece forbidden in any amount because it was its species: regarding that we say "remove," for it does not forbid through its release since it did not become nevelah; therefore he did not conclude in the Gemara's language "and what is not its species outweighs it and nullifies it." Only that it is still difficult: why do I need that it afterward fell into not its species? Even if it fell again into its species, nevertheless since the Rama necessarily holds as Nekudos HaKesef wrote, since another force is mixed into it, it is not [simply] its species as above: if so even if it fell into its species it is called not its species; and it is a great stretch to say that this is truly his intent in writing "and into not its species," meaning even its species is not its species. Rather, even without this it is a stretch in my eyes that the practical difference be as simple according to R' Yehuda who holds same-species is not nullified, for we do not hold like him; therefore it appears to my humble opinion that it refers regarding a davar sheyesh lo matirin, where we hold same-species is not nullified and not-its-species is nullified; and accordingly it fits that the Rama follows his own view, for he wrote in Toras Chatas the reason we distinguish regarding a davar sheyesh lo matirin between its species and not its species: Shach brought it in siman 102 s.k. 4, and this is his language: that the importance is not from the prohibition itself, but since it will have permission afterward, etc.; and whenever it mixed with not its species the permission is not called by the name of the prohibited thing but by the name of the thing mixed into it, and it is like something that has no permitters (end quote). And if so regarding a davar sheyesh lo matirin even not its species would not be fit to be nullified except because the prohibition is called by the name of the permitted; if so in such a case even the absorbed any-amount with another force mixed into it: granted according to R' Yehuda that same-species is not nullified by scriptural decree, in such a case it is called not its species as Nekudos HaKesef wrote; but regarding a davar sheyesh lo matirin, since in any case it is called by the name of the prohibition even if it is not its species it is not nullified; therefore the Rama needed to say it afterward mixed into truly not its species; and this is a straight reasoning in my humble opinion. But Nekudos HaKesef necessarily does not hold my reasoning, for he too holds like the Rama that that absorbed any-amount forbids, and wrote that there is a practical difference regarding a davar sheyesh lo matirin if it first fell into its species and forbade the piece in any amount, and afterward the piece fell even into its species it is nullified, for the second time it is not its species since another force is mixed into it: behold even regarding a davar sheyesh lo matirin he holds it is not called its species and is nullified. And perhaps in Nekudos HaKesef he holds like the Ran's reason in Nedarim brought in Taz there siman 112 s.k. 5: that the Rabbis disagree with R' Yehuda and hold same-species is nullified only where the prohibition has no permission: then it is not [truly] same-species, for even though they are one species they differ in that this is forbidden and this permitted; therefore regarding a davar sheyesh lo matirin the Rabbis agree that where it is same-species it is not nullified, for they are exactly equal: both one species, and also regarding forbidden and permitted they are equal, for the prohibition too will have permission; and this is the reason a davar sheyesh lo matirin is not nullified; therefore in not its species it is nullified, for even though equal regarding forbidden and permitted they differ in their species (end of his words). And according to this reason one may well distinguish if another force is mixed into it also regarding a davar sheyesh lo matirin it is called not its species; and even though Shach copied the Rama's reason in Toras Chatas as above, nevertheless in his book Nekudos HaKesef one can say according to himself the Ran's reason was more reasonable to him.
