And Bach wrote that even if they became mixed before Pesach in a matter where they were nullified in sixty, and even though it is also noten ta'am bar noten ta'am of the permitted — nevertheless one may not use that water l'chatchila, since it is possible to find other water easily; and so too in Yoreh De'ah siman 95; and Taz wrote: in my humble opinion it appears that here sometimes it is forbidden even b'dieved even before Pesach, even if the sour water remained above and did not become mixed, for they stood and persisted and are as if in their own form; unless he drew from it beforehand several times, for thereby it becomes mixed see there. And Chayei Adam wrote that one does not forbid b'dieved even though it was not nullified; nevertheless since it occurred before Pesach it is noten ta'am bar noten ta'am of the permitted, and one does not forbid b'dieved see there:

That became mixed. Meaning: even to permit in cold here it is forbidden, since the chametzing substance itself became mixed. And if a wheat kernel or other chametz is found in this pit — it depends on the dispute that the Mechaber wrote seif 12 whether it is forbidden in cold during Pesach; and see above s.k. 20 and s.k. for Mem-Dalet from what is written; and see Chayei Adam. Taz wrote: once a servant brought a vessel with honey called mead to drink, and one wheat kernel was found in it; I said that it does not forbid in cold, even though it chametzed, and even though it is written in Shulchan Aruch seif 12 "and there is one who forbids during Pesach" — we have already proven that we did not find this distinction, that cold does not impart taste at all, and even on Pesach it is permitted; and also there is no concern here that perhaps the wheat kernel was at the hour the honey was boiling, for if so it was already nullified before Pesach from what was expelled from it, and it does not return and revive even though the wheat kernel is still inside it — nevertheless it was cold at that time and does not impart taste — end quote.
