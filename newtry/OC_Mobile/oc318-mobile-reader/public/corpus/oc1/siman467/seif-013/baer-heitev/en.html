Pesach. And the Bach wrote that even if they mixed before Pesach in such a way that they were nullified in sixty, and even though it is also taste imparted to taste imparted of permitted [food], nevertheless one should not use those waters l'chatchila since one can easily find other water; and so he wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 95. And Taz wrote: in my humble opinion it appears that here sometimes it is forbidden even after the fact, even before Pesach, for the chametzed waters remained above and did not mix, for they rest and stand and are as if intact, unless he drew from it several times beforehand, through which it mixes; see there. (And he compares it to [a pot] not stirred and covered in Yoreh De'ah siman 92; see there.) And Chok Yaakov wrote that one should not forbid after the fact: even though it was not nullified, nevertheless since it was done before Pesach it is taste imparted to taste imparted of permitted [food], and one should not forbid after the fact; see there.
That mixed. Meaning, even according to those who permit cold, here it is forbidden since the body of the chametz mixed. And if a wheat kernel or other chametz is found in this cistern, this depends on the dispute the Mechaber wrote in se'if 12 whether cold forbids during Pesach; and see above s.k. 20 and s.k. 40 from what is written there; and see Chok Yaakov. Taz wrote: Once a servant brought a vessel with honey called mead to drink, and one wheat kernel was found in it; I said that it does not forbid in cold even though it became chametz; and even though Shulchan Aruch se'if 12 wrote "and there is one who forbids during Pesach," we already proved that we do not find this distinction, for cold imparts no taste at all and even on Pesach it is permitted; and one need not be concerned here lest the wheat kernel was [there] while the honey was boiling, for if so what was released from it was already nullified before Pesach and does not return and revive; even though the wheat kernel is still inside it, nevertheless it is cold at that time and does not impart taste (end quote). And it is difficult: perhaps it was pickled in it for a full day, as written in s.k. 30. And one must say the incident was the first night of Pesach; and examine carefully.
