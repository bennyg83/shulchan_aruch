There is. See the Taz. Flour in honey without prior soaking is fruit juice, as in siman 462:6. But this applies specifically to eating it as is. To make mead or a similar drink, even flour in honey, if there was not sixty at first, may later leaven when water is added before it is well cooked. Before Pesach, however, it is permitted and becomes nullified in sixty with the water; from the fifth hour until night the matter follows the laws explained there.
And that which. The comparison to saffron means that since we see the saffron dissolves in the cooked dish and nothing remains, then even if threads of non-kosher meat were mixed in, their way is to change and become like saffron. We therefore follow its present state. This is somewhat strained as an explanation of the Taz. There is also a concern of non-Jewish wine there. See the Magen Avraham, siman 216, seif katan 3.
And know. In Yoreh De'ah 84, the Shach writes that honey has the nature of preserving and sustaining things placed in it, as the Rashba wrote in responsum 80, that whole items are preserved, like the case in Bava Basra where Herod stored something in honey for seven years. But cut items deteriorate quickly. According to this, whole wheat in honey requires separate consideration.
