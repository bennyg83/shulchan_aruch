Regarding ordinary [wheat]. See Baer Heitev; and he wrote in responsa of Maharan Rotenburg siman 35 that the Gaon Maharam, son of the Mechaber of blessed memory, answered regarding rains that fell on heaps: there are wheat kernels that sprouted, but if there are sixty of permitted against it, one may permit even l'chatchila, not only in time of need, to grind it, and there is no [problem] of nullifying a prohibition, for his intent is not for that, as written in Yoreh De'ah siman 84; only that the Bach wrote that one should bake before Pesach; and that of siman 267, grain lying in an attic, since it was not ground and baked before Pesach it is dry with dry and returns and revives on Pesach: for this one should sell it before Pesach like other chametz mixtures; see there. And see responsa Me'il Tzedakah siman 69, who wrote that in the year 5472 there were rains in the harvest days, and we saw that the wheat we harvested a day or two afterward for matzas mitzvah was fully finished and dried, and we were uncertain: perhaps they were already finished and dry when the rain was on the ground, for the Rashba ruled that it accepts chametzing even while attached to the ground; and he concluded that one need not be concerned for this even if they dried completely and are in their husk, unless when rains were abundant day and night: strong rain that knocks stalks and sheaves to the ground and rain sprinkles on them forcefully, and it is like ones lying pickled in a vessel full of water; but with rain that is not so strong and not for a long time, one should not be stringent unless we see them cracked open; and know that they required guarding only from the time of harvest and not from when they finished while attached; and further, dew never ceases and falls on them every day, and no posek mentioned anything about this, etc.; see there.
