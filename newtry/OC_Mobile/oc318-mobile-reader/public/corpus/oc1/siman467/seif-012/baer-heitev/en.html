And the essential ruling is according to the Rabbi, and so agreed Ateret Zekenim and Taz; especially if the wheat is not split open it is plain that one need not be stringent as written; and without the wheat softening one may be lenient in every matter — and so agreed Bach and the later authorities:

Rabbinic. Rokeach wrote: a piece of chametz was found in a bucket of water and it was not known when they took the water from the bucket whether there was chametz in it or not, and they cooked in it and they permitted the cooked dish; but the water in the bucket became forbidden because a gentile who brought the water perhaps put chametz in it at that hour — end quote. And Taz wrote: these words have no explanation, and it appears there is a scribal error and it should read: when they took water from the well; and the intent is that they drew water first from the well to cook, and afterward drew water in a bucket and chametz was found in the bucket and it was not known whether there was chametz in the well at the hour they drew to cook — from that itself they cooked in sour water; in this he ruled that the cooked dish is permitted, only the water in the bucket alone is forbidden; but the cooked dish is permitted because the gentile drawing to the bucket put the chametz at that hour, but that chametz was not in the well at all. And it is astonishing on Beit Yosef who brought it and did not notice this — and how can it be difficult, for it implies if the chametz was before it was cooked it would be forbidden, but chametz in cold is not forbidden; and it is possible he holds like Rashba who holds that also in cold it forbids; but we hold like Rosh and Tur that it does not forbid in cold, and it appears that if the chametz soaked in cold water from time to time it is forbidden for all, for soaked is like cooked — end quote. And so too Bach and Rashal. An incident with one who took into his mouth a piece of matzah and a piece of chicken and ate both together, and he felt in his mouth that there is a wheat kernel and removed the food from his mouth and saw a wheat kernel, and it is not recognizable whether it was inside the matzah or inside the chicken. The chicken may be permitted by force of double uncertainty — perhaps now it fell to the chicken in cold, and if you say it was previously — perhaps it was digested; but if it was in the matzah — necessarily it was inside it at the time of baking, for if it fell on it in cold it would roll and fall off; nevertheless it appears to me the matzah is also permitted, since rabbinic prohibition of uncertainty we attribute to leniency that it was in the chicken; nevertheless for removal by law it is forbidden from that matzah from the place he took the piece. And further it appears to me that even a piece of meat is permitted, for we do not establish presumptive prohibition from place to place, and we say "now it fell on the meat while it was still cold"; and if so, the matzah is also permitted; but if the meat was placed in a bowl and he ate from it — the meat is forbidden as written, for from time to time we establish presumptive prohibition — perhaps it was in the bowl at the hour they poured the meat into it; and a second vessel forbids on Pesach. And if we say it is double uncertainty — perhaps it was in the matzah, and if you say in the meat — perhaps now it fell — this is not an uncertainty, for if you say it was in the matzah you must forbid the matzah, and we do not say this even in rabbinic prohibition and even in double uncertainty; and if so, the meat and matzah are forbidden; however according to the majority it is recognizable whether the wheat was cooked or baked with it or not.
