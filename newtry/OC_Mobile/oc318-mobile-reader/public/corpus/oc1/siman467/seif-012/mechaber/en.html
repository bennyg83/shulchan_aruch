If a chicken was scalded , meaning boiled in water to remove the feathers , and afterward a split wheat kernel was found in the water, the chicken is forbidden. If a split wheat kernel or leavened bread was found in a vessel of cold water and there is not enough to impart flavor , since the water is cold , and one cooked a dish or kneaded dough with that water, it is permitted. Some forbid this if it occurred during Pesach. {Rama: Rema: Some permit even in the case of scalding chickens, since we say perhaps it fell there afterward. Some forbid in the case of other meat but permit in the case of chicken on account of a double doubt. Regarding the custom, it seems proper to forbid other meat or a whole wheat kernel even in chicken, specifically when the chickens were scalded meaning the water was hot , but if the water was cold and the chickens soaked there, one should not forbid on account of doubt. Where one can say the wheat was digested inside the chicken such as when it is broken, even in scalding there is reason to permit on account of double doubt. However if the mixtures became mixed together and there is great loss and it would prevent Yom Tov joy, one may be lenient in all this regarding doubt about a rabbinic prohibition which is the smallest amount, and one may permit other dishes that contain a mixture from that meat dish that soaked in the water or was scalded , so it seems to me.}
