(52) If they scalded, etc. — even though it was explained in Yoreh De'ah siman 68 seif 1 one may not scald even in a second vessel, here it deals that it was scalded beforehand on the feather [Peri Megadim]:

(53) The chicken is forbidden — for we say perhaps the wheat kernel was in water at the time of scalding; we do not say here it was found and here it was, except regarding from place to place or from piece to piece, as above in seif 11; but in one place, such as where there is room to suspect in time and say perhaps the wheat kernel was in water previously — we do not say it fell now. And behold the Mechaber wrote here split wheat kernel, and the Rama did not gloss on this at all as he glossed above in seif 9: some say here he agrees with the Mechaber that specifically when split open and not when softened, because there is another uncertainty lest it fell now; and some say the Rama relied on his gloss above in seif 9; and so is implied in the Taz that he did not permit in our matter when softened except in benefit and not in eating; however I found in Beit Me'ir that this law that the Mechaber left in dispute is stated (apart from the view mentioned in the Rama); and therefore it appears in my humble opinion that one who relies to permit in this when softened in a second vessel even for eating has not lost. And if it did not soften, even if the scalding was in a first vessel, it is also permitted:

(54) And there is not in it, etc. — the water is cold; meaning, two things are required: that the chametz be minimal such that it does not impart taste in water where there are sixty in the water against it, and also that the water be cold; and therefore we are lenient even during Pesach, for since any amount is rabbinic we are not stringent in cold:

(55) Permitted — and if it is known that the wheat kernel fell from the outset into water, it is forbidden, for pickled is like cooked. And even if there is room to suspect lest it fell from the outset into water, the view of some Acharonim is to be stringent; and nevertheless in a wheat kernel that is not split open, only softened — one should not be stringent in uncertainty. And even in split wheat kernel — specifically when the wheat kernel was found and afterward they cooked in that water; but if the wheat kernel was found in the water after they cooked, one may be lenient in any-amount prohibition, for it is a double uncertainty — lest the wheat kernel fell afterward; and if you say from before, perhaps it did not fall from the outset into water and is not pickled — it is permitted according to this view in cold, and even according to the one who forbids in cold during Pesach; at least in a place of pressing need or great loss one should not be stringent, as below:

(56) If it is during Pesach — those who hold that even in cold it does not impart taste — nevertheless there is any amount; and therefore during Pesach, when its prohibition is in any amount, it is forbidden; and specifically when the wheat kernel was found and afterward they cooked in that water; but if found after they cooked, one should not be stringent in cold because of uncertainty:

(57) For we say perhaps, etc. — meaning, since any amount is rabbinic we are lenient in its uncertainty and suspend that it fell afterward; and the same if the chicken is still in the water but the water cooled — we suspend that it fell when the water cooled, and it is permitted according to those who permit in cold:

(58) In other meat — in such a case where they left it in hot water after salting and rinsing, and after they took it from there a split wheat kernel was found there — we establish from time to time and say the wheat kernel was there before they took the meat:

(59) By force of double uncertainty — lest it was in the chicken itself and was already digested, and leavening is not applicable to it; and if you say it is not from the chicken, lest it fell now; and the Mechaber's first view holds that even in a wheat kernel that is from a chicken, leavening is applicable:

(60) Whole — for in a whole one we decide there is leavening, because it is recognizable that it was not digested:

(61) And they removed the chickens from there — and after the chickens were removed from there they found a split wheat kernel there, even whole; and he cited chickens not specifically, for the same applies to other meat too — one does not forbid because of uncertainty, and we suspend that after taking the meat from there the wheat kernel fell, since the water was cold; and some permit entirely in cold:

(62) One does not forbid because of uncertainty — but if it is certain that the wheat kernel was when the meat fell into the water, his view is that one should conduct according to the one who forbids in cold during Pesach; and see in Levush and Taz and other Acharonim whose view is like the Mechaber's first view mentioned, that one does not forbid in cold in every manner; and therefore in a time of pressing need or great loss one may rely to be lenient according to them, and especially if the wheat kernel is not split open, only softened — certainly one should not be stringent. And all this is with a wheat kernel; but a slice of bread or dough found in the water — the Acharonim wrote to forbid even in cold, for the nature of a slice of bread and dough is to crumble and dissolve in water:

(63) And one may permit other cooked dishes, etc. — it implies that that cooked dish itself, even in great loss and prevention of Yom Tov rejoicing, is forbidden with a single rabbinic uncertainty [and see Biur Halacha]; however in the double uncertainty mentioned above it is permitted even without great loss; and all this is in a single uncertainty of any-amount prohibition which is rabbinic; but in double uncertainty of Torah prohibition one may permit only through retention, and all the more so to sell to a gentile but not in eating; and specifically where retention is possible; but if retention is impossible lest it spoil thereby — it is permitted even in eating; and especially where there is certainly a medicinal drink — certainly one should not be stringent:

(64) That fell into water or was scalded — it is not in one manner: if the meat fell in cold, even if the kernel is found while the meat is still there, it is also permitted, for there is a rabbinic uncertainty lest in cold there is no release and absorption at all. And was scalded deals when found afterward a wheat kernel in the water after they removed the meat or chicken from there — and it is permitted even if scalded in a first vessel; and the reason is there is uncertainty lest it fell now, and it is any-amount prohibition which is rabbinic — therefore they permit where there is a medicinal drink:
