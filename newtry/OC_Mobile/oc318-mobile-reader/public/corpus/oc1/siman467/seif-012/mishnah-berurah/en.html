(52) If they scalded, etc. Even though it is explained in Yoreh De'ah siman 68 se'if 11 that one may not scald even in a kli sheni, here it deals with a case where it was salted beforehand on the feathers (Peri Megadim).
(53) The chicken is forbidden. For we say perhaps the wheat kernel was in the water at the time of scalding; for we do not say "here it was found, here it was" except regarding from place to place or from piece to piece, as above in se'if 11; but in one place, such as where there is room to doubt regarding time and say perhaps the wheat kernel was in the water beforehand, we do not say it fell now. And behold the Mechaber wrote here a cracked wheat kernel, and the Rama did not gloss anything on this as he glossed above in se'if 9: some say that here he agrees with the Mechaber that it is specifically cracked open and not when softened, because there is another doubt perhaps it fell now; and some say the Rama relied on his gloss above in se'if 9; and so it appears in Taz that in our matter he permitted when softened only for benefit and not for eating. But I found in Beis Meir that this law which the Mechaber stated without specification is subject to dispute (aside from the view mentioned in the Rama); and therefore it appears to my humble opinion that one who relies to permit herein when softened in a kli sheni even for eating has not lost. And if it did not soften, even if the scalding was in a kli rishon it is also permitted.
(54) And there is no, etc., for the water is cold. Meaning: and the water is cold; for two things are required: that the chametz is small, without measure to impart taste in the water, there being sixty in the water against it, and also that the water is cold; and therefore we are lenient even during Pesach, for since any amount is rabbinic we are not stringent in cold.
(55) Permitted. And if it is known that the wheat kernel soaked a full day in water it is forbidden, for pickled is like cooked. And even if there is room to doubt perhaps it soaked a full day in water, some later authorities also hold to be stringent; and nevertheless regarding a wheat kernel that is not cracked open but only softened one should not be stringent in a doubt. And even regarding a cracked wheat kernel, specifically when the wheat kernel was found and afterward they cooked in those waters; but if the wheat kernel is found in the water after they cooked, one may be lenient regarding a prohibition of any amount, for it is a double doubt: perhaps the wheat kernel fell afterward, and even if beforehand, perhaps it did not soak a full day in water and is not pickled, which according to this view is permitted in cold; and even according to the view of those who say cold forbids during Pesach, in any case in a place of need or major financial loss one should not be stringent, as below.
(56) If it is during Pesach. For he holds that even though cold does not impart taste, nevertheless there is any amount; therefore during Pesach, when its prohibition is in any amount, it is forbidden; and specifically when the wheat kernel was found and afterward they cooked in those waters; but if found after they cooked one should not be stringent in cold from a doubt.
(57) For we say perhaps, etc. Meaning since any amount is rabbinic we are lenient in its doubt and attribute that it fell afterward; and the same applies if the chicken is still in the water but the water cooled: we attribute that it fell when the water cooled, and it is permitted according to those who permit in cold.
(58) Regarding other meat. In such a case where they placed it in hot water after salting and rinsing, and after they took it from there a cracked wheat kernel was found there: we establish from time to time and say the wheat kernel was there before they took the meat.
(59) By force of a double doubt. Perhaps it was in the chicken itself and was already digested and chametzing does not apply to it; and even if it is not from the chicken, perhaps it fell now; and the Mechaber's first view holds that even regarding a wheat kernel from a chicken chametzing applies.
(60) Whole. For regarding a whole one we hold there is chametzing in it because it is recognizable that it was not digested.
(61) And the chickens soaked there. And after the chickens were taken from there they found there a cracked wheat kernel, even whole; and he did not specifically mention chickens, for the same applies to other meat too: one should not forbid from a doubt, and we attribute that after taking the meat from there the wheat kernel fell, since the water was cold; and some permit entirely in cold.
(62) One should not forbid from a doubt. But if the wheat kernel was certainly there when the meat soaked in water, his view is that one should practice like those who forbid in cold during Pesach; and see Levush and Taz and other later authorities whose view is like the first view mentioned in the Mechaber that one should not forbid in cold in any case; and therefore in a time of need or major financial loss one may rely to be lenient like their view; and especially if the wheat kernel is not cracked open but only softened, certainly one should not be stringent. And all this is regarding a wheat kernel; but a slice of bread or dough found in the water: the later authorities wrote to forbid even in cold, for the nature of a slice of bread and dough is to crumble and dissolve in the water.
(63) And one may permit other cooked dishes, etc. Implying that that cooked dish even with major financial loss and deprivation of Yom Tov joy is forbidden with a single rabbinic doubt (and see Biur Halacha); but with the double doubt mentioned above it is permitted even without major financial loss. And all this is with a double doubt of a prohibition of any amount which is rabbinic; but with a double doubt of a Torah prohibition one may permit only for keeping, and all the more so to sell it to a non-Jew, but not for eating; and specifically where keeping is possible; but if keeping is impossible because it will spoil through this, it is permitted even for eating; and especially where there is major financial loss, certainly one should not be stringent.
(64) That soaked in water or was scalded. They are not the same case: meat that soaked in cold, even if they found the kernel while the meat was still there, is also permitted, for there is a rabbinic doubt: perhaps cold does not release and absorb at all; and scalding, which is in hot, deals with a case where afterward they found a wheat kernel in the water after they removed the meat or chicken from there, and it is permitted even if scalded in a kli rishon, for the reason that there is a doubt perhaps it fell now, and it is a prohibition of any amount which is rabbinic; therefore they permit in a place of major financial loss.
