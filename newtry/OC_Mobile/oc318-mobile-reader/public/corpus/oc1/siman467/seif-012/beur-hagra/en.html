Se'if 12: If they scalded, etc. For we do not say that after they removed the chicken it fell; we do not say ["now it fell"] from time to time, as above in se'if 11.
And if it was found, etc.: there is no, etc. This is the language of the Tur: Since the water is cold, the wheat kernel does not impart taste into it; and even full chametz that fell into a cistern is permitted for this reason. And Beis Yosef wrote: And even though from the Rosh's words in chapter "Kol Sha'ah" regarding that of se'if 11 where a wheat kernel was found in dough he wrote "and we are not concerned lest the wheat kernel was in the water," etc., implying that if it had been in the water it would forbid [even] in cold: there it deals with during Pesach, where [prohibition is] in any amount, and here it deals with before Pesach; and a proof is from what the Tur wrote, that in cold water the wheat kernel does not impart taste, etc.; for if during Pesach, why does he need [the concept of] imparting taste, given that it forbids in any amount? And that is what [the Shulchan Aruch] wrote, "and there is no," etc.: that the water is cold; unlike Magen Avraham and Baer Heitev, for according to them he should have said "and the water is cold"; and he wrote this "and there is no," etc., because he wishes to say "and there is one who forbids," etc. And so it appears to me that there is no reason to distinguish between during Pesach and before regarding whether cold absorbs or not; therefore he wrote "and there is no," etc.: the reason it does not absorb in cold is because it is [only] any amount; therefore some forbid during Pesach; and his reason is that he holds as Riva wrote in Tosafos to Pesachim 76a s.v. "It was taught," and the same applies that one needs sixty against the rinse-water, only that its prohibition is a small matter, which is not so during Pesach; and it deals even with less than sixty, unlike Magen Avraham, as appears to me.
And there is one who permits. Meaning, since "any amount" is rabbinic, we are lenient in its doubt, as written in Shabbos 34a and Eruvin 5b and other places; and as the Rif wrote regarding a cooked dish in which a wheat kernel was found, as above in se'if 9; and even though the Rama does not hold so, he wrote this view here because in cold or with major financial loss he relies on this view, as below.
And there is one who, etc. Meaning the double doubt: perhaps after they removed the chicken it fell; and even if it was in the chicken, perhaps it was digested in the chicken; as written in chapter 11 of Ohalos, we learned regarding digestion how long it remains in its innards, etc.; and regarding fowl and fish, enough time that it would fall into fire and be burned; and we learn prohibition from tumah, as written at the end of Avodah Zarah: Just as they said regarding taharos, so, etc.; and see siman 442 se'if 9; and as the Rif wrote in the third chapter of Pesachim: It is derived that whatever does not convey food-tumah, etc. But Tosafos in Menachos 69a and the Rash in Ohalos there raised a difficulty from what is written in Bechoros 7b: A kosher fish that swallowed a non-kosher fish is forbidden to eat; and they answered in the name of R' Hai that one must distinguish between prohibition and tumah; and as written in the first chapter of Beitzah, therefore R' Yehuda only raised difficulty regarding, etc.; and in Chullin 86b: If they said a doubt of tumah to purify, etc. And they further answered that fish are hard; and as written in Menachos there: Meat is different because it is soft, etc.; bones are different because they are hard; and therefore we say there: wheat in cattle dung, etc.: take it for eating, it conveys food-tumah, and even for menachos, only because "offer it now to your governor." And they further answered: perhaps that of Bechoros follows R' Yehuda ben Beseira, who said in Ohalos there regarding fowl and fish [the measure is] a full day; and it deals with a case where it did not wait. And the Ra'avan wrote that even according to the first Tanna, perhaps the fish swallowed [it] a moment before it was caught and it did not wait the measure of digestion; and as written in Chullin 71a; and see Rashi there s.v. "Do we not deal," etc.; and there is no digestion except while alive, as written in the Tosefta there and the Rash brought it there: A dog that ate flesh of a corpse, etc.: if it died, if it did not remain in its innards while it was alive three days [plus] a full day, the house is tamei; and regarding fowl, etc. And that is what [he wrote:] by force of a double doubt, and like the view of the Ra'avan.
And regarding practical [law], etc. For the conclusion of Tosafos there and the Rash there is that both regarding prohibition and regarding tumah, for anything whole that is recognizable as not digested and not chewed in the mouth, such as whole wheat in cattle dung and whole fish (for that is why it said a fish that swallowed while whole), see there. And it appears from Tosafos in the conclusion that he is not concerned for those answers; therefore he ruled here in cold or with major financial loss like the first view, that we are lenient in a doubt of "any amount" since it is rabbinic; and regarding a broken wheat kernel in a chicken he took the conclusion of Tosafos that it is digestion, only that he was concerned for the Ra'avan's residual doubt and permits with a double doubt; but regarding a whole [kernel] or meat, since there is only one doubt, and it is in hot [food], and there is no major financial loss, he is stringent like Tosafos and like their conclusion, as appears to me.
