If it is during Pesach. This is written according to his answer to the Tur's difficulty contradicting itself in seif 11; but according to what is difficult anyway, it is permitted here even on Pesach, for cold does not impart taste at all:

By force of double uncertainty. The author of the Or Zarua questioned that this is not double uncertainty, for the mind of fools is not a mind, and it is possible that one who is expert knows to assess that it was not digested; and I do not know the difficulty, for this matter cannot be known — it is like entities in a full stomach; and all such cases are certainly uncertainty, as in Yoreh De'ah siman 81. And one can say: here in this seif regarding grain on top he wrote the Rama that one may not permit for eating because of double uncertainty; and one should not say here there is another leniency that there is only rabbinic prohibition in any amount, but regarding grain there is uncertainty in all the grain — this is not so, for above too in some places it implies the majority is permitted; and so is the law in that very responsum of the Rashba that the Beit Yosef brings in a year when rains were abundant — what is written in Shulchan Aruch seif 4, and there it is explained that the majority is permitted; and further, wherever double uncertainty helps, it helps even for d'oraisa prohibition; and therefore it appears he was stringent there for eating only because there is possibility without loss — it is like a matter that has permitters; and as the author concluded in that law that we copied in seif 2, concluding: since there is no loss in keeping it, etc. — unlike food needed for Pesach, where we follow practical halachah, as appears to me. Rokeach wrote: a piece of chametz was found in a bucket of water and it was unknown when they took the water from the bucket whether chametz was in it or not, and they cooked in it and they poured out the cooked dish — but the water in the bucket is forbidden, for perhaps the gentile who brings water put chametz in at that hour — end of his words; and the Beit Yosef brought these words and they have no explanation; and it appears there is an error, and it should read: when they took water from the well — the intent is they drew water first from the well to cook, and afterward drew water in a bucket and chametz was found in the bucket, and it was unknown whether chametz was in the well when they drew to cook — automatically they cooked in chametz water; with this he ruled the cooked dish permitted, but the water in the bucket alone is forbidden, for the gentile drawing to the bucket put chametz at that hour, but that chametz was not in the well at all. And it is astonishing on the Beit Yosef who copied and did not notice; and how can it be — difficult, for it implies if the chametz was before it was cooked it would be forbidden, but chametz in cold does not forbid, as above; and it is possible he holds like the Rashba that the Beit Yosef brings, who argues on the Rosh and holds cold also forbids, and we hold like the Rosh and Tur that cold does not forbid; and it appears if chametz soaked in cold water from the eve — forbidden to all, for soaked is like cooked; and so the author of the Or Zarua in the name of Rashal. The Tur wrote, and this is its language in Sefer Rokeach: a wheat kernel found in a pot — the spoon with which they stirred in it is forbidden; if he placed it in another pot, all are forbidden; and the Beit Yosef on this: it is simple, and why did he not copy it in Shulchan Aruch? And I say this requires deep study, for at first glance this passage is not halachah, for the Talmud Yerushalmi siman 93 in the name of Rabbeinu Tam wrote: what is forbidden in any amount does not impart taste, and any amount from it does not forbid in any amount afterward — meaning from what it releases; and as the Tur wrote there: even if forbidden in its species in any amount — for R' Yehuda we do not say two any-amounts; and if so here, where the spoon forbidden from the pot containing a wheat kernel is forbidden because of any amount — why should it forbid another pot in any amount? And so in the Levush siman 92: chametz and yeast forbidden in any amount do not impart taste, and if it fell again into another pot it is nullified in the majority if unrecognized, and if recognized one discards and the rest is permitted — end of his words; and there we wrote that this if unrecognized nullification is not the intent, and they said this only when recognized one discards; and its release is not forbidden in any amount since it itself was forbidden in any amount; and if so this case here is very difficult; and it appears to me to answer properly: here with the spoon we are concerned lest the spoon touched the wheat kernel itself in the pot and was forbidden from it, not from the cooked dish in the pot. And we find such in Yoreh De'ah siman 95 regarding bowls washed in a dairy churn — they forbid there because the bowls touch the griddle and receive taste from it, and we do not follow the water in the griddle; here too the spoon receives from the wheat kernel itself; and one should not say here as well that the way of the spoon is to touch everything in the pot when stirring, and it was forbidden by taste imparted, not by any amount, for there had to be sixty in what was absorbed in the spoon against the wheat kernel; and it is possible there is not so much absorption, even though we assess the whole spoon everywhere — that is for stringency to assess against it because we do not know how much comes out, but for leniency no, for perhaps there is not so much absorption that there would be sixty in it against the wheat kernel — therefore it properly forbids (but if one inserts) another spoon into that pot that was forbidden because of inserting the first spoon — that spoon is not forbidden so as to forbid another pot when they insert it, since it is forbidden only in any amount from the cooked dish, it does not forbid others in any amount; and similarly if they removed the wheat kernel from the pot it fell in and someone inserted a spoon — it does not forbid afterward by insertion into another pot in any amount, unless there is sixty against the spoon, and it is permitted, as appears to me clearly; and this is a peg that will not move in seif 4:
