And so. See the Taz. His holy words require explanation. The Rosh on Pesachim 40 wrote that a split wheat kernel in matzah may not require removal of a layer, for splitting may not be absolute chametz, only close to chametz, and since the dough did not leaven, all the more so the kernel did not. Some forbade that matzah and some forbade those baked with it, but he did not accept that. The Ra'em wrote that one cuts away the place to which the taste spread. The Beit Yosef understood that the Rosh ultimately agreed that splitting is a sign of leavening. The Mechaber here cites the Rosh's words as law, that a split kernel in dough or baked matzah requires removal of a layer as a stringency, either because of pressure in kneading or because taste exudes at the place of contact. The opinion that forbids the entire dough or matzah seems to follow the Rashba, who gives two reasons: the kernel touched the dough while being rolled, and the water carries the taste throughout. The Rama's gloss that during Pesach we forbid and before Pesach we do not can be explained either because a minute amount is forbidden during Pesach but nullified before Pesach, or because the Rashba's concern applies only then. In a case of major loss with merely softened, unsplit wheat there remains room for analysis whether to be lenient for eating, and for benefit there is greater room to permit.
