Marginal note: the reason the poskim wrote is: perhaps it was digested; and those who forbid hold that it is not digestion. And behold in my poverty I wonder at both of them that they gave no boundary for the matter: in how much is digestion; and those who permit imply that even if found within a full day of their eating it is still a doubt perhaps it was digested; and those who forbid hold that even more than a full day is not digestion. And in truth there is a complete mishnah in Parah chapter 9 mishnah 5: A cow that drank mei chatas: its flesh is tamei for a full day; and there the Ra'avad explained because if it waited more than this they were digested in its innards. If so it is proven that the measure of digestion is a full day, and less than this is certainly not digestion; for if it were a doubt he should have said its flesh is doubtfully tamei; and since he said its flesh is tamei it implies certain tumah. Also without this, if it were only a doubt it ought to be entirely tahor, for it is a doubt of tumah in something that has no mind to ask, whose doubt is tahor; necessarily its tumah is certain; and if so, within a full day it is certainly not digestion, and conversely after a full day it is certain digestion, since he did not teach us that by force of doubt it is tahor, for there is a practical difference for several things; and also if it came by human hands it would be fitting that its doubt be tamei, for example if a person gave it to drink and the like, and then since it is by human hands it is a doubt of tumah that came by human hands and ought to be doubtfully tamei. And since he did not distinguish thus, necessarily it is certain digestion after a full day. And it is possible there is a distinction between food and drink, for drink digests faster. But in any case from this it is proven that at least regarding drink a full day is the measure of digestion; and there is no leisure now to expand more on this; examine carefully. And behold I was uncertain in my large work on the laws of Pesach regarding a cooked dish cooked during the days of Pesach and remaining until after Pesach, and afterward chametz was found in it, and this proves that it did not fall in afterward but was inside while it was Yom Tov: do we follow the time of cooking, when its law is any amount, or follow the time of finding, and after Pesach its law is like before Pesach, which is only in sixty or in majority? And there is a practical difference that the vessels will afterward be permitted for the coming Pesach like when found before Pesach, which is only in sixty; and we expanded on this there. And behold now a manuscript that was in the hand of the Gaon Moreinu HaRav Zalman Margaliot of blessed memory came to my hand, copied from Sefer Rokeach, and there its author is Rabbeinu Elazar, etc., and he enumerates there his family that he was a grandson of the author of Sefer Chasidim, and he is one of the early authorities; and I found in it letter 88 in the laws of Pesach, and this is his language: An incident that they cooked a fowl afterward on the seventh of Pesach and ate some of that fowl on that day it was cooked, and it was Motzaei Shabbos, and the next day on Shabbos which was the last of Pesach they found one wheat kernel in that remnant left for him on the seventh day, and they asked whether to permit or forbid; there is one who forbade from that which chametz in its time, whether in its species or not in its species, is forbidden like Rav; so I heard in Worms: since it was cooked on the seventh day of Pesach it is forbidden, etc. (end quote). Behold it is proven that he holds that if it had been cooked on the eighth day he would have permitted in sixty; only since it was cooked on the seventh day, even though the finding was on the eighth day, because we follow the cooking. And from him we learn for our case: if found after Pesach and the cooking was on Pesach, we follow the cooking; and I was greatly pleased by this with God's help; and understand; examine carefully.
