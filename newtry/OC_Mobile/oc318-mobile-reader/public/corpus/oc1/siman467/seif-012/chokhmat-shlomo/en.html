(There seif 12 in the gloss) And there is one who forbids in other meat and permits in chicken. I would say: the poskim wrote the reason — perhaps it was digested, and those who forbid hold it is not digestion. And behold I wonder at both of them that they did not give a boundary to the matter — in how much is there digestion; and the lenient ones imply they hold that even if found within the time of digestion for eating it is also an uncertainty perhaps it was digested, and those who forbid hold that even more than the time of digestion it is not digestion; and in truth an explicit mishnah in tractate Parah chapter 9 mishnah 5: a cow that drank waters of purification — its flesh is impure for the time of digestion; and the Ra'avad explained there because if it waited more than that it was digested in its intestines; if so it is proven that the measure of digestion is the time of digestion, and less than that is certainly not digestion, for if it were an uncertainty it would be to say its flesh is uncertain impurity; and from the fact that it says its flesh is impure it implies certain impurity. Also otherwise if it were only an uncertainty it should be pure entirely, for it is an uncertainty of impurity in something that has no knowledge to ask — its uncertainty is pure; and necessarily its impurity is certain; if so within the time of digestion is certainly digestion, and conversely after the time of digestion is certainly digestion, since it does not teach that because of uncertainty it is pure — for there is practical difference for several matters; and also if it came by human action it should be that its uncertainty is impure, such as if a person gave it to drink; and when it does not distinguish thus, necessarily it is certain digestion after the time of digestion. And it is possible there is a distinction between food and drink, that drink is digested faster; but at least from this it is proven that at least in drink the time of digestion is the measure of digestion, and I have no time now to expand further — examine well. And behold I was uncertain in my large composition on the laws of Pesach regarding a cooked dish cooked during the days of Pesach and remained until after Pesach, and afterward chametz was found in it — this proves it did not fall in afterward but was inside at the time it was Yom Tov; if we follow the time of cooking when its law was in any amount, or we follow the time of finding after Pesach when its law is like before Pesach, only in sixty or in majority. And the practical difference is whether the vessels are permitted afterward for the coming Pesach as with found before Pesach which is only in sixty; and we expanded there. And behold now a manuscript came to me that was in the possession of the Gaon R' Zalman Margaliot, copied from Sefer Rokeach, and its author Rabbeinu Elazar, etc., and he lists his family that he was a grandson of the author of Sefer Chassidim and one of the Rishonim; and I found in it in letter peh chapter 8 of the laws of Pesach, and this is its language: An incident where they cooked poultry after the seventh of Pesach and ate some of that poultry on that day it was cooked, and on the morrow on Shabbat which was the last of Pesach a wheat kernel was found in that portion remaining from the seventh day, and they asked whether to permit or forbid; some forbade from this that chametz in its time, whether in its species or not in its species, is forbidden like Rav — so I heard in Worms in the year they cooked on the seventh day of Pesach — end quote; behold it is proven that he holds that if it had been cooked on the eighth day it would be permitted in sixty, only since it was cooked on the seventh day, even though the finding was on the eighth day, we follow after cooking; and from this we learn for our case if found after Pesach and the cooking was on Pesach, we follow after cooking; and I benefited greatly from this there's help — understand and examine well:
