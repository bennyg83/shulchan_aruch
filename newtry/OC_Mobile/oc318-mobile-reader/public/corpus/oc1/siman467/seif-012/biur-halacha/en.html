If they scalded, etc. See Taz, who wrote that ordinary scalding is in a kli sheni; and according to what the Mechaber wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 68, that after the fact a kli sheni does not cause absorption, apparently one must say it deals with a kli rishon; and if because of the blood prohibition, it deals with a case where they salted with the feathers before scalding, as I copied in Mishnah Berurah in the name of Peri Megadim. But it is possible that the Mechaber's view is like those who are stringent whom the Rama cited above in siman 447 se'if 3 in the gloss, that even in a kli sheni it is forbidden; and so I found in Peri Chadash, who is uncertain about the Mechaber's intent; and regarding the core law of a kli sheni, Peri Chadash's view is to be stringent on Pesach; see there; and see above in siman 451 in Mishnah Berurah s.k. 11.
And if a cracked wheat kernel was found, etc. And barley in water: see above in siman 447 in Magen Avraham s.k. 6, that it imparts a spoiled taste; and the later authorities do not disagree with him there except regarding wheat; see there.
If the mixtures mixed and there is major financial loss, etc. This implies that the first cooked dish one may not permit even through major financial loss; and so it appears in the later authorities. But in Beur HaGra it implies that from the ground of major financial loss alone he permits, and even in hot [food], for then we follow the first view in the Rama that permits in scalding; and one must say according to him that when the Rama mentions mixtures, it means that through mixtures major financial loss is common; and if so, when the Rama wrote regarding a cooked dish of meat that soaked in water (that is, in cold), and there he permits even if the wheat kernel was found in it while the meat was still soaking in it, the main [reason] is likewise major financial loss; therefore if it happened that there was also major financial loss through the first cooked dish, it too is permitted in cold, for we rely on the first view in the Mechaber that permits in cold. And with this the difficulty of Magen Avraham above in siman 447 s.k. 9 is resolved: there in the gloss it implies that in cold it is permitted, and here he forbids in cold: there it deals with a place of major financial loss; and this requires further study.
