Cracked open. See Baer Heitev s.k. 24; and now like that [case] found in a responsum siman 51: and it was on Pesach that one wheat kernel was found in cold water, and from that water they had already made a cooked dish and baked matzos, and there was doubt whether the wheat kernel was cracked open, and they permitted by force of a double doubt: perhaps it is not cracked open; and further, behold there is a dispute of the authorities whether cold water forbids the cooked dish; and further, he concluded from R' Shmuel Gaon that even if certainly cracked open one may permit; and even though Shulchan Aruch wrote "and there is one who forbids during Pesach," behold we do not find this reasoning explicitly, only because he found difficult the Tur's words against each other, and to resolve them he distinguishes between found on Pesach and before it; but the Gaon resolves the Tur's wonders with a spacious explanation: hence when the water is cold, even if cracked open and even on Pesach it is permitted, for the wheat kernel imparts no taste; and see responsa Rosh Yosef and Chok Yaakov.
Permitted. Magen Avraham explains: that is regarding a small amount of chametz with sixty in the water against it. And know that the Levush expanded to permit in cold in the name of great sages and elders in the name of Maharil of Prague; but Maharash of Ostrovha wrote to Maharam of Lublin to forbid; and so they practice, unlike Taz; see there.
