Split open. See Be'er Heitev s.k. 24; and accordingly when that case is in Tur siman 451 and it is found on Pesach in cold water one wheat kernel and from those waters they already made a cooked dish and baked matzot in it — and there was uncertainty whether the wheat kernel was split open — they permitted by force of a double uncertainty that perhaps it was not split; and further behold there is a dispute of the great ones whether cold water forbids the cooked dish; and further he raised from R' Shmuel Gaon that even certainly split open one may permit; and even though Shulchan Aruch wrote "and there is one who forbids during Pesach" — this reasoning we do not find explicitly except because the Tur's words contradicted each other and in order to settle them he distinguished between found on Pesach and before it; but the Gaon resolves the Tur's puzzlements with a broad explanation that when the water is cold, even split open and even on Pesach it is permitted for the wheat kernel does not impart taste — see in Tur Rosh Yosef and Kaf HaChayyim:
