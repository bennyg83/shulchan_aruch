The chicken is forbidden. For we say perhaps the wheat kernel was in water at the time of scalding — we do not say here it was found here it was, except from place to place or from piece to piece, as in siman 11; but in one place, such as scalding, where there is uncertainty in time and place — perhaps the wheat kernel was in water before — we do not say now it fell, for so we find chapter 1 of Chullin and chapter 1 of Niddah that we do not say now it deteriorated where there is no presumption; however what the Rabbeinu Yosef wrote in the name of the Mordechai: if a grain of wheat is found in a bowl near the chicken it is permitted because of double uncertainty (as the Rama wrote in the gloss) and not permitted because of here it was found here it was; and one can say: since it is common to find wheat kernels in chickens that eat wheat, we say perhaps it was in the chicken, as in Yoreh De'ah siman 57 we rely on the common — therefore it is permitted only because of double uncertainty — end of his words; Mishna Berurah siman 69 — and it is difficult, for if so even double uncertainty is not, for a common thing is not uncertainty at all, for even for leniency we rely on it, as in Yoreh De'ah siman 57, and so Orach Chayyim HaShalem; rather it must be specifically there it is very common, but a somewhat common thing we consider double uncertainty, as in siman 84; and if so the Mishna Berurah should not have brought from siman 57; and see in the author of the Shulchan Aruch siman 6 in the Beit Yosef that an uncommon thing is not considered double uncertainty; and it appears to me: even though it is common to find wheat kernels in chickens, when they scald them nothing remains inside, especially for Pesach need — therefore it is not called regular uncertainty; and that the Mordechai did not permit because of here it was found here it was, and in the bowl it fell — because the Mordechai holds a second vessel is forbidden on Pesach, as in siman 447 seif 3, and from time to time we establish prohibition; and if you say it was in the bowl when they turned the chicken into it, it was forbidden — therefore he permitted because of double uncertainty that now it is cold and does not forbid — it deals where there was no gravy there:

And there is not enough to impart taste. It appears thus he says: the chametz is minimal and does not impart taste in the water — there is sixty in the water against it — therefore permitted; for since rabbinic prohibition in any amount we are not stringent in cold; and nevertheless some forbid, etc. And the Levush expanded to permit in cold, and wrote in all places he passed he saw great sages and elders who did not forbid in cold — end of his words; and Mahari Cohen that the Gaon Maharal of Prague ruled to permit, but Maharash of Austria wrote to Maharash of Lublin to forbid, and so most of the world practices, and in Krakow; and it appears it depends on custom — end of his words. And in truth, even though the Levush justified the Tur's words not to forbid in cold, behold the Mordechai forbade in roasting meat even if the wheat kernel was found after roasting — see there; and it is possible he forbade because there was gravy, as in siman 447; and so the Beit Yosef here in the name of geonim, and in siman 12 in the name of the author of the Or Zarua — see there; therefore it appears to me regarding meat where there is gravy it is forbidden by law in cold; and similarly if there is concern it dripped from above, also forbidden by law, as in Yoreh De'ah siman 105; and in other cold cases it depends on custom or according to the matter of time of distress; and see seif 11 that in dough one should be stringent, and the same in dough because through kneading it leavens and imparts its taste throughout:

And there is one who permits, etc. It appears from his words he holds the Rabbeinu Yosef forbids in cold even if found after removing the meat and it is not necessary:

For we say perhaps afterward. The language is not precise — how is it permitted from uncertainty? Rather the reason: we establish presumption and say now it fell — so R' Yehuda Vein; and the Beit Yosef and R' Yehuda Vein wrote in the name of the Rashba: if a wheat kernel is found in a vessel in which they rinsed the chicken, we say here it was found here it was, like blemishes, and we do not say it came from the chicken; and so he ruled practically — end of his words; and it is difficult, for the Rashba ruled if a wheat kernel is found in matzah or dough they forbid the matzah or dough, as in siman 11, and we do not say now they fell; and it is difficult according to the Rama's explanation why he wrote we do not say it came from the chicken, etc. — he should have written we do not say it was before in water; and further it is not comparable to blemishes — therefore it appears to me it deals: they salted the chicken and afterward rinsed it in a vessel in cold, and afterward a wheat kernel was found in the vessel — there is concern it was in the chicken and was salted with it; for this he wrote we are not concerned, and brings proof from blemishes: we say in chapter 6 of Ketubot blemishes found in her father's domain — we say presumably there were blemishes already before she was betrothed; and if found in the husband's domain we say now they arose; here too, since the wheat kernel was found in the vessel we do not say it was in the chicken, for that is from place to place; but in matzah or dough it is from time to time, as written above; and if so there is no dispute at all — so it appears to me essentially:

By force of double uncertainty. Perhaps now it fell, and if you say it was before in the chicken, perhaps it was digested; and those who disagree hold specifically for impurity digestion helps, not for prohibition, as written in Yoreh De'ah end of siman 83 — see there in Beit Yosef and so there in Shulchan Aruch; and in the Agudah in Bekhorot it is written: even for the answer of R' Meir of Pontremoli one may not permit a wheat kernel in a chicken, for presumably it swallowed it whole and it is not digestion — see there; it implies even if it broke afterward it is forbidden:

And they roasted chickens there. It should read: not specifically chickens were mentioned, for here it deals with a whole wheat kernel that does not require digestion; if so there is no distinction between chickens and meat — if the wheat kernel is found after removing the meat from there, they do not forbid the meat:

That dripped into water. Explanation: even though the wheat kernel was found inside while the meat was soaking in it, nevertheless it is rabbinic prohibition uncertainty, for some permit in cold — therefore one may permit other cooked dishes:

Or was scalded. Explanation: and afterward the wheat kernel was found — one can say now it fell; and that which he ruled above seif 2 forbidden to eat because of double uncertainty — that is to eat the wheat kernels themselves, but the mixtures are permitted; and if so, if there is double uncertainty in matzah, forbidden to eat it. The Bach wrote: in single uncertainty even to keep is forbidden, even on the intermediate days; and in double uncertainty permitted to keep, and all the more to sell to a gentile, and not for eating that perhaps it was digested — this is not full uncertainty; some say digestion does not help in prohibition, as above; and specifically full chametz such as bread or dough, but a wheat kernel found in water — the cooked dish cooked in it before is permitted, for double uncertainty: perhaps now it fell, and if you say before it fell, perhaps it did not soften until now, and without softening there is no forbidding at all even according to our custom — therefore; and it is implied from his words that even in hot one does not forbid without softening — see there. An incident: one took into his mouth a piece of matzah and a piece of chicken and ate both together, and felt in his mouth there is a wheat kernel and removed the food from his mouth and saw a wheat kernel, and it is not recognizable if it was in the matzah or in the chicken — the chicken may be permitted by double uncertainty: perhaps now it fell to the chicken in cold, and if you say before, perhaps it was digested; but if it was in the matzah — necessarily it was inside at the time of its baking, for if it fell on it in cold it would roll and descend; and nevertheless it appears to me the matzah is also permitted, since rabbinic prohibition in any amount we suspend to leniency that it was in the chicken, as in Yoreh De'ah siman 111 regarding two pots: in one there is sixty — we rely on that which has sixty, and both are permitted; and nevertheless the amount of removal is forbidden by law from that matzah from the place he took the piece; and further it appears to me even a piece of meat is permitted, for we do not establish prohibition from place to place, and we say now it fell on the meat while still cold — if so the matzah is also permitted; but if the meat rested in a bowl and he ate from it, the meat is forbidden, as stated, for from time to time we establish prohibition — perhaps it was in the bowl when they turned the meat into it; and a second vessel forbids on Pesach; and if we say there is double uncertainty in the matzah and you say in the meat — perhaps now it fell — this is not uncertainty, for if you say it was in the matzah you must forbid the matzah, and we do not say this even for rabbinic prohibition, even in double uncertainty;
