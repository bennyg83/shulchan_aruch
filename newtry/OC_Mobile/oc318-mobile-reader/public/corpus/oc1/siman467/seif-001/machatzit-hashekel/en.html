In the Tur he wrote 26 and some say perhaps he will grind them, etc., for one does not buy flour from the market, etc., and the Ran who wrote that even if the splitting is recognizable it is forbidden to sell them to a gentile lest he sell them to an Israelite and the Israelite not intend to examine them. Or lest the gentile grind them and return and sell them to an Israelite — this is because the Ran wrote before this according to the Rif regarding guarding from harvest time in the passage "and a person needs to guard, etc." in the name of a Gaon that one does not buy flour from the market except in pressing circumstances. And this is only for matzah of the commandment; but other bread that a person eats on Pesach, etc. — it implies that it is permitted to buy flour from the market for other matzot. But the Tur — it is possible he holds that even for other matzot one does not buy from the market, for now the practice of gentiles is to moisten the wheat before grinding; and with this what Kaf HaChayyim questioned is not difficult at all, that the Israelite will take the flour and leave it in his house and make a prohibition in that he leaves chametz in his house, and even if it was nullified nevertheless there is a d'rabbanan prohibition — no difficulty here, for it is forbidden to make matzot from them; and it is a distant concern that an Israelite will buy flour about which there is concern for leavening, for from this reason he is not permitted to make from them since their practice is to moisten them; and from the Torah he will buy this flour and leave it without need; and it is possible that according to what is forbidden to make matzot from them for all the days of Pesach, since their practice is to moisten, it is possible that also to leave it in his house is forbidden; in any case it is a distant concern, end quote.
