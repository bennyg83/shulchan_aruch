1) [Se'if 1] Grain that was submerged in a river, etc. There is concern that some kernels became chametz, although this cannot be identified. Tur; Levush. This implies that if the chametz is identifiable, it may be sold, even in a large quantity to one non-Jew according to Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 4. We are not concerned that the non-Jew might mill and resell it, since flour is purchased in the marketplace only in pressing circumstances, as stated in siman 453. This is contrary to Olas Shabbos. Magen Avraham, at the beginning of this siman. Ran in the chapter Kol Sha'ah, however, writes that even when kernels split and the chametz is identifiable, they may not be sold to a non-Jew, lest the non-Jew mill and then sell them to a Jew. Olas Shabbos, subsection 1, writes likewise and questions Tur's implication that identifiable chametz may be sold. Magen Avraham therefore writes that his ruling is contrary to Olas Shabbos. Chok Yaakov, subsection 1, cites Ran and questions Magen Avraham: we should still be concerned that a Jew might purchase the flour before Pesach, retain it at home, and violate the prohibition against chametz being seen. He explains that Tur means that even though the chametz cannot be identified and one knows only that there is a concern of chametz, we nevertheless fear a mishap when it is sold. This concern certainly applies initially when the chametz is known and identifiable. See there. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1, resolves Chok Yaakov's objection by explaining that Magen Avraham holds that the Sages did not issue a decree on this account, since the Jew nullifies it by declaring all unseen chametz ownerless. See there. Mekor Chaim in the explanations likewise agrees with Magen Avraham. See there. Erech HaShulchan, subsection 1, permits selling it to a non-Jew because of a compound doubt: perhaps the non-Jew will sell it to another non-Jew, and even if it is sold to a Jew, perhaps it will be sold as visibly affected wheat. See there. Beis David, siman 232, however, agrees with Ran. See there. The matter is therefore disputed. Initially, if possible, one should sell a small amount to each buyer or sell it under an arrangement requiring its return after Pesach, as stated above in siman 448, se'if 3. If this is impossible and a substantial loss is involved, one may rely upon the permitting authorities. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 4, likewise writes that one may rely upon them where a substantial loss is involved.
