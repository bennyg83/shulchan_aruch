Introduction to siman 467:

First one must investigate siman of leavening in dough — when is it complete chametz and when stiff. And the sign of leavening in wheat and the five species of grain: what are they, and if whole wheat — is it complete chametz whenever it has a sign of leavening, or stiff? And behold in dough the Sages gave a measure like grasshopper horns — complete chametz liable for karet; if its face turned pale — it is stiff, as in siman 459 in Tur and Shulchan Aruch seif 2 and Pesachim 46a and 48b. And there is further stiff from fruit juice with water as in siman 462 — there we explained that pale face for us is d'rabbanan; but stiff from fruit juice with water where leavening is recognizable in them — one might say even according to those who say stiff it is in any amount; see there and nevertheless it is possible in Beit Yosef there is no practical difference regarding transgressing Pesach upon it — at end of siman 447. And it appears specifically mostly fruit juice with little water — behold mostly water more than half and little fruit juice — we follow the majority; and it has power to leaven; and if recognizable like grasshopper horns — liable for karet; but fruit juice alone without water — even if it stood all day and its cracks intermingled — that is not leavening in them. And Pesachim 38b: cooking leavens it, etc., meaning even within the measure of a mil in lukewarm water according to there. And it is difficult: say cooked in fruit juice alone without water — even if afterward water was mixed with it, still it does not leaven, for it is already cooked; and the law is the true law even for us in 462 in pressing circumstances permitted in fruit juice to cook — if afterward water is mixed it does not come to leavening; and in a whole grain so too one might say in pressing circumstances that we are not concerned it did not cook well — see Magen Avraham 463:4 and it will be explained from this inside. And for Rashi who explained fruit juice alone is stiff — if so. However cooked in fruit juice as it happens and afterward water is mixed — it appears it is complete chametz; and proof from 463 there: perhaps it did not cook well:

And if dough stood the measure of a mil even if there is no sign of leavening at all — it is complete chametz liable for karet, even though not found thus in explicit explanation — nevertheless such it is; and in my humble opinion chapter 5 siman 13 from pressing circumstances the measure of a mil is complete chametz; and so pale face is forbidden — it implies at first glance measure of a mil and pale face are one; on the contrary infer from the beginning the opposite in deaf dough: he measured with it the measure of a mil — as Magen Avraham wrote that grasshopper horns with making a sound are one; and from it the same applies to measure of a mil. And this is when he left it without occupation — behold if occupied all day even any occupation it does not leaven; and such is that of dripping dripping there 39b and Orach Chayyim 459. And this is all when signs of leavening were not born — if born, even pale face does not help — occupation in dough and grasshopper horns liable for karet; and from the ordinary case they said according to the majority: all who are occupied do not come to leavening. Also casting cold helps for dough as in siman 457 seif 2 — see Magen Avraham there 6; he left b'dieved uncertain — see end there letter 11; nevertheless certainly lukewarm like the heat of saliva does not nullify leavening. And behold casting cold helps even all day and even from above, for if he read on the first Yom Tov day of Pesach the casting of cold helps until the end of day 2; and even though soaked is like cooked and cooked leavens — this is an error, for for purging and absorption it is like cooked not for leavening; and so if he sees a sign of leavening casting cold does not nullify it, as stated:

However all this is in dough; but whole wheat and five species of grain upon which water came — it was not explained explicitly what is their clear sign to be complete chametz and liable for karet on them — only this alone we find in the words of the Sages Pesachim 40a: if wheat grains split open they are forbidden; if not split open — permitted — see there dispute of Shmuel and Mar Ukva. And if no sign of splitting arose and grains fell into water and did not stand the measure of a mil — it is obvious a whole grain is no worse than dough in such a case that there is no leavening; and so in Rosh chapter 30:40a in a split grain found in dough — since the dough did not leaven before a mil, so too the grain; and see end of siman and Magen Avraham letter 4 brought proof on this and it will be explained inside; but casting cold on a grain — one might say it does not help, for it enters all parts of the dough unlike a grain; but measure of a mil — obviously a grain is no worse than dough. And if a sign of leavening arose in whole grains — that is splitting — it must be judged whether they stood the measure of a mil from when water came upon them or did not stand a mil — if it is complete chametz clearly liable for karet or stiff. For us stiff is d'rabbanan; nevertheless without any splitting arising and the grains stood the measure of a mil from when water came upon them — it implies it is obviously not chametz at all; and proof from what Rabbenu Efrayim challenged from the incident of the ship that sank in a basket that Rava did not ask if they split open and needed to distinguish between resting at ease and placing — see Rosh chapter 30:40a there the grains stood more than a mil — we learn all that did not split open are permitted even if they stood the measure of a mil; and below I will explain further. And the Levush in seif 9 wrote: a cooked dish in which wheat and barley were found — even if they split open literally it is not complete chametz from the Torah but only complete chametz d'rabbanan; if not split open there is no complete chametz d'rabbanan — nevertheless people practice to forbid the cooked dish — see there; it implies even if they split open in cooking and split literally — nevertheless it is only chametz d'rabbanan; and in cooking less than a mil or less — see Penei Yehoshua Pesachim 39b.
