Introduction to siman 467.
First. One must investigate the sign of leavening in dough: when it is absolute chametz and when it is chametz nuksheh. Likewise, what are the signs of leavening in wheat and the five species of grain, and whether whole kernels become absolute chametz once they show a sign of leavening, or only chametz nuksheh. Regarding dough, Chazal gave the measure of cracks like locust horns as absolute chametz, and whitening as another sign, as explained by the authorities.
And if. Dough remained for the time it takes to walk a mil, even if there is no sign of leavening at all, it is absolute chametz and one is liable to kareis. Although this is not found explicitly, it is so. See the Rambam, chapter 5, halacha 13 of Chametz U'Matzah, where the measure of a mil is chametz. The expression that its surface whitened and it is forbidden might imply that the mil and whitening are one measure, but on the contrary, the mil is a separate measure.
However. All this concerns dough. But regarding whole wheat kernels and the five species of grain upon which water came, it is not explicitly clarified what their definitive sign is to be absolute chametz and incur kareis. We find only in Chazal, Pesachim 40a, regarding soaking wheat, that if they split they are forbidden and if they did not split they are permitted.
In this way. We learn from here that whole wheat upon which water came, and which split and remained at least the time of a mil, is chametz according to the Torah and one is liable to kareis. What the Rosh wrote that a split wheat kernel in dough is doubtful chametz refers either before the time of a mil or while one is still working the dough, so that the dough itself has not leavened. But where the kernel remained properly in water, splitting is a sign of chametz.
And there is. One still must ask how the Sages, the Rif in Perek Kol Sha'ah and the Rambam in chapter 5 of Chametz U'Matzah, said that a cooked dish containing an unsplit wheat kernel is permitted. From Pesachim 40a, whether by soaking or by remaining still, it should be problematic. The answer is that according to Rabbeinu Ephraim or as the Magen Avraham writes, this is only a doubt of D'Rabbanan in a minute mixture, and the law is lenient where it did not split.
Know. Baked matter that cannot come to cooking is different. Baked chametz bread placed afterward into flour with fruit juice may possibly leaven it. The baraisa about moldy bread that became unfit for a dog but remains fit to leaven other doughs is no proof, for there it speaks of water, and through the chametz the dough leavens quickly. See the Magen Avraham, seif katan 15, in the name of the Rashba's responsum.
Know further. It is proper to explain the matter of liquid gravy, which conducts and extracts through the whole item and within the whole item. This includes any clear substance, water, fruit juice, oil, or clear fat, in which something cooked will exude and absorb throughout. This is learned from Yoreh De'ah 104:2 regarding a mouse in soft fat that moves about, which is treated as kavush. Thus soft liquid conveys taste throughout.
Another point. Regarding a whole wheat kernel, where splitting is the sign of leavening as explained, if it is found in fruit juice, even with splitting we do not presume that it had become chametz earlier, because fruit juice does not normally cause splitting, though one in a thousand may split from fruit juice. If it does split from fruit juice, this is not the same sign as leavening with water. The matter requires judging by what is usual.
And this. A wheat kernel in honey, even without sixty, is permitted after the fact, because fruit juice does not leaven, as indicated by the Levush and Olas Shabbos. There is also a broader issue whether chametz is a davar sheyesh lo matirin, with some saying it is and some saying it is not, as discussed in siman 447, here, in the Levush, and in Yoreh De'ah 102 by the Shach and Taz. There is room for leniency in several cases.
We maintain. In Yoreh De'ah we rule that an item forbidden by a minute amount is not subject to chatichah na'asis neveilah, because it is not comparable to meat and milk, where a full taste is required. A practical result is a pot containing food in which a split wheat kernel was found, so everything is forbidden, and afterward, when it was not ben yomo, that pot was placed in an oven together with other pots. One may say this is only aroma of a minute amount, and the law follows the principles explained there.
Regarding salting. See seif 14, where, with Hashem's help, it will be clarified whether to distinguish between something edible because of its salt and something not edible because of its salt. Brine from salted meat, even where the meat is edible because of its salt, is considered hot, as in Yoreh De'ah 91:5. If it fell on a vessel, that too will be explained there with Hashem's help.
Or. See the Taz and Magen Avraham, unlike the Olas Shabbos, who was concerned that the non-Jew may grind it and sell it to a Jew. The Ran wrote this according to his own approach that unsplit grain is permitted by soaking and he establishes the case as split kernels. But according to us, who follow Rabbeinu Ephraim in seif 2, that since the kernels rest they become chametz, and so too Rashi in Pesachim 40, this wheat is not recognizable, and the concern is framed differently.
