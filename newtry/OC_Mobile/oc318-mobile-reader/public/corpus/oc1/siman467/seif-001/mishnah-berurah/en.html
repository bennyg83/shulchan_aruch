(1) Grain that was submerged, etc. — in the Tur it is written and the chametz is not recognizable, etc. It implies that if the chametz is recognizable it is permitted both when selling to a Jew and when selling to a non-Jew, for there is no further concern that he will return and sell it to a Jew and he will eat it on Pesach, for the Jew will recognize it; and there are among the poskim who are stringent lest the non-Jew grind it and sell them — however, for our custom that we do not buy flour for Pesach from the market, several Acharonim lean that there is no concern; and in a place of pressing need one may be lenient:

(2) And he informs him — but one does not rely on the proclamation, for perhaps there is someone who did not hear the proclamation. And when he informs him it is permitted to sell to him even a large amount; and without notification, even a small amount — it is forbidden to sell to him lest he come to eat it on Pesach:

(3) So that it will be consumed, etc. — that is, even if you say he will sell it to a Jew, the Jew will eat it before Pesach; and according to this, on the eve of Pesach in the fifth hour in a matter where it is impossible that it will be consumed before Pesach, it is forbidden to sell to a gentile even a small amount, lest he sell that small amount to a Jew; and nevertheless in a place of great loss several Acharonim agreed that all this is with an ordinary gentile; but a gentile known not to touch it but only to guard it until after Pesach and return it to him, as was explained in siman 448 — a Jew may sell him a large amount of leavened grain:

(4) Before Pesach — and in other prohibitions where their prohibition is not recognizable, it is forbidden to sell to a gentile even little by little; only here where there are several uncertainties they were lenient [Acharonim]:
