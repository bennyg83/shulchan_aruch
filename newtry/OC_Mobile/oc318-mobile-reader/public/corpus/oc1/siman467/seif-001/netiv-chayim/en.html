{Rama seif 2} Those that became moistened. I will copy for you the words of the Agur, from which the words of the Rama are taken, and thereby we will come to the explanation brought in the Beit Yosef, and this is its language: The Agur — his father was asked about wheat that was spread in an attic under the roof and water passed through the roof and dripped on the grain in some places where there was so much water that they were touchable to touch back, and he answered that Ra'em and Ribba were stringent and said regarding wheat (since they have splitting) that if there is an abundance of water, even if it did not split open the wheat is forbidden; nevertheless to rely on the permission that Ra'avyah wrote and that the Mordechai brought at length in the name of Ra'avyah — to eat some of it before Pesach or to sell to a gentile little by little and the rest is permitted by force of a double uncertainty — perhaps rain did not descend upon them, and if you say rain descended upon them, perhaps they did not split open (meaning: perhaps they did not leaven); but the lower wheat that is at the floor of the attic — it is nearly certain that water descended there to the thickness of a finger, and those one discards and the rest is permitted to keep; but to eat from them on Pesach — for even though regarding "it shall not be seen" the uncertainties are nullified in their majority regarding eating, which is in any amount, we are not lenient — end of his words. And at first glance these words have no understanding. And it appears to me that the question was thus: through dripping of the roof water drips on the grain in some places, and there was also a dripping gutter near the spread grain until water descended on the attic floor under the grain; and now you can understand the answer to it. And behold what the Rama wrote — those that became moistened are forbidden, but other places, etc. — this is not explained in the words of the Agur. Rather it was difficult for him if we explain the words of the Agur as referring to those that did not become moistened, for without that there is already a double uncertainty that permits even if they actually leavened — one, perhaps they already ate from the forbidden ones, and if you say they did not eat, one can say they will now eat from the permitted ones, as with a ring of idolatry that became mixed and one of them fell into the sea and all were permitted to benefit together; and the Rama explained this that the Agur wrote to eat some of it or to sell to a gentile little by little — meaning those known places that became moistened, and the rest of the pile need not eat or sell some of them, and everywhere else is permitted by force of uncertainty. And with this the difficulty of the Taz is resolved — that which the Rama does not permit in eating refers to a place where there is no uncertainty that he has other kosher wheat without doubt for matzah of the commandment, as written in Kol Bo; however if he has no other wheat they are permitted even in eating, for a double uncertainty helps even for a d'oraisa prohibition — end quote:
