Grain that was submerged, etc. — if a sheet of water or snow fell upon the grain before grinding, one discards the upper wheat kernels and the rest is permitted; and specifically a small amount of water or snow, and its place is known; but grain placed in an attic and much rain descended through the roof and those that became moist became moistened — those that became moistened are forbidden; and in other places it is permitted to keep them on Pesach, but to eat them on Pesach is forbidden (Beit Yosef). Some are stringent even regarding honey that they extract from the hive before a Jew eats it raw, because of a decree lest it be confused; so too my teacher in the name of Maharshal that he too received thus; and specifically before Pesach one may make a drink from honey, for even if there were a mixture of flour in it, it was already nullified in sixty; but on Pesach itself it is forbidden because chametz on Pesach is in any amount. And some say that specifically the drink is permitted to make before Pesach, but the honey itself that a Jew cooked before Pesach — even though he strained it from the bees in it — they are forbidden to give it during Pesach into food; and one who is lenient has not lost, and one who is stringent — may he be blessed; and I already wrote in the name of my teacher in Bach that even honey that they draw from the hive is also forbidden because it comes to be confused, and so is the custom not to eat it; tsuker too is forbidden to eat. Tsuker kandil is permitted to keep — so proved Magen Avraham. A cooked dish that was cooked and wheat or barley was found in it, and also the spoon with which they stirred in the pot and placed it in another pot — all are forbidden (Tur, Rokeach). If a wheat kernel was found embedded in dough, see in responsum of Maharshal siman 79. If they scalded a chicken and a wheat kernel or barley kernel was found — it appears that even in great loss we forbid everything with a single uncertainty, and not as the author of the Hagah wrote to be lenient in this; and where there is a double uncertainty it is permitted to keep, and all the more so it is permitted to sell to a gentile, for it is not sold at a higher price on account of the wheat kernel, and it is found that he has no benefit from the wheat kernel at all; and a wheat kernel found in a vessel of water, an embedded wheat kernel and dough — both are equal, that while they are still in water they do not leaven, and when they come up from the water both leaven; and regarding practical halachah, as written in Hagahot Maimoni, if the wheat kernel softened they forbid the water, and all the more so full chametz or dough that leavened that fell into a pit — its water is forbidden and the cooked dish is forbidden too, and not as some are lenient — my teacher (in Bach):
