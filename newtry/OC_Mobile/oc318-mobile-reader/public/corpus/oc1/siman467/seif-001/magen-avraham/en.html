In the Tur it is written, ‘and the chametz is not recognizable in them,’ etc. This implies that if the chametz is recognizable, it is permitted, and we are not concerned that he may grind it and sell it, since flour is not purchased from the marketplace except in pressing circumstances, as written in siman 453. This appears correct to me, unlike Olas Shabbos. And he informs him. But one may not rely upon a public announcement. See Shiltei Gibborim, chapter 5 of Avodah Zarah.
So that it will be consumed. Meaning, even if you say that the non-Jew will sell it to a Jew, that Jew will eat it before Pesach. Kesef Mishneh explains that when he purchases only one kav at a time for his household, he needs it and will consume it before Pesach, and there is therefore no concern. This requires study, for if so, why does the Gemara say that it will be consumed before Pesach, implying that if we know it will not be consumed, such as where it is sold during the fifth hour on Erev Pesach, it is forbidden? According to Kesef Mishneh, it should be permitted because he purchases it for household use. Moreover, Avodah Zarah 65a likewise discusses wheat upon which yayin nesech fell. Rava permitted selling it to a non-Jew, but Rabbah bar Livai objected, so Rava retracted and instructed that it be sifted and sold to a non-Jew not in the presence of a Jew, since Jews do not purchase bread from non-Jews. Tosafot ask how he could have erred there after Rabbah had already objected in the incident in Pesachim. They answer that both incidents came before him at one time, he ruled in both that it might be sold to a non-Jew, Rabbah objected, and he retracted. If so, why did he trouble them there to sift it and sell it? He should likewise have instructed them to sell it little by little. It follows that selling little by little helps specifically with chametz, because even if the non-Jew sells it to a Jew, the Jew will eat it before Pesach. For other prohibitions, selling little by little does not help, since we remain concerned that he may sell it to a Jew or give it as a gift to a Jewish friend. Indeed, nothing is smaller than an egg, yet one may not sell an egg from a neveilah or tereifah to a non-Jew, even a single egg, as implied by the Gemara and poskim. See Yoreh Deah, end of siman 86. In siman 84, Beit Yosef cites a ruling that worm-infested flour must be cast into a river, implying that no remedy exists. Rama writes in siman 154:5 regarding yayin nesech that became mixed in, ‘Nevertheless, he should not sell a large quantity together, lest the non-Jew resell it to a Jew,’ implying that selling a little at a time raises no concern. These words are taken from Rabbeinu Yerucham, but I have heard and do not understand, for Rabbeinu Yerucham could not have intended this. There, the taste of wine is detectable, so how would a Jew purchase it from a non-Jew? Rather, his intent is that he should not sell the entire mixture together except for the value of the yayin nesech. See Rabbeinu Yerucham and you will find this to be so. Even his words require study, for Tosafot at the end of chapter 3 of Avodah Zarah write only that he need not sell it together except for the value of the yayin nesech, but if he wishes, he may sell it together. They write similarly in Yevamos 81b, at the end of Avodah Zarah, and in Zevachim 72. This is not the place to elaborate. In a matter where there is concern that it may be resold to a Jew, however, there is no permission to sell it little by little except with chametz, as explained. See Yoreh Deah 112:11. Knesses HaGedolah cites Rashdam, Yoreh Deah 45, and Maharit, Yoreh Deah 2, as writing: although there is a double doubt, perhaps a Jew will eat it before Pesach, a double doubt created by one's own act is forbidden. This requires study, for the case concerns a large quantity of chametz. Perhaps their meaning is that the non-Jew may sell it to many Jews, a little to each. This apparently follows Kesef Mishneh, that the permission depends upon the small amount being consumed by the non-Jew himself. See also the end of Yoreh Deah siman 57.
