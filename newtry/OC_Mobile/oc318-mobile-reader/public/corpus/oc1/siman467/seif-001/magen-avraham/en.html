In the Tur it is written: and the chametz is not recognizable in them, etc. — it implies that if the chametz is recognizable it is permitted, and we are not concerned lest he grind and sell them, since buyers do not buy flour from the market except in time of distress, as in siman 453, as stated above, and not as the author of the Shulchan Aruch. And one informs him — but they do not rely on proclamation (Shulchan Gavoah chapter 5 of Avodah Zarah):

So that it will be consumed. Meaning: even if you say he will sell it to an Israelite, the Israelite will eat it before Pesach; and the Kesef Mishneh wrote the explanation: when he buys only measure by measure for his household he needs it and it will be consumed before Pesach — there is nothing to fear — end of his words; and it requires study, for if so why does he say and it will be consumed before Pesach — it implies that if they know it will not be consumed before Pesach, such as selling it on the eve at the fifth hour, it is forbidden; and for the Kesef Mishneh it should be permitted, for he buys it for his household need; and further, in Avodah Zarah chapter 4 seif 5 it says regarding wheat over which rain fell, and Rava permitted selling to a gentile, and Rabah bar Rav Huna challenged him and he retracted and decided to sift and sell to a gentile not in the presence of Israelites (for they do not buy bread from gentiles); and Tosafos questioned: after Rabah already challenged him in the incident in Pesachim, how did he err here a second time? And Tosafos answered: both incidents came before him at once and he ruled in both to sell to a gentile, and Rabah challenged and he retracted — therefore it is difficult why he troubled them there to sift and sell — he should also have ruled to sell little by little to a gentile; and necessarily specifically for chametz little by little helps, for even if you say he will sell to an Israelite, the Israelite will eat it before Pesach; but for other prohibitions little by little does not help, for we are still concerned he will sell them to an Israelite or give as a gift to his Israelite friend; and further, you have nothing smaller than an egg, and nevertheless they do not sell an egg of nevelah or tereifah to a gentile; and it is implied from the Gemara's language and the poskim even one egg b'dieved siman 86, and in siman 84 the Rabbeinu Yosef brings flour that became wormy to throw into the river — it implies there is no remedy. And what the Rama wrote in siman 454 seif 5 regarding yeast that became mixed — nevertheless one may not sell much together lest he return and sell it to an Israelite — it implies little by little there is nothing to fear; behold these matters are taken from the words of R' Yehuda Vein, and I heard and did not understand — for R' Yehuda Vein never intended thus, for there they detect the taste of wine, and how will an Israelite buy from a gentile? Rather his intent is not to sell everything together to a gentile except the value of the yeast in it — see R' Yehuda Vein; and you will find like my words; but his words too require study, for Tosafos chapter 3 of Avodah Zarah only wrote that one need not sell it together except the value of the yeast, and if he wishes he may sell it together; and so too in Yevamot page 81b and end of Avodah Zarah and in Zevachim page 72 — and this is not the place; but for a matter where there is concern lest he return and sell to an Israelite, there is no permission in little by little except for chametz, as stated; and in Avodah Zarah siman 112 seif 1, and so in the name of Rashdam in Yoreh De'ah siman 45 and R' Yehuda Vein in Yoreh De'ah siman 2, and this is its language: and even though there is double uncertainty that perhaps the Israelite will eat it before Pesach, since it is uncertainty made by one's hands it is forbidden — end of his words; and it requires study, for it deals with much chametz; and one can say thus: perhaps the gentile will sell to many Israelites, to each a little — it appears he holds like the Kesef Mishneh's explanation that on little it depends that the gentile will eat it himself; and in Avodah Zarah double uncertainty 57:
