In the Tur he wrote "and the chametz is not recognizable in them" — it implies that if the chametz is recognizable it is permitted to sell, for the buyer will see and not buy; Taz and Magen Avraham. But the Ran wrote that even if the chametz is recognizable it is forbidden to sell it to a gentile, for we are concerned lest he grind them and afterward sell to a Jew; see Chayei Adam (who rejected the reasoning of Magen Avraham) that since they do not buy flour from the market in a land, in a time of distress there is no concern — for that which is in siman 453 deals with matzah of the mitzvah specifically — see there; and he explains the intent of the Tur that it is a stringency, for even though the chametz is not recognizable and it is only a mere concern, nevertheless we are concerned; all the more so in certainty we are concerned for mishap:

To keep them. And if one transgressed and kept them and planted them in the ground and their growth sprouted — their growth is permitted, as stated above in siman 443:

And one informs him. But one does not rely on the proclamation; Shaarei Gibborim. And when one informs, even many are permitted to sell to a Jew; and even little by little — if he sells to a Jew he need not inform him — Chayei Adam see there. And Taz forbids selling much even when one informs, lest he forget to inform. And if he sold kav by kav it is permitted by force of uncertainty — perhaps he will not sell it to a Jew and perhaps he will eat them before Pesach — see there:

So that it be consumed. Even if you say that also that little amount he will sell to a Jew, nevertheless that little amount the Jew will eat before Pesach; and therefore it implies that orally at the fifth hour regarding a matter where it is impossible that it be consumed before Pesach it is forbidden to sell to a gentile even a little, lest he sell to a Jew; and such is the agreement of Magen Avraham. Nevertheless it appears to me that if grain became chametz for a Jew — much grain on the eve of Pesach and it is in a matter where it is impossible for him to sell to a gentile so that it be consumed before Pesach and there is great loss — it is permitted to sell to a gentile who knows him and recognizes him that he will not touch it at all but guard it for him until after Pesach, and he is permitted to say to him "until you take it," etc., as written siman 448 seif 4; and then there is no concern for anything — Chayei Adam see there. And see Magen Avraham who raised that selling little by little is forbidden in other prohibitions — only here regarding chametz, for there are many uncertainties, they were lenient; and see Chayei Adam:

To a Jew. And if the gentile said to the Jew even one word after he sold him wheat — "these wheats are for strawberries" — it is as written in Yoreh De'ah siman 17 that a gentile is not believed in what he said after he sold them and they left his possession; however, if he is trusted by him it is forbidden — Magen Avraham implies from his words that before he sold them, since they were in his possession, he is believed.
