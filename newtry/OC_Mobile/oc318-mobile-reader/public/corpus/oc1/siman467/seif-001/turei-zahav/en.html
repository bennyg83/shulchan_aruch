Or water fell upon them. In the Tur he wrote: and they have concern for leavening and it is not recognizable in them — end of his words; for if it were recognizable there is no concern, for the buyer will see the leavening:

And one informs him so that he will eat them, etc. In our Gemara it says except one — Rava: let us buy them measure by measure for Israelites so that we consume them before Pesach. And this seif is the language of the Rambam, and his version does not read to Israelites but to a gentile one sells little by little; but to an Israelite one may sell even a large amount once one informs him of the matter — so wrote the Maggid. But the Rosh and Tur have the reading to Israelites, and the author of the Ittur wrote: even if he did not inform the Israelite it is permitted as long as he can eat it before Pesach; and the Rambam wrote that one must inform him, and the Tur wrote, and it is reasonable like the author of the Ittur — from the fact that it is permitted only for a small amount, if one informs him even a large amount is also permitted, etc. Rashal wrote, and one might say: have our mouths been sealed so that we say to the Israelite that it is chametz — why should we rely on the permission that it will be consumed before Pesach not for the need of the matter, since we can inform him? Therefore it appears that it deals with informing, and nevertheless one may not sell more lest he forget and come to a stumbling block — end of his words. And I wonder greatly at the words of the rabbi, for the Rambam holds that one must inform him, and for him it is truly permitted even a large amount, as he states here explicitly; and the Tur's difficulty is according to his reading that reads to Israelites — therefore he questioned even a large amount; but according to what the Maggid said that the Rambam's version does not read to Israelites — the Tur's difficulty is resolved, for if so the large amount is also permitted to an Israelite, only that Rava teaches us that even for a gentile, where informing is not applicable, nevertheless one sells to him measure by measure; and it appears that one should be concerned for the Tur's reading, which is the reading of most books — namely to Israelites, and it is not permitted except with informing; and nevertheless one should not sell except a little, for the reason that when he sells a large amount and will rely on the informing there is concern lest he forget to inform one of the buyers and a stumbling block will come — unlike when he sells a little to a gentile, where there is a double uncertainty that perhaps he will not sell it to an Israelite; and if you say he will sell it to him, perhaps he will eat it before Pesach, as stated above — for practical halachah:
