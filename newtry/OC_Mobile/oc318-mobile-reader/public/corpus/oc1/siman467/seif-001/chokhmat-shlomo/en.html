Seif 1. "Grain that sank in a river, etc.; rather, he sells it to a Jew and informs him so that he will eat it, etc." NB: This implies that we rely on the notification. This requires study from an explicit Mishnah in Demai 3:3, where according to the first tanna we do not rely on notification. However, according to those who permit only a little at a time, this is resolved, for here there are two favorable factors. But according to those who permit even much at once through notification, this requires great study from an explicit Mishnah; even in rabbinic matters, according to Rabbi Yosei one does not rely on notification. See there. This requires very great study. It would be proper to elaborate on this, but the time does not allow; for now it requires study. Examine carefully.
There, in the same seif. "And if he sold it to a non-Jew before Pesach, he should sell a little to each one, etc." NB: See the Magen Avraham, who brought in the name of the Kesef Mishneh that if it is a little, he needs it for his household, while the Magen Avraham disagrees with him. In my humble opinion there is proof for the Kesef Mishneh from Tosafot at the beginning of the second chapter of Pesachim, in the sugya of Chizkiyah. Tosafot asked there how the Mishnah that one may send a thigh to a non-Jew can accord with Rabbi Yehudah, since Rabbi Yehudah holds that it is forbidden to give a gift to a non-Jew. Their words are seemingly astonishing: who told them that "one may send a thigh to a non-Jew" refers to a gift? Perhaps it refers to a sale, for the main novelty is that we are not concerned lest he sell it to a Jew with its gid hanasheh, and if so it could speak by way of sale. It is forced to say that the language "send" implies for free. But according to the Kesef Mishneh it is well resolved: if it were by way of sale, there would be no need to give the reason that its place is recognizable, for without that there is no concern that he will sell it to a Jew, since it is only a little and surely he needs it for his household. Why, then, do we need the reason that its place is recognizable? Perforce it speaks by way of gift, and Tosafot asked properly. This proves the words of the Kesef Mishneh and resolves the matter. While on this topic I will mention what is difficult to me in Tosafot there, s.v. veharei dam: they asked that we should say, since neveilah was permitted, it and its blood were permitted. What is the question? Regarding chelev and gid hanasheh, which exist in every animal, it is fitting to say that when neveilah was permitted, it and its chelev and gid were permitted. But regarding blood, the Gemara says that blood through which the soul departs is called blood, while blood through which the soul does not depart is not called blood. A neveilah may have died on its own and have no such blood; therefore it was permitted. But if there is blood through which the soul departs, perhaps it is forbidden in benefit. What, then, was their question? This requires study. Examine carefully. Regarding sugar in whose production liquor is placed, whether it must be sold for Pesach, see my work on Even HaEzer, mahadura 8, siman 82, in my responsum to the holy community of Tulchin, where I wrote that it need not be sold; see there, for I explained the reason. Examine carefully.
