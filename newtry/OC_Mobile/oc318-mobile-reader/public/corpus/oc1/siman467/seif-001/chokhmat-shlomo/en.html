Seif 1 — Grain that was submerged in a river, etc., rather one sells it to a Jew and informs him so that he eats it, etc. I would say: it implies that one relies on the admission, and uncertain from an explicit mishnah in tractate Demai chapter 3 mishnah 3 that the Tanna Kamma holds one does not rely on informing; however for the lenient ones only a little at a time works well, for here there are two factors for leniency; but to permit even much together through informing — very uncertain from an explicit mishnah, and in rabbinic law even according to R' Yosef one does not rely on informing — see there. And very uncertain, and it would be fit to expand on this but time does not allow — uncertain, end quote. (There in that seif) And if he sold it to a gentile before Pesach he sells a little to each one, etc. I would say: see in Magen Avraham that he brought in the name of the Kesef Mishneh that if it is little by little for his household he requires it, and Magen Avraham disagrees — see there. And it appears in my humble opinion there is proof for the Kesef Mishneh from the words of Tosafos beginning of chapter 2 of Pesachim in the passage of Chizkiyah — the Tosafos questioned how the mishnah "A person sends a thigh to a gentile" fits R' Yehuda who permits giving a gift to a gentile, etc.; and at first glance their words are astonishing — who told them that "a person sends a thigh to a gentile" deals with a gift? Perhaps it deals with a sale! Behold the main novelty is that we are not concerned perhaps he will sell it to a Jew together with its gid; and if so it holds that it deals with sale. And it is strained to say that "sends" implies for free; but according to the Kesef Mishneh it works well, for if it is through sale there is no need for the reason that its place is recognizable, for without this there is no concern he will sell it to a Jew since it is certainly little for his household; and why do I need the reason that its place is recognizable? And necessarily it deals with a gift, and they properly questioned the Tosafos, and it is proven like the words of the Kesef Mishneh, and it works well. And while I am on this I will mention what was difficult for me in the words of the Tosafos there, s.v. "And behold blood" — they questioned: let us say since the carcass was permitted its blood was permitted; and what is the question? Granted regarding milk and gid of any animal one can say when the carcass was permitted it and its milk and gid were permitted; but regarding blood, behold we say in the Gemara that blood from which the soul departs is called blood, and that from which the soul does not depart is not called blood; if so the carcass may have died by itself and has no blood, and if so it was permitted for that reason; but forever if there is blood from which the soul departs one can say it is forbidden in benefit — and what did they question? Uncertain, end quote.
