Grain that sank in the river, etc., rather he sells it to a Jew and informs him so that he may eat it, etc. Marginal note: it implies that we rely on the notification; and this requires further study from an explicit mishnah in Demai chapter 3 mishnah 3, where the first Tanna holds that we do not rely on notification; however according to those who permit only little by little it fits well, for here there are two leniencies; but according to those who permit even much together through notification, this requires much study from an explicit mishnah; and regarding a rabbinic [matter], even according to R' Yose we do not rely on notification; see there. And this requires great study. And it would be fitting to expand on this, only that time does not allow, and for now it requires further study; examine carefully. (There) in the aforementioned se'if. And if he sold it to a non-Jew before Pesach he sells little to each one, etc. Marginal note: see Magen Avraham who brought in the name of Kesef Mishneh that if it is little by little, it is what his household needs, and Magen Avraham disagrees with him; see there. And it appears in my humble opinion there is a proof for Kesef Mishneh from Tosafos at the beginning of the second chapter of Pesachim in the sugya of Chizkiyah, where Tosafos asked: how does the mishnah "One may send a thigh to a non-Jew" follow R' Yehuda, given that R' Yehuda holds it is forbidden to give a gift to a non-Jew, etc.? And ostensibly their words are astonishing: who told them that "One may send a thigh to a non-Jew" deals with a gift? Perhaps it deals with a sale: for the main novelty is that we are not concerned lest he sell it to a Jew with its gid; and if so he must hold it deals by way of sale. And it is forced to say that the language "sends" implies gratis. But according to Kesef Mishneh it fits well: if it were by way of sale he would not need to say the reason that its place is recognizable, for without that there is no concern that he will sell it to a Jew, since it is little and certainly needed for his household; and why do I need the reason that its place is recognizable? Necessarily it deals by way of gift, and Tosafos properly asked, and it is proven like Kesef Mishneh, and it fits well. And while on this I will mention what is difficult for me in Tosafos there s.v. "And behold blood," who asked: let us say since nevelah was permitted, its blood too was permitted; and what is the difficulty? Granted regarding chelev and the gid ha-nasheh, which are in every animal, it is fitting to say when nevelah was permitted, its chelev and gid were permitted. But regarding blood, behold we say in the Gemara that blood with which the soul leaves is called blood, and blood with which the soul does not leave is not called blood; and if so a nevelah may have died by itself with no blood in it, and therefore it was permitted; but forever, if there is blood with which the soul leaves, one can say it is forbidden for benefit; and what did they ask? And this requires further study; examine carefully. And regarding the law of sugar into which they put spirits when making it, whether one must sell it for Pesach: see my work on Even HaEzer, Mahadura Ches, siman 82, in my responsum to the community of Tolchin: there we wrote that one need not sell it; see there, for I explained the reason of the matter; examine carefully.

"And if he sold it to a non-Jew before Pesach, he should sell a little to each one, etc." NB: See the Magen Avraham, who brought in the name of the Kesef Mishneh that if it is a little, he needs it for his household, while the Magen Avraham disagrees with him. In my humble opinion there is proof for the Kesef Mishneh from Tosafot at the beginning of the second chapter of Pesachim, in the sugya of Chizkiyah. Tosafot asked there how the Mishnah that one may send a thigh to a non-Jew can accord with Rabbi Yehudah, since Rabbi Yehudah holds that it is forbidden to give a gift to a non-Jew. Their words are seemingly astonishing: who told them that "one may send a thigh to a non-Jew" refers to a gift? Perhaps it refers to a sale, for the main novelty is that we are not concerned lest he sell it to a Jew with its gid hanasheh, and if so it could speak by way of sale. It is forced to say that the language "send" implies for free. But according to the Kesef Mishneh it is well resolved: if it were by way of sale, there would be no need to give the reason that its place is recognizable, for without that there is no concern that he will sell it to a Jew, since it is only a little and surely he needs it for his household. Why, then, do we need the reason that its place is recognizable? Perforce it speaks by way of gift, and Tosafot asked properly. This proves the words of the Kesef Mishneh and resolves the matter. While on this topic I will mention what is difficult to me in Tosafot there, s.v. veharei dam: they asked that we should say, since neveilah was permitted, it and its blood were permitted. What is the question? Regarding chelev and gid hanasheh, which exist in every animal, it is fitting to say that when neveilah was permitted, it and its chelev and gid were permitted. But regarding blood, the Gemara says that blood through which the soul departs is called blood, while blood through which the soul does not depart is not called blood. A neveilah may have died on its own and have no such blood; therefore it was permitted. But if there is blood through which the soul departs, perhaps it is forbidden in benefit. What, then, was their question? This requires study. Examine carefully. Regarding sugar in whose production liquor is placed, whether it must be sold for Pesach, see my work on Even HaEzer, mahadura 8, siman 82, in my responsum to the holy community of Tulchin, where I wrote that it need not be sold; see there, for I explained the reason. Examine carefully.
