Split wheat kernel — see in the Taz that he wrote that according to the Hagah above in seif 9, even if it did not split open but only softened, this entire seif applies, and so is the opinion of the rest of the Acharonim (Bach, Pri Megadim, Peri Chadash, Ma'amar Mordechai, the Gra, Chayyei Adam — unlike the Shulchan Gavoah who tends to say that in roasting and salting the Rama agrees that splitting open is required, and so is the opinion of Kaf HaChayyim regarding salting); only that the Rama relied here on what he wrote in seif 9. And behold in great loss, such as when he is poor and it is a large important piece, or prevention of Yom Tov joy — it appears from the Gra that one may be lenient and rely on the Mechaber's opinion that peeling suffices, and so is the opinion of Peri Chadash to peel deeply and discard all around it and it is permitted in eating; and behold all the other Acharonim did not mention for practical purposes this opinion; however if it softened and did not split open, perhaps one may rely on this; and it is possible that also the Gra's opinion to be lenient in great loss is specifically when it softened; and see in responsum of the Rama siman 128, it is proven there that even when softened they are accustomed to forbid that entire piece, and perhaps in great loss he too agrees to be lenient — uncertain. And know further that in uncertainty whether it softened one should not be stringent at all (Acharonim):

But the essential ruling to forbid, etc. — behold above we wrote that for us even if it is not embedded, only softened, and the Acharonim wrote (Kaf HaChayyim siman 447 seif 6 and Shulchan Aruch HaRav) that specifically if we see the wheat kernel is moist; but if we see it did not become moist from the salt, such as that it is dry — even though it softened it does not forbid, for it does not release at all, for it is pure salted and impure bland; and similarly if chametz fell on salted meat, even on Pesach one may be lenient in a place of great loss as with other prohibitions:
