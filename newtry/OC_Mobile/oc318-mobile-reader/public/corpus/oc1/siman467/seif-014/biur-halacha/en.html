A cracked wheat kernel. See Taz, who wrote that according to the gloss above in se'if 9, even if it did not crack open but only softened, this whole se'if applies; and so is the view of other later authorities (Bach; Olas Shabbos; Peri Chadash; Maamar Mordechai; Shulchan Aruch HaRav; and Chayei Adam; and not like Shulchan Gavoah, who tends to say that regarding roasting and salting the Rama agrees that cracking open is required; and so is the view of Chok Yaakov regarding salting); only that the Rama relied here on what he wrote in se'if 9. And regarding major financial loss, for example if he is poor and it is a large important piece, or if it is deprivation of Yom Tov joy, it appears from Shulchan Aruch HaRav that one may be lenient and rely on the Mechaber's view that peeling suffices; and so is Peri Chadash's view, that it suffices to dig deep and discard all around it, and it is permitted to eat. And all the other later authorities did not mention this view for practice; but where it softened and did not crack open, it is possible to rely on this; and it is plausible that Shulchan Aruch HaRav too, who is lenient in major financial loss, intends specifically where it softened; and see responsa of the Rama, siman 28, where it is evident that even where it softened the custom is to forbid that whole piece; and it is possible that in major financial loss he too agrees to be lenient; and this requires further study. And know further that in a doubt whether it softened, one should not be stringent at all (later authorities).
But the main [law] is to forbid, etc. Above we wrote that for us, even if it is not cracked open but only softened [it forbids]; and the later authorities wrote (Chok Yaakov siman 447 s.k. 6, and other later authorities) that this is specifically if we see that the wheat kernel is moist; but if we see that it did not become moist from the salt, for example if it is dry, even though it softened it does not forbid, for it releases nothing at all, for it is [like] a clean salted [piece] and the impure unsalted; and likewise if chametz fell on salted meat, even on Pesach one may be lenient in a place of major financial loss as regarding other prohibitions.
That whole piece, etc. See Mishnah Berurah; and see Magen Avraham, who heard that a wheat kernel was found in a barrel of fish salted for Pesach, and the Gaon Moreinu HaRav R' Binyamin ordered to cast that row into the river and sell the rest to a non-Jew; and see Chemdas Moshe, who wondered at this, and it would have sufficed to forbid only that fish on which the wheat kernel was found (and Machatzis HaShekel likewise wondered at this; see there); and he answered that perhaps it was in such a way that there was doubt whether the wheat kernel touched the whole row; for if not so, we do not establish a presumption of prohibition from piece to piece, and we say "here it was found, here it was" (end quote). And according to the Taz's words that we wrote in Mishnah Berurah, even in that manner one should not forbid the whole row.
Through some peeling. See Mishnah Berurah: that is where there is room to doubt perhaps the wheat kernel touched them; and Olas Shabbos wrote that a piece about which there is doubt whether it touched at all that piece on which the wheat kernel was found needs not even peeling.
