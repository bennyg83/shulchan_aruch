Dried fruits. Shaarei Teshuvah and in Noda B'Yehuda Mahadura Tinyana siman 91 he wrote on what Magen Avraham wrote to permit even in an oven in which bread was baked, etc. — perhaps Magen Avraham did not pay attention to see that it is customary to scatter much flour under the bread, and perhaps some remained in the oven in its own form, etc. (in my humble opinion one can say that most of that flour remained stuck to the bread that was only at the time of its going from the peel to the oven, and if a little falls from there the heat of the oven makes it ash and in any case very charred until no taste of chametz remains when they put the fruits in the oven — uncertain); and in the land of Poland in all the provinces they are not accustomed to permit, neither in prunes called plumen nor in apples nor in dried pears — only what was dried in one's house and in one's oven for the festival of Pesach; and when he came to this country they were accustomed regarding fruits bought from those ovens standing in the gardens of the nobles where they do not bake there, but from those brought to sell in the market there is no permission; and in lands of the Ger they were accustomed to prohibition even from gardens — it is difficult to permit even though there is permission on that which is in the garden to make wine from them before Pesach; nevertheless one may not permit if there is concern that a breach will emerge to treat customs and prohibitions lightly — see there:
