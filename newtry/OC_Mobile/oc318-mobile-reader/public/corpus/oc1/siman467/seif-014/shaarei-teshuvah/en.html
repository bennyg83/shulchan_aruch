Dried [fruits]. See Baer Heitev; and in Noda BiYehuda, Mahadura Tinyana, siman 91, he wrote on what Magen Avraham wrote to permit even in an oven in which bread was baked, etc.: perhaps Magen Avraham did not notice that it is common to scatter much flour under the bread, and perhaps some remained intact in the oven, etc. (In my humble opinion one can say that most of that flour remained stuck to the bread, which is only at the moment it goes from the peel into the oven, and if a little falls from there the oven heat makes it ash, and in any case it is very charred until no taste of chametz remains when they put the fruits in the oven; and this requires further study.) And in the land of Poland in all the provinces they do not practice permission, neither for prunes called plums, nor for apples, nor for dried pears: only what was dried in one's house and oven for the festival of Pesach. And when he came to this country they practiced [leniency] for fruits bought from those ovens standing in the gardens of the nobles where they do not bake; but from those brought to sell in the market there is no permission. And in the lands of Hungary, where they practiced prohibition even from the gardens, it is hard to permit, even though there is permission regarding what is in the garden to make spirits from them before Pesach; nevertheless one should not permit if there is concern that damage will result to treat customs and prohibitions lightly; see there.
