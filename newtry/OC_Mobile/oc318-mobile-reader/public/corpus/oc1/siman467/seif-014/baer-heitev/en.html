Salting. Meaning while he was still salting it; but if it was found after he salted and rinsed, we say "now it fell" — for if it exists that it was on it beforehand it would fall off in rinsing when he rinsed it; and specifically when found on the chicken, but if found inside the chicken it certainly was there beforehand — Magen Avraham; and Chayei Adam wrote that in siman 447 I wrote that pure salted and impure rinsed — also on Pesach there is to permit; and here it deals where the wheat kernel was also salted with the chicken; and I saw meticulous ones careful on Pesach before scalding and salting to open the chicken well or cut it in two and check well lest a wheat kernel be found inside it. And it is written in Shachnahag: one who does so is diligent and rewarded:

All of them. Meaning when the wheat kernel is found on the chicken, for there is room to doubt that perhaps it touched all of them; but if found inside the chicken the others do not require peeling — Magen Avraham:

They are stringent. Because we estimate in all prohibitions salting in sixty — if so, also in chametz it forbids all of it (see Yoreh De'ah siman 105):

The essential, etc. Because he holds that we do not say in rabbinic prohibition that prohibition goes out from piece to piece through salting; and what they estimate in other prohibitions in sixty is because of a definitive matter that we need not estimate between fat and lean — we go with this even to leniency as written in Yoreh De'ah siman 105; if so, also in chametz sixty suffices. And further that specifically in a prohibition where greasiness applies do they estimate in sixty, but not in chametz prohibition where spreading does not apply. And further that where there is another side to permit we rely on his question that he holds chametz is nullified in sixty — and you have no greater side of permission than this, that many gaonim hold that taste does not spread in salting; and one may make a compromise to forbid that piece and no more; and one who wishes to be stringent may decide to burn that piece and keep the others. And if they were cooked without peeling they are permitted, for peeling is only a stringency; nevertheless it appears to me that the piece requires peeling, only the peel does not forbid the piece even though it was cooked with it. And specifically where there is no doubt that perhaps it touched the other pieces; but if there is doubt that it touched the other pieces — by law all are forbidden; and even though we know with certainty that it touched only one of them and we do not know which one it touched — by law all are forbidden and require peeling, for chametz on Pesach is not nullified in the majority; and if so, if it was cooked without peeling it forbids their mixture; for specifically where peeling is only a mere stringency the peel does not forbid the piece, but where peeling is required by law — if it was cooked without peeling it forbids the piece — Magen Avraham; and see what is written in Yoreh De'ah siman 91 s.k. 7; and see what is written above siman 447 s.k. 6; and Taz wrote: since we saw in this law dispute between early and later — Rashal in responsum siman 79 rules to burn everything, and so Darkei Yosef and Bach. And Mishna Berurah disagrees to rule like the lenient and is lenient in the other pieces even without peeling. And Rama in responsum siman 108 wrote: one who wishes to be stringent may decide to burn that piece and keep the others until after Pesach. Therefore it appears to me to practice practically in this manner: if a wheat kernel is found on salted meat dry without brine — one may permit keeping everything; only the place where it was found one peels, and the rest is permitted to keep until after Pesach. But if the wheat kernel is found inside the brine of meat or fish — then it is considered as cooked, for in a small measure it is like cooked in brine or in vinegar, as explained in Yoreh De'ah siman 105; and all the more so if it rested there from time to time — then its law is that everything resting in the brine is forbidden, for it is as if cooked in one pot and requires removal; but what rests above the brine one may also keep until after Pesach, for taste does not spread in brine upward, as explained in Yoreh De'ah siman 68. And therefore what touches the meat in the brine one peels, and the rest one keeps or does as Rama wrote at the end of the seif — to sell everything to a gentile — meaning what is not in the brine; for what is in the brine is forbidden to sell, for it is full chametz — end quote; and Chayei Adam agreed with him see there. And it is plain that all this is when the wheat softened as written s.k. 18 see there. It is written in responsum of Tzemach Tzedek siman 434: if a little beer remained in a vessel and they took water and rinsed meat in it after salting on Pesach — even though water nullifies the power of salt, nevertheless since it weakened it a little and absorbed somewhat, it has the law of a wheat kernel salted with chicken for each of the opinions in this seif see there; and see Chayei Adam s.k. 46. It is written in responsum of Maharam of Lublin siman 91: a wheat kernel placed in a barrel of salted cabbage — the entire barrel is forbidden, for soaked is like cooked see there. And Magen Avraham wrote: and it should be said that it deals where the brine rises above it; and if the brine does not rise above it, it has the law of salting.
