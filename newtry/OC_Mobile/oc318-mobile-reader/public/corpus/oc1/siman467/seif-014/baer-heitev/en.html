With rinsing. For cold without sauce according to all does not forbid; and even though with a chicken some dampness is impossible to avoid, nevertheless in cold it does not cause absorption according to all except through some soaking. (In responsa Tzemach Tzedek he was stringent where a little beer remained in a vessel and they took water in it and rinsed meat in it, that one should compare it to the law of a wheat kernel salted with a chicken; and Chok Yaakov cast doubt on this; see there.) Or through working dough, as above.
Salting. Meaning while the salt is still on it; but if it was found after it was salted and rinsed, we say it fell now, for if it had been on it beforehand it would have fallen off in the rinsing when they rinsed it; and this is specifically when found on the chicken, but if found inside the chicken it was certainly there beforehand (Magen Avraham). And Chok Yaakov wrote: in siman 447 I wrote that a clean salted [piece] and an unsalted impure [piece], even on Pesach one may permit; and here it deals with a case where the wheat kernel too was salted with the chicken; and I saw careful people on Pesach, before searing and salting, open the chicken well or cut it in two and check carefully so that no wheat kernel be found inside. And Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah wrote: one who does so is diligent and rewarded.
All of them. That is, when the wheat kernel is found on the chicken, for there is room to doubt perhaps it touched all of them; but if it is found inside the chicken, the others need no peeling (Magen Avraham).
Are stringent. Because we assess for all prohibitions in salting with sixty; if so, regarding chametz too it forbids the whole. (See Yoreh De'ah siman 105.)
The main [law], etc. Because he holds that we do not say regarding a rabbinic prohibition that prohibition goes from piece to piece through salting; and what we assess for other prohibitions with sixty is because it is a fixed rule so that we need not assess between fat and lean, and we follow this even for leniency, as written in Yoreh De'ah siman 105; if so, even regarding chametz sixty suffices. And further, specifically regarding a prohibition to which fatness applies do we assess with sixty, but not regarding the prohibition of chametz, to which spreading does not apply. And further, where there is another side to permit we rely on the She'iltos, who holds chametz is nullified in sixty; and you have no greater side to permit than this, that many Geonim hold that taste does not spread in salting; and one should make a compromise to forbid that piece and no more; and one who wishes to be stringent should rule to burn that piece and keep the others. And if they were cooked without peeling it is permitted, for peeling them is only a stringency; and nevertheless it appears to me that the piece needs peeling, only that the peel does not forbid the piece even though it was cooked with it. And this is specifically where there is no room to doubt perhaps it touched the other pieces; but if there is room to doubt that it touched the other pieces, by law all are forbidden; and even if we know with certainty that it touched only one of them and we do not know which one, by law all are forbidden and require peeling, for chametz on Pesach is not nullified in the majority; and if so, if it was cooked without peeling it forbids their mixture; for specifically where peeling is only a mere stringency the peel does not forbid the piece, but where peeling is required by law, if cooked without peeling it forbids the piece (Magen Avraham); and see what I wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 91 s.k. 7. And see what I wrote above in siman 447 s.k. 6. And Taz wrote: Since we saw in this law a dispute among early and later [authorities], for Maharshal in a responsum siman 79 rules to burn everything, and so is the view of the author of She'erit Yosef and so is the view of the Bach. And Mab disagrees to rule like the lenient ones, and is lenient for the other pieces even without peeling. And the Rama in a responsum siman 108 wrote: one who wishes to be stringent should rule to burn that piece and keep the others until after Pesach. Therefore it appears to me to practice thus: if a wheat kernel is found on dry salted meat without brine, one may permit to keep everything, only peel the place where it was found, and the rest one may keep until after Pesach. But if the wheat kernel is found inside the brine of meat or fish, then it is considered like cooked, for in a small measure, pickled in brine or vinegar is like cooked, as explained in Yoreh De'ah siman 105; and all the more so if it rested there a full day. Then the law of all that rests inside the brine is to forbid, for it is as if cooked in one pot, and it requires destruction; but what rests above the brine one should also keep until after Pesach, for there is no spreading upward in brine, as explained in Yoreh De'ah siman 68. And therefore what touches the meat inside the brine one peels, and the rest one keeps, or one does as the Rama wrote at the end: sell everything to a non-Jew, meaning what is not inside the brine; for what is inside the brine is forbidden to sell, for it is full chametz (end quote); and Chok Yaakov agreed with him; see there. And it is plain that all this is where the wheat kernel softened, as above s.k. 18; see there. He wrote in responsa Tzemach Tzedek siman 74: if a little beer remained in a vessel and they took water in it and rinsed meat in it after salting on Pesach, even though water weakens the power of the salt, nevertheless while it is weakening it absorbs some, and it has the law of a wheat kernel salted with a chicken according to each of the views in this se'if; see there; and see Chok Yaakov s.k. 46. He wrote in responsa Maharam of Lublin siman 91: a wheat kernel lying in a barrel of salted cabbage forbids the whole barrel, for pickled is like cooked; see there. And Magen Avraham wrote: and one must say it deals with brine rising over it; and if the brine does not rise over it it has the law of salting. And likewise I heard that a wheat kernel was found in a barrel of fish salted for Pesach, and the Gaon Moreinu HaRav R' Binyamin, av beis din of the community of Posen, ordered to cast that row into the river and sell the rest to a non-Jew; and see Magen Avraham.
