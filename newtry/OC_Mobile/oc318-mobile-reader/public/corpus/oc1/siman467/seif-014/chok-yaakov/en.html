Rinsing is sufficient. A mere rinse suffices, because cold without liquid does not forbid according to anyone, as the Magen Avraham wrote. Although a chicken cannot be entirely without some moisture, in any event cold does not cause absorption according to anyone unless by some soaking or by working with dough and the like. The Tzemach Tzedek, responsum 30, wrote that if a little remained from the wheat on the chicken, the law follows the circumstances explained there.
After salting. The Magen Avraham explained that this means while it is still in its salt. But if it was found after it had been salted and rinsed, we say it fell only now, for if it had been there previously, it would have fallen off during rinsing. This applies when it was found on the chicken. If it was found inside the chicken, it was certainly there earlier. See Niddah 3.
And to permit the others by peeling. If he transgressed and cooked them without peeling, see above in siman 447, seif katan 9. See the Magen Avraham, who wrote various distinctions in this law. The main point is as I wrote there. As to the basic law, the later responsa contain many opinions: some ruled to burn everything, and some later authorities permit in cases of loss, each according to the details.
