And there are those who are stringent, etc. Because we assess in all prohibitions in salting at sixty, as in Yoreh De'ah siman 105, because we are not expert between weak prohibition and fat — if so also in chametz it forbids entirely:

But the essential ruling, etc. Because he holds we do not say in rabbinic prohibition that prohibition goes from piece to piece through salting; and what they assess in other prohibitions at sixty is because of the matter that ceased, that we need not assess between fat and weak — with this they are lenient, as in Yoreh De'ah siman 105; if so also in chametz sixty suffices; and further, specifically in a prohibition where greasiness applies do they assess at sixty, but not in chametz where spreading does not apply; and further, where there is another side to permit, they rely on the question that chametz is nullified in sixty — you have no greater side of permission than that many geonim hold taste does not spread in salting; and one should make a compromise to forbid that piece, as in seif 11 to forbid that matzah and no more; and one who wishes to be stringent should rule to burn that piece and keep the others resting with it in the barrel; and further one may permit other pieces through benefit by redemption, as in siman 447 seif 1 — end of Rama in responsum siman 128; and so the Mishna Berurah siman 69; and he ruled if the others were cooked without peeling they are permitted, for it is only a stringency to peel them, as in Yoreh De'ah siman 91 — end of his words; and nevertheless it appears to me the piece requires peeling, only the peel does not forbid the piece even if cooked with it; and so is his intent in Yoreh De'ah there, as stated; and examine there that not like the later ones wrote. Also what the Mishna Berurah wrote here even without peeling is permitted — it appears specifically where there is no concern perhaps it touched the other pieces; but if there is concern perhaps it touched the other pieces, by law all are forbidden, as in Shulchan Aruch in Yoreh De'ah siman 105; and even if they know certainly it touched only one and we do not know which — by law all are forbidden and require peeling, for chametz on Pesach is not nullified in the majority, as in siman 447 seif 9; and if so if cooked without peeling it forbids in its mixture, as in Yoreh De'ah siman 69 and siman 98 — see there; specifically in the lower law the lenient prevails, for it is only a mere stringency, for at first they said in the Gemara peeling is not required, only they are concerned lest it absorbed a crumb — therefore the peel does not forbid the piece; but where peeling is required by law, if cooked without peeling it forbids the piece; and with this all the later ones' complaints on the Rama are resolved; and so in the Drisha. And it is written in responsum of Maharam of Lublin: a wheat kernel resting in a barrel of salted cabbage — the entire barrel is forbidden, for soaked is like cooked — end of his words; and it should read it deals where the brine rises over it; and if the brine does not rise over it, it has the law of salting, as in the Rama's responsum; and see siman 447 seif 5; and so I heard a wheat kernel was found in a barrel of fish salted for that purpose, and the Gaon R' Binyamin Av Beit Din of the holy community of Pozna commanded to throw that row into the river and the rest to sell to a gentile; and Mordechai Margaliot further wrote: before Pesach a full crop of wheat kernels was found in salted brine — even though the Rama ruled in Yoreh De'ah siman 105 that chametz on Pesach forbids only the amount of peeling, nevertheless I was not satisfied to permit that chicken, since there was much chametz there, etc. — end of his words; and the Bach's view is like the stringent in salting on Pesach, and so in Shiltei Gibborim; and in Darkei Moshe he wrote he heard in Krakow some are stringent and therefore did not wish to rule on this neither prohibition nor permission — end of his words; and the Mishna Berurah wrote he was in Krakow and did not hear they are stringent on this — therefore; and it is possible the Rama leaned toward that elder author of Shiltei Gibborim:
