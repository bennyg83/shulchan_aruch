Rinsing is sufficient. Cold contact without liquid does not prohibit according to any opinion. See seif 11.
After salting. Meaning, while the salt is still upon it. If the wheat is found only after the chicken was salted and rinsed, we say that it fell only now, for had it been there previously, it would have fallen off during the rinsing in water. This applies specifically when it is found upon the chicken. If it is found inside the chicken, it was certainly there earlier. See Niddah 3a regarding a basket with a bottom.
He peels all of them. One must say that the wheat was found upon the chicken, so there is doubt whether it touched all the chickens. If it was found inside one chicken, the others do not require peeling.
And some are stringent, etc. Regarding all prohibitions transmitted through salting, we require sixty, as written in Yoreh Deah siman 105, because we are not expert in distinguishing between a lean and fatty prohibition. Accordingly, chametz likewise prohibits the entire item.
But the primary ruling, etc. This opinion holds that with a D'Rabbanan prohibition we do not say that a prohibition passes from one piece to another through salting. The requirement of sixty for other prohibitions was adopted as a fixed rule so that we need not distinguish between lean and fatty substances, and we apply that rule even leniently, as written in Yoreh Deah siman 105. Accordingly, even with chametz, sixty is sufficient. Moreover, only prohibitions involving fat are measured in sixty, but not chametz, to which diffusion of fat does not apply. Further, where another basis for leniency exists, we rely upon Sheiltos, who holds that chametz is nullified in sixty. No greater basis for leniency exists than the fact that many Geonim hold that flavor does not spread through salting. A compromise should therefore be made to forbid only that piece, just as in seif 11 only that matzah is forbidden. One who wishes to be stringent should rule that the piece be burned while the other pieces lying with it in the barrel may be retained. The other pieces may also be permitted for benefit by redeeming the value of the chametz, as written in siman 447:1. Rama writes this in responsum 28, and Masas Binyamin rules likewise in siman 69. He further rules that if the other pieces were cooked without peeling, they remain permitted, because peeling them is only a stringency, as written in Yoreh Deah siman 91. Nevertheless, it appears to me that the piece itself requires peeling, but that the peel does not prohibit the piece even if it was cooked together with it. This is also his intent in Yoreh Deah there. Examine the matter carefully, unlike the later authorities. Masas Binyamin's statement that here the others are permitted even without peeling applies, in my opinion, only where there is no doubt that the wheat touched the other pieces. If there is such a doubt, all are forbidden by law, as written in Shulchan Aruch, Yoreh Deah siman 105. Even if it is certain that the wheat touched only one but we do not know which one, all are forbidden by law and require peeling, because chametz on Pesach is not nullified by a majority, as written in siman 447:9. If they were then cooked without peeling, they prohibit the mixture, as written in Yoreh Deah simanim 69 and 98. Only regarding the rule that the lower item prevails is peeling treated as a mere stringency, since the Gemara initially says that peeling is unnecessary and we are concerned only that a small amount may have been absorbed. The peel therefore does not prohibit the entire piece. Where peeling is required by law, however, if the piece was cooked without peeling, it prohibits the entire piece. This resolves all the objections of the later authorities to Rama and is likewise implied by Derishah. Maharam Lublin writes in a responsum that if a wheat kernel lies in a barrel of salted cabbage, the entire barrel is forbidden because soaking is like cooking. One must say that the brine rose above the cabbage. If the brine did not cover it, the case follows the law of salting, as explained above from Rama's responsum. See what I wrote in siman 447:5. I likewise heard of a wheat kernel found in a barrel of fish salted for Pesach, and the Gaon Rabbi Binyamin, head of the beis din of Poznan, ordered that the row containing it be cast into the river and the remainder sold to a non-Jew. Maharam further writes that before Pesach a crop filled with wheat was found among salted chickens. Although Rama rules in Yoreh Deah siman 105 that chametz on Pesach prohibits through salting only the amount requiring peeling, I did not have the courage to permit that chicken because a large quantity of chametz was present. Bach follows the stringent opinion regarding salting on Pesach, and Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah rules likewise. Darchei Moshe writes that he heard that some in Krakow were stringent and therefore did not wish to rule either prohibition or permission. Masas Binyamin writes that he was in Krakow and never heard that they were stringent. Perhaps Rama referred to the elderly author of Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah.
