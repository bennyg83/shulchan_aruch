And certainly this is true, as we mentioned in siman 461 seif 4 regarding adhesion to matzah — only that case is different, for there is nothing that spreads entirely, unlike when there is meat that spreads — we are concerned lest it went and seasoned the wheat kernel and it returns to spread in the meat, as in Yoreh De'ah siman 98 seif 5; and even a thin piece of prohibition and the permitted of its kind, etc. — we can say here too with matzah and meat; but I already cast a stone on this passage in Yoreh De'ah there. And there are those who are stringent. The reason we hold in all prohibitions salting is like cooking — so too regarding chametz; and the Rama decided to be lenient in the other pieces, since also in other prohibitions it is only a stringency of the custom that peeling suffices in salting according to most poskim; and therefore on chametz on Pesach, which is rabbinic prohibition in any amount, one may be lenient; and it would have been fit to be lenient even in that piece that peeling should suffice; only since we see the wheat kernel and it rests on it, one should be stringent on it like the stringent ones; and there is an error in what he wrote, etc. — and it should read to sell everything to a gentile, even though we do not practice thus in other chametz mixtures, as in siman 447; and he means that here in salting it is more lenient than other chametz mixtures mentioned in siman 447. And for the law we saw dispute among the early ones in the Mordechai chapter 20 whether to forbid in salting like cooking; and the later ones — Rashal in responsum siman 79 — rule to burn everything, and so too the author of She'erit Yosef and the author of the Or Zarua. And the Mishna Berurah disagrees with him to rule like the lenient ones, and he is lenient in other pieces even without peeling; and the Rama in responsum siman 128 wrote to permit other pieces, and this is its language: even one who wishes to be stringent should rule as he heard some sages instruct to burn that piece and keep the others after Pesach; and in my humble opinion one should practice thus: if a wheat kernel is found on salted dry meat without brine, one may permit keeping everything this way — where it was found one peels, and the rest one keeps, since there is only uncertainty of contact and one does not eat from it on Pesach but keeps it for after Pesach; and this certainly has no stumbling block, since for many great early and later authorities it is eaten, and we do not keep it; but if the wheat kernel is found inside the brine of meat or fish, then it is considered like cooked, and all the more so if it rested there from the eve — its law is as all that rests in the brine is as if cooked in one pot and all is forbidden and requires destruction, as in Yoreh De'ah siman 105 regarding soaked in brine or vinegar that in a small measure it is like cooked; but what rests above the brine one may also keep until after Pesach, for spreading does not go upward in brine, as explained in Yoreh De'ah seif 9 that there is no spreading upward in brine; and therefore what touches the meat in the brine one peels, and the rest one keeps or does as the Rama wrote at the end to sell everything to a gentile; and it appears here too one should peel the place of the wheat kernel first, for this is full chametz, as appears to me; and this is when it is not in the brine, but if it is in brine it is forbidden to sell, for it is full chametz, and one should do as written nearby.
