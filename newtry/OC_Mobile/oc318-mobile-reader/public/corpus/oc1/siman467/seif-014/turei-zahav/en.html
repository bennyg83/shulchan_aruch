One peels all of them. In a responsum of Mab, siman 69, he disagrees with this, that peeling is not required for the rest; for if because of concern lest they touched the wheat kernel itself, we do not establish a presumption of prohibition: rather, here it was found, here it was; and he expanded somewhat on this. And it is astonishing on him that he did not look in Shulchan Aruch Yoreh De'ah siman 105 se'if 9, which wrote there: and if it is unknown whether the fat touched all of them, all of them, etc. He further raised a difficulty: here peeling is not required at all, since the wheat kernel does not spread at all like a lean item; and he expanded that we are not stringent regarding chametz on Pesach to say of it that it spreads. And certainly this is true, as we noted in siman 461 se'if 4 regarding adhesion to matzah; only that case is different, for there there is nothing that spreads entirely, unlike when there is meat that spreads: we are concerned lest it went and fattened the wheat kernel and it returns to spread in the meat, as written in Yoreh De'ah siman 98 se'if 5; and even if the forbidden piece is lean and the permitted is fatty, etc.; we can say the same here regarding matzah with meat; but I already cast an axe on that passage in Yoreh De'ah there. And I noted Maharshal's words that in salting at least we do not say this reasoning; and nevertheless peeling is required even for the rest because of concern of contact, as written; and in that responsum he noted there that wherever peeling is required and it is not possible, it is permitted like the view of Riva. And I brought many bundled proofs in Yoreh De'ah siman 91 that we do not hold like Riva; rather one needs sixty against the peel.
And there are those who are stringent. The reason is that we hold regarding all prohibitions that salted is like cooked; so too regarding chametz; and the Rama decided to be lenient for the other pieces, since even regarding other prohibitions it is only a stringency of the custom that peeling suffices in salting according to most poskim; and therefore regarding chametz on Pesach, which is a rabbinic prohibition of any amount, one should be lenient; and it would have been fitting to be lenient even for that piece, that peeling should suffice; only since we see the wheat kernel lying on it, one should be stringent on it like the stringent ones; and there is a scribal error in what he wrote "as written," etc., and it should read: to sell everything to a non-Jew, even though we do not practice thus regarding other chametz mixtures, as written in siman 447; meaning that here in salting it is more lenient than other chametz mixtures mentioned in siman 447. And regarding practical law we saw a dispute among the early authorities in the Mordechai chapter "Kol Sha'ah" whether to forbid in salting like cooking; and among the later authorities, Maharshal in a responsum siman 79 rules to burn everything, and so the author of She'erit Yosef, and so my master and father-in-law of blessed memory. And Mab disagrees with him to rule like the lenient ones, and he is lenient for the other pieces even without peeling; and the Rama in a responsum siman 108 wrote to permit other pieces, and this is his language: even one who wishes to be stringent should rule as I heard some sages instruct: burn that piece and keep the others after Pesach; and in my humble opinion one should practice thus: if a wheat kernel is found on dry salted meat without brine, one may permit to keep everything this way: peel the place of finding, and keep the rest, since there is only a doubt of contact, and one should not eat from it on Pesach but keep it for after Pesach; and this certainly has no pitfall, since according to many great early and later authorities they eat it, and we do not keep it; but if the wheat kernel is found inside the brine of meat or fish, then it is considered like cooked, and all the more so if it rested there a full day, and its law is that all that rests inside the brine is as if cooked in one pot and all is forbidden and requires destruction, as written in Yoreh De'ah siman 105 regarding pickled in brine or vinegar, that in a small measure it is like cooked; but what rests above the brine one should also keep until after Pesach, for there is no spreading upward in brine, as explained in Yoreh De'ah se'if 9 that there is no spreading upward in brine; and therefore what touches the meat inside the brine one peels, and the rest one keeps, or one does as the Rama wrote at the end: sell everything to a non-Jew; and it appears that here too one should first peel the place of the wheat kernel, for this is full chametz, as appears to me; and this is when it is not inside the brine; but if it is in brine it is forbidden to sell, for it is full chametz, and one should do as written nearby. And what the Shulchan Aruch wrote in this se'if, "if a cracked wheat kernel is found": it is plain that for us the law is thus if it softened, even though it is not cracked open, as above.
