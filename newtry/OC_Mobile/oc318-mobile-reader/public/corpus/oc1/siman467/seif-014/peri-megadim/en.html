It is forbidden. See the Taz, the Magen Avraham, and the Chok Yaakov. From time to time we establish prohibition, but from place to place we do not establish prohibition. In the Tur's incident of scalding, Rabbeinu Raavan forbade, though he did not mention a split kernel. The Mechaber follows his own view in seif 9, that specifically a split kernel forbids, whereas with merely softened wheat even in a primary vessel the minute mixture is permitted. The phrase 'before boiling' means before they boiled the chicken, not merely cold. The Taz's words can be aligned with the Magen Avraham by distinguishing time from place. His discussion of double doubt must be understood carefully, for a whole split kernel is not considered digested, and if split it is definite chametz according to our view.
Behold. The Mechaber in seif 9 was lenient with merely softened wheat in a minute mixture because a single doubt in a D'Rabbanan matter is lenient, while here he was stringent with a single doubt. One can answer that from time to time is worse, since we establish it according to its present state where there is no clear prior presumption of permission. See Yoreh De'ah 105 regarding doubtful soaking in a D'Rabbanan matter, and siman 111 in the Shach regarding whether we say 'I say' in a minute mixture. According to this, the same law applies to other meat as well.
Furthermore. One must consider that in seif 9 the Mechaber was lenient with softened wheat because he rules like Shmuel, and even in seif 2 it is a doubt only when the kernels are themselves present, whereas a minute mixture is D'Rabbanan. Even according to the Rama, who forbids because the halacha follows Mar Ukva, if it did not soften we are expert and it remains a doubt. The broader issue is whether a matter that has a Torah-level prohibition from one side and a D'Rabbanan doubt from another side is treated stringently, as discussed in Yoreh De'ah 110 and by the later authorities. It may depend on whether the Torah doubt was not known until the D'Rabbanan doubt arose. The matter requires study, and with Hashem's help will be clarified further.
