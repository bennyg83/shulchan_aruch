(68) Split wheat kernel — see in Biur Halacha that for us this seif applies even when only softened:

(69) Rinsing suffices — for cold without gravy, according to all it does not forbid; and even if the chicken was rinsed, nevertheless since the wheat kernel was not steeped in water it does not forbid; and nevertheless rinsing is required, and one may not rely on the rinsing they rinse after salting:

(70) Peeling suffices — and nevertheless peeling is required even though it is cold, for we rule salting is like boiling and it absorbs the moisture of the wheat kernel into the meat to the depth of peeling. And all this when found while still salting on it; but if found after it was salted and rinsed, we say it fell now, and rinsing alone suffices; for if it had been on it previously it would have fallen during rinsing when they rinsed the meat in water. And specifically when found on the chicken externally; but if found inside the chicken, presumably it was previously, even at salting time, and what prevented it from falling during rinsing was that the chicken's edges held it back:

(71) And there are those who are stringent to say one deepens — meaning, not like the first view that it suffices to peel the meat around the place of the wheat kernel alone; rather one deepens all around it and cuts; and the reason is since on Pesach its prohibition is in any amount, we say something spreads in all the surroundings of the place of the wheat kernel:

(72) He peels all of them — and it deals when the wheat kernel is found on the chicken, for there is room to suspect lest the wheat kernel touched all of them; but if found inside the chicken, the others do not require peeling:

(73) And there are those who are stringent to forbid all, etc. — because we are stringent to forbid in all prohibitions in salting in sixty [because we are not expert between weak prohibition and fat prohibition] — evidently it spread throughout; if so, automatically in chametz whose prohibition is in any amount, it forbids all of them:

(74) But the essential view — because those hold we do not say in any-amount prohibition which is rabbinic that it goes out from piece to piece through salting; in chametz which is weak, they were stringent only at the measure of sixty to forbid from piece to piece even in weak, and not in any amount. And further that specifically in a prohibition where fat applies do we estimate weak even at sixty, because we are not expert between weak and fat, and we say it spreads until sixty — unlike chametz where spreading is not applicable at all, and our judgment is if we are stringent to forbid that entire piece:

(75) Through some peeling — and this is in a manner where there is room to suspect lest the wheat kernel touched them too, as above in note 72; and even if we know for certain it touched only one of them and we do not know which one it touched — all require peeling, for chametz on Pesach is not nullified in the majority, as written siman 447 seif 9; and if they were cooked without peeling, their mixture is forbidden:

(76) To sell all, etc. — see in Acharonim whose view is that the Rama refers only to the other pieces; but that piece on which the wheat kernel was found — one may not permit to sell according to the gloss above in note 10; and from other Acharonim it is implied that here where it is only through salting we are lenient to sell even that piece, only that one must peel first from the meat at the place where the wheat kernel was found:

(77) To a gentile — see in the Taz who wrote he ruled practically that the same applies: permitted to retain the other pieces until after Pesach, since they are only uncertainty of contact; and also that piece one may retain, only that in it one must peel first the place of the wheat kernel; and from the words of Magen Avraham it is implied that his view is like the Rama's view that that piece is forbidden to retain; and it appears that when softened and did not split open, one may rely on the words of the Taz; and Kaf HaChayim agrees to the words of the Taz:

(78) As written siman 447, etc. — it is a textual error and should read: even though we are not accustomed thus in other chametz mixtures, as written siman 447 seif 1; and its explanation is that there it is explained that in absorption of any-amount chametz one may not sell to a non-Jew even after redeeming the value of the chametz; here in salting we are lenient in this. And know that all this where the Rama is lenient regarding the other pieces through peeling or through selling to a non-Jew — specifically when the wheat kernel is found on salted meat at the time of salting, or after salting and rinsing and they salted again for the pot and they found the wheat kernel on the meat without brine; but if the wheat kernel is found inside the brine, then everything is considered cooked, for in a slight measure it is like cooked through brine — then everything resting in the brine must be forbidden and requires peeling; but what rests above the brine one does as the Rama wrote, that in brine we do not say it spreads upward or that one retains until after Pesach — as we wrote above according to the Taz; and likewise if a wheat kernel is placed in a barrel of salted cabbage, the entire barrel is forbidden, for pickled is like cooked; and specifically when the brine rises upon it; but if the brine does not rise upon it, it is ordinary salting law, and its law is as above in notes 76 and 77. If a little beer remained in a vessel and they took water into it and rinsed other meat in it after its salting on Pesach, the view of Kaf HaChayim is to permit the meat even without peeling from essential law, for although the water was forbidden because of the beer mixed in them, nevertheless the meat did not absorb anything from those waters, since it is cold; and even though the meat was salted, nevertheless at the time they rinsed it, and salting is like boiling — in any case it did not absorb from the water, because the water with which they rinsed nullifies the power of the salt and it has no power to absorb water into the meat; however because of the stringency of chametz on Pesach, one should peel a little of the meat where those waters touched it.
