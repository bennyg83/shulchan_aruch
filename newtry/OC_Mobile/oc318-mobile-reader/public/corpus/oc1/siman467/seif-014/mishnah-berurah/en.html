(68) A cracked wheat kernel. See Biur Halacha that for us this se'if applies even when only softened.
(69) Rinsing suffices. For cold without sauce according to all does not forbid; and even if the chicken was rinsed, nevertheless since the wheat kernel was not soaking in water it does not forbid; and nevertheless rinsing is required, and one should not rely on the rinsing after salting.
(70) Peeling suffices. And nevertheless peeling is required even though it is cold, for we hold salted is like hot, and the moisture of the wheat kernel is absorbed into the meat enough for peeling. And nevertheless all this is when found while salt is still on it; but if found after it was salted and rinsed, we say it fell now and mere rinsing suffices, for if it had been on it beforehand it would have fallen off in the rinsing when they rinsed the meat in water; and specifically when found on the chicken from outside; but if found inside the chicken presumably it was there beforehand even at the time of salting, and the reason it did not fall in rinsing is that the bottom of the chicken held it back.
(71) And there are those who are stringent to say one digs deep. Meaning not like the first view that it suffices to peel the meat only opposite the place of the wheat kernel; rather one digs deep all around it and cuts; and the reason is that since on Pesach its prohibition is in any amount, we say any amount spreads all around the place of the wheat kernel.
(72) One peels all of them. And it deals with when the wheat kernel is found on the chicken, for there is room to doubt perhaps the wheat kernel touched all of them; but if found inside the chicken the others need no peeling.
(73) And there are those who are stringent to forbid all, etc. Because we are stringent to forbid regarding all prohibitions in salting with sixty (because we are not experts between a lean prohibition and a fatty one); thus it spread throughout; if so automatically regarding chametz whose prohibition is in any amount it also forbids all of them.
(74) But the main [law]. Because he holds that we do not say regarding a prohibition of any amount which is rabbinic that it goes from piece to piece through salting regarding chametz which is lean; and only with a measure of sixty were they stringent to forbid from piece to piece even regarding lean, and not in any amount. And further, specifically regarding a prohibition to which fatness applies do we assess regarding lean even with sixty because we are not experts between lean and fatty, and we say it spreads up to sixty; which is not so regarding the prohibition of chametz to which spreading does not apply at all; and it suffices if we are stringent to forbid that whole piece.
(75) Through some peeling. And that is in a manner where there is room to doubt perhaps the wheat kernel also touched them, as above in s.k. 72; and even if we know with certainty that it touched only one of them and we do not know which one, all require peeling, for chametz on Pesach is not nullified in the majority as written in siman 447 se'if 9; and if cooked without peeling it forbids their mixture.
(76) To sell everything, etc. See Eliyah Rabbah whose view is that the Rama refers only to the other pieces, but that piece on which the wheat kernel was found one may not permit to sell according to the gloss above in s.k. 10; and from other later authorities it implies that here, where it is only through salting, we are lenient to sell even that piece, only that he should first peel from the meat at the place where the wheat kernel was found on it.
(77) To a non-Jew. See Taz who wrote that he instructed for practice that the same applies that one may keep the other pieces until after Pesach since they are only a doubt of contact; and also that piece he may keep, only that for it he must first peel the place of the wheat kernel; and from Magen Avraham's words it implies his view is like the Rama that that piece is forbidden to keep; and it appears that when softened and not cracked open one may rely on Taz's words; and so Chok Yaakov agrees with Taz's words.
(78) As written in siman 447, etc. It is a scribal error, and it should read: even though we do not practice thus regarding other chametz mixtures as written in siman 447 se'if 1; and its meaning is that there it is explained that regarding absorption of any amount of chametz one cannot sell to a non-Jew even after redeeming the value of the chametz; here in salting we are lenient in this. And know that all this, that the Rama is lenient for the other pieces through peeling or through sale to a non-Jew, is specifically when the wheat kernel is found on salted meat at the time of salting or after salting and rinsing and they salted a second time for the pot and found the wheat kernel on the meat without brine; but if the wheat kernel is found inside the brine then everything is considered like cooked, for in a small measure it is pickled through brine, and then all that rests in the brine one must forbid and it requires destruction; but what rests above the brine one should do as the Rama wrote, for in brine we do not say it spreads upward, or keep it until after Pesach as we wrote above according to Taz's view. And likewise if a wheat kernel is found lying in a barrel of salted cabbage the whole barrel is forbidden, for pickled is like cooked; and specifically when the brine rises over it; for if the brine does not rise over it it has merely the law of salting, and its law is as above in s.k. 76 and 77. If a little beer remained in a vessel and they took water in it and rinsed meat in it after its salting on Pesach: Chok Yaakov's view is that one may permit the meat even without peeling according to the basic law, for even though the water was forbidden because of the beer mixed into it, nevertheless the meat absorbed nothing from this water since it is cold; and even though the meat was still salted when they rinsed and salted is like hot: nevertheless it absorbed nothing from the water because the water they rinsed with nullifies the power of the salt and it has no power to cause the water to be absorbed into the meat; but because of the stringency of chametz on Pesach one should peel a little of the meat where this water touched it; and Chayei Adam's view is that by law the meat needs peeling since there are cracks in it, and even if the meat was not salted at all.
