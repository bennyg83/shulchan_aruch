(69) Rinsing suffices — for cold without gravy, according to all it does not forbid; and even if the chicken was rinsed, nevertheless since the wheat kernel was not steeped in water it does not forbid; and nevertheless rinsing is required, and one may not rely on the rinsing they rinse after salting:

(70) Peeling suffices — and nevertheless peeling is required even though it is cold, for we rule salting is like boiling and it absorbs the moisture of the wheat kernel into the meat to the depth of peeling. And all this when found while still salting on it; but if found after it was salted and rinsed, we say it fell now, and rinsing alone suffices; for if it had been on it previously it would have fallen during rinsing when they rinsed the meat in water. And specifically when found on the chicken externally; but if found inside the chicken, presumably it was previously, even at salting time, and what prevented it from falling during rinsing was that the chicken's edges held it back:

(71) And there are those who are stringent to say one deepens — meaning, not like the first view that it suffices to peel the meat around the place of the wheat kernel alone; rather one deepens all around it and cuts; and the reason is since on Pesach its prohibition is in any amount, we say something spreads in all the surroundings of the place of the wheat kernel:

(72) He peels all of them — and it deals when the wheat kernel is found on the chicken, for there is room to suspect lest the wheat kernel touched all of them; but if found inside the chicken, the others do not require peeling:

(73) And there are those who are stringent to forbid all, etc. — because we are stringent to forbid in all prohibitions in salting in sixty [because we are not expert between weak prohibition and fat prohibition] — evidently it spread throughout; if so, automatically in chametz whose prohibition is in any amount, it forbids all of them:

(74) But the essential view — because those hold we do not say in any-amount prohibition which is rabbinic that it goes out from piece to piece through salting; in chametz which is weak, they were stringent only at the measure of sixty to forbid from piece to piece even in weak, and not in any amount. And further that specifically in a prohibition where fat applies do we estimate weak even at sixty, because we are not expert between weak and fat, and we say it spreads until sixty — unlike chametz where spreading is not applicable at all, and our judgment is if we are stringent to forbid that entire piece:

(75) Through some peeling — and this is in a manner where there is room to suspect lest the wheat kernel touched them too, as above in note 72; and even if we know for certain it touched only one of them and we do not know which one it touched — all require peeling, for chametz on Pesach is not nullified in the majority, as written siman 447 seif 9; and if they were cooked without peeling, their mixture is forbidden:
