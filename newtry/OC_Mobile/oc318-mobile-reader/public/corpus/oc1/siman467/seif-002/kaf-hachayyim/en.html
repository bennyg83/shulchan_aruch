12) [Se'if 2] This grain that was submerged in a river, etc. Beis Yosef cites authorities who distinguish between wheat and barley. Barley must split, whereas wheat, which naturally has a crease, is considered split. He concludes, however, that Rif and Rambam make no distinction between wheat and barley, and this is the ruling to follow. This also appears from the unqualified language of the Shulchan Aruch here, which prohibits every type of grain even when it did not split. Olas Shabbos, subsection 2, writes likewise. This also follows from the later authorities' unqualified use of the word grain, implying that every type of grain is prohibited even when it did not split.

14) There. Or water fell upon it. This applies even to a small amount of water. Bach; Taz, subsection 3; Olas Shabbos, subsection 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 2; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 6.
15) There. It is prohibited even if it did not split, etc. Although regarding soaking grain it is prohibited only if the kernels split, that case differs because one is actively handling them. Tur in the name of Rabbeinu Ephraim; Magen Avraham, subsection 3. Below in se'if 9, the Shulchan Aruch permits a cooked dish if a kernel did not actually split, even though the kernel rests undisturbed in the food. This may be because the cooked dish is hot and therefore resembles scalding, which does not cause leavening according to Tur, simanim 461 and 467. See there. We are concerned only that the kernel may have been in the water before it boiled, and therefore are not so stringent. Another and primary explanation is that a minute admixture in a cooked dish is only rabbinically prohibited, and we are not so stringent, as Magen Avraham writes in subsection 12. See there. Based upon Rambam, chapter 5, the reason appears straightforward: grain that was mixed with water or cooked but did not actually split is not chametz under Torah law. The prohibition of a minute admixture is also rabbinic, and we do not impose two rabbinic stringencies. Chok Yaakov, subsection 7. He writes there that all this explains the authorities and the Shulchan Aruch, but in practice Rema concludes in the gloss to se'if 9 that the custom is to prohibit under all circumstances. See there. See our words there, with the help of Heaven.
16) There. It is prohibited even if it did not split, etc. This means that even when we see no defect and the kernel is not at all close to splitting, it is nevertheless prohibited. It is unlike soaked grain, which is actively handled. Therefore, placing it upon the opening of a barrel or using a similar test to determine whether it became chametz does not help. Ma'amar Mordechai, subsection 4.
