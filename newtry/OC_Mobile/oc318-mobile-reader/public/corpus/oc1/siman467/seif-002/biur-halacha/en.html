This grain that was submerged, etc. — behold in the Beit Yosef it is found that some divide between wheat and barley in this matter, and from the Mechaber's silence it appears that he holds that all types of grain have the same law, and so too the Taz; and therefore know that what the Rama wrote "and therefore one must be careful," etc., "or rain dripping on the wheat" — he mentioned the usual way of speaking because presumably one buys wheat for matzah; but the same applies to all types of grain. And know further that in the Beit Yosef it is found that some poskim hold that there is a distinction between much soaking and a little dripping; however the Mechaber was silent on this, and the Taz wrote that since the Mechaber cited the view of Rabbeinu Efrayim that therefore splitting open is not required because it rested at ease (and according to him one need not distinguish between little dripping and much soaking), it implies that even a little dripping forbids; and the Gra cited it and so I cited in the Mishna Berurah, and so I found also in Bach and the Pri Megadim cited it; however in Bach it appears that his opinion is that only l'chatchila one must be careful with a little dripping not to buy them to eat on Pesach, and so too is the opinion of the Peri Chadash on what the Rama wrote "that they should not come," etc., "for then even if they did not split open they are forbidden" — the Peri Chadash wrote and this is its language: meaning when abundant rains descended until the stack was soaked in water, etc.; but l'chatchila it is obvious one must be careful even with a little dripping — end of his words; and it appears from Beit Me'ir that b'dieved in a place of great loss one may rely on this:
