(Magen Avraham, s.k. 12.) And therefore they disagree only regarding soaking. Marginal note: that is to say, since he is occupied with it, it does not come to chametzing; and I do not know what difficulty there is, for in se'if 2, even though they did not crack open, even if this reasoning were not certain, since they are resting [in water] they are chametz; nevertheless, since it is a Torah-level doubt (perhaps even though they did not crack open it is chametz), but here, where we deal with a prohibition of any amount, it is a rabbinic doubt. Also, what he answered, that the language "comes to chametzing" means it is a doubt and therefore here it is permitted because it is a rabbinic doubt, is difficult of him against himself, for in s.k. 16 he wrote that a doubt whether it became chametz is not [treated as] a doubt, because it is a doubt from lack of knowledge. Therefore it appears as I wrote, and the language "comes to chametzing" can be intended that the Tur holds cold does not make chametz, and these wheat kernels that sank in soaking water: that is, so long as they are in the water they are not chametzed, but after they leave the water they come to chametzing.
