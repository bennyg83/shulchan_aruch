For they do not buy bread, etc.; and for us see below in this s.k.
In the incident of Pesachim 40b regarding a boat of wheat that sank in the river: at first Rava also instructed to sell them to a non-Jew, and Rabbah bar L. challenged him from this kilayim beraita, and he retracted and permitted to sell little by little as written in Shulchan Aruch; if so he already knew of the kilayim beraita: how did he err a second time in the yayin nesech incident?
And if so it is difficult why he troubled, etc. Granted if the two incidents did not come before Rava at one time there is no difficulty why he did not permit in that of yayin nesech to sell them little by little: perhaps at that time this permission did not occur to him and did not enter his mind, for such is the way of people that many times they grasp things that did not previously enter their mind. But according to what Tosafos wrote, that at one time he ruled on both incidents: and since he ruled regarding chametz to sell them little by little, why did he trouble them regarding yayin nesech and not rule for them also to sell little by little?
And it implies, etc. Even one egg: for such is the language of the Gemara and poskim: one may not sell an egg of nevelah and treifah to a non-Jew; they took "egg" in the singular and not "eggs" in the plural, implying that selling them little by little has no remedy; and why he did not give a remedy to grind and bake them: perhaps in his place the custom is to eat non-Jews' bread, and then there is no remedy except to sell to a non-Jew a slice of bread as the Rama wrote at the end of Yoreh De'ah siman 134; and perhaps there was much wheat and it was impossible to sell so many slices, for one will not easily find buyers for a slice of bread, and to keep them a long time is forbidden because of a [possible] pitfall.
And what the Rama wrote, etc. Regarding yayin nesech mixed in water: stam yeinam is nullified in six parts water and then permitted even for drinking; and if mixed in water and there is not enough to nullify the wine, sell them to a non-Jew except for the value of the yayin nesech in it; and the Rama concluded: however he should not sell much together, etc., implying that little he has no [problem]; if so it is proven that even regarding other prohibitions little by little is permitted.
They sense the taste of the wine. And even though if a Jew tastes it the Jew would commit a prohibition: this is not so, for one cannot say the non-Jew will sell it to a Jew, for the non-Jew knows that if the Jew tastes it he will sense the wine taste and return the water to him, claiming it is a mistaken purchase. And even if you say we should nevertheless be concerned, tasting is forbidden only by rabbinic law: he should not be concerned for such a remote concern regarding a rabbinic prohibition.
Rather his intent, etc. That there are two remedies in the matter: one is the remedy of Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel mentioned in Avodah Zarah folio 74: sell all of it to a non-Jew except for the value of the yayin nesech in it. And the second is the remedy of R' Eliezer explained on folio 49b: take the benefit to the Dead Sea; and see there in Yoreh De'ah siman 134 in the Tur and Beis Yosef and the distinction between these two remedies. For if he sells them except for the value of the yayin nesech in it he must sell everything at one time; but through the remedy of taking the benefit to the Dead Sea he may sell them little by little; and to this Rabbeinu Yeruham intended: however he should not sell together. And what the Rama concluded there, "lest he sell it back to a Jew," is not from Rabbeinu Yeruham's language, but the Rama added so from his own understanding according to the Rama's grasp; but Magen Avraham holds that what Rabbeinu Yeruham wrote "however he should not sell together" means it was permitted only through taking the benefit to the Dead Sea, and through this it is permitted to sell little by little; but a remedy to sell specifically everything together, namely to sell it except for the value of the yayin nesech in it, we do not hold that permission. (And according to Rabbeinu Yeruham's language that Beis Yosef brought there in siman 134 one cannot intend thus, and Sefer Rabbeinu Yeruham is not before me.) But his words too require study, for Tosafos at the end of the third chapter of Avodah Zarah, etc.: meaning Rabbeinu Yeruham wrote so in the name of Tosafos, and Tosafos's intent is not as Rabbeinu Yeruham understood; for in Avodah Zarah 49b: A certain man whose barrel of yayin nesech mixed into his wines came before R' Chisda, who said to him: Take four zuz and cast them into the river and it will be permitted for you. And Tosafos wrote, and this is their language: "Take," etc., and to sell them each by itself he told him so, as I explained (end quote). And Rabbeinu Yeruham understood that Tosafos's intent is specifically to sell them each by itself, namely through the remedy of casting the value of the yayin nesech, that is four zuz, it was permitted for him, but it was not permitted for him to sell them at one time through the remedy of except for the value of yayin nesech in it; and on this Magen Avraham wrote that on the contrary Tosafos's intent is the opposite: certainly if he wishes to sell them at one time except for yayin nesech it is permitted; only that R' Chisda came to lighten for him and told him to cast four zuz into the river and through this he may sell them little by little each by itself. But regarding a matter, etc.: meaning according to how Magen Avraham explained Rabbeinu Yeruham's words unlike the Rama: if so there is no proof from Rabbeinu Yeruham to permit regarding other prohibitions to sell little by little lest he sell it to a Jew; rather yayin nesech is different, for no Jew will buy wine from a non-Jew; but regarding chametz [it differs]; and not like Shach in Yoreh De'ah siman 84 s.k. 17 who permitted also regarding other prohibitions to sell to a non-Jew little by little; and see Peri Chadash. And see Yoreh De'ah siman 112 se'if 11, who wrote: Bread of a Jew that a non-Jew baked without a Jew's assistance is forbidden, and even to sell it to a non-Jew is forbidden in a place where they practice eating non-Jews' bread, lest he sell it back to a Jew, unless he cuts it into pieces, for pieces of bread are forbidden to buy from a non-Jew. Implying that even little by little it is forbidden to sell to a non-Jew regarding other prohibitions, since he needed to give a remedy to cut the bread into pieces and did not write to sell little by little.
For it is a double doubt made by hands: meaning since he causes there to be a double doubt it is like nullifying a prohibition l'chatchila.
And this requires further study, for it deals with much chametz. For little by little is truly permitted; and therefore what he asks, that it is a double doubt, is to permit selling much together. And if so he took the doubt "perhaps a Jew will eat it before Pesach," since it is much it will not be finished before Pesach; for if it can be finished before Pesach then it is called little by little. It implies ostensibly that he holds like Kesef Mishneh's explanation: for if he held as Magen Avraham wrote that little by little is permitted because even if sold to a Jew the Jew will finish it before Pesach, then even little by little is permitted only by force of a double doubt: perhaps he will not sell it to a Jew, and even if he sells it perhaps the Jew will finish it before Pesach; and he holds one may not make a double doubt by hands; therefore necessarily he holds like Kesef Mishneh's explanation that little by little is permitted because we say he bought it for his own need and will not sell it at all. And accordingly, according to Magen Avraham who holds little by little is permitted because of a double doubt, one must say it is permitted to make a double doubt by hands, and he was not concerned for R. Y.T.'s difficulty that even much would be permitted: perhaps the non-Jew will sell to Jews much little by little; for perhaps this is not a common doubt, for presumably when he bought much together so he will sell it. And see Yoreh De'ah at the end of siman 57, where there it implies like R. Y.T. that one may not make a double doubt by hands: for Shach wrote there in s.k. 51 on what the Rama wrote there: a doubt of treifah, some certify even though we hold to forbid: nevertheless it is permitted to sell it to a non-Jew, etc., since there are certifiers; and Shach wrote that one may not be lenient in this except regarding scratching [derusah], for which anyway there are sides to permit; implying but regarding another prohibition, even if there is a dispute in the matter, they do not permit selling it to a non-Jew; and the reason: even though it is a double doubt: one, perhaps the main [law] follows the certifiers, and even if like the stringent ones, perhaps he will not sell it to a Jew: nevertheless it is a double doubt made by hands and forbidden; and not because it is a double doubt of dispute and worse than other double doubts: on the contrary a doubt of dispute is better than other double doubts; rather Shach's intent is because it is a double doubt made by hands; and as the Rav Minchas Yaakov proved in Kuntrus HaSfeikos in the novel sections se'if 2, and wondered at what Shach wrote in siman 66 s.k. 10 which implies the opposite; see there; and see Peri Chadash in Kuntrus HaSfeikos, klal 18.
