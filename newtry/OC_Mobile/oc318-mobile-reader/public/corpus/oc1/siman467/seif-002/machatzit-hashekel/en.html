In the incident in Pesachim page 40b regarding a ship of wheat that sank in the river — at first Rava also thought to sell them to a gentile, and Rava challenged him from this baraita of kilayim and retracted and permitted selling little by little as written in Shulchan Aruch; if so he already knew from the baraita of kilayim — how did he err a second time in the incident of stam yayin:

If so, why did he trouble them, etc. Granted, if both incidents did not come before Rava at one time — there is no difficulty why he did not permit in this incident of stam yayin to sell them little by little, for it is possible that at that time this permission did not occur to him and did not enter his mind; for such is the way of people that many times they understand things that previously did not enter their mind. But according to what Tosafos said that at one time he ruled on both incidents — since he ruled in this incident of chametz to sell them little by little, why did he trouble them in this incident of stam yayin and not rule for them also to sell little by little:

And it implies, etc., even one egg — for such is the language of the Gemara and the codifiers: one does not sell an egg of nevelah or tereifah to a gentile — they used the singular egg and not eggs plural. It implies there is no remedy to sell them little by little; and that which they did not give a remedy to grind them or bake them — it is possible that in their place the practice was to eat gentile bread; and then there is no remedy except to sell to a gentile a slice of bread as the Rama wrote at the end of siman 134 in Yoreh De'ah; and it is possible the wheat was abundant and it was not possible to sell so many slices, for buyers for slices of bread are not easily found; and to leave them a long time is forbidden because of spoilage:

And that which the Rama wrote, etc., in stam yayin that became mixed in water — for stam their wine is nullified in six parts water and then it is permitted even for drinking; and if it became mixed in water and there is not enough to nullify the wine — one sells them to a gentile except for the value of the stam yayin in it; and the Rama concluded: however one may not sell much together, etc. — it implies that little he does not have; if so it is proven that even in other prohibitions little by little is permitted:

They will taste the taste of wine. And even so, if an Israelite tastes them he will make a prohibition for the Israelite. This is not so, for one cannot say the gentile will sell it to an Israelite — the gentile knows if an Israelite tastes them he will perceive the taste of wine and the water will return to him, for he will claim it is a mistaken purchase. And even if you say nevertheless we are concerned — nevertheless this taste is forbidden only d'rabbanan, so there is no reason to be concerned for such a distant concern in a d'rabbanan prohibition:

Rather his intent, etc., is that there are two remedies in the matter: one, the remedy of R' Shimon ben Gamliel mentioned in tractate Avodah Zarah page 74 — that one sells all of it to a gentile except for the value of the stam yayin in it. And the second, the remedy of R' Eliezer explained on page 49b — that one brings the benefit to the Salt Sea; see there in Yoreh De'ah siman 134 in the Tur and Beit Yosef and the distinction between these two remedies. For if one sells them except for the value of the stam yayin in it, one must sell everything at once; but through the remedy of bringing benefit to the Salt Sea one may sell them little by little; and for this R' Yosef Ish Yerushalayim concluded: however one may not sell together; and that which the Rama concluded there lest he return and sell it to an Israelite is not from the words of R' Yosef Ish Yerushalayim but the Rama added this from his own understanding according to the Rama's understanding. But Magen Avraham holds that what R' Yosef Ish Yerushalayim wrote "however one may not sell together" means it is not permitted except through throwing benefit into the Salt Sea; and through that one is permitted to sell little by little. But the remedy to sell specifically all together — namely to sell it except for the value of the stam yayin in it — we do not hold this leniency (and according to the language of R' Yosef Ish Yerushalayim that Rabbeinu Yosef brought there siman 134 it cannot be understood thus, and the text of R' Yosef Ish Yerushalayim is not clear). But also his words require study, for Tosafos at the beginning of chapter 3 of Avodah Zarah, etc., means that R' Yosef Ish Yerushalayim wrote thus in the name of Tosafos; and Tosafos' intent is not as R' Yosef Ish Yerushalayim understood, for in Avodah Zarah page 49b: this man in whom a barrel of stam yayin became mixed in his donkey-loads came before R' Chiyya; he said to him: take four zuz and throw them in the river and I will permit you. And Tosafos wrote — and this is its language: take, etc., and sell them only by itself — he told him thus as I explained, end quote; and R' Yosef Ish Yerushalayim understood that Tosafos' intent is specifically to sell them only by itself — namely through the remedy that one throws the value of the stam yayin — namely four zuz — and it is permitted for him; but he was not permitted to sell them at once through the remedy of except for the value of the stam yayin in it; and on this Magen Avraham wrote: on the contrary, Tosafos' intent is the reverse — for certainly if he wishes to sell them at once except for the stam yayin it is permitted; rather R' Chiyya came to be lenient for him and said throw four zuz in the river and through that he is permitted to sell little by little only by itself. But in the matter, etc., means according to what Magen Avraham explained the words of R' Yosef Ish Yerushalayim — if so there is no proof from the words of R' Yosef Ish Yerushalayim to permit in other prohibitions to sell little by little lest he sell it to an Israelite; rather stam yayin is different, for an Israelite will not buy wine from a gentile except in pressing circumstances — unlike Shach in Yoreh De'ah siman 84 seif katan 17 who permitted also in other prohibitions to sell to a gentile little by little; see Peri Chadash. And see Yoreh De'ah siman 112 seif 1 who wrote: bread of an Israelite that a gentile baked without Israelite assistance is forbidden, and even to sell it to a gentile is forbidden in a place where they are accustomed to eat gentile bread lest he return and sell it to an Israelite — unless he cuts it into slices, for slices of bread are forbidden to buy from a gentile. It implies that even little by little it is forbidden to sell to a gentile in other prohibitions — since they needed to give the remedy to cut the bread into slices and did not write to sell little by little:

For it would be safek sefeika — meaning since he holds that there should be safek sefeika, it is like nullifying a prohibition l'chatchila:

And it requires study, for here it deals with much chametz — for little by little is truly permitted; and therefore what he questions — behold it is safek sefeika — is to permit selling much together. And if so he used the safek that perhaps an Israelite will eat it before Pesach since it is much and will not finish before Pesach; for if it is possible it will finish before Pesach it is called little by little — it appears at first glance that he holds like the explanation of Rambam that if he holds like what Magen Avraham wrote that little by little is permitted — that even if he sells it to an Israelite the Israelite will finish it before Pesach — if so even little by little is not permitted except from the aspect of safek sefeika that perhaps he will not sell it to an Israelite, and even if he sells it perhaps the Israelite will finish it before Pesach; and he holds one may not make safek sefeika in one's hands. Or he holds like the explanation of Rambam that little by little is permitted, for we say he bought it for his own need and will not sell it at all; and according to this, for Magen Avraham who holds little by little is permitted because of safek sefeika — it must be said it is permitted to make safek sefeika in one's hands and he was not concerned for the questions of R' Yom Tov Lipmann that even much would be permitted lest the gentile sell to many Israelites little by little, for it is possible this is not a common safek — that presumably as he bought much together so he will sell it. And see Yoreh De'ah safek sefeika 57 there Shach in seif katan 91 on what the Rama wrote there: safek tereifah — there are those who permit it even though we hold to forbid; nevertheless it is permitted to sell it to a gentile, etc., since there are those who permit; and Shach wrote one may not be lenient in this except in pressing circumstances, for otherwise there are sides to permit; it implies but in another prohibition, even if there is a dispute in the matter, they do not permit selling it to a gentile; and the reason: even though there is one safek that perhaps the main law is like those who permit, and if you say like those who are stringent — perhaps he will not sell it to an Israelite — nevertheless it is safek sefeika made in one's hands and forbidden, for we do not say because it is safek sefeika of a dispute it is better than other safek sefeika; on the contrary, a dispute-safek is preferable to other safek sefeika; rather Shach's intent is because it is safek sefeika made in one's hands, as Rabbeinu Menachem proved in his questions on the novelties seif 2; and he wondered at what Shach wrote in siman 66 seif katan 10 that from his words it implies the reverse — see there; and see Peri Chadash in his questions general 18:
