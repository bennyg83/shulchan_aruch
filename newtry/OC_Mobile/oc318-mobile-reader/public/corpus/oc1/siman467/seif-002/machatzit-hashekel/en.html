And it implies, etc., even one egg — for such is the language of the Gemara and the codifiers: one does not sell an egg of nevelah or tereifah to a gentile — they used the singular egg and not eggs plural. It implies there is no remedy to sell them little by little; and that which they did not give a remedy to grind them or bake them — it is possible that in their place the practice was to eat gentile bread; and then there is no remedy except to sell to a gentile a slice of bread as the Rama wrote at the end of siman 134 in Yoreh De'ah; and it is possible the wheat was abundant and it was not possible to sell so many slices, for buyers for slices of bread are not easily found; and to leave them a long time is forbidden because of spoilage:

And it requires study, for here it deals with much chametz — for little by little is truly permitted; and therefore what he questions — behold it is safek sefeika — is to permit selling much together. And if so he used the safek that perhaps an Israelite will eat it before Pesach since it is much and will not finish before Pesach; for if it is possible it will finish before Pesach it is called little by little — it appears at first glance that he holds like the explanation of Rambam that if he holds like what Magen Avraham wrote that little by little is permitted — that even if he sells it to an Israelite the Israelite will finish it before Pesach — if so even little by little is not permitted except from the aspect of safek sefeika that perhaps he will not sell it to an Israelite, and even if he sells it perhaps the Israelite will finish it before Pesach; and he holds one may not make safek sefeika in one's hands. Or he holds like the explanation of Rambam that little by little is permitted, for we say he bought it for his own need and will not sell it at all; and according to this, for Magen Avraham who holds little by little is permitted because of safek sefeika — it must be said it is permitted to make safek sefeika in one's hands and he was not concerned for the questions of R' Yom Tov Lipmann that even much would be permitted lest the gentile sell to many Israelites little by little, for it is possible this is not a common safek — that presumably as he bought much together so he will sell it.
