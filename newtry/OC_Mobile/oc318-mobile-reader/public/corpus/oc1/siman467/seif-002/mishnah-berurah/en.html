(6) That water fell on it — and even a small amount; and see in Biur Halacha:

(7) Even though it did not split open — behold in seif 9 the Mechaber wrote even though it did not actually split open — that is, even if it softened such that they are close to splitting; and here he changed his language and wrote even though it did not split open, to teach that even though it did not soften at all, nevertheless it is forbidden:

(8) For since it rested at ease — meaning, even though regarding sweetness there are poskim who hold it is not forbidden unless they split open, nevertheless according to all we require that they be close to splitting — that case there is different because for sweetness one is engaged with it and does not leave it so much to become leavened, unlike here where it rested at ease:

(9) Leavening — and specifically when it rested the measure of time for leavening; but if it has not yet rested the measure of time for leavening, it has not yet leavened; and if so, even if it occurred on Pesach it is permitted to sell it to a gentile before it rested the measure of time for leavening; and specifically with intent to eat immediately. A non-Jew who told a Jew after he bought wheat from him that it is for sweets is not believed, even if he speaks guilelessly, since he said this after he sold them and they left his possession; however, if the non-Jew has a presumption of trustworthiness in his eyes, it is forbidden, even if he was not speaking guilelessly:

(10) And rain descended — that is, enough that there is room to suspect that much became moist; but if a small amount of water or snow fell on the grain before grinding and its place is known, one scrapes off the upper wheat kernels and the rest is permitted:

(11) And a double uncertainty does not help — and see in the Acharonim who agreed one should not be stringent in this except in a place where there is no loss in retaining them after Pesach; but in a place of loss or prevention of Yom Tov rejoicing, one may permit even in eating:
