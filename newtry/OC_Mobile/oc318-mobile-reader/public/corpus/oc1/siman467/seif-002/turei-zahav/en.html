For since they rested at ease. The Tur wrote this in the name of R' Eliezer: even though regarding what descends below they are not forbidden unless they split open — that case is different, for there the Torah law is under discussion, therefore they do not come to leavening until they split open; but here, since they rested at ease, they come to leavening; and the Ran in the name of Ra'ah wrote that here splitting open is not required, for it deals with wheat that splits open in its natural growth, and specifically regarding barley splitting open is required; and Ra'am distinguished between small dripping and abundant soaking, as explained in the Beit Yosef; and since he mentioned here the view of R' Efrayim, it implies that we hold there is no distinction between wheat and barley nor between small dripping and abundant soaking.

By force of double uncertainty. It requires further study regarding the Rama's view in this, for he wrote these words — the Agur brought them in the Beit Yosef regarding wheat over which water passed via the roof, that we rely on the permission that Ra'avyah wrote; and the Mordechai brought at length in the name of Ra'avyah to eat some of them before Pesach or to sell to a gentile little by little and the rest is permitted by force of double uncertainty — perhaps water did not descend upon them, and if you say water descended upon them, perhaps they did not leaven (meaning: were not leavened); but the lower wheat that is near the floor of the attic — it is nearly certain that water descended there to the thickness of a finger or half a finger, and those one discards and the rest is permitted to keep; but not to eat from them on Pesach, for even though regarding it shall not be seen uncertainties are nullified in their majority regarding eating, which is in any amount — we are not lenient — end of his words. That which he wrote to eat some of them, etc. — it appears there is an error in his words, for this permission to eat some of them the author of the Or Zarua brought in the name of Ra'avyah in the lengthy Mordechai — that it is permitted even on Pesach, for we can say it is a double uncertainty: perhaps they already ate from the forbidden ones, and if you say they did not eat, one can say they will now eat from the permitted — as with a ring of idolatry that became mixed and one fell into the Great Sea and all were permitted to benefit together, for certainly there is permission; and even if they did not eat from it, one may take from it and throw it into the sea, or eat from them before Pesach, and the rest are permitted; and there is no nullifying prohibition l'chatchila since it is before Pesach, and you have no matzah that is not more than the prohibition — two prohibitions — and in double uncertainty we follow leniency even for d'oraisa prohibition — end of his words. And in the Agur that we mentioned he mixed two reasons for permission — namely to eat some of them, which applies even if there is certainly chametz among them; and he also mentioned double uncertainty from perhaps they did not leaven — and it appears to me there is some leap in his words. And in any case, since the essence of the permission here is by force of double uncertainty, why is it forbidden in eating on Pesach? For even in d'oraisa prohibition it is permitted in double uncertainty — all the more so in rabbinic prohibition in any amount; and further, the Rama himself who wrote here that double uncertainty does not help for eating — he wrote in siman 453 seif 3: if there is grain among them that sprouted — for if there are sixty in what did not sprout it is permitted if one grinds them, and that is even to eat on Pesach — behold they do not say it returns and revives; and why did he rule here forbidden because it returns and revives? And necessarily one must say that here it deals where there are not sixty against the prohibition, and the essence of his emphasis teaches that it is not permitted even in double uncertainty. First difficulty: why does double uncertainty not help here in truth? Further, Ra'avyah truly permitted because of double uncertainty even for eating, as stated in his name, and also in the next seif it is proven thus — it requires study. And for the law, it implies even according to all that the prohibition for eating is because there is no loss in keeping them — it implies that if there is loss, such as when he has no other grain, it is permitted even in eating.
