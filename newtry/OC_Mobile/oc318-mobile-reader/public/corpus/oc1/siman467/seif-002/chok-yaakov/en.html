This grain that sank in the river. Whether it is permitted to nullify this grain in a majority or in sixty before Pesach, since it is still a time of permissibility, was discussed at length by the later authorities. See what I wrote above in siman 442, seif katan 18, and in siman 447, seif katan 15, for what emerges from their words in practice.
Since they lie still, they come to leavening. So wrote the Rosh and the Tur in the name of Rabbeinu Ephraim. According to the Tur and Rosh, regarding all soaked wheat, even where they do not lie still, if one wishes to eat the kernels themselves, even if they did not actually split but are close to splitting, they are forbidden. If so, here the reason that they lie still is needed to forbid them even where the kernel did not soften. But according to those authorities who rule like Shmuel, that actual splitting is required for soaked grain, perhaps where they lie still this is only one added stringency, and they must at least be close to splitting. Otherwise they are permitted. The Mechaber holds that eating the kernels themselves is a doubt in a Torah matter and is ruled stringently, as explained in seif 9. Therefore he ruled here that where they lie still, even if they did not soften and are not close to splitting, they are forbidden, like the Tur and Rosh. This is why he wrote here plainly that although they did not split they are forbidden, and did not write as he writes below in seif 9, 'even if they did not actually split,' to show that even when they are not close to splitting they are forbidden here when eating the kernels themselves. But if found in a cooked dish, the Mechaber rules below in seif 9 that if they did not actually split, even if close to splitting, it is permitted. Although they lie still within the dish, since a boiling dish is like scalding, which does not leaven according to the Tur in simanim 461 and 467, the only concern is that it was in water before boiling, and for this we are not so stringent. More essentially, in a dish it is only a mixture of a minute amount, which is D'Rabbanan, and we are not so stringent, as the Magen Avraham writes in seif katan 12. According to the Rambam, the reason is simple: grain that was moistened or cooked but did not actually split is not chametz according to the Torah, and for a D'Rabbanan minute amount we do not prohibit two D'Rabbanan levels. The Lechem Mishneh explains the Rambam this way, and by this path the Rif and Rambam can be understood without difficulty, unlike the strained reading of the Beit Yosef. According to this, if this grain was mixed with its own kind, even in less than sixty, it is only D'Rabbanan and there is room for leniency according to the Mechaber. I have elaborated to settle the authorities and the Mechaber with proper reasoning, but in practice the Rama concludes in seif 9 that the custom is to forbid in every case. See there. It is also clear from the Mechaber in seif 1 and this seif that even if they did not split, it is forbidden to sell them to a non-Jew before Pesach unless selling little by little, unlike the Aviasaf in the Mordechai, Perek Kol Sha'ah, who permits on the basis of a double doubt. See Shach, Yoreh De'ah, end of siman 57.
They come to leavening. If it has not yet remained for the time it takes to walk a mil, the Magen Avraham wrote that even if this occurred on Pesach, one may sell it to a non-Jew before it becomes chametz.
But to eat them on Pesach is forbidden. Even if some of them were eaten before Pesach, one may not permit eating the remainder, as the Bach concludes, unlike the Raavyah. Only with dry mixed with dry, where one item was eaten, or in three mixtures, is there room to permit eating two, as explained in Yoreh De'ah, siman 110. But liquid-like mixtures have no such permission. So too the Magen Avraham wrote. Above in siman 453 it was explained that flour mixed with flour is considered a liquid-like mixture, and I likewise concluded in my Kuntres HaSefeikos, seif katan 19, unlike the Shach.
And a double doubt does not help. In truth, in this siman and in several places it is clear that we rely on a double doubt even for eating. See the Taz and Shach. The reason given is that since according to Mar Ukva even unsplit grain is forbidden, this is only a double doubt in dispute, not in the reality of the matter, and we are not so lenient in such a doubt. In my Kuntres HaSefeikos, chiddush 2, seif katan 2, I explained that, on the contrary, a doubt in dispute is stronger than a doubt in the factual matter. Nor can one say that the Rama is stringent here because all of this is one doubt, whether these wheat kernels became chametz from the water or not, for I also rejected that reasoning in Kuntres HaSefeikos, siman 120. See the Olas Shabbos for several reasons. The main point appears to be the conclusion of the Taz: here they were stringent because there is no loss in merely keeping it. But in a case of loss, or where Yom Tov joy would be prevented, and the like, one may permit even eating.
