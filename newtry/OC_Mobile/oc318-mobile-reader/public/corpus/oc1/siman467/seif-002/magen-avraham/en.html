To leavening. And if it had not yet rested the measure of mil it had not yet leavened (and so in Talmud Yerushalmi siman 123 and in the Agur); and if so, even if it occurred on Pesach, one may sell it to a gentile before it rested the measure of mil. An incident: an Israelite bought wheat from a gentile, and afterward another Israelite came and asked the gentile to sell him wheat too, and he said I have no wheat, for the Jew I sold to is my friend and I sold him the wheat I washed and intended to grind for my household need and sold them to that friend — it appears similar to what is written in Yoreh De'ah siman 16 that a gentile is not believed in what he said after he sold and they left his hand; however if he is believed to him it is forbidden — end of his words; and see there siman 127. The Bach wrote in the Mordechai at length in the name of Ra'avyah: wheat that split open and their splitting is not recognizable that became mixed in a pile — forbidden to keep; and if he transgressed and kept them, they are forbidden after Pesach and forbidden to sell to a gentile; but if they ate from the grain before Pesach, permitted to eat on Pesach, as in Yoreh De'ah siman 110 seif 7 that one may eat the remainder two by two; and even if they did not eat from it, one may take from it and throw into the sea, and the rest are permitted; and there is no matter of nullifying prohibition l'chatchila, for Pesach has not yet arrived (and see siman 447 in the name of the Rashba); and even though one may eat only two by two, one by one is forbidden — here too you have no matzah that is not more than the measure of prohibition — two prohibitions — therefore all is in it he wrote that keeping mixed wheat is permitted; dry with dry is nullified — so the Bach for the law; and what Ra'avyah wrote when some were eaten that the remainder may be eaten on Pesach — because he holds it does not forbid more than removal; but for us who hold it forbids entirely — if so any amount of chametz remaining forbids all the matzah, for it imparts taste in any amount (Bach); and see siman 461; and according to what is written in siman 447 seif 9 that before Pesach it is nullified in the majority — if so permitted to grind before Pesach and nullify in sixty, siman 453 seif 3, and so Yoreh De'ah siman 109; and there is room to study in the Bach — see there.
