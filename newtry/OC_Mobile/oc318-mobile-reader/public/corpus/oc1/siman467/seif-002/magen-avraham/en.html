To the point of becoming chametz. If it has not yet remained for the time needed to walk a mil, it has not yet become chametz. This is also implied by Terumas HaDeshen, siman 123, and by Agur. Accordingly, even if this occurred on Pesach, it may be sold to a non-Jew before the time of a mil has elapsed. An incident occurred in which a Jew purchased wheat from a non-Jew. Another Jew later asked the non-Jew to sell him wheat as well. The non-Jew said: I have no wheat, for the Jew to whom I sold is my friend, and I sold him wheat that I had washed and intended to grind for my household, because I loved him. It appears to me that this resembles the ruling at the end of Yoreh Deah siman 16, that the non-Jew is not believed in what he says after he has sold the item and it has left his possession. Nevertheless, if the Jew personally trusts him, it is forbidden. See there and siman 127. Bach cites Mordechai HaAruch in the name of Raavyah: wheat kernels that split, though the splitting is not visible, and became mixed into a pile, may not be retained. If one transgressed and retained them, they are forbidden after Pesach and may not be sold to a non-Jew. But if some of the grain was eaten before Pesach, the remainder may be eaten on Pesach, as stated in Yoreh Deah 110:7, where the remaining pieces may be eaten two at a time. Even if none were eaten, one may take one and cast it into the sea, and the others become permitted. This is not considered intentionally nullifying a prohibition, since Pesach has not yet begun. See what I wrote at the end of siman 447 in the name of Rashba. Although they may be eaten only two at a time and not singly, here every matzah contains more than the measure of the forbidden substance, so each constitutes two against one. Kol Bo writes that the mixed wheat may be retained, because dry matter mixed with dry matter is nullified, and Bach rules accordingly. Raavyah's statement that after some were eaten the rest may be eaten on Pesach follows his view that the prohibition extends only to the amount requiring removal. According to our view that the entire item becomes forbidden, however, any minute amount of chametz remaining prohibits the entire matzah, because chametz imparts prohibition even in the smallest quantity. See the end of siman 461. According to what is written in siman 447:9, that before Pesach it is nullified by a majority, it may be ground before Pesach and nullified in sixty. See siman 453:3 and Yoreh Deah 109. Bach's treatment requires study. It may also be learned that if dry matter became mixed with dry matter on Pesach and one of them was eaten, the others are permitted, as written in Yoreh Deah siman 110. Perhaps the third mixture is also permitted, as written there. These leniencies do not apply to a liquid mixture.
