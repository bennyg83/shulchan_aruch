From his words — recall, I still remember what I heard from the mouth of the great famous Gaon Av Beit Din and Rosh Yeshiva Maharar Shaul Nerya of the Ashkenazic community in Amsterdam, may he live. Certain matters that were renewed for him in Beit HaMidrash Rishon L'Tzion — he delivered a sermon on what Magen Avraham brought siman 442 s.k. 1: the Tur wrote that every matter that is not forbidden in eating from the Torah is permitted to mix and leave — and so too Semag; but Tosafot at the beginning of Pesachim wrote it is forbidden, for the Rabbis did not distinguish, and they proved thus from the mishnah that se'or shall be burned — and even though it was hard and there is no d'oraisa prohibition in eating it, nevertheless it shall be burned d'rabbanan, etc.; and according to the Tur's opinion one must say that regarding se'or it deals on Yom Tov, etc. — end of his words; and it is clear to everyone this answer is very strained. But in my humble opinion I cannot understand the Tur's method; on the contrary Tosafot's words need explanation — for Peri Chadash from that which R' Gidal said, Rav said: one who consecrates from six and above — even in Cordovanian wheat we are not concerned for its sanctity, for it is hard chametz — and nevertheless we are not concerned for its sanctity; if so it is proven that even before its time — meaning from six and above until erev — the Sages forbade hard chametz even in benefit; and on daf 28 R' Yehuda holds before its time and after its time they are equal for every matter regarding full chametz from the Torah. If so, just as the Rabbis decreed on hard chametz before its time to forbid it in benefit — so too they decreed to forbid hard chametz after its time in benefit according to R' Yehuda; and since that regarding se'or shall be burned R' Yehuda taught it as brought on daf 48 — see there — there is no proof from there at all. For according to R' Yehuda even if one leaves it after its time it should be forbidden in benefit d'rabbanan as it is forbidden before its time d'rabbanan — therefore it shall be burned. But for us who hold before its time like R' Yehuda and after its time like R' Shimon — full chametz after Pesach is forbidden only because of a penalty where one transgressed bal yeira'eh upon it; but regarding hard chametz on which one did not transgress bal yeira'eh they did not decree, unlike before its time when full chametz is forbidden from the Torah — therefore they decreed on hard chametz d'rabbanan; therefore one who consecrates from six and above — even in hard chametz we are not concerned for sanctity; but after its time hard chametz is permitted even d'rabbanan since one did not transgress bal yeira'eh upon it; if so it is permitted to leave it even l'chatchila in order to eat it after Pesach — and the same through mixtures that are comparable to hard chametz; and there is no difficulty on the Tur at all; and what Tosafot wrote at the beginning of Pesachim needs explanation — I expanded elsewhere. This [and another matter]: siman 444 in Magen Avraham s.k. 9 — you may wonder on what he wrote above siman 249 that it is forbidden to make a betrothal feast on erev Pesach and it is from the Yerushalmi, etc. — end of his words — I do not know what difficulty at all; behold it is explicit in Shulchan Aruch there — feast and drinking that one is not accustomed on weekdays; but if one makes a betrothal feast in a manner one is also accustomed on weekdays it is permitted — and one may say here it deals thus; and see like what Hagahot Maimoni explained siman 554 s.k. 12 — see there. And further (the third matter) in 444 Magen Avraham s.k. 12 "if so it implies at the sixth hour when it cannot be nullified it revives," etc. — see in Sefer Even HaOzer newly printed in Magen Avraham Propes edition who expanded to distinguish on this; and one of his proofs from what is taught "one who goes to slaughter his Pesach" — and there even when it cannot be nullified, etc., for Pesach is more severe for it is a positive commandment with karet, etc., and certainly the positive commandment of ceasing work overrides it — and the same for all those explicitly taught there, etc., and I did not grasp his mind at all — for certainly it cannot slaughter Pesach while chametz is in his possession and he did not nullify it, at least "you shall not slaughter upon chametz the blood of My sacrifice" is written, and we hold even if it is not with him in the courtyard. If so, on the contrary, that regarding one who goes to slaughter his Pesach certainly deals at the hour when he can still nullify, and the same for the other matters explicitly taught — and this is straightforward.
