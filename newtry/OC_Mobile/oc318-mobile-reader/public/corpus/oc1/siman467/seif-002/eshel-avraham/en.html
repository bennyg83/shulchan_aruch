And if they cracked open and their cracking is not recognizable, and they mixed in a heap, they are forbidden even after Pesach and forbidden to sell to a non-Jew; and when he ate from it one-in-a-hundred it is permitted to eat them on Pesach; see Yoreh De'ah siman 96: you have no matzah that does not have more than the measure of prohibition, and it is two-and-two; Bach in the name of Avi HaEzri; and he disagrees on the ground that that remaining any-amount forbids the whole matzah; see the end of siman 461; and see Magen Avraham what he wrote on Kol Bo's words; and see Baer Heitev s.k. 9.
But to eat them. Taz and Peri Chadash raised a difficulty: behold even regarding Torah [law] we rely on a double doubt, all the more so regarding a rabbinic any-amount; and see siman 453 se'if 3; and one must say it deals with a case where there is not sixty against the prohibition; and see there until he concludes that it is forbidden to eat them since there is no loss in keeping them; and accordingly when he has no other grain it is permitted. And Shach in Yoreh De'ah wrote the reason: since according to Mar Ukva even if they did not crack open it is forbidden, and it is a double doubt of dispute and not of factual reality. And according to Chok Yaakov this double doubt is more lenient, and he agreed with Taz; see there. While speaking of him I will also recall what I heard from the mouth of (the great famous Gaon, av beis din and rosh yeshiva Moreinu HaRav R' Shaul, light of Israel and the Diaspora, of the Ashkenazi community of Amsterdam, may it be built up) some matters that were newly [explained] to him in the beis midrash Rishon LeTzion. He preached on what Magen Avraham brought in siman 442 s.k. 1: the Tur wrote that anything not forbidden to eat according to the Torah is permitted to mix and keep, and so wrote Semak; but Tosafos at the beginning of Pesachim wrote that it is forbidden, for the Sages did not distinguish, and proved this from the mishnah "si'ur must be burned," and even though it is noksheh and there is no Torah prohibition in eating it, still it must be burned by rabbinic [law], etc.; and according to the Tur one must say that [case] of si'ur deals with Yom Tov, etc. (end quote). And it is clear to every reader that this answer is very forced. But in my humble opinion there is no difficulty on the Tur's view; on the contrary, Tosafos's words need explanation: for Peri Chadash [raised] from that which R' Giddel said in Rav's name: One who betroths from the sixth hour onward, even with Kurdish wheat, we are not concerned for his kiddushin, for it is noksheh chametz, and even so we are not concerned for his kiddushin; if so it is evident that even before its time, namely from the sixth hour until evening, the Sages forbade noksheh chametz even for benefit; and on folio 28 R' Yehuda holds that before its time and after its time are equal in every matter regarding full chametz according to the Torah. If so, just as the Sages decreed on noksheh chametz before its time to forbid it for benefit, so too they decreed to forbid noksheh chametz after its time for benefit according to R' Yehuda; and since that of "si'ur must be burned" R' Yehuda taught, as on folio 48, see there, there is no proof from there at all. For according to R' Yehuda, even if he keeps it for after its time it would be rabbinically forbidden for benefit just as it is rabbinically forbidden before its time; therefore it must be burned. But for us, who hold before its time like R' Yehuda and after its time like R' Shimon, that full chametz after Pesach is forbidden only as a fine where he transgressed "bal yeraeh," but regarding noksheh chametz where he did not transgress "bal yeraeh" they did not decree; which is not so before its time, when full chametz is forbidden according to the Torah, therefore they decreed on noksheh rabbinically; accordingly one who betroths from the sixth hour onward even with noksheh: we are not concerned for the kiddushin; but after its time noksheh chametz is permitted even rabbinically since he did not transgress "bal yeraeh"; if so it is permitted to keep it even l'chatchila in order to eat it after Pesach; and the same applies through mixture, which is similar to noksheh; and there is no difficulty on the Tur at all; and what Tosafos wrote at the beginning of Pesachim needs explanation; and I expanded elsewhere. This [and another]: siman 444 in Magen Avraham s.k. 9: It is astonishing on what I wrote above in siman 249 that it is forbidden to make an engagement feast on erev Shabbos, and it is from the Yerushalmi, etc. (end quote). I do not know what difficulty is so great: behold it is explicit in Shulchan Aruch there: a feast and drinking that one is not accustomed to on weekdays; but if he makes an engagement feast in a way that he is also accustomed on weekdays it is permitted; and one can say that this is what is dealt with here. And see further as Hagahos Ashri explained in siman 554 s.k. 12; see there. And further (a third): in 444 Magen Avraham s.k. 12: "If so it implies that in the sixth hour when one cannot nullify he should return" (end quote). See Sefer Even HaOzer newly printed in the Magen Avraham of the Proops press, who expanded to disagree with this, and one of his proofs is from the teaching "One who goes to slaughter his Pesach," and there even where one cannot nullify, etc., for Pesach is more severe, a positive commandment that carries kares, etc., and certainly it overrides the positive commandment "you shall rest," and the same for all those taught together (end quote). And I did not grasp the depth of his intent at all, for certainly one cannot make the Pesach so long as chametz is in his possession and he did not at least nullify, for "you shall not slaughter upon chametz the blood of My sacrifice" is written, and we hold [this] even if it is not with him in the Azarah. And if so, on the contrary, that of "One who goes to slaughter his Pesach" certainly deals with a time when he can still nullify; and the same for the other matters taught together; and this is plain. And fourth (I know) from the famous Gaon aforementioned and more than them, but the page is too short to contain it: siman 451 se'if 4 in the gloss; see Taz and what they expanded on this. And in my humble opinion those who are lenient are R' Eliezer of Metz and Avi HaEzri, who hold chametz is called "permitted that absorbed," and hagalah or light libun suffices for it, as in Hagahos Maimoniyos chapter 2 of Hilchos Chametz (end quote); and on this the Rama wrote that they practice like the first view to consider chametz like "forbidden that absorbed," which needs full libun; and specifically for things that need libun by law regarding other prohibitions, the same regarding chametz they practice so, because we consider chametz like "forbidden that absorbed"; but for things that we do libun only as a stringency, like that of se'if 6: even though Rabbeinu Yoel holds there that full libun is needed for other prohibitions except for meat-and-milk, nevertheless since other poskim do not hold so even for other prohibitions to require full libun; only that we are stringent to require full libun for other prohibitions like Rabbeinu Yoel: nevertheless regarding chametz, which for many poskim is called "permitted that absorbed," we practice for libun to be lenient with light libun in that of se'if 6, as appears to me clearly as the Rama's intent, and it is correct for law. And what Taz wrote to distinguish between intact and absorbed, see there: it is difficult for me, for skewers and grates of consecrated [items] they roast intact meat on them, and even so hagalah suffices for them because "permitted that absorbed," as at the end of Avodah Zarah, and from there comes the law of "permitted that absorbed"; if so one cannot distinguish thus; and this requires further study.
