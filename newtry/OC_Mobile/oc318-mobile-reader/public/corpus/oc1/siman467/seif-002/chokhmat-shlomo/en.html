(There seif 8 in the gloss) And the custom in these countries not to eat honey, etc. I would say: behold an incident came before me from a certain wealthy man from this country who brought a drink called rum resembling arrack which gentiles are more sharp than wine, and he brought it from the city of London and also a kosher letter from the rabbi of the community of London, and there it says how this drink is made in the Indies in a manner that has no leavening concern at all; and in the community of London they were accustomed forty years to drink this rum on Pesach, and the rabbi of London and this wealthy man requested from me to give my agreement and the force of my decree to certify it also in our country on Pesach; and this is what I answered to your son among your people: far be it from me to put my hand in this to breach the fence and in my opinion it is forbidden on Pesach — not only according to Magen Avraham in this siman who holds that honey in its own form is forbidden if it was already crushed, and necessarily they decreed on this lest it be confused with other honey; even though in other honey there is no severe prohibition, only a distant concern — nevertheless they decreed on this lest it be confused; if so here too with this drink; and also according to the Taz who permits honey in its own form and is not concerned lest it be confused — from the place it appears that this drink is different from honey, for this resembles what Magen Avraham wrote in this siman seif 10 regarding saffron in the name of the Rashba, that even what he grew in his house is forbidden because of a decree, meaning since saffron is not common to grow in our countries and they will think it is saffron brought from afar; but fruits that a Jew dried are permitted and there is no concern for confusion (because it is common in our country, but this drink) and like honey from the hive as mentioned above — end quote. And if so this itself is the Taz's reason to permit honey and there is no concern for confusion because it is common in our country, but this drink which is not common in our country resembles exactly the law of saffron, and even though we know it was made with certification it is forbidden because of a decree lest it be confused; so too here; and why is it not similar to Pesach wine which they are accustomed to permit to drink and are not concerned lest it be confused with chametz wine — because the permission for this wine is common in our country, but these drinks which are not common in our country one must be concerned lest it be confused. And further it appears that also in wine they did not permit in early days except because of prevention of Yom Tov joy, for in their time good wine was not common in our country and wine was prevalent among everyone, and it was prevention of Yom Tov joy to forbid it; therefore because of this they established it on the law that it is permitted; but drinking this rum is not equal for everyone and there is no prevention of Yom Tov joy in this, and also now in our country wine is cheaper than these drinks; therefore it is fit to be concerned lest it be confused. And further it appears more — this resembles what the poskim wrote above siman 463 that one should not knead the pots themselves with baked matzah in a place where there are workers and breaches are common — all the more so here in our generation which is very unrestrained and breaches are very common, one may not be lenient in such a case, and it is more severe than a decree on baked matzah; and also resembles what the poskim wrote in siman 460 that one may not be lenient in what the early ones were stringent to make panadish or paladish — see there; and this too is only because of a decree and concern for confusion; and the same here; and also the matter of kitniyot species that the Sages forbade is only because of concern for confusion; and the same here. And further it appears that what they permitted Pesach wine is according to the majority, for the taste of Pesach wine differs from the taste of chametz wine and one can easily distinguish the taste between them; therefore they are not concerned lest it be confused; and behold nevertheless after they erred and deceived Israel — they lost their souls by not paying attention to distinguish between this and that; but at least the majority, since if they pay attention they can easily distinguish, therefore they are not concerned lest it be confused. But in this drink the taste is not recognizable between those from chametz and those from other things; also since it is not common in our country and they are not expert in its taste to distinguish between this and that — certainly one must be concerned lest it be confused; and one cannot say since arrack in general is made from rum as several people said, if so there is no decree on a drink that if arrack itself arrack is forbidden only because of the custom of kitniyot and it is a decree on a decree — this is not so, for apart from what we find on Pesach they were stringent stringency upon stringency and decreed on this a decree on a decree, as is clear from several places, one of them in siman 447 regarding taste imparted for spoilage, that even taste imparted by taste and any amount is forbidden — behold each one by itself is also not forbidden except because of Pesach stringency, nevertheless they decreed even when both combine together although it is a decree on a decree. And apart from this it appears it is one decree, for both arrack and this drink, even if made from rum, we decree on this lest it be confused with that made from chametz — all is one decree. And further it appears that its main prohibition here is because of concern for mixtures, lest chametz wine become mixed in, as they forbade gentile milk because of concern for mixtures of something impure, and they also forbade gentile morayis because of mixtures of stam yeinam; if so here too they forbade arrack because of concern for mixtures of chametz wine, and so with rum it is forbidden because of mixtures of wine; and if so since there is a trace of d'oraisa prohibition, even if one is known not to have mixed chametz wine in, it is forbidden because of concern for confusion, even though with morayis they did not decree on this; on chametz they were more stringent, and especially there stam yeinam is rabbinic unlike chametz which has Torah prohibition — and this is exactly like the law of saffron forbidden because they drip wine on it, and the Rashba forbade even what he grew in his house because of concern for confusion; and the same here; and it is not similar to Pesach wine for which they did not so decree for the reason mentioned; and further one can say on the contrary — chametz wine whose prohibition is more severe and it is known that it is chametz itself — if one were lenient with Pesach wine they would not err to permit chametz wine. And it resembles what we say in general: these are words of Torah and need no reinforcement; these are words of the Scribes and need reinforcement; so too here — arrack itself, since it is itself from kitniyot species, therefore it is not severe in their eyes and is only because of concern for mixtures of wine in it, and is only a rabbinic decree; therefore it needs more reinforcement in this, even when known not to have been mixed in; and if not this law does not stand; and especially in our generation, according to the breach of the generation — would that we had in our power to forbid even Pesach wine, how good it would be, and how many stumbling blocks already follow from this; only that it is not in our power to decree what is already breached; but since we are ashamed of the old ones, we are reluctant to add the new and breach further to permit also arrack — far be it; and in a small matter it will lead to leniency to drink all arrack on Pesach; therefore we are reluctant for our hand to be with this to uphold this certification and permit to conduct with permission what our fathers did not contemplate; these are the words of the speaker in truth for the honor of the Torah without destroying its foundation to dust — may the good God send us the teacher of righteousness and instruct us according to law and halachah, for the sake of the holy soul Shlomo Kluger, Sunday 28 Adar Rishon year "so that you may be vindicated in your words" here in Brod:
