(S.k. 7) Only, etc. Specifically that which is in barrels; and Eliyah Rabbah and Chok Yaakov wrote: specifically when it is in a barrel with the wax it is nullified in sixty at cooking time: meaning even though the honey itself is not sixty, nevertheless at cooking time they add water and there is certainly sixty; therefore they do not permit the honey plain even when it is in barrels; and likewise to make the drink if the honey was not in a barrel, namely that which they bring in small vessels, is also forbidden: for even to make a drink where they add water, nevertheless there is not sixty, for in those small vessels they put much flour there.
And to eat the honey plain is forbidden in every case: meaning even if the non-Jew extracted it from the hive before us and we knew he did not mix flour into it, which would be permitted by law: even so it is forbidden lest it be confused with other honey; so it is in the Bach.
And it is forbidden to make the drink on Pesach. For on Pesach it is not nullified in sixty, for it forbids in any amount. And specifically if it was cooked before Pesach and nullified, it does not afterward return and revive on Pesach, for liquid does not return and revive; and see Taz.
And the Levush, etc. But honey by itself, etc., is forbidden to eat since they did not add water and there is not sixty. And it appears to me that one who is lenient has not lost, for generally they mix only a little, and also the honey alone has sixty.
However, honey, etc. This too is from the Levush's language.
He was precise, etc. Because that is forbidden, etc., and it is fitting to be stringent as the Levush wrote above this; and it appears to me that one who is lenient has not [lost], and one who is stringent should be stringent, and may blessing come upon him.
Only that which they bring in combs, etc. And according to the Bach this too is forbidden lest it be confused, as above; and the Levush is not concerned for this; and see Magen Avraham s.k. 10.
And some practice, etc. And according to the Bach's words it is forbidden lest it be confused; and all according to the custom. And without this the later authorities wrote that honey of all lands is not the same; see Chok Yaakov and Eliyah Rabbah.
