Even. See the Taz in the name of the Bach, who wrote that at least softening is required. The reason is that some are stringent out of concern to rule like Mar Ukva in Pesachim 40a, that anything left on top of the grain and splitting is forbidden, and we are not expert. But if it did not soften and remains hard, we are expert that it did not reach leavening. See the Pri Chadash here.
An incident. In the holy community of Komarno they made round cakes called kneidlach from ground matzah, kneaded with goose fat, salt, and pepper without any water, fried them in fat surrounding them on every side, then placed them in a bowl and poured boiling gravy from a primary vessel over them. A softened wheat kernel was found in one of them.
Response. If the kernel merely softened and did not split, it appears that the food is permitted. What should we be concerned for? Fruit juice with eggs does not leaven, and a little salt, even ours, once its form has changed, does not cause leavening; see siman 462 in the Magen Avraham, seif katan 6. Melted goose fat has already lost the rinsing water. See also siman 467. Therefore, in this case the softened kernel alone does not forbid.
If. If the round cakes were put in a bowl, cut, and gravy was poured over them, and a softened wheat kernel was found, there is room to forbid because of pouring from a primary vessel. All the more so, some are stringent even without the time of a mil, for hot water has no such measure. Although the item had already been cooked in fat, perhaps it was not fully cooked. Certainly if the kernel is cut and white flour is found inside, we see that it was not properly cooked. See siman 463 in the Magen Avraham, seif katan 4. With Hashem's help this will be explained further.
