(41) It is permitted to sell it to a non-Jew. After he removed the wheat kernel from it and only the taste of chametz that the wheat kernel released into the chicken remains; and he should not keep it in his house lest he come to eat it.
(42) And in these lands, etc. And in a place of very major financial loss the later authorities agreed to be lenient like the Mechaber's view as written above at the beginning of siman 447.
(43) And if it was found, etc. That is even if the mixture was made before the eighth day; and the same applies even if he transgressed and kept it until this day.
(44) It is permitted to keep it. Because it is a doubt of Yom Tov versus weekday, and a mixture of any amount is rabbinic; therefore we are lenient to keep it until after Pesach, and then it is permitted even for eating. (And some say that even on the seventh of Pesach if a cracked wheat kernel is found in a cooked dish or in a chicken one covers them with a vessel until Motzaei Yom Tov and then eats the cooked dish or chicken and burns the wheat kernel; and in a place of loss one may rely on this.) And the same applies regarding [chametz that] gives a spoiled taste: even though we are stringent on Pesach as explained in siman 447, nevertheless it is permitted to keep it; but regarding full chametz which is forbidden according to the Torah, even on the last day it is forbidden to keep it.
