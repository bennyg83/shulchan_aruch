The incident, etc., a gentile is not believed even when speaking offhandedly:

After he sold them — and all the time they are in the gentile's possession the gentile is believed; but in what he says that he washed them to enhance his purchase — Shach brought there the dispute of the codifiers whether he is believed. But Kaf HaChayyim wrote here that it requires study whether what he says that he already washed them — one might say to enhance his purchase; on the contrary one might say in this his purchase is diminished rather than enhanced:

For after washing they are more beautiful than before washing; if so, if he had said he had not yet washed them — nevertheless they are beautiful on their own account — thus he would have praised his purchase:

As the Bach, etc., it is forbidden to leave them even if there are sixty, for chametz on Pesach in any amount; and even if he transgressed and left them they are forbidden because he transgressed Beit Yosef — meaning the prohibition in any amount is only d'rabbanan, namely for eating; but regarding that he transgressed Beit Yosef — for the chametz is in his possession — such is in the Bach; but we do not hold thus, as explained above siman 447; and so wrote Magen Avraham in this seif katan; and Magen Avraham did not bring all his words but only to learn from his words one law. And it is what he wrote in this seif katan: and one may learn that if, etc., as written in Yoreh De'ah siman 110: things that are not nullified because of their importance — nevertheless if one was lost or one was eaten of them, the remainder are permitted two by two, for there are two sides to permit: one, perhaps that one which was lost was the prohibition and there is no prohibition at all; and if you say the prohibition remained here — nevertheless since they eat two by two, in every eating there is one of them certainly permitted; and as one of them that was permitted, so too the second:

And in my humble opinion safek sefeika 447 in the name of Rashba that nevertheless it is like nullifying a prohibition l'chatchila, and Magen Avraham agreed there that even if he does not forbid more than the amount of removal — therefore it is properly two by two, for in every matzah there is more of the permitted than the prohibition, even though the baking of the permitted with the prohibition is in the same matzah; nevertheless he holds even if he does not do more than the amount of removal — nevertheless the permitted remains more than the prohibition and it is two by two. Unlike for us who hold that in its place it forbids everything; if so you are concerned the prohibition was not lost and the prohibition is in that matzah — if so there is no two by two, for the small amount of prohibition forbade the entire matzah. And according to what is written siman 447 seif 4 seif katan 11 and so safek sefeika 447: since it was already nullified in the majority and permitted to eat, so too before Pesach one may add to it up to sixty and it is not called nullifying a prohibition; and even though on Pesach it returns and revives — therefore he wrote grind them, and flour in flour is wet in wet; and we hold in siman 447 seif 4 that wet in wet does not return and revive; and grinding too is not nullifying a prohibition, for his intent is not to nullify but only to grind for the need of baking, as written above safek sefeika 447; and see there seif 453 and so in Yoreh De'ah siman 109 that flour in flour is called wet in wet:
