(15) We are not concerned, etc. So long as we do not see in them a sign of chametzing, because we follow the majority and the majority do not become chametz; and even those heaps on which we saw that rains fell, we attribute for leniency: perhaps the rains entered only the upper sheaves but not the belly of the heap; and even if they entered, perhaps they did not manage to become chametz. And nevertheless if one sees in the wheat brought before him that there are sprouted ones among them, even the Mechaber agrees that one must check if there is sixty against them; and it is plain that when they take the wheat to sell and rains fall on the sacks until the sack is almost entirely soaked in water and the wheat too became moist, they are forbidden by law on Pesach, for the defect is revealed to the eye; and one must warn about this, for the sellers are not careful about this since most are not learned in Torah; and one must also warn grain sellers in a year when rains were abundant or when water came down on the sacks: then they are obligated to sell the grain as the law of selling chametz, and they are forbidden to sell of them to a non-Jew after the time of its prohibition (Chayei Adam).
(16) Regarding ordinary wheat, etc. Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah wrote as practical law that he permitted buying wheat for Pesach even if they were detained in pits, so long as we did not see a change in their appearance and they are not moist; and the later authorities copied him.
