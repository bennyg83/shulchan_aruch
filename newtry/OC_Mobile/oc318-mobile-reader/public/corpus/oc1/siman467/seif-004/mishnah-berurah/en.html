(15) We are not concerned, etc. — any that we do not see in them a sign of leavening, since we follow the majority and the majority do not become leavened; and also those stacks where we saw rain fell upon them — we suspend to be lenient, lest the rain entered only in the upper sheaves but not in the belly of the stack; and if you say it entered, perhaps it did not have time to leaven. And nevertheless if one sees in the wheat brought before him that there are sprouts, the Mechaber too concedes one must check whether there are sixty against them; and it is obvious when one brings wheat to sell and rain descends on the sacks until the sack is almost entirely steeped in water and the wheat too became moist — they are forbidden by law on Pesach, for the corruption is revealed to the eyes, and one must warn about this because sellers are not careful about this since most of them are not Torah scholars; and one must also warn grain sellers that in a year when rains were abundant or water fell on the sacks — then they are obligated to sell the grain according to the law of selling chametz, and it is forbidden to sell from them to a gentile after its time of prohibition [Chayei Adam]:
