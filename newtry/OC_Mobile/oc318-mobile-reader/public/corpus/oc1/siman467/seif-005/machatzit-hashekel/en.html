(S.k. 5) We are not concerned that they go, etc. Know that what Beis Yosef wrote in se'if 5 regarding grain, etc., and it does not need suction, etc., and it accepts chametzing: these are the Rashba's words on what is written in Pesachim 33a that if one separated terumah of chametz it has no sanctity, for it is written "give it to him" and not to his fire: that is specifically where it had no time of fitness, such as when it became chametz while attached. And it was difficult for the Rashba: if so we should not eat matzah, for certainly rains fell on the wheat while attached.
And therefore he wrote specifically when they dried and no longer need the ground, then they accept chametzing. And in responsa Me'il Tzedakah siman 69 he disputed the Rashba with proofs and wrote: behold Abaye said on folio 40a: This pot in which they dry toasted grain (Rashi: a vessel in which they dry roasted grains in an oven), if inverted (that is, the pot's mouth downward) it is permitted, for the water coming out of the roasted grains does not make chametz, for by force of their dripping they do not make chametz; but if upright with the pot's mouth upward it is forbidden, for the water remains in the pot and makes chametz. And Rava said even if upright it is permitted, for fruit juice does not make chametz; and although we hold like Rava, nevertheless it is possible Rava too agrees with Abaye in this, that by force of their dripping they do not make chametz; only that he added to permit even upright because fruit juice does not make chametz. And if so one can say that even if the wheat still needs the ground they nevertheless accept chametzing; only that ordinarily we say by force of their dripping they do not make chametz: unless there was a sweeping rain until it bent the grain and it lies on the face of the earth, for then "by force of their dripping" does not apply; or we saw that it sprouted or cracked open while attached; and such a case is what the Gemara deals with when it said it became chametz while attached: for example where we saw they sprouted while attached; but ordinarily, even if they no longer need ground suction, we are not concerned lest it became chametz even if rains fell, for we say by force of their dripping it does not make chametz. And he wrote that one may rely on this ruling, all the more so when it is a time of need; see there where he expanded.
