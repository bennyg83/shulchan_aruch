And if it did not actually split. Even if it softened until it was close to splitting, it is also permitted. See what I wrote above in seif katan 7, where the matter is explained with its reason.
The cooked dish is permitted. The Magen Avraham wrote that the Beit Yosef cites the Kol Bo that if three barley kernels are found in a cooked dish, there is a presumption that there are many more, perhaps split ones. The Mechaber omitted this in Shulchan Aruch because we do not rule this way. The Taz in Yoreh De'ah, siman 84, seif 9, wrote in the name of Maharshal that such a presumption is said only in cases where the matter is commonly so, not in every case.
Rather, they forbid everything with a minute amount. Since the Rama did not place this gloss immediately after the Mechaber's statement that those wheat or barley kernels are burned, where he should have written that everything is burned, as he writes nearby in seif 10, it appears that the Rama's view is that where they did not actually split, since the great authorities permit the dish, he does not require burning everything, but only forbids by stringency where applicable.
Everything with a minute amount. The vessels used with them, and the dishes later prepared in those vessels, are also forbidden, even though there are two levels of minute admixture. So wrote the Magen Avraham, unlike the Taz, as I wrote above in siman 447, seif katan 5.
