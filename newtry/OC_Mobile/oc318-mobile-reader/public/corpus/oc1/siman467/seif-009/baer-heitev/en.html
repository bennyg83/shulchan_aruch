Everything. The lid and the spoon and also the vessels used with them; and the same applies to other cooked dishes into which some of that cooked dish mixed, or with which those vessels were used: all is forbidden; and so they practice, even though there are two "any amounts," [so rule] the later authorities, and not like Taz, as above in siman 447 s.k. 20; see there. And Chok Yaakov wrote: since the Rav did not write here "they burn everything" as he wrote in se'if 10, it certainly appears that the Rav's view where they did not truly crack open is like all the great poskim to permit the cooked dish even for eating; if so, this stringency suffices to forbid eating but not benefit; see there; and see Taz at the end of this siman and in s.k. 15; and the Bach wrote that in any case we require that the wheat kernel softened, for otherwise one should not forbid at all; and so wrote Taz.
