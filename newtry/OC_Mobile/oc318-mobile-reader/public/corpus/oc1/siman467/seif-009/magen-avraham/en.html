If they did not split open. For since there is a dispute in the Gemara — some say permitted without splitting open, and rabbinic prohibition in any amount — we follow uncertainties to leniency (Rif); it implies if mixed in less than sixty it is forbidden — see Yoreh De'ah siman 98; and Hagahot Maimon questioned: behold in seif 2 he ruled even if they did not split open it is forbidden; and one might say specifically to eat wheat kernels as they are, but to forbid the cooked dish they were not stringent; and it is difficult, since they are lenient only because of dispute, and necessarily they disagree only regarding what descends below, but where they rested at ease all agree it is forbidden — if so why were they lenient in the cooked dish? And one can say: he wrote above it comes to leavening — it implies they will come to leavening but are not yet certainly chametz, only uncertainty — therefore they permit the cooked dish; see Tur. The Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Kol Bo: if three barley kernels are found in a cooked dish, presumption there is much and perhaps they split open (Darkei Moshe); and it appears to me they omitted this intentionally in Shulchan Aruch, for we do not hold thus, for the Taz wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 84 seif 9 in the name of Rashal that we do not say thus except regarding worms that multiply from their kind, but regarding a matter that comes from outside no; and further, one can check if barley is in it; however in a pile of wheat over which rain fell, one can say this reasoning — see there seif 2:

They forbid everything. The cover and the spoon — and similarly other cooked dishes mixed from that cooked dish; and so they practice (Bach); and so the Tur in the name of Rokeach: a wheat kernel found in a pot — the spoon with which they stirred in it is forbidden; if placed in other pots, all are forbidden — end of his words; and so siman 12; and the Taz in Yoreh De'ah siman 92 wrote that if a piece was forbidden from chametz in any amount and fell into another pot, it does not forbid through its release — see there; and he was excessive in his measures that everywhere they practice to forbid; and so Mahari Cohen in Hagahot Orach Chayyim of the Rama; and even though the piece does not impart taste, nevertheless regarding the prohibition it forbids in any amount; and there are ramifications, such as a thing made for its taste is not nullified, and where it is not made for taste it is nullified — chametz within its time in any amount, and after its time in sixty; or regarding if mixed in its species and not in its species — we say it ascended from it as if it is not — end of his words; and so the Bach; and see in the Drisha — nevertheless what he wrote chametz within its time, etc. — explanation: if any amount of chametz mixed in a cooked dish during Pesach and he kept it until after Pesach and mixed from that cooked dish into another cooked dish, sixty is not required, only against the any amount; and it is difficult, for the Bach himself wrote in siman 447 that a thing forbidden in any amount that passed over Pesach is permitted without mixture; and in Sefer Nikudat HaKesef it is written: and if it was intentional — meaning when it passed intentionally it is forbidden; but according to siman 447 even if intentional it is permitted — see there; also what he wrote siman 55 that chametz b'dieved forbids in its species in any amount because it is deishilam — the Talmud Yerushalmi already decided it is not deishilam; and so siman 461 regarding double matzah; also what he wrote dry with dry is not nullified on Pesach because deishilam, and not in its species is nullified in sixty — he did not examine the Mordechai that wrote explicitly the reason: as moist in any amount, so dry; and so Yoreh De'ah, and so Talmud Yerushalmi; and if so even not in its species it is not nullified; and even a piece forbidden because of any amount that became mixed with others dry — all are forbidden:
