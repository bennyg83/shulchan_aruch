They did not split. Since the Gemara records a dispute, with some permitting kernels that did not split, and the prohibition of even the smallest amount is D'Rabbanan, we rule leniently in a doubt, as Rif writes. This implies that if it became mixed in less than sixty, it is forbidden. See Yoreh Deah siman 98. Hagahos Maimoniyos asks that in seif 2 it was ruled forbidden even if they did not split. It answers that this stringency applies only to eating the wheat itself, but they were not stringent enough to prohibit a cooked dish. This is difficult, for the leniency rests only on the dispute, and the dispute appears to concern soaked grain. Where the kernels lie at rest, all agree that they are forbidden. Why, then, are we lenient regarding the cooked dish? One may answer that the earlier wording, ‘they may come to become chametz,’ implies that they will become chametz but are not certainly chametz yet. The cooked dish is therefore permitted. See Tur. Beit Yosef cites Kol Bo that if three barley kernels are found in a cooked dish, it is presumed that many are present and perhaps some split. Darchei Moshe cites this. It appears to me that Shulchan Aruch intentionally omitted it because we do not rule so. Taz in Yoreh Deah 84:9 cites Maharshal that such a presumption applies only to worms, which reproduce from within, but not to something that comes from outside. Moreover, the dish can be examined for barley. In a pile of wheat upon which rain fell, however, this reasoning may apply. See seif 2 and what is written there.
They prohibit everything. This includes the lid and spoon, and likewise other cooked dishes into which some of this dish was mixed. This is the custom, as Bach writes. Tur likewise cites Rokeach: If a wheat kernel is found in a pot, the spoon used to stir it becomes forbidden; if the spoon was then placed in other pots, they all become forbidden. The same is implied at the end of seif 12. Taz in Yoreh Deah siman 92 writes that if a piece became forbidden by the smallest amount of chametz and fell into another pot, it does not prohibit that pot through the flavor it emits. He has gone too far, for everywhere the custom is to forbid it. I likewise found this written in Rama's gloss to Issur VeHeter. Although the piece itself does not become intrinsically forbidden, the absorbed prohibition nevertheless prohibits even in the smallest amount. This has practical consequences, such as where an ingredient added for flavor is never nullified, while where it is not added for flavor, chametz during its prohibited period prohibits in any amount and after that period requires sixty; or where it became mixed with its own kind and another kind, in which case we regard its own kind as though absent. Bach writes likewise. What Rama writes that chametz during its prohibited period, etc., means that if a minute amount of chametz became mixed into a cooked dish during Pesach, and he retained it until after Pesach, and some of that dish then became mixed into another dish, one requires sixty only against the original minute amount. This is difficult, for Bach himself writes in siman 447 that something prohibited by the smallest amount and retained over Pesach is permitted after Pesach without any mixture. Nekudas HaKesef explains that it was retained intentionally, meaning that intentional retention makes it forbidden. Yet according to what is written at the end of siman 447, even intentional retention is permitted. See there. Also, what he writes at the end of siman 55, that chametz on Erev Pesach prohibits its own kind in any amount because it is an item that will become permitted, was already rejected in Toras Chatas, which rules that it is not treated as such an item. The same is implied in siman 461 regarding a folded matzah. He also writes that dry matter mixed with dry matter is not nullified on Pesach because it will become permitted, while unlike kinds are nullified in sixty. He did not see Mordechai, who writes explicitly that just as a liquid mixture is prohibited in any amount, so too is a dry mixture. This is also ruled in Yoreh Deah and Toras Chatas. Accordingly, even unlike kinds are not nullified, and even if a piece became forbidden by the smallest amount and was then mixed dry with other pieces, they are all forbidden.
