Se'if 9: A cooked dish. In chapter "Kol Sha'ah," 40a: The Sages taught: One may not soak, etc. Rav Chisda said: [Only] if they cracked open, etc.; and Shmuel said, etc. And we hold in the first chapter of Avodah Zarah: for a Torah [question] follow the stringent, and for a rabbinic [question] follow the lenient; and so here, for a mixture of any amount is rabbinic.
Or wheat. To exclude the Ra'ah, who forbids wheat according to all opinions even if they did not crack open at all, since they have [exuding] juices; and so wrote Hagahos Ashri there s.v. "And some say," etc., and he brings a proof from the last chapter of Avodah Zarah 65b. And that is not so, for the conclusion of chapter "Kol Sha'ah" is that wheat is more lenient than barley, and that [sugya] of Avodah Zarah is not relevant here. And he further brings a proof from that boat that sank in soaking-water, and they did not require there that they had cracked open; and the Ran wrote that it is not a proof, for there were cracked ones among them; and see above se'if 2. And one also cannot say that wheat, even if cracked, is permitted, for if so why do we need guarding for soaking? But in the Tosefta chapter 3 it implies a distinction between wheat and barley, for it teaches there: One who soaks wheat and barley in water and they became chametz: they are forbidden; R' Yose says: One who soaks barley in water and they cracked open: they are forbidden; and in vinegar, etc.; and it is taught in the name of R' Yose because of the latter clause "and in vinegar," etc., which he himself said, as written in the Gemara.
The cooked dish is permitted. For in a rabbinic matter we follow the lenient view, as written in the first chapter of Avodah Zarah; and even though its root is according to the Torah, as written in the third chapter, where we say "I say: since it is not present as it entered," even though its root is from the Torah (Ran).
And the custom is not, etc. For they practice like Mar Ukva, and we are not expert in the measure fitting for cracking open. And see Sefer Mitzvos Gadol and Mordechai. And it appears to me that the reason for the custom is as written in se'if 2 regarding this grain, etc.; and the Rif holds that even in such a case we require that they cracked open; and regarding the incident of the boat, as the Ran wrote, there were cracked ones there, as above; and likewise in the Tosefta, "One who soaks barley," etc., as above.
