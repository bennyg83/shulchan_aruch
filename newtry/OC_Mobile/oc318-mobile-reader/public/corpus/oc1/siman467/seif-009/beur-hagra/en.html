Seif 9 — cooked dish. In chapter Kol Sha'ah 40a — taught: one may not soak, etc., R' Chananel: they did not split open, etc., and Shmuel said, etc., and we conclude in chapter 1 of Avodah Zarah regarding Torah law we follow the stringent and regarding scribal law we follow the lenient — and so too here since mixtures of any amount is rabbinic:

Or wheat. To exclude from Ra'em who forbids wheat per his words even if they did not split open at all since they have splitting, and so wrote Eliyah Rabbah there s.v. ve'yeish omrim, etc., and he brings proof from the latter chapter of Avodah Zarah 65b — but it is not so, for the conclusion there of Kol Sha'ah is that wheat is lighter than barley, and that case in Avodah Zarah is not relevant here; and further he brings proof from that cellar steeped in chaff and they did not require there split open, and the Ran wrote that is not proof since there they were split open — and see below seif 2; and also one should not read that wheat even if split open is permitted, for if so the guarding below is unnecessary. But in Tosefta chapter 3 it implies a distinction between wheat and barley, for it teaches there: one soaks wheat and barley in water and they leavened — forbidden; R' Yose says: one who soaks barley in water and they split open — forbidden, and in vinegar, etc., and it teaches it in the name of R' Yose from the latter part — and in vinegar, etc., for he stated it as written in the Gemara:

The cooked dish is permitted. For in rabbinic law we follow leniency, as written in chapter 1 of Avodah Zarah, even though its essence is from Torah law as written in chapter 5 that we say it is different since it does not exist as in normal circumstances — even though its essence is from Torah — Ran:

And the custom is not, etc. That they conduct like Mar Ukva and we are not expert in the measure fit for splitting. Semag and Mordechai.
