(36) If they actually split open — behold in the Gemara there is a dispute regarding soaking barley in water: some hold it is not chametz until they split open from it; and some hold that if they are swollen so much that they are close to splitting, they are also forbidden; and we decide stringently regarding eating them in their own form, for it is forbidden even if they did not actually split open; but regarding forbidding the cooked dish in which the kernels are found, which is only a slight rabbinic prohibition — we decide leniently that one does not forbid until they actually split open; this is the explanation of the Mechaber's view:

(37) Forbidden — even in benefit. And also all vessels used with this cooked dish while it was still hot where the hand is scalded in it — they are forbidden to use on Pesach; rather one must set them aside until after Pesach, as was explained at the beginning of siman 447. And if a little of this cooked dish became mixed into another cooked dish, that cooked dish too is forbidden; and likewise if one used with it a spoon with which he stirred in this pot and placed it in another pot while it was boiling — all is forbidden, for since its prohibition is in any amount, we say that here too it releases something:

(38) Rather they forbid everything — that is, the cooked dish and also all vessels used to cook it, as stated above according to the Mechaber's view when split open. And behold the view of the Taz in note 15 that the cooked dish is forbidden even in benefit; however Kaf HaChayim and several other Acharonim agreed that in a place of great loss one may rely to permit at least in benefit:

(39) Even if they did not split open — since the Rama's rule implies we do not distinguish whether it is close to splitting or not, because we are not expert in this; nevertheless several Acharonim agreed that at least if it softened we require; and if it did not soften, even if found in hot food, one does not forbid.
