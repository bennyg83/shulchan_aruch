Se'if 11: If it was found, etc. For if it is not cracked open, according to all opinions it is permitted, for presumably it was not in water, since [the water] is guarded; and if it was in flour or dough it does not hurry to become chametz more than the dough, for when cracked open it is not itself chametz, only it hurries to become chametz more than what is written there regarding soaking in vinegar, etc. (Rosh there).
One should remove, etc. As written in chapter "Keitzad Tzolin," 75b: He should take its place; if its sauce dripped onto fine flour, one takes a handful, etc.
And there is one who, etc. See Magen Avraham. And further, because the water in the dough, etc.; and according to what the Rosh wrote regarding a "house of sourdough," that kneading is like hot water, it is like hot with sauce that forbids the whole, as written in Chullin 96b and 108a-b; and all the more so regarding baked matzah.
And they permit, etc., and we say. As written in Kesubos 75b.
And if it was found. For specifically from place to place we say "here [it was found], etc.," but not from time to time, as written there regarding while she was still in her father's house, that we do not say "now they were born"; and in Niddah 3b, one who draws up, etc.: this is only, etc. Even though from place to place Chizkiyah and R' Yochanan disagree there, for R' Yochanan holds that regarding tumah we are stringent; and see Tosafos there 4a s.v. "and who." And one can say that Chizkiyah holds that even regarding tumah we do not establish [a presumption of prohibition]; and nevertheless Chizkiyah agrees with R' Mona as written there; and see Magen Avraham s.k. 18.
But before [Pesach]. For it is nullified in sixty; and even though according to the first reason of the Rashba, that it touched here and here, one should forbid even before Pesach, one need not be concerned for this since it is cold; and see Yoreh De'ah siman 105 se'if 9 in the gloss: and if he does not know, etc.; and all the more so here. And nevertheless enough to remove is required even before Pesach, for we consider it like hot at the place of its contact, as appears to me; only that in a case of doubt we are not stringent.
