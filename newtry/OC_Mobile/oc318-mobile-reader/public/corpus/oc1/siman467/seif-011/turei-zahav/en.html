The distinction of during Pesach is in the Beit Yosef, who questioned what the Tur wrote in the name of the Rosh regarding a wheat kernel found embedded in dough or matzah — even though permitted, etc., and we are not concerned lest it was in water, because Israelites are accustomed to guard the dough, etc. — it implies that if it had been in water the matzot would be forbidden; and afterward he wrote an incident where an embedded wheat kernel was found in a vessel of water and the water is permitted; and one might say that here it deals during Pesach and that incident deals on the eve of Pesach; and they already questioned on this, for certainly that incident also deals on Pesach as the Tur implies; and I wondered — for according to the Tur, behold the Tur wrote afterward and so Avi HaEzri permitted wheat that fell into a pit, and wrote they do not leaven, etc.; and it appears the case is not comparable to the proof, for that one is specifically in dough, but an embedded wheat kernel certainly leavens in cold water like a stalk that leavens in its husk; but it appears to permit from the Tur because the water is cold the wheat kernel does not impart taste in them, and even full chametz that fell into a pit is permitted for this reason — end of his words; behold the wheat kernel is forbidden in cold and does not forbid — therefore there is difficulty on the Rosh, for if the wheat kernel had been in water it would certainly have been forbidden, and you have no permission in matzah except because the matzah is not forbidden in cold; with this it appears the Rosh holds this is specifically when resting in cold, but in kneading the dough warms somewhat through the work and compresses the wheat kernel in the hand or vessel — certainly there is concern it squeezed some taste from it and forbids; and all the more so in baking; and if so it is not necessary to distinguish between before Pesach and during Pesach. And in Maharil it is written: a wheat kernel was found on the eve of Pesach in matzah — Semak permitted removal of the place; and if a wheat kernel was found on Pesach in matzah before baking, Maharil ruled to burn it by itself in a bonfire; and if they removed the wheat kernel from it and baked it, they must burn it, and the rest of the matzot baked with it he permitted. And a wheat kernel found in dough — the entire dough is forbidden; so ruled Maharash, and Maharshal agreed in responsum siman 79, because through kneading it leavens and gives some taste; and so too the Rama's words here, and so the author of the Or Zarua — if the wheat kernel softened. And what the Rama wrote here that we say here it was found, here it was — the Levush questioned: if the wheat kernel fell there automatically it was not in water and is not forbidden at all — difficulty on the Rama; the Rama's intent here is that we are concerned lest a wheat kernel fell like a chametz kernel from elsewhere into this matzah — here it was found and here it was. And the Levush explained there is prohibition only from the wheat kernel to baked matzah, but in dough he permits because it does not leaven, and wrote the custom is not like the Rama's words to forbid in dough or in matzah that is not baked.
