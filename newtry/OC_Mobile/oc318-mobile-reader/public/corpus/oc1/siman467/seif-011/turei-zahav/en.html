And so they practice during Pesach. The distinction of "during Pesach" is in Beis Yosef, who raised a difficulty on what the Tur wrote in the name of the Rosh: when a cracked wheat kernel is found in dough or in matzah, even [if] it is permitted, etc., and we are not concerned lest it was in the water, because Jews are accustomed to guard the water, etc., implying that if it had been in the water the matzos would be forbidden; and afterward he wrote an incident of a cracked wheat kernel found in a vessel of water, that the water is permitted; and he answered that here it deals with during Pesach and that incident deals with erev Pesach. And they already raised a difficulty on this, for certainly the incident too deals with Pesach as the Tur's implication; and I wondered that there is no difficulty, for the Tur afterward wrote: and so Avi Ezri permitted wheat kernels that fell into a cistern, and wrote that they do not make chametz, etc.; and it appears to me that the case is not comparable to the proof, for that is specifically in dough, but a wheat kernel certainly becomes chametz in cold water like a boat that sank in soaking-water; rather it appears to permit for this reason: since the water is cold the wheat kernel does not impart taste into them, and even full chametz that fell into a cistern is permitted for this reason (end quote). So the wheat kernel becomes forbidden in cold and does not forbid [the water]; if so there is no difficulty on the Rosh: if the wheat kernel had been in water it would certainly be forbidden, and you have no permission for the matzah except because the matzah is not forbidden in cold; and in this it appears the Rosh holds that this is specifically when lying in cold, but in kneading, where the dough warms somewhat through the work and he mashes the wheat kernel by hand or with a utensil, certainly there is concern that he squeezed from it a taste of any amount and it forbids; and all the more so when baked; and if so it is not necessary to distinguish between before Pesach and during Pesach. And in Maharil it is written: a wheat kernel found on erev Pesach in matzah: Semak permitted with removing its place; and if a wheat kernel is found on Pesach in matzah before baking, Maharil instructed to burn it in a fire by itself; and if they removed the wheat kernel from it and baked it, one must burn it, and the other matzos baked with it he permitted. And a wheat kernel found in dough: the whole dough is forbidden; so instructed Maharash, and Maharshal agreed to this in a responsum siman 79, because through kneading it becomes chametz and imparts a taste of any amount; and so are the Rama's words here; and so ruled my master and father-in-law of blessed memory if the wheat kernel softened. And what the Rama wrote here, that we say "here it was found, here it was": the Levush raised a difficulty: if the wheat kernel fell there, automatically it was not in water and is not forbidden at all; there is no difficulty, for the Rama's intent here is that we are concerned lest a wheat kernel already chametzed fell from elsewhere into this matzah, and here it was found, here it was; and the Levush explained that there is prohibition from the wheat kernel specifically for baked matzah, but regarding dough he permits on the ground that it does not make chametz; and he wrote that the custom is not like the Rama's words to forbid in dough or in matzah that is not baked. And behold, in the Tur he compares dough here to baked matzah, implying that if it had been in water there would be prohibition; and without doubt the author of the Levush did not see the words of the sefarim we mentioned that forbid even in dough; and the main [law] is as Magen Avraham wrote here.
