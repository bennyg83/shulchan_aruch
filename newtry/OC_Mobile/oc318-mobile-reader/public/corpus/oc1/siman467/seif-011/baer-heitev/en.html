And so they practice. The Levush expanded to challenge the Rav on this, and his view is to permit in dough in every case, since cold does not impart taste; see there. And Taz concludes that even though regarding other cold [cases] one may permit like the Levush, nevertheless in dough, through working the dough one mashes the wheat kernel and it becomes chametz and forbids the whole dough during Pesach if it softened; and likewise regarding chametz bread one should be stringent even in cold, for there is concern for crumbs. And so concluded Or Zarua, and so is the conclusion of the Bach and Maharshal; see Magen Avraham and Taz. And Chok Yaakov wrote: and nevertheless it appears to me according to what I wrote above, that if the wheat kernel is not cracked open one may permit benefit on chol ha-mo'ed; if so all the more so here in cold, where many poskim permit in every case; and one who is lenient even for eating has not lost, for it is possible that even where cracked open one may be lenient in cold on chol ha-mo'ed; see there.
Permitted. Meaning we say it fell now into this matzah and was never in the dough. And Magen Avraham wrote: if the wheat kernel was full of water and moist through and through, then certainly it was in water and in dough, so one should forbid the whole dough; and nevertheless if one can attribute that it was absorbed from elsewhere from water and fell into the matzah, we attribute [so], and all according to the circumstances, for we attribute to what is common, whether for leniency or stringency, and it is not called a doubt at all. And Magen Avraham further wrote: that we say "here it was found, here it was," because we do not establish a presumption of prohibition, is specifically from place to place or from piece to piece; but from time to time in one piece we do establish a presumption of prohibition. For example, if he has dough in hand and cut from it and a piece of dough remained in his hand and a wheat kernel was found on it, all are forbidden, for we say perhaps it was there beforehand; and one also cannot say it is a double doubt, for "perhaps it did not become chametz" is a doubt from lack of knowledge and is not [treated as] a doubt; and so ruled Taz in s.k. 14; see there; and see Peri Chadash Yoreh De'ah siman 41 s.k. 11; and responsa Tzemach Tzedek siman 43; and responsa Panim Me'iros siman 15; and responsa Shevut Yaakov part 2 siman 17 and siman 19. He wrote in responsa Maharam of Lublin siman 78: an incident of one who took cabbage from the barrels in a bowl and brought it elsewhere, and there a wheat kernel was found on it, and he permitted the cabbage in the barrels, for we say "here it was found, here it was" (end quote); and Magen Avraham wrote: but if a wheat kernel is found in the barrels, even the cabbage in the bowl is forbidden, for we say perhaps the wheat kernel was in the barrels beforehand, for from time to time in one piece we establish a presumption of prohibition, as written; and see s.k. 25 in Magen Avraham, who wrote: and if they moved it elsewhere and a wheat kernel was found on it, we say it fell now, for it is from place to place; see s.k. 40.
