11) There. And sell it to a Jew. An incident occurred in which a Jew purchased wheat from a non-Jew. Another Jew later asked the non-Jew to sell wheat to him as well. The non-Jew said, 'I have no wheat. The Jew to whom I sold it is my friend, and I sold him the wheat that I had washed and intended to mill for my household, because of my affection for him.' It appears to me that this resembles the law in Yoreh De'ah, siman 16, se'if 11: after the non-Jew sold the wheat and it left the non-Jew's possession, the non-Jew is not believed. If the Jew personally considers the non-Jew trustworthy, however, it is prohibited. Magen Avraham, subsection 4; Chok Yaakov, subsection 5; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 3; Yad Aharon in Hagahos HaTur; Chok Yosef, subsection 4; Mekor Chaim, subsection 5. Chok Yaakov writes there that this implies that before the wheat is sold, while still in the non-Jew's possession, the non-Jew is believed. He rejects this, because the non-Jew may be speaking to improve the sale. See there. Chok Yosef there, however, challenges Chok Yaakov and writes in the name of several authorities that while the product remains in the non-Jew's possession, the non-Jew is believed to prohibit it even where the statement might have been made to improve the sale. See there. Zivchei Tzedek on Yoreh De'ah, siman 16, subsection 34, writes likewise in the name of several authorities. See there.
