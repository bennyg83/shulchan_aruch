On the day. See the Taz. According to the Mechaber in siman 447:10, an impaired taste is permitted, and according to our practice it is forbidden. It is therefore obvious that one may keep it on the eighth day where it is a secondary vessel or a minute amount. But with less than sixty, it is forbidden to keep, as in the Magen Avraham, seif katan 14. The general rule is that whatever may be kept may certainly be sold and used for benefit, but there are cases that may not be kept yet may be sold. The Levush implies that with two D'Rabbanan factors, the Sages did not impose the stringency on the final Yom Tov, though this should not be learned for other days of Pesach.
And know. Regarding roasted food by the fire that became forbidden from chametz, see Yoreh De'ah 105 for the distinctions. Where sixty is required by law, removal of a layer is not needed. Where sixty is required only as a stringency and by law removal of a layer would suffice, then on the eighth day such a case may not be kept until the required layer is removed and discarded, and the rest may be kept. If it became forbidden on Erev Pesach until night and then was cooked before night, the rules of chatichah na'asis neveilah and the need for sixty against the forbidden layer depend on the principles in Yoreh De'ah 96 and 106.
And know. On the eighth day of Pesach, with actual chametz or with a mixture lacking sixty, even though one did not violate bal yera'eh according to the Torah because we are expert in the calendar, nevertheless Rabbi Shimon's penalty after Pesach applies, as explained in Tur, siman 448, regarding chametz bread given as a gift to a Jew. The two days are treated equally so that people not come to belittle the law. Even if he made dough on the eighth day and saw he had no time to bake it before it leavened, and he nullified it first but then transgressed and kept it, we do not say that this is merely a double D'Rabbanan in such a case. See Magen Avraham in siman 447, seif katan 46, and siman 446 regarding how one destroys it on Yom Tov.
