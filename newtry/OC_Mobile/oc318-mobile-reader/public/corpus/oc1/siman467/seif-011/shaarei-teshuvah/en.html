And see Ginas Veradim, klal 4, siman 10: in Egypt, where they bring wheat by ship and the sailors mix water, clay, and earth into it, even though some wheat becomes chametz, they practice leniency because it is nullified in the rest of the wheat; and likewise when they heap the stack after beating it with a grooved threshing-board, dew falls on the wheat night after night for the whole period until they winnowed it and brought it into the storehouse, at least about a month, and the dew makes chametz; yet they practice the leniency of Orach Chaim and [say it does] not revive, and they bake even on Pesach, and they are not concerned for what Maharit wrote in part Orach Chaim siman 1, that one must bake before Pesach; or [one may] answer Maharit that regarding dry [matter] such as flour one may not be lenient; see there. And therefore one should not be stringent for them when some matter dependent on this dispute of "returns and revives" occurs, such as that dispute of shaking in a sieve that I cited above in siman 451; see there.
