(45) Split open — and for us, if it softened even though it did not split open — and specifically regarding eating; but in benefit one may be lenient in great loss without split open, even without removal of place:

(46) The amount of removal of place — for its edge is judged like roast, which forbids the amount of removal; and in dough, even though it is cold, nevertheless we say that through the pressure of kneading something releases around its place:

(47) The entire dough — for through kneading it heats somewhat, and the water in the dough carries the release of the wheat kernel that released through the pressure of kneading throughout the entire dough:

(48) To forbid that matzah, etc. — and even though the implication of the Rama's rule is that he forbids all of it even in great loss, nevertheless if it softened and did not split open, perhaps one may be lenient in great loss through removal of place:

(49) That a wheat kernel was found in it — that a wheat kernel was found in it when it was baked, as it should read. And this is whether the wheat kernel was found in the matzah after baking, or when it was still dough after being formed, or in a piece of dough; and we do not say that the wheat kernel was initially in the large dough and all of it became forbidden — rather here it was found, here it was, meaning where the wheat kernel is found now in this matzah or in this piece of dough, there it was initially when it fell (that is, after this matzah was cut from it the wheat kernel fell into it), and it never fell into the large dough — for we do not establish prohibition from place to place. And if the wheat kernel is full of water and moist through and through, then it is recognizable that it was in water, and in the dough one must forbid the entire dough; and nevertheless if there is room to suspend that it was swallowed from elsewhere from water and fell into the matzah — everything is according to the circumstances, for we suspend in what is common both to be lenient and to be stringent, and it is not called an uncertainty at all. And one who had dough and cut from it several matzos and gave to others, and one matzah remained in his hand and a wheat kernel was found on it — all the matzos from that dough are forbidden, for we say perhaps it was in that dough before he cut it into pieces; for although we do not establish prohibition from place to place and we say here it was found, here it was — we do not say from piece to piece from time to time do we establish prohibition, and we say perhaps it was in this dough before it was cut [Magen Avraham and other Acharonim]; and there are among the Acharonim who hold that even here one may be lenient regarding the other matzos because of a double uncertainty, lest it fell now; and if you say from before, perhaps it did not become leavened until now; and in a place of great loss one may rely to be lenient. And Magen Avraham cited above that this is the law in one who took cabbage from a barrel in a bowl and brought it to another house or another place, and there a split wheat kernel was found in the cabbage in the bowl — the cabbage in the barrel is permitted, for we do not establish from place to place and we say here it was found, here it was — meaning it fell in the bowl now; but if the wheat kernel was found in the barrel, then even what is in the bowl in the other house is forbidden, for this is from time to time and we establish prohibition retroactively. And in any case in a place of great loss one may be lenient even if found in the barrel for those householders who took previously, for there is a double uncertainty — lest it fell in the barrel now; and if you say from before, perhaps the wheat kernel did not become leavened until now; if they did not take immediately when found in the barrel, there was no time to leaven in that period. And there are Acharonim who wrote that all this is when the wheat kernel is split open; but if it did not split open, only softened — one may permit even the cabbage in the barrel if there is great loss there, by force of double uncertainty. And if the brine does not rise upon the cabbage, in any case one may permit the cabbage in the barrel, for it is not considered pickled there but ordinary salting, and it is forbidden only at the place where it is found, and the rest is permitted, as the Rama wrote in the gloss in seif 4. Chayei Adam wrote: pickles and kvass — the custom in our country is to make them for Pesach; one must be careful to sort the pickles before adding water, for it is common to find kernels in them:
