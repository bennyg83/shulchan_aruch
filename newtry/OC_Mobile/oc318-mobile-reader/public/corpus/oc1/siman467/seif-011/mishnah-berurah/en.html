(45) Cracked open. And for us if it softened even though it did not crack open; and specifically regarding eating; but for benefit one may be lenient in major financial loss when not cracked open even without removing its place.
(46) Enough to remove its place. For baking has the law of roasting which forbids enough to remove; and regarding dough, even though it is cold, nevertheless we say that through the pressure of kneading any amount was released around its place.
(47) The whole dough. For through kneading it warms somewhat, and the water in the dough carries the release of the wheat kernel that was released through the pressure of kneading throughout the dough.
(48) To forbid that matzah, etc. And even though from the Rama's unspecified language it implies he forbids the whole even in major financial loss: nevertheless when softened and not cracked open it is possible to be lenient in major financial loss through removing its place.
(49) In which the wheat kernel was found. In which it was found when baked: so the text should read. And that is whether the wheat kernel is found in the matzah after baking or while it is still dough after it was rolled out, or in a piece of dough; and we do not say the wheat kernel was originally in the large dough and the whole was forbidden; rather here it was found, here it was: that is, here the wheat kernel is found now in this matzah or in this piece of dough: here it was originally when it fell (that is, after the matzah was cut from it the wheat kernel fell into it), and it never fell into the large dough; for we do not establish a presumption of prohibition from place to place. And if the wheat kernel is full of water and moist through and through, then it is recognizable that it was in water and in dough: one should forbid the whole dough; and nevertheless if one can attribute that it was absorbed from elsewhere from water and fell into the matzah, all according to the circumstances, for we attribute to what is common whether for leniency or stringency, and it is not called a doubt at all. And one who had dough in hand and cut several matzos from it and gave to others and one matzah remained in his hand and a wheat kernel was found on it: all the matzos from that dough are forbidden, for we say perhaps it was in that dough before it was cut into pieces; for even though we do not establish a presumption of prohibition from place to place and we say "here it was found," etc., and not from piece to piece: from time to time we do establish a presumption of prohibition and say perhaps it was in this dough before it was cut (Magen Avraham and other later authorities). And some later authorities hold that even here one may be lenient for the other matzos because of a double doubt: perhaps it fell now, and even if beforehand, perhaps it did not become chametz until now; and in a place of major financial loss one may rely to be lenient. And the aforementioned Magen Avraham wrote that so is the law regarding one who took cabbage from a barrel in a bowl and brought it to another house or elsewhere and there a cracked wheat kernel was found: the cabbage in the barrel is permitted, for we do not establish from place to place and we say "here it was found, here it was," meaning it fell now in the bowl; but if the wheat kernel is found in the barrel then even what is in the bowl in another house is forbidden, for this is from time to time and we establish retrospectively. And nevertheless in a place of major financial loss one may be lenient even if found in the barrel for those householders who took beforehand, for there is a double doubt: perhaps it fell now in the barrel, and even if beforehand, perhaps the wheat kernel did not become chametz until now: unless when they took it immediately it was found in the barrel and there was no time to become chametz in that time. And some later authorities wrote that all this is when the wheat kernel is cracked open; but if it did not crack open but only softened one may permit even the cabbage in the barrel if there is major financial loss by force of a double doubt. And if the brine does not rise over the cabbage, anyway one may permit the cabbage in the barrel, for it does not have the status of pickled but only the law of salted, and only the place where it was found on it is forbidden and the rest is permitted as the Rama wrote in the gloss in se'if 14. Chayei Adam wrote: regarding "yeagdes kvass" that they practice in our land to make for Pesach, one should be careful to sort the yeagdes before putting in the water, for kernels are commonly found in them.
(50) And specifically during Pesach. For then chametz on Pesach forbids in any amount; but before Pesach the taste of the wheat kernel is nullified in sixty; and even so enough to remove one must take from it, lest it released only around it.
(51) Only enough to remove. Even if found in baked matzah, enough to remove suffices.
