The entire dough. Rashba writes in responsum 176 that the wheat kernel did not touch only one place. Rather, as the dough was rolled it touched here and there. One cannot say in such a case, ‘Here it was found, and here it had been,’ as discussed in seif 12. Moreover, the water in the dough carries what emerges from that kernel throughout the entire dough. The law of removing only the outer layer was stated only regarding dry contact with dry matter. Even where there is moisture without liquid, the Gemara in Chullin 112a states that roasting diffuses flavor to the extent of a fingerbreadth. All the more so does this apply to dough, where the moisture is greater. Therefore, if the dough was divided into several matzos and the kernel was found in one, all are forbidden. If, however, it was found only after the matzah had been removed to another place, one may say, ‘Here it was found, and here it had been,’ as explained below. Bach writes that the entire dough is forbidden only when the wheat was softened, but if it was not softened all is permitted. See seif 2. Maharam Lublin writes in a responsum that one person took cabbage from a barrel into a bowl, carried it to another house, and a wheat kernel was found upon it there. He permitted the cabbage remaining in the barrel, because we say, ‘Here it was found, and here it had been.’ If, however, a wheat kernel is found in the barrel, even the cabbage in the bowl is forbidden, for perhaps the kernel had previously been in the barrel, as written in seif 12 regarding a chicken. The same applies where one has dough, cuts matzos from it and gives them to others, retains one matzah, and later finds a wheat kernel upon it. All are forbidden, because perhaps it had been there previously. Only from place to place do we not establish a prohibition retroactively. According to Bach, who treats the matter as a double doubt, it would always be permitted for this reason. This does not appear correct to me, for the doubt whether it became chametz is a lack of knowledge, which the poskim in Yoreh Deah do not treat as a halachic doubt.
Here it was found, and here it had been. Meaning, we say that it fell into this matzah only now and was never in the dough. If the wheat kernel is filled with water and moist from side to side, however, it appears that it had been in the water and dough, and the entire dough must then be forbidden. Nevertheless, one may still say that it absorbed water elsewhere and only now fell into the matzah.
Only the amount requiring removal. This applies even to a baked matzah. See the end of siman 461.
