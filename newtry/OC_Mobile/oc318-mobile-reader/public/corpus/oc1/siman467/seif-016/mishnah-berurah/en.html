(86) The bird is permitted. Roasting does not forbid beyond the thickness of a peel, and the crop takes the place of that peel and is discarded. Even according to our practice that roasting can forbid up to sixty times the prohibited substance, one need not be stringent here beyond the thickness of a peel, because singeing is not comparable to ordinary roasting. The bird is not held in one place for an extended period, but is moved continually back and forth, as explained in Yoreh De'ah, siman 68. If a wheat kernel is found outside the crop after singeing, l'chatchilah the area should be peeled. B'dieved, if it was cooked without peeling, it is permitted. Those who are meticulous open the chicken thoroughly, or cut it in half, before singeing and salting it for Pesach, and inspect it carefully to ensure that no wheat kernel is found inside. One who does so is diligent and rewarded, as stated by Knesset HaGedolah.
(87) They must be burned. We assume that they became chametz from the moisture found in the crop, as stated by the Levush.
(88) For they are not considered digested. Although with respect to ritual impurity they are considered digested, prohibitions are different. See Biur HaGra, who writes that according to this reasoning there is no distinction between whole and broken kernels. In his ultimate treatment of the law, however, he inclines to the view that no distinction should be made between prohibition and impurity, and even whole kernels are considered digested. Sefer Tanya states that all Pesach preparations should not be performed by a non-Jew, and it is likewise proper that they not be performed by minors, as stated by the Peri Megadim.
