Seif 16: The bird is permitted. See Taz and Magen Avraham.
And the wheat, etc., unlike, etc. Prohibition cannot be derived from the laws of impurity. This is Tur’s ruling. There is therefore no distinction between a whole kernel and a broken one. See Tosafos, Bechoros 7b, and Chullin 94a, in the passage beginning “Rava said.” The ruling adopts this answer of Tosafos because all their other answers are strained. They distinguish between whole and broken kernels, but if so, why does the Gemara challenge from bones? Perhaps the bones were whole and were swallowed. Tosafos force an answer there, and Raavan likewise answers that the time needed for the item to fall into the fire is brief. The Tosefta there similarly says concerning birds, etc., “immediately.” The correct answer is that one should be stringent in accordance with Rabbi Yehudah ben Beseira, because in a Torah matter one follows the stringent opinion, as Tosafos in Chullin and Hagahos Ashri write. Since hardened chametz is prohibited only by Rabbinic law, one need not follow that stringency. Certainly one should not forbid the roasted bird, as is implied by Tur. One cannot say that Tosafos distinguish between prohibition and impurity, because even Tosafos do not retain that distinction in their conclusion in Menachos. A proof is the statement in Chullin 116b, “but one may use as a setting agent, etc.,” and the discussion of the stomach of a kosher animal. See Rif there. Tosafos, who wished to distinguish between prohibition and impurity, follow their own view that distinguishes between liquid and congealed material. The primary answer, however, distinguishes between hard and soft material, as the Gemara states explicitly. Therefore a fish is forbidden, and certainly wheat kernels, which are firm. This is the meaning of the statement there concerning wheat, etc., “to the small one,” etc.
