(seif katan 16) Here, etc., now it fell, etc., meaning since it is from place to place we say: once found in matzah we say here it was found in matzah and was never in the dough at all:

If so it appears it was in water, etc., meaning presumably it was in the water with which the dough was kneaded; and the Bach wrote: perhaps now it fell, etc., meaning even if the reasoning of here it was found, etc., was not accepted and one says because of this it certainly fell now — nevertheless one does not emerge from doubt to certainty; and with another safek added it becomes safek sefeika; and there is a practical difference between two reasons whether we permit through safek sefeika or through here it was found, etc. — Magen Avraham explained in this seif katan; but without softening everything is permitted — meaning even in hot, as Magen Avraham wrote seif katan 25 that we hold like Mar Ukva that even if they did not split open but are fit to split open they are forbidden; and since we are not expert whether they are fit to split open — but without softening it is certain they are not fit to split open — therefore it is permitted even for us:

And it implies, etc., and he brought it to another house, etc., for it is from place to place; but if a grain was found in a barrel then it is from time to time. And according to the Bach's view, etc., and this is the practical difference between the Rama's reason and the Bach's reason that it is lack of knowledge — it should be that the safek the Bach wrote that perhaps it did not leaven is lack of knowledge, for one who understands and recognizes knows whether it leavened or not; nevertheless even if it certainly leavened behold there is a safek that perhaps now it leavened, as Magen Avraham wrote in the name of the Bach seif katan 25 that we say perhaps now it softened — and so in Sefer Ateret Zekenim; nevertheless there is a practical difference whether the grain was found in matzah that remained in his hand after baking — there is no need for perhaps now it leavened; but perhaps it did not leaven — one can say. But this is safek of lack of knowledge, for we do not call that safek. However Shach wrote in siman 453 seif katan 15 in the name of responsum of Mishna Berurah that in safek sefeika even two safeks from lack of knowledge — nevertheless in pressing circumstances we rely on this safek sefeika; and the reason Shach wrote in his general rules of safek sefeika in siman 110: in pressing circumstances when there is safek sefeika, even if it can be clarified, one need not clarify — if so lack of knowledge in safek sefeika does not impede; whereas Peri Chadash wrote there that Shach contradicts his own words in siman 455 seif katan 4 — there is resolution; and Peri Chadash himself holds safek sefeika does not help where there is lack of knowledge — he follows his view that where it can be clarified in safek sefeika one must clarify:
