(S.k. 16) Here, etc. It fell now, etc.: meaning since it is from place to place, for we say: since it was found in the matzah, we say here it was found in the matzah and it was never in the dough.
If so it appears it was in the water, etc. Meaning, presumably it was in the water with which the dough was kneaded; and the Bach wrote, and this is his language: perhaps it fell now, etc.: meaning even if one does not grasp the reasoning of "here it was found," etc., and thereby say that certainly it fell now, nevertheless it has not left the realm of doubt, and joining another doubt it is a double doubt; and there is a practical difference between the two reasons whether we permit by force of a double doubt or by force of "here it was found," etc.: Magen Avraham explained at the end of this s.k. But when it did not soften everything is permitted: that is even in hot, as Magen Avraham wrote in s.k. 25: for although we hold from doubt like Mar Ukva that even if they did not crack open but are fit to crack open it is forbidden, and since we are not experts what is fit to crack open: but when it did not soften it is certainly not fit to crack open; therefore it is permitted even for us.
And Moreinu HaRav, etc. And they brought it to another house, etc.: for it is from place to place. But if a wheat kernel is found in a barrel then it is from time to time. And according to the Bach's words, etc.: and this is the practical difference between the Rama's reason and the Bach's reason that it is lack of knowledge: it requires understanding. Granted that the doubt the Bach wrote, "perhaps it did not become chametz," is lack of knowledge, for one who understands and recognizes knows whether it became chametz or not: nevertheless even if it certainly became chametz there is still a doubt: perhaps it became chametz now. And as Magen Avraham wrote in the name of the Bach in s.k. 25: we say perhaps it softened now; and so too in Sefer Eliyah Rabbah. Nevertheless there is a practical difference if the wheat kernel is found in matzah that remained in his hand after baking: one cannot say "it became chametz now," but "perhaps it did not become chametz" one can say. But this is a doubt of lack of knowledge that does not help [as] a doubt. However Shach wrote in siman 53 s.k. 15 in the name of a responsum of Mab that regarding a double doubt, even if both doubts are from lack of knowledge, nevertheless in major financial loss we rely on this double doubt. And the reason is that Shach wrote in the rules of double doubt in siman 110 that in major financial loss when there is a double doubt, even if one can clarify one need not clarify; and if so it does not impede that there is lack of knowledge in the double doubt, for even if he had knowledge and could clarify through good examination he need not clarify. Only that Peri Chadash wrote there that Shach contradicts himself in siman 55 s.k. 4, and there is room to resolve; and Peri Chadash himself holds that a double doubt does not help where there is lack of knowledge: he follows his own reason that wherever one can clarify in a double doubt one must clarify.
