Seif katan 19, and there is no, etc. This refers to land. Although the Shulchan Aruch wrote plainly, its plain meaning is as though explicit, that it speaks specifically of land, similar to announcement, which speaks of land, for regarding movable property we find no place where announcement is required. On the contrary, it is explained in Choshen Mishpat, siman 109, seif 3, that slaves, documents, and movable property are not announced; see there for the reason.
And we do not say that he is like an agent. Meaning, do not say: what is this teaching us? There is no law of overcharge for land. On this he wrote that it teaches us that one should not say he is like an agent, etc., as stated in Choshen Mishpat, siman 227, seif 30. It teaches that he is like the party himself who sold. So it is in the Ran.
It implies that with movable property, etc. For there is no advantage here over other sales except that it teaches that he is like the party himself. If so, there is no overcharge for land. But regarding movable property, where even the party himself generally has the law of overcharge, the same applies here, unlike what the Taz wrote. It seems that the words of the Ran escaped the Taz’s notice in passing. The same applies to land according to the “some say” brought by the Rama in siman 227, seif 29, that by half or more than half, according to each view as it holds, there is cancellation of the sale; the same applies here.
