(115) He returns to his office — and one may not change him even a majority for a Torah scholar, unless there is a complete claim against the appellant. But if there is clear evidence that they already despaired of him, he lost his presumption. The poskim wrote that the same applies to a parnas who left due to duress and they appointed another in his place — when his duress passes, he returns to his office; and one does not give the second any share in the office on account of hatred of the first; nevertheless one should not treat the second with contempt, and they should give him some honorable appointment in communal needs. If he left his office on account of a sin he committed, even unwittingly, he does not return to his office, as we rule in Makkot regarding a murderer who fled to a city of refuge and returned — he does not return to his previous office (Magen Avraham); and Avraham Zuckerman is uncertain here, for perhaps only regarding a murderer — see in Ritva there what he wrote on this.
