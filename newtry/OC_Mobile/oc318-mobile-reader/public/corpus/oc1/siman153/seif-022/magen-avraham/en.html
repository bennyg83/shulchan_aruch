He returns to his office. One may not change him even a majority for a Torah scholar (Ra'avad volume 2 page 70), unless there is a claim against the appellant; and nevertheless, if there is a claim not to change him, they do not change him (there). And if there is clear evidence that they despaired of him, he lost his presumption (there). And the same applies to a parnas who left due to duress and they appointed another in his place — when the first duress passes, he returns to his office (so Tosafot Yoma 13). And their words require study: nevertheless why did the gemara need to rule like R' Yehuda — behold R' Meir also agrees that the first returns to his service? And it is possible it comes to say that the second should have no honor of parnasut on account of hatred of the first — requires study (later I found Maharsha asked this question). And it implies in Mordekhai chapter Chezkat HaBatim that if he left his office on account of a sin he committed, even unwittingly, he does not return to his office, as we find regarding a murderer, and as Rambam wrote chapter 7 of Hilchot Rotzeach.
