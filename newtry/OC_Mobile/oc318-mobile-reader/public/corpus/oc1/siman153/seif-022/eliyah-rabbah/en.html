One who forbids, etc. It implies that the primary first opinion is that they are public property. But in Yoreh Deah, end of siman 283, he also wrote the permissive opinion in the singular. However, the Rama wrote there: “and see Orach Chaim, siman 153,” and perhaps he intended this. So too in the Darkei Moshe he wrote that the practice follows those who permit. All this is by law. Nevertheless, one who sells it does not see a sign of blessing. All this is regarding a Torah scroll, but regarding its accessories, according to everyone it is permitted. It also appears to me that with other books, everyone agrees regarding an individual. It also appears that even regarding a Torah scroll, if he bought it initially to sell it, and certainly if he received it for his debt, it is permitted according to everyone to sell it. The Magen Avraham wrote that according to the one who forbids an individual to sell a Torah scroll, the same law applies that even the seven leaders of the city in the presence of the townspeople and a chaver ir may not sell it. So it also implies from the Rivash cited by the Beis Yosef. But from the Levush in Yoreh Deah it implies that everyone agrees that the seven leaders of the city in the presence of the townspeople may sell it, and this requires study.
