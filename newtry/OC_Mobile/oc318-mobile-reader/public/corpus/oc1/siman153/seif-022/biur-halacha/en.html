That he is regular — see in Mishna Berurah; and see in Choshen Mishpat siman 149 in Pitchei Teshuvah what he wrote in name of Sha'ar HaMishpat who brought from Shulchan Aruch HaRav that if another wants to add in money, the authority is in his hand [unless the congregation explicitly empowered him also in this matter]; and therefore it speaks here that the other did not add money over the first, or it speaks that they empowered the first explicitly that another should not be able to add over him. However in responsa Radbaz part 4 siman 11 it is explained not like his words — see there; and this requires further study for practice.

But if when they gave to two, etc. — not specifically regarding rolling — for the same applies to one who has chazakah that the congregation prays in his house, and because of constraint when the homeowner was constrained they were forced to gather in another place — he did not lose his chazakah, and when the constraint passes they will pray there [teshuva Ranach and Elef Lamed]; and similarly all such cases.

Returns to his mitzvah — at first glance even the first wealthy one, within the rental time of the second, also returns to his mitzvah immediately — similar to high priest who had constraint on Yom Kippur and they appointed another in his place, who returns to his service immediately when his constraint is removed; and regarding the payments it is reasonable he should pay per the value of the time still remaining in the year. It is in Beit Yosef in name of Mordechai that inheritance does not apply to mitzvot, but whoever gives more to charity acquires it — end of his words; and see in Keneset HaGedolah who wrote that if the custom in the city was that whoever the congregation established in some mitzvah — it is his and his seed's, they do as their custom. He also wrote if one acquired a mitzvah for a fixed time and died within the time, his heirs enter in his place for the fixed time; and see in Birkei Yosef note 12 what he brought in name of teshuva Keneset HaGedolah that the son stands in place of his father in mitzvot — and I do not have that teshuva to examine it; and see in Sefer Chakrei Lev siman 53 what he elaborated on this.
