That he is accustomed. See Mishnah Berurah. See also Choshen Mishpat, siman 149, in Pischei Teshuvah, what he wrote in the name of Shaar Mishpat, who cited Sefer Chassidim, that if another person wants to add money, he has permission to do so, unless the congregation explicitly granted the first person this right even regarding that. According to this, here it speaks either where the other person did not add money over the first, or where they explicitly granted the first person that another could not add over him. However, in Responsa Radbaz, volume 4, siman 11, it is clear not like his words. This requires study in practice.
But if, when they gave it to the second, etc. This is not specifically regarding rolling the Torah scroll. The same applies to someone who has a presumption of right that the congregation prays in his house, and because of unavoidable circumstances affecting the homeowner they were forced to gather elsewhere; he has not lost his presumption of right, and when the unavoidable circumstance passes, they pray there. This is from Responsa Ranach and Olas Tamid. The same applies to all similar cases.
He returns to his mitzvah. Seemingly, even if the first person became wealthy again during the term of the second person's rental, he returns to his mitzvah immediately, similar to a Kohen Gadol who had an unavoidable circumstance on Yom Kippur and they appointed another under him, who returns to his service immediately when his unavoidable circumstance is removed. Regarding payment, it stands to reason that he pays according to the remaining portion of time in that year. It is stated in Beit Yosef in the name of the Mordechai that inheritance does not apply to mitzvos; rather, whoever gives more to tzedakah acquires the right. See Knesses HaGedolah, who wrote that if the practice in the city is that anyone whom the community established in a mitzvah has it for himself and for his descendants, they follow their practice. He further wrote that if one bought a mitzvah for a fixed period and died during the period, his heirs enter in his place for that fixed period. See Birkei Yosef, letter 12, who brought in the name of Responsa Knesses HaGedolah that a son stands in place of his father for mitzvos, but I do not have that responsum available to examine it. See also Sefer Chikrei Lev, siman 53, where he discusses this at length.
