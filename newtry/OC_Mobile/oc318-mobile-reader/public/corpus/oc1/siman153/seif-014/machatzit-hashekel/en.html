And so it appears from what the Beis Yosef wrote in the name of the old Mordechai, etc. And even the Rambam and the Rosh agree to this, etc., unlike the Beis Yosef. The Beis Yosef brought in the name of the old Mordechai, who wrote in the name of Raavyah, and the essence of his words is that the reason cities cannot sell is because in the time of the Talmud, cities were not subject to the seven leaders of the city as villages were. Therefore he wrote that if the cities also chose seven leaders and acted according to them, as in our generations, the same law applies. He also wrote the same where all the people of the city consent, and he further wrote that this is specifically when they built it from their own funds, for then the seven leaders or everyone’s consent helps. The Beis Yosef wrote that he follows his reasoning, that the reason for cities is that others contributed to its building, and therefore when they built it from their own funds it is permitted. But according to the Rambam and the Rosh, whose reason is that the residents build with the intent of the entire world, the fact that they built it from their own funds does not help. This is because the Beis Yosef understood the Mordechai to mean that now, at the time of sale, they chose seven leaders to conduct themselves according to them, and likewise consent of all means at the time of sale. Therefore he correctly wrote that according to the Rambam and Rosh this does not help even if they built it from their own funds. But the Magen Avraham explained the Mordechai as meaning that at the time of building they appointed seven leaders and made it dependent on their opinion, or said at the time of building that they make it dependent on the opinion of all of themselves. Now, at the time of sale, if all consent, or in the first case where they appointed seven leaders, if the seven leaders consent at the time of sale, they are permitted to sell. This is the law explained here in the Shulchan Aruch, if they made it dependent on the opinion of an individual or on their own opinion. Nevertheless it specifically requires that they built it from their own funds, as the Magen Avraham wrote, for if others assisted, making it dependent on an individual or on themselves does not help. If so, the Rambam and the Rosh also agree to all this, since they made the condition immediately at the time of building, as explained here in the Shulchan Aruch. Thus the difficulty of Mahari Chaviv is resolved. He does not mean his first difficulty, why the Tur stated “dependent on the opinion of an individual,” since even if they made it dependent on their own opinion the law is so, for that is still not resolved according to the Magen Avraham’s words, and one must say either as Mahari Chaviv wrote in the name of a manuscript or as the Beis Yosef wrote above. Also, how did the Magen Avraham write that Mahari Chaviv’s difficulty is resolved, when that difficulty was not raised by Mahari Chaviv himself but by a manuscript? Rather, he must mean what Mahari Chaviv concluded, writing about the manuscript: “This is not sufficient, for there is a difficulty with it,” but he did not explain the difficulty. He also wrote that from the words of the Rosh and Nimukei Yosef he learned the true law, implying that according to the Rosh that difficulty is resolved, but we do not know what the difficulty is or how it is resolved according to the Rosh. The Magen Avraham understood that Mahari Chaviv held that making it dependent on an individual helps even if it was built from others’ funds, for so the plain language implies. On this Mahari Chaviv found it difficult: if it was built from others’ funds, how does making it dependent on an individual or on themselves help? According to the words of the Rosh it is understandable, for he wrote specifically Rav Ashi, who was the great one of the generation, and presumably the entire world agrees to his opinion, and therefore it helps even if it was built from others’ funds, as the Magen Avraham also wrote above. This explains what he wrote, that according to his words Mahari Chaviv’s difficulties are resolved, for the Magen Avraham explains that the statement that it helps when they made it dependent on an individual or on themselves applies only when they built it from their own funds, and not from funds of others at all.
