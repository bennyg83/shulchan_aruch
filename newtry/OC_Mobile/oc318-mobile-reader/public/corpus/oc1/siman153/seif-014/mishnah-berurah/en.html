He cannot, etc. For it became a vow from the moment he said, this land is for a synagogue, as we say, with your mouth, this is tzedakah.
They may change it. Even for a mundane matter. Although the land had been designated for a synagogue, designation is not effective, as above in seif 8. Even if he stands and cries out that he wants it only to be for a synagogue, we do not heed him, since at the outset he stated it without qualification and did not double his condition by saying that if they do not build a synagogue they must return the land to him. We say that from the outset he gave it according to their intent, and now he is retracting, which he cannot do.
They are not permitted. Meaning that he can prevent them from building a beis midrash on it, even though that is a stricter sanctity, as explained at the beginning of the siman. The reason is that since it was given with all of them in mind, his power is no less than that of one of the other townspeople, who can object when they wish to change. Although above in seif 6 it is explained that the townspeople may change to a stricter sanctity even without the seven leaders of the city, there it speaks where no one among the townspeople objects explicitly. See the Magen Avraham, who asked that in Yoreh Deah, siman 259, seif 2, it is stated explicitly that the owners cannot object to building a beis midrash, since it is a stricter sanctity. He pressed himself to answer. See the Peri Megadim, who questions his answer, and also the Beur HaGra raised this difficulty of the Magen Avraham and left this law requiring study.
To change it. The Mordechai concludes that nevertheless Reuven also cannot change it and take it back, even if they cannot now build it because of the government, for perhaps after time they will find favor and be allowed to build it.
And the seven leaders of the city, etc. Meaning, in the presence of the congregation.
