He stands and cries out. Since it was given with the intent of all of them. This requires study, for from a synagogue to a beis midrash, even if the owners object, it is permitted, as written in Yoreh Deah, siman 259, seif 2. So too it implies at the beginning of this siman and in seif 6, that for a stricter sanctity the presence of the townspeople is not required. One must say that nevertheless, if one person stands and objects, they are not permitted to change it; and Yoreh Deah speaks of one who is not among the townspeople.
Their law is like a distinguished communal scholar. Since the Rama did not write this gloss above in seif 7, it implies that only here the seven leaders of the city are effective, for the townspeople accepted them for every matter. But above, regarding large cities where people from elsewhere come and donate to the synagogue, the fact that the townspeople accepted them upon themselves is not effective. Similar to this the Beis Yosef wrote in Choshen Mishpat, end of siman 109, in the name of the Ritva, that beis din has no power to stipulate concerning another city. Nevertheless, regarding minors they may stipulate here, as the Ran wrote. The Mishnah Berurah wrote that nowadays the seven leaders of the city may sell even in large cities, and so the Beis Yosef wrote in the name of an old Mordechai. End quote. This is specifically when they built it from their own funds, as written there in the Beis Yosef, and see what I wrote in seif 7. So too it implies in the responsum of R. Meir of Lublin, siman 59. Nevertheless, he permitted there to sell the ground of the synagogue in the community of Lukow, because when they built the synagogue everything they did was done with the permission and counsel of the leaders of the province, and everyone follows the leaders of the province. Also, presumably it is agreeable to the whole world to sell it, because there is no way to build a synagogue again on that place because of the priests who prevent it, and there is also concern that if it remains empty, the priests will build their house of worship on it. Therefore certainly it is agreeable to everyone to sell it, and it is a benefit for them, and one may acquire benefit for a person not in his presence. Nevertheless, for extra care, they should leave the place of the aron kodesh empty and make it a vegetable garden, for it is not as disgraceful as other uses and it is also in purity. End quote. This requires study, for planting is a disgraceful use, as written in siman 151, seif 11. One must say that he speaks of vegetables that do not require plowing, which is not so disgraceful.
