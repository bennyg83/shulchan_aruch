An individual. See Baer Heitev. See Responsa Pnei Yehoshua, siman 4, concerning a synagogue that burned and the community wishes to build elsewhere and leave the original site vacant while treating it with sanctity. This is certainly permitted. If they wish to make a vegetable garden there, the seven trustees of the city acting in the presence of the townspeople are required, though a majority of the seven trustees suffices. If they wish to sell it and a majority of the townspeople agree, that suffices even without the seven trustees. See there. See below, seif katan 18. See also there, siman 7, regarding the synagogue of the community of Horodno, where visitors sometimes remain for half a year. The fact that occasional visitors from elsewhere arrive does not by itself make it a city synagogue. He also writes that where the seven trustees of a city are formally selected and function like exilarchs, with no townsman interfering in their affairs, it is possible that even in cities they may sell without the townspeople. In any event, since there is a stumbling block, it is considered a communal mitzvah, and one may rely on permitting the sale at least in the presence of the townspeople. See there.
