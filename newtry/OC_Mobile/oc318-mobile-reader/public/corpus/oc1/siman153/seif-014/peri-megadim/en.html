There is no Taz citation relevant in seif 20, not in seif 17. And see Taz likewise that Maharshal disputes; and in Avodah Berurah note 32 he wrote in Shulchan Aruch that it deals with one who stipulated explicitly that sanctity should not apply to it as in seif 8, and thereby the case of seif 18 is resolved — see there. And behold in seif 18 one can say one may not take out to secular use, since they consecrated it to read in public (Magen Avraham note 22); but this is only regarding a Torah scroll and not its accessories, even though he gave it to read publicly for clothing — there are those who permit in seif 10 that one may sell it; also Levush concluded in seif 18 that heirs receive nothing, and one must say as Avodah Berurah wrote. And it is difficult what Magen Avraham wrote in note 14, that accessories of sanctity remain in sanctity and one cannot take them out to secular use, even zt"h in the presence of the people of the city; if so, the same applies to an individual with his own thus; if so, why did he specify that they brought it to a synagogue — one may not take out to secular use; even an individual with his own too; and to say to distinguish: an individual with his own can take out accessories of sanctity to secular use but not of the public — it is fine that which in chapter ten of Hilchot Sefer Torah, silver apples, etc., that I brought in note 14; requires study, for one should not distinguish, for they stated anonymously that accessories of sanctity are buried (siman 154 seif 63), and silver vessels are accessories of sanctity — see siman 154 in Magen Avraham notes 8 and 14; and it is possible that because they brought it for the many, one may not take out even the money to secular use; and requires study.
