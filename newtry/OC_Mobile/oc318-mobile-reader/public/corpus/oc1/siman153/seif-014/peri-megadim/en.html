One may not. See Taz. The reference belongs in seif 20, not in seif 17. See Taz, who wrote that Maharshal disagrees. Eliyah Rabbah, note 32, wrote that the Shulchan Aruch speaks where he explicitly stipulated that the sanctity should not take effect on it, as in seif 8, and with this the case of seif 18 is resolved; see there. In seif 18 one can say that he cannot render it profane because he consecrated it for public reading. See Magen Avraham, note 22. However, that is not so, for this applies specifically to a Torah scroll, not to its accessories, even if he gave them for public reading. According to the Levush, under the permissive view in seif 10, he may sell it. The Levush also concluded in seif 18 that the heirs have nothing in it, and one must say as the Eliyah Rabbah wrote. But it is difficult from what the Magen Avraham wrote in note 14, that accessories of sanctity remain in sanctity and cannot be rendered profane even by the seven leaders in the presence of the townspeople. If so, the same should apply to an individual with his own property; why specifically because he brought it to the synagogue can he not render it profane? Even in his own property it should be so. To say that one can distinguish, that an individual with his own property can render accessories of sanctity profane but not public ones, and thereby the case in the tenth chapter of Laws of a Torah Scroll regarding silver apples, which I brought in note 14, would be resolved, requires study, for one should not distinguish, since they said generally that accessories of sanctity require genizah in siman 154, seif 3, and silver vessels are accessories of sanctity; see siman 154, Magen Avraham notes 8 and 14. Perhaps once he brought it for the public, he cannot render even the money profane. This requires study.
