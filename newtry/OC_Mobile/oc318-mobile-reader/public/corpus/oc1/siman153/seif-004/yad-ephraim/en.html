On Taz s.k. 2 — and it appears the questioner holds that this too is called, etc. Below s.k. 5 he wrote that per the answerer who establishes it in "they sold and left over" — meaning ZT"H b'mao"h — it must be that for dukseusya it is reshut; per this the difficulty returns to its place — it should answer well that it deals with "they collected and left over," and that which requires stipulation is for reshut. And it appears to reconcile his words per what Tosafot and the answerer wrote — that which mentions "they left over" even though ZT"H b'mao"h may take out even the body of the money — comes to inform us that even though they already bought the sacred object, nevertheless if they stipulated, yes; if not, no — see there. And now it is all the more so that the questioner held that in "they sold" a condition does not help at all, and necessarily it deals with "they collected" — he proved well not like Rava who permits in "they collected" for lighter kedushah at least — meaning that granted if we say without stipulation even for lighter kedushah it is forbidden, it is all the more so that he mentions "they left over" to inform us that even in "they left over" it is forbidden without stipulation even for dukseusiot which is a mitzvah matter, unlike if we say for lighter kedushah it is permitted only for reshut it is forbidden — if so Tosafot's difficulty is hard: why mention "they left over" — it is obvious that if they stipulated at time of collection everything is per condition even regarding body of money — and one could say it comes to inform us that even in mutar specifically requires stipulation; without stipulation forbidden for reshut — for it could enter one's mind it is permitted even for reshut — meaning that in truth Rava said stam mutar and Rashi explained his words — namely for lighter kedushah — behold we see reason gives that for reshut it is forbidden from reasoning, and if so from Torah we are laden thus until the condition had to inform us this; and therefore he answers him that it deals with "they sold and left over" and informs us specifically with consent of city sons, and then even for dukseusya which is reshut it is permitted; if not so, forbidden even in "they left over" even for lighter kedushah — and this is a fine amplification. And nevertheless requires study on Taz that they strained to say the questioner holds dukseusya is a mitzvah matter — for even reshut for the questioner works well: if for lighter kedushah forbidden in "they collected and left over," it is all the more so teaches that without stipulation even for lighter kedushah forbidden, and with stipulation even for reshut permitted, like the answerer in "they sold and left over" which is explained thus; unlike if for lighter kedushah permitted in "they collected" — if so what does it teach regarding reshut — obviously forbidden; and always both for questioner and answerer dukseusya is reshut. And perhaps his intent is because in the mishnah there "books do not take a cloth," etc., and similarly in their surplus — Rabba said they only taught when they sold and left over — and now it is all the more so: granted for the answerer who explains the baraita that always in "they sold and left over," and this is what it means in a case when they did not stipulate ZT"H, etc. — if so it is all the more so that it means without stipulation ZT"H deals with the mishnah that says "and similarly in their surplus" — meaning also in surplus of books they do not take a cloth, forbidden to lower even to lighter kedushah; but with stipulation ZT"H even for dukseusya which is reshut permitted. Unlike for the questioner who establishes it in "they collected and left over" — if so this baraita does not refer to the mishnah, for the mishnah deals with "they sold and left over" and this baraita is a matter on its own regarding "they collected and left over." If so it is difficult: the questioner wished to prove from this that even for lighter kedushah it is forbidden — for if there were prohibition only for reshut, why "they left over"? And if to inform us that nevertheless stipulation is required for reshut — obvious from reasoning, as Rashi wrote from his own reasoning; and on this basis it is difficult, and per your reasoning that holds lighter kedushah also forbidden — nevertheless how is this proven in the baraita that without stipulation forbidden even for lighter kedushah — perhaps the beginning of "without stipulation" is the reverse of the end "permitted even for dukseusya which is reshut," and in the end permitted thus but not for lighter kedushah — if so he should have explained also that for lighter kedushah forbidden; and one could say the tanna of the baraita relied that automatically we know intent to forbid without stipulation even for lighter kedushah from this reason itself — for otherwise what does it teach, obvious from reasoning — meaning why did it omit to explain in the beginning which is the essence, and mention in the end even dukseusya which is obvious on its own that with stipulation at collection time permitted in every manner even in the money itself, and a fortiori in mutar more — it should have mentioned in the beginning that even for lighter kedushah forbidden, for this is the essence it comes to inform us. But what can you say — perhaps since we do not know how the baraita refers, perhaps in truth the beginning of the baraita explicitly forbids from lighter kedushah — if so even if you say lighter kedushah permitted, it is all the more so — perhaps written in beginning of baraita permitted for lighter kedushah and forbidden for reshut, and on this it says in a case when they did not stipulate, etc., to inform us that in any case for lighter kedushah permitted even without stipulation, only for reshut forbidden without stipulation, and with stipulation permitted even in such a case. And on this Taz answered: in truth the questioner's reason is not because we do not know what the beginning is — in truth this reasoning to say so is not at all sound; rather the questioner holds it is truly proven from this that dukseusya is a mitzvah matter, and if so if we say even for lighter kedushah forbidden, it is all the more so informs us forbidden even in mutar without stipulation — even for a mitzvah matter — and this is proven from what it mentions in the end "even for dukseusya," implying in the beginning even for dukseusya forbidden. And one could say: if so difficulty on precision of the end implying specifically mitzvah matter, but for reshut one cannot say — for automatically implied since lowering from kedushah is by stipulation there is no difference between mitzvah and reshut; and it mentions thus because there is no way to stipulate it should be for reshut entirely, or perhaps not customary to make from it reshut even though permitted. Unlike if we say dukseusya is reshut: if so if it comes to inform us forbidden without stipulation even for mitzvah matter, it should have explained this amplification — for even from dukseusya of the end nothing is proven that it deals with reshut; and therefore he answered him that it deals with "they sold and left over," and this is what it means in a case when they did not stipulate ZT"H — and if so it is all the more so that it comes to inform us forbidden without stipulation even for lighter kedushah; and one could say it should have explained — meaning since it refers to the mishnah it did not need to explain this, for in the mishnah it is clear one does not lower to lighter kedushah.
