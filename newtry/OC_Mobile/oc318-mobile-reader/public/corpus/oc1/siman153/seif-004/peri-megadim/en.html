For the support. See Taz. He holds as I wrote in note 13, that there is a difference between where they have a synagogue, according to his approach in siman 152, note 1, and according to the Magen Avraham where they have one and built another with less expense, where it is permitted to sell a synagogue and a Torah scroll to study Torah and marry a woman, and all the more for redeeming captives, as Tosafot in Bava Basra wrote, cited by the Taz and Shach in Yoreh Deah 252:1, and by the Rama in Even HaEzer there; see there. But when they have only one, they do not sell it even for redeeming captives, and certainly not to marry a woman. The same applies to a Torah scroll in such a case; one can say that whenever the public has only one, they will cease reading from it. See siman 132, for even in all communal needs the concern of negligence applies. See Megillah 26a, negligence, etc. Accordingly, the question of the Perishah in Yoreh Deah 252 is not difficult, for apparently the Rama in Even HaEzer there cites the view of Bava Basra that one may sell a Torah scroll for redeeming captives, and it seems to conflict with here in seif 13, where he was silent about the Mechaber's ruling that they should not sell after building, etc. There is no contradiction, because here it speaks where they have only one.
