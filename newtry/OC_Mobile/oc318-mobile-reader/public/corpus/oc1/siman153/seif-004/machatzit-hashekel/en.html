And what Rav Yaakov Beirav wrote regarding Rambam chapter 8 of Hilchot Matanot Aniyim, brought below in seif 13 — meaning when they do not have another synagogue; for in such a case even to demolish is forbidden, as Rama wrote in seif 7; and although to demolish is forbidden even if they have another synagogue, as Magen Avraham wrote at end of siman 152, and so in seif 7 s.k. 16; in a perfect case, to exchange the synagogue for another, one may say they may exchange for another already built, as Magen Avraham wrote regarding the words of Rama there in s.k. 16; and so one must say the words of Rambam chapter 11 of Hilchot Tefillah — although he wrote to build with money of the synagogue — meaning the word "to build" is not precise, but to exchange the sanctity for which they give money for the sake of another sanctity, and they do not take a new synagogue; and the permission is only what is written "another synagogue still." A difficulty arises: what helps this, for even in such a case even to demolish is forbidden? And one must say: since it is for the sake of greater sanctity, the prohibition is not from the law but on account of doing what is better; as Magen Avraham wrote in s.k. 33, and the reason will be explained there in Avodah Berurah: since it is only a stringency, it suffices with what they were stringent where there is no other synagogue — where one says for negligence, lest they hurry to build quickly; but where there is another synagogue, they are stringent even to demolish, although there is a place to pray, for they are concerned they will be negligent forever and have only one synagogue; but for greater sanctity they are not concerned they will be negligent forever and never build another synagogue at all, for Ran did not concern himself with this at all even to demolish, as written siman 152. And it is possible for greater sanctity, since it is only a stringency, they were lenient where there is another synagogue, for they rely on the opinion that the reason one does not demolish is on account of prayer lest they have no place to pray; and if so, even if there is no other synagogue, only a place to pray, it is also permitted; and see above end of siman 152 in what Magen Avraham wrote: but understand that it is not permitted according to those who hold on account of prayer; and where there is a place to pray only when they demolish the old in order to build new and temporarily — what helps having a place to pray; but when selling for greater sanctity, a place to pray does not help, for they will never have a synagogue unless they truly intend to build another synagogue after a time. Nevertheless it appears to me Rav Yaakov Beirav in his long book does not hold so, for he challenged Rambam who forbids selling even for greater sanctity, and we learned: if they sold a synagogue, they may buy an ark, etc., because it is greater sanctity; and answer Rambam deals with l'chatchila forbidden to sell, and the mishnah deals b'dieved only when they already sold; and per the words of Mahari Avraham, Machatzit Hashekel, the mishnah deals when they have another synagogue.
