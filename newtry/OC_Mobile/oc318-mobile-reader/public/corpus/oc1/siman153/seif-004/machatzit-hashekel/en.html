Seif katan 4, some say, and the Bach wrote, etc. At first glance, the “some say” written in the Shulchan Aruch are the Ran, who forbids, and Mahari Abuhav is the one who permits. For the Beis Yosef brought in the name of the Ran that from the question in Megillah 27a, “May one sell an old Torah scroll in order to buy a new one? Since he does not elevate it, is it forbidden, or perhaps since there is nothing higher to which to elevate it, etc., it is proper?” and it concludes that where there is concern for negligence it is certainly forbidden. The question is only where it is already written and lying in the scribe’s house and nothing is lacking except payment of the money; what is the law in such a case? It was not resolved. On this the Ran wrote that therefore the Talmud was uncertain whether to permit buying an equal sanctity only with respect to a Torah scroll, since there is nothing higher in sanctity than a Torah scroll. But regarding other things, where one can elevate them to a stricter sanctity, it was never uncertain, for it is certainly forbidden. The Beis Yosef then brought as follows: Mahari Abuhav wrote that the words of the Tur, who wrote like the language of the Shulchan Aruch in seif 2, “but the opposite, to lower them from their sanctity, no,” imply that we are particular only that they not lower them from their sanctity, but to buy something equal to them is permitted. End quote. From this it implies that the Beis Yosef held that the Ran and Mahari Abuhav disagree, and these are the two opinions brought in the Shulchan Aruch. On this the Magen Avraham wrote that the Bach wrote not like the Beis Yosef, but that the Ran and Mahari Abuhav do not disagree. Initially it is certainly forbidden, and Mahari Abuhav agrees; the Ran speaks about this, for he brings proof from the above question, which speaks initially.
That is after the fact, for that is what the Tur discusses, and so appears from his language, which is like the language of the Shulchan Aruch in seif 2: “The people of the city who sold, etc.” After the fact, even the Ran agrees that it is permitted.
But regarding a Torah scroll we adopt stringency, etc. Even where there is no negligence, for the Talmud was uncertain only where there was no negligence, as above, and nevertheless it was not resolved, so even in such a case we follow stringently. The Magen Avraham wrote this to exclude the view of Rabbeinu Manoach, whom the Beis Yosef brought in Yoreh Deah, and so was ruled in the name of the Levush, that where there is negligence it is permitted; see there. According to this, the Shach wrote there in the name of the Bach that even if he first laid out money of his own and bought the new one before selling the old one, intending afterward to be repaid from the old one, it is also forbidden; see there.
But the language, etc. He wished to resolve that the words of the Bach are reasonable, and without doubt the Beis Yosef also ultimately retracted. Although he wrote in the Shulchan Aruch “and some permit,” he must mean initially, for after the fact no one forbids. Therefore “and some permit” means initially. Although according to the Bach’s words we did not find anyone who permits initially, to this the Magen Avraham wrote that the Rambam must permit initially; if so, one may say that the “some permit” refers to the Rambam, as the Magen Avraham writes afterward.
The villagers, etc. With its money, another synagogue, etc. They must stipulate with the buyer that he not make it a bathhouse, etc., as is explained in the Shulchan Aruch, seif 9. End quote. It is explained that he permitted initially to sell a synagogue and buy another synagogue. Thus, an equal sanctity is permitted initially.
And it appears to me that his opinion means: how does the Rambam answer the Ran’s proof from the question of the Talmud regarding a Torah scroll?
That specifically a Torah scroll, etc. They cannot remove, etc. If so, when they exchange it for an equal sanctity and do not elevate it in sanctity, there is some disgrace and removal of sanctity. Therefore, were it not that there is nothing higher to which to elevate it, the Talmud would have found it obvious that it is forbidden to exchange it for something equal to it.
If so, although, etc. Meaning, granted that they can remove its sanctity, that is specifically through the seven leaders of the city in the presence of the townspeople, and then both the money and the synagogue are completely non-sacred and leave their sanctity. But here the Rambam wrote plainly, implying even where there are not seven leaders in the presence of the townspeople.
For in this case its sanctity is not removed. Meaning, there is no actual removal of sanctity, only a slight removal, as above. Regarding this, namely a slight removal, everyone certainly consents, since they have another synagogue. Since everyone agrees to this, we no longer have concern, even though the sanctity is somewhat removed, for with everyone’s agreement they can remove even all of the sanctity.
See what I wrote in siman 152, seif katan 2, where he wrote a similar reasoning. However, this reason applies only to a synagogue, but not to Chumashim, Prophets, and Writings, for with them it is impossible to remove their sanctity through sale, and they remain in their sanctity in the purchaser’s possession. If so, the Rambam agrees that it is forbidden to exchange them for something equal to them.
And according to this it is forced, for it appears that what Rabbi Beis Yosef wrote in the Shulchan Aruch, whether it is permitted to buy, etc. Another sanctity equal to it, implies that it refers to all the things mentioned in seif 2. If so, how did he write “and some permit”? According to the words of the Magen Avraham, this is the Rambam, and he permitted only with a synagogue. If so, one must force the explanation that “and some permit” refers to only one matter, namely a synagogue.
And what the Beis Yosef wrote, that according to the Rambam in chapter 8 of Hilchos Matanos Aniyim, and it is brought below in seif 13. This is when they have no other synagogue, for in such a case even demolishing is forbidden, as the Rama wrote in seif 7. Although demolishing is forbidden even if they have another synagogue, as the Magen Avraham wrote at the beginning of siman 152, and as he wrote here in seif 7, seif katan 16, nevertheless regarding exchanging one synagogue for another, one can say that this means exchanging it for another that is already built, as the Magen Avraham explained the words of the Rama above in seif katan 16. So too the words of the Rambam in chapter 11 of Hilchos Tefillah must be explained, for although he wrote “to build a synagogue with the money,” one must say that the word “to build” is not precise. But when exchanging for a sanctity, where they give the money for another sanctity and do not buy a new synagogue, and the only permission is that they have another synagogue, this is difficult: what does that help, since even in such a case demolishing is forbidden? One must say that since this is for a stricter sanctity, where the prohibition is not strict law but only the better practice, as the Magen Avraham wrote in seif katan 33, and the reason will be explained there with the help of Heaven, since it is only a stringency, it is enough that they were stringent where there is no other synagogue, lest there be negligence and they not hurry to build it quickly. But where there is another synagogue, even though regarding demolition we are stringent even where there is a place to pray, nevertheless there the concern is that they may become negligent forever and have only one synagogue. Regarding a stricter sanctity, however, we are not concerned that they will be negligent forever and never build another synagogue, for the Ran was not concerned for this at all even regarding demolition, as explained above at the beginning of siman 152. Perhaps regarding a stricter sanctity, since it is only a stringency, they were lenient when there is another synagogue, relying on the opinion that we do not demolish only for the reason that they will have no place to pray. If so, even if there is no other synagogue but there is another place to pray, it is also permitted. See above at the beginning of siman 152 regarding what he wrote in the Magen Avraham. However, it must be that the opinion that permits because there is a place to pray permits only when they demolish the old one in order to build a new one, where for the moment it helps that there is a place to pray. But when they sell it for a stricter sanctity, a place to pray does not help, for they will have no synagogue forever, unless they truly intend to build another synagogue after a time. Nevertheless, it appears to me that Rabbi Beis Yosef in his long work did not hold this way, for he asked against the Rambam, who forbids selling even for the sake of a stricter sanctity: but we learned in the Mishnah, if they sold a synagogue, it is permitted to buy an ark, etc., because that is a stricter sanctity. He answered that the Rambam speaks initially, that it is forbidden to sell, while the Mishnah speaks only after the fact, when they already sold it. According to the Magen Avraham’s words, he could have said that the Mishnah speaks where they have another synagogue.
