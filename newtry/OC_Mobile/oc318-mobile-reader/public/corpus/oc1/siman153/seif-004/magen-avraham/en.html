Some forbid. The Bach wrote that there is no dispute; initially it is certainly forbidden to sell, as the Ran wrote from the fact that they asked whether it is permitted to sell an old Torah scroll in order to buy a new one, since there is no way to elevate it, which implies that with other matters it is certainly forbidden. What the Tur wrote, that it is forbidden to lower them from their sanctity, which implies that to a sanctity like it is permitted, refers after the fact, if they already sold them, and this is also the view of Mahari Abuhav. But regarding a Torah scroll we adopt stringency, that it is forbidden to sell it in order to buy another with its proceeds even when there is no concern for negligence. This is also the view of the Rif, Rosh, Tur Yoreh Deah, and the Shach there in siman 270. However, the language of the Rambam implies that even initially it is permitted to sell a synagogue in order to buy another with its proceeds, for he wrote in chapter 11 of Hilchos Tefillah: the villagers who wished to sell their synagogue or to build another synagogue with its proceeds, etc.; see there. Therefore the Beis Yosef wrote here, and some permit. It appears to me that his view is that specifically regarding a Torah scroll, where the sale itself is forbidden because they cannot remove its sanctity, this applies; but with a synagogue, they can remove its sanctity, as written in seif 9. Therefore, even though they did not sell it through the seven leaders of the city in the presence of the townspeople, so they cannot remove its sanctity, for perhaps there is one person who does not agree to this, nevertheless they may sell it in order to build another with its proceeds, for in this case they do not remove its sanctity. See what I wrote in siman 152. And what the Beis Yosef wrote, that according to the Rambam initially one may not sell a synagogue even in order to buy a stricter sanctity with its proceeds, refers to where they have no other synagogue, and that is what he wrote in seif 13.
