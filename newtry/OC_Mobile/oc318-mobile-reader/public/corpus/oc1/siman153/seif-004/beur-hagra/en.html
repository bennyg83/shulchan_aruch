Seif 4, some forbid. From what is stated there, 27a: they asked, etc.; or perhaps since, etc. This indicates that in other cases, since there is another elevation in sanctity, it is certainly forbidden.
And some explain, etc. From there there is no proof, for there it is initially, as stated there: the Mishnah is after the fact, etc. At first glance it implies that after the fact it is permitted in all of them, and that the inference from the latter clause is precise, since it resolves: come and hear, but if they sold, etc., and it does not ask: say the first clause, books may be used to buy a Torah scroll, but a Torah scroll for a Torah scroll, no, since it does not teach: books and a Torah scroll may be used to buy a Torah scroll. What the Bach and Magen Avraham wrote, that the Ran and Mahari Abuhav do not disagree and that the Ran speaks only initially, they did not examine the Ran, who wrote: and in Sefer HaMaor it is written, etc., this Mishnah is difficult, the first clause contradicts the latter clause, etc.; rather, etc., since it does not elevate it, it is forbidden, etc. Certainly, this is implied from the Gemara, etc. Thus even after the fact he holds that it is forbidden. He holds that since the Gemara states plainly that because it does not elevate it, it is forbidden, it follows that the inference from the latter clause is not precise; for if it were, how would we know it is forbidden? But initially it is certainly forbidden according to all, and even regarding a Torah scroll the view of the poskim is stringent. After the fact, however, regarding a Torah scroll it is certainly permitted, as stated there in the Gemara.
