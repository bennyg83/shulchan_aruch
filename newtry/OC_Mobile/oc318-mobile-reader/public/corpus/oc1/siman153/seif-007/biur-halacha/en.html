(7) But of cities, etc. — he wrote in responsa Masat Binyamin siman 33: that in lands where the custom of communities is to appoint upon them leaders and administrators, and they have a stick and strap in all public matters — there is no distinction between villages and cities; and in every matter they have power in the hands of the leaders to sell a beit haknesset, even to one who will make it a tavern for beer, and they may also take out the money to secular use; even if the people of the city protest in their hands — until here. (50) To change it, etc. — behold this follows from the words of Rashba; and at first glance, per the view of Magen Avraham that we do not have the reading "to minor sanctity" in its light — specifically when it is forbidden to change it at all, even to severe sanctity [such as when the dedicator commanded thus], then proclamation is required; but if they have permission to change to severe sanctity, such as through the seven good men of the city not in the presence of the people of the city, as above — then it is considered as theirs and proclamation is not required; and so is proven in truth the intent of Rashba: for he hangs the reason on "he can give it as a gift," and he himself in his novellae in chapter Bnei HaIr holds that regarding a gift we require only the seven good men alone; and so is also the view of Ramban. However it is difficult: for if so, per the ruling of Magen Avraham himself, who ruled below in s.k. 26 that for a gift specifically the seven good men in the majority are required — automatically for a sale through the seven good men not in the majority, proclamation should be required; and it is possible that Magen Avraham relied mainly on the reason of Ran, whom Magen Avraham brought in his name in s.k. 18: there it states that we do not find proclamation except in a matter that is imposed on the court to do, such as a court that sells to collect for others, etc.; but in a matter that we do ourselves and do not need the court for it — proclamation was not mentioned regarding it — until here; and it implies explicitly: per this, the seven good men alone also do not require proclamation, for behold they are the people of the city themselves and the main ones who sell from their own; and so it implies from the language of Tur; and Rama brings it in Choshen Mishpat siman 103 seif 1 in Hagahot: that since it was sold not per his knowledge in court, appraisal and proclamation are required — see there. And in truth it is explained in Ran and other rishonim there: in any case, the seven good men alone are not weaker than all the people of the city without the seven good men; and if so, if we say that through the seven good men alone proclamation is required because it is forbidden to change it to minor sanctity — if so, when all the people of the city sold without the seven good men, proclamation should also be required, for they too are not permitted to change it to minor sanctity; and this is not reasonable at all, that the sons of the city from their own require proclamation; rather, perforce it does not depend on this at all: that if the Sages were stringent in a matter of sanctity not to be lenient in them and in their money, and decreed that their power is not strong enough to remove to secular use unless the people of the city agree in the presence of their heads and important ones — nevertheless this matter is not relevant to being stringent on sellers from their own (and in order to raise in sanctity) that they should require proclamation; and likewise regarding the seven good men who are the main people of the city — even though the Sages decreed that they do not remove sanctity except when all the people of the city stand at the time of removal — nevertheless from this there is no proof that they did not rely on them regarding the matter of sale money; and it is further reasonable that Rashba also agrees to the reason of Ran, except that he did not need it for his view regarding a gift, for he holds that even with the seven good men alone it suffices; and we expanded on all this because I saw Avodah Berurah attacks Magen Avraham and strains there to settle our reading and to be stringent where one may not change to minor sanctity that proclamation is required, and strains the words of Rashba and Ran; and in my humble opinion the main view is as Magen Avraham, as we wrote.
