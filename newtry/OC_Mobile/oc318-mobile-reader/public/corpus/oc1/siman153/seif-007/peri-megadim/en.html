Seif 7. See Taz and Magen Avraham. In all these laws, one must distinguish between the body of the sanctified item, its proceeds, surplus money, and money that was merely collected. The body of sanctity is stricter, whereas money sometimes depends on whether it came to the treasurer or whether there was a stipulation.
Seif 7. A Torah scroll is stricter than other sacred objects, because one does not sell it except to study Torah or to marry a woman, and even after a sale there are limits on using its proceeds. Other sacred objects may be lighter in this respect, especially where the seven leaders of the city act with the townspeople.
Seif 7. The main point is that equal sanctity, lower sanctity, and optional use are not the same. For optional use a sale or stipulation requires a stronger basis, while for an elevation in sanctity there is more room for permission.
Seif 7. It still requires study whether the rules of a synagogue, a Torah scroll, and other accessories of sanctity are fully parallel, for each has a different degree of sanctity and different concerns of negligence.
