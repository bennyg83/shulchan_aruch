Of villages. The later authorities agree that in a place where many outsiders are not commonly found, it is called a village, even if passersby sometimes come through, since they are not fixed in the city but merely pass through incidentally. But any place where many outsiders commonly come to pray, such as a commercial center where merchants regularly gather, even if the town is small, or a place with a prominent Torah scholar to whom many people come, is considered like a large town.
Even if they built. For presumably others gave them the money so that they should do with it whatever they wished, since it is not common for outsiders to come there. Therefore, even if those others do not later agree to the sale, it is permitted to sell it.

Their communal leaders. The seven leaders of the city.
The seven leaders of the city. The Rashba, part 1, siman 617, wrote, and the Rashdam, siman 175, brought it, that the seven leaders of the city are not the seven people most distinguished in wisdom, wealth, or honor, but seven people whom the public appointed as communal leaders over the affairs of the city, and they are like guardians over them. He wrote further there: if you ask, if they accepted them upon themselves, why do I need seven? He answers there that if they accepted upon themselves people to deal with a specific and defined matter, such as in our case, where they chose them for the sale, even one person too may be appointed, and whatever he did is done. Rather, it speaks where they appointed leaders over themselves without specification, to supervise communal affairs. Therefore, when they are seven, they have authority over every matter as though all the townspeople had done it, even though they did not appoint them explicitly for this matter. But fewer than seven do not have equal power to be like all the townspeople until they receive explicit authorization from the townspeople. So wrote several poskim.
But, etc., for whatever they wish. Meaning, even for some non-sacred matter.
Explicitly, etc., everything, etc. Meaning, in this manner his law is like the seven leaders of the city in the presence of the townspeople, who may afterward use the money for whatever they wish, since they authorized him for this. But if they gave him permission to sell without specification, the money remains in its sanctity.
They sell publicly, etc. Meaning that whenever the seven leaders of the city sell publicly and not privately, and the townspeople did not object to them, this is called in the presence of the townspeople. The word 'or not' that the Rama wrote is merely habitual language and is not precise; it is also absent from the Mordechai and from the long Darkei Moshe.
Even if they built it from their own funds. Since outsiders come there, presumably the townspeople consecrated it also with those who enter in mind. Therefore they have no authority to sell it, lest there be a person at the end of the world who had been near this city and was accustomed to enter it, and it was consecrated with his intent, and he does not agree to the sale. Therefore, even if the seven leaders of the city agreed to the sale in the presence of the townspeople, their sale is not a sale, for those outsiders are not subject to the townspeople and their leaders. Even if they later wish to elevate the money to a higher sanctity, such as to buy a Torah scroll with it and the like, they also have no authority to do so, for perhaps those others would not agree to this. All this is when it is unspecified. But if it is clear to us that they built it only with the townspeople in mind, such as where they stipulated at the time of building, or where at the time of building it was a village and afterward many residents increased in it, and others did not assist them, its law is like that of a village. The Magen Avraham wrote in the name of the Mabit that even a synagogue of a large city, if they do not pray in it, may be sold.
It is not sold. This is specifically where they sold it in a way that it will not afterward be a synagogue. But if afterward too it will remain a synagogue in which many people pray, it is permitted, for what difference does it make to those who come from elsewhere whether the synagogue belongs to these or to those.
Unless they made it dependent, etc. Meaning that at the time of building they made the synagogue dependent on his intent. Therefore his intent is afterward considered like the seven leaders of the city. All this is specifically where they built it from their own funds. But if others donated for this, it does not help that they made it dependent on the intent of an individual, for perhaps there is one person at the end of the world whose mind is not at ease with this.
With the consent of the public. For certainly their intent was not that an individual should sell their synagogue without their knowledge. Rather, the intent of the individual helps for this, that the leaders of the city are not needed, but it must be in the presence of the townspeople alone, and an ordinary person also cannot prevent him. If they explicitly made it dependent on the individual, that he may sell and do whatever he wishes without their consent, he does not need to act with their consent. See Biur Halacha, what we wrote regarding this.
The same applies to all matters of sanctity. Meaning, for example, the ark, the wrapping cloth, and the like mentioned in seif 2. Their law is like the synagogue itself regarding this, that wherever it was made with everyone in mind, such as in large cities, these too were presumably made with everyone in mind. This is what the Mechaber wrote, that all of them are drawn after the synagogue. Likewise, anything belonging to the synagogue, such as courtyards or a mikveh, we say similarly, and they cannot sell them or close them in order to prevent guests from using them. Nevertheless, they are not more stringent than the synagogue. Likewise, what was mentioned in this seif, that the seven leaders of the city in the presence of the townspeople in villages may use the money for non-sacred purposes, is not specifically by a synagogue, but even by accessories of sanctity the law is so. Nevertheless, there is a distinction between them, for with accessories of sanctity, although it is permitted to use their proceeds when they were sold by the seven leaders of the city in the presence of the townspeople in villages, they themselves remain in their sanctity in the buyer's hand, for they have inherent sanctity and do not become non-sacred. But a synagogue and accessories of a mitzvah may be used, as written below in seif 9. See below in seif 10 and siman 154, seif 3, in the Mishnah Berurah and Biur Halacha.
For all of them are drawn after the synagogue. All synagogue vessels, benches, and curtains in the synagogue have the law of the synagogue. Therefore, when the seven leaders of the city sell them in the presence of the townspeople in villages, they may use the money for whatever they wish, but otherwise not. This is according to the simple meaning of the Magen Avraham, who copied the Ran's words in the name of the Yerushalmi on the words of the Shulchan Aruch. In Sefer Kovetz on the Rambam, Hilchos Tefillah chapter 11, he wrote that it appears to him that even in large cities they may sell them according to the Ran, for specifically regarding the sale of the synagogue itself we say that the public does not waive its share to the seven leaders of the city, unlike in this case. Therefore the treasurer may sell them together with the townspeople and use the money for non-sacred purposes.
Its law is like a communal synagogue. It is sold by their authority alone, and we do not require the donor's intent in this at all.
All this. Meaning, what was said regarding villages, or a large city where they made it dependent on the intent of an individual.
When they have another synagogue. The Magen Avraham explains that this means where they now built another one. But if they already had two synagogues from before, it is forbidden to sell one. According to the Taz at the beginning of siman 152, it is permitted even in this manner, and the Eliyah Rabbah there likewise agrees with him, and so it also implies from the Beur HaGra. But all this is specifically when that synagogue is spacious and holds everyone, for otherwise it is obvious that according to all opinions it is forbidden. The later authorities wrote that even in large cities, where they built another one that is better, it is permitted to demolish the first, for certainly everyone is pleased with this. We already copied this above in siman 152. Likewise, the wood and stones of the first synagogue may be used and removed to non-sacred use through the seven leaders of the city in the presence of the townspeople, for what concern do people of the world have with this, since they have a good place to pray.
It is forbidden, etc. Even through the seven leaders of the city in the presence of the townspeople, and even when they sell it in order to buy another, similar to demolition, which is forbidden even in this manner, as above in siman 152.
To sell it. Unless they want to use the money to buy another in a manner where there is no concern for negligence, such as where a synagogue is ready to be purchased and only the giving of the money is lacking. Then it is permitted through the seven leaders of the city in the presence of the townspeople.
That is sold, and it is permitted to change it, etc. This matter is based on what is explained in the Rashba, regarding one who instructed that this house should be for the poor, that they should eat its produce, and that the members of the congregation should not do any evasion. Afterward the congregation wanted to sell the house and buy something else with the proceeds that would bear produce for the poor. The Rashba wrote that they need an announcement at the time of sale, since he instructed that the members of the congregation not do any evasion. If so, certainly the public cannot give this house as a gift to whomever they wish. If so, likewise they are not permitted to sell without an announcement, for wherever they did not announce, we view it as though they cheapened this land, since perhaps if they had announced it, someone would have been found who would give more. What difference is there between giving all of it as a gift and cheapening part of it? End quote. Now we will explain the words of the Rama: anything, etc., and it is permitted to change it, meaning that the sellers otherwise have authority to do whatever they wish with the item sold, does not require announcement, for even if because of the lack of announcement it was sold cheaply, we do not care. Is it worse than if they gave it to someone as a gift?
And there is no overcharge. This speaks of the sale of land and houses, because there is no law of overcharge for land. It teaches that you should not say that they are like an agent, where even with any amount the sale is void because the sender can say, I sent you to improve, not to ruin. Rather, they are like the owners. But in selling movable property, there certainly is a law of overcharge.
But an item that it is forbidden to change. This is the correct reading. This is as above in the name of the Rashba, that an announcement is required so that it not be sold cheaply; consequently, there is also a law of overcharge, even with land.
It requires announcement. Specifically with land. But in the sale of movable property, no announcement is required, as stated in Choshen Mishpat, siman 109.
