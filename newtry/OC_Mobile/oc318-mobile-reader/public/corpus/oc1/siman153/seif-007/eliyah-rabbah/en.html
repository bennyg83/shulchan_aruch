And some permit, etc. The Taz elaborated and wondered that in Yoreh Deah, siman 272, he ruled anonymously that it is forbidden regarding a Torah scroll. This is not difficult at all, for one may say that there it is because of negligence, as explained there, and so one may explain the words of the Tur there. One may also say that, as I explained, here they permit specifically after the fact, while in Yoreh Deah it speaks initially. Although the Taz wrote that it is stated in the Talmud that specifically regarding a Torah scroll there is a distinction between initially and after the fact, because there is no higher sanctity to which it can be elevated, but with items that can be elevated, their after-the-fact case is like initially, this is not so in the Talmud. Rather, it is possible to explain that even regarding other books there is a distinction; see there and examine carefully, as I wrote in the name of the Bach. It appears to me that the opinion of “some permit” is the words of Mahari Abuhav and Rabbeinu Yonah cited by the Beis Yosef, and it appears that this is also the opinion of Rabbeinu Manoach cited by the Beis Yosef there in Yoreh Deah. This is unlike the Magen Avraham, who wrote that they are the words of the Rambam and that he holds there is no prohibition except regarding a Torah scroll; see there. This is not correct for one who examines the sugya there; see there and examine carefully. The Taz also rejected this reasoning entirely. However, the Tosafot Yom Tov there explained the Rambam’s words that by law it is permitted in all these matters to buy something equal, only it is preferable to buy something of higher sanctity, and this appears to me to be primary. The Taz wrote: in our time we see many people sell books and buy others with their money. It seems that nowadays, when one first buys the book, his intent is that it should be his as long as he needs it, as in siman 154, seif 8 in the gloss. To me it seems the reason is that books of an individual may be sold, as below, seif 10; see there.
