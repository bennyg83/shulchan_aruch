And if they accepted, etc. If they explicitly specified that the individual has permission to sell it and to do with the money whatever they want, then what he did is effective. But if it was given to him without specification to sell, then the money retains its sanctity.
Of large towns, etc. If it is known that they made it only for a small group and guests do not enter it, it is like a village synagogue, as stated in Megillah 26 regarding a synagogue of craftsmen.
Unless it depends, etc. The Magen Avraham explained that this is specifically when they built it from their own funds; but if others donated for it, the fact that it depends on the view of an individual does not help, and so it implies in Nachalas Tzvi. In Responsa Masas Binyamin, siman 33, he wrote that in these lands, where the practice of the communities is to appoint leaders and officers over them, and they have authority and discipline in all communal affairs, there is no distinction between villages and large towns. In all cases the leaders have power to sell the synagogue even to drink beer in it, and the money also becomes non-sacred, even if the townspeople object to them. The Nachalas Tzvi disagrees and says that even nowadays it is forbidden unless it is known that they built it from their own funds and explicitly made it dependent on the view of one individual. From Magen Avraham, seif katan 37, it implies that whenever they built it from their own funds, nowadays we do not require that they make it dependent on the view of one individual, and one may be lenient. See seif katan 17. In the Raavan, at the beginning of the simanim of the book, I found proof for the Masas Binyamin, for the town officers are called chaver ir. The Taz wrote that even regarding a synagogue of large towns, the prohibition applies only when they sold it in a manner that afterward it will no longer be a synagogue. But if it will remain a synagogue, it is permitted, even from many to an individual, like the Rabbis in Megillah 27b.
With the consent, etc. If they explicitly made it dependent on the individual’s view, that he can sell and do whatever he wants without their consent, he does not need to do it with their consent. Ateres Tzvi.
For any sacred matters, etc. This means that it is permitted to use its money when it is from villages. Nevertheless, they remain in their sanctity. But the synagogue itself and accessories of a mitzvah may be used.
It is forbidden to sell it, etc. This is the language of the Magen Avraham: unless they want to buy another with the money in a manner where there is no concern for negligence, or they want to buy something of greater sanctity; and so it implies in the Ran in the name of the Ramban. This is not clear, for since it is of large towns, we are concerned that perhaps there is someone who does not want the greater sanctity, and so wrote Responsa Masas Binyamin there. One must say that the Magen Avraham was speaking about villages, and even this requires study. Masas Binyamin wrote there that even in large towns, where they built another better one, it is permitted to demolish the first one, for certainly everyone is pleased with this; and the wood and stones from the first one are also permitted for use. This is because they built another one, and it is like selling this for that, where presumably everyone is pleased. But if they did not build another one, even the stones and wood are forbidden, and the Magen Avraham challenged him for no reason; see there. The Magen Avraham wrote in seif katan 37 as follows: In Responsa Maharam Lublin he permitted selling the land of the synagogue of the holy community of Lukowa, because when they built it as a synagogue they did so with permission and counsel of the leaders of the province, and everyone follows the leaders of the province. Also, presumably it is agreeable to all to sell it, since there is no way to rebuild a synagogue on that place. Nevertheless, to be extra safe, they should leave the place of the aron kodesh empty and make it a green garden, for it is not as disgraceful as other uses. It requires study, for planting is a disgraceful use, as written in siman 151, seif 11. One must say that it discusses vegetables that do not require plowing, for it is not so disgraceful. This is the language of the Magen Avraham. I did not reach the end of his intent, for Maharam wrote explicitly there that although planting is a disgraceful use, nevertheless it is not repulsive, etc. Thus even planting vegetables is a disgraceful use; rather, he meant that it is not repulsive like the four things listed in seif 9, which are worse than planting, as is explicit there. I also wonder at him for copying “they should leave the place of the aron kodesh empty,” etc., but that is not so. Rather, this is his language: the place on which the men’s synagogue stands is the main sanctity and should remain empty, etc.
Every sacred matter, etc. Its meaning is that since it is permitted to change it, it is considered theirs, so that they can give it as a gift, as below in seif 11. With something that is his, announcement is not relevant. Also, there is no ona’ah with real estate; and with movable property, even where announcement is not relevant, as stated in Choshen Mishpat, siman 109, nevertheless there is ona’ah, unless they knew that it was worth more, for then it is no worse than a gift. This is unlike a case where it is forbidden to change it to a lighter sanctity, and certainly where the public has no power to change it at all; then certainly they cannot give it as a gift, it is not considered theirs, announcement is required, and there is ona’ah even with real estate, as explained in Choshen Mishpat there. So it appears to me. According to this, the Ran in chapter Bnei HaIr and Responsa Rashba, siman 617, do not disagree at all. Examine carefully. With this, all that the Taz and Magen Avraham strained over is resolved; see there, for I have been brief.
They built a house, etc. This means that they built it from non-sacred money, as explained in seif 4. However, it appears to me that even if they built it from synagogue money, it nevertheless does not have synagogue sanctity for all matters, only that it is forbidden to change it. Magen Avraham. See below, siman 42, that in all matters we require designation verbally and by an act. However, one who donated a place upon which to build a synagogue, or a house for a synagogue, or a covering for a Torah scroll, see Responsa Chavos Yair, siman 59. He wrote that he cannot retract, for it is considered like a vow, but he may use it for ordinary use, for sanctity does not take effect on it through designation. In siman 147, seif katan 3, I wrote even more than this. The Magen Avraham wrote that if he made it for a Torah scroll that he has in his home, as long as it was not used for the Torah scroll he may retract. This is not clear, and requires study.
