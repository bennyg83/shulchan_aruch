Others. For the others gave them money so that they should do with it whatever they wish.
In their sanctity. That is, it is forbidden to change them to a lesser sanctity.
That they wish. Even for a mundane matter, since the seven leaders of the city stipulated, the money leaves to mundane status. Taz. See above, s.k. 7.
The individual. This applies when they built it from their own funds. But if others donated for this, it does not help that they made it dependent on the opinion of the individual, for perhaps there is someone at the end of the world whose mind is not comfortable with this. If they do not pray in a synagogue of large towns, they may sell it. Mabit, part 3, siman 143. They may sell a cemetery. In R. M. M., siman 67, he wrote that with anything belonging to a synagogue, such as courtyards or a mikveh, we say that presumably many people gave for this, and they may not sell them or close them to prevent guests from using them, end quote. Nevertheless, it is not more stringent than a synagogue. Magen Avraham.
Sanctity. All synagogue utensils, benches, and curtains; see Magen Avraham.
To a lesser sanctity. The Magen Avraham deletes these words, and the text should read: that it is forbidden to change it requires public announcement. See there.
