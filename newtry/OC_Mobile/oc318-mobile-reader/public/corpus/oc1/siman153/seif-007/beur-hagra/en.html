Seif 7, these words apply to villages, where, etc., but of large towns, etc., even, etc. See Tosafot there, s.v. keivan, etc. The Rosh and Rambam agreed with the first explanation. So the Rashba proved from the fact that it asks there from the baraisa, etc. If it were so, what is the question? That which belongs to partners becomes impure through negaim, and only that which is designated for everyone does not become impure, as stated in the first chapter of Yoma. Here, because of the partners who are not present here, it cannot be sold. But according to the first explanation it is understandable, for it is consecrated for everyone and has no owners, similar to the Mikdash. This is what is meant by zutei hava, meaning, many people from elsewhere did not enter it. And what is stated, and they made it, means that they made it for themselves and not for the many.
And even, etc. As above, for the phrase and they made it is not precise, but means that it was for their own need. The proof is from what is stated there: Rav Ashi said, this, etc.; and here too, they gave it with them in mind.
Nevertheless, etc. From the Mishnah, as above.
And this is, etc. As stated in the Tosefta, chapter 2: Rabbi Menachem bar Yosei said, with money from a synagogue they may not buy the town square. Rabbi Yehudah said: when does this apply? When the leaders of the city did not stipulate with them. But if the leaders of the city stipulated with them, they may change it to whatever they wish.
And the same applies, etc. The Gemara there, and according to Rashi's explanation. The Rashba explained Rava's statement as stated in the Tosefta, and that is what is meant by, which they did not sell, etc. It is not difficult, for they sold through the seven leaders of the city not in the presence of the townspeople. Similarly, in the Mishnah it says, the townspeople who sold, etc. The Ran explained likewise. Nevertheless, they agree to the second law, that the seven leaders of the city not in the presence of the townspeople also have this law, as stated in the Yerushalmi: three from a synagogue are like a synagogue, meaning, for example, one of individuals such as coppersmiths; seven from the city are like the city, meaning, like the townspeople without the knowledge of their leaders. In what case are we dealing? If they accepted them upon themselves, even one person also, meaning, if they explicitly accepted him to sell this. And if they did not accept them, even many also not. Rather, it is unspecified, meaning, they appointed them generally to oversee communal affairs, and it teaches us that even though they did not explicitly accept them for these matters, the law applies. So the Ran there. This is what is meant by, but if they agreed, etc., and if they accepted upon themselves, etc. Meaning, what it says, even one also, is stronger, like the seven leaders of the city in the presence of the townspeople, since they explicitly agreed.
And all, etc. For this is what is meant by, in the presence, etc.
