(7) [Se'if 2] Townspeople who sold a synagogue, etc. They may even initially sell a synagogue to purchase an aron. 'Sold' is used because of the latter clause: in the reverse direction, even after selling an aron they may not purchase a synagogue with its proceeds [Prishah; Eliyah Rabbah]. This also follows se'if 6. Beis Yosef reads Rambam as forbidding initial sale even to raise the proceeds' sanctity, but Yad Aharon and Erech HaShulchan challenge this from the Gemara and cite several poskim, including Rambam, permitting it initially. This concerns a synagogue eligible for sale, such as a village synagogue, as in se'if 7.
(8) There: Townspeople who sold, etc. If a minority protests selling the synagogue and there are no appointed trustees overseeing communal affairs, the majority decides [Olas Tamid]. See below, paragraphs 44 and 112.
(9) There: Meaning the sanctuary, etc. It is uncertain whether other sacred books may be placed in the aron. This apparently is no descent, since the aron remains in sacred use; invalid Torah-scroll sheets, Chumashim, and other books are customarily placed there. It remains uncertain when the Torah scroll is removed and only other books are placed inside [Olas Tamid from Maharshal]. Bach at the siman's end is stringent, even forbidding other books or invalid sheets beside a Torah scroll. Since custom places other books there, however, it is as though an initial condition designated it for both, as siman 154:8, gloss, states. With such a condition, even when no Torah scroll is present, other books are not a descent. This justifies custom. Magen Avraham, siman 154:14, similarly says the court's implicit condition may permit it, and an aron built as a room plainly permits it. Nevertheless, when constructing an Aron Kodesh one should explicitly stipulate that other books may be placed there, including when it contains no Torah scroll. See siman 154:1, gloss, for what is called an Aron Kodesh, our comments there, and paragraph 64 on se'if 8.
(10) There: If they sold an aron, they may buy a Torah-scroll wrapping with its proceeds. They may likewise buy wrappings for Chumashim, Nevi'im, and Kesuvim [Maamar Mordechai], unlike Prishah's implication that Ran requires specifically a Torah-scroll wrapping.
(11) There: If they sold a wrapping, they buy books with its proceeds, etc. Even proceeds of Torah and Chumash wrappings may buy Nevi'im and Kesuvim. Conversely, with book proceeds one may not buy wrappings: even proceeds of Nevi'im and Kesuvim may not buy Torah or Chumash wrappings [Ran from Yerushalmi; Beis Yosef]. Beis Yehudah, Orach Chaim 54, says wrapping proceeds may likewise buy tefillin, an ascent because tefillin equal other sacred books. Making tefillin bags from Torah-scroll wrappings is plainly forbidden as a descent.
(12) There: If they sold a wrapping, they buy books with its proceeds. Meaning each Chumash separately, as well as Nevi'im and Kesuvim; Levush agrees. This appears to equate all three, yet Yoreh De'ah 282:19 says Chumashim have greater sanctity, as does Megillah 27a. Magen Avraham therefore forbids buying Nevi'im or Kesuvim with Chumash proceeds; Nevi'im and Kesuvim are equal, as Rema there states. Eliyah Rabbah explains that Shulchan Aruch and Levush equate them only relative to a Torah wrapping: even Nevi'im and Kesuvim exceed it, so their proceeds cannot buy one. Among the books themselves, Chumashim are more sacred. Nehar Shalom and Tosefes Chaim agree.
(13) There: Meaning each Chumash separately. Our printed Chumashim, even all five together, are below one Chumash written according to Torah-scroll rules [Eliyah Rabbah]. See above, siman 143:2, gloss.
(14) There: And likewise Nevi'im and Kesuvim. Their equality concerns the parchment sanctified through their writing. In the spiritual level of reading verses, Nevi'im exceeds Kesuvim. Shaar HaMitzvos, Vaeschanan, says Torah reading corresponds to Tiferes, Nevi'im to Netzach and Hod, and Kesuvim to Yesod and Malchus. For why Kesuvim precede Nevi'im on Rosh Hashanah, see Beis Yosef, siman 591, in Rosh's name.
(15) There: It is forbidden to lower them from their sanctity, etc. This is when townspeople sold without their leaders' consent, or seven town trustees sold without the townspeople present. If seven trustees agreed to the sale in the townspeople's presence, they may spend the money as they wish, as below in se'if 7 [Beis Yosef; Levush 3].
