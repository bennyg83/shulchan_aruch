(א) On exchanging the mutar (surplus): Taz brought Rashi Megillah 27a on "but when they collected money" — "they may lower" (moridin) — which implies leniently, i.e., not to reshut (communal property). And he agreed that Tur, Rambam, and Shulchan Aruch also hold thus, only to a lighter mitzvah. And for me there is a difficulty here, for per his view the Mechaber was zocheh the document to two people when he wrote "and if when they collected money," etc., and this is from Be'er HaGolah (Be'er HaGolah cited nothing). And it is difficult — what does the Gemara ask there? Establish the teaching in such a case, as Beit HaRoeh wrote in siman 14. And it must be said that dukseusya is reshut and mundane, and Be'er HaGolah said it is permitted to lower, and as his wording "permitted to lower" — to reshut, no. But per Rashi "they may lower" — if so, it is difficult what Taz asked here — what does it ask, perhaps it taught these for reshut? And it must be said that the questioner holds that dukseusya is also a light mitzvah (and the answerer that Taz answered in siman 5 — also for reshut, and he takes one of them). If so it is obvious that it asks nothing, for say as Be'er HaGolah as we said dukseusya is a light mitzvah and permitted in the manner that Rashi does not hold as Be'er HaGolah, and the Mechaber wrote "we exchange the mutar" — and the law is that Be'er HaGolah lowers, not as Taz said who was zocheh the document to two people, ostensibly, as I wrote.

(ב) And in the essence of explaining this law — behold Beit Yosef in his long book brought two answers: (1) that they collected money and it came into the hands of the gabba'im — it is an act (ma'aseh); therefore the money itself is forbidden to change them to a lighter mitzvah even if they later wish to give other money in exchange (rather they must exchange immediately and kedushah applies to both; and similarly in selling a sefer Torah, if the money was exchanged the first coins go to mundane use), and the mutar is permitted, etc. — and this is the wording of Rambam, chapter 1 of Hilchot Tefillin 14: "they sold a sefer Torah — they may not lower," and in Taz 15 "and similarly they collected money" implies similar to this that it is forbidden to change the money from their kedushah as there (but in mutar it is not similar), and Tur likewise "and similarly," etc. And on this Ramakh challenged in Kessef Mishneh that he brought there — on the Gemara Rava Megillah 27a "mutar they may exchange" — the body of the money, no — not that it is forbidden to lower them; the money, for kedushah did not apply to them at all in hazmanah (designation), like one who weaves a garment, etc. (see Tosafot there Sanhedrin 48a on "mutar," and Beit HaRoeh siman 8). Rather they do not fulfill their vow except from lighter to heavier; in this one estimates the intent of the donor — for heavier it is obvious that the intent of the one who vowed is agreeable to this; and therefore Ramakh challenged Rambam who wrote "and similarly," etc. And the Mechaber changed and did not write "and similarly" but only explicitly "if," etc. — meaning their vow is not fulfilled in the money unless from lighter to heavier, etc.; but from heavier to lighter and not to buy later anything heavier — their vow is not fulfilled; but certainly the money has no kedushah, and therefore the language of "lowering" applies to them. Unlike the law that if gabba'im collected, etc., Be'er HaGolah changed his wording and said "permitted to lower" even though above only lowering (per Rashi); nevertheless he informed us that lowering the body of the money is also forbidden even if they later give other money to fulfill their vow — nevertheless, since they already bought a sefer Torah and sold it, the money is caught in kedushah, like the people of the city who sold, etc., and without stipulation even the mutar is forbidden to remove those coins from their kedushah, as I wrote. And he held this view from that which Rava said Megillah 26b: old stones — forbidden to lend them; but new ones — permitted (see seif 11 where the law of atika is mentioned). And he does not distinguish in new ones themselves — if they came into the hands of gabba'im it is forbidden to lend them unless they exchange immediately, for kedushah applied to them — learn from this that when they came into the hands of gabba'im hazmanah also applies. And similarly Shulchan Aruch seif 13: "they collected," etc., for a light mitzvah and they will fulfill their vow later — kedushah does not apply to them, for hazmanah is not a thing (and Magen Avraham there siman 34 answered this — it deals with ZT"H b'mao"h that mutar they may exchange them — see there). And behold the master brought proof for his words from Sanhedrin 48a, that he challenges Rava: mutar of a corpse — why to heirs? He does not challenge from the body of the money — why to the dead? Hazmanah is not a thing; and necessarily, if they came to gabba'im it is also fine for heirs; and if they did not come to gabba'im he challenged from the body of the money — why to the dead? But per his method that vow is the topic, it is all the more so, and it is per Rashi's method that for reshut certainly not, and all agree in this as he preceded. If so it is difficult from there why he challenges from mutar — that in any case when they came into the hands of gabba'im it is forbidden to change entirely, only to lighter — meaning to heirs; but per what he wrote it is all the more so, for the reason forbidden for reshut is that their vow is not fulfilled — one estimates their intent that mutar would stam be for lighter, unlike there. And Beit HaRoeh siman 8, and per what he wrote it is all the more so. And what he wrote in that which he challenged "if you stipulated — why to me," and the explanation that it entered his mind that dukseusya is a mitzvah — we already wrote above what is difficult with this. And what Beit HaRoeh wrote siman 9, that even for dukseusya the language "even" implies only lighter helps with a condition, and from heavier to lighter no condition is needed — we find "even" not literally lighter but only as if he said even dukseusya which is reshut — a condition helps, and without a condition from heavier to lighter is permitted. And see Magen Avraham 5 what he challenged against Rava from Rambam and R. Nachman and it is not in dispute — see there.
