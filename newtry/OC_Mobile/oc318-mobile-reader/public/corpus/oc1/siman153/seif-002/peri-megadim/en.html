One changes. The surplus. See Taz, who cited Rashi on Megillah 27a, s.v. but they collected money, from which it implies leniently that for a lesser mitzvah it may be changed, but not for an optional use. He agreed that the Tur, Rambam, and Shulchan Aruch also hold this way, specifically to a lesser sanctity. In my poverty, I have a difficulty here. According to his view, the Mechaber has validated one document for two different parties, for what he wrote, if when they collected money, etc., is from Hagahot Ashri. The Be'er HaGolah cited nothing. It is difficult: why does the Gemara ask there, if they stipulated, let it establish the case in this way, as Eliyah Rabbah wrote in note 14. One must say that a dukhsusya is optional and mundane, and Hagahot Ashri said that it is permitted to lower, as his language states, permitted to lower, implying that to optional use it is not permitted. But according to Rashi, who says they lower, the difficulty remains, as the Taz asks here: perhaps a stipulation helps for optional use. One must say that the questioner thought dukhsusya was also a lesser mitzvah, and the one who answered, the seven leaders of the city in the presence of the townspeople, and dukhsusya is optional, as written in note 5, could likewise have answered for optional use, and he chose one of them. If so, there is plainly no difficulty, for perhaps it is like Hagahot Ashri, as we said, that dukhsusya is a lesser mitzvah and is permitted. Thus Rashi does not hold like Hagahot Ashri. The Mechaber wrote that one changes the surplus, and the law of Hagahot Ashri, that one may lower, fits well, as stated. But according to the Taz, the document seems to serve two different purposes, as I wrote.
And regarding the main point. In the explanation of this law, the Beit Yosef in his long work brought two answers. One is that when they collected money and it came into the hand of the treasurer, that is considered an act; therefore the money itself may not be changed to a lesser sanctity even if they later want to give other money in exchange, unless he immediately exchanges it and the sanctity takes effect upon the second money. So too, when a Torah scroll is sold, if the money is exchanged, the first money becomes profane. The surplus, however, is permitted. This is the language of the Rambam, Laws of Tefillah 11:14, if they sold a Torah scroll they may not lower it, and in halacha 15, likewise if they collected money, it implies that it is similar, that it is forbidden to change the money from its sanctity, as there. However, with regard to surplus it is not similar. The Tur wrote likewise. The Ramach objected to this, as the Kesef Mishneh brings there, from the Gemara's statement of Rava in Megillah 27a that the surplus may be changed, implying that the body of the money may not. This does not mean that it is forbidden to lower the money, for no sanctity takes effect on it at all through designation, like weaving a garment, but rather that they do not fulfill their vow except from a lighter sanctity to a stricter one. In that regard we assess the donor's intent, that certainly with respect to greater sanctity the vow is satisfied. Therefore the Ramach objected to the Rambam, who wrote all this. The Mechaber changed the language and did not write likewise, but wrote this as a separate law: if, etc., meaning that they do not fulfill their vow with the money except from lighter to stricter sanctity, but from stricter to lighter, or not to buy the stricter thing at all afterward, they do not fulfill their vow. But certainly the money itself has no sanctity, and therefore the language of lowering does not apply to it. By contrast, in the law that if they collected money and bought, Hagahot Ashri, etc., he changed his language and said that it is permitted to lower. Although above too it is only lowering, as Rashi explains, nevertheless he taught us that even the body of the money is forbidden to lower, even if they later give other money to fulfill their vow. Since they already bought a Torah scroll and sold it, the money is seized with sanctity, like townspeople who sold, etc., and without a stipulation even the surplus is forbidden to lower from its sanctity, as I wrote. He strengthened this reasoning from Rava in Megillah 26b, that old bricks may not be loaned out, but new ones may. See seif 11, where the law of old bricks is mentioned. He does not distinguish among new bricks themselves, that if they came into the hand of the treasurer it would be forbidden to loan them unless they exchange them immediately because sanctity took effect. This proves that even when they came into the treasurer's hand, designation is still not significant. This also appears from Shulchan Aruch, seif 13, that if they collected for one mitzvah, they may change it to a lesser mitzvah and later complete their vow, and sanctity does not take effect on it, because designation is not significant. The Magen Avraham there, note 30, answered that it speaks of the seven leaders of the city in the presence of the townspeople, where it is permitted to change it; see there. The master brought proof for his words from Sanhedrin 48a, where it asks why the surplus for the dead belongs to the heirs; it does not ask regarding the body of the money, why it should go to the dead, since designation is not significant. If it came to the treasurer, the surplus also should properly go to the heirs, and if it did not come to the treasurer, it should have asked about the body of the money, why it goes to the dead. But according to his approach, which speaks about fulfilling the vow, it is fine. This follows Rashi's approach, that for optional use certainly not, and all agree to this, as he prefaced. If so, the difficulty there is why it asks from the surplus, since if it came into the treasurer's hand, at any rate it is forbidden to change entirely, only to a lesser purpose, meaning to the heirs. But according to what he wrote it is fine, for the reason optional use is forbidden is that they have not fulfilled their vow, since we assess their intent that the surplus should presumably be for a lesser mitzvah, unlike there. See Eliyah Rabbah, note 8, and according to what I wrote it is fine. What he wrote regarding the question, if they stipulated why do I need it, and answered that one might have thought dukhsusya is a mitzvah, I have already written above what is difficult with that. What Eliyah Rabbah wrote in note 9, that even dukhsusya implies all the more a lesser sanctity, where a stipulation helps, although from stricter to lighter no stipulation is needed, one can say that we find the word even where it is not an actual all the more, but as if it said, even dukhsusya, which is optional, is helped by a stipulation, whereas without a stipulation from stricter to lighter is permitted.
