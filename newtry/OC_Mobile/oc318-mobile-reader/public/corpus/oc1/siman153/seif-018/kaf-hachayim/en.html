(18) [seif 4] Whether it is permitted to buy, etc. — some forbid and some permit. The opinion of those who forbid is the opinion of Ran, brought in Beit Yosef, who wrote that since we asked in the gemara regarding a Torah scroll, where there is nothing higher to elevate to, it follows that it is obvious to us that regarding those other [items] where there is something higher to elevate to, it is forbidden to sell them and buy another of the same level. The opinion of those who permit is the opinion of Mahari Avuhav, who wrote that from the words of the Tur, who wrote: but the reverse, to lower them from their holiness — no, it implies we are only careful not to lower them from their holiness, but to buy another of the same level is permitted; and he wrote that he found this written in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah — until here. Bach wrote that what Mahari Avuhav said, that buying another of the same level is permitted, is only if they already sold it; b'dieved it is permitted to buy another of the same level, although there is a higher elevation to buy a Torah scroll with that money; but l'chatchila it is forbidden to sell them in order to buy another of the same level, as Ran wrote, for it is obvious, and Mahari Avuhav does not disagree with Ran at all in this; and regarding a Torah scroll we asked whether it is permitted l'chatchila, since there is nothing higher to elevate to, and it was not resolved, and we are stringent as Ran wrote — end of his words. And since Bach wrote nothing on the words of Shulchan Aruch, who wrote here that some forbid and some permit, it implies Bach holds that l'chatchila according to all opinions it is forbidden to sell in order to buy another of the same level, and there is no dispute about this; the dispute is only if they already sold — some forbid buying another of the same level and some permit. This is also the view of Shulchan Aruch, that it deals with a case where they already sold, and on that he wrote two opinions: some forbid and some permit; but l'chatchila according to all opinions it is forbidden to sell in order to buy another of the same level, as written in Yoreh De'ah at the beginning of siman 270 regarding a Torah scroll; see there. And although from Ran brought in Beit Yosef it implies Ran speaks about l'chatchila, one who looks there in Ran in chapter Bnei Ha'Ir on the Rif will see that it implies there that even if they already sold for other things, one must elevate since it is possible, except regarding a Torah scroll since it is not possible; see there. And so Maran, may his memory be blessed, explained in Kesef Mishneh the words of Rambam, may his memory be blessed, in chapter 11 of Hilchot Tefillah, law 14; see there. And so Avnei Nezer sections 6 and 7, and Birkei Yosef section 2 wrote, and they resolved the questions of the Taz on Shulchan Aruch and questioned Magen Avraham; see there. And so too Maamar Mordechai section 2 wrote, and wrote that in this all the words of the Taz were rejected, and also Magen Avraham did not scrutinize sufficiently in understanding the words of Bach; see there. And so too HaNei Shalom section 2 wrote and answered all the questions of the Taz; see there. And also the yesh omrim in Hagahot Beit Yosef questioned Magen Avraham regarding what he wrote about Rambam from the words of Kesef Mishneh mentioned; see there. However, Ateret Zekenim section 2 wrote that in Badek HaBayit siman 154 it is proven explicitly that Rabbeinu Yonah and the Tur ruled leniently and permit even l'chatchila; see there; except that he wrote that nevertheless it appears we follow that even if they already sold, we require elevation, and it is forbidden to buy another sanctity of the same level; for such is the opinion of R' Ephraim and Raavad and Ran and Rashba in volume 18, etc.; see there. And so Chida in Machzik Berachah section 1 wrote that from Badek HaBayit siman 154 it is proven that Maran held Rabbeinu Yonah permits even l'chatchila, which is the opposite of what I agreed in Birkei Yosef, that also Maran agrees they dispute only b'dieved; except that he wrote it is possible to reconcile the view of Maran, may his memory be blessed: he held Rabbeinu Yonah permits even l'chatchila, and he relied upon him specifically b'dieved; and he concluded that regarding halacha, although Maran held that l'chatchila they dispute, since it is possible to bring the views close and say they dispute b'dieved, but l'chatchila also the Tur and Rabbeinu Yonah forbid, as Beit Tov wrote; and so appears from the words of the Meiri that it is correct to say that l'chatchila all agree to forbid; see there. It follows that most poskim hold that b'dieved they dispute, but l'chatchila all agree it is forbidden; and most poskim also hold that Maran's view is so. And see our words above siman 13 section 7, where we wrote that everywhere Maran brings "yesh omrim" and "yesh omrim" and the first view is to be stringent and the second is to be lenient, his view is to rule as the second, and only to be concerned l'chatchila for the words of those who forbid; see there. Therefore, in this law, l'chatchila it is forbidden to sell an item of kedushah in order to buy another of the same level; and b'dieved, if they already sold, if it is possible to elevate, one must buy something higher; and if it is not possible, for example if they need that same item and no elevation is required, or for example if they already bought that item of the same level, one may rely on those who permit. And see further below siman 154 section 73.
