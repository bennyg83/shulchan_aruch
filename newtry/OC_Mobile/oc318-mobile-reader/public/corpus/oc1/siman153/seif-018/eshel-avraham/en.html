Consecrated property. Members of a burial society made silver objects for the synagogue of a particular congregation and kept them in their own custody. Most or all of those members later passed away, others succeeded them, and the successors likewise safeguarded those objects. Recently, because of a quarrel, these men left that congregation to pray with another congregation, and the original congregation now demands that they surrender the objects. Responsa Mahari Bazan rules that the congregation is correct; see siman 2. When a minority of a congregation demands its share in sacred objects and wishes to separate from the congregation, see Responsa Magen Gibborim, siman 1, which rules that the objects are divided according to the number of males aged thirteen and above. It also rules there that the law of ‘either divide or buy out the other’ does not apply to a synagogue.
