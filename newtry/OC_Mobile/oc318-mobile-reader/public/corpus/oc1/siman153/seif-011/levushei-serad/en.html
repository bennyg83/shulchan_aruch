There. And this is what is written in seif 13. The Magen Avraham comes to say how the Beis Yosef found this law in the Rambam, for in truth many poskim permit. Therefore he wrote that this is the language of the Rambam cited in seif 13, that they should not sell a synagogue for redemption of captives, and certainly not for a more stringent sanctity, namely in such a case where they have no other synagogue. Although in seif 13 the Magen Avraham explained that by law it is permitted, see there, that is because he rules like the Shach and Taz, who explain the Rambam’s words that way. But the Beis Yosef explains that by law it is forbidden, and here the Magen Avraham writes the view of the Beis Yosef. So it is clear in Magen Avraham, seif katan 7, where he wrote there that the implication from seif 13 is as he wrote here in the name of the Rambam, that it is forbidden to buy a more stringent sanctity when they have no other synagogue, because the plain language of seif 13 implies that by law it is forbidden. He concluded there, “and see Yoreh Deah, siman 252,” and his intent is that there the Shach and Taz agreed that by law it is permitted. Therefore in seif 13 the Magen Avraham wrote their view, and it implies that he ruled like them. In any event, here he explained the view of Rabbi Beis Yosef, and this is clear. It seems to me that the Peri Megadim in seif katan 17 did not understand this correctly; see there. I will not elaborate, for my intention here is only to explain the plain meaning of the words of the later authorities, of blessed memory, and no more. This is easy to understand.
