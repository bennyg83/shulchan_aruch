And so they may exchange them for others. This is the wording of the Tur, and its source is Megillah 26b, where it says to those people, exchanging them is permitted. Now, according to what the Mechaber ruled above in seif 4, that there are those who forbid exchanging the sanctity for something of equal sanctity, one must explain that what the Gemara permitted means exchanging for something that is an elevation of sanctity, but not for other bricks. So wrote the Korban Nesanel. But according to this, the wording of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch is difficult. According to what the Beit Yosef wrote above, that the Tur permits it for something equal to it, the wording of the Tur is fine, but the wording of the Shulchan Aruch still remains difficult. Perhaps it speaks where the bricks of the old synagogue were not good, and this is included in a visible defect, where all agree it is permitted, as I wrote above in Mishnah Berurah, seif katan 11. To pledge them, etc. In Responsa Beis David he permits pledging silver sacred objects only as security for a loan, provided he does not use them at all. This is cited in Ikrei Dinim, and it is obvious.
Or to rent them, etc. Regarding a conditional sale, Tosafot, s.v. renting and pledging, implies that it is equal to pledging and renting, and is forbidden. In the novellae of the Ritva he is lenient in this; see his reason there.
It is permitted to lend. This means the majority of the townspeople, or the appointees through whom every matter is done, as stated by the later authorities.
