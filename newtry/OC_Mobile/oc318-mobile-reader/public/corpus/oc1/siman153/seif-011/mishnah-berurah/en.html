A synagogue. An old one.
From an old synagogue. Meaning, one in which they already prayed, as above in seif 8.
They may give. This means the seven leaders of the city in the presence of the townspeople. Some say that even without the presence of the townspeople they may do so. In large cities it is forbidden in every case.
As a gift. The recipient may use them for non-sacred use.
For if they did not have, etc. Or if they hope to derive benefit from him at some time. According to this, to give it to people of a distant city, who certainly did not benefit from them and will never benefit from them, is forbidden, for there is no other item upon which the sanctity of the synagogue can take effect. Therefore its sanctity is not removed, and it is forbidden to use it. All this speaks where they give it to use for non-sacred purposes. But to give it to them as a gift so that they pray in it is permitted, for there is no lowering from sanctity, as written in the gloss. See the Magen Avraham, who leans to say that the same applies that it is permitted for them to give a Torah scroll, and likewise all accessories of sanctity, as a gift, since they do not lower them from their sanctity by doing so.
To pledge them or to rent them. Since the body of the object has not yet been sold, it remains in the domain of the first owners and stands in its sanctity.
Or to lend them. Meaning, for a period of time, after which he returns the same objects intact. But as a loan absolutely, where the borrower will return other bricks in their place, it is certainly permitted, for that is an exchange, which we permit.
Even through the seven leaders of the city. Even if it was also in the presence of the townspeople.
For there is no other item, etc. Although he received money when he pledged them or rented them, one cannot say that the sanctity takes effect upon the money, for the body of the object is not acquired by him, and it still remains in its sanctity while he is using sacred property.
For they lower them from their sanctity. Meaning that the borrower or renter uses them for non-sacred purposes.
Even a Torah scroll, etc. All the more so one may lend a synagogue to pray in it. See Beis Teshuvah, that this is specifically when the borrower or renter reads from the Torah scroll, and likewise prays in the synagogue with ten. Otherwise there is disgrace to sanctity. The Magen Avraham, in seif katan 27, wrote that from the plain wording of the poskim it implies that it is permitted in every case.
