They may give. This means the seven leaders of the city in the presence of the townspeople, as written in seif 6 and seif 9. Rif, Rosh, and Ran. In large cities it is forbidden.
For were it not, etc. But to give it to a distant city from which they certainly will have no benefit is forbidden. Certainly according to the one who forbids selling a Torah scroll, it is forbidden to employ a device whereby they give them the Torah scroll as a gift and they give them money as a gift, for this is forbidden. Beis Yosef, Rivash. Nevertheless, his words require study regarding what he forbids in giving a Torah scroll as a gift, for one does not lower it from its sanctity thereby, as written in the gloss. So too Tosafot wrote, and Rashi wrote that specifically when they wish to remove the sanctity of the synagogue, then another matter is needed upon which its sanctity can take effect. But when they give it to the public so that they will pray in it, its sanctity remains upon it and no other matter is needed for the sanctity to take effect upon. So too the Knesses HaGedolah wrote in the name of Mabit, part 2, siman 28, that even one of large cities is sold in this manner. From the public to an individual is also forbidden unless there is another matter upon which its sanctity can take effect; then it is permitted, for we rule like the Sages. The Bach erred in this and wrote things about Tosafot that are not according to the law. However, the view of the poskim is that from the public to an individual is permitted whenever they do not remove its sanctity, as written in the gloss, and so the Beis Yosef wrote from the fact that the poskim stated it without qualification. The Mishnah Berurah wrote that if they built a new synagogue and demolished the old one by the authority of the seven leaders of the city in the presence of the townspeople, the sanctity departs from the old one and takes effect upon the new one, and they may change the old one to whatever they wish, for it is no worse than a gift. But otherwise, even the seven leaders of the city in the presence of the townspeople have no power to make them into a non-sacred matter, since there is no other matter upon which their sanctity can take effect. So it implies in Tosefta, chapter 2: stones of the Temple that became damaged require genizah. There was an incident of one who bought the synagogue of Alexandria and used it for all his needs; they forbade only that its first name should not still be called upon it. End quote. This implies that while it has not been sold, it is forbidden to use it for non-sacred purposes. This requires study, for why is this different from a menorah, regarding which he wrote in Yoreh Deah, siman 259, seif 3, that it is permitted to change it to a non-sacred matter? One must say that a synagogue is different, since its main purpose is that matters of sanctity be said in it; its sanctity cannot depart for nothing, because of the honor of sanctity, as the Ran wrote. However, the Rosh wrote in a responsum at the beginning of klal 5, brought in Bedek HaBayis, that it is permitted to make a synagogue into a dwelling house, for the seven leaders of the city may change it to a non-sacred matter, etc. So too he wrote in the first chapter of Bava Basra, that a menorah may be changed because it is a synagogue need, and the synagogue itself may be sold, etc.; see there. This implies that he holds it is permitted to change a synagogue. This requires study, for how does its sanctity depart for nothing? Perhaps the Rosh speaks where they built another in its place and the sanctity took effect upon it, as written above. So too the Nimukei Yosef wrote that the case where a menorah may be changed speaks where they made another.
