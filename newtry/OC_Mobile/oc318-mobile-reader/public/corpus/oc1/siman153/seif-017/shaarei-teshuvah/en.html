That they pray there. See Baer Heitev. See Chavos Yair, siman 59, regarding one who, during a fair, provided a room for visitors to pray in. The house was later sold to another person and then to another, and it had been explicitly stated that the room remained non-sacred. Nevertheless, the last owner also used it for prayer on the Yamim Noraim, and now wishes to live in that room and provide another room instead. He writes at length that this is certainly permitted, and all the more so since he is doing an additional kindness by providing another room in its place, for which he is deserving of blessing. But if he explicitly vowed that this particular room would forever serve at fixed times as a synagogue, providing another room on the upper floor does not suffice, because perhaps the worshippers prefer the first room. It is then like a public synagogue, which at least requires the consent of the townspeople. See there. See similarly Responsa Noda BiYehudah, second edition, siman 17, regarding a village synagogue in which a room had been designated and prayers and supplications containing Divine names that may not be erased were written on the walls. The consent of the villagers is required, and the proceeds should be used to support Talmud Torah, etc. In any event, it is forbidden to distill liquor there, because the smoke blackens the walls and causes the Divine names to be erased.
