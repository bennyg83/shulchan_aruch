Another. The same applies to one who acquired some matter of honor or of a mitzvah: it is not removed from him unless there is some claim by the challenger. This is not only for matters practiced constantly, but even for something that comes from time to time, and so wrote the Raanach, part 2, siman 70. See R. Y. Halevi, siman 44, and R. Sh. Halevi, Orach Chaim, siman 9. Even if a Torah scholar comes to acquire some mitzvah, it is not removed from the one established in it, even if he is an am ha’aretz. If this person sold his house to Reuven, we do not have complete proof that the buyer acquires that chazakah; Raanach, part 2, siman 70. All this discusses only where they wish to remove the mitzvos and honor entirely from the one established in them. But if another comes to acquire mitzvos and the first will also remain with his mitzvah, he cannot prevent it, even though some who prayed in the first synagogue now pray in the second synagogue; even so he cannot prevent it. Raanach, part 2, siman 70. A society that learned in the house of a wealthy man for several years and afterward wished to learn two months in each house may not change; Be’er Esek, siman 5. See Choshen Mishpat, end of siman 149.
