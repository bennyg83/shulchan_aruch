Seif katan 17, it is forbidden, etc. For there is no concern for negligence. That is when there is already a built synagogue and they buy it immediately.
Or if they wish to buy a stricter sanctity. For in such a case it is enough if they have another synagogue, as the Magen Avraham wrote above in seif 4 at the end of seif katan 4, and as he writes here immediately.
See what I wrote in seif 4 in the name of the Rambam, etc., and in the Gemara there, and see what I will write with the help of Heaven in seif katan 33.
The Mishnah Berurah wrote, etc. That presumably, etc.; but if it is clear to us, etc.; and although from the language of the Rosh and Rabbeinu Yerucham it implies, etc., presumably the people of the city consecrated according to their own intent.
And not necessarily, etc. At the time of building, etc. Nevertheless, from the language of Tosafot, etc. I do not understand, for according to everyone, even if they built it without specifying, etc. It appears that the Magen Avraham understood the words of Responsa Masas Binyamin as follows: that according to Tosafot, Ramban, and Ran, if a synagogue was built, for example, many years ago, forty or fifty years, and now at the time of sale they do not remember with whose intent it was built, whether with the intent of the townspeople alone or with the intent of all who come to pray, in this case we say that presumably they originally built it explicitly with the intent of all who come to pray. But if it is clear to us that they built it only with the intent of the townspeople, meaning it is clear that they built it without specification, we say their intent was certainly only for the townspeople alone. Although we see that many people come from elsewhere to pray, we do not say that even though they did not stipulate at the time of building that they were building with the intent of the whole world, nevertheless afterward, when people came from other places to pray, they consecrated it with the intent of everyone. Rather, it is called a village. The plain language of the Rosh and Rabbeinu Yerucham implies that they hold that even if we know they built it without specification, nevertheless afterward, when others come to pray, they consecrated it with the intent of everyone, for the consecration takes effect when they begin to pray and not at the time of building, as written in seif 8. On this the Magen Avraham wrote that this is not so, for there is no dispute in this. Even according to Tosafot, Ramban, and Ran, whenever they built without specification and did not stipulate that they were building specifically with the intent of the townspeople alone, it has the law of a city synagogue. See there in Responsa Masas Binyamin, where it is explained somewhat more.
