Mishna Berurah wrote, presumably, etc. — but if it is clear to us, etc. Although from the language of Rosh and Rivash it implies presumably the people of the city consecrated for their own intent — and Levush, etc., at the time of building; nevertheless from the language of Tosafot, etc., and I did not understand that according to all, even if built unspecified, etc. It appears Mahari Avraham understood the responsum of Mishna Berurah: that according to Tosafot and Ramban and Ran, if the synagogue was built in this manner many years ago, forty or fifty, and now at the time of sale they do not remember for whose intent it was built — whether only the people of the city or all who come to pray there — we say presumably it was built originally with the intent of all who come to pray; but if it is clear to us they built only with the intent of the people of the city alone — meaning we are certain their intent was only for the people of the city. And although we see many come from outside to pray, we do not say that since they did not stipulate at building that they build for the intent of the whole world, nevertheless afterwards when they come from other places to pray they consecrated it for the intent of all; rather it is called a village. And Rosh and Rivash — the plain language implies they hold that even if they know it was built unspecified, nevertheless afterwards when they come to pray they consecrated it for the intent of all, for consecration takes effect when they begin to pray, not at the time of building, as written in seif 8. And on this Mahari Avraham wrote it is not so — there is no dispute here. And also the view of Tosafot, Ramban, and Ran: whenever it was built unspecified and they did not stipulate they build specifically for the people of the city alone — it has the status of a city. And see in the responsum of Mishna Berurah, explained somewhat more.
