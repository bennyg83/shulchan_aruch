They may not, etc. Because of the ways of peace, so that people should not say about this household that they are unworthy people, and therefore the public refrained from praying there. Therefore, even if the public has some reason for the matter, they still are not permitted.
The public. If only part of the public wants to separate and pray elsewhere, they are permitted, since some still pray in his house and the mitzvah is never nullified from it. But a society that had been learning in one house for several years, and afterward they want to learn two months in each house, may not change.
To change it. The same applies to one who acquired some matter of honor or a mitzvah; we do not remove it from him, unless those who object have a complete claim. This is not only with matters that apply constantly, but even with matters that come only from time to time, the law is likewise. The later authorities wrote that people who established themselves in burying the dead cannot prevent others who come to bury, for in performing a mitzvah everyone wants to merit. In countries where there are societies appointed for this, who leave their affairs throughout the year when this mitzvah comes their way, one must give to the society according to their custom.
In another house. Meaning, in the house of an individual. But if they want to build a fixed synagogue, they are permitted, and there is no concern here of the ways of peace.
