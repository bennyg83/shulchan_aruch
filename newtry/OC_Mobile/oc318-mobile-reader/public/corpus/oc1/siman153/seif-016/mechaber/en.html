[In the case of] a person that lent his house for a synagogue and has a dispute with another congregant - he cannot forbid that person [from entering], unless he forbids the whole congregation.Rem"a: And that is specifically when he lent it from the outset without conditions. But if he stipulated from the outset that anytime that he will want to protest, he will have the right to protest (prevent someone's entrance), or if he he did not explicitly lend [it] to them, but rather just allowed them to enter his house, he has the right to protest as he wishes (Mahara"m Padua, Section 85).
