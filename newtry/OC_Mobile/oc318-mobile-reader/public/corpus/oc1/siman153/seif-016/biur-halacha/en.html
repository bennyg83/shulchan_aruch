One who lent his house for a synagogue. This means that he lent it so that it should have the full name of a synagogue. But if he lent them his house merely so that they would come to pray in it, this is not included, as Eliyah Rabbah writes in the name of Maharit Tzahalon.
He cannot, etc. This is not precise, for if he transgressed and prohibited it, it is forbidden, as written in Responsa R. Yom Tov Tzahalon. Rather, he is not permitted to do so, because of the ban that the early authorities enacted over this, as written in Kol Bo and in Responsa Maharam of Rothenburg. This is like this expression found in the Beit Yosef, as Eliyah Rabbah explains. As for what is stated in seif 15, that if he prohibited it, it is nothing, and there too it is because of the enactment, perhaps there the enactment was made that way. Nevertheless, this requires study.
To prohibit it. Maharash Duran wrote in responsum, siman 7, that if there is another synagogue in that city he can prohibit it. For granted, when there is no other synagogue, what can that individual do? He would remain without prayer with the congregation, and the one prohibiting it causes him to stumble and prevents him from communal prayer. This is not so when there is another synagogue. Rabbi Akiva Eiger copied this briefly. According to the reason for the enactment that we wrote in Mishnah Berurah, his ruling is not compelling.
But if he stipulated from the beginning, etc. The reason is that since he stipulated, his condition is valid. This is the wording of Darkei Moshe. It implies from this that his condition was that he could object even to an individual.
