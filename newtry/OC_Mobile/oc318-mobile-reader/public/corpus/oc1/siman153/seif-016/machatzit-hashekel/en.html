Seif katan 14, the words, etc. Meaning, if they sold, etc. That is, what is written in the Shulchan Aruch, “the same applies to all sacred items, etc.,” should not be understood to mean that they are equal to a synagogue in every respect. Rather, they are not equal regarding the object itself: a synagogue that was sold may be used by the buyer for non-sacred purposes, but sacred items that are sold remain in their sanctity even in the buyer’s possession.
Seif katan 16, and all this, etc. And it implies, etc., for it refers to that. Meaning, the Rama explains that with a village synagogue, it was permitted only when they have now built another. On this he says: but one of cities, etc., is not sold, implying that in the very case where it is permitted for villages, it is forbidden for cities.
Because it is not in their power to remove, etc. For it depends on the intent of the entire world, since it was built with the intent of the entire world, as above.
But demolishing is permitted when they have now built another. For to this the entire world certainly agrees, since they have another synagogue, as I wrote above in siman 152, seif katan 2. And if the concern is that they do not wish the first sanctity to be removed, since we have not heard the opinion of the entire world, through demolition the first sanctity is still not removed from the wood, stones, and ground. So too it appears from the Rambam that they are exactly alike, meaning a synagogue or the wood and stones of a demolished synagogue.
It does not appear to me as written. That nevertheless, so it should be written.
As written in Choshen Mishpat, siman 359, it is forbidden to steal even in order to pay something better than it. End quote.
It is impossible to remove their sanctity without their consent, even though they lose nothing, for even something better may not be given without the owner’s consent.
