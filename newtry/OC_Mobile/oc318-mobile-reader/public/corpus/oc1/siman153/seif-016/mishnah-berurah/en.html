He cannot forbid it. This means because of the cherem of Rabbeinu Gershom that applies to this. The reason appears to be that they were concerned for that man's disgrace. See Gittin 57. This is unlike where he forbids it to everyone, where there is no embarrassment. What is said that he can forbid it to the entire congregation is because he lent it to them only without specification; but if he lent it to them for a fixed time, see Yoreh Deah, siman 221, seif 6 and 7, where there is a dispute whether the prohibition takes effect during that time.
To object to whomever he wishes. This is the correct reading, and so it is in Darkei Moshe.
