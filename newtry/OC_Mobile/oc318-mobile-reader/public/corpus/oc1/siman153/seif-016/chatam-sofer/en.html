There, Magen Avraham, seif katan 49. And their words require study. Note: in my humble opinion their words may be resolved as follows. They hold that according to Rabbi Meir, who is not concerned for enmity, the reason is that in other appointments the first does not return to his authority at all. If so, granted that by a Kohen Gadol the first returns to his service, nevertheless he will not have enmity if the second also serves with him, for in other appointments the first has nothing at all; thus it is enough for him that he returns to his service. But Rabbi Yosei holds that in all appointments the law is that the first returns to his service. If so, we are concerned for enmity. Therefore it was necessary to rule that the halacha follows Rabbi Yosei, and this is easy to understand. See regarding this for practical halacha in my responsa, Chatam Sofer, Orach Chaim, simanim 12, 13, and 41, where I wrote about appointment to a rabbinic position, whether the son inherits his father’s seat, and elaborated to clarify this matter. See also Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat, simanim 21 and 22.
