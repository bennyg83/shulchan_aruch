Unless it depends, etc. The Magen Avraham explained that this is specifically when they built it from their own funds; but if others donated for it, the fact that it depends on the view of an individual does not help, and so it implies in Nachalas Tzvi. In Responsa Masas Binyamin, siman 33, he wrote that in these lands, where the practice of the communities is to appoint leaders and officers over them, and they have authority and discipline in all communal affairs, there is no distinction between villages and large towns. In all cases the leaders have power to sell the synagogue even to drink beer in it, and the money also becomes non-sacred, even if the townspeople object to them. The Nachalas Tzvi disagrees and says that even nowadays it is forbidden unless it is known that they built it from their own funds and explicitly made it dependent on the view of one individual. From Magen Avraham, seif katan 37, it implies that whenever they built it from their own funds, nowadays we do not require that they make it dependent on the view of one individual, and one may be lenient. See seif katan 17. In the Raavan, at the beginning of the simanim of the book, I found proof for the Masas Binyamin, for the town officers are called chaver ir. The Taz wrote that even regarding a synagogue of large towns, the prohibition applies only when they sold it in a manner that afterward it will no longer be a synagogue. But if it will remain a synagogue, it is permitted, even from many to an individual, like the Rabbis in Megillah 27b.
