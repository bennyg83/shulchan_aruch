It is nothing. This is with respect to the enactment, but by law he can prohibit it, as written in Yoreh Deah, siman 224; see there. It is written in Hagahos Maimoniyos in the name of R. Shimshon: a community had a box in which they placed the tithe, and one of them prohibited his share, and he permitted it to them. End quote. This is difficult: how is it different from a synagogue? Perhaps what they put into the box, he had already set aside from his mind, and it stood to be spent according to the desire of the leaders of the community, so it was like not being his to prohibit to them. This is unlike a synagogue, which stands forever, and each person seeks to retain his share forever. Nevertheless, it is good to request release from his vow. Yam Shel Shlomo, Bava Kama, chapter 5, seif 35. It appears to me that tithe is not his, as stated in the Mishnah, chapter 11 of Nedarim.
