Taz wrote (it should read note 13, and the citation is in error as written in note 14) and see further in Magen Avraham note 44 on this, and here one cannot elaborate. The writer said: I saw fit to explain somewhat the laws of sanctity and its accessories and other matters, and from the true helper I will ask help to explain properly on this.

Know that the terms mentioned in the Talmud and poskim on this are these: (1) kedushah; (2) accessories of sanctity; (3) accessory of an accessory; (4) mitzvah; (5) accessories of a mitzvah; (6) accessory of an accessory of a mitzvah — and I will explain somewhat. Kedushah: Torah, Prophets, and Writings are words of sanctity; also Talmuds and midrashim are included in words of sanctity, and it is forbidden to treat them disrespectfully — see Yoreh De'ah 282 and in siman 154 note 13; and even printed books have sanctity in them — Taz there Avodah Berurah note 8, and Magen Avraham siman 154 note 14 wondered how they place sacred writings on the pages of books — see there; and in Avodah Berurah note 9 he wrote for Taz there note 7 that when it is not fit for more stringent, better to do lighter — one can say this is in accessories of sanctity, not in kedushah itself; and especially sacred writings of Prophets and Writings — one can say they require burial (Magen Avraham there note 9), and it will be explained further below on this. And if there are Divine Names that may not be erased — one can say this is not erasure but covering; and see responsum Me'il Tzedakah to the gaon R' Yonah Laniado zt"l siman 23 from the gaon Panim Me'irot zt"l: in a synagogue they wish to plaster lime on the names — for all it is permitted, and even the author there forbids lime with water, for it is erasure, unlike binding book pages with glue — one can say it is not erasure but covering; and still requires study.

(ג) There is a difference between Torah and Nevi'im and Ketuvim: in Torah, for use it is forbidden until there is a partition, as explained in Rambam 276, and in other books a vessel within a vessel suffices. And in Yoreh De'ah 282:8 they said chumashim suffice in a vessel within a vessel; and in Orach Chayim Rambam wrote chumashim are like a sefer Torah, and one must distinguish whether they are made rolled or not. And similarly it appears from Beit Yosef Orach Chayim Rambam that Tosafot on "rolled" is the topic, and with this Shulchan Aruch deals, and in Yoreh De'ah "not rolled," and Beit Yosef there explains "not rolled," and the halacha is like RaSh, but nevertheless he was stringent in Orach Chayim for those made rolled. And one should see that "rolled" implies they are equal to a sefer Torah literally for all its laws — for otherwise from where do we learn that chumashin rolled require a partition for use, since a house in which there is a sefer Torah was stated — learn from this that it is literally equal, only that one does not read chumashin in public because of honor of the congregation; and this is the opinion of RaShba in a responsum that Beit Yosef brought in Yoreh De'ah 282 regarding standing for chumashin if made rolled like a sefer Torah for all their matters; and in Shulchan Aruch in the gloss there 282:2 "our chumashin" — one need not stand; and similarly Levush — they are not made in their proper form; if rolled like a sefer Torah, therefore likewise one does not sell except to learn Torah and to marry a woman — chumashin in their proper form, and one stands before them. And the opinion of Rambam, chapter ten of Hilchot Sefer Torah 1:2, is not so — any one that has one error and lacks one letter has no kedushah of a sefer Torah — see there; and you see that in Orach Chayim Rambam and Yoreh De'ah 282 ruled not according to his view, and what is written of Rambam — all that are not made per the repair — combined the view of Rambam; but if made per the repair, one complete chumash — its law is like a sefer Torah, and it requires study. And in Yoreh De'ah 282:19 chumashin on a sefer Torah — forbidden, not like RaShba; and it requires study for RaShba, for Megillah 27a and Kelim 134:66 — a pouch for a chumash is forbidden.

(ד) And a sefer Torah that was written in other languages — whether the language or the script — has no kedushah of a sefer Torah; however kedushah of other books is in it. And see Magen Avraham siman 334 siman 17; and some say other books like gemarot and midrashim — it is obvious that they may be written in any language. And regarding fire on Shabbat — one saves gemarot and midrashim; and that which Megillat Esther one does not save — since there are no Divine names in it, for it was written not per the repair — its judgment is Assyrian script on hide; unlike gemarot and midrashim, which one may write in any matter and language — and see Yoreh De'ah 282, see there. And see Orach Chayim 31 seifim 42 and 43, see there. And if there are eighty-five letters to collect in a sefer Torah, or there is a Divine name — it has kedushah, as explained in siman 334 seif 12.

(ה) And what they said regarding kedushah of the Name that it is forbidden to erase — etc., Yoreh De'ah siman 276 siman 12 — that even though it was written not for the sake of kedushah, nevertheless anything not for the sake of repair is forbidden to erase; and see responsa Me'il Tzedakah siman 23 in the name of Panim Me'irot — to plaster lime on writing in a synagogue, which I brought above. And see Rambam, chapter 5 of Foundations of the Torah 1, in Hagmiyuni and Halacha 16 there on "on his flesh" — it implies even if not written for the sake of kedushah, nevertheless kedushah is in it; and see below siman 334 sk 21. And in Taz 16 it implies that if they did not write it for the sake of idol worship — kedushah is in it; and Levush concluded that certainly they wrote it for the sake of idol worship. However those coins about which there is a doubt whether perhaps they intended the Creator, may His Name be blessed — kedushah is in them, even though Israel did not write them in kedushah, for they did not intend to sanctify them.

Say now: words of sanctity have levels: (1) a fit Torah scroll; (2) chumashim (meaning one complete book of one chumash — if lacking, it does not have the law of sanctity of chumashim); (3) Prophets; (4) Writings. And behold Beit Yosef in Yoreh De'ah siman 282 in the name of Rashba's responsum, that chumashim are like a Torah scroll for all their matters, only one does not read in public (on account of respect for the congregation); and we already asked above; and behold in the gemara Megillah 26a, chumashim are not like a Torah scroll, and so in Yoreh De'ah 282 seif 19; and in the mishnah there 26a, "one buys books" — per Ran's explanation, chumashim — behold lighter than a Torah scroll; and Rashi explained Prophets, Writings — and it is strained to say when not made in a scroll it deals; and in Orach Chayim below Rama siman 67, chumashim in a scroll like a Torah scroll implies one law applies to them, for otherwise from where would we be stringent; and in siman 153 seif 61 he ruled thus, chumashim are lighter — it implies even in a scroll from the anonymous ruling; and requires study. And further, behold in Be'ur he wrote in siman 153 seif 3, a Torah scroll in which an error was found is like chumashim, even one complete chumash in a scroll; and requires study on Rama siman 66, as I wrote. And in Prophets and Writings it implies in Yoreh De'ah they are equal, in 282:19 in the gloss; and Magen Avraham siman 153 note 2 sided thus, that one can say Prophets are more stringent; and there is a practical difference in several matters; and regarding fire on Shabbat in siman 334, Magen Avraham note 15: written Torah precedes oral Torah — one can say the more stringent comes first: Torah, then chumashim, then Prophets, etc.; also Torah in ink on parchment as required is obvious from paper, etc. A get — if there is sanctity in it: Magen Avraham in Mishna Berurah note 16 in the name of Bach that there is no sanctity, and Orach Chayim holds there is sanctity.

A difference exists between kedushah and accessories of sanctity in several aspects: (1) in kedushah itself, designation is effective — if one processed hide for tefillin, it is forbidden to write mundane matters in it (but from more stringent to lighter is permitted — Magen Avraham 32 note 10), as in siman 42; and in its accessories designation is not effective. (2) In kedushah, stipulation does not help at all if he wrote sanctity in it; and in its accessories stipulation helps even while still in sanctity, as in siman 154 seif 8; but repulsive use — stipulation does not help, as in siman 151 in Magen Avraham note 14. Eating and drinking — see Mishna Berurah note 6 in Magen Avraham (misprint: siman 153, and it should be siman 151 seif 11). Third: kedushah — if they sold it, wherever it is stated in kedushah it remains; and its accessories — one can say in selling zt"h in the presence of the people of the city or one's own, even though he did not stipulate initially, one can say it goes out to secular use; and in Magen Avraham siman 153 note 14, accessories of sanctity do not go out to secular use; and we asked there Yoreh De'ah 282:14, silver apples in Tur — for Rosh it goes out to secular use and the buyer may do with it what he wishes; and also in siman 153 seif 18 to take them out to secular use implies they themselves; and Avodah Berurah deals with stipulation — if so, Rambam would not have forbidden; and so that case of seif 18; and requires study.

And know that tefillin straps are accessories of sanctity, but the place of the yod and dalet for Rashi is kedushah itself (Avodah Yosef siman 154); and the house of the head — one can say it is kedushah itself, for the shin is the four-letter Name; and certainly the hand ones are accessories of sanctity; and see further in siman 154 in Taz note 2 at length on this.

A difference exists between accessories of sanctity and accessories of a mitzvah: these, meaning a mitzvah, are cast aside after their mitzvah — see note 21. And accessories of sanctity require burial in a guarded place (and in siman 154 seif 4, "they may," etc. — not necessarily obligatory — see there). Also that one does not take them down from more stringent to lighter in kedushah — but in a mitzvah one may take them down, as ruled in Yoreh De'ah siman 259 note 11 in the name of Mordechai — see there; and at first glance in a dispute stated: per Ran chapter Bnei HaIr, in a synagogue there is sanctity properly, unlike Ramban who holds it is a mitzvah; and nevertheless in a synagogue they may take an ark and it is forbidden to take down — and we hold like Ran that in a synagogue and everything within it there is sanctity (Magen Avraham note 28); and nevertheless it is forbidden to make a chair or benches to sit on them, for this is an accessory of sanctity of a Torah scroll that is in it, and forbidden to take down even from one fixed sanctity to temporary, as in siman 154 seif 66, ark, chair; but from temporary to somewhat temporary is more permitted — see responsum Maharam Padua siman 82, Magen Avraham siman 154 note 13.

And know that an accessory of sanctity is forbidden to use for mundane use, even after it wore out, and requires burial; and an accessory of an accessory (anything that does not serve the body of the sanctity — nevertheless an ark and chest in which a Torah scroll rests, even if it rests in a wrapper, is an ark and chest, accessory of sanctity — see Yoreh De'ah 282:12 and Rambam chapter 10 Hilchot Sefer Torah law 4 — see there) — some say it does not require burial, even while still fit; there is no sanctity in it at all, and permitted for mundane use; and from this I say to him from what Beit Yosef wrote in siman 154: our table — one may lean on it, for a cloth is spread on it; and so Levush siman 154 seif 63, an accessory of an accessory is permitted to use; and do not answer me from siman 154 seif 66 in the gloss that a column is forbidden — marker woods, even though it is an accessory of an accessory — behold there is sanctity in it; on the contrary, from there is proof, for behold in Piskei Maharai zt"l siman 225 he wrote that nevertheless a column of synagogue sanctity is in it like benches, etc. — behold if not so, there is no sanctity in it at all; and therefore those who make a silver cover or vernacular for hand tefillin — it is an accessory of an accessory and has no sanctity at all; but for the head it implies it is an accessory of sanctity on account of the shin — see further in siman 154 in Taz note 2.

And accessories of a mitzvah that are cast aside after their mitzvah — not while still fit for their mitzvah — see note 21 in Bach: shofar and lulav after Rosh HaShanah and Sukkot — nevertheless they remain fit for their mitzvah for the coming year; it is forbidden to use them (Magen Avraham siman 338 note 12); and Shabbat 36a, a shofar fit to draw water implies it is permitted after its mitzvah; and requires study. And see note 21 and 266:8, repulsive use is forbidden even in accessories of a mitzvah; and see Ran, Ramban, Ba'al HaMaor, and on a stolen lulav in that which they would bequeath their lulavim to their grandchildren — they asked to make canopies — see there.
