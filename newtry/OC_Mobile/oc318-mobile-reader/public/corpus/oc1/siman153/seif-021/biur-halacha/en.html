It is forbidden to make from the hire of a harlot, etc. See Mishnah Berurah. Nevertheless, I am uncertain: granted that when she consecrated it we do not accept it from her, perhaps at any rate it is forbidden to use it for mundane purposes, since this prohibition is only D'Rabbanan and may not be strong enough to uproot her vow entirely. It would require genizah. This requires study.
But only a forbidden relationship. See Mishnah Berurah. The Rambam's view is that it is called the hire of a harlot only when that act of immorality involved a negative commandment or kareis. But if this act of relations did not involve a negative commandment or kareis, even though she had already had relations with those liable to kareis or with those prohibited by a negative commandment and became a zonah, nevertheless her hire is not forbidden. Tosafot's view is that once she had already become a zonah, her hire is forbidden in every case, since he gave it to her as hire. The later authorities hold that regarding a synagogue we rule leniently in all these disputes, since they are only D'Rabbanan.
But these unmarried women, etc. Seemingly one should be lenient in this only regarding a woman who is abandoned specifically to Jews. But those harlots who sit in houses of prostitution and make themselves available to all should not be treated leniently according to the above view of Tosafot, who holds that once she became a zonah one time through forbidden relations, then even if afterward she had relations with one permitted to her, her hire is forbidden. If so, in houses of prostitution, where they make themselves available to all, she has certainly already become a zonah through relations with a non-Jew or a slave, as explained in Even HaEzer, siman 6. If so, even if we know that she received this hire from a Jew, the name of hire of a harlot still applies to it. Even according to the Rambam, who requires that the hire itself come from forbidden relations, at any rate one should be stringent in a city whose majority is non-Jewish, for we assume that the hire came from them as long as we do not know clearly that she received it from a Jew.
Who consecrate an item. This wording implies that were it not for this reason they would be stringent, even though it is not clearly known that it was from hire given to her. The reason is that we assume that all their possessions are from the hire of a harlot, similar to that case in Bava Kamma 113 regarding tax collectors. See there.
