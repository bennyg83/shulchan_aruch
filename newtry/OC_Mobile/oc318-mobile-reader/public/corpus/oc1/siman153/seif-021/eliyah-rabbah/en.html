So long as he did not consecrate it, etc. Meaning, they were not prepared for the synagogue to read from them, and he did not actually consecrate them. It requires study what people rely on when they sell a Torah scroll and use its money, even when they gave it to the synagogue. One must say that since this is the custom, it is as though he stipulated initially that the sanctity of the public should not take effect on them, as written in Yoreh Deah, siman 259, seif 2.
