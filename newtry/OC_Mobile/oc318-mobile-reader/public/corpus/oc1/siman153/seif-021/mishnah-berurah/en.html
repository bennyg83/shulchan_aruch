That they used them. See above, siman 147, seif 1, in the gloss, in the Mishnah Berurah, what we wrote regarding this.
A common person. All the more so from things that were used for idol worship. In that case, even after the fact it is forbidden, and they must sell them and buy another item.
For an accessory of sanctity. But for a mitzvah it is permitted, as written above in siman 157, seif 1, in the Mishnah Berurah. Invalid books of non-Jews that are bound in silk for beauty, one may not take the silk to make a tallis from it, for they are repulsive like garments of an idol. All the more so it is forbidden to make accessories of sanctity from them. Therefore it is forbidden to take the bindings and bind our sacred books with them.
It is forbidden to make, etc. Just as the Torah forbade bringing them to the Temple and the altar, as it says, you shall not bring the hire of a harlot or the price of a dog, etc., for it is an abomination to Hashem your G-d, etc. Nevertheless, this is only D'Rabbanan.
From the hire of a harlot. The prohibition of hire takes effect upon everything he gives her. Even if he agreed with her for one lamb as her payment and gives her many, they are all forbidden, for they all come to her as hire. The law of hire is specifically when he gives her the hire and afterward cohabits with her. But if he cohabited with her and afterward gave her the hire he had stipulated with her, it is permitted, since at the time of cohabitation that hire was not acquired by her. Also, the prohibition of hire applies specifically when the adulterer gives it to the adulteress; but if the woman gave it to him, the hire is permitted. If he gave the hire to a male to lie with him, the hire is forbidden.
The price of a dog. For example, where he exchanged a dog for some item.
Such as a synagogue. For example, where he gave the stones or beams to the harlot, or exchanged them for the dog. The same applies that it is also forbidden to make synagogue vessels from the hire.
Or a Torah scroll. For example, where he gave her parchment. The same applies that Torah scroll covers are forbidden. Likewise, it is forbidden to make any mitzvah item from the hire or the price, such as a lamp or oil for the synagogue. The Peri Megadim implies that the same applies to tzitzis and a tallis of mitzvah, that they too should not be made from this.
From the hire itself. As we wrote earlier. If the hire or the price changed, for example olives that he made into oil, or grapes that he made into wine, it is permitted.
He gave her money. The same applies if he took money in exchange for the dog; it is permitted to buy a mitzvah item with it.
A prohibition of ervah. Meaning those liable to kareis. Some say that the same applies to those forbidden by negative commandments. Regarding a non-Jewish harlot, all agree that her hire is forbidden.
