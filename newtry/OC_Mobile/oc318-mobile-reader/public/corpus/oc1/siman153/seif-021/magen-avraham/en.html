From the hire of a harlot. It is clear in the fifth chapter of Avodah Zarah that if he cohabited with her and afterward gave her the hire, it is permitted, for at the time of cohabitation it was not acquired by her, and what he gave her afterward was merely a gift. But if he gave it to her and afterward cohabited with her, it is forbidden. If the woman gave him hire, the hire is permitted. If he gave hire to a male to lie with him, the hire is forbidden. If she made a change, such as wheat that she made into fine flour, it is permitted, and their offspring are also permitted. One who says to a harlot, take this lamb and cohabit with so-and-so, it is forbidden. Likewise, if he agreed with her for one lamb and gave her many, all are forbidden. All this is in Rabbeinu Yerucham. If the lamb was in her courtyard at the time of cohabitation, it is forbidden, for she acquired it at the time of cohabitation. Even if he made it an apotiki for her, saying: if I bring you money from now until such-and-such day, good, and if not, take the lamb as your hire, it is forbidden, since it stands in her courtyard. The Rambam wrote in chapter 4 of Hilchos Issurei Mizbeach: if he gave it to her and did not cohabit with her, but said to her, let it be with you until I come to you, when he cohabits with her it is forbidden. For if he gave it to her without specification from now, he merely gave her a gift. Tosafot. If she preceded and consecrated it before he cohabited with her, and afterward he cohabited with her, this is a doubt whether it is the hire of a harlot; therefore it may not be offered, but if it was offered, it is accepted. This is where he said to her at the time he gave it: whenever you wish, acquire it from now. But if he did not say this to her, she cannot offer something that is not hers. End quote. So it is in the Gemara. It appears to me that regarding a synagogue we adopt leniency, for it is only D'Rabbanan; the verse speaks specifically of the Beis HaMikdash, as it is written, the house of Hashem your G-d, and so it implies in the Gemara. Also, all the poskim did not mention this matter except Rabbeinu Yerucham. Therefore we adopt leniency in its doubt. Furthermore, the Rambam ruled that if it was offered, it is accepted. If so, for us regarding a synagogue, even initially we may bring it. Similar to this appears in siman 272 regarding wine for kiddush; see there.
The exchange price of a dog. For example, if he exchanged a lamb for a dog. Two partners divided: one took ten lambs and one took nine and a dog. If among the ten there is one whose value is like the value of the dog or more than its value, he removes it from the ten and the rest are permitted. If each one of them is worth less than the value of the dog, all are forbidden. Rambam. He further wrote: the hire of a harlot and the exchange price of a dog are permitted for Temple maintenance, for they are changed; but the actual hire may not be made into plates for the Temple. End quote. This requires study: what is the meaning of what he wrote, for they are changed? Is it not possible that he gave him that very object itself? See Avodah Zarah 46b. It also requires study what he wrote that when their values are equal, he selects one of them and the rest are permitted, for in the Gemara we say this according to the view that there is retroactive clarification, and so it appears in Bechoros 57. But according to the view that there is no retroactive clarification, it does not help that he selects one. Since he ruled in chapter 6 of Bechoros regarding tithe like Rabbi Yehudah and like the Ran, how did he rule that it is permitted when he selects one of them? This requires study in Yam Shel Shlomo, Bava Kama, in the laws of retroactive clarification. In any event, it appears to me that for us it is permitted, for we rule that in D'Rabbanan matters there is retroactive clarification, as written at the end of siman 413.
A prohibition of ervah. A non-Jewish harlot, a maidservant, or those forbidden by negative commandments. Rambam and Rabbeinu Yerucham. It appears to me that since he used the phrase a non-Jewish harlot and did not say simply a non-Jewish woman, it implies specifically where someone had already cohabited with her, making her a harlot. If so, when another afterward cohabited with her and gave her hire, it is the hire of a harlot. So Rashi wrote on page 76a. So too Tosafot wrote in the sixth chapter of Temurah, beginning of s.v. the first version, and so too in Yevamos 61a, end of the page. Their language is: Rava disagrees only where she committed harlotry, for he derives it from a Jewish harlot; but if she did not commit harlotry, he agrees that she is not forbidden to a kohen as a harlot according to the Torah, for desecration does not apply to her. But where she converted, where marriage acquisition applies to her, she is forbidden according to the Torah even if she did not commit harlotry. End quote. So too they wrote in Avodah Zarah 36b and in Bava Kama 70b in Tosafot. In the Gemara it implies that kehuna and hire are equivalent, for in Temurah Rava derives them from one another through a common inference. The Rambam follows his own view, for he holds that those forbidden by negative commandments are also called a harlot, as written in Even HaEzer, siman 7, and therefore he wrote here as well that it is hire. But a woman who is promiscuous is permitted to a kohen, as he wrote there, and so it is in the Gemara in Yevamos 61. Therefore it is not hire, as he wrote here in the name of Rabbeinu Yerucham. But Tosafot and the Rosh hold that specifically those liable to kareis make her a harlot, but those forbidden by negative commandments are not called a harlot; rather, they are forbidden to a kohen from another verse, as Tosafot wrote in Hacholtz, page 44b, see there, and the questions of the Beis Yosef in Avodah Zarah will be resolved. Therefore they wrote in Temurah that if he cohabited with those liable to kareis, it is hire even without specification, since through that cohabitation he made her a harlot. But where she had already become a harlot and another cohabited with her, it is not hire unless he said to her explicitly: take this as your hire. Now the Rama wrote in Darkei Moshe: see in Avodah Zarah which one is called a harlot. End quote. His intent is that although Tosafot wrote here that with a promiscuous woman it is hire, we do not rule like them, but like Rabbeinu Yerucham, for the Gemara explicitly implies that hire and kehuna are equivalent, and the words of Tosafot require resolution. He wrote here a prohibition of ervah, meaning specifically those liable to kareis. Since there is a dispute regarding those forbidden by negative commandments, we adopt leniency here, for the prohibition is D'Rabbanan, as I wrote above. He does not discuss a non-Jewish woman here, for it is not the way of a non-Jewish woman to consecrate something. This is clear to me. See the book Be'er HaZevach, and see Bechoros 56b.
