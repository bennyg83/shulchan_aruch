Harlot's payment (etnan zonah). It is obvious here that according to one opinion, if he had relations with her and afterwards gave her the payment, it is permitted — for at the time of relations it was not acquired to her, and what he gave her afterwards is a mere gift that he gives her; but if he gave her and afterwards had relations with her, it is forbidden. And if the woman gave him the payment, the payment is permitted; and if he gave the payment to a male to lie with him, the payment is forbidden. And if she made a change, such as wheat and made it into flour, it is permitted; and likewise their offspring are permitted. One who says to a harlot: take this lamb and have relations with so-and-so — forbidden; and likewise if he stipulated with her one lamb and gave her many, all are forbidden (so in Rashi). And if the lamb was in the courtyard at the time of relations, it is forbidden, for it was acquired to her at the time of relations; and even if he made it collateral for her, saying: if I do not give you money from here until such-and-such a day it is better, and if not you take the lamb as your harlot's payment — forbidden, since it stands in the courtyard. Rambam wrote chapter 4 of Issurei Mizbeach: he gave her and did not have relations with her, but said to her: let it be with you until I have relations with you (for if he gave her unconditionally from now it is a mere gift he gives her — Tosafot) — when he has relations with her it is forbidden. If she preceded and dedicated it before he had relations with her, and afterwards he had relations with her — behold there is a doubt whether it is harlot's payment; therefore it may not be offered, and if it was offered it is accepted. And this is when he said to her at the time he gave: when you wish, acquire it from now; but if he did not say so to her, she cannot offer something that is not hers — until here; and so in the gemara. And it appears to me that regarding a synagogue we are lenient, for it is only d'rabbanan, for the verse speaks specifically regarding the Temple, as it is written "the house of the the Master your Heaven," and so in the gemara; and also all the poskim did not mention this matter, only Rivash; and therefore we are lenient in our doubt. And furthermore Rambam ruled: if it was offered it is accepted; and if so, we in a synagogue may bring it even l'chatchila; and similarly regarding wine for kiddush, see siman 472 and in the name of Avodah Berurah.
