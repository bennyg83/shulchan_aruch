The writer said. In my novellae to Megillah, chapter Bnei Ha'ir, I wrote at length, and here I will write briefly. Know that the sanctity of a synagogue and of a beis midrash is D'Rabbanan and not according to the Torah. See the Ran there regarding a gift, with what is its sanctity removed, 26b, where he concludes that the sanctity of a synagogue is from the words of the Sages, which implies that it is not according to the Torah. Similarly, in Sukkah, siman 638, Taz, seif katan 1, the schach is according to the Torah from the comparison to the festival, and even this is not by mere designation. But regarding the festival offering, designation is effective, as explained where one says, this animal is an olah, even in his heart. Partitions are only D'Rabbanan. See Shabbos, chapter Bameh Madlikin, the root of them all is blood; there he performs the mitzvah in a degrading manner. See there at length. However, the sanctity of a Torah scroll, that one may not treat it disgracefully, what is its status? It is derived by a kal vachomer: if one stands before those who study it, all the more so before it. See Yoreh Deah 282. This is a full kal vachomer, and honoring the face of an elder is according to the Torah. Also, the rule that one may not lower from a stricter sanctity to a lighter one, even if you say that these things have sanctity according to the Torah, it is possible that the prohibition to lower them is D'Rabbanan. Although Rashi and the Levush cited Tosafot regarding coating for the altar, etc., one can say that it is D'Rabbanan and an asmachta, for in Pesachim 26a Rabbi Dosa permits it for an ordinary kohen. And the Rambam, Laws of Temple Vessels 8:5, ruled like the first Tanna that the garments of the kohen gadol are entirely forbidden from the phrase that he leaves them there entirely, implying that otherwise, even though he lowers them from a stricter sanctity to a lighter one, nevertheless it is permitted.
Sanctity. See Taz. There are two initial questions here. First, may one sell one item of sanctity in order to buy another like it? Second, if he already sold it, may he buy something like it? From the Ran in the Mishnah of Bnei Ha'ir it is explained that even partial initial action is like initial action itself, for in his explanation he says so: since he began with inferior items, he used that formulation for all of them. Thus, in truth, for equal sanctity it is forbidden, and the Mishnah taught, if they sold, which shows that partial initial action is exactly like initial action. Certainly, from the question later on 27a there is no proof except that actual initial sale is forbidden for equal sanctity in the other items, since the question was asked about a Torah scroll, where there is no higher elevation. But the Ran holds that partial initial action is exactly like initial action. Mahari disagrees with the Ran and permits even actual initial sale, for it is all one matter. The Beit Yosef brought the beginning of the Ran's words but not the end, and wrote that Mahari disagrees. One might have said to distinguish between after the fact and initially, as the Bach wrote, but as we said, there is no distinction, for this too is initial action. This also seems to be Rashi's view there, 27a, s.v. after the fact, for otherwise what should they buy with it, since partial initial action is like initial action. So too it appears to be the view of the Rambam, Laws of Tefillah 11:14, who concludes that if they sold a Torah scroll, they may buy with its proceeds only another Torah scroll, for there is no sanctity above a Torah scroll. Since he did not use the language of the Mishnah, that they may not buy scrolls, and since there is no higher sanctity, this is the meaning: a Torah scroll is permitted, for what else should be done with its proceeds, but with other items, equal sanctity is forbidden, because partial initial action is like initial action. What he wrote in halacha 17, that villagers who wanted to sell their synagogue to build another or the like, seems to imply that equal sanctity is permitted even initially. See Tosafot Yom Tov and Lechem Yehudah of Mahari Ayash. One can say that he is speaking of a case where it is permitted, such as where there is a flaw in it or the like, and in my novellae I wrote at length about this. In Shulchan Aruch, one opinion says this, and there are those who explain that there is no distinction between partial or actual initial action. Since he brought the latter explanation last, and especially since it is a D'Rabbanan matter, one can say that his view is to be lenient. However, regarding a Torah scroll he ruled in Yoreh Deah 270, and so too the Tur, that it is forbidden to sell an old one to buy a new one. Regarding a Torah scroll there is only actual initial sale, for otherwise what will he do with the proceeds. One can say that there it is because of negligence, but the Taz and Shach there, note 3, not like the Levush there, explain it because of negligence; see there. The Taz raised two difficulties against Mahari. First, why did he infer from the Tur and not from the Mishnah, scrolls and a wrapper, that equal sanctity is permitted? One can answer that this is because the Tur wrote, but the reverse, to lower them, is not permitted, and not merely, but the reverse is not permitted. He answered that certainly the Tanna mentioned two matters incidentally with one, but the rule of since does not apply, as explained in many sugyos of the Gemara. In a Mishnah, however, he mentioned each item separately and did not include them all together. One can say as the Ran wrote, that since he began with what is lower than a Torah scroll, that they may not buy scrolls, but may buy a Torah scroll because what else should be done with its proceeds, he used the same formulation for the others and did not want to make a separate clause for equal sanctity. But the Tur included them together, saying the reverse, to lower them, and the difficulty remains: how could he teach a law that is not true, since even equal sanctity is forbidden? We do not say that he included so much incidentally because of one item. This proves that in truth equal sanctity is also permitted with the other items. If you ask that if so one might mistakenly think that even by a Torah scroll initial sale is permitted, for partial initial action is like actual initial action, he relied on Yoreh Deah 270, where he rules without qualification and forbids a Torah scroll, which certainly implies that even where there is no concern of negligence it is forbidden, as we said. With Hashem's help, it will be explained further elsewhere. See what I write below on this.
That which. The prohibition to lower applies specifically when not done by the seven leaders of the city. But when done by the seven leaders with the presence of the townspeople, with Hashem's help it will be explained below in seif 7 and seif 9. See Beit Yosef and Levush here. We have a doubt, for in the Mishnah only a synagogue is mentioned; I do not know whether, if they sold a beis midrash, it is permitted to buy an ark and the like. See the Tur, who wrote that they collected money for a beis midrash and a synagogue, but did not write the gradations regarding a beis midrash. See Perishah. As for what was written, that if a Torah scroll is forbidden initially, all the more so other things, according to my view there is no distinction between partial and initial sale. One can say as the Kesef Mishneh wrote in the tenth chapter of Laws of a Torah Scroll, halacha 2, that because of the stringency of a Torah scroll they ruled stringently, but not regarding other things. See Bach, Yoreh Deah 270. The question in the Gemara was also asked specifically regarding a Torah scroll because of its stringency, as the Magen Avraham wrote in note 4. In Kesef Mishneh there, he first wrote that it is because of stringency, and afterward cited the words of Rabbeinu Manoach because of negligence; these contradict each other. One must say that the words of Rabbeinu Manoach are not relevant to the Rambam, who ruled without qualification. See Perishah, who wrote that actual initial sale is treated stringently, like the Bach, for we rule stringently. One can say that there it is because of the stringency of a Torah scroll, and our question should be stated as the Ran wrote in the Mishnah and Gemara 27a. This requires study.
And what. What the Taz wrote regarding scrolls that are sold and others are bought, see Eliyah Rabbah, note 7, and below seif 10, where by an individual's property we hold so; see there. There is no need to lengthen, and with Hashem's help it will be explained further below. In my novellae to Megillah 27a I wrote a difficulty, and I will copy it here.
It seems to me. In my poverty, there is a difficulty here. In seif 4 he did not decide, but brought those who forbid and those who permit, implying that in a D'Rabbanan matter we are lenient. Yet in Kesef Mishneh, chapter 10 of Laws of a Torah Scroll, he wrote that we rule stringently because of the stringency of a Torah scroll, implying that with other items of equal sanctity it is permitted. Below, in siman 154, seif 9, he wrote one opinion without qualification, and so is the halacha; see Shach, Yoreh Deah 242, that wrappers of this Torah scroll are forbidden for another Torah scroll, and this is from the Ran in the name of the Rashba. See there. According to the one who says that other items of equal sanctity are permitted, wrappers should also be permitted there. To say that his doubt was from an old one to a new one, which is somewhat an elevation, and that this is implied by the question in the Gemara, since there is no other elevation, then how did he resolve it from a wrapper, where there is no slight elevation? Also, it appears that other items where there is an elevation are forbidden, as the Beit Yosef wrote in siman 153, but where there is no elevation they are permitted with other items, according to the Beit Yosef, since he brought only the beginning of the Ran's words. Wrappers of one Torah scroll for another have no other elevation. Also in the Gemara there, from the latter clause, chumashim, but not for a wrapper of a Torah scroll, it implies that a Torah scroll for a Torah scroll is permitted. It did not ask that chumashim for the wrapper of other chumashim are permitted, which seemingly implies that wherever there is an elevation it is obvious that it is forbidden, and it inferred specifically that a Torah scroll for a Torah scroll is permitted. All this requires study, and there is no need to lengthen.
