(1) [Se'if 1] A synagogue may be converted into a study hall, etc. A study hall has greater sanctity than a synagogue, and one ascends in holiness rather than descends [Rambam, Hilchos Tefillah 11:14; Levush; Bach; Olas Tamid].
(2) There: A synagogue may be converted into a study hall, etc. Magen Avraham says siman 90:18 implies a synagogue may become an individual's home study hall; Kol HaRamaz agrees. Eliyah Rabbah says its sanctity is apparently diminished. Beis Yosef, siman 151, says an individual fixing a study place in his home for personal use certainly gives it less sanctity, implying it is below a synagogue. Pischei HaDevir cites Meiri on Megillah, rejects Magen Avraham, and agrees with Eliyah Rabbah; he also defends Eliyah Rabbah from Bigdei Yesha. Maharsha, Taavah LaEinayim at the end of Megillah 244, explicitly says a synagogue is superior to an individual's Torah-study house. Chukos Olam on Magen Avraham and Tosefes Chaim agree.
(3) An individual's synagogue for personal use may plainly become his individual study hall; the reverse is forbidden [Eshel Avraham].
(4) There: But a study hall may not become a synagogue. Even after the fact, separation does not help [Prishah].
(5) There: But a study hall may not become a synagogue. This concerns a synagogue without a fixed study program. If the newly built synagogue replacing the study hall contains a permanent daily yeshivah teaching Scripture, Mishnah, halachah, and aggadah, so it retains the name study hall and is known as the great house where prayer and Torah are magnified, conversion is permitted [Davar Shmuel 346]. Yad Aharon and Baer Heitev attribute to Davar Shmuel that one hour of fixed study gives study-hall status, but he did not write that and his quoted wording does not imply it. Tosefes Chaim says a one-hour study hall has no advantage over a synagogue.
(6) There: But a study hall may not become a synagogue. Rosh, principle 5, permits a congregation, with its leaders' agreement, to rent a study hall as a residence and change it even to secular use. A study hall exceeds a synagogue only insofar as it may not be converted into one, not so greatly that townspeople can never change it. Maran cites this in Bedek HaBayis, as does Knesses HaGedolah. Rabbi Shmuel Boton, however, says a public study hall may not be sold, like a public synagogue [Davar Shmuel]; if so, it may likewise not be rented as a residence. See below, paragraph 51, beginning 'who come there,' and se'if 11.
