That an individual with his own, etc. The reason is that over his own property he has power like the seven leaders of the city in the presence of the townspeople have over public property, where it is permitted through this sale to use the money for whatever they wish, as stated above.
Even a Torah scroll. The Olas Tamid wrote that even according to this opinion, which permits it and says that the money does not become seized with sanctity, nevertheless all agree that one does not see a sign of blessing from this money, and so wrote the Eliyah Rabbah.
As long as he did not consecrate it, etc. The Magen Avraham leans to say that if he gave it to the public to read from it, it is forbidden according to all opinions, even if he did not actually consecrate it. He wrote further: it requires study what people rely on when they sell a Torah scroll and use its proceeds, even if they gave it to the synagogue. One must say that since this is the custom, it is as though he stipulated from the beginning that the sanctity of the public should not take effect upon it, as written in Yoreh Deah, siman 259, seif 2, in the gloss.
And there is one who forbids. According to this opinion, even the seven leaders of the city in the presence of the townspeople are also forbidden to sell a Torah scroll and use its proceeds unless it is for the benefit of Torah study. If he sold it, he must buy another Torah scroll with its proceeds. From the Levush in Yoreh Deah it implies that through the seven leaders of the city in the presence of the townspeople, all agree it is permitted to use its proceeds. The Magen Avraham wrote that this is specifically by a Torah scroll; but with accessories of sanctity, all agree that an individual may sell them and use their proceeds for whatever he wishes. However, from the Mechaber in Yoreh Deah 282:18 it is proven that the opinion that forbids holds that even with accessories one may not use their proceeds, for an individual does not have power like the seven leaders of the city in the presence of the townspeople. The later authorities wrote that even by a Torah scroll, if he originally bought the Torah scroll in order to sell it, and all the more so if he received it as payment of a debt, he may sell it according to all opinions and use its proceeds for whatever he wishes. It is stated in Yoreh Deah, siman 270, that other books have the same law as a Torah scroll and may not be sold except to study Torah or to marry a woman. Here, however, the Mechaber wrote that there are two opinions even regarding a Torah scroll; perhaps there it speaks where he consecrated it for the public to read from it.
To study Torah. Meaning, to support himself from its proceeds with profit. The Peri Megadim wrote further that it requires study whether one may sell a Torah scroll to buy Gemara, poskim, and their commentaries, for the reason one may sell a Torah scroll for Torah study is because study is great, for it leads to action. If so, perhaps the same applies in such a case, where it is impossible to study without books.
