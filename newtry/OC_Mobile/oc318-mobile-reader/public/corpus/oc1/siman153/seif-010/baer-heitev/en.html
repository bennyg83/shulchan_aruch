Who forbids. According to his words, even the seven leaders of the city in the presence of the townspeople, and a distinguished city scholar, are forbidden to sell them and use their proceeds, unless for Torah study, etc. Nevertheless, it seems to me that with its accessories, according to everyone an individual may sell them and use their proceeds. In Yoreh Deah, siman 282, the Shulchan Aruch records the dispute there also regarding its accessories, and this requires study. Perhaps what is written there, “and there is one who forbids,” refers only to a Torah scroll. The Magen Avraham wrote: it requires study what the world relies on when they sell a Torah scroll, even if they gave it to the synagogue to read from it, and use its proceeds. One must say that since such is the custom, it is as though they stipulated at the outset, as written in Yoreh Deah, siman 259, seif 2 in the gloss. If he bought it initially in order to sell it, and all the more so if he received it for a debt, it is permitted to sell it. Taz. See Responsa Ginnas Veradim, rule 2, siman 31, who wrote that nowadays, when all our scrolls are presumed to have disqualifications, it is permitted for an individual to sell a Torah scroll freely; see there. See Chavos Yair, siman 59, and see Yoreh Deah, siman 270.
