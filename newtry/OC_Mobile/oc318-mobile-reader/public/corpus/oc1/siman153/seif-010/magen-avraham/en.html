It is permitted to sell it. This requires study, for in Choshen Mishpat, siman 248, seif 11, it is written: one who says, my property is for so-and-so, whether a Torah scroll is included in property or, since it is not sold, it is not included in property, as is stated in the Gemara. There it speaks of an individual’s scroll, for if he had consecrated it, it would be obvious that he could not give it as a gift. One must say that what is written here, that he consecrated it, means that he gave it to the synagogue to read from it, but did not actually consecrate it; and nevertheless it is forbidden to sell it. Choshen Mishpat also speaks in this manner. Nevertheless, it requires study, for he should have explained this. It also requires study what the world relies on when they sell Torah scrolls even after giving them to the synagogue to read from them, and use their proceeds. One must say that since this is the custom, it is as though he stipulated from the beginning that the sanctity of the public should not take effect upon them, as written in Yoreh Deah, siman 259, seif 2, in the gloss.
And there is one who forbids. According to his words, even the seven leaders of the city in the presence of the townspeople and a distinguished communal scholar are forbidden to sell them and use their proceeds, unless it is in order to study Torah, etc. Nevertheless, it appears to me that regarding its accessories, according to everyone an individual may sell them and use their proceeds. The main proof of the Rivash is from what we say: one may not sell a Torah scroll except in order to study Torah, etc.; but its accessories may be sold. The Rambam also warned to forbid only a Torah scroll. So it implies in the Kesef Mishneh and in the Beis Yosef, Yoreh Deah, siman 282. However, there in Shulchan Aruch he wrote the dispute also regarding its accessories, and this requires study. Perhaps what he wrote there, there is one who forbids, refers only to the Torah scroll. As in Yoreh Deah, end of siman 270, where other books have the law of a Torah scroll and may not be sold unless in order to study Torah or marry a woman, and so the Tur wrote there. This requires study, for here he permits an individual to sell what is his own. Perhaps there it speaks where he consecrated them to the beis midrash for the public to read from them. See there in Yoreh Deah, siman 282, seif 8.
