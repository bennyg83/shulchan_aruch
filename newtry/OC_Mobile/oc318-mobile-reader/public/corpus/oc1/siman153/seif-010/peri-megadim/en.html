And there are. See Taz. The Be'er HaGolah cites the Rambam for the opinion that forbids. One who examines the Beit Yosef will see that according to what is written in Laws of Tefillah 11:11, that it is permitted specifically through the seven leaders of the city in the presence of the townspeople, but the body of the money is not permitted; the same would apply to a Torah scroll, that the surplus is permitted. But one can say that the opinion that forbids is the view of the Beit Yosef himself, who objected to the Rosh, who compares an individual's Torah scroll to the seven leaders of the city in the presence of the townspeople regarding a synagogue, where they may initially sell it. This is unlike a Torah scroll, where he commits a prohibition, for one may sell it only to study Torah, to marry a woman, or for redeeming captives. Say that after the fact it is forbidden to use it for profane purposes. He answered that the Rosh holds that an individual's Torah scroll may be sold even not to study Torah. If so, the opinion that forbids is our ruling, that even an individual may not sell unless it is to study, as in Yoreh Deah 270, and accordingly even the surplus is forbidden. The leniency regarding an individual's accessories of sanctity and the like is that the body of the money is permitted, for it is like the seven leaders in the presence of the townspeople. According to each explanation there is both a leniency and a stringency. However, the Mechaber in seif 7 ruled without qualification that through the seven leaders in the presence of the townspeople the body of the money is permitted. Therefore, here, where he forbids, it is because a Torah scroll differs from other items, as the Beit Yosef wrote, that if one transgressed and sold it, they are not permitted to sell it except to study, etc., and even after the fact one may use the money only to study Torah, to marry a woman, or for redeeming captives. The two opinions disagree in two ways: the opinion that permits allows it initially, while the opinion that forbids prohibits it even after the fact. In the Levush it sounds that the opinion that forbids is because an individual lacks power, but would agree that the seven leaders in the presence of the townspeople may permit it.
