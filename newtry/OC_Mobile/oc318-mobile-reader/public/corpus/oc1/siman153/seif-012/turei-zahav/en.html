He may not sell his right. Mordechai, at the end of the chapter HaChovel, proves this from the teaching that a son precedes all others in succeeding his father. One might think this applies even when he is unworthy to fill his father’s position; Scripture therefore says, “and the kohen who is anointed and whose hand is consecrated,” limiting succession to one who is worthy to fill his father’s place. All the more so, the holder may not sell the position to another without the congregation’s consent. It appears to me that this applies specifically when the congregation honored a person with the right, comparable to a Kohen Gadol who is appointed without purchasing the office. If, however, someone purchased the right from the congregation, he acquired all its associated rights like any other acquisition. Nevertheless, it is clear that even then he may sell it only to someone as suitable as himself, for that was the basis upon which the congregation granted him the right.
