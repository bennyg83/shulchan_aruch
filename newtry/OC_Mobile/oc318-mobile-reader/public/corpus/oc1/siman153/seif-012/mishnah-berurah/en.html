To build a synagogue. The same applies to any other mitzvah matter.
He cannot sell. It is logical that the community gave him authority over that mitzvah only for that man and his descendants, and not to sell it to others, unless they stipulated explicitly that he may sell his right. The Taz wrote that the Shulchan Aruch discusses where they honored him with it for free. But if he purchased it from them with money, it is like any ordinary acquisition, and he may sell his right to another. Nevertheless it is clear that they gave him this right only for someone as worthy as he is, and so wrote the Eliyah Rabbah. Likewise, in the law of the Levush, where they explicitly authorized him to sell his right, that too is specifically to someone as worthy as he is.
