To marry off an orphan girl, etc. So wrote R. M. Alashkar, siman 42, and the Levush. Therefore I wondered in Eliyah Zuta about the Olas Tamid, Chelkas Mechokek, and Beis Shmuel at the beginning of Even HaEzer, who wrote simply that this applies specifically to an orphan boy, but one may not sell to marry off an orphan girl, since she is not obligated in peru u’rvu, and they did not mention the Levush and R. M. Alashkar cited above. I also brought proof from Kesubos 67: “Our Rabbis taught: an orphan boy and orphan girl, one marries off the orphan girl and afterward the orphan boy, because her shame is greater than his.” If so, if one sells for an orphan boy, certainly for an orphan girl. I further brought from Megillah 27, which says that one sells only to learn Torah and to marry a woman, because “He did not create it for emptiness; He formed it to be settled.” We rule that women too are commanded in “He did not create it for emptiness,” as Tosafot wrote in Gittin 41, and so I found in Magen Avraham. So too it implies in Bava Basra 14, s.v. shene’emar, and so ruled Piskei Tosafot there, and the Levush in Yoreh Deah, siman 249, seif 7. This is also unlike the Beis Shmuel in the later edition. Also, what he wrote, that since the Shiltei Gibborim wrote that a woman should not sit without a husband because of suspicion, it implies that she is not commanded in sheves, is not difficult at all, for one can say that he preferred to give a reason that applies even when she is not fit to bear children. But it requires study from Even HaEzer, siman 154, seif 6. See further in Eliyah Zuta what I explained about the words of Tosafot at the beginning of Chagigah 2b.
