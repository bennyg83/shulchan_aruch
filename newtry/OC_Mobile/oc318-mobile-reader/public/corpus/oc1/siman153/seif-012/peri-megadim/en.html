And he comes. See Taz. In my poverty, his words require consideration. Know that this seif is the language of the Rambam, Laws of Gifts to the Poor 8:11, and the Kesef Mishneh brought the Gemara in Bava Basra, that they collected money and a redeeming captives case occurred. In Yoreh Deah 252 the Beit Yosef explained that redeeming captives is mentioned because of the extremity of the matter, and the same applies to other mitzvos. Accordingly, if they bought stones and beams, they may not sell them except for redeeming captives. One must say this depends on whether it came into the treasurer's hand. The meaning is: if they collected money and it did not come to the treasurer, they may change it to a lighter mitzvah, whereas the case of seif 5, the statement of Rava in Megillah 27, that it is forbidden to change the body of the money, is where it came to the treasurer. Still, it is forced, for why does he not distinguish in the money itself this way and instead distinguish where they bought stones, which presumably already came into the treasurer's hand? The Beit Yosef here had a difficulty: how can the Rambam forbid selling stones except for redeeming captives, when in Megillah one may sell a synagogue to buy an ark? Perhaps that sale is only after the fact. The fact that a built synagogue may not be sold even for redeeming captives is no difficulty, for he wrote that they should collect its redemption from the public; if that is impossible, then indeed they sell it for redeeming captives. See Shach, Yoreh Deah 252, note 1. But regarding stones that were bought, where they may not be sold for any mitzvah, even a stricter one, and even if the mitzvah will be entirely lost, the question remains. He answered that the Rambam holds that they sold means only after the fact. However, one may sell a Torah scroll to study Torah and marry a woman, so all the more a synagogue should be permitted initially. How then can he say that if they bought stones, only for redeeming captives? One must force the answer that a synagogue is stricter in this respect than a Torah scroll, and according to the Rambam one may not sell it initially even to study, unlike the Rosh in seif 6. But this is difficult, for it is explained explicitly in Laws of Tefillah 11:17 that villagers who wanted to sell their synagogue to build another or to buy with its proceeds an ark may do so. Thus explicitly, they may initially sell a synagogue for a stricter sanctity. How then can he forbid it in Matanot Aniyim except for redeeming captives, even if the mitzvah will be entirely lost? This requires study. The Beit Yosef wrote that the Rosh in seif 6 disagrees with the Rambam, and here in Shulchan Aruch he brought both, the law of seif 6 and seif 13, and according to his view they contradict. One must say that he relied on the final clause, that just as for redeeming captives they collect from the public, and if impossible they certainly sell the synagogue, so too regarding stones already bought by the treasurer, they do not sell for a stricter mitzvah if it is possible to collect from the public, and in seif 6 it speaks where it is impossible.
And behold. The Taz wrote that there is no need for what the Beit Yosef wrote in Yoreh Deah 252, and his reading was, perhaps a mitzvah may occur, etc. This means that here it speaks even of a lighter mitzvah: they fulfill their vow, since their intention is to repay afterward, only in the form of a loan, as written in seif 5, note 2; see there. This follows his approach. He asked why, when they bought stones, one may not change them except for redeeming captives, whereas in seif 6 even a synagogue may be sold to study. The same difficulty applies to a built synagogue: even for redeeming captives it is not sold, while there, even to study it is sold. But one can say that its redemption should be collected from the public, and in truth if impossible it may be changed; whereas regarding stones bought, it implies that even if the mitzvah will presently be lost, it is difficult. He answered here that it speaks where there is no other synagogue, following his approach in siman 152, note 1, where he reads place, and if there is another synagogue one can say that the one who says negligence does not hold the reason of praying, and the statement works well. See what I wrote in my novellae to Megillah. In seif 6 it speaks where there is another synagogue. Accordingly, where there is no other synagogue, it may not be sold for redeeming captives either, even if they cannot collect from the public. But if impossible, they do sell it, for nothing stands before pikuach nefesh. Still, whenever they have no other synagogue, they do not sell it to study or marry a woman. A Torah scroll is different, for the phrase a person's dwelling is not sold does not apply, as I wrote in Yoreh Deah 252, note 2, according to his approach, and all the more it is forbidden for stricter sanctity. He disagrees with the Magen Avraham, note 17. The Lechem Mishneh, Laws of Tefillah 11:14, also wrote the Taz's distinction, that it speaks where there is no other synagogue.
And in the holy words. At first glance, the words of the noble master are astonishing. In my humble opinion, I understood his holy words as follows. He holds that certainly, if they may sell for redeeming captives, the same applies to studying Torah and marrying a woman, for they are great mitzvos. In truth, they may also sell a synagogue to buy an ark, as written in seif 7; therefore they may sell it and the money remains in its sanctity. This shows that even initially it is permitted, namely for a stricter sanctity. But redeeming captives, although it is a great mitzvah, nevertheless its proceeds do not elevate to a stricter sanctity. Therefore redeeming captives is a novelty, and all the more an ark and a wrapper, which elevate to stricter sanctity. There is no need to lengthen. Know that in Bava Basra 3b, Tosafot, s.v. if so, wrote that if they built, they also may not demolish the old until they pray in the new. That means they may not demolish the old one until they pray in the new, for then it is forbidden to sell the new one for redeeming captives. But in seif 6 he permits selling a synagogue even after they prayed in it in order to study and marry, and all the more for redeeming captives. One can say that certainly, wherever it is possible to collect from the public, he knew they would not do this, and the conclusion is that even when they built, they would not do so, because a person's dwelling is not sold. But if it is impossible, they certainly sell it, as stated.
And according to the Magen Avraham. There is a pleasant path in this, which I wrote in note 9.
