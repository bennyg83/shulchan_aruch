Seif katan 12, the great city, etc. To explain the matter: in Megillah they say that a village synagogue may be sold, but a city synagogue may not be sold, because it belongs to many people. The Rishonim disagree whether the reason is that outsiders contributed money to its building, or that the residents built it with the intention of all who come to pray there. According to the first reason, if they built it entirely from their own money, they may sell it. According to the second reason, even if they built it from their own money, they may not sell it, because they built it for the sake of all who come from elsewhere to pray there. The halacha follows the stringent view. The Talmud also says that Rav Ashi said of the synagogue of Mata Mechasya that although people came to it from elsewhere, since they came relying on him, if he wished he could sell it. From this the Tur wrote the law cited in the Shulchan Aruch, that a city synagogue may not be sold unless they made it dependent on the opinion of an individual. The Beis Yosef brought the words of Mahari Chaviv, who asked: if this condition was at the time of building, why say that they made it dependent on the opinion of an individual? Even if it was dependent on their own opinion they could sell it. Perhaps because it was a large city, one must be concerned that one person might be absent at the time of sale. He wrote that this is not sufficient, for there is a difficulty, and from the words of the Rosh and Nimukei Yosef we learn the true law, that this applies specifically to a unique person of the generation like Rav Ashi, whose opinion all Israel accept, but there is no substance when they make it dependent on one ordinary individual, and even if they make it dependent on the opinion of all of them it does not help. The Beis Yosef wrote that this explanation cannot be placed into the words of the Tur, and therefore he explained that when the Rosh wrote specifically Rav Ashi, a unique person of the generation, that is because then even without explicit condition we assume that all agree to his opinion. But if they explicitly made it dependent on the opinion of an individual, even the least of people, it helps; and the same applies if they made it dependent on their own opinion. As to why the Tur stated “dependent on the opinion of an individual,” as the manuscript asked, the Beis Yosef gave one answer: he stated the usual case, for usually many people do not agree to one opinion. Now we can come to the words of the Magen Avraham. Since the simple implication of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch, which wrote “but of cities, even if they built it from their own funds,” from the word “even” implies all the more so if they built it from others’ funds, and on this it continues, “unless they made it dependent on the opinion of the individual,” implying that this applies to both cases, even if it was built from others’ funds. Therefore the Magen Avraham needed to say that this is not so.
For granted, Mar bar Rav Ashi, meaning Rav Ashi as should be written, presumably, etc. The difficulty was that Mata Mechasya must have been built from funds of others as well, for there are authorities who say that the reason a city synagogue cannot be sold is that presumably others who came there also contributed to its building. If so, since Mata Mechasya was a city, others certainly helped build it. Granted, we do not rule this way, for we do not make it depend on the giving of money by others; rather, the reason a city synagogue cannot be sold is that the residents built it with intent for all who come to pray there. Nevertheless, those authorities would not disagree with the earlier authorities about the fact that in cities it is usual for outsiders also to help with money for its building. If so, one cannot say that Rav Ashi said he could sell the synagogue of Mata Mechasya because he knew they had built it from their own funds. If that were the essential reason, Rav Ashi and the Talmud should have explained that Mata Mechasya had been built from their own funds, since in an ordinary city outsiders also contribute. Rather, even in Mata Mechasya others had helped, as in every city, and nevertheless Rav Ashi said he could sell it. Therefore the Magen Avraham must distinguish between Rav Ashi, who could sell even without explicit condition and even if built from funds of others, and an ordinary individual, for whom even if they explicitly made it dependent on his opinion, if it was built from others’ funds it does not help, lest one person at the end of the world does not agree.
