They collected the money, etc. All the later authorities cited the wonder of the Kesef Mishneh in the name of Rabbi Moshe Kohen, that this is like spinning for weaving, which according to no opinion is considered significant. I wonder at their wonder, for Tosafot in Sanhedrin 48, s.v. mutar, escaped them; Tosafot wrote that money is stronger than spinning for weaving, because it is fit to buy anything with it. Yet it is difficult, for nevertheless it should not be stronger than weaving, and we rule that designation is not significant. So the Responsa Re’em, siman 53, asked, and answered that giving money into the hand of a gabbai is like an act; see there. The Shach brings this in Yoreh Deah, siman 259, and so it also implies in the Bach, and so the plain meaning of the Rama and Levush implies, as they wrote “if it came into the hand of the gabbai.” The Magen Avraham asked: is there any greater act than building a synagogue, and even so it does not have sanctity, as written in seif 8? But what can I do, for he did not examine the responsum of the Re’em itself, which asks this and answers that his intent is not to forbid it to himself except after prayer, and if he does not pray it remains non-sacred, unlike when he gives the money into the hand of the gabbai, where he relinquishes it; see there at length. Also, what the Magen Avraham asked, that in Sanhedrin it says that when they collected money for the dead it is called designation and not an act, is not difficult at all, for there it discusses leftover money of the dead and not money for the need of the dead itself. One may also distinguish between collecting charity and collecting for the dead. Now the Taz, seif katan 3, wondered about the Rama’s distinction mentioned above from Megillah 26, where Abaye asks from a baraisa that specifically a condition is required, and it does not answer that it discusses where it came to the gabbai, and therefore specifically a condition is needed; rather, there is no distinction. I did not understand the end of his reasoning, for Rava also speaks where it came into the hand of the gabbai, as he says “they collected and there was leftover,” and even so he permits even without a condition. If so, one cannot answer that way. Also, what he elaborated in seif katan 2, that the reason collected money may not be changed is not because of designation but because of a vow, and if he wants to borrow and repay, he may change it, see there, he took a view of his own. The proof he brought from the fact that the dead acquires what is needed for the dead itself is no proof, since it came into the hand of the gabbai, as I explained. It is obvious that the seven leaders of the city in the presence of the townspeople may change the money, as below, seif 7.
