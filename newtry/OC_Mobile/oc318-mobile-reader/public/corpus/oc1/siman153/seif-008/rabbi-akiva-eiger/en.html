Magen Avraham, seif katan 27. It is written in Mishnah Berurah that if they built a new synagogue, at first glance this is astonishing, for he already wrote in the gloss in seif 7 that all this speaks where they have another synagogue. See Magen Avraham there, that now they built another synagogue for this purpose, but otherwise even to sell through the seven leaders of the city in the presence of the townspeople is forbidden. If so, the dispute regarding a gift is also where they built another synagogue, and even so, were it not for the reasoning that if he had not given them benefit, they would not have given it to him, it would be forbidden. We would not have said that its sanctity transfers to the other synagogue. If so, the reasoning of the Mishnah Berurah is refuted. One must say, for one who examines the Ran, that the main reasoning of the Mishnah Berurah mentioned above is from the words of the Ramban, who wrote everything according to his principle, that drinking beer there is permitted because the seven leaders of the city in the presence of the townspeople may uproot the sanctity of a synagogue for nothing. If so, it must speak where they built another synagogue, for otherwise why would demolishing it be forbidden? According to his words, the dispute regarding a gift indeed speaks where only the seven leaders were involved. But according to the Ran, that the permission to drink beer there is because the sanctity takes effect on the money, and that sanctity is weak and they have power to render it profane, then without this there is no difficulty from the law that it is forbidden to demolish it, for there there are no substitutes upon which sanctity can take effect. If so, one can say that the dispute regarding a gift speaks where they did not build another synagogue, and therefore it needs the reason that had he not given them benefit, they would not have given it to him. But if they built another synagogue, one can say that even without that reason it is permitted, for the sanctity is transferred to the other synagogue. According to this, the law of the Mishnah Berurah is permitted either way: according to the Ramban it is permitted because the seven leaders of the city in the presence of the townspeople can uproot sanctity for nothing if they built another synagogue; and according to the Ran, it is a transfer onto the other synagogue. And that which we rule in the gloss in seif 6 like the Ramban, while here regarding a gift we rule that it specifically requires the seven leaders in the presence of the townspeople like the Ran, is because here we follow the stricter side and there the stricter side. But the law of the Mishnah Berurah is permitted either way. See there.
