At first glance, the meaning of the Shulchan Aruch, that it is permitted to change it, refers to a synagogue and the like, which becomes profane as in seif 9, and therefore requires no announcement. But something that may not be changed to a lighter sanctity, such as accessories of sanctity, and certainly scrolls, requires an announcement. The Magen Avraham, note 20, emended it as a textual error, etc. This is also the view of the Taz here, that permitted to change it means to give it as a gift, as in seif 11, meaning that one may change it from what it originally stood for, like a house for the poor, as the Magen Avraham wrote. In this, the Taz and Magen Avraham agree. But regarding where there is no law of overreaching, they disagree. According to the Taz, the Rama states two matters; according to the Magen Avraham, note 19, it speaks of land. According to the Magen Avraham this is fine: he began with two matters, that it needs no announcement because it is like his own, and therefore there is no overreaching in land, like an ordinary agent, for even by land there is overreaching when one can say, I sent you to improve, etc.; and he ended with one matter, that it requires announcement, and automatically there is overreaching. But according to the Taz, who says it refers to movable items, what difference and reason is there between requiring an announcement or not? For even one's own movable items are subject to overreaching. Why did he begin with two matters and end with one? See Sema, Choshen Mishpat 227, note 48, that nowadays a synagogue and other charity endowments do not have the law of Temple property; apparently he alluded to that here. This implies even accessories of sanctity.
