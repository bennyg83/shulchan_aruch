Taz — behold at first glance in Shulchan Aruch the explanation is: permitted to change it — meaning a synagogue and the like that goes out to secular use, like a Torah scroll — no proclamation is needed; but forbidden to change it to lighter accessories of sanctity, and all the more so books require proclamation; and Magen Avraham note 20 emended a misprint, etc.; and so Taz here, that permitted to change it means to give as a gift, as in siman 151, that it means permitted to change from what it referred to initially, like a poorhouse, as Magen Avraham wrote. And in this Taz and Magen Avraham agree; but regarding no fraud they dispute: for Taz, Rama said two things; and for Magen Avraham note 19, it deals with land; and for Magen Avraham it is fine that he began with two — proclamation is needed, for when it is his, and therefore there is no fraud in land as agent of the world, for even in land there is fraud — for your ordinance you sent, etc.; and he concluded with one: proclamation is needed, and automatically there is fraud; but for Taz, who deals with movable property — if so, what distinction and reason is there between needing proclamation or not, for in his movable property too there is fraud; and why did he begin with two and conclude with one. And see Choshen Mishpat 227 note 48: nowadays in synagogue and other charity of the poor, the law of consecrated property does not apply, and it appears he hinted here — see there; and it implies even accessories of sanctity.
