And even a Torah scroll. The reason is that we say one may not sell a Torah scroll except in order to study Torah, which implies that in order to study Torah it is permitted even to sell a Torah scroll. Responsum of the Rosh; see there, where he elaborated. This matter is an open rebuke to those people who are lax in maintaining Torah in their cities.
Or to marry off orphans. Even orphan girls, for although a woman is not commanded in being fruitful and multiplying, nevertheless 'He formed it to be settled' applies also to a woman. The same applies that one may sell a Torah scroll for redeeming captives. Magen Avraham, and the Eliyah Rabbah agreed in the name of several poskim. See below in seif 13, in the Magen Avraham there, where it is proven that it is not proper to sell even a synagogue, even for redeeming captives, unless it is impossible to obtain other money for this from the public. In this manner it is certainly permitted even where they have only one synagogue. Since seif 7 has many details, I will first introduce a brief preface: what was mentioned above regarding selling a synagogue applies if it is of a village, for presumably they built it only with themselves in mind, and therefore the majority of the townspeople have the ability to sell it, provided that afterward they do not lower the money from its sanctity. When it is sold also with the consent of their seven leaders, then they may do with the money whatever they wish, meaning to remove it to non-sacred status, as stated below. But if it is a synagogue of a large city, we assume that at the time of building they built it also with the entire world in mind. Even if they gave all the building money from their own funds, nevertheless we assume that they built it for everyone. Therefore, even if afterward the seven leaders in the presence of the townspeople agreed to sell it, their sale is not a sale. Now we will explain the words of the Shulchan Aruch.
