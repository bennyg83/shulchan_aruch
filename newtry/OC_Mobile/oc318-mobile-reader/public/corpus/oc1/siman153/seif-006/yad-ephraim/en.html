Seif katan 22: As written in Yoreh Deah, siman 259, seif 2, in the gloss. See what my great-uncle, the gaon, wrote in Toras Chaim to Sanhedrin 21a: An individual who writes a Sefer Torah for himself and gives it to a synagogue for public reading, formally consecrating it, does not act properly. Once it is consecrated, it belongs to hekdesh rather than to him, and he no longer fulfills his obligation with it, because the word “for yourselves” requires that it belong to you, as we find regarding the four species. One cannot say that fulfillment depends only upon the act of writing, for if an individual wrote a Sefer Torah and it was later lost, he clearly must write another. Therefore, one should not consecrate a Sefer Torah unless he writes another for himself. See there. See also my Responsa Beit Ephraim, Yoreh Deah, siman 62, regarding whether partners who jointly write a Sefer Torah fulfill the mitzvah.
