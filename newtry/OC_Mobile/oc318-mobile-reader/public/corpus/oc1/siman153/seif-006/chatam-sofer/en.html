It is difficult for me, for it is proven that they hold that regarding a woman there is only the matter of sheves, which is from Divrei Kabbalah, and for that we do not sell, whereas regarding a man there is also the mitzvah of peru u’rvu, which is according to the Torah, so sheves is a great mitzvah. See Beis Shmuel, siman 1, which implies that the woman’s obligation of sheves is no stronger than the positive commandment of “in the evening do not withhold your hand,” which is from Divrei Kabbalah; see there. As for what the Eliyah Rabbah wrote from Kesubos that marrying off an orphan girl comes first because of her shame, it appears to me that this is only to give precedence, not to override. It further appears to me that with respect to the mitzvah of tzedakah, removing her shame comes before helping the orphan fulfill a positive commandment. But to sell a Torah scroll, one specifically needs fulfillment of a great mitzvah, because we ascend in sanctity. This is simple. Know that all this is when he cannot find a way to learn Torah or marry otherwise. But otherwise, although it is still a mitzvah, nevertheless it is lighter than other positive commandments, as Tosafot wrote in the name of the She’iltos in Parshas Emor, Avodah Zarah 13a. But when it is impossible in another way, the She’iltos rules as is proven from what is written in his name at the end of the first chapter of Bava Kamma. Examine all this well.
