The Rama ruled like both answers of the Beit Yosef: if the money came into the treasurer's hand, sanctity takes effect on that money and it is forbidden to lower it even if he later gives other money; and even before it came into the treasurer's hand, he must at least fulfill his vow. The Taz asked why the Rama needed to write about stones, etc., for it is an all the more case from money. The Beit Yosef said it by way of an all the more, that the case of a built house in seif 8, where each one donated wood, must be where it did not come into the treasurer's hand, for otherwise it is all the more from collected money that came into the treasurer's hand. One can say that it is not an all the more, for one might say that money is like woven material, as Tosafot in Sanhedrin 48a, s.v. surplus, wrote, and therefore designation of this kind, when it came to the treasurer, is significant; but stones are inferior, like spun thread for weaving, since they lack building, and are not considered designation at all. One might have said that even when they came to the treasurer, no sanctity takes effect upon them; therefore he teaches otherwise. What he wrote that this is difficult from Megillah 27a means as follows. Even according to the Taz's approach, that he has not fulfilled his vow, etc., one can say that when it came to the treasurer a stipulation is required for the surplus. If so, why does the Gemara ask? Perhaps Rava speaks where it did not come into the treasurer's hand, but they collected it among themselves and did not deliver it to the treasurer. The body of the money cannot be changed to fulfill his vow, but the surplus may be changed; whereas if it came to the treasurer, perhaps a stipulation is required. If so, it is difficult according to the Rama, who ruled like both answers of the Beit Yosef, why the Gemara asks. And if one says that Rava must allow changing the surplus even when it came to the treasurer, since he did not establish the Mishnah in such a case and established it specifically by a sale, one can say that the phrase likewise in their surpluses implies that the surplus is equal to the body of the money. Since in surplus a stipulation helps, this proves that it speaks of a sale, etc. Rather, we must say that there is no significance at all to its having come to the treasurer.
