(16) [Se'if 3] A Torah scroll found to contain an error, etc. This includes one missing or extra letter. Since it is invalid, it lacks Torah-scroll sanctity and has the status of Chumashim [Beis Yosef from Rivash; Rambam, beginning of Hilchos Sefer Torah 10]. This appears true even when repair is possible, since it is presently invalid [Magen Avraham]. Apparently this applies when the error, omission, or addition existed at initial writing, so Torah-scroll sanctity never took effect. If it began valid and a letter was later erased, it may not be lowered because it can be repaired and restored [Pischei Olam from Kehillas Yaakov].
(16) There: Its law is like Chumashim. It may be sold like other sacred books. Rivash permits selling an error-filled Torah scroll even to repair a synagogue. For communal property this requires seven trustees with congregational consent; an individual's scroll requires only his own consent [Olas Tamid].
(17) There, gloss: See above, siman 143. Siman 143:4, gloss, permits reading under pressure from one error-free Chumash in a Torah scroll even if other Chumashim contain errors. This implies a scroll usable with a blessing in such circumstances has more sanctity than ordinary Chumashim. Maran disagrees, as explained there in paragraphs 43–44, unlike Olas Tamid's unresolved objection. Pri Megadim says that even if one complete Chumash permits reading when no other scroll exists, an entirely valid Torah scroll may still have higher sanctity, and remains uncertain. Therefore, where possible treat it with Torah-scroll sanctity; where impossible, follow Maran that it has Chumash status.
