Seif katan 5, one may not, etc. For it is comparable to thread spun in order to weave. Meaning, they say in Sanhedrin 48a that although Abaye and Rava disagree regarding one who wove a garment for the dead, Abaye holds that it is forbidden for benefit like shrouds of the dead, which are forbidden for benefit, because designation is significant, while Rava holds that it is permitted for benefit because designation is not significant, and we rule like Rava. But when it was spun for the purpose of weaving shrouds, even Abaye agrees that this is not designation. Although according to one answer of Tosafot there, s.v. “the surplus of the dead,” they wrote that money, since it is fit to buy all things with it, is stronger than thread spun for weaving, and perhaps is like something woven. One can say that the Ramach holds like the first answer of Tosafot there, which does not hold this reasoning. Nevertheless, this is not enough to resolve the opinion of the Rambam and the Shulchan Aruch, who hold like the second answer of Tosafot, that money is like something woven. For what does this matter? We rule like Rava, and even with something woven, designation is not significant. In any event, money is not stronger than something woven. Moreover, the master of this teaching in tractate Megillah, regarding money that was collected, is Rava himself.
For it is like accessories of a mitzvah, etc. His proof is that we rule that something whose body itself is fit for sanctity has no redemption. If so, how are they permitted to sell a synagogue? It is even more difficult when the seven leaders of the city sell it in the presence of the townspeople, for even the money becomes completely non-sacred. But even things that do have redemption, their sanctity takes effect on the redemption money. It must be that a synagogue has no actual sanctity, but is only like accessories of a mitzvah, such as sukkah, lulav, and Chanukah lamp, which during the time of their mitzvah may not be treated disgracefully, but once the time of their mitzvah has passed, they may by law be discarded. If so, when the seven leaders of the city sold it in the presence of the townspeople, it is like the time of its mitzvah has passed, and its sanctity departs, as with sukkah and lulav after the festival. But when it was not in the presence of the townspeople, perhaps there is one person who does not want to remove its sanctity, and if so the sanctity rests on the money, and it must be used for a stricter sanctity so that there not be weakening and degradation of the mitzvah, for the time of its mitzvah has not yet passed and its sanctity has not departed entirely. But actual sanctity does not take hold of the money. These are his words.
And so the Rosh wrote, etc. They gave the money only with the intent that they build, etc. At first glance this is a different reason from what the Ramban wrote, that the prohibition is because of weakening and degrading the mitzvah. Rather, it is because this is a change from the owners’ intent, and he is like a robber. But the Ran rejected this, for if so, even after the fact, if they changed it to a lighter sanctity, it should not be effective, and this is not so; see there. Therefore one must say that what the Rosh wrote, that they gave the money only with the intent that they build, means that they still want its sanctity, and it is like sukkah and lulav during the festival, where it is forbidden because of weakening and degrading the mitzvah.
Only that the public is permitted, etc. This refers to the seven leaders of the city in the presence of the townspeople, or through the methods explained in Yoreh Deah. The difficulty of the Ramach is thereby resolved: although designation is not significant, nevertheless it is understood well, for we do not need to forbid because of sanctity and designation, but because of degradation of the mitzvah. What the Magen Avraham answered in seif 8 is explained at the end of this seif katan.
For when they collected money for a dead person, etc. It wished to say that Abaye and Rava are like Tannaim, for it was taught: the surplus of the dead, meaning what they collected for the needs of the dead person and what remained after they made from the collection the needs of the dead, belongs to his heirs, according to Rabbi Meir. Rabbi Nasan says: they make a structure on his grave. What is the point of their dispute? Is it not about designation, that the collection is a designation, and Rabbi Meir holds designation is not significant, and therefore it is given to his heirs, while Rabbi Nasan holds designation is significant, and therefore he holds they make a structure? It answers according to Rava that everyone holds designation is not significant. Rabbi Nasan’s reason is that since the dead was disgraced through this collection, it may not be changed, and they should make a structure on his grave. Rabbi Meir holds that with respect to the heirs, the dead person forgives his disgrace, and therefore it belongs to the heirs. In any event, it is proven that the collection is only like designation. If so, the answer of the Re’em brought by the Shach is not correct, and the difficulty of the Ramach returns to its place.
Therefore it appears to me that the reason, etc. These are the words of the Ramban above, that the reason is because of degradation of the mitzvah. The Magen Avraham only added to explain that there is no degradation except when it was collected for the purpose of a synagogue, which is similar to money collected for the needs of the dead.
For since they collected for its need means for the need of the synagogue.
As opposed to an individual who donates. Meaning, according to this, since there is no degradation except because it was collected, if they did not collect, but an individual donated all the money needed for the synagogue, there is no degradation.
Or if they bought it with communal money that had already been collected not for a synagogue, but for a non-sacred purpose, and afterward they built a synagogue with the collected money, then in such a synagogue too there is no degradation in a case like this.
And what is written in seif 8 means that according to this the earlier difficulty from seif 8 is resolved: even if they already built the synagogue, as long as they did not pray in it, it did not become sanctified, since it was built with non-sacred money, or by an individual who donated it, and there is no degradation through changing it.
And the reason is. Meaning, why, when the public collected money specifically for a synagogue, is there degradation if they change it? Since they publicized the matter and collected it for this mitzvah, changing it is like disgracing the mitzvah for which it was collected.
