Magen Avraham, seif katan 12: In the presence of the townspeople, etc. Beit Yosef cites Mordechai that the rule concerning city synagogues applies only in an ordinary case. If the residents appointed seven trustees and conduct affairs according to their authority, then if the city residents built the synagogue from their own funds, it is as though they stipulated from the outset. This shows that even where affairs are conducted through the seven trustees, the synagogue must still have been built from the residents’ own funds. Beit Yosef writes that according to Rambam and Rosh, even if they built it with their own funds, because it is a city synagogue they built it for the benefit of everyone. Magen Avraham responds that this is incorrect: Rambam and Rosh also agree that the residents may retain this authority if they stipulated initially. In Mordechai’s case, their practice of conducting affairs through the trustees is itself equivalent to an initial stipulation.
