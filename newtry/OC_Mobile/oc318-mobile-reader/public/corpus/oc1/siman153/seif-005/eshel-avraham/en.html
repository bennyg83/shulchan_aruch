Responsa Shav Yaakov, siman 12, discusses one who instructed before his death that a synagogue be built in a certain place, but the plan did not succeed. The place where the deceased resided, and where his sons reside after him, now needs a different improvement: the synagogue’s vaulted roof is extremely old, and they wish to replace it with a stone vault. He rules that in this manner they fulfill the deceased’s instruction, and this is even more appropriate than building elsewhere, just as in charity the poor of one’s own city take precedence. One cannot argue that because they already have a synagogue the mitzvah is not as significant, for beautifying the synagogue is itself a great matter, as the verse states, ‘and to glorify the place of My Sanctuary.’ The incident involving Bava ben Buta proves this, for according to one resolution he saw no structural defect and sought only to make the building more beautiful. He concludes, however, that it should be rebuilt in the manner stated in Shulchan Aruch, siman 152; see there. See many additional laws on this subject in Responsa Aderes Eliyahu, siman 10.
