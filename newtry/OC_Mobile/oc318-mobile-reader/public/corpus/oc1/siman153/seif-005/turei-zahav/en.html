On exchanging the mutar, etc. "They may lower" — for it has not yet come to use of heavier kedushah, so Rashi — implies that in any case one is obligated to take it out for something of kedushah, only that even lighter kedushah is permitted. And it appears Tur, Rambam, and Shulchan Aruch also intend thus — they wrote in the latter part that even for lighter they may exchange. Proof: here it says "they exchange," and in seif 7 regarding "they agreed," ZT"H b'mao"h wrote they may take the money for whatever they want — even for reshut. And it seems here that requires a mitzvah matter — even all needs of the public are called mitzvah matters. See nearby proof. He challenged in Kessef Mishneh in the name of Ramakh, etc. — say, why not exchange the money — it is like stretching warp for weaving — there is one who says it is nothing; and even regarding ritual baths they said at the beginning of the chapter there is no kedushah in them — all the more when they collected money. That of collected money we can explain otherwise, or say it is not halacha — end of his words. Further Beit Yosef challenged below regarding one who builds a house stam, etc. — it is difficult: just as when they collected money for building a synagogue, kedushah of the synagogue applies to the money — why do we not say that if they volunteered wood and stones for synagogue building, kedushah applied to them and they cannot change except to heavier kedushah, etc. He answered: distinguish whether they came into the hands of gabba'im; as written below, new bricks have no kedushah — specifically they did not come into gabba'im's hands; but if they came — forbidden; and he wrote this is strained. Mochiach wrote this is Torah of truth; I am astonished — in my humble opinion there is no difficulty, for what is said "hazmanah is not a thing" is not relevant here: in anything subject to a vow for kedushah one must fulfill from the vow; if he comes to fulfill and designates — he may change it, for hazmanah is not a thing, and he fulfills another way per his vow. In one who built a house stam, as seif 8 — not holy until used — nevertheless he does not fulfill his vow and must make a synagogue and pray; only before prayer there is no kedushah. Likewise new bricks: obligated to fulfill vow and make synagogue; before the act no kedushah. Likewise weaving a garment for the dead or stretching warp — all hold hazmanah is not a thing to forbid benefit — no prohibition on the garment, but the vower must fulfill and give some garment. What is written here — do not exchange money except for heavier kedushah — not because kedushah is in the coins, for hazmanah is not a thing; rather if they wish to fulfill vow collected for synagogue by doing another mitzvah from the money — we do not say they fulfill until they build synagogue; if they take coins for another matter and give others for the synagogue — permitted, no kedushah in collected coins, only they did not fulfill obligation until built. Likewise siman 13: collected money may go to mitzvah — even lighter; by loan he takes coins until complete — only where hazmanah alone; but where an act of kedushah exists, kedushah is in the thing and forbidden even for mitzvah. Old synagogue bricks — forbidden to take by loan and substitute. Proof: obligation from vow — Sanhedrin 48, Rava: hazmanah not a thing; challenged from mutar of corpse to heirs — collected for dead, surplus to heirs — only hazmanah; answer: give heirs for dead's disgrace — he waives so mutar goes to heirs; challenged only on mutar; for the dead himself obviously acquired; not mere hazmanah — must give from vow. Mordechai: one who vowed land for synagogue, priests withheld — Maharam: cannot change without consent, cannot revert — vow formed; wait for permission. Likewise collecting for synagogue — obligated by vow, cannot divert coins though not holy. This is clear; Beit Yosef wrote nearby bricks may be lent, belong to congregation — foundation of all we said. Difficulty from ch. 50 mutar to heirs from dead's waiver — not relevant why mutar must go to kodesh per Rashi; why not reshut? Here collecting for ST or synagogue — stam intent surplus also for mitzvah; collecting for dead — intent for heirs, dead waives disgrace only if mutar to heirs. And one may say on Rashi: in mutar need at least lighter kedushah — Megillah 27, Rava: collected and left over — permitted; Abaye: when not stipulated; if stipulated even dukseusya permitted — hire for governor; if sold and left over stipulation would not help — certainly collected and left over; reason of stipulation — if not stipulated, not so. What does he challenge — perhaps stipulation needed even for reshut, but for kedushah even light, no stipulation. Questioner holds this is mitzvah since public needs; proof leniency for public needs on Shabbat.
