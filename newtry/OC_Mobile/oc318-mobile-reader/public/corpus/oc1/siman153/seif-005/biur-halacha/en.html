Know that all these points are to resolve the matter so that this law should not contradict the general law of designation, where we hold that designation is not significant. For this reason, the Beit Yosef, the Rama, and the Magen Avraham were forced to distinguish all these laws. In truth, this law is not stated explicitly in the Gemara; rather, the Rambam, Tur, and Mechaber inferred it from the Gemara's expression, they collected and there was extra, which implies that without extra it is forbidden, even when they collected money. However, the view of the Meiri and the Tashbetz, volume 2, simanim 170 and 171, is that extra is not specific. Just as there the Gemara used the expression they sold and there was extra regarding the condition of the seven leaders of the city in the presence of the townspeople, where according to most poskim extra is certainly not specific, so too even without extra, when they collected money, sanctity has not yet taken effect upon it. Even if they built a synagogue before praying in it, it is not sanctified, as is proven in the Yerushalmi there and brought by the poskim. The Ramach likewise challenged the Rambam and questioned this law; see Kesef Mishneh, chapter 11 of Hilchos Tefillah. This is also somewhat implied in the Yerushalmi, which after bringing the law that a synagogue is not sanctified until they pray in it, asks about service vessels and discusses it at length, even though the vessels were made from communal collection and had already come into the possession of hekdesh. This shows that collection and coming into the treasurer's hand do not help. Even according to the view there that service vessels become sanctified from the time of consecration, that is because bodily sanctity may not follow the rules of designation, as many poskim in fact ruled, unlike here, where it seemingly implies that everyone agrees. See Rambam, chapter 1 of Hilchos Beis HaBechirah, law 20. The Taz likewise rules like the Tashbetz, Meiri, and Ramach, except that according to him even the Rambam and Tur agree, and they forbade only taking the money out for some other matter permanently, because in any event one must fulfill his vow. But to take the money temporarily for some purpose and then return it is certainly permitted, for the money has no sanctity, since designation is not significant, even when it came into the treasurer's hand. This requires study for practical halacha.
