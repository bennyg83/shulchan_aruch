One may not change. The Kesef Mishneh asked in the name of the Ramach: we hold that designation is not effective, as written in seif 8, and certainly money is like spun thread for a garment. He left it requiring study. It does not appear so to me, for in truth money has no sanctity; even the synagogue itself has no sanctity, for it is like accessories of a mitzvah, as the Beis Yosef and the Ran wrote in the name of the Rambam, see there. Rather, there is a concern of weakening the mitzvah, and therefore it is forbidden to change the money. But it is obvious that money has no sanctity. So too the Rosh wrote in responsa, klal 13, siman 14. What is forbidden in changing the money is because the townspeople gave the money only with the intention that they build a synagogue, and it is forbidden to change it without their consent. So too the Rosh wrote in the first chapter of Bava Basra and in Yoreh Deah, siman 256. However, the public may change it, for it was given according to their intent. The Shach in Yoreh Deah, siman 259, wrote in the name of the Re'em, part 1, siman 53, that the reason is that once they collected the money, it is an act and not mere designation. So too the Bach wrote. This is difficult, for there is no greater act than building a synagogue, and nevertheless it is not sanctified, as written in seif 8. Further, in Sanhedrin 48 it is stated that when they collected money for a dead person it is called designation and not an act. Therefore it appears to me that here too the reason is like by a dead person, as I wrote, that once they collected for his need, it is a disgrace if they change it; unlike an individual who donates, or where they bought with public money that had already been collected. See Yoreh Deah, siman 356. And what is written in seif 8, that designation is not effective, speaks where the congregation bought it with non-sacred money that they had collected without specification. The same applies to new bricks; it is permitted to change them in such a case. It is obvious that the seven leaders of the city in the presence of the townspeople may change the money, as written in seif 7.
For whatever they wish. Even for a non-sacred matter; so it implies in the Gemara. The reason is as I wrote, since the intent of the townspeople was made dependent on the money.
If it came to the treasurer, it is forbidden to change it. But through the seven leaders of the city in the presence of the townspeople it is permitted, as written in seif 7. See Yoreh Deah, simanim 256 and 259.
It is permitted to change it. Meaning, to borrow them and give others in their place. The same law applies to money as well, and so it is written in Yoreh Deah, siman 259. But if they collected wood and stones, it is forbidden to change them, as written above.
