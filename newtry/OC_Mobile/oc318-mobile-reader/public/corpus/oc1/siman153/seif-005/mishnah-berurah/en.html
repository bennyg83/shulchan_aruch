One may not change, etc. If the gabbaim wish to borrow the funds and later replace them with other money, according to the Magen Avraham it is forbidden, for the donors want the mitzvah to be performed specifically with this money. According to the Taz it is permitted. However, through the seven leaders of the city in the presence of the townspeople, all agree that the money may be changed, as stated in seif 7.
For whatever they wish. This means even for something that has no sanctity at all, provided it is a public need. The reason is that since the intent of the townspeople was made dependent on the money, the surplus is therefore permitted.
Even if they bought and sold, etc. The Eliyah Rabbah and the Gra question this, for it is possible that since afterward they bought without specification from all the money, the sanctity took effect on the entire purchase, and it is forbidden afterward when they sold and had a surplus.
But if they did not stipulate, etc. The reason is that then, when they bought, the sanctity took effect on everything they bought, and the condition at the time of sale does not help afterward.
The sanctity of the money took effect, etc. According to the Magen Avraham, whom we cited above in seif katan 13, it is forbidden to borrow them and give others in their place.
And it is forbidden to change them except, etc. Although it is proven below in seif 8 that even if they built it for the sake of a synagogue, we require specifically that they prayed in it, but as long as they did not pray in it they may change it, for designation is not effective, there it speaks where the synagogue was not made through collection of money collected for its need from the townspeople, but where the congregation bought it with non-sacred money that they collected without specification, or each person brought his wood and stone and they built a synagogue. But when money was collected for this from the townspeople, it is certainly forbidden to change it even if they have not yet prayed there at all. The same applies if there is still money from the collection, or wood and stones bought from it, as the Mechaber and the Rav ruled. Some say the reason is because it already came into the treasurer’s hand, and the Magen Avraham wrote the reason that since the money was collected from the townspeople for the synagogue, there is disgrace afterward if they change it to a lower sanctity.
And if they brought wood, etc. According to the explanation of the Magen Avraham, this speaks where they did not go to collect them from the townspeople, for that is no worse than collecting money, but where many people volunteered on their own and brought them. Then it is permitted to change them before they came into the treasurer’s hand. See Biur Halacha, s.v. one may not change.
