(9) And if the seven good men of the city sold it, etc. — the buyer may do even those four things — such is the view of Rambam and Rosh, and Tur and Mahariu followed them. Raavad argues on this, as is explained in the poskim; and per his view, the Gemara did not permit a disgraceful use except for a sword alone without anything else, except that it stood in the synagogue beforehand; but they were not permitted to profane a house of prayer to make it a bathhouse and the like. And Ran and Meiri brought these two views and did not decide; and Rashbatz, volume 2, siman 170, upheld the view of Raavad — see there. And in truth, it is a greater leniency per what Ramban, Rashba, and Ritva decided: that in a beit haknesset it is like other mitzvah implements, such as sukkah, lulav, and shofar and the like; except that if the seven good men of the city agreed in the majority to sell it — then they revealed their intent that they have no further need for it, and it is like mitzvah implements after their time, when their mitzvah is finished and they are discarded — see there in their words. And if so, per what the Mechaber ruled in siman 21 seif 2 regarding worn-out mitzvah tallitot, that one may not designate them for a disgraceful use but throws them away and they decay — if so, the same would apply in our matter, and all the more so per the view of Ran, who imposed holiness upon it from their words; if so, it is even superior to other mitzvah implements, and certainly one may not use it for a disgraceful use. However, one may say that the law in siman 21 mentioned is in truth not agreed upon, for the mara de'atra of that sugya in siman 21 is Raavad, as brought there in Beit Yosef; and it is possible he follows his reasoning here; and it is possible Rambam in truth argues on that law there [see there in siman 21 in the explanation of the Gra, the source of this law from the sugya in chapter Kol HaKelim; and in truth Rambam there explains that it deals with all garments, and one may say tallitot of mitzvah; and the sugya there will be settled in another way — see Rashba and Meiri there]. However, the Mechaber ruled like Raavad in siman 21 mentioned, and also in siman 664 seif 8 this law is brought regarding hoshanot in the name of Or Zaru'a, chapter Bnei HaIr — see there in Or Zaru'a, who brings thus in the name of the ancient book: meat on coals — if so, how do we permit here for the buyer to make a bathhouse or latrine. And it is possible to say: per what we wrote above in Biur Halacha, another reasoning in the reason of the sons of the city in the majority — because presumably all consecrate with their knowledge, and it is as if they consecrated conditionally; it is possible that per this reasoning it is permitted even for a disgraceful use, since they hung it on their knowledge and they agreed to this; but it is still difficult, for it is not reasonable at all that they gave with their knowledge so much, even to make a latrine in it. What emerges from our words: it is reasonable that just as they ruled in siman 21 regarding tzitzit and in siman 664 regarding hoshanot that one may not use them for something disgraceful — the same applies here; and all the more so because it is a fixed thing and recognizable, and great disgrace to use it with lightheadedness, as Ritva wrote in the sugya of the storehouse and pledge — for this reason it is stricter in a beit haknesset than other mitzvah implements — see there.
