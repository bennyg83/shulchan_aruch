And if the seven good men of the city sold it, etc., the purchaser may make even these four uses. This is the view of the Rambam and the Rosh, followed by the Tur and Rabbenu Yerucham. The Raavad disagrees, as explained in the poskim; according to him, the Gemara permitted a degrading use only for a ruin that was already a ruin, though a synagogue had previously stood on it, but not that they may profane a house of prayer and make it into a tannery and the like. The Ran and Meiri cite both opinions and do not decide. Tashbetz, part 2 siman 170, upholds the Raavad's view. In truth this is a great leniency, for the Ramban, Rashba, and Ritva decided that a synagogue is like other tashmishei mitzvah, such as sukkah, lulav, shofar, and the like; only when the seven good men of the city, in the presence of the townspeople, agree to sell it, they reveal that they no longer need it, and it is like tashmishei mitzvah after the time of their mitzvah, which may be discarded. If so, since the Mechaber rules in siman 21:2 regarding worn-out tzitzit garments that one may not designate them for degrading use, but only discard them and let them perish, the same should apply here. All the more according to the Ran, who holds that rabbinic sanctity was placed upon it, it is stronger than other tashmishei mitzvah and certainly may not be used degradingly. True, one could say that the ruling in siman 21 is not universally accepted, for the source of that teaching is the Raavad, as cited there in the Beit Yosef, and perhaps he follows his own view here; perhaps the Rambam disagrees there too. See the Gra there, who derives the law from the sugya in Kol HaKelim, though the Rambam explains that sugya as referring to all garments, not specifically mitzvah garments, and the sugya may be resolved differently; see Rashba and Meiri there. Yet since the Mechaber rules like the Raavad in siman 21, and in siman 664:8 this law is also brought regarding hoshanot in the name of Or Zarua, chapter Bnei HaIr, who cites the ancient work Basar Al Gabei Gechalim, how can we permit the purchaser here to make it into a bathhouse or privy? One might answer based on what we wrote above in Biur Halacha, that the power of the town leaders in the presence of the townspeople rests on the presumption that all consecrated it with their consent, as though on condition; perhaps for that reason even degrading use is permitted, since the matter depends on their intent and they agreed to this. Still, it remains difficult, for it is not at all reasonable that they gave their intent so broadly, even to make it into a privy.
The conclusion of our words is that it is reasonable that just as we rule in siman 21 regarding tzitzit and in siman 664 regarding hoshanot that one may not use them for degrading purposes, the same applies here, and all the more so, because this is a fixed and recognizable object and it is a great disgrace to use it frivolously, as the Ritva writes in the sugya of rental and collateral, that for this reason a synagogue is more severe than other tashmishei mitzvah; see there.
