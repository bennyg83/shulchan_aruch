The villagers, a synagogue. The same applies to one of large cities where, at the time of building, they made it dependent on the intent of an individual, as explained above at the end of seif 7.
A permanent sale. Since the townspeople or the leaders of the city agree to the sale, as above in seif 7.
Except for a bathhouse, etc. For all these are very disgraceful uses, and it is forbidden to make them in the place where there had been a synagogue. Even if the building fell in that place and only a mound remains, it is forbidden to use that place for these four things. Likewise, it is forbidden to sow in that place, for all this is a disgrace to the place that had originally been a synagogue.
And if the seven sold it, etc. The reason is that then the sanctity departs from that place entirely. See the Peri Megadim, who is uncertain whether perhaps we specifically require that they stipulated explicitly at the time of sale and authorized the buyer for this.
The buyer may make, etc. The same applies that through the seven leaders of the city in the presence of the townspeople, it is permitted to use the money for whatever they wish, even for a non-sacred matter.
Even these four things. See Biur Halacha, that this law is not clear, and initially one certainly must be careful regarding it.
