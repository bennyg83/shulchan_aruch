And another mitzvah need arose. At the beginning of Bava Basra 3b, most editions of our Gemara read: “If they collected money and it remains, what is the law? He said to him: Perhaps an urgent mitzvah need will arise and they will give it away. If they prepared bricks, what is the law? He said to him: Perhaps an urgent mitzvah need will arise, and they will sell them and give away the proceeds.” This implies that even collected money may be spent only for an urgent mitzvah need. How, then, does Rambam write here simply “a mitzvah need”? Some editions initially read, “Perhaps some mitzvah need will arise and they will give it away.” This was Rambam’s version. It therefore includes any mitzvah need, even a lesser one, since the money is taken only as a loan until it is replenished. See what I wrote in seif 5. The statement that purchased stones may not be sold for another mitzvah is difficult, since seif 6 rules that they may be sold to support Torah students. The answer is that seif 6 concerns a community that has another synagogue, while here they have no other synagogue, which is the case discussed at the beginning of Bava Basra. Beit Yosef unnecessarily offered a different explanation.
