For he reverts from his renunciation. And it is difficult what is written from siman 371 seif 2 regarding an Israelite from this courtyard who went to dwell elsewhere etc., that he does not restrict, for we say once it was permitted it is permitted — why do we not say here likewise, if he returned and renounced on Shabbat that he cannot return and forbid, since it was already permitted at the entrance of Shabbat. One can say that case is different — there the permission depends on the entrance of Shabbat, for he must remove it from his mind at the entrance of Shabbat that this Shabbat should not be in this house; therefore the permission depends on the entrance of Shabbat alone, which is not so here, where he can renounce domain even from nightfall, as written siman 380; therefore the permission must be for all of Shabbat specifically, as above.

And according to Rashi chazakah is not effective etc. — for Rashi says on the incident of R' Gamliel who said a Sadducee lived with them in an alley and renounced his domain, and he told him: Father, hurry and they carried the vessels to the alley before he carries out and forbids you — Rashi explained: they held in the alley, and he can no longer revert from his renunciation; and when the day was sanctified we deal with it — chazakah from erev Shabbat is not proven, end quote. It appears this is what he meant: in truth there is no complete chazakah here to acquire a complete acquisition, but he only needs to uproot the Shabbat prohibition; and this is not relevant on erev Shabbat, for there is no prohibition of carrying yet until the day was sanctified and carrying was forbidden; and you who will carry your vessels there will reveal through this that your courtyard is all such. It appears simple, and in vain did Moreinu HaRav Zalman reject this Rashi; Rashi explained from the Tur's versions and wrote that it is a scribal error — rather the matters stand as they are, that it depends on the prohibition of carrying, as written.
