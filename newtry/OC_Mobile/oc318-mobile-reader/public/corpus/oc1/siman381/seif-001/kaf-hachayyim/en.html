1) [Se'if 1] One who renounced his rights and carried out, etc. This means that he transgressed and carried out. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1. It is obvious that if he renounced only his rights in the courtyard, he is forbidden to carry out, as stated above in siman 380, se'if 1. Even if he also renounced his rights in his house, in which case carrying out is permitted as stated there in se'if 2, this applies only after those to whom he renounced his rights have taken possession through use, as stated there in subsection 14. Before they have taken possession through use, his action constitutes a retraction of his renunciation, and the courtyard once again becomes jointly owned by residents who did not establish an eruv, so carrying from a house into the courtyard is forbidden.
2) There. One who renounced his rights and carried out, etc. Even if he also renounced his rights in his house, if he intentionally carried out before the others took possession through use, he prohibits carrying, as stated above in siman 380, subsection 14. This is contrary to Olas Shabbos. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1, and Ma'amar Mordechai, subsection 1, write likewise.
3) There. And afterward he carried from his house into the courtyard, etc. If he brought an object inside, however, it is permitted. This is the correct text, as Eshel Avraham and Machatzis HaShekel likewise write. An exception is when he also renounced his rights in his house, in which case bringing an object inside is forbidden. Magen Avraham, subsection 1. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 2, however, writes that the cited words of Magen Avraham require examination. Even HaOzer, siman 380, se'if 4, likewise challenges these words of Magen Avraham, as does Ma'amar Mordechai, subsection 2. See there. Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 2, however, agrees with Magen Avraham and resolves the objections of Eliyah Rabbah and Even HaOzer. See there. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1, rules likewise. See above, siman 380, subsection 20.
4) There. If he acted unintentionally, he does not prohibit carrying. This is because he did not intend to retract his renunciation. Levush.
5) There. For he retracts his renunciation, etc. This is unlike siman 371, se'if 2, regarding a Jewish resident of this courtyard who went to spend Shabbos elsewhere, where we say that once Shabbos began in a permitted state, it remains permitted. The case there is different because the permission depends upon the onset of Shabbos. Here, however, one can renounce his rights even after nightfall, as stated in siman 380, se'if 1. The permitted state must therefore remain in effect throughout the entire Shabbos. Taz, subsection 1. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 3, and Nehar Shalom, however, challenge this reason of the Taz. Nehar Shalom explains that here carrying was permitted only because of his renunciation, and whenever he retracts, he uproots the renunciation and it is as though it never existed.
6) There. For he retracts his renunciation, etc. He cannot subsequently renounce his rights again. Tosafos on Eruvin 69a, beginning with the word 'Carried.' See Rabbeinu Yehonasan on the Rif, whose words imply that if he did not act publicly, he may subsequently renounce his rights again. See there.
7) There. But if they had already taken possession through use, etc. This means before the person who renounced his rights carried out.
