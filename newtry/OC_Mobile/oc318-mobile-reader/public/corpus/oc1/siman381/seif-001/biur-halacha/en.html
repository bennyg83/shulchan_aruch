One who renounced his domain etc. deliberately restricts — see in Yad Ephraim where it is explained that even if he carried out on erev Shabbat as well it is considered like reverting from his renunciation and he will forbid them when the day becomes sanctified; but this is not necessary, for it is possible to say that even though renunciation can be from while it is still day, nevertheless regarding reverting from his renunciation it does not apply except when he carries out on Shabbat and not when he carries out on a weekday, for the matter is not proven that he reverts from his renunciation, for his renunciation was only for Shabbat day.

Or they brought in etc. — in this all agree that bringing in is equivalent to carrying out, as the Acharonim wrote.

And according to Rashi etc. — this wording implies he adopts the primary view as the first opinion; but in truth it requires further study, for the first opinion is only the view of the Tur, and Rashi's view is not singular, for Rabbeinu Yehonasan and the Ritva likewise wrote, and it is proven in Tosafos 68b s.v. vehotzi'u, and as written in Sefer Beit Meir. And what the Bach wrote to reject Rashi's view — the Acharonim already rejected his words, and one should be stringent. And know that according to Rashi the matter is simple that it is not called chazakah except through bringing in and carrying out, which is a matter forbidden without renunciation, as explained in Tosafos and Ritva; but per the Tur that chazakah helps even from erev Shabbat, it is possible that according to him chazakah helps through using the courtyard itself even without carrying out and bringing in, as with chazakot in general; nevertheless it is not necessary, and see.
