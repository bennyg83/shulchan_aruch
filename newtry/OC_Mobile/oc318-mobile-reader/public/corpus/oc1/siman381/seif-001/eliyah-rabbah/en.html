One who relinquishes, etc. Meaning, where he did not relinquish the rights to his house. But if he also relinquished his house, the concept of retracting his relinquishment seemingly does not apply, as Olat Shabbos writes. He overlooked the Levush in siman 380, seif 2, and the Beit Yosef there. Rashi on 26a, s.v. Anshei, also makes this explicit, and Shibolei HaLeket likewise implies that even when one relinquished the rights to his house, he can be regarded as retracting unless the others have already taken possession.
And he carried out, etc. But bringing items in is permitted, unless he also relinquished the rights to his house, in which case bringing them in is prohibited. See seif 4, as stated by Magen Avraham. This is because bringing something into a domain he relinquished is not regarded as returning and taking possession. Yet this requires study. If so, what did Tosafot on 68, s.v. VeHotzi'u, ask from the wording 'and he carried out'? Perhaps the case is where he did not relinquish his house. Indeed, on 69 the Gemara establishes the Mishnah as referring to an unspecified case. See there.
For he thereby retracts, etc. The Taz asks why this differs from siman 371, seif 2, where once Shabbos was permitted it does not later become prohibited. He answers that there the permission depends on the onset of Shabbos, because he must have removed his intent at the onset of Shabbos not to spend Shabbos in his house, and therefore the permission depends on the onset of Shabbos. Here, however, one may relinquish rights even after nightfall, so the permission must remain throughout Shabbos. End quote. This is incorrect, for I already explained at the beginning of siman 371 that even if he leaves on Shabbos and later changes his mind, it is permitted. Furthermore, the Mishnah on 79 indicates that everyone agrees it is prohibited here. According to Beit Shammai there, relinquishment may be done only before Shabbos, so the difficulty would remain. Nor is it satisfactory to say that Beit Shammai follows Rabbi Meir in the Mishnah on 86, for then the Gemara should have said the matter depends on a tannaitic dispute. In any event, the Taz's distinction is strained. In my humble opinion, there was never a difficulty. There, when he changes his mind, he does so for the entire Shabbos. Here, when he relinquishes his rights, he does not relinquish them for the entire Shabbos, but only so long as he does not need them, as written at the end of this siman. See also seifim 3 and 4 in siman 371.
And according to Rashi, etc. The first opinion is primary, as stated by Nachalat Tzvi.
The Levush writes: If these made an eruv, etc., no relinquishment is required, etc. One cannot argue that relinquishment is nevertheless required so that it be permitted to carry into and out of the other courtyard, as stated in siman 378, seif 2, because here they are a group and do not become guests, as explained in siman 380, seif 4.
The Levush writes: If the inner courtyard made an eruv, etc., for one may not relinquish, etc. So writes the Beit Yosef. This is difficult, because the first rationale should suffice: a foot permitted in its own place does not prohibit. One may answer that he preferred this rationale, that they cannot relinquish, over the first rationale, according to which there is simply no need for relinquishment.
