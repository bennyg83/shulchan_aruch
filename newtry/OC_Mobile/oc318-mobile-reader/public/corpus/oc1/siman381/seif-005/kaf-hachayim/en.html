(5) There. For he reverts from his renunciation etc. — and it is not similar to siman 371 seif 2 regarding an Israelite from this courtyard who went to dwell elsewhere etc., that we say once it was permitted it is permitted, for that case is different — there the permission depends on the entrance of Shabbat, for he must remove it from his mind at the entrance of Shabbat that Shabbat should not be in his house; therefore the permission depends on the entrance of Shabbat, which is not so here, where he can renounce domain even from nightfall, as written siman 380 seif 1; therefore the permission must be for all of Shabbat specifically. Taz siman kuf alef. However R' wrote in siman 3 and Nahar Shalom challenged this reason of Taz; rather the reason Nahar Shalom wrote there: here it was not permitted except because of his renunciation, and whoever reverts uproots his renunciation, and he is as if there was none at all.
