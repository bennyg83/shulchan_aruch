(19) There is renunciation of domain from house to house — as renunciation helps from courtyard to courtyard.

(20) And even the one who renounces etc. to his fellow's house — for everything belongs to his fellow since he renounced to him; and even before his fellow held in his house he can carry out from it to his fellow's house. And even if the renouncers were many, they are also permitted to carry out from their houses to their fellow's houses. And it is not similar to one who renounces the domain of his courtyard, who has no permission to carry out from his house to the courtyard even if he also renounced the domain of his house, until they hold first; and even if they held, it only helps with individual who renounced to many, but not individual to individual, as above in siman 380; and all the more so many to individual — there, since he had a share in the courtyard but renounced to them, when he carries out from his house to the courtyard it appears he reverts from his renunciation and holds his share in the courtyard; which is not so when he carries out to his fellow's house, where he never had a share — it is not relevant to say he thereby holds his fellow's domain; and also regarding his house itself it is not relevant to say he reverts and acquires it through carrying his vessels out from it to his fellow's house, for on the contrary he withdraws from it.

(21) Returning and holding etc. — and even though above in siman 380 seif 1 it is explained that if one renounced the domain of his courtyard to his fellows he is permitted to carry out from their houses to the courtyard, and we do not say he reverts and acquires his courtyard through carrying out — there it deals with individual relative to many, and he is only considered a guest relative to them; but here it deals with individual relative to individual, and individual relative to individual is not called a guest, as above in seif 4; and therefore he reverts and acquires what he renounced initially; and accordingly even if the one for whom he renounced also held, it is forbidden for the renouncer to carry out from his house, as above in siman 380 seif 4.

(22) From the room to his house — and the same applies from house to his room, because it is like carrying from domain to domain; and see the Acharonim who raised that even if he also renounced the domain of his room, it is explicitly forbidden to carry out from one to the other, for in this he is returning and holding his domain, as above regarding from his fellow's house to his house.

(23) And some say one may not renounce etc. — because one must be concerned lest the law of eruv be forgotten from these houses open to one another, that they will all renounce their domains to one and they too will be permitted to carry out each from his house to his fellow's house, since he does not appear to be returning and holding his domain through this carrying out; which is not so when he leaves one room, that it is forbidden to carry out from it to the house, and if there were an eruv he would be permitted — he suffers through the eruv, and again there is no concern that they will all renounce and the law of eruv will be forgotten.

(24) And then it is permitted to bring in etc. — see in Magen Avraham that he wondered on this, for it necessarily deals with individual relative to individual; for if individual relative to many who made an eruv, then it is again not relevant to say lest the law of eruv be forgotten since they made an eruv; rather it deals with individual relative to individual; and if so how is it permitted to bring in from his fellow's house to his house, since individual relative to individual is not called a guest and it is as if he is returning and holding his house, and even if his fellow held it does not help. And see Chemed Moshe who resolved that this law of the Rama deals with individual relative to many, except those many dwelled in one house and did not need an eruv, and this too is called many, and this individual is called a guest relative to them; therefore he is permitted to bring in from their house to his house even before they held. And see in Biur Halacha what we wrote in the name of the Gra.
