For then he was returning and holding the domain he renounced — see Mishna Berurah what we wrote on this and we followed the words of Magen Avraham on this; and in truth even though in the words of Tosafos it implies somewhat that the act was with many who renounced to an individual, and likewise in Ritva it is proven that at any rate it was individual against individual (whereas for us in siman 380 the law is like many against individual), nevertheless it is not mentioned there any implication that the reason for this view that forbids carrying out from his fellow's house is because individual against individual is not called a guest; and likewise the Mechaber was silent and did not mention this reason. And it appears more in their words that even if the renouncer was an individual and renounced to many, it is also forbidden for him to carry out from their domain to his domain, even though in general when one renounces his courtyard he is permitted to carry out from his fellow's house to the courtyard; this case is different, for it deals with after his renunciation he carried out his vessels and brought them into his fellow's house, and afterward carries them out and brings them in again to his domain as before — he appears like one who wishes to return and acquire his house that he renounced; in such a case we do not say he is like a guest. See Tosafos and Rosh and Or Zarua who are precise thus in their words; and see Etz Almogim who also addressed the wording of Tosafos on this. And this was possibly the view of Olat Shabbat in the name of Beit Yosef and Ba'al Halacha Berurah, who distinguished this law between whether he preceded and held or not: if he preceded and held, he is permitted even to carry out from his fellow's house, for they hold that here it does not deal with the renouncers being many, but rather the opposite — he renounced to many; and therefore if he preceded and held he cannot return; nevertheless when he did not precede and hold, it is forbidden for him to carry out, and we do not say he is like a guest, because this case is different — he carried out those same vessels that he had brought in before, for it appears he wishes to return and acquire anew. And see what is written in the gloss.

And then it is permitted — see what is written in Mishna Berurah, the question of Magen Avraham and what Ba'al Chemed Moshe resolved. And according to their words, this view does not disagree at all with the first view regarding whether bringing in is permitted; for the Mechaber deals with many against individual, and the Rama deals with individual against many. And in truth, that we needed to push so much and remove the matters from their simple meaning is only because of the Shulchan Aruch's view that ruled in siman 380 that even individual against individual is also called many against individual, that he is not made a guest; but we already wrote as written in the name of Even HaEzer and Perishah that individual against individual is called a guest, and Perishah proved his ruling from this law that he holds derives from the words of Tosafos daf 64; and accordingly the words of the Rama come according to their simple meaning, and to disagree comes with the words of the Mechaber who forbids bringing in, and his view is that one should permit even bringing in, for he is like a guest — individual relative to individual is called a guest [and even though in the words of Tosafos it implies that all the residents renounced to that one house, and if so it is many against individual, that for all opinions he is not a guest — Perishah wrote that where the many are not partners in one domain as in a courtyard but each dwells separately, they are not called many and each is an individual separately and is called a guest]. And one who examines Ritva there will see clearly that his view is likewise. See there that he brings the view of R' Yehuda which are the words of the Rama here, and explains explicitly because individual relative to individual is called a guest. And in the words of the Mechaber one must explain as we explained above, and not on the basis that individual against individual is not called a guest, but rather Magen Avraham was compelled to all the forced readings in order to uphold the Shulchan Aruch's view above siman 380; and thus I found the Gra wrote that this view that then it is permitted, brought in the gloss, disagrees with the Mechaber's view.
