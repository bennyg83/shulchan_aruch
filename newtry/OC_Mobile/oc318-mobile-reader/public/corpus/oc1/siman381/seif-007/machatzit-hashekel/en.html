(Siman kuf zayin) And then etc. — and their words are puzzling etc., for the opinion of some authorities is the words of Rabbenu Tam who disagreed with Rashi and holds that from house to house one does not renounce, for the law of eruv is not forgotten; for granted in a courtyard, if one renounces the domain of his courtyard and not his house, it is forbidden to carry from his house to the courtyard — if so he will make an eruv on the coming Shabbat in order to permit carrying from his house to the courtyard (and if he renounced also the domain of his house, as explained above siman 380 seif 2, it is permitted to carry from his house to the courtyard — see Tosafot daf 64a s.v. im ken etc.; nevertheless, when an individual renounces to an individual, it is forbidden to carry since he is not considered a guest as explained end of siman 380; and if they renounced to the many, the many made an eruv and the law of eruv is not forgotten — not so from house to house, for the courtyard does not interrupt between them; when one renounces to his fellow he is permitted to carry even from the house of the one who renounced to the house for which he renounced, for in this he does not appear to be holding what he carried from his domain to his fellow's domain — as above, specifically when he brings into his domain he appears to be holding); and if so, even individual against individual it is permitted to carry from the house of the renouncer to the house for which he renounced, and thereby the law of eruv will be forgotten and they will not make an eruv (and we are concerned lest they also forget to renounce — so Maharsha there). And Rashi's opinion, which is one opinion, Tosafot wrote that we are not concerned for this, since in any case if they do not make an eruv the renouncer will be forbidden to bring into his house and will suffer when he makes an eruv. And Rabbenu Tam must hold that we are not concerned for the prohibition of bringing in, for necessarily he brings his fellow's vessels as to his house — in this he does not care, for the presumption is that when a person uses, he uses his own vessels and not another's, as they say daf 72; and his vessels per the majority are not found in his fellow's house; and also when he takes out his vessels to his fellow's house, that he should not again be permitted to bring in — in this too he should not be so concerned, for it is not so common and he will not suffer through making an eruv, and thereby the law of eruv will be forgotten; but when he leaves for himself one room, then it is forbidden to carry from that room to the house and he will suffer through making an eruv to carry from the room — thereby he will make an eruv and the law of eruv will not be forgotten; and therefore Maharshal wondered at what Rav Beit Yosef and Rama permitted to bring in this way from his fellow's house to his house; and why is it similar to one who renounced the domain of his courtyard to his fellow individually even though he did not renounce the domain of his house and it is forbidden to carry from his house to the courtyard — nevertheless it is forbidden to carry from his fellow's house to the courtyard; the same here, that he renounced his house and not a room — it is forbidden to carry from the room to the house; nevertheless it is forbidden to carry from his fellow's house to his house, for here the house has the law of a courtyard and the room has the law of a house. It is not fitting to say lest it be forgotten etc., for the many made an eruv as stated.
