[18] [Levush] Even to place them, etc. The Shulchan Aruch does not mention this here, and one must say that it relied on seif 10, where it had already written the rule. In truth, it requires study why these laws were not written in a single clause, as the Tur wrote them. It is also difficult why it was necessary to state that one may not place the piece in his fellow’s bowl, for this follows by a kal vachomer from the preceding rule that one may not place it on the table. In my humble opinion, the explanation is as I wrote in my commentary to Shulchan Aruch. Maseches Derech Eretz states: “A person should not bite from a piece of bread and return it to the bowl. Likewise, he should not bite from a piece of bread and give it to his fellow, nor drink from a cup and give it to his fellow.” It appears to me that this does not refer to the portion that he bit, for that may not even be placed on the table. Rather, it refers to the remaining portion, similar to the case of one who drinks from a cup and gives the remainder in the cup to his fellow. This is likewise evident from Rokeach, siman 329, who writes: “A person should not eat from a piece of bread and return it to the bowl.” Since he uses the term “eat” in place of “bite,” it implies that he eats what he bit and returns only the remainder. Shulchan Aruch therefore divided the cases: seif 10 discusses the portion that he actually bit. I likewise understand the Tur. Its statement that one may not place it on the table refers to the portion that he bit, while its statement “or in the bowl” refers to the remainder, even though both were written in one clause. This also resolves the question how the Tur derived the prohibition against placing it on the table, which is not mentioned in Maseches Derech Eretz. The answer is as explained. This is unlike Lechem Chamudos, page 224, who was forced to write that the Tur’s version also read, “and he should not place it on the table.”
