17) To drink, etc. Levush writes that the reason is lest the other become upset and choke while drinking. This implies that one must wait specifically when the other drinks, but need not wait for another activity. Rabbeinu Yonah, however, writes in the third chapter of Berachos concerning the statement that two people wait for one another at the bowl: this means that while one drinks, cuts bread, or waits to receive from the piece of the one who recited HaMotzi, the other should wait for him as a matter of proper conduct. Darkei Moshe, paragraph 2, and Derishah, paragraph 1, cite this; see there. This explicitly implies that drinking is not exclusive, and one must likewise wait for another activity. Magen Avraham, paragraph 7, writes the same, contrary to Levush. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 6, likewise writes that Levush overlooked the words of Rabbeinu Yonah; see there. Yad Aharon, Hagahos Beis Yosef; Shulchan Gavoah, paragraph 1; Beis Menuchah, paragraph 12; Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 3. It appears that this applies only among equals. If, however, one's father, mother, older brother, or anyone superior in wisdom and count, whom they are obligated to honor, reclines with them, his honor requires the younger people to wait for the elder even in the middle of eating; the elder should not wait for the younger. Chesed LaAlafim there.
