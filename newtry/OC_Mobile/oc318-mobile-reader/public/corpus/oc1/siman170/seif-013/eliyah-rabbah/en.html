[16] [Levush] So it appears to me, etc. Malbushei Yom Tov writes: This is not his own ruling, but that of his teacher, the Rama, who wrote this gloss in the Shulchan Aruch. In my humble opinion, however, there is still a difference between them. According to the Rama, one may say, “Come eat with me, and I will eat with you another time.” According to the Levush, however, this appears forbidden. Rather, he should initially say simply, “Eat with me,” and afterward add, “If you do not wish,” etc. It is possible, however, that the Levush merely explains the phrase “Eat with me what you fed me,” meaning that this is his intention, but he does not state it explicitly. In any event, there is a difference in wording between “Eat with me and I will eat with you” and “Eat what you fed me.” Maharshal and Bach explain that in Yerushalayim they reverse the wording: one may say, “What you fed me, eat with me,” because these words do not indicate that his fellow fed him on condition that he later feed him in return. Rather, he himself now wishes to repay him with kindness, just as the other fed him as an act of kindness. But if he says, “Eat with me what you fed me,” his wording indicates that his fellow lent him food so that he would later feed him in return, and this appears like interest. Taz implies that all these formulations should be prohibited.
