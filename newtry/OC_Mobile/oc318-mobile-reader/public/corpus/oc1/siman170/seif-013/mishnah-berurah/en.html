(29) To the house. This means to the host. It is obvious that this refers to one who is not lodging there for payment.
(30) “Give me something to eat.” If food was placed before him, he need not wait further until they explicitly tell him to eat.
(31) Because of interest. Meaning, it appears like interest, but strictly speaking it is not interest, since when he first fed him he did not intend this repayment.
(32) But it is permitted to say, etc. He does not intend this as a debt at all. Rather, he speaks this way as a matter of courtesy so that the other person will not refuse his words, will eat with him, and will not feel uncomfortable eating with him without payment. The Taz is inclined to be stringent even in this case; see there.
(33) Even at a meal, etc. At the time of eating he does not tell him that it is repayment for what occurred previously. Rather, he gives it merely as a gift.
