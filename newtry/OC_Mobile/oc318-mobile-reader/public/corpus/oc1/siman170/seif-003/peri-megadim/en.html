The Tur writes: “Whatever the host tells you, do.” He then recounts an incident in which guests entered before Rabbi Shimon, who ordered them to eat, and they vowed and falsely swore not to do so, whereupon he struck them. This incident appears to contradict the rule, for they refused, yet he struck them for fabricating a vow. See Perishah, who raises an additional question: how could they fabricate a vow? Taz answers that these were vows of encouragement. He therefore struck them because they failed to obey the rule, “Whatever the host tells you, do.” Even according to Magen Avraham, seif katan 10, they should have complied after he urged them. Alternatively, by making a vow they themselves acted improperly, and all is resolved. See Perishah for another answer. One may ask why they did not also violate the rule that one may not refuse a great person, as stated by the Gemara. See Magen Avraham, seif katan 10, and Maharsha at the end of chapter 7 of Pesachim. It is difficult to say that the guests were as great as Rabbi Shimon, since he struck them. This requires study. The ethical conduct involved also requires study. See Rambam, chapter 1, halachah 5, of Hilchos Mamrim; with Hashem’s help, this will be explained in the general introduction.
