[8] One who eats, etc. The same applies to one who drinks, as mentioned above. Magen Avraham writes in the name of Tosafos, Bechoros 44b, that one should not drink water in public, but should turn his face away; and outside the meal, even when drinking other beverages, he should turn away. In my humble opinion, he was not precise. Tosafos there, s.v. ve’ein, and Hilchos Derech Eretz, specifically mention water, implying that only with water is there a distinction between during and outside a meal. With other beverages, however, even outside the meal one need not turn away. I found the same in Piskei Tosafos.
[9] He should do, etc. This is stated in Pesachim 86b. Magen Avraham asks: When they asked him, “Why, when they gave you a cup,” etc., he should have answered that it was because of the rule, “Whatever the host tells you,” etc. He answers that it is nevertheless improper manners to comply immediately before he is urged. This is difficult, for the Gemara implies that he immediately sat upon the bed. I saw that Maharsha explains that he preferred to answer from the formal rule that one does not refuse a great person. Yet the question remains: why did they ask, when they knew the rule that one must do whatever the host says? In my humble opinion, the answer is that the phrase “they gave him a cup” implies that they merely handed him the cup and did not tell him to drink. This is therefore not included in “Whatever the host tells you.” Examine this carefully. “Except leave”: Bach explains that one must perform every service within the house, except that if the host tells him to leave the house in order to serve him outside, he need not do so. Perishah, however, explains: Whatever the host merely tells you to do, do it, except if the host tells you to leave, do not leave unless he strikes you or throws out your belongings, because they said that a guest who leaves at once both disgraces and is disgraced. Lechem Chamudos, page 504, writes that it simply means leaving the house, and for this reason the poskim omitted it. The Gemara states that even if the host throws something, the guest may not deviate from his instruction.
[10] [Levush] Who withholds, etc. Lechem Chamudos there gives an additional reason: so that the members of his household and his guests should not refrain from eating because they think he is particular about their eating.
[11] [Levush] For example, he takes hold, etc. Lechem Chamudos writes that this refers to while the pieces are still in the bowl. Once he has removed one from the bowl, it is not considered gluttonous.
