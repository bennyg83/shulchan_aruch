16) [Seif 2] Two people wait for one another, etc. This applies provided that the delay is not lengthy and that the one who pauses does not engage in ordinary conversation. Eliyah Rabbah at the end of paragraph 6, in the name of Tzedah LaDerech, folio 59; Machatzis HaShekel, paragraph 7. This appears to apply specifically when they eat from one bowl, but if each eats from his own bowl, it is obvious that he need not wait.
17) To drink, etc. Levush writes that the reason is lest the other become upset and choke while drinking. This implies that one must wait specifically when the other drinks, but need not wait for another activity. Rabbeinu Yonah, however, writes in the third chapter of Berachos concerning the statement that two people wait for one another at the bowl: this means that while one drinks, cuts bread, or waits to receive from the piece of the one who recited HaMotzi, the other should wait for him as a matter of proper conduct. Darkei Moshe, paragraph 2, and Derishah, paragraph 1, cite this; see there. This explicitly implies that drinking is not exclusive, and one must likewise wait for another activity. Magen Avraham, paragraph 7, writes the same, contrary to Levush. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 6, likewise writes that Levush overlooked the words of Rabbeinu Yonah; see there. Yad Aharon, Hagahos Beis Yosef; Shulchan Gavoah, paragraph 1; Beis Menuchah, paragraph 12; Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 3. It appears that this applies only among equals. If, however, one's father, mother, older brother, or anyone superior in wisdom and count, whom they are obligated to honor, reclines with them, his honor requires the younger people to wait for the elder even in the middle of eating; the elder should not wait for the younger. Chesed LaAlafim there.
18) Tosafos in Bechoros 44b states: One should not drink water before many people, but should turn his face away. Outside a meal, he should turn away even when drinking other beverages. Magen Avraham there. See Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 8, for his challenge to Magen Avraham, and Birkei Yosef, paragraph 3, who resolves it; see there. Birkei Yosef concludes, however, that nowadays people drink water before many people even outside a meal, and the halachic authorities omitted this rule because they understand it according to Rabbeinu Tam's explanation cited by Tosafos there. Indeed, the common practice is to drink both water and other beverages before many people, whether during a meal or outside a meal.
19) Kesubos 65a states: A woman whose husband is not with her should not drink wine. A woman staying as a guest may not do so even when her husband is with her. If she is accustomed to drinking wine in her husband's presence, she may drink a small amount when he is absent. Magen Avraham there; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 12. Magen Avraham writes that the same applies to other intoxicating beverages. Eliyah Rabbah there, however, writes that one might distinguish wine because it gladdens the heart of man. Nevertheless, it appears that Magen Avraham is correct, for we see that every intoxicating substance brings gladness. One must therefore be concerned about this with other beverages as well. Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, siman 42, paragraph 16, writes the same. See Even HaEzer, siman 70, seif 2.
20) Pesachim 119b states: A person should not eat two and should not drink two. This means that one should not eat or drink pairs because of danger. Tur. Tosafos in Chullin 107b, however, write that nowadays we are not particular about this because danger through this is not common in the present era; see there. Beis Yosef cites this and therefore expresses surprise that the Tur included the rule, since it is not common nowadays. See Perishah and Bach, who resolve this; see there. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 14, likewise writes that nowadays we are not particular about pairs; see there. R' Yaakov Castro writes the same in his glosses; Machazik Berachah, paragraph 2.
20) The Yerushalmi at the beginning of the first chapter of Kesubos states: A younger person may not sit before someone older than he until the elder tells him, 'Sit.' It derives this from the verse, 'Remain here,' followed by, 'and they remained.' Kisei Eliyahu, paragraph 1.
