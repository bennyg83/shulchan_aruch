The elder reaches out. My father-in-law, of blessed memory, found this difficult. Why is the other person called a glutton, when the law above is that the one who breaks bread reaches out first, unless he wishes to honor another? He therefore explains this as a case where they are not eating from one bowl, but each has his own bowl or fruit before him. This is difficult, for siman 167, seif 15, states that if each has his own loaf before him, he need not wait even for the one who breaks bread. It appears to me that the case certainly involves one bowl. The novelty is that even if the younger person received permission from the elder, he may not reach out first. Permission is effective above only in that the recipient of permission also receives a distinction, but it does not entitle him to reach out first.
