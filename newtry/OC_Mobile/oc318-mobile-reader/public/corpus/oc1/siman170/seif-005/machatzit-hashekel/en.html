Seif katan 5: He washes, etc. I do not know why Beit Yosef reversed their wording. The Gemara in Yoma 30a states: One who leaves to urinate washes one hand and returns. If he conversed with his fellow and remained away for an extended period, he washes both hands and returns. When he washes, he should not wash outside and then enter, because of suspicion; rather, he enters, sits in his place, and washes both hands. Thus, the law that he must wash inside is taught after both cases, implying that it applies to both urination and prolonged absence. So too in the Tur and Rambam. Beit Yosef, however, reversed the order and wrote first, immediately after the law of urination, that he must wash inside, and only afterward wrote the law of prolonged absence. This implies that after a prolonged absence he may wash even outside.
Perhaps after a prolonged absence he must always wash inside, meaning even if he intends to eat, because suspicion still applies, since there is no presumption that he is particular about cleanliness. This may be why Beit Yosef reversed the Gemara's order, so that one should not mistakenly think that after a prolonged absence there is also a distinction between drinking and eating. In truth, after a prolonged absence, even for eating he must wash inside. Although according to our version of the Gemara, “He should not wash outside,” etc., “rather, he enters, sits in his place, and washes both hands,” implies that the law of suspicion primarily refers to prolonged absence, which requires washing both hands, whereas after urinating he washes only the hand with which he rubbed himself, and the Gemara there says that this applies only to drinking, but for eating he may wash outside, which would imply that even after prolonged absence there is a distinction between drinking and eating, as Chemed Moshe asks. Nevertheless, Rif and Rosh do not have the words “he washes both hands” in their text, and there is no doubt that this was not their version.
So too writes Mishmeres HaBayis, page 127b, and this is also the view of Riva in Tosafos Yeshanim there.
