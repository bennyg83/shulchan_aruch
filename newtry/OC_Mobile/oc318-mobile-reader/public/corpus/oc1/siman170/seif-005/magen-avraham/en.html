The host shall do. We say at the end of the seventh chapter of Pesachim: he said to him, why, when I told you, sit on the bed, did you sit, and not on the ground? He said to him: whatever the host tells you, do, except leave. Why, when they gave you the cup, did you accept it the first time? He said to him: one refuses a lesser person, but one does not refuse a great person. Tosafot wrote regarding the fact that he sat on the bed that one cannot say there that one does not refuse a great person, for it is a matter of arrogance and authority, and he should not have sat if not because of the rule that whatever the host tells you, do. This is difficult: what did he ask him at the end, why, when they gave you the cup, etc.; he had already answered that whatever the host tells you, do. One can answer that although he is obligated to do so, it is not derech eretz to do it immediately until he urges him, but for a great person one does it immediately. This also appears somewhat from Rashi; see there. What it says, except leave, the Bach explained: for example, if the host told him to go in the marketplace to do some business for him, he need not do so, for it is not honorable for him to go in markets with which he is not familiar. In Mateh Moshe he explained that if the host tells him to leave the lodging, he should not leave, as we say in Eiruvin, until they remove his belongings. This is correct. It is also stated there that even with temporary lodging it is forbidden to change.
