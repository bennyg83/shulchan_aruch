See. Eliyah Rabbah, seifim katanim 23 and 24, cites many beneficial practices, as is his good custom. People of refined conduct avoid even a seudas mitzvah, and Ateres Zekeinim writes likewise. Shelah writes that one who eats excessively violates three prohibitions: “Take heed to yourself,” “lest you forget,” and “Do not make yourselves repulsive.” One should not eat or drink while standing, nor stand immediately after eating. He should drink enough to require a final blessing, whether Bircas HaMazon, Me’ein Shalosh, or Borei Nefashos. A person should be socially agreeable and should not cry among those who rejoice, etc. Shelah writes that the froth referred to as “shuyam” means that which ferments in the barrel, not that which rises when poured into a vessel. Nevertheless, one should also be careful regarding uncovered liquids, namely wine, water, and milk. Although nowadays they are permitted, one who guards his soul should distance himself from them. See there for many beneficial practices.
