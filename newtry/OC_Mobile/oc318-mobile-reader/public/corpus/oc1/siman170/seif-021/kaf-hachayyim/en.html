21) [Seif 3] One leaves a portion at the edge of the bowl, etc. Bach writes that since there is a dispute regarding the textual versions, every conscientious person should act stringently and leave an edge portion both from food cooked in a pot and from food prepared in a pan; see there. Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef, paragraph 1, and Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 7, cite this. In Shaar HaMitzvos, parashas Eikev, however, the Rav, of blessed memory, writes that there is no concern nowadays. The reason for leaving an edge portion is for a poor person who might arrive during the meal, or to leave a portion for the attendant serving at the meal. Since a portion for one's wife and household remains in the pot after food has been served to the householder, this remaining edge portion suffices. This is especially so because the practice of poor people entering during the meal and the institution of the attendant no longer exist among us. Therefore, this law is entirely inapplicable at present. Nagid U'Metzaveh writes the same, cited by Yad Aharon in Hagahos HaTur. See our words above in siman 157, paragraph 15, regarding the precautions a person must take in his eating and drinking; see there.
