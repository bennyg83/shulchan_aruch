(40) They are not permitted, etc. This is a Rabbinic enactment because of an incident related in Chullin 94a. A person once invited three guests during years of famine and had only about three eggs to place before them. The host’s son came, and the first guest took his portion and gave it to him; the second and third did likewise. The child’s father came and found him holding one egg in his mouth and two in his hands. He struck him to the ground and the child died. When the mother saw this, she went up to the roof, fell, and died. The father too went up to the roof, fell, and died. See there. Magen Avraham and Eliyah Rabbah write that this applies specifically to the Gemara’s case, where the meal was limited and the host would be embarrassed if the guests lacked food. If there is abundant food on the table and no shortage will result, they may give some to them. All the more so, if everyone has finished eating and food remains, it may be given to them from the leftovers. Sefer Shemen Rokeach is inclined to be stringent in all these cases.
(41) Or to his servant. Some say that the prohibition applies specifically to his servant, but one may give it to the attendant serving at the meal. Others say that it is forbidden even to give it to the attendant.
