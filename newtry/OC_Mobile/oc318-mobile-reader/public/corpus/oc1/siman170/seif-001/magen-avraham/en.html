Do not converse. Even in divrei Torah, as is stated in the Gemara. Any table at which divrei Torah were not said is as though they ate from offerings to the dead. The Shelah wrote that one should actually learn and not rely on Birkas HaMazon.
One hand that he rubbed. He must recite the blessing over netilas yadayim, as written at the end of siman 164, unlike the Agudah. However, if he wants only to drink and not to eat, no blessing is required, for because of a concern alone no blessing is required.
He does not wash at all. The Levush wrote that this applies specifically for eating, but for drinking he must wash, lest people suspect that he rubbed and did not wash. This does not seem correct, for if so, even if he went outside and did not urinate he should need to wash because of suspicion. Rather, this is necessarily what it means: perhaps there is someone who saw him rub and did not see that he washed, and will suspect him; but where he did not rub, there is no concern for this. So it also appears from the Bach. In some versions the gloss was written below as referring to eating, but this is not so. The Agudah also wrote that it applies even to drinking.
And he distanced himself. Nevertheless, he need not recite HaMotzi, as the Rama wrote in siman 178.
He washes both his hands. For drinking, he washes in their presence, as above. So it is in the Gemara, the Tur, and the Rambam, and I do not know why the Beit Yosef reversed their language. Perhaps he holds that when one has distanced himself, he always washes in their presence, for there is no concern of delicacy of mind. So the Terumas HaDeshen wrote.
Lest he eat. Nevertheless, he washes in their presence, since the primary matter is drinking, and we are concerned that they will say he did not set his heart to wash his hands. Shelah. See what I wrote in siman 212.
