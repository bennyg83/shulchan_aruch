(1) One does not converse during a meal - Even in words of Torah, and specifically during the actual time of eating, because of danger, as explained immediately below. But between one dish and another it is permitted, and it is a mitzvah for every person to learn Torah at his table, for any table at which words of Torah were not said is as though they ate from sacrifices to the dead. Shelah wrote that one should learn Mishnah, halachah, aggadah, or books of mussar; he does not fulfill this by reciting Birkat Hamazon. At least he should say some psalm, and it is good to say after the blessing of Hamotzi the psalm "Hashem is my shepherd, I shall not lack," for it is words of Torah and a prayer for his sustenance.
(2) Lest it precede, etc. - For when the voice goes out, that cap over the windpipe opens, food enters it, and he is endangered, as Rashi explains.
(3) He washes one hand - He must bless al netilat yadayim if he wishes to eat, as Magen Avraham writes. See what we wrote above at the end of siman 164 in Mishna Berurah about this.
(4) With which he rubbed - If he rubbed with both hands, he must wash both. It is stated in the Gemara that it is a mitzvah to rub away drops of urine if they splashed on his feet, so that he should not appear as one whose member is severed, as above in siman 3.
(5) He does not wash at all - Whether for eating or for drinking.
(6) He certainly requires washing - Regarding the blessing al netilat yadayim, some Acharonim wrote not to bless even in this case; but above in siman 164 we decided like the view of Chayei Adam that in this case he must bless, see there.
(7) And this applies to drinking - Meaning, he returns home only to drink, for even for this he needs washing, lest he come to eat, as just below in the gloss.
(8) And if he spoke, etc. - This refers to where he went outside.
(9) He washes both hands - Since he delayed an hour or two, he diverted his mind from his meal and was not careful to guard his hands, and they are active, as Rashi explains. Birkei Yosef wrote in the name of the book Tzeror HaChayim that when he holds bread in his hand while speaking, he does not need washing, for his mind is on it and he will not divert his attention.
(10) Since he diverted his mind - This refers both to eating and to drinking, since he delayed. Nevertheless, he does not need to bless Hamotzi again, even if he certainly touched an unclean place, as Magen Avraham writes.
(11) But otherwise he does not need, etc. - As the Mechaber ruled above in siman 158 seif 6.
