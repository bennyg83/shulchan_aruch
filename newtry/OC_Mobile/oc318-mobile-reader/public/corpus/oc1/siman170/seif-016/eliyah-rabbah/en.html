[Levush] Between what remains and to his fellow, etc. The later authorities write in the name of Maharshal that the prohibition is specifically to hand the cup directly to another person. It is permitted to place it before him on the table, because then the other person need not drink from it if it disgusts him. I found the same in the Perishah. Lechem Chamudot, folio 224, writes that it is unreasonable to say the concern is that the second person will drink and thereby endanger his life. Rather, if he is fastidious he may refuse to drink at all and die of thirst. Even according to this, he may agree that placing the cup before him is permitted, because then he can drink other water. When it is handed directly into his hand he may be embarrassed to choose other water. This seems clear to me. The Taz writes that the testament of Rabbi Eliezer HaGadol strongly warns against drinking from a cup from which another person drank, because perhaps the first person has an illness and harmful breath passed from his mouth into the remainder. The Taz concludes that in other countries people are very careful about this.
[Levush] Who is not fastidious, etc. The first chapter of Tamid states that even for a fastidious person wiping the rim where the mouth touched, or pouring out some of the water, is effective. Lechem Chamudot rules accordingly. This creates some difficulty for the Taz's citation from Rabbi Eliezer HaGadol above. Perhaps Rabbi Eliezer HaGadol speaks where the next person is a stranger whose condition is unknown, so there is greater concern.
