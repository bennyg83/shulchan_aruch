(37) And give it to another person, etc. Perhaps, out of embarrassment, the other person will accept it and drink against his will, although he may find it repulsive to drink what this person left, since saliva may have become mixed into it and he could become ill as a result. Accordingly, this applies specifically when he gives it into the other person’s hand. If he places it before him and the other person takes it on his own, there is no concern. The Taz writes: I saw in the ethical will of Rabbi Eliezer HaGadol that he strongly warns against drinking what another person has left, lest that person has an illness within his body and his breath entered the remainder. See there. See below, seif 22, where wiping the place touched by the mouth is effective, or, with water, pouring out a small amount through the place from which he drank. This appears to contradict Rabbi Eliezer HaGadol. Perhaps Rabbi Eliezer HaGadol discusses a case where the first person who drank is someone whose health is unknown, in which case there is greater reason for concern. Eliyah Rabbah. See Peri Megadim.
