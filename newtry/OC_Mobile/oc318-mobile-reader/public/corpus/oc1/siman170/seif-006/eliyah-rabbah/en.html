[6] [Levush] And he may choke, etc. Malbushei Yom Tov writes: In my opinion, it is prohibited even without this concern, as Rama writes in Choshen Mishpat, siman 176 and siman 72. This is somewhat difficult, for if so, it should be prohibited even with three people, since Rama’s wording is that when members of a group eat together and one eats a great deal, the others may remove him. The Levush’s reason is also strained, and Magen Avraham likewise writes that “drinking” is not meant specifically. It appears that the Levush derived his view from Beit Yosef, who writes: “It appears to me that the meaning is that when one person drinks, the other waits for him,” etc. The Levush understood that the rule applies specifically when he drinks, because of danger. But he overlooked the words of Rabbeinu Yonah in the chapter Sheloshah SheAchlu: “While one person drinks, cuts bread, or waits to receive a piece from the one who recited HaMotzi, his fellow should wait for him because of proper conduct. Here the teaching concerns only proper conduct. It does not address the requirement to leave him his share when he interrupts for other matters and delays for a long time.” Beit Yosef too requires explanation for writing, “It appears to me,” without mentioning Rabbeinu Yonah’s words. Tzeidah LaDerech, page 59, writes that two people wait for one another, provided that the delay is not prolonged and that he does not interrupt with idle conversation.
