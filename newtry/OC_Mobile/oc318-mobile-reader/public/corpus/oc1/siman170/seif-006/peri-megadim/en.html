The elder. See Taz. Bach asks from siman 167, seif 14, where the elder breaks the bread, and seif 17, where the one who breaks bread reaches out first. If so, this is a matter of law, not merely proper conduct. One might answer that the elder honored the younger person to break the bread, or that the host breaks it. If so, however, why is the younger person called a glutton? There it is stated that one may honor the elder to reach out first, so he should not be considered a glutton. Bach answers that each person has his own bowl. He then asks from siman 167, seif 15, that one need not wait for the one who breaks bread. It may be answered that there the concern is an interruption, unlike here. Bach therefore concludes that the case is where the elder gave the younger person permission to break the bread, yet it is still improper for him also to reach out first, for that is gluttonous. Receiving permission gives him one distinction, namely to break the bread, but not to reach out first. Magen Avraham, seif katan 6, explains like Bach, and therefore in siman 167, seif katan 37, he explains that the elder gave him permission to break the bread. See there. Taz explains that case as referring to the host. Taz’s words may also be explained differently. See Perishah. There is no need to elaborate.
