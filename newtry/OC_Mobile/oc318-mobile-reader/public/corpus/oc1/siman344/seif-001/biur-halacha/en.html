(א) One who travels in a desert, etc. — and likewise if he forgot which month he is in or the count of days of the month in Nisan, Sivan, Tishrei, and it could occur in days when the moon is not visible — likewise impossible to know and recognize the festival days; he must conduct with doubt in sanctity until he knows the festival days have passed [Peri Megadim in Mishbetzot]:

(ב) And then he does melacha, etc. — Tosefta wrote: if he can fast one day without danger it is forbidden to do melacha that day, for there is no pikuach nefesh; see in Bigdei Yesha who disagrees and holds one need not afflict himself with fasting to hurry leaving the desert — he expanded there:

(ג) Limited — apparently why did they not permit him to profit one or two days much more than his livelihood so he could rest four or five days from melacha whereby he might observe Shabbat properly, unlike now doing each day and certainly desecrating Shabbat with Torah melacha; one can say now when he works for his limited livelihood it is not called desecration of Shabbat, for each day pikuach nefesh touches him — therefore this is preferable to profiting extra days and desecrating Shabbat willingly in doubt; we can learn for a soldier whom the government requires to do some Torah melacha — he may do it on erev Shabbat and certainly need not desecrate tomorrow Shabbat — as we explained in Sha'ar HaTziyon; but if he had no leisure all day until twilight — better to do melacha tomorrow though Shabbat, for then he is compelled and has no prohibition, unlike now desecrating willingly in doubt; one can reject: our case is two days unlike one day here; what difference beginning of Shabbat entry versus middle — better to precede, perhaps he will be saved from desecration — it requires study:

(ד) Even on the day he sanctifies — regarding tefillin in my humble opinion he must don them, for most days are weekdays and we follow the majority; and what he does for kiddush is only commemoration as Rashi explained; and what is forbidden in melacha each day — perhaps because the Sages made it like fixed even if we say this Shabbat day is fully fixed d'oraisa and is like half-and-half — nevertheless from Torah doubt we do not emerge; and even per Magen Avraham that the seventh day is forbidden even in shevut like second day Yom Tov — that is only stringency not leniency; and on second day Yom Tov one is also exempt from tefillin — there it differs because the enactment was for all Israel lest they come to Yom Tov violation and they made it like full Yom Tov [except corpse matters permitted] lest they treat lightly — and the Sages have power to uproot Torah in sit and do not do; know second day Yom Tov was enacted even for us expert in lunar fixation, unlike here where he forgot Shabbat — the Sages did not make a full enactment for him to uproot Torah mitzvot applicable on weekdays; further there is reason to obligate him: Shabbat exemption is because they are a sign — one doing melacha for livelihood lacks that sign [it is not reasonable that kiddush alone is the sign, for the essence of the sign is Hashem resting from His melacha on the seventh day and also ceasing]; and one can answer: if so every dangerously ill person permitted to desecrate Shabbat should be obligated in tefillin — therefore because he ceases other melachot not useful for healing, the name of sign applies; likewise in our case only melacha needed to earn livelihood lest he die in desert from hunger was permitted — and even that in limited measure is not the sign and recognition that every day he is permitted all melachot and this day forbidden, unlike here where the seventh equals all days where no melacha is permitted; also perhaps the sign is that all Israel rest that day unlike here — but this reason is not so clear; nevertheless for the first reason he is obligated; but at Shabbat prayer time do not don tefillin lest there be two contradictions;
