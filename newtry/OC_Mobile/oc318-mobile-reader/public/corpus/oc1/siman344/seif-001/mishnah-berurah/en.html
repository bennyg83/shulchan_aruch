(א) One who travels in a desert — and likewise if captured among non-Jews and forgot Shabbat [Yerushalmi]; but sometimes in this he can clarify from their calculations which day is our Shabbat:
(ב) From the day it entered, etc. — meaning from the day doubt fell in his heart when Shabbat is — and that day itself is day one for counting six weekdays:
(ג) With kiddush and havdalah — he makes kiddush on bread and wine as required, and the Sages enacted this as a remembrance lest Torah of Shabbat be forgotten; if he has no wine he sanctifies through prayer, for he prays the Shabbat prayer that day, as Peri Megadim wrote — he fulfills d'oraisa even if that day is truly Shabbat, as explained above siman 271 in Mishna Berurah — see there; and he also makes havdalah in prayer saying "You have favored us" — he fulfills havdalah by stretch:
(ד) And if he has, etc. — even if he has only plain bread — again forbidden to do melacha with caravan members to earn a cooked dish, for there is no pikuach nefesh:
(ה) From what to support himself — from his money or selling an item or obtaining on credit from caravan until he reaches settlement, or as a gift from them:
(ו) Forbidden for him — even on all six days, for each day is doubt of Shabbat; and although most days are weekdays — this is called established, for Shabbat is recognizable to him:
(ז) Limited livelihood — each day applies: he may not earn from melacha more; even on his sixth day to prepare for his seventh he may not do melacha, lest today be Shabbat and tomorrow weekday — how will he desecrate what is not pikuach nefesh need; but for livelihood it is permitted lest he die in the desert from hunger; b'dieved if he profited one day more than his livelihood — forbidden to do melacha tomorrow until his supply is finished [Tosefta]:
(ח) And permitted to walk — even many parsaot, for if he remains in the desert forever he will desecrate Shabbat and die in drought:
(ט) Even on the day he sanctifies — in order to hurry to leave the desert; but other prohibitions, even only rabbinic shevut, are forbidden on his seventh day [Magen Avraham]; and Avnei Nezer is lenient on shevut even on his seventh day, since it is only rabbinic doubt:
