[6] (26) On Pesach. This refers to where he found chametz in his house during Pesach, and the same applies on the eve of Pesach from the sixth hour and onward. It teaches that just as it is forbidden to feed it to his animal, which is considered benefit, so too it is forbidden to feed it to an ownerless animal. The later authorities wrote that the same applies to chametz that remained over Pesach.
[6] (27) Even to others' animals. Meaning those of a non-Jew [Gra].
[6] (28) That is ownerless. For he has benefit by fulfilling his will to satiate the animal. See Beis Yosef, that even when one finds chametz that is not his, it is also forbidden to throw it before a dog.
