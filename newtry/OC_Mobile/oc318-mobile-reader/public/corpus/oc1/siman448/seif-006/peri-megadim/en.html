[6] "And not." See Taz. Mas'as Binyamin in siman 43 wrote at length, and Magen Avraham brought him in note 5, that the reason is that he did not make it ownerless with everyone in mind. It is forbidden to sue the non-Jew if he does not want to return it, but one may sue the Jew who bought it, since one can sue a Jew who bought unlawfully. It appears that one should sell the room and the chametz for a large sum, much more than its value, and have it stand as a loan; under their laws there is no concern for voiding the sale because of overreaching. See Choshen Mishpat 227:26. If so, no person will give that sum. However, there is no proof from se'if 3, where one sells for a small amount, because there the owners make it ownerless and there is no concern for voiding the sale; see Chok Yaakov, Taz, and Terumas HaDeshen 120. Nevertheless, here too one can say the reverse. And if it is only a little more, less than the measure of overreaching, it is certainly permitted ab initio, as stated, and no person will increase the price. With Hashem's help this will be explained further in Magen Avraham.
