[6] [Levush:] And non-Jews, etc. Even his servant (Bach and Taz), excluding his Canaanite slave or maidservant, whose hand is like the hand of the master (Sha'ar Ephraim). Likewise, one should not sell to an apostate, for even though he sinned, he is a Jew. There was an incident in which they sold him liquor, and the amount was large, and it was difficult to lose the liquor, and it was likely that the Jew would not obey in this matter. I said that the apostate should exchange it with another non-Jew and take other liquor from him, and then it is permitted to the Jew, or he should sell it and the money is permitted to the Jew, for chametz does not render its proceeds forbidden (Taz). See the end of siman 463. In the responsum printed at the end of Sha'ar Ephraim I wrote at length to express wonder at Responsa Sha'ar Ephraim, siman 4, which wrote to permit the liquor itself to others and compares it to the law of exchange. This is incorrect, for there it speaks about the money, but the chametz itself is certainly forbidden. I brought proof from Hagahos Maimoniyos cited in Beis Yosef at the end of siman 447, who wrote that the non-Jew should be warned not to go back and sell it to another Jew; see there. I alluded to this briefly in my book Eliyah Zuta. I saw that the glosses to Sefer Sha'ar Ephraim there conceded to my words, but wrote that perhaps one can reject his proof from the Hagahos Maimoniyos mentioned above, for the concern was lest the other Jew sell it back to that same Jew. This, in my poor opinion, is a crooked path, this distant concern. Moreover, why did he warn the non-Jew? He should have warned that the other Jew should not sell it to that Jew. Furthermore, if so, what proof does he bring from the ship that sank in the sea? There the concern is during Pesach, when it is forbidden to every Jew, as stated in Pesachim 40. Know this also from the fact that if so, what does the Talmud ask from a garment in which kilayim was lost? That is forbidden to every Jew. Examine this carefully. Chok Yaakov wrote: one should prevent buying liquor or beer from them immediately after Pesach, for it is chametz that remained over Pesach.
