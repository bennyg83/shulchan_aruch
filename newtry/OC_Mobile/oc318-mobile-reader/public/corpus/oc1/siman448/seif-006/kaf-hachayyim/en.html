6) There. It is permitted even for consumption. The Mishnah states in Pesachim 28a that chametz belonging to a non-Jew that remained over Pesach is permitted for benefit. This does not mean that consumption is prohibited. Rather, because the Mishnah wished to state that chametz belonging to a Jew is prohibited for benefit, it used the corresponding formulation that chametz belonging to a non-Jew is permitted for benefit. Rif and Rosh. In the name of the Yerushalmi, Rosh writes that the Mishnah's formulation applies in a place where people do not customarily eat bread baked by a non-Jew. In a place where they do customarily eat it, the chametz is permitted even for consumption. Beis Yosef cites his words. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 2. See Yoreh De'ah, siman 112, se'if 2, regarding which bread baked by a non-Jew is customarily treated leniently.
