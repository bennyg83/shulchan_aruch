(א) with pleasure. In the words of Demento, he went on and went on to “The Hashem’s Prayer”:

(ב) Outside. The way of the matter is stated — likewise even a non-Jew residing in the courtyard, even if he is his servant (Taz). This is when he has no Canaanite slave (Kaf HaChayyim); and likewise in responsum Sha'agat Aryeh siman 3 that one may not sell chametz to a maidservant acquired by a Jew. Bach wrote: in a place of trouble one may permit selling to a non-Jew all chametz in the room, even the room itself (see Choshen Mishpat siman 196 and R' Yehuda); and he must tell him even though I did not write you a deed for this sale I transfer it to you with money alone; and he must give him the key. With this, b'dieved there is no prohibition since by their law the buyer can hold it even without a key (Kaf HaChayyim). Also beware not to leave any seal on the room or on the chametz so the non-Jew cannot enter there.

(ג) And give it to him. If the gentile does not want to return the chametz after Pesach — forbidden to sue in their courts; forbidden for a Jew to buy from him after Pesach, and if he bought must resell to original owners (Mishna Berurah siman 43; see Magen Avraham; see Yad Aharon). Reuven and a gentile were partners and both sold chametz together to Shimon before Pesach and received money but Shimon had not yet pulled chametz from partners' domain and Passover passed — Shimon must burn it (Bnei Yaakov siman 3; see Netivot Shalom 163; Kaf HaChayyim s.k. 14; Pri Chadash).

(ד) Finished. One may give through his brother or wife; he need not tell his wife to return later. Incident: one had chametz and wanted to sell to a gentile; he schemed not to mention his chametz but left it in a friend's room knowing the friend would sell that room with chametz inside to the gentile and gave the key; the friend sold the room and the hidden chametz was sold with it without the selling Jew or buying gentile knowing — forbidden (Chinuch Beit Yehuda siman 13).

(ה) Sale. It is permitted to tell him: Here is this chametz for you, and give me chametz after Pesach in return — so wrote the Gaon Maharash (end of his words). Forbidden to sell his chametz to an apostate, for though he sinned he is a Jew. If he sold, Taz wrote b'dieved one may tell the apostate to exchange it with another non-Jew or sell to a non-Jew, and the money is permitted to the Jew. There was an incident with one who held a deposit in a village — he sold his deposit to an apostate for a large sum and it was hard to lose the deposit and it was close that the Jew would not comply; I said he should exchange the deposit with another non-Jew and take another deposit from him, and then it is permitted to this Jew (Taz; see responsum Kaf HaChayyim siman 41). And for this reason one should prevent not buying on credit rent or deposit from a non-Jew immediately after Pesach.

(ו) On condition. Because of the stringency of chametz (Magen Avraham, Kaf HaChayyim).
