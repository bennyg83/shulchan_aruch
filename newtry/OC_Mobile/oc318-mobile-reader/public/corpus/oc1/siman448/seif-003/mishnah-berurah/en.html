(א) Forbidden in benefit — for the Sages penalized him since he violated bal yera'eh and bal yimatzei (Gemara).

(ב) (h) It was assumed that he had no knowledge of the same as:

(ג) Or under duress — meaning he knew but was under duress and could not burn it; and even though in this he did not violate, nevertheless we penalize the permitted because of the forbidden lest he leave it l'chatchila and violate — see Biur Halacha. Beit Hillel wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 332: I was asked about a Jew who before Pesach had grain in a non-Jew's mill, and the grain remained until chol hamoed; on the seventh day the non-Jew ground it, made bread, baked, and brought the bread immediately after Pesach — I answered it is permitted to take the money from the non-Jew, and that non-Jew may eat or sell to a non-Jew (end of his words); and Chak Yakov agreed.

(ד) (b) And if they sold and so on, and in which buyers bought a buyer, we wrote to anemia at the end of the article:

(ה) To a gentile — and to a Jewish apostate forbidden to sell, for his law is like a Jew in every matter, and he further violates lifnei iver; the chametz is forbidden after Pesach in benefit. B'dieved if he sold to a Jewish apostate for a small amount because he knows him and knows he will return after Pesach — since he erred thinking an apostate's law is like a gentile — one may permit in great loss: tell the apostate to exchange the chametz with a gentile for other chametz or sell it, and the exchange or money is permitted, as explained end of siman 443.

(ו) Outside the house — Acharonim wrote there is no concern whether the non-Jew himself lives in the Jew's house or not; the main concern is that the non-Jew take the chametz out of the Jew's house; but the non-Jew himself, even if he is the Jew's servant and lives in his house, we are not concerned (unless he is acquired forever, for then his hand is like his master's hand and selling does not help to remove from domain). What they required here to remove from domain even though already sold and it is a mere deposit without liability — generally we do not require this except a mere partition, as above siman 440; Kaf HaChayyim wrote the law is likewise here and they did not require removal except so the non-Jew acquires by pulling.

(ז) And he will return and give it to him — if the gentile does not want to return it, forbidden to sue him in their courts or compel him otherwise; if he gave only partial payment and the rest is a loan he refuses to pay — certainly permitted to sue in their law for payment or chametz as payment of debt. Acharonim wrote: another Jew may not buy the chametz from the gentile after Pesach; if he bought he must return to original owners; if he overpaid the gentile improperly, he need return only proper amount.

(ח) Provided he gives, etc. — even through his agent or wife he can transfer chametz to a non-Jew, but not through a non-Jew, for there is no agency for a non-Jew and his sale is nothing. Likewise he cannot transfer to a non-Jew's agent, for the Torah detailed agency only for Jews; even if the agent is also a non-Jew — no agency for a non-Jew, whether from non-Jew to Jew or non-Jew to non-Jew (see Biur Halacha what some poskim wrote).

(ט) (To) a complete gift – and a gift is by a eyebrow or attraction which cannot be fulfilled:

(י) Without any condition — lest the non-Jew violate the condition and the gift be void retroactively and he violate bal yera'eh, as below seif 4.

(כ) Or that he sell — and by which acquisitions does a non-Jew acquire? There are opinions among poskim: some say his acquisition is specifically by money; some say a non-Jew acquires only by pulling to his domain or lifting something liftable; some wrote further that other acquisitions apply to a non-Jew, such as acquisition of movable property through land, acquisition of courtyard, and kinyan chalipin (sudar) — but one must be careful that the sudar belong to the non-Jew and not suffice to give their sudar at acquisition like with Jews, for that is only from the reason of zechiah which we do not apply to a non-Jew; some doubt these acquisitions and say we find them only in.

(ל) Absolute sale — if a Jew leaves his chametz in a room of a friend who sells his chametz, he must inform the selling Jew and make him an agent to sell; if he left without knowledge, forbidden after Pesach since the Jew did not know to transfer and the non-Jew did not know to acquire (Acharonim). Further, one may tell a non-Jew: Here is chametz for you, and give me other chametz after Pesach.

(מ) For a small amount — one need not fear void sale, for he knows himself it is worth more and agrees to transfer so it not remain in his domain forbidden in benefit. Better he sell to his buyers, for then presumably the gentile will return after Pesach (see Biur Halacha on our custom today). Acharonim wrote: though l'chatchila one should sell with money and pulling, if chametz is abundant and impossible to pull all, or chametz is elsewhere the gentile cannot pull — he may transfer by other acquisitions such as marking practiced in some places, hand-clap (tschlak), perutah where custom completes sale, or key transfer where custom (but at least say I sell you such-and-such chametz for such sum, not merely I sell the key). Or sell through agav — sell or rent land/room and say acquire my chametz there; agav acquires even not piled; land acquired with money and deed, rental with money alone; partial gold (auf gab) suffices with loan for rest. If house not his but rented, sale does not help — rent room stam for vessels without saying for chametz (see siman 449). Jew bought from gentile, paid, did not pull — l'chatchila sell before Pesach; if not sold and Passover passed, permitted per majority who need pulling; if pulled without money, some forbid; if local custom buys with money, Pri Megadim leans stringent (see Shaarei Teshuvah s.k. 9).

(נ) In order to return, we were after Passover:

(ס) Does not work — even though throughout the Torah a gift on condition to return is a gift if the condition is fulfilled, here the Sages required an absolute gift because of chametz stringency. B'dieved if the condition was fulfilled that he returned — Acharonim dispute; see Biur Halacha. Where he said: I give you and you return it to me — see Biur Halacha.
