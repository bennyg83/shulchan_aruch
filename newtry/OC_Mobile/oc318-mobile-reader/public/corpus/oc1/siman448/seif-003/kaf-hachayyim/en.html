3) There. Chametz belonging to a non-Jew, etc. If a Jew shares ownership of chametz with a non-Jew and it remained over Pesach, the Jew's half is prohibited for benefit. Nevertheless, when they divide it after Pesach, the non-Jew's share is permitted for benefit. Since chametz that remained over Pesach is prohibited only rabbinically as a penalty, we apply retroactive designation. Whether it is permitted for consumption requires further consideration. Chukas HaPesach, subsection 1, beginning with the words 'It appears,' however, writes that it is permitted even for consumption. See there. Shulchan Aruch HaRav writes likewise. The Jew's share is certainly prohibited for benefit. Chok Yaakov, subsection 2; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1; Yad Aharon in Hagahos HaTur; Mekor Chaim, subsection 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 27; Chayei Adam, there, subsection 25. Mekor Chaim there considers whether the non-Jew's share is permitted only when it was kept in the non-Jew's house, and concludes that it is permitted even when kept in the Jew's house. See there. Responsa Sha'agas Aryeh, siman 90, however, permits even the Jew's share. Machazik Berachah, subsection 1, and Sha'arei Teshuvah, subsection 1, cite this ruling. Although Mekor Chaim in the explanations there challenges it, Responsa Maharam of Rothenburg, Later Responsa, siman 9, reconciles the position of Sha'agas Aryeh. See there. Responsa Zera Emes, volume 3, siman 46, also permits benefit even from the Jew's share where a very substantial loss is involved. See there.
