Every hour and in the Ritba and in the Maharam Halava where they all turned from the issue of chametz to the prohibition of eating alma and there are already some of the latter who commented on the above sayings of the Ramban, who is in Gezal Akoum chametz of Israel and on whom the Passover passed from the side of Ar Lathir and copied in the Peri Megadim and so on from Ri'at Algazi:

(ב) He had passed on to him, and he was given to him that he would be blessed with him

(ג) Even if he left by mistake or under duress — see Mishna Berurah. Mechaber wrote anonymously and did not explain whether when through mistake or duress he did not burn and also did not nullify — therefore forbidden; but if he nullified though he did not check because of duress, permitted since at least the essence of the law d'oraisa was fulfilled; or even if he nullified and left without burning because of duress, also forbidden — Acharonim dispute; see below seif 5 regarding nullification — for stringent view there, certainly here where he did not check at all though under duress; but per lenient view there, one can say likewise here even without checking, since under duress it is no worse than checked, nullified, and found after Pesach; so in Torat HaShelamim.

(ד) And if he sold — see Mishna Berurah that if he left a seal on the chametz at sale time, even b'dieved forbidden; see Pri Megadim who leans this is specifically in a small sale — selling all chametz for a small amount — for then it is void sale, and leniency is only because truly the chametz is not in his domain since Torah forbade benefit and Scripture placed it in his domain for bal yera'eh; therefore whoever reveals he does not want them and is pleased they leave his domain, even for a small amount suffices; and therefore whoever leaves a seal beforehand appears not to want the sale — unlike our sales where we make a proper deed for each measure for such-and-such sum and add extra money as a loan and the chametz is his.

(ה) Provided he gives — see Mishna Berurah there is no agency for a gentile, even gentile to gentile — from Magen Avraham and Sha'agat Aryeh, unlike Mishna Berurah and Shach Choshen Mishpat siman 243; see Mateh Yehuda who expanded — when chametz was already brought to the gentile sender's house, certainly the sale stands since with the seller's knowledge it was brought to the buyer gentile's house and the gentile knowingly received in his house, and the agent's act does not detract, for he is like a monkey (see there); he concludes even if the agent were a Jew, all agree we say no agency for a gentile — nevertheless it helps for the reason stated; and it is obvious his words apply only if he brought chametz to the buyer's house — but per our custom we transfer.

(ו) Absolute sale — Rav Bnei Yeshua siman 2 letter 5 wrote: since selling chametz to a gentile must be absolute without evasion, it appears after Pesach when the gentile returns chametz to the Jew he must acquire by one of the acquisitions and not merely receive back — as poskim wrote regarding gift on condition to return siman 258; it appears after Pesach it suffices to acquire chametz and rooms from the gentile with money alone and the gentile returns the sale deed — for though a Jew does not acquire land from a gentile with money alone without a deed, there he does not rely — unlike here where he returns the deed (end of his words).

(ז) For a small amount — see Mishna Berurah; today all Israel's custom is not to sell for a small amount but for a price close to chametz value, and they take from the gentile some gold (called auf gab) as initial payment and add the rest as a loan, as explained in Choshen Mishpat siman 190 — this works. Acharonim wrote: beware not to raise the price much above value in a large sum lest it be void sale; Pri Megadim implies therefore b'dieved it does not block since by their law he cannot retract; and implies if that place's custom is to retract for void sale it blocks — requires study. Further wrote it does not work when he says: I sell you this house or my chametz.
