(There seif 3) Jew's chametz over which Passover passed forbidden in benefit, etc. I copy several notes and legal novelties with Hashem's help. [1] I was asked: one owed a woman money and refused to pay; when she pressed him to pay what she was owed because she needed to marry, he pitied her and gave her chametz as collateral for the debt; a third party held the chametz on condition: if the match concludes as she said the third party gives her the chametz; if not, returns to the borrower; now the match did not conclude — the borrower wants his chametz and the woman withholds since he truly owes her — what is her claim to his condition; meanwhile Pesach arrived and the woman wants to sell the chametz to a gentile per our custom — she or the third party would sell and the chametz owner protests they should not sell, saying the chametz belongs to him; I was asked what to do. I answered briefly: clearly the woman must sell it and her sale is valid as law; although Taz siman 443 implies on the borrower falls to burn — that is only collateral at loan time; collateral not at loan time — explicit in Talmud: creditor acquires collateral and can sanctify wife and make all acquisitions with it — likewise must sell; if sold, sale certainly helps; if collateral were unconditional, sale would help even if borrower protested; now although collateral was conditional, nevertheless sale by creditor and third party helps — his condition then ineffective since it was truly hers — she may seize from borrower by force — clearly her sale helps — main law: creditor acquires collateral as Shach Choshen Mishpat 72; if his condition helps — since they must return and when unwilling intent is to steal — like misappropriating deposit — robber; we rule robber who sells — buyer acquires through despair and change of domain; most poskim no difference whether despair before or after change of domain — here intent to steal; when prohibition time arrives they despair — despair and change of domain — sale to gentile helps; I later found Mekor Chayyim wrote thus if robbed chametz and sold to gentile it helps (see there) — likewise here; all this l'chatchila; b'dieved if borrower later protests he did not sell and contests woman's sale as improper — certainly not believed to cause loss to woman; if woman's sale was proper, permitted through her sale; if as borrower claims improper — borrower violated bal yera'eh and must sell as Taz 443 — presumably he acted properly and sold; not that sale ineffective because borrower robbed and robber and owners not despaired cannot dedicate — here different: not intent to steal outright only as collateral — borrower may sell collateral as Taz 443 (see my innovations there); and either way: if judged as robbery — robber's sale helps through despair and change; if not judged as robbery — borrower may still sell as Taz implies; since borrower had to sell — we testify he presumably sold before violating bal yera'eh; even if now says he did not sell — not believed to harm creditor — one does not make himself wicked nor believed to harm others as we rule regarding coercion; further: Beit Shmuel Even HaEzer 28: even in robbery owners not despaired cannot dedicate — but with both parties' consent dedication works — likewise in sale: if one will not agree to other's sale, selling alone would violate bal yera'eh — we testify each agrees in heart to other's sale lest he violate bal yera'eh — as each sold with other's consent; although initially protesting, we testify he later agreed in heart; also may say at moment of protest he consented — per Rashba's innovations to Kiddushin 23 brought in Avnei Milu'im 1: where complete benefit exists, even standing and protesting we disregard protest (see there) — here saving from Torah prohibition on owner is complete benefit — even screaming he does not want to sell we disregard — we testify he is pleased — certainly this chametz is permitted; borrower not believed to make himself wicked to lose chametz in creditor's hand; correct in my view. [2] Asked in our time: law of the kingdom to pay the king tax on beer thus and so — but if beer is transported via commissioner outside the country the king returns the tax; on Pesach the Jew forgot to sell his beer in the manner the law obligates burning and pouring — may he keep it to transport outside so the king returns tax paid for the beer? I answered: forbidden; although not considered benefiting from chametz since he does not take money for chametz but takes his own — tax applies only if sold in this country; if clarified sold abroad retroactively no tax was due and he takes his own — nevertheless forbidden for another reason: he wants its existence until transported abroad; explicit proof Avodah Zarah 63: hired to break beer barrel for him — do we say since he wants its existence it is forbidden? Rashi: this Israelite wants the barrels' existence not to break now until he breaks them for wages — forbidden; or perhaps any reduction of prayer is fine; gemara concludes any reduction of prayer is fine; we rule Yoreh De'ah 133 permitted from reduction of prayer — implying if not reduction of prayer it is wanting existence until he breaks them — fits there: if not broken no reduction of prayer; here ultimately he will pour — not reduction of prayer — certainly forbidden wanting existence until transported — clear and correct he must pour immediately. [3] Asked by rabbi of Kazin: one did not sell wheat before Pesach, sprouts were visible and impossible to sort — what is their law? I answered: I already showed him Levush in Taz siman 432: even when prohibition visible it is nullified d'oraisa, only rabbinically not nullified — Taz wondered greatly; he did not see Ra'avad in Kilayim explains likewise, and Tosefta wondered on him as Taz on Levush — nevertheless we have two pillars of the world who hold even visible nullified; I spoke much elsewhere. Further dispute Rambam/Rashba in Beit Yosef Yoreh De'ah 115 regarding gentile vinegar — Rambam: visible only when impossible to sort not nullified; Rashba: even visible whatever cannot be sorted is nullified; Taz Yoreh De'ah 104 expounded (see there); per Rashba here cannot sort — nullified; for chametz over which Passover passed all these sides lean to leniency nullified in majority; if possible better sell to gentile — permitted in benefit not eating; if impossible permitted sell even to Israel; during Pesach do not rely on this leniency — sort what possible, cast leaven to sea or cast all to sea — correct, no more expansion. [4] Chametz found in room sold to gentile with chametz and they forgot some chametz not sold — does it help? Say chametz permitted since gentile acquired through courtyard-find — see omissions in Nedarim Zarizin vol. 2:97 incidentally responsum Tarnopol: chametz permitted for two reasons: they are not accustomed to such gentile arriving; also sale to gentile before prohibition time and despair only at prohibition time — comes to his hand before despair and does not acquire as Shach Choshen Mishpat 268 (see there); also unconscious despair not despair — Bava Kamma deals when he knew of him then despaired when prohibition time arrives; we wrote there condition that gentile acquire with money helps even for those who hold condition ineffective if deficiency in acquisition body but acquisition valid and deficiency only from person — condition certainly helps (see there). [5] Sale of chametz with vessels — if vessels need immersion see my Avodah Zarah 248:34 responsum Nizhniv incidentally vessels need no immersion (see there). [6] If chametz digested through one person — we wrote above letter 1 sale helps; see my responsum Yoreh De'ah Hilchot Bekhor chapter 9 edition 5 siman 199 Nizhniv also whether hodaah acquisition helps — wrote there (see there). [7] See Magen Avraham s.k. 4: 'we follow their law' community — for we say in Bekhorot, etc.; see my work on Yoreh De'ah siman 320 edition 5 siman 76 responsum Skaly resolving Magen Avraham's question correctly in several aspects (see there).
