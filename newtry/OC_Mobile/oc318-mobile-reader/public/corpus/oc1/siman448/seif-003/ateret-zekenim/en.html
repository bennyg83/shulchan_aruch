To a non-Jew outside the house, etc. — this certainly has no taste or smell, for it is worse that the chametz is in his house; and further Terumat HaDeshen did not write outside the house — implying he spoke of the chametz being outside the house, and we are not concerned about the non-Jew even if in his servant's house, for below he permits even one who recognizes him and is accustomed, etc. All this I wrote per Beit Yosef in the name of Terumat HaDeshen; but the essence of the matter appears in the Gemara daf 6 — he designated a house for the non-Jew's chametz, he need not burn — we learn even in a Jew's house it helps to designate a corner; only Terumat HaDeshen did not come to exclude designating a corner, for that is also called outside the Jew's house; and it is obvious the chametz should be placed where the gentile can enter himself — all the more not.
