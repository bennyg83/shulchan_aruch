[3] Know that a person's courtyard, if he wants it, acquires even items forbidden for benefit, as in siman 446 and Mishbetzos Zahav there, where when the Jew lifts it he acquires it. However, ordinary items forbidden for eating presumably are not made ownerless by their owners. See Maharsha, of blessed memory, Beitzah 38b, that ordinary items forbidden for eating are made ownerless by the owners. I discussed this in my Pri Megadim to Yoreh De'ah, and it requires examination. I am uncertain regarding items forbidden for benefit: are they presumptively ownerless or not? A practical difference is a convert who has a deposit by a Jew and dies before nightfall, and before the convert died the Jew said, "My courtyard should acquire it for me," and he died, and afterward the Jew retracted and said that he did not wish to acquire it before the convert died. According to the view that where he transgressed only the positive commandment of tashbisu they did not penalize him, as we will want to say in notes 6 and 7 and in Magen Avraham 3, and according to the view that they did penalize even for transgressing a positive commandment, the case can be drawn at the sixth hour. Now, if ordinary items forbidden for benefit, whether biblically or rabbinically, are ownerless, the Jew already acquired it, and his later retraction is ineffective, for it is no longer in his possession to make ownerless; he transgressed bal yera'eh, and it is forbidden after Pesach. But if they are not presumptively ownerless, the Jew never acquired it, and since he retracted before the convert died, it is not the Jew's and is permitted after Pesach. I thought to learn this from the Yerushalmi, chapter 9 of Shevi'is, halachah 5: R. Chizkiyah went up to R. Yirmiyah and said to him, "Acquire my stores for me." He said to him, "Keep these coins with you for me," and said to him, "Your place is rented to me." He said to him, "Did you not make them ownerless? You went back and acquired them." The Gaon, R. Eliyahu, of blessed memory, explained that R. Chizkiyah said that produce of shemittah at the time of bi'ur is made ownerless by the Merciful One, and he acquired it, etc.; R. Yirmiyah said that he rents his place and it acquires for him. R. Chizkiyah said: according to us, who did not make them ownerless, it is forbidden as under the law of the time of bi'ur; see there. But this can be rejected.
