"It is forbidden to give his animal to a non-Jew to feed it during the days of Pesach, etc." Note: regarding the law of selling animals on Pesach so that they will eat chametz on Pesach, Tevu'os Shor forbids this because evasion does not work in a Torah matter. I already wrote at length about this elsewhere. Now I have found an explicit proof to the words of Tevu'os Shor, that evasion does not work in a Torah matter, from Yerushalmi, Pesachim, chapter 3, halachah 3. This is its language: R. Yehudah's position is reversed; there he says it is permitted to evade, and here he says it is forbidden to evade. R. Ba said: here it is a shevus, and here it is liability for a sin-offering. See Korban HaEdah there. It is proven that in rabbinic law one may evade, but not in Torah law. The main explanation is the second explanation of Korban HaEdah, that if one bakes, it is lashes. It is proven that in a Torah prohibition evasion is forbidden, as the view of Tevu'os Shor. This is a clear proof. Although R. Eliezer permits and we rule so, that is for another reason, so that he not transgress bal te'acher, as written in the Yerushalmi. But in general he concedes to R. Yehudah that evasion is forbidden in Torah law. There is also a new reason to forbid selling the animals, according to what I wrote in a responsum to the holy community of Litnovitz, in my work on Orach Chayim from the year 5607, page 33: the non-Jew does not intend to acquire. Perforce, what makes the sale effective is that they were lenient in rabbinic law. If so, with animals, where the issue is a Torah prohibition, this sale is ineffective, because the non-Jew does not intend to acquire at all. However, I found a clear proof that evasion is effective in Torah law, unlike the words of Tevu'os Shor, from the second chapter of Avodah Zarah, 24, where it answers there: through Jewish merchants he bought, etc. This is difficult, for it is evasion. Certainly the Jewish merchant did not give many tens of thousands for it for himself, for why would he need it? Perforce, he bought it only for the public. If so, it is evasion, and there it is a Torah prohibition to buy from a non-Jew, and yet it is permitted. There is also proof from the gifts of Beis Choron, where the sages said: any gift that, if he consecrated it, it would not be consecrated, is not a gift. Tosafos wrote that specifically where it is in the manner of evasion it is not a gift, but with a complete gift, even if he cannot consecrate it, it is a gift. If so, it is proven that the gifts of Beis Choron were evasion. Nevertheless, it is only because he explicitly stipulated that "it is not before you except so that my father may come and eat with us," meaning because he said explicitly, "it is not before you except so that my father may come and eat with us." Had he not stipulated so, it would have been a gift even though it was in the manner of evasion, and even though it is Torah law. This proves against the view of Tevu'os Shor, and it requires examination. Examine this. Regarding kvass liquid that stood in beer barrels, what is the law of the kvass for Pesach?
