[7] (29) To give his animal, etc. Whether for free or for payment.
[7] (30) To feed it, etc. For he benefits from chametz on Pesach, since the non-Jew fattens his animal with it, and it is forbidden to benefit from chametz on Pesach even if it belongs to a non-Jew. After the fact, if it ate chametz on Pesach, its meat is not forbidden because of this, as is proven in Yoreh De'ah, siman 142, se'if 11.
[7] (31) During the days of Pesach. Specifically in this manner. But those who place their animals with a non-Jew long before Pesach, with the non-Jew feeding them from his own food, and they do not explicitly stipulate regarding chametz, and Pesach occurs during that time, it is permitted even if he learns that he feeds it chametz.
[7] (32) If he knows, etc. But if it is not known that he will feed it chametz, it is permitted to give it to him, and he need not be concerned that perhaps he will feed it chametz.
[7] (33) That he feeds it, etc. If he cannot do otherwise, the custom in our country is to sell the animal to the non-Jew according to law before Pesach, using the acquisitions explained in Yoreh De'ah, siman 320, and the animal should be with the non-Jew on Pesach, or he should transfer the animal to him together with the cowshed, and the non-Jew should feed it, and the Jew should have no involvement with them on Pesach, provided that he does not tell him to feed it chametz. All this is where the non-Jew has food of his own. But if he has no food and the Jew sells him, together with his animal, also its food, which is chametz, some are stringent about this because it appears like evasion, and as though the whole sale was only so that the non-Jew would feed it chametz. They wrote that he should do it in this manner: he should sell the food to another non-Jew, and it is permitted to broker for the non-Jew who buys his animals that he can buy malt from such-and-such non-Jew. The custom in our country is that even if he does not feed it actual chametz, but only wants to give his animal grain on Pesach, he also sells it to a non-Jew with the cowshed, and the non-Jew feeds it. For if he does not sell it, he will have to give it a little at a time in a dry place and stand over it while it eats, to destroy what remains so that it not become chametz from its saliva, as explained in siman 466, se'if 2. Regarding milk from an animal that eats chametz, even if it belongs to a non-Jew, the later authorities dispute this. Pri Megadim's view is to permit milk milked after a full twenty-four hours from when it ate chametz, and some are lenient even on the same day if it eats permitted foods in the morning and evening.
