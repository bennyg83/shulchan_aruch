"If a non-Jew brings a gift to a Jew, etc." Note: we learn in the Talmud at the beginning of Chullin: Rava said to him, a baraisa supports you: the chametz of transgressors is permitted immediately, because they exchange it. They assumed: who is this? It is R. Yehudah, who holds that chametz over which Pesach passed is biblically forbidden. As for the initial assumption that it is R. Yehudah, and how it did not occur to them that perhaps it is the sages, it appears to be based on what we learn in Shevi'is 5:5: from when may a person buy luf on the night after shemittah? R. Yehudah says: immediately. The sages say: from when the new crop becomes plentiful. It is proven that according to the sages, we do not attribute it to permission; even where they were not established there as transgressors, they nevertheless suspect perhaps it is from transgression. If so, here too, the presumption that one does not leave permitted food and eat forbidden food helps only to remove them from suspicion as transgressors, making them like other Jews. But according to the sages, even with an ordinary person we do not attribute to permission, only when the permitted supply has become plentiful. If so, why is it permitted there immediately? Therefore the initial assumption was that perforce there it is R. Yehudah, who holds in shemittah that it is permitted immediately. If so, here too it is permitted immediately because they exchange it. And since perforce it is R. Yehudah, automatically he holds that chametz over which Pesach passed is biblical. Afterward the Talmud rejects this: in truth, it can be the sages, and what they hold there, that it is not permitted until the new crop becomes plentiful, is with biblical shemittah; but with rabbinic law they concede that it is permitted immediately. Therefore here too, since chametz over which Pesach passed is rabbinic, it is permitted. The initial assumption found it forced to explain that Mishnah only with biblical shemittah, for it sounded to it that even in a rabbinic case the sages forbade. Therefore it had to be R. Yehudah, who holds there too that with shemittah it is permitted immediately, and it teaches without qualification, implying that even in a Torah case he holds so. The Talmud rejected this: really it is the sages, and they hold that chametz over which Pesach passed is rabbinic, so they permit immediately, while there it speaks of biblical shemittah. This is well resolved. Examine carefully. So it appears correct in my humble opinion in explaining the passage.
