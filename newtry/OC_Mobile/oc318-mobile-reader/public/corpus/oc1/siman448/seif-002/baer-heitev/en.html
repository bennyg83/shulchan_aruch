[2] Non-Jew. Beis Hillel in Yoreh De'ah, siman 132, wrote: I was asked about a Jew who, before Pesach, had grain at a non-Jew's mill to be ground, and the grain was delayed at the mill until Chol HaMoed of Pesach. When the seventh day of Pesach arrived, the non-Jew went and ground the Jew's grain, made bread from the flour, baked the bread, and brought the bread to the Jew immediately after Pesach. I answered that it is permitted to take payment for that bread from the non-Jew, and that non-Jew should eat it or sell it to a non-Jew; see there. Chok Yaakov agreed, and wrote: in siman 449 I wrote that doubtful chametz that remained over Pesach may be permitted even for eating, and if so the same applies here, for it is at least doubtful, since perhaps the non-Jew exchanged it. According to this reason, it is also possible to be lenient if a non-Jew stole a Jew's chametz and Pesach passed over it, though this requires examination. In Responsa Chinuch Beis Yehudah, siman 11, he likewise brought a similar incident, where the mill owner ground a Jew's grain on Pesach, and after Pesach he brought the flour and said that he had soaked the grain before grinding it. He concludes there that it is permitted even for eating, because we hold that chametz that remained over Pesach is only rabbinic even when it is definite chametz, and where it is doubtful chametz, we hold that a rabbinic doubt is treated leniently. Here there is a reversible double doubt: first, perhaps he did not soak it; and if you say he soaked it, there is still a doubt whether he soaked it so much that it became chametz; and if you say it became chametz, there is a doubt whether he soaked it and ground it after Pesach. See there, and see Chok Yaakov. If non-Jewish servants in a Jewish mill soaked wheat on the last Yom Tov of Pesach so that it would be ready after Yom Tov to grind, it is permitted to mix it with other wheat, grind everything, and sell it to a Jew. Maharikash in the name of Radbaz; see Pri Chadash.
[2] A gift. If a non-Jew gave or sold wheat to a Jew, and the Jew received it from him, and after Pesach it became known to him that there was chametz inside it, the chametz is permitted after Pesach like the chametz of a non-Jew. This requires examination. Chok Yaakov.
[2] The last. The last Yom Tov is mentioned as a greater novelty. (For on the other days he transgresses biblically.) If it is on the last Yom Tov, then neither covering it with a vessel nor a partition of ten handbreadths is required, because they were not concerned that he might come to eat from it in a short time. But if it is during Pesach, it requires covering with a vessel or a partition of ten handbreadths, as written above in siman 440. It appears that the same applies if a non-Jew forgot his chametz by a Jew; its law is also like the gift here. Chok Yaakov.
[2] Accept it. If he transgressed and accepted it, it is forbidden even for benefit after Pesach. Even though the last Yom Tov is rabbinic, and biblically he does not transgress bal yera'eh and bal yimatzei, nevertheless whatever the sages enacted, they enacted in the manner of biblical law, and so that the second day of Yom Tov not be treated lightly. This requires examination. Chok Yaakov.
[2] That he should say. Then, even if the non-Jew left it in the Jew's house on the first Yom Tov, it is permitted. Nevertheless, if he did not say this, by strict law it is permitted even if it was in his guarded courtyard, for his courtyard does not acquire it for him against his will, since he does not want to acquire a prohibited item. But if he disclosed that he wants it, acquisition applies to him (see 446:3). It seems to me that all this is with a gift and the like, where it is not the Jew's responsibility. But if the non-Jew brings it to the Jew's house to sell it or to deposit it with him, then the Jew must say explicitly that he does not want to accept it, so that he not become responsible for it. For even if by law he is not responsible for it, but he knows that the non-Jew is forceful and will compel him to pay, it is also forbidden, as I wrote above in siman 440. Chok Yaakov.
