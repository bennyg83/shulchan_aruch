(א) Non-Jew. Beit Yosef wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 332: I was asked about a Jew who had grain in a non-Jew's mill to grind, and the grain remained in the mill until chol hamoed of Pesach; when the seventh day of Pesach arrived the non-Jew went and ground the Jew's grain and made bread from the flour and baked the bread and brought the bread to the Jew immediately after Pesach — I answered that it is permitted to take the money for that bread from the non-Jew, and that non-Jew may eat it or sell it to a non-Jew (end of his words); and Kaf HaChayyim agreed and wrote that in siman 449 I wrote that a doubt of chametz over which Passover passed may be permitted even in eating, and if so here too it is at least a doubt lest the non-Jew exchanged. For this reason one may also be lenient if a non-Jew stole chametz outright.

(ב) Doron. If a gentile gave or sold wheat etc. to a Jew and he accepted, and after Pesach it became known there was chametz inside — l'chatchila it appears the chametz is permitted after Pesach as gentile's chametz; proof from Nedarim 35: the recipient can say I wanted leniency not prohibition — Rashi, Ran, Rosh; unlike one who takes hekdesh money unintentionally. But what another gave him.

(ג) Last. For emphasis it states last Yom Tov (on other days he violates d'oraisa). If on last Yom Tov — no need for covering vessel or ten-handbreadth partition, for they did not fear he might eat in the brief time; but during Pesach need covering or partition as siman 440. It appears likewise if a gentile forgot his chametz with a Jew — same as gift here (Kaf HaChayyim).

(ה) That he should say. Then even if the non-Jew left it in a Jew's house on the first Yom Tov it is permitted; nevertheless if he did not say so it is permitted by law even if it was in his guarded courtyard — he does not acquire for him against his will, for he does not want the prohibition to be acquired. And if he reveals his mind that he wants it, acquisition applies (see 446 seif 3). It appears to me all this is regarding a gift and the like where there is no liability on the Jew; but if the non-Jew brings to a Jew's house to sell or to deposit with him, then the Jew must explicitly say he does not want to accept it so as not to become liable for it — for even if he is not liable by law but knows the non-Jew is violent and will compel payment, it is forbidden, as written above siman 440 (Kaf HaChayyim).
