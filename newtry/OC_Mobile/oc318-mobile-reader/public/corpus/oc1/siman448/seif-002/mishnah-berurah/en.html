[2] (3) On the last day. Even though it is rabbinic, and all the more so on Pesach itself.
[2] (4) The Jew should not accept it. Because of bal yera'eh. If he accepted it, it is forbidden for benefit after Pesach, because it is Jewish-owned chametz that remained over Pesach.
[2] (5) It should not be recognizable, etc. For if it is recognizable that he wants it, such as if he says to him, "Place it in such-and-such hidden place in my courtyard," his courtyard acquires it for him. But if it is not recognizable, even though the non-Jew leaves the gift in his house, it is permitted, for presumably he does not want to acquire a prohibited item, and his courtyard does not acquire it for him against his will. Therefore, even if this occurred on the first Yom Tov, it is also permitted.
[2] (6) And it is good that he say that he does not want, etc. That is, although by strict law even if he was silent it is permitted, nevertheless, for greater clarity he should explicitly say that he does not want it, etc. In this manner, even if he shows him a place to leave it, it is permitted. He must be careful to cover it with a vessel until evening if it is Shabbos or Yom Tov, even the last Yom Tov, unless he brought it on the last Yom Tov near nightfall, because in a short time there is no concern that he might forget and eat it. If it is Chol HaMoed, he must make in front of it a partition ten handbreadths high, as explained above in siman 440.
