[5] Even though he nullified it. Even though he did not transgress biblically, nevertheless we are concerned that he will act evasively and say that he nullified it although he did not nullify it (Tur). See siman 447. The same applies to one who made his chametz ownerless; so wrote the Rosh and the poskim in the name of the Yerushalmi, unlike Olas Shabbos, who did not write so. R. Shmuel HaLevi, siman 12, wrote: one who came by ship, it would appear permissible to declare all his chametz ownerless and then reacquire it after Pesach and sell it, but not eat it, because this is an uncommon matter; yet because of the stringency of chametz, my heart did not permit me to act in practice (Kenesses HaGedolah). It is obvious that even if the chametz is not with him, he can sell the chametz to a non-Jew through one of the modes of acquisition mentioned above, and then the non-Jew acquires the chametz wherever it is. See above, siman 246, se'if 3.
