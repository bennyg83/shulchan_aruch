Taz permits even promising him that he will give him profit, while Olas Shabbos says that ab initio it is forbidden. The world is lenient, as Taz wrote. See Eliyah Rabbah 12, and this is without a condition. If one sold chametz to a non-Jew in the sixth hour, one can say that it is a sale. At first glance, this depends on the dispute whether, if one did an action in violation of the law, it takes effect; see Choshen Mishpat 207 and siman 208, and even regarding rabbinic law. See Kiddushin regarding one who sold them and betrothed with their money, and see Responsa Pnei Yehoshua, Orach Chayim, simanim 12 and 14, which I brought above, and see siman 431 in Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah in the responsa of the rabbis of Venice. Nullification does not help in the sixth hour, but a complete sale or making it ownerless in the public domain does help at least in a case like this after Pesach. However, in siman 446 in Beis Yosef it does not sound so. But regarding sale to a non-Jew, one can say that it is effective. See Taz 446:1, and this requires examination.
