[5] (25) Forbidden even though he nullified it. Or made it ownerless, and its prohibition is even for benefit. Even though when he nullified it he does not transgress bal yera'eh, nevertheless the sages were concerned that if we permit it when he nullified it, every person may leave his chametz until after Pesach and say that he made it ownerless before Pesach so that we would permit it to him. Know that several later authorities wrote that even if he also checked according to our custom and chametz was found after Pesach, it is also forbidden for benefit, for they did not distinguish in the matter. Some later authorities are lenient where he checked and nullified it and it was found afterward, for what should he have done? He did everything according to the law. Their view is that at least for benefit one should not forbid it, and in a case of great loss one may rely on them. See Biur Halacha, se'if 3, s.v. "even," and what I wrote there. One who was on a ship or on the road and had chametz with him, and had no non-Jew with whom he could sell it, and he stood and made it ownerless before witnesses: whether he may acquire it again after Pesach and benefit from it is disputed by the later authorities, and most incline to forbid. Nevertheless, in a case of great loss one may rely on the lenient view and permit him to sell it to a non-Jew or exchange it with him for another item. If he was on the road and remembered that he had chametz in his house and had no one to whom he could sell it on the eve of Pesach in the proper manner, and he stood and made the chametz in his house ownerless before witnesses, it appears that he may rely on the lenient view and acquire it again after Pesach and benefit from it. Since the chametz was not in his hand, he could not sell or destroy it according to the sages' enactment; he was therefore obligated by law to make it ownerless so that he not transgress bal yera'eh. If so, we have no reason to penalize him by forbidding his chametz when he acquires it again.
