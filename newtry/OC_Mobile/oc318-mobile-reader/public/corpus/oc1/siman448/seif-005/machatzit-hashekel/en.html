(א) (s.k. 5) Does not work, etc. Radbaz, etc. — meaning Radbaz's words can be explained two ways: either because all Pesach days the condition is pending and unclear that it belongs to the gentile, and since it is doubtful the Jew violates; or because it is in the gentile's hand not to fulfill the condition, and a matter in his hand to do or not fulfill is as if done and the gentile did not fulfill the condition and it is certainly the Jew's. At first Magen Avraham explained Radbaz's intent from the second reason; therefore he wrote: I would say unlike siman 441, even though there too one can say since it is in the Jew's hand to redeem.

(ב) And likewise regarding challah in the Gemara daf 40 and below siman 457 seif 2 — one who separates challah from impure dough on Pesach yom tov may not call it challah until baked, for if he calls it challah while still dough it is forbidden to bake, for it is not fit for any person since it is impure; if he leaves the challah so and does not bake it, it will leaven; and even though it is not the Jew's and will not reach a kohen, we say the Jew violates since if he wishes he can ask a sage about vows and annul vows and it returns to being fit — therefore even if he did not ask, he violates because it is in his power to ask, unlike there where it is in the Jew's hand to ask about his vow and it is like his own.

(ג) But here if the gentile wishes, etc. — meaning here the matter is not in the Jew's hand to fulfill or not fulfill the condition but in the gentile's hand; therefore it need not be the Jew's since the matter is not in his hand.

(ד) And what he wrote that all this when the condition is pending, etc. — now he comes to reject: do not say even without "since" he violates because the matter was doubtful all Pesach days as above.

(ו) Therefore for chametz — if he says and you return it to me after Pesach it works, for he intended a gift all Pesach days; and even stam if he said and you return it to me, we are witnesses his intent is to return specifically after Pesach. Also per Taz there who distinguishes: when he says and you return it to me it does not imply a condition but return and return from the first giving; for chametz with revealed intent it suffices — we are witnesses he did not intend return but an absolute gift, only he asks him to return afterward, as Magen Avraham wrote.
