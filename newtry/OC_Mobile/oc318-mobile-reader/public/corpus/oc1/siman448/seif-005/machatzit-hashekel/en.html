[5] Se'if katan 5: It is ineffective, etc. "And Radbaz, etc." And this is not comparable to what is written in siman 441 regarding a non-Jew, etc. This means that Radbaz's words can be explained in two ways: either because throughout Pesach the condition is pending and it is not clarified that it belongs to the non-Jew, and since the matter is doubtful, the Jew transgresses; or one can say that since it is in the non-Jew's power not to fulfill the condition, and something that is in his power to do or not fulfill is treated as though it were already done, the non-Jew has not fulfilled the condition and it is certainly the Jew's. At first Magen Avraham comes to explain Radbaz's intent by way of "since." About this he wrote: this is not comparable to what is written in siman 441, even though there too one could say "since," namely, since it is in the Jew's power to redeem it, and nevertheless it is not considered the Jew's chametz and he does not transgress. That case in siman 441 is different, for "since it is in his power to redeem it" requires money, and in such a case we do not say that it is as though it had already been done. Here, however, since it is in the non-Jew's power not to fulfill the condition, which does not require money, we say "since," etc., and it is as though the non-Jew had already not fulfilled the condition.
[5] The same applies regarding challah in the Gemara, page 47, and below in siman 457, se'if 2, that one who separates challah from impure dough on Yom Tov of Pesach may not designate it as challah until it is baked. For if he were to designate it as challah while it is still dough, he would be forbidden to bake it, since it is not fit for any person, as it is impure. If the challah remains that way and he does not bake it, it will become chametz. Even though it does not belong to the Jew, and it also has not come into the kohen's possession, we nevertheless say that the Jew transgresses regarding it, since if he wishes he can request release from it; for it is like a vow, where one can ask a sage about vows and he releases him, and it returns to being untithed, and becomes his. Therefore, even if he did not request release from it, he transgresses because of "since," since it is in his power to request release from it. There it is different, for it is in the Jew's power to ask about his vow, and it is like his; since it is in his power, it is as though he did it.
[5] But here, if the non-Jew wishes, etc. This means that here the matter is not in the Jew's power to fulfill or not fulfill the condition, but in the non-Jew's power. Therefore, there is no reason to say that it is the Jew's, since the matter is not in his power.
[5] And what he wrote, that all this is when the condition is pending, etc. Now he comes to reject the possibility that one might say he transgresses even without "since," because the matter is in doubt throughout Pesach, as above.
[5] But, etc. "And you will return it to me, etc." Chok Yaakov wondered, for on the contrary, in Yoreh De'ah, siman 305, the opposite is explained in se'if 8, and these are the words of Rashba: if the father redeeming his son said to the kohen, "Here are five sela'im on condition that you return them to me," after the fact his son is redeemed; but if he said, "Here are five sela'im and you will return them to me," it is not a gift and his son is not redeemed; see there. One can distinguish. Certainly according to the Shach and Derishah there, who wrote in se'if katan 8 to distinguish that specifically with "on condition that you return it to me," the giver intends that at least for some time, or for an hour, it should be a complete gift. But when he says "and you will return it to me," it implies immediately, and he did not resolve in his heart to make any gift, even for one hour. Therefore his son is not redeemed; see there.
[5] Therefore, regarding chametz, it is not only that if he says "and you will return it to me after Pesach" it is effective, because he intended it as a gift for all the days of Pesach. Even without specification, if he said "and you will return it to me," we are witnesses that his intent is that he return it specifically after Pesach. And even according to Taz there, who distinguishes that when he says "and you will return it to me" it does not sound like a condition but like a retraction, retracting from the first giving, regarding chametz, since a disclosure of intent suffices, we are witnesses that he did not intend a retraction but a complete gift, and is merely asking him to return it to him afterward, as Magen Avraham wrote.
