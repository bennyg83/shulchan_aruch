(א) To a gentile (Taz) — it must be only outside the house; b'dieved even without designating a corner, as long as he did not accept liability, permitted; l'chatchila they wrote one should sell the room too (Magen Avraham 4; and see there will be explained). Pri Megadim letter 13 in the name of Sha'agat Aryeh siman 3: one may not sell chametz to his Canaanite slave; Pri Megadim letter 6. Here too others sell for him; see Yoreh De'ah 267:17: without immersion his body is not acquired — nevertheless his handiwork and all his master's; practical difference only if he immersed to freedom; and so letter for Magen Avraham. Immersed — obviously violates bal yera'eh; possible also bal yimatzei until night; elsewhere we wrote from this; if one said to slave: I give you a gift on condition to return after Pesach.

(ב) “And the Hashem, who has not been sent to the room, shall not be exalted to thee, and shall not be given to thee.”

(ג) Incident: one sold large barrels of burnt wine and left a seal after the sale — there is room to permit; even before sale our sale with proper deed each quart for such sum and the gentile if he wishes opens and does what he wants — only the seal until measurement — if so permitted, possible even l'chatchila; in Kaf HaChayyim he wrote because of evasion (Bach); this in small sale — void sale; only with revealed intent suffices per Rabbenu Chananel; whoever left seal beforehand appears not to want sale — unlike such a case as stated. Also what Bach wrote not to leave seal on the room — on barrels the gentile can sell to his fellow by pulling and measure after Pesach; Pri Megadim: gentile does not rely, unlike sale.

(ד) And what he wrote one may not sell chametz to an apostate — apostate's chametz forbidden to all Israel (Ateret Rosh); likewise Choshen Mishpat part 1 law 4 and Chullin 4 chametz of sinners, etc.; Kaf HaChayyim letter 14: one may not buy after Pesach immediately from an apostate; possible doubt lest made after Pesach permitted (Kaf HaChayyim 449:1; Chullin 4:2 s.v. permitted; Maharsha there; and he said he speaks truth — even rabbinically he does not abandon leniency lest he anger — in Rosh Yosef we wrote there. However one can say l'chatchila doubt or rabbinic doubt — we do not act; see 110, 120: buying cooked things from gentiles stam bal yera'eh and doubt there from difficulty).

(ה) And know — an apostate who married a gentile woman, the child is a gentile, and the reverse child is Jewish — see Even HaEzer on this; b'dieved requires study — one can say better than an infant captured among gentiles, and you have no greater coercion; even coerced Jew forbidden — this is better; b'dieved if bought in absolute terms, one can say permitted. To sell to apostate woman to gentile l'chatchila no; if impossible with no other buyer, on condition she hold for herself and not sell even to her husband — as Taz letter 6 wrote, permitted.

(ו) And what he wrote incident, etc. siman 443 Taz and Magen Avraham 5, 6 — exchanging chametz with others permitted; nevertheless specifically with great loss — here loss is slight, no advice; also lest the gentile sell to a Jew; see Mishna Lamelech chapter 5 of Laws of Marriage halacha 1 end s.v. general principle whether benefit prohibitions d'rabbanan forbidden to exchange oneself (see there); Rashi Chullin 4b explains Rabbanan also forbidden to exchange; if you say he explained to remove R' Yehuda's mind — they gain it is rabbinic; in Tosafot s.v. permitted one can say so; in Rosh Yosef I expanded there.

(ז) If they acquired chametz over which Passover passed from an apostate and did not know he was apostate, etc. — obvious mistaken transaction; see Mishna Lamelech chapter 5 halacha 7 (in Mishna Lamelech) and Laws of Sale chapter 16 halacha 14 and Yoreh De'ah 119:13; if the apostate wants to return money he may, for he takes his own as stated; see Yoreh De'ah siman 99 Shach letter 17 — I wrote in the name of one gaon an incident: bought immediately after Pesach certainly made on Pesach — only one barrel made after Pesach — one can say permitted to add and nullify (see there). Without great loss do not permit — there is double doubt to prohibition: perhaps halacha is like Rambam we do not add rabbinically; perhaps all was made.
