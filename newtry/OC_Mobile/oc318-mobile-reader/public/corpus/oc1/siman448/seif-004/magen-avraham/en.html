[4] He transgresses bal yera'eh. For one who deposits his chametz with a non-Jew transgresses, as written at the end of siman 440 (Maggid Mishneh). One must say that this refers to where the Jew made the condition depend on himself, such as where he stipulated that if the Jew has money he will return it to him; in such a case it is not a sale at all, as written in Choshen Mishpat, siman 207; see there. It is worse than a pledge, for with a pledge he says to him, "If I do not redeem it before Rosh Chodesh Iyyar, it shall be yours from now." But here the power to redeem it is always in his hand. Alternatively, the Jew made the sale depend on a condition, such as if he said to him, "If you do such-and-such for me, the chametz is yours." It follows that as long as the condition has not been fulfilled, his sale is not a sale. But if he said, "I hereby give it to you from now on condition that you return it," or "on condition that you do such-and-such," this is a complete sale, as written in Choshen Mishpat, siman 212, se'if 4, and in Even HaEzer, siman 28, se'if 20; see there. It is forbidden only because of the stringency of chametz, as I wrote above in se'if 3.
