In Sefer Chok Yaakov he wrote in the name of Responsa Even Shoham that the intent is not that by transferring the place it becomes the non-Jew's courtyard and his courtyard acquires the chametz for him, for courtyard is derived through agency, and there is no agency for a non-Jew. Rather, the intent must be by way of agav, that movable goods are acquired together with land. According to this, he must use the language of agav and not the language of "with," as written in Choshen Mishpat, siman 202, according to one view there. However, Responsa Avodas HaGershuni, end of siman 120, wrote that for this reason the Tur in Yoreh De'ah, siman 320, regarding removing the sanctity of a firstborn, wrote that the clearest of the acquisitions is that he transfer the place to him. In that responsum he gave the reason why this acquisition is the clearest: with pulling, the non-Jew's intent is only to acquire with money, and whenever the buyer does not intend to acquire, the acquisition is ineffective. He brought proof from Bava Metzia 10, where a poor person fell on pe'ah and they remove him from it, for he did not acquire it. Although we hold that a person's four cubits acquire for him, nevertheless, since he fell on it, he revealed that he wants to acquire by falling and not through his four cubits, and therefore the four cubits do not acquire for him. Thus the acquisition depends on the buyer's intent. So too wrote Beis Yosef in siman 134, and also the Bach there, and he wrote that Responsa Maharam Mintz wrote likewise and brought many proofs. Therefore transferring the place is preferable, because his courtyard acquires for him without his knowledge; this is the content of his words. According to this, he necessarily holds that the reason is that a courtyard acquires for a non-Jew, not because of agav. For if it were because of agav, we would still require the non-Jew's intent, and on the contrary, acquisition by agav is difficult to explain to a non-Jew, and it is unlikely that the non-Jew intends to acquire by agav. Rather, according to Responsa Avodas HaGershuni, the reason must be through the law of courtyard. But then the difficulty of Responsa Even Shoham mentioned above applies to him, for there is no agency for a non-Jew. I found that in Responsa Avodas HaGershuni, end of siman 105, he sensed this and wrote: one can also say, regarding how a non-Jew's courtyard acquires for him, since courtyard is based on agency and we hold there is no agency for a non-Jew, and I wrote at length about this in another responsum. This is his language. I did not find anything about this in his printed responsa that came into my hands. Perhaps one can say that he holds like the Mas'as Binyamin brought by Magen Avraham, that a non-Jew can become an agent for a non-Jew, since both are not members of the covenant. Only when the sender is a member of the covenant must the agent also be a member of the covenant, and the same in reverse: when the agent is a member of the covenant, the sender must also be a member of the covenant. But when both sender and agent are not members of the covenant, it is acceptable. According to this, even though courtyard is based on agency, nevertheless a courtyard acquires for a non-Jew, because the courtyard is also not a member of the covenant and can become an agent for a non-Jew. Responsa Even Shoham follows its own approach, for it was not clear to him whether a non-Jew can become an agent for a non-Jew, as written in Sefer Chok Yaakov in his name, and therefore he wrote not to transfer to a non-Jew by way of courtyard, but only by way of agav. If so, since Magen Avraham proved from the Yerushalmi that a non-Jew cannot become an agent for a non-Jew, then we hold like Responsa Even Shoham, that courtyard does not acquire for a non-Jew, and one must transfer the chametz to him by way of agav.
[4] I already wrote that it is difficult to transfer to a non-Jew by way of agav, because the non-Jew does not rely in his mind on acquiring by agav, and wherever he does not intend to acquire by this mode of acquisition, he does not acquire, as I wrote in the name of Responsa Avodas HaGershuni in the name of the poskim. Therefore it occurred to me that it is preferable to sell to a non-Jewish woman who has no husband. In such a case one can say that Responsa Even Shoham concedes that her courtyard acquires for her, for it is explained in Choshen Mishpat, siman 243, that a minor boy has no courtyard, since for a man courtyard was included through agency, and there is no agency for a minor. But a minor girl has a courtyard, for regarding a woman courtyard was included through "hand," and a minor girl has a hand. If so, a non-Jewish woman should have a courtyard, since she has a hand. Although the fact that courtyard was included for a woman through hand is learned from a get, as stated at the beginning of Bava Metzia, and a get does not apply to a non-Jew, nevertheless one can say that since a non-Jew was not excluded from this acquisition, the law is the same with a non-Jewish woman. Know that acquisition by agav is mentioned only with regard to a Jew, in the case of Asa, as it is written, "with fortified cities that are in Yehudah," as stated in Kiddushin 27, and nevertheless Responsa Even Shoham took it as obvious that it is effective with a non-Jew. And although with exchange acquisition the Shach disagrees with Rabbeinu Tam in Choshen Mishpat, siman 423, and holds that it is ineffective with a non-Jew, that is because regarding exchange it is written, "and this formerly in Israel." If so, through the law of courtyard she acquires for a non-Jew even without her knowledge, as Responsa Avodas HaGershuni wrote. However, all this will not help and does not provide a cure for the fracture of my people at this time, when it is known among Jews and non-Jews that the sale is only to remove the prohibition of chametz. Even the God-fearing who tremble at the word of Hashem and sell wholeheartedly, nevertheless the non-Jew does not intend to acquire the place or the chametz. If so, through what is the chametz acquired by the non-Jew, since even the courtyard is not acquired by him, because he does not desire at all to acquire the courtyard? And even by way of agav alone, regarding what I wrote above, I saw in Sefer Urim VeTumim, siman 123, that agav does not acquire for a non-Jew, and he took the matter as obvious and did not mention the words of Responsa Even Shoham mentioned above; see there. My heart tells me that specifically in earlier days, when this matter of selling chametz to a non-Jew was occasional, and the sale was wholehearted both for the Jewish seller and for the non-Jewish buyer, it was effective. But now, because of our many sins, there are many ignoramuses for whom even in their own eyes the matter is only a rote commandment and an ancestral custom, all the more so that the non-Jew has no intention to acquire at all. In my poverty of understanding, the matter is very difficult in my eyes. What shall I do? Because of the weakness of my intellect, through our many sins, I do not know how to repair it, unless we rely on what Beis Yosef wrote in the name of Ran in siman 431, that since in any case the chametz is not in his domain, etc., and as Magen Avraham wrote in the name of Mas'as Binyamin.
