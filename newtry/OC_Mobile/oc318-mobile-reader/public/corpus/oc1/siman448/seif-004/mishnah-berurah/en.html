[4] (22) Instead of your buying chametz for one hundred, etc. This means that he should buy a large amount of chametz for the sum of two hundred, even though he needs to buy only for one hundred, so that when I need it after Pesach I will buy it from you and give you profit on the entire sum.
[4] (23) Perhaps I will need, etc. The later authorities wrote that he is permitted even to promise that he will go back and buy it from him and give him profit.
[4] (24) On condition. This refers to where he said it in the language of "if," meaning: if you do such-and-such during Pesach or after Pesach, the chametz is given to you or sold to you. As long as the condition has not been fulfilled, the chametz is not yet sold or given, and therefore he transgresses bal yera'eh. But if he said to him, "I hereby give it to you from now, on condition that you do such-and-such," the chametz belongs to the non-Jew immediately if the condition is ultimately fulfilled, and it is forbidden only because of the stringency of chametz, as above at the end of se'if 3. Alternatively, it refers even to a case of "from now," but the condition was that whenever he brings money, he must return it to him. With this condition, the sale or gift does not take effect at all, since at every moment it is in his power to redeem it, as explained in Choshen Mishpat, siman 207, se'if 6. Know further that the later authorities agreed that the same applies: it is forbidden to say to a non-Jew, "I sell you the chametz on condition that you keep it for yourself and not sell it to anyone else except me." Since this is in the language of condition, if the non-Jew violates it and sells it to someone else, the sale is void retroactively, and it emerges that the chametz was never acquired by the non-Jew; we are concerned that he may sell it, and the Jew will transgress bal yera'eh. But if he said to him, "I hereby sell it to you as a complete absolute sale, and I have reserved this right in it, that if you want to sell it, you shall not sell it to anyone except me," some say this is permitted even ab initio. For even if the non-Jew violates this and sells it, the sale will not be void because of this, since he did not say it in the language of condition; only the non-Jew's sale will be void.
