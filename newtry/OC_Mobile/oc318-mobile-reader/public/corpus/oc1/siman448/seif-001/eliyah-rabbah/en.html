[1] Of non-Jews, etc. If one has a partnership with non-Jews in chametz and Pesach passed over it, when he divides with him after Pesach, his portion is forbidden for benefit and the non-Jews' portion is permitted for benefit, because bereirah applies in rabbinic matters. Regarding eating, this requires examination. Chok Yaakov. See Yoreh De'ah, siman 146, se'if 4, regarding a convert and non-Jews who inherited their non-Jewish father, where the convert may say to the non-Jews, "You take the idol, and I will take the money, etc.," because a convert's inheritance is rabbinic; but if they entered a partnership, it is forbidden. If so, here the same should apply regarding chametz. However, there the reason is that he wants the prohibited item to continue existing; see Tosafos, Avodah Zarah 64. If so, the same applies if the partnership has chametz and money, that it is forbidden; nevertheless, chametz does not render its proceeds forbidden. See the end of siman 443. There was an incident involving a Jew who before Pesach had grain at a non-Jew's mill to grind, and on the seventh day of Pesach the non-Jew ground it, made bread from it, and brought it to the Jew after Pesach. In Beis Hillel, siman 132, he permitted taking the money for the bread from that non-Jew, or that the non-Jew sell it and give its money to the Jew, because he is taking payment for his damage, as with forbidden wine there. Chok Yaakov wrote that perhaps one may permit it even for eating, because perhaps the non-Jew exchanged the grain. For these reasons it is also possible to be lenient if a non-Jew stole a Jew's chametz and Pesach passed over it, though this requires examination. This is the end of his words. It appears that one should be lenient even if it is recognizable and known that it is a Jew's chametz, for it is under the non-Jew's responsibility and is like a case where a non-Jew lent money to a Jew against his chametz for a fixed time, etc., at the beginning of siman 441, and he did not redeem it. It does not appear comparable to the end of siman 440, where a Jew deposited it with a non-Jew and the Jew accepted responsibility, so that the Jew nevertheless transgresses, because here he took it by theft. It is also possible to compare it to what is written in Yoreh De'ah, siman 120, se'if 11, regarding a vessel stolen by a non-Jew where the owner despaired of it, that it requires immersion; see there.
