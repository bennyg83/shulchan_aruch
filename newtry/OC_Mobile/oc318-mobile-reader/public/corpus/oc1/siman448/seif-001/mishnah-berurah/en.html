(1) Chametz, etc. This applies when it is dough, or even bread in a place where bread baked by non Jews is customarily permitted.
(2) Belonging to a non Jew. The same applies to ownerless chametz. Since no prohibition was violated by retaining it, the Sages did not prohibit eating it. Therefore, if a convert died before Pesach and left chametz, whoever first acquires it after Pesach may even eat it. If a Jew and a non Jew jointly owned chametz through Pesach and divided it after Pesach, the portion assigned to the non Jew is permitted for benefit, because in a rabbinic matter we apply bereirah and regard it as clarified retroactively that this portion belonged to the non Jew. Mekor Chayim inclines to limit this to chametz that remained in the non Jew’s house; if it remained in the Jew’s house, the matter depends upon a dispute. It is possible that in the permitted case it may even be eaten. The Jew’s share is forbidden for benefit. The same law applies when two Jews jointly owned chametz and one sold his share to a non Jew. The portion that falls to the Jew who sold his share is permitted, while the other Jew’s portion is prohibited.
