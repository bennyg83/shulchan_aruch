The Bach asked from the baraisa on 31b, and the Pri Chadash asked from se'ifim 3 and 4, from the Tosefta that one may say to the non-Jew, and from Pesachim 30a, "Remove and acquire the chametz of the soldiers." Taz answered everything. One can say that according to Chizkiyah, who reads "it shall not be eaten" with a tzere as referring to within its time, before its time it is permitted for benefit and forbidden only for eating. Therefore according to R. Yehudah, see Tosafos, Pesachim 28b, s.v. "vechad." The Rif and the Rosh, of blessed memory, imply that they rule like Chizkiyah, the teacher of R. Abahu in chapter Kol Sha'ah, unlike the Rambam, of blessed memory, in chapter 8 of Hilchos Ma'achalos Asuros, halachah 9. In the Gemara on 29a, where it asks, "If it is R. Yehudah, then even that of a non-Jew, etc.," it asks according to R. Abahu. According to Chizkiyah, the Mishnah is fine: according to R. Yehudah, chametz of a non-Jew after Pesach is permitted for benefit but forbidden for eating because of benefit from it, while that of a Jew is also forbidden as a penalty. Baal HaItur rules like R. Yehudah, and those baraisos are like R. Shimon. As for Baal HaItur ruling like R. Shimon before its time, as the Tur wrote in his name in 443, one can say that after its time he ruled like R. Yehudah. We too hold before its time like R. Yehudah and after its time like R. Shimon. In Pesachim 28b it sounds that from the time it is forbidden for eating it is forbidden for benefit, like R. Yehudah. That is from a kal vachomer from orlah, where no transgression was performed; see Pnei Yehoshua. However, after Pesach, when according to R. Shimon it is also permitted for eating because of benefit from it, there is no such kal vachomer from orlah, as stated. What the Bach wrote from "if he did not check, he should check during the festival and after the festival" will be explained with Hashem's help in note 7 and in Magen Avraham 8. Chametz of a non-Jew during Pesach is forbidden for benefit, for we do not hold like Rav Acha bar Yaakov; see Pri Chadash. Therefore it is forbidden to smell non-Jewish bread. See Eruvin 64b and the Tur, where Baal HaItur wrote that after Pesach, if he nullified it, it is forbidden for eating and permitted for benefit, and there is no reason, etc. According to what Taz wrote, even ownerless chametz is forbidden for eating and permitted for benefit according to R. Yehudah and Chizkiyah. This requires examination, and see Chok Yaakov 3.
