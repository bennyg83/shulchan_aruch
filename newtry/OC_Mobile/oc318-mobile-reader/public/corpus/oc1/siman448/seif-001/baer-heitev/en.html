A non-Jew. It appears to me that if a Jew and non-Jew jointly own chametz and it remained over Pesach, then although the half belonging to the Jew is forbidden for benefit, when they divide it after Pesach, the non-Jew’s portion is permitted for benefit. Since after Pesach the prohibition is only Rabbinic, we apply the principle of retroactive clarification. Whether it is permitted for eating requires study. Chok Yaakov. If a convert died and left chametz that remained over Pesach, whoever first acquires it after Pesach may use it. When the convert died, the chametz became ownerless, and no person possessed it in a manner that would violate the prohibition. Chok Yaakov.
