"But here, if the non-Jew wishes, he may keep it, etc." This means that we say "since, etc., it is considered like his" only when it is in his power to acquire it; then we say it is as though he had already acquired it. But with something that is not his, and it is also not in his power to acquire it but depends on others, we do not say that it is considered like his. Therefore, if the non-Jew returned it after Pesach and fulfilled the condition, it is permitted. Nevertheless, it appears that ab initio it is certainly forbidden to do this, lest the non-Jew not return it, and the chametz then turns out retroactively to have belonged to the Jew. The Radbaz only came to give a reason to forbid the chametz after Pesach as a matter of law, and about this Magen Avraham wrote that this is not so, for "since" does not apply here, since it is not in his power to acquire it. Nevertheless, in my humble opinion the Radbaz holds that even though it is a condition of "on condition that," which is like "from now," nevertheless before the condition is fulfilled it stands in doubtful prohibition of bal yera'eh, for perhaps the condition will not be fulfilled. Since on Pesach it stood in doubtful prohibition, we penalize him. Similar to this the commentators wrote regarding the incident of Batsheva, where they said to David, "one who has relations with a married woman, what is his death penalty, etc." Even though everyone who went out to war wrote a bill of divorce, nevertheless at the time of relations he did not know whether Uriah would die, and at that time he transgressed a doubtful prohibition of a married woman. This is not at all comparable to the case in siman 449 of a non-Jew who lent, etc., because there it depends on the Jew, and when he firmly decides not to redeem it at all he is not committing a prohibition. Here, however, it depends on the non-Jew, and perhaps he will not fulfill the condition, so it emerges that on Pesach he transgressed a doubtful prohibition of bal yera'eh. One may also distinguish that here the fulfillment of the condition is through an active deed, and there is greater concern that it may not be fulfilled. By contrast, in siman 441 the fulfillment of the condition is through inaction. We find similarly regarding a get, that with a condition requiring an active deed, she may not marry ab initio. So too regarding whether the seller must clarify that the condition was fulfilled, or regarding betrothal, there is a distinction between whether the condition requires an active deed or not.
