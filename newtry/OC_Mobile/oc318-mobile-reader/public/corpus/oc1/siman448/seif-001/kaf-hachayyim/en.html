1) [Se'if 1] Chametz belonging to a non-Jew, etc. The Yerushalmi in the chapter Kol Sha'ah teaches: If a convert died and left chametz, and Pesach passed over it, whoever first takes possession of it after Pesach may use it according to all opinions. This does not contradict the Babylonian Talmud in Bava Kama 110b, which states that one who stole a convert's chametz and retained it over Pesach may not derive benefit from it. There, the convert did not die until the prohibition of chametz had already taken effect. Alternatively, even if the convert died before Pesach, the chametz was in the robber's possession when the prohibition took effect. Here, however, when the convert died the chametz remained ownerless. Consequently, no one violated the prohibition against chametz being seen, for the verse excludes ownerless chametz just as it excludes chametz belonging to others. Chok Yaakov, subsection 1; Mekor Chaim, subsection 1; Chayei Adam, rule 124, subsection 24. The same therefore applies to any ownerless chametz, which is permitted after Pesach.
