[1] Permitted even for eating. This excludes the view of Baal HaItur, cited by the Tur in his name, that it is forbidden for eating. My teacher and father-in-law, of blessed memory, asked from the case of a shop in the following siman, which is a baraisa in chapter Kol Sha'ah, where we explicitly say that if it is of a non-Jew it is permitted for eating, and he left it unresolved. It seems to me that Baal HaItur rules like Rava in chapter Kol Sha'ah, 28, that according to R. Shimon the reason is because of a penalty, and automatically according to R. Yehudah it is forbidden for eating. If so, that baraisa of the shop follows R. Shimon according to Baal HaItur's view.
