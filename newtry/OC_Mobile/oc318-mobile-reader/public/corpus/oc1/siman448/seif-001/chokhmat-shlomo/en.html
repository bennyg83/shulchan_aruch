Seif 1 — chametz of a gentile over which Passover passed, etc. I will copy here several legal novelties very needed for the law, with Hashem's help. (1) An incident came before me: one had chametz needed for the king's tax; because he did not pay the tax as required, the king's officers learned of it, took the chametz to the tax treasury belonging to the king, and the matter went to court, pending whether all the chametz would be forfeited to the king or the Jew would prove his right and judgment would go forth to return the chametz. Now the questioner stood and asked according to law what to do on Pesach with this chametz standing in the king's house, and whether sale to a gentile helps. One must examine whether sale to a gentile helps at all, for we rule: a thing that is his but not in his possession — he cannot sell it; here too, since taken to the king's officers it is not in his possession and he cannot sell it, yet it remains his and he violates bal yera'eh. One cannot say it is like a gentile robbing his chametz — when a gentile robs his chametz the Israelite does not violate bal yera'eh, as Acharonim wrote; but that is not so: in robbery the gentile is at least responsible and if lost the gentile can be compelled in court to pay — therefore it is in the gentile's responsibility and the Israelite does not violate bal yera'eh; here the king's officers are not responsible — if lost, even if later clarified the Jew wins in court they need not pay him — not in gentile responsibility; possibly here he violates bal yera'eh. However one may be lenient in sale for two reasons. First: we rule one cannot sell what is his but not in his possession — this is specifically if a robber took it intending to steal completely with no intent to return; here the king's officers do not intend to steal outright but to return if he wins in court — if the king wins the chametz is retroactively the king's and not his at all — law of the kingdom is law and no bal yera'eh; if judgment is that it is the Israelite's, their intent from now is to return it — not in the category of 'not in his possession,' and better than collateral where clear lien exists from the debt preventing sale; here no clear lien. Second: since we rule robber and owners not despaired — neither can dedicate — likewise sell; we learn from 'a man who dedicates his house' — his house in his possession; and chametz of an Israelite in a gentile's possession not violating bal yera'eh we learn from 'in your houses' — your house in your possession — excludes Israelite chametz in gentile possession that is his but not in his possession, as Mechilta explicitly states, brought by Rishonim and Acharonim; both laws from one word 'house' — if in our case in his possession for bal yera'eh, likewise for sale; if not in his possession for sale, not in his possession to violate bal yera'eh — therefore permitted to sell to a gentile so the gentile stands in his place to acquire if he wins. For greater certainty I gave him a regulation: after sale declare before three he makes it ownerless and despairs completely — pity monetary loss; therefore from ownerless side it may not help if not in possession to sell, not in possession to make ownerless — their laws are equal; nevertheless despair helps — despair properly works for something not in possession, as despair's essence is in robbery or theft; if in possession he already sold and sale helps; if not in possession despair helps and it leaves possession entirely; therefore from despair side it suffices even before two, not requiring three except to satisfy all concerns I commanded him (also to return) also actual ownerless requiring three before witnesses — satisfying all views; even in collateral one cannot generally sell, here either way: d'oraisa he does not violate bal yera'eh only rabbinically and it appears as his — he can sell and it helps; examine this for I shortened; see Acharonim on chametz sale laws l'chatchila requiring pulling until end (see there); Shach Yoreh De'ah siman 320 requires money paid at sale time not later; in responsum Hilchot Bekhor 1793 I wrote that is at pulling time — specifically if acquisition not in buyer's courtyard; if in courtyard at payment, suffices even if money given later while in courtyard (see there). (2) Regarding writing chametz sale in royal protocol — it seemed obvious it need not be written since it is only to remove prohibition; now studying Eruvin 62: R' Chiya said valid lease required, R' Shimon weak lease, etc.; gemara concludes: what is valid lease — moharakei and aburnakei; Tosafot and Rosh: royal seals and ministers' agents — thus even though lease is only to remove prohibition, valid lease is required by law of the kingdom; R' Shimon holds weak lease — perhaps only in Eruvin where many leniencies exist in eruvin more than other rabbinic prohibitions; also for some poskim eruvin has no Torah basis — even for those who hold beyond twelve mil is rabbinic, nevertheless its essence is not considered Torah law as elsewhere (see there) and Hagahot Gra on Eruvin first chapter (see there) — they were lenient there; but from where to be lenient on chametz whose essence is Torah with many stringencies — all hold valid sale and lease required; to minimize dispute is preferable; main thing it should be written among courts — requires study why they did not practice thus. (3) Whether hodaah acquisition works for prohibition — see Ketzot HaChoshen 194 and my large work on Pesach laws siman 448; now clear proof: hodaah works from Gittin chapter 3 page 30b: Israel told Levi 'maaser is in your hand,' etc.; Abaye explained (see there) — the sugya wonders how Levi can separate terumat maaser since not yet acquired; second difficulty: why not acquire through owner's courtyard — Rishonim questioned, brought in commentators and Tiv Gittin on Gittin newly printed; in my view it resolves: since he said 'a kor of maaser is in your hand' — it is admission he has maaser in hand — through admission he acquires and it becomes his and he can separate elsewhere — proves hodaah works for prohibition and makes acquisition. This also resolves second difficulty: not like Taz Choshen Mishpat 189 that deposited courtyard does not acquire for depositor — since Israel admitted maaser is in Levi's hand it is as though maaser is in Levi's hand and courtyard does not acquire for him; even for disputants see Ketzot 194 — here it fits: hodaah makes acquisition though not for priestly gifts since chalipin is way of sale not gift as first chapter Bava Metzia — 'giving' written in priestly gifts; from Torah it is mere giving (see there) and giving to his courtyard is giving in priestly gifts — here every Israelite is presumed fit and we testify he wants to fulfill giving — he says 'a kor of maaser in your hand' and acquires for Levi's son — nevertheless way of sale, not fulfilling giving mitzva — we testify he admits in effective manner intending to give courtyard where kor maaser rests — as given to Levi's son's courtyard fulfilling giving mitzva like R' Gamliel and elders in Bava Metzia chapter 1; therefore generally deposited courtyard acquires — not that he gave place to remain depositor's — deposited courtyard; but here we testify he gave place through this admission too; conversely Levi sold only maaser not place — no mitzva, no presumption he returned and gave place, sold only maaser body not place — maaser place remains Levi's — depositor did not acquire produce. Great proof for this law and sugya resolution. Yerushalmi Terumot brought in Tiv Gittin: R' Yochanan in R' Yanai's name — Israel told Levi a kor maaser, etc., Levi's son made terumat maaser elsewhere, Israel gave another to Levi's son — only resentment: if this gave before that acted — but if that acted before this gave, not like this mishnah — if before teruma nullified, if after teruma his teruma is teruma entirely from him [commentators: 'all from him']; resolved per R' Yosi: owner who separated maaserot — what he did is done (see there Tiv Gittin). Main commentators' explanation: Yerushalmi initially as Israel's agency (see there) — difficulty: whatever cannot be done cannot appoint agent — he himself explained this is the gemara's refutation 'all from him.' In my view Yerushalmi initially not so — truth that hodaah makes acquisition, but only admission before witnesses in matter where mockery or false oath not applicable; admission without witnesses he may retract — speech comes and nullifies speech; Yerushalmi: if this gave before that made terumat maaser — only speech that he admitted kor maaser in hand — he may later retract and say 'I mocked you' — speech nullifies speech as agent cancellation before teruma; if that acted first then this gave — act done, speech does not nullify act — therefore refutes 'all from him' for Levi's son to act — not his; how does his act help that owner cannot retract speech — answers per R' Yosi owner may separate maaserot — while owner did not retract, Levi's son acts as his agent as though owner acted — speech no longer nullifies act — resolves well. Further proof: Gittin — slave who married before master or left tefillin — freed; gemara 39 questioned: owner who makes his slave ownerless needs bill of manumission — here like admission for money but why help for prohibition — must be hodaah makes acquisition for prohibition like writing him a bill of manumission; examine well.
