(1) [Seif 1] On Shabbat and Yom Tov one does not say Bar'khu, etc. — and the reason the Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Rivash: Bar'khu was instituted before the berachot of Kri'as Shema; and if the entire congregation heard it before the berachot, one does not say it at all after the prayer; but that which they say it after the prayer — the Beit Yosef wrote that it is to discharge those individuals who did not pray with the congregation, and the congregation goes on their way and those individuals remain there and each one prays for himself; but on Shabbat and Yom Tov, since they delay leaving and everyone has already prayed, one does not say it at all — see there; and this is per the plain meaning of the matter; but in Sha'ar HaKavanos he wrote two kavanos for Bar'khu — one for Bar'khu before Yotzer, and one for Bar'khu after the final Kaddish; and he wrote that also in the two times of Bar'khu of Ma'ariv on Friday night one should intend these two kavanos — the first in the first Bar'khu and the second in the second Bar'khu — see there on daf 50b; and similarly on daf 70b see there; and similarly in Pe'er Chayim, Sha'ar HaShabbos end of chapters 13 and 14; and similarly the Shatz on daf 16a on these words of Maran — that this is not the view of the Rav z"l, but rather that even on Shabbat and Yom Tov one must say it, as explained in Sha'ar HaKavanos — end of his words. And similarly Mahari Berav in siman 286 letter 4: in Eretz HaTzvi and Eretz Mitzrayim the custom is to say Bar'khu also on Shabbat and Yom Tov after the final Kaddish. And similarly the Chasam Sofer letter 22: thus it is fitting to conduct per the sod. And similarly some say in the Mishna Berurah in Hagah Berurah letter 5; and similarly the Rokeach in the name of synagogues — the custom is to say it both on weekdays and on Shabbat — see there.
