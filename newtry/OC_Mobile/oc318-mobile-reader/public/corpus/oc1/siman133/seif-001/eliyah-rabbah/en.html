Levush brings here the responsum of Maharam Mintz regarding kaddishim, and since it is also brought in the Minhagim I did not need to bring it. Now I will explain their words with the help of Heaven:
[1] Nevertheless, the first time, etc. The Shach wrote in Yoreh Deah siman 376 as follows: That which a guest has one kaddish means that he is like a resident in the twelve-month period. According to this, where a resident does not have one kaddish, such as where there is a mourner within thirty days present and the like, the guest has nothing; end quote. According to this, where the custom is that a mourner within thirty days has only the kaddishim after Aleinu, then a guest within twelve months has one kaddish before the amud after "Shir mizmor le-Asaf," and similar cases, unless the custom is not to allow a guest anything at all except the kaddish after Aleinu; and this requires study, why so. It appears that this is specifically here in Prague, where there are many synagogues, and after yahrzeit mourners say kaddish in their own synagogue they run to another synagogue; if so, the mourners would lose out, and sometimes the resident yahrzeit mourner who does not run to another synagogue would be pushed aside entirely, which is improper. Therefore the custom was not to give him anything except the kaddish after Aleinu. But a genuine guest who has not yet said kaddish at all certainly has one of the other kaddishim. Likewise, where the custom is that a yahrzeit mourner has only one or two kaddishim, a guest within twelve months has one kaddish remaining from the yahrzeit mourner. The glosses to the new Minhagim wrote: If the guest is also within thirty days, he has one kaddish before the amud; and the same applies if the guest has yahrzeit and there are also many resident yahrzeit mourners, the first time he has the law of a resident; end quote. This requires study, for perhaps only against a twelve-month mourner does he have the law of a resident. It is somewhat implied from the fact that Maharam Mintz wrote generally that if both are yahrzeit mourners, the resident completely pushes aside the guest, etc.; presumably with yahrzeit it is like the first time. However, nowadays the custom is that a guest with yahrzeit has one kaddish. It appears obvious to me that the same rule applies to what they customarily do for a twelve-month mourner on the day he stops saying kaddish, meaning also when there is no mourner within thirty days and no yahrzeit mourner.
