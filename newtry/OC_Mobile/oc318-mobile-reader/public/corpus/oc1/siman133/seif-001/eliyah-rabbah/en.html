The Levush cites here the responsum of Maharam Mintz concerning Kaddish. Since it is also cited in the books of customs, I did not need to reproduce it. I will now explain their words, with the help of Heaven.
Nevertheless, the first time, etc. The Shach in Yoreh Deah siman 376 writes that the reason a guest is entitled to one Kaddish is that he has the status of a resident mourner within the twelve months. Accordingly, where a resident mourner of twelve months would not receive a Kaddish, such as where a mourner within thirty days is present, the guest receives nothing. On this basis, in places where a mourner within thirty days receives only the Kaddish after Aleinu, a guest who is within twelve months should receive one of the other Kaddishim, such as the Kaddish before the amud after Shir Mizmor LeAsaf, unless the local custom is not to give a guest any Kaddish except the one after Aleinu. Why that should be so requires study. It seems that this limitation applies specifically here in Prague, where there are many synagogues. After those observing a yahrzeit say Kaddish in their own synagogue, they run to another synagogue. If a guest were given another Kaddish, the regular mourners would lose it, and sometimes even a resident yahrzeit observer who does not run to another synagogue would be displaced entirely. This is improper. Therefore the custom developed to give a guest only the Kaddish after Aleinu. But a true guest who has not yet said any Kaddish certainly receives one of the other Kaddishim. Likewise, in places where a yahrzeit receives only one or two Kaddishim, a guest within twelve months receives one of the Kaddishim remaining after the yahrzeit observer's allotment. Hagahot Minhagim HaChadashim writes that if the guest himself is within thirty days, he receives one Kaddish before the amud. The same applies if the guest has a yahrzeit and there are also many resident yahrzeits; the first time he has the status of a resident. This requires study, for perhaps he has resident status only against a mourner within twelve months. Maharam Mintz's general wording that if both are observing yahrzeit the resident entirely displaces the guest somewhat implies that yahrzeit is also treated like the first time. Nevertheless, the present custom is that a guest observing yahrzeit receives one Kaddish. It is obvious to me that the same rule applies to the custom that a mourner within twelve months receives special Kaddishim on the day he ceases saying Kaddish, namely only where no mourner within thirty days or yahrzeit observer has precedence.
