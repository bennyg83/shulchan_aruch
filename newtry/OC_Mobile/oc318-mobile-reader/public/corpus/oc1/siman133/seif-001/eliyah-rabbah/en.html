The Levush brings here a responsum of Maharam Mintz regarding Kaddishim; and since it is also brought in Minhagim I did not need to bring it; and now I will explain their words with the help of Heaven: (1) "Nevertheless the first time, etc." — the Shach wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 376, and this is his language: that the fact that a guest has one Kaddish — that is, that he is like a resident of twelve months; and according to this, where a resident does not have one Kaddish — such as when there is a thirty-day mourner here and the like — a guest has nothing, until here. And according to this, where the custom is that a thirty-day mourner has only Kaddishim after Alenu — if so, a twelve-month mourner has a guest's one Kaddish before the amud after the psalm "A Psalm of Asaph," and the like; if not — where the custom is not to give a guest at all except Kaddish after Alenu — and this requires investigation as to why. And it appears specifically here in Prague, where there are many synagogues, and after they say the yahrtzeit Kaddish in their synagogue they run to another synagogue — and if so the mourners would lose out; and sometimes a resident's yahrtzeit completely pushes off [the guest] in a synagogue that does not run to another synagogue, and this is not correct; therefore they customarily give him only Kaddish after Alenu; but a complete guest who has not yet said Kaddish at all — certainly he has Kaddish from the other Kaddishim; and similarly where the custom is that a yahrtzeit has only one or two Kaddishim — a twelve-month mourner has a guest's one Kaddish remaining from the yahrtzeit. He wrote in Hagahot Minhagim Chadashim: if also a thirty-day guest has one Kaddish before the amud; and the same law applies if a guest has yahrtzeit; and there are also many yahrzeitim from residents — the first time he has the status of a resident, until here. And it requires investigation — for perhaps specifically against a twelve-month mourner he has the status of a resident; and it is somewhat implied from what Maharam Mintz wrote anonymously that if both are yahrtzeit, the resident completely pushes off the guest, etc.; and presumably yahrtzeit is like "first time." However, now they customarily give a guest with yahrtzeit one Kaddish; and it appears to me that it is simple that such is the law in what they customarily [do]: that a twelve-month mourner, on the day when he stops saying Kaddishim — that is, also when there is no thirty-day mourner and yahrtzeit.
