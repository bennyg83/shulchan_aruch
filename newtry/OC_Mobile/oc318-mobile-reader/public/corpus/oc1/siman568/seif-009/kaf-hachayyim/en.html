9) There: Or his rav. It is a fine custom to fast on the day one's primary rav died, because a person is obligated in greater honor and fear of his rav than of his father. Shelah, folio 201a. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 15, questions this, because the fast on the day one's father died is not because of honor but because of danger, as his fortune is impaired; see there. 9) The Yerushalmi states at the end of Moed Katan: Rabbi Avahu said, "May such-and-such come upon me if I tasted anything throughout a day on which a Torah scholar died." When Rabbi Yonah heard that a Torah scholar had died, even if he had already eaten and drunk, he completed the remainder of that day as a fast. Eliyah Rabbah there cites this.
