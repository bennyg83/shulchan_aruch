[In siman 161 he was asked about one who is in doubt whether his father died on the 4th or the 5th of the month, how his law should be regarding all yahrzeit customs. And he wrote that this is obvious: the mourners should not let him say Kaddish to cause them loss, for each day they will tell him, "Bring proof that that day is it"; but the doubt is whether he is obligated to arrange for himself a minyan in a place where he does not cause loss to the other mourners. And he founded this law on how he resolves the contradiction of the Ran in chapter "Arvei Pesachim" regarding the first two cups or the later ones, whether they require reclining; and the Ran wrote: since a rabbinic enactment would be uprooted entirely, we do not say "doubt of rabbinic law is to be lenient," and both require reclining; and in the first chapter of Megillah he wrote that a city that is in doubt whether it is surrounded by a wall or not should read on the 14th, which is the first festival they encounter; see there. And he wrote that the Ran holds that we do not say "doubt of rabbinic law is to be lenient" where he will certainly transgress a rabbinic [requirement]; for example, a doubtfully walled city: granted that each day he can postpone, saying perhaps today is not the day of obligation; nevertheless after both days have passed and he read on neither of them, in that we do not say "doubt of rabbinic law is to be lenient"; therefore on the first day he encounters [he reads], and then automatically on the second he is in doubt whether he read or did not read, and he is exempt, for a doubt is to be lenient. However, regarding reclining it would not suffice if they reclined at the first two cups, for this doubt applies to all of Israel; and if everyone reclines at the first [cups], and before Heaven it is revealed that they enacted the later two, then the rabbinic enactment of reclining would be uprooted entirely; and in such a case we do not say a doubt that uproots entirely is to be lenient. And see this reasoning in Tosafos, Niddah, folio 44b, end of s.v. "dekayim lan begavvah"; see there. And it is not comparable to a doubtfully walled city, where there is no doubt upon those particular individuals who are in doubt; and even if all the doubtful ones read on the 14th, nevertheless the rabbinic enactment is fulfilled in the cities that are certainly walled; see there. And accordingly, in our case: if there are still other brothers elsewhere who know with certainty the day of the yahrzeit, then the doubt rests only upon this man's head, and it is no more severe than a rabbinic mitzvah; and he should do all the yahrzeit practices on the first day he encounters, and on the second day he is exempt. But if he has no brothers, then on the yahrzeit day the father needs tikun for his soul on his day of judgment, and there is none to stand for him: this is similar to the majority where the father's enactment would be uprooted entirely; therefore he should do it on both. But to sit fasting two days is difficult, and all the more so for one who engages in Torah, for whom his reward would literally go out in his loss, etc.; and one may be lenient not to fast except on the first day; see there. And in siman 162 he was asked regarding one for whom a doubt fell on his yahrzeit day, and he already fasted for some years, and now forgot whether it is the 25th or the 26th of Sivan, and it is hard for him to sit fasting two days. And he wrote: and it appears to me that he should fast on the 25th; for even if it were tomorrow on the 26th, nevertheless he advances prayer before the trouble, or the trouble upon himself lest his fortune be harmed, or to lighten his father's judgment for tomorrow. And on the 26th: granted, if it were only a rabbinic doubt we would say that since he already fasted on the first, it is again a rabbinic doubt and to be lenient; but here it is a Torah mitzvah of accepting a fast, and every acceptance of a mitzvah is Torah-level from "from your mouth," this is tzedakah, as the Ran wrote in the first chapter of Nedarim, folio 8a; see there. Therefore on the 26th it is a Torah-level doubt, and he should ask [annulment] of his vow, as the Magen Avraham wrote regarding Erev Tishah B'Av; therefore both regarding fasting and lighting a candle he should do on the 25th. But Kaddish: the owners of the yahrzeit will push him from postponement to postponement, and he should be concerned for his soul to say some psalm publicly and say Kaddish on both days.]
