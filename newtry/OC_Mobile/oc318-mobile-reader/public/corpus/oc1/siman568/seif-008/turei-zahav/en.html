Yoreh Deah siman 402 I wrote they also do yahrzeit in the first year on the death day not burial day — if he died erev Shabbat on Rosh Chodesh Shevat and there is no time to bury until Sunday which is 3 Shevat — yahrzeit is on Rosh Chodesh Shevat although mourning will not be twelve months by fullness since mourning is from burial day — nevertheless mourning stops here after yahrzeit since it is impossible otherwise, for there is no mourning after twelve months; not as Mateh Moshe wrote, and in Turei David 294 if he died on Rosh Chodesh Nisan when mourning days completed on 29 Adar and the world practices that even on Rosh Chodesh Nisan that whole day they do not cancel mourning of twelve months — perhaps because they say Kaddish and Barchu and fast on father's death day — therefore they practice mourning that day; but by law they need not until the day after twelve months completes they are permitted; also “some say” for one who fasts and says Kaddish and Barchu on death day and he died today buried tomorrow — mourning is counted from burial day, end — do not err to learn that if burial is delayed several days from death they should mourn until burial and it would be more than twelve months from death — one certainly does not mourn after twelve months, for after that time law is not stretched — the Omnipresent forbid to say wicked judgment in Gehinnom is twelve months from burial and sometimes thirty-three or more from death — heaven forbid to say so; rather once judgment days complete mourning stops too; so one who did not hear of a death until four months from death — when he begins mourning from that day, twelve months is everyday practice — he need only until year from death completes; there in Turei David there was no gap between end of last day of twelve months and yahrzeit which is immediately after, so mourning continued that day since mourning was not interrupted any time yet, as it appears correct and clear. It also seems if he died on 1st of Rosh Chodesh Kislev which is 30th of Cheshvon and then Cheshvon was short with only 29 days and the 30th day is considered Kislev — yahrzeit is on 29 Cheshvon, and always since at death it was the 30th counted to Cheshvon month, mourning began on day that is end of month, and now on 29th is end of month — on 28 Cheshvon twelve months completed and 29th is yahrzeit; we always follow the month. If one died during Chanukah days, yahrzeit is not on Chanukah day but after Rosh Chodesh day — we follow how many days in the month was the death, and so yahrzeit — all this seems plain.
