And not on the day of burial. In Yoreh De'ah, siman 402, I wrote that also in the first year they observe the yahrzeit on the day of death and not on the day of burial; that is, if he died on Erev Shabbos on Rosh Chodesh Shevat and there is no opportunity to bury him until Sunday, which is the 3rd of Shevat, the yahrzeit is on Rosh Chodesh Shevat, even though mourning will not be twelve months in fullness, for mourning is from the day of burial; nevertheless mourning ceases here after the yahrzeit, since it is impossible otherwise, for there is no mourning after twelve months; and not like Sefer Mateh Moshe on this. And in Terumas HaDeshen, siman 292: if he died on Rosh Chodesh Nisan, the days of mourning were completed on the 29th of Adar, and the world practices that even on Rosh Chodesh Nisan that entire day they do not cancel the law of twelve-month mourning; and possibly because they say Kaddish and Barchu and fast on the day his father died, therefore they practice an aspect of mourning on that day; but by law one need only [cease] immediately when the day after the completion of twelve months enters, and they are permitted. And there is also a practical difference for one who fasts and says Kaddish and Barchu on the day of death, and he dies today and is buried tomorrow, for mourning is counted from the day of burial; end quote. Do not err to learn from this that if burial was delayed some days from the day of death, he should observe mourning until the day of burial and it would be more than twelve months from the day of death; and I have already seen one who erred in this. For certainly there is no mourning after twelve months, for after that time the attribute of judgment is no longer stretched; for would the judgment of the wicked in Gehinnom be twelve months from the day of burial, and sometimes thirteen months or more from death? Heaven forbid to say so. Rather, as soon as the days of judgment are completed, mourning likewise ceases. And similarly one who did not hear that a relative had died until a long time after the death: does he begin to mourn from that day when mourning fell upon him for twelve months? This is everyday practice: that he need only [mourn] until the completion of the year from the death. But there in Terumas HaDeshen there was no interruption between the end of the last day of twelve months and the yahrzeit that is immediately after it; then mourning continues also to that day, since mourning had not yet been interrupted for any time, as appears correct and clear to me according to my humble opinion. It further appears to me: if he died on the first day of Rosh Chodesh Kislev, which is the 30th of Cheshvan, and afterwards there was a short year in which Cheshvan had only 29 days, and the 30th day is counted to Kislev, then the yahrzeit will be on the 29th of Cheshvan, and so forever; for at the time of death the 30th day was counted to the month of Cheshvan, so mourning began on the day that is the end of the month, and now on the 29th day it is the end of the month; consequently on the 28th of Cheshvan twelve months were completed, and the 29th day is the yahrzeit; and we always follow the month. And similarly if a relative died for him during the days of Chanukah, the yahrzeit is not on that day of Chanukah, but we follow the day of Rosh Chodesh: how many days into the month the day of death was, and so shall the yahrzeit be. And all this appears obvious.
