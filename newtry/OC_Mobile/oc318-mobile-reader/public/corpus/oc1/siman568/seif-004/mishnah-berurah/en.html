(30) Three days. And they are accustomed to fast two more hours on the night of the fourth [day], so that it be by way of hours a hint for "Return, O Israel, unto Hashem your God"; and those who eat at the beginning of the night have not lost out, for it is the numerical value of kindness. And those who fast two consecutive days: it is good that they fast one hour at night after the fasts and also cease one hour while it is still day [that is, before the start of the fasts], for then it is counted like twenty-seven fasts: the first day is one fast, and afterwards each hour counts as one day. And "hour" is not precise, for part of the day is like the whole.
(31) By force of teshuvah. That is, by force of the tikkunei teshuvah clarified in the sefarim. And nevertheless it is better that he fast spaced [days], so that at every time his heart be humbled and his sins be before him always (Magen Avraham).
(32) He must fulfill what he vowed. See Peri Megadim, who is in doubt to the contrary: if he vowed to fast two consecutive days, whether he may change them to fasting forty days that are not consecutive; see there.
(33) Here he agrees. For there he did not accept upon himself with the intention that he be distressed; therefore he may borrow. Which is not so here, for in the end he will be distressed; therefore he must fulfill what he accepted upon himself (Darkhei Moshe).
(34) Before Yom Kippur. And he should begin to fast after the 15th of Av, and he should not fast on Shabbos or Rosh Chodesh; and those fasts that they Fast for a count of forty days need not be completed. And the one who fasts need not accept a fast for each day separately; rather he may accept them at one time when he begins.
(35) That one who accepted upon himself. And even if he did not say it by way of a vow, but only as a mere acceptance of a fast.
