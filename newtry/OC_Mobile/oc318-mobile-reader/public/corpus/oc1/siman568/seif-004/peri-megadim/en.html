And not Taz — his view here and Yoreh Deah 402 note 9 that yahrzeit is always even in the first year on the death day, and he rules afterward mourning after yahrzeit even if burial was distant several days from death — immediately after twelve months from death he completes mourning entirely; but Shach there note 10 and Nodah B'Yehudah — certainly mourning is not interrupted until twelve months from burial day not death day; therefore if burial day is distant from death two or three days, in the first year he must fast on burial day, for mourning twelve months begins from burial; if he fasts on death day he immediately interrupts mourning also for fasting and mourning afterward — one should not do both; therefore he must fast year one on burial day and from then on death day; unlike when he died that day and was buried tomorrow — even in first year he fasts death day and stops mourning on yahrzeit day, for at least twelve months are complete; so Turei David 294, and some say, etc., give reason for world practicing mourning on yahrzeit day, for sometimes one dies near synagogue and is buried the day after — if they stop mourning on yahrzeit, twelve months are not fully completed from burial since mourning twelve months is also counted from burial like seven and thirty in Yoreh Deah 394 — therefore they practice mourning on yahrzeit day. Therefore one can imagine two brothers: one was at the death and sent the deceased to another city for burial, and brother two was at burial place and learned there brother one begins mourning immediately as Yoreh Deah 394 seif 61, and brother two from burial when learned there — if two or three days between death and burial, brother one fasts year one yahrzeit on death day and brother two from burial day, see there well. But Taz holds for mourning twelve months we always count from death day not burial, and the same for seven and thirty days mourning; therefore in year one he fasts death day and twelve-month mourning is also complete. What Turei David wrote “some say,” etc. — perhaps also some reason: sometimes one dies today and is buried tomorrow — they practice mourning on yahrzeit since yesterday twelve months completed, yahrzeit continues until burial day; but if gap between yahrzeit and burial, certainly mourning stops after yahrzeit, for for him mourning twelve months stopped from death day immediately. In Turei David mourning is always counted from burial, and so Shach there, and Taz omitted intentionally “always,” nevertheless he explains they wanted mourning on yahrzeit so mourning always from the bowl, and on the day before it completed, and sometimes dies today buried tomorrow as said; but if distant two or three days mourning stops after twelve months, end. What is written regarding full or short year — see in Magen Avraham end.
