And not. See the Taz: his view, of blessed memory, here and in Yoreh De'ah 402, letter 9, is that the yahrzeit is always, even in the first year, on the day of death, and afterwards mourning ceases after the yahrzeit even if burial was distant from death by several days, for immediately after twelve months from death the mourning is completed entirely. But the view of the Shach there, letter 10, and in Nekudos HaKesef, is that mourning certainly does not cease except after twelve months from the day of burial, not from the day of death; therefore, if the day of burial is distant from the day of death by two or three days, necessarily in the first year he fasts on the day of burial, for twelve-month mourning begins from burial; and if he were to fast on the day of death he would immediately interrupt mourning, and to fast and observe mourning afterwards is not to be done; therefore necessarily he fasts in year one on the day of burial, and from then on on the day of death. Which is not so when he dies today and is buried tomorrow: even in the first year he fasts on the day of death and ceases mourning on the yahrzeit day, for in any event twelve months have been completed. And that is what is written in Terumas HaDeshen 292, "and also there are those who say," etc.: he gives a reason for the matter; therefore the world customarily observes mourning on the yahrzeit day, for sometimes (and it is somewhat common) he dies today close to nightfall and is buried on the following day; and if they cease their mourning on the yahrzeit, twelve months have still not been completed in full, for twelve-month mourning is counted also from burial, like mourning of seven and thirty days in Yoreh De'ah 375; and therefore it is not surprising that they practiced mourning on the yahrzeit day. And according to this, one can imagine two brothers: one was present at the death and they sent the deceased to another city to be buried there, and the second brother was at the place of burial and it became known to him there; the first begins mourning immediately, as in siman 375, se'if 1, and the second from burial when it became known to him there; and if between death and burial there were two or three days, the first fasts in year one the yahrzeit on the day of death, and the second from the day of burial; examine well there. But the Taz, of blessed memory, holds that regarding twelve-month mourning we always count from the day of death, not from burial, and it is not comparable to mourning of seven and thirty; therefore in year one he fasts on the day of death, and twelve-month mourning is likewise completed. And what Terumas HaDeshen wrote, "and there is a practical difference," etc., possibly also gives some reason: sometimes he dies today and is buried tomorrow, so they observe mourning on the yahrzeit since yesterday twelve months were completed and they extend the yahrzeit as well, and it continues until the day of burial; but if there was a gap between the yahrzeit and burial, mourning certainly ceased after the yahrzeit, for according to him twelve-month mourning ceases from the day of death immediately. And in Terumas HaDeshen it is written that mourning is always counted from the day of burial, and so it is in the Shach there; and in the Taz he deliberately omitted "always," and nevertheless explains that they wished to observe mourning on the yahrzeit so that mourning would always be from the day of burial, and yesterday it was completed, and sometimes he dies today and is buried tomorrow as stated; but if distant by two or three days, it ceases after twelve months; understand. And what he wrote regarding a full year or a short year, God willing will be explained in Magen Avraham, letter 20.
