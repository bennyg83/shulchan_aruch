(There in the Mechaber, se'if 4.) See Chatam Sofer, Orach Chaim, siman 173, and in the Yoreh De'ah section, siman 155 [regarding tikkunei teshuvah for one who had relations with a niddah. The main part of teshuvah is regret, confession, and abandoning the sin. But as for the afflictions to save him from punishment for what he transgressed, we have none who know how far; and one who has a pain goes to a physician to look into the sefarim that speak of this. However, it appears to me to innovate in this matter: the Rokeach wrote to fast forty days for each relation, and it is known that all his words are words of Kabbalah. But the later kabbalists wrote to fast sixty-three days, the gematria of "niddah" with the letters and the inclusive [one]. And one should be concerned to be stringent like their words. However, what is found in the kabbalists' sefarim, that if he wishes to hasten and lighten it upon himself, then if he fasts an interruption of two consecutive days and two nights it is counted as twenty-six days, and in the Shulchan Aruch, Orach Chaim, siman 568, se'if 4, he wrote that it is forty days: for this matter we have no support at all from the Rishonim; therefore one should not rely on this as a count of forty days. But regarding the days that the kabbalists added, they said and they said: among these days he may fast an interruption of two consecutive days and nights to complete; therefore he should fast thirty-eight separate days (for even without this the Magen Avraham there, s.k. 15, wrote that he should fast spaced [days]; see there) and one interruption of two consecutive days and nights, which together are forty days; and according to the view of the kabbalists that the interruption is counted as twenty-six days, it amounts to sixty-four days, and he fulfills both. And it appears to me that it is proper and correct that a baal teshuvah, after he has fulfilled all the proper bounds of teshuvah, fix for himself one day in the year, either the day on which he transgressed or the day on which he set it upon his heart to return to Hashem. That day shall be for him each year a day of fasting and teshuvah with weeping and groaning and confession and regret, in the manner of "and my sin is before me always"; and a fortiori from one for whom a miracle occurred who was in bodily danger and was saved from it, for whom it is proper to fix a day in the year as a remembrance of the miracle: all the more so should he do so for the danger to his soul and his spirit from which he was saved through teshuvah].
(There in Magen Avraham, s.k. 20.) And in the responsum of Maharil. Gloss: And in siman 112 he added that advancing calamity earlier does not apply, since it is only mere distress; see there. And elsewhere I elaborated; and the view of Yaavetz is that the main [practice] is to fast on the second. And I also did not understand: what of it that it is only mere distress? Behold, even Tishah B'Av is only mere distress, and even so they postpone and do not advance, because we do not advance calamity earlier. And see the Yerushalmi that Milchemes brought at the end of the first chapter of Megillah. And further it appears to me: granted that in the language of people unspecified Adar is the first, as we hold like Rebbi Yehudah regarding documents, and consequently regarding one who vows we hold like him, as the Magen Avraham wrote; nevertheless in the language of the Torah the second is primary, for one reads the Megillah in it, as Tosafos wrote at the end of chapter "Konam yayin"; and if so, regarding saying Kaddish on the yahrzeit day, which is the day of judgment of the soul above, certainly that judgment above is in second Adar according to the language of the Torah; and if so, one must necessarily say Kaddishim on it; and consequently it is obvious that the intention of the one who vows is to fast on that day on which they say the Kaddishim; and reason decides thus according to my humble opinion. And see Responsa Chatam Sofer, Orach Chaim, siman 163 and siman 14.
