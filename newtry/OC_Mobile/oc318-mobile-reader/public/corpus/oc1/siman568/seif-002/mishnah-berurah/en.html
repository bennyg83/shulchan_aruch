(9) For a matter of a mitzvah. For example, for a bris milah or a siyum of a tractate, and even if he has no personal stake in it. And nevertheless in all this he must Fast part of the day, a short time more than he is accustomed: for example, if he is accustomed to eat in the fourth hour, he should eat in the fifth hour. And there are those who say that even if they pressed him for joy of companionship it is permitted because of ways of peace; but from the wording of the Shulchan Aruch, which wrote "because of the honor of a great person," it implies specifically because of his honor when he presses him, but because of other people, no.
(10) He borrows and repays, etc. And he need not annul the vow.
(11) At the beginning of the vow. That is to say: even though they accepted it from yesterday at Minchah, since this acceptance is not now the beginning of the vow, but rather from what was agreed by him at first to Fast some day, and there he did not clarify the day.
(12) It is called "this fast" and he is not, etc. See the Taz, whose view is that if he is distressed by his fast and he fasts part of the day, he may borrow and repay another day even on "this day"; but the view of Magen Avraham and several other later authorities is that one may borrow and repay only with acceptance of an unspecified fast, and even there specifically when he has fasted part of the day, as above.
(13) Mondays and Thursdays all year. For it is a designated day, and he cannot exchange it for other days; and also he cannot change it to another year, since he said "the year," meaning specifically this year. But if he vowed to Fast Mondays and Thursdays half a year or a year and did not say "the year," it is not called "this day," and he may borrow and repay on other Mondays and Thursdays after the first half-year or year is finished; but for other days such as Sunday, Tuesday, Wednesday, and Friday he cannot exchange, for perhaps he is particular specifically upon these days that are days of favor. And later authorities wrote that one who vowed to Fast Mondays and Thursdays may exchange and Fast Thursdays and Mondays, for his intention was only for these days that are days of favor.
(14) A dream-fast. Or a yahrzeit.
(15) Even during the Ten Days of Repentance. Meaning: that these days specifically are designated, and one might have thought that it is forbidden to eat.
(16) A bris milah. Or a pidyon haben or another seudas mitzvah.
(17) A mitzvah, etc. And he need not Fast another day for this.
(18) To eat. And it is obvious that in a place where it is permitted to eat at the meal, it is not a Fast at all, and afterwards it is permitted to drink and eat even in his house; however, before the meal it is forbidden to eat and drink in his house; however, the baalei bris are permitted to eat and drink immediately, for it is their festival. And in Sefer Eliyah Rabbah he is in doubt that perhaps specifically after the Minchah prayer; see there.
(19) For they were not accustomed, etc. And if he knows before Rosh Hashanah that he will eat between Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur at a bris milah or other seudos mitzvah, he is obligated to Fast one more day before Rosh Hashanah; for for this reason they Fast four days before Rosh Hashanah corresponding to the four days on which they eat; and all the more so if the seudas mitzvah occurs in the four days before Rosh Hashanah, that they Fast one day corresponding to it. However, if Rosh Hashanah is on Thursday, so that there are only four days before Rosh Hashanah, he need not Fast one day before Shabbos; for originally they accepted upon themselves only to Fast during the days of Selichos and not on the other days.
(20) To Fast, etc. And if day 31 of his son's birth falls on Shabbos, so that he must do the redemption on Sunday, which is a pidyon not in its time: Magen Avraham is in doubt whether it is a seudas mitzvah and it is permitted to eat when it falls during the Ten Days of Repentance or not (and it is not comparable to a bris milah, which is a seudas mitzvah even not in its time; for there every moment is its time so that he not remain uncircumcised; which is not so regarding pidyon haben). And therefore he leans that he should make the meal afterwards at night. [And in Dagul Merevavah he wrote that it is better that he do the redemption and the meal on the night of Motzaei Shabbos; see there.] And the Rama wrote that all this is specifically in such a case; but on other fasts that the congregation decrees, it is forbidden to eat even at a seudas mitzvah. And therefore if a pidyon haben falls on a communal fast day, even though they practice to redeem him on the day of the Fast, they do not make the meal by day but only afterwards at night (Magen Avraham). And in the gloss of Chatam Sofer he leans that even in this he should do the redemption and the meal on the night of the entry of day 31; see there. Magen Avraham further wrote: this requires further study when they Fast the Twentieth of Sivan in the kingdom of Poland, whether it is permitted to eat at a seudas mitzvah [for perhaps it is included in other communal fasts that are forbidden, as above]. However, in our times for the most part they make all the meals at night; however, once the Twentieth of Sivan fell on Erev Shabbos and there was a meal of a bris milah, and the rav ordered [them] to eat by day because people would not be found who would go at night. And in a city where they do not Fast the Twentieth of Sivan, and there are people there from places that do Fast: if they need to complete the quorum, they are permitted to eat even on all the days (Chayei Adam).
(21) And if he accepted upon himself, etc. That is to say: all this is specifically when he did not accept the Fast upon himself, but only relied on the custom, or answered Amen when they bless Mondays and Thursdays; for then he did not accept it upon himself as a vow, and if he wishes not to Fast he is permitted. Therefore even if he did not know that they would invite him to a meal and he began to Fast, nevertheless it is permitted to eat at a seudas mitzvah. But if he accepted the Fast upon himself at Minchah, doing more than the custom, he must fulfill and Fast even at a seudas mitzvah (Levush). And the view of Magen Avraham and some later authorities is that even when he accepted upon himself at Minchah it is permitted, since his acceptance was for the sake of the custom; and the Rama is dealing with one who does not Fast all Mondays and Thursdays or all of the Ten Days of Repentance, but fasts only one day or two days; and it turns out that this one must by law accept at Minchah, and his law is like one who fasts in the middle of the year, for it does not depend on custom; and therefore he is obligated to Fast. And behold, from the Gra it is implied that he explains like the Levush; and nevertheless it appears that in this he may borrow and repay another Fast; for even without this there are several Rishonim who hold that even when he says, "Behold I accept to Fast tomorrow," he may borrow and repay.
(22) In his fast. That is, in a private Fast; but a Fast that the congregation decrees: redemption does not avail unless the congregation stipulated thus.
(23) He may redeem it, etc. Because it is an assessment of intention that with this in mind he accepted: that a Fast is counted as tzedakah; and since he gives the value of his fat and blood to the poor, he has fulfilled.
(24) With money. And the measure of the redemption: the wealthy according to his wealth; for the reason is that the distress of money is weighed against the distress of the Fast; and therefore each according to what he is.
(25) When he accepted upon himself a mere Fast. That is to say, and he did not designate at the time of acceptance for which day; for when he designated and then said, "Behold I am fasting tomorrow," its law is like one who vows for "this day," as above in se'if 2. And see Biur Halacha.
