He may redeem it with money, etc. - Where he accepted upon himself an ordinary fast. See the Rosh in the name of the Ra'avad, that it is an assessment of his intent that he accepted it with this in mind, and what they said, "he may borrow and repay," means that the matter depends on him: if he wishes, he may give their equivalent to the poor and not fast, and if he wishes, he may repay his fast; these are his words. In my humble opinion, we should be lenient in this only according to the cases explained above in which he may borrow and repay, namely where he accepted a fast without specification. But where he says, "I am hereby in a fast tomorrow," where we hold that he may not borrow and repay because he designated that day, all the more so he cannot exchange it for money. Although according to the Ra'avad it is always permitted, that is because he holds that even for "this day" he may borrow; but for us, who hold that with "this day," and likewise when he says "I am hereby in a fast tomorrow," he may not exchange it for another day, certainly the same applies that he cannot exchange it for money. Afterward I found in the Biur HaGra, in his source for this law, and it is proven as I wrote. According to this, it is simple that on a yahrzeit, and all the more so on a ta'anit chalom, he cannot redeem it with money.
