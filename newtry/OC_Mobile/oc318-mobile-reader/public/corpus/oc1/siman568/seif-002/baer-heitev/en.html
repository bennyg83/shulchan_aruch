Monday. It is difficult for me: here he said that in this case one cannot borrow even when distressed, for it is "this day"; and above, siman 552, se'if 11, if Erev Tishah B'Av falls then, he borrows and repays. And in truth there is a dispute among the poskim whether we say even regarding "this day" that one borrows and repays. And one must say that that [ruling] above in siman 552 is not the law, but rather specifically where he fasts up to a large measure of the day and afterwards borrows and repays, as I wrote above; also here it avails if they fast part of the day and afterwards borrow and repay, even on "this day." Taz. That Monday and Thursday are called "this day" and a loan does not avail regarding them: that is specifically when he wishes to fast on another day in place of Monday or Thursday, for we say that he accepted upon himself specifically Monday and Thursday, which are days of mercy; but if he wishes to borrow from this Monday onto another Monday, or onto another Thursday, that is, after they complete the number of days of his vow that he set for himself, he may properly do so. Taz. And that we say Monday and Thursday are like "this day" is specifically if he vowed to fast Mondays and Thursdays in this year; but if he did not say "in this year," it is not called "this day." Magen Avraham, see there.
A mitzvah. It appears to me that if he knows before Rosh Hashanah that he will eat between Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur at a bris milah, he is obligated to fast one more day before Rosh Hashanah; for for that reason they fast four days before Rosh Hashanah corresponding to the four days on which they eat; and all the more so if a milah occurs in the four days before Rosh Hashanah, that they fast one day corresponding to it. However, it appears to me that if Rosh Hashanah is on Thursday, so that there are only four days before Rosh Hashanah, he need not fast one day before Shabbos; for originally they accepted upon themselves only to fast during the days of Selichos and not on the other days. And it is obvious that when a pidyon haben or other seudos mitzvah fall on Mondays and Thursdays or on the Ten Days, it is likewise a mitzvah to eat. But on other fasts that the congregation decrees, it is forbidden to eat even at a seudas mitzvah. And if a pidyon haben falls on a communal fast, the practice is to redeem the son on the day of the fast and to make the meal at night (Shach in Yoreh De'ah, siman 305, s.k. 12). And the Magen Avraham wrote: if day 31 falls on Shabbos and Sunday is a fast of the Ten Days of Repentance, since it is a mitzvah not in its time and one can postpone it further, it is not called a seudas mitzvah, and he should make the meal at night; but in its time it is permitted to eat, as I wrote. And this requires further study regarding when they fast on the Twentieth of Sivan, whether it is permitted to eat at a seudas mitzvah; however, once it fell on Erev Shabbos and there was a milah, and the rav ordered [them] to eat because people would not be found to go at night. And it is obvious that in a place where it is permitted to eat at the meal, it is not a fast at all, and afterwards it is permitted to eat and drink even in his house; however, before the meal it appears to me that it is forbidden to eat in his house; however, the baalei bris are permitted to eat immediately, for it is their festival. Magen Avraham. And see siman 559, s.k. 15, what is written there; see Magen Avraham.
