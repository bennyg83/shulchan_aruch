For example, for a seudas mitzvah in which he has a stake; and nevertheless he must fast part of the day, a short time more than he is accustomed (Terumas HaDeshen, siman 271). However, he brings there that the Or Zarua holds even for joy of companionship it is permitted because of ways of peace; and so wrote Hagahos Asheri; see in Beis Yosef; and so wrote the Levush. Therefore it appears to me to be lenient regarding a seudas mitzvah even though he has no stake in it. And see siman 225, and see siman 551, se'if 10, and siman 444, se'if 7, and siman 558. And in Sefer Beis Shmuel he wrote that chanukas habayis in Eretz Yisrael is a seudas mitzvah, but not in chutz la'aretz. And in Yam Shel Shlomo at the end of chapter 7 of Bava Kamma he wrote: if he did not leave room at first for drinking in his house and for lightheaded conduct, but rather makes a meal to inaugurate it first in order to say words of Torah in it and to expound in it related to the occasion, it is a seudas mitzvah; end quote. It is stated at the end of Berachos: whoever benefits from a meal in which a Torah scholar is present is as if he benefits from the radiance of the Shechinah.
He did not fix the days. And even though they accepted at Minchah, it is permitted (Beis Yosef).
Mondays and Thursdays all year. For it is a designated day, for he fixed specifically this year; see Yoreh De'ah, siman 220. And the Levush wrote that there are those who say this is not called "this day," and he was not precise; for in Terumas HaDeshen he did not write so except according to the view of the Or Zarua, who holds that even "this day" one may borrow, and it is forbidden only a fixed day such as the day on which his father died; but according to us, who hold that on "this day" it is forbidden to borrow, if so this too is included in "this day." And if he vowed to fast Mondays and Thursdays half a year or a year and did not say "this year," it is obvious that it is not called "this day"; and so the Rama wrote in siman 115: one who swore to fast Mondays and Thursdays is permitted to fast other Mondays and Thursdays; and nevertheless it is forbidden to fast another day, for he is particular specifically upon these days because they are days of favor.
Even during the Ten Days of Repentance. Meaning: that these days specifically are designated, and one might have thought that it is forbidden to eat.
It is a mitzvah to eat. It appears to me that if he knew before Rosh Hashanah that he will eat between Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur at a bris milah, he is obligated to fast one more day before Rosh Hashanah; for for that reason they fast four days before Rosh Hashanah corresponding to the four days on which they eat; and all the more so if a milah occurs in the four days before Rosh Hashanah, that they fast one day corresponding to it. However, it appears to me that if it is on Thursday, so that there are only four days before Rosh Hashanah, he need not fast one day before Shabbos; for originally they did not accept upon themselves except to fast during the days of Selichos and not on the other days. And if he fasts until after the congregation leaves the synagogue, it is called a fast, as is written in siman 562, se'if 2; but not earlier than that, for originally they accepted upon themselves thus. And it is obvious that when a pidyon haben or other seudos mitzvah fall on Mondays and Thursdays or during the Ten Days, it is likewise a mitzvah to eat.
For they were not accustomed. But on other fasts that the congregation decrees, it is forbidden to eat even at a seudas mitzvah; and so it is implied in siman 572, se'if 2. And see what I wrote in siman 559, and in Yoreh De'ah, siman 305, in the Shach, s.k. 12, who wrote: if a pidyon haben falls on a communal fast day, they practice to redeem on the day of the fast and to make the meal at night; end quote. And this requires further study: if day 31 falls on Shabbos and Sunday is a fast of the Ten Days of Repentance, whether they are permitted to eat; for Tosafos wrote in Moed Katan at the end of folio 8 that milah is permitted to be done during chol hamoed since its time is fixed, and one should not cancel this because of that; but a meal of pidyon haben requires further study; and one should not say: behold its time is fixed; that is fine in its time; not in its time, how would it be permitted; end quote. And it is difficult: do we not also find milah not in its time? Rather, necessarily milah, even though its time has passed, every moment is its time, for it is forbidden to remain uncircumcised; but pidyon haben, once its time has passed, they can postpone it further, as appears to me according to the view of Tosafos; and if so one may say it is not called a seudas mitzvah; therefore it appears to me that he should make the meal at night; but when it is in its time it is permitted to eat there. And this requires further study regarding when they fast the Twentieth of Sivan in the kingdom of Poland, whether it is permitted to eat at a seudas mitzvah. However, in our times for the most part they make all the meals at night; however, once the Twentieth of Sivan fell on Erev Shabbos and there was a meal of a bris milah, and the rav ordered [them] to eat by day because people would not be found who would go at night. And it is obvious that in a place where it is permitted to eat at the meal, it is not a fast at all, and afterwards it is permitted to eat and drink even in his house; however, before the meal it appears to me that it is forbidden to eat and drink in his house; and so it is implied from what I wrote above in the name of Terumas HaDeshen. However, the baalei bris are permitted to eat immediately, for it is their festival.
And if he accepted, etc. Meaning: specifically when he relied on answering Amen (as is written at the end of siman 563), then it is permitted to eat; but when they accepted at Minchah it is a vow and needs annulment (Levush, siman 492). And the reason is: even though he answered Amen he is not obligated to fast; therefore it is permitted to eat at a seudas mitzvah; and even if he began to fast and did not know that they would invite him to a meal, nevertheless it is permitted. But it appears to me another explanation: for this is the wording of Rabbeinu Beis Yosef in the name of Hagahos Maimoniyos: but if he accepted at Minchah on one of the Days of Repentance, he needs annulment; end quote. And it is difficult: he should have said simply, if he accepted at Minchah he needs annulment, for it also refers to Mondays and Thursdays; rather this is the meaning: if they all fast because of the custom, then even though he accepted the fast upon himself at Minchah it is permitted to eat, for he fasts with the intention of the custom; but if he fasts on one of the Days of Repentance, for then he is not fasting because of the custom but only accepted upon himself now to fast one day, it is forbidden to eat, for it is like one who fasts in the middle of the year, as appears to me.
