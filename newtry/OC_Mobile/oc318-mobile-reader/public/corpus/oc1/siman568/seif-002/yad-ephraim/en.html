In Magen Avraham note 5 that chanukat habayit in Eretz Yisrael is mitzvah but not in chutz laAretz, etc.; see in Tashbetz Katan siman 170 in Laws of Chanukah he wrote Maharam z"l: specifically to praise and thank, but not for feast and joy, and all the more chanukat habayit which is optional, end of his words — implying even in Eretz Yisrael; see below siman 249 and what is written in the name of Yeshuot Yaakov, see there who wrote: but meals of chanukat habayit are certainly friendship, especially in chutz laAretz, and so I found Maharam ruled; nevertheless it seems one who fears the Omnipresent and wishes to inaugurate his house in Torah, etc., see there; in my humble opinion Maharam's reason is he holds seudat mitzvah like there regarding Chanukah days of altar inauguration; however Maharshal there rules chanukah meal is seudat mitzvah, for they established Yom Tov because of chanukat haMizbeach, see there (see above siman 444 that seudat sivlonot the night before wedding, even a meal long before the wedding when they send gifts called seudat kinyan, is seudat mitzvah; see above siman 558 and below siman 244 regarding meal the night before circumcision, see there); also one may distinguish that even if chanukah meal is not seudat mitzvah per Maharam, nevertheless it is only remembrance of what was long ago, and all the more now the Temple is destroyed in chutz laAretz — although they enacted yearly Chanukah days as remembrance, they enacted only to thank and praise, unlike one who inaugurates his house now and makes feast in joy of heart that the Omnipresent benefited him and merited building a house — perhaps it is seudat mitzvah; but Maharam wrote “all the more chanukat habayit,” and it seems therefore Maharshal ruled nevertheless one who fears the Omnipresent and wishes to inaugurate his house in Torah, etc.; although Maharam learned a fortiori from Chanukah and there is no greater inauguration than that — nevertheless it is not comparable, for there is remembrance of the past, unlike here now his heart is toward the matter and the sign is he inaugurates his house in Torah; all the more according to Maharshal that chanukah meal is seudat mitzvah, and at Shelomo's building he made seven-day feast and they did not fast on Yom Kippur as in Moed Katan — although one should not compare to Temple inauguration, nevertheless there is some support to say chanukat habayit for mitzvah is somewhat seudat mitzvah regarding borrowing a non-fixed fast; there is in Yerushalmi Sotah chapter Mashuach Milchamah: can one who builds a house in chutz laAretz return? Scripture teaches “and you did not inaugurate it” — this one is not obligated to inaugurate; Korban HaEdah there from here we learn chanukat habayit meal is not mitzvah, and one who vowed not to eat from a non-mitzvah meal is forbidden; however in Bavli in its chapter “and you did not inaugurate” excludes a robber; Rambam wrote both, see there; perhaps specifically when Israel dwelled there there is no mitzvah in chutz laAretz, for they said whoever dwells in chutz laAretz, etc., unlike now R' Yehudah said whoever ascends from Babylonia, etc., and many poskim hold one need not ascend to Eretz Yisrael — it may be explained as I wrote; if one makes the meal to rejoice that the Omnipresent gave him a house and his inauguration is for the matter, it is somewhat seudat mitzvah; nevertheless for a vow it seems one should not be lenient.
