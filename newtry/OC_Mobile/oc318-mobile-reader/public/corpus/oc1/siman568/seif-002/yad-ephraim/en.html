In Magen Avraham, s.k. 5. That chanukas habayis in Eretz Yisrael is a mitzvah, but not in chutz la'aretz, etc. See in Tashbetz Katan, siman 170, in the laws of Chanukah, where he wrote: and Maharam of blessed memory says specifically to praise and give thanks, but not for feasting and rejoicing, and all the more so for chanukas habayis, which is optional; end quote. And it implies even in Eretz Yisrael. And see below siman 609, and what is written in the name of Yam Shel Shlomo; see there, where he wrote as follows: but meals of inaugurating the house are certainly a path of friendship, especially in our many sins; and so I found that Maharam ruled. Nevertheless it appears that one who fears God and wishes to inaugurate his house with Torah, etc.; see there. And in my humble opinion Maharam [holds] according to his reason, that a seudas mitzvah is like there, when they are the days of the dedication of the altar; however Maharshal there rules that a Chanukah meal is a seudas mitzvah, for they established a festival because of the dedication of the altar; see there. (And see above siman 444, that a meal of sivlonos on the night before the chuppah: even the meal they make a long time before the wedding when they send sivlonos, called a meal of kinyan, is a seudas mitzvah. And see above siman 558 and below siman 640 regarding the meal on the night before the milah; see there.) And one may also distinguish: even if we say that a Chanukah meal is not a seudas mitzvah according to Maharam's view, nevertheless that is because it is only a remembrance of what was in ancient times, and all the more so now that it is destroyed in our many sins; and even though they established to make Chanukah days each year as a remembrance, they were established only to give thanks and to praise. Which is not so regarding one who inaugurates his house now and makes a feast in the joy of his heart that Hashem repaid him and he merited to build himself a house: it is possible that it is a seudas mitzvah. But Maharam wrote "and all the more so for inaugurating a house," and it appears that for that reason Maharshal decided that nevertheless one who fears Hashem and wishes to inaugurate his house with Torah, etc. And even though Maharam learned an a fortiori from Chanukah, and there is no greater inauguration-mitzvah than that, nevertheless this is not comparable to there, for there it is a remembrance of the past; which is not so here, where now his heart is toward Heaven, and a sign of the matter is that he inaugurates his house with Torah. And all the more so according to what Maharshal holds, that a Chanukah meal is a seudas mitzvah; and at the building of Shlomo he made a feast of seven days and they did not fast on Yom Kippur, as is stated in Moed Katan. And even though one should not compare to the inauguration of the Beis HaMikdash, nevertheless there is some support to say that a meal of inaugurating a house for the sake of a mitzvah is somewhat a seudas mitzvah regarding being able to borrow a fast that is not fixed. And it is stated in the Yerushalmi of Sotah, chapter "Mashuach milchamah": Can one who builds a house in chutz la'aretz return [from war]? Scripture teaches "and he has not inaugurated it": that which it is a mitzvah to inaugurate, excluding this which it is not a mitzvah to inaugurate. And Korban HaEdah wrote there: from here we learn that a meal of inaugurating a house is not a mitzvah, and one who vowed not to eat from a meal that is not of a mitzvah is forbidden to eat. However in the Bavli in our chapter it expounds "and he has not inaugurated it" to exclude a robber; but in the Rambam he wrote both, etc.; see there. And possibly this is specifically at a time when Israel was dwelling [in the land], then there is no mitzvah in chutz la'aretz, for they said whoever dwells in chutz la'aretz is like, etc.; which is not so in this time, for R' Yehudah said all who ascend from Babylonia, etc., and the view of several poskim is that in any event there is no mitzvah to ascend to Eretz Yisrael: one may properly say as I wrote. And if one who makes the meal to rejoice that he merited from Hashem a house, and his inauguration is for the sake of Heaven, it is somewhat a seudas mitzvah; nevertheless regarding a vow it appears that one should not be lenient.
