(There, se'if 7.) When the day on which his father or his mother died falls in Adar, etc. Gloss: See Magen Avraham, s.k. 20, regarding one for whom a relative died on the first day of Rosh Chodesh Kislev, and in the following year Cheshvan was short, and he concluded there by distinguishing whether the first year was short; see there. And behold, in my work Sefer HaChaim I concluded not like him, but rather that that day is as if lost and not in the world at all; therefore he should always fix it on the 29th of Cheshvan. But after I reconsidered I regretted this; for we do not advance calamity earlier, as is written in the first chapter of Megillah; therefore, since that day is as if lost, he should fix it on the day of Rosh Chodesh in order to postpone it, and not advance it to the 29th of Cheshvan. Furthermore, since this is a dispute whether the first day of Rosh Chodesh belongs to the coming month or to the past, and from the words of the Magen Avraham himself it is implied that according to those who hold it belongs to the coming [month], the yahrzeit should properly be on Rosh Chodesh; therefore, even though it is a dispute that it belongs to the past month, nevertheless regarding mourning the law follows the lenient view in mourning, and all the more so regarding yahrzeit, which is only a custom; therefore he should always fix it on Rosh Chodesh Kislev, even if the first year was short. And so I ruled as practical law here in the holy community; and especially given the weakness of the generation, if he fixes it on the 29th of Cheshvan he would need to fast; therefore it is proper to fix it on Rosh Chodesh, as appears correct to me according to my humble opinion; examine carefully.
