In Adar. One whose father died on the first day of Rosh Chodesh Kislev, and the following year Cheshvan is short and Rosh Chodesh Kislev is only one day: this requires further study when he should fast; whether on the 29th of Cheshvan, which is the day before Rosh Chodesh Kislev, for always the second day is primary, since we count the moados from the second; or we say that in any event the name of Rosh Chodesh is upon it. And it appears to me that if the first year was short, he should fix it on the 29th of Cheshvan, for the thirtieth day is counted to the past month; but when the first year was also full, then he must fix the day on Rosh Chodesh, and therefore even in the years that follow after them, even if they are short, he should fix it on Rosh Chodesh, for it is like a vow, in which we follow the language of people. And see Taz, s.k. 4. And see siman 55, se'if 10. And if he died on the first day of Rosh Chodesh Adar, he should fast the following year on the first day of Rosh Chodesh of first Adar, and not on the 29th of it; for always first Adar stands in place of second Adar. Magen Avraham.
In both of them. See in Yoreh De'ah, siman 402, in the Shach and in the Taz. And in Knesses HaGedolah in the name of R' Shlomo HaLevi, se'if 16, he wrote that one should fast in the first. And in Sefer Chassidim, siman 712, he wrote: one whose father died in first Adar, and he would fast in a plain year in Shevat and in Adar; see there. And the Magen Avraham wrote: and according to all opinions the mourners need give him Kaddish only once; and similarly Mateh Moshe wrote in the name of Maharshal: one who does not know the day on which his father died should choose for himself one day, but he should not encroach upon others' boundary to say Kaddish.
