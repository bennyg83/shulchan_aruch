In second Adar. For it is similar to one who vowed until Adar: if he knew that the year was intercalated, it is forbidden until second Adar; and likewise here he knew (Beis Yosef; Mahari Weil). And it is difficult: for Rabbeinu Beis Yosef wrote in Yoreh De'ah, siman 220, se'if 8, that according to the Rambam the law is thus, but most poskim hold that even if he knew that it is intercalated, it is until first Adar. And further it is difficult: that is fine if the first year was not intercalated and he began to fast in Adar, then it is similar to a vow; but if the first year was intercalated and he comes to consult in which Adar he should fast, how is it done? And it appears to me to bring a proof from what we say in Taanis, folio 18a, "for an intercalated month," and Rashi explained that the year is intercalated, etc.; it implies explicitly that one practices in second Adar. And they also said there on 18b that the day of Turianus and the day of Nikanor are all practiced in second Adar; for if you do not say so, nothing is challenged from Purim; see there. If so, also the day on which his father died one should practice in the second. And in Terumas HaDeshen he brings a proof to practice in the first from what we say in the first chapter of Megillah, that reading the Megillah in the second is because connecting redemption to redemption is preferable; implying that if not for that reason we would do it in the first; see there. And in Responsa Maharil, siman 31, he rejected this proof because regarding Megillah one would presume to do it in the first, for we do not pass over mitzvos; therefore. And in the Gemara it implies that if not for the verse they would do Purim in both; see there. And possibly also the days written in Megillas Taanis were practiced in both; and so it is somewhat implied at the end of Megillas Taanis, which wrote: there is no difference between first Adar and second Adar except reading the Megillah, etc.; implying that it comes to teach that all the days written in Megillas Taanis were practiced in both. And further, the 14th and 15th are forbidden in eulogy and fasting in both; therefore by law one should fast in both. But since the day on which his father died is only a custom, he need only do as he practiced at first, for originally he accepted upon himself with that in mind. But one who vowed to fast on the day his teacher died and the like must fast in both, as appears to me. And see Yoreh De'ah, siman 402, in the Shach and Taz. And in Knesses HaGedolah in the name of R' Shlomo HaLevi, se'if 16, he wrote that one should fast in the first. And in Sefer Chassidim, siman 712, he wrote: one whose father died in first Adar, and he would fast in a plain year in Shevat and in Adar; end quote. And it appears to me that according to all opinions the mourners need give him Kaddish only once; and similarly Mateh Moshe wrote in the name of Maharshal: one who does not know the day on which his father died should choose for himself one day, but he should not encroach upon others' boundary to say Kaddish. And one whose father died on the first day of Rosh Chodesh Kislev, and the following year Cheshvan is short and Rosh Chodesh Kislev is only one day: this requires further study when he should fast; whether on the 29th of Cheshvan, which is the day before Rosh Chodesh Kislev, for always the second day is primary, since we count the moados from the second; or we say that in any event the name of Rosh Chodesh is upon it, and therefore he should fast on Rosh Chodesh. And in Even HaEzer, siman 126, there are views whether day 30 is counted to the past month or to the coming one. And it appears to me that if the first year is short, he should fix it on the 29th of Cheshvan, for truly in the Gemara of Nedarim it is implied that day 30 is counted to the past month (see what I wrote in siman 55, se'if 10); but when the first year is also full, then he must fix the day on Rosh Chodesh, and therefore even in the years that follow after them, even if they are short, he should fix it on Rosh Chodesh, for it is like a vow, in which we follow the language of people, as Maharil and Mahari Weil wrote. And see Yoreh De'ah, siman 220. And if he died on the first day of Rosh Chodesh Adar, he should fast the following year on the first day of Rosh Chodesh of first Adar, and not on the 29th of it; for always first Adar stands in place of second Adar.
