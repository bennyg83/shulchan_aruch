A circumcision, etc. The same applies to pidyon haben or another mitzvah meal. If the thirty-first day falls on Shabbos and on Sunday there is the fast mentioned above, he should make the meal at night. But a circumcision, even not in its proper time, is performed during the day. When they fast on the twentieth of Sivan in the province of Poland, they make the meal at night, unless it falls on Friday. But regarding other fasts decreed by the public, it is forbidden to eat even at a mitzvah meal, as stated by the Magen Avraham. He further wrote that in a place where it is permitted, it is forbidden to eat in one's home before the meal, except for the participants in the bris, who are permitted to eat immediately, for it is their Yom Tov. But after one ate at the meal, it is permitted for everyone even in his home. This requires study from the end of siman 559, where even a participant in the bris is permitted only after Minchah, and that is from the Gemara. Perhaps these fasts are more lenient than Tisha B'Av that was postponed. Know this, for below it is forbidden to make a meal. Alternatively, this is what he means: they are permitted immediately after Minchah, even before the meal.
