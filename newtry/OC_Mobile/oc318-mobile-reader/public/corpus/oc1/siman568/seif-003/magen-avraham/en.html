And this requires further study, for he should be obligated to fast immediately, lest he die beforehand. And so it is implied in Yoreh De'ah, siman 219, which wrote: one who vowed to buy a house is obligated to buy the first house that is suitable for him. And so Beis Yosef wrote in Choshen Mishpat at the end of siman 75 in the name of the Rivash, siman 335; and Sheyarei Darkei Moshe, that he must repay the vow immediately lest he die. And that which we say at the beginning of Nedarim, specifically when he said "I will not depart from the world until I am a nazir," then he must be a nazir; see there folio 3: that is because you find no other way; for if he said simply "it is upon me to be a nazir," he is a nazir immediately; and if he said "I am a nazir when I wish," behold he said "when I wish"; see there in the Ran. But when he said "I vow to fast one day," he must fast the first day he is able to fast; and all the more so when he is obligated a fast by force of teshuvah, lest he die without teshuvah. And this is the wording of Hagahos Maimoniyos, the secondary ones of Bava Metzia: one who vowed to fast and was forced by circumstance may repay on a short day even though the vow was during long days; end quote. It implies that ideally it is forbidden to borrow; however, for a short time we are not concerned lest he die, as the Rosh wrote in Nedarim.
