Or on Erev Yom Kippur, etc. So wrote the Beit Yosef. This requires study from Sefer Chasidim, siman 226, which states as follows: A Jew had a dream on Erev Yom Kippur and fasted on Erev Yom Kippur and on Yom Kippur, and it did not help him because he did not examine his deeds. This implies that he need not fast after Yom Kippur for the fast of Erev Yom Kippur, and it is difficult to resolve. Now in Taz, siman 288, he wrote that it appears clear to him that even if one did not fast on Yom Kippur because of compulsion, he does not require a fast for his fast, for Yom Kippur atones for positive commandments and negative commandments, and one would certainly be liable for this to many fasts, yet Yom Kippur atones, all the more so in this case. This is not clear, for it is an explicit Mishnah in the sixth chapter of Kerisos that those obligated to bring chatas-offerings and asham-offerings, if Yom Kippur passed over them, are still obligated to bring them after Yom Kippur. In the Gemara there, the same applies to those liable to lashes for whom Yom Kippur passed, and the same applies to this fast. The remaining laws of these matters were explained in siman 688.
