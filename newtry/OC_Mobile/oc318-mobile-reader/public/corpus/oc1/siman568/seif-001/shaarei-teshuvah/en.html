And Birkei Yosef wrote in the name of Nechpeh BaKesef that one who was observing a fast and took some food and recited a blessing over it and then remembered the fast should eat less than a kezayis because of the blessing, and the fast still counts for him; and in Admas Kodesh he murmured about this, and his words do not appear [correct], and likewise Maharam Germizan wrote that one should do thus; see there. (And this requires further study regarding the four fasts, which appear forbidden: as soon as he remembers he should say [an annulment formula for] one who is on his deathbed.) And see there in the name of Pri HaAretz, part 2: if he was eating and they told him that dawn had risen, he should spit it out; and see what is written here in Eshel Avraham on this. And see Machazik Berachah in the name of Eshel Avraham: if he accepted in his mind that the next day, Thursday, would be Erev Rosh Chodesh, and afterwards it became known to him that it was not so, for Rosh Chodesh is on Shabbos, he needs no annulment, and they should annul it for him as a good advice on Yom Tov. And he further wrote: if he said, "I will be fasting on Thursday if I am released from such-and-such a matter," and forgot and did not accept it at the time of Minchah, he is obligated to fast; see there. And see there in the name of the Radbaz, part 2, siman 277: one who vowed to fast one year on Mondays and Thursdays and wishes to annul it, they do not annul for him until the first Monday has entered. And Beis Yehudah, part 2, wrote: one who was accustomed to fast every Erev Rosh Chodesh, and on the day before Erev Rosh Chodesh he was ill and estimated that he could not fast, may annul the day before Erev Rosh Chodesh.
