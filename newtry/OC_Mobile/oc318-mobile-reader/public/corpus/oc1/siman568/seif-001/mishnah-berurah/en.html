(1) And he forgot and ate. And even if he ate intentionally and ate much, he should not eat more.
(2) He completes his fast. That is to say, he is obligated to complete that day, and he does not fulfill by fasting another day, since in his vow he specified this day; namely, that yesterday he said, "Behold I am fasting tomorrow."
(3) And the same applies, etc., to a communal fast. And it appears that on a communal fast, even though he ate, he may say Aneinu in his prayer, for it is fitting to say "answer us on this fast day" as the Sages enacted to fast on it, as above in siman 565. But if it is a private fast, even though he is obligated to complete: so long as he ate a measure that would have lost his fast if he had not vowed "this day," he should not say Aneinu (Nahar Shalom).
(4) And when he began to fast, etc. The reason is: since at the beginning of accepting the Fast he did not designate that day specifically, therefore even though yesterday he accepted upon himself at Minchah and said, "Behold I am fasting tomorrow," he may fulfill his obligation by completing another day.
(5) And he ate a kezayis. But less than a kezayis: even though ideally it is forbidden to eat even a bit, nevertheless after the fact he fasts and completes and needs no other day; and it is forbidden to eat more today, since he accepted it as a fast from yesterday. And more than this: even if he ate a kezayis, only he did not eat it at one time and paused in between more than the time of achilas pras, they do not combine, and its law is like less than a kezayis, which does not lose his fast thereby. And regarding drinking, the measure is if he drank a cheekful; but less than this is not called drinking after the fact, and he completes his fast and need not Fast another day.
(6) And he is obligated to fast. And if he accepted to Fast two consecutive days and forgot and ate at night, he fasts two other days.
(7) Another day. Complete; but on that day it is permitted to eat.
(8) For even with a vow to Fast this day. See Beis Meir, who concludes that this is specifically when he says, "Behold I am fasting tomorrow"; but if he vowed to Fast some designated day and forgot and ate on it, according to all opinions he must complete his fast and need not Fast another day unless his intention is for atonement of his sin and his inadvertence. And the same applies to a dream-fast or a yahrzeit; and all the more so on the four fixed fasts or another communal fast: if he forgot and ate on it, he is certainly obligated to complete the fast and need not Fast another day unless his intention is for atonement. And so it is stated in Maharil, who ordered one to Fast afterwards for atonement.
