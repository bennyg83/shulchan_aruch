And "he ate": we said this only if he swallowed; but if he [only] tasted, no; even if he said an unspecified day, if he did not eat but only tasted, he fasts and completes that day (Yerushalmi, chapter "Konam yayin," and the Rif brought it in the first chapter of Shabbos and in the first chapter of Taanis). And it appears that this "tasting" means tasting up to a revi'is and then spewing it out, as above in siman 567; otherwise it is like actual eating, etc. There are those who say that what we say regarding a known day, that he completes his fast, means if he wishes; but if he does not wish, he borrows his fast and repays even on this day (Hagahos Maimoniyos, chapter 5; and so Beis Yosef wrote in the name of a responsum of the Rashba). And see above in the following se'if whether we hold as practical law that a person may borrow and repay even on this day. And regarding a dream-fast, which one cannot borrow to repay: if he forgot and ate a kezayis, he fasts and completes (Beis Yosef in the name of the Semak). Even if he ate a full meal under a vow of "this day" for a fast, or on a communal fast, he should not eat more that entire day, and he should complete his fast (so it is implied in Terumas HaDeshen, siman 156); and this is obvious.
