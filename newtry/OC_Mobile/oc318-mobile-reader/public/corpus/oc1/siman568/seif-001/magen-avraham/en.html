And he forgot and ate. Even if he ate intentionally and ate much, he should not eat more (Bach).
A kezayis. It appears to me that this is specifically when he ate at one time; but if he paused in between and waited more than the time of achilas pras, they do not combine. And regarding drinking, its measure is a cheekful, as on Yom Kippur; see siman 612, for the Ran compares it entirely to Yom Kippur, and he holds that its measure is a koseves.
And he is obligated to fast. And if he accepted to fast two consecutive days and forgot and ate at night, he fasts two other days (Kol Bo; Beis Yosef, siman 568).
And there are those who are stringent. The reason is: since there are those who say that even on "this day" one may borrow a fast and repay, therefore when he ate he lost his fast and is obligated to fast another day; therefore they are stringent like both views, that he is obligated to fast that entire day and also another day. But regarding a fixed fast, such as the Seventeenth of Tammuz, on which it is forbidden to eat, he need not fast another day unless his intention is to fast for atonement of his sin and his inadvertence (Terumas HaDeshen). And Darkhei Moshe wrote: once someone forgot and ate on the Tenth of Teves, and Maharil ordered that he fast Bahab; end quote. And a communal fast: its law is like the Seventeenth of Tammuz regarding this, that he need not fast another day except for atonement.
