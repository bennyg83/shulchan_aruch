Three days, etc. The practice is to fast another two hours on the night of the fourth day, so that there will be seventy-four hours, and the mnemonic is the verse, 'Return, Israel, until,' and those who eat at the beginning of the night have not lost, for that is the numerical value of chesed, and the numerical value of 'so I will come to the king,' as stated in Mateh Moshe, 784. At the end of the abbreviated Reishis Chochmah it says that those who fast should fast one hour at night, stop one hour while it is still day, and an hour is not literal, for part of the day is like the whole day, as stated by the Magen Avraham. One who fasts six consecutive days can survive, but not more, as stated in Chesed L'Avraham. A minor boy of twelve years and one day who accepted a fast, his vow is valid even if he has not produced two hairs, and the father cannot annul it; see siman 203 in Yoreh Deah. A minor girl of eleven years and one day, her vow is valid, but the father can annul it on the day he hears it until she is twelve years and six months old, as written in Yoreh Deah, siman 234. The Magen Avraham wrote in the next siman that even if the year is a leap year, once six months have arrived she is considered a bogeres. See Even HaEzer, siman 13, regarding a nursing woman.
