(48) "And a compulsory fast occurred among them." This applies even if, when he made his vow, he did not say "these days," but merely accepted upon himself to fast 40 consecutive days beginning today. (49) "They count for him." This applies specifically to a compulsory fast that did not arise through a vow. If he already had a fasting obligation through a vow, for example he vowed to fast every Monday and Thursday and afterward vowed to fast 40 consecutive days, the Monday and Thursday fasts do not count toward the 40, because these are 2 separate vows, unless he explicitly states that he intends them to be included in the count of 40. Nevertheless, one who had already been accustomed to fast during the 10 Days of Repentance, which is itself similar to a vow, and afterward, on 15 Av, vowed to fast 40 consecutive days, may count the 10 Days of Repentance within the 40. His clear intent is to parallel the 40 days during which Moshe ascended the mountain, as stated in seif 4, and it is therefore as though he explicitly said "these 40 days." It is obvious that even if several obligatory fasts occur during them, they count toward his total. One who is accustomed to fast the 10 Days of Repentance and whose father's yahrzeit falls between Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur need not add another fast before Rosh Hashanah on account of that day, just as Tzom Gedaliah and Yom Kippur are obligatory yet count among the 10 days. If his father's yahrzeit falls before Rosh Hashanah during the Selichot period, he must fast 4 additional days besides that yahrzeit. If the yahrzeit falls on the first day of Selichot or on Erev Rosh Hashanah, he need not fast a different day in its place, because those days are permanently included in the established group of 10 fasting days.
