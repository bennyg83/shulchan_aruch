Magen Avraham writes: One who is accustomed to fast during the Ten Days of Repentance, and whose father’s or mother’s yahrzeit occurs between Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur, need not fast five additional days before Rosh Hashanah. This is comparable to Tzom Gedaliah and Yom Kippur, which are obligatory fasts and count toward his customary total. If the yahrzeit occurs before Rosh Hashanah during the days of Selichos, he must fast four additional days besides that day, because he did not establish those specific days as the exclusive days of his practice. If the yahrzeit falls on the first day of Selichos or on Erev Rosh Hashanah, he need not fast another day in its place, because those days are permanently fixed. This requires study where Rosh Hashanah falls on Thursday, leaving only four days, and the yahrzeit falls on Monday. Must he complete another day during the preceding week or not? It appears to me that he need not fast an additional day. The same applies if the community decreed a public fast during that week. It counts toward his customary fasts, and he need not fast another day in its place. A community decreed fasting on Mondays and Thursdays throughout the summer and divided the congregation into four groups, with one quarter fasting each Monday or Thursday. If the seventeenth of Tammuz falls on Thursday, or if the post-Pesach Monday, Thursday, Monday fasts fall on a day when that group would have fasted in any event, it requires study whether they must fast the following Monday in compensation, or whether their own fortune caused the overlap. It appears to me that because they are required to fast specifically as an act of repentance, they must fast another day. Even one who is accustomed to fast on Erev Rosh Chodesh, if it falls on Monday, cannot count the Monday obligation toward that voluntary practice, because an obligatory matter must come from unconsecrated resources. One who is in any event accustomed to fast every Monday, Thursday, and Monday, however, plainly counts the day toward both practices. Magen Avraham. See there.
