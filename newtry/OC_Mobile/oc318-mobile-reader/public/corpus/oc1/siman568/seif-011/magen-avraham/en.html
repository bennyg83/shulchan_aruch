Counts for him. In my humble opinion one who says “I am a nazir when I have a son” — Maharam in Laws of Simchas brought there are two vows, and so any matter of obligation comes only from voluntary matters (Maharil there); if so with two vows such as vowing Monday-Thursday fast then vowing forty consecutive days — Monday-Thursday do not count, as Shach chapter 3 of Shevuot wrote, except he wrote specifically if not literally swallowed within his vow days — e.g. on 15 Av he vowed forty consecutive days and is accustomed to fast ten days of repentance — since not all ten days are swallowed in his vow days he must complete vow count after the ten days as Tosafot chapter 2 Nazir 15, “if I am a nazir when I have a son,” etc., end; requires study, for in the Gemara 13 they say one who says I am a nazir after twenty days and from now one hundred days, etc., counts twenty then thirty then fifty; Tosafot 13 wrote: one who says I am a nazir when I have a son and nazir one hundred — if a son is born within seventy he swallows it and needs no other thirty, as the Gemara teaches his intent was to swallow his son's portion; what he did not say explicitly “I am a nazir when I have a son and from now seventy days” is because his intent was to be nazir one hundred if no son — but here he should have said I fast forty days except he certainly wants forty days without the ten — Rambam ruled even regarding nazir son which is swallowed it does not count, see chapter 4 of Nezirut; all the more here to be strict; nevertheless it seems specifically on other days of the year as written, but if he vowed on 15 Av certainly his intent is only on forty days Moshe was on the mountain as seif 64 — as if he said these forty days, even if several fasts were within they count; see Pri Megadim siman 239 seif 14, Tur, Rabbenu Yerucham, Rama; Tur's view is as written since he should have said I fast two more days and said I fast twenty-two days — his intent on the other twenty-two; but in nazir if he said nazir thirty days then nazir forty days — we say the first thirty count, for he cannot say he should have said nazir ten days since nazir is not less than thirty and there is no room to expand; Taz and Shach wrote there we follow the intent of the vower; one accustomed to ten days repentance who had father's death day between Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur — need not fast five days before Rosh Hashanah per Eshel Avraham and Yom Kippur, for they are obligatory and count; if father's death was before Rosh Hashanah in days of Selichot he must fast four days besides that day since he did not fix those specifically; see siman 288 seif 14; if it fell on first Selichot day or erev Rosh Hashanah he need not fast a corresponding day elsewhere, for those days are fixed forever within the ten days; here requires study if Rosh Hashanah is Thursday leaving only four Selichot days and death on Monday whether he must complete another day the week before or since only Selichot days are fixed for fasting he need not fast another day — so it seems, and the same if the congregation decreed fast that week it counts, see seif 63: congregation decreed Monday-Thursday fast all summer, divided congregation in four parts each Monday-Thursday a quarter fasts — if 17 Tammuz fell on Thursday or communal fast after Pesach when they normally fast — requires study if those whose turn it was must fast the following Monday or we say their fortune caused; it seems since they must fast for repentance they fast the following day; even one accustomed to fast erev Rosh Chodesh and it fell on Monday it does not count, for obligatory matters come only from voluntary, but one accustomed Monday-Thursday anyway clearly counts.
