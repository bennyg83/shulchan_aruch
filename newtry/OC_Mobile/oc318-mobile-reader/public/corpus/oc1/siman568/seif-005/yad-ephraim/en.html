In the Taz, s.k. 4. That after twelve months are completed they are permitted, also, etc. In Terumas HaDeshen there he was in doubt whether we say regarding twelve-month mourning that part of the day is like the whole; and at first he wished to say that we do say so, and afterwards he retracted to say that we do not say so, and one must wait until Nisan enters; and upon this he wrote that the world practices that even on Rosh Chodesh, etc. And that is to say: according to this, at first glance there is no practical difference in the law of part of the day, since they practice mourning even after twelve months are completed; and upon this he said: and possibly because they say Kaddish, etc.; but by law one need not, etc. That is to say: if so, there is properly a practical difference in this question; and in any event according to the law, whether one must wait until Nisan enters or part of it suffices because part of it is like the whole; and he holds to rule that one must wait; but according to the custom there is no practical difference, for they extend mourning until one day after twelve months are completed entirely. And afterwards he wrote that there is also a practical difference for one who fasts, etc.; that even according to the custom there is a practical difference in this ruling that we do not say part of it is like the whole: for example, one who is accustomed to fast on the day of death (that is to say, that there in siman 293 he wrote that there are those who practice fasting on the day of death and also on the day of burial) and says Kaddish, etc., and he was buried the next day, that is to say on the second day after it: for example, if he died on Rosh Chodesh Nisan and was buried on the 3rd of Nisan, then the yahrzeit is Rosh Chodesh Nisan, and the completion of mourning that we count from the day of burial is on the 2nd of Nisan, when twelve months were completed; and in this there is a practical difference in this ruling, that it does not suffice for him with part of the 2nd of Nisan, but rather the twelve months from the day of burial are completed for him until the 3rd of Nisan. And what Terumas HaDeshen wrote "and he was buried the next day" is not precise, but rather means on the day after the day of death; so it is clear to one who examines Terumas HaDeshen in the continuation of his words there. And from now on, what the Taz wrote "Do not err to learn from this," etc., "but rather there in Terumas HaDeshen there was no interruption," etc.: his words are puzzling.
