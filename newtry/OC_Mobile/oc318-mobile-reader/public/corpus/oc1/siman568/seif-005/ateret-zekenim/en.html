Like the law of one who fasts, etc.: that the [makeup] fast should be immediately afterwards (Mordechai, first chapter of Shabbos). A fine [imposed] on one who violated an early cherem: to fast forty days, each week Monday and Thursday; and if he wishes to fast against that three consecutive days and three nights, he is not exempted thereby, so that he set upon his heart his distortions for many days and be atoned through the length of time (and Maharim, siman 62). And two consecutive days are reckoned like seven days (Lechem Min HaShamayim in the laws of fasting). And the Shovavim are the forty days from parshas Shemos until Mishpatim, and they are especially suited regarding the sin of keri more than all other days of the year; and they are in the sod of erev and in the sod of Israel when they were in the exile of Egypt in mortar and bricks, as is known, that they were drops of keri from Adam HaRishon that he begot in the one hundred thirty years, and they were rectified in the exile of Egypt; therefore in the time of these parshiyos there is a segulah to accept the teshuvah of those who fast in them for the sin of keri; therefore one begins from parshas Shemos, when the enslavement begins, and they conclude in parshas Mishpatim for the reason explained among us; but it was not finished to be rectified until the verse "When you acquire a Hebrew servant," in the sod of "If he comes in by himself, he shall go out by himself," to hint that in the month of Aviv they went out rectified.
