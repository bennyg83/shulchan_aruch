[Levush] And regarding vows, follow, etc. This is difficult, for was this what he intended when he said he would fast? The reason that fasting for hours is effective, regarding the fact that he does not require release from the vow, was stated only to mean that the hours he fasted suffice for him, since he borrows and repays. This does not depend on ordinary human language at all, but means that he has fulfilled his vow. So it appears from the Rosh in the first chapter of Taanis, and he forbids in every case of borrowing and repaying. The Shulchan Aruch omitted this, as noted by the Malbushei Yom Tov. So too the Taz explained at the end of siman 552, and so it is also written in Kitzur Piskei HaRosh, that one may borrow even the four fasts and repay after fasting part of the day. So too Rabbeinu Yerucham wrote on page 163. Although from the other authorities, and from the Terumas HaDeshen and Mateh Moshe there, it appears that for an unspecified day even part of the day is not required, nevertheless one should not be lenient against the Rosh and Rabbeinu Yerucham whom I have cited. The Magen Avraham wrote that if one vowed to fast Monday, Thursday, and Monday for half a year or for a year, and he did not say 'this year,' it is obvious that this is not called this specific day. This requires study, for in the Levush he wrote 'or for half a year,' and this is from Terumas HaDeshen, siman 275. Perhaps it too discusses where he said 'this half-year,' but if he merely said an unspecified half-year, he may complete it after he has completed the fixed number. In this manner one may also resolve the words of the Taz; examine there, for I have abbreviated. The Maharam Mintz, siman 115, wrote that one who swore to fast Monday, Thursday, and Monday may change and fast Thursday, Monday, and Thursday, and may also fast intermittently. Nevertheless, another day is forbidden, for he was particular specifically about these days, since they are days of favor.
