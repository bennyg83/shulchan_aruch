Seif katan 3: Who cannot, etc. “Can speak” is not the correct reading. Taz asks that one who cannot speak properly certainly cannot speak before a king. Chemed Moshe answers that the case concerns one whose mind is clear but whose tongue is heavy, as commonly occurs with one who has drunk wine, so that he must speak slowly. One who speaks before a king must in any event speak deliberately and not rapidly. He can therefore speak before a king, although in ordinary conversation with another person, where people do not generally speak so slowly, he is properly described as unable to speak normally. This explanation is correct.
Tosafos in the chapter Hadar, Eruvin 64a, s.v. shikor, writes that this obligation is D'Oraisa. Meaning, since the Sages were uncertain, he must recite the blessing because of the doubt.
As written in siman 99, where Rama rules that an intoxicated person may recite other blessings. This is taken from Mordechai and Hagahos Maimoniyos, who explain that nowadays, in any event, we do not concentrate properly.
