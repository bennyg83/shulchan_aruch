If he blessed and there was feces. I did not descend to the end of the view of the Rabbeinu, author of the Shulchan Aruch, on this—for this follows the words of Tosafot in chapter Hader, and thus: "A drunk shall not pray, and if he prayed—his prayer is an abomination and he did not fulfill," since he cannot speak before the king, and he must return and pray; and likewise in Berakhot: one who was praying and found feces before him—they conclude that he must return and pray; requires study whether one can equate blessings to tefillah regarding feces and drunk; and therefore not to all matters that appear—for a tipsy person shall not pray more than a revi'it, and it is obvious that a tipsy person blesses God and all blessings, as the Yerushalmi says, and even when dozing—end; behold there is no room for doubt in blessings, except specifically if we say that this dozing is tipsy alone; but if we explain that dozing means drunk, nevertheless he is valid, as written in the previous seif to this—there is no doubt here at all regarding drunk nor regarding feces; if so, since he already ruled that a drunk person is valid—meaning he cannot speak properly and is disqualified for tefillah, nevertheless valid for blessings—how did he bring afterward the doubt of Tosafot and Rosh within the time of speech? And the Levush did not bring the doubt regarding drunk but regarding feces, and did not refine well—for certainly drunk and feces are equal, as Tosafot explicitly that I copied; and to me, what the Mechaber wrote in seif 4: all who cannot speak properly is not so bad as a drunk whose measure is that he cannot speak before the king—for this is not certain: whoever does not speak properly is not able to speak before the king; and we do not find a distinction in tefillah in siman 99 except between tipsy and drunk—that tipsy need not return and pray, and drunk must return and pray; meaning tipsy can speak before the king and drunk cannot speak before the king; but there is no distinction within drunk itself; and furthermore Rama wrote there in explanation that the same drunk who is disqualified for tefillah is valid for blessings—so what doubt is here? One must answer with difficulty that the Rabbeinu relied here on seif 4 on what he wrote in seif 5 that there is doubt regarding drunk; and since birkat hamazon is d'oraisa we go stringent and do not exempt him from blessing God; therefore in other blessings he should not bless when he is drunk; still difficult—for Rama in siman 99 equates all blessings to obligation regarding drunk, and here he wrote nothing; requires study.
