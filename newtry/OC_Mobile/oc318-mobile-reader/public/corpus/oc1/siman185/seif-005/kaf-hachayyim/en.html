This implies that if he did not articulate it with his lips but merely contemplated it in his heart, he did not fulfill his obligation, as Beis Yosef writes in the name of Rashi, Rabbeinu Yonah, Rosh, and the Tur. Bach likewise writes that this is the view of all the halachic authorities except the Rambam in chapter 1 of Hilchos Berachos and Sefer Mitzvos Gadol, who hold that he fulfilled his obligation. The Tur holds that he did not, and the Shulchan Aruch rules accordingly. This is our practical ruling; see there. Olas Tamid, paragraph 2; Magen Avraham, paragraph 1; Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 1; Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1. Pri Chadash likewise rules this way in Mayim Chaim, cited by Zechor LeAvraham, paragraph 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 3; Derech HaChaim, paragraph 1.
5) Maamar Mordechai writes there that in a case of doubt, when one is uncertain whether he recited the blessings, it is proper and correct to contemplate them in his heart. He thereby fulfills his obligation at least according to the Rambam and Sefer Mitzvos Gadol. There is no basis whatsoever to say that this involves concern for a blessing in vain, since he does not articulate it with his lips. He writes that Ginas Veradim, rule 1, end of siman 43, rules likewise; see there. Giv'as HaMoreh, siman 23, and Eshel Avraham there write the same. Birkei Yosef, siman 62, appears to agree; see there. We likewise wrote above in siman 157, paragraph 10, in the name of several authorities; see there. Further proof may be brought from the Arizal, of blessed memory, who arranged many Names upon which to direct intent during prayer. As is known, it is forbidden to pronounce these Names aloud, yet they produce an effect above through intention alone. This implies that something forbidden to pronounce may be contemplated and produces an effect above. The same applies in cases of doubtful blessings: although one may not pronounce the blessing because in cases of doubt concerning blessings we rule leniently, one may contemplate it, and it produces an effect above if he is obligated in that blessing. See further what we wrote above in siman 174, paragraph 49, and understand this.
