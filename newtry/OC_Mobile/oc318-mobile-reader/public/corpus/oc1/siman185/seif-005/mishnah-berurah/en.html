(5) If he blessed and there was, etc.—meaning regarding tefillah we hold above siman 76: one who prayed and found feces before him or beside him within four amot in a place where it was fitting to suspect and he was negligent and did not check—must return and pray, because it is said "the sacrifice of the wicked is an abomination"; and the same law applies to shema; Tosafot and Rosh were uncertain whether in birkat hamazon too we say so, or perhaps birkat hamazon is different, for it is lighter than there—for regarding one who is tipsy, even though he can speak before the king, we hold that he should not pray; regarding birkat hamazon, certainly it is permitted when tipsy.
(6) Explanation: completely drunk—meaning he became so drunk that he cannot speak before the king; they were uncertain whether he must return and bless when his wine passes and the food is not yet digested [nevertheless this refers where he did not reach Lot's drunkenness, for otherwise he is considered a fool and exempt from all mitzvot, and according to all he must return and bless]; the previous seif that permits to bless refers to one who can speak before the king; many Acharonim challenged—for since he cannot speak properly, presumably he cannot speak before the king, and nevertheless the Yerushalmi permits. Halacha: the Acharonim conclude that if it happened that he became so drunk, nevertheless he blesses; l'chatchila he should be careful to bless before he comes to that; see above siman 99 s.k.31 in Mishna Berurah.
(7) If he must return and bless—but l'chatchila also they agree that it is forbidden to bless any blessing, and likewise words of Torah opposite feces from the Torah, for it is written "and your camp shall be holy"; is prayer written in the verse? Rather, all holy matters are forbidden to recite in a place that is not clean [Ramban in his Likkutim see there]; they are only uncertain when he already blessed and found afterward; halacha: the Early Authorities and Rav David HaLevi in the name of Bach—when he found feces before him within his four amot, he must return and bless birkat hamazon if the food is not yet digested; and so I found in Ramban's Likkutim on Berakhot, that he brought initially a doubt that the authors of Tosafot doubted this, and afterward concludes that there is no distinction between tefillah and other holy matters—see there, and see Bach.
(8) And because of urine, etc.—this too is in this manner: he blessed birkat hamazon and afterward found urine within four amot; therefore he says "it is obvious," etc., that even l'chatchila it was permitted for him to bless all the while he did not know that urine was here—for opposite urine it is only a rabbinic prohibition, and on their doubt they did not decree; as above siman 76 s.7—that if he had known initially that urine was here and transgressed and blessed opposite them, the Mechaber would not say "it is obvious" that one law applies with finding feces before him [Peri Megadim in the name of Lechem Yehudah see there].
