[5] [Levush:] For regarding prayer, one who is intoxicated, etc. Olas Tamid challenges the Levush: If so, there should likewise be a doubt regarding other blessings, in light of Rama's ruling in siman 99 that an intoxicated person may recite other blessings. His words are astonishing, for Tosafos to Eruvin 64a, s.v. shikor, explicitly indicates that there is indeed a doubt regarding other blessings just as there is regarding Bircas HaMazon. The same is implied by the Rosh, Mordechai, and Agudah there. Olas Tamid further writes that, because of the doubt, he must repeat Bircas HaMazon, since it is D'Oraisa and a doubt concerning a Torah obligation is treated stringently. He adds that this is comparable to a doubt caused by lack of knowledge, which is not treated as a true doubt. In my humble opinion, this is entirely unlike a doubt caused by lack of knowledge. That concept applies, for example, to the case of a broken wing in siman 53, where an expert can determine whether it broke before slaughter. Here, however, the Sages themselves were uncertain, and all halachic doubts are of this kind. Nevertheless, as practical halachah the Bach writes in siman 99 that we rule that he must repeat the blessing, as with Krias Shema and prayer, in accordance with the conclusion implied by Tosafos and Mordechai. This plainly applies both to Bircas HaMazon and to other blessings, and the same is implied by Nachalas Tzvi and by Olas Tamid himself there.
