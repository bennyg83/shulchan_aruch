1) [Seif 1] Birkas HaMazon may be recited in any language. For it is written, 'You shall eat and be satisfied,' etc., meaning in any language in which you recite the blessing. Sotah 33a; Tur and Beis Yosef. This applies specifically when one understands that language, as stated above in siman 62, paragraph 3; see there. It refers to one who does not know how to recite the blessing in Lashon HaKodesh. One who knows how to do so fulfills the mitzvah in the preferred manner by reciting it in Lashon HaKodesh, as Bach writes in siman 193. This is likewise true according to the Arizal, of blessed memory, who provides numerous intentions for the text of Birkas HaMazon, as stated in Shaar HaMitzvos, parashas Eikev, and in the siddur of Rashash, of blessed memory. These can apply only to the established order in Lashon HaKodesh, as we will write regarding some of them below in siman 187, with the help of Heaven. We have already written several times above in the name of the later authorities that even one who does not direct the intentions of the Arizal, of blessed memory, must nevertheless recite that text and follow that order to which the intention applies. In His kindness, may He be exalted, He will arrange them in accordance with the intention that we ought to direct. Therefore, leniency applies specifically to one who does not know how to recite the blessing in Lashon HaKodesh. One who knows must recite it specifically in Lashon HaKodesh. From this you may understand how great the virtue of the mitzvah of Birkas HaMazon is, and how careful one must be to pronounce its words and letters precisely, omitting no letter, especially regarding the intentions of the Names.
