1) [Seif 1] Birkas HaMazon may be recited in any language. For it is written, 'You shall eat and be satisfied,' etc., meaning in any language in which you recite the blessing. Sotah 33a; Tur and Beis Yosef. This applies specifically when one understands that language, as stated above in siman 62, paragraph 3; see there. It refers to one who does not know how to recite the blessing in Lashon HaKodesh. One who knows how to do so fulfills the mitzvah in the preferred manner by reciting it in Lashon HaKodesh, as Bach writes in siman 193. This is likewise true according to the Arizal, of blessed memory, who provides numerous intentions for the text of Birkas HaMazon, as stated in Shaar HaMitzvos, parashas Eikev, and in the siddur of Rashash, of blessed memory. These can apply only to the established order in Lashon HaKodesh, as we will write regarding some of them below in siman 187, with the help of Heaven. We have already written several times above in the name of the later authorities that even one who does not direct the intentions of the Arizal, of blessed memory, must nevertheless recite that text and follow that order to which the intention applies. In His kindness, may He be exalted, He will arrange them in accordance with the intention that we ought to direct. Therefore, leniency applies specifically to one who does not know how to recite the blessing in Lashon HaKodesh. One who knows must recite it specifically in Lashon HaKodesh. From this you may understand how great the virtue of the mitzvah of Birkas HaMazon is, and how careful one must be to pronounce its words and letters precisely, omitting no letter, especially regarding the intentions of the Names.
2) Bach writes that the ordinary letter peh does not appear in Birkas HaMazon because whoever recites Birkas HaMazon with proper intent is not ruled by wrath, fury, or anger, whose Hebrew words contain that letter. Sefer HaChinuch, parashas Eikev, writes that some say whoever is careful about Birkas HaMazon will have his sustenance available to him honorably throughout his life. This is also found in a work called Gan. Sefer Chassidim, siman 46, relates that a man died several years before his appointed time. After twelve months, he appeared to a relative in a dream. The relative asked how he was treated in the world where he now was. He replied: Every day I am judged for not having been meticulous to recite Birkas HaMazon and the blessings over fruit with heartfelt intent. They say to me, 'You directed your intent toward your enjoyment.' The relative asked: Is the judgment of the wicked not limited to twelve months, while more than twelve months have already passed for you? He replied: They do not judge him with punishments as severe as during the first twelve months. Bach; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur, paragraph 1; Ateres Zekeinim, paragraph 1; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1. See our words above in siman 183, paragraph 31, and siman 5, paragraphs 12 and 5, and understand this.
3) There: Birkas HaMazon may be recited, etc. Why do we not recite over it, 'Who has sanctified us with His mitzvos and commanded us to recite Birkas HaMazon,' as with other mitzvos? If one had to recite a blessing over the blessing, saying, 'Who has sanctified us to recite a blessing,' he would likewise require a third blessing over the second, saying, 'Who has sanctified us with His mitzvos and commanded us to recite a blessing over the blessing,' and similarly a fourth and fifth without end. Therefore, no blessing was instituted over any blessing in the world. Beis David, siman 83. In my humble opinion, the reason appears to be that a blessing gives praise to Hashem, may He be exalted, for having privileged us with this mitzvah. This applies only to a mitzvah that is not itself a blessing. Regarding a mitzvah that is itself a blessing, this is inapplicable. Therefore, no blessing was instituted over it.
