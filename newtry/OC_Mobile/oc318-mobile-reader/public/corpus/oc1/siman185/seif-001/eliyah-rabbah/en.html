In any language, etc. The Rokeach writes that all the letters appear in Birkat HaMazon except two particular letters associated with anger, teaching that no anger, wrath, or flood rules over a meal when Birkat HaMazon is recited with concentration, and that one merits the roses surrounding the river in Eden. Sefer HaChinuch writes that one who is careful with Birkat HaMazon will have his sustenance provided honorably throughout his life. Sefer Chasidim, siman 46, tells of a man who died and appeared in a dream to a relative. He said that every day he was judged because during his life he was not careful to recite HaMotzi, Birkat HaMazon, and blessings over fruit with heartfelt concentration. They tell him that he cared only about his own enjoyment. The relative asked: are the wicked not judged only twelve months, and have those months not already passed? He answered that after the first twelve months they no longer judge him with punishments as severe as those of the initial period.
[Levush] That he contemplated, etc. It seems to me that if because of illness or another compulsion he recited Birkat HaMazon mentally, he fulfills his obligation, comparable to a baal keri. Magen Avraham. There is stronger support from the law of Keriat Shema above in siman 62. See also siman 101.
In a loud voice, etc. This applies when the listeners are not proficient in Birkat HaMazon. If they are proficient, there is no need to recite it aloud. So write Lechem Chamudot and the later authorities. Reishit Chochmah, however, implies that one should always bless aloud, because the voice awakens concentration and reminds a person to mention the special insertion for Rosh Chodesh or Shabbos. When blessing silently he may forget it entirely.
