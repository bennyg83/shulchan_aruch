Who cannot speak. It appears to me that he nevertheless can speak before a king, for otherwise Beit Yosef would hold that he may not recite the blessing initially, as stated in seif 5, where there is doubt even after the fact. This is also the view of Tosafos in the chapter Hadar, and the same law applies to all blessings. See the Rosh there and in the chapter Ein Omdin. However, the Rosh's abbreviated rulings to the chapter Hadar imply uncertainty whether one may initially recite a blessing while so intoxicated that he cannot speak before a king, whereas his abbreviated rulings to the chapter Ein Omdin somewhat imply that it is obvious that he may do so. It therefore appears to me that ideally he should recite Bircas HaMazon before reaching such a state. If he nevertheless became intoxicated, he should recite it, since the obligation is D'Oraisa. The plain sense of the Yerushalmi likewise implies that he should recite it, and the same appears from the Zohar, Terumah, page 273. This is certainly true nowadays, when in any event we do not concentrate so well, as stated in siman 99; see there.
