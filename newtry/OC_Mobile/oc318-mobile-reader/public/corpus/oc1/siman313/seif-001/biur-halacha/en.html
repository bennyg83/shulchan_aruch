(א) Even if it is not tied — and when tied one does not need even intent. Bach and Eliyah Rabbah:

(ב) And we do not say, etc. — see in Mishna Berurah; and if it is not standing to open except at distant times — forbidden as adding to a permanent tent, so wrote Magen Avraham in section 7; and Peri Megadim; and perforce we require that the plug be tied and hanging from before the day:

(ג) And that he intend, etc. — see in Beit Yosef that so is the conclusion of Rosh, and not like Ran who requires that it have the status of a utensil like the rod below; however I found that many early authorities hold like him — behold they are Ramban in Milchamot and Rashba in his novellae in his conclusion and R' Eliyahu Mizrachi in his Sefer Yere'im; and perforce requires further study for practical application:

(ד) From erev Shabbat to close with it — namely since they prepared it for this there is no prohibition of handling; but otherwise forbidden due to handling; but regarding building it does not apply to a window plug at all, so wrote Bach; and likewise Peri Megadim; and according to this what Shulchan Aruch wrote "to close with it" is not precise — the same applies if he prepared in intent for some matter it suffices; and so Peri Megadim explained [and he wrote further that if it is a utensil — even if it is a utensil whose melacha is forbidden, such as a board on which one sets a table — it is permitted for bodily need and one does not need even intent]; and so is implied from Rosh that the reason is due to prohibition of handling; and in Biur HaGra he wrote that Shulchan Aruch's reason is like Rashi — that otherwise it appears as adding to the building:

(ה) That it be fit to crush, etc. — and even if he designated only for this — nevertheless it appears to me it is not yet called a utensil whose melacha is forbidden, since it was only mere designation for this; and I found in Orach Chayyim siman 279 letter 2 that he also sides as written; and see there further what he distinguishes in this:

(ו) And one does not lock with it unless, etc. — however when tied one does not need even intent that he intend on it from before the day. Bach and Eliyah Rabbah:

(ז) That it be tied — but with mere intent that he intended on it from erev Shabbat there is a d'oraisa prohibition according to Rashi who holds the halacha that a bolt lying down is forbidden even in the Temple; but according to Rosh there (Eruvin 102a) who holds the halacha is like R' Yosi that lying down is permitted even in the Temple — and because it was fixed from yesterday for this it is not building [as Rashi explained on Shabbos 126a] — if so this prohibition is only d'rabbanan; and especially according to Rabbeinu Tam — see on Shabbos 126a s.v. vehamunach; and all this regarding a bolt that does not have a knob on its head; but when there is a knob on its head — possibly that according to all it is only d'rabbanan, since it has the status of a utensil on it. I again saw in Biur HaGra that he wrote according to Rashi it is permitted through a knob even without tying at all; and nevertheless our doubt remains in its place according to Shulchan Aruch who forbid without tying — even through a knob; if people would think regarding lying down like Rashi's view that it is d'oraisa — possibly that even here with a knob it is d'rabbanan:

(ח) If there is a knob on its head — namely automatically; but if he made a knob for its head — it is implied in Beit Yosef that this according to all is permitted even without tying at all, since he performed an act on it that proves it is a utensil — it does not appear like building at all; and more than this — this is permitted even in nikmatz [namely when the bolt enters the hole of the threshold and pierces beneath it in the ground — explained in seif 2 as forbidden — and in this permitted]; and from Biur HaGra it is proven that he holds according to Tur there is no distinction at all in this — for he holds the law of a handle is one law with the knob of the Mishnah; and all the more one may not permit this in nikmatz according to him — only according to Rif — see there; and see what we wrote nearby:

(ט) With a thin rope — and see in Mishna Berurah in the name of Taz and Gra; and even though Gra wrote nearby that according to Tur it is permitted through a knob even when not tied — namely not tied to the door but to the bolt-bar — nevertheless at least tied; however we require — otherwise we contradict Gra's words against each other, for he wrote explicitly that so is proven in Shulchan Aruch that we require tied; and with this what he concluded afterward is resolved — that according to Rashi it is permitted even when not tied at all — namely that according to him it is permitted even when not tied at all — neither to the door nor to the bolt-bar:
