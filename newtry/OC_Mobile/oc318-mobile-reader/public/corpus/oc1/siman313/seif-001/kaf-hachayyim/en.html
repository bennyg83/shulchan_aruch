1) [Seif 1] A window stopper, etc. It is permitted even over an opening in a roof, because adding to a temporary covering is permitted. Tosafos; Magen Avraham, paragraph 1; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 1; Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1. This means that the ruling does not apply only to a window stopper on the side of a building. Even covering an opening in a roof is permitted because it merely adds to a temporary covering. This is stated there in Tosafos.
2) There: A window stopper, etc. If a corpse is in a house, one may not close a window facing another house to enable Kohanim to enter the other house, because this constitutes making a repair. Whether one may instruct a non-Jew to close it is disputed by the authorities. Responsa Rabbi Yaakov HaLevi, siman 34; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1, however, disagrees and writes that it may be closed even after the person has died and this is not considered making a repair; see there. Eliyah Rabbah at the end of this siman rejects Magen Avraham's words. Yad Aharon, Hagahos HaTur, likewise writes that Rabbi Yaakov HaLevi's view is primary; see there. Tosefes Shabbos at the beginning of siman 315, however, writes that the law follows Magen Avraham. Responsa Beis Yehudah, volume 2, siman 29, likewise challenges Rabbi Yaakov HaLevi and permits it simply, like Magen Avraham. He cites several later authorities who agree. He also writes that iron hinges and locks on a door or window are considered attached, contrary to Taz, and that Panim Meiros, volume 2, siman 92, and Divrei Ben Shlomo, siman 223, agree. After extensive discussion, he concludes that a Kohen may close the doorway even after the person has died and even on Shabbos. He should diligently keep his doors locked, with no one leaving or entering, until the corpse is removed; see there at length. Birkei Yosef and Shaarei Teshuvah cite him.
3) There: Such as a board, etc. This applies even to something that has no status of a utensil at all and that was never used during the week to stop the opening. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1.
4) There: Even if it is not tied. If it is tied, it is permitted even if one gave it no thought at all. Bach; Olas Shabbos, paragraph 1; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1. Similarly, if it was used once before Shabbos to stop the opening, that use gives it the status of a utensil even if one did not intend to use it as a stopper on Shabbos, since this is an item ordinarily designated for that purpose. Shulchan Aruch HaRav there.
5) There: We do not say that it is like adding, etc. This is because it is ordinarily opened and closed frequently. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 2; Eliyah Rabbah there. If it remains fixed for long intervals, however, adding it to a permanent structure is forbidden, as Magen Avraham writes in paragraph 7. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1. Consequently, if it remains fixed for long intervals, it must be tied there before Shabbos.
6) There: Provided that he had it in mind, etc. This removes the prohibition of moving muktzeh. Thought alone suffices, even if he intended to use it as a stopper during the week, as with palm branches in siman 308, seif 20. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 3; Levush Serad.
7) There: Provided that he had it in mind, etc. The reason is muktzeh. Accordingly, if it was a utensil, such as a kneading board whose designated use is forbidden, it may nevertheless be used for its own permitted function and requires no prior thought. Mishbetzos Zahav at the beginning of this siman.
8) There: Provided that he had it in mind, etc. Prior intention before Shabbos is required only for an item ordinarily left there permanently. An item that is never ordinarily left permanently but only temporarily, such as a garment and the like, may be used to stop the opening even if he did not have it in mind before Shabbos. Chayei Adam, klal 39, paragraph 8.
9) There: To stop it with the item. This is not exact. The same applies if he prepared it for any purpose; intention alone suffices. Mishbetzos Zahav at the beginning of this siman.
10) There: A reed that he prepared to serve as a bolt likewise need not be tied to the door. This is the correct reading. Levush Serad.
