(א) (s.k. 1) A window plug, and even a skylight in the roof, etc. And "window" means from the side in the wall; and they thought specifically in its wall it is permitted to add, for it is lenient, for according to Rashi there is no prohibition of making a tent in walls and partitions except in making a roof; we do not rule thus, but rather as is in the chapter Kol HaKelim daf 125b that there is also a prohibition of making a tent in walls; nevertheless they thought walls are lenient and therefore an additional tent was permitted; but not in a skylight in the roof — it teaches us that this is not so:

(ב) R' Yehuda haLevi, etc., from the words of Rashi in Shabbos daf 157b, and so he wrote: and a cracked barrel, etc., and the deceased is placed, etc., and before the death of the deceased they plugged on Shabbat the window with a board, etc., end of Rashi's words; and it refers to what was taught there: an incident in the days of, etc., that they plugged, etc.; and the explanation of the matters I already wrote in siman 306 in Magen Avraham, see there:

(ג) Also in the responsum of Maharil, etc., when they asked him whether the kohanim can compel the relatives of the deceased to remove the deceased when the kohanim wish to enter the synagogue to pray; and he answered that they cannot compel, as is written above siman 311 in Magen Avraham s.k. 14; and he brought proof from Megillah, chapter Bnei HaIr daf 26b, where it states: this synagogue of the Jews of Rome that had an opening to this storeroom (meaning a room) in which a deceased was placed (Rashi: a deceased who was not buried) and kohanim wished to enter to pray; Rava said: lift the chest (for they thought according to Rava that a chest does not receive tumah and interposes before tumah); place it there — meaning that they placed the coffin there in that opening to seal it (and see above in Magen Avraham siman 154 s.k. 14); and why could he not compel to remove the deceased and they would not need to seal by means of the coffin — nevertheless they cannot compel. And Maharil wrote: and it is forced to establish on Shabbat — meaning that in any case they can compel; and that which they needed there to seal and he did not command to remove the deceased is because it was Shabbat and impossible to move the deceased openly, for this is forced since it was not mentioned that it was Shabbat (and he also rejected there that even if it was Shabbat it would be permitted, see there); but if not for the forced explanation he would establish it on Shabbat, and it would be difficult: there the deceased had already died — how did Rava command to seal:

(ד) And further, that Tosafos at the end of the chapter Kol HaKelim daf 126b, on what was taught there: R' Eliezer and the Rabbis disagree — R' Eliezer holds an additional temporary tent is forbidden and the Rabbis hold permitted; and he wished to decide the law like R' Eliezer that it is forbidden, and he challenged: but we taught (daf 137b) an incident, etc., that they plugged the window with a board and tied the measuring rod with reed — to know if there is an opening of a handbreadth in the barrel; and from their words we learned that one plugs and measures and ties (end in siman 306 in Magen Avraham) — behold a practical ruling that they permitted to plug, meaning an additional tent; and Tosafos challenged: perhaps there it is different, even for R' Eliezer it is permitted because there is a mitzvah, as they say there regarding measuring that it was permitted specifically because there was need of a mitzvah; and one could answer: granted regarding measuring, where there is no prohibition except that it is like a weekday act, it was permitted in a place of mitzvah; but an additional tent for R' Eliezer, who compares it to building, is more stringent, and even for need of mitzvah it should not have been permitted; or that we rule like the Rabbis who hold there is no prohibition at all in an additional tent:

(ה) And they challenged in Sefer Ateret Zekenim and in Sefer Teshuvah: what proof do they bring from the words of Tosafos to permit after the death of the deceased — behold R' Yehuda haLevi also deduced from the words of Tosafos that this incident deals before the deceased dies; and one could answer in Sefer Teshuvah responsum 315 that Magen Avraham holds that we elevate a level: if for R' Eliezer in a place of mitzvah it is permitted — according to his view that an additional tent in a place of mitzvah before the deceased dies — if so, for the Rabbis, where there is only concern of fixing, one should permit even after the deceased dies at present. However, if Magen Avraham holds thus from his own reasoning — this is not in my humble opinion sufficient to reject the words of R' Yehuda haLevi; but in my humble opinion, per the plain meaning, Magen Avraham holds even according to R' Yehuda haLevi what Rashi and Tosafos wrote that it deals before the deceased dies — that is specifically per the conclusion that an additional tent is permitted even for a secular matter; but per the initial thought of Tosafos that an additional tent is forbidden and was permitted only for a matter of mitzvah — it must deal after he already died; for if he had not yet died, what mitzvah is there — perhaps he will not die at all on Shabbat and there will be no need on the day of Shabbat; and on a roof, even if he did not come to permit, there is a prohibition, as is explained responsum 315; and further, presumably everyone was concerned that he would die on Shabbat; and if so, if he does not die on Shabbat and dies at Motzei Shabbat — behold he fixed on Shabbat for a weekday need; or per the view of Tosafos it deals when he already died (and per the view of Tosafos who compared and equated the prohibition of plugging with the prohibition of measuring, and measuring was permitted because of mitzvah — it must be that the measuring was after death when they needed the board for the deceased or needed the window; but before death there is no need for mitzvah, as stated); and Tosafos' challenge — from where do they prove from this incident that an additional tent is permitted, not like R' Eliezer — perhaps it deals after he already died and was permitted for need of mitzvah. And accordingly there is no contradiction in Magen Avraham's words, since per the view of Tosafos it must deal after death, and nevertheless, since there was need of mitzvah there — for from that reason measuring was permitted, namely for need of kohanim to enter the synagogue to pray or the study hall to learn and the like — an additional tent was permitted; and all the more the prohibition of fixing, per the conclusion that it follows the Rabbans that an additional tent is permitted even for a secular matter — if so, after death there is only concern of fixing, which should be permitted in a place of mitzvah; and since there it was for need of mitzvah — how did he say that what Rashi and Tosafos wrote "before the deceased dies" is precise — it must be that even after death it is permitted in a place of mitzvah; and therefore it must be that the intent of Rashi and Tosafos is as Magen Avraham explained afterward (and it would be that per the conclusion of Tosafos an additional tent was not permitted because of mitzvah for R' Eliezer; nevertheless the prohibition of fixing and a proof is not more stringent than measuring, which is permitted in a place of mitzvah); and all this Magen Avraham wrote per the view of R' Yehuda haLevi, who considers this fixing; but Magen Avraham concludes that there is no concern of fixing here:

(ו) And it is not fixing, etc. — now he comes to reject the words of R' Yehuda haLevi from reasoning that forbids after death and permits before death: what prohibition of fixing is there more after death than before death — for also after death the vessels that were inside were already impure and it helps only for the future; and in such a case it is not considered fixing, for it was permitted before death even though it would be a fix for the future:

(ז) And what was said "before," etc. — meaning: that which R' Yehuda haLevi deduces from the language of Rashi and Tosafos that wrote "before the deceased dies" — the intent is not that after death it was forbidden, but rather they used ordinary language, for presumably they would fix on the better side, namely before death, so that nothing would become impure:

(ח) And it is clear to Rashi, etc. — meaning: perhaps the language "before he dies" that Rashi and Tosafos wrote is because after death it is forbidden, and not because of fixing, as R' Yehuda haLevi relied upon, since Magen Avraham already wrote that this is not fixing but because of the prohibition of a partition, for we rule in responsum 315 that there is no prohibition in a partition except a partition that permits; but what is made for modesty is permitted; and if so, after death it is like a partition that permits, unlike before death, which is not called a partition that permits, for there is not yet a deceased; and therefore one could say that according to Rashi this is Rashi's intent, for there they plugged the window with a board, and a window is from the side, and Rashi's view is that from the side there is no prohibition of partition and tent even if made to permit (and however, Tosafos daf 125b on "all agree in truth" challenged from this on Rashi's explanation, and according to Rashi's view it must be that that which they plugged the window with a board — the window was above on the roof):
