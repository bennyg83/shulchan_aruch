[1] Provided that he intends, etc. If it is tied, however, no prior intention is required (Bach), as Rabbenu Yerucham, netiv 12, part 16, also implies. His view is that the authorities who permit without designation refer to something regularly used for this purpose. See seif 4.
[2] According to Rashi, etc. Shiltei Gibborim, chapter Kol HaKelim, writes that Rashi and Rabbenu Tam do not disagree. Rashi discusses a reed used as a shoe peg, where there is a building concern, whereas Rabbenu Tam discusses a reed used like a key, where the only issue is movement. Tur and Rabbenu Yerucham there make clear that they do disagree. Nevertheless, this distinction answers why in siman 308, seif 10, Shulchan Aruch and Levush rule without qualification like Rabbenu Tam. Here, because of the building concern, they took Rashi into account. Rabbenu Yerucham there nevertheless writes that the primary view is Rabbenu Tam.
[3] [Levush] Because it is called, etc. A reed used to remove olives from a press, even if also used to lock a door, is considered a utensil whose primary function is prohibited (Magen Avraham). The same applies to a bolt with a knob at its end (Tosafot Yom Tov).
[4] Even if it is tied with a rope, etc. This implies that if it is entirely untied, a knob at its end does not suffice. Bach writes that this is Beit Yosef's conclusion. I am surprised, because Beit Yosef appears to conclude the opposite, and Perishah likewise writes that Beit Yosef agrees the knob suffices. As practical law, it appears to me that one may be lenient when necessary, for I found Tzedah LaDerech and Shiltei Gibborim in the name of Riaz at the end of Eruvin permitting it. Tosafot Yom Tov there writes likewise, Nachalat Tzvi inclines to this view, and Semak, Kol Bo, Semag, and Maharshal's commentary also imply it. Taz's objection that the Gemara should have said the dispute concerns an untied bolt is no difficulty. It says "not bound" incidentally because in the first clause, where it is bound, all agree. This is straightforward.
