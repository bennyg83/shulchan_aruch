(א) A window plug — and even on a skylight in the roof is permitted, and it is not considered a tent, for the tent was already made, and when they plug it is only adding to the tent — meaning covering the opening that was in the tent — and it is also temporary. R' Yehuda haLevi wrote that that which is stated in the gemara that they plugged the window on Shabbat because of the deceased, so that tumah would not enter the house and render the vessels impure — deals specifically before the deceased dies, for then it is only an additional temporary tent, and it is also not yet called fixing; but after his death it is called a complete fix, and Magen Avraham disagrees and holds that this is not called fixing, for the vessels that were inside were already impure and it helps only for the future, that what will afterward be in the house should not become impure; and at present one sides like Magen Avraham, and see in Peri Megadim:

(ב) And we do not say, etc. — since its way is thus to open and close constantly, and see in Biur Halacha:

(ג) And this is when he intends, etc. — and this helps even if he has not yet ever used it for this; and all the more if he already used it, even only once, for the status of a vessel already descended upon it, and he need not even intent; and know that that which we require his intent from erev Shabbat — deals specifically with a thing whose way is to be nullified there; but a thing whose way is never to be nullified but only temporarily, such as a garment and the like — permitted to seal with it even if he did not have intent upon it from erev Shabbat [Chayei Adam]:

(ד) That he prepared it, etc. — meaning that one inserts it next to the door in the wall [and not that he closes the frame or two half-doors with it]; and therefore it is considered somewhat like a bar explained later, that intent does not help for it:

(ה) One need not tie it, etc. — it should also state: one need not, etc.:

(ו) That he prepare it for this — meaning that since he prepared it, there is upon it the status of a vessel and it emerges from the category of building; only according to Rashi one requires specifically that it be fit for some other use; and according to Rabbenu Tam one need not prepare it except to lock with it — in this too there is the status of a vessel upon it and it emerges from the category of building:

(ז) And according to Rabbenu Tam, etc. — and at a time of need one may be lenient like Rabbenu Tam, for thus Shulchan Aruch ruled anonymously above in siman 308 seif 10:

(ח) And it is called, etc. — for the melacha of locking is for permission:

(ט) By means of this, etc. — but a reed with which one crushes olives — even though one locks with it — is called melachto for prohibition, since its essence is made for this; we follow the majority of its use:

(י) And permitted to move it — meaning even not for need of its body and its place, only need of something slight; and this is what Rama noted, as was explained above in siman 308; but in our matter, where we deal regarding locking with it, which is for need of its body — permitted, since there is upon it the status of a vessel, even if it stands to crush olives with it:

(כ) For building — meaning that it appears like other bars and pegs that one inserts in a wall, for in this there is concern of building; and therefore we require that it be tied:

(ל) That it is similar to a vessel — meaning that since it is a vessel it is not like building, for it appears to everyone that temporarily he places it there:

(מ) With a thin rope — it is implied that in any case it is tied; nevertheless we require; and so Taz decided that tying is indispensable; and in Sefer Eliyah Rabbah it is written that at a time of need one may rely on those who are lenient by means of a knob, since it is a vessel even without any tying at all; but in Biur HaGra it is implied that he sides with the law like Taz; and if the bar is attached to the door and they pull it and insert it in the hole — permitted according to all even without tying [Chayei Adam]:

(נ) In it — that if he wishes to move it by means of that rope it immediately breaks:

(ס) And even if it is not, etc. — and one might have thought that it is not so recognizable that it is only for locking:

(ע) In the door bolt — or in the doorpost behind the door:

(פ) And if there is not at its head, etc. — because since the bar does not have the status of a vessel, we require that there be more recognition that it stands only for locking, so that it not appear like building:

(צ) The knot is thick — and otherwise forbidden, even if the bar hangs in the air and is not dragged at all on the ground:

(ק) That it is forbidden only, etc. — it refers to above, where he wrote that it is permitted even with a thin rope; and upon this he concluded and wrote that it is forbidden only when he removes it — meaning when it is not tied at all; and when he removes it from the place of the hole where he locks the door there, he places it in a corner — then it does not appear prepared for locking and is like building:
