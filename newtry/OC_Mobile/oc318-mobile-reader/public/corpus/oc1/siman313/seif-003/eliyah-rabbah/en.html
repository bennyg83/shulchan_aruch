[8] [Levush] Because of striking, etc. Olat Shabbat writes that he does not know why Levush does not say the prohibition is building, since even though building and demolition generally do not apply to utensils, when the piece is driven firmly into place it is actual building and one is liable. Olat Shabbat overlooked that Rashi at the beginning of chapter Kol HaKelim explains exactly so, and Beit Yosef cites him in siman 308. Rashba in Avodat HaKodesh, p. 39, Rosh, and one explanation in Ran likewise state this. It is also surprising that Olat Shabbat contradicts what he himself wrote in siman 308, seif 9. As practical law the matter requires study. Ran's second explanation says that when insertion requires force and craftsmanship, it is regarded as making a new utensil and involves building. He writes similarly in chapter HaBoneh, as do Tosafot there, Semag, Semak, Agudah, and Shiltei Gibborim in the name of Riaz. According to Rashi and his school, however, it appears prohibited even without special force or craftsmanship whenever one is careful that it fit tightly into the hole and presses it firmly. Perhaps this is also Raavad's view cited by Beit Yosef. Rambam, chapter 10, writes that one who restores the door of a pit, cistern, or attached structure is liable for building, implying even without driving it firmly because the doors are attached to the ground. Eruvin 52 explicitly implies this. It appears to me that this is what Maggid Mishneh, chapter 22, and Bach in siman 308, seif katan 6, intended, though they did not spell it out.
