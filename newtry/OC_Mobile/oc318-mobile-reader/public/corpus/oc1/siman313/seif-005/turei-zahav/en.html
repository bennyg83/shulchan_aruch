Forbidden even to force it back, etc. — and Beit Yosef wrote and it was already explained in siman 308 that doors of utensils permitted to take them from them on Shabbat, but not doors of something attached to the ground due to building; and it is implied that one is liable for a chatas both for taking them and for returning them — end of his words; and this due to building that he mentions — namely when returning; and automatically one who takes it is demolishing; and here there is the chapter of one who finds tefillin in a cover of a pit or cistern — that he not return; and if he returned he is liable for a chatas due to building; and if so whoever takes it is demolishing and liable for a chatas; and from this one can learn that one must protest what people customarily do at large meals in summer — removing the window by a non-Jew on Shabbat — and this is a great prohibition, since the Jew is certainly liable for a chatas in this — forbidden through a non-Jew due to shevut; and all the more so those who take the curtains from the windows; and even Jews are accustomed to do this and do not know that there is chatas liability in this:
