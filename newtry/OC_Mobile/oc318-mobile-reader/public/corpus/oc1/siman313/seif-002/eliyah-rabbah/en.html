[5] The threshold became damaged, etc. It appears to me that the prohibition applies specifically where originally there was a complete threshold below, and the hole did not extend to the bottom, but after damage the bolt now enters the ground. If from the outset one made a cavity in the ground, it should be permitted, as people commonly make holes in the wall on both sides and insert a bolt from one side to the other. What difference is there whether it enters a wall or the ground? This is not compelled, however. Bach implies a distinction between a bolt entering a wall and one entering the ground. According to him, even a cavity originally made in the ground remains prohibited. Agudah at the end of Eruvin somewhat implies this. It appears that if it was properly constructed so the bolt cannot bore into the ground, it is permitted.
[6] And it is prohibited, etc. If one made a handle for it so that it resembles a mallet, it is permitted even if it is not tied or hanging. The same applies if one made an iron ring in the middle of the bolt for pulling it back and forth.
