2) There: A window stopper, etc. If a corpse is in a house, one may not close a window facing another house to enable Kohanim to enter the other house, because this constitutes making a repair. Whether one may instruct a non-Jew to close it is disputed by the authorities. Responsa Rabbi Yaakov HaLevi, siman 34; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1, however, disagrees and writes that it may be closed even after the person has died and this is not considered making a repair; see there. Eliyah Rabbah at the end of this siman rejects Magen Avraham's words. Yad Aharon, Hagahos HaTur, likewise writes that Rabbi Yaakov HaLevi's view is primary; see there. Tosefes Shabbos at the beginning of siman 315, however, writes that the law follows Magen Avraham. Responsa Beis Yehudah, volume 2, siman 29, likewise challenges Rabbi Yaakov HaLevi and permits it simply, like Magen Avraham. He cites several later authorities who agree. He also writes that iron hinges and locks on a door or window are considered attached, contrary to Taz, and that Panim Meiros, volume 2, siman 92, and Divrei Ben Shlomo, siman 223, agree. After extensive discussion, he concludes that a Kohen may close the doorway even after the person has died and even on Shabbos. He should diligently keep his doors locked, with no one leaving or entering, until the corpse is removed; see there at length. Birkei Yosef and Shaarei Teshuvah cite him.
