(א) (s.k. 8) Since they are not, etc. As Beit Yosef wrote, it refers to store doors that were made in his place in this manner — permitted since there is carving, etc., it is like a threshold; and since they stand many boards one next to the other, even though they are not joined together, nevertheless it is not one board, since there are many boards; and behold store doors are made to open and lock, and even so they permitted only since there are many boards — it is implied that with an actual one board it is forbidden even in a place made to open and lock:

(ב) But Bach wrote in the name of Tosafos: and that which was taught there regarding a widow's door that drags — one may not lock it on Shabbat; and there are two explanations: one said that it is one board, and one said that it has no lower threshold; and Tur decided, and so Shulchan Aruch, that we go stringently according to both explanations — we require many boards and also that it have a lower threshold; and Tosafos wrote that this widow's door must deal when it has a hinge, for otherwise why specifically a widow's door — even other doors without a hinge are forbidden; and Bach challenged: perhaps it always deals with those that have no hinge; rather it deals with one made for entry and exit — in other doors permitted even without a hinge, and in a widow's door forbidden; or that Tosafos hold that where it is made for entry and exit it does not have the law of a widow's door and has the law of other doors; and Tosafos properly inferred: behold Tosafos hold that in a widow's door, if made for entry and exit, it does not have the law of a widow's door and does not require a threshold, and even one board is permitted:

(ג) And one may be lenient, for Rambam is more lenient. He meant that Rambam decided, since it is a rabbinic matter, we are lenient per the leniencies of both explanations — it suffices with one: if made from many boards, even if it has no threshold; and if it has a threshold, even one board is permitted. Behold Rambam is more lenient on one side than Tosafos: Rambam permitted even if not made for entry and exit, and Tosafos did not permit except when made for entry and exit; nevertheless on one side Tosafos are more lenient, for they hold that where made for entry and exit it does not require a threshold and also one board — where there are two adverse factors — nevertheless permitted; but for Rambach at least one is required, as stated; and if so, what Magen Avraham wrote "and one may be lenient," etc. — the intent is not to be lenient like Bach's view that when made for entry and exit, even if there are two adverse factors it is permitted — for from this there is no proof from Rambam; rather he refers to above, that it is implied in seif 1 that when made for entry and exit, even one board is permitted regarding a window, and it deals where there is only one adverse factor; in this one may rely on Bach in the name of Tosafos to permit, for according to Rambam such a case where there is one favorable factor is permitted even if not made for entry and exit; and it would be that we do not rule like Rambam — nevertheless made for entry and exit one may combine Bach's reasoning in the name of Tosafos and Rambam's view to permit where there is one favorable factor:
