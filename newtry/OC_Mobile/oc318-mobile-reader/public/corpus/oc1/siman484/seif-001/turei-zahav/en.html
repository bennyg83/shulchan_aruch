(א) And afterward he blesses for each one. Although for blessings of enjoyment we hold one cannot bless for others unless he enjoys with them — unlike bread blessings of matzah and kiddush of the day which are obligatory, therefore he can discharge others even without enjoying; and karpas blessing is also a mitzvah and he can discharge others. Rosh wrote: since the enactment is for recognition for children it is like a mitzvah blessing; Maharshal challenged: we say in chapter Afikoman and since it is only for recognition it is not a mitzvah; this requires further study — not that Rosh meant it is truly a mitzvah, rather since the Sages enacted it for recognition there is obligation and it is not like blessings of enjoyment; and practical law: Shach in Yoreh De'ah 214 and Chayei Adam and I wrote in other Acharonim follow Shach there and Chayei Adam; and I saw other Acharonim reconcile Shach's words; see Kaf HaChayyim to Sefer Ketubot Even HaEzer siman 218.

(ב) And he blesses for them Birkas Hamazon — meaning he does not join with them for zimmun, for he did not eat with them.

(ג) He prompts word by word. And there is no taking the Name in vain here, for it is like a minor who recites Hallel and they answer after him what he says; and so too Tur in the name of Rosh. And one might say: behold Rosh holds in chapter 3 "they ate" that one who did not eat can discharge in Birkat Hamazon one who ate — because of partnership; and if so, why is prompting word by word needed here? This is not difficult — for Rosh wrote there that d'rabbanan he cannot discharge, but Semag wrote plainly one can discharge, implying even d'rabbanan; and on this Tur disagrees in siman 186. Tur wrote: what Rav Alfasi [wrote] "and he blesses for them Birkat Hamazon," etc., and Rav Amram wrote there is no enactment to discharge them — for if he blesses Birkat Hamazon for them, then he cannot drink at his home Birkat Hamazon — end of his words; and Beit Yosef explained: since he blesses Birkat Hamazon for them, therefore he must eat a kezayit with them — then he cannot drink again at his home Birkat Hamazon — meaning he cannot eat and drink again at his home, for it is forbidden to eat on Passover night two meals, lest it appear as eating two Pesach offerings; and Rav Amram deals with one who preceded doing the seder in other houses — and there is no place according to this in what he wrote "then he cannot drink at his home Birkat Hamazon." And for practical law Ra'avad holds there is no prohibition when eating twice and blessing for them — from the prohibition of eating appearing like two Pesach offerings; what does this matter to that? — for eating afikoman is in place of the Pesach offering, and he eats it only once; rather the prohibition is that he cannot drink the fourth cup on which he says Hallel, lest he add to the cups — since he blessed Birkat Hamazon a second time, behold there are four cups; and therefore Rav Amram said: since he will bless here on a cup, how will he drink at his home the cup of Birkat Hamazon that he blesses at his home — behold there would be four cups; and what will he do with Hallel, which belongs on the fourth cup? As explained — this is correct. And what Tur wrote afterward "and so Rav Kohen Tzedek," etc. — on the essence of the law he agrees with Ra'avad that there is no enactment to bless Birkat Hamazon twice, only he gives another reason — namely that it is forbidden to eat twice on the night; for since he blessed the first time, eating matzah was removed from him, and although he did not eat afikoman, the kezayit of matzah he already ate counts for his obligation, as above siman 477 in the name of Avi HaEzri and Ittur; if so, what helps him that he eat a second kezayit of matzah later for afikoman — he already fulfilled; but Ra'avad did not give this reason of Rav Kohen Tzedek — for since he intends at the time of the first Birkat Hamazon not to rely on the kezayit of matzah he ate at first, but to return and eat again — then he eats afikoman — as explained; therefore he gives the reason from the prohibition of drinking after four cups; and all this deals only with one who blesses Birkat Hamazon twice — for Ra'avad from his reason, for Rav Kohen Tzedek from his reason; but if he interrupts his eating and goes to another house and eats afikoman there and blesses Birkat Hamazon there, there is no prohibition at all, even for Rav Kohen Tzedek — for in this case it is certainly one eating, as written siman 484 and as written siman 479, as I wrote. Tur further wrote: possibly even Birkat Hamazon one can bless in order to discharge them, since it is obligatory, etc., like dipping vegetables — end; and Beit Yosef understood that Tur wrote this as an explanation of the Rif's words, and it is not so — rather this is his own view, at least unlike Rosh, Rav Kohen Tzedek, and Ra'avad, who found no enactment in this; and if so, why not say that the Rif also does not hold thus — for behold he wrote "and he blesses for them" — meaning "and not he"; their reason for not agreeing to this is as Beit Yosef in the name of Avudraham, who also disagrees with Tur from the Yerushalmi, who said: every mitzvah from which a person is exempt, he discharges the public with it — except Birkat Hamazon, as written "and you shall eat and be satisfied" — one who ate blesses; but Beit Yosef rejected this — for since it is obligatory on this night, it is like the motzi blessing discharging others on this night even though he does not enjoy; but it appears there is another reason that the Rif, Rosh, and other Geonim did not agree to this — for certainly there is a great difference between Birkat Hamazon and other blessings obligated on this night: one cannot say that he discharges another and blesses on his behalf, except for something that is preparation for a mitzvah — namely that the other will perform a mitzvah after this one performs the blessing belonging to it; therefore regarding motzi, eating matzah, and dipping vegetable — properly this one blesses and the other eats that eating of matzah; unlike Birkat Hamazon, which is not preparation for a mitzvah that one can say that through it this one will fulfill the mitzvah — for he already did what he did, only became obligated to the Holy One, blessed be He, to give praise and thanks for the past in what he already ate, and another cannot give praise on his behalf, since the other will not perform a mitzvah because of him afterward; and this matter is exactly like one who sees kings of Israel, who is obligated to bless — can one think that one who already blessed for his seeing, and another comes who does not know the blessing and also sees the king, would require the one who already blessed to bless a second time on his behalf? Certainly not; and no proof is needed to refute this — the same applies here exactly: since there is no fulfillment of a mitzvah for the one who is not expert after this expert blessed Birkat Hamazon, it is certainly a blessing in vain; and it appears that Tur himself was careful about this, for he wrote: since they enacted four cups and enacted one of them on Birkat Hamazon, it is obligatory like dipping vegetables — behold he enacted what I questioned when he said that here too there is fulfillment of a mitzvah after Birkat Hamazon — that he will drink the fourth cup; he did not say the reason that since he has an obligation to bless on what he ate, only that certainly there must afterward be an enactment of a mitzvah; but this is not a true enactment — for Birkat Hamazon has no obligation and connection to the cup he will drink afterward, only praise for eating that passed; only borei peri ha'gafen is relevant to what he will drink afterward, and by virtue of this the Sages relied on it that the third cup be adjacent to it for counting four cups; but Birkat Hamazon was not enacted for the cup — unlike motzi and vegetables, where the blessing comes for them; and this matter is clear in my opinion, and I wondered at our master Tur who did not delve into this — and it is accepted and reasonable.
