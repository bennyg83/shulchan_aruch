His shoe. This does not mean a shoe specifically. Any covering is effective. A shoe is mentioned so that one should not think that because it is subordinate to his body, it is as though he covered the excrement with his bare foot, in which case his body certainly does not constitute a covering. The ruling therefore teaches otherwise.
That it should not, etc. Meaning, even if the excrement does not spread onto the outer sides of the shoe and is not visible. Although with a glass lantern it is permitted even when the excrement touches and adheres to it, because it is not visible from outside, his shoe is worse because it is presently serving as his garment. Therefore, if he removed it and covered the excrement with it, it is permitted.
Touches. See Olas Tamid and Peri Megadim, who incline to say that if, after reciting Krias Shema and its blessings, he found excrement adhering to the underside of his shoe, he must repeat Krias Shema without its blessings. See there for the reason. If the excrement also spread onto the outer sides of the shoe, see below, seif 8, seif katan 30, where this will be discussed.
