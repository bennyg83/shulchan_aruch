(4) His sandal—not specifically a sandal; rather, anything that covers helps. He specified sandal lest you say it is nullified relative to his body and is as if he covered it with his bare foot, for certainly his body is not considered a cover—this he teaches.

(5) That it not be, etc.—meaning even if the excrement does not spread on the sides of the sandal from outside and is not visible; and although in a glass lamp even if touching and adhering to it is permitted because it is not visible from outside, a sandal is worse because now it is his garment; therefore if he removed it and covered with it, it is permitted.

(6) Touching—see in Be'er Heitev and Peri Megadim who lean to say that if after he recited Keriat Shema and its blessings he found excrement adhering to the underside of his sandal, he goes back and recites Keriat Shema without its blessings—see there the reason. And if the excrement spread also on the sides of the sandal from outside, see below seif 8, s"k 30, what is written there.
