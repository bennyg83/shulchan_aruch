Forbidden — the Taz [writes], and the content of his intent: the Rambam, of blessed memory, in chapter 3, halacha 13, uses the analogy of floating on the water — that is, the way the water moves; and the person standing on the bank of the water — even if he distanced the feces from before him, it is from the sides; and therefore it suffices in four amot as behind him, not so before him when it actually passes like something fixed, and one needs as much as one can see. And the Levush likewise [writes] as much as one can see, and it is implied from his words that as much as one can see is forbidden by Torah law, for the Merciful One depends on covering until it is covered from the eye. And see in siman 79 in Taz 1 it will be explained from this; and so too the Perishah. But the Magen Avraham and Eliyah Rabbah appear to be lenient, and see note 4 — the verse it shall not be seen does not refer to passing feces, and four amot suffice, see there — and this is the Rashba in siman 79, seif 2, that regarding passing feces we only require and your camp shall be holy; and in our chiddushim we expanded. And see in siman 79, Taz, sign 1, I will explain in this introduction. And see Darkei Moshe — he agreed somewhat, specifically within four amot; and in the Shulchan Aruch he returned and did not emend anything. And we already wrote: wherever there is a dispute, he returns and reads without its blessings; and in prayer he does not return and pray — per the view that prayer is rabbinic; and even per the view that prayer is Torah law, he does not return and pray. And see the sign after this.
