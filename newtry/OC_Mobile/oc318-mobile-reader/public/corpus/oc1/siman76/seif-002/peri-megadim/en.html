The substance of his intent is that Rambam, in chapter 3, halachah 13, uses the comparison of excrement floating on the surface of the water, meaning along the direction of the water's movement. Thus, when a person stands at the edge of the water and the excrement has moved away from directly before him, it is then at his side, and four amos suffice, as with something behind him. But when it passes directly before him, it is treated like stationary excrement and he must distance himself as far as he can see. Levush likewise writes that he must distance himself as far as he can see, and his words imply that within his line of sight it is forbidden on a Torah level, because the Torah made the matter dependent on covering until it is hidden from the eye. With Hashem's help, this will be explained in siman 79, in Taz seif katan 1. Peri Chadash writes likewise. Magen Avraham and Eliyah Rabbah, however, appear to rule leniently. See Eliyah Rabbah, os 4, who writes that the verse "and you shall not see" does not apply to passing excrement, and that four amos suffice. See there. This follows Rashba in siman 79, seif 2, who maintains that for passing excrement we require only that "your camp shall be holy." I discussed this at length in my novellae, and with Hashem's help I will explain it in siman 79, in Taz seif katan 1, with this principle as an introduction. See Darkei Moshe, who agrees somewhat that the prohibition is specifically within four amos. In Shulchan Aruch, however, Rama ultimately did not add any gloss.
