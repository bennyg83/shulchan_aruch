4) [Seif 2] Excrement in a pit: he places his sandal over it and recites, etc. We do not say that the sandal is subordinate to his body and therefore is not considered a covering. Beit Yosef in the name of the Rosh; Tov Taam, ot 2. Maamar Mordechai, ot 1, writes that the cited words of the Rosh imply that, if he is barefoot, placing his foot over the pit to cover it is ineffective, because his body is not considered a covering. This is also logical, for a covering must be made with another object; this appears clear. Mahari Ayash writes likewise in Beit Yehuda, Orach Chayim, siman 38, and adds that the same applies to covering with other limbs: covering with the body is not considered covering; see there. Batei Knessiyot cites him. He writes likewise in Lechem Yehuda, page 11b, and Yad Aharon, Mahadura Batra, Hagahot HaTur, ot 1, cites him. Yafeh LaLev, ot 2. It appears to me that specifically covering it with his own foot is ineffective, but covering it with another person's foot is certainly effective. Lechem Yehuda there; Yad Aharon there, ot 2.
5) There. Excrement in a pit: he places his sandal over it and recites, etc. Similarly, if a child is sitting on a chamber pot and his garments hang down on both sides to the ground so that nothing of the chamber pot is visible, this is considered a covering and it is permitted to recite opposite it. Beit Yosef there. Batei Knessiyot and Yad Aharon, Mahadura Batra, Hagahot HaTur, ot 3, cite this; Yafeh LaLev, ot 1. Kisei Eliyahu, ot 1, writes likewise. This applies when no foul odor from it reaches him, as Maran writes in this seif and as Yafeh LaLev writes there. See above, ot 1.
6) There. He places his sandal over it and recites, etc. The same applies to another garment or similar object, because Scripture made the matter dependent upon covering. Mishbetzot Zahav, ot 1. A covering is effective even if it is specifically designated for the waste, such as a metal chamber pot with which one covers excrement, because the Torah made the matter dependent upon covering and the excrement is in fact covered. Eshel Avraham, ot 1. See siman 87, seif 1.
7) There. For it is considered covered. One should not cover excrement with hot ashes, because this harms the body from which the excrement emerged. Sefer Chasidim, siman 919; Yafeh LaLev, ot 3.
8) There. And since there is no foul odor, etc. See what we wrote above, ot 1.
