Seif katan 2, To recite, etc. The Bach rules this way because the Beit Yosef writes without qualification in Shulchan Aruch, "It is forbidden to recite opposite it." This implies that its law is like actual excrement, regarding which siman 79 explains that in front of him he must distance himself as far as he can see. The Beit Yosef follows his own approach in his commentary on Rambam's ruling that when excrement is passing by, or a pig's mouth passes by, it is forbidden to recite opposite them until they have moved four amos away. Rambam's wording implies that even in front of him four amos suffice, because passing excrement is treated more leniently than stationary excrement. Mahari likewise writes this. However, because Beit Yosef found no source in the Gemara for this distinction, he explains that Rambam refers to excrement behind him. In front of him, even passing excrement is forbidden as far as he can see. The Bach writes that Rambam's wording there supports Mahari's explanation that even in front of him four amos suffice. As for the absence of an explicit source in the Gemara, perhaps Rambam found this in a Tosefta. The Bach also cites some support from the Gemara. See there. He therefore rules in practice in accordance with that view.
