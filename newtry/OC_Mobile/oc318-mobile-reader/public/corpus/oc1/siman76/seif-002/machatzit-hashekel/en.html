(Siman kaf-bet) To recite, etc. The Bach ruled that since the Rav (Rabbi Yosef Karo) ruled in the Shulchan Aruch that it is forbidden to recite Shema opposite it, it implies that its law is like actual feces, as explained in siman 79 before this, that one must distance a full eyesbreadth — because he follows his view that he wrote in his work on what the Rambam wrote regarding passing feces and a pig’s mouth: it is forbidden to recite opposite them until they pass from him. Another matter: from his wording it implies that even before it suffices with four amot; passing feces is lenient in this compared to fixed feces, and so too Mahari"a. However, because the Rav did not find proof for this distinction in the Gemara, therefore he explained that the Rambam deals with it after it passes, but before it, even with passing feces, it is forbidden as a full eyesbreadth. But the Bach wrote that the Rambam’s words there imply like Mahari"a’s explanation, that even before it four amot suffice, and if because no proof for this was found in the Gemara — perhaps the Rambam found it thus in the Tosefta, and the Beit Yosef also somewhat relied on the Gemara — see there. Therefore the Bach ruled according to his view.
