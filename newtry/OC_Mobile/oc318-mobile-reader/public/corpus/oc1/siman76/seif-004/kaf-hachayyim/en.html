4) [Seif 2] Excrement in a pit: he places his sandal over it and recites, etc. We do not say that the sandal is subordinate to his body and therefore is not considered a covering. Beit Yosef in the name of the Rosh; Tov Taam, ot 2. Maamar Mordechai, ot 1, writes that the cited words of the Rosh imply that, if he is barefoot, placing his foot over the pit to cover it is ineffective, because his body is not considered a covering. This is also logical, for a covering must be made with another object; this appears clear. Mahari Ayash writes likewise in Beit Yehuda, Orach Chayim, siman 38, and adds that the same applies to covering with other limbs: covering with the body is not considered covering; see there. Batei Knessiyot cites him. He writes likewise in Lechem Yehuda, page 11b, and Yad Aharon, Mahadura Batra, Hagahot HaTur, ot 1, cites him. Yafeh LaLev, ot 2. It appears to me that specifically covering it with his own foot is ineffective, but covering it with another person's foot is certainly effective. Lechem Yehuda there; Yad Aharon there, ot 2.
