On his clothing. If it is on his hand and has no substance, but is merely a stain, its odor has dissipated, and it is on his hand in the manner of accumulated sweat residue, he may recite Krias Shema opposite it. Beit Yosef.
A hole. Meaning, there is a partition between him and the privy, and he extended his hand inside the partition. Otherwise, it is forbidden to recite Krias Shema opposite a privy. Rashi.
Permit. For it is written, "Let every neshamah praise Hashem," meaning the mouth and the nose are included, but the other limbs need not be clean. Therefore, even when they are not clean, it is permitted. The reason of those who forbid it is that it is written, "All my bones shall say: Hashem, who is like You?" We therefore require all of them to be clean. The Magen Avraham writes that this implies that if it is on his clothing and covered, it is permitted according to all opinions. See there, and see Peri Chadash.
