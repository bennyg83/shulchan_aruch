And covered by his garments. If it is on his hand and has no substance, but only mere dirt, and its odor has gone, and it is on his hand similar to beads of sweat, it is permitted to read opposite it, Beit Yosef, Talmidei Rabbeinu Yonah, and Rashba.
Through a hole. Rashi wrote: there is a partition between him and the bathroom, and he extended his hand inside beyond the partition. The Baal HaMaor wrote that otherwise it is forbidden to read opposite a bathroom. See what I wrote in siman 83.
Some permit. For it is written, every soul shall praise Hashem, meaning the mouth and nose are included, but not the rest of his limbs; and even if they are not clean, it is permitted.
And some forbid. For it is written, all my bones shall say, Hashem, who is like You; we require that all of them be clean. So it is in the Gemara. This implies that if it is on his garments and covered, it is permitted according to all opinions, and so it implies in siman 78. It is difficult: how is this different from touching it with his sandal, which is forbidden, as written in seif 2? In the Rosh it implies somewhat that the sandal is subordinate to the body, while other garments are not subordinate to the body. One may also answer that since it is the way to walk, the excrement becomes somewhat exposed through his walking, as Talmidei Rabbeinu Yonah wrote according to their approach; see there. The Baal HaMaor also wrote this distinction explicitly.
Its law is like excrement. This implies that if it is on his flesh and covered, it is permitted, and so too according to the first view. In Shabbos 114 they said: any Torah scholar on whose garment semen is found is liable to death because of those who cause hatred. This implies somewhat that regarding tefillin and study, there is no concern. This is the language of the glosses, chapter 3 of Hilchos Yom HaKippurim: and so Maharam ruled regarding a kohen who saw a seminal emission on Yom Kippur, that he may wash the filth; and the same applies to other people who are accustomed to wash it every day during the rest of the year, that on Yom Kippur too it is permitted to wash it. End quote. This implies that there is no prohibition because of prayer, only that it is permitted to wash it, just as one who is soiled with mud. So too it implies in Rashi, who wrote that he should rub off the seminal emission because of interposition, implying that because of prayer we are not particular. However, in Sefer Chasidim, siman 509, it is written that even on his garments it is forbidden. See what I wrote in siman 240, seif 8.
