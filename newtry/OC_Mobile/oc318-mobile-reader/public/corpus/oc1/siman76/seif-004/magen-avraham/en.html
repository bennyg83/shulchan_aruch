And covered in his garments. And if it is on his hand and there is no substance in it but mere filth, and its odor went away, and it is on his hand — similar to beads of sweat — it is permitted to recite opposite it [Beit Yosef, Terumat HaDeshen, and Rashba].

Through a hole. Rashi wrote: there is a partition between him and the bathroom, and he extended his hand inside the partition; and the Me'or wrote that otherwise it is forbidden to recite opposite the bathroom — siman 83, what he wrote.

There are those who permit. For it is written “all the breath shall praise the Omnipresent” — meaning the mouth and nose are included, not his other limbs; and even if they are not clean, it is permitted.

And there are those who forbid. For it is written “all my bones shall say, the Omnipresent, who is like You” — it requires that all be clean [so in the Gemara]. It implies that if it is on his garments and covered, it is permitted for all, and so in siman 78. And it is difficult what he wrote about touching in his sandal that it is forbidden, as he wrote in siman kaf-bet; and in the Rosh it seems somewhat that the sandal is nullified relative to the body and other garments are not nullified relative to the body. And one may explain: since it is usual to walk and the feces is somewhat revealed through walking, as Terumat HaDeshen wrote per their way — see there; and so too the Me'or alongside this distinction.

Its law is like feces. It implies that if it is on his flesh and covered it is permitted, and so for the first view; and in Shabbos page 114 they said: any Torah scholar found with semen on his garment is liable to death because of “making hated” — it implies somewhat that for tefillin and study there is no concern; and this is the wording of Hagahot Maimoniyot chapter 3 of Yom Kippur, and so ruled the Rambam on a kohen who saw semen on Yom Kippur to wash the filth, and the same for other people who are accustomed to wash it every day on the rest of the days of the year — that on Yom Kippur too it is permitted to wash him — so far. It implies there is no prohibition in the matter because of prayer, only that it is permitted to wash him as in the case of Ami soiled with mud; and so in Rashi, who wrote “he shall rub the semen” because of interposition — it implies that for prayer we are not strict. However, in Shaarei Teshuvah siman 499 he wrote that even on his garments it is forbidden — see there siman 200 siman kaf-chet.
