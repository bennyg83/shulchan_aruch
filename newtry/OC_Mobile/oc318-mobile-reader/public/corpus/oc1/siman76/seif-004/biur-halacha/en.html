There was excrement, etc. If his knees are wet with urine to the degree that they are moist enough to wet something else, and they are covered by clothing, see below, siman 78, in Mishnah Berurah.
And it is covered by his clothing. See Peri Megadim, who writes that the required size of the garment covering it is not explained, whether three tefachim are required or three fingerbreadths suffice. I do not understand his intent, for seif 6 states that any amount of excrement may be nullified by covering it with saliva. This demonstrates that no minimum measure is required for the covering. The verse also states explicitly, "You shall dig with it, turn back, and cover, etc." Nor can one say that this case is worse because his flesh is soiled with excrement, for if so, what would be accomplished by a larger covering? See also Peri Megadim regarding the Magen Avraham's statement in seif katan 3 that if it is on his hand, etc., one may recite opposite it. Peri Megadim explains that only another person may recite opposite it, but the person himself may not. See there. I cited all this in Mishnah Berurah, seif katan 10. Although the words of Rashba and Talmidei Rabbeinu Yonah cited by the Beit Yosef imply that even the person himself is permitted, as Levushei Serad also challenges, the second proof of Levushei Serad that Rashba forbids it can be rejected. In truth, however, everything is resolved. Otherwise, one could ask how Magen Avraham could rule without qualification in accordance with Beit Yosef, Talmidei Rabbeinu Yonah, and Rashba to be lenient, when Rambam concludes, "Several Geonim ruled that one may not do so, and it is proper to follow their ruling." Beit Yosef cites this at the end, which indicates that he agrees that one should be stringent in practice. The correct explanation is that Rambam means that according to the strict law it is permitted, since it does not have the status of excrement, but nevertheless it is proper to be stringent in accordance with their view and not recite Krias Shema with hands soiled in this manner, as Kesef Mishneh writes. Accordingly, another person is certainly permitted to recite opposite it. This is Magen Avraham's intent in writing "opposite it." His citation of Talmidei Rabbeinu Yonah and Rashba means that they explicitly agree that it does not have the status of excrement, unlike the Geonim, meaning Rabbeinu Chananel and those like him cited by the Rosh, whom Rambam records as ruling prohibitively in accordance with Rav Chisda. Thus, at least another person may recite opposite it. Perhaps all this is the intent of Peri Megadim in concluding, "See Beit Yosef and you will understand."
They carried it past him, etc., to recite opposite it. See Mishnah Berurah regarding my statement that when they carry it across the width of his field of view, four amos suffice. This is based on the Taz. Although according to Magen Avraham in siman 79, seif katan 2, the sides in front of him are treated like the area directly in front of him, and Levushei Serad likewise writes that one should be stringent here, I did not wish to state this as an unqualified ruling. Eliyah Rabbah is lenient even regarding the sides in front of him, and in this particular matter many later authorities rule like the Bach, even when the excrement is directly in front of him. It therefore appears that one need not be stringent regarding the sides in front of him in this case.
