If there was feces, etc. — and if his knees are moist from urine, within a handbreadth with intent to dab, and covered with a garment — see below in siman 78 in the Mishna Berurah.

And covered in his garments — see in Peri Megadim that he wrote that the measure of the garment that covers has not been explained, whether we require three handbreadths or whether three fingers suffice. And I do not understand his intent, for it is stated in siman 76 that feces, however small, can be nullified by spittle — therefore we do not require any measure for covering. And the verse is explicit: "you shall dig in it and turn back and cover, etc." — to say that here it is stricter because his flesh is soiled from the feces is not so, for if so, of what avail is it if it is a large covering. And see further in Peri Megadim that he wrote regarding what Magen Avraham wrote in note 23: if it is on his hand, etc., that it is permitted to recite Keriat Shema opposite it — specifically opposite it, but he himself is not [permitted] — see there [and I brought all this in Mishna Berurah note 10]. Although from the words of Rashba and Terumat HaDeshen brought in Beit Yosef it implies that he himself is also permitted, and Levush Serad similarly questioned [and the second question that he asked and proved that according to Rashba it is forbidden can be refuted]. However, in truth it is reasonable, for otherwise there would be a question on Magen Avraham: how did he rule leniently in the manner of Beit Yosef and Terumat HaDeshen and Rashba without qualification — did not Rambam conclude on this matter "and several geonim ruled that it is forbidden to do so, and it is proper to act accordingly," and Beit Yosef brought all this at the end — therefore he agrees with their words to be strict in practice. However, in truth everything is reasonable: Rambam's intent is that by the letter of the law it is permitted, for there is no feces there actually present; nevertheless it is proper to be strict as they said, not to recite Keriat Shema with hands soiled as these, as Kesef Mishneh wrote — if so, at any rate afterward it is certainly permitted to recite opposite it, and this is Magen Avraham's intent when he wrote "opposite it." And what he cited from Terumat HaDeshen and Rashba — that is, that they agreed on this explicitly, that there is no actual feces there — and unlike the geonim [namely Rabbenu Chananel and the like brought in Rosh] that Rambam brought, who ruled to forbid like Rav Chisda. If so, at any rate afterward it is permitted to recite opposite it, and perhaps this is all Peri Megadim's intent when he concluded and wrote "and see Beit Yosef and you will understand."

And they carried it past, etc., to recite opposite it — see in Mishna Berurah what he wrote: if they carried it past the breadth of his shoulders, etc. — this is only according to Taz. And although according to what Magen Avraham wrote in siman 79 seif 2, that the sides before him are considered as before him, it would have been proper to be strict also in this; and Levush Serad similarly wrote — nevertheless I did not want to obscure it thus, because on the sides before him Eliyah Rabbah is also lenient, and especially in this matter, where many acharonim rule like Bach even when literally before him — therefore it appears that one need not be strict regarding the sides before him in this.
