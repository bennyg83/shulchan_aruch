[4] It is forbidden, etc. — it implies even as far as his eyes can see, etc.; Levush similarly wrote, and this is how they explained what Rambam wrote in chapter 3: "It is forbidden to recite opposite him until four amot pass from him" — its explanation is not "before him," but the explanation of "opposite him" is from behind him or at his sides. But Darkei Moshe and Bach objected to him that the term "opposite him" is specifically before him; and even so, distancing four amot suffices, for there is a distinction between standing feces and passing feces. And that Beit Yosef questioned that this distinction is not revealed in the Gemara — they wrote to answer that perhaps Rambam saw it thus in the Tosefta. And it appears to me there is proof for their words from the Gemara that challenges at the end of the chapter "One whose dead lies before him," from this that Rav Chisda said: a person stands opposite the latrine and prays — on Rav Huna, who said "before him" one distances as far as his eyes can see — therefore the term "opposite him" is specifically before him; and so it appears from Rashi's explanation there — and examine closely. And it is known that Rambam follows the Gemara's language; and it is strained to think Rambam deals with one passing across the breadth of his shoulders, as Taz and Nachalat Tzvi wrote — that immediately when it passes before him it is not "opposite him" — and examine closely. And further it appears to me: since Rambam had to explain "until four amot pass from him," it proves that he deals even with before him; for if specifically behind him or from the side, he already wrote before this in the fundamental law of feces that four amot distancing is required. Further it appears to me: revelation and proof from Gemara page 25, which states the reason on this that passing feces is forbidden: "and your camp shall be holy" the Merciful One said — and here there is none — up to here; and he did not mention either the verse "so that He will not see in you." And similarly Raavad siman 163 did not write that it is forbidden except because of "and your camp shall be holy" — but perforce there is no prohibition because of "so that He will not see" but only because of "your camp shall be holy" alone. If so, since he distances four amot he is not in His camp, as will be explained in siman 79 in the Heavenly seif. Further it appears to me some proof from what Rav Papa said: a pig's mouth is like passing feces — he should have said simply "like feces" — but passing feces is lighter than standing; therefore he said "like passing feces" — for even though a pig passes before him, one might have said it should not be like feces, since passing feces itself we maintain — nevertheless, a pig's mouth is not like it; it teaches us that even so it is considered like it and forbidden. And that the Gemara challenges "obvious" — this means it challenges that this too is obvious, that there is no pig's mouth without feces, as Rashi explained there — and examine closely; therefore the main ruling appears like the words of Darkei Moshe and Bach.
