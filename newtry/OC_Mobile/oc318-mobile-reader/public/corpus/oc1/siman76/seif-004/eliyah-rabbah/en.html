It is forbidden, etc. This implies even as far as his eye can see, etc.; so the Levush wrote. That is, they explained what the Rambam wrote in chapter 3, that it is forbidden to read opposite it until it passes four cubits, not as referring to in front of him. Rather, opposite it means behind him or to his sides. But in Darchei Moshe and the Bach they challenged him, for the expression opposite it means specifically in front of him, and even so a distance of four cubits suffices, for there is a distinction between stationary excrement and passing excrement. Regarding what the Beit Yosef asked, that there is no indication of this distinction in the Gemara, they answered that it is possible that the Rambam saw this in the Tosefta. It appears to me that there is a proof for their words from the Gemara, which asks at the end of the chapter Mi Shemeiso from Rav Chisda's statement that a person may stand opposite a bathroom and pray, against Rav Huna, who says that in front of him he must distance as far as his eye can see. Thus, the expression opposite it means specifically in front of him. So it also implies from Rashi's explanation there, and examine this well. It is known that the Rambam follows the language of the Gemara. It is forced to think that the Rambam speaks where it passes across his width, as the Taz and Nachalas Tzvi wrote, for as soon as it passes before him it would no longer be opposite him; examine this well. It also appears to me that since the Rambam needed to explain, until they pass four cubits from him, this proves that he speaks even of something in front of him. For if he meant only behind him or to the side, he already wrote before this in the main law of excrement that one must distance four cubits. It also appears to me that there is indication and proof from the Gemara, page 25, which gives the reason for why passing excrement is forbidden: the Torah said, and your camp shall be holy, and here it is not. End quote. It did not also mention the verse, and He shall not see in you, etc. Similarly, in Raavan, siman 163, he wrote that it is forbidden only because of, and your camp shall be holy. Perforce, there is no prohibition because of, He shall not see, but only because of, your camp shall be holy. If so, once he distances four cubits, it is not in his camp, as will be explained in siman 79, with the help of Heaven. It also appears to me there is some proof from the statement of Rav Pappa that the mouth of a pig is like passing excrement. He should have said simply, it is like excrement. Rather, passing excrement is more lenient than stationary excrement; therefore he said it is like passing excrement, teaching that even though the pig passes before him and one might have said it is not like excrement, since passing excrement itself is what we hold, nevertheless the mouth of a pig is like it and forbidden. And when the Gemara asks that this is obvious, it means to ask that it is also obvious that a pig's mouth is not without excrement, as Rashi explained there. Examine this well. Therefore the words of the Darchei Moshe and Bach appear primary.
