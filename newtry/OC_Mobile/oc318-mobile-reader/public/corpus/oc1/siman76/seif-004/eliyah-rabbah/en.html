And some forbid, etc. This implies that if it was on his garments and covered, it is permitted according to all opinions. Above in seif 2, regarding one who touches it with his sandal, one can say as Sefer Tamim De'im, siman 240, wrote, that there it is completely exposed when he lifts his foot. End quote. One can also say that the sandal is subordinate to the body, unlike other garments. See later, siman 87, where it implies that the sandal is like a chamber pot of excrement.
And some say that they permitted only, etc. Lechem Chamudos, page 23, wrote: after the Rambam wrote to rule like the Rif, he again wrote that several geonim ruled that if his hands were soiled it is forbidden for him to read, and this is proper to do. Similarly, in the Shulchan Aruch, after he wrote all three, he wrote that it is proper to act according to the stringent view. I do not know why the Levush omitted this. End quote. In my humble opinion, the Levush aimed well by omitting it, for I saw that Rabbeinu Yerucham, page 26, wrote that the majority agree that it is permitted. So too, in the Beit Yosef, his view appears to permit it, except that at the end he brought the aforementioned words of the Rambam, and therefore he wrote so in the Shulchan Aruch. So the view of Lechem Chamudos also implies, and the Bach ruled like the Shulchan Aruch because of the aforementioned words of the Rambam; see there. So too, Nachalas Tzvi wrote that this is the reason of the Shulchan Aruch. This is not clear. Although the Rambam concluded that this is proper to do, nevertheless I saw in Kol Bo, page 5, that he copied the beginning of the Rambam's words, permitting without qualification, and did not copy this conclusion at all, that it is proper to be stringent. Thus the later authorities were not concerned for this stringency. It also appears to me that there is no proof at all from the Rambam, for he had the reading in the Gemara, his hands are soiled, and he explains it as literally soiled hands, and speaks where they are not covered, as is explicit in the Rambam there. Therefore he properly wrote that one should be stringent. But according to us, who hold that we do not read, his hands are soiled, it is not speaking of soiled hands at all, but of one who inserted his hands into the bathroom through a hole. In this, it appears that the Rambam agrees that one may permit it. So I found explicitly in Sefer Tamim De'im, siman 240, that the Raavad distinguishes this way and rules so. It appears that the same applies to excrement on his flesh: since it is covered, the Rambam agrees that it is permitted. Therefore the permitting view appears primary to me. Nevertheless, one should be stringent like the third opinion. The Bach wrote: nevertheless, certainly with soiled hands, or where he inserted them through a hole, if he has no water, he wipes his hands with a pebble or with something that cleans, and reads Krias Shema and blesses. End quote. At first glance this is astonishing. If he inserted them through a hole, what does wiping help? Even washing would not help, for it is like one who immerses while holding a sheretz in his hand. And if it speaks where he withdrew them from the hole, it is difficult: why do they need wiping, since they are not soiled? In the Gemara and poskim they dispute only when they are in the hole of the bathroom. I then saw in Kesef Mishneh there that he wrote that the explanation of the Gemara was proven to be: his hands are soiled from the bathroom, meaning that he inserted them there and they received filth from its grime and from the air of the bathroom. End quote. It is possible that the Bach speaks of such a case.
Its law is like excrement, etc. So Maharil wrote in the laws of Yom Kippur, and concluded: if it is still moist, he should wipe it well; if it is dry, he should scrape it off; and if he cannot scrape it, he should rub it with water. End quote. See later, siman 617, and the Beit Yosef there. It implies that if it is on his garments and covered, it is permitted according to all opinions. But the Shelah wrote in siman 66: one who is married should be careful not to wipe himself with the shirt he is wearing after separating from his wife following relations. If semen comes upon his shirt for any reason, he should not wear it unless he washes it well. He also wrote that it is good for a person who cannot immerse to rub with water his hands and his ervah, to purify himself from the attached seminal emission. See later, siman 240, seif katan 15.
