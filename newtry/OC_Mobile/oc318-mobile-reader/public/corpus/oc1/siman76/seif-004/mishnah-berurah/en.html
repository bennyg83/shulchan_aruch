On his clothing. If a person's hands are soiled from wiping himself after using the privy, but no actual excrement remains, only ordinary grime whose odor has dissipated, comparable to accumulated sweat residue, another person may recite opposite it even without covering it. This is specifically another person, because it is not considered excrement. The person himself, however, may not recite Krias Shema or pray while his hands are soiled with accumulated sweat or from scratching his head until he washes them. If he has no water, he must at least clean his hands, as explained above in siman 4. This is from Peri Megadim. See Biur Halachah.
A hole. Meaning, there is a partition between him and the privy, and he extended his hand beyond the partition. Otherwise, some authorities in siman 83 hold that it is forbidden to recite opposite the wall of a privy because it is treated like excrement.
Permit. Their reason is the verse, "Let every soul praise Hashem," meaning that the mouth and nose are included in the praise, but not the other limbs. Therefore, even if the other limbs are unclean, it is permitted. The prohibitive opinion reasons from the verse, "All my bones shall say: Hashem, who is like You?" which requires that all of them be clean. It is obvious that according to this, if one of his limbs is within four amos of excrement, it is likewise forbidden for this reason. If the excrement is on his clothing and covered above, it is permitted according to all opinions. Nevertheless, every person should be careful that his clothing is always clean, especially during prayer.
Such as the joints of his arms. Meaning, covering them with clothing is effective. This is from Peri Megadim.
In accordance with the stringent opinion. In a pressing circumstance one may rely on the permissive opinion, which holds that only the mouth and nose are included in the praise, but not the other limbs. This is from Chayei Adam.
Its law is like excrement. Meaning, one should therefore be stringent when it is on his flesh even though it is covered, just as with excrement, as explained above. If it is on his clothing and covered, it is permitted according to all opinions, as explained above regarding excrement. Some say that it is not like excrement. However, Sefer Chassidim, siman 509, indicates that even when it is on his clothing and covered, he should wash it off or remove the garment so that the impurity not remain attached to him. All the more so if, Heaven forbid, he experienced a seminal emission through sin, so that it not remain as a reminder of the transgression.
