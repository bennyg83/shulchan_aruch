(10) In his garments—and one whose hands are soiled from wiping in a lavatory, if there is no substance of excrement but only ordinary filth and its odor has gone and it is on his hand, is similar to wiping sweat—is permitted to recite facing it even without cover, specifically afterward, because it is not like excrement; but he himself, whenever his hands are soiled from wiping sweat and scratching the head, it is forbidden to recite Keriat Shema and prayer until he washes his hands, or at least if he has no water he cleans his hands, as above in siman 4 [Peri Megadim]; see Biur Halacha.

(11) Hole—meaning there is a partition between him and the lavatory and he extended his hand inside the partition; for if not, behold some say in siman 83 that it is forbidden to recite facing the partition of a lavatory since it is considered like excrement.

(12) Permit—for it is written "Let every soul praise the Omnipresent"—meaning the mouth and nose are included in praise, but not other limbs, for even if they are not clean it is permitted per the permissive view. And those who forbid—their reason is because it is written "All my bones shall say, the Omnipresent, who is like You"—we require that all be clean. And it is simple that therefore if one of his limbs is within four amot of the excrement it is also forbidden for this reason. And if the excrement is on his garments and covered from above, all permit. Nevertheless every person should beware that his garments always be clean, especially at prayer.

(13) Such as under his arms—meaning then garment cover helps [Peri Megadim].

(14) Per the stringent view—and in time of pressure one may rely on the permissive view that specifically mouth and nose are included in praise but not other limbs [Chayei Adam].

(15) Its law is like excrement—meaning one should be stringent with it when on his flesh even if covered, like excrement and as above; but when on his garments and covered, all permit, as with excrement. And some say it is not like excrement; nevertheless in Shaarei Teshuvah siman 299 it is evident that even on his garments and covered he should wash or remove so impurity not adhere to him—and how much more when he stumbled, the Omnipresent forbid, through keri that it not be a reminder of prohibition.
