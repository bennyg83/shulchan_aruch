That [his sandal] not [touch feces] — the Taz [writes]: regarding a lantern, and if the lantern touches feces it is permitted, for nevertheless it is a covering; not so a sandal touching feces, for it is his garment and he knows there is a question in Berachos 25b, and it was not resolved. The Rosh wrote stringently, and they explained as written in the Shulchan Aruch; and the Rosh and R' Eliezer in chapter 3 of the laws of Kerias Shema explained it in a pit — four [amot] regarding touching, and specifically when he stepped and it is stuck to it; nevertheless it is not a safek safeka, for after the ruling of the Mechaber and the Rambam there is nothing [left to doubt]. And behold, a sandal — likewise another garment and the like, for the verse regarding covering does not depend on interrupting a domain (as written in Magen Avraham 1). And one could say: if the question is from the reason of a Torah prohibition, or nevertheless it is permitted since it does not touch his body and is considered covered — only rabbinically, and stringently, for one does not make a rabbinic safek lechatchilah, as the Taz wrote in siman 3. Also, if we say nevertheless it is required — still, if when he said the two blessings before Shema and set down his sandal and it touched, was he exempt from blessing again? For a doubt in blessings is rabbinic and lenient; and furthermore it is a safek safeka — perhaps lenient, and perhaps in blessings one does not return and bless, as the Mechaber wrote in siman 185. And since so — even though when he afterwards read Shema it is a Torah doubt and he returns and reads — in siman 65 it is explained: whoever reads, reads with its blessings; one could say it is different here, for he has already been discharged from blessings. And so too if after he read it became known to him that there is feces in a pit — one could say likewise; and in our chiddushim we expanded. And now we saw that in any case, as the Prisha wrote in seif 7, to distinguish between a doubt whether he is obligated and a doubt whether he did — and we wrote that there is a chazakah, see there — and here because of the chazakah the law should not change. And the proof: one who coated [a knife] or waited with minority signs, which is a doubt of neveilah (as written in Peri Megadim Yoreh De'ah 18, in Shach 1, and other places) — if so, the Ateres Tzvi was correct in what he wrote that one does not return and bless. And see here note 2; and as written, and see note 1; and see siman 185, Eifer Chaim — when he removed his sandal, even if it touches and is stuck, it is permitted; and it is obvious, like feces in a lantern; and all the more so. And see Levush — it implies that nevertheless regarding touching it is forbidden.
