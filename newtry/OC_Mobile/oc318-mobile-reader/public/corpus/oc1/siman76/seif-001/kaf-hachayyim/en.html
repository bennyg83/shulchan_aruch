1) [Seif 1] Excrement in a glass enclosure: it is permitted to recite opposite it, etc. If a foul odor reaches him, it is prohibited. Even the authority who maintains below, siman 79, seif 2, that a partition is effective against a foul odor agrees that it is prohibited here, for a partition formed by a separate domain differs from a mere covering. Lechem Chamudot, chapter Mi SheMetu, ot 152; Shiyurei Kenesses HaGedolah, Hagahot HaTur, ot 1; Magen Avraham, seif katan 1; Eliyah Rabbah, ot 1. Whether, since it is covered, he must distance himself four amot from the point at which the odor ceases will be explained below, siman 79, with the help of Heaven, ot 1.
1) There. It is permitted to recite opposite it, etc. See Yafeh LaLev, ot 1, who writes that one who guards his soul should distance himself from it and not see it; see there.
2) There. Excrement in a glass enclosure, etc. One need not distance oneself from vomit even though it is repulsive. Nezirut Shimshon; Rabbi Akiva Eiger; Pithei Olam, ot 1. In Tov Ayin, siman 8, he writes that a great authority was uncertain whether a person in prison may fulfill a mitzvah in a filthy place through action alone (for example, by taking a lulav and etrog without a blessing) or whether even the act of a mitzvah is prohibited there. Ba'er Heitev brings proof that it is permitted; see there. Pithei Olam there.
3) There. Because the Torah made the matter dependent upon covering, etc. For this reason, even at night or for a blind person it is prohibited whenever the excrement is uncovered, since the matter does not depend upon seeing it. Perishah, ot 1. See below, siman 79, seif 1, and what we wrote there, with the help of Heaven.
