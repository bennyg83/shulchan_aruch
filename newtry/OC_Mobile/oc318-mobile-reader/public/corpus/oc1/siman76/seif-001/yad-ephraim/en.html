(Magen Avraham, note 6) And furthermore one could say: since it is customary, etc., and as the Terumat HaDeshen wrote — as they hold, see there: the explanation is that the Terumat HaDeshen holds that touching is permitted, only stuck to his sandal is forbidden. And also in their view it is difficult: why is it that if it is stuck to his sandal it is not better than if it is stuck to his flesh and covered with his garment, which is permitted per the Mahari"a? Rather, in truth the Terumat HaDeshen wrote regarding the case of feces on his flesh — specifically when it is covered by itself without garments, such as between his wrists. But in another place that is visible immediately, garment covering does not help — and this is the Mahari"a's view in this section. And if so, all the more so the distinction per Terumat HaDeshen's way: if stuck to the sandal it is forbidden, since it is not covered by itself and will be revealed through walking. If so, likewise one must distinguish for us too that touching is forbidden — that is, specifically regarding covering of the sandal, since it is customary to walk and through walking a little is revealed; its meaning is that for us we consider what touches as stuck to his sandal, and because it is destined to walk and a little is revealed through walking — we consider it as revealed and it is forbidden. And I found in the chiddushim of the Rashba that he wrote, of blessed memory: and regarding stuck to his sandal it was not resolved for them — because sometimes it is revealed when he walks, etc. And it is possible that for this reason the Magen Avraham leaned; and there is here a slight omission of language.
