(22) Permitted to recite—and where one found excrement and does not know whether it is dogs' or other animal or beast feces or human feces—the view of Magen Avraham is we follow what is prevalent: if children are more prevalent than dogs, even in a house it is forbidden; and likewise inversely in a refuse heap if dogs are more prevalent we attribute it to dogs to be lenient—specifically if he checked the refuse heap and found no other feces besides this, for otherwise we say that an unspecified refuse heap is presumed to have excrement since people regularly relieve themselves there; and so ruled Acharonim like Magen Avraham.

(23) Presumption of a house—and if small children are prevalent there, one must check.

(24) Presumption of a refuse heap—not a full presumption, for if so even urine would be forbidden; rather meaning that it is usual to find excrement there, therefore it is forbidden until one checks. I found so in Levush.

(25) Doubt about urine—and if he knows there was urine here and is in doubt whether a revi'it of water was poured into it and the like, Chayei Adam wrote that since prohibition was established we follow stringency even in a rabbinic matter; nevertheless in time of pressure such that Keriat Shema and prayer are nullified thereby, he may rely on the poskim who rule that even here we follow leniency in a rabbinic matter. And if this happened with excrement—that is, he knows there was excrement in the house and is in doubt whether they removed it—it appears forbidden to recite and pray until it is clarified for him, for that is a Torah matter.

(26) Even in a refuse heap, etc.—meaning after they checked it and no excrement was found and also no foul odor reaches from it; for if not, he would be exempt on account of the refuse heap itself, as written in siman 79 seif 8.

(27) Facing the column—Magen Avraham wrote in the name of Rosh that dripping urine is not considered facing the column and is permitted by Torah law; in Hagahot Rabbi Chaim Tzanz he questions this—see there.
