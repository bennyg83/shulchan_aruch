[7] Its law is like feces, etc. — so wrote Maharil, laws of Yom Kippur, and concluded: if it is still moist he wipes it well; if dry, he scrapes it off; and if he cannot scrape, he rubs with water — up to here. And see below siman 617 and in Beit Yosef there. And it implies that in his garments and covered it is permitted according to everyone; but in Shlah he wrote siman 66: one who is married should beware not to wipe himself with the shirt he wears when he separates from his wife after relations; and if semen comes on the shirt for some reason he should not wear it unless he bathes well. He further wrote it is good for one who cannot immerse to rub his hands and nakedness with water to purify from sticky keri; and see below siman 200 note 15.
