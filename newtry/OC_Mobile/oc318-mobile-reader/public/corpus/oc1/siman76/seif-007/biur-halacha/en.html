When there is uncertainty whether there is excrement in a house, one may recite. See Mishnah Berurah, seif katan 22, where I wrote that this applies specifically if he inspected the refuse heap. This resolves Peri Megadim's unresolved difficulty with Magen Avraham in Eshel Avraham, seif katan 10. See there.
But uncertainty regarding urine, etc. Meaning, he is uncertain whether urine is present there. It appears to me that the same applies when he finds liquid spilled on the floor of a house and does not know whether it is water or urine. One may be lenient even where young children are commonly present, because spilled water is as common as urine. Chayei Adam writes that we follow what is most common, and that if young children are in the house the filth is presumably from them, even regarding urine. There, however, he discusses a case where we know that the substance is urine, and the uncertainty is whether it is human or animal urine. We therefore follow what is most common, and if young children are more common we attribute it to them. This differs from our case. The ruling of Chayei Adam itself seemingly requires examination, for regarding urine we rule leniently even in a refuse heap, where urine is commonly found. This indicates that we do not follow what is most common regarding urine. One may distinguish between uncertainty concerning the identity of the substance itself, and a case where the substance is certainly present and we must determine to what source to attribute it. In the latter case, we attribute it to what is most common even in a Rabbinic matter. This requires study.
