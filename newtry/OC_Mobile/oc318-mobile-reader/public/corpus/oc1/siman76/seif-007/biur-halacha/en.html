If there is a doubt whether there is feces in the house, it is permitted to recite — see Mishna Berurah note 222, what he wrote, and specifically if he checked the garbage heap; and with this it is reasonable what Peri Megadim left as a question on Magen Avraham in Eliyah Rabbah note 10 — see there.

But if there is a doubt about urine, etc. — meaning that he is in doubt whether there is urine there; and in our humble opinion, the same applies if he found in the house something spilled on the ground and does not know whether it is water or urine — one may be lenient even in a place where small children are commonly found, for thus water that is spilled is commonly found like urine. And what Chayei Adam wrote, that we follow what is commonly found — that if there are small children in the house, presumably the filth is from them — even regarding urine there it would apply only when we know that it is urine and the doubt is whether it is human urine or animal urine; therefore we follow what is commonly found — that if small children are more commonly present, we attribute it to them — unlike in our case. And regarding his ruling in Chayei Adam itself, at first glance one should examine, for behold regarding urine we hold to be lenient even in a garbage heap where urine is commonly found there — therefore we do not follow what is commonly found regarding urine. And one may distinguish: whether the doubt is about the thing itself, or the thing is certainly present and we need to attribute it to something — in the latter case we attribute to what is commonly found even in a rabbinic matter — and this requires study.
