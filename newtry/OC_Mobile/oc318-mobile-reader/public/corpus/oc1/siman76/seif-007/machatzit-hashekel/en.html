(Siman kaf-zayin) Its law is like feces. It implies that if it is on his clothes and covered, it is permitted for all; and if on his flesh and covered, it is permitted per the first view — so it must be; and afterward he brings proof for both rulings, even though proof is not needed since he wrote its law is like feces, and certainly one does not rule more strictly than feces. Nevertheless he brought proof to exclude the view of Shaarei Teshuvah, who brought this siman kaf-zayin:

And in Shabbos page 117, etc. — this is proof for the first ruling, that on his clothes and covered it is permitted — therefore for all; for it is explained thus in the Gemara, and therefore no posek disputes this. And there it deals with when covered, for they said there: Rabbah bar Chanah said in the name of R' Yochanan: any Torah scholar found with semen on his clothes is liable to death (Rashi: revav — fat and grease), as it is said “all who hate Me love death” — do not read “who hate Me” but “who make Me hated” (Rashi: who make themselves repulsive in people’s eyes — this makes the Torah hated). Ravina said: revad it was stated (Rashi: semen), and they do not dispute: this in a cloak (which is outer, even revav), this in a garment (which is under, revad has no revav) — behold semen deals with the undergarment, where it is covered by an upper garment; and since the Gemara needed the reason of “making hated,” we learn that for Torah and prayer there is no concern when it is on his clothes and covered.

And this is the wording of Hagahot Maimoniyot, etc. — this is proof for the second ruling, that on his flesh and covered it is permitted per the first view:

From a kohen who saw semen and wants to raise his hands:

It implies there is no prohibition in the matter — and that is even though it is on his flesh; nevertheless because he holds like the first view that when covered it is permitted even on his flesh:

Only that it is permitted to wash him, etc. — that is, since from the law’s side there is no concern, why was it actually permitted on Yom Kippur when bathing is forbidden for this? He said in the name of: Ami, who is soiled with mud, is permitted to wash — for only pleasure-bathing was forbidden, specifically as stated later siman 613, seif 3 in the gloss there. And so in Rashi at the end of Yoma; but in Rashi he did not write “he shall rub the semen” but “he shall rub” because of an interposition. And it refers to what was taught: our Rabbis taught — one who sees semen on Yom Kippur descends and immerses, and in the evening he shall rub. And on this Rashi wrote “he shall rub because of interposition,” and the Gemara challenges: in the evening — that is, Motzei Yom Kippur — “what was, was” (and what help is rubbing for interposition that was on Yom Kippur?), rather perhaps from the evening he shall rub (Rashi: on the eve of Yom Kippur he shall rub only himself with hot water, so that if he sees semen tomorrow he can immerse without interposition — so far). And the Magen Avraham understood what Rashi wrote per the first view, that in the evening — on Motzei Yom Kippur — he shall rub; for Rashi wrote because of interposition — therefore it means semen interposes; and if so it implies specifically because of interposition he shall rub, but for prayer he need not rub since it is covered even though on his flesh. That is, also per the first view rubbing is not because of semen — for semen inhibits even for prayer; rather for this he need not rub the whole body, but it suffices when he wipes the place of semen; rather the rubbing mentioned in the baraita means the whole body lest there be something stuck somewhere that interposes, as Rashi explained for the conclusion. Still it is difficult: from where for Rashi, according to what he thought, would he explain rubbing the whole body lest something stuck to the body — perhaps rubbing means the place soiled with semen (and even so he challenges well “what was, was”). And the reason is not because of interposition but because of prayer. It works out that for the conclusion, when it deals with the eve of Yom Kippur when he has not yet seen semen, it must be because of interposition; but per the first view, from where for Rashi to explain because of interposition? Rather necessarily also semen for prayer has no concern when it is covered even on his flesh; and therefore the reason is because of interposition — and that is, Rashi also holds like those who permit on his flesh.

However, in Shaarei Teshuvah, etc. — even on his clothes, etc. And the proof of the Magen Avraham from tractate Shabbos cited above, that the Gemara needed the reason of “making hated” — that is, for prayer’s side there is only a light prohibition and no death penalty; not so because of “making hated,” there is a death penalty.
