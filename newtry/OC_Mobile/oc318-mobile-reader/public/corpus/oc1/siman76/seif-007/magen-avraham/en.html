Doubt. The same applies if there was a doubt whether it was human excrement or dog excrement: in a house it is permitted, but in a refuse heap it is forbidden, Shiltei Giborim, Riaz. It seems to me that we follow what is common, as written in Yoreh Deah, siman 57, seif 13, that we attribute to what is common, whether to be lenient or stringent. Here too, if children are more common than dogs, even in a house it is forbidden; and the same applies the reverse, if in a refuse heap dogs are more common, we attribute it to dogs.
Opposite the stream. The Rosh wrote that dribbling is not considered opposite the stream and is permitted according to the Torah.
