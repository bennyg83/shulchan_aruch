If feces was carried past him, it is forbidden to recite opposite it, and so forth. This applies even as far as he can see in front of him, and behind him for four amos from the point where the odor ceases. (Beit Yosef in the name of Rambam.) Others say that even in front of him, since it is passing by and continuing onward, a distance of four amos suffices. (Rabbi Yosef Abuhav and my teacher in Bach.) Nevertheless, when the feces merely passes across the area opposite him, all agree that four amos suffice. There is no distinction between Krias Shema and other blessings. (See above, siman 99.)
