Opposite it — meaning: we require distance before him as far as the eye can see, and behind him four amot. The Bach ruled that since the feces is passing and does not stand in one place, a distance of four amot suffices even before him, as the Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Mahari. The Taz raised that if they pass feces before him across the width of a person, then four amot are enough — for before it reaches four amot, or after it has distanced four amot, it is permitted, since he is then at its side; and we say also in siman 79 that at its side is like behind it, where four amot suffice. But directly opposite him he must distance as far as the eye can see — see above. And likewise the Pri Chadash: behind him four amot, and before him as far as the eye can see.
