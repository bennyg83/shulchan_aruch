Opposite it. Meaning, one must distance himself from it in front of him as far as he can see, and behind him four amos. The Bach rules that since the excrement is passing by and is not stationary in one place, distancing four amos is sufficient even in front of him, as the Beit Yosef writes in the name of Mahari. The Taz concludes that when someone carries excrement past him across the width of the person's field of view, four amos are sufficient. Before it comes within four amos, or after it has moved four amos away, it is permitted, since it is then at his side, and in siman 79 we likewise say that something at his side is treated like something behind him, for which four amos suffice. When it is directly opposite him, however, he must distance himself as far as he can see. See there. The Peri Chadash likewise writes that behind him four amos are required, and in front of him as far as he can see.
