It is forbidden to recite Krias Shema opposite it. Rambam writes regarding this, and regarding the mouth of a pig, that one must wait until it has passed four amos away. Beit Yosef writes: His words appear to indicate that even when they pass in front of him, once they are four amos distant it is permitted. This is unlike stationary excrement, from which one must distance himself in front as far as he can see. Since these are passing and continuing onward, four amos suffice. Rabbeinu HaGadol Mahari likewise writes this. However, I found no explicit source for this in the Gemara. It therefore appears to me that Rambam refers to something behind him, and for that reason four amos suffice once it has passed him, but in front of him he must distance himself as far as he can see. My father-in-law the Bach agrees with the first explanation and questions Beit Yosef for changing Rambam's wording, which concerns something passing before him. In my humble opinion, neither explanation is correct. Beit Yosef was certainly correct regarding the law, that one must distance himself as far as he can see. However, I am astonished by what he wrote in his first explanation, that this case is different because the excrement or the mouth of the pig is passing and continuing onward. The Gemara explicitly discusses passing excrement. Abaye maintains that one may recite Krias Shema opposite it and need not interrupt, comparing it to a metzora who passes beneath a tree, where a pure person standing beneath the tree does not become impure. Rava rejects the comparison, because the law of a metzora depends on permanence, as it is written, "He shall dwell alone," whereas here the Torah states, "Your camp shall be holy," and here it is not holy. Thus, regarding excrement we do not distinguish between stationary and passing excrement. From where, then, may one derive a leniency according to Rava, who is stringent even with passing excrement, by distinguishing whether it is within four amos? Rather, the plain meaning of the Gemara and the poskim is that passing excrement refers to another person carrying excrement across the width of the field of view of one who is reciting Krias Shema. This is implied by Rashi, who explains that they carry a chamber pot of excrement before him in order to remove it. The essential dispute between Abaye and Rava concerns whether he must interrupt during the time that the excrement passes directly before him or close to him within four amos. Before it enters four amos, or after it has moved four amos away, all agree that it is permitted, because it is then at his side. We state below in siman 79 that something at his side is treated like something behind him, for which four amos suffice. This was likewise Rambam's intent when he wrote of excrement floating on the surface of the water, meaning that the person stands near the water and the excrement floats from one side to the other. While it passes directly before him it is forbidden, until it moves four amos away and is then at his side. Tur did not need to state that it becomes permitted once it is four amos away, because he explicitly wrote that it is forbidden to recite Krias Shema opposite it, implying that when it is not opposite him but at his side it is permitted. In truth, however, when it is directly opposite him, distancing four amos does not help at all, in accordance with Beit Yosef. This is clear in my humble opinion.
