It is forbidden to read opposite it. In the Rambam it is written regarding this and regarding a pig's mouth — until four amot pass from him — the Beit Yosef wrote, and these are his words: it appears from his words that even though they pass before him, since he distanced four amot from it, it is permitted. And one could say regarding feces standing in one place that one must distance from it before him as much as one can see — that is, since they pass and go on their way, distancing four amot suffices; and so too our great rabbi, the Mahari"a — except I did not find an explicit statement of this in the Gemara; therefore it appears to me that it deals with behind him, and therefore four amot suffice when they have passed four amot from him; but from before him one must distance as much as one can see — so far. And the Maharach, of blessed memory, agreed to the first explanation and wondered at the Beit Yosef for changing the language of the Rambam that it deals with passing before him. And in my humble opinion — neither according to this word nor according to that word; and behold it is certain that the Beit Yosef intended correctly regarding the law that one must distance as much as one can see. And what he wrote in the first explanation that it is different here because the feces or pig's mouth pass and go — I wondered: would a holy mouth say so? For we explicitly say regarding passing feces that Abaye holds it is permitted to read opposite it and one need not interrupt; and he compares it to the case of a metzora passing under the tree, and the pure one standing under the tree is not impure — so too regarding passing feces; and Rava rejected him: metzora is different, for the matter depends on fixedness, as it is written "he shall dwell alone"; but here "and your camp shall be holy," the Merciful One said — and this is not present; apparently regarding feces we do not distinguish between fixed and passing. And whence do we derive leniency according to Rava, who is stringent also regarding passing, and to distinguish between four amot or not? Rather it is clear that the matters are according to their plain sense: passing feces in the Gemara and poskim means that the person reading Shema has another person pass feces before him across the width of a person — and this is the meaning of Rashi's words, who explained that they carry before him a basket of refuse to pass it — so far. For it teaches on what is written that the essence of the dispute of Abaye and Rava is whether one must interrupt at the time the feces is going directly before him or near him within four amot; but before it reaches four amot or after it has distanced so — it is permitted for everyone, since it is then at his side. And we say below, siman 79, that at his side is like behind him, for four amot suffice. And this is also the Rambam's intent: there was feces floating on the water — that is, when the man stands by the water and the feces floats from this side to that side, and in the meantime it comes directly before him — then it is forbidden until it distances and is at his side four amot. And the Tur did not need to mention "when he distanced four amot it is permitted," for it is explicitly written "forbidden to read opposite it" — implying all that is not opposite him but at his side is permitted. But in truth, opposite him directly, distancing four amot does not help at all, and per Beit Yosef's words this is obvious in my humble opinion.
