Opposite it. See Beit Yosef, who holds that there is no distinction between stationary and passing excrement. Therefore, even here he must distance himself from it as far as he can see when it is before him, and four amos from the point where the odor ceases when it is behind him. Taz and Peri Megadim likewise rule this way. If, however, excrement was carried across the width of the area before him, four amos suffice. Thus, before it comes within four amos of him, or after it has moved four amos away, it is permitted, because it is then at his side. In siman 79 we say that something at his side is treated like something behind him, for which four amos from the point where the odor ceases suffice. Until the point where the odor ceases, it is treated like actual excrement, since it is foul odor that has a source, as explained below in siman 79. Bach and Eliyah Rabbah rule that since the excrement is passing and does not remain in one place, four amos from the point where the odor ceases suffice even when it is before him. One should be stringent initially. After the fact, if he already recited or prayed while the excrement was passing before him outside four amos, see Peri Megadim, who writes that wherever there is a dispute he must repeat Krias Shema without its blessings, but he does not repeat the Amidah.
