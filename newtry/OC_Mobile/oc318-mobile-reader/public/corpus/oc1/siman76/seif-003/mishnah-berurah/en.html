(7) Before him—the same applies if it was floating on the water.

(8) Facing it—see in Beit Yosef who holds one should not distinguish between fixed excrement and passing excrement; therefore here too we require distance from before him as far as his eyes can see, and behind him four amot from where the odor ceased—and so ruled Taz and Peri Megadim. But if they passed excrement before him along his width, then four amot suffice—meaning before it reaches his four amot, or after it has distanced four amot from him it is permitted, since then it is from his side. And we say in siman 79 that from his side is considered like behind him, for which four amot from where the odor ceased suffice [for until where the odor ceased it is like actual excrement since foul odor with substance is fundamental, as below in siman 79]. Bach and Eliyah Rabbah ruled that since passing excrement does not stand in one place, distancing four amot from where the odor ceased suffices even from before him. One should be stringent ab initio; and post factum if he already recited and prayed and excrement was passing before him beyond four amot, see Peri Megadim who wrote—and this is his language: we already wrote that wherever there is a dispute, he goes back and recites Keriat Shema without blessings, but in prayer he does not go back and pray—see there further.

(9) That it is like a chamber pot—and if the window is open and a pig passes, whether it is permitted to recite, see siman 79 seif 2 in Biur Halacha and Mishna Berurah what is written there.
