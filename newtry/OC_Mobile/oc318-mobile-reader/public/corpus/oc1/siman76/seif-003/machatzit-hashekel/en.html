(Siman kaf-gimel) And covered, etc. On his hand, etc., it is permitted, etc. The Beit Yosef, Terumat HaDeshen, and Rashba hold that in the Gemara it states: feces on his flesh, or his hands were in the bathroom — Rav Huna said it is permitted to recite Shema, and Rav Chisda said it is forbidden. The reason for their dispute is explained there, and as the Magen Avraham wrote in siman kaf-hei and vav. And there are poskim like Rav Huna and there are poskim like Rav Chisda, and these are the two views brought here in the Shulchan Aruch. The Rif and Rambam have the reading: feces on his flesh, or his hands soiled from the bathroom. The Rosh questioned this reading — for if so, how is Rav Huna permitted, since the feces is visible, and even when dry it is forbidden? Terumat HaDeshen wrote: even though the Rambam resolves this in his wording that it deals with when there is no substance in it but mere filth, and from its smallness and dryness it is judged as if there is no feces there. However, on this it is difficult: in such a case why would Rav Chisda dispute, since it is written “all my bones,” etc. — behold, since it is judged as if there is no feces there, all his bones are clean, etc. — so far Terumat HaDeshen. The Rashba also wrote that the Rambam deals with when there is no actual feces but like beads of sweat; however the Rashba added that even when moist it is permitted because there is no odor, and the only concern is “and your camp shall be holy.” And since it is covered, it is permitted — so far. If so, if the Magen Avraham deals with when dry, it is permitted for all, even per the second view that ruled like Rav Chisda — for Terumat HaDeshen challenged thus on the Rambam’s reading: in such a case why would Rav Chisda forbid? And for the Rambam’s reading one needs resolution. But according to our reading, that it was in the bathroom — in truth the external reasoning remains that in such a case, which is like beads of sweat, it is permitted for all, even for Rav Chisda, and they dispute about something else. And so too when moist — the Rashba only permitted because they are covered; one may say as Terumat HaDeshen challenged: if so, in what do Rav Chisda disagree and forbid? And it works out that for the Rambam’s reading one must push off; but according to our reading the external reasoning remains that even for Rav Chisda it is permitted.
