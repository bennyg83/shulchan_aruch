Seif katan 3, And covered, etc. "On his hand, etc., it is permitted, etc." Beit Yosef, Talmidei Rabbeinu Yonah, and Rashba. Know that the Gemara states: If there is excrement on his flesh, or his hands are inside a privy, Rav Huna says that he may recite Krias Shema, while Rav Chisda says that he may not. The basis of their disagreement is explained there, as Magen Avraham writes in seifim ketanim 5 and 6. Some authorities rule like Rav Huna and others like Rav Chisda. These are the two opinions cited here in Shulchan Aruch. Rif and Rambam read the text as: "Excrement is on his flesh, or his hands are soiled from a privy." Rosh challenges this reading, because if so, how can Rav Huna permit it when the excrement is visible, since even dry excrement is forbidden? Talmidei Rabbeinu Yonah write that although Rambam resolves this through his wording, explaining that there is no actual substance but only a superficial stain, and because it is minute and dry it is treated as though no excrement were present, a difficulty remains. Why would Rav Chisda disagree in such a case because of the verse, "All my bones shall say, etc."? Since it is treated as though no excrement is present, all his limbs are clean. This is the substance of Talmidei Rabbeinu Yonah's words. Rashba likewise writes that Rambam refers to a case where no actual excrement is present, but only something like accumulated sweat residue. Rashba adds that even when it is moist, it is permitted because it has no odor, and the only concern is "your camp shall be holy." Since it is covered, it is permitted. Accordingly, if Magen Avraham refers to a dry stain, it is permitted according to all opinions, even according to those who rule like Rav Chisda. Talmidei Rabbeinu Yonah themselves challenged Rambam's reading by asking why Rav Chisda would forbid such a case. Rambam's reading requires a forced resolution, but according to our reading, which states that his hands were in a privy, the straightforward reasoning remains that a residue like accumulated sweat is permitted even according to Rav Chisda, and their disagreement concerns something else. Regarding a moist residue, Rashba permitted it only because it is covered. One might raise Talmidei Rabbeinu Yonah's challenge and ask, if so, on what basis does Rav Chisda disagree and forbid it? Although Rambam's reading requires a forced answer, according to our reading the straightforward reasoning remains that even Rav Chisda permits it.
