In a place where it was reasonable. Meaning, in a place where young children or the like are commonly present.
And afterward found it. Within four amos of him. If it was found beyond four amos but within the distance of his full field of vision, see below, siman 81, seif 2, and Mishnah Berurah there.
He must repeat. This is because "the sacrifice of the wicked is an abomination," since he should have inspected the place. It is obvious that the same applies if, after praying, he found that his clothing or shoes were soiled and he knows they became soiled because before prayer he had been in a filthy place, such as a privy or a refuse heap, and he was careless about remaining clean when he left. He must repeat because he was negligent. If, however, he sat at night in a place where cat excrement or the like was present, it adhered to his clothing, and he prayed in the beis midrash, then upon returning home found the filth attached to his garment, he need not repeat Krias Shema or the Amidah. This resembles a place where there was no reason to suspect filth. This is from Shaarei Teshuvah.
And recite. Together with the blessings. The same applies to the Amidah. If he recited Bircas HaMazon or other blessings, see below at the end of siman 185 and what is written there in Mishnah Berurah regarding the conclusion of the later authorities. If he completed the Amidah and then found excrement after the prayer time had passed, he does not make it up with another Amidah, because he was negligent, and compensatory prayer applies only when one was prevented by circumstances beyond his control.
He need not repeat. He is considered compelled by circumstances, for what could he have done?
He need not repeat. Even initially he would have been permitted to recite as long as he did not know, as explained above in seif 7. If, however, he became aware that urine was present and nevertheless recited or prayed, he must repeat Krias Shema and the Amidah, because he violated a definite Rabbinic prohibition. Peri Megadim writes this. See Chayei Adam, klal 3, din 33, and Biur Halachah below in siman 185.
