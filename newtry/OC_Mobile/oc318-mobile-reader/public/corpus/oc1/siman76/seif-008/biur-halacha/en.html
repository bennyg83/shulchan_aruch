He recited in a place, etc., and afterward found it. See Peri Megadim, who writes that this applies specifically when he found it afterward. If he prayed in a place where there was reason to suspect excrement, then left without checking and cannot return to inspect the place, it requires study whether he must repeat the Amidah. See there. Peri Megadim inclines to say that perhaps on a Torah level he discharged his obligation after the fact in every case. Yeshuos Yaakov concludes as a matter of halachah that if one recited in a place containing a refuse heap and then left, he must repeat Krias Shema and the Amidah. A refuse heap is presumed to contain excrement, so the uncertainty is close to certainty and we rule stringently even regarding a Rabbinic obligation. In a place where there was merely reason for suspicion, meaning it was neither presumed permissible for recitation nor presumed to contain excrement, he is not obligated because of the uncertainty alone to repeat Krias Shema. He must repeat it only if excrement was actually found afterward.
He must repeat, etc. See Peri Megadim in siman 79, Mishbetzos Zahav, and Nishmas Adam, who question whether this requirement is Torah-level or Rabbinic. Their concluding tendency is that it is only Rabbinic, because on a Torah level he discharged his obligation after the fact. Nevertheless, according to all opinions, if he is in a filthy place and cannot leave, such as one imprisoned in a jail or a similar case, it is preferable initially not to pray at all, because praying there violates a Torah prohibition.
