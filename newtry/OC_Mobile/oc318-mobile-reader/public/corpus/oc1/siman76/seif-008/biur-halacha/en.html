He recited in a place, etc., and afterward found — see Peri Megadim who wrote specifically if he found it afterward; but if he prayed in a place where it is fitting to be uncertain, and he went on his way and did not check, and it is impossible for him to return to the place and check — requires study whether he should return and pray — see there, that he inclines to say that perhaps by Torah law in all cases he fulfilled his obligation bedi'eved. And in Yeshuot Yaakov he concludes for halacha that if he recited in a place where there is a garbage heap and went to his place, he must return to recite and pray, for we presume that a garbage heap contains feces — if so, it is a doubt close to certainty, and we rule stringently even in rabbinic matters. But in a place where it is fitting to be uncertain — that is, which is neither in a presumption of permission to recite nor in a presumption that feces are there — in such a case of doubt he is not obligated to return and recite; only if he found it afterward, specifically.

He must return, etc. — see Peri Megadim in siman 79 in Mishbetzot Zahav and in Nishmat Adam, who are uncertain whether it is from Torah law or rabbinic; and in the end their words incline that it is only rabbinic, for by Torah law bedi'eved he fulfilled his obligation in this. Nevertheless, all agree that if he is in the filthy place, even though he cannot leave from there [such as one imprisoned in a prison house and the like], lechatchilah it is better for him not to pray at all, for he violates a Torah prohibition in this.
