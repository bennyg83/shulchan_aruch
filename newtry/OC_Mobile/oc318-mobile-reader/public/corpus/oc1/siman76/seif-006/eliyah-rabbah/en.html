[6] And there are those who say he did not permit, etc. — Lechem Chamudot page 23 wrote: and Rambam, after writing to rule like Rif, returned and wrote "and several geonim ruled that it is forbidden for him to recite if his hands were filthy, and it is proper to act thus"; and similarly in Shulchan Aruch, after writing for all three, he wrote "and it is proper to act like the strict view"; and I do not know why Levush omitted this — up to here. And in my humble opinion, Levush was right to omit it, for I saw that Rabbeinu Yerucham page 26 wrote that the majority agree it is permitted; and Beit Yosef too implies his view is to permit, only that he brought at the end the words of Rambam mentioned above, and therefore he wrote thus in Shulchan Aruch; and Lechem Chamudot's view similarly implies; and Bach ruled like Shulchan Aruch because of the words of Rambam mentioned above — see there; and Nachalat Tzvi similarly wrote that this is Shulchan Aruch's reason. And it is not clear: although Rambam concluded "and it is proper to act thus," nevertheless in Kolbo page 5 I saw that he copied the beginning of Rambam's words to permit as the plain ruling, and did not copy at all this conclusion that one should be strict — behold that acharonim did not heed this stringency. And further it appears to me there is no proof at all from Rambam, who has the Gemara reading "his hands were filthy" and explains "his hands were filthy" literally, and deals with when they are not covered, as explained in Rambam there — therefore he properly wrote one should be strict. But for us, who maintain we do not have the reading "his hands were filthy" — if so, it does not deal with hands literally filthy at all, but deals with one who inserted his hands into the latrine through an opening — in this it appears Rambam would agree there is permission; and I found similarly in Sefer Tamim De'im siman 200 explicitly, that Raavad distinguishes in this and ruled thus. And it appears the same law applies to feces on his flesh: since it is covered, Rambam agrees it is permitted; therefore it appears to me the main ruling is as the permissive view, and nevertheless one should be strict like the third view. Bach wrote: but certainly with hands literally filthy, or when he inserted through an opening, if he has no water — he wipes his hands with a stone or something that cleans, and recites Keriat Shema and blesses — up to here. And at first glance it is puzzling: when he inserted through an opening, of what avail is wiping; and even immersion would not help, for it would be like one immersing with a sheretz in his hand. And if it deals with when he returned and removed them from the opening — it is hard: why do I need wiping, behold they are not filthy. And in Gemara and poskim they disagree only when they are in the latrine opening. Again I saw in Kesef Mishneh there, who wrote that the Gemara's explanation was proven: "his hands were filthy" from the latrine that he inserted them there and they received filth from its uncleanness and the latrine's vapor — up to here. And perhaps Bach deals with such a case.
