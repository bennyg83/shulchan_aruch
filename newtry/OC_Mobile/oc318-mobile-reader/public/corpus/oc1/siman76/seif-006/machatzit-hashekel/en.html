(Siman kaf-vav) And some say, etc. — on his clothes and covered, it is permitted for all, for “all my bones” are clean.

And in the Rosh it seems somewhat that the Rosh wrote on what is stated in the Gemara: Rava said — feces in a depression, he places his sandal on it and recites Shema, etc., and we do not say the sandal is nullified relative to his body and is not considered covering — so far. From this it implies that these are Rava’s innovations. And since Rava specified sandal specifically, and so too the Rosh specified sandal, it implies that other garments are not nullified relative to the body, and it is simple that it is considered covering. (Although this is not full proof; one may say Rava used the ordinary way of speech, and the Rosh specified sandal since Rava spoke of a sandal.) And if so, it works out that a sandal, even though it is nullified relative to the body, nevertheless is considered covering; nevertheless regarding touching one may distinguish: for the reason he specified sandal is that it is nullified relative to the body — therefore if the sandal touches it is forbidden, as if his body touched the feces; not so other garments that are not nullified relative to the body — even though the garments touch the feces, it is not as if the body touches the feces, and for this reason it is permitted.

And one may explain as Terumat HaDeshen wrote according to their way: this “some say” that the one who permits did not permit, etc., the armpits, etc. — they are Terumat HaDeshen and the Me'or, and Terumat HaDeshen wrote the reason that covering with garments does not help because sometimes it is revealed and would be visible; therefore it is forbidden. Not so under the armpits, which remain covered forever; and if so, the same applies even for one who permits even with covering garments — that is, specifically garments that for the majority remain covered; not so a sandal that necessarily through walking will be revealed — he admits it is not considered covering. And in the Me'or it is explained more, for he wrote: “Feces on his flesh in a place where it is concealed, etc., and it does not tend to be revealed — for if in a place where it tends to be revealed, it is no worse than feces stuck in his sandal,” etc. — it is explicit that the reason a touching sandal is forbidden is because it tends to be revealed; rather the Me'or equated also other garments that sometimes are revealed to a sandal that will surely be revealed. And per the first view that permits even through covering garments, he holds one may distinguish: specifically a sandal that will surely be revealed, not so garments that are not revealed except by chance.
