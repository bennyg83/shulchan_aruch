Seif katan 6, And some say, etc. If it is on his clothing and covered, it is permitted according to all opinions, because all his limbs are clean.
And the Rosh's wording somewhat implies, etc. The Rosh writes regarding Rava's statement in the Gemara, "If excrement is in a cavity, he places his shoe over it and recites Krias Shema": "We do not say that the shoe is subordinate to his body and therefore is not considered a covering." This implies that this is Rava's novel point. Since Rava specifically mentions a shoe, and the Rosh likewise mentions a shoe, it appears that other garments are not subordinate to the body and therefore obviously constitute a covering. This is not a conclusive proof, because Rava may merely have described the ordinary case, and the Rosh may have mentioned a shoe because Rava's case concerned a shoe. Nevertheless, although a shoe constitutes a covering despite being subordinate to the body, contact may be treated differently precisely because the shoe is subordinate to the body. If the shoe touches the excrement, it is forbidden because it is considered as though his body touched the excrement. Other garments, however, are not subordinate to the body. Therefore, even if they touch the excrement, it is not considered as though his body touched it, and it is permitted.
One may further explain, etc. Talmidei Rabbeinu Yonah follow this approach in explaining the opinion cited as, "Some say that the permissive opinion permitted it only, etc., at the joints of his arms, etc." This is the view of Talmidei Rabbeinu Yonah and Baal HaMaor. Talmidei Rabbeinu Yonah explain that covering it with clothing is ineffective because the area may sometimes become uncovered and visible. It is therefore forbidden. Beneath the joints of his arms, however, it remains permanently covered. Accordingly, even the opinion that permits covering with clothing may do so only regarding garments that generally remain covered. A shoe, which will necessarily become exposed through walking, is not considered a covering even according to that view. Baal HaMaor states this more explicitly: "Excrement on his flesh in a concealed place, etc., which is not ordinarily exposed, for in a place ordinarily exposed it is no better than excrement attached to his shoe, etc." His words clearly show that contact with the shoe is forbidden because it is ordinarily exposed. Baal HaMaor equates other garments that sometimes become uncovered with a shoe that will certainly become exposed. The first opinion, which permits even when covered by clothing, distinguishes between a shoe that will certainly become exposed and garments that become uncovered only incidentally.
