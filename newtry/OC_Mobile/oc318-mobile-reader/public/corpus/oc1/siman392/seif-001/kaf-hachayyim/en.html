1) [Se'if 1] A city that was the property of an individual, etc. This is the wording of Rambam, chapter 5, halachah 19. Rashi, however, explains that a city of an individual means one into which six hundred thousand people did not regularly enter, and it then became a city of the public because residents were added or markets were established there. See there. Tosafos there and the Tur write likewise. Olas Shabbos, subsection 1, writes that the Shulchan Aruch must follow Rabbeinu Yehonasan's explanation of the Rif, which accords with Rambam, and therefore rules in accordance with the Rif and Rambam. See there. It can also be said that the Shulchan Aruch rules like Rambam because Rashba and Maggid Mishneh likewise hold this opinion, as stated in the following subsection.
2) There. A city that was the property of an individual, etc. Rashba writes: What constitutes a city of an individual? Any city that an individual built for himself in order to rent it to the public, or that he subsequently sold to the public, or that he built to settle residents there while retaining for himself the roads, plazas, and highways, as kings ordinarily do. Maggid Mishneh there, Beis Yosef, Magen Avraham, subsection 1, Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1, and Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1, cite his words.
3) There. Even if it became a city of the public, etc. This applies as long as the identity of its original owners has not been forgotten from the city. Rashba in his novellae.
4) There. Even if it became a city of the public, etc. This applies even if it has two entrances, so that people enter through one and leave through the other. If it has only one entrance, even a city of the public may establish a single shituf, as stated below.
5) There. They all establish a shituf, etc. This discusses a city surrounded by a wall ten handbreadths high and having doors, so that it is a complete private domain. Maggid Mishneh there, cited by Beis Yosef. Beis Yosef writes, however, that Rashi's words imply that the discussion applies even when the city has no wall. This means that each of its alleyways was rectified according to the applicable law explained in siman 364. See there. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1, and the later authorities write likewise. See below, se'if 5, and above, siman 391, subsection 26.
6) There. They all establish a shituf, etc. They must specifically establish a single shituf. Since the city was originally the property of an individual, the entire city is regarded as one courtyard, and its residents prohibit carrying for one another unless they establish an eruv together. Tur. See below, se'if 4.
7) There. And they may carry throughout the entire city. This means that no area need be excluded. Only a city of the public that has two entrances requires an excluded area, as explained below.
8) There. And it has one entrance, etc. This is because it does not resemble the encampment in the wilderness. Rashi; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1.
