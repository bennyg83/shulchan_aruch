Equivalent to walking twenty two cubits, etc. This means even without speaking. Speaking creates an interruption even for a shorter time, even if the words are Torah. Magen Avraham at the beginning of siman 179 implies this, unlike what he himself writes at the end of siman 165. The Bach and Taz agree. The Perishah writes that Tosafot's point is not that less than twenty two cubits can never be an interruption. Rather, the novelty is that one should not think twenty two cubits is too short to count as an interruption; at minimum, that amount certainly is an interruption, while even less may also be one. He proves this at length from Zevachim 33 and from the Rambam, chapters 3 and 5 of Hilchot Maaseh HaKorbanot. This also resolves Magen Avraham's question. However, Maharshal and those who follow him, whom I cited in the preceding siman, hold that even walking twenty two cubits is permitted so long as one does not speak or perform another act. The Taz also implies this. The Taz and later authorities discuss at length the Gemara in Elu Devarim, but they did not consult Birkat Avraham, folio 197. In any event, after the fact it appears that one need not wash again even after walking twenty two cubits, provided he did not divert his attention. Knesset HaGedolah and Olat Tamid imply this. Hilchot Maharan writes that just as on Friday evening near dark one must ask whether tithes were taken and an eruv made, so before netilat yadayim one should ask whether everything needed for Kiddush and HaMotzi is ready, so that he will not have to interrupt. The Shelah, folio 85, writes that it is good and proper to say Tehillim 23 immediately after eating the bread over which HaMotzi was recited, because it expresses trust in Hashem regarding sustenance and all other needs throughout life and even after death, that one should not go to Gehinnom.
