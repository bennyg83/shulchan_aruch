Some say that one need not be careful, etc. This is the opinion of Rabbeinu Yoel and the Rif cited by the Tur, who hold that the statement that a blessing must immediately follow washing of the hands refers specifically to mayim acharonim. This is difficult, for at the beginning of chapter Eilu Devarim it is stated that Beis Hillel hold that one first pours wine into the cup and afterward washes his hands. A Baraisa there explains that this is preferable because the meal must immediately follow washing of the hands. Tosafos there ask why this was not counted among the three matters that must immediately follow one another, and answer that since it is stated in the Mishnah in Eilu Devarim, there was no need to list it. It follows that the rule of immediacy between washing and the blessing, meaning the meal, applies also to the first washing. My father-in-law, of blessed memory, writes that Rabbeinu Yoel holds that we do not rule in accordance with that alternative explanation, since it was not counted among the other cases of immediacy, and he does not accept Tosafos' answer. In my humble opinion, there is no difficulty. Regarding interruption and merely sitting idle, we say that there is a prohibition with mayim acharonim, similar to the other cases of immediacy listed there, such as immediately proceeding from semichah to slaughter and from redemption to prayer. In those cases there is a prohibition even if one performs no act but merely sits idle. By contrast, regarding immediacy between the first washing and the meal, there is no prohibition in merely sitting idle, but only in performing an act in between. Even regarding an intervening act, Tosafos in chapter Arvei Pesachim write that Kiddush recited in between is not an interruption. Mixing the wine is different because the wine is mixed with hot water and requires great precision not to make it deficient or excessive. Tosafos further write in the name of Rabbeinu Tam that Beis Hillel's case refers to a weekday, because if he washes before mixing he may become occupied with other matters and fail to eat, whereas on Shabbos there is no concern because the table is set before him. When Rabbeinu Yoel permits an interruption between washing and HaMotzi, he permits only sitting idle. It is obvious that he did not permit performing an act in between, for that could lead to diversion of attention. This is the meaning of the requirement that the meal immediately follow washing of the hands. This appears clear and straightforward in the opinion of Rabbeinu Yoel and the Rif.
Twenty-two amos. This was the distance from Shaar Nikanor to the slaughterhouse. Accordingly, such a distance necessarily constitutes an interruption. Tosafos in chapter Eilu Ne'emarin write this, and Beit Yosef cites them in siman 128. This measure is not considered immediate and is certainly an interruption. Maharshal writes in responsum 34 that only regarding mayim acharonim do we require that the blessing immediately follow the washing, whereas regarding the first washing this is merely a preferred caution.
