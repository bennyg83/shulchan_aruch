(א) One who traps a deer — the same applies to other species [so too Or Zarua; and see in Biur Halacha]:
(ב) Sleeping or blind — for these are accustomed to slip away when they feel a person's hand; and therefore when he traps them it is actual trapping; unlike a lame person and the like who cannot slip away; and even if the lame person can walk somewhat — nevertheless they can reach him in one stride; therefore they are considered trapped and standing:
(ג) Or old — and the same applies to a child [Rambam, and so in the Yerushalmi; and a child is called before he can run, so Meiri]:
(ד) Exempt — but forbidden; and therefore when one occasionally finds a sick hare lying on the road — even though it cannot move from its place — forbidden to take it, for it is rabbinic trapping; and some say liable — and see in Biur Halacha; and in any case forbidden to take it because of muktzeh:
(ה) It is trapping — namely if he did not do an act himself at all, only through what he incited the dog and the dog seized it — it is only rabbinic trapping; and if he also did an act himself for this — such as the deer fled from the dog and was weary and tired and he was pursuing it and the dog reached it through this — it is full trapping; and even if he only stood before it and frightened it until the dog arrived and seized it — it is also a toldah of trapping and he is liable, for such is the manner of hunters:
