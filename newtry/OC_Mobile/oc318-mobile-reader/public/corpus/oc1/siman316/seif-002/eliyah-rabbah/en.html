[6] It is considered trapping, etc. Rama writes this in the name of Kol Bo. Rambam says: one who sends dogs to hunt, and the animal flees because of the dog, and he himself pursues the hunted animal or stands before it and frightens it until the dog reaches and catches it, is liable. The same applies to birds. This implies that if he himself performs no act, he is exempt, and Kol Bo speaks only of a prohibition (Magen Avraham). I am surprised, because Rama's phrase "it is trapping" implies liability, and even more surprisingly, Magen Avraham apparently did not see Kol Bo itself, which writes: "One who sends dogs after game and pursues it so that the dog will reach and catch it is liable." Thus Kol Bo explicitly says liable. The phrase "and pursues" means he performs an act, following Rambam as usual. Rama and Levush should therefore be understood the same way. Responsa Rabbi Yom Tov Tzahalon, siman 245, writes that if one saw a deer and merely called to it, without performing any physical act or bringing it into a guarded place, and then sat there until evening, there is no concern because he never touched it.
