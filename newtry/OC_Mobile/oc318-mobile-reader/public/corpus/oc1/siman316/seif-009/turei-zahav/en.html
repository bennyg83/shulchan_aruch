(א) Louse. That is, the black one that jumps; and it breeds and multiplies; and they say in chapter Eight Creeping Things we derive from the Mishkan: "rams dyed red" — just as rams breed and multiply, so all that breed and multiply; therefore liable for killing a louse. But the white louse does not breed and multiply but comes from human sweat — not liable for killing it, only forbidden due to a decree lest one kill a louse:

(ב) Forbidden to trap it. But there is no trapping liability here, for it is a matter whose species is not trapped:

(ג) Unless it is on his flesh and biting him. It is implied on his shirt inside — forbidden; and Tosafos chapter 8 Creeping Things wrote: if it bites a person, permitted to take it and throw it off him due to pain; but if in that situation he does not fear being bitten, such as on his outer garment outside — forbidden to take it, but he can knock it off — end of their words. And Bach challenged: from the beginning it is implied not permitted to take it except when biting; thus on his garments inside forbidden — and so appears from Semag and Semak, who wrote: this applies when it jumps here and there; but if it was biting — permitted — it is implied only when biting; and from the end it is implied specifically on his outer garment outside there is concern lest it bite him, therefore forbidden to take it; but on his garments inside permitted, lest it bite him — and so Ran in the name of Tosafos, and the Maggid in the name of Tosafos, and Rashba; and so appears from Rashi's words — end of his words. And from his language here it appears to be stringent — it is difficult: if so why did Tosafos mention outer garment at all — they should have written the greater leniency, even on his shirt inside; and one may say he teaches that nevertheless he can knock it to the ground, for in Asheri he also wrote like Tosafos and did not write at all "but he can knock it to the ground"; and so in Tur — only it appears specifically on his outer garment that is the upper garment far from flesh — forbidden; but on his shirt close to flesh — permitted, and it is included in "on his flesh" that the conclusion states: when the shirt is on his flesh, the louse is on his flesh and biting him — for Rashi explained "fit to bite him" — know that otherwise why mention flesh at all — he should have said only "biting"; and all Semag and Semak's words — rather certainly even if not actually biting but on his flesh where there is concern of biting, it is called biting; and so appears Tur's intent, who wrote: and specifically from the ground or from his garments; but if on his flesh and biting — permitted to trap. And Bach wrote it appears to be stringent; in my humble opinion it does not appear so from his language — for otherwise he should not have mentioned ground or garments at all; and further, even on flesh one could say forbidden as long as not biting, from what was conditioned "on his flesh and biting" — and this is certainly not so, for he should have distinguished on flesh itself between biting or not; rather he says "fit to bite him"; if so, included in "on his flesh" is also on his shirt inside, and even on his shirt outside permitted, for close to flesh; they forbade only on a cloak that is the upper garment far from flesh. And so too from Ra'ash's words, who wrote: but if on his flesh, permitted to take it and throw it from on him, for he does not intend trapping but as one occupied so it not bite him, and there is no prohibition of moving, for it is like a thorn in Reshut HaRabbim — end of his words. Behold he used future tense "so it not bite" — implying not yet bitten; and one may say this means "so it not bite further," and he should have said "so it not bite again"; and further, we permit moving even like a thorn in Reshut HaRabbim where we permit moving even though not yet harmed, only lest harm occur — and one could say moving is d'rabbanan whereas here even complete trapping is d'rabbanan since species not trapped; rather certainly due to mere concern lest it bite, since possible to bite, permitted — as above in Tosafos' intent; and see the great decisors — Ran and the Maggid — explained Tosafos' words thus in explanation; and Tur's words also appear thus, as I wrote; and possibly Shulchan Aruch's intent here too is as I stated: "biting" means fit to bite; and for practical law: whoever can knock it to the ground without taking by hand easily should do so on his shirt inside; but if wearing shoes and hard to knock off the louse without first taking by hand — one relying on the lenient authorities — Tosafos, Rashi, the Maggid, Rashba — who permitted in explanation has not lost, especially in d'rabbanan matters as appears to me; and so we see most of the world do; Heaven forbid to say they all failed:

(ד) He should not kill them. Due to decree lest he also kill a louse — like killing a camel; but they did not decree regarding delousing on a louse. And Tur wrote a louse is permitted to kill — this applies when taking it from his garments or flesh; but one who delouses his garments — forbidden to kill also a louse etc. It is explained this means specifically if found on his garments by chance — permitted to kill and they did not decree due to a louse; but if searching for it — forbidden even on a garment. It is difficult: Tur further wrote specifically when delousing garments they decreed killing a louse due to killing a louse; but when delousing his head — permitted to kill a louse and they did not decree due to a louse, for they are not found on the head — end of his words. The refinements contradict each other: first when delousing garments — forbidden only, implying on flesh permitted even by delousing; then he said on the head specifically permitted, implying on the body forbidden where lice are common. One may say: on the body it is uncommon to search for a louse there, and certainly they did not decree there due to a louse, and they need not mention this except the head where delousing is customary — nevertheless they did not decree, as above:
