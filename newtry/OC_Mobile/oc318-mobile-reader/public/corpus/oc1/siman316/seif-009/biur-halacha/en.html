(ד) (4) "To kill it" — see in Mishna Berurah that we copied in s.k. mem alef the words of Magen Avraham for the law; and see in Peri Megadim that he wondered about him why he obligates in this — is this too not a creature, for it is formed from mold of the fruit, and it is strained to say that while attached it is considered as formed from the earth — see there. And in truth, per what is explained in Rambam chapter 2 of Ma'achalot Assurot that a worm in something attached is considered a sheretz that crawls on the earth even though he did not explain that it was created on the earth — see there — if so this is not strained at all; but his wording is still unclear regarding what he wrote that it is called a complete sheretz — is it not so that those born from manure and from fruits that spoiled are not in the category of sheretz [see Rambam Sefer HaMitzvot mitzvah 177]. And regarding the essence of the law I wonder at Peri Megadim who decided to say that even those born in fruits while attached are not a creature, for they are formed from mold of the fruit — and he means: behold they are like crawling creatures born from manure and they are not reproduces (reproduce), and in truth this is not so, for one who examines in Sefer HaMitzvot negative commandment 178 sees explicitly that those born from fruits have the power to reproduce and multiply even though the beginning of their creation was not from male and female, and they are in the category of sheretz that crawls on the earth — only those born from fruits after they spoiled; and even the words of Magen Avraham are difficult to fit — why did he mention specifically those born in fruits while attached? Is it not that Magen Avraham speaks his words per the view of Rambam, and there it implies that even those born in fruits after they were detached have the power to reproduce and multiply since they were not formed from mold of the fruits; and what Magen Avraham wrote about worms in legumes — meaning after they spoiled — and as is proven from the beginning of his words. And behold in truth per what the commentators and Magen Avraham decided from their general rule that per Rambam a flea does not reproduce — if so how does Rambam hold that the flea mentioned in the Gemara is the black one that jumps that was formed from earth, and is it not explicit in the Gemara that it reproduces — and this is a wonder with no answer; and from this itself Ramban and Rashba proved that the flea mentioned in the Gemara is another species, and it is a great strain what they answered on it that since they were formed from earth it is considered as reproducing — for the language of the Gemara does not imply so; if so, in our humble opinion the view of Rambam in our matter is that he follows his method in Sefer HaMitzvot there that even species not formed from male and female also have the power to reproduce and multiply [meaning through laying eggs], as he said there explicitly regarding species born in fruits and in seeds that they have the power to reproduce and multiply; and if so, the same applies to those formed from earth, and we do not limit it for us except those formed from molds called in the category of romesh on the earth and not shoret (language of multiplying), as Rambam wrote there in Sefer HaMitzvot; and therefore in our matter too Rambam was precise and wrote crawling creatures whose formation is from manure and from fruits that spoiled, etc., and it is as he explained there that these species are not reproduces; and if so per him a flea not formed from mold is also reproduces even though the beginning of its creation was not from male and female but from earth [and what he said in Chullin 127 "I will exclude a mouse half flesh half earth that does not reproduce" — we are not compelled to say because of this that everything born from earth does not reproduce]. And behold one who examines in Sefer HaMitzvot, in the hasagot of Ramban there in root 9 sees that he disputes Rambam and holds that every species not formed from male and female has no power by nature to reproduce and multiply; and per his method he disagreed in our matter on Rambam regarding a flea and holds that if so, since it was formed from earth it is not reproduces, and the Gemara that says a flea reproduces — if so it is another species; but Rambam who holds that the flea is the one accepted in our hands, which is the black one that jumps formed from earth, and the Gemara says it reproduces — we can say that from this Rambam learned his words in Sefer HaMitzvot there that even those species not formed from male and female also have the power to reproduce and multiply; and further per Ramban that those species whose formation is not from male and female cannot in any way by laws of nature later be reproduces — how did the Gemara take and ask regarding lice and challenge the words of Rav Yosef — is it not certain the law is with Rav Yosef, and the baraita can challenge him — is it not that a louse does not come from male and female but from sweat; and also from the body of the passage it fits better per Rambam who says there R' Yehuda — if so they disagree only regarding a louse that is not reproduces, but regarding other repugnant creatures and crawling things that reproduce they do not disagree; and per Ramban even regarding other repugnant creatures and crawling things commonly found that are not reproduces, such as those formed from earth or born from fruits or from mold; but per Rambam we can say that he includes everything except those born from mold, as above; and also from the Torah portion itself it implies somewhat like Rambam, for at first he includes worms born in olive pits and also those that spread to the ground and returned, and afterwards he said regarding romesh on the earth he includes even those that are not reproduces; and per Ramban that those species not formed from male and female are not reproduces — is it not that the first verse itself proves that even without reproduces, and why is an extra verse needed for this; and what Ramban brought as proof from what is stated in Beitzah regarding a chicken that was covered from the ground its egg does not hatch — one can say specifically a chicken whose nature of creation is to give birth from male and female, and therefore its egg born not from a male has no power to develop from it male and female; not so these fleas and similar to them whose way of nature at the beginning of their creation every day is to be born not from male and female, and also in their power to give birth afterwards not through a male. The conclusion from our words: even those species whose beginning of creation was not from male and female, such as those born in seeds and in fruits whether detached or attached — nevertheless they have the power to reproduce and multiply, and one who kills them is liable; and it was not limited except a louse born from sweat, or formed from manure and from fruits after they spoiled.
