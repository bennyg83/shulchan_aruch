(א) Called in Aramaic — namely the black one that jumps:
(ב) Forbidden to trap it — even though it is something not in its species trapped — nevertheless forbidden rabbinically, as above in seif 3; and it was needed to teach us to exclude the view of some poskim who hold that this too is in the category of species that may be trapped and killed; and if he is in doubt at night whether it is a louse or a flea — permitted to trap it and forbidden to kill it; and permitted to shake out garments from fleas on Shabbat — but he should be careful not to take them in his hand, for it is in the category of trapping — only shake them off him:
(ג) Unless it is, etc. — namely that because of pain of stinging the Rabbis did not decree on the prohibition of trapping; and permitted to trap it and throw it; and this is what he wrote "its sting" [Magen Avraham, and so Gra — this "its sting" specifically]; and some are lenient also when it is on his shirt from inside to take it in his hand and throw it lest he come to stinging; and if he can shake it to the ground without taking it in his hand — certainly correct to be stringent in this:
(ד) And forbidden to kill it — the reason we distinguish between flea and louse is that all melachos of Shabbat we derive from the Mishkan; and we derive from death of any living thing to obligate from slaughter of red heifers that were in the Mishkan for their hides; and not precisely through slaughter — the same applies through striking and choking or suffocation and all such cases — since through this taking a soul occurs, he is liable. And we say: just as red heifers that reproduce — so too everything that reproduces; to exclude a louse that does not come from male and female but from sweat — not considered a creature. But a flea — even though it also does not reproduce — nevertheless since its formation is from earth there is vitality in it as if created from male and female; and liable upon it because of taking a soul; and even if the flea stings him — forbidden to kill it:
(ה) He should not wear them out — to weaken their strength so they will not return to him; and see in Sefer Or Zarua what he wrote on this:
(ו) Permitted, etc. — wherever he finds it, whether on his flesh or on his garments, by chance:
(ז) To kill it — the reason as stated. And creeping things whose formation is from dung and from fruits that spoiled and the like — such as worms of meat and worms inside legumes — one who kills them is exempt [and forbidden rabbinically]; but creeping things that reproduce from male and female or whose formation is from earth, such as lice — one who kills them is liable [Rambam]; and specifically worms that formed from fruits after they were detached; but those that grow in fruits while attached — there is d'oraisa prohibition to kill them, for it is called a complete sheretz [so Magen Avraham according to what Peri Megadim explained; and in Biur Halacha I clarified that exemption from detached fruits is specifically when they formed after spoiling]:
(ח) He should not kill them — for since fleas are commonly found there, they decreed lest he also kill fleas. And nevertheless permitted to throw them in water [Magen Avraham], for killing itself is only a decree and they did not decree on this; but regarding fleas where there is concern of d'oraisa — forbidden also in this, for it is like actual killing. One should not take lice from fox skins and the like, because he detaches from the wool — and it is psik reisha:
(ט) But one who delouses, etc. — for at first fleas are not commonly found there and it is not relevant to decree because of them. And Taz wrote that the same applies when delousing on his flesh — also permitted to kill them there, for fleas are also not commonly found there that he will search for fleas; and they did not decree on this except when delousing his garments:
