(א) And its sting. It is implied that if it is on his flesh and does not sting him — forbidden to trap it; and some say to remove a thorn on Rosh Hashana, as is written siman 328 seif 18, that here there is no concern for damage but mere pain; but some say permitted to take it from his garments from inside:

(ב) And forbidden to kill it. And one who kills it is liable:

(ג) Permitted to kill it. The language of the Rambam chapter 11: creeping things whose formation is from dung and from fruits that spoiled and the like — such as worms of meat and worms inside legumes — one who kills them is exempt; but creeping things that reproduce or whose formation is from the earth, such as lice (even though it does not reproduce) — one who kills them is liable. And it is implied in Machatzit HaShekel that what he said at the beginning "exempt" is not precise but rather permitted l'chatchila, similar to a louse; nevertheless the worms that grow in fruits while attached — forbidden to kill, for it is called a complete sheretz:

(ד) He should not kill them. Since lice are commonly found there, they decreed lest he also kill lice; nevertheless it appears to me that it is permitted to throw them in water — and so too in the Gemara. One should not take lice from fox skins and the like, because he detaches from the wool (Shaarei Ephraim siman 268).
