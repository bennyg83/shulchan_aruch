(א) (s.k. 9) And even though bees are not in their species, etc. — and so too Rashba, etc., who rejected the words of Rabbenu Yehudah who wrote on the Tur that he rejected Beit Yosef's proof from bees, for in the beehive itself it says "provided," etc. — from which it is implied that when he does not intend it is permitted; we establish there specifically in a case that is not psik reisha; but if it were psik reisha, even if he does not intend it is forbidden. Rather for the Tur to reject: bees are different because in their species they are trapped — therefore even if he does not intend, when it is psik reisha it is forbidden; but flies that are not in their species trapped — if he does not intend, even when it is psik reisha it is permitted (namely according to Terumat HaDeshen that Beit Yosef brought, that in a rabbinic prohibition psik reisha is permitted). And he rejected Beit Yosef's words, for we say there in Beitza that it challenges R' Yehuda who holds a vessel is not moved except for the need of something movable; and the baraita teaches spreading a mat over a beehive in summer days and in rainy days, provided he does not intend to trap; and it answers that it deals when there is honey in it; and it challenges in rainy days that there is no honey in the beehive — what then? It answers that there are two cakes of honey that they place in the beehive so the bees will sustain themselves from them in rainy days; and it challenges that two cakes are set aside for the bees' need; and still he moves for the need of something not movable; and it answers when he thought about them from erev Shabbat to eat them; and it challenges from the answer "when he thought about them" and not "R' Shimon holds there is no muktzeh" — from which it must establish it according to R' Yehuda; and if so the end is difficult "provided he does not intend to trap" — and for R' Yehuda what does this help? For he holds something not prepared is forbidden; and it answers "and you will understand" — even if it is according to R' Shimon, R' Shimon agrees regarding psik reisha; and it answers: always the entire sugya is R' Yehuda; and the practical difference is when there are windows at the sides of the beehives — for even if he covers from above, nevertheless they are not trapped, for they can flee through the windows; and one should not say "provided he does not intend to trap" but rather "that he not make it a trap" (namely that he not seal the windows); and it challenges: obvious, for trapping is a primary melacha; and it answers: lest you say in its species trapped it is forbidden, in its species not trapped it is permitted — it teaches us.
