(א) Eight, etc. — for behold at first the Mechaber explained the law of trapping regarding animal and bird, for in those species they are unspecified trapped for a purpose; and now the Mechaber explained the law of trapping creeping things, where there is a distinction: regarding those species that have hides — namely the eight creeping things mentioned in the Torah, the weasel and mouse and toad, etc. — there is trapping, for presumably he also traps them for this purpose, unless his intent was explicitly not for a purpose — then exempt because it is melacha she'ein tzricha legufah; but other creeping things have no hides and are unspecified trapped not for a purpose, unless his intent was explicitly for some purpose — then he is liable if it is something in its species trapped:

(ב) And one who wounds — the reason for liability for wounding is because of taking a soul in that place, for blood is the soul; and therefore even if blood goes out however little, or the blood coagulates however little — liable:

(ג) In them — he specified wounding in creeping things because among them are creeping things that have no hide and he is not liable until blood goes out, as the Mechaber concludes; but all the more so there is liability for wounding in animal, beast, and bird, for all have hide, and liable even in coagulated blood; and all this deals when he needs the blood of the wound for his dog or for some matter, or for flesh in which blood coagulated to feed his dog with its blood — otherwise it is in the category of destructive act and exempt. And some poskim hold that therefore he is liable for coagulated blood, for this is not in the category of destructive act but repair, for the world's manner is to strike them in order to weaken them so they will be easy to subdue. And if he wounded a person in a manner of vengeance, or another's animal, beast, or bird in this manner, and blood went out or coagulated — the Rambam's view is that he is liable in all cases, for he is not in the category of destructive act; and the reason is because he settles his mind through this thing and his inclination rests; and since his anger subsides through this thing — behold it is as repairing and he is liable; and the same applies to tearing in his anger — Raavad exempts, see there. And according to all there is prohibition in this, for all who perform destructive acts in any of the melachos of Shabbat — even though exempt from chatas, nevertheless forbidden; and therefore one should be very careful not to strike any living thing on Shabbat with a blow that can lead to wounding, for in any case there is prohibition according to all; and people stumble in this. And likewise forbidden to suck a boil, for through sucking blood emerges that is absorbed in the flesh — and if so he makes a wound [Magen Avraham siman 328 s.k. 34]. And if he lets blood from a person for healing when there is no danger, or from an animal — liable according to all, for it is in the category of repairing; and also it is melacha needed for its purpose, for he wants now this wounding in order to heal. And according to this extracting a tooth is d'oraisa melacha, for it is in the category of wounding for healing [so too Magen Avraham siman 328 s.k. 3, Peri Megadim, Chayei Adam; and see in Biur Halacha and in Mordechai — it is implied that extracting a tooth has only prohibition and not liability of chatas, for the wounding of blood made thereby is not needed for its purpose; and so from Gra]:

(ד) Liable — for coagulated means it gathered and collected in one place and does not return and be absorbed in the flesh; and therefore liable because of taking a soul in that place, for blood is the soul, as stated:

(ה) He is not liable — the reason is that we rule a wound that returns is not called a wound; and therefore other creeping things whose hide is very soft like flesh — quickly blood coagulates in it and afterward returns to its former state; therefore he is not liable until blood goes out; unlike the eight creeping things whose hide is not soft — blood does not coagulate in it until a complete wound is formed that does not return to its former state; and one should obligate even for coagulated blood because of taking a soul in that place:

(ו) Not for a purpose — such as to play and the like — exempt because it is melacha she'ein tzricha legufah. One who traps fish from the river — even if he immediately placed them in a bowl of water so they will not die — liable because of trapping; and if he left them until they died — also liable because of taking a soul; and not precisely died, but even if he left them until it dried like the width of a rock between its fins and it still twitches and he returned it into water — also liable because of taking a soul, for it can no longer live [and we say in the Gemara that it does not mean literally dried but when moisture flows from there and the finger is drawn there when he places it upon it]. And if so one must be careful not to command a non-Jew to take a fish from a barrel of water and leave it on dry land even if he fears perhaps it will die and the water will be lost — for we do not permit d'oraisa prohibition through a non-Jew in a place of loss unless he commands him to place it immediately in another pool of water; and moving living things, which is rabbinic prohibition — we permit through a non-Jew in this:

(ז) And for the Rambam liable — even not for a purpose at all, whenever he intended for melacha, for he holds melacha she'ein tzricha legufah liable; and most poskim rule like the first view that exempt from chatas because we require melacha machsheves, and forbidden rabbinically:
