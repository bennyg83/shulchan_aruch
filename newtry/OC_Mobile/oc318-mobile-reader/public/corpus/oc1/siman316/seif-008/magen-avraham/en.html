(א) In the Torah. In parashat Shemini — the weasel, etc.:

(ב) He traps them. For they are unspecified trapped for the sake of the hide, for it is not their manner to cause damage [Ran]:

(ג) Beneath the hide he is liable. Some say that for wounding he is liable because of mafrek, which is a toldah of threshing; and even though threshing applies only to produce of the ground — an animal is called produce of the ground, and he is not liable until blood goes out from place to place like a dried fig; and some say because of dyeing; and some say because of taking a soul in that place — and this most commentators agreed upon. And for wounding a person or another's animal in a manner of vengeance — the Rambam obligates, for he gives satisfaction to his inclination; and Raavad exempts — see there. And see Eliyah Rabbah what he wrote; and the same applies to tearing — they disagree; and it appears to me that wounding for healing — liable according to all — see what he wrote:

(ד) And other creeping things. Since their hide is soft, quickly the blood coagulates even though there is no wound there:

(ה) Not for a purpose — exempt. For it is melacha she'ein tzricha legufah. One who takes a fish from a bowl of water and leaves it until it dries like a rock between its fins — liable for taking a soul, for it can no longer live (Rambam, Gemara); and it does not mean literally dried but when moisture flows from there and the finger is drawn there when he places it upon it (Rabbenu Yerucham, Chiddushei Aggadot, Gemara).
