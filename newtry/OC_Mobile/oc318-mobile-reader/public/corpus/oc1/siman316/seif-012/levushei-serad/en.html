There. "Ran misled me on this law." The Mishnah teaches: to what is this similar — one who locks his house to guard it and finds a deer guarded inside; Ran explained: such as there is a deer in the house and the door is closed and it is guarded thereby; he came and this one locked with a bolt — although adding guarding, permitted; we do not say he trapped it as if without locking perhaps the door would open and the deer escape; so too the second may sit beside the first since the first filled the doorway, even after the first left the deer remains trapped because of the second — end of Ran's language. Ran and Rashi permit only when the doorway was closed from the door itself so trapping was never nullified — this is the essence of permission for the second too; otherwise why mention closed and locked? Magen Avraham wrote Ran too holds even if opened once permitted; deals even when the first's walking left the place empty — only Tosafot's challenge on "to what is this similar" — therefore explained if the second added guarding on the first it is also permitted; all deal with the case the closed door opened during the first's walking — requires study:
