(א) Animal and bird in his possession — includes all types of living things that he accustomed in the house and they became domesticated:
(ב) Permitted, etc. — namely even outside; and the reason is since they are domesticated and accustomed in the house and automatically will return to their house in the evening and it is easy to catch them — therefore even when they left the house behold they are as trapped and standing and trapping does not apply to them:
(ג) To trap them — provided he does not take them in his hand, for all living things are muktzeh; only chase them until they enter a narrow place and lock before them:
(ד) Large — but if the courtyard is small — in any case they do not need trapping; even rabbinic prohibition does not exist:
(ה) That if, etc. — namely that now that they grew up among people they do not need trapping, for they will come on their own to their house in the evening; and nevertheless since they rebelled and it is not easy to catch them and the courtyard is large — it appears like trapping and forbidden in any case rabbinically:
(ו) Forbidden — namely when they are outside or inside in a large house where there is measure of trapping — namely that he cannot reach in one stride, as at the beginning of the siman; and later authorities ruled like this view. And if the birds went outside and he fears they will be stolen — even though forbidden to catch them himself with hands — nevertheless if small children trap them — one need not protest against them; also permitted to tell a non-Jew to trap them, for it is shevut deshevut [Chayei Adam]. And he also wrote permitted to stand before the bird so it will not flee in order that automatically it will return to the house; also Magen Avraham brought in the name of Agudah permitted to drive the bird from behind it so it will enter the house if the house is large so he cannot reach it there in one stride, for there is no full trapping here; and Chayei Adam wrote that if that bird's manner is that it does not slip from the hand — as there are hens that immediately sit when they want to catch it and need not chase it — in that there is no prohibition of trapping at all according to all if it was already accustomed in the house; and permitted to bring it in through driving or chasing it even to a small house or its coop; and so one may be lenient:
(ז) To trap — and therefore one should be careful not to bring the birds on Shabbat from the house [when it is large and lacking trapping there] to the place of their coop, for there the place is narrow and it is called full trapping. And if he intends to bring them to the coop only so they will not do damage in the house — perhaps there is room to be lenient; and see in Biur Halacha. And if they were new birds not yet accustomed in the house — there is prohibition in all cases; and even if they entered the coop on their own — forbidden to lock the coop door before them:
(ח) But a cow and horse, etc. — meaning that regarding cow and horse that are species of domestic animals — trapping does not apply at all, even rabbinically, even if he acquired them anew and they are not yet accustomed to come to his house, for they are not made to slip from a person's hand; and permitted to seize them and close them in a narrow place. And regarding other beast and bird in his possession that are made to slip from a person's hand — even though they do not need a trap to catch them, for they will come on their own to their house in the evening — nevertheless there is rabbinic prohibition of trapping; and all this when they did not rebel. But when they rebelled — even cow and horse are liable to chatas for trapping them, even if they grew up from the outset in his house; and this is what he wrote "and all the more so other beast and bird," etc. — namely when the cow and horse rebelled completely and fled from his possession until he must seek a trap or some snares to catch them; and also they do not come in the evening to his house:
(ט) And all the more so, etc. that rebelled — it also deals when they rebelled and fled and do not return to his house in the evening but sleep in the field like geese and gander that sleep on the water; and difficult to catch them without trap and snare; and also the Mechaber agrees that in this one who traps them is liable to chatas for trapping. And later authorities wrote that from this we learn that the same applies if he bought geese and chickens anew and they are not yet accustomed at all in his house — if they went out they will not return to the house but peck in the garbage — one who traps them is liable. And if they left the house — forbidden to trap them even through a non-Jew [Chayei Adam]; and it appears that if the house is large so he cannot reach in one stride — permitted through a non-Jew to bring them inside if he fears loss will occur; and if small children chase them to return them inside the house — one also does not protest against them; and see in Biur Halacha:
