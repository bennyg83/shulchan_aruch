An animal and bird in his possession. Thus wrote Hagahot Maimoniyot: an animal and bird in his possession — one who traps them is exempt but forbidden. And that which the Mishnah teaches "but one traps animals and birds" — this is on Yom Tov when he traps them l'chatchila in order to slaughter and eat them; but here on Shabbat — no; and such as those accustomed to multiply — when he wants to catch them and they are in a large house, etc., and even to drive them in a narrow place where it is easy to catch — forbidden, etc., end of his words — Rabbenu Baruch. And there is doubt, for that which the Mishnah teaches "but one traps," etc. — we establish it in the Gemara in a small enclosure where they are trapped in it, as is written siman 696; if so on Shabbat even in a small house it is forbidden; and afterward he wrote "and such as," etc., and they are in a large house — this even on Yom Tov is forbidden; and so too in Beitza daf 24 — doves of a dovecote are accustomed to multiply (Rashi explained: they are accustomed to slip away and flee because of people and fly until their nest, and one must go up after them; and there too they slip away from him and descend). Geese and chickens are not accustomed to multiply; and one challenged what Hagahot Maimoniyot wrote afterward "and so it is in the Yerushalmi — a horse that rebelled," etc.; and in the Yerushalmi it is stated that he is liable to a chatas; and if so the Mishnah deals when they did not rebel, and nevertheless exempt but forbidden — and how did Rabbenu Yehudah rule permitted l'chatchila? Therefore it appears that in Hagahot Maimoniyot he intended what is written in Shaarei Gibborim in the name of Raavan — and thus: on Shabbat forbidden to take geese and chickens and animals that a person has in his house, such as deer that he raises in his house; but regarding domestic animals — permitted; and a cat — forbidden; and so it is taught in the Tosefta: one who traps geese and chickens and doves of the dovecote — exempt; from which it is implied forbidden. And behold Hagahot Maimoniyot holds as is written siman 697 seif 7 that geese and chickens are permitted to catch on Yom Tov; and therefore he wrote that specifically on Yom Tov in order to eat them, but on Shabbat forbidden. And what he wrote afterward "and such as accustomed," etc. — it is a textual error, and it must read "and regarding domestic animals that are accustomed to multiply," etc., forbidden (and so wrote in the name of Sefer HaZichronot that there is a textual error in Hagahot Maimoniyot, and perhaps he intended what was written). And he concludes in Hagahot Maimoniyot: therefore one must be careful not to catch a rebellious horse and rebellious cow, etc., and so it is in the Yerushalmi, etc., see there; and you will find that his words are accurate and straight, and so is essential, as the Rama wrote; and so too in Agudah — a hen that left the house — forbidden to trap it; but one may drive it until it enters the house, since it is wide and they cannot reach it in one stride — it is not trapping; and if small children or a non-Jew trap it to return it — one does not protest against them, end of his words. Therefore one should not be lenient in this at all; and see siman 697 in the name of the author of Mishna Berurah that his view too is to forbid. Rashal in a responsum wrote: even though in the Gemara and Semag it is stated for prohibition, and in Hagahot Maimoniyot he wrote to permit — only at the end he wrote "one who is stringent — praiseworthy," and specifically in a large courtyard; but in a house — permitted l'chatchila, see there, etc. It must be said that it is a different gloss; and also so he wrote for prohibition. And what Rabbenu Yehudah wrote on this — all exemptions of Shabbat are exempt but forbidden except those three — there is doubt, for this Mishnah comes from those three, as is stated at the beginning of Shabbat; and if so it is also exempt but forbidden — and how did he write permitted to trap them?
