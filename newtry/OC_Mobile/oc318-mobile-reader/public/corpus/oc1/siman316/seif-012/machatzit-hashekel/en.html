(א) (s.k. 12) That he not put it to sleep, etc. — and for the Rambam the reason, etc. — meaning the Rambam who ruled like R' Yehuda that melacha she'ein tzricha legufah — liable. And nevertheless he rules for practical law that here perforce it must be because he is not trapping in its manner, etc. And in Sefer Chayei Adam he understood that he means since he traps through change, as the Rambam concludes there how he does — he turns a vessel over them or encircles them or ties them, end of his words. And he challenged this — why should this be called change and not trapping in its manner; and they brought proof that this is full trapping — see there. And in any case in my humble opinion one cannot say thus, for then Rabbenu Yehudah should have explained and written "and for the Rambam" that he requires trapping through change, as is written in the next seif regarding eight creeping things; and for the Rambam he is liable. Or that in truth this is not change, and even trapping in its manner is permitted for the Rambam because he is merely preoccupied — and these are Machatzit HaShekel's words chapter 10 law 17 regarding splitting a morsa and trapping a snake, whose law and reason are the same; and thus: for this is not melacha she'ein tzricha legufah — he is doing the melacha itself with intent; but these matters his intent is not for melacha — for all his intent is to remove saliva and save himself, etc., end of Machatzit HaShekel's words. And this he called there beforehand "unintentional" — see there; and meaning melacha she'ein tzricha legufah is called one who extinguishes when he intends that coals be made — this is melacha she'ein tzricha legufah; but when his intent is not to make coals but for another purpose, that he pities the candle and oil and the like — this is melacha she'ein tzricha legufah; namely that extinguishing is the melacha and the essence of the melacha's body is making coals; and nevertheless even if he has no intent for coals — namely she'ein tzricha legufah — nevertheless he wants to do the melacha, namely extinguishing; but she'ein tzricha legufah — namely for coals — unlike here in splitting a morsa where the melacha is opening and in trapping the melacha is trapping so it cannot flee. And if so in splitting a morsa to remove saliva and not to make an opening he has no intent for melacha at all; and likewise in trapping a snake so it will not harm him — the intent is not for the melacha of trapping but to save himself from damage; and this too is called in the Gemara without intent "merely preoccupied," like one preoccupied with forbidden fats and sexual prohibitions; nevertheless according to Machatzit HaShekel's view there must be a distinction between melacha she'ein tzricha legufah and psik reisha; and see in Magen Avraham siman 278:

(ב) For they are not in their species trapped, as is written seif 3; and in Sefer Or Zarua and Sefer Chayei Adam they challenged him — behold a snake is perforce in its species trapped. Also Taz chapter 16 of Shabbat Mishnah 7 in the passage "and regarding a scorpion," etc., wrote, and thus: because the snake is not in its species trapped, etc.; but one might ask from what we learned in chapter 2 of Eduyot that one who traps a snake for healing is liable — from which we learn that it is in its species trapped, etc.; therefore it appears, etc., end of his words. And in Sefer Teshuvah he emended Magen Avraham's words that it should read "for they are in their species trapped," see there. Therefore in my humble opinion Magen Avraham's intent is that Machatzit HaShekel gave two reasons per the Rambam — even though he holds melacha she'ein tzricha legufah liable, nevertheless he rules for practical law here, and thus: and likewise trapping a snake when he does not trap in its manner but is merely preoccupied with it, as will be explained below (I brought his language that this is not melacha she'ein tzricha legufah, etc.); or since many are harmed by it it is considered danger to lives, etc., end of Machatzit HaShekel. And Magen Avraham copied one reason of Machatzit HaShekel that it is merely preoccupied; and he comes to justify why he did not also copy Machatzit HaShekel's second reason; and for this he wrote "even if they do not kill — permitted," and so too the Rambam explicitly; and if so for reason one that it is merely preoccupied — it is fine that even if they do not kill it is permitted; but for reason two that it is considered danger to lives — if we say he means actual danger to lives that it is impossible that a person will not die — if so when it certainly does not kill it should be forbidden; but behold the Rambam wrote even if they do not kill — permitted. And if he means even if they do not kill with certainty for any person, nevertheless since many are harmed by it they were lenient as if it were danger to lives — and so too Machatzit HaShekel's language; and on this Magen Avraham wrote that if so it is difficult why in truth it is permitted — behold it is in its species trapped; and he brought proof from seif 3 where he wrote there that everything not in its species trapped is exempt; and Magen Avraham wrote there s.k. 6 — even if he trapped for a purpose; if so Tosafos' difficulty above is difficult; it is impossible to call "not in its species trapped"; if so for trapping for healing he should be liable, for it is for a purpose; from what that is not in its species trapped — even trapping for a purpose is exempt; perforce it must be in its species trapped; and if so there is d'oraisa prohibition in trapping it; and for healing they do not permit because of widespread harm; and on this Magen Avraham wrote "and see siman 334" — meaning what he wrote there regarding extinguishing wooden coals in a place where many are harmed — per the Tur who holds melacha she'ein tzricha legufah exempt and it is only rabbinic prohibition — permitted to extinguish because of widespread harm; but for the Rambam who holds melacha she'ein tzricha legufah liable and there is d'oraisa prohibition in extinguishing the coals — forbidden to extinguish; behold they do not permit Torah prohibition because of widespread harm; therefore perforce the Rambam's reason for permitting trapping a snake must be that it is merely preoccupied, as Machatzit HaShekel's reason one:
