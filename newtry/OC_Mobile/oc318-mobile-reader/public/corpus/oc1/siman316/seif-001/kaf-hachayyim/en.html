1) [Seif 1] One who traps a free bird in a cabinet, etc. The Gemara, Shabbos 106b, explains that one is not liable for bringing a bird into a house specifically when it is a free bird. Although this species ordinarily lives in houses, it is not trapped there because it slips from corner to corner. With any other bird, however, once one brings it into a house and locks the door before it, he is liable because it is trapped. Beis Yosef. See below, paragraph 6. Ramban in Parashas Metzora implies that the general term 'bird' includes turtledoves, pigeons, geese, and chickens. Free birds are the very small birds that live on tree branches, sing, and chatter. They atone in the purification of a metzora for the sin of chatter. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1.
2) There: One who traps a free bird in a cabinet, etc. Even in a small house, a free bird cannot be caught. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1. Thus, even in a house whose windows are closed it is not considered trapped, because further trapping is still required. Taz, paragraph 1. Bach and the later authorities write the same.
3) There: One who traps a bird, etc. Trapping has no minimum measure. Even if one traps a bird intending to eat it, the bird need not contain the minimum edible measure of a dried fig, because we do not follow his intention. Rabbi Yosef Molcho, responsa manuscript, siman 31; Birkei Yosef, paragraph 1.
4) There: One who traps a bird, etc. If a bird entered a house through an open window, although it is not trapped there, one may not close the window or block the opening. It appears to me that in cold weather, when the cold or another discomfort causes suffering, one may close it if his sole intent is protection from the cold. Since this bird is not subject to Torah-level trapping, this is an inevitable rabbinic consequence. With another animal or bird to which Torah-level trapping applies, however, it is forbidden even when he has no such intention, because it is an inevitable consequence. Chayei Adam, klal 30, paragraph 2.
5) There: One who traps a bird, etc. If one encounters a nest on Shabbos with the mother resting upon the chicks, is he obligated to fulfill the mitzvah of sending away the mother bird? Responsa Chasam Sofer, Orach Chaim, siman 100, was asked this question. He answers that according to the reason of the sacred Zohar, that the mitzvah is intended to arouse the lament of the supernal Mother over her children, it is obvious that on Shabbos and Yom Tov one may not arouse lament above. Even according to the revealed Torah, it is improper. He writes there that even when no liability for trapping applies and there is only a rabbinic prohibition of muktzeh, it is nevertheless forbidden; see there. Pischei HaDevir, paragraph 1, cites him; see there for his comments. See also Rashash on Eitz Chaim, Gate 15, chapter 3, who writes that one must take care not to perform this mitzvah when Ze'eir Anpin and Nukva possess expanded consciousness, such as during prayer and similar times, during the forty-nine days of the Omer, or from Rosh Hashanah through Shemini Atzeres, because expanded consciousness is present throughout these times; see there. Accordingly, this mitzvah also has no place on Shabbos, because expanded consciousness enters on Shabbos and is not withdrawn, as is known.
6) There: And other birds, etc. Levush writes the same. Bach writes that no distinction should be made between a free bird and other birds, and that this is the view of Rif, Rambam, and Rosh; see there. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2, writes likewise. Even HaOzer, siman 497, paragraph 9, however, writes that Rambam and the halachic authorities also agree with the Shulchan Aruch that a distinction exists between a free bird and other birds; see there. This is the view of the later authorities.
7) There: And other birds, etc. Since the author states the rule without qualification, it implies that no distinction is made between geese and chickens and other birds. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 2. See above, paragraph 1.
8) There: And a deer into a house, etc. The same applies if one brings it into a garden or courtyard and locks it in. Rambam, chapter 10, halachah 19.
9) There: In which they are trapped, etc. This means that they can be caught with one chase, one can reach them in a single run, and one need not sit and rest in between. So writes the Maggid Mishneh. This is also the measure for creatures that require no further trapping, as stated in siman 497. The Gemara gives another measure: wherever the shadows of the walls meet. Tur and the author omit this measure because it is not uniform, since everything depends upon the height of the walls and width of the house. In the time of the Gemara there was a known standard for this, as Tosafos writes, and the authorities therefore cite only the other measure. All this applies when the windows are closed. If they are open, it obviously is not considered trapping because the bird can fly through the windows. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 2. This refers to a bird. A deer or other animal that cannot fly is considered trapped and one is liable even if the windows are open and the house is unroofed.
10) There: In which they are trapped, etc. One who removes fish from the sea into a bowl of water is liable. If he removes them from the sea into a pool of water, he is exempt. One who traps a lion is not liable until he brings it into its enclosure, where it is confined. Rambam, chapter 10, halachah 19; Magen Avraham, paragraph 2; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2; Tosefes Shabbos there. Similarly, one who traps an animal in a weak net that it can tear and escape from is not liable to bring a sin offering. He should leave it in the net and not take it in his hands. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 6.
