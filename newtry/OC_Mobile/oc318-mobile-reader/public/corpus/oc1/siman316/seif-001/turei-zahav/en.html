Free wild bird. In the Gemara they explain what we taught: "one who traps a bird for a coop — liable due to trapping; but for a house — not" — we deal with a free wild bird that does not accept dominion and lives in a house like in a field, slipping from corner to corner; if so, even in a house whose windows are not open it is not trapped, for it still lacks trapping; and even though Rashi explained in the Mishnah that the reason for a house is open windows — it is implied that with closed windows there is trapping — this is according to R' Yehuda, who does not hold to distinguish between lacking trapping or not; but Rashbag holds that lacking trapping is exempt — there is no distinction regarding windows; but other birds are liable even in a house. Therefore he distinguished here between one bird and another. But Tur did not distinguish in this — it appears to me he holds that what Rashbag said in our Mishnah is not the general rule "all lacking trapping are exempt, all not lacking trapping are liable," but it refers to a coop mentioned first: if it is a large coop where they are not trapped, if still lacking trapping — exempt; and in this is included the distinction between one bird and another — everything according to whether it is trapped and not lacking trapping, then liable. Therefore Tur wrote "one traps a bird for a coop" that is trapped there — his intent is both the bird according to what it is and the coop according to what it is. And so in what he wrote afterward: "and a deer for a house or aviary" that is trapped — his intent is a house with open windows where no bird is trapped, as Rashi explained in the Mishnah for R' Yehuda, and only an animal is trapped there. And in Shulchan Aruch here, where he wrote regarding other birds that there is a distinction in a house — that is, a house without open windows, as explained in the Gemara; regarding a free wild bird likewise with animals there is a distinction in a house between large and small — therefore he wrote that it is trapped there, as stated, to align Shulchan Aruch with Tur; and there is no dispute in law between them; and Maharshal entered into forced reasoning to make a dispute between them — and this is unnecessary:
