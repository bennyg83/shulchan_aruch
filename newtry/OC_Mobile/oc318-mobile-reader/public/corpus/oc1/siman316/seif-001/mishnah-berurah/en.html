(א) One who traps, etc. — namely that he opened the gate of the tower and made snares until the bird entered into it and he closed upon it — even if he did not yet seize it in his hand he is liable, for in closing the tower the trapping was completed. But in what he brought it into a house — even a small house and also its windows closed — it is still not trapped, for the nature of the dror bird is that it does not accept authority and dwells in a house like in a field, slipping from corner to corner, and they cannot catch it; and therefore it is not called trapping d'oraisa:

(ב) For the tower — of wood and made like a large cage; and in a vivarium it is called enclosures in which they raise them there:

(ג) And other birds and deer, etc. — it teaches them separately: regarding birds and likewise other fowl he is not liable until the vivarium and house are roofed and also its windows closed so it can flee through there; and regarding deer and other animals he is liable even if it is not roofed and also its windows are open:

(ד) That they are trapped — to exclude if the house and vivarium are large so they cannot catch it in one stride — namely one run — but must catch breath before reaching it; it is not called trapping d'oraisa even if it is roofed; and automatically one who traps it there in a house or vivarium is liable. See below in Mishna Berurah s.k. 58. One who scoops a fish from the sea into a bowl of water is liable, for it is trapping; but if he uprooted it and chased it into a pool of water — it is not trapping, for there too it slips into holes and cracks. See below in Mishna Berurah s.k. 133. One who traps a lion is not liable until he brings it into his den in which it is enclosed:

(ה) Exempt but forbidden — this refers to the entire seif; and therefore regarding a dror bird that entered a house through the door or window — even though it is not trapped there — nevertheless forbidden to close the door and window. And in a time of cold when there is pain from cold or other pain — Chayei Adam wrote that it is permitted to lock if his intent is only to save from cold and he does not want trapping of the bird at all, since there is no d'oraisa trapping in it, even though it is psik reisha in a rabbinic matter; but regarding other animal and bird where d'oraisa trapping applies in a house — forbidden even if he does not intend at all, for it is psik reisha:
