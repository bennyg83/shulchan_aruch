[1] And other birds, etc. Rif, Rambam chapter 10, Rosh, and Tur do not distinguish between a free dwelling bird and other birds. Lechem Mishneh and all the later authorities are surprised by this, and their answers are strained. In my humble opinion they follow Tosafot on Beitzah 24a, who write that in a roofed enclosure trapping occurs only when one no longer needs a trap to catch the animal, whereas if one still requires a trap, such as in a house or large enclosure, it is considered trapping. According to this, however, why did the Gemara distinguish between a free dwelling bird and other birds instead of distinguishing between a large and small enclosure, as it does with an animal? It therefore appears that when the Gemara concludes, "Now that you have come to this, the contradiction regarding an animal is also resolved by distinguishing a large enclosure, etc.," it retracts the earlier distinction concerning the free dwelling bird. Ran in Shabbos and Beitzah does distinguish a free dwelling bird, and thus Rif's position must be explained differently. Perhaps he holds that even in a large house trapping does not occur with other birds, while the prohibition in a trap here follows Rashi's explanation in Beitzah. One can thereby reconcile Responsa Mas'at Binyamin, siman 87. Alternatively, all agree that whenever an additional trap is needed it is prohibited, but even when no additional trap is needed, such as a small house, a free dwelling bird remains prohibited. This appears to be Shulchan Aruch's view. He first says that a free dwelling bird is trapped only in a tower, implying that other birds are trapped even in a house, and then says "other birds and a deer into a house in which they are trapped." It is strained to say that "in which they are trapped" refers only to the deer. Levush indeed appears to divide the cases: other birds even into a house, and a deer, etc. He thus follows the first explanation I gave of Ran. Nevertheless, the unqualified wording of Rif, Rambam, Rosh, and Tur requires saying that there is no distinction between a free dwelling bird and other birds. Rashba in Avodat HaKodesh, p. 38, also implies this. All this appears to concern closed windows, but with open windows it is not trapping, as Rashi's explanation of the Mishnah implies. Yet if so, the Gemara could have answered one case concerns open windows and the other closed windows. This can be resolved.
[2] In which they are trapped, etc. Meaning any enclosure where one can run after the animal and reach it in a single run, without maneuvering or excessive effort (Mas'at Binyamin there). Rashba in Avodat HaKodesh writes similarly: in one run, without having to pause for breath before reaching it. One who traps a lion is not liable until he brings it into its cage, where it is actually confined (Gemara; see seif katan 10). One who lifts fish from the sea into a bowl of water has trapped them, but placing them into a large pool of water is not trapping (Rambam).
[3] One who traps a deer, etc. This is the Gemara's wording. It appears the same applies to other species normally trapped, as Rambam and Semag imply when they write of trapping a sleeping or blind animal without mentioning a deer. Yet this requires study, for Rokeach, siman 78, cites the Yerushalmi: "One who traps a sleeping deer is liable, because it closes one eye and opens the other." This implies specifically a deer, whose nature is not to sleep with both eyes closed. I examined the source in the Yerushalmi, chapter Shemonah Sheratzim. It appears Rambam holds that the Yerushalmi disagrees with the Bavli because the Yerushalmi exempts one who traps a blind animal and therefore treats sleep like blindness, whereas the Bavli, which holds one liable for a blind animal, likewise holds one liable for a sleeping one. This requires study.
