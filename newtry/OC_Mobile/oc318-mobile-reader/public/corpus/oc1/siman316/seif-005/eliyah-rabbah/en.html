[12] [Levush] It is permitted to spread, etc. Because his intention is not to trap them (Malbushei Yom Tov). Shulchan Aruch indeed appears to say so. Yet this requires study from Beitzah 36b, where the Gemara reformulates the Mishnah's condition from "provided he does not intend to trap" to "provided he does not make it into a trap." Why was that reformulation necessary? The plain wording that he must not intend to trap would also work, even according to Rabbi Shimon, since intention should still matter. Also, why does the Gemara ask that the rule is obvious? It would teach a significant point that even here he must not intend to trap. Responsa Rabbi Yom Tov Tzahalon writes that when wasps leave the hive and settle on a nearby wall, people sometimes take a board, smear it with something attractive, place it before them, speak soothingly until they gather on it, then carry the board into the hive. Even a Jew may perhaps do this on Shabbos because speaking to them is not trapping, since he does not confine them and does not apply glue or anything else on Shabbos to prevent escape; they remain free to leave. Certainly one may instruct a non-Jew to do it.
