[8] Its species is not normally trapped, etc. Even if he traps it for a use, he is exempt (Tosafot; Ran at the beginning of chapter Shemonah Sheratzim).
[9] Not to close a chest, etc. Levush omits Rama's limitation to a small chest and prohibits even a large one. Kol Bo writes that one should not lock a chest or cabinet containing food when many flies are present unless he leaves an opening through which they can escape. Even a small hole not visible to the flies suffices, as Tosafot write regarding bees.
[10] [Levush] And some permit, etc. Bach is stringent like the first view. Taz is lenient when there is doubt whether flies are present. In my humble opinion one should not be lenient in summer where food is present, and Agudah in chapter Mashilin writes likewise. Beit Yosef objects that flies differ from bees because confining them is like bringing a lion into its room. Bach responds sharply that the lion analogy relates only to biblical liability, while a rabbinic prohibition remains even when the lion is merely brought into a courtyard. It appears to me that Beit Yosef's intent is that a lion is a species normally trapped, unlike flies, whose species is not normally trapped, creating two rabbinic factors. In any event one can say that a lion is not considered guarded in an ordinary place, whereas flies are effectively guarded in any closed chest.
