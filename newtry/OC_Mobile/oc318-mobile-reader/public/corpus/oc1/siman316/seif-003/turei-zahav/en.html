(א) Therefore flies etc. And therefore one should be careful etc. Tur in the name of Ba'al HaTerumot wrote prohibition in this; and he wrote on it: and it appears to me one need not investigate this etc.; and further they said "provided he does not intend to trap" — evidently if not intending it is permitted. It is difficult — for this is not so, for we explain it in the Gemara: what is "provided he does not intend to trap"? That he should not make it a trap — that is, it has windows — he should not cover those windows; for otherwise it is psik reisha; and R' Shimon who said davar she'eino mitkaven mutar nevertheless agrees that psik reisha is forbidden. Rather, there must be a place they can exit; and so Tosafos there in chapter Meshilin: there is a small hole the bees do not see — on this R' Yehuda and R' Shimon disagree: for R' Yehuda a large hole visible to bees is required; for R' Shimon a small one suffices. If so, here in a box with flies a small hole is also required — why did Tur permit entirely even without a small hole? And so Bach challenged. And Bach wrote further: what Tur distinguished between a small hive and a large one — we too distinguish between a small box and a large one; if so your words depend on measures — there are also large and small in Bechoros. It appears Tur's words are correct. First let us analyze his language: he wrote "and it appears one need not investigate this" — not "and it appears it is permitted" — rather he too holds it is forbidden when he sees flies with certainty in the box; but he says one need not investigate whether there are flies; and he gave one reason: even if it were psik reisha regarding flies that have no place to exit, nevertheless it is not forbidden because it is not called trapped, for it is not certain they will come to his hand — perhaps after he opens the cover and wishes to take them they will fly and not one will reach his hand. On this he said: and some say regarding bees in a hive — Tur intended that in any case they are trapped, for every hive is small for this purpose so not all bees can escape — impossible otherwise, for bees are large and fill the hive and it is impossible he not catch one; unlike a box with flies where they can escape when he opens it, and mostly we see this due to their smallness; and every box is large relative to them for escaping. With this Bach's challenge on Tur is resolved. And on Tur's second challenge it appears this is the intent: first, even if there are certainly flies, it is not called trapped like bees; and further, even if you say it is certainly psik reisha and forbidden, nevertheless in a doubt whether there are flies there one may permit, for there is no psik reisha — this would be doubt psik reisha, which is davar shelo b'mitkaven and permitted, like in a hive where there is no psik reisha when there is a small hole as stated — likewise here there is not certainly psik reisha, for perhaps there are no flies; and this is like where there is no psik reisha, permitted when not intending, as above — to establish Tur's words correctly; for he did not come to dispute Ba'al HaTerumot in explanation, but "it appears one need not investigate afterward" and one may close the box without checking for flies; and so appears to me for practical law: if he sees flies there he should wave them away first; but need not search further; and all the more if he waved once and we are not concerned more remain — and our master Tur is fit to rely on this, especially since we do not find anyone disputing him; and Maharshal explained Tur's words that regarding bees it is not psik reisha, for it is not pleasing to him that they be trapped and unable to exit to eat flowers and make honey — and he did not analyze that certainly any matter whose species is not trapped is not pleasing to him in that trapping at all; rather the Sages decreed due to trapping of a species that is trapped, which is pleasing. And Rabbeinu Yosef wrote: rather if one can reject Ba'al HaTerumot's words, thus one can reject: bees are different, for a hive is a place where they are trapped, like bringing a lion into his den; but flies in a box and hive are not their trapping place — perhaps they are not considered trapped until seized by hand — end of his words. And I wonder at the great Bach: if he did not wish to reject our master Tur, certainly his rejection has no substance; for a lion in a den stands there and is not angry to destroy his enclosure place; but in a place that is not his place he destroys and is not trapped, as Rashi explained at the end of chapter HaOreg; unlike flies in a box — wherever they cannot exit from there they are trapped; therefore we must say when he opens the box they will flee afterward — this is our master Tur's reasoning. Bach further wrote: or necessarily R' Shimon forbade spreading a mat on a hive only as psik reisha with bees whose species is trapped; but flies whose species is not trapped — all that is not intending is permitted even with psik reisha — end of his words. And Maharshal challenged him well: in chapter Meshilin it is explicit afterward that bees are a species not trapped, and nevertheless require specifically no psik reisha:
