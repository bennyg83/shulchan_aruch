And he went away. Taz wrote that he went into the house — for otherwise how is it possible that the second fills the place of the first without first the place becoming empty of one who sits, and then the deer is not trapped; and it turns out that when the second returns and fills the opening he trapped it and is liable. Rather as he explained, end of his words. And one might ask: is it that one who has a hen in his house and the door opened — it should be forbidden to close it? Rather in any case, since it is already trapped it is permitted; here too so. For Rashi wrote that this is only when he is guarding a deer that he had from yesterday. And it appears to me that the language of the Ran misled him — see there; but in truth the Ran comes to say that even though he adds guarding upon guarding it is permitted — and all the more so where he does not add anything at all, it is obvious that it is permitted. And the Ran holds that this was not needed to teach us in the Mishnah and to say "to what is this similar," etc., as Tosafos wrote there. And what he wrote that the deer is tied and one came and locked — it deals when the door was not yet locked while they tied the deer inside — see there. Nevertheless Machatzit HaShekel wrote: the Ramban wrote — one sat on the opening and did not know that there is a deer inside, and afterward it became known that there is a deer there — permitted for him to sit until dark, because trapping preceded thought. And Rashba wrote that in the Yerushalmi it appears that it is permitted to lock to guard his house even though thereby the deer is trapped automatically — permitted, provided he does not intend to guard the deer alone, end of his words; and one should not be lenient so much, end of his words. And the Ran also challenged Rashba, for it is psik reisha; and in Shaarei Gibborim he wrote to reconcile Rashba's view — see there; nevertheless one should not be lenient. A bird entered beneath his wings — he sits and guards it until dark [Gemara]; permitted to open the house because of the deer or to release it from his trap, provided he does not move it (Machatzit HaShekel):
