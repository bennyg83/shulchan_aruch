(א) He can, etc. — and even if his intent is for the deer's sake — permitted, since it is already trapped through the first:
(ב) And he went away — it deals when at the time the first went the second did not move from his place at all; and such as when his going was into the house, or the second sat inside the hollow of the opening inside toward the house and the first sat inside the hollow of the opening outward — it turns out that when the first went he remained in his place; and therefore permitted for him l'chatchila to sit until dark even though he intends on Shabbat for the deer's sake, since he does not do a new act but guards the deer that is already trapped. If it were the reverse — that the second sat outside and the first inside — then the second would need to rise from his place when the first went outside, and automatically the deer's trapping ceased; and when the second returns to sit in his place to fill the opening — behold he is liable because of trapping. Forbidden to lock his house in order to guard his household utensils if he knows there is a deer inside, even though he does not intend for trapping the deer — for it is psik reisha and forbidden from the Torah; and all the more so if he intends for both. One sat on the opening and did not fill it, and the second sat and filled it — the second is liable, for through him the trapping was done. A bird entered beneath the wings of his garment — even though it is already trapped and standing — nevertheless forbidden for him to take it with his hand because of muktzeh of living things; rather he sits and guards it until dark, or if he has a house he goes and places it there. Permitted to open the house before the deer or to release it from his trap, provided he does not move it:
