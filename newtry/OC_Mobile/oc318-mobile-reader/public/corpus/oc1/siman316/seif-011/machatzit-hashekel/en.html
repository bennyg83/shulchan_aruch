(א) (s.k. 11) And he went, etc. — that he went into the house, etc. — meaning that the width of the opening is only enough for one person to sit; therefore through the first person's sitting he filled the entire hollow of the opening and the deer was trapped; and what he wrote "and afterward the second came and sat at his side" — the explanation "sat at his side" does not mean to his right or left but sat before him; and such as when the doorposts were long and there is measure in them for two people to sit inside — if so, through the second's sitting he added guarding, for the second also filled the entire width of the opening — and this is certainly permitted, for here it was already trapped and standing through the first; rather even afterward when the first stood, the second need not stand even though now it is guarded only through the second alone; nevertheless since at the beginning of his sitting it was permitted, therefore Taz wrote that perforce it deals that the first went into the house; and if so the second's initial sitting was when the first already sat and it was trapped through the first — the second's sitting is permitted; therefore afterward the second need not stand when the first went before him. But if the first had gone outside — if their sitting were as stated one before the other and each filled the opening — how is it possible that the first went outside and passed over the second? Perforce the second gave him room and cleared space for the first to exit; if so, immediately when the first went outside and part of the opening was open — the first's trapping ceased; and when the second afterward filled the opening — behold the second trapped it from the outset. And so too one can say it deals that they sat one beside the other and each filled half the opening — difficult: at the beginning when the second sat, why is it called that he sat permissibly? For the first filled the entire opening; nevertheless when the second came to sit beside him — perforce the first contracted and left empty space for the second to sit; if so the second trapped it through his sitting initially; and likewise when the first exits — perforce it deals that the second sat before the first and each filled the entire opening as stated, and the first entered before him into the house:

(ב) For Rashi wrote, etc. — on the Mishnah daf 106b: one sat on the opening, etc., even though the first stood, etc., and the second is exempt:

(ג) To what is this similar — locking his house to guard it and a deer is found guarded inside — thus Rashi explained; and so too even though the first stood — this is only when he guards a deer that he had from yesterday, end of his words. It is implied from this language that the permission for the second's sitting is because it was already trapped once, even though at the time of the second's sitting the first's guarding ceased; and therefore Rashi wrote "that he had from yesterday" — for if according to Taz's explanation, Rashi should have said "since now at the time of the second's sitting it is trapped through the first":

(ד) The language of the Ran misled him — brought in Taz, who wrote on the end that teaches "to what is this similar," etc., and thus: such as when there is a deer inside the house and the door is of itself and it is guarded thus, and this one came and locked further with a lock — even though he adds guarding upon its guarding it is permitted; and we do not say this is like trapping that animal, because if he had not locked perhaps the doors would open and the deer would escape (and this is what the Rama wrote in the gloss). So too the second is permitted to sit beside the first, since the first filled the opening — even though after the first departed the deer remained trapped through the second. Or such as when the deer was tied in this house and he locked — even though the deer was released, this one cannot open his house so the deer will exit, since when he locked it was permissibly; this second too, since when he sat it was permissively he sat — they do not obligate him to depart from there after the first departed, end of his words. And from the fact that the Ran cited both versions where he added guarding upon guarding — it is implied that at the time the second added the guarding — and the same here with the second's sitting — perforce it deals that the first's guarding still stands and this added upon it; therefore Taz was forced to explain it and establish the Mishnah in this version that Taz wrote:

(ה) But in truth the Ran comes, etc. — that what the Ran wrote in the version where he adds guarding upon its guarding, and likewise establishes the Gemara in "to what is this similar," etc. — the intent is not as Taz understood, that he comes to limit that specifically when he adds guarding; but rather it was already guarded and standing. But if the first's guarding ceased — forbidden for the second to sit to fill the opening; rather on the contrary the Ran cited to include that even though he adds guarding upon guarding — one might think this is worse than if the second had not sat until the first stood and went, that when the second sat it is not guarded more than when guarded while the first sat and he did not add in guarding — certainly permitted:

(ו) As Tosafos wrote, etc. — he brings proof from Tosafos' words that one might think that when he adds guarding it is worse; and thus: why is this similar, etc.; and you might ask what comparison is there between this and that; and it appears to Rivavan that one might think that he comes and sits beside him — it should be forbidden because it appears that he comes to add guarding for the deer, end of Tosafos. Behold it is explained that when he adds guarding it is worse; nevertheless for the Ran who establishes also the Gemara in "to what is this similar," etc., in adding guarding upon guarding — according to us one must resolve Tosafos' difficulty "what comparison is there between this and that":

(ז) And what he wrote that the deer, etc. — it deals such as when the house was not yet locked, etc. — for otherwise without tying the deer, if it opens again — permitted to lock it according to Magen Avraham's view, and as he wrote proof above from the hen:

(ח) And in Shaarei Gibborim he wrote to reconcile Rashba's view — brought in Hagahot Maimoniyot, and thus: we find to learn from Rashba's view that even though we rule psik reisha is forbidden — that is specifically where in that act he does psik reisha he does not intend and does not do a permitted act with it; but if in that psik reisha he does he also does a permitted act with it and also intends for the permitted act — then even if he also does the psik reisha and also intends for it — it is permitted. And in truth this is a great leniency, and I wrote in my novellae that every act where it is possible to do aside from the psik reisha — then even if he does that act of psik reisha it is permitted; and from Rashba's words there is great support for my words, for they are a fortiori from his words, end of Shaarei Gibborim chapter Ha'oreg:
