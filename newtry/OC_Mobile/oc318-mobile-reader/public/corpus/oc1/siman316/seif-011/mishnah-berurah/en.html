(א) On the ground — and on a paved floor depends on the dispute in siman 336 seif 2 regarding honor of the house in a paved courtyard — see there; and for the Rama who forbids there in all cases — the same here, forbidden even on a paved floor. And on a bench — according to all permitted, for smoothing does not apply here; and regarding smoothing itself there is no room to forbid, for smoothing applies only when he smears something upon another and his intent is that it should be smoothed; but here he wants it to be absorbed:
(ב) And even though automatically, etc. — meaning that sometimes he smooths; and there is concern perhaps he will forget and intend and equal hollows, as is written siman 302 seif 6 — nevertheless here permitted because of repulsiveness:
(ג) Permitted because of repulsiveness — even though when trampling on spittle he usually also rubs somewhat — nevertheless they permitted because of repulsiveness, since he does not rub back and forth and it is not psik reisha. And specifically when it is in the course of his walking even though he intends to trample on the spittle — but he does not intend to smooth it; but forbidden to go to the place of spittle in order to trample on it, for this is not called in the course of his way; rather he will go and place his foot on the spittle. And he should be careful not to rub at all — this is permitted in all cases. Magen Avraham wrote: nowadays, since people are not particular about spittle because of repulsiveness — one must be careful that there be no rubbing at all when trampling on it; only place his foot on the spittle. And regarding saliva that comes from the mouth or nose — certainly there is repulsiveness; and permitted to trample it in the course of his way. And in a synagogue even spittle is permitted to trample in the course of his way, for they treat it with honor, as above in siman 90 seif 13; and it appears that even in his house when paved one should not be more stringent with spittle than with saliva:
