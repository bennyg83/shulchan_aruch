(א) (s.k. 7) Small — but large — the Rama holds that according to all it is permitted, etc. — for the Tur wrote, and this is his language: Beit Yosef wrote — forbidden to lock on Shabbat the box that has flies in it, but, etc., in a manner that they can exit from there; and it appears to me that one need not be precise in this that the flies are not trapped in the box, for if he comes to open the box and take them they will flee:

(ב) And it is not comparable to bees in a beehive, for the Mishnah teaches spreading a mat over a beehive provided he does not intend to trap — for the beehive is small and the bees are trapped in it; and furthermore in the beehive itself it teaches "provided he does not intend to trap" — from which it is implied that when he does not intend it is permitted, end of the Tur. And the Rama brought these two views — first he brought Beit Yosef's view who is stringent, and afterward he brought the Tur's view. And Rama wrote: according to Beit Yosef — therefore there is, etc., a small box — it is implied that in a large one even Beit Yosef agrees it is permitted, even though the Tur who disagrees wrote to distinguish between this case here and a beehive; it is implied that Beit Yosef forbids even in a large one. But the Rama holds that in a large one even Beit Yosef agrees; and one can explain only in a small one — since if a person were standing inside the box they would be trapped, even though if he opens the box they will fly out — nevertheless there is trapping there; and the Tur permits even in a small one because of his first reasoning that if he opens the box they will flee; and he did not write that a beehive is different except according to Beit Yosef — according to what the Tur understood of Beit Yosef, who forbids even in a large one. On this Niv Chayim wrote: one can say that for Beit Yosef this reasoning that if he opens they will flee is unnecessary; nevertheless in a large one there is room to permit; and there is no proof from a beehive that is small.
