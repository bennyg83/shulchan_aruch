And the custom is to blow on the bimah; and there are places that customarily blow on the first day on the bimah and on the second day in his place (Kol Bo); and we do not practice so, but rather blow both days on the bimah. And it is fitting to give [the opportunity] to blow to a righteous and worthy person, provided that there be no dispute in the matter (Sefer Chasidim, siman 764). And after the Torah reading and the blasts that are blown in the order of the prayer, they blow [both of] them; and there are places that blow near the chazzan. In the holy congregation of Vienna they used to blow even those blasts of the order of the prayer on the bimah, and that baal tokea ascends the bimah immediately before they begin Musaf and stands there until the end of the prayer; and I recall that this custom was instituted by the outstanding gaon, Moreinu HaRav R' Wolf, of blessed memory, when he was head of the beis din and rosh yeshiva there. And at the time that he blows after the Torah reading, the congregation sits; but for the blasting that is in the order of the blessings, which is according to the Torah, the listener too must stand. And a mnemonic for some of the things for which the congregation must stand is: “The counsel of Hashem stands forever” [stand] upon [them]; see above in se'if 5.
