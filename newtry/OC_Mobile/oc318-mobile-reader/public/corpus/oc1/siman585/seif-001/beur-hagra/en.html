Seif 1: He must. See Rosh at the end of Pesachim, siman 41, and see Baal Halachos Gedolos.
And the custom is, etc. Midrash Rabbah at the beginning of parashas Shoftim states: Rabbi Yitzchak said that two things are at the right hand of the Holy One, blessed be He, righteousness and Torah. Righteousness, as it is written, “Your right hand is filled with righteousness.” Torah, as it is written, “From His right hand came a fiery law for them.” Two things are in His hand, the soul and judgment. The soul, as it is written, “In Whose hand is the soul of every living thing.” Judgment, as it is written, “My hand takes hold of judgment,” etc. Rokeach writes that one should mention His righteousness during the Torah reading, because both righteousness and Torah are associated with the right hand, in order to benefit the soul at the time of judgment. This is the meaning of the verses in Tehillim 89, “Righteousness and justice,” etc., followed by, “Praiseworthy is the people who know the shofar blast,” etc. Pesikta relates that Rabbi Yoshiyah bar Nachmani said in the name of Rabbi Shimon ben Lakish: The verse states, “God ascends with a blast.” When the Holy One, blessed be He, ascends and sits upon the throne of judgment, He ascends with judgment. When Israel take the shofar and sound it, the Holy One, blessed be He, rises from the throne of judgment and sits upon the throne of mercy, etc. See Rokeach there. This is the meaning of “righteousness and justice,” etc., for the left side is included within the right. This is also the meaning of, “My lamp is in your hand, and your lamp is in My hand,” etc., as explained above. Therefore, judgment is placed together with the Torah.
