1) [Seif 1] One must blow the shofar while standing. Scripture states, "It shall be a day of teruah for you," and we derive the word "for you" from the same expression stated regarding the Omer and counting the Omer, which are performed standing, as it says, "From when the sickle begins upon the standing grain." Do not read it merely as "standing grain," but as "standing upright." Tur. Hagahot Maimoniyot, chapter 3, writes likewise in the name of Sefer Mitzvot Katan, and Beit Yosef cites it. Kesef Mishneh, Hilchot Temidin UMusafin, law 23, writes in the name of R' Yitzchak ibn Ghayyat that this was received as a tradition from our teachers and supported by the verse. This was cited above in siman 489, letter 15. Birkei Yosef, here letter 1, likewise cites it and writes that we find the same in Midrash Pesikta, Parashat Lech Lecha and Parashat Emor, as he wrote above in siman 8. In siman 8, letter 2, he cites Pesikta that we learn from the Omer regarding every mitzvah in which the word "for you" appears, such as brit milah, tzitzit, and lulav: "Every male among you shall undergo brit milah," "It shall be for you as tzitzit," and "You shall take for yourselves." All of these mitzvot are performed standing. Accordingly, it appears that the blessing too should be recited standing, as with the Omer and tzitzit. Yad Ephraim on Magen Avraham, seif katan 1, writes likewise, as do Mateh Ephraim, letter 3; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, letter 4; and Mishnah Berurah, letter 1. It also appears that one who hears the blessing from the shaliach tzibbur in order to fulfill his obligation should stand, because hearing is considered equivalent to responding. Machatzit HaShekel at the beginning of siman 690 writes likewise. Ben Ish Chai, Parashat Nitzavim, letter 15, however, writes that some places have the custom to stand during the blessing over shofar and some do not; in his city the custom was not to stand. 2) Know that the gezeirah shavah deriving "for you" regarding shofar from "for you" regarding the Omer applies also to the listeners. Since the principal shofar blasts are those of Mussaf, one need stand only for them, and they are therefore called the blasts sounded while standing. The earlier blasts, called the blasts sounded while seated, are primarily to confuse the Satan. The blower himself was required to stand because mitzvot of this type are performed standing, or alternatively because of the honor of the congregation. Radbaz, part 1, siman 25; Mor Uketziah; Eliyah Rabbah, letter 2; Mateh Yehudah; Machazik Berachah, letter 1; Erech HaShulchan, letter 1. Erech HaShulchan notes that manuscript rulings of R' Yeshayah the First hold that the blasts sounded while seated are the principal ones. In his Chukat HaPesach, however, in the addenda to this siman, he agrees with Radbaz. The later authorities rule likewise. 3) One who did not hear the shofar blasts in the order of the blessings and then comes to hear them must stand while listening. Radbaz; Magen Avraham, seif katan 1; Shulchan Gavoah, letter 3; Birkei Yosef, letter 3; Shaarei Teshuvah, letter 1. Likewise, an individual who hears the shofar in order to fulfill his obligation and will not later hear the blasts in the order of the blessings must stand. If he heard them while seated, he fulfilled his obligation. Mateh Ephraim, letter 5; Mishnah Berurah, letter 2. See below, letter 5. 4) "One must blow while standing." One should not lean upon something in such a way that if the support were removed he would fall. Derashot Maharil, Hilchot Shofar; Darkei Moshe; Magen Avraham; Pri Chadash; Eliyah Rabbah, letter 1; Birkei Yosef, letter 4; Shaarei Teshuvah; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, letter 1; Chayei Adam, rule 141, letter 6; Mateh Ephraim; Mishnah Berurah. 5) "One must blow while standing." If he blew while seated, he fulfilled his obligation, because shofar is derived from the Omer, and regarding the Omer itself one fulfills the obligation after the fact while seated, as stated in siman 489. Magen Avraham; Pri Chadash; Eliyah Rabbah, letter 3; Birkei Yosef, letter 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav; Chayei Adam; Mateh Ephraim; Mishnah Berurah. See above, siman 489, letter 16. If the blower has already fulfilled his obligation and blows for another individual, he may blow while seated, but the listener must stand. Siddur Amudei Shamayim; Pithei Olam, letter 2.
