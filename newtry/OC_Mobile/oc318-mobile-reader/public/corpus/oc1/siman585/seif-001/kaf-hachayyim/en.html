1) [Seif 1] One must blow while standing. For it is written, "It shall be a day of sounding for you," and we derive the word "for you" from the same word written regarding the Omer and the counting of the Omer, which are performed standing, as it is written, "From when the sickle begins upon the standing grain." Do not read it as "standing grain," but as "standing upright." Tur. Hagahos Maimoniyos, chapter 3, likewise writes this in the name of Sefer Mitzvos Katan, and Beit Yosef cites his words. See Kesef Mishneh, Hilchos Temidin UMusafin, law 23, who writes in the name of R. Yitzchak ibn Ghayas that this is a tradition received from our teachers, which they supported with a Scriptural verse, see there. His words were cited above, siman 489, os 15. Birkei Yosef, in this siman, os 1, also cites his words and writes that we likewise find this in Midrash Pesikta, Parashas Lech Lecha and Parashas Emor, as he wrote above, siman 8, see there. See there, siman 8, os 2, where he writes in the name of the Pesikta that we learn from the Omer regarding every mitzvah in which the word "for you" is stated, such as circumcision, tzitzis, and lulav: "Every male among you shall be circumcised," "It shall be for you as tzitzis," and "You shall take for yourselves." All of these mitzvos are performed standing, see there. According to this, it is implied that the blessing must also be recited standing, as with the Omer and tzitzis, etc. So writes Yad Efrayim on Magen Avraham, s.k. 1; and so writes Mateh Ephraim, os 3; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, os 4; Mishna Berurah, os 1. It is implied that one who hears the blessing from the shaliach tzibbur in order to fulfill his obligation must also stand, since hearing is considered equivalent to responding. So too writes Machatzis HaShekel at the beginning of siman 690, see there. However, Ben Ish Chai, Parashas Nitzavim, os 15, writes that in some places the custom is to stand during the blessing over the blowing of the shofar, while in other places they did not adopt this custom, and in our city the custom is not to stand, end quote.
2) Know that the gezeirah shavah by which we derive the word "for you" stated regarding shofar from the word "for you" stated regarding the Omer applies to the listeners as well. Since the principal shofar blasts are those sounded during Mussaf, one need stand only for them; they are therefore called the blasts sounded while standing. The blasts sounded while seated are only to confuse the Satan. They required the blower to stand in accordance with the law of mitzvos that are performed standing, or alternatively, out of honor for the congregation. Radbaz, vol. 1, siman 25, see there. So too writes Mor Uketziah; Eliyah Rabbah, os 2; Mateh Yehudah; Machazik Berachah, os 1; Erech HaShulchan, os 1. However, Erech HaShulchan writes there that the manuscript rulings of R. Yeshayah the First hold that the blasts sounded while seated are the principal blasts, see there. But in his work Chukas HaPesach, at its end, in Shiyurei Erech HaShulchan on this siman, he concurs with the Radbaz, see there. So too write the later authorities.
3) One who did not hear the shofar blasts in the order of the blessings and then comes to hear the blasts must stand while listening. Radbaz, ibid.; Magen Avraham, s.k. 1; Shulchan Gavoah, os 3; Birkei Yosef, os 3; Shaarei Teshuvah, os 1. Likewise, an individual who hears the blasts in order to fulfill his obligation and will not later hear them in the order of the blessings must stand. If he heard them while seated, he fulfilled his obligation. Mateh Ephraim, os 5; Mishna Berurah, os 2. See below, os 5.
4) Ibid. One must blow while standing. He may not lean upon an object in such a manner that if the object were removed he would fall. Derashos Maharil, Hilchos Shofar; Darkei Moshe; Magen Avraham, ibid.; Pri Chadash; Eliyah Rabbah, os 1; Birkei Yosef, os 4; Shaarei Teshuvah, ibid.; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, os 1; Chayei Adam, klal 141, os 6; Mateh Ephraim, ibid.; Mishna Berurah, ibid.
5) Ibid. One must blow while standing. If he blew while seated, he fulfilled his obligation, for shofar is derived from the Omer, and regarding the Omer itself one fulfills his obligation after the fact, as written in siman 489. Magen Avraham, ibid.; Pri Chadash; Eliyah Rabbah, os 3; Birkei Yosef, os 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ibid.; Chayei Adam, ibid.; Mateh Ephraim, ibid.; Mishna Berurah, ibid. See above, siman 489, os 16. If the blower has already fulfilled his obligation and blows for another individual, he may blow even while seated, but the listener must stand. Siddur Amudei Shamayim; Pischei Olam, os 2.
