See the Taz; and one must understand his words: for in any case, even if the first one has already fulfilled his obligation, even so after the fact it is not a blessing in vain; for all the blessings (even though he has already fulfilled [his obligation]) he may discharge others of the blessing. And see the Bach, who wrote that for one who is expert one may say he does not discharge [others], and only for one who is not expert; therefore he said that the first one fulfills [his obligation] with the second one’s blasting, if so, even for an expert the law is so. And in the name of the gaon R' Leib Chanelis of Lublin, of blessed memory, [someone] wrote that it resembles the case of [one who said Borei peri ha-]adamah over a lupine that fell from his hand, [where] he must recite the blessing a second time; [therefore] it teaches us here that since he recited “to hear,” and the blast was not lost from him, for he fulfills [his obligation] by hearing the second (see there. And regarding Kiddush (siman 273), specifically those who do not know) see there. And see siman 206, se'if 6 regarding a lupine (see there). And according to this, when one recited “to blow the shofar,” which according to the Bach after the fact he has fulfilled [his obligation] (see Magen Avraham 2) and he is unable to blow, apparently the second should recite the blessing, for from the first the blast was lost, and his intent was to bless specifically upon his own blowing; and this is worse than if he had already fulfilled his obligation, [where] his intent was that others fulfill [theirs] with his blessing. But that is not so, for “to blow” also has the intent of hearing; and see siman 206 for the ruling regarding a lupine. And with God’s help in the Magen Avraham it will be further explained.
