And it is not a blessing in vain. And the Tur concluded on this: since he fulfills [his obligation] with the second one’s blasts; and the Beis Yosef wrote that he was not precise, [for] since the second one fulfills [his obligation] with his blessing, even if he himself does not fulfill [his obligation] with the second one’s blasts (for example if he goes away) it was not a blessing in vain. And in my humble opinion, the Tur’s words here are exactly in the manner that the Rama wrote in siman 284, se'if 20: if they called up for maftir someone who does not know how to read the haftarah, another may say it, but ideally one should not do so (end quote). Here likewise: ideally this one should not recite the blessing intending that another will blow; and if so, one might have thought that it is a blessing in vain, and we would not say “even though he has already fulfilled [his obligation], he may discharge others,” unless the one who blesses [also] blows; [therefore] it teaches us that after the fact it is at least permitted, and the first one fulfills [something] through the second one’s blasting; therefore there is no blessing in vain here. And I have seen interpretations of this that are unnecessary, in my humble opinion.
