S.k. 3. And it appears to me that this custom is for women who do not know [how to recite the blessings]; but one who knows should recite the blessing himself and fulfill [his obligation] according to all opinions; but if he does not know, the baal tokea may properly recite even the Shehecheyanu blessing; and so wrote Kenesses HaGedolah, etc., so it should read. And that is to say: Kenesses HaGedolah wrote that the Rambam, who wrote that one who performs a mitzvah for others recites a blessing on its performance, but Shehecheyanu he recites only on a mitzvah he performs for himself, is dealing specifically with one who circumcises another’s son, for he too performs the mitzvah, and he is obligated to recite the blessing on performing the mitzvah, but he is not obligated to recite Shehecheyanu, for example because it is not for himself; and according to this, regarding blowing the shofar, even though he has already fulfilled [his obligation], the baal tokea should recite the blessing “to hear” [and] the blessing Shehecheyanu, since he is his agent (end quote). And that is to say: also regarding one who circumcises another’s son, if his fellow does not know and he intends to discharge both of them of the blessings, he can likewise discharge him; rather the Rambam is not dealing with one who wishes to discharge his fellow, but only regarding the obligation that rests upon him as the one performing the mitzvah, in that the Rambam says that only the blessing of performance rests upon him, but not the blessing Shehecheyanu, since he is not performing it for himself.
