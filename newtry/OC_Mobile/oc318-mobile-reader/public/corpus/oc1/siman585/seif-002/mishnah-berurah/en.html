(4) He should recite “to hear,” etc., That is to say, he should not say “to blow for You with the shofar,” for the matter does not depend on blowing but on hearing; for one who blows and did not hear the sound of the shofar has not fulfilled [his obligation], as is explained in siman 587 (see there). And he should not say “be-kol,” which implies doing His will; and after the fact, if he recited “to blow the shofar” or “on the blasting of the shofar” or “to hear be-kol shofar,” he has fulfilled [his obligation]. If they took the shofar in the middle of the blasts and brought him another shofar, he need not recite a blessing [again]; but between the blessing and the beginning of the blasts he must recite a blessing.
(5) Or that he has already fulfilled, etc. The later authorities concluded that in this manner, where the baal tokea himself has already fulfilled [his obligation], it is better that the hearers recite both blessings themselves, unless they do not know how to recite the blessings themselves, then he should recite on their behalf to discharge them; and the custom of the world is to be lenient and discharge them in every case, and one should not protest against them, for so it is primary according to the law, as the Peri Chadash and the Gra wrote. One who becomes unsure whether he heard the sound of the shofar [or took the lulav] on day one, which is according to the Torah; he blows and does not recite a blessing; and on day two, which is only from the words of the Scribes, he need not blow, for a doubt of the words of the Scribes [is treated] leniently.
(6) And he should blow tekiah-shevarim-teruah-tekiah three times, etc., For the reason for this order, see below in siman 590, se'if 1 and 2.
(7) On the right side; of his mouth, because it is written, “And the Satan stands at his right to accuse him.”
(8) If it is possible to blow thus. And if it is impossible for him, one should not be particular even if he positions it on the left side, and also the mouth of the shofar toward the left.
(9) And likewise he should turn the shofar upward, etc., That is to say, it is good that he turn the mouth of the shofar upward and not to the sides.
