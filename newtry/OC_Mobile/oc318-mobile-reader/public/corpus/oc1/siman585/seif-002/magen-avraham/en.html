The sound of the shofar. And he should not say “be-kol” [“to obey the voice”], which implies doing His will; and if he recited [the blessing] “to blow the shofar,” he has fulfilled [his obligation] (Bach).
And there is no difference. And it is better that the hearer recite the blessing, except that people do not practice so (Terumat HaDeshen). And regarding Shehecheyanu he wrote nothing; and the Rambam wrote that he himself should recite Shehecheyanu; and so wrote Minhagei [Maharil] in the name of the Mahariv; and so wrote the Hagahos Maimoniyos; but the Beis Yosef wrote that the custom is not so, and on that basis the Rama wrote the gloss. And it appears to me that this custom is for people who do not know [how to recite the blessings]; but one who knows should recite both blessings himself and fulfill [his obligation] according to all opinions; and so wrote Kenesses HaGedolah, that the Rambam is dealing specifically with one who circumcises another’s son, or who makes a sukkah or affixes a mezuzah for another, and this is the main [view]. If he is in doubt whether he heard the blast or took the lulav on the first day, he blows and does not recite a blessing, and on the second day he need not blow (Kenesses HaGedolah; Rivash); see Yoreh De’ah siman 28 and above siman 67.
On the right side. Of his mouth, because it is written, “And the Satan stands at his right to accuse him”; and the Shlah wrote that so it is proper according to Kabbalah, not like the Levush. And in the Hagahos Maimoniyos he wrote: and not on the left, because the hand-tefillin protect (Agudah); and if so, a left-handed person should blow on his own right (see siman 27, se'if 6).
He should turn. Meaning the shofar upward (language of the Maharil). It appears to me that his intent is that he position the mouth upward and not to the sides. And in the Levush he wrote, “he should turn the shofar a little upward,” implying that he means he should raise the mouth a little upward.
