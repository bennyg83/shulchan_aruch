Se'if 2: Before. Pesachim 7b, and Yerushalmi.
He should recite the blessing. Yerushalmi.
And there is no. Rosh Hashanah 29a.
Even so he recites the blessing. As is written at the end of chapter 3: “All the blessings,” etc. and see the Hagahos Ashri there, s.v. “for example,” etc., and “the blessing of the mitzvos,” etc., “even,” etc. and all the more so in such a case, where he himself blows for his [own] sake.
The baal tokea. That is to say, to exclude the Terumat HaDeshen, that only the hearer recites the blessing.
Two blessings. That is to say, to exclude [the view of] the Mahariv, who wrote that he does not recite Shehecheyanu, as the Beis Yosef wrote in the name of that source (see there). And it does not appear to me that it is relevant to the Rambam’s words; for here he recites the blessing on behalf of the hearer; and so wrote the Kesef Mishneh, chapter 11 of Hilchos Berachos, in the name of the Ramach, law 10; and the Rambam derives [it] from one who makes a sukkah for himself, etc. (Sukkah 46a); and see there in Tosafos and the Rosh.
And he should blow. Rosh Hashanah 34a.
And it is good. Zechariah 3.
