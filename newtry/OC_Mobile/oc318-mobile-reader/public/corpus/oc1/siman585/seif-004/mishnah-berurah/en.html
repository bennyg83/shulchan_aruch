(13) So that he not become confused; But it is not an interruption, for it is considered a need of the prayer, for these blasts were fixed in the order of the blessings.
(14) And if he is confident, etc. And if he is not confident and there is no other who knows how to blow, some say that in this manner he should not blow until after the prayer, and all the more so when he has already fulfilled [his obligation] with the blasts while seated; and some say that if there is no one else there he may blow in the order of the blessings even though he is not confident, for he should trust that Heaven will assist him to do [it] properly. And if he prays from the siddur and the machzor, according to all opinions his law is like one who is confident.
(15) He may blow, even if there is someone else there; for blasting is not considered an interruption at all, as above.
(16) And specifically, etc., for there we are concerned that he will become confused and be unable to return to his prayer.
(17) They say to him, “Finish” And in places where they practiced to divide and give the seated blasts to one and the standing [blasts] to another, or also to divide the sequences (Malchuyos to one, Zichronos to another, etc.) they should do according to their custom, for Israel are holy and they cherish the mitzvos.
(18) To call out. The one who prays Shacharis calls out for the baal tokea; and where there is a moreh tzedek, he calls out. Also the first blast he should call out before him, even though it is not so common that he err. Once they were unable to blow, and he turned the shofar over and recited “Vihi Noam” into it, and they were then able to blow [Magen Avraham in the name of the Maharil]; and see in Dagul Merevavah that this is specifically for the standing blasts, but for the seated blasts it is a complete interruption between the blessing and the blasting; and see in Shaarei Teshuvah, who somewhat resolves this matter; and nevertheless, ideally it appears to my humble opinion that it is not worthwhile to do so. And if he needed to take another shofar, he need not go back and recite a blessing, since it was lying before him on the table (presumably his intent was upon all of them. The one who calls out and the baal tokea) it is fitting for them to study well the laws of the shofar and the order of the blasts, for sometimes for some reason a mistake falls in the mouth of the one who calls out or in the inadvertence of the baal tokea, and one must know how to act, whether to blow onward or to return to the beginning of the sequence [Mateh Ephraim].
