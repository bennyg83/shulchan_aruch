In Taz, s.k. 3. After the fact it is permitted; it appears that he means that after the fact it is permitted to blow with the blessing that he recited intending that another would blow; and even though he himself does not blow, he fulfills his obligation of the blasting of that blessing through what the second one blows; and this “fulfills” does not mean the obligation of the mitzvah of shofar, but rather the obligation of the blasting that he is compelled to [perform] because of the blessing. But what the Taz brought from siman 284 requires further study; for there it is dealing with one reading in the Torah and one reading the maftir with the blessings (see there). Or in my humble opinion it is simple that the Tur, who wrote “since he fulfills [his obligation] with the second one’s blasting,” is not referring to the one who blesses, but to the hearer, that is to say, since the hearer fulfills [his obligation] with the second one’s blasts, this one’s blessing properly helps and was not in vain. And it seems more that one should read in the Tur “since they fulfill [their obligation] with the second one’s blasting,” meaning that the congregation fulfills [its obligation] with the second one’s blasting; and likewise in the Tur before us in print the word “they fulfill” appears with a mark on the tzadi, and it is possible that it indicates the word “they fulfill”; and if so, the Tur’s words come as their plain meaning.
