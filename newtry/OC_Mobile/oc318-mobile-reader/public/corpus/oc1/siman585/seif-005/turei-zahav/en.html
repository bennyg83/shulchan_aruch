In the Tur he brought the view of R' Yechiel his brother, who wrote concerning the Sephardim who customarily hire one person to blow; whence do they have this permission? For it appears to me that it is forbidden, as we learned in a beraisa: one who hires a worker to watch a cow and a child; it is forbidden to take Shabbos wages except by way of embedding [them in weekday pay], etc. And the Beis Yosef brought the words of the Mordechai in chapter “Although”: from here Rav ruled that there is concern regarding the chazzanim whom they hire to pray on Shabbos; and R' Shmuel says there is no prohibition, for they give wages to fulfill the mitzvah of praying (end quote. And there is a difficulty against R' Shmuel from that [case] of one who hires to watch a cow and a child that the Tur brings) and that is certainly a mitzvah: watching the cow so that it be a para adumah, and likewise the child so that he not become tamei, for the procedure of the para was done through children; and in chapter “The Gold” this mishnah is brought. And one must say that R' Shmuel holds that a mitzvah that depends on Shabbos itself and has no relevance to weekdays (such as the prayers of Shabbos) is different; and so it seems from chapter “A place where they were accustomed,” that the wages of the translator whom they were accustomed to have translate on Shabbos have no sign of blessing; implying that there is no prohibition, as the Beis Yosef wrote on this; and that too is because the translator translated specifically on Shabbos. And this requires further study regarding the Beis Yosef, who wrote in Yoreh De’ah siman 74 regarding one who sells a Sefer Torah, that he does not see in it a sign of blessing, and the Beis Yosef wrote, quote: it implies that since he does not see in it a sign of blessing, it is forbidden for him to sell it, etc. and here he wrote the opposite. The Tur brought the Gemara: R' Yehudah said, Any year in which they do not blow at its beginning, they sound an alarm at its end; what is the reason? Because the Satan is not mixed up. And Halachos Gedolos explains: not where it fell on Shabbos, but where an unavoidable circumstance occurred and they did not blow (end quote). And there is a difficulty: for the Merciful One exempts one under duress everywhere. And furthermore there is a difficulty: it is stated in the Maharil that “they sound an alarm at its end” means where [they omitted] willfully; but if it was canceled due to duress, no harm will befall them, and that is unlike the words of Halachos Gedolos. And it appears to me that this “they sound an alarm at its end” is according to what we find regarding a bad dream, where we say there that his gloominess assists him, meaning that through his worrying and becoming gloomy he is afterwards saved from the evil that had been decreed upon him. So too regarding these blasts of Rosh Hashanah: if they were compelled and did not blow, that is a cause from Him, may He be blessed, that such happened to them, and thereby the Holy One, Blessed be He, shows them that it will go ill for them at the end of the year, and the practical implication is that they should repent and grieve over this, and thereby what they had become liable for will be atoned. Therefore Halachos Gedolos said that if it fell on Shabbos it is not apt to say that the Holy One, Blessed be He, is showing them [this]; rather specifically when such a circumstance occurred, they should reflect and grieve, and their gloominess will assist them so that afterwards no trouble at all befall them (and thus the words of the Maharil are also correct. And this “they sound an alarm at its end”) the Beis Yosef explained that enemies will come upon them and sound the alarm with a shofar of schemes; and the Maharshal explained that evil will come upon them. And once there came to me in a dream an interpretation of the verse “With trumpets and the sound of the shofar, sound an alarm before the King, Hashem” explaining it according to this Gemara: that they should be careful to fulfill the teruah by means of the shofar before there be a teruah, God forbid, at the end of the year from Him, may He be blessed; and with this [the verse] said “before the King, Hashem” that is to say, before the teruah of the King, Hashem, blessed be He, God forbid. And the Tur further brought, quote: a hint to the matter (“ein satan ve’ein pega ra” [no Satan and no evil affliction], the initials [spell] shofar) meaning when there is a shofar there is no evil affliction. And there is a difficulty: where is “no Satan” hinted? On the contrary, the opposite is implied, God forbid. And it appears to me that it is in the manner we find in the Midrash, that Samael himself advocates for Israel and says, “Have You a people like angels,” etc. Therefore it says here “Satan and no evil affliction” that is to say, even though the Satan is there, nevertheless there is no evil affliction, for he himself is turned to good; automatically it is as if he is not there at all, and “no Satan” is well [accounted for].
