(s.k. 3) With a gentile: meaning that the Jew, etc. And according to this, the animal belongs to the gentile, and the gentile is the seller.
As is written in Hagahos Ashiri: that is to say, that so the meaning is implied there, and not like the explanation of the Levush that he brings afterward.
And the Levush explained the opposite: that the animal belongs to the Jew, and the Jew is the seller.
See at the end of siman 497. That is to say: even though in siman 497 it was permitted to sell to a gentile treifah meat or hindquarters in the manner that one sells to a Jew without mentioning money and weight, that is specifically treifah, which one cannot sell to a Jew; they permitted their end because of their beginning; which is not so regarding kosher meat, which can be sold to a Jew.
And his inference of, etc. "To his fellow" implies to exclude a gentile, etc. Similarly Rashi inferred in tractate Sukkah, folio 39, regarding what was taught: one who buys a lulav from his fellow, etc. And Rashi wrote, and these are his words: it appears to me that we should read "one who buys a lulav from an am ha'aretz," for it deals with an am ha'aretz, etc.; and an am ha'aretz relative to a chaver is not called his fellow; end of his words. And if so, all the more so a gentile relative to a Jew is not called his fellow. And Tosafos there rejected the words of Rashi; and one of their proofs is from what was taught on Shabbos folio 150: a person should not say to his fellow "hire workers for me," and they establish it as his fellow the gentile; end of the words of Tosafos. And see there in Sefer Kapos Temarim.
And similarly he inferred, etc. Hagahos Ashiri in the first chapter of Avodah Zarah, folio 7, brought the Gemara there from what was taught in tractate Shabbos: a person may say to his fellow on Shabbos "does it appear that you will stand with me toward evening." Even though his intent is that in the evening, namely Motza'ei Shabbos, he will hire him, nevertheless it is permitted; for specifically speaking in such a manner is forbidden, but thought is permitted. And Hagahos Ashiri inferred there from that it teaches "to his fellow": specifically to a Jew it is permitted to say thus, but not to a gentile. But Tosafos rejected the explanation and hold that since it is only thought, which is permitted on Shabbos, what difference is there to me between a gentile and a Jew? Therefore one must say that Tosafos did not infer from the wording "to his fellow," as Tosafos wrote in Sukkah as above.
If so, it is possible, etc. If so, according to Tosafos one should not distinguish also here, and even to a gentile it is permitted.
