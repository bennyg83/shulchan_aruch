He divides for them. Specifically without a lottery; for example, where they forgo to one another and are not particular if his fellow takes a nicer portion; but if they are particular and need a lottery, it is forbidden (Shelah). And see what I wrote at the end of siman 322 in the name of Tosafos, that members of a group who are particular are forbidden to divide even without a lottery, for we decree lest they come to measure, weight, and counting; but neighbors who are not associates, even if particular, are permitted to divide without a lottery; see there.

With a non-Jew. Meaning that a Jew is forbidden to say to a non-Jew "behold I am with you for half, a third, or a quarter" (so it is implied in Hagahos Ashiri; see there). And it appears to me that the reason is because one is not accustomed to partner with a non-Jew, so it appears more like buying and selling. And the Levush explained that it is forbidden to sell to a non-Jew in such a manner kosher meat; see at the end of siman 497. And the inference of Hagahos Ashiri is from that it teaches "a person says to his fellow," implying to exclude a non-Jew; and similarly he inferred in the first chapter of Avodah Zarah that it is forbidden to say to a non-Jew "does it appear that you will stand with me toward evening." But Tosafos on Shabbos folio 150 rejected this explanation, as I wrote in siman 307, se'if 7; if so, it is possible that here too "his fellow" is not precise, and this requires further study; see siman 516.
