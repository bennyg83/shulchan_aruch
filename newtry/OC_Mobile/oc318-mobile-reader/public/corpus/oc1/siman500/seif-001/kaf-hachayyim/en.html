1) [Se'if 1] Meat may not be purchased at a fixed price, etc. This is the manner of commerce, and commerce is forbidden on Shabbos and Yom Tov. Rashi, Beitzah 27b; Levush.
2) There. Meat may not be purchased at a fixed price, etc. Even regarding an animal whose value was already fixed on erev Yom Tov, it is nevertheless forbidden to mention a fixed price. Olas Shabbos, subsection 1.
3) A group that wishes to take an entire animal from a butcher may not fix a price with him for it. Instead, the butcher brings two animals of equal value and says to them, 'See that this one is like that one.' They take one of them, slaughter it, and divide it among themselves without casting lots, as written in siman 322. See there. After Yom Tov, they determine the value of both animals, and each person pays the value of his portion. The buyers may not say to the butcher, 'Whatever you will take tomorrow for that animal, I will give you for this one that we slaughter today,' because this is the fixing of a purchase price, which may not be mentioned on Yom Tov. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1.
4) A person may not say to the buyers, 'I am a partner with you for a sela,' meaning that he will take together with them a portion of meat worth a sela. It is forbidden to mention a sum of money on Yom Tov. Instead, he should say, 'I am a partner with you for one-third of the meat,' or, 'for one-fourth.' It then is not recognizable or apparent as commerce, but as a partnership. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 2.
5) Rashba writes in Avodas HaKodesh, Beis Moed, gate 2, subsection 4: 'It appears that initially even a count of people should not be assigned to an animal by saying, 'Let five people join in this animal,' because this resembles a weekday activity. If they joined in it on erev Yom Tov, however, they may add to their number the next day and assign another five people to it.' Pri Chadash cites this and writes that Rashba appears to have derived it from the Yerushalmi. The Babylonian Talmud, however, implies that there is no concern in having one group join in an animal without fixing a price. Pri Chadash nevertheless writes that although this appears to be the primary ruling, one who is stringent will be blessed. See there. See below, subsection 10.
6) There. Likewise, he may not say to him, 'I am a partner, etc.' This means that although he does not purchase from the other person but joins him as a partner in the animal itself, it is nevertheless forbidden because he mentions a monetary amount. Mishnah Berurah, subsection 2.
