(s.k. 1) He divides, etc. This law mentioned in the Shulchan Aruch derives from what was taught on folio 27b: they do not number upon an animal initially on Yom Tov, etc. And the Gemara explained the Mishnah as is written here in the Shulchan Aruch: they do not buy with fixing a price, etc. And Peri Chadash brought in the name of the Rashba, who wrote that it is forbidden l'chatchila for four or five people to number upon an animal on Yom Tov that each take a fourth or fifth share, for that is like a weekday act; rather they must number on erev Yom Tov. But if they numbered upon it on erev Yom Tov, it is permitted to add upon them more other people on Yom Tov who did not number upon it on erev Yom Tov. And Peri Chadash wrote upon this that so it is in the Yerushalmi, which explained the Mishnah "they do not number upon the animal," etc., thus. And from that our Talmud did not explain the Mishnah thus, it is implied that our Talmud disagrees with the Yerushalmi and holds that it is permitted for people to number upon an animal on Yom Tov, and we rule like our Talmud. And even so he concluded, and one who is stringent, may blessing come upon him; see there.
Specifically without a lottery, etc. But etc., and they need a lottery, it is forbidden. It appears that his intent is: since they are particular and need a lottery, even without a lottery it is forbidden, lest they come to measure, etc. For if not so, but specifically by lottery it is forbidden, why should he distinguish between those who are particular and those who are not? He should distinguish between those who cast lots and those who do not. And it is forced to say that he took the usual way; nevertheless he should rather have shortened and said that it is forbidden to cast lots. Therefore one must say that those who are particular, even without a lottery it is forbidden; except that he does not distinguish between members of a group or neighbors as Tosafos distinguished; but neighbors, even if particular, are permitted, etc. And the reason Magen Avraham wrote in siman 322, and these are his words: since they are not regularly familiar with one another, we do not decree lest they come to a prohibition; end of his words. And know that in Sefer Eliyah Rabbah he wrote that even a lottery by means of a knife, namely that they place a knife between the two portions and ask someone who did not see how the knife was placed to say to whom the portion lying toward the sharp edge of the knife belongs, and who takes the other portion, and as the speaker says so it stands: this too is called a lottery and is forbidden.
