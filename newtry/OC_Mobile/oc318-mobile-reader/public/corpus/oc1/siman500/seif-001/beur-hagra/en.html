And some say specifically. From that it says "to his fellow," but on Shabbos 150a we say a non-Jewish fellow; and see Tosafos there s.v. "he says," etc.; and see Tosafos at the end of chapter 3 of Yom Tov, 29b, s.v. "by," etc.; and so wrote there the Rosh, Ran, Maggid Mishneh, and Mordechai. And although in Hagahos Mordechai he wrote in the name of Raavan to forbid, nevertheless he wrote the reason lest he increase for him, which is not so here. And I looked in Raavan there, who wrote: but a non-Jew is forbidden; and so wrote Halachos Gedolos; and that is what the Rosh brings there; and it is forbidden because of attached [growth] and the like. And the Mordechai proved from his words to the contrary, that there is no distinction, and he brought proof from the end of chapter 3 of Eruvin regarding a turnip that came, etc., and from chapter 4 there regarding rams that came to Mavrakta. And although in Hagahos Mordechai there he rejected his proof, his words are forced; and see Beis Yosef, siman 517; and see Shulchan Aruch at the end of siman 497, se'if 17, "but it is permitted," etc., and siman 517, se'if 1; and in the Mordechai at the end of chapter 3 of Yom Tov at length.
