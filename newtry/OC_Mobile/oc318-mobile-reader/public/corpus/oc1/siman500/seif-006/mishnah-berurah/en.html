{27} Even if slaughtered on erev Yom Tov. And as above regarding salting. And all this is when he does nikkur of meat that he intends to eat on Yom Tov; but other meat it is forbidden to nikkur, and even if slaughtered on Yom Tov, for it is exertion for the need of a weekday; unless he wants to salt it because he is concerned lest the meat stink, and to salt it is forbidden before nikkur, as is written in Yoreh De'ah, siman 65: in this it is permitted for him to nikkur also other meat by circumvention, namely after he did nikkur of one piece for the need of the day he says "this piece is not so good in my eyes, only another piece," and he does nikkur of it, and so all of them. And specifically when slaughtered on Yom Tov; but if slaughtered on erev Yom Tov and he had opportunity to nikkur it while it was still day, it is forbidden for him to nikkur on Yom Tov only the meat that he needs for eating on Yom Tov, for the nikkur does not dull the taste of the meat at all, and he should have done nikkur before Yom Tov.
{28} And it is good to change a little. Namely, that he do nikkur with a cleaver or an axe. And even though above regarding salting, even if he could have salted it on erev Yom Tov he need not a change: there, because meat that lingers much in salting dulls the taste; which is not so here, for by nikkur it does not dull the taste at all, and he should have done nikkur on erev Yom Tov.
{29} If he can change. And if it is impossible for him to change, it is permitted even without a change. And even though he could have done it on erev Yom Tov, because regarding nikkur there is no aspect of melachah at all, only mere exertion. And the hindquarters, which one cannot nikkur well by means of a change, one should be stringent not to nikkur them if slaughtered on erev Yom Tov and there was time to nikkur on erev Yom Tov; and in a place of pressure one should be lenient for the need of Yom Tov. Meat that has undergone nikkur that is on the third day from its slaughter, and if it waits after Yom Tov it will be forbidden to eat it except roasted, as is explained in Yoreh De'ah: it is permitted to rinse it on Yom Tov since it is fit for eating. And even though this exertion is for the need of a weekday, he does not do an act, but only pours water upon it. [Nevertheless it is proper l'chatchila that the butchers eat a piece of it; and if possible to leave the rinsing until the second day of Yom Tov, they should not rinse on the first day of Yom Tov.] But on Shabbos, when it is impossible to cook and eat, it is forbidden [Magen Avraham]. And see above in siman 321 in Mishnah Berurah, s.k. 21, what we wrote in the name of the later authorities: that through a non-Jew there is certainly room to be lenient to rinse; and if it is impossible through a non-Jew, it is permitted also through a Jew. Magen Avraham further wrote that if this occurred with meat that has not undergone nikkur, even on Yom Tov it is forbidden to rinse it, because since it is forbidden to nikkur what is not for the need of Yom Tov by a circumvention of saying "I will eat a kezayis from it," since he could have done nikkur while it was still day, as above, consequently it is not fit for eating, and therefore its law is like on Shabbos, that it is forbidden to rinse it. And now, according to what the later authorities were lenient also on Shabbos in a place of pressure for the reason that since it is fit for moving, consequently it is also permitted to rinse it, also regarding meat that has not undergone nikkur one should not be stringent for this reason, as I explained in Biur Halacha. Nevertheless, if possible through a non-Jew it is better through a non-Jew; or when the meat lies in a vessel it is good that he wash his hands upon it until the meat is soaked in water.
