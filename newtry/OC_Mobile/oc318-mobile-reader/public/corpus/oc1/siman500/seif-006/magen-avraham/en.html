They are accustomed to nikkur. And there is in this no [prohibition of] selecting food from food, because it is impossible to eat otherwise; for Rav permitted separating challah on Yom Tov from dough that was kneaded on erev Yom Tov; end of the words of Hagahos Maimoniyos. And one may wonder: on the contrary, whenever it is impossible to eat otherwise, selecting applies, as is written in siman 319, se'if 10. And furthermore, most of the Geonim do not hold that of Rav. And in Hagahos Ashiri it is proven that he permitted nikkur only because of loss, and that is where one slaughters on Yom Tov; for when he slaughtered on erev Yom Tov, the nikkur does not dull its taste; on the contrary, it improves it. Therefore it appears that one should nikkur by means of a change, such as with a cleaver or an axe (and as I wrote in siman 504) regarding spices. And even though pounding is forbidden even by means of a change (and as I wrote in siman 506, se'if 2), that case is different, for they are accustomed to do much at once, and we are concerned that he will intend to do it on Yom Tov and be distracted from the joy of Yom Tov, as the Rambam wrote; end of the words of Terumat HaDeshen. And his words are puzzling, for he explained a strange explanation in the words of Hagahos Maimoniyos, and his words are as their plain sense: what he wrote "because it is impossible otherwise" did not come to give a reason why there is no selecting here, etc.; that is to say, this is not called selecting, for selecting applies only to something mixed, but here this stands alone and that stands alone. And so wrote the Mordechai simply: and selecting does not apply here; end of his words. And even according to what is written in siman 510, that is regarding something that is complete refuse, or regarding two kinds of foods; but here it is one kind, and it is not refuse, but rather a prohibition rests upon it. And what Hagahos Maimoniyos wrote "because it is impossible," etc., means thus: at any rate, since he could have done the nikkur on erev Yom Tov, one should forbid doing it [on Yom Tov]; and upon this he says that since it is impossible to eat it otherwise, it is permitted to do it; and he brings proof from Rav, who said: if one slaughtered a bird on erev Yom Tov, it is forbidden to cover [its blood] on Yom Tov, since it is permitted to eat it on Yom Tov without covering; but if one rolled dough on erev Yom Tov, it is permitted to separate challah on Yom Tov, since it is forbidden to eat it without challah; so too here, since it is forbidden to eat it without nikkur, it is permitted; so it appears to me as the clear meaning of Hagahos Maimoniyos. And so he wrote explicitly in Sefer Raavan; see there. (And he concludes:) even though where there would be selecting it is permitted, as is written in siman 510, se'if 2, regarding legumes; end of his words. And even according to those who forbid in siman 495 food for the soul that it would have been possible to prepare on erev Yom Tov, that is regarding a complete melachah; but here it is not selecting, as above. And what Terumat HaDeshen wrote, that most of the Geonim ruled not like Rav, that is because it is a complete repair, for beforehand the whole dough was tevel; know that even those who permit for food for the soul forbid separating challah; but here it is not considered a repair, for the meat is food wherever it is, only that he cuts away what is forbidden to eat. And what Terumat HaDeshen wrote, that in Hagahos Ashiri he permitted only because of loss: the wording of Hagahos Ashiri before us is, "and because of loss it is permitted to nikkur the meat, and even if he needs only part of it, for it is similar to that of one who circumvents and salts bone by bone"; end of his words. It is implied that to nikkur what he wants to eat is permitted without loss, even if slaughtered on erev Yom Tov. And what Hagahos Ashiri wrote "and because of loss," it appears to me that he deals with a case where if it waits after Yom Tov the meat will stink, and he needs to salt it by circumvention, and it is impossible to salt it unless he does nikkur; therefore it is permitted. Nevertheless, specifically when slaughtered on Yom Tov; but when slaughtered on erev Yom Tov it is forbidden, as Terumat HaDeshen wrote.
And it is good to change. It is implied that if it is impossible to change, it is permitted without a change. And in Yam shel Shlomo he ruled that the hindquarters are forbidden to nikkur, for it is impossible to nikkur them with a change; and if slaughtered on Yom Tov, everything is permitted; end of his words. And the same law applies if slaughtered close to Yom Tov so that it was not possible to nikkur beforehand. And according to what is above, one should be lenient in every case regarding nikkur. And if the meat is on the third day from its slaughter, and if it waits after Yom Tov it will be forbidden to eat it except roasted, it is forbidden to nikkur and rinse it by means of a circumvention of saying "I will eat a kezayis from it," as Terumat HaDeshen wrote, for when slaughtered on erev Yom Tov it is forbidden, for he should have done the nikkur on erev Yom Tov; and so it is implied in the Ran regarding salting, who wrote that he caused the loss to himself, for he should have done the nikkur on erev Yom Tov. But if he did the nikkur on erev Yom Tov, it is permitted to rinse it on Yom Tov, since it is fit for eating, as is written in siman 321, se'if 11. And it is not comparable to rinsing vessels, which is forbidden as is written in siman 323, for there he does an act with his hands of rinsing them, which is not so here, where he pours water upon it, and it is sufficient that it be permitted like vegetables. But on Shabbos it appears to me that it is forbidden, as is written there.
