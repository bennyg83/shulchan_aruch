They are accustomed to nikkur. In Terumat HaDeshen, siman 80, he wrote, and these are his words: in a gloss in the name of Ravyah he wrote that it is permitted to nikkur meat on Yom Tov even if slaughtered on erev Yom Tov, and there is in this no [prohibition of] selecting food from food, because it is impossible to eat otherwise. It appears that he means: since it is impossible to eat otherwise, therefore it is not considered selecting. And one may wonder at that reasoning from that of chapter Tolim: between the winepresses it is permitted to put cloudy wine with its lees into the strainer, because even without this he can drink it; thus on the contrary, because it is possible without this selecting, therefore there is no selecting in it; end quote. And I see a great man, but I do not see a refutation; for this is not comparable to that: certainly whenever there is no necessity to separate one from the other, but there is a possibility to drink even though they are mixed, the separation does not forbid in this, for there is no selecting here; but if one must separate and it is impossible without it, then one must distinguish: if there is a possibility to take the food from there and not deal with the refuse at all, but he takes the food and eats and the rest remains there, then it is forbidden to select the food one by one and make a gathering of it; but if it is impossible to take the food and separate it from what remains, like that of nikkur, it is permitted, since it is impossible to separate otherwise, and the separation is the necessity. Further he wrote there that in Hagahos Ashiri regarding that of salting bone by bone it is proven that Riva held that regarding nikkur selecting applies, from that he permitted only because of loss; otherwise it would be forbidden; and therefore [it must be] for the reason of selecting. And that which is permitted because of loss is where he slaughters on Yom Tov, for it is impossible beforehand; but one who slaughters on erev Yom Tov: the nikkur does not dull its taste, and on the contrary it stinks more, as is proven from the words of Riva; therefore it appears that one should nikkur by means of a change, etc.; end of his words. And therefore the Rama wrote here at the end of the siman that it is good to change if he can change something. And according to my humble opinion there is no proof from Hagahos Ashiri that there is here a prohibition of selecting; for there the prohibition was only because of unnecessary exertion, for so he wrote there: because of loss, so that the meat not stink, they permitted by circumvention. And Riva says that upon this we rely to nikkur the meat, and even if he needs only part of it, for it is similar to that of one who circumvents and salts bone by bone: it is also permitted to salt large pieces even though he knows certainly that he will eat only part of them; end of his words. Thus, regarding what he needs on Yom Tov it is obvious that it is permitted; rather he needed to give a reason for the remainder, which is unnecessary exertion; and even so he permitted to nikkur all of it, lest he choose for himself to eat a different piece. And this is simple according to my humble opinion, that there is no prohibition of selecting here; and also from the wording of the Rama it is implied thus according to the law, for he wrote "it is good to change a little," etc., and not an obligation; all the more so according to Beis Yosef, who wrote that the world is accustomed to nikkur, etc., as is written here. And I saw in Maharshal in the first chapter of Beitzah that he wrote in the name of Maharil, who brought in the name of the Mordechai: where it is possible to salt the meat on erev Yom Tov, it is forbidden to salt it on Yom Tov, and so is the custom. And there is a reason for the matter: for there is meat that tastes better when it is in salt a day or two, such as beef; and furthermore, since it is only preparing something from the prohibition of blood, it is not called actual food for the soul. And afterward he wrote in the name of Maharas: they asked him if it is permitted to nikkur the hindquarters of an animal, for the forepart was not enough for him, and he said that regarding the forepart it is permitted to nikkur by means of a change; however, not the hindquarters, which need extensive nikkur: it does not appear to permit even by means of a change, for he cannot nikkur well properly; end quote. I further found: even to nikkur a chicken that was slaughtered on erev Yom Tov, one should do so by means of a change; but when it was slaughtered today, even hindquarters it is permitted to nikkur without a change in order to eat them today; end of the words of Maharshal. And there is from the words of Maharil no proof for stringency, for he follows the words of the Mordechai in the first chapter of Beitzah in the name of R. Eliezer to forbid salting on Yom Tov what was slaughtered on erev Yom Tov; but in Terumat HaDeshen, siman 80, he wrote that one of the great ones wrote that he received from his teachers that we do not practice like that of the Mordechai, etc.; see there. And so the Rama ruled. And also regarding nikkur, Beis Yosef and the Shulchan Aruch already ruled like most of the poskim that he brings, that it is permitted to nikkur simply, implying even regarding the hindquarters; and so the Rama did not distinguish. Therefore we should not be stringent also regarding the hindquarters by way of a change that is possible; for there is no distinction between them: even though there is more exertion, what of it, for food for the soul is permitted even with several exertions. And I do not know a distinction in this between slaughtered on erev Yom Tov or on Yom Tov regarding hindquarters more than the forepart. Therefore it appears to my humble opinion that where there is scarcity of meat and there was no opportunity to nikkur before Yom Tov, it is proper to nikkur on Yom Tov also the hindquarters; and Maharshal also agrees to this, as it appears to my humble opinion.
