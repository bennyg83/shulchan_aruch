To change. And Maharil further wrote that the hindquarters, which need extensive nikkur, it does not appear to permit even by means of a change; and see Yam shel Shlomo in the first chapter of Beitzah. And the Taz wrote that we should not be stringent also regarding the hindquarters by way of a change, and there is no distinction in this between one who slaughtered on erev Yom Tov or on Yom Tov regarding the hindquarters more than the forepart; therefore it appears that where there is scarcity of meat and there was no opportunity to nikkur before Yom Tov, it is proper to nikkur on Yom Tov also the hindquarters; end of his words. So too wrote Magen Avraham; see there. If the meat is on the third day from its slaughter, and if it waits after Yom Tov it will be forbidden to eat it except roasted, it is forbidden to nikkur and rinse it by means of a circumvention of saying "I will eat a kezayis from it," for when it was slaughtered on erev Yom Tov it is forbidden, for he should have done the nikkur on erev Yom Tov. But if he did the nikkur on erev Yom Tov, it is permitted to rinse it on Yom Tov, since it is fit for eating; but on Shabbos it appears to me that it is forbidden (Magen Avraham; see there). And in Sefer Edus B'Yaakov, siman 1, he wrote that even on Shabbos it is permitted to rinse; see there. And see Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah; and see Magen Avraham, siman 321, s.k. 7; and see Baer Heitev, Yoreh De'ah, siman 69, s.k. 45, what is written there.
