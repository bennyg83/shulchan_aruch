Se'if 6: They are accustomed, etc. Hagahos Ashiri there s.v. "and because of loss," etc.; see there. And so wrote Maggid Mishneh, chapter 1, in the name of the Rashba. But according to what is written that regarding salting it is permitted because it dulls the taste, it would be forbidden here, and there is no proof from salting; rather one may say that his proof is from how they were lenient to salt a great deal when only one [piece] is needed; and see the Rosh there; and see what I wrote in siman 567. And in Hagahos Maimoniyos he wrote that because it is impossible to eat otherwise, it is permitted even if slaughtered on erev Yom Tov, and proof from what is written there: if one rolled dough on erev Yom Tov, he separates challah from it on Yom Tov. And he wrote: however, we wrote that the halachah is not like Rabbah but like the father of Shmuel; and so he ruled in siman 506, se'if 3. And what he wrote "because it is impossible," etc., so wrote Raavan there. And Eliyah Rabbah [on] "he rolled," etc.: it appears to me that because of covering [the blood] he does not refrain, etc., for it is permitted to eat without covering, which is not so regarding challah; and for this reason it appears to me that it is permitted to nikkur meat on Yom Tov even though it is like selecting, etc., and even if slaughtered on erev Yom Tov, since he cannot eat without nikkur. And he wrote: and that is what is written there, 37a: we do not lift, etc.; what is the reason? R. Yehudah taught: it was needed only, etc. And the explanation: why is it forbidden, for it is impossible to eat it without this? And he answers: it was needed only, etc., that specifically to give to a kohen is forbidden, but he sets his eye on this side and eats on another side. And it is clear from his words that challah is not rectified by setting his eye, etc., and therefore it is permitted.
And it is good, etc. As what is written regarding salt that does not dull the taste, that it is permitted with a change; there, Yoreh De'ah 14a.
