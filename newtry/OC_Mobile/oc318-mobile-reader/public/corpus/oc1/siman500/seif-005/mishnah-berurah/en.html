{21} At once. With a scatter of one hand; for example, where there are three pieces here, he places them one beside another and takes salt the fullness of his palms, the measure of salting for all the pieces, and scatters upon them at once.
{22} Even though he does not need. For Yom Tov, except one; and the reason is that one exertion is an exertion for all the meat, for he salts them together. And even though he adds a little exertion, for he must turn over each piece by itself in order to salt them on the other side, nevertheless since according to the Torah there is no processing in foods, as we wrote above in siman 321, the Sages were lenient to salt at once if he is concerned lest the meat stink.
{23} And some say it is not permitted to circumvent, etc. The wording is confused, for circumvention is not mentioned at all in the words of the Mechaber. And many commentators wrote that something is missing here in the words of the Mechaber, and it should read: and it is permitted to circumvent and salt a piece and afterward take another and say "this one I want"; that is to say, that even when he does not salt at once there is a remedy, namely by circumvention, that he say "this other one I want now"; and they permitted him this thing so that he not refrain from slaughtering if he knows that they will not permit him to salt all the meat, and it come to stink. [And some wrote that even if slaughtered on erev Yom Tov it is also permitted to salt all of them on Yom Tov by circumvention.] And upon this the Rama glossed: and some say it is not permitted to circumvent, etc.
{24} Only before eating, etc. For in truth he needs to eat then some piece; but if he already ate and does not intend to eat any more today, it is forbidden to circumvent and salt, even though he will cook and taste a little, since he tastes only in order to permit himself salting of the remaining pieces, and as is explained below in siman 503, se'if 1; see there. And even if he wants to salt all the pieces together, it is also forbidden in such a case, for it is not applicable here to say "one exertion for all of them," for in truth he does not need even one. And the later authorities agreed that all this is where it is not known to him certainly that it will stink if he does not salt it; but where it is known to him certainly, it is permitted to salt it even after eating, and even one after another by circumventing for each piece to say "from this I will eat today," and he must eat a kezayis from one of them. And even this they permitted only when he slaughtered on Yom Tov, so that he not refrain in the future from slaughtering; but when he slaughtered on erev Yom Tov, since he could have salted it on erev Yom Tov, it is forbidden to salt meat that he does not need on Yom Tov unless he salts before eating.
{25} And it is permitted to salt, etc. That is to say: when he salts it to prepare it to remove its blood [and for the reason that if he had salted it on erev Yom Tov he would need after a second rinsing to salt it yet a second time and leave it in salt until tomorrow (for otherwise it would spoil), and it is a known matter that when meat lingers much in salt it dulls the taste; therefore it is permitted to salt the meat that he wants to eat that same day, and incidentally it is permitted to salt the remaining meat since it is at once or by circumvention, as above]. But if it was already salted and rinsed because of blood, it is forbidden to salt it a second time on Yom Tov so that it not be lost, and even in the morning before eating; for it is not the way to salt meat at the time of cooking it, and on the contrary they rinse it of its salt at the time of cooking; and if so the matter is evident that he salts only to repair the meat, and therefore it is forbidden. However, on the night of the second day of Yom Tov it is permitted to return and salt it by circumventing for each piece to say "from this I will eat tomorrow," and so he should do that he eat a little of them tomorrow.
{26} Even though it would have been, etc. Nevertheless, fish that are improved when lying in salt it is forbidden to salt on Yom Tov when he could have salted them on erev Yom Tov. [And this does not include putting salt into the pan in which they cook them, for there is no prohibition in salting except because of a decree of processing or because of exertion not for the need of Yom Tov; which is not so in this, where he puts it into the cooked dish.] And kinds of soft fish that spoil when they rest a long time in salt, it is permitted to salt them on Yom Tov even if he cut them open on erev Yom Tov, and their law is like salting meat for every matter.
