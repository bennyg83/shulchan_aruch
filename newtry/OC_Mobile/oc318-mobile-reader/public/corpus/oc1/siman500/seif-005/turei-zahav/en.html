Several pieces at once. This is a statement of Shmuel, and Rashi explained that it is one exertion for all of them. And in the Gemara there is further that of Rav Adda: he circumvents and salts bone by bone; Rashi explained: after he salted this one to eat today, he says "its fellow is pleasant to me to eat today," and he returns and salts it. And the Tur did not mention that of Rav Adda, and likewise the Rambam in chapter 1 of Hilchos Yom Tov. And Maggid Mishneh wrote that the Rambam explains that they disagree: Shmuel permitted only all at once, and Rav Adda permitted even one after another by circumvention. And so Beis Yosef wrote according to the view of the Tur, who ruled like Shmuel. And I am astonished for a while: for the Tur already wrote in siman 499 that it is permitted to salt meat upon the hide, a little here and a little there, until he salts all of it. And it is obvious that there it does not deal with one piece that he salts in many places upon the hide; for certainly with one piece he will not salt so much salt that it suffice for the whole hide. And one cannot say that he should increase greatly to salt it; that is certainly forbidden on Yom Tov, since according to the law he need not salt it much more than is proper, as is written in Yoreh De'ah, siman 69; and whenever this one salts much more than is proper, the matter is evident that he does it for the hide, and it is forbidden. And likewise the Rambam in chapter 3 wrote this law, and these are his words: he salts a little meat from here and a little from there until he salts the whole hide. Thus it does not refer to one piece of meat; for if so he should have said "he salts meat a little from here," which would then be possible to explain of one piece, and the wording "a little" would mean part of it; but when he says "he salts a little meat," it implies some meat here and some meat there. And furthermore, even if you explain it of one piece, nevertheless the law regarding many pieces is likewise thus; for the reason of the prohibition according to Maggid Mishneh is that he does not salt all at once; if so, what difference to me one piece or many? Rather it is simple according to my humble opinion that also with many it is permitted; if so it is explicit that even with two circumventions it is permitted: namely the circumvention of salting the meat and the circumvention of salting the hide. If so, the Rambam and the Tur did not need to mention that of Rav Adda regarding salting meat, for they already mentioned it with a greater novelty. And if so, this condition that we mention here, that it is permitted only before the morning meal, applies all the more so to that of the hide that we mentioned; and so Beis Yosef wrote. Except that my teacher and father-in-law, of blessed memory, disagrees and says that regarding a hide it is different, for they permitted their end because of their beginning; and that is puzzling, for Rashi explained in chapter 2, folio 17, and likewise a responsum of the Rashba that Beis Yosef brings in siman 503, that in that of Rav Adda the reason is also because they permitted their end because of their beginning; and if so there is no distinction at all between them. And it is clear according to my humble opinion that this law of salting meat here is exactly the law of salting meat upon the hide; and they mentioned it there for a novelty, and here there is no need for it. And one cannot say that that upon the hide deals only with light salting, namely for roasting, but like for a pot it is forbidden even with meat: that is not so; for that they need there only a little salting is because of the hide; for if you say because of the meat itself, Abaye in the Gemara should have taught us this law, that specifically roasting for the meat itself without a hide; rather it is simple that they mentioned "for roasting" only for the sake of the hide, but for the meat itself it is permitted in every way; and that is like Rav Adda. And upon this the Tur and Rambam relied in not writing that of Rav Adda, since from that upon the hide it is proven that for the meat itself it is permitted in every manner, and we need "for roasting" only because of the hide.
Even though it would have been possible, etc. The reason there is that meat is better when it does not linger in salt. And he wrote, see there in Terumat HaDeshen, siman 80: regarding fish there is a distinction; there are species that spoil if they linger in salt, and then it is permitted to salt them on Yom Tov even if they were cut open on erev Yom Tov; and species that are better when they linger in their salt, one should not salt them on Yom Tov if they were cut open on erev Yom Tov. Nevertheless it appears to me that one may cook them and put salt into the pan in which he cooks them, since the main reason for the prohibition is an aspect of processing, and that does not apply to a cooked dish where he puts the salt into the water.
