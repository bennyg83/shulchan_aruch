Even though it would have been possible, etc. From this wording it implies that all the more so when it was slaughtered on Yom Tov, it is certainly permitted to salt, and even by circumvention for all the meat. And see Magen Avraham in the name of Terumat HaDeshen, who leans that specifically if it was slaughtered on the first day of Yom Tov, so that it would spoil if one waited until the third day, or if the second day of Yom Tov falls on erev Shabbos so that it would spoil if one waited until Sunday; but if the second day of Yom Tov falls on a weekday and it was slaughtered then, it is forbidden to salt by circumvention, for one can wait from salting until tomorrow, and that is better for the meat. And I saw that the Shulchan Aruch HaRav and the Chayei Adam omitted this ruling; afterward I found in Maamar Mordechai that he also hesitates regarding the ruling of Terumat HaDeshen, and he brings that from the words of the Rivash, siman 254, it is proven not according to his words. And it is also possible because this law depends on the nature of the season: if the time is very hot and it can spoil in one day, it is certainly permitted to salt all the meat by circumvention; therefore they omitted this ruling.
