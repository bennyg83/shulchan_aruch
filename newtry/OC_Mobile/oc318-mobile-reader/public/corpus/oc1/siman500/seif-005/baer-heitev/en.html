From erev Yom Tov. The reason is that meat is better when it does not linger in salt; but regarding fish there is a distinction: there are species that spoil if they linger in salt, and then it is permitted to salt them on Yom Tov even if they were cut open on erev Yom Tov; and species that are better when they linger in their salt, one should not salt them on Yom Tov if they were cut open on erev Yom Tov. Nevertheless it appears to me that one may cook them and put salt into the pan in which he cooks them, since the main reason for the prohibition is an aspect of processing, and that does not apply to a cooked dish where he puts the salt into the water. And Yam shel Shlomo wrote that the custom is to forbid salting meat that was slaughtered on erev Yom Tov; see there. And Magen Avraham concluded that where there is certain loss that it will stink, it is permitted to circumvent and salt even after eating, just as they permitted regarding an animal in danger in siman 491, se'if 7; and specifically when it was slaughtered on Yom Tov; see there.
