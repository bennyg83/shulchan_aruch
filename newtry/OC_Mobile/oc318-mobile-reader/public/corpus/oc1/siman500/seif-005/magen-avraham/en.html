It is permitted to salt, etc. This is a statement of R. Yehudah in the name of Shmuel in the Gemara; and we say that Rav Adda bar Ahavah circumvents and salts bone by bone. And Beis Yosef wrote that the Rambam ruled like Shmuel, that specifically salting all at once is permitted, for it is one exertion; but if he salted one piece, it is forbidden to circumvent and afterward salt another piece and say "this one I want"; end of his words. And if so, this requires further study regarding what the Rama wrote, "and some say it is not permitted to circumvent," etc.: circumvention, who mentioned it? He should have written the opposite, that before eating circumvention is permitted. And one must say that he holds that even though it is one exertion, if he comes to salt many pieces after eating and say "I will eat a kezayis from it," it is forbidden, as is written in siman 503. And the Bach wrote that if he slaughtered on Yom Tov, it is permitted to circumvent even after eating and to salt even bone by bone so that the meat not stink, as is written in siman 499, se'if 3; and so it is implied in Rashi and Tosafos, folio 17b.
Even though it would have been possible to salt it. For it is known that when meat is in salt it dulls its taste. And even though we say above that it is permitted to circumvent and salt on Yom Tov so that it not stink, from which it is proven that it is preferable when it lies in salt, one may say that there it deals with the first day of Yom Tov: if he does not salt it until the third day it will stink; but from erev Yom Tov to Yom Tov it is preferable when it lies without salt. And regarding fish there are species that improve in salt, and those one must salt on erev Yom Tov (Terumat HaDeshen). And it appears to me that he deals with salting to remove its blood; and the meaning is: if he had rinsed and salted it on erev Yom Tov to remove its blood, he would need after a second rinsing to salt it again, for afterward it spoils when it stands without salt, and it is better when the meat hangs thus in the air without rinsing and salting; therefore it is permitted to salt it on Yom Tov. And Yam shel Shlomo ruled like Maharil that it is forbidden to salt meat on Yom Tov; and see siman 495 in the gloss. And it appears to me that what is permitted to salt meat on Yom Tov in the morning by circumvention so that it not stink is where he salts to remove its blood and leaves it in salt until after Yom Tov (see Beis Yosef, Yoreh De'ah, siman 70, se'if 5, that some forbid l'chatchila; or after he salted and rinsed, he salts on the night of Yom Tov by circumvention, saying "from this I will eat tomorrow"; but in the morning, even before eating, after he salted and rinsed, it is forbidden to salt it a second time, for even if he needs to eat it, he cooks it immediately and does not need salting, as appears to me). And it appears to me that where there is certain loss that it will stink, it is permitted to circumvent and salt even after eating, just as they permitted regarding an animal in danger in siman 498, se'if 6; and specifically when slaughtered on Yom Tov, as is written in se'if 6; and so it is implied in the Ran.
