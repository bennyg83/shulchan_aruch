{8} It is forbidden to weigh. And even if he does not weigh with a regular weight, but places in the pan a vessel or a knife and his intent is tomorrow to weigh it, nevertheless it is forbidden, for it is a weekday act. The later authorities wrote that even if he is not so exacting with the weight, but only slightly reduces or adds, it is also forbidden, for nevertheless it appears as weighing.
{9} And even to put it, etc. That is to say: and in the other pan weights are placed.
{10} In which they usually weigh. For then it appears as weighing. And see Biur Halacha: the later authorities wrote that this applies only to putting it to guard from mice; but in a place where he weighs explicitly, even if it is not hanging in its place, it is forbidden.
{11} And even to weigh a portion, etc. A portion is a share; that is to say, when they have meat to divide and they place one share in one pan and the second in the opposite pan to equalize them.
{12} Who takes, etc., and estimates. Specifically in this, which is mere estimation; but if he takes a weight in one hand and in the other hand meat to estimate against the weight, it is forbidden.
{13} Even this is forbidden. Because he is accustomed to this, he knows how to aim the weight precisely, and it is like weighing on scales. And the later authorities wrote that we are stringent in this only when he sells; but for himself in his house we have no concern. It is forbidden to weigh meat in water, namely a vessel that has notches [that is, grooves], and he puts water in it and places the meat in it, and when the water reaches the notches he knows how many litras are in the meat: this too is forbidden, and even if he slightly reduced or slightly added, when it is recognizable that he intends for the weight [Gemara].
{14} Cuts in his usual way. Magen Avraham wrote that it is permitted to say to the butcher "give me a litra of meat," for he does not intend for the weight, but to inform him how much he needs, and as the Rama wrote above in siman 323, se'if 4; and see there in Mishnah Berurah, s.k. 20, and Biur Halacha.
