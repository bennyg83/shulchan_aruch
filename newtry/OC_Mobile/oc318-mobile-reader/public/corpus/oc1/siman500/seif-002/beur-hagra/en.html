Seif 2: Even in his house. Tur. He learned it from what is written on 29a: "and the baker, in order," etc.; otherwise it is forbidden. This is unlike the Yerushalmi there, which teaches: Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel says that a person may even reconcile the portions with a litra-weight to know how much reached him, like this case: R. Mana bought carobs of shemittah and came to ask R. Chizkiyah, who said to him: the rabbis practice like this ruling of Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel. See Beit Yosef. It appears to me that from the Yerushalmi the opposite is proven, for it says there: R. Yosi in the name of Shmuel: even to hang it on a scale-pan because of mice is forbidden. Meaning, this is what it says, "we do not pay attention." R. Yosi bar Bun said: not for this reason; meaning, he did not say "we do not pay attention" because in such a case it is permitted, but because we learned: R. Yehudah says one may weigh meat against a vessel or against a cleaver, and the Sages say we do not pay attention to a scale-pan at all. The first tanna holds that we do pay attention, meaning against a vessel or cleaver, and regarding such a case the Rabbis said we do not pay attention. It follows that hanging it on a scale-pan because of mice is permitted. Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel teaches: a person may even reconcile, etc., as above. Since we do not rule like the Yerushalmi, which concludes that because of mice it is permitted, all the more so we do not rule like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel.
And even to weigh, etc. There: if like the Rabbis, etc., and we rule like the Rabbis. Although R. Yehudah ruled like R. Yehudah, Abaye rejected him. See the Yerushalmi there, which also concludes to distinguish between a bekhor and Yom Tov because we do not pay attention to the scale-pan at all, except that it says there that even R. Chiyya and R. Shimon son of Rabbi forbid on Yom Tov; see there.
