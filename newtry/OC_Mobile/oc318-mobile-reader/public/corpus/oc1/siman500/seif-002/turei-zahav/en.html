It is forbidden to weigh even in his house. In the Gemara, "in his house" is not mentioned; only that one should not weigh meat. And these are the words of the Tur: and the Rashba wrote: at first glance it appears that all this deals only with one who buys from the butcher or with partners who divide between themselves; but to weigh in his house to know how much to cook is permitted. However, regarding measuring flour it is implied that even in his house it is forbidden; end of the words of the Tur. And Beis Yosef wrote: from the Yerushalmi it appears like the Rashba, for the Mordechai wrote in chapter Ein Tzadin: Yerushalmi: a person may settle himself with a litra to know how much reached him; meaning, although they said we do not attend to a scale-pan, they said that only of a butcher, but for a householder, if he wishes to know how much reached his share, it is permitted to weigh, for so is the way of a householder to count; end of his words. And to me it appears that our Gemara does not hold thus, for Rav and Shmuel disagree regarding a woman measuring flour for her dough, and it is implied that they disagree in her house; and even Rav permitted only that she take challah generously; but where that reason does not apply, Rav also forbids; thus far in Beis Yosef. And according to my humble opinion there is no dispute between the Gemaras, for we find regarding Shabbos concerning reckonings of "what of it" that it is permitted like R. Shimon in siman 306, se'if 6: it is permitted to reckon how much he paid wages to his workers, since he has no practical outcome thereby; so too in the Yerushalmi it is permitted to reckon how much reached his share.
