(א) (s.k. 7) And one does not separate etc. — its law is like cooking on Shabbat; its law is explained siman 318: intentionally forbidden for him forever and for others permitted at Motzei Shabbat immediately; and regarding what is written permitted also for himself immediately:

(ב) Ran — not like Rambam as it should be in my humble opinion — this Ran in Beitzah on the mishnah one does not raise terumot and maaserot on Yom Tov — that were mentioned in the mishnah everything forbidden because of shevut and explained here in Shulchan Aruch — Ran brought Yerushalmi and all of them if done whether under compulsion etc. whether intentionally etc. what was done is done — all this Ran wrote and this is what is written: however regarding terumot we learned that if he separated terumah and tithe on Shabbat it is like cooking on Shabbat — unintentionally he may eat, intentionally he may not eat — end Ran; and it is chapter 2 of Terumot mishnah 3; and since Ran wrote that the law of one who separates terumah and tithe on Shabbat its law is like cooking and this is not the language of the mishnah except in the mishnah it teaches the tithe on Shabbat — unintentionally he may eat, intentionally he may eat — see Tiferet Yisrael; rather Ran by reasoning holds its law is like cooking and it turns out he wrote that the tithe — unintentionally he may eat, intentionally he may not eat — meaning he copied the language of the mishnah; and the mishnah comes like R' Meir who holds thus regarding cooking (and it is possible Ran ruled like R' Meir); but automatically we hear for us that we rule regarding cooking like R' Yehuda as above siman 318; and since Ran holds the tithe its law is like cooking — the same that regarding tithe the law is as is written siman 318; but in Rambam it turns out that regarding cooking he ruled like R' Yehuda in chapter 6 law 23 — nevertheless regarding tithe on Shabbat he copied in chapter 23 law 15 the plain mishnah;
