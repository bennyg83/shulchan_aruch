1) [Seif 1] One may not ride upon an animal. The reason is a decree lest one cut a branch to direct it, as stated above in siman 305, paragraph 62. See our words there on seif 18, with the help of Heaven.
2) There: One may not ride upon an animal. A person traveling through a wilderness may use a stratagem on Shabbos by going to sleep in the litter shortly before the caravan departs, saying that he wishes to sleep. The non-Jews will then carry him and place the litter upon the camel, as customary. Even if he is not a Torah scholar, it is preferable that he use this stratagem rather than walk and violate the techum. Radbaz in the new responsa printed in Ashkenaz, siman 560. Maharikash alludes to this. Elsewhere, however, Radbaz discusses the matter at length and writes that walking is preferable to riding the camel, since riding involves two or three prohibitions whereas walking involves one. Therefore, if walking presents no danger, it is preferable to walk; see there. Machazik Berachah here, paragraph 3, and siman 404, paragraph 2. See our words there in paragraph 9. We already wrote about this above in siman 266 regarding the gloss to seif 2; see there.
