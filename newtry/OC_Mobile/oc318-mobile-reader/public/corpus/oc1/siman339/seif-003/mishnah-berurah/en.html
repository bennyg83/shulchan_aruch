(א) One does not clap etc. — whether in mourning and anger to arouse distress or because of joy — all because of a decree lest one fix a musical instrument to arouse the mourner or the rejoicer:

(ב) And one does not dance — and on Simchat Torah it is permitted to dance at the time they recite praises of the Torah because of honor of the Torah since there is only a rabbinic prohibition of shevut; but in other joy of a mitzvah such as at weddings — nevertheless forbidden; and also for honor of the Torah it is not worth permitting except clapping and dancing and not rattling bells and nuts, and all the more so other musical instruments which are forbidden [Acharonim]:

(ג) Or one against etc. — such as those who tap the thumb on the forefinger in melody and make pleasant motions with this [Beit Yosef]; and this is what he concluded "as singers do"; and if he does this forcefully in order to wake his fellow from sleep — it appears from Magen Avraham that it is permitted for it is not a manner of song; however according to what he explained in Tosafos Shabbos the view of Rashi implies that according to Rashi one should be stringent here too; and so it appears in Peri Megadim:

(ד) To be lenient in everything — this "to be lenient in everything" refers to clapping and slapping and dancing and not to the other matters mentioned in this seif; and even in clapping and dancing it is not worth abandoning the custom except not in a place of mitzvah but because of "leave them" etc.:
