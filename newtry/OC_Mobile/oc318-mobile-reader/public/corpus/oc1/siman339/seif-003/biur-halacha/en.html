Or on the board, or one opposite the other. See its explanation in Mishnah Berurah, from the Beit Yosef and later authorities. For this reason he wondered at the Terumat HaDeshen, who wrote against the Rambam's words. In our text of the Rambam the reading is "on the board, one opposite the other," and according to this it refers to the prior clause: he struck on the ground or on the board, one opposite the other, which is the manner of musicians. This excludes a single knock on the ground or board, which is not the manner of musicians, and hints that knocking on a door and the like is permitted, as the Rambam explicitly wrote in his commentary to the Mishnah at the end of Eruvin. But one can still say that snapping with the finger is not the manner of song. What the Rambam wrote in his commentary to Yoma, that he made pleasant movements with it, does not prove that it was music; his intent is that since it was only to keep the Kohen Gadol awake, why did they not simply knock on something? He answers that they used pleasant movements, which is more respectful before the Kohen Gadol than ordinary knocking.
And clapping in an unusual manner is permitted. The source of this law is the Yerushalmi, as explained in Beit Yosef. Since the Yerushalmi specifies only clapping in an unusual manner, it implies that shaking bells and the like, which is more in the manner of music, is not helped even if done unusually. Moreover, even regarding clapping itself we have no clear proof from the Yerushalmi that it is permitted, since Rabbi Meir is stringent there, and the Beit Yosef answers this with difficulty. We therefore should not be more lenient than what is explicit in the Yerushalmi.
To be lenient in all. See Mishnah Berurah: this means ordinary clapping and dancing, not with shaking bells, and certainly not with musical instruments, which is a complete prohibition. See the novellae of Rabbi Akiva Eiger, who refers to Zikhron Yosef. I obtained it, with the help of Heaven, and will copy some of his holy words in response to a great rabbi, Rabbi Yaakov:
"Trembling and dread seized me; in all their troubles I am troubled, over the great breach made by the lawless among our people in their place, to transgress brazenly against the law and judgment of our holy Torah and the religion of Judaism. Not only did they profane Yom Tov by making dances and circles, which Chazal decreed against even for men alone or women alone, as taught in chapter Mashilin, 'one may not clap and one may not dance,' and as ruled in Shulchan Aruch siman 339 and siman 524. Every understanding heart knows that clapping and dancing, even without instruments and kinds of music, is forbidden by law because of concern that one may repair a musical instrument, all the more with musical instruments and players. The Rama's statement that the custom is to be lenient does not refer to this manner, as Eliyah Rabbah writes; and Responsa Sha'ar Ephraim siman 36 forbids even on Chol HaMoed. Worse still, on the other side they made a celebration and danced in circles, young men with maidens, and married men and married women mingled with them. Woe to their souls, for they have brought evil upon themselves by publicly transgressing the law; the Torah girds sackcloth over them. May the Merciful One save us from this punishment. Your honor asked whether he acted properly by protesting against them so they not continue sinning on the second day of Yom Tov. The answer: he certainly acted properly to protest with all his strength against such people. Every rabbi and teacher in his city is obligated to protest and abolish dancing and circles on Yom Tov, all the more young men and maidens together, and all the more the mixed crowd of men and women. As explained in Orach Chayyim siman 529, men and women should not mingle. There is no distinction here between married and unmarried women. There is no greater step toward the yetzer hara and toward licentiousness than this, when these uncultured young men look at the faces of women and maidens while dancing and hold the hands of women, following the deeds of their ancestors. About them I call out, 'hand to hand, he will not be cleansed' from the judgment of Gehinnom. Sometimes they hug and kiss to fulfill their desire, coming to the deed of Er and Onan; sometimes through this they arrange secretly to seclude themselves by day or night, and their deeds are in darkness, Heaven forbid, and the land becomes full of depravity. It is known that most maidens today have already reached the age of seeing blood, and according to the Rambam and many Rishonim and Acharonim, one who touches them affectionately or with bodily closeness violates 'to a woman in the impurity of her niddah you shall not draw near.' Even ordinary closeness is forbidden, and one who hugs or kisses for desire would have received lashes in earlier times, as the Rambam writes in chapter 21 of Hilkhot Issurei Bi'ah and Semag mitzvah 353. Even according to the Ramban, it is at least rabbinic, as written in Chavot Yair siman 182, Responsa Matzat? siman 19, and Even HaEzer beginning of siman 21 and simanim 22 and 25. Regarding the prohibition of niddah relations, there is no difference between married and unmarried women, as the Rama writes in Yoreh De'ah siman 183; this is obvious and from its obviousness need not be written, except to remove from the hearts of ignoramuses who think there is no prohibition with an unmarried woman. The truth is that there is no distinction, Heaven forbid. In sum, there is no greater fence against ervah than abolishing dances of men and women together, married or unmarried, for there is no greater step toward promiscuity, arayot, and habituation to sin than this. We who have received His Torah are obligated to make a fence and safeguard that this not be done."
There in his responsum he also records what he answered to a nobleman who asked him about this. He answered that for us it is a grave sin and contrary to Judaism. He showed him what is written in Judges 21, "they said, behold, a festival... the daughters of Shiloh came out to dance in dances"; since it does not say "with young men" or "lads," it implies that the maidens danced only among themselves. Likewise Jeremiah 31 says, "then shall the maiden rejoice in the dance, and young men and elders together"; it does not say, "then shall the maiden rejoice with young men in the dance." Likewise Zechariah 12 says, "and their wives separately." From this there is a kal vachomer in a time of joy and dancing, with the yetzer hara inciting to sin, as in Sukkah 52. Not for nothing did Iyov say, "I made a covenant with my eyes; how then could I gaze..." The nobleman praised his answer and said, "I see that your words are good and correct." According to our religion it is forbidden even to look at colored garments of a woman, all the more to gaze at her face, whether maiden or married. For this reason the av beit din and community in Furth and in the holy community of Metz enacted that young men should not dance with maidens at all, even at weddings; rather, the maidens dance by themselves and the young men by themselves. See there, where he discusses this further.
