[2] We do not judge, etc. One may not arrange litigants' arguments on Shabbos. The regular daily service should not be suspended except for a complaint concerning a virginity claim or a communal claim, as stated by the later authorities. The Yerushalmi derives from 'in all your dwelling places' that batei din may not judge on Shabbos, as cited in Sefer HaChinuch, Parashat Vayakhel. The Magen Avraham overlooked this. Sefer HaChinuch explains that Hashem desired to honor this day so that everyone, including wrongdoers and those subject to judgment, should find rest, like a great king who summons the people of the city to a feast for a day.
[3] And we do not separate tithes, etc. This resembles consecrating the produce that one separates. Moreover, the act is effective and resembles repairing something. If one did separate, its law is like food cooked on Shabbos in siman 318. In either event he has repaired the produce, as stated by the Rambam in chapter 23. See siman 322, seif katan 12. One should be especially careful not to do this initially, because many transgressions can result, as stated by the Magen Avraham.
[4] He should not have relations, etc. Olat Shabbos writes that according to the Rama, who permits bringing a virgin under the chuppah on Shabbos for a man who has no wife or children, the same should permit him to have relations with a widow on Shabbos. This requires practical study because there is danger involved, as the Maharil writes. This is incorrect. The Rama and Levush permitted the chuppah only because of human dignity and financial loss from the meal, as the Rama explains in responsum 125. See siman 524.
[5] [Levush] They designate privacy, etc. This applies specifically after the chuppah. Before the chuppah, privacy is ineffective and it is prohibited for a betrothed man and woman to be secluded together, as stated by Masat Binyamin. He further writes that seclusion sufficient for full relations is required, although they should not actually have relations. He also writes that some communities have the practice to arrange seclusion even for a bachelor marrying a widow, because some question whether our form of chuppah acquires even a virgin. This is proper. These remarks concern a place where first relations are performed on Shabbos, as in siman 280. The Maharil writes that if a widow's immersion falls on Friday night, she should not immerse on Motzaei Shabbos either but wait until Sunday night. Chelkat Mechokek, siman 60, permits immersion and relations because even if the father delivered her to the husband's agents, he acquires her. His words do not appear correct, as is clear from Masat Binyamin there, and the Magen Avraham also objects. It appears that if her immersion falls on Motzaei Shabbos she may immerse then, and so ruled my revered grandfather of blessed memory. Likewise, she may enter the chuppah on Friday while still tamei, because he will not have relations with her on Shabbos.
