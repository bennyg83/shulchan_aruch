(Se'if katan 2) We train, etc. See there in Tosafos. This is in Tosafos Yeshanim, and so too in Terumas HaDeshen, siman 94. They asked about what we learned in Yoma 82, that we train minors by hours: but a minor who eats neveilos, the court is not commanded to separate him. If so, can we train them, when separation itself should be required? Rabbeinu Yitzchak answered that training minors applies only to the father, but not to another person, and therefore they are not commanded to separate him. They asked from the case at the beginning of Sukkah, that Queen Helen sat with her seven sons in a sukkah, which shows that even where there is no father, training applies. They answered: perhaps they had a father who trained them for this; and even if they had no father, their mother trained them only as a general mitzvah matter. End quote of Tosafos. So too in the Terumas HaDeshen mentioned above. Tosafos wrote similarly in Nazir 28b, and on page 29a the Gemara concludes explicitly according to Reish Lakish that a mother is not obligated to train her son. Although Rabbi Yochanan there disagrees with Reish Lakish, and according to Rabbi Yochanan it is not necessary to say that a woman is not obligated to train her son, nevertheless it is better to minimize the dispute and say that Rabbi Yochanan too agrees with Reish Lakish on this point. See above in the Magen Avraham, siman 343.
Nevertheless, it seems to me that she should not give, etc. Meaning, the mother, although she is not obligated in training.
And it is not comparable to sukkah, where she is permitted, etc. For if you would not say so, what did Tosafos ask from Queen Helen sitting with her seven sons in a sukkah, as above? It would have been forbidden for her to give them food outside the sukkah.
And she is not obligated to bring him in, etc., because the mother is not obligated in training.
Furthermore, there it is a positive commandment. Meaning, eating in the sukkah is a positive commandment.
A negative prohibition is more severe than a positive commandment, as stated in the first chapter of Yevamos in the sugya of 'aleha,' and as is also proven in the second chapter of Yevamos. End quote of Terumas HaDeshen, siman 94. Therefore, since eating in a sukkah is only a positive commandment, even giving them food is permitted. However, the Magen Avraham at the beginning of siman 269 rejected this, according to what we rule that even a rabbinic prohibition may not be fed directly to a minor, all the more so a Torah positive commandment. Therefore one must say that what he wrote here, 'furthermore, there it is a positive commandment,' leaves his first answer primary: sukkah is different, because in the eating itself there is no prohibition. In truth, directly feeding a minor outside the sukkah is forbidden even for a woman, and so he wrote explicitly below in siman 640. Rather, after he previously distinguished that on Yom Kippur the eating itself is prohibited, he now writes another reasoning: even if Yom Kippur were exactly comparable to sukkah in this respect, there is more room for leniency with sukkah, because it is a positive commandment, than with Yom Kippur. However, the Terumas HaDeshen at the end of the responsum challenged the Tosafos mentioned above and proved from Eruvin that the mother too is obligated to train her son, and he concluded that one should not be lenient; see there.
That which is not, etc. In his twelfth year. Meaning, when he is eleven complete years old, as the Rema wrote, one should be lenient only with a thin youth.
Because everyone studies, etc. We rely on Rashi's view in Kesubos 50a according to the Bach's explanation of Rashi's words; see there. But when he is twelve complete years old and enters his thirteenth year, Rashi, and also the Bach, agree that he must complete the fast rabbinically, except for a thin youth, where we rely on the 'some say' opinion brought by the Rema. In Eliyah Rabbah he wrote in the name of the Bach on what the Rema wrote, 'and one may rely on them regarding a youth who is thin': our custom is so with all children, since they all study, and also nowadays they are ordinarily considered sick. In truth, the Bach was lenient only when he is eleven, as above. It is possible that in Eliyah Rabbah he wrote this from his own reasoning based on the Bach's reasoning. And also because ordinarily they are considered sick, meaning nowadays, when weakness has descended to the world. The Bach concluded: since we are not expert when it is danger to life, in a doubt involving life we rule leniently.
