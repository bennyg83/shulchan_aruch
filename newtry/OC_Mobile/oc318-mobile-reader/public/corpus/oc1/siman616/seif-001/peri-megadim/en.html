And it is permitted. See the Taz, Yoma 78b, and the Magen Avraham, letter 7, who explained this way. See the Bach. The Taz explained, 'A woman may rinse,' etc., as referring to an infant who cannot take it himself; otherwise, it is forbidden for her to rinse her hand in water and give him bread, and he should take it himself, because she derives benefit from washing her hands. See siman 613. That which it is permitted to feed them with one's hands even where the infant could take it himself, see siman 612, se'if 10 in the gloss, in the Terumas HaDeshen brought by the Beis Yosef here. According to the one who forbids, see the Magen Avraham there. One can say that trimming vegetables and cracking nuts from Minchah onward is permitted because of distress; otherwise even touching would be forbidden. What the Rav wrote in the gloss, that a minor who is in training is also trained regarding washing, is so in the Tur. Although he holds in 611 that these matters are rabbinic, nevertheless one is obligated to train them, as I wrote elsewhere, and all the more so according to us, where all of them are from the Torah as in siman 611. Regarding training a minor on the four fasts, see Tosafos Yom HaKippurim, chapter 2, 1; with Hashem's help, in Hilchos Tishah B'Av and fasts it will be explained that we train only on Yom Kippur.
