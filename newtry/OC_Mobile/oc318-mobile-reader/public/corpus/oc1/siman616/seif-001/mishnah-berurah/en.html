(1) They are permitted in all these - In washing, anointing, eating, and drinking, for the decree was not made upon minors. An adult may feed him, give him to drink, anoint him, and wash him. This applies specifically to washing in cold water, but he may not wash his whole body in hot water, even if it was heated before Yom Kippur, because washing in hot water is not forbidden only because of the mitzvah of affliction; even on every Shabbat of the year it is forbidden to wash in hot water.
(2) Except for wearing shoes, for we are not so concerned, etc. - Meaning, not wearing shoes is not an affliction for a minor, unlike eating, drinking, washing, and anointing, which help the child's growth, as was the custom in their time. Whether one must protest if the minor puts on shoes by himself is explained in siman 343.
(3) To tell a non-Jew to wash them, etc. - Meaning, even with hot water heated that day, for an ordinary infant has the status of an ill person regarding hot water. But it is forbidden to tell a non-Jew to heat it. The later authorities wrote that nowadays, when the practice is not to wash and anoint a minor every day, and withholding washing and anointing is not considered an affliction for him at all, one should not wash or anoint him on Yom Kippur even through a non-Jew.
