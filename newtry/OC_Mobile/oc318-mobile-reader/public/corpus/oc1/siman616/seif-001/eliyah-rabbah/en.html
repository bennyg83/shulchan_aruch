[1] [Levush:] But to wash, etc. That which is stated in the Gemara that they are initially permitted means that an adult pours water for them with a vessel and they wash themselves. The Magen Avraham wrote that anything which is because of affliction was not decreed upon a child, and therefore it is permitted for an adult to wash and anoint them with his hands, as is explicit in the Gemara. But washing with hot water, which is forbidden even on Shabbos, is forbidden even for an infant, and was permitted only through non-Jews. End quote. This is difficult, for on page 78 it says that the reason they are initially permitted is because it is their growth, as Abaye said, etc., hot water, etc. Thus even with hot water it is permitted for an adult to wash them with his hands. Also, what the Magen Avraham wondered against the Bach and the Mordechai, who permit an adult to wash a child with hot water, saying that the complete Mishnah teaches that we wash the child both before circumcision, etc.; but even without circumcision it is permitted. End quote. In my humble opinion, this can be well resolved: the Mishnah speaks specifically on Shabbos and not on Yom Tov. Examine the Mordechai there carefully, for I have shortened, since at the end of this siman it is explained that nowadays the custom is not to wash them.
