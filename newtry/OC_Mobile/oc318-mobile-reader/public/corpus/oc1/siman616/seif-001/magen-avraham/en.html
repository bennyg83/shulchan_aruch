To tell a non-Jew. But a Jew is forbidden to wash and anoint them, because he derives benefit when he washes and anoints his hands [Beis Yosef]. One must say that what is stated in the Gemara, 'but they are initially permitted,' means that an adult pours water for them from a vessel and they wash themselves. The Bach wrote that it is permitted for a Jew to wash him with hot water heated yesterday, and through a non-Jew it is permitted to heat hot water for them; so wrote the Mordechai, chapter 2 of Beitzah. End quote. This is astonishing in my eyes, for we learned a complete Mishnah: we wash the child both before the milah, etc. But even without milah it should be permitted. The Ran there and the Maggid Mishneh in chapter 2 of Hilchos Shabbos wrote that even for milah, only sprinkling is permitted, and it clearly implies that for any other child everything is forbidden. Therefore it seems to me that certainly anything that is because of affliction was not decreed upon a child, and therefore an adult may wash him in cold water and anoint him with his hands, as is explicit in the Gemara. But washing with hot water, which is forbidden even on Shabbos, is forbidden even for an infant, and was permitted only through a non-Jew, as written in siman 328, end of se'if 17. This is what the Tur intended, as seems clear to me. But to tell a non-Jew to heat hot water for him is forbidden, as is explicit in Eruvin 68. The words of Rabbeinu Yehudah in Mordechai, chapter 2 of Beitzah, require analysis. Perhaps he is lenient on Yom Tov. See siman 511, se'if 2 in the gloss, where it is proven that this is the Rema's view. Nowadays the custom is not to wash them or anoint them at all (Levush), and so it appears in siman 331.
