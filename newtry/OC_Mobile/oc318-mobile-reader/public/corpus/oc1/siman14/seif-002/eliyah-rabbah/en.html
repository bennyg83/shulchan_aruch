[2] "And some are stringent," etc. For it is written "sons" and not daughters; and further, similar to tefillin: whoever is in tying, etc.; and those who permit hold that where it is explicit, it is explicit — Levush. And it seems to me that a practical difference emerges between these two reasons: per the second reason, even a minor who reached chinuch may not make tzitzit, as below siman 39 in tefillin; and likewise sukkah and lulav they may not make, since they are not obligated; and per the first reason they are valid in sukkah and lulav, and likewise a minor is fit to make tzitzit. On what the Levush wrote above, "and where it is explicit," etc. — I brought in Eliyah Zuta that Malbushei Yom Tov wrote; this is not his language, but because wearing is not written in tzitzit itself, therefore "all that is not in wearing" — so wrote Tosafos Menachot 43, end quote. And now it seems to me to reconcile that the Levush's intent is also thus: since wearing is not written explicitly, we do not learn tzitzit from tefillin — for just as they are exempt from tefillin, so they are exempt from tzitzit, for we say where it is explicit it is explicit; but if wearing were written in it, we would learn the same in tzitzit — and so is explained in Beit Yosef in the name of the Mordechai, and it is clear. Magen Avraham wrote: requires study — but in Kiddushin 36 and Menachot 61 we exclude gentiles and women from "the children of Israel," end quote. And it seems to me that we find in Menachot 42 that Rav Ashi said the reverse — gentiles are fit to make tzitzit, for it is written "Speak to the children of Israel and they shall make for themselves tzitzit" — "and they shall make" through others, and this is because for them it is superfluous; if so, it is difficult on one who says it is invalid for them — why do I need this? Rather, he expounds it to include women, who are not excluded from "children of Israel" but gentiles are. And on one who says to validate gentiles it is difficult — why not write "children of Israel" and not "for them"? And it seems to me that he holds the reasoning "all that is not in wearing," and I would have said gentiles are invalid — therefore "children of Israel" and "for them" were needed to validate; but one who says a gentile is invalid holds that this reasoning does not apply in tzitzit, "all that is not in wearing," as above. If so, the external reasoning that gentiles are valid — therefore he learned from "children of Israel" to invalidate gentiles and from "for them" to validate women, and in this they disagree; and in this I explained well in yeshiva study the words of Tosafos Menachot 42 s.v. "from where," etc., that they listed and did not say, etc., in Israel, etc. — and in my novellae it will be explained.
