Without intent: that is, he tied the upper knot, which is from the Torah, without intent; but if he tied the upper knot with intent, even though afterward he made the other knots and windings without intent—even where there are other tzitzit—there is room to validate. Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham.

On the Rambam who validates. Explanation—the Rambam holds that although spinning requires lishmah, nevertheless attaching to the garment does not require lishmah; he infers this from "the children of Israel," to exclude gentiles; hence a Jew without intent is valid. See Beit Yosef, what he wrote. And the Taz in subsection 2 made as if he did not know Joseph and answered on his own what the Beit Yosef answered—see there—and said to him: remove them from the garment and replace them with intent on the garment; for one might say this speaks of Shabbat, or when one of the head strings tore; and if he removes them they would be forbidden to place on the garment, as stated above in siman 12, subsection 2.
