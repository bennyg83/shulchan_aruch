Without intention. Meaning, he tied the upper knot, which is D'Oraisa, without intention. If he made the upper knot with intention, then even if he afterward made the remaining knots and windings without intention, the tzitzis may be validated where other tzitzis are unavailable. Olas Tamid.
Regarding Rambam, who validates. Rambam maintains that although the spinning must be for the sake of the mitzvah, attaching the strings to the garment need not be. He derives this from the verse's reference to “the children of Israel,” excluding non-Jews. This implies that a Jew who acts without intention is valid. See Beit Yosef for his explanation. Taz, seif katan 2, acted as though this explanation were unknown and independently offered the same resolution as Beit Yosef; see there. One might ask that the strings should be removed from the garment and reattached with intention. The answer is that the case may concern Shabbos. Alternatively, one end of a string may have been severed, so that if the strings were removed it would be forbidden to reattach them, as explained above in siman 12, seif katan 2.
