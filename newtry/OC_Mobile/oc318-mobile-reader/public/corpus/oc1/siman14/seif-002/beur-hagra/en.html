Section 2, "If he inserted, etc." The Rambam follows his own view, for he explained what is stated there, 42b, "from the remnants, etc.," as not being due to the requirement of being made for its own sake, as I wrote above in siman 11, section 5. The proof is that Rav holds that tzitzit made by a gentile are valid, while a bill of divorce written by a gentile is invalid because it must be written for its own sake, as stated there, 23a. Even according to the second version, this is specifically because an exclusion is written. This is what is stated in Sukkah 9a: "Evidently, we require spinning for its own sake"; they disagree only about spinning. So too according to Rashi's explanation. See above, siman 15, section 4. But according to the explanation of Tosafot, "from the remnants, etc." means that it must be made for its own sake, and the Rosh wrote likewise. He follows his own view, holding that there is no proof from a bill of divorce, as is stated in Avodah Zarah 27 regarding circumcision, which is valid according to Rabbi Yehudah. See what I wrote above in siman 11, sections 2 and 5.
