(6) Without intention. Meaning, he tied the upper knot, which is D'Oraisa, without intention. If he tied the upper knot and made one winding with intention, then even if the remaining knots and windings were made afterward without intention, they may be validated even where other tzitzis are available.
(7) Regarding Rambam, who validates. This applies even if he inserted the strings and made all the knots and windings without intention. Rambam maintains that although spinning must be for the sake of the mitzvah, attaching the strings need not be. He derives this from the verse's exclusion of a non-Jew: if a non-Jew is excluded specifically, a Jew who acts without intention is valid. One might ask that the strings should be removed and reattached with proper intention. The answer is that the case concerns one close to Shabbos, without enough time to remove and retie them. Alternatively, one end of a string may have been severed, so that if the strings were removed it would be forbidden to reattach them, as explained in siman 12.
(8) He should not recite a blessing over it. This applies even if only the insertion into the corner was done without intention, and all the more so if the upper knot was tied without intention. Rashi, Rosh, and Tosafos maintain that attaching the strings for the sake of the mitzvah is required according to the Torah. The verse excluding a non-Jew teaches that the work remains invalid even if a Jew stands over him and instructs him to act for the sake of the mitzvah. One must therefore be careful initially, before inserting the strings, to state explicitly that he is attaching all these tzitzis for the sake of tzitzis, just as explained above in siman 11 regarding spinning and twisting.
