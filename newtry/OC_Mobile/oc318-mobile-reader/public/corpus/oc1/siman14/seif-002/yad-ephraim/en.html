In Taz, se'if katan 5: For it is written, 'your garment,' and not another person's, etc. In my humble opinion this is no difficulty. There, 'your garment' excludes one who borrows from another a tallis that does not have valid tzitzis; he is exempt. The reasoning that the lender intended an effective transfer enabling fulfillment does not apply there. Here, however, the borrower takes a tallis with tzitzis specifically to fulfill his obligation. This resembles kiddushin, where the parties certainly intend an effective transaction. Taz's further question is likewise resolved by what I wrote. His proposed assumption, however; that borrowing a lulav is invalid by a Scriptural decree, etc.; is very strained. Perhaps the Torah's case is one where the lender does not know that the borrower needs it to fulfill his obligation.
