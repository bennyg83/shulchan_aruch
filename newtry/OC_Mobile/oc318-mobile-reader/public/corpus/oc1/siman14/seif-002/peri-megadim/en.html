Regarding Rambam. See Taz and Beit Yosef. See Magen Avraham, seif katan 1. According to all opinions, the exclusion of a non-Jew is not because his act lacks intention for the sake of the mitzvah, but is a Scriptural decree. Therefore, even if a Jew stands over him, which according to Rosh is effective in siman 11, seif 1, he is invalid here. See Bach. The language of the Mechaber and Rambam implies that if a Jew acted without intention, even if he made all the knots and windings without intention, it is valid. I do not understand why. The verse says, “You shall make fringes for yourself,” meaning for the sake of your obligation. This applies to spinning and twisting through the juxtaposition to shatnez, and all the more so to the knots and windings through the word “fringes,” as Levush writes in siman 11. From that word we also derive “you shall make,” and not something made through an invalid state, and that applies to tying, not merely insertion. If so, the word “for yourself,” meaning for your obligation, should likewise apply to tying. Only insertion without intention should be valid. This requires study.
