(Taz there seif 5) And similarly it is difficult from what the Rosh wrote in the first chapter of Kiddushin — and similarly Magen Avraham asked in siman 658 seif 3; see there. And it appears to me for our case that certainly it is as a gift on condition to return, for he is not particular about the time since he did not set a time — this applies to say regarding a borrowed tallit or ring; but regarding an esrog, if he did not say "for me" and did not set a time, then he may return it to him even after the festival. And "we are witnesses" does not apply, for he did not give it to him with this intent; therefore we say regarding an esrog that he did not give it as a gift, and likewise Rama in Choshen Mishpat siman 241: if he did not set a time to return, the recipient may return it when he wishes. And if it is a thing needed by the giver, such as an esrog, and he said on condition that you return it to me — certainly he gives it that he return it while he needs it and he must return it immediately. And if he said on condition that you return it and did not say "for me," he may return when he wishes. And therefore, in such a case where we assess his mind that he gave him the gift on condition to return and did not say "for me" and did not set a time that he may return when he wishes — in this we certainly do not say "we are witnesses" when he asked him that his intent was to give it to him; understand well.
