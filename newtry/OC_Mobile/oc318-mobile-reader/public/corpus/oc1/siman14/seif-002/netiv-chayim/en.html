Taz there, seif katan 5. The same difficulty arises from Rosh in the first chapter of Kiddushin, siman 52, and Magen Avraham raises it in siman 658, seif katan 3. It appears to me that there is no difficulty. A borrowed tallis or ring is certainly treated like a gift on condition that it be returned, and the lender is not particular about the time of return because no time was fixed. An esrog is different. If the giver did not say “to me” and did not fix a time, the recipient could return it even after the festival, yet it is obvious that the giver did not intend this. Therefore, regarding an esrog, we say that it was not given as an unconditional gift. Rama writes similarly in Choshen Mishpat, siman 241: if no time for return was fixed, the recipient may return it whenever he wishes. If the item is needed by the giver, such as an esrog, and he said, “on condition that you return it to me,” he certainly intended that it be returned while he still needs it, and it must be returned immediately. If he said only, “on condition that you return it,” without saying “to me,” it may be returned whenever the recipient wishes. Therefore, where we assess that the lender intended a gift on condition of return, but he neither said “to me” nor fixed a time, the recipient may return it whenever he wishes. In such a case we certainly do not presume that when he merely lent it he intended to give it as a gift. Consider this carefully.
