"If a Jew tied," etc. — Know that from the words of the Beit Yosef it is clear that even if he tied explicitly not l'shemah, it is valid per the Rambam; and conversely according to Rashi and the Rosh, even when unstated it is invalid — for otherwise there is no proof that the Rosh disagrees with the Rambam, for from the thorns and from the threads they certainly did not attach them l'shemah, and so wrote in the Perishah; and see in the responsum of R' Eliezer Azikri in siman 4, who settles why we do not say here that when unstated it refers to l'shemah. Afterward I found in the responsum of Mishkenot Yaakov in siman 20 that his view is that the Rambam too was lenient only when unstated, because it refers to l'shemah — see there.

"Without intent." — And if he thought in his mind l'shemah, it seems to me that he may bless on it, although regarding spinning we remained in doubt above in siman 11 — it requires further study whether thought helps here. One reason to permit: there is a doubt lest the halachah is that thought suffices — see in the Rosh in Hilchos Ketanos; and perhaps the halachah follows the Rambam. And another reason: spinning threads when unstated is not for the sake of tzitzit, unlike attaching the threads, where when unstated it refers to l'shemah; and combining all these reasons, it seems to me he may bless on it. But lechatchila it is better that he express it explicitly with his mouth, and so I brought in the law in Mishna Berurah. Know further that it seems to me that even if the intent l'shemah was only in attaching the first tzitzit and the rest he tied when unstated, nevertheless he may bless, for we say: whoever acts according to his initial intent is acting — although at first glance they are two separate actions. The proof is from that which is implied in Zevachim 42b in the passage beginning "this is for its sake," etc., that if he thought at slaughter l'shemah and at receiving it was unstated — even if we say that when unstated it does not refer to l'shemah — nevertheless whoever acts according to his initial intent is acting (as I explained above in siman 11 regarding spinning and twisting — see there in Biur Halacha in seif 2). And one may say that there the four services are all one mitzvah — is it not so in our matter too, that all four tzitziyot are one mitzvah, for they impede one another? But if he says explicitly before beginning to attach that all the tzitziyot he will attach afterward shall all be for the sake of tzitzit, then he does not need all this — and so too at the beginning of siman 11 in Biur Halacha, see there.

"He does not bless on it." — See in Mishna Berurah in what he wrote: even if it was only the attaching without intent. Here at first glance there would be room to say that specifically if he made the knot — according to what the Peri Megadim brought in the name of the Levush, a reason on that which we rule in siman 11 seif 13 that specifically if he made the knot — for the verse of "fringes you shall make for yourself" implies that once a fringe is made, from it we derive "you shall make" and not from what is already made; if so, automatically the same applies to what we derive "you shall make for yourself" for the sake of your obligation, that attaching tzitzit to the garment also refers to it according to Rashi and the Rosh — specifically if he also made the knot and chulyah, as is implied. But from the words of the Beit Yosef in the passage beginning "the Rambam wrote," etc., clearly attaching l'shemah is required — see there — it is proven explicitly that inserting into the corner alone also requires that it be l'shemah; and afterward I found this proven even more explicitly in Tosafos Menachot 42b s.v. "the thorns," etc., see there. Nevertheless, it is possible that if he forgot to intend l'shemah at the time of inserting and at the time of the knot he intended l'shemah, he may bless, for we say: the end proves the beginning; and that case of the thorns is different, for "the end proves the beginning" does not apply there; and although this itself is a doubt, as the Gra wrote above in siman 11 seif 1, and as the Rambam ruled in chapter 2 of Shechitah and chapter 4 of Laws of Acquisition and Gift — nevertheless there is still a doubt here, lest the halachah follows the Rambam that l'shemah is not required, and also lest here when unstated it refers to l'shemah, and there are those who ruled thus — see in Mishkenot Yaakov siman 20 and in Aruch HaShulchan. But one may reject somewhat, for there are decisors [Maharshal and the Derishah in Yoreh De'ah siman 4] who hold that we do not say "the end proves the beginning" at all — it requires further study. Were I not afraid, I would say something new in our matter: the essence of the dispute of the Rosh against the Rambam is when a Jew tied tzitzit in the garment of another Jew who asked him to insert these threads into it — for per the Rambam it is valid even in this, for he infers from that which the Gemara invalidates specifically when a gentile ties tzitzit on a garment, that it speaks of when he tied on a Jew's garment, as is implied in the Gemara Menachot 42b from the latter version of Rav, "and they made for themselves others" [and the language of the Rif here, "from where do we learn one who buys tzitzit from a gentile," etc. — I do not understand; and I wonder at the commentators who did not note this; and perhaps the Rif's view is that these two versions do not disagree; in any case all decisors hold that in every manner it is invalid through a gentile] — and we may say in this that the Rosh's view is that this second Jew did not intend in his tying for the sake of tzitzit, but merely to fulfill the request of the first who asked him to insert these threads into it [{Rama: and if so, if he asked him explicitly to insert these threads into it for the sake of tzitzit, it is valid according to all — and as above in siman 11 seif 1, "spin tzitzit for my tallit"; and at first glance even if he said to him when unstated, "insert these tzitzit for my tallit," it is valid, as there, "spin tzitzit for my tallit"; and with difficulty one may distinguish between them — end Rama gloss}] But if he himself tied the tzitzit threads on his garment when unstated, it is possible that even per the Rosh here when unstated it refers to l'shemah — for why did he tie these threads on his garment if not for tzitzit? And especially in these tallitot of ours that at the time of purchase are designated only to fulfill the mitzvah of tzitzit — certainly afterward, when he tied the tzitzit threads into them, he tied them for tzitzit; and similar to what the Magen Avraham brought in siman 589 seif katan 4 in the name of the Radbaz, see there. And all this I wrote only to justify the custom that they were not careful about this; but every God-fearing person should be stringent on himself to be careful to express explicitly with his lips that he is attaching them for the sake of tzitzit.
