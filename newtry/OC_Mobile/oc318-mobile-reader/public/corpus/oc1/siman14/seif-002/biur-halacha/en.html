A Jew attached, etc. Know that Beit Yosef's words demonstrate that according to Rambam it is valid even if the Jew explicitly acted not for the sake of the mitzvah. Conversely, according to Rashi and Rosh, even an unspecified act is invalid. Otherwise there would be no proof that Rosh disagrees with Rambam, since strings made from wool torn from thorns or from loose tufts were certainly not attached for the sake of the mitzvah. Perishah writes the same. See responsa Rabbi Akiva Eiger, siman 4, which explains why we do not say that an unspecified act is presumed to be for the sake of the mitzvah. I later found that responsa Mishkenos Yaakov, siman 20, maintains that even Rambam is lenient only where no contrary intention was expressed, because the act is presumed to be for the sake of the mitzvah; see there.
Without intention. If he thought in his mind that he was acting for the sake of the mitzvah, it appears that he may recite a blessing. Although regarding spinning we remained uncertain above in siman 11 whether mental intention suffices, here one may be lenient. First, there is a double doubt: perhaps mental intention suffices, as may be inferred from Rosh in Hilchos Sefer Torah, and perhaps the halachah follows Rambam. There is also another reason. Spinning ordinary strings is not presumed to be for the sake of tzitzis, whereas attaching tzitzis strings to a garment is ordinarily presumed to be for the sake of the mitzvah. Combining all these considerations, it appears that he may recite a blessing. Initially, however, it is preferable that he explicitly state his intention aloud, and I ruled accordingly in Mishnah Berurah. It further appears that even if he intended for the sake of the mitzvah only while attaching the first tzitzis and attached the others without explicit intention, he may nevertheless recite a blessing, because one who continues an activity is presumed to act upon his original intention. Although these appear to be separate acts, Zevachim 2b, Tosafos s.v. “But for the sake of,” implies that if one intended properly during shechitah and received the blood without explicit intention, then even according to the view that an unspecified act is not presumed to be for the sake of the mitzvah, we nevertheless say that he continues upon his original intention. I explained the same above in siman 11 regarding spinning and twisting. One cannot object that there the four services form one mitzvah, because here too all four tzitzis form one mitzvah and prevent one another. If he explicitly states before beginning that all the tzitzis he will attach are for the sake of the mitzvah, none of this analysis is necessary, as explained at the beginning of siman 11 in Biur Halachah.
He should not recite a blessing over it. See Mishnah Berurah, where I wrote that this applies even if only the insertion of the strings was done without intention. At first glance one might argue that the requirement applies only if he also made a knot. Peri Megadim cites Levush's explanation of the ruling in siman 11, seif 13, that only after making a knot does the verse's wording, “You shall make fringes,” apply, and upon this the Sages expounded, “you shall make,” and not something made through an invalid state. If so, the same should apply to the expounding of “you shall make for yourself,” meaning for the sake of your obligation, according to Rashi and Rosh. It should apply only after he also made a knot and a winding. Yet Beit Yosef's words, beginning “Rambam wrote,” clearly show that the mere insertion into the corner must also be for the sake of the mitzvah. I later found this demonstrated even more explicitly in Tosafos on Menachos 42b, s.v. “from thorns.” Nevertheless, if he forgot to intend for the sake of the mitzvah while inserting the strings but did intend properly when making the knot, perhaps he may recite a blessing, because his final action demonstrates his original intent. The case of strings taken from thorns is different, because there the later action cannot demonstrate the original intention. Although the principle that the end may reveal the beginning is itself uncertain, as the Gra writes above in siman 11, seif 1, and as Rambam rules in chapter 2 of Hilchos Shechitah and chapter 4 of Hilchos Zechiyah U'Matanah, there is another doubt here: perhaps the halachah follows Rambam, who does not require intention. There is also the possibility that in this context an unspecified act is presumed to be for the sake of the mitzvah, as some authorities rule. See Mishkenos Yaakov, siman 20, and Artzos HaChayim. One may object that some authorities, including Maharshal and Derishah in Yoreh Deah, siman 4, maintain that the final action never reveals the original intent. The matter therefore requires study. Were I not hesitant, I would suggest a new distinction. The essential dispute of Rosh with Rambam concerns a case in which one Jew attached the tzitzis to another Jew's garment at the owner's request. According to Rambam it is valid even there. The Gemara invalidates specifically when a non-Jew attached tzitzis to a Jew's garment, as implied in Menachos 42b from Rav's second formulation, “others shall make for them.” Rif's wording, “From where do we know that one who purchases tzitzis from a non-Jew, etc.,” is difficult, and I am surprised the commentators did not address it. Perhaps Rif understood that the two formulations do not disagree. In any event, all the poskim agree that tzitzis made by a non-Jew are invalid in every case. Rosh's reasoning may be that the second Jew did not attach the strings for the sake of the mitzvah, but merely to fulfill the first Jew's request. If the first Jew explicitly asked him to attach those strings for the sake of tzitzis, it would certainly be valid according to all opinions, just as above in siman 11, seif 1, where one says, “Spin tzitzis for my tallis.” It would apparently also be valid if he simply said, “Attach these tzitzis to my tallis,” though one can distinguish with difficulty. By contrast, if a person himself attaches tzitzis strings to his own garment without saying anything, perhaps even Rosh agrees that the unspecified act is presumed to be for the sake of the mitzvah. Why else would he attach these strings to his garment? This is especially true of our talleisim, which are purchased solely to fulfill the mitzvah of tzitzis. When he later attaches tzitzis strings, he certainly does so for the sake of the mitzvah, comparable to Magen Avraham's ruling in siman 589, seif katan 4, in the name of Radbaz. I write this only to justify the common practice, which is not careful about this. Every God-fearing person should nevertheless be stringent and explicitly state that he is attaching them for the sake of tzitzis.
