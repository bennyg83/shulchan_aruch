It is permitted to take another person's tallis and recite a blessing over it. The gloss explains that this is because a person is pleased for a mitzvah to be performed with his property. It appears to me that using it regularly without permission is forbidden, but occasional use is permitted. The same applies to another person's tefillin, provided that one does not remove them from their place, neither from the owner's home nor from the synagogue, but puts them on and leaves them there. Tumas Yesharim and Yam Shel Shlomo. If they were found folded, he must refold them, because the owner's folding them demonstrates that he is particular about lending them.
But, etc. The general rule is that initially it is forbidden for anyone to use another person's sefer without permission. If, however, the sefer was deposited with a Torah scholar, it is permitted even if the owner objected to his using it. The same applies when it was given as collateral to a Torah scholar. Even if the borrower later objected that the scholar should not learn from his sefarim, his objection is disregarded, since he said nothing at the time of the loan and the Torah scholar acquired the right to use them as a borrower. He must, however, pay for any damage he causes. This appears correct, unlike Sema in Choshen Mishpat, siman 73, who distinguishes between a case where the lender stipulated at the outset that the scholar could learn from the sefarim, in which case the condition is effective even without deducting from the debt, and a case where no such stipulation was made, in which case learning from them is forbidden. It is obvious that if the sefarim are accidentally destroyed while he is using them, he is liable even for unavoidable loss.
