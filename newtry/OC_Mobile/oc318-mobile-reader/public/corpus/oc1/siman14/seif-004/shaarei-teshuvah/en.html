Concerning Rambam. See Be'er Heitev. Regarding the statement that Turei Zahav “made himself,” etc., Birkei Yosef writes likewise in the name of Yad Aharon. He adds that one who examines Turei Zahav will see a slight departure from Beit Yosef, although both ultimately lead to the same conclusion; see there. Authorities challenged what is written in the name of Magen Avraham, that the case concerns Shabbat, because Shulchan Aruch says, “if he has no other tzitzit.” See Yad Ephraim, who writes that the text should be emended to “shortly before Shabbat”; I too have seen it emended this way. The meaning is that with other strings one can make only one knot and one set of windings, but to untie these strings (which requires deliberation) and then retie at least one knot and one set of windings, there is insufficient time. See Machazik Berachah, who writes in the name of several sages that even on a weekday such a case can occur when the time for prayer is about to pass. Regarding what one sage wrote; that there is concern the strings may be ruined, leaving him with no mitzvah at all, and therefore it is preferable to rely on Rambam; this is incorrect. On a weekday, when the work can be done properly, there is no concern that they will be ruined; it is preferable to perform the mitzvah according to all opinions. See there. According to his words, one may say that shortly before Shabbat, even if enough time technically remains to untie and retie them, one should nevertheless refrain if the work cannot be done deliberately, because hurriedly untying them may ruin them. See Machazik Berachah further, who suggests that the discussion may concern thin, worn tzitzit that are liable to be ruined if they are untied; see there.
