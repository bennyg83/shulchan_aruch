(א) “His friend’s tallis.” The Bach wrote: only occasionally; if it is a fixed practice it is forbidden. Yesh Shalom wrote it is forbidden to take them from his house to synagogue or the reverse.

(ב) “And to bless.” This needs study: granted, taking it is permitted because a person is glad to perform a mitzvah with his money—but why should he bless? Granted, when he borrowed with permission it is as if it was given to him as a gift on condition to return, as the Rosh wrote; the same applies to a lulav: if one lent it to him to fulfill with, he fulfills his obligation, as Even HaEzer siman 28, seif 9, and Choshen Mishpat siman 195, seif 4, say. But to take without his knowledge is forbidden, as written there, and likewise below siman 589, seif 5, in the Hagahah—so why is it permitted here? We must say as I wrote: even though he is exempt from tzitzis he may bless, like women.

(ג) “That he fold it.” Even not in the order of its first folding; on Shabbos, even if he does not fold it at all it is permitted, as I wrote—see Orach Chayim siman 302 on human dignity.

(ד) “That we are concerned.” Lest he read in them so much that they tear from heavy use—end Nachalas Yisrael. From this it implies that even occasionally it is forbidden to learn from them; even though in siman 307, seif 3, we say a person is glad to perform a mitzvah with his money even when short of funds—that case is different because it is a small matter—and Choshen Mishpat siman 292, seif 20, in the Hagahah, and the Sema there: even if one deposited books with a Torah scholar it is forbidden to learn from them if he knows the depositor would mind; the Shach there disagrees and wrote it is permitted, and his words fit the explanation of the Mordechai. But from what Beis Yosef wrote here in the name of the poskim it implies this is borrowing without permission—see Choshen Mishpat siman 72, seif 1, siman 163, seif 6, and Yoreh Deah siman 172, seif 1.
