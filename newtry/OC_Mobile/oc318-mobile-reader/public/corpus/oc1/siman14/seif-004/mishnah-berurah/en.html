(13) Another person's tallis. Meaning, without the owner's knowledge. A person is pleased for a mitzvah to be performed with his property. This applies only occasionally, not regularly. Even occasional use is permitted only in the place where the tallis was left. It is forbidden to remove it from the owner's home to the synagogue, or the reverse, because the owner may object and it would then constitute theft. Peri Megadim writes that whenever the owner is present, one should ask permission rather than rely upon the presumption that he consents, since the matter can easily be clarified. This is certainly true if one knows that the owner objects.
(14) And recite a blessing over it. See Magen Avraham. Derech HaChayim writes that it is preferable to intend not to acquire it and not to recite a blessing.
(15) That he should fold it. He should fold it as it was originally folded. On Shabbos he should fold it differently from its original folds. Magen Avraham is lenient on Shabbos and permits leaving it unfolded.
(16) Without his knowledge. This is forbidden even for occasional use, because we are concerned that he may read them extensively until they tear from excessive handling. The common practice is that when people find a siddur or machzor in the synagogue they take it in order to pray from it. I do not know a basis for this permission, for why should a siddur differ from other sefarim? Peri Megadim.
