(13) His friend's tallit — meaning, without his knowledge; for a person is pleased that a mitzvah be done with his property specifically on a temporary basis, but on a permanent basis it is forbidden. And even temporarily is only in that place; but to remove it from his house to the synagogue, or the reverse, is forbidden, for perhaps he is particular, and it is theft. See in Peri Megadim who wrote that in any case one should be careful when the owner is with him to ask him, and one does not rely on presumption where one can clarify easily; and all the more so if he knows he is particular.

(14) And to bless on it — see in Magen Avraham, and see in Derech Chaim who wrote that it is better to intend not to acquire and not to bless.

(15) That he fold it — in its original fold. On Shabbat he folds it, but not in its original fold. And Magen Avraham is lenient on Shabbat not to fold at all.

(16) Without his knowledge — even on a temporary basis alone, for we are concerned that perhaps he will read in them a great deal until they tear from much handling. And people are accustomed, when they find a siddur or machzor in the synagogue, to take it in order to pray from it; and I do not know permission for this, for how is a siddur different from books? [Peri Megadim]
