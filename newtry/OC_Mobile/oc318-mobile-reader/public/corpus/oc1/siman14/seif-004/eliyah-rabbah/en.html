[4] Regarding Rambam, etc. Beit Yosef understood Rambam to require spinning and twisting for the sake of the mitzvah, and therefore strained greatly to reconcile his words. In my humble opinion Rambam's words are straightforward. He takes Rav's view that spinning need not be for the sake of the mitzvah, because the halachah follows Rav in prohibitory matters, as Mordechai writes and Maadanei Melech cites on page 108. Rambam nevertheless requires initially that the spinning be for the sake of the mitzvah, but validates it after the fact. This resolves everything, and I have been brief. I found support in Sefer HaChinuch, by Raah, in parashas Shelach: “Tzitzis spun by non-Jews are invalid, as it says, ‘Speak to the children of Israel, and they shall make.’ But if a Jew made them without intention, they are valid after the fact.” This implies that even spinning by a Jew without intention is valid after the fact. Although in practical halachah one should not be lenient regarding spinning, one may be lenient regarding attaching the tzitzis, in accordance with Shulchan Aruch and Levush, since Raah rules like Rambam. This rejects Bach, who dismisses Rambam entirely from practical halachah and disputes Shulchan Aruch. Magen Avraham asks that the strings could be removed and reattached with proper intention. One may answer that the case concerns Shabbos. Alternatively, one end of a string may have been severed in a manner that leaves it valid, but if removed it would be forbidden to reattach, as stated above in siman 12. Magen Avraham writes the same.
