[4] "On the Rambam," etc. The Beit Yosef understood in the Rambam that spinning and twisting require l'shemah, and therefore he strained greatly to reconcile his words; and in my humble opinion the Rambam's words according to their plain sense are that he was concerned for Rav's words that we do not require spinning l'shemah, because the halachah follows Rav in prohibitions, as the Mordechai wrote — they brought it from Maadaney Melech 108 — except that lechatchila the Rambam requires spinning l'shemah, but b'dieved it is valid. And with this everything is reconciled; I shortened. And proof for my words I found in Sefer HaChinuch for the Rosh in parashat Shelach — this is his language: tzitzit that gentiles spun is invalid, as it says "Speak to the children of Israel and make"; but if a Jew made without intent, b'dieved it is valid — end quote. It implies that with a Jew, even if he spun not l'shemah, it is valid b'dieved; and although for practical law it does not appear to be lenient in spinning, nevertheless in making tzitzit it appears to be lenient as Shulchan Aruch and Levush ruled, since the Rosh ruled like the Rambam, unlike the Bach who rejected the Rambam's words entirely from halachah and attacked the Shulchan Aruch. Magen Avraham asked: but is it not possible to permit them and make them with intent? And one may say it speaks of Shabbos; and one may further say it speaks of when one of the threads was severed in a matter that is valid, and if he permits them it will be forbidden to make them, as above siman 12; and so wrote Magen Avraham.
