Another person's tallis. This is permitted occasionally, but regular use is forbidden. Bach. Maharshal writes that it is forbidden to take it from the synagogue to one's home, or from the home to the synagogue.
That he should fold it. He may fold it even differently from its original folds. On Shabbos it is permitted even if he does not fold it at all. Magen Avraham. See Olas Tamid and Tumas Yesharim, siman 46.
Through his study. It is forbidden to learn from the sefarim even occasionally. See Choshen Mishpat 292:20 in the gloss, where it is explained that if sefarim were deposited with a Torah scholar, he may study and copy from them if he does not own comparable sefarim. Taz writes that the same applies to sefarim given as collateral to a Torah scholar. Sema writes there that if the depositor objects, it is forbidden. Shach disagrees and permits it; see there. Magen Avraham writes that Shach's interpretation of Mordechai appears correct, but the authorities cited here by Beit Yosef imply that one who uses them is a borrower without permission.
