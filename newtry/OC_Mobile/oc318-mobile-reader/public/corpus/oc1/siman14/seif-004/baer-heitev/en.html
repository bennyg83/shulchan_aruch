A friend's tallit: incidentally it is permitted, but on a fixed basis it is forbidden for anyone. And Maharshal wrote that it is forbidden to take it from the synagogue to his house or the reverse. Magen Avraham.

That he fold: even not in the order of its first folding. And on Shabbat, even if one does not fold it at all it is permitted. Magen Avraham—see Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham. And in Tomer Devorah siman 46.

In his study: And even incidentally it is forbidden to study from them. See Choshen Mishpat siman 292, end, in the gloss, where it is explained that if one deposited books with a Torah scholar it is permitted to study from them and copy if he has none like them. The Taz wrote, and the same applies to a pledge with a Torah scholar; and the Sema wrote there that if the depositor objects to this it is forbidden, but the Shach there disagrees with him and wrote that it is permitted—see there. And Magen Avraham wrote that the words of the Shach fit the explanation of the Mordekhai's words, but from what the Beit Yosef wrote here in the name of the poskim it implies it is a borrower without consent—see there.
