(9) Exempt. One who nevertheless attaches tzitzis and recites a blessing has not lost, just as women may recite blessings over time-bound positive commandments although they are exempt. Consequently, he may also go out wearing it into a public domain on Shabbos, because the tzitzis adorn the garment.
(10) D'Rabbanan. He recites a blessing over it. A renter has the same law as a borrower. Olas Tamid proves this from Tosafos on Menachos 44a, s.v. “A tallis.” Baal HaIttur, however, remains uncertain concerning rental.
(11) He recites a blessing over it. Some are stringent and maintain that he should not recite a blessing unless the lender explicitly gave it as a gift on condition that it be returned. Most later authorities agree with Shulchan Aruch. Since the lender lent it so that the borrower could recite the blessing, and the borrower cannot recite the blessing unless it belongs to him, it is treated as though given as a gift on condition that it be returned. Initially it is certainly preferable to ask the lender to give it explicitly as a gift on condition that it be returned, but this is not indispensable. This applies when he borrowed a tallis specifically designated for the mitzvah. If it is an ordinary garment worn as clothing, with tzitzis attached only because most of it is open and it has four corners, or if he borrowed a tallis merely to receive an aliyah, lead the prayers, or ascend to duchan, all of which are matters of honor, he does not recite a blessing. Perhaps the owner intended to lend it only as clothing, not transfer ownership. Some disagree and maintain that one recites a blessing in every case. Derech HaChayim therefore writes that in all such cases it is preferable to intend not to acquire it, so that according to all opinions no blessing is required, except when borrowing a tallis during prayer in order to fulfill the mitzvah. This discussion concerns a privately owned tallis. Regarding communal talleisim, such as those commonly found in synagogues, everyone agrees that one recites a blessing even when wearing it only to lead the prayers or receive an aliyah. The community purchased it with the intention that each wearer acquire it, like a communal esrog. See Biur Halachah in the name of Shaarei Ephraim.
(12) Immediately. See siman 658, seif 5, regarding a lulav, and Peri Megadim there. The same principle applies here. He must return the tallis to the giver at the proper time. If the owner already fulfilled the mitzvah that day, he must at least return it before prayer the next morning, so that the owner will have his own tallis with which to fulfill the mitzvah. If he did not return it and the owner has no other tallis, the gift appears to be retroactively void and it is not called “your garment.” Even if the owner has a tallis katan, it is reasonable that he did not give the tallis on that understanding, unlike Nehar Shalom. It is also explained there that after fulfilling the mitzvah, the recipient must give it back to the original owner as a gift, because it became his property. The same is obvious here. It likewise appears obvious that if the tallis or one of its tzitzis was damaged while in the borrower's possession, even through unavoidable circumstances, and he did not return it in the condition in which he received it, he does not fulfill the mitzvah. See siman 658, seif 4, and Rabbi Akiva Eiger there.
