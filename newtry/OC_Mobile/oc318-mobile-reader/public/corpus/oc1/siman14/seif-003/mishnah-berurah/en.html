(א) Exempt — One who attaches tzitzis to it and blesses has not lost out, like women who may bless on a positive time-bound mitzvah though exempt, per Hagahos Maimoniyos; therefore he may go out with it on Shabbos to a public domain, for it beautifies the garment.

(ב) Rabbinically — And one blesses on it. A renter is like a borrower (Olas Tamid proves this from Tosafos Menachos 44a s.v. tallis, etc.); in the Itur I found he wrote that rental is doubtful to us.

(ג) He blesses on it — Some are stringent and hold not to bless unless the owner explicitly gave it as a gift on condition to return; but most later authorities agree with the Shulchan Aruch. The reason: he lent it with the intent that he bless on it, and since he cannot bless unless it is his, it is as if it were given as a gift on condition to return. Lechatchila it is certainly better to ask the lender to give it as a gift on condition to return, but this does not hold one back. Specifically if he borrowed a tallis designated for the mitzvah; but if it is a garment meant for wearing and they only made tzitzis on it because most of it is open and it was not made for the mitzvah of tzitzis, or if he borrowed a tallis to receive an aliyah, pass before the ark, or ascend the duchan—which is only for honor—one does not bless, lest the owner intended only to lend it for wearing and not to transfer ownership. Some disagree and hold that in every case one blesses; therefore Derech Chaim wrote it is better in all these cases to intend not to acquire so he need not bless according to all views, except a tallis borrowed at prayer time to fulfill his obligation. All this is for an individual’s tallis; but for a communal tallis like those in our synagogues, all agree one must bless even when wearing it only to pass before the ark or receive an aliyah, for a communal tallis was bought with intent that whoever wears it it should be his, like an etrog; see what we wrote in Biur Halacha in the name of Shaarei Efraim.

(ד) Immediately — See siman 658, seif 5 regarding a lulav and in Peri Megadim there; from this we learn for our matter too that one must return it to the owner in its time: if today the owner fulfilled his obligation with it, the borrower should return it at latest tomorrow before prayer so the owner too has what to fulfill with. If he did not return it and the owner has no other tallis, it seems the gift was uprooted retroactively and we do not call it “your garment”—even if he has a tallis katan, nevertheless it is reasonable he did not have this in mind when he gave his tallis—unlike Netiv Chaim. It is also explained there that after fulfilling his obligation he must return it to the owner as a gift so it belong to the owner, for it became his; the same is obvious here. It seems obvious that if the tallis or one of its tzitzis was ruined in the borrower’s possession, even through accident, and he does not return it as he took it, he does not fulfill the mitzvah; see siman 658, seif 4, and Chiddushei Rabbi Akiva Eiger there.
