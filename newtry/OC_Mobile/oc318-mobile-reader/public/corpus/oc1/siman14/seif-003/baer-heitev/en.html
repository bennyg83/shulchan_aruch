He recites the blessing over it immediately: for he lent it to him with the understanding that he would bless over it. And the Taz wrote: it seems to me that the obligation to recite a blessing applies specifically to a tallit designated for the mitzvah of tzitzit, like synagogue tallits—for in that case he certainly does not give him ownership except to perform the mitzvah and bless over it. But a four-cornered garment that one wears as ordinary clothing into which one inserts tzitzit because it has four corners—one who borrows such a garment from his friend does not recite a blessing, for we have no certainty that it was lent for the mitzvah—perhaps it was lent merely for clothing—therefore Taz. Maharshal ruled that every God-fearing person should not wear a tallit that is not his own; and if he has no other tallit he should wear it without a blessing, for blessings are not indispensable. He also wrote that even according to those who bless on a borrowed tallit, this refers to the tallit one wears at prayer when putting on tefillin; but one who wears it in order to go up to the Torah or to pass before the ark certainly should not bless over it. And the Rosh in Hilchot Tzitzit holds that one who passes before the ark does bless; and so in Piskei Tosafot on Menachot siman 103, that priests who wrap themselves in a tallit that is not theirs when they ascend the platform do bless; and so wrote Magen Avraham and Eliyahu Zuta. Also in responsa Shaar Efraim siman 19 and responsa Beit Yaakov siman 114 they ruled not like Maharshal—see there—and the Taz agreed with Maharshal and ruled not to bless on a borrowed tallit with tzitzit, and all the more so if he took it without his friend's consent, as in section 4 that one does not bless over it—see there. And the author Yad Aharon refutes the proofs of the Taz—see there. See in Sefer Bnei Chayei. (And see in Eliyahu Rabba, who brought several responsa to reject Maharshal's words and ruled to bless even on a borrowed tallit, even for going up to the Torah.) Question: Is a person obligated to lend his tallit to one who lives in a village so that he will need to borrow a tallit from others in his city? He ruled in responsa Beit Yaakov siman 114 that he is not obligated to lend his tallit to another, and he may bless on a borrowed tallit—see there.
