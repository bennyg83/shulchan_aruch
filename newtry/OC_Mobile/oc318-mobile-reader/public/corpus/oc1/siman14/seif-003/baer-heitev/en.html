Taz writes that the obligation to recite a blessing applies specifically to a tallis designated for the mitzvah of tzitzis, such as the talleisim of a synagogue. In such a case the lender certainly transfers it only so that the borrower may perform the mitzvah and recite the blessing. If, however, it is an ordinary four-cornered garment worn as clothing, to which tzitzis were attached only because it has four corners, a borrower does not recite a blessing. There is no certainty that it was lent for the sake of the mitzvah, for perhaps it was lent merely as clothing. Maharshal rules that every God-fearing person should avoid wearing a tallis that is not his own. If he has no other tallis, he should wear it without a blessing, since blessings do not prevent fulfillment. He further writes that even according to those who recite a blessing over a borrowed tallis, this applies to the tallis worn during prayer together with tefillin. One who puts it on merely to receive an aliyah or to lead the prayers certainly does not recite a blessing. Rosh in Hilchos Tzitzis, however, implies that one who leads the prayers does recite a blessing. Piskei Tosafos on Menachos, siman 103, likewise states that kohanim who wrap themselves in talleisim that are not theirs when ascending to duchan recite a blessing. Magen Avraham and Eliyah Zuta rule the same way. Responsa Shaar Ephraim, siman 19, and responsa Beis Yaakov, siman 114, likewise rule against Maharshal. Taz agrees with Maharshal and rules that one should not recite a blessing over a borrowed tallis with tzitzis, and certainly not over one taken without the owner's permission, as discussed in seif 4. Yad Aharon rejects Taz's proofs; see there. See Bnei Chayei. Eliyah Rabbah cites several responsa rejecting Maharshal and rules that one recites a blessing even over a borrowed tallis used merely to receive an aliyah. Question: Must a person lend his tallis to someone living in a village if doing so will require him to borrow a tallis from others in his city? Responsa Beis Yaakov, siman 114, rules that he is not obligated to lend his tallis and thereby place himself in the position of reciting a blessing over a borrowed tallis.
