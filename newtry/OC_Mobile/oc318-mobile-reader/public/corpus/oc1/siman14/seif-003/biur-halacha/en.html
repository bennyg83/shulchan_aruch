"A loan when it already has tzitzit," etc. — See in Mishna Berurah at the end of seif katan 11 in what he wrote regarding the congregation's tallit, that they acquired it with this intent, etc. — so too wrote in Darkei Chayim; and I wonder why this is needed — did not Eliyah Rabbah write that it is like a partners' tallit, which was explained in seif 5 that it is obligated; and so too is found in the Mordechai in the laws of tzitzit in section 590. And more than this, that the Mordechai wrote in explanation that the Rashbam in Bava Batra 137b is not needed — and his intent is regarding an etrog acquired with this intent, etc., that in this case too a partners' tallit is obligated. And perhaps the reason of Darkei Chayim is that sometimes the city is very large and a perutah's worth does not reach each one, similar to what Rashi explained in Sukkah 27b s.v. "all the citizen," see there; and in Shaarei Efraim he wrote that it is better to take a borrowed tallit at the time of his aliyah to the Torah than to take the congregation's tallit — for with the congregation's tallit one enters into doubt whether he is obligated to bless; and if he took the congregation's tallit and wishes to bless, he should intend then that he wishes to wear it as a garment of tzitzit and wrap his head in it as in the morning, and he need not then stand wrapped for the measure of four amos, etc.; and if he does not wish to bless on it, he should not wrap his head at all, only wrapping the body alone. And he gave the reason for his words in his Shaarei Teshuvah, and these are his words: on the congregation's tallit one blesses as on a partners' tallit; nevertheless it appears specifically when he wraps his head in the manner of morning wrapping — for otherwise one does not bless, although there is the view of some decisors who disagree and hold that wrapping the body suffices; nevertheless his intent is not to wrap at all, only to wear it temporarily for mere honor — we do not call "with which you cover yourself" such a temporary wrapping; and even on his own tallit, if his time is pressing so that he cannot actually wrap in it and places it on the shoulders alone for a short time at the time of reading and intends to remove it immediately — it requires further study in such a case, end quote. And his words are weak in part, for did not the Shulchan Aruch agree above in siman 8 that wrapping is not indispensable? And also his second reason from Maharshal, Magen Avraham, Eliyah Rabbah, and other later authorities implies that on his own garment he is certainly obligated to bless and they did not distinguish at all whether he wraps his head or not — it is implied from their words that in every manner he is obligated through this wearing of tzitzit. Nevertheless, what Shaarei Efraim wrote above, that he should intend then in his wearing for a garment of tzitzit and not for mere honor — his words are very correct according to law, for we rule in siman 60 seif 4 that mitzvot require intent, and it is indispensable, as explained there and as written in siman 8 in Mishna Berurah seif katan 19, see there. Afterward I found support for Shaarei Efraim — namely what was written in Beit HaTefillah in siman 18 seif katan 4 in the name of the Levushei Serad, that one who wraps because of honor of the congregation does not bless; and presumably his reason is as Shaarei Efraim wrote, that perhaps this is not called the wearing that obligates tzitzit; and see Magen Avraham at the end of siman 19 in what he wrote in the name of Tosafos — it requires further study.
