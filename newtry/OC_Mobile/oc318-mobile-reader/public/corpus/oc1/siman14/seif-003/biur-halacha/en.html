A borrowed garment when it is already fringed, etc. See Mishnah Berurah, at the end of seif katan 11, where I wrote regarding a communal tallis that it was purchased with the intention that each person who wears it should acquire it. Derech HaChayim writes this, but I am surprised why the reason is needed. Eliyah Rabbah writes that a communal tallis is like a jointly owned tallis, which seif 5 rules is obligated. Mordechai likewise writes this in Hilchos Tzitzis, siman 950, and explicitly states that one need not rely upon Rashbam's reasoning in Bava Basra 137b concerning an esrog, namely that it was acquired with this intention. A jointly owned tallis is independently obligated. Perhaps Derech HaChayim's reason is necessary where the city is so large that each person's share is worth less than a perutah, comparable to Rashi's explanation on Sukkah 27b, s.v. “all the citizens”; see there. Shaarei Ephraim writes that it is preferable when receiving an aliyah to borrow a private tallis rather than use the communal tallis, because a communal tallis creates a doubt whether a blessing is required. If one takes the communal tallis and wishes to recite a blessing, he should intend to wear it as a tzitzis garment and wrap his head in it as he does during Shacharis. He need not remain wrapped for the time needed to walk four amos. If he does not wish to recite a blessing, he should not cover his head at all, but only his body. In Pis'chei She'arim he explains that one should recite a blessing over a communal tallis as over a jointly owned tallis, but apparently only if he covers his head as in the morning. Otherwise, even according to the minority of authorities who maintain that covering the body alone suffices, his intention is not to wrap himself at all, but merely to wear it briefly for honor. Such a temporary wrapping is not called “with which you cover yourself.” Even regarding his own tallis, if he is hurried and cannot wrap himself properly, but merely places it upon his shoulders briefly during the reading and intends to remove it immediately, the law requires study. His words are somewhat weak, because Shulchan Aruch above in siman 8 rules that wrapping is not indispensable. Maharshal, Magen Avraham, Eliyah Rabbah, and the other later authorities imply that one certainly recites a blessing over his own garment and do not distinguish whether he covers his head. Nevertheless, Shaarei Ephraim's instruction that he intend to wear it for the sake of the mitzvah rather than merely for honor is entirely correct as a matter of law, because siman 60, seif 4, rules that mitzvos require intention, and that intention is indispensable, as explained there and in Mishnah Berurah to siman 8, seif katan 19. I later found support for Shaarei Ephraim in Baer Heitev, siman 18, seif katan 4, in the name of Lechem Chamudos, who writes that one who wraps himself merely because of the honor of the congregation does not recite a blessing. Presumably his reason is that this may not be considered a form of wearing that obligates tzitzis. See Magen Avraham at the end of siman 19, citing Tosafos. The matter requires study.
