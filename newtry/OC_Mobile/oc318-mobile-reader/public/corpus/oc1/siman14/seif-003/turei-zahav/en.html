A borrowed garment when it is already fringed, etc. This is Rosh's view. The rule that a borrowed tallis is exempt means that the borrower need not attach tzitzis. If he borrows a tallis that already has tzitzis, the lender lent it so that he could recite the blessing. Since he cannot recite the blessing unless it belongs to him, we regard the lender as having given it as a gift on condition that it be returned. This resembles one who borrows a garment, tells the lender that he is going to visit a sick person, and then the sick person dies. The borrower may tear the garment for mourning. This is also the reason for seif 4, which permits reciting a blessing over a folded tallis found without its owner. We presume the owner consents and treat it as though transferred as a gift on condition that it be returned. Rosh further writes that those who rise early for Selichos and wrap themselves in borrowed talleisim intending thereby to avoid a blessing are mistaken, for they are obligated to recite the blessing. It appears to me that Rosh's obligation to recite the blessing applies specifically to a tallis designated for the mitzvah of tzitzis, such as synagogue talleisim or a garment called arba kanfos. In such a case, the lender certainly gave it only so that the borrower could perform the mitzvah and recite the blessing. An ordinary four-cornered garment worn as clothing, to which tzitzis were attached merely because it has four corners, is different. A borrower does not recite a blessing, because there is no proof that it was lent for the mitzvah; perhaps it was lent merely as clothing. This resembles an ordinary borrowed garment, which the borrower is not obligated to fringe. Maharshal writes in Yam Shel Shlomo, chapter Kol HaBasar, siman 53, that Rosh's proof from the borrowed garment torn for mourning is not comparable. There, the borrower cannot go naked, and therefore the lender necessarily transfers sufficient ownership. The same applies to an esrog, because the mitzvah cannot be fulfilled otherwise. A tallis, however, need not be worn at all. Maharshal concludes that every God-fearing person should avoid wearing a tallis that is not his. If no other tallis is available, he should wear it without a blessing, since blessings do not prevent fulfillment. He adds that even according to those who recite a blessing over a borrowed tallis, this refers to the tallis worn during prayer together with tefillin, which serves as a reminder like tefillin. One who wears it only to receive an aliyah or lead the prayers should certainly not recite a blessing. Rosh's proof from the torn garment is also difficult in my eyes. There the borrower tears it lawfully because he borrowed it for that purpose, and every person may use a borrowed item for the purpose for which it was borrowed. Tzitzis is different. Even if it was borrowed for the purpose of reciting the blessing, one cannot recite a blessing over a borrowed tallis unless it was actually given as a gift. The torn garment provides no proof that the lender intended a gift. See what I write in the following seif.
