A jointly owned tallis is obligated, etc. See Mishnah Berurah, where I wrote that the other partner must consent to his wearing it. This is from Peri Megadim. Although the basic ruling appears to require considerable reflection, for how is this worse than an ordinary jointly owned tallis? There too, half the garment does not belong to him intrinsically, but is merely lent to him, and the Torah exempts a borrowed garment. Thus only half the garment and two tzitzis are intrinsically his, yet the Torah revealed that it is sufficient for him to own a share of the garment. The same should apparently apply here. Nevertheless, Peri Megadim's ruling is correct for another reason. We rule that even where the Torah does not require ownership, as with tefillin, a stolen item is invalid because it is a mitzvah performed through a transgression, as Magen Avraham writes below in siman 25, seif katan 26. Here too, since his wearing involves theft, the mitzvah of tzitzis is performed through a transgression and he does not fulfill it.
