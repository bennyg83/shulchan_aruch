Permitted. Taz expanded. And behold, at first he asked what was written in siman 649 seif 5 in the gloss that on the first day it is forbidden because it is words of Torah and perhaps he is not pleased with a gift on condition to return; and what was written here that there is a Torah concern lest he did not give it as a gift on condition to return — how can he take his friend's tallit without his knowledge and wear it? And he answered, etc., and I did not merit to understand his holy words here — what Torah prohibition concern is there here in borrowing with its tzitzis? And in that borrowing without its tzitzis is exempt — one should not ask; behold it is a Torah doubt lest he borrowed it as a gift on condition to return, and how does he go without tzitzis? And a person is pleased for the permanence of the mitzvah — this is not so, for when there are no tzitzis on it it is not pleased, etc.; and also presumably he did not borrow in order to affix tzitzis on it, for a person is pleased for the permanence of the mitzvah himself and to affix tzitzis. And behold, in a tallit as a gift on condition to return, and the recipient is pleased with this — if one of its tzitzis was severed he is obligated to affix tzitzis on it, for it is now his, and he transgresses the condition of the borrower; requires study. Afterward he asked what was written here that Mevasser and Yerechah were stringent in siman 8 seif 9 regarding a blessing — we do not establish presumption, and for a deed we establish presumption; if so, on the first day why not with lulav? And he answered that on the first day one is very particular because it is missing and pierced — all the more so they disqualify it — and it is similar to books more than on the second day, when one is not particular. But when he borrowed from him — why invalid on the first day? Let us say that certainly he lent it as a gift on condition to return. But Magen Avraham in siman 649 letter 9 in truth wrote that if he borrowed from him so that he know he wishes to bless and fulfill — it is well; and there we deal with where he explicitly does not want a gift on condition to return — then on the first day he does not fulfill, and on the other days he fulfills; and in the end Taz brought that one should not take his friend's tallit and bless on it unless he explicitly stated a gift on condition to return — then he blesses well.
