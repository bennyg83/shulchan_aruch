It is permitted. See Taz, who discusses this at length. He first asks why Rama rules in siman 649, seif 5, that on the first day one may not take another person's lulav without permission because the mitzvah is D'Oraisa and perhaps the owner does not wish to give it as a gift on condition that it be returned. Why is tzitzis different? Tzitzis is likewise D'Oraisa, yet one may rely upon the owner's presumed consent and recite a blessing. Taz answers that there is no Torah prohibition in wearing a garment without tzitzis. One is merely obligated to attach tzitzis to his own garment. Here the garment is not his, so that obligation does not apply. He then asks that siman 8, seif 9, is more stringent regarding the blessing over tzitzis than regarding wearing, requiring inspection because of the blessing. If the presumption that the owner consents is strong enough for a blessing over tzitzis, it should all the more suffice to fulfill the first-day lulav obligation by assuming the owner gives it as a gift on condition that it be returned. He suggests that on the first day the owner is more particular that the esrog not be damaged, because even a slight deficiency or puncture may invalidate it. Therefore, the presumption of consent is weaker. But he then asks why an explicitly borrowed lulav is invalid on the first day. Since the lender knows the borrower can fulfill the obligation only if it is given as a gift on condition that it be returned, we should presume that was his intent. Magen Avraham in siman 649, seif katan 9, indeed writes that if the owner lends it knowing that the borrower wishes to recite the blessing and fulfill the obligation, it is valid. The case there concerns one who explicitly says that he does not wish to give it as a gift on condition that it be returned. On the first day the borrower then does not fulfill the obligation, while on the later days he does. Taz ultimately concludes that one should not take another person's tallis and recite a blessing unless the owner explicitly gives it as a gift on condition that it be returned. See Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 6.
