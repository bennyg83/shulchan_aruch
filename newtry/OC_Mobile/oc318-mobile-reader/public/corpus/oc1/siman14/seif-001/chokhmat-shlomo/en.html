Seif 1: A woman is fit to make them, and some are stringent, etc. NB: Were I not afraid, it would appear that they do not disagree — for certainly by themselves a woman is unfit to make them so that a man fulfill through them; but as an agent, if a male appointed her as agent to make tzitzit for him — then with a gentile she is unfit, for they are not fit for agency; and a woman is fit, for although we rule that regarding something that has no "for its sake" within it one cannot appoint an agent, nevertheless here, since a woman can fulfill a positive commandment that is time-bound, and all the more so according to those who hold she may even bless — therefore she is considered to have it within herself, as stated in the first chapter of Kiddushin [23b] regarding a slave. If so, the same here: since they share in the mitzvah — if she wishes to fulfill it herself and then make for herself, she can also be agent for a male; although for the male, a woman's making not as agency does not help, nevertheless as agency it helps. Therefore what the Mechaber wrote, that a woman may make them, means as agency for her husband; and what the Rama wrote, "and some are stringent," means not as agency — in this it does not help for men to fulfill their obligation. And with this the difficulty of the Magen Avraham is resolved, what he asked: "the children of Israel" is written, not "the daughters of Israel" — there the intent is that not as agency her making does not help, but as agency for the male it helps. And much could be said on this, but time did not allow; perhaps God will grant me at another time to explain more — examine well. And see in Peri Megadim, who was uncertain whether it is permitted to make tzitzit at night; and in my novellae in the responsum to the community of Chernovitz I answered that it seems to me permitted to make them at night. Proof from chapter 5 of Chullin: R' Eliezer ben Yaakov asked — it is written, etc., "and it shall be seven days," etc. — only at night for appeasement and also for offering; if so, it is proven that although at night it is not fit for offering, nevertheless it is permitted to dedicate it; all the more so with tzitzit, that although their time is not at night, nevertheless it is permitted to make them at night. And see Taz in Yoreh De'ah siman 3, that for leniency we derive Chullin from kodashim by kal vachomer — so it seems clear to me, examine well. Now, on this law whether a woman is fit to make them, the decisors disagree — see in Beit Yosef. And the Rosh brought proof from the sukkah of the thief and robber that it is valid although they are not obligated; and in my view it appears conversely — from there it would be proven the opposite, for the Gemara says there that that tanna of thief and robber prefers it because it is fixed, and teaches "nevertheless" to include the sukkah of the shepherdess that is not fixed; and that tanna prefers the shepherdess because they are obligated, and teaches "nevertheless" to include thief and robber who are not obligated. The difficulty remains: why do I need "nevertheless" to include thief and robber? There is a kal vachomer — if the shepherdess, which is not fixed, is valid, all the more so thief and robber, which are fixed, are valid. And one cannot say they are not obligated, for there is a kal vachomer from tzitzit and the like, where we require making l'shemah and nevertheless they are valid through a woman — all the more so sukkah, where we do not require l'shemah, for the shepherdess is valid, all the more so valid through a woman; and from "they are not obligated" is a kal vachomer from tzitzit that they are valid — why do I still need to include thief and robber? Rather, certainly in tzitzit a woman is unfit because we require l'shemah; therefore it compares well to tefillin, for we say: whoever is not in the making is not in the tying — so too in tzitzit, since we require the act be for the sake of the mitzvah, and if he did not make them for the mitzvah, even one obligated is invalid; if so, since we require thought for the mitzvah, we require also one obligated in this mitzvah. But if he is not obligated, his thought is not potent thought, since we require making l'shemah — we require one obligated like this mitzvah. But like sukkah, where we do not require l'shemah — and if she did not make it for the sake of the festival it is valid — therefore we do not require also one obligated in this mitzvah; therefore although they are not obligated, the sukkah is valid. And I wonder at the Rosh on this proof — for how is sukkah comparable to tzitzit? And conversely, it appears proven from there to the opposite. And the Magen Avraham wrote in seif katan 3 that a woman is unfit for sukkah and lulav; and his intent appears for lechatchila, for b'dieved it is explicit in the Gemara that the sukkah of the shepherdess and thief and robber is valid — only his intent is lechatchila; and so it is implied in Peri Megadim; and so it is explicit in Beit Yosef that lechatchila a woman should not make a sukkah. And in my humble opinion it appears to bring proof that even lechatchila a woman may place sechach, for the difficulty remains on that Gemara — how do the two baraitot divide with opposite reasoning, that for this one thief and robber is preferable and he holds fixed is preferable to one not obligated, and this one holds one obligated is preferable to not fixed — these are opposite reasonings; and many times Tosafos asked how they divide with opposite reasonings — the difficulty applies here too. Therefore it appears there is a distinction between lechatchila and b'dieved — namely, although these sukkot were already made, nevertheless you find lechatchila and b'dieved: if he already sat in it on the first night, whether he must return and eat in the sukkah; and between lechatchila, whether he may initially enter and eat in it. And at first glance, what he wrote that the sukkah of thief and robber is preferable because it is fixed has no understanding — for conversely in sukkah, permanent dwelling is inferior and the essence requires temporary dwelling; if so, temporary should be a greater virtue than sukkah — why here does he compare a greater virtue in what is fixed? And what of it that this was not fixed — behold temporary dwelling is valid. It is strained to say it follows R' Yehudah, who holds a sukkah requires permanent dwelling; if so, our difficulty above would be resolved well — these are not opposite reasonings, for this baraita of thief and robber follows R' Yehudah, who holds permanent dwelling is required, and therefore the virtue of fixed is preferable to the virtue of those obligated; but the other follows the Rabbis, who require temporary dwelling, and for them the virtue of those obligated is preferable to fixed — therefore he listed the shepherdess first. However, since the Gemara does not explain that one follows R' Yehudah and one the Rabbis, it is implied that both follow all — if so, what is the virtue of fixed? But one must say: even though the Rabbis hold temporary dwelling is required, this is only if he makes it for the sake of the mitzvah; here there would be room to say that the body of the building, temporary, his thought makes it permanent since he makes it for the mitzvah — the mitzvah fixes it and it is already considered — if so, it works out: if he makes it for the mitzvah, it is considered permanent dwelling from the side of the mitzvah, since it was made for sukkah; through the mitzvah it becomes permanent. But in the sukkah of thief and robber and the shepherdess, which were not made for the mitzvah — here, since the mitzvah did not fix them to be permanent, what is fixed is a greater virtue; therefore thief and robber, which are fixed, is stronger to him. If so, according to this it works out well that in truth according to some decisors we rule mitzvot do not require intent; and even for those who require intent, there are some views that where there is an act, all agree intent is not required; if so, in sukkah too intent for the mitzvah is not required — even if he sat one night in the sukkah and ate in it not for the mitzvah, but when unstated he also fulfilled. If so, according to this one may distinguish between lechatchila and b'dieved: where he comes to sit lechatchila in the sukkah of thief and robber or the shepherdess — here the virtue of what is done by those obligated is greater than the virtue of fixed; for the virtue of fixed, since he wishes to sit in it lechatchila for the mitzvah, therefore in his thought, since he intends to sit for the mitzvah, he has already made it; and since there is thought for the mitzvah and it was made in his thought already, the virtue of fixed is not a virtue — for sukkah requires temporary dwelling; therefore the virtue is greater than when done by those obligated. But when not by those obligated, in tzitzit it is forbidden lechatchila — therefore it teaches that the sukkah of the field is valid even lechatchila; and as stated, where we do not require l'shemah we are not particular about those obligated. But the other baraita speaks of b'dieved — where he already sat in it and did not intend for the mitzvah, whether he fulfilled the obligation of the first night; then it is the reverse: regarding those obligated there is no novelty, for even in tzitzit, where l'shemah is required, b'dieved it is valid according to decisors who hold thus, and brought in Beit Yosef — all the more so in sukkah it is valid b'dieved; and regarding fixed, properly it is a novelty, since he did not intend for the mitzvah — if so, the mitzvah did not fix it and in itself it is not fixed; therefore it would be thought it is not a sukkah at all — therefore it teaches that nevertheless he fulfilled. Therefore the baraita that listed thief and robber first speaks of b'dieved, that he sat in it without intent — therefore fixed is stronger to him; and the other baraita that listed the shepherdess first speaks of lechatchila — therefore those obligated are stronger. And therefore what emerges from this explanation is clear from the Gemara that in tzitzit lechatchila they are invalid through a woman and b'dieved valid, and in sukkah even lechatchila valid. However, what is my strength that I should decide against the fathers of the world — therefore in sukkah too, where possible easily, one should strive not to make them through thief and robber; but where it is not easily possible, it is permitted to make them through them — so it seems to me; examine all this well. (There) Seif 4: permitted to take a fellow's tallit and bless on it, etc. NB: See in Magen Avraham seif katan 8 what he concluded in his reason that although exempt, nevertheless he may bless as women bless; and I saw in the siddur Derech HaChayim of the Gaon of Lissa, z"l, who disagrees, for he wrote that Tosafos and the Rosh above speak of one who borrowed a tallit and tied his own tzitzit in it; but if he borrowed a tallit with tzitzit, it is like borrowed threads — certainly he does not bless, as a borrowed etrog — certainly he does not bless, see there. And in my humble opinion the law is with the Magen Avraham — his case is not comparable, for certainly if he borrowed an etrog he does not bless, since the Torah obligated him to take his own etrog and not borrowed; if so, since he is obligated to take his own and does not fulfill with borrowed, and even if he takes borrowed he must afterward take his own again and bless — therefore it is a blessing in vain. And it is possible that if he knows he will not have his own etrog today, he may in truth bless on borrowed — it is no worse than day one than day two, as the Chayei Adam wrote regarding other invalid items valid on the second day, that if he has no other he blesses on them even on the first day; and the same with borrowed. But if he can obtain his own afterward, since he is obligated to make tzitzit from his own and wherever he is obligated in his own he cannot bless on borrowed — but if he has no tallit and no tzitzit, since he is not obligated at all in tzitzit, he may certainly bless on borrowed, as women, and as one who borrowed a tallit and made tzitzit in it from his own — what difference is there? All that by law he is not more obligated, although exempt from making it — wherever the obligation is not placed on him in the more proper manner, he may bless on it. And the words of the Magen Avraham are correct, and the words of that Gaon, z"l, do not appear correct; and in truth they are not comparable — examine well.
