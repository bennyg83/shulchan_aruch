Tzitzis, etc. The reason is that a non-Jew is not capable of acting for the sake of the mitzvah. From this we learn that if a Jew made them without intention, they are likewise invalid. When made by a non-Jew they are invalid even if a Jew stands over him and instructs him to make them for the sake of the mitzvah. Even according to Rosh, who validates tefillin processed by a non-Jew when a Jew stands over him, and the same would apply to spinning, he nevertheless agrees that attaching tzitzis to the tallis is invalid when done by a non-Jew, even if a Jew stands over him. Some maintain that both spinning and attaching are invalid even when a Jew stands over the non-Jew and tells him to act for the sake of the mitzvah. My teacher, Bach. Some are stringent and require that men make them. If a woman attaches the tzitzis to the tallis, they are invalid, because one who is not obligated in a mitzvah cannot prepare it. According to this reason, it would likewise be improper for a woman to bind the lulav. Tosafos in Gittin.
