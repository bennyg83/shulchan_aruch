Tzitzit, etc. The reason is that gentiles are not fit to perform acts requiring lishmah. From this we infer that if a Jew made them without intent they are also invalid, and regarding a gentile they are invalid even if a Jew stands over him and tells him to make them with intent. And even per the Rosh, who permits tefillin made by a gentile when a Jew stands over him—and the same applies to spinning—nevertheless regarding making tzitzit on a tallit he admits they are invalid if made by a gentile even if a Jew stands over him. And some say that both in spinning and in making, even if a Jew stands over him and tells him to make them with intent, they are invalid. (My teacher in the Bach.) And some are stringent to require that men make them. If a woman fixes the tzitzit on a tallit they are invalid, for all mitzvot from which a woman is exempt she cannot fix. Therefore it is not proper for a woman to bind the lulav. (Tosafot in Gittin.)
