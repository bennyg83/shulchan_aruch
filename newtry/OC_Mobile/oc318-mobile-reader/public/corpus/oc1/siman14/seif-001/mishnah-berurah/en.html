(א) Made — Meaning: he attached them to an invalid garment, even if a Jew is standing over him teaching him to make them for the sake of the mitzvah; whereas if a non-Jew spun or wove them in this fashion it is valid for the Rosh, as we wrote above in siman 11, seif 2. Specifically if he inserted them in the corner or made the first coil or first knot; but if a Jew made the first coil and first knot and the non-Jew finished the remaining coils and knots, which are only an ancillary mitzvah, it is valid.

(ב) To require men — And even if one stands over them it does not help, since what is written “the sons of Israel” and “you shall make” also excludes the daughters of Israel.

(ג) That they make them — Specifically attaching them to the garment; spinning and weaving may be done by women.

(ד) And good to do so, etc. — Likewise it is fitting and proper not to attach tzitzis to an adult’s garment lechatchila through a minor under thirteen years; but if he is thirteen years and one day, even if he has not brought two hairs, even lechatchila one need not be stringent. Orchos Chayim is lenient even lechatchila through a minor. All this is specifically when an adult stands over him teaching him to make them for the sake of the mitzvah; but otherwise, even if the minor already attached them to the garment, one must untie them and reattach them.

(ה) Lechatchila — But after the fact it is permitted. Even if no one stood over her she is believed to say she attached them for the sake of tzitzis.
