(1) That they made. Meaning, they attached the strings to the garment. It is invalid even if a Jew stands over the non-Jew and teaches him to act for the sake of the mitzvah. If, however, the non-Jew merely spun or twisted the strings under such supervision, it is valid according to Rosh, as explained above in siman 11, seif 2. The invalidity applies specifically if the non-Jew inserted the strings into the corner, made the first winding, or tied the first knot. If the Jew made the first winding and knot and the non-Jew completed the remaining windings and knots, which are only an enhancement of the mitzvah, it is valid.
(2) To require men. Even an adult standing over them is ineffective, because the verse says, “The children of Israel shall make,” which is understood to exclude the daughters of Israel.
(3) That they should make them. This refers specifically to attaching the strings to the garment. Spinning and twisting may be done by women.
(4) And it is preferable to do, etc. It is likewise proper not to have a minor under thirteen attach tzitzis initially to an adult's garment. Once he is thirteen years and one day, even if he has not shown signs of maturity, there is no need to be stringent initially. Artzos HaChayim is lenient even initially through a minor. All this applies only when an adult stands over him and teaches him to act for the sake of the mitzvah. Without such supervision, even if the minor already attached them, they must be removed and reattached.
(5) Initially. After the fact it is permitted. Even without an adult standing over her, a woman is believed to say that she attached them for the sake of the mitzvah.
