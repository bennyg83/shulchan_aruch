(Seif katan 1) That he made, etc. This is invalid even if a Jew stands over the non-Jew, according to Rosh. Rambam proves that attaching the tzitzis cannot require intention for the sake of the mitzvah. If it did, why would the Torah need the verse “the children of Israel” to exclude a non-Jew? Regarding spinning, where no such exclusion is written, spinning by a non-Jew is nevertheless invalid, as stated at the beginning of siman 11, because spinning must be for the sake of the mitzvah and a non-Jew is not capable of that intention. If attaching likewise required intention, no separate exclusion would be needed. Rambam therefore concludes that attaching need not be for the sake of the mitzvah. Rosh, however, rules that attaching does require intention, and this is our accepted ruling in the following seif. Rambam's question must therefore be answered. Magen Avraham explains that Rosh follows his own view that when a Jew stands over a non-Jew during spinning, the work is considered performed for the sake of the mitzvah. The verse “the children of Israel” is therefore needed regarding attachment to invalidate a non-Jew even in such a case, although the act is considered intentional. Rambam, who maintains that supervision does not help during spinning, can use the verse as proof. According to him, if attachment required intention, a non-Jew would already be invalid because his act lacks intention, and the verse would be superfluous.
