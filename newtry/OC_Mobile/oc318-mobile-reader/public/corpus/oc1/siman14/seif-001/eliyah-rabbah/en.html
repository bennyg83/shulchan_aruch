[1] "That he made," etc. Explanation: he attached the threads or made the chulyah and knots — Levush. And it seems to me that if a Jew made the first chulyah — namely wrapping and knot — and a gentile finished it, it is valid; and all the more so when a Jew finished without intent, and so is implied in Beit Yosef siman 11 and in this siman. The Bach and all later authorities wrote that if a gentile made it, even with a Jew standing over him it is invalid. And Olas Tamid wrote: and with a Jew assisting there is room to doubt — end quote. And in my humble opinion it is simple that it is invalid, for we maintain in the chapter HaMitzniyot 93a that assisting has no substance. And even for Beit Yosef in Yoreh De'ah siman 271, who mentions a Torah scroll on account of assisting — that is regarding that it is l'shemah, since a Jew assists we say it was made per the Jew's intent, as is implied in Beit Yosef and Bach there. But here it is not a reason because of l'shemah, but a decree of Scripture — see above siman 11 seif katan 4.
