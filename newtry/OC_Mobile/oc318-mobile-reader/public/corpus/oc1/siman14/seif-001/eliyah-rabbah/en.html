[1] That he made, etc. Meaning, he attached the strings, or made the first winding and knots. Levush. It appears to me that if a Jew made the first winding, meaning one wrap and one knot, and a non-Jew completed the rest, it is valid. All the more so is it valid if a Jew completed the remaining windings and knots without explicit intention. This is implied by Beit Yosef in siman 11 and here. Bach and all the later authorities write that if a non-Jew made them, they are invalid even if a Jew stands over him. Olas Tamid remains uncertain where a Jew assists him. In my humble opinion it is clearly invalid, for Shabbos 93a rules that assistance has no halachic substance. Beit Yosef in Yoreh Deah, siman 271, mentions assistance regarding a Sefer Torah only because it helps establish that the work was performed for the sake of the mitzvah. Since a Jew assists, we say that the non-Jew acts according to the Jew's intention, as is implied by Beit Yosef and Bach there. Here, however, the invalidity is not because of lack of intention, but because of a Scriptural exclusion. See above, siman 11, seif katan 4.
