To require men. See Mishnah Berurah. This is the reason of Maharam cited in Hagahos Maimoniyos. I did not copy Rabbeinu Tam's reason, cited in Magen Avraham, that anyone not subject to wearing is not subject to making, because that rationale would also apply to other mitzvos, such as lulav and sukkah. Darkei Moshe implies that he took only Maharam's view into account, and I found the same in Olas Tamid. If so, there is no reason to be stringent regarding a minor. Artzos HaChayim likewise rules this way. Nevertheless, since Peri Megadim and Derech HaChayim record as practical halachah that initially one should be careful not to have a minor do it, it is proper and correct to follow their ruling. It is obvious that a minor may attach tzitzis to his own garment according to all opinions, provided that he has reached the age of education and is therefore obligated D'Rabbanan in the mitzvah of tzitzis. He is considered subject to wearing for purposes of fulfilling his own obligation with the tzitzis he made. It further appears to me that even after he becomes thirteen years old, he need not remove those tzitzis, since this is already an after-the-fact situation and Rabbeinu Tam's view need not be taken into account after the fact. This applies where he knows that he originally attached them for the sake of the mitzvah.
And it is preferable to do, etc. See Mishnah Berurah. I must clarify my words. Although Rama is lenient after the fact regarding a woman, and she may certainly do it in every manner just as a man does, a minor requires an adult standing over him. Chayei Adam writes that a minor who knows how to act for the sake of the mitzvah may be validated after the fact like a woman. This too must mean specifically where an adult stands over him. Even Taz, who rules below in siman 460 that a minor who understands how to act for the sake of the mitzvah is no longer treated as an ordinary minor, means that supervision by an adult is effective, as is evident to one who examines his words there. I am therefore surprised by Artzos HaChayim, who is lenient initially through a minor. Even if Rabbeinu Tam's concern is entirely disregarded, one should still initially be stringent because of the requirement of intention for the sake of the mitzvah. Seif 2 rules that attaching the strings requires such intention, in deference to Rashi, Rosh, and other Rishonim who maintain that this requirement is according to the Torah. Below, in siman 460, it is ruled that the kneading of matzah for the mitzvah, which likewise requires intention for the sake of the mitzvah, should not initially be done by a deaf-mute, mentally incompetent person, or minor, even when an adult stands over them, as explained by the later authorities and the Gra there.
