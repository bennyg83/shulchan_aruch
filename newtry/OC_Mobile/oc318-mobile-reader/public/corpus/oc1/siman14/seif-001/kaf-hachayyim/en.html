1) [Seif 1] Tzitzit made by a non-Jew are invalid, etc. This means that if the non-Jew attached them to the garment, they are invalid, even if a Jew stood over him supervising. Regarding spinning, however, they are valid according to the Rosh, as stated in siman 11, seif 2. Magen Avraham, seif katan 1; Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 1; Peri Megadim, Eshel Avraham, ot 1; Chayei Adam, klal 11, ot 30. Even if the non-Jew merely inserted the threads into the corner, or made one winding or one knot, they are invalid. Peri Megadim there in the name of Levush; Pithei Olam, ot 1. They are invalid even if a Jew assists him. Eliyah Rabbah there, cited by Shaarei Teshuvah, ot 1, unlike Olat Tamid, ot 5. If, however, a Jew hung the threads in the corner and made one winding and one knot in a manner that makes them valid by Torah law, and the non-Jew then completed them, they are valid. Eliyah Rabbah there; Peri Megadim there; Shaarei Teshuvah there; Pithei Olam there. See below, ot 9.
2) If. If a Jew and a non-Jew attached the tzitzit together, the law depends upon the dispute between Beit Yosef and the Shach concerning slaughter in Yoreh Deah, siman 2, seif 11, where both hold the knife and slaughter: Shulchan Aruch rules the slaughter invalid, while the Shach rules it valid; see there. Pithei Olam there in the name of Responsa Peri Tevuah. Accordingly, for us, who rely upon the rulings of Maran, the tzitzit are invalid.
3) There. 'A woman is qualified to make them.' Taz, seif katan 1, writes that this excludes the view of Rabbeinu Tam, who disqualifies women from making tzitzit just as they are disqualified from making tefillin in siman 39, seif katan 1, etc. The fact that our women spin tzitzit is permitted even according to Rabbeinu Tam, for he objects specifically to attaching the tzitzit to the garment, as stated nearby; spinning is a different matter. Levush, seif 1, likewise writes that preparing, spinning, and twisting the tzitzit may be done by women even according to the stringent authorities; see there. Shiyurei Kenesses HaGedolah, Hagahot Beit Yosef, ot 1, and Maamar Mordechai, ot 2, cite this. Olat Tamid, seif katan 3; Birkei Yosef, ot 3, and in his Machazik Berachah, ot 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ot 2; Chayei Adam there; and Pithei Olam, ot 2, write likewise. This is unlike Korban Netanel at the end of chapter HaSholeach and his Netiv Chaim, who write that according to Rabbeinu Tam even spinning by women is prohibited. Machazik Berachah there likewise proves the contrary from the words of the halakhic authorities; see there.
4) There, in the gloss. 'Some are stringent and require men to make it, etc.' A minor has the same law as a woman. Magen Avraham, seif katan 3; Shulchan Aruch HaRav there; Chayei Adam there; Chesed LaAlafim, ot 1; Shitilei Zeitim, ot 3. This is so even if an adult stands over him and instructs him to make it for the sake of the mitzvah of tzitzit. Shulchan Aruch HaRav there. According to this view, he is disqualified from making them until he is thirteen years old and has grown two hairs, as with writing tefillin in siman 39. Machatzit HaShekel; and Peri Megadim, Eshel Avraham, ot 3, writes likewise according to Magen Avraham. Although Peri Megadim writes there that this is a forced interpretation, since all the authorities cited above rule like Magen Avraham, and since we initially take Rabbeinu Tam's view into account as Rema writes, one should not initially allow a minor to make them until he is thirteen years old and it is definitively established that he has grown two hairs. This is unlike Pithei Olam, ot 3.
5) A tumtum. A tumtum is qualified to make them, because a Torah-law uncertainty is treated stringently by Torah law and the tumtum is obligated to wear tzitzit. According to the authority who expounds 'sons, but not daughters,' it may be said that this means definite sons, but not one whose status is uncertain. The law of an androgynos depends upon the dispute whether the androgynos is a distinct created category. Peri Megadim, Mishbetzot Zahav, ot 1; Pithei Olam, ot 1.
