A woman. See Taz. The citation there is a printing error; it should be siman 39, seif 1. According to Rabbeinu Tam, all those invalid there, including a minor and an apostate, would likewise be invalid here. There is another reason, namely, “the children of Israel,” and not a woman. According to that reason, a minor would be valid. See Magen Avraham. What Magen Avraham writes, that even according to Rabbeinu Tam a woman may spin, appears correct from siman 11, seifim 1 and 2, regarding women and non-Jews, since Rama did not add any gloss and evidently agreed with the Mechaber. The verse is particular only about attaching the strings, as explained in the next paragraph: “Speak to the children of Israel, and they shall make upon the corners of their garments,” meaning attaching them to the garment, not spinning and twisting. Levush explains it this way in siman 11, seif 14, and here. Levush follows his own view that a woman is invalid because the verse says “the children,” and not the daughters. That works, because the verse “You shall make fringes for yourself” does not say “children” and therefore does not exclude women from spinning and twisting. According to Rabbeinu Tam's reason, however, that whoever is not subject to wearing is not subject to making, the verse “You shall make fringes upon the four corners of your garment” should exclude women even from spinning and twisting, since this verse is the source that spinning and twisting must be for the sake of the mitzvah, from its juxtaposition to the prohibition of shatnez. The rule must therefore be that “they shall make upon the corners of their garments” concerns insertion and tying, while “you shall make upon the four corners of your garment” concerns spinning and twisting, similar to shatnez, and perhaps also tying, because of the word “fringes,” as Levush writes. According to this, Taz appears to combine two incompatible explanations. This requires study. Know that according to Rabbeinu Tam, a tumtum may make tzitzis, because a Torah-level doubt is treated stringently and he is subject to wearing. According to the reason of “the children,” and not the daughters, one might say that only a definite male is included, not one of doubtful status. The law of an androgynos depends upon whether that status is regarded as a distinct creation. With Hashem's help this will be explained in siman 39. According to the Mechaber, a non-Jew may spin under Rosh's view.
