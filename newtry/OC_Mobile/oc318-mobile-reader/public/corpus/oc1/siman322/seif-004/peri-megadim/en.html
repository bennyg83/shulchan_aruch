At the end of chapter 39; the sugya in chapter "one who asks" folio 149b — the plain meaning is like Rambam chapter 23 law 17; and even though the halacha is not like Rav in Bava Metzia folio 75 — Taz explained there forbidden to lend from his sons: the explanation that he gives interest to his sons to accustom them to the taste of interest and accustom them in livelihood to lend to a non-Jew — forbidden lest he also forbid lending to an Israelite; unlike here where he does not intend and does not accustom — permitted since he is not strict, for everything is his; see Yoreh De'ah 105 letter 4 in Taz and Shach (13); and according to this explanation it is permitted to lend them with interest so they give him interest — to separate from its prohibition he does a mitzvah; and requires study for practical law; but according to Maggid Mishneh who distinguishes between d'oraisa and rabbinic — difficult: why should we say in brief that since Shmuel said they transgress on account of measuring and weighing rabbinically with others — with his sons he is not strict they were lenient in rabbinic matters and not bring that Rav's view is not the halacha; and nevertheless requires study; and Ateret Rosh letter 14 what is written there that in a mitzvah more lest he come to be drawn after, as is written siman 621 letter 1; and nevertheless one may say on the eighth day which is rabbinic — no; and how did he learn rabbinic from d'oraisa? And Rambam's language chapter 23 law 18: forbidden to cast lots and to play with dice on Shabbat — meaning equally with others who are strict, for it resembles commerce and trade since they are strict; on a weekday permitted since dice is only rabbinic theft; meaning with a large portion against a small one; when equal they did not decree such a case; and what Maggid Mishneh wrote: playing with dice even on a weekday forbidden — meaning a large portion against a small one with others; and it will be explained further in Magen Avraham letter 7:
