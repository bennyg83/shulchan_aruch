(א) Animal food — meaning a thing fit for animal eating; and it is implied in the gemara that even if they do not stand for them, such as soft woods fit for them to eat — likewise not called a utensil name:
(ב) On account of fixing a utensil — and permitted even to move, for a thing fit for eating is prepared for every need:
(ג) Therefore, etc. — and some hold that specifically such as this, where he makes a utensil from it initially, fixing a utensil does not apply; but if he fixes an already-made utensil with edible matter, such as when there is a hole in the utensil and he takes a piece of bread and cuts it to the measure of the hole — fixing a utensil applies [Ran, and they cited it in Chayei Adam; and see in Eshel Avraham that according to him there is liability for a chatas]:
(ד) Even with a knife, etc. — and even if he is particular about the measure there is no concern of prohibition on account of cutting (mechatech), since it is edible matter [Chayei Adam]:
(ה) Even to take it — meaning even taking it stam without cutting off is forbidden on account of muktzeh like other woods; and all the more if he wishes to cut it off in order to pick his teeth with it — forbidden on account of a rabbinic prohibition, for it is like fixing a utensil; and specifically if he cuts it off by hand — then he is exempt from a chatas since it is melacha done in an unusual manner, and forbidden on account of a rabbinic prohibition as we wrote; but if he cuts it off with a utensil in order to pick his teeth with it or to open a door with it — then it is a full melacha, for through this he makes it a utensil, and he is liable [R' Avraham in the name of Rosh and Rabbeinu Yeruham]:
(ו) Forbidden — and even though there is concern of human dignity, for sometimes the flesh between the teeth is seen outside and it is disgraceful to him — nevertheless forbidden, for he should have prepared a splinter from erev Shabbat in the place of the meal [gemara]; and therefore if his fellow invited him to a meal on Shabbat and it was impossible for him to prepare anything from erev Shabbat to pick his teeth with — permitted for him to move a splinter to pick his teeth with if he has no alternative; but in a place where this is not considered a disgrace to him — forbidden [Magen Avraham]:
