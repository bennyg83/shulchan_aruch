And some say with his sons, etc. — this is the view of Rambam, as is explained in Beit Yosef in the distinction between Ri'f and Rosh and Tur on one side and Rambam on the other: Rambam follows the plain meaning of the sugya in chapter "one who asks"; but Ri'f and Rosh rejected the halacha since it is established according to Rav, who said permitted to lend interest to his sons, etc.; and we do not rule like Rav in chapter "if not," for we say lest he come to be drawn after; and if so, here too forbidden with a large portion against a small one on account of gambling; here too they decreed lest he come to be drawn after; and the Rav HaMagid labored to reconcile Rambam's view and said that interest is different since it is d'oraisa, unlike the prohibition of gambling which is from their words — they did not decree regarding sons and household members; and one may wonder: if so, what did the gemara challenge at first — "if so, with his sons and household members too"? For the prohibition with others is on account of that which Shmuel said: members of a fellowship who are strict with one another transgress on account of measuring and weighing, etc. — meaning lest they come to this; and all those prohibitions are only rabbinic when you examine them; and we may also say they did not decree regarding sons and household members on account of this prohibition which is rabbinic; and further we find Rambam himself decreed in a decree of lest he come to be drawn after even in rabbinic matters, as in siman 499 where Beit Yosef wrote that Rambam wrote in a responsum not to bless on the cup on the eighth day and give to a child lest he come to be drawn after, as is explained there — this reason; and they learned there from what is stated in the gemara that one does not bless time on the cup in the Temple and give to a minor lest he come to be drawn after; and regarding the twenty cubits it is a d'oraisa prohibition to drink, and on the eighth day it is only rabbinic — nevertheless he equated them; and we may say the same here. And it appears to me the distinction between d'oraisa and rabbinic in this is not that Rambam follows his view that he holds in chapter "any interest" that a person may lend interest from his sons in order to accustom them to the taste of interest and hide their money to lend to a non-Jew with interest and it will be sustenance for them; and in truth there is no prohibition of interest in this since he is not strict with them and gives them the interest as a gift; and so Rambam explicitly regarding interest in this, since he is not strict with them; and on this we conclude there "and it is not so, lest they come to be drawn after to lend even to an Israelite with interest," for the reason will be pleasing in their eyes; and if so we are not concerned in this except regarding the prohibition of interest lest he accustom them intentionally to this, for habit will harm him afterward; unlike other prohibitions — if he is not strict with household members and gives them as a gift even a large portion against a small one we are not concerned lest he come to be drawn after, for there is no reason for this decree, for he does not intend to accustom them to this; and this itself that the gemara answers is the reason as R' Yossi said: Rav permitted to lend, etc. — meaning his reason is since a person is not strict — therefore there is no interest here; likewise there is no prohibition of gambling here; and even though we reject Rav regarding interest on account of lest he come to be drawn after — here this does not apply; and we rule well according to this view that a person is not strict — it is not rejected; and where the decree of lest he come to be drawn after does not apply we certainly rule like Rav that there is no prohibition of gambling here; and therefore Rambam concluded the reason well "since they are not strict" — in this is the main learning from Rav per the halacha; as it appears to me, correct in my humble opinion:
