[6] To cut off, etc. Specifically by hand but not with a knife (Yam Shel Shlomo there); and Taz siman 501 expanded to disagree since even cutting off with a knife for a utensil he is not liable unless for smell — permitted initially — until here; and in what I explained above that he is liable — if so there is proof to the contrary; and what he challenged: the gemara folio 33 should have changed thus that what the mishnah teaches "he shall not cut it off" deals with a knife — there is no difficulty, for unspecified cutting implies it deals by hand as is explained in Rosh and the poskim mentioned above; and further one may say: behold Rashal wrote there that similar to rubbing he spoke, namely by hand — if so impossible to establish with a utensil; also what he wrote that in the gemara it compares explicitly cutting to scent to breaking barrels that deals with a utensil — until here — no proof at all, for the questioner challenges from breaking barrels that they did not decree and answers that it deals with broken pieces where liability does not apply even if he intends to make a utensil — therefore they did not decree to break even with a utensil; but cutting off to smell, where there is concern of liability when cutting with a knife for a utensil — they decreed even for smell; and what proof he brought from Rosh that he learned from this that for animal food even with a knife permitted to cut off — in my humble opinion he did not delve deeply, for regarding animal food there is no concern of liability at all and there is nothing to decree except lest he cut off from a splinter; he brings proof well from permitting in breaking barrels, namely in broken pieces, and they did not decree on account of other barrels except they did not decree except where there is concern in that thing itself — examine this; therefore it appears to me to forbid like Rashal; and even though Taz concluded it appears clear and simple to permit — one should not heed this, for I already refuted all his proofs clearly; and all this in tough ones, but in soft ones certainly Rashal too concedes permitted with a knife;
