(ב) For a sick person — the same applies to a healthy person; only he stated it as the usual manner of speech:

(ג) And he rubs it, etc. — it must read: and he cuts it off and rubs it. And rubbing means he rubs it between his fingers in order to bring out its scent; and cutting off too is for this, for the place of the cut is moist and its scent wafts; and many Acharonim agreed that that which is permitted to cut off tough ones is specifically by hand, but not with a utensil, even though he only cuts off in order to smell and does not intend at all to make it a utensil — therefore forbidden to cut off the myrtle with a knife, a decree lest he come to cut off to pick his teeth with it, and in this one is liable as stated in s.k. 13. And even by hand it is not permitted to cut off tough ones except to smell; but to pick teeth with — forbidden according to all:

(ד) Both tough — meaning even tough ones that are not at all fit for animal food — nevertheless permitted to cut them off and to split them as much as he wishes, whether he splits a large or small piece [Rambam]; and specifically in all this where it is only to add scent through rubbing and cutting off; but forbidden to create a scent, such as to place spices in a garment and the like so the garment smell — forbidden [Acharonim]. And incidentally, since we spoke in Shulchan Aruch regarding cutting and cutting off in the matter of animal food and fragrant woods, we will also set forth here the law of the melacha of cutting (mechatech) in other matters. (1) One who cuts leather or anything a person cuts and is particular about its measure — whether wood or metal or even feathers of a bird, since he is particular about its length and width and cuts with intent — liable; but one who cuts in a destructive manner or without intent for its measure but as one who acts unawares or as one who plays — exempt [Rambam chapter 11]. And how well did the prophets (Nechemiah 10) and our Sages z"l profit us in what they forbade us commerce and trade on Shabbat, for sometimes one can come to liability for a chatas, such as when he sells some merchandise and cuts from it per the measure his customer needs — for he is liable on account of cutting [Chayei Adam in general rule 36]. (2) The melacha of cutting does not apply in foods, as we wrote above in s.k. 12. (3) Whether the melacha of cutting applies specifically with a utensil or even by hand — see what we wrote in Biur Halacha that it depends on each thing according to its nature: a thing whose manner is to cut specifically with a utensil — one is liable only with a utensil; and a thing whose manner is to separate it into two by hand — liable even by hand; and it is reasonable that there is a rabbinic prohibition in every manner, as we wrote in Biur Halacha, see there.
