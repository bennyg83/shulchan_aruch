Both tough and soft — at first glance difficult: why did they permit to cut off tough ones to smell, since he does not intend in the cutting to make a utensil but only to smell and fixing a utensil does not apply — let it be forbidden on account of cutting in tough ones that are not in the category of animal food, for in every melacha of cutting the reason is not because through this he makes a utensil but this itself is the melacha, as is known from what the gemara said Shabbos 74b: one who combs flax and is particular about the strand — liable also on account of cutting; and perhaps since it deals with cutting off by hand as we wrote in Mishna Berurah s.k. 17 — even on account of cutting there is nothing here, for unspecified cutting is with a utensil; and so it appears to my humble opinion from Rosh who wrote that therefore he is exempt when cutting off by hand to pick his teeth with it since it is like an unusual manner; and Rashi too wrote that cutting not with a knife is fixing in an unusual manner and not proper fixing; and if the melacha of cutting applies even by hand — why is he exempt here from fixing and he should be liable on account of cutting? However in Eshel Avraham I saw a novel matter that tends to say that the melacha of cutting applies even by hand; and his words are difficult: why did they permit to cut off tough ones? And perhaps unspecified cutting to smell he is not particular about the measure, for he does not intend except that through the cutting the scent waft more; but in truth if he is particular about the measure, such as when his fellow asks him to give him from his fragrant woods and he cuts off some amount from them in order to give him — likewise it can occur here sometimes a d'oraisa prohibition [and in this there will be room for the law of Shakhne'ahag that Peri Megadim wondered about, see there]; but unspecified cutting a splinter to pick his teeth with it — it appears from Rambam that he is particular about the measure, since he obligates him there on account of cutting; see Rambam chapter 11 — namely that he wishes to make it nice so it be good to pick his teeth with [and see in Maggid Mishneh there that his view is that he is not liable except when cutting with a knife and not by hand, like Rosh; and from his not obligating him on account of cutting it implies he holds the melacha of cutting does not apply by hand, as we wrote; and we have no complaint against Eshel Avraham who wishes to reconcile Rambam's words — therefore he says according to his words a novel matter, and he has no proof for his words; and as we wrote, proof for our words from Rosh and Rashi that cutting does not apply by hand]. And for practical law it appears to me to decide that the melacha of cutting depends on each thing according to its nature: a thing whose manner is to cut specifically with a utensil — cutting does not apply except with a utensil; and similar to what we find regarding shearing on Shabbat 94b on account of there being no manner of shearing by hand; and a thing whose manner is to separate it into two by hand [as is found regarding some merchandise whose manner is to separate into two by tearing by hand] — liable on account of cutting if he separates by hand; and similar to what we find regarding detaching, that he is liable even if he detaches with his hand and not with a utensil, as Rashi explained Shabbos 73b s.v. "there is no manner," see there; but all this specifically when he is particular about the measure, as we cited from Rambam's language. But what we wrote that it depends on each according to its manner — at first glance this is only regarding d'oraisa; but rabbinically it would be forbidden in every manner, similar to shearing that is forbidden according to all even by hand even though there is no manner of shearing thus; and therefore we must say that which they permitted to cut off initially to smell is because by default he is not particular about the measure, as stated; and it is possible to distinguish and say that the melacha of cutting does not apply at all in cutting by hand; but this — since it is reasonable that in a thing whose manner is to separate by hand cutting applies even by hand — by necessity one may not be lenient rabbinically in every manner, as it appears to my humble opinion:
