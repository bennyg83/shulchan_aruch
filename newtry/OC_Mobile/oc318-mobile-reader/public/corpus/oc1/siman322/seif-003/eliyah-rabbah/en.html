[3] [Levush] If it did not extinguish, etc. This implies it is preferable on the second day of Yom Tov to relight wicks that burned the previous day if they were extinguished on a weekday or between two festival days of the Diaspora, as with an egg in siman 513. The end of siman 673 also implies this, unlike those who add a new wick as an enhancement of the mitzvah. Perhaps Chanukah differs because each day an additional light is added, and the blessing relates to that day's required light. The essential mitzvah is one light, while those who beautify add according to the day. According to this, even on Shabbos, if there is one new wick that fulfills the essential mitzvah, it is permitted to use the older ones for the additional lights. It is also possible that Chanukah lights are remnants of a mitzvah, unlike the two days of Yom Tov where one day is weekday. The matter will be discussed further below.
