(א) We translate [it] — so that everyone may understand.

(ב) With intention — for we say in the Gemara: From the day the Temple was destroyed, the world stands on the kedusha of the siddur. When they announce something, one must announce before the chazan begins to say Ashrei, and not between Ashrei and Lamenatzeiach; and all the more so when the congregation is saying Ashrei or the order of kedusha, lest their intention be nullified thereby.

(ג) And see above siman 59 — for there the Rama concludes that the custom has spread to say it even when praying alone; nevertheless l'chatchila it is better to say it with a congregation. Therefore if the congregation has begun to say "Ve-atah kadosh" and he has not yet said Ashrei and Uva le-Tzion, he should say it with them, and afterward he should say Ashrei and Lamenatzeiach; and also the two verses before "Ve-atah kadosh," namely Uva le-Tzion and "Ani zot beriti" etc.

(ד) And regarding the kedusha in the translation, etc. — meaning that the verses of kedusha in the holy tongue are said only with a minyan, and the verses of kedusha in the translation are said only by an individual, because whatever is in the language of translation is not said with a multitude. Therefore when they say kedusha de-sidra with a congregation, each individual says quietly to himself the translation of the kedusha, for then it is not called "with a congregation"; and this is what the Rama concluded — that they should not say them in a loud voice. And if one prays not with a congregation, he may also say the translation in a loud voice [Sha'agat Aryeh].
