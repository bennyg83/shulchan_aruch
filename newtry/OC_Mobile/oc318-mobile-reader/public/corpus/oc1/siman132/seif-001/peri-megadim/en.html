An individual says it. Taz in Perishah raises a difficulty: since we do not hold like the Zohar that in the holy tongue an individual says kedusha, as explained in siman 59 seif 3 — likewise regarding the translation there is no halacha like it; see there. And Levush in truth [differs]; Taz and Aziel [recorded it], and the reason they say it quietly is because it was enacted that the people understand — their language was translation; see there. Nevertheless this is not forced — one can say that here we hold like the Zohar. And see siman 101 seif 4 regarding the Aramaic language: although our prayer the Holy One, blessed be He, accepts Himself without any intermediary, nevertheless certainly a good advocate on the right; and in Aramaic — enemies — and there is none who advocates well for him, and he will not be, heaven forbid, a prosecutor; therefore perhaps for this reason they say it quietly.

See Levush: after Tachanun one interrupts between them and the verses they say afterward (and on a day when there is Musaf they say Keriat Shema immediately), and they say Ashrei and Tehillah, for the Sages said: Whoever says Tehillah three times is assured that he is a child of the World to Come. And they are stringent: once in the verses of song, twice before Uva le-Tzion, a third time before Minchah (Orach Chaim siman 51 seif 7; Beit Yosef 234 in the name of Maharai in his writings 509, and Magen Avraham 108 ot 5; and what Mahari Polak wrote, the Taz brought below) — not regarding the beginning of the enactment but why they enacted it specifically between chapter 18 and Uva le-Tzion; see there. And on Shabbat and Yom Tov, Aleinu and Barchu — because it was enacted in the language of supplication; see there. But in Beit Yosef in the name of Shelah in the name of Rashi, that this was enacted for Torah, etc.; see there — Aziel copied it. Nevertheless, the order of kedusha from "Ve-kara zeh" etc. — why do they not say it on Shabbat, since it was enacted when they decreed, etc.; and in Or Zarua in the name of Matmonim he wrote that it was enacted so that those coming after kedusha would hear kedusha — which is not so on Shabbat and Yom Tov. And it appears that the reason the enactment was not annulled, lest heaven forbid, etc.; and it is possible that on Shabbat and Yom Tov there is Musaf and the enemies have gone after Shacharit (unless Rosh Chodesh and Chol ha-Moed would prove otherwise), and they enacted it earlier — "May He answer you on a day of trouble," because the order of kedusha was enacted due to a decree, etc.; see Levush.
