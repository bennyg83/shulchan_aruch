Magen Avraham wrote: When announcements are made, etc. The correct text is: “and Sema in Choshen Mishpat, siman 267, seif katan 4.”
This is what he writes there: Although Kedushah DeSidra is a matter in its own right, just as Maariv is distinct from Minchah, nevertheless the order of Kedushah begins immediately after the Kaddish following Tachanun. Therefore, Ashrei is recited again. It is thus preferable to make the announcement before Ashrei, and better still after the conclusion of the prayer. These are his words.
This is also implied by Beit Yosef, siman 550, who writes that in places where a communal fast is announced on the preceding Shabbos, it should be announced after Maftir and before Ashrei. Since Ashrei belongs to Mussaf, one should not interrupt between it and the Mussaf prayer. The same applies here: since Ashrei belongs to Kedushah DeSidra and was instituted for its sake, one should not interrupt between Ashrei and Kedushah.
For some say that an individual may not, etc. Meaning, Kedushah DeSidra, as stated above in siman 59. Although we rule that even an individual may recite it, as Rama writes in siman 59, nevertheless, wherever possible it is preferable to satisfy all opinions.
The same would apply that the announcement should be made before reciting the two verses, for at first glance those two verses have no connection to Kedushah. See Beit Yosef for the reason they are recited. Although Levush writes that according to the reason cited by Beit Yosef in the name of Kol Bo, “Why do we recite this Kedushah,” the two verses are connected to Kedushah, Kol Bo states as follows: Since they were unable to recite Kedushah within the Amidah because a decree forbade responding to Kedushah and guards watched whether they would respond, after the prayer, when the guards departed, they would recite “And one called to another,” meaning the threefold Kedushah, and translate it because of the children. They did this twice, corresponding to the Kedushah of Yotzer and that of the Amidah. Although the decree has now ceased, they did not abolish Kedushah DeSidra, lest sin bring the decree back. Levush explains that for this reason they first recited “A redeemer shall come to Zion,” as an apology before the Holy One, blessed is He, for delaying Kedushah until then because the oppression of the kingdoms prevented them from reciting it within the Amidah. They therefore pray for redemption and say, “A redeemer shall come to Zion.” Since they were likewise prevented from engaging in Torah, they also say, “And as for Me, this is My covenant,” reminding Him of His promise that Torah would not depart from our mouths. Afterward they say, “Who dwells upon the praises of Israel,” which the Sages interpret to mean that the Holy One, blessed is He, rests His Shechinah among us to hear the praises of Israel. The word yoshev denotes remaining, and we say this as a supplication: although we delayed it, it was not through rebellion or betrayal, and therefore we entreat Him to remain and hear our prayer as is His way. According to this, the two verses are indeed connected to Kedushah. Nevertheless, this is not the primary reason for reciting Kedushah DeSidra. Rather, it is based on Sotah 49a: From the day the Beis HaMikdash was destroyed, no day passes whose curse is not greater than that of the preceding day. On what does the world endure, since the curse continually increases? On Kedushah DeSidra and on the response “Amen, yehei Shemei Rabbah” following aggadic study. Rashi explains that through Kedushah DeSidra, all Israel engage daily in a small measure of Torah by reciting Scripture and its translation, since at that time they understood Aramaic, and they are therefore considered as engaging in Torah. Seif katan 1: Aleinu, etc. One should say, “the abode of His glory is in the heavens,” and not, “and His glorious throne is in the heavens.” Bach explains that one cannot say, “and His glorious throne is in the heavens,” since the heavens themselves are, figuratively speaking, His glorious throne, as it is written in Yeshayah, “The heavens are My throne.”
For this is the Scriptural wording, and it is preferable to use the language of Scripture, as Tosafos write in Berachos 52b, s.v. borei, etc.
