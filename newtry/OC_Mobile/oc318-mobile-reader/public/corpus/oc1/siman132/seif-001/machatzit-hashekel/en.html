Magen Avraham wrote: when they announce etc., and Semag in Choshen Mishpat siman 267 note 4 — so it should read.

This is his language there: even though the order of kedushah is a matter in itself, like Maariv to Minchah, nevertheless immediately after the kaddish of Tachanun the order of kedushah begins, and therefore they repeat and say Ashrei; therefore it is better to announce before Ashrei, and better after the conclusion of the prayer — end of his words.

And so too in Beit Yosef siman 559, who wrote that in places where they announce a public fast on the Shabbat before it, they announce after the haftarah before Ashrei — behold, since Ashrei belongs to Musaf, one may not interrupt between it and the Musaf prayer; the same law applies here, since Ashrei belongs to the kedushah that was instituted for its sake, one may not interrupt between Ashrei and kedushah.

For there are those who say that an individual etc. — meaning the order of kedushah, as above siman 59; and it is that we hold that even an individual says it, as Rama wrote siman 59; nevertheless, wherever possible to go according to everyone, it is preferable.

And the same law applies that one may say before he says the second verses, for apparently the two verses have no connection to kedushah; and see in Beit Yosef the reason for saying them. And even though Levush wrote that for the reason that Rabbi Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Kolbo — why do we say this kedushah — there is connection of the two aforementioned verses to kedushah, and this is his language in Kolbo: because they could not say kedushah within the prayer, for they decreed that one not answer kedushah, and enemies were lying in wait for them if they answered kedushah; and after the prayer, when the enemies were going away, they would call and "this called to that" etc., namely the triple kedushah, and they translate it because of the word. The same applies twice corresponding to the kedushah of Yotzer in prayer; and even though now the decree is nullified, nevertheless the kedushah of the order was not nullified, for they fear lest sin cause etc. — end of his words. And so too in Levush: therefore they then preceded saying Uva Letziyon Go'el, for we apologize before Heaven for having delayed kedushah until now because subjugation to the kingdom causes us to be prevented from saying it within the prayer; therefore we pray for redemption and say Uva Letziyon etc.; and likewise they prevent us from engaging in Torah, therefore they also say the verse "And I, this is My covenant" etc. to remind before Him, may He be blessed, that He promised us the Torah would not depart from us; and afterward "He sits — the praises of Israel" — they expounded from this that Heaven rests His Shechinah among us to hear the praises of Israel; and "sits" is language of dwelling, and we say this in language of supplication even though we finished it, for it is not in rebellion and treachery, therefore He forgives that He delay to hear our prayer as is His way, may He be blessed; and if so, properly there is connection of the two verses to kedushah; nevertheless this is not the essence of saying kedushah, but according to what is written in tractate Sotah daf 49: from the day the Temple was destroyed there is no day whose curse is not greater than its fellow's; and the world — on what does it stand? Since the curse goes and increases, we say the kedushah of the order and Amen Yehei Shemei Rabbah of the aggadah; and Rashi explained: the kedushah of the order was instituted because all Israel engage in Torah every day a small matter that they say — the verse and its translation (for then they understood the translation), and behold it is as if engaging in Torah; and since it is practiced among all Israel etc., and here there are two sanctifications of the Name and Torah study is beloved — end of his words. And for this reason there is no continuation of the two verses of Uva Letziyon etc. to kedushah; and according to this, properly one should precede saying kedushah with the congregation.

And see in Yalkut parashat Ekev remez 955 at the end, where he gave a reason for the kedushah of the order; and it is also a kind of reason of the Gemara in Sotah mentioned above.

On Aleinu etc., one should say "moshav yekaro bashamayim" (whose dwelling is in the heavens) and not say "v'chisei kevodo bashamayim" (and His throne of glory is in the heavens). The Bach wrote the reason: how can one say "and His throne of glory is in the heavens" — behold, the heavens themselves are His throne of glory, as it is written in Isaiah "The heavens are My throne" etc.

For so it is written, and it is good to adopt the language of the Scripture, as Tosafos wrote in Berachos 42b s.v. b'bara etc.
