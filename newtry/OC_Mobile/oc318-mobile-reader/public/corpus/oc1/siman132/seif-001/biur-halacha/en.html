Pamphlet — article on kaddish, in which the laws of kaddish are explained briefly, collected from Levush, Magen Avraham, Knesset Yechezkel, Derech HaChayyim, Magen Giborim, and other later authorities. There are five categories in saying kaddish, and they are: a son of seven days, a son of thirty days, a son of twelve months, the day of cessation [namely that on it one stops saying kaddish], and yahrzeit. Each has its own rank. The reason for these categories and their rank appears to be that the Sages established them per the measure of strict judgment, and kaddish is to protect. Therefore the first seven days need more mercy and merit; thirty needs more mercy than twelve months. Yahrzeit is for two reasons: either that on that day the mourner's fortune was impaired, as Levush wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 402 seif 12; or so that there be satisfaction for his father and mother to protect them and atone for their souls. The day of cessation — mourners practiced to give him all the kaddishim because since they practice stopping a month early, and in return that they waive a month of days for them, they gave him this precedence; and for this reason he does not take precedence to push aside a yahrzeit and a son of thirty, for they gain nothing through him, for otherwise they have a law of precedence over him. One counts the seven and thirty only from the day of burial, even if much time passed between death and burial. One does not count from the day of death nor from the day it became known to him, as Rama wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 376 seif 4, because the main laws of Gehinnom begin from closing the gravestone. Yahrzeit is counted from the day of death, as written in Yoreh De'ah siman 402 seif 12 in the gloss regarding fasts. There is no difference whether he was at the death or only at the burial, as Levush wrote there, even in the first year. The day of cessation — it appears from Levush that one counts from the day of burial; but if there are many days between death and burial, one counts only from the day of burial. We do not say "part of a day is like a whole day" regarding seven and thirty, for even at Minchah prayer he still has the merit of seven and thirty, as the later authorities wrote. They rule to say kaddish a month early before the yahrzeit, and one day before the yahrzeit day in the month after it — namely if his yahrzeit falls on the 7th of Shevat, he must say kaddish only until six days in Tevet inclusive. And if the year is leap, they stop two months early, for the months caused exemption of the law from him, and they stop also one day before as above. A great son of seven who goes on Shabbat to synagogue — even if he made a minyan in his house on weekdays — nevertheless he has all the kaddishim and pushes aside a son of thirty and yahrzeit, and all the more so a son of twelve months from all kaddishim; and these are prayer kaddishim, but the song of the day or Tehillim kaddish — the yahrzeit takes one kaddish without lots, and the rest of Tehillim kaddishim the yahrzeit casts lots with the other mourners [Knesset Yechezkel]. A small son of seven who goes all seven to synagogue [and likewise a great one if he sits the days of mourning in synagogue] has all the kaddishim always, even against a son of thirty; only for a yahrzeit he has one kaddish; and if there are many yahrzeits against all the kaddishim, the small son of seven is completely deferred. If mourning of seven was nullified from him because of a festival, nevertheless all seven days from the day of burial he has the law of seven regarding kaddish [and likewise a festival does not nullify from him the law of a son of thirty]; nevertheless he has only the law of a small son of seven, since he goes all seven to synagogue. And likewise if his father died on a festival, he has the law of a small son of seven regarding kaddish on the festival; and after the festival, even though he observes mourning, nevertheless he has no law of seven regarding kaddish, only that they count seven days from the day of burial, and afterward he has only the law of a son of thirty. One who has greater merit regarding kaddish, such as a son of seven and a son of thirty and a yahrzeit, has merit regarding prayer to pray also before the amud. Regarding kaddish within seven: guest and resident are equal in all kaddishim, and a guest son of seven pushes aside a son of thirty and yahrzeit residents. A great son of seven and a small son of seven are equal; nevertheless if there is another yahrzeit and there are three kaddishim, then the yahrzeit is completely deferred and the great son of seven says two kaddishim and the small son of seven one kaddish, for the great one can say to the small one "I am not taking the kaddish from you but from the yahrzeit, for I precede him for kaddish," and so too above. A son of thirty has all obligatory kaddishim against a mourner of twelve months; but Tehillim kaddish or song of the day or of the psalm of Chanukah dedication belongs to mourners of twelve months, and likewise kaddish of spreading Shema that they make before the door. And a son of thirty with a yahrzeit — the yahrzeit says one kaddish at Maariv and the son of thirty two kaddishim at Shacharit and Minchah [and if the yahrzeit did not come to synagogue at Maariv, he says the first kaddish at Shacharit]; and if there are many yahrzeits, the son of thirty is deferred from all. And a son of thirty pushes aside the day of cessation not to say more than one kaddish [Rabbi Akiva Eiger and Knesset Yechezkel]; and if he is a son of thirty for his father and a son of twelve months for his mother, he has only the kaddishim of a son of thirty, for one remembrance applies here and there; but if there are several yahrzeits such that the son of thirty is completely deferred, he has at least kaddish in Tehillim kaddishim like other mourners of twelve months [Knesset Yechezkel 215, not like Magen Avraham in the name of Maharan, and so is primary]. A yahrzeit pushes aside a mourner of twelve months from all prayers and obligatory kaddishim from that day; and Tehillim kaddishim and song of the day and of the psalm of Chanukah dedication he cannot push aside to the mourners. And kaddish of the psalm "A song for the Shabbat day" — if his yahrzeit fell on Friday, certainly he has no share in them; and if the yahrzeit fell on Shabbat, it implies from Rabbi Akiva Eiger that it belongs to the yahrzeit; but now the custom has expanded that a yahrzeit has only the kaddish of Aleinu [Peri Megadim], and in Knesset Yechezkel it implies that they cast lots among mourners and yahrzeit; and so too in the kaddish said after the five megillot or in the rabbinic kaddish said after "With what does one light." If there are two equal mourners, such as of seven or of thirty or twelve months of yahrzeit, they cast lots between them; and whoever the lot fell on him to say at Maariv — the second has one kaddish at Shacharit without lots, and on the third kaddish they cast lots [and likewise if there are many they cast lots, and whoever says Maariv by virtue of the lot is not placed in the lot until the other mourners each say one kaddish, and when it returns, heaven forbid, they all cast lots together anew]; and all this applies when they cannot pray before the amud; but if both can pray before the amud and both wish to lead, they cast lots between them in this manner: one prays until Ashrei and Uva Letziyon and the second prays Ashrei and Uva Letziyon; and if one cannot pray and the other can pray, nevertheless the one who prayed did not lose his merit in kaddishim, since the other cannot pray; and likewise if one wishes to lead and the other does not wish — it is as if he cannot pray. Two who have a yahrzeit and one wishes to leave early on the road tomorrow and said to him "give me now the kaddish of Maariv and you take tomorrow," and the second says "no, rather let us cast lots" — the law is with the second, for he can say "I wish to advance myself to the mitzvah." If there are several yahrzeits and they cast lots and two had equal large letters in the count, the lot is completely void and they cast lots anew — all the yahrzeits; however, if one had a large letter that gives him the count of the lot and after him two are equal, then the first won and the others cast lots — all of them, even the one who had a letter smaller than these two and did not win, only the first. One who had a yahrzeit and could not say kaddish, such as that he was on the road or kaddish did not reach him, may say kaddish at Maariv prayer of the day after the yahrzeit. One who forgot the day of his father's and mother's death chooses for himself one day in the year on which to say kaddish, provided he not seize others' rights in kaddishim. And likewise if he is in doubt whether his father and mother died, he may say kaddish out of doubt if he does not steal from others. One who always prays in synagogue or house of study — if he comes to say kaddish in another synagogue, the mourners there may push him aside, even if he is within seven, for he ranks below a guest because he has no place to pray and this one has a place to pray. When there are brothers in synagogue who say kaddish after their father or mother and there is another mourner who is alone for his father — they all divide by lots together, and we do not consider those brothers sons of one man as one man, for all are obligated to say kaddish for honor of father and mother. Magen Avraham wrote in the name of responsa of Rama that if one has no fitting son, one should give kaddish to his son's son; but the other mourners say two kaddishim and he says one kaddish, and they follow the custom on this. The mourner prays before the amud even on days when they do not say Tachanun; but on days when they do not say Lamnatzeiach and Aleinu, the mourner may not pray before the amud; however on the eve of Yom Kippur the custom is that a mourner prays before the amud. Magen Avraham wrote: it is better for his son to hire one to say kaddish in his place than that one say for free; and see in responsa of Chatam Sofer that one may not hire merit in place of mourners. And now we will explain somewhat the laws of resident and guest. If both are sons of thirty or sons of twelve months or both yahrzeit — the resident pushes aside the guest completely from praying and saying kaddish at the amud [but in spreading Shema and kaddish at the door both are equal and divide by lots]; nevertheless the first time there is permission for a guest son of twelve months to pray and say orphan's kaddish in place of a resident son of twelve months, and so too every thirty days there is permission for a guest to pray and say orphan's kaddish once. Yahrzeit resident and son of thirty guest — the guest says kaddish third and the resident says the first and second kaddish. Yahrzeit guest and son of thirty resident or son of twelve months resident — the yahrzeit guest has only one kaddish, namely the first kaddish. Son of thirty guest and son of twelve months resident — both are equal and divide in all prayer kaddishim. If there is a son of thirty resident and yahrzeit resident and yahrzeit guest — the yahrzeit guest also has one kaddish, and the yahrzeit resident cannot say to the yahrzeit guest "I am first against you," for the yahrzeit guest says "I am not taking from you but from the son of thirty"; and the yahrzeit resident says the first kaddish and the yahrzeit guest kaddish second and the son of thirty kaddish third. And now we will explain what is called guest and resident: if the deceased has children in another city and they came there, they are not called residents on account of their father who lived here and was also buried here — only those who dwell within the community; and all who dwell in the community are called residents, whether those who pay tax or servants of the community, even the poor of the city who have permanent residence — all we consider as residents. If a person establishes his residence within the community, then from the time he is obligated to bear the burden with the residents of the community he is considered a resident. If the homeowner has a teacher or youths studying in yeshiva, or a shamash hired for a time and he is free that he has no wife and children — he too is called a resident; but if he has a wife and children in another place, his law is like a guest. And if the homeowner raises in his house an orphan even for pay and he has no father and mother in another place — he is called a resident; but if he has a father and mother in another place he is not called a resident, even if he raises him as charity, all the more so if he is with him for pay.
