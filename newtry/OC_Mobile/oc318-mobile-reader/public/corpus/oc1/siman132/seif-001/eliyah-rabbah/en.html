(1) Kedushah etc. Levush wrote that what they instituted "Answer us on the day of trouble" before the order of kedushah is because kedushah was instituted through trouble; and R' David Abudraham wrote that what they instituted at the end of the prayer is as found in Midrash Tehillim: when a person prays they say to him "May your prayer be accepted," and therefore David said it after eighteen psalms — "For happy is the man" and "Why do the nations rage" are one — until here. Kolbo wrote siman 13 that they say it in a whisper except the first verse, because written matters one may not say by heart, and the first verse is very familiar. R' David Abudraham wrote: "You are holy, sitting" — language of waiting and preparing for the prayers of Israel before the angels; or that Heaven waits until the last one among Israel enters and accepts their prayers together. R' David Abudraham wrote: the multitude with those who say kaddish "le'olam ule'olmei olmaya" is an error; rather one must say kaddish "be'olam ule'olmei olmaya," meaning in the world of angels, which is separate and apart from them; and if you say, since He is so separate, all the more so He would not set His eyes on the act of man — "Heaven of hosts" said "the whole earth is full of His glory" etc. And if you say, why did they institute in this kedushah "Heaven shall reign" that Moshe said, and in the standing kedushah they say "Heaven shall reign forever" that David said — one may say that because we translate this order and there is no translation for written matters; and the Rosh wrote that he had a tradition that on translating the verse "Heaven shall reign forever" a heavenly voice went out and said to Yonatan ben Uziel: Who is he who reveals the secrets of men? Because there is an end in it — until here; and Kolbo wrote: one says "Heaven shall reign" in order to translate that of Onkelos, which is greater than Aquilas. Levush wrote: this, that the custom is not to say kedushah on Shabbat and Yom Tov at Shacharit because it is in language of supplication — his words are strained, for behold he said it at Minchah in language that they say every day. Rather it appears to me the main reason, as Moshe Mat wrote — this is his language: I found written that they instituted it at the end because of the amei ha'aretz who delay coming to prayer, so that they not lose hearing kedushah; and they instituted for them a prayer like a small prayer within the prayer — Ashrei, and afterward kedushah and remembrance of Zion and Torah study; therefore in this order of kedushah there are eighteen mentions corresponding to eighteen blessings; therefore they do not say it on Shabbat and Yom Tov at Shacharit because everyone comes to synagogue before kedushah; and what they practiced to say it at Minchah is because they would preach before Minchah and it was customary to speak at the end of the sermon in verses of redemption and kedushah, and also amei ha'aretz come to hear the sermon — until here. And so I saw in Abudraham, and therefore it is in the language of translation so that amei ha'aretz understand; and according to this it appears that those who delay must intend this kedushah even while occupied with another matter. Magen Avraham wrote: if one comes to synagogue at the time when they say the order of kedushah, he should say it with them even before his prayer; and the same law applies that he may say it before he says the second verses, namely Uva Letziyon etc. and "And I, this is My covenant" etc., and afterward he says the second verses; and all the more so that he may say Ashrei and Lamnatzeiach etc. afterward; and see the beginning of siman 292. When one says "Blessed is He, our Heaven, Who created us" etc., one should say it with intention and joy of heart; pause between "those who seek You, Heaven" and "Heaven desires." Abudraham wrote in the laws of Tishah BeAv that one does not say in a house of mourning "And I, this is My covenant," for a mourner is forbidden in words of Torah.
