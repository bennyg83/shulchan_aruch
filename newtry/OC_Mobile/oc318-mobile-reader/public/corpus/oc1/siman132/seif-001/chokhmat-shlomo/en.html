Seif 2 in the gloss: and there are those who wrote to be careful to say pitum haketoret from the text and not by heart etc. Noda B'Yehuda: behold this is only on the understanding that is simple from what is written "whoever occupies himself with the portion of the olah, it is as if he offered an olah" — namely as if he offered it himself; so too he said regarding pitum haketoret "and if one omitted one of its spices he is liable to death." But according to what is written in my composition Orach Chayyim siman 1, and I proved that speech is not literally like offering but only as if he handed it to the kohen to offer — and so I found in Tosafos Shabbos — and it is recognized, for behold we are certainly impure from the dead and laymen; therefore he is certainly not liable to death if he hands incense missing one of its spices to the kohen; and perhaps this is Beit Yosef's reason, who brought Magen Avraham who wrote that one is not liable to death through speech; and perhaps it is from this reason, and understand well. (There) in the gloss: and they say orphan's kaddish after Aleinu etc. Noda B'Yehuda: behold a case came before me of one who had a doubt whether his father died on the 20th of the month or the 21st, and in any case on one of the two days he died; and he had a doubt whether his father died on the 19th or the 20th or the 21st; and on the 19th there were other yahrzeits and the one in doubt could not say kaddish; and on the 20th and 21st both doubts came to say kaddish, and this one in doubt claims that he is in doubt only on these two days that he will say the kaddishim on these two days, for then on one day he will certainly say as required, but on the second even on both days it remains doubt; therefore he is preferable. I was asked what his law is, and behold I saw in responsa Me'ir Netivim, who was asked on this and brought proof from Yevamos daf 96 regarding five whose offspring were mixed up, that we do not say one should perform yibum for all of them — it proves that if there is doubt that perhaps each one fulfills the mitzvah, it is preferable to what fulfills only one alone — see there. And in my humble opinion this is not proof: one, that they say there according to what Tosafos wrote in Pesachim regarding five whose Pesach hides were mixed up, who challenged there that behold semichah is not indispensable, and they answered that it is better one enter in doubt of karet than the majority act improperly — see there; it is implied explicitly from their words that there is a difference whether there were only two between whom there is doubt which acted improperly, and whether the majority acted properly — then they may not now act improperly for the minority who has doubt of karet; if so, here too perhaps there specifically because there are five in this manner, for it is impossible to fulfill the mitzvah with certainty except if one performs yibum for all; and if so, if we say to fulfill the mitzvah with certainty, only one would act and he is the minority among all; but if each performs yibum for one, they make each a doubtful mitzvah, and a doubtful mitzvah is also a mitzvah; therefore it is more preferable that the majority do a doubtful mitzvah than the minority do a certain mitzvah; and especially since there it applies per the one who says the mitzvah of yibum takes precedence — if so, if we say they all perform chalitzah, they would certainly act improperly; therefore since the majority would not fulfill the mitzvah of yibum but rather the majority would act improperly and perform chalitzah for their yevamah, therefore the majority may not act improperly for the individual who would act properly. And perhaps even there, if there were only two mixed up, we would say one should perform yibum for all and one would do a certain mitzvah rather than both do doubt; and even if we say that also with two the law is so that each performs yibum for one and chalitzah for one — nevertheless it is not similar to here, for if we say one performs chalitzah for all, then this one certainly acts improperly per the one who says the mitzvah of yibum takes precedence; if so, just as this one would do a certain mitzvah, so the two would certainly act improperly in reverse; and whatever they act improperly is only through this one acting properly; therefore what is the strength of this from that that this one should certainly act improperly that this one should certainly act properly — therefore both should do doubt. But in our case, if the one in doubt does not say kaddish on the 20th and 21st, he is not certainly acting improperly, for perhaps it is not his yahrzeit at all but his time was on the 19th of the month; if so, this one only does doubt of acting improperly, not certainly; and this second one would act certainly properly; and even though he did not say kaddish on the 19th, if so this one too would act certainly improperly — nevertheless what he did not say on the 19th did not cause him but another, and not through this one acting certainly properly did he cause this one to act certainly improperly, but through another; but through him there accrued to him only doubt of acting improperly; therefore here the certain is preferable to the doubt. But there, whatever this one acts improperly is only through this one acting properly; therefore what is the strength of this from that? And further, there it was active to say to this one to act and perform chalitzah in order to perform yibum for one — there the other can say "what is your certainty to me that I should act improperly so that you act properly, and my doubt is preferable to your certainty" — a person is close to himself. But here we do not force the second to act, but it is sit and do not act, and we are in doubt about the congregation praying there to whom they will give more kaddish, to this one or that one; therefore regarding others to whom both are equal one like the other, we say it is more fitting to give kaddish to this one who will say certainly than to this one who will say only in doubt; and in my humble opinion it is correct, not like the opinion of Sefer responsa Me'ir Netivim mentioned above, and understand well, and so I found.
