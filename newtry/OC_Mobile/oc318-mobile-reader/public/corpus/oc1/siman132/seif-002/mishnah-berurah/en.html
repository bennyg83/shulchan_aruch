(ה) To leave the synagogue, etc. — for since the world is sustained through this, one must be careful not to treat it lightly. And according to what we explained above that it is proper to return to say them with the congregation, automatically one must be careful not to leave beforehand, even if he wishes to say them by himself, unless in a time of pressing need.

(ו) Kedusha de-sidra — this is the kedusha of Uva le-Tzion.

(ז) Aleinu — Magen Avraham wrote in the name of the Ari that they should say it after all three prayers; and so is the custom in our country, except in large synagogues where they pray Maariv close to Minchah (and see below siman 235) — they do not say Aleinu after the conclusion of Minchah, for Aleinu that they say after Maariv applies also to Minchah [Magen Giborim in the name of Seder ha-Yom]. And there are several versions of Aleinu, brought in Or Zarua, and one should not change any version, for each version has a foundation [there].

(ח) With intention — and one should say Aleinu with awe and fear, for the entire host of heaven listens, and the Holy One, blessed be He, stands with the heavenly retinue, and all respond and say, "Happy is the people for whom it is thus; happy is the people," etc. [Magen Avraham].

(ט) "And we bow" — and one must bow so that he not appear like a heretic, heaven forbid.

(י) Kaddish Yatom after Aleinu — for always, after verses are said, Kaddish must be said; and in Aleinu there are also verses, and Kaddish is needed after it. However, they accustomed themselves with this Kaddish to leave it for an orphan whose father and mother died, because there are small orphans, or even adults, who cannot be agents of the congregation to say Kaddish and Barchu after their father and mother [for if they were able to pray before the amud, that is better than saying Kaddish]. And it is already known from the incident of R' Akiva the great benefit for the dead when he has a son who says Kaddish and Barchu, and especially within the first year; therefore they enacted and left this Kaddish, which requires nothing more from orphans, whether small or great. And at the end of the siman I copied in Biur Halacha all the laws of Kaddish in brief, under the title Ma'amar Kaddishin Yikra.

(11) He may say it if [his parents] are not particular, etc. — meaning specifically when he estimates that they will not be particular about this. And it is reasonable that all this is regarding Kaddish Yatom, because this Kaddish is designated for orphans; but to pray before the amud or to lead Keriat Shema, he need not be concerned at all that they might be particular about this.

(12) And one should say, etc. — because the ketoret was offered twice each day, as written in the Torah.

(13) Evening — meaning after Minchah, or before it, for the ketoret was not offered at night.

(14) And morning after the prayer — and it requires study why we say it on Shabbat and Yom Tov after Musaf, whereas the ketoret precedes the musaf offerings according to all opinions, and it pertains to the tamid offering. It is possible that our intention is to take leave from words of Torah; and in writings it states that the reason is to drive away the kelipot; and in Shelah it is written to say it before prayer and after it.

(15) And they say the song, etc. — see in Magen Avraham, who concludes that the Rama's intention is to say each day the song belonging to that day (meaning: on Sunday the Levites would sing for David, "A psalm of Heaven — the earth and its fullness," etc., until the end of the psalm; and likewise each day its psalm), but his intention is not that they say the mishnah of the song that the Levites would say, etc. Nevertheless the world is accustomed to say this mishnah after Pitum ha-Ketoret, as printed in the siddurim.

(16) Only at Shacharit — for although in the Temple they would say song over the wine libations also for the afternoon tamid, nevertheless because several times the priests were busy and would bring the libations of bein ha-arbayim at night, and at night they do not say song — therefore the custom is to say the song only at Shacharit. Which psalm is customary on Yom Tov for the song of the day will be explained later in the laws of Yom Tov.

(17) Lest he skip — and in Beit Yosef he questions this, for there is death only through burning, not through recitation; and furthermore, death applies only when intentional; therefore the meticulous are accustomed to say it every day [Magen Avraham]. And some wrote that those who say it only on Shabbat do not have it so fixed in their mouths, and therefore they say it from the siddur.

(18) And he bows and leaves — for in the Temple too, when they finished the service they would bow and leave; and see in Magen Avraham.
