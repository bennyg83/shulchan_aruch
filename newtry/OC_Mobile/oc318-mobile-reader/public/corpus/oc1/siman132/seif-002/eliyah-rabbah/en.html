[2] An individual recites it, etc. This alludes to what Beit Yosef wrote in siman 59 in the name of the Zohar, that a passage in the language of Targum should not be recited communally. He gave the mnemonic, "Two readings of Scripture and one of Targum." Perishah explains that Scripture, which is in Lashon HaKodesh, is recited specifically by "two," meaning communally, and not literally only two people, but at least ten; Targum, however, is recited by an individual. This requires study, for we do not rule like the Zohar, since even the Lashon HaKodesh portions may be recited by an individual. If that element is rejected, this one should be rejected as well. Perhaps for this reason Levush gave another explanation. His words make clear that according to the Zohar, two do not suffice for the Kedushah in Yotzer; ten are required. Beit Yosef in siman 59 likewise appears to accept Rashba's view that the Zohar requires ten. See there. This excludes Olas Tamid, who writes that according to the Zohar two or more may recite it. See siman 59, where Levush writes that he saw in the Zohar that the Targum should be recited quietly. This is inaccurate. Rather, Beit Yosef writes that since it is recited by an individual, it should be said quietly. I found in Abudraham that the custom to recite this order quietly is because of the verse "Hashem shall reign forever," which Moshe said. It properly should have been placed first, before "Holy," yet they placed it last, following the pattern of the Kedushos in which kingship comes at the end. Proof of this is that the chazzan recites the first verses aloud with their translations, whereas the verse "Hashem shall reign" and its translation are recited quietly.
