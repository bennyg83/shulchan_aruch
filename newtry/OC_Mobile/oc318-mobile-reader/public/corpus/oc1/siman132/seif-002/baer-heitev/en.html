(1) We should praise. In the scriptures he wrote that they should say it after every prayer from 3 prayers and so on in Kena 3 C. Rald, but in the book of two hands in the name of Toulat Ya'akov he wrote that in the offering and at the end of us he will say a verse and the the Omnipresent will be for the king and so on. He will say and a meeting will happen. 11 He is our God, there is no more Shabat. And we went up in fear and awe because the whole army of the matter is listening and the Almighty is standing with a retinue from above and they all answer and say blessed are the people who are blessed by the people that the the Omnipresent is his God from Tmm.

(ב) And we bow. "And he shall bow and prostrate" — a great prostration. The Arizal — Magen Avraham.

(ג) Orphan's kaddish. One who is in doubt whether his father or mother died may say kaddish when there is no other mourner there — Maharil. Sons of sons may say kaddish, but the other mourners say two kaddishim, and they are kaddish 1; and there is no difference whether they are from the father's side or the mother's side. And in responsa of Knesset Yechezkel he wrote that specifically a son's son, but a daughter's son does not say kaddish, and all the more so a daughter has no kaddish at all in the synagogue; and if they wish to make a minyan for her, permission is in their hands — see there end of part 14. A yahrzeit has all the kaddishim of that day, even the kaddish of Perakim and of Ruth and of Shir HaShirim — Rambam siman 80, and so too Magen Avraham. And in Knesset Yechezkel there he wrote that everything obligatory, such as prayer kaddish and the kaddish of Shir HaYichud, and likewise Barchi Nafshi and Perakim, belongs to the yahrzeit. But Megillat Kohelet, Ruth, and Shir HaShirim they cast lots, since they are only a mere custom; they are similar to the kaddish of Tehillim and the song of the day, and they cast lots. And the kaddish that they say on Friday night after the psalm "A song for the Shabbat day" is for mourners, unless the yahrzeit is in the company of receiving Shabbat, and everything is according to custom. And in Knesset Yechezkel there he wrote that a yahrzeit that fell on Friday has no connection at all; only mourners with a yahrzeit that falls on Shabbat cast lots — see there. When they cast lots among four or five and among them are two who have equal letters, such as tav, and the others have a smaller count than that, then the lot is void and they all need to cast lots anew, and the two cannot say "the others have already lost their share, we both cast lots." But if one has tav and the others are equal and have less, then certainly the first one won. If two or three cast lots and the next day another one came, the division is void and they need to cast lots anew, and the one who said kaddish at Maariv is not in the lot; and if he was in the city and did not come, he lost his right, for since he did not come he certainly waived to them that he be last. If one said kaddish and the next day another came and said "I say today corresponding to the kaddish you said yesterday, and on the third kaddish we will cast lots," and that one says "since you did not come yesterday I won the kaddish, and the two of today we will divide between us" — the law is with the second, and he says the first kaddish and on the second they cast lots. However, if there are four yahrzeits and three came yesterday, the three say all the prayer kaddishim and the fourth says the kaddish of the song of the day. And if there are many yahrzeits, even though they say all the kaddishim, there is not enough to divide among all; the one who did not come at Maariv lost his right. All the poskim agree that even though one can pray and the other cannot, regarding kaddish the one who prayed did not lose; and if both can pray, they cast lots who is first and who is second. Two who have a yahrzeit and one wishes to leave early on the road tomorrow and said "give me now the kaddish and you take tomorrow," and the second says "no, rather let us cast lots" — the law is with the second, for he can say "I wish to advance myself to the mitzvah." Two compelled ones: one was killed and one died — the son of the killed one takes precedence over the son of the one who died naturally, for since he was killed he has some atonement and he certainly repented before his death, which is not so with one who died on his own. When there is a son of thirty days, they give him all the kaddishim, and the kaddish of the psalm for mourners. In places where the custom is that a resident takes precedence over a guest, nevertheless once there is permission for a guest to pray and say kaddish; and Shach wrote that if there is a son of thirty days, a guest has no kaddish at all. One who learns in a yeshiva has the law of a resident. And a teacher, if he has no wife in another place, is called a resident. And see in Knesset Yechezkel what he wrote on this. And regarding kaddish we do not say "part of a day is like the shell day." And even though one person's relative died in the middle of the week and another's died on Sunday, both are equal on Shabbat. One who mourned his father and afterward his mother died has all the kaddishim by the law of thirty days, and nevertheless he divides the other kaddishim on account of his father with the other mourners — Magen Avraham in the name of Maharan, and Knesset Yechezkel challenged him that if so, one who mourned his father and mother would say kaddish double relative to other mourners, and this is not so, for one remembrance applies here and there; and he resolves it, for example if he is a son of thirty days, then the prayer kaddishim are his and the sons of twelve months say Tehillim kaddish and the like; and if it happened on some day that there are several yahrzeits, then the son of thirty is completely deferred from prayer kaddishim — in this one may say that he divides with the mourners in the other kaddishim on account of his father, but otherwise he does not say kaddish double relative to other mourners, for one remembrance applies here and there. A great son of seven who goes on Shabbat to the synagogue takes precedence over a yahrzeit, and all the more so over a son of thirty; but a small son of seven has the law of thirty and a yahrzeit has one kaddish — Mishna Berurah siman 73, and Shach siman 376 wrote that even for a son of thirty there is one kaddish against a small son of seven, and Magen Avraham ruled that the small one takes precedence over the son of thirty completely, only for a yahrzeit there is one kaddish. One whose mourning was nullified on a festival, or who buried his dead on a festival, has the law of seven and the law of a small seven, since he goes all seven to the synagogue, and a yahrzeit has one kaddish. And if on Shabbat there is a small seven, a great seven, and a yahrzeit, the great one says two kaddishim and the small one one kaddish, so that the great one may say to the small one "I am not taking the third kaddish from you, but from the yahrzeit." In Orach Chayyim there is a doubt whether a great son of seven who makes a minyan in his house perhaps equals on Shabbat a small son of seven and a yahrzeit has one kaddish; and we do not divide on this and we practice as written. A son of thirty has all the prayer kaddishim, and the other mourners of twelve months say Tehillim kaddishim and the like. A yahrzeit resident and a son of thirty resident — a yahrzeit has one kaddish at Maariv, and a son of thirty two kaddishim at Shacharit. A yahrzeit guest and a son of thirty or a son of twelve months resident — a yahrzeit guest has one kaddish. A guest son of thirty and a twelve-month resident divide all the prayer kaddishim. A yahrzeit resident and a yahrzeit guest — the resident takes precedence over the guest. A yahrzeit resident, a yahrzeit guest, and a son of thirty resident — then the guest says one kaddish at Shacharit from the two kaddishim that belong to the son of thirty, and the yahrzeit resident cannot say to the yahrzeit guest "I am first against you," for the guest says "I am not taking from you, but from the son of thirty." If a yahrzeit resident and a yahrzeit guest regarding Tehillim kaddish for which they cast lots — the yahrzeit with the mourners: if the yahrzeit is a resident he waives the lot so that the yahrzeit guest says one kaddish; the mourners may not push back, for against them a yahrzeit guest comes first. However, if they cast lots and the mourners won, the yahrzeit guest says "my luck caused it"; but a yahrzeit guest is with them in the lot, for against them he is as one of them. And in the spreading of Shema before the door, namely the minyan that they make in the ezrah before the door, guest and resident are equal; however, the kaddish of Tehillim and the song of the day and other psalms, and the kaddish after Shir HaShirim and Ruth and Kohelet — a guest has no share, only a yahrzeit, as I wrote. The common custom on the day when they stop saying kaddish at the completion of eleven months — he says all the kaddishim; however, he does not take precedence over a yahrzeit and a son of thirty, and he stops saying kaddish a day or before and not on the same day as the yahrzeit. For example, if one died on the 3rd of Shevat, he must stop on the 2nd of Tevet, for the reason they stop is so that one not think his father wicked; and if he says on the 3rd of Tevet it will be the first day of the twelfth month and it will be considered a month, as if he said twelve months — therefore he says on the 2nd of Tevet and stops on that day, and the 3rd of Shevat is the yahrzeit. A yahrzeit is always the day of death and not the day of burial, even if the burial was delayed many days, even in the first year. If his mother died and the father objects that he not say kaddish, he has no power to protest. A guest who said one kaddish in some synagogue has no further kaddish in any synagogue of this community until the thirtieth day. All this I brought from the words of early and late poskim; and see in Knesset Yechezkel end of part 14, and see in Sefer Eliyah Rabbah, who brought several laws and responsa on these matters.

(ד) Evening and morning after the prayer. Taz challenged, for it is stated in the Gemara: you have nothing that precedes the morning tamid except the incense alone, and they instituted prayer corresponding to the tamidim — if so, we should precede pitum haketoret before the prayer of eighteen. See there what he raised, and according to this I practiced myself to say pitum haketoret before Baruch She'amar until here. And Magen Avraham challenged: why do we say it after Musaf, and behold the incense preceded the musafim for everyone? And he resolves that perhaps our intention is to take leave from words of Torah — see there. And in Shelah he wrote to say it at Shacharit before the prayer and after the prayer. And in the evening one says before Minchah prayer, but not after Maariv prayer, for burning incense is invalid after the lighting of candles — see Knesset HaGedolah and Magen Avraham and in Sefer Be'er Sheva.

(ה) The song that the Levites. I found written that what Magen Avraham wrote from the mishnah "the song that the Levites" etc. is an error; for the Tur's intention is to say each day the song belonging to that day, and so too Rambam in the order of prayer and so too Knesset HaGedolah; and Magen Avraham wrote, it appears to me that the Tur's intention is to say the mishnah as well in order to take leave from words of Torah — see there — and he concludes that on Shabbat the custom is to say the mishnah as printed in all siddurim. Knesset HaGedolah wrote: I saw a custom in all places on Rosh Chodesh and the intermediate days of the festival and Chanukah and Purim — in place of the song that the Levites would say, they say another song related to the day: on Rosh Chodesh Barchi Nafshi; on the intermediate days of Pesach Hodu LaHashem; on the intermediate days of the festival "As a hart panteth"; on Chanukah the psalm "A song of the dedication of the House to David"; and on Purim "Upon the hind of the dawn." And I wonder who permitted them to change the song that the Levites would say every day — see there what he raised, that one should not skip the song the Levites would say every day, but rather that they say the song the Levites would say every day and the song related to the day, and so too the Rama siman 25. However, some give precedence there to the song of each day first, for frequent precedes infrequent; but on Chanukah one says the psalm of the dedication of the House before the psalm that the Levites would say every day because of publicizing the miracle — see there, and see Magen Avraham.

(ו) Perhaps he will skip. See what Rabbi Be'er Sheva challenged in tractate Kerisos daf 46 on Beit Yosef this siman, and so too in Taz and Magen Avraham. And in responsa Sha'ar Efrayim siman 16 he wishes to resolve Beit Yosef — see there — and it is not so the implication of Beit Yosef's words, for from Beit Yosef it implies that even if one did not put in it a herb that produces smoke for which one is not liable to death — see there — and this is the opposite of the words of the Rabbi author of Sha'ar Efrayim, and it requires further study; and see in Sefer Mishneh LaMelech, and in Sefer Mikra'ei Kodesh daf 125 side b, and in Sefer Birchas HaZevach, and in Peri Chadash (and see in Sefer Eliyah Rabbah, who expanded greatly on this). "Water of feet" mentioned in pitum haketoret — Kolbo wrote that there is one spring whose name is mei raglayim. And what Magen Avraham wrote "because of honor" means that that spring has a disgraceful name; and in Be'er Sheva he expanded to prove that he literally means mei raglayim — see there. And see in Bnei Yaakov.

(ז) And he prostrates himself and goes out. Maharil made three prostrations when he went from his place toward the Holy Ark, and when going out from the door of the synagogue each time like a student taking leave of his teacher; when he goes out from the synagogue he should not go out with his back to the sanctuary but should turn aside, and likewise when descending from the bimah — see there Magen Avraham.
