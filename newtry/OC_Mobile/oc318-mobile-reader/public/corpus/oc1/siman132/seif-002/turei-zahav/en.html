And one should say Pitum ha-Ketoret, etc., after the prayer. It is difficult for me from what we say in the chapter "Tamid nishchat" (Pesachim daf 59): the Sages taught — you have nothing preceding the morning tamid except ketoret alone, as it says, "In the morning, in the morning." And we say at the beginning of Berachot: prayers were enacted corresponding to the tamidim — Shacharit corresponding to the morning tamid; if so, we should precede Pitum ha-Ketoret to the prayer of eighteen [blessings], and why do we delay it until after the prayer? And it appears to answer: that ketoret precedes the tamid for offering it on the altar to burn it — and so Rashi explains there: "You have nothing preceding ketoret" means for the fires; but its slaughter and sprinkling — in this the tamid precedes, as Abaye [says] in the order of the arrangement: the blood of the tamid precedes the two lamps, and the lamps precede the ketoret; if so, the prayer of eighteen is in place of the service of the blood of the tamid, and therefore ketoret is after it. And further: I questioned that in the siddur of Abaye, remembrance of ketoret is very late; rather it appears to me that prayer is in place of offering the limbs — for in the place of its slaughter and sprinkling, the parashah of the tamid is in its place; for otherwise why need two things in place of the tamid — the parashah of the tamid and the prayer of eighteen? Rather certainly as I said. And there is proof from what Tur wrote, siman 235: Maariv is in place of limbs that were not consumed during the day — from which it follows that Minchah is in place of limbs burned first during the day; and accordingly I practiced myself to say the parashah of ketoret before Baruch she-amar, so that ketoret be between the blood of the tamid and the limbs; and although in Tur it is written after the prayer, it appears to me that both are correct. Who are the "feet" mentioned in Pitum ha-Ketoret — Kol Bo wrote: a spring whose name is Mei Raglayim; and what is written "because of honor" means: because that spring has a disgraceful name. Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Orchot Chaim: ma'aleh ashán is only a repair for the ascent of smoke; therefore there is not even an obligation if he did not bring it — and I overlooked Talmud Yerushalmi in the chapter "Hotzi'u lo" (Yoma daf 53): if he did not bring ma'aleh ashán, he is liable to death. Maharil wrote in the name of Mahari Polak in the name of R' Menachem of London: from that which the Yerushalmi in the chapter of Berachot states that one must wait after the prayer of Yotzer for the walk of four mil before the prayer of Musaf — therefore it appears they enacted Tehillim in the interim for individuals, and along with the individuals they enacted it also for the congregation, even though there is much waiting in the reading of the Torah. And what they are accustomed to say before the prayer, "Ashrei yoshvei" — because it is language of gift: they would wait one hour before prayer, and after prayer they would also wait one hour, as written, "The righteous shall sit before Your face" [I again found in Sefer Etz Chaim thus, siman 227]. Therefore it appears they enacted Aleinu le-Shabe'ach; and I am accustomed to say after the prayer, "Mi yoshev," "Ach tzaddikim yodu le-Shimcha," "Yeshvu yesharim et panekha" — parallel to "Ashrei yoshvei," end of his words. And after Maariv one should wait a little before Aleinu le-Shabe'ach (Rashbatz), end of his words. Further I am accustomed to say every day at the hour when the chazan says "Sim shalom," these three verses: "Al tira," "U-mesho'at resha'im," "Ki imanu El," "Ve-ad ziknah," "Ani hu," "Ani asiti," "Va-ani esbol va-ami'alet." For it is in the Midrash that Mordechai asked the children of the house of study what they were engaged in; they told him: each one a verse from these verses, and he rejoiced in the matter. And I found that in Provence they say them every day.
