When they announce something, one should announce before the chazzan begins Ashrei and not between Ashrei and Lamenatzeiach, and all the more so at the time they say Ashrei that their intention be nullified — (Amudei Shash and Sema in Choshen Mishpat siman 267, and likewise in Beit Yosef siman 455). And it appears to me that if one came to synagogue at the time they say the order of Kedushah, he should say it with them even before his prayer, for some say an individual does not say it; and likewise he can say it before he says the two verses, namely "Uva l'Tzion" etc. and "Va'ani zot briti" etc., and afterwards he says the two verses — see in Yalkut parashat Ekev; and all the more so he can say Ashrei and Lamenatzeiach afterwards in order to say it with the congregation. Aleinu: it is written in writings that they should say it after each of the three prayers; and so too the Kol Bo siman 234 that so they practice there. But in Sefer Shetei Yadot in the name of Toldot Yaakov it is written not to say it at Mincha, end of his words. And at the end of Aleinu one should say the verse "V'hayah Heaven l'Melech" etc. [Kavanos]; one should say "Umoshav yekaro" [Tur]; one should say "Hu Elokeinu ein od" for so it is written in the verse [Tashbetz].

Bow. And he should bow and prostrate so as not to appear as a denier [Emek HaBerachah], and siman 113: the Gaon in Levush wrote here the laws of kaddish, and I am adding a matter that was renewed to me — R' Moshe Minz wrote in siman 80 in a responsum that yahrzeit has all the kaddishes of that day, even kaddish of chapters and of Song of Songs and of Ruth, end of his words. And I saw the practice to cast lots on Song of Songs; it appears to me the custom spread because they did not see R' Moshe Minz's responsum, for it was not printed in those days — behold all the customs of kaddish were established according to him, and why should this law be diminished? And the kaddish they say on Friday night after the psalm "A Song for the Sabbath Day" belongs to mourners if not that the yahrzeit is in the company of kabbalas Shabbos — and everything follows custom. When they cast lots, four or five, and among them two who have equal letters such as "tav" and the others have a count less than this — then the lottery is void and all must cast lots anew, and the two cannot say to the others "You already lost your portion." And we learned "Cast lots," and proof from Yoma 22 that when they are equal the overseer says to them "Point!" and they all cast lots, and even though those two were closer than others [so I heard from the Gaon R' Meir, Av Beit Din of Krakow]. But if one has "tav" and the others are equal and have less — then certainly the first won and the others cast lots even if among them there are unequal ones as above. Two or three who cast lots, and the next day another one came — the division is void and they must cast lots anew, as written in Choshen Mishpat siman 175 se'if 3: two brothers who divided and a brother came to them from overseas — the division is void; and if he was in the city and did not come he lost his right, for query: since he did not come he certainly waived for them that he be last, as written in Choshen Mishpat siman 176 se'if 25. And likewise we say in Bava Batra chapter 9 regarding groomsmanship: if he was in the city he should have come. One said kaddish, and the next day another came and said "I say today corresponding to the kaddish you said yesterday, and on the third kaddish we cast lots," and the other says "Since you did not come yesterday I won the kaddish, and the two of today we divide between us." Some wish to compare to what is written in Choshen Mishpat siman 171 se'if 68: one of the partners who used the courtyard several years — the second cannot say "I will also use as long as you used," for all the time they did not divide each uses his own. And likewise we say he said his kaddish yesterday. And it appears to me this is not correct, for the reasoning there is that we say he used his portion and it still exists; but here one kaddish does not belong to both of them, and it is similar to two who have movable property in partnership and each ate some that his fellow eats corresponding to it — and likewise here. And there they lack money; here nothing is lacking. For if the first had not said, the mourners would say it — this is not nevertheless; he can say "I knew and relied that you would come to the house of mourning and say kaddish, therefore I stood outside." And we do not say that since he did not come he waived everything, but rather this one waived for him that he be first and he after him. The custom is widespread that when two yahrzeits cast lots on a day when there are three kaddishes, the one who wins first says two kaddishes — not as written in Sefer Tzemach Tzedek siman 26 — and they cast a new lot on the third kaddish; see end of Sukkah. Two who have yahrzeit and one wishes to rise early for travel tomorrow and said "Give me now the kaddish and you take tomorrow," and the second says "No, but let us cast lots" — it appears to me the law is with the second, for we do not compel against middas Sedom since there is benefit in insistence, lest it fall on him for Maariv and he say a second time, as written in Choshen Mishpat siman 311; and so I found siman 174 — see there. And even though the Shach there wrote specifically when damaged, nevertheless his words are not compelled, and also it is not similar to this case; and further he can say "I wish to advance myself to the mitzvah," for when three cast lots we do not say each should say one kaddish, but because each wishes to advance himself to the mitzvah. It implies in Beit Yosef siman 111 that it is better for our son to hire one to say kaddish in his place than one say for free — see there; and in Yoreh De'ah siman 376; and in responsa of the Rama that if he has no son it is fit to give also to a son of his son kaddish, but the other mourners say two kaddishes and he one kaddish. Two forced converts — one was killed and one died, and the son of the killed says kaddish saying his father intended to return, and the son of the deceased also wishes to say kaddish — the son of the killed overrides the son of the deceased, since he was killed he has atonement; and further that there were other forced converts at the dead and he did not confess before them, from which we learn his intention was not to return — see Beit Yosef siman 203 and Yoreh De'ah siman 340 se'if 5. The practice: when there is a son of thirty days they give him all the kaddishes and kaddish of the psalm to mourners; and when there are four yahrzeits they give each one kaddish. And behold it happened on that day they said Song of Songs and they practiced casting lots among all the mourners and yahrzeits, and lots fell on one of the yahrzeits; and the mourners said "Since each has one kaddish we will not give you kaddish of the psalm," and the yahrzeit says "What I merited from Heaven — His mercy on her and her fortune caused it." It appears to me the law is with the yahrzeit. So too in the name of the Ran may he rest in Eden regarding kaddish we do not say part of the seventh day is like all of it — even though one had a death in the middle of the week and this one had a death on day one, both are equal on Shabbos day. Also ruled that one who mourns for his father and afterwards his mother died has all the kaddishes from the law of thirty days, and nevertheless he divides in the other kaddishes because of his father with the other mourners, end of his words. One whose mourning was nullified by a festival or buried his dead during a festival has the law of seven days (so wrote in Yoreh De'ah); and it appears to me nevertheless he is obligated to give the yahrzeit one kaddish, similar to a minor. And that which on Shabbos the yahrzeit gets nothing — this is because he can say on Motzei Shabbos, and so in their custom — see there. And it appears to me a minor of seven years overrides a son of thirty days, and so in Hagahot Rama there. Some refrain from saying Shir HaYichud — see in responsa of the Rama their reason, and he wrote to say it and it was established according to kabbalah. Written in Rivash siman 115 that if his mother died and the father is strict that he not say kaddish, it is forbidden to say it, for honor of the father comes first; and Rama in Yoreh De'ah siman 376 wrote he has no power to protest. They practice in some places that on the day he ceases saying kaddish they give him all the kaddishes.

Evening and morning. And the Arizal wrote not to say it in the evening; and it is possible he means thus: after Mincha prayer or before it — and this requires further study why we say it after Musaf, for behold the incense preceded the musaf offerings of all, and it belongs to the daily offering. And it is possible our intention is to take leave from words of Torah; and in writings it says the reason is to drive away the shells. And in Shelah it is written to say it in the morning before prayer and after it.

The song. So too what is written regarding the mishnah of the song that the Levites etc. — it is an error; the intention of the Tur is to say each day the song belonging to that day, and so too the Rambam in his commentary in the order of prayer — see there; and so in Kol Bo. And it appears to me the intention of the Tur is to say the mishnah as well in order to take leave from words of Torah, for behold he wrote on Shabbos etc. "For the day the Shabbos ceases" etc. "R' Eliezer said: Rosh Chodesh, Torah scholars" etc. — and certainly this is not the matter of the song of the day. And further, why need he say "R' Eliezer" etc. — behold after verses they say forever Kaddish, unlike after mishnah; therefore we say "R' Eliezer" etc., as written siman 54. And so in Darkei Moshe, that Rashi's version reads "in the Temple" and Rokeach reads "in the Temple," and so in Maharil who emended in his siddur like Rokeach — it implies we say the mishnah, for otherwise what difference in the version? Rather, necessarily we say the mishnah. Nevertheless here in Shulchan Aruch the Rama reversed the Tur and the mishnah and wrote "And they say the song that the Levites" etc. to indicate they say the song and not the mishnah; and this is why he concluded Shacharit alone — meaning in the evening they did not say song as the Tur wrote — see there — because here it refers to weekday; but on Shabbos the practice is to say the mishnah as printed in all siddurim. And in Beit Yosef in the name of Orach Chayyim that the songs were not their mitzvah except in the morning, end of his words (and see in Sefer Shiyurei Kenesset HaGedolah). R' Moshe Alshich challenged in a responsum: a complete mishnah we learned chapter 4 of Rosh Hashana that the Levites became confused in the song of between the evenings etc. — if so they would say song! And he answered that nevertheless it does not delay the sacrifice except at Shacharit etc. — see there. And it appears to me as Tosafos wrote end of Rosh Hashana: if they brought the libations at night they would not say song; and even though l'chatchila one should not delay it until night, nevertheless if he did so it is valid — unlike the song of morning where it is impossible to delay at all. And this is the language of Maggid Mishnah chapter 3 of Prayer: the song on the sacrifice they would say while the day was still great, for it is impossible to sing after the tamid of between the evenings even though the day is still great, for after it there is incense and candles, end of his words. If so, one can say likewise here: since Mincha prayer finished, it is as if they already offered the tamid, and it is impossible to say song after it — for in the Temple they would say at the time of libation of the wine on the sacrifice; but in the morning it was permitted to say after it, for the time of song is all day, as implied in the Gemara end of Rosh Hashana that on Yom Tov they would not say the weekday song but other songs; therefore there are places that say other psalms on Yom Tov according to Tractate Soferim, but they are not those said in the Temple. And see in Sukkah 25 regarding Shabbos and Rosh Chodesh and the intermediate days of the festival; and now they are not precise about this, for they do it merely as remembrance; and they also say the song after Musaf. And in truth in Musaf there was a song by itself, as stated end of Rosh Hashana; and in Kol Bo he wrote our custom that on a day when there is Musaf they say the song of the day after Shacharit, and after Musaf they say the song of Musaf (Ma'aseh Rav siman 25).

Skip one. And Beit Yosef wrote he is not liable for death except because he entered the Temple with empty entry — and this does not apply now. And Beit Shmuel wrote he erred, for it says in Yoma 53 that even in a place where entry does not obligate, such as if he erred in entry and was intentional in burning — he is liable for burning; and further it says there that if he did not put in it the raising of smoke he is liable for death — if so, death. Therefore one must beware of the eleven spices and the raising of smoke; and in others there is not so much strictness. And further Beit Yosef wrote death is only in burning, not in saying; and further death is only when intentional — therefore the precise ones practiced to say it every day. And in Bach in the name of R' Shlomo Luria: how much more so "R' Eliezer" etc., for behold Kaddish refers to Aleinu, as explained above; and nevertheless they practiced to say it.

And he bows. Maharil would make three bowings when he went from his place toward the holy ark, and when leaving through the door of the synagogue — each time like a student taking leave of his teacher [Darkei Moshe]. When leaving the synagogue he would not go out with his back toward the Sanctuary but would turn aside; likewise when descending from the bimah [Ateret Zekenim on Rosh Chodesh].
