And proof from Yoma 22b, where we say there: although all the services in the Temple the priests assigned by lot who would merit, but for the removal of ashes in the morning the Sages did not initially ordain a lottery until they saw the matter come to danger — until they did not assign by lot they ordained for it a lottery. And we say there the reason: since the removal of ashes was done before dawn when it was still night, the presumption was that the priests were not considered fit for service, since all the services are by day; therefore priests did not come to quarrel over who would remove the ashes and no lottery was needed, but whoever wished would remove. And they also gave another reason: since it was done before dawn and there is compulsion of sleep, many priests would not come to remove, and there was no need to quarrel; and if many wished to remove they would run and ascend the ramp which was thirty-two cubits long, and whoever reached first into the four cubits adjacent to the altar merited the service. And if two were equal in those four cubits mentioned, he would say to them "Point!" And Rashi and R' Eliyahu Mizrachi explained that all of them would point — for this was the lottery: the priests would each extend one finger and the overseer would say a total count, and whoever completed the count at his hand won — meaning he says a total of one hundred or two hundred and begins counting from one and goes around, God forbid, on their fingers (for it is forbidden to count Israel) until he reached the total of one hundred or two hundred as he said. Behold, those two who were adjacent within the four cubits of the altar alone merited, and nevertheless they need to cast lots with all of them. And in Sefer Eliyah Rabbah he rejected this, for that case is different — for it is impossible that only those two alone could assign by lot, as the Taz wrote, end of his words. And this is the language of the Taz there: it is impossible to make a lottery with fingers between two, for if the agreement of the count is even or odd, or separate from whoever begins, we will know how it ends — and what lottery applies in this? Nevertheless, this is not a rejection of the Taz, for he wrote so according to Rashi and R' Eliyahu Mizrachi who said "Point!" to all the priests — for whence do we know to explain thus? Perhaps only for those two who were equal within the four cubits. And therefore the Taz said it is impossible to explain thus — for what lottery applies here? Nevertheless, in the view of the one who says the total count would begin to count — certainly if there are only two, whoever wishes wins: if he wishes to win the second he says an even total number, and if he wishes to win the first from whom he begins he says an odd total number. Unlike when the priests are many, where it is impossible to know quickly in whom the account will end. But even per the Rambam and Ritva that the one who says the total number would not begin to count — and so also with two it would be possible that they point, since the one who says the number would not know from whom to begin to count — nevertheless the language "Point!" implies a complete lottery like in all other lotteries, and with two this does not apply. And also there would be no need to say "Point!" — for it is forbidden to count Israel, and therefore they need to extend their fingers to count the fingers; nevertheless with two there is no need to count them at all, but rather one says the count will begin with Reuven and end with Shimon, and afterwards, after he did not know from whom to begin, he says a total number of one hundred or two hundred, and automatically we know without counting and numbering that Shimon won; and likewise if he says 101 we know Reuven won. Nevertheless, as Rashi and R' Eliyahu Mizrachi wrote, to all the priests he says "Point!" But in any case the law of Magen Avraham is compelled — for on the baraita itself it is difficult: how is it possible that those two priests who were equal within the four cubits merited — why should all of them point? They should assign lots only to those two. And not to mention the view of the Rambam and Ritva mentioned above, that it is possible through counting as above — but even according to those who say the one who says the number would begin to count, it is impossible to assign lots with two as above; they should ordain with two that the one who says the count will not begin to count, or through another lottery — and why should those two lose since they already merited and assign lots with all the priests? Or that in such a case the lottery is entirely void and the two also did not merit — and the same applies here. However, in the responsum of Me'il Tzedek siman 10 he disagrees with Magen Avraham for another reason — for that case is different, for even the two who merited through their running did not achieve complete acquisition, for it is not sufficient that through being light on his feet he merits the service; rather, since they originally thought no lottery was needed for the reasons above, and when it happened that many wished to remove they would say one merits through his running even though it is not complete acquisition, since it was not possible otherwise, since they did not wish to ordain a lottery for the reasons above — but when two are equal and therefore needed to assign lots between those two, why should the others lose since the two also did not achieve complete acquisition through their running? Unlike here regarding kaddish, where the two merited from Heaven complete acquisition — the others lost, and those two alone cast lots between themselves. And see there, who derived from this other laws as well.

It is further explained there: even if they cast lots anew and it fell to the third brother that same field they had divided between them, nevertheless the first two brothers who each won by the first lottery in field one can each say to his fellow "The lottery and division of one is already void, and we too between ourselves cast lots anew." If so, the same applies here: if two cast lots and one won to say Maariv and the second to say Shacharis, and for Mincha they had not cast lots, and before the first said kaddish at Maariv a third came to them — in any case they cannot say to him that he says Mincha as above, and they must cast lots with the third as above. Nevertheless, if through lots it fell that the third says Mincha, even so the first two — their lottery is void, and the one who won Maariv — his acquisition is not acquisition, and he must cast a new lot with whoever won Shacharis, as we say regarding the field.

And if he was in the city etc., as written in Choshen Mishpat siman 176 seif 25: two partners who have a claim on one from the market — one of the partners sued that one from the market and the partner was liable in judgment; the second partner can sue that one from the market again and argue a different claim. But if the second partner was in the city when the first sued and did not come to court, he certainly waived and relied on the claim and lawsuit of his partner, and cannot sue him again.

And likewise it is in Bava Batra chapter 9 daf 145: their custom was that when one married a woman at the joy of his wedding canopy, his friends and groomsmen would come to rejoice with him and each brought in his hand a gift for his joy; and again when one of his groomsmen married a woman, he too needed to come to his joy and bring with him a gift for the joy of his wedding canopy. And this was collected in court if he did not bring a gift and did not come. And if he was in the city when his groomsman married and did not come to his joy, he must pay him the full value of the gift, and he cannot say "I did not know that he was coming, and if I had known I would have come with my gift in my hand and rejoiced with you in your joy" — for we say: since he was in the city he certainly heard and knew and waived the joy; he must pay him the whole gift, and that gift is called groomsmanship.

And Magen Avraham hinted to look at the end of Sukkah, that this is called right in that he says first, as Tzemach Tzedek wrote: the showbread had twelve loaves, and on Shabbos they divided them between the outgoing watch and the incoming watch (for the priests were divided into twenty-four watches, and each week one watch served in the Temple, and on Shabbos a new watch entered to serve and the watch that served that week went out). And the Tanna Kamma holds they gave the outgoing watch six loaves and likewise the incoming watch six loaves; and R' Yehuda disagrees and holds the outgoing watch took only five loaves and the incoming watch takes seven loaves. And the reason the incoming watch takes one extra loaf beyond what is due to it is in reward for closing the doors — for the outgoing watch opened the gates of the Temple at dawn and the incoming watch must close them in the evening, since the outgoing watch already passed. And the Gemara challenges: let the outgoing watch say to the incoming watch "Give me the six loaves due to me, for you have no advantage in that you take one extra loaf — for corresponding to this, next Shabbos you too will need to give one loaf to the incoming watch on the coming Shabbos." And it answers that nevertheless there is advantage for the outgoing watch, as people say "A small onion now is better than a large onion later," and Rashi explained: if a person wishes to give his fellow immediately a small onion called "butsina," and if he leaves it to grow and does not take it immediately it becomes a large onion called "kari" — a person prefers to take immediately even though now it is only a small onion. And this "my small onion is better than your large onion" is because one does not know what a day may bring forth. All the more so in a matter of mitzvah it is good to advance its fulfillment while it is in his hand and not wait, for he does not know what follows. Therefore in law Magen Avraham agreed with Tzemach Tzedek, but he already wrote that the custom is not so; and know that below he brings Magen Avraham proof that this is called right in that he merited first from the custom.

For we do not compel against middas Sedom — meaning this one benefits and this one does not lose.

As written in Choshen Mishpat siman 311, where it is stated there: one who rents from his fellow a ship and the renter said "This ship I will load with wine" and did not specify this wine but only unspecified whatever he wishes, and he loaded wine and the ship sank with the wine — he need not pay him rent, for he can say to him "Give me my ship that I rented and I will load other wine, for I did not specify wine but I did specify the ship and I said 'this ship.'" And even though the owner wished to give him another ship, nevertheless the renter can withhold; and even though this is middas Sedom — what difference to me this ship or another — nevertheless we do not compel, since he has benefit in his insistence, for through this he is exempt from rent. And the same applies here.

And likewise siman 174 (this proof was also brought by Sema siman 311), where it is stated there: two who have two fields and wish to divide them, and one of them has a field adjacent to another field jointly owned between them — and he said "Give me without lottery that field adjacent to the field I already have, so that my two fields will be adjacent to each other" — even though the two fields are equal, nevertheless the other can withhold and say "No, but let us take by lot," even though this is middas Sedom, for the fields are equal; nevertheless since he has benefit in his insistence we do not compel, for it is possible it will fall to him by lot the field adjacent to his fellow's field, and in order that he agree to exchange with this one who has a field adjacent to hers so that his two fields will be together, he will add money to him. And even though the Shach — meaning in siman 311 — wrote specifically when damaged, meaning specifically there where his wine sank, therefore they were lenient for him not to compel against middas Sedom when he benefits from insistence, but not generally (in truth the Shach in his conclusion there also retracted from this — see there).

Like a minor — meaning a minor of seven years who goes to synagogue all seven days and has all the kaddishes. Nevertheless, if there is a yahrzeit, the minor must give the yahrzeit one kaddish. And that which on Shabbos the yahrzeit gets nothing — is against the adult of seven years, as above.

It would appear at Motzei Shabbos — even though the yahrzeit day already passed, it went away, nevertheless he has benefit in saying kaddish since it is in the prayer adjacent to the yahrzeit and it was not possible for him in another matter.
