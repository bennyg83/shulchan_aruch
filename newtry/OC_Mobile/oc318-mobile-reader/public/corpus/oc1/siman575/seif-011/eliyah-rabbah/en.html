[Levush] Which is "Hodu," etc. So wrote Beis Yosef in the name of the Rif; but Magen Avraham ruled like the Piskei Tosafos on Taanis that Beis Yosef brought, that one should begin from Shir HaMaalos, "Behold, bless," etc., through "those who stand in the house of Hashem at night," because the song of the rains is at night; and he says "Who raises vapors from the ends of the earth"; end quote. And this requires further study: for in Pesachim folio 118, from where is Hallel HaGadol? R' Yehuda says from "Hodu," etc.; R' Yochanan says from Shir HaMaalos, etc.; and all the poskim there ruled like R' Yehuda, as he wrote in siman 480. Also I did not know the explanation they wrote, that the song of the rains is at night. Again I saw in the Yerushalmi at the end of chapter 3 of Taanis: which is Hallel HaGadol? R' Chanina said from "Hodu," etc.; R' Yochanan said, provided [one includes] "those who stand in the house of Hashem"; and why are these two passages [joined]? Because the descent of rains is included in them. According to R' Yochanan it is fine, for it is written "Who raises vapors"; and according to R' Chanina, what of it, because it is written "Who gives food," etc.; end of his language. And were it not that I am afraid, I would say that an error fell into the Piskei Tosafos, and one must say "the descent of rains is included" (as in the Yerushalmi), and they erred and printed "the song of the rains is at night"; and one should not ask according to this why they begin from Shir HaMaalos, for one may say he holds that it is one passage with "Halleluyah," etc., "those who stand," or for another reason; and a proof is that in our Talmud too R' Yochanan says from Shir HaMaalos, as above; and see Tosafos, Pesachim folio 114a. And nevertheless, for the practical law, since there is a dispute in our Talmud and we hold not like R' Yochanan, one should not change; and even though here there is a reason, that it is written in it "Who raises vapors," certainly in Pesachim too there is a reason for R' Yochanan; and further, here and there the Tanna in the Mishnah and the beraisa stated "Hallel HaGadol" without specification: how shall we explain to divide its meaning? And further, in the Tosefta at the end of chapter 2 of Taanis I also found so; in the commentary on the Mishnah at the end of chapter 3 of Taanis I saw that he wrote this language: from "Hodu" until Shir HaMaalos; end quote; and that is astounding.
