See the Taz: on every communal fast they read "Vayechal" in Shacharis; see siman 566 regarding this; and at Mincha, on Monday and Thursday according to our [practice], "Dirshu"; and see below. The Rambam, of blessed memory, wrote in chapter 1 of Hilchos Taaniyos [that it is] a positive commandment to cry out and sound the trumpets; Magen Avraham brought this at the beginning of siman 576 and wondered why we do not sound [them] today; see there. And see Mishneh LaMelech in Parashas Derachim, Derech Mitzvosecha, that the Semag did not bring this mitzvah of sounding at a time of trouble; see there. And in the verse (Bamidbar 10, Behaalosecha) it is written "in your land"; one may say that outside the Land, no; and even in Eretz Yisrael itself, when [the Temple] is not in our hands, one may say no; and this requires further study. And see Maggid Mishneh, chapter 1 of Hilchos Taaniyos, and Mishneh LaMelech there, that the Rambam, of blessed memory, included one positive commandment for the offerings and for trouble; and they wondered there why he did not count them as two positive commandments, since they are at different times, like the shofar of Rosh Hashanah and the Yovel on Yom Kippur; see there. And see Sefer HaChinuch, Parashas Behaalosecha: I learned a partial resolution, that their intent is one: that a person needs great intent with a korban (and that he not offer it except to Heaven, not to any constellation or star, as they were accustomed previously; and see the Rambam, of blessed memory, and remaining books on the reason for the offerings); and likewise at a time of trouble one needs great direction toward Hashem; and that is also as is written nearby, that one should not say it is chance or from the heavenly configuration; man is material and needs great arousal; and for this we were commanded to sound the trumpets; and since the intent is one, he counted it as one positive commandment; so one may say somewhat. And the shofar of Rosh Hashanah and of Yovel have two intents for them: this for freedom and this to break our heart and return in repentance and to remember the binding of Yitzchak; and a fast with trumpets, and shofar on Rosh Hashanah; therefore it works out well. And God willing, in Magen Avraham at the beginning of siman 576 more will be explained.
