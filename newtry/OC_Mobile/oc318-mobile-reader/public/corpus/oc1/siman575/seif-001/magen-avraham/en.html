This is no difficulty, for there the Gemara refers to a Torah scholar who is asked a halachic question within the area of his learning and answers it. One who has not reached that level is not obligated to fast, but if he wishes to fast, he may do so and there is no concern of arrogance. This is clear in the Gemara. Know also that Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel states in the Gemara that every person, even one who is not a Torah scholar, may fast. The reason must be that an observer assumes that he is fasting for some other reason, since many people fast. This is also implied at the end of chapter 2 of Berachos regarding Rav Shisha, and at the beginning of siman 574 regarding one who travels from a place where they are fasting to a place where they are not fasting, who must fast; we are not concerned that this will cause dissension, since every person is permitted to fast. It therefore requires study why at the end of siman 554 the ruling follows Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel regarding work on Tishah B’Av, while here it should likewise have stated that every person may fast. The Tur is also difficult, for in siman 70 he rules like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel and not like the baraisa. One must say that fasting is unlike refraining from work. When an observer sees him fasting, it is evident that he does so because of the lack of rain, and this is considered arrogance. See what I wrote in siman 581, seif 2; see also Choshen Mishpat, siman 162, in Beit Yosef, that at times the halachah is not ruled like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, and Tosafos Yom Tov, Pesachim, chapter 4.
