It implies they are not obligated to fast; Ra'avyah challenged, for in the Gemara it implies they are obligated to fast — the answer is there it speaks of a Torah scholar whom they ask a halachah matter in his learning and he answers; but if he has not reached that level he is not obligated to fast, but if he wishes he fasts and there is no issue of yohara, as is clear in the Gemara. Know that Rabbenu Gershom said in the Gemara that every person, even if not a disciple, may fast; the reason must be that the observer says he is fasting anyway because most people fast; so too in chapter 2 of Berakhot regarding Rav who marries; so too in end of siman 574 that one traveling from a place that fasts to a place that does not fast should fast and we are not concerned for dispute, for every person has permission to fast. If so, there is doubt in siman 554 where he ruled like Rabbenu Gershom regarding melacha on Tisha BeAv — if so here too he should have ruled every person may fast; also on Tur's question in siman 70 he ruled like Rabbenu Gershom and not like the baraita (and one must say) regarding a fast of forty days for work — nevertheless the observer seeing him fast proves he does it for rain and it is yohara; see siman 581 seif 2 and Machatzit HaShekel siman 582 in Beit Yosef that sometimes they rule not like Rabbenu Gershom; in Taz chapter 4 of Pesachim.
