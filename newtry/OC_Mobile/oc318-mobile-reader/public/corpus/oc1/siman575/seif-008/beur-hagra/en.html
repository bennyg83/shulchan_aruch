Seif 8: They fast and cry out. This is Rambam’s language. He explains every “sounding” concerning rain and crops in chapter 3 as verbal crying out. This requires study, for why is it different from every other sounding there, which he explains as sounding a shofar, as written above in siman 576? Rambam also writes this explicitly, and the Gemara groups these cases together with the other calamities, as stated there: The Rabbis taught, they sound the alarm for the trees, etc.; for diphtheria, etc.; and they sound the alarm for locusts, etc. See there, 14a, and Lechem Mishneh there.
And similarly if, etc. All this is Rambam’s language, and he explains “a parsah” as a nearby distance.
And similarly if they reached, etc. The Gemara there, according to the text of Rif and Rosh, which does not read “even.”
Until there passes, etc. As above in seif 6. The Yerushalmi, chapter 3, halachah 2, teaches: They sound the alarm for the tree within a parsah, etc. It then asks: Accordingly, even farther than this? Farther than this would require a miracle, and one does not sound the alarm for a miraculous occurrence.
