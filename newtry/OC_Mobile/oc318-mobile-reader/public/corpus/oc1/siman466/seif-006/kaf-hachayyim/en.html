If the chametz belongs to a non-Jew who lent the poultice to a Jew or placed it upon the Jew's wound, and the Jew bears no liability for it, it is permitted. If the non-Jew has no poultice, the Jew may transfer ownership of flour to the non-Jew, who prepares a poultice from it and places it upon the Jew's wound. Magen Avraham, subsection 2, in the name of Kesef Mishneh, chapter 5 of Hilchos Yesodei HaTorah, halachah 8, who writes this in the name of Orchos Chaim. See there. Mateh Yehudah in siman 448, se'if 5, agrees. See there. Eshel Avraham, subsection 2, and Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 4, write likewise. We also wrote this above in siman 465, subsection 6, in the name of Eliyah Rabbah. See there. Mekor Chaim, subsection 1, however, questions this permission. Once the poultice is placed upon the wound it will be completely ruined, so it is certainly not a loan whose object will be returned intact; it is effectively a complete gift. He therefore prohibits it unless the dressing already became spoiled in the non-Jew's possession before it was placed upon the wound, as stated in siman 442. See there. Peri Chadash likewise declines to permit this case. See there. Although Mateh Yehudah there resolves the objection and agrees that it is permitted, it nevertheless appears that initially one should be stringent when possible. The poultice should be arranged so that it becomes spoiled in the non-Jew's possession before being placed upon the wound, or it should be kneaded with fruit juice, or another type of dressing should be used. If this is impossible and the person is in great pain, one may rely upon the permitting authorities. The Jew should intend not to acquire it at all; it remains in the non-Jew's ownership while upon the wound, until it is thrown into the refuse.
