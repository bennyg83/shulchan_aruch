(29) It does not come, etc.: that the continuous striking of the drip prevents the flour soaked in the drip-water from becoming chametz; and even if the moisture spreads beyond the place where the drip strikes, nevertheless through the continuous striking of the drip that strikes with force and absorbs drop after drop, it does not allow all around it to become chametz.
(30) That the drip be continuous, etc.: that is to say, that the drops descend in succession one after another all day; for otherwise the delays between drop and drop will combine for the measure of a mil, as above in siman 459 se'if 2 in the gloss. And there are among the later authorities who explain the reason: that when they do not descend in succession they have no power to prevent the chimutz.
(31) And he should bake it immediately when it ceases: that is to say, he should not leave the flour that became moistened with water without being worked; rather he should knead and bake immediately, as explained above in siman 459 se'if 2. And this is l'chatchila; but after the fact it is forbidden only when it stood the measure of a mil after the drip ceased.
(32) And if he is in doubt: for it is a Torah-level doubt, and even to keep it is forbidden. And know that likewise regarding grain the law is thus: if the drip is continuous upon it drop after drop, even the entire day it does not come to chimutz; and if it does not descend drop after drop, they become chametz through the water; and even when drop after drop descends upon it, immediately when it ceases he must grind them.
(33) Forbidden: see Peri Megadim, whose view is to be stringent in every case regarding moist flour, for we are not expert to know what is called drop by drop; see there.
