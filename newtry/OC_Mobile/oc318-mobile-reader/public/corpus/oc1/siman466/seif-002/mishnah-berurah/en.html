(3) He must destroy them: for saliva causes chimutz, as above; and therefore he must destroy them. And even though the animal did not stand in its eating for the measure of a mil, nevertheless if he does not destroy, it will come to chimutz and he will transgress bal yera'eh.
(4) Since he already nullified: and if so he left the Torah-level concern, for even regarding certain chametz he is then obligated in its destruction only by rabbinic law; and therefore so long as it is not clear that there is saliva, we do not presume a prohibition. And the later authorities wondered at him: what does the bitul of "all chametz" on erev Pesach help for chametz that now on Pesach became chametz? And they wrote that the wording is awkward, and the intent is if he explicitly nullified before he gave to his animal and said: all the barley that remain from my animal, I nullify.
(5) Even though he does not see, etc.: for presumably there is saliva in them. And the later authorities wrote that all this is regarding a horse and the like; but regarding an ox and a cow, certainly one should be stringent even as a matter of course, for it is known that they emit saliva in their eating, as above in siman 324 se'if 17. And it is proper to be stringent regarding all animals to destroy the leftover even though he sees no saliva upon it; and therefore it is good l'chatchila that he give to his animal little by little so that nothing remain from its food.
