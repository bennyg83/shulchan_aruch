Since it was already nullified. This does not refer to bitul before Pesach, for this was not included in the bitul since it became chametz afterward; and see above siman 446; rather, since he placed it before his animal, he nullified it and abandoned it of his own mind. Even though certain chametz must be destroyed, as explained, and see above siman 453, and so that his animal not eat chametz, as explained above at the end of siman 448: nevertheless as a matter of course, when we do not know that it became chametz, we are not concerned for this, as appears to me simply as the Rav's view; and not like Magen Avraham, who left the Rav in "requires further study." And nevertheless it is good to give them little by little so that they leave none, as Mahariv and Maharil wrote above in siman 465 s.k. 3.
Even though he sees no slime upon it. The Bach wrote that the Mordechai as well forbids only when slime is found; and so [he wrote] in Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah, and he challenged the Rav here since the Rokeach explained his words explicitly as specifically when slime is found (and Mahariv), who wrote without qualification to destroy all that remains: this is certainly a stringency and extra zeal; end quote. And nevertheless it appears to my humble opinion that this is specifically regarding a horse and the like, which is what the Rokeach deals with there in siman 285; but regarding a cow, which ordinarily has slime, as stated in Shabbos folio 141a that a cow's mouth is foul; and see above siman 324: therefore certainly one must destroy as a matter of course; therefore he should give before it little by little, as written in s.k. 4.
