He should sift the flour. And the place that became moistened will be like dough and remain above. And it appears to me that if he need not eat from the flour on Yom Tov, it is forbidden to sift it on Yom Tov (see siman 506); rather he should sift it through a non-Jew in order to destroy the chametz; see siman 459 se'if 4. And even though sifting is a melachah according to the Torah, here since there is no waste it is not an av melachah. And it appears to me that if he wishes he may give it to a non-Jew as a gift before it stood the measure of a mil; see siman 467 and siman 458 se'if 2.
For it crumbles and mixes. Even though flour in flour is nullified in sixty, since there are visible crumbs it returns and revives, as written in siman 447 se'if 4; and if so it is forbidden to eat it and permitted to keep it (and see Yoreh De'ah siman 104 and in Beis Yosef siman 115). But if it dried on Pesach, it is like chametz that mixed on Pesach, for so long as it was moist it did not mix, and if so it mixed on Pesach and it is forbidden to keep it; this appears to me the reason of Rav Yosef Karo [Beis Yosef]. The Bach wrote: mice that ate from flour: if it dried before Pesach, he should sift it and keep it until after Pesach; and if found thus on Pesach, everything is forbidden for benefit. And if the sack lies in its place without shaking and the flour did not mix, then he should take in his hand the place of the bite and tie the sack there beneath the bite with a little flour, and move all the flour in the sack and sift it, and it is permitted to keep it; and even so it is forbidden to eat it on Pesach. And all this is specifically when it is apparent that the mouse ate some of the flour; but if it bit only the sack and there is doubt that perhaps it ate nothing, even if found on Pesach it is permitted to keep it and forbidden to eat it, and even if found before Pesach, for perhaps there is chametz and it dried and crumbles; and sifting is not required since there is no concern of chametz; and in a responsum I explained all this; end quote. And Maharam of Lublin wrote in siman 119 regarding sacks bitten from mice eating, and they sifted them, and afterward there is concern that perhaps the places dried until that moisture adhered to the sacks and separated and mixed into the flour: and behold I checked two sacks and did not find in them any substantial adhering matter; additionally this is a time of pressing need; accordingly I instruct that they take other guarded flour for the first two nights, and the aforementioned flour they bake before Pesach; and the flour that they sifted in that same sieve with which they sifted the doubtful flour, one need not be stringent regarding it, and it is permitted for everything; end quote. And Taz wrote that even if water fell upon it and it dried, if he recognizes the place that became moistened he should take it and sift the rest; and all the more so regarding a mouse bite, that he take around the bitten place and sift the rest and eat it on Pesach; end quote. And it appears to me that regarding mice one should be more stringent, for these mice are wicked and go throughout the entire sack and eat; and all the more so when they lie in a barrel, for then it certainly goes through all the flour; therefore the aforementioned Geonim were stringent in this.
