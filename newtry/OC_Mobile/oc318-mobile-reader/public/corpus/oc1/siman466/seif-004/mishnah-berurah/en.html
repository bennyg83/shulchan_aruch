(9) Water fell: even on Pesach itself.
(10) Or the sack became moistened: from water; and according to the view of those who forbid in the previous se'if, even if it became moistened from wall sweat. And since the sack became moistened, presumably the moisture reached even the flour in that place.
(11) The moistened place: with the flour in this place; and he must take of the flour with the sack around the moisture according to the estimate of the mind as to how far the moisture reached, and a little more.
(12) All the flour in the sack: and it is proper l'chatchila to sift afterward also all the remaining flour. And there is one who wrote that all this is if the moisture was toward the bottom of the sack, for in that case this remedy helps, that he take from the moistened place to the edge of the sack; but if the water fell upon the sack from above, what does it help that he take from above at the moistened place? Perhaps the water reached into the depth of the sack, as is the way of water that enters deep below, and it is possible it dried there as well, and it is impossible to recognize how far the moistened place is. And therefore in that manner one should not be lenient without sifting from the law even after the fact. And nevertheless it is possible that before Pesach one should not be stringent in this after the fact if he did not sift.
(13) And if it is impossible to do so: for example, when it became moistened from its side in many places, or a large part of the sack became moistened, or they threw the sack from place to place and presumably the flour mixed within it.
(14) And the rest is permitted: for in the place that became moistened the flour will stick and be like dough and remain above in the sieve, and the flour that passes through the holes of the sieve certainly the water did not touch them. And if it is on Yom Tov, when it is forbidden to sift flour more than what he needs, he should sift it through a non-Jew in order to destroy the chametz. And if the water fell on Yom Tov and the water has not yet stood in the flour the measure of a mil, he may give to a non-Jew as a gift and thereby fulfill [the requirement], since it has not yet become chametz.
(15) Sifting does not help: and even before Pesach. And the later authorities wrote that specifically sifting does not help when it dried; but the first remedy that the Mechaber mentioned, to take hold of the moistened place [and in a manner that they did not move the sack from place to place, and one can recognize the moistened place well, as above] properly helps, and even if it dried on Pesach: and he should take in his hand the moistened place with the flour around the moisture, and the rest is permitted for eating. And nevertheless it is proper to sift the remainder, as above.
(16) For it crumbles, etc.: that is to say, into fine crumbs, and it will go down through the holes of the sieve and mix with the flour. And even though there is certainly sixty against it and they are nullified before Pesach, nevertheless when the days of Pesach arrive when they are forbidden in any amount, it will return and revive. And even though regarding wet in wet we do not say it returns and revives, as above in siman 447 se'if 4, and flour in flour is wet in wet: one can say that crumbs do not dissolve well in flour and stand on their own; and it is possible that even at the time of kneading the dough they also did not dissolve and mix, and when the days of Pesach arrive they return and forbid.
(17) And it is forbidden to eat it, etc.: and even if they baked before Pesach, for even though the crumb was baked it is chametz and was not nullified, as above. And see Peri Chadash, whose view is that it is called wet in wet and does not return and revive; and in any case it is permitted to bake before Pesach; and so many later authorities agreed that after it was baked it became one mass and was nullified and does not return and revive. And see Magen Elef who wrote a further support for this: that he take some of the flour and eat it before Pesach, and we attribute the chametz to what he ate; and one may rely on this in a place of loss. And nevertheless he should take other matzos for the first nights, for in any case this is not properly guarded; and the same applies in every case where we permit by reason of a double doubt and the like.
(18) On Pesach: and regarding mice that bit the sacks: if it is recognizable that they ate from the flour and the moisture dried, some say that if it is during Pesach, all of it is forbidden, for we attribute that they went in several places in the sack [and saliva in a mouse's mouth causes chimutz like saliva of other animals and beasts]; and therefore taking hold of that place does not help. And if it is before Pesach, he should sift the flour and keep it until after Pesach, or sell it, and like the law of moistened with water above. But many later authorities hold for practical law that also in this it helps to take hold around the place of the bite with the flour next to it carefully, and the rest he should sift and eat on Pesach; and this counsel helps even if it is during Pesach. And nevertheless it is proper to be stringent like those who say, except in a place of pressing need or loss. And all this is when the sack lies in its place without shaking and the flour did not mix; but if he moved the sack from place to place, or they bit in several places in the sack and it is impossible to grasp and sift, this counsel does not help even according to the view of those who are lenient, and its law is like the law of moistened with water, as above. However according to what we wrote at the end of the previous s.k., that also when the sack became moistened with water, sifting helps in a place of loss to bake before Pesach, it is obvious that the same applies also in this; and see Shaarei Teshuvah what he wrote regarding this.
(19) Rather he should sift it: in order to destroy the chametz that will be found in it.
(20) And if it dried on Pesach, etc.: and the reason is that we hold that chametz that mixed on Pesach is in any amount, and if it mixed not in its time it is by giving taste. And therefore if it dried on Pesach, even though the moisture was beforehand, nevertheless so long as it was moist it did not mix, and when did it begin to mix? When it dried and crumbled crumbs into the flour: if so the mixture was made on Pesach, which is in any amount, and it is forbidden to keep it [and even if he sifts it, behold we are concerned that tiny crumbs go down through the holes of the sieve, as above]; which is not so if it dried before Pesach, for we hold that it is in the measure of giving taste in all the flour, and here it does not have that measure. The later authorities wrote that this entire se'if deals with a case where we know that the sack became moistened from water; but if there is doubt that perhaps its moisture is from fruit juice, one should be lenient to eat it on Pesach through sifting even if it dried during Pesach, for it is a double doubt: perhaps it was moistened from fruit juice; and even if you say from things that cause chimutz, perhaps nothing of the dried moisture mixed into the flour. And nevertheless if he can easily find other flour for Pesach, it is not worthwhile l'chatchila to rely on a double doubt to eat it on Pesach; rather he should put it away until after Pesach. And the same applies if it became moistened with some thing about which there are disputes among the poskim whether it is in the category of fruit juice or water: it too is permitted through sifting.
(21) It is forbidden to keep it: and he did not write that he throw it outside, for he follows his own view below in siman 467 se'if 10; see there; and according to him he may sell to a non-Jew except for the value of the prohibition in it. But according to the Rama's view there, that they practice to burn everything, the same applies in this. And know that according to what the later authorities agreed, that in a place of great loss one may rely on the Mechaber's view in se'if 10, and as we wrote above in siman 447 in the Mishnah Berurah s.k. 3, he may sell the entire sack to a non-Jew except for a little of the chametzed flour in it. And it is obvious that this is when there is a non-Jew before him.
