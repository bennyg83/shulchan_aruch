(9) Water fell—even on Passover itself:

(10) Or sack became moist—from water; per 'some forbid' previous seif—even from wall sweat. Since sack moistened, moisture presumably reached flour there:

(11) Moist place—with flour there; take flour with sack around moisture per estimate somewhat more:

(12) All flour in sack—l'chatchila afterward sift all remaining flour; some wrote only if moisture on bottom of sack this remedy taking from moist to sack edge helps; if water from above on sack—how does taking from above help—perhaps water penetrated deep like water enters depth below and perhaps dried there—cannot recognize moist place—therefore no leniency without sifting even b'dieved; perhaps before Passover not stringent b'dieved without sifting:

(13) If impossible—moistened many places, or large part of sack, or threw sack place to place—presumably flour mixed inside:

(14) Rest permitted—where moist flour sticks like dough remains above sieve; flour through sieve holes certainly untouched by water; if yom tov forbidden sift more than need—sift through gentile to destroy chametz; if water fell yom tov and water not in flour mil measure yet—gift to gentile before leavened:

(15) Sifting does not help—even before Passover. Acharonim: specifically sifting not help when dried—but first remedy take moist place helps even dried on Passover if sack not moved and moist place recognizable well as above—rest permitted eat, nevertheless good sift remainder:

(16) For it crumbles, etc.—fine crumbs descend through sieve holes mix with flour; though certainly sixty against them nullified before Passover—on Passover days forbidden smallest amount reawakens; though wet in wet we do not say reawakens as 467:4 and flour in flour is wet in wet—crumbs do not blend well, exist independently—perhaps even at dough kneading not dissolved—on Passover days reawaken and forbid:

(17) Forbidden to eat, etc.—even if baked before Passover—for crumb baked is chametz not nullified as above. See Pri Chadash holds wet in wet does not reawaken—still many Acharonim agree after baking one lump nullified does not reawaken; see Magen Alef wrote further take some flour eat before Passover and hang prohibition on what ate—may rely in loss; nevertheless take other matzot first nights—for not guarded properly—likewise wherever we permit from double doubt:

(18) On Passover—and mice bit sacks: if visible ate flour and moisture dried—some say if on Passover all forbidden—for we attribute mice went many places—mouse saliva leavens like other animals—therefore taking that place not help; before Passover sift and retain or sell as water-moist law. Many Acharonim hold also here taking around bite with flour helps, sift rest, eat Passover—even on Passover; nevertheless good stringent first view except pressure/loss; all when sack unmoved flour not mixed; if shook sack or many bites impossible take and sift—no remedy even lenient view—law like water moistening. Per end previous s.k. even sack moist from water in loss may bake before Passover—here likewise. See Shaarei Teshuvah on this:

(19) Rather sift—to destroy chametz found:

(20) If dried on Passover, etc.—we hold chametz mixed on Passover in smallest amount; if not mixed in time gives taste—if dried on Passover though moisture before Passover—all while moist did not mix—when began mix when dried and crumbled into flour—mixture on Passover in smallest amount—forbidden retain—even if sifted fear small crumbs through sieve holes as above. Unlike dried before Passover—we hold gives taste in all flour, here no such measure. Acharonim: whole seif when known moistened from water; if doubt fruit juice—lenient eat on Passover by sifting even dried on Passover—double doubt perhaps fruit juice, if leavening things perhaps nothing mixed from dried moisture; nevertheless if easy find other flour for Passover—l'chatchila do not rely double doubt eat—hide until after Passover; likewise if moistened something disputed fruit juice or water—permit by sifting:

(21) Forbidden to retain—not wrote throw out—for he goes to 467:10; per him may sell to gentile except prohibition value; per Rama custom there burn all—here likewise.
