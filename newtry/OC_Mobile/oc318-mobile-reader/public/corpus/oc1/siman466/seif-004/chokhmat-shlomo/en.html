(There, se'if 4) Water fell on flour, or the sack became moist, etc. Marginal note: behold regarding the law of a mouse's bite, see Chok Yaakov, whose view is to be lenient, and he brought a proof from Parah chapter 9. And behold the great rabbi, av beis din of the holy community of Cartshov, Moreinu HaRav Nachum Ginzburg, may his light shine, wrote to me that one can reject his proof, for one can distinguish between drinking and eating: for in drinking, where it need not chew in its mouth, its way is only to swallow and not to emit slime, which is not so in eating; thus far his words in brief. And I replied to him that in my view Chok Yaakov's proof is correct: for if it were so that a mouse's way is to emit slime from its mouth, then in any case it would not leave the category of doubt, and perhaps it emitted slime from its mouth before drinking or afterward, and behold there is a chazakah of tumah; and one must [conclude] against one's will that it was known to them that a mouse's way is not to emit slime from its mouth at all. And if so, if we rely on this estimation even in a place of a chazakah of tumah, all the more so it is proper to rely on this in a place where there is no chazakah of prohibition, and on the contrary there is also a chazakah of permit; and Chok Yaakov's words are correct for practical law; and analyze carefully.
