See Taz: whenever he can take hold of the sack at the moistened place and a little more, it helps even during Pesach and even if it dried, for there is no concern of any amount; and whenever it is impossible to take hold of the sack that became moistened in many places, then if it is still moist, sifting helps, and if not, it is forbidden. And in Magen Avraham 8, God willing, this will be explained. And what is written in siman 453 letter 2 to distinguish between mixed before grinding or afterward: but other later authorities wrote that flour is wet in wet; but crumbs are dry in wet and forbid on Pesach in any amount; and see Magen Avraham, and God willing there it will be explained. And if mice ate from the flour, that is in a sack; but in a barrel one can say they went through all the flour, as Magen Avraham wrote in letter 9, and there God willing it will be explained. And what is written regarding chicken dung, that he should sift outside: according to the Bach, because chicken dung is not fruit juice but a toldah of its own, and he should throw outside because of bal teshaktzu; and Taz wrote because of "present it to your governor," bread of Yom Tov, etc.; and it implies that he also holds that dung is not in the category of fruit juice. And if he kneaded thus and it stood less than the measure of a mil, one should not forbid; and see siman 462 in Magen Avraham 1.
