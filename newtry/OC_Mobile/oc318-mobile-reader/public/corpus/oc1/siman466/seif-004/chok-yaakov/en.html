Water fell upon the flour. And in a similar case the Bach is also stringent in siman 453 regarding flour from which mice ate; but if they bit only the sack and there is room to doubt that perhaps it ate nothing of the flour, even if found during Pesach one should permit keeping it. And in the responsa of Maharam of Lublin, siman 119, he is somewhat lenient regarding sacks bitten by mice in a time of pressing need; see there. Also Taz permits taking around the place of the bite, and the rest he should sift and eat on Pesach even if it dried; end quote. And Magen Avraham wrote, and this is its wording: and it appears to me that regarding mice one should be more stringent, and these mice are wicked and go throughout the entire sack and eat; and all the more so when it lies in barrels, for then it certainly goes through all the flour; therefore the aforementioned Geonim were stringent; and so wrote the Mabit to be stringent for this reason; see there. And in my humble opinion it appears that one should not be stringent at all regarding mice that ate from flour, and [should] permit through sifting in every case: because the core law of flour that mice ate is not mentioned at all in any early posek; only the later authorities derived this law from wheat that mice bit, where we hold like the view that mouse saliva causes chimutz, as explained above in siman 453. And in my humble opinion, even though mouse saliva causes chimutz, nevertheless when they ate from flour there is no concern of chimutz at all. And a clear proof for this is from the mishnah in Parah chapter 9 mishnah 3: that all creeping things that drank waters of purification do not disqualify, because no saliva comes from their mouths, except a weasel; R' Eliezer says, even a mouse; and the law is not like R' Eliezer, as the Rambam and the Rash and Rabbeinu Ovadiah of Bartenura wrote there; and so the Rambam ruled explicitly in Parah chapter 9 law 12, that no moisture comes from its mouth; and so wrote Tosafos in Chullin chapter 1. Behold even regarding liquids that are apt to receive moisture they do not disqualify, all the more so flour, where every grain is separate from its fellow, and there is no concern at all that it moistens from what it is not eating. And it is not comparable to wheat, which is one mass, and because of the strength of the bite we are concerned that some moisture remains in what remains of the wheat kernel; which is not so regarding flour, where one should be lenient in every case; and this is clear. But my opinion is not decisive against the great later authorities, and especially since they are stringent and I am lenient: in any case one should permit tying the place of the bite and permitting the rest in every case through sifting, as Taz ruled; and one who is stringent in this does not lose for his flour. And all this is when one sees no moisture at the place of the bite; but if one sees any moisture at the place of the bite, one should attribute it to a common thing: perhaps the mice urinated on the flour; and see nearby s.k. 14 whether there is concern of chimutz from urine, and it is like having become moistened with water.
He should take in his hand all the moistened place. And nevertheless he should sift it afterward; Bach and Maharshal. And Taz wrote that it is a mere stringency.
He should sift the flour. And Magen Avraham wrote: and if he does not wish to eat the flour on Yom Tov, for it is forbidden to sift it on Yom Tov, he should sift through a non-Jew, or give it to a non-Jew as a gift before it stood the measure of a mil; end quote.
And sifting does not help. It implies that if he takes in his hand all the moistened place, it helps even when it dried; and so ruled Taz; and even during Pesach he permits in such a case: for only where the entire sack or a large part of it became moistened, then it is impossible to take the moistened place; but if it is possible to take the moistened place, it is permitted.
And mixes with the rest. Even though flour in flour is called wet in wet and we do not say it returns and revives, nevertheless crumbs are different, for they do not mix well; so is the agreement of the later authorities, not like the Levush who wrote specifically when there is not sixty; and see above siman 453. And my brother-in-law the Rav in Or Zarua wrote in the name of the Derishah: where there is room to doubt whether it was moistened from fruit juice and the like or from things that cause chimutz, one should be lenient to eat it on Pesach even if it dried, by force of a double doubt: perhaps it was moistened from fruit juice; and even if you say from other things that it became chametz, perhaps nothing mixed into it; end quote. And see above siman 465 regarding the laws of a double doubt.
