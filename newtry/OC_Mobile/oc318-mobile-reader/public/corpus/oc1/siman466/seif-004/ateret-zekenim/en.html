He should take in his hand and afterward must sift all the remaining flour (Maharshal); and so is the custom. Flour in a sack that mice bit—its law is likewise: if before Passover one must sift the flour; if crumbs like dry dough crumbs remain on the sieve they are discarded and the flour may be retained until after Passover. If found thus on Passover it is forbidden for benefit and sifting does not help. If the sack rests in its place without shaking and the flour did not mix, he takes in his hand the bitten place and ties the sack there under the bite with some flour; the remainder may be retained by sifting; nevertheless it is forbidden to eat on Passover. All this only when it appears the mice ate some of the flour; but if they bit only the sack and there is doubt perhaps they ate nothing—even if found on Passover it may be retained and is forbidden to eat; even if found thus before Passover, lest there is chametz that dried and crumbles.
