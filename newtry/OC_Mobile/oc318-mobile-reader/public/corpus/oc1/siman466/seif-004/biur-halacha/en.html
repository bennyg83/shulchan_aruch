If this is impossible, etc., he should sift the flour, etc. This is the view of Rokeach. Although Rosh writes that this is a great leniency, the Mechaber relied upon it because Mordechai also agrees with this reasoning. Only where the affected material has dried does Mordechai likewise rule stringently in accordance with Rosh. In that case the Mechaber therefore adopts the stringent view and does not follow Rokeach. See Nesiv Chayim and Magen HaElef, who apparently did not take this distinction into account.
If it dried during Pesach, etc. See Mishnah Berurah. The wording of the Shulchan Aruch appears to imply that even if the sifting was performed during Pesach, the flour may still be retained. Magen HaElef questions this. Since it must be sifted, the mixture is now being formed in the flour through the holes of the sieve, so why may it be retained? Careful examination of the source of this law cited in Beit Yosef likewise indicates that it must specifically be sifted before Pesach. When the source says that during Pesach everything must be discarded, it means that it was not sifted before Pesach. There is no difference between whether it dried before Pesach or during Pesach. Rather, before Pesach there is a remedy through sifting, whereas if it was not sifted then, there is no remedy, because it is impossible that some minute crumb did not become mixed in. Pri Megadim also struggles greatly to explain the Mechaber’s reasoning. Magen HaElef therefore concludes that this is likewise the Mechaber’s intent. If it dried before Pesach, it can be remedied by sifting. If it dried during Pesach, sifting provides no remedy and it may not be retained. The Mechaber merely described the ordinary case, because when it dries before Pesach people are careful to sift it before Pesach as well.
