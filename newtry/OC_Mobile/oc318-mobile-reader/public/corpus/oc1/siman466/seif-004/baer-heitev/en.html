Impossible. Such as when moistened in many places:

He should sift. The moistened place will be like dough and remain above. It appears if he need not eat from the flour on Yom Tov it is forbidden to sift on Yom Tov—see siman 507—rather sift through a gentile to destroy chametz; or give to gentile as gift before it stood mil measure—Magen Avraham. (See siman 446 and 459):

Sifting. But if he takes in hand all the moistened place it helps even if dried—Chafetz Chayim and so Taz; even during Passover he permits in such case—for only where entire sack or large part moistened then impossible to take moist place; but if possible to take moist place—it is permitted. Chafetz Chayim:

And mixes. Although flour in flour is called wet in wet and we do not say it reawakens—nevertheless crumbs are different for they do not mix well and reawaken as siman 467 seif 4; therefore forbidden to eat and permitted to retain. But if dried on Passover it is like chametz mixed on Passover—for while moist it did not mix, therefore mixed on Passover and forbidden to retain—Magen Avraham. Or Zarua wrote: where doubt whether moistened from fruit juice etc. or chametzing things—lenient to eat on Passover even if dried from double doubt perhaps fruit juice, and if you say other things perhaps nothing mixed—see there. Bach wrote: mice bit sack and ate flour and dried—before Passover sift flour, discard crumbs on sieve, flour may be retained; if found thus on Passover all forbidden for benefit. If sack unmoved without shaking, take bitten place (with some flour), tie sack there, sift, may retain, nevertheless forbidden to eat on Passover—all when visible mouse ate flour; if bit only sack and doubt perhaps ate nothing—even found on Passover may retain, forbidden to eat, even before Passover lest chametz dried and crumbles—end. Maharam of Lublin siman 119 ruled on sack bitten by mice: if baked matzot before Passover from that flour—not forbidden to eat on Passover except first two nights take other guarded flour; flour sifted from that sieve—no stringency, permitted for all—end. Taz wrote even if water fell and dried, if he recognizes moist place take it and sift rest; certainly for mouse bites take around bitten place, sift rest and eat on Passover—end. Magen Avraham wrote: in mice be more stringent, these mice are wicked and go through whole sack and eat, especially in barrel—therefore Geonim were stringent—end. So Mabit for this reason. Chafetz Chayim agreed like Taz to tie bitten place and permit rest by sifting in every case—end.
