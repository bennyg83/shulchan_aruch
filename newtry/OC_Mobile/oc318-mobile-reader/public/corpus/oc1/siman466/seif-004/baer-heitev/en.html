Sifting. But if he takes in his hand all the moistened place, it helps even when it dried; Chok Yaakov; and so ruled Taz; and even during Pesach he permits in such a case: for only where the entire sack or a large part of it became moistened, then it is impossible to take the moistened place; but if it is possible to take the moistened place, it is permitted; Chok Yaakov.
And mixes. Even though flour in flour is called wet in wet and we do not say it returns and revives, nevertheless crumbs are different, for they do not mix well and [thus] return and revive, as written in siman 447 se'if 4; and if so it is forbidden to eat it and permitted to keep it. But if it dried on Pesach, it is like chametz that mixed on Pesach, for so long as it was moist it did not mix, and if so it mixed on Pesach and it is forbidden to keep it; Magen Avraham. And Or Zarua wrote: where there is room to doubt whether it was moistened from fruit juice and the like or from things that cause chimutz, one should be lenient to eat it on Pesach even if it dried, by force of a double doubt: perhaps it was moistened from fruit juice; and even if you say from other things that it became chametz, perhaps nothing mixed into it; see there. The Bach wrote: mice that bit a sack and ate flour and it dried: if before Pesach, one must sift that flour, and the crumbs that remain in the sieve he throws away, and the flour is permitted to keep; and if it was found thus on Pesach, everything is forbidden for benefit. And if the sack lies in its place without shaking, he should take in his hand the place of the bite (with a little flour), and tie the sack there, and sift, and it is permitted to keep it; and even so it is forbidden to eat it on Pesach. And all this is specifically when it is apparent that the mouse ate some of the flour; but if it bit only the sack and there is doubt that perhaps it ate nothing, even if found thus on Pesach it is permitted to keep it, but forbidden to eat it, even if found thus before Pesach; end quote. And Maharam of Lublin, siman 119, ruled regarding a sack of flour bitten from mice eating: that if they baked matzos before Pesach from that flour, one need not forbid eating them on Pesach; however for the first two nights one should take other guarded flour. And the flour that they sifted in that same sieve with which they sifted the doubtful flour: one need not be stringent regarding it, and it is permitted for everything; see there. And Taz wrote that even if water fell upon it and it dried, if he recognizes the place that became moistened he should take it and sift the rest; and all the more so regarding a mouse bite, that they take around the bitten place and sift the rest and eat it on Pesach; see there. And Magen Avraham wrote: and it appears to me that regarding mice one should be more stringent, for these mice are wicked and go throughout the entire sack and eat; and all the more so when it lies in a barrel, for then it certainly goes through all the flour; therefore the Geonim were stringent; see there. And so wrote the Mabit to be stringent for this reason. And Chok Yaakov agreed like Taz that one should permit tying the bitten place and permitting the rest in every case through sifting. And this is specifically when one sees no moisture at the place of the bite; but if one sees any moisture at the place of the bite, one should attribute it to a common thing: perhaps the mice urinated on the flour; and see there further.
