Take in hand, etc.—implies rest of flour permitted even on Passover; Maharshal wrote must afterward sift as written after:

Sifting does not help—implies; but first remedy take moist place helps even dry—for we remove all doubtful place; Mordechai chapter 21: case before R' Yitzchak of Vienna—sack of flour fell in mud on Passover—shake sack, said sift flour so moisture remains above, discard, rest permitted—only while moist; if dried moisture not helped sifting—for crumbled mixed, nothing remains above—take other flour for Passover, retain this if finds like dough discard, rest keep after Passover—only if dried before Passover may keep until after; on Passover throw all—end. He did not mention taking moist place—for impossible entire sack or large part in mud—no remedy except sifting—said sifting not help when dried lest crumbles through sieve holes; but remedy take moist place—why not help when dried—for removed doubtful place; Maharshal in name of Rosh on this remedy still must sift all remainder—custom—appears mere stringency; proof Tur: little water or snow on grain—scrape top, take bottom—no sifting in grain—sifting Rosh mere stringency since can do so—not forbid without afterward sifting; certainly dried and sifted after removal—not forbidden lest dry fell through sieve; see 453:3 nullification flour in flour. Mice ate flour—take that place well around, sift rest, eat Passover—not worse than water one place—and not stringent even on Passover—for water on flour law includes Passover from outset. Though Maharshal exaggerated 453 unlike here. Tur in name of R' Eliezer Garmiza: sack flour chickens sat, moist from droppings—does not leaven; nevertheless sift flour, throw dough outward, rest permitted—end. Rokeach adds Menachot 69: barley in dung—if for Menachot bring to your pit not chametz prohibition—end; plain why sift and throw outward—for matzah mitzvah and other matzot for yom tov—not proper disgrace; difference if did not sift—no prohibition at all:
