From the sweat of the wall it is permitted. That is the view of the Rosh here, that it is similar to human sweat, which is not machshir and does not cause chimutz. And according to this it appears that this is specifically when it sweats from itself or from the heat of fire and the like; which is not so if it sweats from the heat and vapor of hot water, for then it is like water that is machshir, as explained in Machshirin chapter 2, and it causes chimutz; and so also the Rivash wrote in a responsum, siman 255, that sweat that comes from liquids is like those liquids for every matter. And even though in the Rosh's words in chapter Kol Sha'ah regarding the woman who rinsed bran it implies that even the sweat that one sweats in the bathhouse also does not cause chimutz, one must say that [there] he sweats from the heat of fire and not from the water.
Permitted. At first glance it appears that he means permitted entirely, namely to knead with water, and they need not bake immediately as when kneading with fruit juice with water, as explained above in siman 462, because this is not true fruit juice but a toldah of its own; and so wrote the Bach regarding chicken dung. However from the words of Hagahos Semak regarding mice eating, brought in Beis Yosef siman 453 and in Natz at the end of this siman in the name of Maharil, it is explained that all these, even though they are not true fruit juice, nevertheless hasten chimutz with water like fruit juice. And if it is still moist, he should sift it and keep the moistened until after Pesach; and if it dried and mixed, one should not knead with them, and they should keep them until after Pesach; and if he kneaded with them, he should bake immediately, as explained above in siman 462.
And some forbid. The reason: Maggid Mishneh wrote that it sweats from the moisture of the water that is in it; and also regarding a wooden wall it is possible that it is from the moisture of water that it received while still attached; end quote. And see the commentary of the Rash on Machshirin chapter 4, who wrote that in such a case it is fruit juice regarding hechsher; see there. And for practical law my brother-in-law the Rav in Or Zarua wrote that the view of those who permit is primary; and Magen Avraham wrote that the primary [view] is like the view of those who forbid, and it is good to keep them until after Pesach. And see the Tur who ruled that dry meat that sweats from the water with which it was rinsed: its sweat causes chimutz; and see above siman 453 s.k. 22 regarding the law of animal sweat; see there.
