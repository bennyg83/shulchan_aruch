(6) The wall: and likewise a wooden wall that sweats.
(7) Permitted: and there is no distinction whether the sweat comes because of scant air, as is common in cellars and caves whose walls and stones sweat, or whether it comes from water that is in it, for example a wall of new bricks that sometimes sweat because the walls have not yet dried, or a wooden wall whose woods are moist and sweat: in every case the sweat does not cause chimutz, as we find regarding hechsher of tumah that sweat is not considered like water to be machshir. And nevertheless it is no better than fruit juice; and if so, according to what we rule above in siman 462 that fruit juice with water hastens chimutz, one should not knead with them l'chatchila; and if it is still moist, he should sift it and keep the moistened until after Pesach, and with the remainder he should knead and bake for Pesach. And the poskim wrote that all this is regarding sweat that comes from walls; but if the sweat comes from the heat and vapor of hot water found in it, for example there is a tub of hot water and through this the sack became moistened: according to all it is a toldah of water and causes chimutz.
(8) And some forbid: they hold that we do not learn chametz from hechsher of tumah, and regarding chametz whatever is moisture of water has power to cause chimutz. And there are who wrote the reason for this view: because the wall sweats from the water that is in it; and even sweat of a wooden wall also has the law of water, for for the most part it too has absorption from rainwater that drips on the walls, and sometimes also rainwater drips upon them after they were cut from the ground. And according to this reason, sweat of stones or metals or glass even according to this view does not cause chimutz and is like fruit juice. And for practical law the primary [view] is like the first view, as the Mechaber wrote without qualification at first, and they are fruit juice. And nevertheless l'chatchila one should be concerned for the view of those who forbid; and even so, to keep it until after Pesach is permitted. And if he wishes to use this flour for Pesach, one should permit it through sifting, and even if it dried: and the soiled flour he should throw away, and the rest he may use.
