(1) And place upon his wound, etc.: that is to say, that through chewing in his mouth the wheat mixes with saliva and they will cause chimutz when they stand upon a wound for the measure of a mil, and he will transgress regarding them bal yera'eh and bal yimatze. And even if he chewed them and placed them upon his wound before Pesach, he must remove them when the sixth hour of erev Pesach arrives, unless they already spoiled such that they became unfit for a dog's food, as explained in siman 442. And the poskim wrote that all this is regarding a Jew's wheat; but a non-Jew's wheat that the non-Jew chewed and lent to a Jew to place upon his wound on Pesach is permitted if the responsibility for the poultice is not upon the Jew, for regarding a non-Jew's poultice he does not transgress bal yera'eh. And if the non-Jew has no wheat, the Jew may transfer wheat of his own to him, and the non-Jew chew them, and then the Jew receive from him the poultice only by way of a loan. And even though chametz is forbidden for benefit, and even a non-Jew's chametz, as above in siman 443: this case is different, for he benefits from this chametz not in the normal way of deriving benefit, and there is prohibition in it only by rabbinic law, and in a place of illness, even when there is no danger, they did not decree. And nevertheless if he can be healed when he softens this wheat poultice with fruit juice, it is not proper to do it with saliva.
(2) Because they cause chimutz: and all this is if his wound has no danger; but when there is danger in it, it is permitted even where there is a negative commandment of bal yera'eh, if only it is possible that this remedy will help him and it is not easy to do in a permitted way; and even complete eating and drinking of chametz is also permitted for his healing, since there is danger in it.
