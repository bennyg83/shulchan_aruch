A person should not chew. And that is the meaning of malugma, as written above in siman 442 s.k. 7. And the Rokeach wrote in siman 271 that even if it was already chewed and placed upon his wound before Pesach, it requires biur and he should remove them; end quote. And so it was explained above in siman 442 that whenever the malugma did not spoil before Pesach, it requires biur; and this is simple from the Talmud and the poskim; see above siman 442. And all this is not like Beis Hillel in Yoreh De'ah siman 155 s.k. 3, who wrote that "he should not chew" applies specifically during Pesach, but if it was already chametz it is permitted to place it on Pesach for some remedy; and so he wrote in the Kesef Mishnah in the name of the Rashba in the laws of Pesach; end quote. And in truth all this is not so; and what he wrote that so [is written] in the Kesef Mishnah, I toiled and did not find this distinction in his words. And the main difficulty that forced this distinction, because he challenges from what the Rambam wrote in Yesodei HaTorah chapter 5 that when not in the normal way of deriving benefit, such as a malugma for a wound, it is permitted to heal with chametz on Pesach: from the outset that is no difficulty, for there it deals with a non-Jew's chametz, which is not so regarding a Jew's chametz, as the Kesef Mishnah wrote there, and so wrote Magen Avraham and in the responsa of Maharshak, siman 27. And all this is regarding a sick person who is not in danger; but regarding a sick person who is in danger, even by a Jew himself from his own chametz it is likewise permitted; and so it is in Magen Avraham in the name of the Yerushalmi, and so wrote in the responsa of Maharshak there; see above siman 465 s.k. 2. And regarding chametz that Pesach passed over it, it is permitted. And he wrote in Toras HaAdam of the Rashba, folio 11b, and this is its wording: However regarding eating it is possible that he treated it like Torah [law], and one may heal only in a place of danger; end quote. And so wrote the Ran at the beginning of chapter Kol Sha'ah, and in the Rivash siman 255; and so it is explained in Yoreh De'ah siman 155, not like what is somewhat implied in the Yerushalmi, chapter Kol Sha'ah, law 1, that even not in the normal way of deriving benefit it is forbidden; see there. Peri Chadash wrote: I was asked about one who has a constant headache and they place a kolir of flour and water and other ingredients on his temples to ease the pain, whether it is permitted to do so on Pesach; and he replied to forbid; and he concludes there that nevertheless to make it with fruit juice is fine; end quote.
