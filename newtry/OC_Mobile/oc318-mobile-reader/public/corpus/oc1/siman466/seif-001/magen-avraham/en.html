And place upon his wound. This is the wording of the Rambam, Yesodei HaTorah chapter 5: in what case is this said, that one may not heal with prohibitions when they are in the normal way of deriving benefit, for example when they feed him shekatzim and vermin or chametz on Pesach; but when not in the normal way of deriving benefit, for example when they make him a poultice or malugma from chametz or from orlah, or they give him to drink things that have bile mixed with forbidden foods, for there is no benefit to the palate in them, it is permitted for a sick person; end quote. And see Yoreh De'ah at the end of siman 155. And this is the wording of the Kesef Mishnah: it is written in Orchot Chayim on what our master wrote that it is permitted to make a poultice from chametz on Pesach since he has a wound: some say he permitted only from a non-Jew's chametz, and the non-Jew lent it to a Jew or placed it on his wound, and there is no responsibility upon the Jew; and if the non-Jew has no malugma, the Jew transfers flour to him and the non-Jew makes a malugma from it and places it on the Jew's wound; end quote. And if so one must say that what we say "he should not chew, etc." is specifically regarding a Jew's [flour], and because he transgresses bal yera'eh; and see Tosafos at the beginning of folio 29. And it is possible that for this reason they said in Kesubos chapter 13 "the language of the Sages is healing," for we learned "he should not chew, etc.": because it was difficult [to understand] why [say] he should not place upon his wound, for the prohibition is because he transgresses bal yera'eh and not because of benefit, for it is not in the normal way of deriving benefit; rather it comes to teach us incidentally that wheat is good for a wound. And a difficulty: in the Yerushalmi at the beginning of Shabbos chapter 14 we say that a glowing coal is a danger and they place chametz upon it on Pesach, which implies that where there is no danger it is forbidden. And one can say that there it is permitted for a Jew himself to place upon it even though he transgresses bal yera'eh. And in Hagahos Maimoniyos, Maachalos Asuros chapter 14, he wrote that we require that the remedy be known; and see there that he is exceedingly stringent; and see what I wrote in siman 450 se'if 6.
