Place on wound. Rambam chapter 5 Foundations Torah: not healed with prohibitions in manner of enjoyment—worms, chametz on Passover; but not in manner of enjoyment—poultice, melogma from chametz or orlah, bitter drinks with forbidden food—no palate enjoyment—permitted for ill—end; see YD 255. Kesef Mishneh: Orchot Chayim on Rambam permits melogma from gentile chametz loaned or placed on wound without Jewish responsibility; if no melogma for gentile Jew buys flour gentile makes melogma—end. Therefore shall not chew—specifically Jewish chametz—bal yera'eh; see Tosafot beginning 29; perhaps therefore Ketubot 83 'words of sages heal'—why teach place on wound—for prohibition bal yera'eh not benefit—not in manner of enjoyment—came teach incidentally wheat good for wound; Yerushalmi refutes 24 Shabbos: dangerous poultice chametz on Passover—implies without danger forbidden; there permitted Jew himself though bal yera'eh; Hagahot Maimonides 14 from Magen Avraham: remedy must be known—see there very stringent; see 453:6:
