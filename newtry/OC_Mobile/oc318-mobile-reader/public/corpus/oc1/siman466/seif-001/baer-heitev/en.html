He chews. This is the meaning of melogma as written siman 442; and Rokeach wrote that even if he already chewed and placed on his wound before Passover he must destroy and remove them—end. And so explained above siman 442 that any melogma not spoiled before Passover requires destruction—not like Beit Hillel YD siman 255 who wrote that not chewing is only during Passover; but if it was already chametz it is permitted to leave on Passover on any remedy—end. In truth this is not so; and the main difficulty that forced this distinction is from what Rambam wrote that when not in the manner of enjoyment, such as melogma for a wound, one may heal from chametz on Passover—initially there is none, for there it is gentile chametz (and no Jewish responsibility on it; Magen Avraham wrote further a remedy that the Jew buy the flour for the gentile and make melogma on eve of holiday, and Pri Chadash doubts much, for it should only be like a gift where we require recognizable action that he does not wish to accept—and here the opposite). It discusses unlike Jewish chametz as Kesef Mishneh wrote and so Magen Avraham and in responsum Emunat Shmuel siman 27. All this for illness without danger; but with danger even through the Jew himself from his chametz it is also permitted, so Magen Avraham and responsum Emunat Shmuel there and above siman 465 seif 2. Chametz that passed Passover is permitted—Chafetz Chayim see there.
