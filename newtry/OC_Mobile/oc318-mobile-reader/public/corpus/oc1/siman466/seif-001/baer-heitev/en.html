He should chew. And that is the meaning of malugma, as written in siman 442. And the Rokeach wrote that even if it was already chewed and placed on his wound before Pesach, it requires biur and he should remove them; end quote. And so it was explained above in siman 442 that whenever the malugma did not spoil before Pesach, it requires biur; not like Beis Hillel in Yoreh De'ah siman 155, who wrote that "he should not chew" applies specifically during Pesach, but if it was already chametz it is permitted to place it on Pesach for some remedy; end quote. And in truth this is not so; and the main difficulty that forced this distinction, because of the challenge from what the Rambam wrote that when not in the normal way of deriving benefit, such as a malugma for a wound, it is permitted to heal with chametz on Pesach, from the outset is not [a difficulty], for there it deals with a non-Jew's chametz (and there is no responsibility of a Jew upon it. And Magen Avraham wrote a further remedy: that he sell flour to a non-Jew and the non-Jew make the malugma; see there; and Peri Chadash strongly doubts this, for it should be no more than a gift [doron], where we require that it be evident from his actions that he does not wish to accept it, and here it is the opposite), whereas regarding a Jew's chametz [it is different], as the Kesef Mishnah wrote, and so wrote Magen Avraham and in the responsa of Asheri, siman 27. And all this is regarding a sick person who is not in danger; but regarding a sick person who is in danger, even by a Jew himself from his own chametz it is likewise permitted; and so wrote Magen Avraham and in the responsa of Asheri there; and see above siman 465 s.k. 2 from what is written there. And regarding chametz that Pesach passed over it, it is permitted; Chok Yaakov; see there. (As a malugma, even for one not in danger; and to eat it, specifically in a place of danger; Rashba.) Peri Chadash wrote: I was asked about one who has a constant headache and they place a kolir of flour and water and other ingredients on his temples to ease the pain, whether it is permitted to do so on Pesach; and he replied to forbid; and he concludes there that nevertheless to make it with fruit juice is fine; see there.
