See Chok Yaakov letter 17 regarding what he wrote, even less than the measure of a mil, and not like Natz; see there; and in Eliyah Rabbah 16; see there. And in truth at first I did not understand Chok Yaakov's words; also Natz wrote only what is written "without interruption," that the delays not combine, as above in siman 459 se'if 2. However I saw that Chok Yaakov's words are built on pillars of reason and truth. For Terumas HaDeshen in siman 123 debated whether the delays combine, and wrote that a certain great authority brought a proof from a drip, which implies there is combination between drop and drop for the measure, etc.; and Terumas HaDeshen concluded that one should distinguish: with complete work the delays do not combine, but with slight work it does not nullify; and a proof from Pesachim 39b: Rav Pappa said, and that is when it is done drop upon drop with no delay between them. And now, if in general the delays combine, what does Rav Pappa teach us here? Rather, with complete work the delays do not combine; and a continuous drip that is not complete work requires drop upon drop so that the delays not combine. And it is possible that this was the view of the great authority who permitted, that the delays do not combine, only that he abbreviated in his wording; end quote. You see from what Terumas HaDeshen first wrote, that a certain great authority brought a proof to permit from drop by drop, [and] "which implies" there is combination of the delays between drop and drop for the measure (of a mil): the word "which implies" are Terumas HaDeshen's words, wanting to explain the great authority, that he held drop by drop one after another with some interruption between the falling of drop one and drop two upon the flour. And so it is somewhat implied from Rashi's explanation: drop after drop, nearby and immediately, with no delay between them; and so is the Ran's wording of blessed memory there. And Terumas HaDeshen explains that even any amount [means] there is no interruption between drop and drop; and what Rashi wrote "no delay between them" means none at all; and so is implied by Taz's wording "without interruption," namely that before the completion of the falling of drop one the continuous striking of drop two comes, and it is like continuous work, only that it is slight work; therefore we require that there be no interruption at all; but if there is interruption, and among all of them for the measure of a mil the delays combine; which is not so with complete work, where the delays do not combine. And this too is the view of the great authority who wants to permit, namely with complete work; and a proof from the fact that Rav Pappa needed to teach us regarding a drip: hence with complete work the delays do not combine (not as Terumas HaDeshen at first understood his words; and that is what he wrote "only that he abbreviated in his wording"; see there carefully). And see Magen Avraham 459 letter 4; and the Rav in 459 in the gloss of se'if 4 wrote without qualification, which implies even with complete work the delays combine: the difficulty returns to its place, why did Rav Pappa teach us regarding a drip? He should have said on the mishnah "three women occupy themselves with dough" at the end of chapter Kol Sha'ah, that the delays combine, as Terumas HaDeshen wrote there. And hence for us [the law of] drop by drop is without any interruption; but if there was any interruption, even though in the combination of all the delays there was not the measure of a mil, nevertheless since from the beginning of the continuous drip until its end there was the measure of a mil and more, and he delayed a little between them, it caused chimutz, for it is not complete work, as said. And that is what Chok Yaakov wrote, not like Natz, as said. And according to this there are three explanations in this of drop by drop, and Chok Yaakov's words are true; and the students should not hurry to learn except with deliberation. And understand this.
