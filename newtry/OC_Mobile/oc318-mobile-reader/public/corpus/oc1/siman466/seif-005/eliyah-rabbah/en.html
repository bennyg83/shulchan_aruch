[5] [Levush] Some permit, etc. From the fact that he changed from the wording of the Shulchan Aruch, who wrote "permit" without qualification, it implies that he holds to be stringent; and the view of the Shulchan Aruch implies to be lenient; and so is proven from what I found in the Derishah, this is its wording: it appears to me that if it is unknown from what the sack became moistened, one should be lenient even to eat it on Pesach even if it dried, by force of a double doubt: a doubt of fruit juice or wall sweat; and if you say from water that dried, perhaps nothing fell into it. And even though in siman 467 the Rama does not permit eating it on Pesach by force of a double doubt, one should compare it to the double doubt that he permits there in se'if 12; until here. Behold he permits wall sweat entirely; but in Magen Avraham he wrote that most poskim forbid, and his words are not compelling to one who studies carefully. Also what he wrote that in the Remazim he forbids: I looked in the Remazim on parchment and he omitted this; and further, we do not find an explanation specifically on what the Rosh challenges regarding the Rokeach. And so it is implied in Sefer Amarcal, this is its wording: once he placed the sack in a cellar and because of the moisture he found moist drops on the sack, and they permitted it, as we learned in Machshirin chapter 2: sweat of houses, pits, trenches, caves, and human sweat are tahor; if he drank tahor water and sweated, his sweat is tahor; if he entered drawn water and sweated, his sweat is tamei; if he dried himself and afterward sweated, [it is] tahor; a pool in the house and the house sweats because of the pool, [it is] tamei. And that which we learned in chapter 6, that all fish brine is presumed tamei: that is because ordinary brine has water put into it. And further we learned there: water that comes from the eye, from the mouth, urine whether of adults or of minors: these convey tumah and are machshir; a zav's flux and his saliva and urine, etc.: these do not convey tumah and are not machshir: sweat and putrid mucus and feces and blood and the liquid of an eight-month [fetus]. However, regarding hechsher it applies only to something called a liquid; until here the wording of the aforementioned book on parchment; and from it one may learn many new laws. Chok Yaakov wrote: if it sweats from the heat and vapor of hot water, according to all it causes chimutz.
