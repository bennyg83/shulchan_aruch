And likewise urine. The Beis Yosef wrote in the name of Orchot Chayim that all toldos of water, such as water of the mouth and nose and eye and ear and urine, cause chimutz; end quote. And since he hinged the matter on being a toldah of water, if so there is no distinction between urine of minors or of adults coming from the orifice of the rectum, as stated in Machshirin chapter 6 mishnah 5 regarding what is called a toldah of water. And even though chicken and animal dung do not cause chimutz, as stated in the Tur and Beis Yosef (and he should sift the dung outside because of bal teshaktzu, as the Bach wrote), one must say that animal urine is different, for it is not called a toldah of water, as the Rambam wrote in Tum'as Ochalin chapter 10, since it is fruit juice regarding hechsher; and therefore the Mordechai wrote regarding mice that urinated on wheat that we do not presume chametz until the chimutz is seen before us. And according to this it appears that Rabbeinu Yeruham and Orchot Chayim do not dispute the Mordechai at all, not as the Beis Yosef implies: for Orchot Chayim wrote explicitly that what is called a toldah of water causes chimutz, namely human urine. However in the Agudah, chapter Kol Sha'ah, regarding mice that urinated on wheat, it implies that they cause chimutz; see there. And water of the mouth causes chimutz as with a human, as stated above in se'if 2. And blood, even though it is otherwise forbidden because of the blood prohibition, nevertheless is nullified in sixty; which is not so for the chametz prohibition, which is in any amount.
And milk. The same applies to milk with a chirik [chelev/fat], even boiling and clear, and all the more so when congealed; Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah. And see above siman 462 regarding the law of fat; and Magen Avraham wrote that bile-water also does not cause chimutz. And it is obvious that all these that the Tur and the Mechaber wrote here that cause chimutz means if it stood without being worked for the measure of chimutz, for they are no worse than water, which through being worked does not come to chimutz; and this is easy.
