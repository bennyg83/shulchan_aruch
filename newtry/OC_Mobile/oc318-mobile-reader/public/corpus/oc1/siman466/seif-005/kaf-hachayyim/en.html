And place it upon a wound, etc. The grains become chametz through the saliva in one's mouth when they remain on the wound for the time needed to walk a mil, and one then violates the prohibitions against chametz being seen and found. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 3. Even if the grain was already chewed and placed on the wound before Pesach, it must be eliminated and removed. Rokeach, siman 271; Chok Yaakov, subsection 1; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 2; Mekor Chaim, subsection 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, there. It must be removed when the sixth hour begins on the eve of Pesach, unless it has already spoiled to the point that it is unfit for a dog's consumption, as stated in siman 442. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, there. See se'if 2 there and our words there, with the help of Heaven.
