Rabbeinu Yonah writes that rice and millet bread require Borei Minei Mezonos because he holds that millet also has that blessing, and that millet is commonly made into bread just like rice. The Mechaber, however, rules that millet requires Shehakol, even when made into bread, because it is not ordinarily used for bread as rice is. The disagreement may not be over reality, since local practices differ. According to this, regarding bread made in many places from Turkish wheat, which is greatly improved by baking and is ordinarily used only for bread rather than cooked as a dish, it is difficult why one should not recite Borei Peri HaAdamah, like wheat flour, were it not that wheat has a further elevation available to it. According to the first answer cited by Magen Avraham, however, this is readily understood. Its improvement is itself its disadvantage: because it changed for the better, Borei Peri HaAdamah no longer applies. Groats made from barley require Borei Minei Mezonos and Me'ein Shalosh. This disagrees with what Magen Avraham writes in seifim katanim 2 and 7 merely because their husks were removed, as discussed there. Regarding buckwheat, where Taz rules Shehakol, we already wrote in Magen Avraham, seif katan 2, that this requires study. Why should the blessing not be Borei Peri HaAdamah? Even if the kernels adhere somewhat, so long as their appearance remains recognizable they should retain Borei Peri HaAdamah, since they have no further superior form. Even when ground into flour, in places where buckwheat, called retchky, is commonly grown primarily to be ground and made into flour, I know no sound reason why one should not recite Borei Peri HaAdamah, like wheat flour, were it not that wheat has a further superior use. See siman 202, Magen Avraham seif katan 18, and siman 204, seif 2. This requires study.
