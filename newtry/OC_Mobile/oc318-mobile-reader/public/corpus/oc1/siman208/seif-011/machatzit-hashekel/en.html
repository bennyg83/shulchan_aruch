(Seif katan 11) It is the majority, etc. This is also Rambam’s view, and Beit Yosef retracted his earlier understanding. Tur writes in the name of the Rosh that this applies specifically when the other cooked food is the majority. Beit Yosef initially writes that Rambam appears to disagree and not require a majority, since Rambam says, “provided it is not mixed with another substance, but is rice alone.” This implies that if it is not rice alone, even where the other food is not the majority, one does not recite Borei Minei Mezonos, unlike the Rosh. In Shulchan Aruch he first cites Rambam’s wording, implying this broader rule, and then concludes with the Rosh’s wording, “if he mixed it, etc., and the other cooked food is the majority.” Bach therefore asks that the ruling appears self-contradictory. Magen Avraham answers that in Shulchan Aruch Beit Yosef retracted and understood Rambam to agree with the Rosh. This is also implied by Kesef Mishneh, which cites the Rosh’s requirement that the other dish be the majority without indicating any dispute.
Would one think, etc. Although Rambam writes without qualification, “provided it is not mixed,” this does not mean that even the smallest admixture changes the blessing. Rather, his unqualified wording means that it is not mixed to the point that the other cooked food becomes the majority. He did not need to state this explicitly, for how could one think that a minute amount mixed into rice would cause the blessing to follow that other food? Thus Rambam does not disagree with the Rosh, and Shulchan Aruch is not self-contradictory. Rambam’s wording provides no proof that he rejects the requirement that the other species be the majority.
And even Beit Yosef wrote only that it appears, etc. Meaning, Beit Yosef himself did not conclusively decide that Rambam disagrees with the Rosh; he wrote only that it appears so.
