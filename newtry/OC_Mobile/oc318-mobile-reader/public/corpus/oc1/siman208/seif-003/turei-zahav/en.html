And if it was added merely to bind, etc. At first glance, even if the five species constitute the majority, since they were not added as food they lack the significance of food and one should not recite Borei Minei Mezonos over them. Although with other foods the blessing generally follows the majority, here the grain is even less significant, for it is not considered food at all when used solely as a binding agent. Accordingly, cakes made principally for the honey and spices, where the flour serves only to bind the mixture, should not require Borei Minei Mezonos. Nevertheless, one should not be lenient, because the substantial quantity of flour provides meaningful benefit. A comparable principle appears in Yoreh Deah, siman 103, regarding one who deliberately increased a prohibited substance, where there is benefit from the increased quantity despite its negative taste. Here too, the five species may retain significance because of their quantity even though they serve only as a binder. This appears to be the doubt raised by Tosafos and cited here in Shulchan Aruch. It is therefore proper to be stringent and eat such foods during a meal. In seif 3, where the grain was added to bind or thicken, the Shulchan Aruch did not require this stringency, because there the five species were not the majority. When used solely as a binder in a minority proportion they are certainly nullified. Here, however, they constitute the majority. This appears correct to me.
And it is proper to be stringent and eat it, etc. Tosafos writes this in the chapter Keitzad Mevarchin, 37a. I am greatly puzzled why they resolved the uncertainty by requiring the food to be eaten during a meal, in which case no separate blessing is recited, rather than instructing one to recite Shehakol, since one who recites Shehakol over any food fulfills his obligation. At the end of siman 204 it is likewise stated that when the proper blessing is uncertain, one recites Shehakol. The Tur here, regarding the uncertainty over the abridged three-faceted blessing cited in seif 4, concludes that when one is uncertain about the first blessing he recites Shehakol, but an after-blessing may be recited only in its established form; therefore, one should eat the food only during a meal. Since the present passage concerns the first blessing, Tosafos should likewise have said to recite Shehakol. It must be that the uncertainty here also concerns the after-blessing: if Borei Minei Mezonos is required initially, the abridged three-faceted blessing will also be required afterward. Accordingly, if one wishes to eat less than a kezayis of this food, or of the honey cakes mentioned above, so that no after-blessing is required, as stated in siman 210, he need not eat it during a meal. He may recite Shehakol beforehand and no blessing afterward. This appears clear.
And he is exempt either way. There is a scribal error here. The text should read “and he is exempt either way,” and this is the reading in Tosafos.
