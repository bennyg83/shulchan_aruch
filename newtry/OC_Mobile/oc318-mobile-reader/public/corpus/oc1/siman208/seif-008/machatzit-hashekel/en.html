(Seif katan 8) For eating and chewing, etc. This is also implied by Berachos 38a, which discusses patisa, flour boiled with water or another liquid. Rav says Shehakol, while Shmuel says Borei Minei Mezonos. Rav Chisda explains that they do not disagree: on a thick mixture one recites Borei Minei Mezonos, and on a thin mixture one recites Shehakol. The unqualified term “thick” implies even if it is not fit to chew. Accordingly, “thin” must mean thin enough to drink. Although the Gemara says merely “thin,” it relies on its preceding unqualified statement concerning a thick mixture. The latter must include even a mixture that cannot be chewed, provided that it is thick, so the contrasting thin mixture must be one fit to drink. This interpretation is more reasonable than the reverse, because it is more natural for the later phrase to rely on the earlier one than for the earlier phrase to rely on a qualification stated only afterward.
This is also implied by Rashi regarding chavitz, etc. I cited his words above in seif katan 2. Rashi’s term “congealed” does not imply a mixture so thick that it must be chewed.
