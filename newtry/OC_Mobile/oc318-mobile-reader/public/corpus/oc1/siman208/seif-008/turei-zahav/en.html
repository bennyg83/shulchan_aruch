Over millet bread, etc. Since millet is not ordinarily made into bread, one recites Shehakol, unlike rice in the preceding seif. Regarding the identification of rice and millet, Rashi explains in the chapter Keitzad Mevarchin that orez is millet, called hirz in the vernacular. Tosafos cites an opinion that orez is reis and dochan is millet, and common practice follows this interpretation. So write Beit Yosef and Levush. Maharil, however, writes that dochan is reis and orez is hirz. My father-in-law, the Bach, writes that a God-fearing person should eat neither cooked hirz nor cooked reis, whether whole or crushed, except during a meal. It appears to me that since neither can require the abridged three-faceted after-blessing, and the only uncertainty concerns the first blessing, whether Shehakol or Borei Minei Mezonos, one should recite Shehakol over both reis and hirz and Borei Nefashos afterward. Groats made from barley kernels crushed in a mill and then cooked certainly require Borei Minei Mezonos and afterward the abridged three-faceted blessing, since barley is one of the five species. Groats made from buckwheat, which is not one of the five species, require Shehakol and Borei Nefashos. This is clear.
