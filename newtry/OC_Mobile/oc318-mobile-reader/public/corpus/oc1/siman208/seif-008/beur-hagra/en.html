Seif 8: On, etc. Rif and Rambam. Although the Baraisa there teaches both rice bread and millet bread, nevertheless, since the Baraisa concerning one who chews grain mentions only rice, and likewise the passage, “These are the cooked dishes,” etc., mentions rice, it follows that on millet one recites only Shehakol. The Rosh rejected this proof and wrote that rice was singled out to exclude the opinion of Rabbi Yochanan ben Nuri, but the same law applies to millet. Rabbeinu Yonah writes that rice is mentioned because even when whole its blessing is Mezonos, whereas millet requires that it be split, since it is not ordinarily eaten whole, as stated above. Both explanations are needed, for according to Rabbeinu Yonah one may ask why millet was not mentioned in the Baraisa concerning one who chews grain, while according to the Rosh one may ask why millet was not mentioned in the Baraisa concerning cooked dishes. Rabbeinu Yonah and the Tur write in the name of a Gaon that the same law applies to panicum, which is likewise sustaining like millet and, according to Rashi there, is itself millet. They, however, explain it in accordance with Tosafos, s.v. Rashi, etc., “and some explain,” etc. Beit Yosef writes that the common understanding follows this explanation. Shulchan Aruch rules without qualification like Rambam, who wrote, “millet bread,” etc., “or of,” etc., and Shulchan Aruch added “panizo.” It appears that this is panicum, except that in their language they call it panizo.
