Completely mashed, etc. This applies specifically when they were pressed through a perforated utensil so that they become very fine, or when one made a cooked dish from legume flour, as Lechem Chamudot and Shelah write. But if he merely mashed them with a spoon, he recites Borei Pri HaAdamah, as Magen Avraham writes. I am surprised, because Shelah, page 92, writes that mashed peas receive Shehakol. In practice the words of Magen Avraham appear correct, and so appears from Olat Tamid, though the matter requires study.
And afterward Borei Nefashot Rabbot, etc. Magen Avraham wonders why one should not recite Me'ein Shalosh afterward, as with bread. In my humble opinion, we descend one level: bread would warrant Birkat HaMazon, so in the doubtful case one recites Me'ein Shalosh; here the food would warrant Borei Minei Mezonot, so when there is not a kezayit of grain within the time of kedei achilat pras one recites Borei Nefashot. Bread is also more significant. See above, seif katan 3. I later found that Perishah explains that above the case concerns even a minute quantity because the five-grain flour was intentionally added for flavor; intentional use gives it significance. Here it was mixed unintentionally. It appears more correct, however, that here the mixture was originally flour with flour and does not impart a distinct flavor, so we require a kezayit within kedei achilat pras, whereas there from the outset it was added to a food or cooked dish to impart flavor.
[Levush] For You, Hashem, are good and beneficent, etc. Lechem Chamudot and Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah write that the Levush does not have the words "and beneficent to all." I say that since the Levush later writes, "and some say, after he says: for You, Hashem, are good and beneficent to all," it is clear that he did have this wording. Here the word "to all" simply dropped out in printing. So also appears in seif 12.
[Levush] In our text, etc. This is likewise the text of Rif, Rambam, Raavan siman 190, Shibbolei HaLeket, Tanya, Tashbetz, Kaftor VaFerach chapter 10, and Sefer HaManhig. Therefore one should not change it.
[Levush] And one should not say it, etc. Bach writes that the sanctity of the Land, which flows into it from the supernal land, also flows into its fruits, which draw nourishment from the sanctity of the Shechinah. Thus it is appropriate to say "and we shall eat of its fruit and be satisfied with its goodness," because by eating its fruits we are nourished from the sanctity of the Shechinah. I am surprised at his surprise, because if so, what is the Gemara's question at the end of the first chapter of Sotah: why did Moshe desire to enter Eretz Yisrael, was it to eat its fruit and be satisfied with its goodness? One could answer that he desired its sanctity. Perhaps Moshe was different because the Shechinah was constantly with him even without eating the fruits of Eretz Yisrael, as Rashi explains at the end of Parashat Beha'alotcha. The Gemara therefore precisely asks whether Moshe needed this benefit. In practical law the matter requires study, for Shibbolei HaLeket, Tanya, Kaftor VaFerach, and Shelah have this wording, while they do not have at the beginning of the blessing "to eat of its fruit and be satisfied with its goodness." Abudarham implies that even Baal Halachot Gedolot has only "to eat of its fruit" and not "and we shall eat," but I saw in Baal Halachot Gedolot that both phrases appear, and likewise in Kol Bo.
[Levush] It is correct, etc. Abudarham, Recanati, and Shelah write likewise, and so rule the later authorities.
