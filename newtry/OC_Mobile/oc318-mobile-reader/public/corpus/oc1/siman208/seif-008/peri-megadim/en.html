See Magen Avraham, seif katan 10. Nevertheless, if one recited Shehakol, he fulfilled his obligation. See Taz, seif katan 5, since there is no uncertainty regarding the after-blessing, which is Borei Nefashos. Taz, however, holds that because of the doubt one should recite Borei Peri HaAdamah, and this covers it even if its proper blessing is Borei Minei Mezonos, because it is nevertheless produce of the ground. See Magen Avraham, seif katan 22, who writes that one who recited Borei Peri HaEtz over grapes did not fulfill his obligation. Taz in siman 204, seif katan 11, apparently disagrees, as I wrote there in Peri Megadim, and follows his position here. According to this, if one recited Borei Peri HaAdamah over bread made from the five species of grain, he would fulfill his obligation after the fact, because it is ordinarily eaten in that form. This differs from millet bread, which is not ordinarily eaten that way. See Kesef Mishneh, chapter 3 of Hilchos Berachos, halachah 7, and Taz, siman 167, seif katan 11. According to Magen Avraham, seif katan 12, however, anything that has changed completely and has been finely ground may have left the category of produce of the ground, so that only Shehakol applies. See Magen Avraham, siman 202, seif katan 6, and here, seif katan 13. Above we discussed wheat flour in Berachos 36a.
