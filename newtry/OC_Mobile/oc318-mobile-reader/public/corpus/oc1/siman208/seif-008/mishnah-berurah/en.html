(33) Over millet bread, etc. Although millet grows from the ground and improves when made into bread, once it has left its original form it can no longer be described as “the fruit of the ground,” while HaMotzi is recited only over bread from the five species of grain. Therefore, one recites Shehakol. Another reason may be that this is not its usual manner of consumption, since millet is not ordinarily made into bread. According to this reason, in countries where corn is regularly made into bread, one would apparently recite Borei Peri HaAdamah, since that is its normal use. Peri Megadim. Responsa Chasam Sofer, siman 50, however, concludes that one never recites Borei Peri HaAdamah over such bread. These crops are not planted for this purpose, but chiefly for birds and for fattening geese, and only under pressure do people make bread from them. Bread is therefore not their primary produce, and Borei Peri HaAdamah should not be recited. See there. He remains uncertain, however, whether perhaps they are included in the category of rice, whose bread and cooked dishes require Borei Minei Mezonos. It is therefore proper initially to eat them only during a meal.
(34) And panizo. Rabbeinu Yonah has the reading panizo; both terms refer to the same species. It is more nourishing than other legumes, yet the ruling teaches that one nevertheless recites Shehakol over its bread, like other legumes.
(35) One recites Shehakol, etc. The same applies to a cooked dish made from their flour, as stated below.
(36) From other species of legumes. The same applies to millet, in accordance with the Mechaber’s earlier ruling that millet has the same status as legumes.
(37) They remained whole. This does not literally require that they remain completely whole. So long as they were not entirely crushed and some of their appearance and form remain discernible, their blessing is Borei Peri HaAdamah. Therefore, one recites Borei Peri HaAdamah over what we call buckwheat porridge. Acharonim.
(38) They were completely crushed. This applies specifically when one prepared a dish from legume flour, which is not their ordinary manner of consumption, or when one mashed them through a perforated utensil until they became as fine as flour, including what is made from buckwheat and called milch groats. If, however, he cooked whole legumes and mashed them with a spoon, this is their ordinary manner of consumption. Moreover, their substance remains present, and he recites Borei Peri HaAdamah.
(39) Or they are not, etc. As stated in siman 205, seif 1.
(40) One recites Shehakol. Chayei Adam writes that if, after the fact, one recited Borei Peri HaAdamah, he fulfilled his obligation.
