(Seif katan 5) Whole, etc. This differs from rice, which is ordinarily cooked whole. Magen Avraham, seif 6, seif katan 10, explains that according to Beit Yosef one recites Borei Minei Mezonos on rice even when it did not become mashed. Here, however, wheat must specifically become mashed and adhere together. Wheat whose husks were not removed does not become mashed and adhere together, and therefore requires Shehakol. Rice is different because it is ordinarily cooked in this manner, as Magen Avraham writes in the name of Beit Yosef. According to Rama, however, rice too requires mashing. On that view, rice and wheat have the same law, and the Kesef Mishneh’s distinction has no place.
