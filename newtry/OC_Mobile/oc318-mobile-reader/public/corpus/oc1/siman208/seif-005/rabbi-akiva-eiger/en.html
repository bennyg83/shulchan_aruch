Seif 5. And afterward he recites Borei nefashos rabbos. Since he did not state here as well that it is a doubt whether he should recite afterward me'ein shalosh, as he wrote in seif 6, it is clear that the Mechaber holds that the doubt of Tosafot is only with raw grain, where one initially recites Borei pri ha'adamah. But where one recites shehakol, he certainly recites afterward Borei nefashos rabbos. Seemingly there is proof to this from the sugya in Berachos 38 regarding a salted olive, where one recites initially shehakol and afterward Borei nefashos rabbos. Thus, even though an olive is nevertheless of the seven species, he does not recite afterward me'ein shalosh, since initially he recites shehakol. But seemingly there is a contradiction to this from Tosafot, who wrote that the Amoraim in the Yerushalmi were in doubt about this, whether one recites afterward Borei pri ha'adamah. One could say that they certainly held that where one initially recites Borei pri ha'adamah, he recites afterward me'ein shalosh, but they were in doubt regarding flour, whether one initially recites Borei pri ha'adamah like Rav Nachman, or shehakol like Rabbi Yehudah, and consequently there is a doubt regarding the final blessing. Perforce, then, there is no distinction between a case where one initially recites Borei pri ha'adamah and a case where one recites shehakol.
However, one of the excellent young scholars answered me well that nevertheless they could have eaten flour and also eaten roasted kernels and another food whose after-blessing is Borei nefashos rabbos, and then recited me'ein shalosh and Borei nefashos rabbos. The Terumas HaDeshen wrote something similar, and it is brought in the Taz at the end of this siman. Perforce, then, regarding the roasted kernels themselves they were in doubt whether one recites afterward me'ein shalosh. Nevertheless, it is explicit in the Rashba, in Chiddushim, that both regarding wheat flour and barley flour there is a doubt in the final blessing, and the Beit Yosef himself brought this. How, then, did he rule here simply not like the Rashba? According to the view of the Rashba, one must say regarding the salted olive mentioned above that since it changed because of the salting, the significance of the seven species was nullified from it. But flour, even after it changed, is fit to make bread from it and recite Birkas HaMazon; therefore, when he did not bake it, he recites me'ein shalosh afterward. Afterward I found that Eshel Avraham addressed this and wrote to answer that the Rashba follows his own view, for he rules like Rav Nachman that on wheat flour one recites Borei pri ha'adamah, since he holds that even when it changed, it remains in its status. Therefore he holds that also with barley flour, although its blessing is shehakol because it changed, it changed only regarding the blessing; nevertheless, regarding the final blessing, even though it changed, it remains in its status. But according to what was ruled regarding wheat flour, that one recites shehakol because it changed, the same applies that it changed regarding the final blessing; see there. According to this, the case of the salted olive is also resolved, for there it follows the initial assumption that over boiled vegetables one recites shehakol, because they changed, and therefore one recites afterward Borei nefashos rabbos. However, aside from the fact that this is not implied by the simple meaning of the Rashba's words, and indeed from the Rashba's proof from the baraisa that it was not made into grain it implies that according to all views, even according to Rabbi Yehudah, one recites afterward me'ein shalosh, in any event this is not enough to settle the words of the Mechaber. For his very ruling that one recites shehakol over wheat flour is only because of a doubt among the great authorities, as the Beit Yosef wrote. If so, there still remains a doubt in the final blessing, for perhaps the law is that initially one recites Borei pri ha'adamah and afterward me'ein shalosh. It is forced to say that this is like a double doubt: perhaps initially one recites shehakol; and even if one recites Borei pri ha'adamah, nevertheless perhaps the blessing afterward is Borei nefashos rabbos, for perhaps even one who chews wheat recites afterward Borei nefashos rabbos. More than this, one can say that the Mechaber holds as primary halacha that one who chews wheat recites afterward Borei nefashos rabbos, as appears from the wording of the Shulchan Aruch, and he brought that Tosafot were in doubt only as a mere concern, to be careful initially. Therefore, regarding wheat flour, since there is an additional factor, namely that one recites initially shehakol because it changed, we certainly rely on this to recite afterward Borei nefashos rabbos. Still, this requires study.
