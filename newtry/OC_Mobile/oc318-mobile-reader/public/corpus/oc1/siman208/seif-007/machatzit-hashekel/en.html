(Seif katan 7) Within, etc. This appears to be Beit Yosef’s view. In Shulchan Aruch he first rules without qualification that one recites Borei Nefashos afterward, and only then writes that Tosafos were uncertain. This is Beit Yosef’s usual method: the first opinion is the principal one in his view.
Perhaps the water is not nullified, etc. Although seif 2 establishes that when one of the five grain species is mixed with another food, one recites Borei Minei Mezonos even if the other food is the majority, here one’s primary intent is for the water, so the water may not be nullified to the grain. Nevertheless, the kernels are not nullified. Although his primary intent is for the water, and this is why the water is not nullified to the kernels, the superior status of the five grain species at least prevents the kernels from being nullified to the water. He should therefore recite separate blessings over the water and the kernels. Since Magen Avraham is not certain that the water is not nullified to the kernels, he wrote above, siman 205, seif katan 6, that one should first recite Shehakol over the water and afterward recite the appropriate blessing over the kernels.
What Shulchan Aruch writes in seif 5, “flour, even wheat flour,” means that this is not limited to barley flour, which is harmful because of worms, as Magen Avraham writes in siman 204, seif katan 8, and therefore certainly requires only Shehakol. Even wheat flour, which is not harmful, likewise requires only Shehakol. This applies not only when it was ground very finely and is unfit to eat, but even when it was ground only somewhat and is fit to eat. The same applies to flour made from roasted kernels: although it is fit to eat, one recites only Shehakol.
