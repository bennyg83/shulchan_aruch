Until it becomes crushed. Beit Yosef was uncertain whether the rice must specifically be crushed into a porridge-like consistency before Borei Minei Mezonos is recited, while whole cooked rice would require Borei Peri HaAdamah like wheat, or whether rice differs from wheat because rice is ordinarily cooked. It appears that Rama added the words “until it becomes crushed” because of this uncertainty. In any event, if one recites Borei Peri HaAdamah he fulfills his obligation, since that was the rice’s original blessing. Although one recites Shehakol rather than Borei Peri HaAdamah over millet bread below, that case differs because millet is not ordinarily eaten in that form. Here, therefore, one should recite Borei Peri HaAdamah and not Borei Minei Mezonos until it is certain that the latter is required.
But rice alone. This is the wording of Rif and Rambam. Rif’s language implies that when rice is mixed with another species it has no special status, even if it constitutes the majority. Rav and Shmuel did not accord rice the blessing Borei Minei Mezonos, and they were refuted by a Baraisa that did accord rice this status. Rif implies that they were refuted only regarding rice in its distinct form, but when it is no longer independently recognizable, even if it is the majority, they were not refuted and rice has no special status. Rambam’s language may likewise be understood this way, unlike Rosh and Tur, who write that rice loses its status only when another ingredient constitutes the majority. Beit Yosef discusses this, yet in Shulchan Aruch he appears to retract and rule that so long as rice is the majority, the mixture is not considered a true mixture, as indicated by his continuation, “and if one mixed,” which follows Rosh and Tur. This is difficult, for the Baraisa that refuted Rav and Shmuel did not discuss rice in a mixture at all. Why, then, should we reject their view in a mixture containing even a small amount of another species? According to what I wrote, that because of uncertainty one should recite Shehakol over rice, there is no practical difference. If the other cooked food is the majority, the blessing certainly follows it; if rice is the majority, one recites Shehakol and thereby exempts the entire mixture.
Like the blessing of that cooked food. Rav and Shmuel said that one recites Borei Minei Mezonos specifically over the five species of grain, provided that they were not added merely as a binder, as explained in this siman. With other species, the blessing follows the majority.
