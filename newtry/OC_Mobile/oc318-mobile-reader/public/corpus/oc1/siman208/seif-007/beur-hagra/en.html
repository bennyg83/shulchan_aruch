Seif 7: And if he cooked it, etc. There it states that rice bread was brought before him, etc.; and there, “These are the cooked dishes,” etc., which refers to food that was cooked. Likewise, there it says, “Although the pieces,” etc. The same applies when it is like bread in its original form, as stated regarding wheat. Since it says, “Although,” etc., it implies that the same law applies even when the pieces no longer remain intact and it is merely cooked, as stated there regarding wheat.
Until it became mashed, etc. This is as stated above regarding the five species in seif 2. It does not mean specifically that they became fully mashed, but that they no longer remained whole, as stated there. Beit Yosef omitted this qualification from Shulchan Aruch because he holds that one should distinguish between wheat and rice. Wheat is ordinarily eaten only when split or crushed, whereas rice is ordinarily eaten in this form; therefore, even when whole its blessing is Mezonos. This is also implied by the Gemara, which says, “These are the cooked dishes: chilka,” etc., “and rice.” On this basis Rabbeinu Yonah rejected the Rif’s view that the ruling applies specifically to rice but not millet, merely because only rice was mentioned, as stated in seif 8. See Magen Avraham.
And afterward, etc. As stated above.
And it, etc. Berachos 36b: “The matter itself: Rav and Shmuel,” and both statements were necessary, etc. Although the latter statement of Rav and Shmuel was refuted, the former was not refuted. Rif and the other poskim.
And the dish, etc. Rosh there writes that if the rice constitutes the majority, it is treated as though it were eaten by itself, as stated in several places. This differs from the five species, as stated in seif 2.
