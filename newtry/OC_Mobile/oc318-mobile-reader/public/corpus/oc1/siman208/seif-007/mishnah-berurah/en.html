(24) One who chews it. Meaning, he eats it raw.
(25) Rice. Rice is reis, and millet is hirz in the vernacular. Beit Yosef and Levush. Some reverse these identifications. Therefore, some are stringent not to eat either of them after it has been crushed except during a meal, or else to recite Shehakol because of the uncertainty. Lechem Chamudos writes that the generally accepted identification is that rice is reis and millet is hirz. This is also evident from Birkei Yosef and Mateh Yehudah. I likewise found in Maaseh Rav, recording the practices of the Gra, that rice is reis and that one recites Borei Minei Mezonos over it.
(26) Until it becomes crushed. This means even if it becomes somewhat crushed through cooking. If the kernels remain whole, one recites Borei Peri HaAdamah. It is possible that even if only the husk was removed from the rice, as with the rice used in our countries, it is no longer considered whole, and one recites Borei Minei Mezonos. Peri Megadim. Nevertheless, it appears there that if one recited Borei Peri HaAdamah he fulfilled his obligation. See there.
(27) Bread. The same applies to a cooked dish.
(28) One recites Borei Minei Mezonos over it. Its bread or cooked dish satisfies and sustains the heart like the five species of grain, and is more significant than other legumes. Nevertheless, bread made from it is not considered full-fledged bread of the five species of grain, and one therefore does not recite HaMotzi.
(29) Borei Nefashos. Bircas HaMazon and the abridged three-faceted blessing apply only to the five species of grain because of their special importance.
(30) Provided that, etc. Although with the five species of grain we rule above in seif 2 that when they are mixed with other species, even as a minority, the blessing follows them, rice is not equally significant. Therefore, if one prepared a cooked dish or bread in which flour from other species constitutes the majority, he recites the blessing appropriate to those species.
(31) Alone. The same law applies when rice is merely the majority. The wording “alone” teaches a greater novelty: even then, the after-blessing is only Borei Nefashos.
(32) It is the majority, etc. Many Acharonim rule this way.
