And the kernels. See Taz. At first glance, the reason for the first blessing is that eating whole cooked kernels is not an unusual manner of eating, and therefore one recites Borei Peri HaAdamah. Tosafos, Berachos 37a, beginning “One who chews,” are uncertain regarding the after-blessing because we do not find that the Sages instituted Me'ein Shalosh over produce whose first blessing is Borei Peri HaAdamah. See there. I later saw that the words of our master Taz are founded upon truth and sound reasoning. According to the Rosh and Tur, Me'ein Shalosh over the fruits is D'Oraisa, as explained in siman 209. What, then, is the basis of the doubt? We must say that the wheat mentioned in the verse praising Eretz Yisrael refers to an important manner of eating, namely bread or porridge that satiates, and it is regarding such food that the verse states, “and you shall bless,” unlike a cooked whole kernel. See Magen Avraham, siman 211, seif katan 8, on this. This is what the Rosh writes there. Tosafos, however, may be uncertain for another reason, because they perhaps hold that Me'ein Shalosh is entirely D'Rabbanan and we do not find that the Sages instituted it over this form, which lacks special importance. The Rosh also writes similarly to Tosafos; see there. Know that when the kernels remain whole, the special importance of the five species of grain is lost, and we follow the majority. If the majority consists of legumes, buckwheat, or the like, then even if nearly half consists of the five species of grain, one recites Shehakol. Levush, seif 4, appears to imply this, for their primary importance is that they satiate and nourish when made into porridge or bread, whereas whole cooked kernels are like other produce. See there. When the quantities are equal, see Perishah, who writes that exact equality cannot be achieved; one should therefore add to whichever species he wishes until it becomes the majority. What I will write, with Hashem's help, in seif katan 10 regarding unripe grapes is also relevant; see there.
