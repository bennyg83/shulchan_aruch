And afterward Borei Nefashos. This applies even according to Tosafos’ view in seif 4, as stated below regarding “if he cooked it,” etc., and “afterward,” etc. See Rashi there, s.v. velo klum, and likewise the other poskim in siman 207. The Rif and Rosh write in chapter 6 of Berachos, seif 8: “We hold that whenever the Baraisa says that afterward he recites nothing, he recites Borei Nefashos,” etc. At first glance, it is difficult to understand how “nothing” can mean Borei Nefashos, and what their source is. It appears that the explanation is as follows. At the end of chapter 6, 44b, Rabbi Yehudah bar Abba said in the name of our master: “After an egg,” etc., “and meat,” etc., “one recites Borei Nefashos,” etc., but not after other foods. Later Amoraim added vegetables and water, and Rav Ashi said, “I,” etc., “follow all of them.” Tosafos and all the poskim ruled like Rav Ashi, since he was later, that one recites Borei Nefashos after vegetables, water, and other foods whose first blessing is Shehakol. See Tosafos there, s.v. avidna, etc.: “And so we practice,” etc. The earlier practice had been to recite it only after an egg and meat. The reason was that they interpreted nefashos as referring to foods derived from living creatures, as stated in the Yerushalmi, Berachos chapter 6: when Rabbi ate meat or an egg, etc., he would recite “Who created living beings,” etc. Therefore, the Baraisa concerning rice and millet taught that afterward one recites “nothing,” because according to their practice they in fact recited no after-blessing at all after rice bread or millet bread. According to our practice, however, which follows Rav Ashi, every case in which the Baraisa says “nothing” means Borei Nefashos. This is the meaning of “and we hold that whenever afterward,” etc., one recites Borei Nefashos.
