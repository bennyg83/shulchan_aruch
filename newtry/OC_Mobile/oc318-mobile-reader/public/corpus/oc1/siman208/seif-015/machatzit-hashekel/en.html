(Seif katan 15) If it does not contain, etc. The correct reading is: “And Beit Yosef wrote in siman 453.”
The rice is drawn after it. Meaning, the rice follows the wheat and is treated as though the rice itself were wheat.
Specifically rice, but not another species mixed with wheat. Likewise, rice mixed with barley or with any of the other five grain species does not follow that grain. This rule applies specifically to wheat mixed with rice.
And regarding matzah. He wishes to derive the laws of HaMotzi and Bircas HaMazon from this Mishnah. Since the Mishnah states that the mixture is obligated in challah and that one fulfills the mitzvah of matzah with it, he first derives HaMotzi from challah because their laws are equivalent, and then derives the same from matzah because its law is likewise equivalent.
And with other species. Meaning, whether rice is mixed with any of the other four grain species besides wheat, or legumes are mixed with wheat, where there is not a kezayis of grain within the time of achilas pras.
Nevertheless, there it concludes. The Yerushalmi concludes, as cited by the Rosh in Berachos, chapter Keitzad Mevarchin, seif 16, by challenging this general principle: on less than a kezayis one does not recite Bircas HaMazon, yet one still recites HaMotzi. It answers that the statement does not concern the quantity that obligates Bircas HaMazon, but rather the type of food. The Rosh explains that bread over which one recites HaMotzi requires Bircas HaMazon afterward, whereas food over which one initially recites Borei Minei Mezonos, such as pas haba’ah b’kisnin when one did not establish a meal upon it, requires only Me’ein Shalosh afterward.
If so, this requires study, etc. Where there is a kezayis of grain within the time of achilas pras, one may recite HaMotzi and afterward Bircas HaMazon. But where there is no such quantity in mixtures other than wheat with rice, the law depends upon the dispute of Maggid Mishneh and Ramban against Raavad, and one should not recite HaMotzi initially.
The phrase “and even a similar case,” etc., introduces a separate difficulty.
And here, since it is mixed, etc. The case necessarily concerns a mixture that lacks a kezayis of grain within the time of achilas pras. For this reason one does not recite Bircas HaMazon afterward. Eating the other species mixed with the grain interrupts the consumption of the grain, so the grain eaten over a period longer than achilas pras does not combine into a kezayis. It is no better than a visible kezayis of grain eaten without any mixture but over a period longer than achilas pras, which likewise does not combine. HaMotzi, however, is still recited, because although the amount does not combine and one is not considered to have eaten a kezayis, HaMotzi is recited even over a minute amount of bread.
Furthermore, regarding challah, etc. Regarding challah, the reason the grain does not combine cannot be the lapse of time, since the entire quantity of grain required for challah is physically present before us. The exemption from challah where there is no kezayis within achilas pras must therefore be because the grain is nullified and treated as nonexistent. If so, HaMotzi likewise should not be recited in such a case.
One may answer that regarding blessings, etc. Bircas HaMazon is Torah-level and therefore follows the law of challah: whatever is called bread for challah is likewise bread for Bircas HaMazon. HaMotzi and Me’ein Shalosh, however, are D’Rabbanan according to our view, although some hold they are Torah-level, as stated in Tur, siman 209. The Sages established that even when there is no kezayis within achilas pras, the superior status of the five grain species requires HaMotzi first and Me’ein Shalosh afterward. Bircas HaMazon, however, is Torah-level and therefore follows the law of challah.
Nevertheless, if he did not mix it, etc. If it was not mixed with other species, one recites Bircas HaMazon, etc. Lechem Chamudos, chapter Keitzad Mevarchin, seif katan 19, after citing Abudraham in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah as quoted here in Shulchan Aruch, writes: “Although his words there concern a mixture with another flour, such as millet flour, it does not appear to me that he intended specifically flour. He spoke of the usual case, and the same law applies whenever one of the five grain species is mixed with another food and the grain is primary.” I do not understand this. The Rosh writes there that whenever the primary ingredient, excluding grain used merely as a binder, is one of the five grain species, even if another species constitutes the majority, one recites Borei Minei Mezonos and afterward Me’ein Shalosh. This is the Gemara’s ruling and the law stated in seif 2, as Magen Avraham alludes here. It therefore appears that Abudraham specifically meant flour, as Magen Avraham writes, although I do not know the rationale for distinguishing flour from other foods.
