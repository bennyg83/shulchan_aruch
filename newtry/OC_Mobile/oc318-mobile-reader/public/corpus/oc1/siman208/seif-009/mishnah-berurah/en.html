(41) One recites Borei Minei Mezonos, etc. As above in se'if 2; see there.
(42) One recites Hamotzi and Birkas HaMazon. It is full-fledged bread.
(43) Within kedei achilas peras. A peras is half an eruv loaf: some say three eggs, others four; a kezayis is half an egg. Prohibitions are measured so that a kezayis eaten over longer than kedei achilas peras does not combine for lashes or a chatas. Likewise Birkas HaMazon requires a kezayis of grain eaten within that interval. The bread must therefore contain at least one-eighth grain flour: eating four eggs of bread then supplies a kezayis of grain.
(44) Initially he recites Hamotzi, etc. Under siman 210, a first blessing has no minimum amount; even the smallest amount receives its species' proper blessing. Grain is important and never nullified in a mixture when its taste is perceptible.
(45) The taste of grain. Otherwise it lacks importance, is nullified, and receives Shehakol and Borei Nefashos.
(46) And afterward, etc. Even if he ate only a kezayis of the bread.
(47) Al HaMichyah. Many later authorities object that nowhere else does one begin with Hamotzi and end with Al HaMichyah, and ask why this differs from the pot mixture that ends with Borei Nefashos when grain is less than kedei achilas peras. The Gra therefore emends the text here to Borei Nefashos. Many later authorities labor to defend Shulchan Aruch [Eliyah Rabbah; Mateh Yehudah]. A God-fearing person should eat such bread only during a meal. One following Shulchan Aruch should not be protested, since many later authorities rule so. Likewise, if the bread contains sufficient grain but he ate too little to consume a grain kezayis, he says Al HaMichyah under Shulchan Aruch or Borei Nefashos under the Gra, not Birkas HaMazon.
(48) In a pot. The grain flour was less than a kezayis within kedei achilas peras, or it was sufficient but he did not eat a grain kezayis. Initially he says Mezonos, but Al HaMichyah requires a grain kezayis, so afterward only Borei Nefashos covers the other ingredients. Biur Halachah explains this applies not only to mixed flours but to grain mixed with honey, spices, or other species. Groats cooked with potatoes, legumes, or similar foods require a grain kezayis within kedei achilas peras for Al HaMichyah. The world nevertheless recites Al HaMichyah after a kezayis of highly spiced pas habaah bekisnin, such as honey cake, even without a grain kezayis. Perhaps the flavoring that enables eating combines with the food, as Magen Avraham 210 suggests. Initially it is best to ensure a flour kezayis.
(49) Initially he recites Borei Minei Mezonos. Some grain taste must be perceptible. A negligible amount with no taste is nullified to the other ingredients and receives Shehakol.
