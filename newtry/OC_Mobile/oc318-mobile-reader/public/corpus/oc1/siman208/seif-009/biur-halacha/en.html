If he mixed millet flour, etc. Regarding rice flour mixed with wheat flour, the matter requires study. It appears to depend on the dispute among the poskim explained in siman 453, seif 2. See there in Chok Yaakov, and see Peri Megadim and Derech HaChayim, who incline to rule that rice is equivalent to millet for our purposes. Magen Giborim, however, disagrees; see there.
And if he made bread from it, he recites HaMotzi. It is reasonable that whenever one has before him bread made from one of the five species of grain, even if it is not refined and is mixed bread containing a kezayis of wheat within kedei achilas pras, it is nevertheless preferable to recite the blessing over that unrefined bread. Peri Megadim.
And Bircas HaMazon. See Peri Megadim, who is uncertain regarding the Torah-level obligation, for which satiation is required, whether the other species combine with the grain toward that measure.
He does not recite Bircas HaMazon afterward. See Shaarei Teshuvah, who writes that if he ate to satiation he must recite Bircas HaMazon, though he does not explain his reason. His work Beis Ephraim is not available to me. Perhaps he was concerned for the view that taam keikar is D'Oraisa and held that this principle also applies to Bircas HaMazon. He therefore ruled stringently where one ate to satiation, since the obligation in general is then D'Oraisa. However, the Gra writes in seif katan 34 that the principal rule is that every after-blessing requires a kezayis within kedei achilas pras, which does not appear to support this. Likewise, the Mechaber states the ruling without qualification, implying that in all cases one does not recite Bircas HaMazon.
In a pot. See what I wrote in Mishnah Berurah, that this does not apply only to flour mixed with flour. Lechem Chamudos likewise writes this, and it is cited in Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah. Although Magen Avraham at the end of seif katan 15 writes the opposite, and several later authorities copied his words, I did not wish to state that ruling without qualification because many later authorities questioned him and agree with Lechem Chamudos. These include Maharam Benet in his commentary to Mordechai, Maamar Mordechai, and Nishmas Adam. Olas Tamid also inclines this way and is cited in Eliyah Rabbah, which also appears to agree. The Gra's commentary in seif katan 35, beginning, “And if he cooked it,” likewise proves explicitly that he agrees with Lechem Chamudos.
He first recites Borei Minei Mezonos. See Mishnah Berurah, where I wrote that the taste of grain must be perceptible. This follows from the flow of the Mechaber's words, which refer back to the preceding case, and Magen Avraham likewise writes this in siman 204, seif katan 25. Although Peri Megadim presses Magen Avraham's language there to mean otherwise, its plain meaning is as we have written, and Eliyah Rabbah agrees. Taz, at the beginning of this siman and at the beginning of siman 202, appears to hold that the grain need not impart taste, deriving this from the Mechaber's phrase, “even if he mixed honey with them.” Mateh Yehudah and Nishmas Adam already objected that this is no proof, since even when mixed with honey one may still detect some grain flavor. They rule as we wrote in Mishnah Berurah. The glosses of Rabbi Chaim Sanzer also disagree with Taz. The Rosh writes, “Whenever its primary ingredient is from the five species, even if most of it is another species,” which also implies that grain flavor is at least required, for otherwise it cannot be called the primary ingredient. I found a similar statement in Or Zarua in the name of Rabbeinu Chananel; see there carefully. Moreover, the Raah in his novellae to the Rif holds that when there is not a kezayis within kedei achilas pras, the grain is not significant at all, and even initially one does not recite Borei Minei Mezonos. Although in practice one should not depart from the Shulchan Aruch, which follows Abudraham in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah that initially one recites Borei Minei Mezonos in every case, the grain must at least impart flavor.
And afterward Borei Nefashos. See Biur HaGra, who explains that this ruling depends on a dispute among the Rishonim. It follows Rashi and Rabbeinu Yonah, as well as the Raah cited above, while Tosafos disagree. I found that Tosafos HaRosh likewise cites Tosafos' question against Rashi, yet in practice his words imply that he accepts the ruling of the Shulchan Aruch, that when there is not a kezayis within kedei achilas pras, one recites only Borei Nefashos afterward. Biur HaGra, beginning “Initially,” derives the first blessing from the statement, “Anything containing one of the five species of grain,” which implies even when there is not a kezayis within kedei achilas pras. According to this, the Rif, who concludes the statement of Rav and Shmuel concerning rihata of Mechoza with, “and afterward one recites Me'ein Shalosh,” must likewise follow Tosafos. In our text of the Gemara, however, this phrase does not appear in the words of Rav and Shmuel.
