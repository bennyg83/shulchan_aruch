[Levush] This is the language of Beit Yosef: Rabbenu Yonah wrote that when fruits are brought from Eretz Yisrael to outside the Land. In a case of doubt, one need not alter the formula used outside the Land, whereas Rashba writes that even outside the Land, if one eats fruits brought from the Land, he says "for the Land and for its fruits." The phrase in Beit Yosef must mean that fruits were brought to outside the Land from Eretz Yisrael. Perishah explains that Rabbenu Yonah means a case where fruits were brought and one does not know whether the particular fruit he wishes to eat is among those brought from Eretz Yisrael; because of the doubt he need not alter the usual formula. Beit Yosef understood the doubt to concern the law itself, how to bless on fruits brought from Eretz Yisrael to outside the Land, and Perishah disagrees. Bach and Magen Avraham also appear to understand it like Perishah. According to this, when outside the Land and uncertain whether the fruit is from Eretz Yisrael, one says "for the fruits," and similarly when in Eretz Yisrael and uncertain whether the fruit came from outside the Land, one says "for its fruits." Olat Tamid rules likewise. However, the text of Rabbenu Yonah before me, with Maharam Tiktin, says: "When fruits from outside the Land are brought into Eretz Yisrael, in a case of doubt one need not alter the formula of outside the Land, but because of the doubt one should tithe them." This explicitly concerns being in Eretz Yisrael and uncertainty whether the fruits came from outside the Land, yet he says not to alter the outside-the-Land formula. It therefore appears that Beit Yosef should be emended to read "fruits from outside the Land to Eretz Yisrael" rather than "from Eretz Yisrael." Beit Yosef would then hold that all the more so, if fruits of Eretz Yisrael are brought outside the Land, the outside-the-Land formula is not changed even when their origin is certain; he therefore follows Rabbenu Yonah with Rashba's dissent. This removes the objections of Perishah and Bach to Beit Yosef. Yet there is a difficulty: Rabbenu Yonah says that because of doubt they should be tithed, whereas an explicit Mishnah at the beginning of chapter 2 of Challah rules that produce from outside the Land entering Eretz Yisrael is liable, as codified by Rambam, Hilchot Terumot chapter 1, Tur Yoreh Deah end of siman 331, and Shulchan Aruch there. Thus even produce certainly from outside the Land is liable in maaser. One may answer that the doubt is whether its processing was also completed outside the Land, in which case if known certainly it would be exempt, as explained in Tur and Shulchan Aruch there; when doubtful it should be tithed. I later found in Kol Bo, page 19, in the name of Rabbenu Yonah: if fruit from Eretz Yisrael comes outside the Land one need not alter the outside-the-Land formula, but one must separate maaser. Thus Rabbenu Yonah explicitly concerns fruit of Eretz Yisrael brought outside the Land, and nevertheless says that even when its origin is certain the formula is not changed. This indeed removes Perishah's and Bach's objections. But then there is another difficulty: produce of Eretz Yisrael brought outside the Land is stated by the Mishnah, Rambam, and Tur cited above to be exempt from maaser even when its origin is certain. I examined Rambam and saw that Raavad objects that if produce of Eretz Yisrael was processed in Eretz Yisrael it is liable d'oraisa, while if processed outside the Land it is liable d'rabbanan. Perhaps Rabbenu Yonah and Kol Bo follow Raavad. Yet this is difficult, because Beit Yosef and Shulchan Aruch there rule Rambam's view without mentioning Rabbenu Yonah and Kol Bo, who agree with Raavad. In practical law it appears to me that if one certainly knows that fruits came from outside the Land into Eretz Yisrael he says "for the fruits," and if they certainly came from Eretz Yisrael to outside the Land he says "for its fruits." In a case of doubt, whether in Eretz Yisrael or outside it, one should use the generic formula.
