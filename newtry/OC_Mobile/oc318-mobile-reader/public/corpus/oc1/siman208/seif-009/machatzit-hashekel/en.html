(Seif katan 9) Regarding rice, the correct reading is: “One should eat it only during a meal.” The phrase “when it became mashed” is evidently a scribal error. Bach likewise rules that in every case it should be eaten only during a meal, and Taz cites this in his name. Even when it did not become mashed, we remain uncertain because we do not know which grain is rice and which is millet. According to Magen Avraham, seif katan 10, one recites Borei Minei Mezonos on rice even when it did not become mashed, whereas on millet one recites Borei Pri Ha’adamah. According to Bach, cited by Magen Avraham there, even without the uncertainty concerning millet, unmashed rice itself is subject to doubt whether Borei Minei Mezonos should be recited.
And Lechem Chamudos writes that our varieties, etc. Shelah provides a mnemonic, as does Maharam of Lublin: “pas hadra’ah.” The word hadra’ah is an acronym for the Yiddish terms: hirz is millet, and reiz is rice.
