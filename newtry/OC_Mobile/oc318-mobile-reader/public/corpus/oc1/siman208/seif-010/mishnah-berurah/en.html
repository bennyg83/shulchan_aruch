(50) An abridged three-faceted blessing. The text is: 'Blessed are You, Hashem our God, King of the universe, for the sustenance and nourishment, for the produce of the field, and for the desirable, good, spacious land You desired and gave our ancestors, to eat its fruit and be satisfied with its goodness. Have mercy, Hashem our God, upon Israel Your people, Jerusalem Your city, Zion the dwelling of Your glory, Your altar, and Your Sanctuary. Rebuild Jerusalem, the holy city, speedily in our days; bring us into it, gladden us in its rebuilding, and we shall eat its fruit and be satisfied with its goodness' (some omit this last phrase) 'and bless You over it in holiness and purity, for You, Hashem, are good and do good to all. We thank You for the land and sustenance. Blessed are You, Hashem, for the land and sustenance.' It is called three-faceted because it parallels HaZan, Birkas HaAretz, and Boneh Yerushalayim. 'For You are good and do good' also parallels the fourth blessing, but the name follows the three Torah blessings; HaTov VeHaMeitiv is rabbinic. For fruit say 'for the tree and its fruit'; for wine, 'for the vine and its fruit,' continuing with the produce and land. The wine conclusion is explained in the next se'if.
(51) Of fruit from outside Eretz Yisrael. Meaning one of the seven species.
(52) And for the fruits. He cannot say 'its fruits,' since they are not Eretz Yisrael's fruits, even if imported and eaten there [Olas Tamid; Eliyah Rabbah].
(53) For the land and its fruits. He praises Hashem for giving us the land that produces those fruits.
(54) One who eats the land's fruits. When known to be Eretz Yisrael produce. In doubt, such as outside the land near its border, say 'for the fruits.' Likewise, in Eretz Yisrael when uncertain whether they grew there or were imported, conclude 'for the land and the fruits.'
