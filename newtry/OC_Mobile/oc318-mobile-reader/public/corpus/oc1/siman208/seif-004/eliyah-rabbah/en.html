[5] [Levush] That it is proper, etc. Therefore, one should be careful regarding the custom to eat whole cooked wheat kernels on Shabbos Shirah. Likewise, throughout the year people eat whole barley, called whole gersten, and whole cooked groats. These should be eaten only during a meal. Bach. Shelah writes similarly regarding groypen, that they are included in the category of barley. I have already written that all this applies where they were not thoroughly mashed. After the fact, if one ate any of these outside a meal, Knesses HaGedolah writes that he recites Borei Nefashos. Although at the end of this siman I will write that his words do not appear correct, here it seems proper to establish the halachah in accordance with him, since according to many poskim there is no doubt at all that the blessing is Borei Nefashos, and the doubt exists only according to Tosafos. However, I am astonished at Bach and Shelah, for groypen and gritz are crushed, their husks are removed, and they are split. Accordingly, everyone should agree that the after-blessing is Al HaMichyah. Why, then, should they be eaten only during a meal? Perhaps they hold that they must nevertheless be mashed and adhere together. This requires study in Kesef Mishneh, chapter 3 of Hilchos Berachos, and Lechem Chamudos.
[6] Even roasted, etc. It appears to me that if they were cooked, their law is like wheat, since he did not write “even cooked.” Moreover, Beit Yosef questions the basic ruling. Darkei Moshe infers from Rashba’s statement that barley is equivalent to wheat regarding the after-blessing, but this is difficult. I examined Chiddushei Rashba, page 26, and saw that he wrote this regarding flour. I further found in Semak, siman 103, that one who chews wheat or barley recites Borei Peri HaAdamah. Therefore, at least when cooked, it is proper to rule that the blessing is Borei Peri HaAdamah. We do not find in Kol Bo that he disagrees regarding cooking. According to this, Rambam does not disagree with Kol Bo. Examine this carefully, unlike Lechem Mishneh.
[7] [Levush] And to swallow it, etc. The wording of Shulchan Aruch is “and to chew it,” with a samech, and so too in Rambam, chapter 3 of Hilchos Berachos. Magen Avraham, however, asks that the beginning contradicts the end, for the wording implies that specifically when it is fit to drink, but if it is thick, even though it is not fit to be chewed, the blessing is Shehakol. See there. Perhaps because of Beit Yosef, Levush altered the wording and wrote “to swallow it,” from the language of feeding or gulping. Likewise, all the other poskim write only that anything fit for eating receives Borei Minei Mezonos. I later found the wording of Levush, “to swallow it,” in Abudraham, page 130.
