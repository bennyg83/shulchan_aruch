Grain. All five species are included.
Whole. Meaning that its husk was not removed and it was not split, but remains as it comes from the threshing floor. This differs from rice, which is normally cooked whole. Kesef Mishneh, beginning of chapter 3. See seif 7 and seif 2.
And Tosafos were uncertain. This applies even when they are raw. It differs from flour in seif 5, over which one recites Borei Nefashos, because there it has undergone a change. Although olive oil also undergoes a change and yet receives Borei Peri HaEtz, there the olive has no further superior form, whereas here the grain has a further superior form when made into bread. Gemara.
During the meal. If one happened to eat them outside a meal, he recites Borei Nefashos afterward. This appears to be the view of Beit Yosef. It seems to me that if one makes a dish from oats called whole gritz and places in it much water, fit only to be drunk as soup, perhaps the water is not subordinate to the kernels. One should recite Borei Peri HaAdamah over the kernels and Shehakol separately over the water, since the principal intent is for the water, as stated in siman 204, seif 1, regarding barley beer. Nevertheless, the kernels are not subordinate to the liquid, because the five grain species are significant, as stated at the end of siman 204. See seif 6 and what I wrote in siman 168, seif 13.
