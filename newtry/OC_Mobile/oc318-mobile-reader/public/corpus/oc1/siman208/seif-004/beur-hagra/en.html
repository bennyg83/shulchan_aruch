Seif 4: Roasted kernels, etc. Tosafos there, s.v. ha-koses, and the other poskim; and as stated there, “These are the cooked dishes,” etc., which implies specifically in that manner. This is the meaning of “and the kernels,” etc.
And afterward Borei Nefashos. Rambam, since regarding one who chews grain he did not rule that the after-blessing is Me’ein Shalosh, as he did at the end there regarding grain that was ground, etc.; and as stated there on 44a, “Rabban Gamliel also,” etc. The Sages did not disagree with him on this point.
And this, etc. Since it states “wheat,” and in Yoma 81a it says, “A non-kohen who chews,” etc., while in Berachos 35b the two are compared, “What are the circumstances? If we say,” etc., this implies like Abudraham, that no blessing is recited at all. Beit Yosef, however, challenged him: since he derives benefit, why should he not recite a blessing? This is also evident from what is stated there on 36a regarding barley flour, etc. Necessarily, the law of terumah is no proof for our case, for in the Yerushalmi at the beginning of chapter 6 of Terumos it is taught: “One who chews wheat of terumah pays the principal but not the additional fifth. Rabbi says: I say that he pays the principal and the fifth.” Rambam there ruled in accordance with Rabbi, and Raavad challenged him. Rambam’s reason is that this is implied in Yoma, as stated above. Nevertheless, he recites only Shehakol, like barley flour, for even according to the view that the blessing on wheat flour is Mezonos, the blessing on barley flour is Shehakol, as stated there on 36a. Tosafos there explain that this refers to flour of roasted grain. This is the meaning of “even,” etc.
