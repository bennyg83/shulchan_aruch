(14) Raw grain. This includes all five species; Rema disagrees regarding barley.
(15) And the kernels remain whole. They were neither initially divided nor mashed through cooking, so they are no more nourishingly significant than other ground produce. If pounding removed the hull, some say cooking makes their blessing Borei Minei Mezonos as a cooked grain dish; others say Borei Pri HaAdamah because the kernels remain whole. It is best to eat them only during a meal, apart from Tosafos's view below. If cooking made them adhere, one may initially bless Borei Minei Mezonos and afterward Al HaMichyah, as paragraph 6 states. For pearl barley whose milling removes both hull and some kernel, making it smaller, those who initially recite Mezonos and Al HaMichyah even without mashing should not be protested.
(16) Only Shehakol. If barley was cooked, Eliyah Rabbah says its blessing is Borei Pri HaAdamah.
(17) They were uncertain, etc. Since it is grain, perhaps its after-blessing must be an abridged three-faceted blessing. It cannot say Al HaMichyah because this is not food, but might say 'for the ground and its produce,' parallel to 'for the tree and its fruit.' Since no such text was instituted, the question remains unresolved.
(18) During the meal. If one ate it outside a meal, the essential law is to recite Borei Nefashos afterward.
