But regarding wine, etc. Know that the Rishonim express several opinions concerning wine, and there are several distinctions. We will record them all for practical halachah.
1. If one drank wine outside a meal and then recited Bircas HaMazon over it, he has fulfilled his obligation after the fact. This is the view of Rabbeinu Yonah and the Rosh, and it also appears to be the view of Rashbam and Baal HaMaor in the chapter Arvei Pesachim. Although Ramban and Ran there hold that wine is not covered by Bircas HaMazon, we do not rule like them, as the Shulchan Aruch rules in this seif. 2. If one drank wine during a meal, he must recite a first blessing because wine is significant and generates a blessing for itself. All agree, however, that its after-blessing is covered by Bircas HaMazon, as the Shulchan Aruch rules in siman 174, seif 6. 3. If one drank wine after the meal had ended, Rabbeinu Yonah, Rashbam, and Baal HaMaor hold that it is automatically covered by the Bircas HaMazon recited over the bread, even without explicit intent to include the wine. The Rosh, however, as is evident from his words in the chapter Keitzad Mevarchin, end of section 26, and as he states explicitly in his Tosafos, together with Ramban and Ran, holds that it requires both a first and an after-blessing and one may not rely upon Bircas HaMazon. If one explicitly intended Bircas HaMazon to cover the wine, that certainly suffices according to the Rosh and those who agree with the view cited in paragraph 1, though according to Ramban and Ran it does not suffice even after the fact. For us this distinction ordinarily has no practical application, because everything is considered part of the meal, as explained above in siman 177, unless one said, “Bring us a cup and let us recite Bircas HaMazon.” In that case the law depends on these opinions. It is therefore proper to intend explicitly that Bircas HaMazon cover the wine, thereby satisfying most poskim. 4. If one drank wine only before the meal, Baal HaMaor, Ramban, and Ran hold that Bircas HaMazon does not cover it. The Rosh holds that it is covered initially, because wine stimulates the appetite and is therefore connected to the meal. The Shulchan Aruch rules accordingly in siman 174, seif 6. Although one certainly should not depart from the ruling of the Shulchan Aruch, it is nevertheless proper initially to intend in Bircas HaMazon to cover the wine he drank.
Bircas HaMazon covers it. The poskim derive this from Berachos 35b, where the Gemara asks, “If so, let us recite three blessings over it.” At first glance, the Gemara's question there concerns only a small quantity of wine, which satisfies, not a large quantity, which stimulates the appetite. Pesachim 108a proves that wine stimulates the appetite and does not satisfy. Whether that passage holds that even a small quantity satisfies, or refers only to a large quantity as Tosafos explain there, it at least proves that a large quantity stimulates the appetite. This is approximately three revi'is cups, as in the passage in Pesachim, and the Shulchan Aruch rules below in siman 471, seif 1, that a large quantity stimulates the appetite. The Gemara's question in Berachos must therefore concern only a small quantity. Seemingly, the Shulchan Aruch should have distinguished that after drinking a large quantity, one does not fulfill his obligation even after the fact by reciting Bircas HaMazon. One cannot answer that Pesachim means wine both satisfies and stimulates the appetite, and that its statement, “If it satisfies,” means if it only satisfies. For then why does the Gemara in Berachos ask from Rava's practice and need to distinguish between a small and large quantity? It could simply answer that wine both stimulates and satisfies. Evidently this is not so. Perhaps once a person became obligated after drinking a small quantity, when Bircas HaMazon applied because the wine satisfied his heart like bread, that obligation does not lapse even if he subsequently drank a large quantity. This requires study.
The same applies if he recited the blessing over dates. This is only after the fact. Initially, he certainly must recite their specific blessing. Even if he ate them during a meal after the meal had ended, he should not cover them with Bircas HaMazon, but must recite both a first and an after-blessing. This is the view of the Gra in siman 174 and here, and Even HaOzer likewise writes this and proves it from the Yerushalmi, unlike Magen Avraham in siman 177, who rules like Rashba. For us this case does not arise, because everything is considered part of the meal and therefore requires only a first blessing, as explained in siman 177.
But if he recited only the first blessing of Bircas HaMazon, etc., he has fulfilled his obligation. See Mishnah Berurah. The ruling nevertheless requires explanation, for his blessing omitted the blessing of the Land and the blessing of Jerusalem. We must say that the Sages required all three themes, sustenance, the Land, and Jerusalem, to be included only initially. After the fact, if he mentioned sustenance alone, he fulfilled his obligation. I later found that Rabbeinu Yonah writes this at the end of the chapter Keitzad Mevarchin and proves from it that Me'ein Shalosh is D'Rabbanan. According to the opinion that it is D'Oraisa, the matter indeed requires study.
