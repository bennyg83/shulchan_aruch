But regarding wine, etc. Know that regarding wine there are several opinions among the Rishonim, and several distinctions in this matter; we will copy them all for practical law.
1. If one drank wine outside the meal and recited Birkat HaMazon over it, he is exempt bediavad. This is the view of Rabbenu Yonah and the Rosh, and so appears to be the view of Rashbam and Baal HaMaor in chapter Arvei Pesachim. Although the Ramban and Ran there hold that wine is not exempted by Birkat HaMazon, we do not rule like them, as the Shulchan Aruch rules in this seif. 2. If one drank during the meal, although he must recite an initial blessing because wine is significant and generates its own blessing, all agree that its final blessing is exempted by Birkat HaMazon, as the Shulchan Aruch rules in siman 174:6. 3. If one drank after the meal was finished, Rabbenu Yonah, Rashbam, and Baal HaMaor hold that it is automatically exempted by the Birkat HaMazon recited over bread, even if he did not explicitly intend the wine. But the Rosh, as is evident from his words in chapter Keitzad Mevarchin, end of letter 26, and as he explicitly wrote in his Tosafot, together with the Ramban and Ran, holds that it requires a blessing before and after and one may not rely on Birkat HaMazon. If he explicitly intended to exempt the wine, it certainly works according to the Rosh and those cited in paragraph 1, while according to the Ramban and Ran it does not help even bediavad. For us this has no practical difference, because for us all is considered within the meal, as above in siman 177, unless he said "bring, let us bless," in which case it depends on these opinions. Therefore it is good to explicitly intend in Birkat HaMazon to exempt the wine, and thereby he fulfills according to most poskim. 4. If one drank before the meal alone, according to Baal HaMaor, Ramban, and Ran, it is not exempted by Birkat HaMazon; but according to the Rosh it is exempted lechatchilah, because wine draws the appetite and belongs to the meal. The Shulchan Aruch rules like him in siman 174:6. Although one certainly should not move from the Shulchan Aruch's ruling, lechatchilah it is certainly proper to intend in Birkat HaMazon to exempt the wine he drank.
The blessing of three exempts it. So wrote the poskim from Berakhot 35b, where the Gemara asks: if so, let us bless three blessings over it. At first glance, the Gemara's question there concerns only a small amount that satisfies, not a large amount that stimulates appetite; this is also evident from Pesachim 108a, that wine stimulates and does not satisfy. Whether the sugya in Pesachim holds that even a small amount satisfies, or whether it speaks of a large amount as Tosafot write there, in any event it is clear that a large amount stimulates, approximately three cups of a revi'it, as in that sugya. So too it is ruled below in siman 471:1 that much wine stimulates. If so, the Gemara's question in Berakhot must refer only to a small amount; regarding much wine, which stimulates and does not satisfy, the question does not apply. The Shulchan Aruch should seemingly have distinguished that with much wine one does not fulfill even bediavad by reciting Birkat HaMazon. One cannot say that the Gemara in Pesachim means that it both satisfies and stimulates, and that "if it satisfies" means only satisfies, for then what is the Gemara's question in Berakhot from Rava drinking, and why did it need to distinguish between a little and much? It could have said that it both stimulates and satisfies. Rather, this is not so. Perhaps, since one became obligated from the moment he drank a little, and Birkat HaMazon then applied to him because the heart was satisfied as by bread, that obligation does not lapse even if he later drank much. This requires study.
And the same applies if he blessed over dates. This means bediavad, for lechatchilah he certainly must recite the blessing specific to them. Even if he ate them during the meal after the meal had ended, he should not exempt them with Birkat HaMazon, but they require a blessing before and after; this is the view of the Gra in siman 174 and in this siman, and the Even HaOzer also writes so and proves it from the Yerushalmi, unlike the Magen Avraham in siman 177, who copies the Rashba as practical law. For us this does not apply, because everything is considered within the meal and requires only an initial blessing, as in siman 177.
But if he recited Birkat HaZan, etc., he has fulfilled. See Mishnah Berurah. Nevertheless, this requires explanation, for his blessing lacks Birkat HaAretz and Birkat Yerushalayim. One must say that they enacted inclusion of all three matters, nourishment, land, and Jerusalem, only lechatchilah; bediavad, if he mentioned only nourishment, he fulfilled. I later found that Rabbenu Yonah writes this at the end of chapter Keitzad Mevarchin, and proves from it that the blessing me'ein shalosh is rabbinic. According to the view that it is Torah law, this truly requires study.
