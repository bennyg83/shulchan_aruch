Regarding. For unripe grapes, siman 202, seif 2, rules Borei Peri HaAdamah. The after-blessing is uncertain, as Tosafos write here, because perhaps no Me'ein Shalosh was instituted for something whose first blessing is Borei Peri HaAdamah, or because it is not ordinarily eaten in this manner. See Magen Avraham, siman 211, seif katan 8. If one recited Me'ein Shalosh, “for the tree and the fruit of the tree,” he fulfilled his obligation after the fact, as Magen Avraham writes in siman 206, seif katan 1. Yet this is astonishing. Initially one would recite Borei Peri HaAdamah, but afterward “for the tree and the fruit of the tree.” Why did the Mechaber not state this above regarding the after-blessing? This requires study.

It is reasonable that whenever one has before him bread made from one of the five species of grain, even if it is not refined and is mixed bread containing a kezayis of wheat within kedei achilas pras, nevertheless unmixed barley bread is preferable for HaMotzi. See siman 168, seif 4. Know that the measure of a pras is disputed in siman 612, seif 4. According to the opinion that it is three eggs, grain comprising one-sixth of the mixture suffices: for bread one recites HaMotzi and Bircas HaMazon, and for a cooked dish Borei Minei Mezonos and Me'ein Shalosh. According to the opinion that it is four eggs, grain must comprise one-eighth. On a cooked dish containing one-eighth grain, one should recite Shehakol and Borei Nefashos because of the doubt. For bread, however, Bircas HaMazon is D'Oraisa, so the doubt is resolved stringently. See siman 168, Taz seif katan 6, and siman 184, Magen Avraham seif katan 8. It requires study whether all the ingredients combine toward satiation, or whether one must be satiated by the grain alone. This will be further explained, with Hashem's help, in Magen Avraham, seif katan 15. If one mixed flour of other species with grain flour, it requires study whether this is considered a mixture of the same type according to the names of the foods. One may argue that other flours possess a qualifying name, such as “rice flour,” unlike wheat and barley flour, which may be considered the same type by their general name. This has practical consequences for an item that will later become permitted and for the laws of Yom Tov. This will be explained, with Hashem's help, regarding flour ground on Yom Tov that became mixed. See Yoreh Deah, simanim 98 and 96; see there.
And behold. The Mechaber's ruling regarding a mixture is the view of Abudraham in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah. Orchos Chaim, however, cites Baal HaEshkol as implying that Rav and Shmuel's rule applies even when there is not a kezayis within kedei achilas pras, because one should not distinguish between flour and other ingredients. See Magen Avraham, seif katan 15. Beer HaGolah cites both Orchos Chaim and Abudraham, which requires some study. I will explain further. When there is a kezayis within kedei achilas pras, one recites HaMotzi and Bircas HaMazon, which shows that this concentration is considered as though the grain itself were present. In a cooked dish, one recites Borei Minei Mezonos and Me'ein Shalosh specifically because the five species of grain are important. Other foods are different. For example, if one mixes tree fruit with produce of the ground and the pieces remain somewhat recognizable, we follow the majority. One should not think this applies only to beverages, as in siman 202 where we follow the majority, and according to the first anonymous opinion in siman 612, seif 10, that beverages do not combine beyond a revi'is. The same rule applies to foods as well, as we have written. See what I will write, with Hashem's help, in siman 210.
