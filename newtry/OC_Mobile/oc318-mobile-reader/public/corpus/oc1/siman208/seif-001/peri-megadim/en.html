One recites. See Taz. So writes the Tur. According to the Tur, the blessing Me'ein Shalosh may be D'Oraisa, as discussed in siman 209; see Beit Yosef there. If so, the matter does not depend upon importance. Perhaps the separate mention of bread in the verse creates a complete break, excluding the five species of fruit from requiring Me'ein Shalosh as well. All the more so according to the view that for bread one Torah-level blessing suffices, as discussed by Magen Avraham in siman 194, seif katan 3. For the five species of fruit, Borei Nefashos may suffice on a Torah level, while the Sages instituted Me'ein Shalosh because of their importance as the produce with which Eretz Yisrael was praised. Know that even according to the opinion that Me'ein Shalosh is D'Oraisa, it is nevertheless derived from a halachah transmitted to Moshe at Sinai, as Derishah writes in siman 209. It appears that such a law does not create a positive commandment but only a general mitzvah, like the prohibition of half a measure. See Ran, Shabbos, chapter Klal Gadol, on the Mishnah of the forty melachos, where he writes that carrying four amos is included within hotzaah, for otherwise punishment could not be imposed based solely on a halachah transmitted to Moshe at Sinai. This will be explained, with Hashem's help, in the general introduction. Accordingly, if one must choose between fulfilling a positive commandment and reciting Me'ein Shalosh, the positive commandment takes precedence. In any event, for us Me'ein Shalosh remains doubtful, as discussed by Magen Avraham in siman 172. The five species of grain were accorded importance even for the first blessing because they nourish and are therefore more significant. See Levush and Nachalas Tzvi, whose words derive from the Tur. Date honey requires Borei Nefashos. Some hold that eating the five species of fruit to satiation creates a Torah-level obligation, but all agree that a kezayis alone creates only a Rabbinic obligation. “To satiation” means that one no longer desires to eat those fruits. See siman 210.
