4) [Seif 2] The five grain species, etc. These are wheat, barley, spelt, oats, and rye. They too are significant because Eretz Yisrael was praised through them: spelt is a type of wheat, while oats and rye are types of barley. They possess a further virtue, because a person lives through them, and if one makes bread from them he recites HaMotzi. Therefore, even if he did not make bread but made a cooked dish, such as a pot-cooked food, etc., he recites Borei Minei Mezonos and afterward the single blessing that summarizes three. Tur; Taz, paragraph 2. What we call buckwheat, however, is not included among the five grain species. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1.
5) There: The five grain species, etc. Over fried cakes made on Pesach from crushed matzah, one recites Borei Minei Mezonos. Responsa Maharshal HaKohen, volume 1, siman 163; Ateres Zekeinim, paragraph 1. We wrote the same above in siman 168, paragraph 85.
6) There: If he cooked or crushed them. This implies that the first clause concerns cooking without crushing. The statement in seif 4 that one recites Borei Pri HaAdamah over whole cooked kernels may refer to a case here in which he cooked them until they became mashed. This is also implied by Levush, seif 4, and below, seif 7; see there. Magen Avraham, paragraph 2. Yaavetz, however, writes that the Shulchan Aruch's word meaning 'cooked them' is a scribal error and should read 'split them.' This is both a common error and an evident one, since the entire seif follows the Rambam in chapter 3, whose text reads, 'split or crushed them,' etc.; see there. Beis David, siman 532, writes the same. Regarding practical halachah, however, there is no difference: whether he split them or cooked them until mashed, he recites Borei Minei Mezonos, as stated in Beis Yosef. If they remain whole, he recites Borei Pri HaAdamah, as stated in seif 4.
7) There: Or crushed them. 'Crushing' here does not mean pounding in a mortar or grinding, but removing their husks while they remain whole, like groats. If their husks were removed but they did not adhere together at all, the law is uncertain. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 2.
8) There: Even if he mixed much honey with them, etc. Regarding the five species, which are especially significant, we do not follow the majority whenever they are included as food. With other things, however, we follow the majority, as stated below in seif 7. Taz, paragraph 3. Magen Avraham likewise writes this above in siman 204, paragraph 25. See above, siman 204, paragraph 60, and siman 202, paragraph 100.
9) There: Or many other species, in a quantity greater than them, etc. This applies when there is a kezayis of grain within the time required to eat a pras, and he ate a pras so that what he consumed contains a kezayis of grain. Otherwise, he recites only Borei Nefashos, as stated above in siman 168, paragraphs 122 and 127; see there. Chesed LaAlafim in this siman, paragraph 3, writes the same; Agurah BeOhalecha, folio 4; see there. This will be explained further below in seif 9; see there.
10) There: And afterward, Al HaMichyah. If one removed bran from the grain and subsequently returned it, Magen Avraham, siman 451, paragraph 2, writes that it is uncertain whether the bran combines to a kezayis for matzah. Eshel Avraham in this siman, paragraph 1, writes that the same applies here regarding Birkas HaMazon and the blessing that summarizes three; see there. In his novellae there, paragraph 2, however, he writes unequivocally that it does not combine; see there. Therefore, if this occurs, one should eat a kezayis excluding the bran in order to avoid doubt.
11) There: And afterward, Al HaMichyah. This applies whether or not one established his meal upon them, and whether he cooked them with water and oil, with water and milk, or with other beverages. Birkei Yosef, paragraph 2. We wrote the same above in siman 168, paragraph 49; see there. If he does not eat the cooked dish but wishes to drink only the water in which it was cooked, see our words above at the end of siman 205, paragraph 11, where we wrote that this is disputed and, because of the doubt, one recites Shehakol. If he eats the dish and also drinks the water, the blessing over the dish covers it; see there.
12) There: Only to bind it, etc. Bach writes that whenever flour is included as a binder, even if the flour is the majority and other items are the minority, one recites Borei Pri HaAdamah, Borei Pri HaEtz, or Shehakol, according to the blessing of those other species; see there. Olas Tamid at the end of paragraph 2 writes the same. Taz, paragraph 4, however, writes that one should not rule leniently in this manner, since in any event he derives substantial enjoyment from the large amount of flour; see there. Mateh Yehudah, paragraph 1, similarly writes that using flour as a binder negates only its significance, not the grain's majority. He writes that the same applies to the law in seif 3, which refers only to almonds constituting the majority. Therefore, if flour is added as a binder, one recites only the blessing over the almonds. The uncertainty there is that perhaps the flour was added for nourishment, contrary to Taz, paragraph 5; see there. Therefore, if most of a mixture is grain added as a binder, one should eat it specifically during a meal, or recite Borei Minei Mezonos over another item and the blessing appropriate to the other species over another item, intending to cover this mixture whichever ruling is correct, thereby avoiding doubt.
13) Regarding lekach, the flour is primary. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2; Eshel Avraham, paragraph 4. The Shelah writes that in pancakes the flour is considered a binder, but Tashbetz, siman 322, writes that one recites Borei Minei Mezonos and afterward the blessing that summarizes three. Eliyah Rabbah there.
