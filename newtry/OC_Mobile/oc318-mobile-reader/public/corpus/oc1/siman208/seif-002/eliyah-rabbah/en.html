[Levush] Rather, he crushed them, etc. Shulchan Aruch says "he boiled them or crushed them," and the Levush did well to omit "boiled them," because seif 4 will explain that boiled kernels receive Borei Pri HaAdamah. Nevertheless, if through cooking they are mashed and stick together, their law is like crushed grain. All this is when the grain is included as food. If it is used merely as a binder, it appears that even when the grain is the majority it is subordinate. Bach writes so, and on this basis Taz writes that the same applies to lekach. It would therefore be preferable to eat it during a meal. I have not seen this practice; rather, people recite Borei Minei Mezonot over it without hesitation, because the overwhelming majority of flour in lekach is present for eating. Shelah writes that pancakes are considered a binder, but I saw in Tashbetz, siman 322, that one recites Borei Minei Mezonot on pancakes and afterward Me'ein Shalosh, supported by several sources in his ruling.
[3] Much honey, etc. Taz writes that this implies that regarding the five grain species, even if they do not impart taste, the blessing follows them, for whenever there is honey one perceives only the taste of the honey. Taz likewise writes at the beginning of siman 202 that the five grain species are significant and the blessing follows them even when they are the minority and do not impart taste. I found the same in Chiddushei Rashba, page 28. Magen Avraham at the end of siman 204, however, explains that taste is specifically required there, since if there is not a kezayis of the five grain species within the time of kedei achilas peras, the after-blessing is only Borei Nefashos, as Beit Yosef writes below in the name of Abudraham. I am astonished at Sheyarei Knesses HaGedolah, who cites Lechem Chamudos as writing this in the name of Abudraham and then comments, “I searched for it in Abudraham and did not find it.” It is a great wonder that he overlooked the words of Beit Yosef and Shulchan Aruch below. I also saw this explicitly in Abudraham at the beginning of Hilchos Berachos. I likewise have a difficulty with Magen Avraham in this matter. In seif katan 15 he writes as obvious that Abudraham refers specifically to a mixture containing flour, whereas with other ingredients one recites Borei Minei Mezonos and Al HaMichyah even if there is not a kezayis within kedei achilas peras, as written in seif 2. He overlooked Lechem Chamudos, page 45, who explains that Abudraham speaks even of other ingredients, and Sheyarei Knesses HaGedolah cites him as saying that Abudraham merely described the usual case. Olas Tamid writes likewise. If Magen Avraham disagrees with them, he should have cited and disputed them.
[4] [Levush] Types of honey, etc. This is repeated above in siman 204, seif 12, but Shulchan Aruch did not repeat it here. It also appears that it is specifically regarding almonds, where there is a majority of the five grain species, that it is proper to be stringent. This does not apply where there is only a minority of grain. One may further say that since it is also commonly made for satiation, it is proper to be stringent.
