(Seif katan 2) That they boiled them, etc. The phrase “and perhaps here,” etc., until “they became mashed,” means that their husks were removed, and this makes it possible for them to become mashed and adhere together, as he writes afterward.
And these are the words of Rabbeinu Yonah, etc. Meaning that Rabbeinu Yonah’s words likewise imply that the law depends upon whether they became mashed and adhered together.
But if he cooked, etc. Meaning that he did not remove the husks. If he removed their husks, then even when the kernels remain whole they may become mashed and adhere together. It must therefore be that both the opening and concluding cases state a definite situation. In the opening case he speaks of grain that was crushed, because such grain ordinarily becomes mashed. In the concluding case he speaks of whole wheat, meaning that its husks were not removed, in which case it cannot adhere together. When the kernels are whole but their husks were removed, prolonged boiling sometimes causes them to become mashed and adhere together, and sometimes it does not.
What Rabbeinu Yonah writes, “and it became similar to chavitz kedeirah,” is explained by Rashi on 36b: “Chavitz kedeirah is a type of congealed food, like milk curdled in an animal’s stomach. In the same way they prepare congealed food in a pot. Below it is explained as flour, honey, and oil.” The term chavitz denotes joining and adhesion, as in Bava Metzia 99b, “one who stole a pressed mass of dates.” Rashi explains there that dates adhering together are called chavitzah.
But the Gemara does not teach harifos, etc. Although this provides no proof from the Gemara, the ruling itself is correct. Rambam implies that there is no distinction between crushed and whole grain; rather, the matter depends upon whether it became mashed and adhered together. Crushed grain is mentioned because it ordinarily becomes mashed, as explained above. Taz, seif katan 11, writes that porridge made from crushed barley plainly requires Borei Minei Mezonos. If he agrees with Magen Avraham, he mentions crushed barley because it certainly became mashed and adhered together. The same law applies to whole kernels whose husks were removed, if they became mashed and adhered together: one recites Borei Minei Mezonos and afterward Me’ein Shalosh.
And specifically, etc. As Tosafos writes on 37a, at the end of s.v. hakoses.
(Seif katan 3)
And to consume it, meaning to eat it; see seif 6. Although the term gemiah ordinarily means drinking, here it must mean eating, namely, that the mixture is thick. If it meant drinking, so that the mixture is thin, this would conflict with seif 6, which states that in such a case one recites Shehakol.
