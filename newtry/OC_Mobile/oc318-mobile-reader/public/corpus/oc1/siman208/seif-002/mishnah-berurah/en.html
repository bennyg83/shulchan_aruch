(2) The five species of grain. Wheat, barley, spelt, oats, and rye. What we call buckwheat or maize is not grain but produce of the ground.
(3) That he divided. The text should read 'divided,' meaning into two or more pieces, called groats. Whole kernels, even cooked, receive Borei Pri HaAdamah, as se'if 4 states. If cooking mashed them thoroughly, however, one recites Borei Minei Mezonos even though they entered the pot entirely whole in their hulls.
(4) Or pounded them. Even without dividing, he removed their hulls by pounding. Later authorities incline to require that cooking make them adhere together before this is considered a cooked grain dish; otherwise they remain like whole kernels under se'if 4. See there.
(5) Harifos and crushed fresh grain. Harifos explains 'pounded them'; crushed fresh grain explains 'divided them.' All are cooked grain dishes.
(6) And porridge. This means thoroughly mashed and stuck together. Later authorities advise eating whole barley or wheat only during a meal, as in se'if 4, when cooking did not make them adhere. If they adhered, one recites Borei Minei Mezonos.
(7) Much more than they. Since grain from the important five species flavors and improves the dish, it is primary. See se'if 9 and its Mishnah Berurah regarding the amount required for Al HaMichyah.
(8) Only to bind it, etc. If the flour neither flavors nor sustains but merely thickens the dish, it is insignificant and subordinate even in a large quantity.
