1) [Seif 1] All the needs of bris milah are performed, etc. The Sages did not decree regarding bris milah lest one carry the child or knife four amos in a public domain, as they did regarding shofar and lulav, because bris milah is especially weighty, thirteen covenants having been made concerning it. Moreover, no person performs a bris unless he is proficient, because danger is involved. Tosafos, Megillah 4b.
2) There: All the needs of bris milah are performed, etc. Bris milah overrides both Shabbos and the prohibition against cutting a tzaraas lesion when both apply, such as when the child has the lesion and his bris falls on Shabbos. Knesses HaGedolah in his published responsa, Orach Chaim, volume 2, siman 66; see there at length. Birkei Yosef, paragraph 1.
3) There: The bris is performed, etc. The Gemara derives this from the verse, 'and on the eighth day.' The phrase is otherwise superfluous. If it merely taught that the bris must be performed by day and not at night, that already follows from 'from eight days old.' It therefore teaches: on that day, even on Shabbos. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 1.
4) There: The bris is performed, etc. Even if the father denies the Torah of Moshe, his son's bris nevertheless overrides Shabbos. Responsa Zekan Aharon, siman 29. See Yoreh De'ah, siman 266, seifim 12 and 13.
5) A mamzer receives a bris on Shabbos. Bedek HaBayis there; Shach there, paragraph 17. Shach writes that Maharil rules the same. Kisei Eliyahu here, paragraph 1, writes likewise. Regarding Karaites, Shach there cites Responsa Rabbi Betzalel Ashkenazi, at the end of siman 3, who writes that since contemporary Karaites do not acknowledge peri'ah, their sons may not receive a bris on Shabbos; see there. Pischei HaDevir here, paragraph 1, writes the same in the name of several authorities. He adds that although today we see them perform peri'ah and recite the blessings at the bris, perhaps this is only because they are subordinate to us and have no Karaite mohel, whereas among themselves they neither perform peri'ah nor recite the blessings; see there.
6) There: And peri'ah is performed. This means uncovering the skin that covers the head of the organ beneath the foreskin. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 1. See Yoreh De'ah, siman 264, seif 3.
7) There: And peri'ah is performed. This is because peri'ah is part of the bris milah. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1.
8) There: And suction is performed. This means drawing blood from where it is attached. Although this creates a wound because the blood is detached from its place only through suction, it is nevertheless permitted because of danger. Rashi on the Mishnah and Gemara there. See Yoreh De'ah in the preceding seif. If a mohel on Shabbos had sores in his mouth and could not perform suction, and another person came and performed it, the latter acted properly. A mohel who knew he could not perform suction but nevertheless performed the bris requires atonement. Admas Kodesh, Orach Chaim, siman 6; Zechor LeAvraham, volume 3, Yoreh De'ah, paragraph 130. See below, paragraph 12.
9) There: And suction is performed. Responsa Binyan Tziyon, simanim 23 and 24, writes that suction must be performed specifically with the mouth and not through another device invented by contemporary physicians. Responsa Yad Eliezer, siman 10, permits suction with a tested sponge, writing that it is better than oral suction and may be permitted even on Shabbos; see there. Although physicians today say that oral suction may itself endanger the child, we have only the words of the Sages to follow. Nevertheless, it appears to me that one should not suck with excessive force on Shabbos. After suction, he should also spray from his mouth upon the wound some vinegar that he took into his mouth. Tiferes Yisrael on the Mishnah. Adam HaRishon's sin involved extending his hands, for he took with his hands and ate with his mouth. In this mitzvah too, the hands perform the bris and the mouth performs suction to rectify that sin. Chesed LaAlafim, Spring 2, River 51; Pis'chei Olam, paragraph 2.
10) There: Cumin and a salve are placed upon it. A salve is like a thick ointment, called marham in Arabic. Rambam in his Commentary to the Mishnah. Alternatively, a dressing or medicinal powder that stops the blood may be used. Yoreh De'ah there. The reason is that without them, the child may become endangered. Levush. If ground cumin was not available before Shabbos, see below, seif 6. Regarding the powder sprinkled after the bris as treatment, it appears immaterial whether one sprinkles it several times or, preferably, applies it all at once. Orach Chaim, paragraph 1, in the name of Eshel Avraham from Hagahos Kodesh. The earth into which the foreskin is placed must be designated before Shabbos, as stated in Yoreh De'ah, siman 265, seif 10; see there. If it was not designated before Shabbos, the foreskin should be cast into a place containing loose earth, or one may take earth that was designated before Shabbos for another purpose, as stated above in siman 308, seif 38, and below in siman 498; see there.
