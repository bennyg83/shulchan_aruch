Seif 1: they do all needs of milah on Shabbat, etc. NB: behold regarding whether there should be only two mohalim or three — Yeshuot Yaakov on this siman wrote it is an error; but in responsum I wrote to the community of Berazan it found place in my work in omissions on Shas Niddah daf 19 incidentally I expanded there on this with Hashem's help — see there on daf 113 and examine well. And behold in our case the dispute of Baal HaMaor and Ramban brought in Ran chapter R' Eliezer of Milah regarding if hot water spilled for milah need whether we follow the end or the present — see in my responsum to Lvov it found place in second omissions in Laws of Oaths siman 239 where I expanded there on this with Hashem's help and examine well; and see in Ran what proof they brought from Beitzah on erev Shabbat. And behold there is similar proof from Shas Niddah daf 38: early chasidim did not use their beds from day four onward lest they come to Shabbat desecration; and why not say now it is permitted and afterward pikuach nefesh overrides Shabbat; or since we know afterward he will come to Shabbat desecration — forbidden to do from the outset. And one should not say they were stringent on themselves, for this is not so; granted if we knew it would be so and he would come to Shabbat desecration — forbidden from law to do so; therefore since they are chasidim they were stringent even on safek lest it happen; but if we say even if we knew it would be so it would be clear permission with no prohibition concern at all — if so they wanted to be stringent on themselves; it would suffice to be stringent in certainty but not to be stringent further in safek since even if it would be certain it would be permission from law; therefore they would not be stringent in safek since even if so it would be full permission from law. Therefore it appears if it would be certain it would be forbidden from law — therefore they were stringent even in safek — examine well. And in the body of Raavad's proofs from slaughter on Yom Tov it appeared Ramban would hold pikuach nefesh was permitted for the ill and not deferred; therefore it works well: granted regarding covering there after slaughter he has no permission to cover except through positive commandment pushing aside negative commandment; and since it is only deferred permission — therefore now before he slaughtered and possibly will not need deferral — obligated to do so; but in pikuach nefesh which is real permission on Shabbat — therefore we follow the present and circumcise properly according to law; and afterward pikuach nefesh overrides Shabbat; and Raavad holds pikuach nefesh is deferred for the ill — therefore he properly brings proof from covering on Yom Tov; and so from many places one could expand more on this — examine well; and see in my chiddushim to chapter 5 of Yoma in Mishneh Torah daf 55b in Tosafos s.v. "one who sends" there I proved Tosafos hold like those who permit that we follow the present; and their words compel in Shas according to this view — see there and examine well; and see what I wrote on this in responsum Yoreh De'ah siman 373 regarding kohen impurity; and see above siman 244 and siman 248 regarding splitting a ship — also proven we follow the present; and see in my work on Avodah Zarah daf 10b where I wrote proof from Tosafos there too for those who permit we follow the present — see there and examine well. And behold one who examines there in Ran proves Ran holds like those who say we do not follow the present but the end — see there — only he distinguishes milah which is fixed on Shabbat unlike slaughter on Yom Tov; and at first glance difficult, for his words contradict themselves from what he wrote in Nedarim daf 16 regarding exchange of benefit prohibitions regarding borrower and owner when they come and are repaid — it implies there l'chatchila permitted to borrow and there is no benefit; and afterward when owners come and are repaid it is b'dieved and permitted b'dieved — see there; and if so apparently proven we do not follow the end but the present; and since now there is no prohibition — permitted to do even though we know ultimately afterward nevertheless the prohibition occurs — we do not concern ourselves since afterward it will be permitted since it is like b'dieved; and if so Ran's words contradict themselves; and one must distinguish d'oraisa from d'rabbanan that in Nedarim it deals only with d'rabbanan prohibition as Ran wrote exchange prohibition here is only d'rabbanan; and in d'rabbanan we follow the present; but in tractate Shabbos dealing with Torah prohibition and likewise slaughter on Yom Tov where d'oraisa melacha is needed — we follow the end; but where only d'rabbanan prohibition — we follow the present as proven from Ran's words in Nedarim; and since this is a great principle in many places — therefore I wrote all this here; and perhaps Hashem will grant me another time to explain more on this; and see Taz Yoreh De'ah siman 317 regarding nostril-slitting of rams where also proven he holds like those who say we follow the present and did not emphasize all the above matters nor the dispute Ran brought — and according to what I wrote above one should hold all his words for those who hold commerce prohibition in neveilah is only d'rabbanan and there is proof for his words from Ran — see what I wrote there in my chiddushim on Yoreh De'ah and examine well. And what Shulchan Aruch wrote "they do all needs of milah on Shabbat" — NB: thus Rambam chapter 2 Laws of Unintentional Sin halacha 8: and so one who had two infants, one to circumcise on Shabbat and one on erev Shabbat, or one on Shabbat and forgot and circumcised both on Shabbat — exempt from sin-offering, for he had permission to circumcise one of them on Shabbat; and Shabbat is deferred for him; and positive commandment even though two bodies since its time is pressing, etc. — end. And behold why he expanded to say Shabbat is deferred for him — it lacks understanding, for it is only permitted since he already wrote he has permission to circumcise one on Shabbat; it would suffice to say positive commandment; and why expand to say Shabbat is deferred for him; and also the word "it is only" — he should have said "and Shabbat is deferred for him." And it appears to me to explain his intent: behold regarding impurity we hold impurity is deferred in public; and so we hold Shabbat is deferred for the ill as Rambam wrote in Laws of Shabbat; but regarding sacrifices Shabbat is permitted as stated in Yoma daf 46. And behold one must study milah on Shabbat whether considered deferred or permitted; and from Rambam's language here it appears Shabbat is deferred for milah, for he wrote Shabbat is deferred for him and did not write it is permitted or permitted for him. True one can say gemara language uses "Shabbat was given to be pushed aside for it"; but in gemara itself one can derive thus; and more proven from Rambam's language that wrote Shabbat is deferred for him than language "it is" implies he speaks of the essence of Shabbat prohibition that is deferred and not this Shabbat; and like gemara language "impurity is deferred in public." And the correct reason why impurity is deferred and likewise Shabbat for ill and public sacrifice permitted: the matter is what can be fulfilled on weekday and has no connection specifically to Shabbat — only by happenstance and chance it occurred on Shabbat; and likewise impurity happened — this is only deferred not permitted, for impurity could not be impure and likewise ill could not be endangered on Shabbat; therefore if impurity or illness occurred on Shabbat it is only deferred; but public sacrifices — some sacrifices apply only on Shabbat or obligation every day including Shabbat — written "tamid" and must be on Shabbat too — in this it was permitted. And if so milah — since it could happen not on Shabbat — is only deferred [the author's son said: our rabbi's intent is the view of the holy Rabbi Ramban in Sefer Torat HaAdam; and see in Sefer Ateret Tzvi to the Gaon of Leipnik Orach Chayim what he wrote on this]; and thereby Rambam's language works well: since there are two advantages — he has permission to circumcise on Shabbat and also Shabbat is deferred for him and not permitted; and also positive commandment — therefore exempt in every case — examine well. So it appears to me correct Rambam's intent for now; perhaps Hashem will grant another time to explain more; and my prayer is fluent in my mouth that our saying be acceptable before Master of all and my toil not be in vain, Hashem forbid — amen:
