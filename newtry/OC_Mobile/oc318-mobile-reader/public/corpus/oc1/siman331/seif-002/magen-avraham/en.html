Twilight. Bach writes in Yoreh De'ah, siman 266, that if the infant was born one twenty-fourth of an hour before the appearance of 3 medium stars, the brit milah is performed on Friday, whereas if he was born later it is doubtful and the brit milah is deferred until Sunday. Although Bach wrote in siman 261 that one should be stringent to accept Shabbos from the end of the 22nd hour, he says that the law of brit milah is different. This requires study, because Bach himself ruled in Yoreh De'ah that twilight begins 3 quarters of a mil before nightfall, and it is well known that this is less than a quarter-hour, as stated in siman 459. Perhaps he erred in haste and thought that it was 3 mil. The primary ruling is as stated in Yoreh De'ah, siman 266, seif 9, that from the end of sunset the time is doubtful. This is 3 quarters of a mil, as explained in siman 261, approximately a quarter-hour before nightfall. If the child was born before that point, the brit milah is performed on Friday, for even with respect to melacha carrying the penalty of stoning we rule accordingly. Radbaz, part 1, siman 282, likewise discusses a child born on Shabbos approximately a quarter-hour before nightfall and writes that, as a matter of halacha, it appears to him that the brit milah should be performed on the following Shabbos, but he did not wish to rule so in practice unless the majority of the sages of Israel agreed, because of the severity of Shabbos desecration. It appears from his words that his hesitation arose because in his place people accepted Shabbos from sunset. If so, the same issue would arise at the end of Shabbos. In our countries, however, where people sometimes do not accept Shabbos until close to a quarter-hour before nightfall, as explained in siman 261, one may rely upon the accepted calculation for brit milah as well, because the great authorities follow it.
