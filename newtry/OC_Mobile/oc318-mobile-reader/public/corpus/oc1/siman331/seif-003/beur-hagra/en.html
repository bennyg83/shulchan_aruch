Seif 3, an 8-month child, if, etc. See Shabbos 135a and 136a, and Yevamos 80a-b. Tosafos explain that Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel and the Sages dispute 2 matters. Regarding a child certainly born in the eighth month, the Sages regard him as certainly nonviable and he does not leave that status until the later period discussed by R' Abbahu, while Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel treats him as doubtful until 30 days and as certainly viable after 30 days. Regarding an ordinary birth of uncertain gestational length, the Sages follow the majority of women and presume the child viable, whereas Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel treats the child as doubtful until 30 days. The passages in Yevamos and Shabbos must therefore be read together. The case in Yevamos concerns a definite 8-month child, while the case in Shabbos concerns ordinary or doubtful births. This also explains why the authorities distinguish between a child whose hair and nails are fully developed and one whose physical development is incomplete. With respect to pikuach nefesh, however, even a doubt is sufficient to desecrate Shabbos. This is evident from Erchin, where Shabbos is desecrated in order to attempt to save a fetus after the mother's death. Ran at the end of Yoma likewise indicates that doubts concerning life are treated leniently. Therefore the statements of those who refuse to desecrate Shabbos for every doubtful 8-month child require careful limitation. The rulings in Even HaEzer concerning a kohen's wife and the need for chalitzah also show that the categories cannot simply be reduced to whether the hair and nails are complete. In cases of ordinary doubtful gestation, the majority presumption of viable births remains significant. The statements of Tashbatz and Bach that even ordinary newborns do not leave doubtful status until 30 days together with completion of hair and nails require study, because that is not the straightforward view of the Sages. Even according to Tosafos, who explain that R' Yehudah and Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel agree in certain aspects of the definite 8-month child, ordinary doubtful births are treated differently. The halacha therefore depends on whether the case is one of a definite 8-month child, an ordinary child of uncertain gestation, or a child with incomplete physical development. The rules of brit milah and pikuach nefesh follow these distinctions, with doubtful danger treated more leniently than ordinary ritual questions.
