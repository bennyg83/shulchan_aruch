Seif 10. Regarding a person who has never performed a brit milah, I am uncertain whether the same law applies on Yom Tov, or whether it is permitted on Yom Tov. The source of the restriction is derived from the sugya in Beitzah concerning an act that requires examination. At first glance, since in siman 498, seif 8, we rule leniently regarding the unresolved question of a bird that was trampled, and therefore the concern in heating new tiles is that they may crack, Mordechai's proof would not stand. One can answer that the reason we rule leniently there is necessarily that on Yom Tov the act is permitted by Torah law under the principle of mitoch, leaving only a rabbinic doubt. Accordingly, regarding brit milah on Shabbos we are stringent because the unresolved question concerns a Torah-level doubt. If so, on Yom Tov, where wounding for a Yom Tov need is permitted, there is room to permit the brit milah and to resolve the uncertainty leniently, understanding the concern in heating tiles as one of cracking. Moreover, Tosafos, Megillah 4a, s.v. Veya'avirenah, write that a person does not perform a brit milah unless he is skilled, which implies that this is simply not the ordinary practice, not that there is an independent prohibition. Ran in the first chapter likewise labors to explain why we do not decree concerning a Shabbos brit milah lest one go to an expert, which appears to indicate that he does not accept Mordechai's ruling as practical halacha. Therefore there is room to rely on this on Yom Tov, where the Shulchan Aruch does not state an explicit prohibition.
