"He should not perform the brit milah." Magen Avraham writes that the same applies to one who has performed the cutting but has never performed priyah: he may not perform priyah for the first time on Shabbos, and the reason is the same. See Responsa Chavot Yair, siman 158. A mohel who comes to perform a brit milah on Shabbos and says that he has already performed 1 procedure on a weekday is believed and need not bring proof. Once he has performed either the cutting or the priyah successfully once, we do not require that he establish proficiency by doing it 3 times. If he were not confident that he could perform it without error, he would not expose himself to the severe possibility of desecrating Shabbos. The Shulchan Aruch states that even the father may perform the brit milah. One might have thought that the father is different because he certainly desires the resulting repair of his son and the act is therefore a psik reisha beneficial to him, whereas another mohel might be treated differently. The ruling teaches that no such distinction is made.
