(36) "He should not perform the brit milah." We are concerned that, because he has never performed it before, he may make a mistake and thereby desecrate Shabbos where no mitzvah was accomplished. The same applies if he has never performed priyah: he may not perform priyah for the first time on Shabbos. A mohel who states that he has already performed a brit milah once on a weekday is believed and need not bring proof. Rama at the end of Yoreh De'ah, siman 266, writes that ideally it is proper to be stringent not to divide 1 Shabbos brit milah between 2 mohalim, 1 cutting and the other performing priyah; rather, the one who cuts should also perform priyah. Many later authorities permit dividing the procedure, and this is the established custom in Poland both on weekdays and on Shabbos. Chayei Adam writes that one should at least not divide the procedure among 3 people, and that the person performing priyah should also perform metzitzah. Yeshuot Yaakov rejects this and permits metzitzah to be performed by another person because it is a separate matter. (37) "On Shabbos." Regarding Yom Tov, one may certainly be lenient on the second day of Yom Tov in the Diaspora. Concerning the first day there is a dispute among the later authorities. If he knows that he is capable of performing the brit milah and there is no other mohel, there is certainly no reason to be stringent. (38) "He may perform the brit milah on Shabbos." This is so even if another mohel is present who has already performed many procedures. (39) "Even," etc. This excludes the view that a father should not personally perform his son's brit milah on Shabbos when another mohel is available, on the ground that for the father it is a psik reisha that is beneficial to him because he is thereby completing the repair of his son. We do not say this. The same beneficial consequence exists for another mohel as well, since he comes specifically to perform the mitzvah, yet the Torah permitted the act. [Terumat HaDeshen]
