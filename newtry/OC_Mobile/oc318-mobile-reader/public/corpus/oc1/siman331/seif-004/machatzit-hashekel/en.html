(א) (s.k. 4) Two arlot — two skins one atop the other. I saw in responsum Pnei Me'orot part 2 siman 31, and thus: I come to mention that which is written in Shulchan Aruch Orach Chayim siman 331: one who has two arlot — their milah does not override Shabbat. And Magen Avraham explained two skins one atop the other — Rashi and Rambam; and the world errs in this and one should warn a kosher mohel when he sees two skins one atop the other not to perform priyah of skin on Shabbat and wait until after Shabbat, for there is no danger at all if he does not perform priyah until after Shabbat — end. It is implied from his words that he understood these two skins mean skins of priyah; and what he wrote "wait until after Shabbat," etc. — it implies his intent that they perform priyah on the first day, for perforce we hold even milah not at its time must specifically be by day; and since priyah skin is thin and if they wait so long it sticks very much to the gid and priyah is impossible — the infant may come to danger and remain uncircumcised all his days:

(ב) (s.k. 4) And more reasonable according to my poor opinion that it means two upper skins called simply skin, but what they perform priyah in many places is called kerum (sometimes also called skin); and do not question how we know there is another skin beneath the upper skin — one can find cases where there is one skin covering the whole gid and it has only a small hole from which urine exits as is usual for foreskin of all infants; and further one can say another foreskin atop it open greatly upward; and so we find in infants who have only one skin but that skin is open greatly upward at the place of urine exit at the head of the gid, and beneath that skin the gid appears circumcised; but one who has two arlot — namely they have one foreskin as all infants, closed and not open except at urine exit place, and atop another skin covering the gid only at the head of the gid at urine exit place open greatly — thereby one sees there are two skins that both need cutting:
