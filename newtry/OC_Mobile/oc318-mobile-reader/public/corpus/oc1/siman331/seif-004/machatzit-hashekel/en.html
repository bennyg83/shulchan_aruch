Seif katan 4, "2 foreskins," meaning 2 skins one above the other. I saw in Responsa Panim Meirot, part 2, end of siman 31, that he understood these 2 skins as referring to the skins involved in priyah. He therefore wrote that when 2 skins are seen one should not perform the priyah on Shabbos but should wait until after Shabbos. His wording implies that the priyah should be done on Sunday, since even a brit milah not performed at its proper time must be performed by day. Yet the priyah membrane is thin, and if one waits so long it may adhere strongly to the organ and become difficult or impossible to separate, potentially endangering the child or leaving him with an incomplete brit milah. It therefore appears more correct, in my humble opinion, that "2 skins" means 2 upper foreskins that are ordinarily called skins, whereas the thin membrane separated during priyah is generally called a membrane, although at times it too is called a skin. One may ask how it can be known before the procedure that there is another skin beneath the upper one. A case is possible where 1 foreskin covers the entire organ and has only the small urinary opening typical of infants, while a second foreskin lies above it and has a much larger opening toward the top. We indeed find infants with only 1 foreskin whose opening is unusually wide at the urinary opening, so that beneath it the organ appears as though there were no visible foreskin. In the case of 2 foreskins, there is 1 ordinary foreskin closed except at the urinary opening, and above it another skin covering the organ but opened widely near the head. In this way one can see that 2 distinct skins require cutting.
