Scalpel from erev Shabbat, etc. Tosafos Yoreh De'ah siman 266 that it is permitted to move the knife after milah, etc.; and it is from Mahariv part 2, thus: permitted to move the knife to return it after milah, for after one moved the vessel permissibly he returns it to whatever place he wishes; and so Ramban — end; his intent is that we hold that any muktzeh in his hand he moves to whatever place he wishes as written siman 308 s.k. 3; but after he put it down from his hand — forbidden to move it as written siman 308; and so Maharil; and so Rashal in responsum; and in Yesh Seder in Beitzah chapter 3 in name of Mahar"a of Prague — except he disagrees and holds there is no muktzeh for half Shabbat on erev Shabbat; and it appears to me to be stringent; and so from what is written siman 499 that upon it is muktzeh on account of monetary loss as explained in gemara daf 123, and permitted to move it to slaughter meat on it for Yom Tov need and afterward forbidden to move it — likewise here; and so from what is written siman 491 in name of Yerushalmi; and that which we hold there is no muktzeh for half Shabbat — this is in things forbidden because he designated them from his mind we say since his mind was on them at twilight — permitted where he returns and takes them as written siman 310; but here after he circumcised with it it returned to its prohibition, for it is muktzeh on account of monetary loss and is like fruits that were fit at twilight and now are not fit — forbidden to move them — likewise here; and likewise a vessel whose melacha is forbidden that one used permissibly — forbidden afterward to move it from sun to shade for this reason that it has the law of a vessel whose melacha is forbidden; and so it appears explicitly from what is written siman 526 s.k. 6 in name of Ramban — likewise even though he used it permissibly — nevertheless it has the law of a vessel designated on account of monetary loss; and Mahari Beit part 3 siman 89 wrote: one who rinsed the knife after milah — there is prohibition since it is not for Shabbat need (see siman 323 s.k. 6); but muktzeh there is none since it is still in his hand — end; therefore one should be stringent not to put it down from his hand until he brings it home to store it; Beit Yosef wrote forbidden to take out the infant from his house to the courtyard to the scalpel, and see return 308 s.k. 41 that through a non-Jew it is permitted since there is no reshut ha-rabbim; and it is obvious forbidden to bring the infant to synagogue through a courtyard not joined even through a non-Jew, for they can circumcise him in his house, and many times they circumcise in the house when there is cold (see siman 621 s.k. 2);
