Twilight — see in Mishna Berurah what I copied in name of Radbaz, and it is not a safek-safeka of safek day safek twilight, and twilight itself is a safek — Peri Megadim wrote that perhaps because they establish the presumption of pregnancy [it appears to me that what he wrote with language "perhaps" is because he holds that presumption is not so strong regarding pushing aside Shabbat, and the practical difference is if an infant was born on Shabbat and we do not know whether by day on erev Shabbat or at night and we have no one to ask, such as if the woman died and the like — we do not say that presumably she gave birth now, and even on a weekday requires study; and see what we wrote below]; and further it is possible to say because there is one doubt there, and so too in Sefer Minchat Kohen; and see in siman 293 s.k. 3 in the gloss; and Rabbi Akiva Eiger already addressed this there — see there; and incidentally since we discuss that matter of twilight I said to mention several things needed for the law, namely in Yoreh De'ah siman 262 seif 6: if immediately when the infant put his head outside the corridor three medium stars appeared, etc. — it is difficult to me: behold we rule in Shabbos 34 that the halacha is like R' Yosi regarding terumah, and R' Yosi holds that two stars are twilight, and in the blink of an eye afterward three stars emerge; if so, within the time one goes out from the birthing house outside to see if it is night — what is it; even if he sees three medium stars, perhaps now they emerged and in the blink of an eye before there were only two and it was only twilight, and the ruling of Shulchan Aruch can only be imagined if at the time of birth itself he sees three stars, such as if the window is open in the house and he sits by the window and when he heard the child put out his head he immediately looked through the window and saw three medium stars — a stretch. However if he sees immediately small stars — it is reasonable to rely that before, at the time of birth, they were medium, for small ones are seen after it is already night as stated in the gemara there. And even this is not so clear, lest they are medium and before they were large visible in daytime, unless there are many small stars — then certainly one may rely on this. Further I said to note what Beit HaChidush wrote in laws of milah, thus: if he put his head outside the corridor and delayed somewhat until he was fully born, and afterward when fully born they saw three medium stars — if per the delay it appears to them that it was day at the emergence of the head, they have only what their eyes see and he shall be circumcised on the eighth even if it occurs on Shabbat, meaning if born at Motzei Shabbat — end of his words; and this follows from Shulchan Aruch Yoreh De'ah siman 262 — see there; but what he concludes there and wrote that if they cannot know — we follow the presumption that now he was born, and if it is on erev Shabbat permitted to circumcise him on Shabbat, etc., and he cited on this Shach siman 266 s.k. 9 — it is astounding, since it deals that he delayed somewhat — therefore there is certainly doubt that at the time of emergence of the head there were only two stars or perhaps one star; and what he cited to Shach there — it is irrelevant, for Semak deals where we do not know at all whether there was any delay from the time the head of the infant emerged outside the corridor until birth, and the birth was certainly at night — therefore he wrote that if they do not know, one follows presumption, meaning we presume her in presumption of pregnancy until now and we do not doubt that there was delay between emergence of head and birth of body; or Semak deals where the infant was born long after nightfall, unlike our case which deals where there was some delay between birth of head and body, and afterward when birth was completed they saw three medium stars — if so we cannot establish her presumption to say until now she was pregnant, for from the Torah through the head it is considered birth — who will say there were not two at first; and the essence of his reasoning from what he wrote "now he was born" is not precise, for perforce it deals that he delayed somewhat — rather perforce his intent is presumption that he was born at night and presumably the three stars were also at first; and in truth presumption is not relevant here — requires study; afterward I found in Azulai in Sefer Seder Taharah that he decides for the law like our words that if one knows there was some delay between birth of head and body — one should be stringent and say perhaps it was then twilight — see there:
