(א) Twilight. See Magen Avraham who raised that if he was born a quarter-hour before nightfall it is a safek, and if born before that they circumcise on the sixth day; and if born on Shabbat a quarter-hour before night they circumcise on Shabbat erev Shabbat; and in Radbaz siman 282 he wrote: an infant born on erev Shabbat after sunset of the sun before three medium stars appeared — if the matter is doubtful whether he was born before the end of sunset or after the end of sunset and before three stars appeared — since there are three doubts, it is deferred to the tenth [day] erev Shabbat; and see at the end of Sefer Darkei Noam, large pamphlet from Milchamot Mitzvah, with all details of laws and many distinctions at length. And see in responsa Bach siman 154. And Maharam Schick siman 41:

(ב) When he is already circumcised. Requires study why they do not practice now to examine afterward. And it appears to me as Bach wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 264 that one must see him circumcised even not at the time of erection on erev Shabbat; and likewise if he was not seen circumcised not at the time of erection — they circumcise on Shabbat. And requires study whether one may rely on this to desecrate severe Shabbat (Magen Avraham). (And see in Shach Yoreh De'ah end of siman 264 and see Yoreh De'ah siman 263 that if there is a pressed foreskin, meaning the skin is very stuck to the flesh — it overrides Shabbat; and in responsum Mahari Beit siman 89 he wrote that if there are tzitzin impeding the milah — it overrides; and in Agudah chapter R' Eliezer of Milah he wrote: one born circumcised on Shabbat — good to circumcise him on the day after, from which it implies he holds there is no prohibition when circumcising on Shabbat; but requires study, for the gemara is arranged in Shabbos 135 that it does not override Shabbat, and it is a great stretch to explain Agudah on another matter on erev Shabbat; and Rif is in doubt on it, and so Maharil — see in Sefer Eliyah Rabbah):
