"Twilight." See Magen Avraham, who concludes that if the infant was born approximately a quarter-hour before nightfall the matter is doubtful, while if he was born earlier the brit milah is performed on Friday. If he was born on Shabbos approximately a quarter-hour before nightfall, the brit milah is performed on Shabbos according to this calculation. Radbaz, siman 282, writes that if an infant was born on Friday after sunset but before 3 medium stars were visible, and there is doubt whether the birth occurred before the end of sunset or after it but before the stars appeared, the brit milah is deferred. See also the extensive discussion at the end of Darkei Noam in the treatise Milchamot Mitzvah, Responsa Bach siman 154, and the later authorities. "Born without a visible foreskin." It requires study why the custom is not to examine afterward. Magen Avraham explains in the name of Bach, Yoreh De'ah siman 264, that the child must appear this way even when the organ is not erect. If he does not appear so in that state, one performs the brit milah on Shabbos. Magen Avraham nevertheless questions whether this is sufficient basis to permit an act involving severe Shabbos laws. See also Shach at the end of Yoreh De'ah, siman 264, and siman 263 regarding a concealed foreskin. Where actual tissue obstructing the brit milah remains, the mitzvah can override Shabbos under the ordinary rules. Some authorities discuss the case of one born without a visible foreskin and indicate that the drawing of covenantal blood should preferably be done after Shabbos. The Talmud in Shabbos 135 is the principal source and the matter remains subject to the distinctions of the earlier authorities.
