(13) "Androgynos." A child who has male genital characteristics and also female genital characteristics. [Rambam, chapter 1 of Hilchot Milah] (14) "Born during twilight." Whether the twilight was on Friday evening or at the conclusion of Shabbos, the brit milah does not override Shabbos, because the eighth day is uncertain. If the child was definitely born before twilight, the normal eighth-day calculation applies. (15) "Born without a visible foreskin." This means that even when the organ is not erect he appears to have no visible foreskin. Where he appears ordinary when not erect and only afterward appears otherwise, the authorities discuss whether the case has this status. (16) "Caesarean section." The mother had difficulty giving birth and the child was removed through the abdominal wall rather than by ordinary delivery. (17) "2 foreskins." Some explain this as 2 organs, each with a foreskin; others explain that there are 2 distinct skins one above the other. (18) "Their brit milah does not override Shabbos." The reason in most of these cases is uncertainty: an androgynos is not certainly male; a twilight birth may not have its eighth day on Shabbos; one born without a visible foreskin may not actually have hidden foreskin requiring a Torah-level brit milah; and in the case of 2 foreskins it is unclear how the Torah's rule applies. A Caesarean birth and a child born to a non-Jewish woman who later converts are excluded for another reason: the mother did not have the ordinary tumat leidah status connected by the Torah to the son's eighth-day brit milah.
