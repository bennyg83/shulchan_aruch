[2] [Levush] During bein hashmashot, etc. The later authorities explain that this applies specifically when the child was born within one quarter of an hour before the appearance of the stars. But if he was born earlier than a quarter hour before the stars on Friday evening, he is circumcised on Friday. Likewise, if he was born on Shabbos a quarter hour before nightfall, he is circumcised on Shabbos. The Radbaz writes that even if there is doubt whether he was born within the quarter hour or before it on Friday evening, he is circumcised on Sunday. We do not say that he should be circumcised on Shabbos because of a double doubt.
[3] Born circumcised, etc. I found it written that this requires study, because why is the custom nowadays not to inspect for this? One must say as the Bach writes, that if the child does not appear circumcised when the organ is not erect, he is circumcised on Shabbos. This requires study as to whether one may rely on this to desecrate the severe sanctity of Shabbos, as stated by the Magen Avraham. See the Shach to Yoreh Deah at the end of siman 274. See also Yoreh Deah siman 263, where if there is a concealed foreskin, meaning skin tightly attached to the flesh, circumcision overrides Shabbos. Responsa Mabit, part 1, siman 89, writes that if there are remnants that invalidate the circumcision, their removal overrides Shabbos. See Yoreh Deah siman 264, seif 5. The Agudah in Perek Rabbi Eliezer DeMilah writes that if a child is born circumcised on Shabbos, it is preferable to circumcise him on the following day. This implies that he holds there is no actual prohibition if it is done on Shabbos. This requires study, because the explicit Gemara in Shabbos 135 says that such circumcision does not override Shabbos, and it is very strained to interpret the Agudah otherwise. See there. The Rif writes that regarding a child born circumcised there is uncertainty whether Shabbos is desecrated. The Maharil likewise writes that 'two foreskins' means, according to Rashi, two layers of skin, one upon the other, while some explain it as two organs. The Levush in Yoreh Deah writes likewise.
[4] Born by Caesarean delivery, etc. This means that the womb was opened through medication and the child was removed, after which the mother recovered, as stated by Masat Binyamin.
