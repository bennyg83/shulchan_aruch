"Twilight." The calculation of twilight for brit milah follows the distinctions explained by the authorities concerning sunset and the appearance of 3 medium stars. If a child is born before the doubtful period, the eighth day is counted from that day; if the birth occurs during the doubtful period, the brit milah is deferred because a doubtful brit milah does not override Shabbos. If the infant's head emerged before nightfall but the full birth occurred after nightfall, one must determine whether the head had emerged sufficiently to constitute birth. Where it is clear from the interval that the head emerged while it was still day, the halachic birth may be attributed to the earlier time. Where the timing is uncertain, one must be stringent. If, after the full birth, 3 medium stars were seen, this alone does not prove that they were already visible when the head emerged. If only large stars had been visible before, they do not establish night. Where many small stars are already visible, there is stronger basis to treat it as night. The authorities therefore rule that where there was a perceptible delay between emergence of the head and completion of birth, one cannot simply apply a presumption that the birth occurred only at the later moment. For Torah law, emergence of the head can itself constitute birth. The time of that emergence must therefore be evaluated independently, and if it remains doubtful the brit milah does not override Shabbos.
