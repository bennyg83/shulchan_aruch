(א) (s.k. 5) Scalpel, etc. And it is from Mahariv, etc. — meaning Rama there in Yoreh De'ah understood thus from Mahariv's words that scalpel after milah is permitted to move; but Magen Avraham brought Mahariv's language and explains Mahariv's words differently; except he disagrees, etc. — meaning nevertheless also Maharshal explained Mahariv's words thus as Magen Avraham wrote; nevertheless he disagrees with Mahariv himself and permits moving it even after he put it down:

(ב) (s.k. 5) And there is no muktzeh for half Shabbat — meaning since at twilight it was not muktzeh, for he needed it to circumcise — even now after he circumcised he has no need for it — there is no muktzeh matter since for part of Shabbat it was fit before milah:

(ג) (s.k. 5) And it appears to me to be stringent, etc. — for this is not the matter of "there is no muktzeh for half Shabbat," and as Magen Avraham explains afterward siman 491 in name of Yerushalmi that after he blessed on the lulav and went out with it and afterward put it down — forbidden to move it on account of muktzeh; evidently in such a case it is not in the category of "there is no muktzeh for half Shabbat":

(ד) (s.k. 5) And that which we hold, etc. — how when he returns and takes as written siman 310 — meaning figs that were moist at twilight and fit for eating — afterward they began to dry and spoil and are unfit for eating — afterward dried completely and again fit for eating; in such a case we say there is no muktzeh for half Shabbat since their designation was only because unfit for eating — now they are fit; granted if they were not fit at twilight — also now that fit — it is muktzeh since designated at twilight designated for the whole day; but since they were fit at twilight — one cannot say muktzeh law on it except because at Shabbat's start they were unfit — on this they said there is no muktzeh for half Shabbat; but scalpel whose designation is on account of monetary loss — if so until after milah it was not muktzeh — nevertheless afterward it returns to its prohibition, for also now it is muktzeh on account of monetary loss and is like fruits that were fit, etc.:

(ה) (s.k. 5) From sun to shade — for his body need or its place need permitted to move a vessel whose melacha is forbidden as written end of siman 308. And so from what is written in name of Ramban who permitted moving burial vessels after burial, for possibly still today they are fit for another deceased, Hashem forbid — but without this it would be forbidden even though until burial permitted to move them; if so likewise scalpel — since after milah he knows he will not need it again today — forbidden to move it. And Mahari Beit wrote, etc. — from this Magen Avraham brings proof for his words that he permitted moving only while still in his hand and did not put it down — therefore one should be stringent, etc.; and Taz Yoreh De'ah siman 266 wrote another reason to permit moving, thus: if he needed to throw away the scalpel afterward and it would be lost — certainly he refrains and does not circumcise; and this is preferable to those three things, etc., that they permitted the end on account of the beginning, etc.; perforce it appears Ramban permitted only to store in that room where he circumcised so it not be lost; but not to another place, etc., since it suffices otherwise, etc.; and he concluded: and even before milah one should not move gratuitously anything milah does not need — end. Behold also Taz perforce did not conclude to permit like Magen Avraham that any muktzeh in his hand he moves, etc.; for according to Magen Avraham all this while he did not put it down — permitted to move to whatever place he wishes and not specifically that room; and if he already put it down — again forbidden to move even to store in that room; and one could permit the end on account of the beginning lest he refrain and not circumcise so the knife not be lost — but he can circumcise and not leave the scalpel until they store it; however with one mohel who does cutting and also priyah — perforce must leave the scalpel after cutting — one can permit on account of the beginning (and even though possible to circumcise through two mohalim and the cutter holds the scalpel in his hand — Zakan Aharon — certainly better one mohel do everything, for according to Rabbeinu Yehuda end Laws of Milah forbidden for two people to circumcise on Shabbat — see there); and since permitted on account of the beginning to take the scalpel again — permitted afterward while in his hand to bring to whatever place he wishes and need not store specifically in that room according to Magen Avraham. And in Sefer Eshel Avraham he wrote thus: and Magen Avraham permits all this while in his hand, etc.; and per this when there is one mohel and perforce must put down the knife to perform priyah — forbidden to move it; therefore good to give to another person's hand; and in this manner it is in Yerushalmi, etc., regarding moving lulav — end. And behold Yerushalmi is not Talmud Bavli and I did not understand his words — what help is it to give to another person, for just as if he put it down forbidden to move according to Magen Avraham — likewise forbidden to give to another person, for specifically while in his hand permitted to move and need not throw it; but giving to another person — how was this permitted, behold for the other it is new moving — how permitted for another to move muktzeh; therefore when there is one mohel one should not permit except on account of the beginning as stated — if so he can leave it after cutting and take again as stated:

(ו) (s.k. 5) Beit Yosef wrote forbidden to take out the infant, etc. — meaning: one should not say forbidden to take out the scalpel to bring it to the infant, but even to take out the infant — one would think there is no prohibition, for a living being carries itself; and the reason forbidden for this Magen Avraham wrote see siman 318 that for a minor such as this we do not say a living being carries itself; and through a non-Jew permitted since there is no reshut ha-rabbim; and it appears to me better to bring the scalpel to the infant, for if he brings the infant to the scalpel he will need after milah to permit shvut of shvut to return the infant to his mother — unlike when he brings the scalpel to the infant he leaves it there until after Shabbat:

(ז) (s.k. 5) And see siman 276, etc. — and it appears to me regarding milah one may rely on them if impossible otherwise; and Eshel Avraham challenged thus: behold he wrote there specifically where his body benefits, etc.; and according to what I wrote above siman 276 s.k. 8 to explain Magen Avraham's words that he wrote this in name of Mishna Berurah specifically where his body, etc.; but to build synagogue forbidden; and he wrote on it: and so from end of chapter 15 that on Shabbat, etc., the intent is that so is the true law forbidden to tell a non-Jew to build synagogue for him because it is distant shvut, but not from Mishna Berurah's reason since his body does not benefit since he did not write; and another reason as explained chapter 15 of Shabbos — if so there is no question here. Taz wrote above Yoreh De'ah siman 266 s.k. 4 that those who carry an infant to circumcise to synagogue in a place where moving is forbidden — they carry the infant less than four amot; if one person carries him to synagogue he must each time within less than four amot sit or stand to rest (and see Magen Avraham Yoreh De'ah siman 266 s.k. 9); but if done through two people each carrying less than four amot — need not sit. Further he wrote nevertheless this permission of taking him from the house which is reshut ha-yachid to karmelit and likewise bringing from karmelit to synagogue which is reshut ha-yachid — therefore the carrying from the house and bringing to synagogue must be through a non-Jew — end. And it appears to me when the non-Jew takes the infant from the house — Israelite should not give to the non-Jew, for then the Israelite does the uprooting; rather the non-Jew himself takes the infant from where it rests; and likewise when bringing to synagogue — the non-Jew places him in one place and Israelite should not take from the non-Jew's hand, for then the Israelite does the placing; and not enough that the non-Jew stands in synagogue to rest and afterward Israelite takes from his hand — nevertheless there is reason to decree lest Israelite take from his hand before he stands to rest as Magen Avraham wrote thus siman 307 s.k. regarding letter a non-Jew brings; and all this according to Magen Avraham when impossible to circumcise in the house; but when possible to circumcise in the house — must circumcise in the house, for shvut of less than four amot is more severe than other shvut — see above siman 349:
