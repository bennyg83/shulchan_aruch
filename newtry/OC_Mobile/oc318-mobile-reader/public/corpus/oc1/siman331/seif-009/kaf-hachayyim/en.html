Responsa Binyan Tziyon, simanim 23 and 24, writes that suction must be performed specifically with the mouth and not through another device invented by contemporary physicians. Responsa Yad Eliezer, siman 10, permits suction with a tested sponge, writing that it is better than oral suction and may be permitted even on Shabbos; see there. Although physicians today say that oral suction may itself endanger the child, we have only the words of the Sages to follow. Nevertheless, it appears to me that one should not suck with excessive force on Shabbos. After suction, he should also spray from his mouth upon the wound some vinegar that he took into his mouth. Tiferes Yisrael on the Mishnah. Adam HaRishon's sin involved extending his hands, for he took with his hands and ate with his mouth. In this mitzvah too, the hands perform the bris and the mouth performs suction to rectify that sin. Chesed LaAlafim, Spring 2, River 51; Pis'chei Olam, paragraph 2.
