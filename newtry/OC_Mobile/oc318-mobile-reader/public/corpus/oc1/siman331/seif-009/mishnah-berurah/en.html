(א) They do not practice — for natures changed:

(ב) And in these countries — meaning in our places even though the infant is not so endangered as in their days — nevertheless there is still great need; therefore for bathing before milah they left on gemara law that permitted bathing him in hot water heated from yesterday; and after milah no concern if they wait until Motzei Shabbat:

(ג) They practice bathing him — and he should beware not to wring a sheet in water, for its wringing is its laundering; and through a non-Jew perhaps there is reason to be lenient; also beware of squeezing the sheet; and if hot water spilled — he can tell a non-Jew to bring water heated for the non-Jew, but he shall not tell a non-Jew to heat initially. An infant who was ill and recovered — Rashbetz forbids circumcising him on the fifth day lest they need to desecrate Shabbat on the third day of his milah; but Shach Yoreh De'ah end of siman 266 and Magen Avraham permit; and so Eshel Avraham agreed to permit; and one should not delay the mitzvah:

(ד) They prepare hot water, etc. — it implies if they did not prepare — forbidden to heat through a non-Jew, for we do not presume him sick in general, only one somewhat distressed; but if the doctor says he definitely needs — one should not be stringent on this:
