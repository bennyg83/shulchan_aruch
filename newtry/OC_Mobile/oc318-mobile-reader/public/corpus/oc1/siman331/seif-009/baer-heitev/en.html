"And they bathe." One should not soak a sheet, because this is laundering. Rather, a non-Jew may place the sheet into the water. If the hot water spilled, one may tell a non-Jew to bring water that was heated for the non-Jew, but should not tell him to heat water specifically for the infant. Beit Yosef writes at the end of Yoreh De'ah, siman 267, in the name of Rashbatz, that an infant who had been ill, was not given a brit milah at the proper time, and then recovered should not be given the brit milah on Thursday, lest the third day after the procedure fall on Shabbos and require desecration of Shabbos. The custom was likewise to avoid Friday. See there Shach and Taz, and see Knesset HaGedolah. Magen Avraham writes that nowadays, when the infant is not ordinarily bathed in the former manner after brit milah, there is no reason for this concern, because it is not common that Shabbos would need to be desecrated on his account. Therefore one should not delay performance of the mitzvah merely for this reason.
