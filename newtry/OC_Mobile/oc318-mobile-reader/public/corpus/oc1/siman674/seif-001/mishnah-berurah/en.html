A Chanukah lamp from a Chanukah lamp. This speaks from the first night onward, when there is more than one lamp and one kindles this from that. Alternatively, on the first night there are two people in one house, each kindling on his own, and they are permitted to kindle from one another, for both are mitzvah lamps. Although it is explained below in siman 675 that one must first place it in its location and only afterward kindle it, Turei Zahav already wrote below that for a short time one need not be particular about this. According to the other later authorities, who hold that even for a short time one must be particular, here it is speaking where both were fixed in their locations, but had long wicks, and the wick reached the lamp before it, so there is no need to remove it from its place.
Without an intermediary. There is no degradation of the mitzvah in this, since both are for a mitzvah. But if one of the lamps went out, one may not kindle it from the others, even without an intermediary, and even if it went out within the required time, because by strict law there is no mitzvah in this kindling, since we hold that if it went out he is not required to tend to it.
Through a weekday lamp it is forbidden. Because it appears as though he degrades the mitzvah lamp, kindling a weekday lamp from it, even though he is doing so for the need of a mitzvah. A shamash lamp is included as a weekday lamp for this purpose. See below, paragraph 7.
Also in this. He holds that since he does it in order immediately to kindle from it a mitzvah lamp, it is not included in degradation.
With Chanukah lamps, etc. Specifically with Chanukah lamps, for the reason he explains below. But with other mitzvah lamps, such as Shabbos lamps, synagogue lamps, and the like, one may kindle from one lamp to another. Darkei Moshe. Chemed Moshe also agreed that this is the common practice, and so wrote Chayei Adam. So too it implies above in siman 154, section 14, that specifically a lay lamp may not be kindled from a synagogue lamp, but lamp from lamp is permitted; and Rema did not gloss there to be stringent at all, implying that it was obvious to him that here there is no custom to be stringent. See Pri Megadim, that through a weekday lamp it is not proper even with other mitzvah lamps. See below what we will write about this.
For the main mitzvah, etc. Meaning, by the strict law one lamp is enough for all members of the household when they all depend on one table. Magen Avraham. According to this, where two homeowners live in one house and by the strict law each one needs to kindle a Chanukah lamp, one may kindle from one to the other. Pri Megadim. See what we wrote above at the end of paragraph 1.
It is not so much for a mitzvah. We already wrote that if one of the lamps went out, by law one may not kindle it from the other lamps, and all the more so if the shamash went out, one may not kindle it from the Chanukah lamp. Maharshal wrote that in the synagogue there is no distinction between the shamash and the other lamps, for all the lamps inside it are called mitzvah lamps. Therefore one should rebuke those who light their lamps by means of the maidservants from the synagogue lamp, even from the shamash, except on Motzaei Shabbos in order to go home and give light in the dark alleyways.
All the more so it is permitted to kindle from them. According to what we wrote above in siman 672, that some authorities hold that as long as they are burning it is forbidden to use their light, the same applies that one may not kindle from them. Nevertheless, it seems that one should be stringent about this only regarding kindling weekday lamps from it. But a mitzvah lamp, which by strict law is permitted even within the required time, at any rate we should not be stringent after the time has passed.
