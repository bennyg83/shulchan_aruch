Through a lamp, etc. For we hold like Shmuel that one may kindle from lamp to lamp, meaning directly from lamp to lamp, but not through a splinter, meaning a weekday object from which he wants afterward to kindle a mitzvah lamp. Since the object from which he kindles is weekday, even though he does it for the need of a mitzvah, the reason is degradation of the mitzvah. Those who permit hold that Shmuel does not accept the concern of degradation of the mitzvah at all, and therefore he permitted even through a splinter.
Unless it is in a manner, etc. In Terumas HaDeshen we learn this from the Gemara's question against the one who says a splinter is permitted, from that which was taught: one may not weigh a sela of maaser sheni against gold dinars, even in order to redeem maaser sheni upon it. Rava answers: it is a decree lest he not align his weights. Rashi explains: perhaps he will find that they are good, or that they are extra, and he will be concerned for them and not redeem the maaser upon them, and it will turn out that he degraded the maaser unnecessarily. From this he learns that if there is concern that the wind will extinguish it before it reaches the mitzvah lamp, it is forbidden. In my humble opinion, it is not comparable. There, it is possible that it will lead to nullifying the mitzvah, as Rashi wrote, because there is no mitzvah obligation upon him. But here, where he wants to kindle from lamp to lamp through the splinter, why should the splinter cause nullification of the mitzvah more than direct kindling from lamp to lamp? Certainly, if the wind extinguishes it, he will go back and kindle it, and will not come to nullify the mitzvah, for he has an obligation to kindle. If you come to be concerned that he kindled the first time in vain, this concern exists even when kindling directly from lamp to lamp. I do not know any greater prohibition through a splinter than from lamp to lamp. Directly from lamp to lamp is permitted even if he kindles and holds the lamp that he wants to kindle and moves it to the lamp from which he kindles, though he must in any case place it in its location, as written in siman 675. This seems to me.
It is not so much for a mitzvah. All the more so, if the shamash went out, one may not kindle it from the Chanukah lamp. Maharshal wrote that in the synagogue there is no distinction between the shamash and the other lamps, for all the lamps inside it are called mitzvah lamps. Therefore one should rebuke those who light their lamps by means of maidservants from the synagogue lamp, except on Motzaei Shabbos because of the dark alleyways, through which Shaul merited kingship. A Havdalah lamp is called a mitzvah lamp. But after he has recited Havdalah over it, the mitzvah has already been performed and one may kindle from it, for even a Chanukah lamp after its required time is no longer called a mitzvah lamp. Until here. What he wrote, that Shaul merited kingship through it, is based on the Midrash: Rabbi Shimon ben Lakish said that Shaul merited kingship only through his ancestor, who would light lamps for the public, and therefore his name was called Ner.
