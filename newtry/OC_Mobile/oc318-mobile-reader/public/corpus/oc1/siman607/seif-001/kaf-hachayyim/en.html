1) [Seif 1] One must confess during Minchah before the meal, etc. Lest a mishap occur during the meal, such as intoxication or the like, and he will be unable to confess afterward. Beit Yosef; Magen Avraham, s.k. 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, os 1; Mishna Berurah, os 1. Rambam, Hilchos Teshuvah 2:7, wrote: Lest he choke during the meal before confessing. The Bach cited his words and wrote that this was the version he had in the Baraisa. Knesset HaGedolah, in Hagahos HaTur, os 1, wrote that Rambam had a different version, see there. It appears to me that Rambam explains "lest a mishap occur during the meal" to mean lest he choke during the meal. Yad Aharon, Hagahos HaTur.
2) Ibid.: Before the meal, etc. Some say that one must also confess after the meal, before the Maariv prayer, based on what is written in the Tosefta at the end of tractate Yoma, see there. This is also Ramban's view, as written in the Beit Yosef. Although the Beit Yosef wrote that the world did not follow Ramban's words, the Shelah, 228b, nevertheless wrote that it is proper for every G-d-fearing person to act according to Ramban, since he brought proof from the Tosefta. He wrote that this was his own practice: when he saw twilight approaching and it was close to nightfall, he confessed with all his strength, see there. The Magen Avraham, s.k. 7, cited his words, as did Eliyah Rabbah, os 11. Pri Chadash wrote: Nowadays, the custom is to recite "Lecha Keili Teshukasi," which contains confession, and one must stand during the confession to fulfill Ramban's view; this is proper. End quote. This applies according to the Sephardic custom. Ashkenazim fulfill this through reciting Tefillah Zakah before Kol Nidrei, which also contains confession, as its text is recorded in Chayei Adam, klal 144, and in Ashkenazic siddurim, see there. The siddur of Rav Yaavetz states that after Tefillah Zakah, shortly before actual nightfall, one privately recites the brief confession, "Ashamnu.
3) It is written in Sefer HaMiddos, entry "Quarrel," os 33: Through confession, the thoughts of your enemies against you will be nullified. Yafeh LaLev, volume 2, os 1. See also below, os 31.
4) The Sages commanded that all other Jews confess on Yom Kippur, both men and women. Sefer Yereim, section on appointed times, os 121. So too wrote Sefer HaChinuch, parashas Naso, mitzvah 363: This mitzvah of confession applies in every place and at every time, to males and females. One who transgresses this by failing to confess his sins on Yom Kippur, which has eternally been established as a day of forgiveness and atonement, has neglected this positive commandment, see there. Yafeh LaLev cited his words there and wrote that he had not heard of women confessing on Yom Kippur. Perhaps they rely upon the confession of the head of the household, who says, "But we and our fathers and the members of our household have sinned," see there. Nevertheless, it is more correct to teach them to confess themselves, because it is a greater mitzvah to perform it personally than through an agent. Children should also be trained and taught to confess, so that they become accustomed to doing so.
5) A support for the custom of saying before the confession, "Please, Hashem, our God and God of our fathers, etc.," appears, in my humble opinion, to be the confession of the Kohen Gadol on Yom Kippur, when he says, "Please, Hashem, atone now for the sins, etc." We derive this in the Gemara, Yoma, chapter 3, through a gezeirah shavah, see there. Kisei Eliyahu, os 1. See above, siman 131, os 7.
6) Ibid., Rama's gloss: An individual recites it after completing his prayer, etc. This means that for an individual, Yom Kippur and erev Yom Kippur are alike, for he recites it only after his prayer. The shaliach tzibbur, however, does not recite the confession within the prayer on erev Yom Kippur. Rather, he recites it silently after the prayer, like all other individuals. During the repetition of the prayer, he does not recite it at all, as stated below in seif 5 in the Rama's gloss. Pri Chadash; Levush; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, os 3.
