To confess - What it says, "we cannot say we are brazen," is based on what is written in Reishis Chochmah, page 144, that one who prays on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur without repentance is brazen, for he asks for blessing, life, and healing, and is not ashamed of his sins. What it says, "stiff-necked," is because perhaps they do not repent with the claim that they are compelled by their inclination. This is the statement "we are righteous." Therefore it says that we cannot say we are stiff-necked, for although He created the evil inclination, He also created Torah as its spice. So too they said: the shamefaced go to Gan Eden, for he will necessarily repent. "We have turned away from Your commandments" is confession over positive commandments. See the omissions to the responsum in Yoreh De'ah from siman 519 to 591. See what the Be'er Hetev wrote in subparagraphs 1 and 2 in the name of the Shelah. So too it is in the Talmud, and Rashi explains: near dark, after eating, lest his mind be confused because of drunkenness and a matter of sinful stumbling. See the Rambam, chapter 2 of the Laws of Repentance. The Ramban holds and explains that it is after he eats, before he accepts Yom Kippur upon himself, so that he enters it in repentance. According to Rashi and the Rambam, the explanation is after he accepted the fast upon himself. The implication of Tosafos is like the Ramban, and so wrote Maharam Chaviv, Minhagei Yerushalayim, and similar to this wrote Maharshal Halevi. See Likkutei Mateh Asher, page 140.
