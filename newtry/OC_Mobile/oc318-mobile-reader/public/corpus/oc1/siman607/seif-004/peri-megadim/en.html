His face to the north - See Taz. So too, it seems, is the custom, specifically with his face to the north. See Magen Avraham 12. The Rav wrote that he should neither stand nor sit, but lean, and so it is with Torah lashes, leaning. I do not know why the custom now is to lie on the ground and spread grasses and the like because of the prohibition of a stone floor, rather than leaning. Perhaps it is for a recognizable sign that this is not lashes through which atonement is achieved, and that all who are liable to kareis are released from their kareis through them; this does not help, see Magen Avraham letter 9. Regarding lashes of rebellion for rabbinic transgressions, or Torah matters that do not have Torah lashes, this requires study, for perhaps one is atoned through such lashes if they are administered according to the law of disciplinary lashes. See the Rambam, Laws of Sanhedrin 16:3, which implies that judges outside the Land administer lashes of rebellion. The practical difference is for atonement and to become valid as a witness; see Choshen Mishpat 34. We have already written in several places that rabbinic invalidations of testimony are because of wickedness that receives lashes of rebellion, and anyone who was lashed can be said to return immediately to validity. The Beis Yosef already wrote that there is some atonement. According to what I wrote, there is a great practical difference if they strike strongly according to the law of lashes of rebellion. See Pri Chadash, Orach Chayim 496, 11 and 13. See the Rambam there, chapter 17, law 1, which implies that from the Torah, even according to the first tanna, there are forty complete lashes, and only rabbinically not to add; see there. The Chinuch in parashas Ki Seitzei, mitzvah 595, wondered about him, and the Kesef Mishneh and Lechem Mishneh resolved it. If so, one can say these lashes are forty complete lashes as a recognizable sign that this is not Torah lashes. About this the Magen Avraham wrote in letter 8 that this is not so, and with the help of Heaven it will be explained there further.
