4) The Sages commanded that all other Jews confess on Yom Kippur, both men and women. Sefer Yereim, section on appointed times, os 121. So too wrote Sefer HaChinuch, parashas Naso, mitzvah 363: This mitzvah of confession applies in every place and at every time, to males and females. One who transgresses this by failing to confess his sins on Yom Kippur, which has eternally been established as a day of forgiveness and atonement, has neglected this positive commandment, see there. Yafeh LaLev cited his words there and wrote that he had not heard of women confessing on Yom Kippur. Perhaps they rely upon the confession of the head of the household, who says, "But we and our fathers and the members of our household have sinned," see there. Nevertheless, it is more correct to teach them to confess themselves, because it is a greater mitzvah to perform it personally than through an agent. Children should also be trained and taught to confess, so that they become accustomed to doing so.
