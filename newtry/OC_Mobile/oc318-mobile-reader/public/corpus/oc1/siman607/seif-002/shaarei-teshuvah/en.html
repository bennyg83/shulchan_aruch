In Pri Megadim, part 2, siman 178, he wrote regarding one who wants to repent, whose heart is aflame and who wants to confess his sins publicly so that he will be ashamed. He wrote that what is implied in Sotah 32, where it says "let him be ashamed and go, so that he be atoned," is specifically with idolatry, where Scripture imposed it upon him, and specifically in matters of an offering. Otherwise it is forbidden to publicize, as is evident from Sotah 8. From here is an answer: it is forbidden to publicize his sins; rather, he should confess between himself and himself. Even for a sin, it is proper to ask a sage what repentance is needed for such a sin. Recently new people have come and say that a person specifically must publicize his sin. Let those words sink and not be said, for this matter is from external books, and Hashem should guide us in the true path, end quote. See Yad Ephraim, where I wrote that from Sotah 8 it is proven that where others are suspected, he may publicize that he did it, as is evident there in Sotah, where Yehudah confessed. Although Rabbi Shimon said, one who specifies his sins is brazen, nevertheless Yehudah did so so that others would not be suspected. This proves the Pri Megadim's point, that without this reason it is forbidden; but where this reason exists, the Pri Megadim also agrees. See the words of my grandfather in Tevuos Shor, siman 5, and in Kerem Shlomo on Chullin, end of chapter 2, and in Sotah; see there. See Eliyah Rabbah, who wrote regarding the Levush, who wrote that one should specify quietly just as Moshe said, etc., and asked against this that in Sotah it implies that with idolatry one should publicize; see there. There is no difficulty, for there it speaks of an inadvertent sin and regarding an offering, and therefore he should specify so that he be atoned. This is unlike the case of Moshe, where they were intentional sinners and Moshe confessed over their sin by way of prayer and supplication. Also, in any event, the Levush there wrote "like Moshe" only regarding the point that quietly it is proper to specify the sin; see there.
