And if he wanted to specify - This implies that it speaks even aloud. It seems to me that this is specifically with a public sin, but with a sin that is not public, according to everyone it is forbidden to say it aloud, as it is written, "fortunate is he whose transgression is forgiven, whose sin is covered," etc., as written in the gloss. Certainly the amoraim do not disagree with the tanna'im. So too in Sotah 32, prayer was instituted silently so as not to embarrass transgressors who confess their sin. Rather, Rabbi Yehudah ben Bava holds that before the Omnipresent one must specify the sin in confession, and Rabbi Akiva holds that even before the Omnipresent one need not specify. In this there is no distinction between public and not public, for everything is revealed before Him. See the Gemara and the Bach, unlike the Beis Yosef. There is a difficulty from what Rashi wrote at the end of chapter 2 of Chullin, that if he had sinned inadvertently, it would have a voice, for one who had a transgression come into his hand inadvertently does not conceal it, so that he should be ashamed and atoned, end quote. But in the verse it is written "fortunate is he whose transgression is forgiven, whose sin is covered," and sin means inadvertent. There is another difficulty, for in Sotah we say that Scripture did not distinguish a place between a sin-offering and a burnt-offering so as not to embarrass him. One can answer that there it is different, for since he says "for my sin," we see that he is not ashamed. This requires study.
Quietly - Mateh Ephraim, siman 102, wrote that a prayer leader who prays on behalf of the public must make the public hear, and if he lowers his voice they rebuke him (Kenesses ha-Gedolah). In Al Chet one should say: "secretly and openly, inadvertently and intentionally" (Shibbolei ha-Leket). So it seems, for one must first ask about the lighter matter. However, this requires study, for if so it will not be in alphabetical order. Therefore it seems to me that since we say "we sinned openly and secretly," we ask for both together, as stated in Temurah, chapter 5. In the Rambam's text the reading is "openly" alone, and similarly "intentionally" alone. The order of confession in Al Chet is: burnt-offering, sin-offering, variable sin-offering, definite guilt-offering and doubtful guilt-offering, lashes of rebellion, lashes, death, kareis, and death by court (Mahara Isserlin). One should strike his chest with his fist (writings of the Ari). In Midrash Koheles it says to strike the heart to say: you caused this for me. One should say: atonement for sins, pardon for iniquities, and forgiveness for transgressions (Minhagim and Shelah in the name of the Rema).
