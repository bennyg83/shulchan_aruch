2) Ibid.: Before the meal, etc. Some say that one must also confess after the meal, before the Maariv prayer, based on what is written in the Tosefta at the end of tractate Yoma, see there. This is also Ramban's view, as written in the Beit Yosef. Although the Beit Yosef wrote that the world did not follow Ramban's words, the Shelah, 228b, nevertheless wrote that it is proper for every God-fearing person to act according to Ramban, since he brought proof from the Tosefta. He wrote that this was his own practice: when he saw twilight approaching and it was close to nightfall, he confessed with all his strength, see there. The Magen Avraham, s.k. 7, cited his words, as did Eliyah Rabbah, os 11. Pri Chadash wrote: Nowadays, the custom is to recite "Lecha Keili Teshukasi," which contains confession, and one must stand during the confession to fulfill Ramban's view; this is proper. End quote. This applies according to the Sephardic custom. Ashkenazim fulfill this through reciting Tefillah Zakah before Kol Nidrei, which also contains confession, as its text is recorded in Chayei Adam, klal 144, and in Ashkenazic siddurim, see there. The siddur of Rav Yaavetz states that after Tefillah Zakah, shortly before actual nightfall, one privately recites the brief confession, "Ashamnu.
