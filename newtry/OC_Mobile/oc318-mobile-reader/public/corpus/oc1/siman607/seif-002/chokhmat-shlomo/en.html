There, seif 2. "He need not detail the sin, and if he wishes to detail it he may." NB: The source of this law is Yoma 86b: "One must detail the sin, as it says, 'Please, this people has sinned a great sin and made themselves gods of gold'; these are the words of Rabbi Yehudah ben Beteira. Rabbi Akiva says, 'Fortunate is he whose transgression is forgiven, whose sin is covered.' If so, why did Moshe say, 'and they made themselves gods of gold'? As Rabbi Yannai said: because of the silver and gold that You gave them in abundance, etc." This is the language of the Gemara. I am astonished from Sotah 32b, where R. Yochanan said in the name of Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai: why did they institute silent prayer? So as not to shame transgressors, for Scripture did not distinguish between a chatat and an olah, etc. The Gemara asks that a chatat is female and an olah male, and answers that there it is covered by the tail. It asks: leave aside a lamb; what can be said about a goat? It answers: there he shames himself, for he could have brought a lamb and brought a goat. It asks: regarding a chatat for avodah zarah, where only a goat suffices, what can be said? It answers: there let him be ashamed and go, so that he gain atonement. Rashi explains: there, let him bring it and be ashamed; Scripture imposed upon him that he be shamed so that he obtain atonement for such a severe punishment. If so, it is proven from there that throughout the Torah the Torah was particular not to reveal the sin, but regarding avodah zarah the Torah imposed that the sin be publicized so he be ashamed. If so, what proof does Rabbi Yehudah bring that one must detail the sin from the verse, "and they made themselves gods of gold," to reject Rabbi Akiva's view that one should not detail because it says, "fortunate is he whose sin is covered"? Avodah zarah is different: regarding avodah zarah the Torah imposed that the sin be publicized, and therefore Moshe Rabbeinu publicized it. But for other sins, where the Torah said to cover the sin, Rabbi Akiva properly says, "fortunate is he whose transgression is forgiven, etc." In particular, regarding Rabbi Akiva's own words, why did the Gemara need to answer the verse "and they made themselves gods of gold" as it did? It could have answered simply that avodah zarah is different, as it says here. This requires study, for the sugyot contradict one another. Examine carefully. While on this matter, I was further troubled by the sugya in Kiddushin chapter 2, 55b, regarding an animal found outside Jerusalem. The Gemara asks: perhaps it is a chatat, and answers: a chatat is in its first year, and this was found in its second year, etc. This is difficult. According to the answer, even now we must establish that it does not speak in every case, only where its year has passed. And according to this, the Gemara asks there: perhaps it is a chatat whose year has passed, and answers that this is uncommon. If so, it would have been better to say that it speaks of a female goat, since we say that with a goat he shames himself, and in truth one may not detail his sins, as stated in Yoma and here in Shulchan Aruch, and one who details his sins is brazen. If so, a goat as a chatat is uncommon, and it could answer that it speaks of a goat. One cannot say that perhaps it is a chatat for avodah zarah; that is not so, for in Chullin, regarding one who says "for my chatat," it is valid, and the Gemara asks perhaps it is a chatat, and answers that if he had sinned the matter would have publicity. Rashi explains that anyone who sins unintentionally publicizes it, etc. At first glance, this itself is difficult: why does it ask, perhaps it is a chatat? If it were a chatat, the matter would have publicity that so-and-so sinned, and we could say that if it were a chatat the matter would be known who sinned, and we could ask him whether his chatat was lost or not. But this is not difficult, for the Magen Avraham asked here on Rashi: on the contrary, it is forbidden to publicize the sin. He answers that there it is different, because since we see that he says "I did not sin," he is not ashamed, and therefore if he had sinned the matter would have publicity, for he publicizes his sin more. But there in Kiddushin one may indeed be concerned for an ordinary person who does not publicize his sins, and it has no publicity. According to this, however, the difficulty from the sugya in Sotah above remains. Why did the Gemara ask? It could have answered that it speaks of a goat. Either way there is no concern: for a chatat for chelev and the like, he would presumably not bring a goat, because "fortunate is he whose sin is covered," and one who details his sins is brazen; and there is no concern that it is a chatat for avodah zarah, because in that case, since it is a mitzvah to publicize it, we again say that if someone sinned there would be publicity that so-and-so sinned, and it would be possible to ask whether his chatat was lost or not. Why did it need to answer that its year had passed and then also that a chatat whose year passed is uncommon? It should have answered that it speaks of a goat, and said that a goat as chatat is uncommon either way: if for chelev, one does not bring a goat; if for avodah zarah, it would have publicity and could be clarified. This requires study. Examine carefully. The Rambam's language in Hilchot Teshuvah chapter 2 is: What is complete teshuvah? One who encounters the same sinful matter, etc. How so? If one had relations with a woman in sin, and after some time was secluded with her while still loving her and with his whole body, etc. I was asked: yichud is forbidden, so how can proper teshuvah be found initially except through a transgression? It is not fitting to say so. I answered that it is well resolved according to what we hold, that two fit men may be secluded with a woman, but with immoral men even two do not help. If so, it can be found where he was secluded with her together with one other person. Since they know about themselves that they are fit and do not intend to transgress, it is not yichud with two; and it is teshuvah, for if they wished to sin again they could, since two can transgress, as we are indeed concerned with two immoral men. Therefore it is considered the same place and the same act. Examine carefully.
There, seif 2, in the gloss. "But when one prays aloud, or the shaliach tzibbur when he repeats the prayer, etc." NB: In Sotah 7 the Gemara asks: why was Reuven needed? He confessed. But the master said: one who details his sins is brazen. It answers: so that his brothers not be suspected, etc. This proves that where many are suspected of some matter, it is a mitzvah for him to detail his sin, that he sinned, so that others not be suspected of it. Yet the Rambam in Hilchot Teshuvah chapter 2 states simply that for a sin that is not publicized, it is forbidden to detail one's sins. This is astonishing, for he did not copy this law as above. In truth, the Gemara in Yoma 86 is also astonishing. It asks a contradiction between verses: it is written, "fortunate is he whose transgression is forgiven," and it is written, "one who covers his transgressions will not succeed." It answers: here with a publicized sin, here with a non-publicized sin; or, here with sins between man and his fellow, etc. Why did it not answer as it answered in Sotah, that the verse "one who covers his transgressions will not succeed" speaks where many were suspected of that matter, in which case if he covers his sins he will not succeed, and it is better for him to confess so that others not be suspected? Also, the Gemara in Sotah is difficult according to what we say in Shabbat chapter 5, that anyone who says Reuven sinned is mistaken, and what is written, "Reuven went and lay," means that he disturbed his father's bed. If so, this is a sin between man and his fellow, and for sins between man and his fellow he is obligated to detail his sins. What, then, is the Gemara asking? This requires study. Perhaps this resolves the Rambam's words: he did not copy the law that where many are suspected one must detail his sin, and this also resolves what the Raavad asked there, why he did not distinguish between a publicized and non-publicized sin. One may say that one answer and another answer disagree, and from where did the Rambam know they disagree? From what I have written, the Rambam derived his law from here. It was difficult to him why the Gemara in Sotah did not answer that it was between man and his fellow. Perforce, the Gemara's question in Sotah follows the view that distinguishes between a publicized and non-publicized sin, and does not accept the distinction between sins between man and his fellow and other sins. Therefore, according to that view, the question is proper: how did Reuven confess? It must answer: so that his brothers not be suspected. But for us, who rule like the view that distinguishes sins between man and his fellow, we no longer need the answer that it was so his brothers not be suspected; rather, it was because it was between man and his fellow. Thus one may say that where it is between man and God, even if others are suspected, one should not detail his sins. This proves that the answers disagree; if they did not disagree, the question would remain why Sotah did not answer that it was between man and his fellow. Thus both rulings of the Rambam are resolved and depend on one another. A new practical law emerges from our words: although the Gemara in Sotah implies that where others are suspected he must confess, according to us we do not rule that way according to the Rambam's method; according to the Raavad's method we do rule that way. This requires study in practical law. Examine very carefully.
