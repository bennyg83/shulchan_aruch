He has permission - See Taz. With this one can also explain what is written in se'if 4, that he may return and confess over them, while in the Gemara 86b it says "this is praiseworthy" according to Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov. See the Rambam, chapter 2 of the Laws of Repentance, law 8, who did not write "this is praiseworthy." See Magen Avraham 6. One can say that since in the baraisa it is not clear that the law follows Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov, as written in letter 10:1, only where his reason is compelling, therefore he wrote as a compromise and by reasoning that he may, not that it is praiseworthy.
