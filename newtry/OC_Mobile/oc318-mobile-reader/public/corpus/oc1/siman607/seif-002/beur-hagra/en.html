Se'if 2. And if he wanted, etc. There: the Rabbis taught, transgressions, etc.; Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov says, or, etc. According to both of them he specifies the sin, and all the more so since we rule in se'if 4 like Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov.
And if he confesses, etc. It is all one matter, meaning that if he confesses quietly and wants to specify, he has permission and it is proper to specify. But aloud it is forbidden to specify, as written in the gloss.
It is proper, etc. For the Rambam ruled like Rabbi Yehudah ben Bava, since the dispute of the first tanna and Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov implies like Rabbi Yehudah ben Bava. Also according to Rabbi Akiva it is proper to specify, since they disagree about transgressions he did not repeat, as above. The Tur ruled like Rabbi Akiva, for the law follows Rabbi Akiva against his colleague, and there it speaks where he wants to specify; therefore it is proper, etc. From the dispute of Rav Yehudah and Rav Nachman there is no proof, as the Magen Avraham wrote, for they certainly do not disagree with a dispute of tanna'im. Rather, they speak about publicizing to people aloud, while the tanna'im Rabbi Yehudah ben Bava and Rabbi Akiva speak quietly before the Omnipresent. This is what is written in the gloss.
But, etc., aloud, etc. As written in Sotah 32b: nevertheless, they instituted prayer, etc.; and in chapter 5 of Avos and Yoma 21a, "they bowed with room." See Rashi there, comment beginning "they bowed," and at the end of chapter 5 of Berachos.
Seventy-eight, according to order, etc. Rokeach, and so wrote the Mordechai.
