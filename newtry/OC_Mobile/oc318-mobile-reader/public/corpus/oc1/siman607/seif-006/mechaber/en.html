The entire congregation receives forty lashes after the Minchah prayer, so that thereby one will take to heart to repent from the transgressions in his hand. {Rama: And the custom is that the one being lashed says confessions at the time he is lashed, and the one lashing says "Vehu Rachum yechaper avon," etc., three times — which are thirty-nine words, corresponding to the thirty-nine lashes (Minhagim). And the custom is to lash with a small strap, whatever [size] it is, for it is only a remembrance of lashes; and one takes a strap of calf[skin], in the manner of the verse "The ox knows its owner" (Kol Bo). And the one lashed should neither stand nor sit, but be bent over (Minhagim), his face to the north and his back to the south (Maharil). Yom Kippur atones only for those who repent [and] believe in its atonement; but one who spurns it and thinks in his heart, "What benefit is this Yom Kippur to me?" — it does not atone for him (Rambam, chapter 3 of Hilchos Shegagos).}
