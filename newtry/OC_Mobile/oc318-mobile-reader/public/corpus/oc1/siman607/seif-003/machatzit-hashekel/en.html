(Subparagraph 3) Quietly, etc. That the prayer leader, etc., must make heard, etc. This is when he confesses generally in the alphabetic formula as arranged in the prayer, but not a private sin. Only the confession that everyone says equally is like that there.
For one should say the lighter matter first. As the Tur wrote in siman 582, as we find with David: first he said, "Who can discern errors," and afterward he asked about intentional sins, as it is written, "also from intentional sinners restrain Your servant." Then, "let them not rule over me; then I will be whole," meaning strong transgressions. He brings a parable for this: Rabbi Abba said, these Kutim know how to beg at doorways. First they say, give me a little water, and he gives him. Give me one onion, and he gives him. He says, an onion without bread harms the heart. So the righteous know how to appease their Creator, as it is said, "the lips of the righteous know favor," end quote of the Tur. It will not be in alphabetic order, for the confession is arranged alphabetically, and the reason is explained in mussar books. "Openly and secretly" is under the letter gimel, and "intentional and inadvertent" is under the letter zayin.
As stated in Temurah chapter 5: one who says, this is the substitute of a burnt-offering and a peace-offering. Rabbi Meir says the first expression takes effect; Rabbi Yosi says that if from the beginning he intended to say both, since a person cannot utter two things at once, his words stand. The same applies to the substitute of a burnt-offering and a peace-offering. We rule like Rabbi Yosi. Thus we follow his intention and are not concerned about what he said first, since he could not speak in another way, for one cannot utter two things at once. The same applies here: since from the beginning he intended both, and he cannot say it in another language because it must be in alphabetic order, we have no problem with it.
The order of confession, etc. This is the language of Piskei Mahara Isserlin, siman 118: burnt-offering, sin-offering, variable sin-offering, definite guilt-offering and doubtful guilt-offering. Most of these come to atone for inadvertent sin, and so it is arranged in parashas Vayikra. Afterward he says the punishments for intentional sin, in this order: lashes of rebellion, forty lashes, death by the hand of Heaven, kareis and ariri, and the four deaths of court, the more severe last. It is more fitting to specify the lighter sin before the severe sins, for we say one who specifies his sins is brazen, end quote. See above in the Tur, siman 582, who brought the midrash's reason to ask first for the lighter matter.
One should say atonement, etc. Minhagim and Shelah in the name of the Rema. In truth, in the Minhagim it appears so. But the Shelah wrote "atonement for sins," because the term atonement means wiping away, so that no trace of the sin remains; this is appropriate for sins, which are inadvertent.
Pardon for iniquities, which are more severe because they are intentional; therefore one should say the language of pardon. Nevertheless, a trace of the sin remains. And forgiveness for transgressions, which are more severe because they are rebellious; therefore one should say the language of forgiveness, since it is not a complete pardon but only patience; see there.
