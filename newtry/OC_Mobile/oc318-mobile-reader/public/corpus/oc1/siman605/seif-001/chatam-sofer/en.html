In Taz, s.k. 4: Rami bar Tamri. Marginal note: So it appears in all the books of the Tur, but it is a scribal error, for the incident of Rami bar Tamri is in chapter Kol HaBasar, where he threw away udders. The proof that the Tur brings from it is from Chullin 95b: ravens came and threw down a liver and a kidney. The Bach was drawn after that error, and thought that they themselves threw them from their houses; therefore he wrote that it had the status of fixed, kavua. This is not so, for ravens took and threw them, and it is considered separated not in our presence, as the Magen Avraham wrote. The Taz, too, was drawn after this error, and therefore he wrote a distinction because it is by way of throwing. That applies only if people threw them, not ravens; this is easy to understand. What the Taz wrote, that where there is no definite prohibition the rule of kavua does not apply, may be questioned from what is written in the gloss in Yoreh Deah, siman 114, at the end of the seif; see there. However, what appears to me in resolving the Mordechai's difficulty is according to what Tosafos asked in Chullin 95a: why do we not follow the majority of the world, who are non-Jews? To this one may answer that we never assume that a person will not watch his own property and will allow a raven to take it. Rather, we attribute it to those who are throwing today; since Jews are throwing today, we attribute that the raven took from those throwing. This reasoning appears in Mishnah 7, chapter 2 of Machshirin. The Rabbis disagree only there, for they hold that everyone is also accustomed to throw because of spoiled fruit and the like; but here they agree that we follow the majority. Therefore we are not concerned for the majority of non-Jews, since they watch their own. See a similar rationale in Tosafos, Bava Basra 23b, s.v. Vadai; see there. This resolves the above difficulty of Tosafos in Chullin, and consequently it is proven that he permitted throwing on Erev Yom Kippur. However, it is not actually proven, and the Mordechai did not deliberate about this, but only: from where do we know that they throw at all? See inside there and you will see.
