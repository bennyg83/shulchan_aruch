And so they are accustomed in all these lands. In the Tur the verses that are recited are written, and this verse is mentioned there: a life for a life.
And they choose white ones. My teacher and father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote that there is something of the ways of the Emorites in this. Although this appears in Maharil, perhaps he did not intentionally seek one out; rather, if one happened before him, he chose it. But to seek one out and buy it at a high price is of the ways of the Emorites. So I received from my master, my father, of blessed memory. End quote. I found written that one should say: This is your exchange, this is your substitute, this is your atonement; the initials form Chatach, which is the name that apportions life to every living thing.
And he places his hands, etc. Although this matter appears in the Tur in the name of the Geonim, it is very astonishing in my eyes, for this looks like consecrating consecrated offerings and slaughtering them outside the Temple courtyard. And although a rooster is not fit for an offering, nevertheless we find a prohibition in siman 469 regarding saying of this meat that it is for Pesach, where Maharil forbade it even with a rooster. All the more so here, where he explicitly performs acts resembling an offering, there is this concern. Therefore one should prevent this matter, and passivity is preferable; so it appears to me, in my humble opinion.
And they throw their innards, etc. In the Tur it is written: There is some proof from that incident in Chullin, where Rami bar Tamri came to the West on Erev Yom Kippur, saw that they were throwing away liver and kidney, went and took them, because nowadays permitted meat is more common. At the end of Yoma, the Mordechai responded to this that there is no proof, for perhaps ravens took them from a Jew's house and not from the roofs. My teacher and father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote about this: If they took them from the house, it should be considered kavua, and anything fixed is considered like half and half, so the majority of permitted food would not help. Rather, certainly the ravens took them from the roof, and it separated from the majority. End quote. In truth, there is no issue of kavua here, for we say that anything fixed, etc., only if there is a definite prohibition there; this is unlike here, where there is no definite prohibition. As for the Mordechai's rejection, that perhaps they took them from the house, that is no rejection, for we say that he saw kidneys being thrown. Anything that is thrown away would certainly give reason to say that it is prohibited, as is stated in Yoreh Deah regarding finding an animal in the refuse dump. Nevertheless here they were permitted, since the majority is permitted. What is written about the roof appears to mean that if he throws it in the public domain, there is concern that perhaps it truly is neveilah, for that is the usual place to throw neveilos; perhaps someone will encounter a neveilah and throw it there, and perhaps a Jew will eat it, so one should not do so initially. This is unlike a roof, where it is generally not the way to throw neveilos there. So it appears to me. The reason is that it is fitting to have mercy on creatures, so that Heaven will have mercy upon him.
