But with Kohanim who are adults, etc. — See Eretz Tzvi, who wrote that Rashi meant that then it is permitted even on a fixed basis; and similarly what he wrote below. However, specifically incidentally means also specifically by himself, but with others it is permitted even on a fixed basis; and so it is implied from Eliyah Rabbah. Peri Megadim wrote that this is not necessarily required, and from the author's language, who concluded that then he may raise his hands even alone, it is implied like Eretz Tzvi; and so ruled Chayei Adam.

However, specifically incidentally, etc. — Eretz Tzvi wrote: it appears that if there is no other Kohen except this one whose beard has not yet filled in, if he is from thirteen years and one day old he raises his hands even on a fixed basis, similar to what is explained regarding the shatz in siman 53, seif 7. Eliyah Rabbah disagrees with him, for there it is different, since they were lenient so that they would not neglect hearing Kedushah, Kaddish, and Barchu. Sefer HaEshkol implies like Eretz Tzvi. A dwarf may not raise his hands alone, even if he is known in his city, lest they say a minor raises his hands — for we are concerned lest there are people here who do not recognize him and will say a minor raises his hands; but if he has a beard, his beard proves it about him. Peri Chadash in the name of the Yerushalmi.

And not on a fixed basis. — See Tosafos, Sukkah 42b, which implies that on Yom Kippur and on other fast days when they increase supplications, even incidentally is considered like a fixed practice. It is surprising that the later authorities did not bring this, and it is uncertain in practice.
