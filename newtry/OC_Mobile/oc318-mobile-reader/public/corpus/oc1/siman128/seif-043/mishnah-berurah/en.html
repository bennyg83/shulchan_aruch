157) He should leave the synagogue. Strictly speaking, a mourner is obligated to bless because he remains obligated in every Torah mitzvah. The custom is not to recite the Priestly Blessing because a kohen should be joyful and glad-hearted while blessing, as the verse says, 'One with a glad heart will bless.' He must therefore leave so he will not be called to ascend. Even on Shabbos, and even when he is the only kohen, he does not ascend. If he failed to leave and was called, however, he must ascend; whether on Shabbos or a weekday, whether alone or with other kohanim; because otherwise he violates a positive command.
158) Even for twelve months. This applies even on Shabbos and Yom Tov, for he is not considered joyful; he may not attend wedding celebrations and the like. Likewise, one who buries a relative during a festival, although mourning has not yet formally begun, may not rejoice throughout the festival. The same applies to an onen even on Yom Tov: Although obligated in all mitzvos then, he is not joyful. As stated, if called he nevertheless ascends. Some say an onen does not ascend even when called.
159) For his father or mother. The same applies throughout the thirty days for other relatives whom he must mourn. If fewer than two other kohanim are present besides the mourner, he may recite the Priestly Blessing during the twelve months for a parent or the thirty days for another relative.
