The same applies if there is no kohen there except him: he also should not raise his hands, except that in that case he need not leave the synagogue when they do not call out "kohanim." Also, the one who washes their hands should not come to him, and then he does not violate a positive commandment, as explained.
They say "Ribbon" while the words are being drawn out in melody. Some wrote that not everyone should say this "Ribbon" when the kohanim ascend the platform, but only one who dreamed a bad dream that night should say it, for even if he dreamed on other days of the year, the nullification is effective only that same day when he saw the dream, as we find regarding a fast for a dream, specifically on that day. Or he should skip the two words "I dreamed a dream," and instead say: "Master of the universe, I am Yours and my dreams are Yours; may it be Your will," etc. (Rosh, Perek Shor Shenagach Et HaParah). This is also correct in my eyes, for it helps if others saw a bad dream about him, since he nullifies it by saying "whether dreams I dreamed or others dreamed about me," as it appears to me. Otherwise, it is improper to say supplications on Shabbat and Yom Tov for no reason, for it is forbidden to arouse oneself with supplicatory matters that break a person's heart. But prayer and request also apply to matters in which one prays that the Holy One, blessed be He, should give him many gifts beyond his needs, and it is permitted to pray for this even on Shabbat and Yom Tov. This was the reason of those who arranged the order of Maamadot, who arranged more requests for good on Shabbat. Levush, composed by Mahar"r Mordechai Yaffe on Rashi's commentary to Parashat Vaetchanan, wrote: In the Zohar, Parashat Naso, when the chazzan prays the repetition of the Amidah and there is no birkat kohanim, when he says "May the Lord bless you" he should face toward the ark, "and guard you" toward his right, "May the Lord shine" toward the ark, "His face toward you and be gracious to you" toward his left, to unite it with the right. It is forbidden to wait the time needed to walk twenty-two amot between washing and the blessing; therefore one must wash close to Retzeh.
