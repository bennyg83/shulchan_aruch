And within seven. All the more so an onen whose dead lies before him, for he is forbidden in the Temple service, and the priestly blessing was a service. Such a case came before me and the halachic authorities agreed with me (Shach). Maharam Tiktin likewise ruled in a Megillah scroll, and this is his wording: In the Zohar it is found that an unmarried kohen is disqualified for service, and a kohen who serves blesses, as it is stated, "And he blessed them, and he descended from offering the sin offering." For this reason an onen should not raise his hands, for he does not serve; and for this reason there is no priestly blessing at night, as is evident in Tosafos at the beginning of chapter Tefillas HaShachar; and see Hagahos Maimoniyos, chapter 3 of Hilchos Tefillah, and the Rosh, Hilchos Yom Kippur, end quote. See what I wrote in se'if 37, that it is not compared to Temple service in all respects. Also see Rambam, chapter 3 of Hilchos Klei HaMikdash, who wrote that an onen Levi is permitted to serve and sing. Also in the Zohar, Parashas Naso, page 275, it wrote the reason that we require him to be married, because the Shechinah does not rest upon one who is not married, etc.; see there. I was also astonished at one who says so, for specifically regarding the Kohen Gadol on Yom Kippur do we require "and he shall atone for himself and for his house," but during the rest of the year we do not require him to be married, and all the more so an ordinary kohen, as stated at the beginning of Yoma. However, the Zohar, Vayikra, page 4, implies that even an ordinary kohen should be married initially. Rather, the main reason is as Rama wrote, and the same applies to an onen, who is disqualified because he is not in a state of joy. Nevertheless, regarding the time of the priestly blessing it is comparable to the service, which requires daytime.
