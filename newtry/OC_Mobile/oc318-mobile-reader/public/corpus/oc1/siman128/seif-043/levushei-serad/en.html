There, only for leniency, that grape pits are permitted. The language of the Gemara there, 26b, is that it derives that an intoxicated kohen is forbidden to recite the Priestly Blessing from the juxtaposition of the section of the Priestly Blessing to the section of a nazir: just as a nazir is forbidden wine, so too a kohen who blesses. The Gemara asks: if so, just as a nazir is forbidden grape pits, should a kohen who blesses also be forbidden them? It answers from the verse, "to serve Him and to bless in His Name": just as one who serves is permitted grape pits, so too a kohen who blesses. It then asks: just as a blemished kohen may not serve, should a blemished kohen also not bless? It answers that the kohen who blesses was compared to a nazir. Why choose the comparison for leniency rather than for stringency? It answers that these comparisons are only an asmachta, and in a rabbinic matter we rule leniently.
