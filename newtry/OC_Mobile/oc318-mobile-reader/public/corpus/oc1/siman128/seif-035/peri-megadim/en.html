One who studies Rambam, Hilchos Bi'as HaMikdash 1:1 and 1:5, sees that all the cases in halachah 1 are exempt but forbidden; only water added to complete a revi'is is more lenient and permitted. Taz holds that all those cases are exempt and permitted. Re'em on Shemini explains that drinking in portions incurs liability even within the time to eat a pras or within the time to drink a revi'is only where it constitutes the ordinary manner of intoxication. Accordingly, perhaps half the measure is permitted for Temple service if the reason half a measure is generally forbidden is that it can combine within those periods, which does not apply here. Taz says other intoxicating drinks are forbidden as in Temple service; according to Beis Yosef they are permitted. See Magen Avraham, letter 55. See Perishah regarding how an after-the-fact invalidity applies to duchaning. Tosafos on Sotah 38a explicitly indicates it. Since a kohen who was called (and according to Taz a lone kohen even without a call) violates a positive commandment, one who blessed while sitting must repeat duchaning after the prayer; the same applies to an intoxicated kohen. Taz adduces proof from this Tosafos that the comparison is complete. Tosafos on Menachos 109a says 'just as a blemished servant may not serve' regarding standing; I questioned this there. It does not appear in our Gemara but is from Sifrei, which I have not presently seen; Magen Avraham, letter 54, cites it in the name of Semag. According to Taz's full comparison, an uncircumcised kohen is apparently disqualified. One might distinguish that a blemish is congenital, whereas lack of circumcision is not: He can arise and circumcise himself. See letter 27. But then the same should apply to impurity, leaving no contemporary kohen fit for duchaning because all are impure through contact with the dead. Perhaps the verse 'to stand before Hashem to serve Him and to bless in His Name' compares only duchaning in the Temple to service: 'in His Name' can mean the explicit Name, and it says 'before Hashem.' Duchaning outside the Temple may not be compared at all.
I shall add a brief word regarding Taanis 26b. It derives an intoxicated kohen's prohibition from a nazir rather than from the comparison to Temple service, even though grape products and pits are permitted there, and Rambam indeed writes that it is compared. See Lechem Mishneh, Hilchos Tefillah 15:4. One can answer that otherwise other intoxicating drinks would also be forbidden; deriving from a nazir permits other drinks. According to Taz, however, one may say that it is indeed derived from the comparison, especially according to what I wrote in letter 9: Sotah indicates a full comparison, while the passage in Taanis follows the first Tanna. See Tosafos on Sotah 38a, who asks regarding blessing quietly. According to Ri, we learn that this undisputed baraisa holds there is no gezeirah shavah, only a comparison, and this is the halachah. Magen Avraham, letter 23, however, cites Tur's requirement of a moderate voice; then Tosafos' question does not begin, and one may rule like the first Tanna. The term 'intoxicated is forbidden' at least implies a revi'is, for less is not called intoxication; the nazir law does not teach a half measure here. Peri Chadash requires examination.
