Who drank it in several intervals, etc. The Beit Yosef writes that the comparison of Birkat Kohanim to the Temple service is merely an asmachta, and therefore the kohen is prohibited from raising his hands only in a circumstance in which, were he to enter the Temple, he would be liable. End quote. This implies that regarding the Temple service there is nevertheless a prohibition in this case. The Kesef Mishneh likewise writes in chapter 1 of Hilchot Biat HaMikdash that when the Rambam states that one who drank a revi'it in several intervals is exempt and does not invalidate the service, a prohibition nevertheless remains. This is exceedingly difficult. If so, from where did the Rambam derive that in this respect Birkat Kohanim was not compared to the service, and that the comparison is only an asmachta? A hekesh is one of the thirteen methods by which the Torah is interpreted, and there is no indication in the Gemara that this hekesh is merely an asmachta. On the contrary, Tosafot in the chapter Arvei Pesachim write that if a kohen recited Birkat Kohanim while seated he must repeat it and recite the blessing again, based on its comparison to the Temple service. Were this only an asmachta, it could not justify uttering Hashem's Name in a blessing that might be in vain. It therefore appears to me that even regarding the Temple service this case is permitted. The Kesef Mishneh likewise cites a responsum of the Rashba: Since the halacha follows Rabbi Elazar, he is neither liable to death nor even under a warning for wine unless he drank a revi'it without interruption, and the Rambam rules accordingly. End quote. Therefore it is also permitted here on the duchan. It is consequently clear that if he drank another intoxicating substance, although regarding the Temple service he is not liable to death, as the Rambam writes in chapter 1 of Hilchot Biat HaMikdash that liability to death applies specifically to wine, nevertheless for another intoxicant, such as a dried fig, he receives lashes and his service remains valid. Regarding Birkat Kohanim, however, it is certainly prohibited, since in any event the Temple service involves a Torah prohibition. All the more so, one intoxicated by beer or mead may not ascend the duchan. Since no explicit measure is given for these beverages, as there is for wine, a kohen may not drink any intoxicating substance at all before Birkat Kohanim, even though according to the Beit Yosef's explanation the comparison applies only to a substance for which one incurs liability regarding the Temple service, as stated.
