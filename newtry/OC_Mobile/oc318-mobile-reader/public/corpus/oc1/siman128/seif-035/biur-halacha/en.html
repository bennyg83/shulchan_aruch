Even unwittingly, etc. — See Magen Avraham, who sides that specifically if he died immediately; if he did not die immediately, one should not be stringent in an unwitting case, lest the spirit confused him and he died.

Even if he repented. — Behold, the author was silent here and did not bring that there are dissenters, as he brought in siman 37 regarding an apostate; it implies that he holds simply, in our case, to be stringent, because a prosecutor cannot become a defense attorney. So Eliyah Rabbah and Mateh Yehudah, in the author's view. However, Eretz Tzvi and the Gra's explanation prove that he holds the author relied on the dissenters he brought in siman 37. Regarding the law, Rama ruled to be lenient and wrote that such is the practice — that is, even intentionally — and as he brought proof and wrote that it is no worse than Avodah Zarah, where there even intentionally he raises his hands; and several later authorities copied this. However, Peri Chadash and Eliyah Rabbah [and HaDaat HaChared copied their view] hold that one should not be lenient even if he repented, if it was intentional. And so I found in Ritva, Makkos, end of chapter 2, which he brought in the name of the authors of Tosafos and in the name of his teacher Rabbeinu Asher HaGadol, to be stringent regarding murder in all its forms; and it is uncertain in law. At minimum, if he ascended, one should not remove him, as Tur wrote in the name of Rambam.
