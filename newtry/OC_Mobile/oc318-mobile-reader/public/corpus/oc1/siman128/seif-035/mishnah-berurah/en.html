127) Who killed a person. For the verse says, 'When you spread your hands, I will hide My eyes from you, etc.; your hands are full of blood.'
128) Even unintentionally. If he was coerced to kill, he may recite the Priestly Blessing. Although murder is subject to the rule 'be killed rather than transgress,' if he transgressed rather than allowing himself to be killed, that alone does not disqualify him from the Priestly Blessing. Maharanach and Peri Chadash.
129) Even if he repented. This view reasons that although nothing stands in the way of repentance, a prosecutor cannot become an advocate: Hands that killed a person are unfit to be raised in blessing even after repentance. One who pushed a pregnant woman and caused a miscarriage may recite the Priestly Blessing, because one is not liable to death for killing a fetus. Later authorities.
130) Some say that if, etc. This applies even when he acted intentionally.
131) This is the custom. Some later authorities are nevertheless stringent at least when the killing was intentional. See Biur Halachah.
