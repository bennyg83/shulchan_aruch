35) [Se'if 6] Although the kohanim washed, etc. Sotah 39a states in Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi's name: A kohen who has not washed his hands may not recite the Priestly Blessing, for it says, 'Raise your hands in holiness and bless Hashem.' Beis Yosef understands Rambam to require washing only when the hands are impure, such as when one did not wash in the morning. If he washed then and knows of no later impurity, he need not wash again. This was the Egyptian custom. Orchos Chaim likewise cites Rabbi Avraham ben Rambam that in practice before his father Rambam and other Torah teachers, every kohen standing in prayer proceeded to the blessing relying on his washing for Shema and the Amidah. Tosafos in Sotah cites a similar view in Rashi's name but says it was copied from a student's gloss; Rashi's own words, 'who did not wash before ascending,' imply washing immediately beforehand. Tosafos further proves from the Bavli and Yerushalmi that even one who washed in the morning must wash again near the blessing, with no delay exceeding the time to walk twenty-two amos. Beis Yosef cites this and says Tur agrees with Tosafos. This is Maran's ruling here, and so rule Levush; Bach; Peri Chadash, os 6; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, os 8; Chesed LaAlafim, os 3; and Ben Ish Chai, Tetzaveh, os 7. It also follows from the Zohar cited below, os 40, and is the custom here in holy Jerusalem. Only when impossible; such as a prayer leader for whom washing would interrupt the Amidah; may one rely on Rambam. See the next os.
