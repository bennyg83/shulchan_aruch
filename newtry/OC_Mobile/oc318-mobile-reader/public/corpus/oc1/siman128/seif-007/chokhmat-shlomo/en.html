(Seif 38) One who drank a revi'it of wine at once should not raise his hands, etc. NB: behold, I will copy here Rambam's language in siman 41 of Hilchot Issurei Mizbeach, halachah 16, and this is its language: "The law of one who enters thus from the altar inward, and the law of one who exits from there — they are one. How so? For example, if he drank a revi'it of wine between the Ulam and the altar, or tore his garments there and exited — he receives lashes," etc. And Raavad wrote there: "It is impossible — if according to his words there is no need that he drink wine between the Ulam and the altar; and what they said in Torat Kohanim, 'from where even for exiting?' — teaches that even if he drank outside and entered the Sanctuary, he is liable; that is, the Merciful One said: at the time of entry you shall not be drunk in the Sanctuary and at the altar; death too applies." And behold, at the beginning of his words there is no understanding at all; and Kesef Mishneh there wrote, and this is its language: "Raavad wrote: 'It is impossible — if according to his words there is no need that he drink wine between the Ulam and the altar,' etc. It appears his intent is that he has no place to obligate for exiting — that is, he brought wine inside and drank there; as if he placed wine inside, etc. And his words are very surprising in his explanation — for what difficulty is this? Is it not possible that he brought wine inside with him? And if it is non-sacred in the Temple courtyard — perhaps he transgressed and brought it in; or that he drank from wine for libations — and there is a difference: even though he misused consecrated property, nevertheless he would receive lashes because of drinking wine too." But Raavad's intent is thus: according to Rambam's view, the intent of Torat Kohanim is to inform us that he is liable even for exiting — why interpret the Tosefta as dealing with one who drank wine inside? It should have interpreted it as dealing with one who drank outside, and regarding receiving two lashings: one for entering and one for exiting. And that Raavad wrote: according to his words, in his explanation, there is no need that he drink wine inside — only if he drank outside do you find it, and regarding receiving two lashings; and this is clear in Raavad's intent; and understand well. But to settle Rambam's words from the above difficulty, it appears: since in Torat Kohanim it said "from where even for exiting? It teaches: 'You and your sons with you'" — and it is surprising, from where do we know this? And see Kesef Mishneh, who addressed this; and in my opinion one may say the intent: his proof is from what is said "You and your sons with you" — it implies "similar to you"; and as the Sages expounded in tractate Avodah Zarah regarding Noach, "with you — similar to you"; similarly here the intent is "with you — similar to you" — that is, Aharon was then inside, as is proven in Parashat Shemini, as it is said to them "and from the entrance of the Tent of Meeting you shall not go out" — it is proven they were then in the Tent of Meeting; and it hinted to him that if then they drink wine, they will be liable. If so, it is proven he is liable also for exiting; therefore one should not say the intent is to require two lashings for entering and for exiting — for this is not learned from the language "with you, similar to you," for Aharon did not then enter drunk; and what similarity is there? And perforce the intent is only that if he drinks wine inside, he is liable for exiting; and understand well. And it is possible to settle Kesef Mishneh's words too: he also interprets Raavad's intent thus; only he asked him: what was the difficulty? For one may say we hold "tafasta mu'at tafasta" — and since so, it is better to establish the extra exposition on if he drank wine inside and obligate him one, than to establish it on if he drank wine outside and obligate him two; therefore Kesef Mishneh wrote that Raavad's intent is: since as if he brought wine inside — if so, it is an uncommon matter; therefore it is more reasonable to expound on if he drank outside and obligate him two, than to expound from it if he drank inside and obligate him one, since it is an uncommon matter; and it comes out well; and understand well.
