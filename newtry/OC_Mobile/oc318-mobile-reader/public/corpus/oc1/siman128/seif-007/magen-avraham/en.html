And he recited the blessing over washing the hands. One may examine this, for he should have said briefly that one does not recite a blessing over this washing. Do we not already know that if he did not recite the blessing in the morning, he blesses over his later washing even if he does not wish to raise his hands? Perhaps his intent is specifically when his hands are clean and he did not touch a filthy place, in which case he need not bless a second time; but if he touched a filthy place, he must bless, for the Tur and Rashi wrote that the blessing must be immediately adjacent to the washing. Although elsewhere Rashi wrote the opposite, Tosafos already wrote that a mistaken student wrote it, unlike what the Bach wrote, that the Beis Yosef wrote so; see there. Even in the Rambam there is no implication against this. On the contrary, the simple reading of his language implies that washing of the hands is required even when he washed in the morning, only that in some places they were accustomed not to wash a second time, as the Beis Yosef wrote, for if a kohen in his service sanctified his hands in the morning, it suffices for the whole day. But if he touched a filthy place, according to everyone he must bless, for this washing is written in the Gemara as obligatory. Yet what can I do, since they were not accustomed this way? Therefore every God-fearing kohen should guard his hands from the time of his morning washing not to touch a soiled place, as appears to me. It is forbidden to delay the walking distance of twenty-two amos between the washing and the blessing (Tosafos); therefore one must wash close to Retzeh, and the chazzan should not prolong Retzeh.
