And he blesses "on washing the hands." One may question: it should have said briefly that we do not bless on this washing, for is it not obvious that if he did not bless in the morning he blesses on his washing afterward even if he does not wish to raise his hands; and it is possible his intent is specifically when his hands are clean and he did not touch an unclean place — he need not bless again; but if he touched an unclean place he must bless, for Tur and Rashi wrote one must bless immediately upon washing; and although elsewhere Rashi wrote the opposite, Tosafot already wrote a student erred wrote not as Bach said the Rabbi wrote — see there; and even in Rambam there is no implication against this, rather his plain language implies he requires washing the hands even when he washed in the morning, only it is customary in some places not to wash again — as the Rabbi wrote, for a kohen in his avodah if he sanctified his hands in the morning it suffices all day; but if he touched an unclean place he must bless per all — for this washing is written in the Gemara as obligatory; but what can I do that they did not practice thus — therefore every God-fearing kohen should guard his hands from the time of his morning washing not to touch an unclean place as above. And there is a custom to delay the time of walking twenty-two cubits between washing and blessing [Tosafot]; therefore he must wash close to Retzeh and the prayer leader should not extend Retzeh.
