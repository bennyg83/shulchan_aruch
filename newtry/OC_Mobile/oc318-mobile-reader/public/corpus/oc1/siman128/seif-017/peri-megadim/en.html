This is inferred from the Mishnah's failure to say that when other kohanim are present a kohen may not serve as chazzan. Maharam of Rothenburg's proof is now sound: The Mishnah says, 'If there is no kohen other than him.' One cannot answer that when another kohen is present it is forbidden for him to be chazzan because the prompter must be an Israelite. Rather, the rule is as explained. Taz's essential point is that the prompter must specifically be an Israelite, not a kohen. If no Israelite knows how to prompt, the kohen does not do so; in any event, prompting is not indispensable. This differs from Levush, se'if 21, who says that a kohen prompts. See what I write in the following letter and in letter 22.
