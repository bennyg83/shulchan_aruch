They endeavor, etc. This is from the words of the Rambam, chapter 14 of Hilchos Tefillah, and he gave the reason: it says ‘say to them,’ implying that the prompter is not from among them. The Kesef Mishneh wrote there that the Yerushalmi says, as Tosafot cite, that Rabbi Chisda said the chazzan must be an Israelite, meaning that one should endeavor after this, but it is not indispensable. I could not understand his words. If he means that when there is no Israelite to prompt, the shaliach tzibbur who is a kohen may prompt, as the Levush wrote, this does not explain the proof from the Mishnah that if there is no kohen there except the chazzan he should not perform Nesias Kapayim, for there too, even though the shaliach tzibbur is a kohen, another is the prompter, as written here: he should appoint an Israelite beside him. It is also difficult that the Levush wrote that this derivation is only an asmachta, for it is not in our Gemara or in the Yerushalmi; the Rambam wrote it, and the Rambam does not write a derivation on his own even as an asmachta. Rather, this is not a derashah at all, but the plain meaning of the verse: the prompter should be from the congregation being blessed, whenever an Israelite can be found. But if no Israelite can prompt, they perform Nesias Kapayim without any prompting, like a city that is entirely kohanim in seif 25, where all ascend to the duchan and there is certainly no prompting. Therefore the Rambam wrote ‘they endeavor’ that the prompter be an Israelite, meaning to fulfill the preferred mitzvah that there be prompting. This is proper and correct.
