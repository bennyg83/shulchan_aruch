One of the things. This is difficult, for it implies that if one of these things is present he does not violate, yet there is even a prohibition, as below in this siman. It seems to me that below we find that even where he has blemishes, if he is familiar in his city he performs Nesias Kapayim; regarding this it says that nevertheless, if he does not perform Nesias Kapayim, he does not violate. So too is implied by the language of the Gemara at the end of HaKorei Es HaMegillah Omed: if he is familiar in his city, it is permitted, but it did not say he is obligated. Therefore here it says that only where there is no matter preventing at all does he violate. Consequently, one who is familiar in his city need not leave the synagogue when they call ‘Kohanim.’ In practice, however, one should be stringent that he leave, for the Yerushalmi cited by Beis Yosef says that Rabbi Shimon ben Pazi and Rabbi Elazar, when their heads were weak, would leave the synagogue when they called ‘Kohanim.’ The Tur wrote. The Tur wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Peretz that if there is only one kohen there, he does not violate, for we derive from ‘say to them’ that it means specifically to many. This does not appear correct, for ‘to them’ refers to Aharon and his sons, meaning ‘say to each of them.’ Beis Yosef in the name of Mahari Abuhav asked from the Gemara that for two one calls ‘Kohanim,’ but for one he does not call, because it says ‘say to them,’ to two. He answered that although the Gemara derives this, it does not leave its plain sense that ‘to them’ refers to Aharon and his sons, and means each one. In my humble opinion according to the Tur, there are three teachings from this verse. The first is its plain meaning, that ‘say to them’ means to each of them, that they are obligated to bless Israel whether as an individual or many. There are also two additional derivations: one, that the kohanim are obligated only after the shaliach tzibbur calls them ‘Kohanim,’ and this calling applies only when there are two, but an individual is not dependent on the call and must go on his own. The second is that the shaliach tzibbur reads each word of Birkas Kohanim to them word by word. If so, ‘to them’ excludes one only regarding violating the positive commandment, which depends specifically on two; but regarding suspicion that people will say he is the son of a divorcee, the concern remains even if he was not there at the call, since the other kohanim bless and he is not with them.
And the Levi should pour water. My teacher and father-in-law wrote in the name of an old machzor in the name of Mahari Molin that if there is no Levi there, if there is a firstborn who opened the womb, he should give the water for washing hands for the duchan.
