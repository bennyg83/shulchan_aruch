(Seif 6) Even though the Kohanim washed their hands in the morning, they return and wash their hands up to the joint, etc. NB: I was asked whether netilat yadayim for raising the hands requires a vessel, as netilat yadayim for eating requires, or not; and I answered: apparently it would seem it requires a vessel, as netilat yadayim for a meal requires, for behold it is comparable to sanctifying the hands for service; and behold, this requires a vessel from the laver; and so it appeared from Rambam's language cited in Beit Yosef, siman 201, who wrote: How? A Kohen who did not wash his hands should not raise his hands, but rather washes his hands up to the joint, in the manner they sanctify for service, etc. If so, since he writes "in the manner they sanctify for service," it implies it is like the law of netilat yadayim for service and requires a vessel. However, accordingly it is difficult: how did Beit Yosef write afterward that if he washed his hands in the morning, he need not wash again? Behold, in the morning he does not require a vessel, and now he requires a vessel; and one may not say we deal with one who washed in the morning with a vessel, for if so, why did Beit Yosef write first "and this is its language," and it implies when his hands are impure, such as if he did not wash his hands in the morning, that he says he must wash, etc. — behold, you find it even if he washed his hands in the morning, only not with a vessel, that he must wash; and so he should have said: granted, if he washed his hands in the morning according to law from a vessel, he says: behold, he washed his hands in the morning according to law from a vessel — he need not wash again. And behold, since he did not write thus, it is proven that he holds it does not require a vessel. But if so, it is surprising what Beit Yosef wrote, "and this is its language: and you should know that for service too one need not sanctify for all the service, but once he sanctifies in the morning and serves all day and all night" — and what proof is that? There he sanctifies according to law from the laver in the morning; therefore it is not a greater proof than here, where in the morning he does not require a vessel, and for netilat yadayim one may say it is comparable to service and requires a vessel. And it is also difficult from what Tosafos challenged those who hold morning washing suffices: if so, derive it because of a negative commandment that requires netilat yadayim; and Beit Yosef answered that there is a difference for a negative commandment, and it suffices for all matters of cleanliness, unlike for raising the hands, where we require water; see there. And why did he not answer that for a negative commandment we do not require a vessel, and for raising the hands we require a vessel? And it is proven that here too we require a vessel. However, one may say this depends on this: for those who hold morning washing suffices, they hold it does not require a vessel; but for those who hold morning washing does not suffice, they hold it requires a vessel; and this depends on this, and depends on the dispute of the poskim; and it is uncertain now in this for law. And the matter is surprising on the early and later poskim, who did not comment on this at all. And behold, the essence of the view of those who hold morning netilat yadayim suffices — there is no proof from this to say that the Sages enacted thus: if his hands are presumed pure from morning netilat yadayim, since then he did not require a vessel — if so, then at that time they were presumed pure; they did not enact netilat yadayim for raising the hands unless he did not wash his hands and they are impure — then they enacted netilat yadayim for raising the hands, and then we require comparable to service, to be with a vessel, since washing is required, it requires like the law of service. And similarly we find in Chagigah: for terumah intention is required, and for non-sacred food intention is not required; and if he immersed for non-sacred food and was presumed for non-sacred food, it helps even for tithe — so one may say here. And one may say: according to all, we require a vessel in any case; Beit Yosef's words are uncertain in this, as explained above; and understand well.
