20) [Se'if 3] If he ascended once that day, etc. If he ascends again, such as during Mussaf or Neilah, even before the same congregation, that same kohen recites 'Who sanctified us, etc.' Maharam Mintz, siman 12, says this is the custom; Magen Avraham, se'if katan 3, and Maamar Mordechai, os 6, cite him. Levush compares the mitzvah to lulav, which is not taken with another blessing after one has fulfilled it once, rather than tefillin, whose blessing is recited each time. Maamar Mordechai says the opposite is more logical: It resembles tzitzis and tefillin, and he blesses every time he ascends, even in the same synagogue, contrary to Levush's concluding doubt. Eliyah Rabbah, os 5, says Levush overlooked Maharam Mintz's explicit ruling and testimony to the custom. Birkei Yosef, Shiyurei Berachah, os 3, agrees and says this is the custom; so too Zechor LeAvraham, os 50, and Shulchan Aruch HaRav, os 4. Universal practice is for kohanim to bless again that day, whether before the same congregation during Mussaf, a fast-day Minchah, or Neilah, or before another congregation; for example, after blessing here he enters another synagogue that has not yet reached the Priestly Blessing. Se'if 28 below says he ascends and recites 'Who sanctified us, etc.,' and no one objects. See below, os 163. See also our words above, siman 17, os 2, that the rule of doubt concerning blessings does not override an established custom.
21) Zera Avraham, Orach Chaim, siman 12, writes that a Jerusalem kohen may recite the Priestly Blessing during Mussaf on the diaspora's second festival day for visitors from abroad who have no kohen. Although for him it is not a time of the Temple service, and their service is nothing to him because the day is entirely weekday for him, the law permits him to bless Israel whenever he wishes; he may therefore bless them just as one may bless several congregations. Rabbi Moshe ben Chaviv, cited at length in Ginas Veradim 1:13, agrees. He concludes that if they have a kohen from abroad, Jerusalem kohanim should not ascend with him, but if they have none, a Jerusalem kohen should ascend. Rabbi Aharon HaLevi, however, writes at length in siman 14 that no decisive proof resolves the doubt, so one should not risk a doubtful blessing. To accommodate the leading sages of Eretz Yisrael who permit it, they should recite the Priestly Blessing but omit 'Who sanctified us.' Shaliach Tzibbur, page 146b-c, and Ikarei HaDat, siman 5, os 7, cite this. See also the glossator in Shaliach Tzibbur, pages 235d-236a: If he can avoid the situation, that is best. Otherwise, if other kohanim are present he ascends with them and says 'Who sanctified us' without the Divine Name or Kingship; if he is alone, he ascends and blesses normally, following Rabbi Moshe ben Chaviv and Mahari. In my view, a Jerusalem kohen who travels abroad intending to return and joins Mussaf on the second festival day, or prays in Jerusalem with diaspora visitors, may ascend with diaspora kohanim and recite 'Who sanctified us' without the Divine Name or Kingship. With others present, his blessing is inconspicuous. If no other kohen is present, he should specifically avoid it and leave before Retzeh to escape the doubt.
