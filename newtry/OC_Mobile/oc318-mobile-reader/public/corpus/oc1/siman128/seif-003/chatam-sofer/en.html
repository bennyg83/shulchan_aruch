If he does not want — I do not enter the synagogue, unlike a firstborn, for he must offer it; see there. For me too it is difficult: otherwise, who mentions his name? Behold, in the verse no time is mentioned; rather, the Merciful One made it depend on service in the Temple, as it is written, "and he shall come down from doing the sin-offering," as is found at the beginning of chapter 2 of Megillah; and in the borders, prayer is in place of service; and when there are two passages in one day, such as Shacharit and Musaf — and similarly two prayers, both of them from the Torah. But at Minchah the Sages canceled it because of drunkenness, as explained at the beginning of the last chapter of Taanis. And if they raised their hands at Shacharit, again at Musaf, even in that same congregation, if they said "ascend to the duchan," he violates a positive commandment. However, in that prayer itself, such as at Shacharit, if another congregation happens to be available for him — even though if he wants he ascends and blesses — but if he does not want, he does not violate. And in this Tosafos deals; and on an ordinary day when there is only one prayer, then he does not violate, even in another congregation, for behold, in the Temple we do not find its counterpart, that one would ascend twice for the morning tamid; and similarly in prayer he does not violate, at least. But regarding two plain prayers that he is obligated to ascend — so it seems to me; and see responsa Chatam Sofer, Orach Chayyim, siman 22; and see responsa Ketav Sofer, Orach Chayyim, siman 12 [in responsa Ketav Sofer, siman 12, he brought the law that there is no concern of a blessing in vain for a Kohen who already raised his hands in that prayer in another congregation, unless his grandfather, our master Chatam Sofer, had already ruled in responsum 22 that he should not raise his hands, so as not to insert himself into a doubt of blessing; and he concludes there in responsa Ketav Sofer regarding the rabbi who asks, who prays at home on Yom Tov Shacharit and Musaf and does not want to delay the mitzvah of raising the hands until he goes to the large synagogue in the city — and there too it is difficult for him to separate himself from the congregation so as not to ascend to the duchan. The advised counsel: when he ascends a second time in the synagogue to the duchan, he should listen and intend to one of the Kohanim who blesses Birkat Kohanim, and he is discharged through his blessing. And how good it would be for him to say to one standing near him that he should intend to discharge him and make him hear the sound of his blessing; and in this there is no concern of a blessing in vain, nor of a negative commandment, for a negative commandment applies only to one who discharges with the mouth — so it seemed to me straightforward. And again I found in Tzelach, Berachos, folio 20, that he is uncertain about this; and in another responsum I expanded on this. And if it is impossible to intend and hear the blessing from another, there is another counsel to fulfill Chatam Sofer's requirement: he should meditate on the blessing in his heart, as we said earlier, that there is no concern of a negative commandment in meditation; or he should say the blessing with his mouth, and "there" and "kingdom" he should meditate in his heart; see there].
