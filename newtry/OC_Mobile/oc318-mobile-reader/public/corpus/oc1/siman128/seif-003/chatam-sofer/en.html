(Ibid.) Regarding the Mechaber, seif 3. Note: This ruling is taken from Hagahos Maimoniyos and Tosafos in the third chapter of Rosh Hashanah. The Peri Megadim wonders how one can infer that if the kohen does not wish to do so, meaning that he does not enter the synagogue, he is not required, unlike a firstborn animal, which he must necessarily offer. See there. I too find this difficult for another reason. Who mentioned “a day” at all? Scripture specifies no particular time. Rather, the Torah made the obligation dependent upon the service of the Beis HaMikdash, as it is written, “He descended from performing the sin offering,” as stated at the beginning of the second chapter of Megillah. Outside the Beis HaMikdash, the obligation depends upon prayer, which stands in place of the Temple service. When there are two services in one day, such as Shacharis and Musaf, and likewise two prayers, both obligations are D’Oraisa. With respect to Minchah, however, our Sages suspended Bircas Kohanim because of concern for intoxication, as explained at the beginning of the final chapter of Taanis. If the kohanim recited Bircas Kohanim at Shacharis, then at Musaf, even in the same congregation, if they are called to ascend to the duchan they violate a positive commandment by refusing. Within the same prayer itself, however, such as Shacharis, if a kohen happens upon another congregation, he may ascend and bless them if he wishes, but if he does not wish to do so he does not violate the commandment. Tosafos is discussing that case. On an ordinary day, when there is only one prayer at which Bircas Kohanim is recited, he does not violate the commandment by declining even in a different congregation, for we do not find an equivalent case in the Beis HaMikdash in which a kohen would ascend twice for the morning tamid. The same therefore applies to prayer, and in any event he does not violate the commandment. Where there are two prayers, however, it is obvious that he is obligated to ascend, as explained above, in my humble opinion. See Responsa Chasam Sofer, Orach Chayim, siman 22, and Responsa Ksav Sofer, Orach Chayim, siman 12. In Responsa Ksav Sofer, siman 12, he concludes as a matter of practical halachah that there is no concern of an unnecessary blessing when a kohen who already recited Bircas Kohanim during that prayer does so again in another congregation. Nevertheless, since his revered father, the Chasam Sofer, had already ruled in responsum 22 that he should not recite Bircas Kohanim so as not to enter into a possible doubt concerning an unnecessary blessing, the Ksav Sofer concludes regarding the questioner, who prayed Shacharis and Musaf at home on Yom Tov and did not wish to delay the mitzvah of Bircas Kohanim until he reached the city’s large synagogue, while at the synagogue he also found it difficult to separate himself from the congregation by not ascending to the duchan, that the proper solution is as follows: When he ascends a second time to the duchan in the synagogue, he should listen to one of the kohanim reciting the blessing before Bircas Kohanim and intend to discharge his obligation through that kohen’s blessing. It is even better that he tell a kohen standing near him to intend to discharge his obligation and to recite the blessing audibly for him. There is then neither an unnecessary blessing nor a violation of “You shall not take Hashem’s Name in vain,” since that prohibition applies only when one utters the Name with his mouth. This appeared obvious to me. I later found that the Tzelach, Berachos 20b, is uncertain about this, and I discussed the matter at length in another responsum. If he cannot concentrate upon and hear the blessing from another kohen, there is another means of satisfying the Chasam Sofer’s concern: He may think the blessing in his heart, as we established above that thought does not violate “You shall not take Hashem’s Name in vain.” Alternatively, he may recite the words of the blessing aloud while thinking Hashem’s Name and His kingship in his heart.
