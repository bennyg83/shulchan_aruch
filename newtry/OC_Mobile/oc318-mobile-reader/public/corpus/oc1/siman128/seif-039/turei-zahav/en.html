If he did not forgive it — in the Gemara Gittin 20a: an incident with a kohen who poured water for Rabbenu Tam; a student questioned: did we not learn in Yerushalmi that one who makes use of the kehunah — behold he has misused consecrated property? And Rabbenu Tam answered: there is no sanctity at this time, for we hold their garments have sanctity upon them — if not, no. And he questioned: if so, all types of sanctity should not apply to them? And Rabbenu Tam was silent. And R' Peter answered: although there is sanctity in it, he can forgive, as said in the first chapter of Kiddushin — a kohen is not pierced because he became blemished, which implies that without a blemish one can be enslaved to him, end quote. And it is difficult to me: behold we hold "and you shall sanctify him" against his will — if he married a divorcée we force him to divorce, as in HaShas Rabbah — if he did not want, "his face" means they afflict him with suffering; and why not say he waives his sanctity? And it appears to me the reason he can forgive is because he has benefit from it, and the Torah did not forbid him something that pleases him — except what the Torah forbade explicitly upon him; but other matters — no. An example: one may let his ox pull weeds on Shabbat, even though a person is commanded regarding resting his animal; nevertheless, since it is written "so that your ox may rest," and it does not rest if one does not let it pull weeds — so too here. And that kohen who poured water for Rabbenu Tam — surely it pleased him that he merited to serve in holiness, and that is his sanctity. And it appears that Rabbenu Tam's silence was not because he did not know how to answer, but because he did not wish to take the name of Torah scholar upon himself and say that for this reason it is permitted — as we find this reasoning of Rabbenu Tam in chapter Ein Ma'aminim, daf 67: Ravina ate at a mourner's meal; Tosafot wrote there, and Rabbenu Tam — Ravina did not wish to take the name and make himself a Torah scholar — so too here it is correct. And therefore, whenever the kohen has no benefit from work he does for others, it appears forbidden to make use of him, and it is like misusing consecrated property even if he forgives.
