They begin. See Taz. His view above in letter 3 is that these derivations are full derashos, and he interprets the reason of the verse as being because of error; therefore even for a single kohen the prompter reads. At the word ‘Yevarechecha’ there is no error; see what I wrote in Magen Avraham about this. The Levush’s view is that these are only asmachtos, and so it appears from Tosafot Menachos 44a, s.v. kol; see there. For one kohen, according to all opinions the prompter reads ‘Yevarechecha,’ as Magen Avraham wrote, letter 20. Regarding whether the chazzan says ‘Kohanim’ aloud for one kohen, we wrote in Magen Avraham. One may also say that according to the Taz’s conclusion there is no prohibition, while according to the initial assumption there is a prohibition, for one kohen does not necessarily transgress, since for one we do not call. In truth, ‘for one we do not call’ means he does not need it, for automatically there is a positive commandment; whereas for two, he calls them, for there is no positive commandment except when he calls them. This is what he wrote in letter 3, that it excludes one, who does not need this beforehand. Understand.
