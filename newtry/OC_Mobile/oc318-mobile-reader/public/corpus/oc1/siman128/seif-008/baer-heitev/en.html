He shall ascend. This does not mean specifically at the beginning of the chazzan's Retzei; he must uproot his feet before the chazzan finishes Retzei. Peri Chadash. Maharikash seeks to prove that a kohen who arrives at the synagogue during Modim may recite Birchas Kohanim even though he was not present for Retzei: The wording 'every kohen in the synagogue uproots himself' implies that one outside it need not uproot his feet during Avodah and may ascend without hearing Retzei. Radbaz, cited by Knesses HaGedolah, disagrees: His walking from home to the synagogue is not an uprooting for the duchan, so he may not ascend. Ginas Veradim, klal 1, simanim 13 and 22, and Peri Chadash agree with Radbaz. Knesses HaGedolah writes that if his home is near the synagogue and he left it before Retzei intending to ascend the duchan, that counts as uprooting. Peri Chadash says this is obvious, for everything depends upon uprooting his feet with intent to ascend. A kohen who did not intend to ascend and therefore did not uproot his feet during Retzei, but remembered at Modim, may not ascend; if he ascended, he must descend. Ginas Veradim, siman 22. If an unavoidable circumstance prevented him from uprooting his feet during Avodah, he may not ascend, but if he did, he need not descend. Knesses HaGedolah in the name of Radbaz. The same applies in the first case of one who walked from home to the synagogue. A kohen praying the Amidah interrupts and ascends if no other kohen is present, but does not interrupt if others are present. Radbaz, part 4, siman 293; see Devar Shmuel, siman 67. If he was told to ascend, he must interrupt in every case. Magen Avraham, se'if katan 40. One might ask: If everything depends on uprooting during Avodah, why do se'if 4 and Rama in se'if 44 tell kohanim who do not wish to ascend to remain outside while the chazzan calls Kohanim? Even if he sat inside, he would not violate the command if he did not uproot himself during Avodah. The answer is that he may indeed sit inside and does not violate it if he neither uprooted his feet during Retzei nor was told to ascend. The Yerushalmi relates that Rabbi Shimon ben Pazi stood behind a pillar and did not go outside; Beis Yosef. This is also proved by se'if 20 concerning a kohen chazzan who does not raise his hands. Shulchan Aruch and Rama advise leaving lest a congregant tell him before Retzei to ascend, which would obligate him. He must therefore leave before Retzei. Nor should he reenter after Modim, because Maharikash and Radbaz dispute whether he should ascend then, and even Radbaz says that if he ascended he need not descend. They therefore give the uncontested rule that he remain outside while the chazzan calls Kohanim. See Magen Avraham se'ifim ketanim 4, 30, and 69; Responsa Zera Avraham of Rabbi Avraham Yitzchak, siman 14; and Ginas Veradim, klal 1, siman 13.
