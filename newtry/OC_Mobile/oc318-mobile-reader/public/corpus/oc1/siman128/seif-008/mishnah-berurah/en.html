25) When the prayer leader begins, etc. Ideally, he should be careful to move as the prayer leader begins Retzeh. If he did not move until after Retzeh began, however, it is still valid as long as the prayer leader has not finished that blessing. Eliyah Rabbah and other later authorities.
26) Retzeh; every kohen, etc. The verse says, 'Aharon raised his hands toward the people and blessed them,' and afterward, 'He descended from performing the sin-offering, etc.' This implies that he blessed before withdrawing from the service. The Sages therefore instituted that in prayer as well, the kohanim begin ascending before the conclusion of the blessing of Avodah.
(27) Until the prayer leader finishes Retzeh — not precise, for even if the Name does not reach until the prayer leader finishes Modim blessing we have no concern — all that his feet are uprooted at Retzeh; and since the beginning mentions Retzeh [latter authorities]. Further they wrote the main uprooting is calling after washing — then it is fit to ascend the duchan; therefore they uproot feet to wash hands before Retzeh and at Retzeh uproot feet to go to the place prepared for the duchan; and b'dieved if he did not wash before Retzeh and if he goes to wash hands the prayer leader will finish Retzeh blessing — nevertheless he uproots at Retzeh to go to the duchan place and afterward washes there [meaning they bring him water there near the duchan place].
28) But if he did not move his feet during Retzeh. Ateres Zekeinim inclines that even moving outside to wash does not yet count, although washing prepares for the Priestly Blessing, because the required movement must be toward the platform, while his movement was outward. Therefore, when the prayer leader reaches Retzeh and the kohanim have not yet entered the sanctuary, the custom is for him to pause until they arrive, lest they have not yet moved from the washing place toward the platform. The poskim write that if a kohen left home for the synagogue and arrived after Retzeh, the fact that he certainly left home before Retzeh does not count, because he did not leave specifically to ascend the platform. If he did leave home for the synagogue specifically to ascend, that movement is effective and he may ascend. Some write that even then he may ascend only if his home was close to the synagogue and he heard the prayer leader begin Retzeh.
29) He may no longer ascend. This applies even when his failure to move was due to circumstances beyond his control.
