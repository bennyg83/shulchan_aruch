7) Who has none of the matters, etc. Many conditions that prevent a kohen from ascending are explained below in this siman. According to several poskim, some disqualify by Torah law, while others are rabbinic according to all views. See below, se'if katan 12.
8) Like one who violates three. These are: 'So shall you bless'; 'Say to them'; and 'They shall place My Name, etc.' Nevertheless, there is essentially only one positive command, 'So shall you bless,' which is phrased as a command to bless. The poskim write that the Priestly Blessing does not override the prohibition against a kohen contracting impurity, because impurity entails both a positive and a negative command; 'He shall not become impure for a person'; 'They shall be holy.' Therefore, even if someone suddenly dies after the prayer leader has already called 'Kohanim,' so the positive mitzvah of the Priestly Blessing has taken effect upon them, they must immediately leave. If a kohen does not know of the death, he must be informed. If, however, the impurity is in a house adjoining the synagogue and the synagogue's openings and windows, or those of the house containing the corpse, can be closed, then even after learning of it he need not leave until completing the Priestly Blessing. The Priestly Blessing is a Torah command, whereas impurity transmitted in this manner is only rabbinic. If the kohen learns of it before washing his hands to ascend, however, it is better that he leave immediately.
9) When 'Kohanim' was called. Without that call he does not ascend, for the verse says, 'Say to them,' which Targum renders, 'when they say to them.' This is the call 'Kohanim' that we recite. See below, se'if katan 10. Magen Avraham writes that this applies only if he moved his feet during Avodah. If he did not, he may not ascend, as stated below in se'if 8; even if the cantor called 'Kohanim' or explicitly told him to ascend, he does not violate the positive command by remaining below. Eliyah Rabbah cites Responsa Maharam Mintz, which doubts this and perhaps obligates him to ascend. He should therefore ensure that he is not then in the synagogue, so he does not enter this situation. Independently, he should leave so people do not think he is disqualified, for who knows that he did not move his feet slightly during Avodah?
10) Or if they told him. This too refers to one present in the synagogue, not one spoken to outside. The Mechaber teaches that there is no legal difference between the cantor's general call 'Kohanim' to all the kohanim and an individual instruction to ascend, or the water-pourer's instruction to wash his hands, which likewise alludes to the Priestly Blessing. All fall under 'Say to them,' and he violates a positive command if he does not ascend. Under Magen Avraham's view above, this too concerns one who moved his feet during Avodah. See Biur Halachah, where we further establish that the Mechaber also includes one told in the synagogue before the conclusion of Retzeh, when he could still have moved his feet, but refused; according to all views, he violates a positive command. See our further discussion there.
