Even. See Taz. The word 'other' is difficult, since the subject is a non-kohen. Know that se'if 42 says a chalal may not raise his hands, based on Rambam, Hilchos Tefillah 15:5. I had thought this was because duchaning is compared to Temple service and a chalal is unfit for service, but that he violates no positive prohibition, since the verse says 'Aharon and his sons,' and Rashi on Emor explains that 'the sons of Aharon' can include chalalim. Nor could one exclude them from 'Thus shall you bless'; you and not chalalim. If so, failure to bless would not be a violation. Rama therefore writes 'even with other kohanim,' alluding to a chalal whose father is a kohen: Even he may not duchan and must descend, because a positive prohibition applies. Since Scripture specifically permits a kohen to become impure for a dead relative, we learn that elsewhere 'sons of Aharon' excludes a chalal. Peri Chadash in se'if 42 likewise says he violates a positive commandment; this appears correct. A tumtum or androgynos may not ascend even without reciting the blessing together with kohanim. Although there is a double doubt (perhaps the person is male, and perhaps a non-kohen has no positive prohibition) it appears that a non-kohen does violate one. Regarding Shabbos 118b, Tosafos in the passage beginning 'If', Bach explains that Rabbi Yose would ascend and say nothing. Even without raising the hands, any non-kohen who recites the blessing violates the prohibition; thus there is no double doubt. Taz explains that the positive prohibition certainly exists, based on Kesubos 24b; Tosafos merely asked which positive commandment it is and rejected Rashi's explanation. Rabbi Yose's statement is explained like Bach and Magen Avraham, letter 1: He ascended but said nothing. Certainly even passive inaction may not be done to obey one's father ('Do not return it') all the more merely to follow a colleague's wishes. Many ask: Since he was not a kohen, how could his colleague tell him to ascend? The answer is that the colleague told him he was a kohen. The novelty is that although his other colleagues knew he was not and would accuse him of a non-kohen blessing, he followed that colleague. This parallels Beis Shammai and Beis Hillel according to the view that they did not act according to their own rulings in deference to the other school. They did not permit a yevamah to marry outside without levirate release; rather, they performed chalitzah even though their colleagues might criticize them, since according to their own view it was a mitzvah to perform yibbum with the co-wife of a forbidden relative and yibbum was preferable to chalitzah. They nevertheless set aside their view for Beis Hillel. See Eliyah Rabbah, letter 3. According to what I wrote, this is well explained.
