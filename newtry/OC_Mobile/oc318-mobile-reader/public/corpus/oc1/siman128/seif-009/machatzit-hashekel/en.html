And he recited a blessing, etc. Had the text merely said that no blessing is recited over this washing, one might understand that this washing never requires a blessing. Since it says that this applies if the kohen washed in the morning, the reason is that he already washed and blessed. If he subsequently touched something filthy, the morning washing was invalidated and he must bless again. Bach's assertion that this wording in Beis Yosef is a student's error is untenable, for Tosafos wrote it.
Rambam's plain wording implies that one may not rely upon the morning washing, despite the testimony concerning his father. If he touched something filthy, even Temple service requires a new sanctification. Olas Tamid asks that the morning washing reaches only the knuckles, whereas washing for duchaning reaches the wrist. One may answer that our custom is to wash to the wrist in the morning as well. See Beis Yosef.
