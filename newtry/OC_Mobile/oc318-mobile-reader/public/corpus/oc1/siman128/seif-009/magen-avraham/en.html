When they uproot themselves. Rashi, Ran, and Tosafos wrote that he does not say "May it be Your will" until he reaches the duchan, while the Tur and Rambam wrote the language of the Gemara (Beis Yosef). It appears to me that this was specifically in their days, when they uprooted themselves at Retzeh, said Modim while walking, and afterward said "May it be Your will." But nowadays, when they stand on the duchan when Retzeh begins, it is obvious that he must first say Modim with the chazzan and only afterward say "May it be Your will," so that he finishes it together with the chazzan, as written above.
Until Amen is completed. This is not precise; rather, until the chazzan finishes "and to You it is fitting to give thanks," so that the congregation will answer Amen to both.
