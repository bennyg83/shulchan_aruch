In the Holy Tongue. See Taz. In my humble opinion, our master intended something profound. Sotah derives three matters (Hebrew, standing, and raised hands) according to one Tanna from a gezeirah shavah; face-to-face and a loud voice from the verse's implication; and the explicit Name from 'they shall place My Name.' See Kesef Mishneh, Hilchos Tefillah 14:11, who says all are derived from the gezeirah shavah. I do not understand, for only three are. See Lechem Mishneh. Perhaps a loud voice and face-to-face also derive from 'Thus shall you bless.' Deuteronomy 27:14 says, 'The Levi'im shall answer in a loud voice'; the word 'to bless' also refers to the Levi'im, and Rashi there explains that they turned their faces. See Re'em. Thus all except the explicit Name derive from the gezeirah shavah. Taz, however, chose separate derivations. For 'Thus shall you bless,' he rules like Rabbi Yehudah in Sukkah that 'thus' logically makes the requirement indispensable. For standing, he follows Rabbi Nasan, who compares blessing to service from 'to stand before Hashem to serve Him and to bless in His Name.' For raised hands, he uses 'Aharon raised his hands and blessed them,' apparently following the first Tanna. Rabbi Nasan's comparison is clearly full, since service and blessing appear in one verse. Mishneh LaMelech, Hilchos Tefillah 14:9, asks against Rashi, who explains that Aharon's blessing there was Yevarechecha, Ya'er, and Yisa, although Sotah derives it by comparison and implies that the earlier event was not Birchas Kohanim. The passage offers several answers. One may add that Aharon raised his hands voluntarily, but the act was not yet commanded or indispensable; the gezeirah shavah teaches that. Rabbi Nasan derives that Aharon and his sons serve for all time by comparing his sons to him. This supports Rashi's Torah commentary: If Aharon's blessing was different, it would be irrelevant to compare his sons to him, since that blessing does not apply for generations. The verse about Aharon and his sons mentions only service, but blessing is compared to service by Deuteronomy 10:8, where Birchas Kohanim is the blessing in question. See Mishneh LaMelech. Shulchan Aruch omits face-to-face. Peri Chadash struggles with this and answers that he relied on se'if 23, but why not include it here? Perhaps these four requirements are indispensable and one has not fulfilled the mitzvah after the fact. A loud voice may derive from the gezeirah shavah or from 'Say to them,' meaning the kohanim speak to Israel; obviously the congregation must hear, and whispering accomplishes nothing. Face-to-face, by contrast, is only a Scriptural support and is not indispensable. Taz's choice to derive Hebrew from 'thus' like Rabbi Yehudah rather than the first Tanna of Sotah 32a, who appears to hold that 'thus' is not indispensable, requires study elsewhere. See Tosafos on Sotah 33a and 38a.
After all this effort, we can defend our master regarding letters 27 and 35, where he treats the comparison as full, although Taanis 27a calls it a Scriptural support. I have seen Eliyah Rabbah and other poskim react sharply to him. In Magen Avraham, letter 54, I wrote briefly that the passage in Taanis follows the first Tanna who disputes Rabbi Nasan. Rabbi Nasan's reasoning, however, makes it a full comparison, since the same verse says 'to stand, to serve, and to bless.' Tosafos on Sotah 38a, s.v. harei hu omer, appears to support this. Tosafos on Taanis in the passage beginning 'If so' writes that it is obvious a kohen may bless before the afternoon. The Gemara indeed states this; their point is that one should not think that according to Rabbi Nasan and the many passages indicating a full comparison between blessing and service, a blemished kohen should be disqualified. They therefore infer from the Talmud's unqualified conclusion that everyone agrees a blemished kohen is valid; otherwise it should have qualified the ruling as following only the first Tanna of Rabbi Nasan, who treats the comparison as a support. That passage also implies that an uncircumcised kohen is disqualified from duchaning because he may not eat sacred foods. With Hashem's help this will be explained in Magen Avraham, letter 54; this is not the place to elaborate. This is what we wished to explain: Taz's words are sound within his view and method. More will be explained, with Hashem's help, in letters 27 and 38. Levush derives standing from the rule that there is no sitting in the upper realm; the Gemara gives Scriptural derivations. Olas Tamid likewise gives other derivations. See there.
