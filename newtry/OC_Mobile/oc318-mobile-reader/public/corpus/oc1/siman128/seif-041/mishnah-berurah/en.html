150) Became impure for a corpse, etc. This applies specifically when he did so intentionally. See Biur Halachah.
151) He accepts. He makes this commitment before a rabbinical court; he need not vow or swear, unlike above in se'if 40, because forbidden relationships differ in that the evil inclination is especially forceful. See Responsa Ksav Sofer, siman 16, concerning a doctor who examines corpses for financial gain. A mere commitment does not suffice for him; he must make a public vow, as in the case of a woman involved in a forbidden relationship. See there.
152) Converted to idolatry or committed sexual immorality. Magen Avraham proves that a daughter profanes her father only through sexual immorality, because he should have guarded her, not through conversion. Conversion is mentioned because one who converted presumably also acted immorally. 'Or committed sexual immorality' means that she did so without converting.
153) They are no longer obligated to sanctify him. This concerns giving him the first opportunity to open and the first blessing, not the Priestly Blessing. Even a kohen who personally violated a prohibition of sexual immorality may recite the Priestly Blessing, as stated above in se'if 39; certainly his daughter's conduct does not disqualify him. Later authorities. Mordechai explains that one may still accord him priestly precedence, but is not obligated to do so.
154) For she profanes her father, etc. This applies specifically when she commits sexual immorality while betrothed or married, the case addressed by the verse, not while unmarried. The later authorities write that nowadays the custom is not to disqualify a kohen because his son or daughter acted immorally or converted.
