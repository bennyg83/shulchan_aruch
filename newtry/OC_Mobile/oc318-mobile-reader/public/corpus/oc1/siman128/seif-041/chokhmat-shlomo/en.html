Seif 41. In the gloss: some say that one whose daughter apostatized to idolatry or committed immorality, etc. Note: See Magen Avraham’s difficulty on this. See my Torah work, parashas Balak, year 5599, on the verse ‘and the people began to commit harlotry,’ where I rejected the proofs of Magen Avraham, seif katan 62, and concluded that one should distinguish between where she did this three times or publicly and where it was once and privately; in the latter case she does not profane her father, while with immorality even then she profanes him. Afterward I brought a proof from the Torah there that even many times and publicly she does not profane her father, from the Yalkut; see there and examine it. What is difficult on the Rama’s gloss from the end of Sukkah, see my work on Shulchan Aruch, Yoreh Deah, siman 219, page 120, where I incidentally wrote a resolution with Hashem’s help; examine it.
Seif 41. In the gloss: some say that one whose daughter apostatized to idolatry or committed immorality is no longer obligated to sanctify him, for she profanes her father. Note: See Magen Avraham, who explained the words of the Mordechai that since she apostatized she presumably committed immorality, and he later wrote that the father was negligent because he should have taught her in her youth, etc.; see there. His words are astonishing. If she is known to have committed immorality, she profanes her father, for presumably he did not guard her in her youth, since had he guarded her she would not have come to immorality. But where she apostatized and we do not know that she committed immorality, only that we say presumably she did, if guarding her in her youth helps even after she apostatizes, why does she profane her father? Perhaps he did guard her from immorality in her youth and she in truth did not commit immorality because of that guarding. And if guarding does not help, then even if she did not commit immorality he was not negligent at all, for guarding would not have helped. One should not say that Magen Avraham means that because of the pleasure of immorality, if he had guarded her in youth she would not have desired immorality and would not have apostatized. This is not so, for then there is no proof from Even HaEzer. There it does not imply that because of desire for immorality she apostatizes, only that presumably she also committed immorality. Also, what proof does he bring from a married woman? There we see that she committed immorality and did not apostatize, and it depends on whether he guarded her in youth. But if she apostatized first, one may say that even if he guarded her in youth she became accustomed to going out once she already apostatized. This requires study; examine it.
