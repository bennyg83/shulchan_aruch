He became impure through contact with the dead, etc. — See Mishna Berurah, that specifically if intentional; so Beit Yosef, and it is straightforward. And behold, from Shulchan Aruch's language it implies that even if he transgressed once incidentally he is also disqualified; and in my humble opinion, from the Mishnah's language [Bechoros 45], which teaches "and one who becomes impure through the dead" — it implies when he is prone to this, then the Sages penalized him; but not if he became impure once. And that he is disqualified in one who marries women in transgression, and even if he married one divorcee — there, since he maintains her as a wife and stands in his rebellion, behold he is prone to this prohibition; and so it is implied from the language of Mahari Weil cited in Beit Yosef, unlike in this case [and that it teaches "and one who marries women" is because it includes in this also a chalalah and a zonah, as Raavad explained there]. And similarly in the language of Mar Shmuel cited in Beit Yosef, it also writes "became impure through the dead" in plural, and similarly Rambam, chapter 6 of Hilchot Bi'at HaMikdash, also wrote like the language of the Mishnah; see there. And behold, even regarding a murderer, which is explicit in Shas as forbidden to raise his hands, also the view of Avi HaEzri and Or Zarua [and cited in Hagahot Maimoniyot] is that specifically when prone to this regularly, and not incidentally; and even though we do not hold thus, nevertheless in our matter, where otherwise all the poskim did not mention it [except Mar Shmuel Gaon and Rashba], as explained in Beit Yosef — it appears that they do not hold this law at all; if so, even though we are accustomed to be stringent regarding one who marries women and one who becomes impure through the dead for raising the hands, like Mar Shmuel and Rashba — it is enough for us to be stringent when prone to this, and not incidentally, since in truth the essence of this law for all is that even regarding service it is only a rabbinic penalty in general; and it is uncertain in law.
