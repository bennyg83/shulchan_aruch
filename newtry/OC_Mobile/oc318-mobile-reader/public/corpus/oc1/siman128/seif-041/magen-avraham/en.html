Profanes. This requires study, for we say in Sanhedrin chapter 7 according to Rabbi Shimon that burning is more severe, for it was given to a kohen's daughter who committed adultery. What is the severity? That she profanes her father. If it were true that the daughter of a kohen who worshipped idolatry also profanes her father, then from where is it known that burning is more severe, since one who worshipped idolatry was subject to stoning? Rather, it must be that specifically through immorality she profanes her father, for he should have guarded her from immorality so that she not seclude herself with a man, as stated in Kesubos, where they stone her at the entrance of her father's house, meaning: See the growth you raised. But in other transgressions he cannot guard her. In the Mordechai it is written as follows: A kohen who has a daughter who apostatized, or even only committed immorality, etc. This means that when she apostatized, presumably she also committed immorality, as stated in Even HaEzer, siman 7, se'if 11; or even if she only committed immorality alone, she profanes her father. So too it appears from the fact that he said daughter and did not say son who apostatized; rather, it must be that we are concerned only for immorality. Although once she apostatized she is no longer in his domain to guard her from immorality, nevertheless he should have taught her in her youth, as Shmuel did, who did not allow his daughters to sleep near one another, so that they not learn a foreign body. Know this, for there is an opinion that even a married woman who committed adultery is executed by burning, and there she is certainly not in her father's domain. Rather, it is as we said.
