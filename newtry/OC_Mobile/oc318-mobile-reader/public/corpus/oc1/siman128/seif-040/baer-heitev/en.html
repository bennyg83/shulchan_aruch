A divorcee. The same applies to a chalutzah. Even if danger to life prevents him from divorcing her and he immediately vows not to benefit from her, he may not raise his hands. The rule that he vows, serves, descends, and divorces applies when he divorces her immediately upon leaving the service. One who cannot divorce her may not raise his hands even after vowing not to benefit from her. Re'em, part 1, siman 59. Magen Avraham saw these words only as quoted by Knesses HaGedolah. Had he seen Re'em in context, he would not have independently asked in se'if katan 59 the very question Re'em raises and resolves. A textual error also fell into Magen Avraham's answer to Birchas HaZevach's question; it is corrected in the newer Proops edition by its editor. A kohen who is wicked, unscrupulous, and brazen is nevertheless not prevented from duchaning according to all opinions. Mahari ben Lev, part 1; Knesses HaGedolah.
In public. See Magen Avraham and what I wrote on Shulchan Aruch, Even HaEzer, siman 96, se'if katan 26. Examine it carefully. See also Peri Chadash in Mayim Chayim, page 6a. If one declared that he was not a kohen and married a woman disqualified to a kohen, then even if he divorced her and vowed not to benefit from any disqualified women, he does not have the status of a kohen for duchaning or for reading first in the Torah. Mahari Halevi, siman 53. See Levush, Even HaEzer, at the end of the work, and Shulchan Aruch, siman 6, and siman 3, se'if katan 6.
