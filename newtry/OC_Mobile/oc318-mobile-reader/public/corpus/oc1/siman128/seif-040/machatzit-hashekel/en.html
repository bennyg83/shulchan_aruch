He may interrupt in order to duchan. Eliyah Rabbah asks that prayer may not be interrupted, as stated in siman 104, and Rashba forbids interruption even during Shema; siman 66 records two views. The answer is clear. Shema may be interrupted for a Torah reading according to the lenient view, and all the more for duchaning when he was called, since otherwise he violates a positive commandment. Even the stringent view regarding Torah reading agrees here because a positive commandment is involved. During the Amidah all agree that one does not interrupt for Torah reading, but duchaning is different because of that positive commandment. Rashba's responsum 135 proves this: A kohen called to the Torah during Shema does not interrupt because the call is only an honor, and inability to ascend causes no disgrace. This implies that if disgrace were involved he would interrupt; all the more so here, where a positive commandment is involved. The same applies during the Amidah.
Tosafos in Sotah supports this conclusion, as Magen Avraham cites in the following se'if katan.
