Se'if 40: A kohen who married a divorcee may not raise his hands. See Magen Avraham's question on Rambam. In the addenda to my Nidrei Zerizin on Yoreh De'ah 228, and in my second work of addenda on vows, manuscript page 173, I resolve Rambam according to Magen Avraham's approach. In Sefer HaChaim I explain Rambam through the concern that the offender will select lenient-minded judges for himself who disregard prohibitory vows. See also my work on Yoreh De'ah, Hilchos Bechor, siman 314, for proof from Bechoros 36b. Rabbi Eliezer was presented a firstborn animal and did not know whether its owner was a kohen; the Gemara concludes, 'He is concerned for a sage's honor, but will commit a prohibition.' This necessarily assumes concern that he will choose three lenient-minded men who disregard the sage's honor and permit it. Otherwise, perhaps he himself is concerned and the three will refuse because a sage is present, or perhaps he is acting deceptively knowing they will refuse without a kohen present. Thus the concern is that he may find three careless judges to permit it, while he himself respects the sage. Examine this carefully.
