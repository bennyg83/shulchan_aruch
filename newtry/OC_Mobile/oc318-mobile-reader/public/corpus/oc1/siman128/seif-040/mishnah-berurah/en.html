147) A kohen who married a divorcee, etc. The same applies to a chalalah, zonah, or chalutzah. The preceding rule that other transgressions do not prevent the Priestly Blessing concerns prohibitions in which kohanim are no more restricted than Israelites. Here, the kohen's added sanctity created the prohibition and he profaned that sanctity. The Sages therefore disqualified him from all priestly functions until he vows with the consent of the public, etc.
148) With the consent of the public. A vow made with public consent cannot be annulled. Without that condition, we fear he may approach a sage to annul his vow, because the evil inclination for forbidden relationships overpowers him.
149) From the women forbidden to him. He must also divorce her, as the later authorities write. The Mechaber did not mention this because his ruling also addresses a case where he already divorced her or she died, as stated at the outset. If he wishes to keep her for any additional time without divorcing her, then even after vowing not to benefit from her, he may not recite the Priestly Blessing and is not called first to the Torah.
