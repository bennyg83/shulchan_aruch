40) There: The Levi pours water, etc. Zohar, Naso 146a, teaches: A kohen who spreads his hands must add holiness to his holiness by sanctifying his hands through a holy hand. That holy hand is the Levi's, from whom the kohen receives the sanctifying water, for it says, 'You shall sanctify the Levites'; they are holy. It also says, 'And also your brethren, the tribe of Levi.' Thus every kohen who spreads his hands must be sanctified by a holy person, adding holiness to his holiness, and should not receive the sanctifying water from an unholy person. Tzinius DiSifra further teaches that the Levi through whom the kohen is sanctified must first sanctify himself. Why specifically a Levi rather than another kohen? Another kohen might be deficient, and a complete kohen should not be impaired through one who is incomplete. A complete Levi, however, was fit to ascend the platform and serve in the Tabernacle, and is called holy, as it says, 'You shall sanctify the Levites.' Beis Yosef cites this, and it is the basis of Maran's words here. The Zohar implies that when no Levi is present, neither an Israelite nor another kohen should wash his hands; he should wash them himself. Bach, however, cites Maharil that in the absence of Levites, a firstborn who opened his mother's womb should pour for the kohanim. Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef, os 3; Olas Tamid, os 13; Taz, se'if katan 3; Ateres Zekeinim, os 1; Magen Avraham, se'if katan 7; and Eliyah Rabbah, os 10, cite this. Magen Avraham notes that this means specifically a maternal firstborn; a paternal firstborn alone has no recognized sanctity today. Shaliach Tzibbur, page 143a; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, os 10; Chayei Adam 32:8; Chesed LaAlafim, os 3; Beis Menuchah, os 11; and Ben Ish Chai, Tetzaveh, os 7, cite it. I have not seen anyone follow this. They apparently rely on the Zohar's plain implication that only a Levi pours; without one, each kohen washes his own hands.
