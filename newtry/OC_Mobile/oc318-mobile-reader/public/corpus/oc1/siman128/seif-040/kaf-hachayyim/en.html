40) There, "and the Levi pours water," etc. Thus it is taught at the end of Zohar, Parashat Nasso, 146a: A kohen who wishes to spread his hands must add sanctity to his sanctity, and must sanctify his hands through a sanctified hand. What is the sanctified hand? It is the Levi, from whose hand the kohen must receive the sanctification of the water, as it is written, "You shall sanctify the Levites," so they are sanctified. It is also written concerning the Levites, "And also your brethren, the tribe of Levi," etc. From here we learn that every kohen who spreads his hands must be sanctified through one who is sanctified, in order to add sanctity to his sanctity, and therefore he should not receive the sanctification of the water from another person who does not have this sanctity. In Tzeniuta DeSifra it is taught that a Levi through whom a kohen is sanctified must first sanctify himself. Why specifically a Levi, and why should the kohen not be sanctified through another kohen? It is taught that another kohen should not do so, because a kohen who is not whole should not cause a whole kohen to be impaired through one who is deficient. A Levi who is whole, however, and is fit to ascend to the duchan and to serve in the Mishkan, is whole and is called sanctified, as it is written, "You shall sanctify the Levites." Beit Yosef cites this, and this is the basis of the Mechaber's words here in Shulchan Aruch. The words of the Zohar imply that if no Levi is present, the kohen should not have an Israelite or another kohen wash his hands, but should wash them himself. Bach, however, writes in the name of Maharil that where no Levites are present, if there is a firstborn who opened the womb, he should pour water for the kohanim to wash before ascending to the duchan. This is cited by Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahot Beit Yosef, letter 3; Olat Tamid, letter 13; Taz, seif katan 3; Ateret Zekenim, letter 1; Magen Avraham, seif katan 7; and Eliyah Rabbah, letter 10. Magen Avraham writes that Maharil's language implies specifically a maternal firstborn, but a paternal firstborn alone has no recognized sanctity nowadays. This is cited by Shaliach Tzibbur, page 143a; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, letter 10; Chayei Adam, rule 32, letter 8; Chesed LaAlafim, letter 3; Beit Menuchah, letter 11; and Ben Ish Chai, Parashat Tetzaveh, letter 7. I have not seen anyone practice this. It appears that they rely upon the plain implication of the Zohar, that specifically a Levi should pour, and if no Levi is present the kohen washes his own hands.
