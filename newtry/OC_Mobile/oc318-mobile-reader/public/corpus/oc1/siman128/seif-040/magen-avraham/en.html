By the consent of many. The Gaon in the book Bircas HaZevach, at the end of Menachos, asked: Why must it be by the consent of many? We rule like the opinion that one must specify the vow, and if so, when he tells the sage the reason for which he vowed, the sage will not release it for him, as stated at the end of chapter 7 of Bechoros and in Gittin chapter 4 in the Gemara. It appears to me that since Amoraim dispute this in the Gemara and the halachah was not decided explicitly, the Rashba and Rambam were concerned that perhaps he will go to a sage who holds like the opinion that one need not specify the vow, and he will release it for him. One cannot object from a widow, where we say in Gittin, chapter 4, folio 34, that she vows to the orphans, meaning that she says, "All fruits in the world shall be forbidden to me if I benefited from my kesubah," and we are not concerned that perhaps she will go to a sage, etc.; that is because we hold that one must specify. One may answer that regarding a matter of prohibition they were more stringent. One may also answer that Tosafos wrote in Arachin, folio 23, that even according to the opinion that one must specify, he need not state the reason for which he vowed, but only the wording with which he vowed; and this is the practice of the world now. The Shach and the Perishah wondered about the custom, but they did not see the words of Tosafos. The Rashba and the Shulchan Aruch hold that he must state the reason for which he vowed, as written in Yoreh Deah, siman 228, se'if 14, but they were concerned lest he come before a sage who follows Tosafos and tell him that he vowed not to marry a divorced woman, while the sage will not know that he vowed so because he is a kohen, and the sage will release it for him. Therefore it rules that we require the consent of many. But regarding the widow, even if she states the wording, "All fruits in the world shall be forbidden to me if I benefited from my kesubah," the sage will not release it for her. We are not concerned that she will not even state the wording, for certainly everyone agrees that the halachah follows the opinion that one must specify. See Even HaEzer, siman 6, in the Beis Yosef in the name of the Rambam, who discusses at length in his responsum concerning a kohen who married a woman disqualified to him. If he said that he is not a kohen and married a woman disqualified to him, even if he divorced her, he is disqualified from the priesthood (Rabbi Yehudah Levi, siman 33; and see Levush, Even HaEzer, at the end of the book).
From the women who are forbidden to him. Even if he vowed that benefit from her is forbidden to him from now, and even if he cannot divorce her because of duress, even mortal danger, he does not raise his hands and is not called first until he divorces her (so wrote Re'em, part 1, siman 59). This requires study, for we say in Gittin, folio 35, that he vows, performs the service, descends, and divorces.
