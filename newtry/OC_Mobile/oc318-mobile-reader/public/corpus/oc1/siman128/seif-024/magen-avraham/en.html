And at their sides. This refers to the sides in front of them; but those at the sides behind them, such as those standing by the eastern wall when the aron kodesh protrudes somewhat into the synagogue and the kohanim stand in front of it, are not included in the blessing. The Bach wrote that nowadays, when each person purchases a place in the synagogue, they are considered under constraint, for one is not permitted to leave his place and push his fellow from his place, and all the more so one who has no place in the synagogue and must stand in the synagogue courtyard, who is considered under constraint, end quote. There is some proof for this from Megillah, folio 22, where Rav would not fall on his face because there was a stone floor before him, and he did not want to go from his place to another place so as not to trouble the congregation. Nevertheless, it appears to me that here they can go to the place of the kohanim, which is empty, or distance themselves slightly from the eastern wall so that they are at the sides of the kohanim, or stand in the middle of the synagogue. And those in the courtyard can obviously stand at the side of the synagogue or toward the west.
