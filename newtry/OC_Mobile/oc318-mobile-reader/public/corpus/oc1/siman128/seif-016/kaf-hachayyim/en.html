16) There: Or if they told him to ascend, etc. Even before the prayer leader calls, if people tell the kohanim to ascend or wash their hands, they must ascend. Mordechai writes that even when other kohanim are present, one who does not ascend violates the command; this is obvious. Olas Tamid, os 7. This implies that without being told he does not violate, even if he never ascends all year. Responsa Rabbi Yaakov of Marvege, siman 38, whose answers came from Heaven and are printed in Radbaz, part 5, writes differently: Where kohanim fit to bless are present but the community does not recite the Priestly Blessing even once a year, both groups violate; the Israelites, who do not call them and appear indifferent to their Father's blessing, and the kohanim, who do not bless voluntarily. The verse says, 'I will bless those who bless you,' from whose positive formulation a negative consequence is inferred. Chesed LaAlafim, os 1, writes that one who does not bless forfeits much good and shows that he neither desires the mitzvah nor the blessing; contempt for the matter will bring harm. A God-fearing kohen who delights in His commandments should therefore never omit blessing and giving satisfaction to his Maker, for Hashem desires Israel to be blessed. How good is the custom of places where kohanim bless daily, and every place should adopt it rather than seek devices to cancel a biblical positive command. Although a kohen violates only when called, the Priestly Blessing is an auspicious time that brings blessing to the upper and lower realms. Its intentions and deepest mysteries are ancient; in general, kohanim should intend that the Supreme One establish the work of their hands and that they act with the intention of Aharon HaKohen and all the holy ones, may their merit protect us. See Shaar HaKavanos, discourse 5 on the repetition, which explains that the Priestly Blessing should be recited during the repetition every day and gives its mystical meaning.
