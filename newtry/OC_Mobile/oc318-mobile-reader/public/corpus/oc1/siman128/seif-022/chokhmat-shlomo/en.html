Seif 22. They endeavor that the prompter should be an Israelite, etc. Note: See Taz in the name of the Rambam, who learned this law from the verse ‘say to them,’ implying that the one saying is not from among them; see there. In my eyes this is very astonishing from the Gemara, page 38, which says that Rabbi Chisda said: we have a tradition that a kohen calls ‘Kohanim’ and an Israelite does not call ‘Kohanim,’ as it says ‘say to them,’ meaning that it shall be from their own. Thus we see the opposite, that ‘say to them’ explicitly means from their own. Although Abaye concludes there that the halacha is not like Rabbi Chisda, that only means that it need not specifically be from their own. From where do we derive the opposite, that it specifically should not be from their own? Why should we create opposite readings, that for Rabbi Chisda ‘say to them’ means from their own, while for Abaye it means the exact opposite, that he says to them and he is not from among them? This requires study; examine it.
