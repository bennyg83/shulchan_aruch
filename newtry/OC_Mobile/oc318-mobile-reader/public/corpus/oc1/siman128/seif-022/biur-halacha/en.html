They strive that the reciter be an Israelite — See in Mishna Berurah; and it is from the words of Magen Avraham; nevertheless it is not proven thus in the words of the Mechaber, for it is possible that his intent is only regarding the reciter — that they strive that he be an Israelite — but we are not strict even l'chatchila to strive that the chazzan specifically be an Israelite so that he himself be the reciter [and although from the words of the Beit Yosef it seems so, nevertheless from the language of Rambam that the Mechaber copied it is not necessary at all; and otherwise the words of the Beit Yosef are ambiguous, as the Taz questioned]. And what concern is it to us if it be after the recitation? And what was mentioned in the Midrash teaches that the chazzan tells the kohanim with each and every word — it is possible that he merely stated the ordinary manner of speech; and so is proven to my mind in Biur HaGra, that he explains thus.

And he calls "Kohanim" — See in Eliyah Rabbah, seif kaf-alef, in the name of Knesset HaGedolah, that he should also say "Our Father, bless us," etc.; and likewise Magen Avraham, seif 71, from him. And in Sefer Chemed Moshe he wrote that we do not require above in seif 10 to say "Our Father" except for the chazzan, so that he can say the word "Kohanim" and there not be an interruption, as is explained in the poskim; and since he is not the chazzan, he can simply call "Kohanim"; and the chazzan too should not say it, since he will not recite the verses — and Peri Megadim cited this.
