And is silent. That is, until the beginning of "Grant peace." It appears that here it is not preferable that the prompter finish "Grant peace" — for specifically above, where the cantor left his place and another came to his place and prompts, therefore it is preferable that he also say "Grant peace"; not so here, where the cantor stands in his place only silent, and another stands beside him and prompts — how can he say "Grant peace" while he is in the cantor's place? And Bach wrote that even here the prompter should finish "Grant peace" and the cantor should say Kaddish; and I have not seen this anywhere, and on the contrary, many times in my youth I was the prompter beside the prayer leader who was a priest, and the prayer leader said "Grant peace," and Bach, may his righteous memory be blessed, was in that synagogue — and such is the essential law.
