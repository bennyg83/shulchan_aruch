To their brothers. See Taz and Magen Avraham in the name of Bach. According to Bach's first answer, this applies even when Jewish women married to Israelites are present; according to his second, it concerns wives of kohanim. If more than ten are present, ten men should answer Amen; that is preferable to the women answering. If no one at all is present to answer Amen, it appears that the kohanim nevertheless raise their hands, because Amen is not indispensable. There is, however, no obligation, for the obligation applies when Israelites tell them to bless. A kohen telling another kohen does not create a violation; whether women, converts, or slaves do so will be explained, with Hashem's help, in Magen Avraham, letter 37. A student asked: If all the kohanim ascend, is not one of them the chazzan? It appears that if they are currently praying, all the fit kohanim except the chazzan ascend, as in se'if 20. If they already prayed, literally all ascend. Taz, letter 17, writes that here duchaning occurs without a call, for who among them could call; this implies that the chazzan also ascends. One might explain that otherwise people could say he is disqualified. There is no proof from Peri Chadash, se'if 22, for in se'if katan 4 he rules unlike Hagahos Maimoniyos.
