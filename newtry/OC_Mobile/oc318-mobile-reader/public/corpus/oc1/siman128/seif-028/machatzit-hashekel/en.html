In my opinion to resolve the Rabbi's difficulty — meaning the Rama's ruling that the kohanim should not begin "Ribon ha-olamim," etc. — stems from the necessity of the Rabbi's difficulty on what we say in Sotah daf 39: kohanim may not begin another blessing until Amen finishes from the congregation's mouth, since kohanim do not say the word until the prayer leader recites it for them — if so what is "until it finishes," etc. — even after Amen finishes from the congregation they do not begin except the reader begins; and per Rambam who does not recite for them the word "yevarechecha" — if so the meaning is: after the kohanim blessed "who sanctified us," etc., they may not begin another blessing, i.e. "yevarechecha," until Amen finishes, etc.; but per Tur who holds the prayer leader also recites "yevarechecha" for them — "another blessing" means what they say "Ribon ha-olamim," etc. — end of the Rabbi's words; therefore Rama per Tur who also recites "yevarechecha" must recite it for them as written seif 13; therefore per his method they should not begin "Ribon," etc. However for practical law even the Rabbi, though he holds like Rambam that "yevarechecha" is not recited for them, nevertheless does not need his answer that they should not begin "Ribon," etc., until Amen from the reader — as the Rabbi wrote above; nevertheless even though there is no necessity, the law is true by reasoning and one should not make dispute between Rambam and Tur; and since Bach questioned this explanation of Beit Yosef:
