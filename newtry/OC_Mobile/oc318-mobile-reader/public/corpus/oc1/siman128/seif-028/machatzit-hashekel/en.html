Magen Avraham asks why the kohanim are warned not to begin Ribbono shel Olam if the chazzan began too early. It would be more important to warn the chazzan not to begin Sim Shalom until even those who prolong Amen have finished, since that blessing must be heard, as Magen Avraham writes in siman 124. Ribbono shel Olam is merely the kohanim's private prayer that their blessing be accepted, and the congregation has no interest in it; there is therefore less reason for concern.
Magen Avraham resolves Beis Yosef's position. Sotah 39b says that the kohanim may not begin another blessing until the congregation's Amen has ended. Since the chazzan prompts each word, what does it mean that the kohanim begin? Rambam holds that the chazzan does not prompt Yevarechecha; thus, after the kohanim's mitzvah blessing, they themselves begin Yevarechecha. Tur holds that even Yevarechecha is prompted, so Beis Yosef explains that 'another blessing' means Ribbono shel Olam. Rama follows Tur and accordingly rules that they may not begin Ribbono shel Olam. Logically the rule is also true according to Beis Yosef and Rambam, even though the Gemara does not compel it. This answers Bach's objection to Beis Yosef.
Magen Avraham offers another explanation: 'another blessing' always means Yevarechecha. Although the chazzan prompts it, the kohanim may need to wait if some members of the congregation continue their Amen after the prompter says Yevarechecha. The prompter himself may begin because the congregation need not hear his prompting. This proves that the prompter need not wait for those who prolong Amen.
