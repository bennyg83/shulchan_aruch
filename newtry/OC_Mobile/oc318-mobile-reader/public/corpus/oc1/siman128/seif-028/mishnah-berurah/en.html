106) He may recite the Priestly Blessing. As explained, performing a mitzvah twice does not violate bal tosif. Still, he is not obligated again after reciting it that day, as stated above in se'if 3. See Mishnah Berurah above, se'if katan 11: Although not obligated to recite it several times in one day, each time he does so he blesses 'Who sanctified us with His commandments, etc.' A kohen in the middle of his Amidah must interrupt and ascend if no other kohen is present, so the Priestly Blessing is not canceled. Afterward he descends and completes his prayer. If another kohen is present, he does not interrupt unless told to ascend or wash; then he must interrupt even when other kohanim are present. Whenever he interrupts his Amidah to ascend, he must move his feet slightly when the prayer leader says Retzeh. If he did not, he may not ascend. Magen Avraham in Radbaz's name and other later authorities. Eliyah Rabbah questions the entire permission to interrupt, even when told to ascend: Although prayer is rabbinic, perhaps the Sages upheld their enactment even against a positive command. Yaavetz likewise rules in his siddur not to interrupt in another blessing. If the kohen has reached the place of the Priestly Blessing in his own Amidah, Yaavetz permits him to move and ascend, because there it is not an interruption but relates to Sim Shalom. If he estimates that he will reach that point together with the prayer leader, he should move slightly toward the platform when the prayer leader begins Retzeh. This permission too applies only if he is confident he will not become confused and can resume; otherwise, interruption is forbidden in every case. This is the substance of Yaavetz's words; see there.
