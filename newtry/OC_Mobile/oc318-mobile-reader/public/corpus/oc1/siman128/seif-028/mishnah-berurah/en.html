(106) He may lift his hands — as above, there is no prohibition of "do not add" in performing the mitzvah twice; nevertheless there is no obligation upon him since he already lifted his hands on this day, and as above in se'if 63. And see above in Mishna Berurah se'if 111 — that although he is not obligated to lift his hands several times in one day, nevertheless each time he lifts his hands he blesses "asher kidshanu bemitzvotav…" A kohen praying Shemoneh Esreh — if there is no other kohen in the synagogue, he must interrupt and ascend the duchan so nesi'at kafayim is not nullified; and after he finishes nesi'at kafayim he descends from the duchan and completes his prayer. And if there is another kohen there he does not interrupt his prayer unless they told him "ascend the duchan" or "wash your hands" — then even if there are other kohanim he must interrupt and ascend. And wherever he interrupts in the middle of prayer to ascend the duchan, he must uproot his feet slightly during prayer when the prayer leader says Retzah; but if he did not uproot his feet when the prayer leader says Retzah he may not ascend [Magen Avraham in the name of Radbaz and Sha'arei Ephraim]. However Eliyah Rabbah casts doubt on the fundamental permissibility of interrupting in the middle of prayer even if they told him to ascend — for although prayer is d'rabbanan, nevertheless perhaps the Sages established their words even where there is a positive commandment; and likewise the Gaon Yaavetz in his siddur — not to interrupt in the middle of prayer for nesi'at kafayim when standing in another berachah, unless he reached in his prayer the place of the kohanim's berachah — then his opinion is that he may uproot his feet and ascend the duchan, for at that place it is not called an interruption but like "Shalom rav" [and when he estimates during prayer that he will reach that place together with the prayer leader, he should be careful to uproot his feet slightly toward the duchan when the prayer leader begins Retzah, as we wrote above]. And this too only if he is assured his mind will not be disturbed and he will return to his prayer — otherwise it is forbidden in every way to interrupt prayer. This is the content of his words see there.
