To answer Amen, etc. Lest his mind become confused and he not know which blessing he must prompt, the second or third verse; Tur. Knesset HaGedolah wrote in the name of Lechem Chamudos that if he is assured he will not become confused he answers Amen, for so it is found in Midrash Rabbah, parashas Ki Savo. He did not see Tosafot Yeshanim, chapter Ein Omdin, where he retracted, for we do not learn halacha from the Midrash. This also escaped Olas Tamid; see there. Nevertheless, it appears to me that for us, who pray from a siddur, there is room to permit, as I will write in seif 21. However, from Responsa Maharil, siman 179, it implies that even for us it is forbidden. All this is with the shaliach tzibbur, but for an individual standing when they perform Nesias Kapayim, it is implied from Bach that it is forbidden because it is an interruption, for only for the shaliach tzibbur is it not an interruption.
He should not perform Nesias Kapayim, etc. Lest his mind become confused and he not be able to return to his prayer. Rather, he stands and another who is not a kohen prompts, as written in seif 22, for if he himself calls ‘Kohanim’ they will say that he is blemished. Nevertheless, this is not indispensable, for if no Israelite can prompt, a kohen may prompt, and even if no one prompts at all, as above, seif 4. So wrote Bach and Taz. It implies that even if no one calls to them word by word, ‘Yevarechecha,’ etc., they bless, as implied in seif 25 regarding a synagogue all of kohanim. Nevertheless, it appears that if there is no Israelite to call for them, it is better that the shaliach tzibbur who is a kohen call than that no one call at all, for in Sotah 38 Rabbi Chisda said a kohen calls ‘Kohanim’; although we do not rule like him, that is where an Israelite is available. So it appears to me, unlike Taz; see Knesset HaGedolah.
