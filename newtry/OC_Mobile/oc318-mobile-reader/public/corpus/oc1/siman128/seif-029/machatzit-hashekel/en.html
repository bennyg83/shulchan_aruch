Pri Chadash writes that the chazzan should not prompt the words from memory. He should read them from a siddur so that he will not become confused.
Tosafos' ruling appears difficult in light of siman 104, where one praying the Amidah may not interrupt even for Kaddish or Kedushah. Bach answers that this Amen is part of the prayer because Birchas Kohanim was instituted within it. Kaddish and Kedushah are not part of the individual's Amidah; they began only because he arrived late or prolonged his prayer.
The Mishnah says that the chazzan does not answer Amen because he may become confused, and that if he is the only kohen he does not raise his hands unless he is certain he can return to his prayer. Since the assurance qualification is stated only in the latter clause and not regarding Amen, assurance permits duchaning solely to prevent its cancellation, but does not permit answering Amen. So rule Bach, from Mordechai HaAruch and Maharam. Lechem Chamudos initially suggests that, according to the Midrashic view, assurance stated regarding duchaning should all the more permit Amen, but he rejects this.
A chazzan may not blow the shofar during the order of blessings when another person is available, even if he is certain that he will not become confused. Here too, permission is granted only to prevent the cancellation of duchaning when there is no other kohen.
Shulchan Aruch here permits it only when there is no other kohen, whereas siman 585 implies that an assured chazzan may blow the shofar even when another person is available.
