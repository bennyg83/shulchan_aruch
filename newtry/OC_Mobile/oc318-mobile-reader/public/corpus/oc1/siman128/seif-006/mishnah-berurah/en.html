19) Although they washed. The Sages based the essential washing on the verse, 'Raise your hands in holiness and bless Hashem': When raising your hands, first sanctify them; meaning wash them; and then recite Hashem's blessing, the Priestly Blessing. This washing specifically requires water; cleaning the hands by another method, as for prayer in siman 92, does not suffice. The Mechaber rules that although he washed in the morning, he must sanctify his hands again before the Priestly Blessing, because the verse implies that the blessing follows immediately after sanctification.
20) They wash again, etc. If he has no water, the later authorities write that Rambam's view may be relied upon: His morning hand washing suffices, provided he did not divert attention from his hands and knows he did not touch a soiled area.
21) Up to the joint. Like a kohen who sanctifies his hands for the Temple service. Rambam. The later authorities therefore write that the washing may specifically require a vessel, pouring by human force, water unchanged from its natural state, and at least a revi'is of water.
And the Levi pours water. When there is no Levi to pour, a firstborn who opened the womb pours, for he too has some sanctity. If there is also no firstborn, it is better that the kohen place the water on himself than that an Israelite pour on his hands. Even if the Levi is a talmid chacham and the kohen is an am ha’aretz, one should be stringent, and certainly if there is one kohen who is a talmid chacham, who saves them all, he is obligated to pour even for an am ha’aretz. So wrote Eliyah Rabbah. Magen Gibborim wrote that this is the primary ruling; see there for his reason.
23) The custom was not. If the Levites diverted attention from their hands, it is proper that they wash first; all the more so if they touched their bodies. Later authorities. Some places customarily add scented oil to the water. Magen Avraham here and Taz below, siman 511, forbid this because it creates fragrance. Eliyah Rabbah and Chacham Tzvi, siman 92, are lenient, holding that creating fragrance does not apply when the oil itself is mixed into the water. If the oil was added before Shabbos or Yom Tov, one may certainly rely on them, even though fragrance is produced on the kohanim's hands, as Shaarei Teshuvah writes.
