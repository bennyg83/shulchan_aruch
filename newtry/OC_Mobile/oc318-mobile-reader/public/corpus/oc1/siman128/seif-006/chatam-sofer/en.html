(There) seif mem-alef in the gloss: a daughter who apostatized. NB: and it is written in Shirrei Knesset HaGedolah that they are not accustomed to disqualify a Kohen for this; and what he wrote, that she apostatized to idolatry — that is, she went and attached herself to another nation; we rely on this presumption that she committed adultery with them, for presumably she apostatized out of desire; and had her father guarded her properly in her youth, she would not have come to this trait; therefore her father is profaned through her. And to this Magen Avraham intended at the end of seif kaf-bet. And all this is regarding an apostate in our time, who goes to another nation and attaches himself to it. But a Kohen's daughter who worships idolatry and does not attach herself to a nation from among the nations, as they worshiped idolatry when they sat on their land in such a way — there is no presumption here that she committed adultery, for who from Israel would listen to her to commit adultery with her? Therefore a Kohen's daughter who worshiped idolatry at the time when there was a Sanhedrin was not judged by burning, and she did not profane her father. With this one may understand Magen Avraham's words here; and it is clear.
