That the kohanim washed. The custom of Egypt is that at Shacharis he does not wash his hands, but at Mussaf he does wash, because of distraction of attention (Radbaz, part 1, siman 147).
The Levi, his hands. If there is no Levi there, a firstborn who is a peter rechem, who has sanctity, should pour water over his hand (Bach in the name of Mahari Molin). From his language it implies specifically a firstborn of the mother; but a firstborn only of the father, we do not find sanctity in him nowadays. If the Levi is a Torah scholar and the kohanim are unlearned, he need not place water on their hands, rather he should wash himself (Rabbi Yehudah Levi, siman 39, and the Rosh, siman 41). Rabbi Leib Saraval disagrees. And if some of the kohanim are Torah scholars, according to everyone he must pour for all of them.
And they were not accustomed. But if they touched a filthy place, it is proper that he wash his hands (Bach); and see siman 92, se'if 6. It is forbidden on Yom Tov to put anything into the water so that it will have a fragrance, because this creates a fragrance, as written in siman 511. It is possible that we also require water that has not changed from its original state, as written in siman 160, and human force, similar to the kohanim in their Temple service; and see siman 4.
