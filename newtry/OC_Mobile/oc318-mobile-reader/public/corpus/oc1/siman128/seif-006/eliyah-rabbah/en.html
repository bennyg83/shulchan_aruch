In the morning, etc. Beis Yosef wrote that the Rambam’s view is that he need not wash again, and that his son wrote so as practical halacha. From this there is wonder on Magen Avraham; see there. Olas Tamid wrote that one may rely on this in pressing circumstances when he has no water for the duchan, provided that he did not divert his mind from them. It is clear that if he did not wash in the morning it is always forbidden, for cleaning with other substances helps only for prayer, but for the duchan water is required. Olas Tamid wrote that it is difficult, since the Rambam wrote that he must wash until the joint, whereas for morning washing it suffices if he washed until the end of the finger joints, as the Shulchan Aruch writes below, siman 613, seif 2. One must say that the Rambam spoke of the initial practice until the joint, while after the fact or in pressing circumstances washing until the finger joints suffices. In my humble opinion, there is no difficulty, for I wrote in siman 4, seif katan 5, that even for morning washing one must wash until the joint. This is also clear from the reason the Levush wrote at the beginning of that siman, that the washing is like sanctification for the service, and so it resembles washing for the duchan. Regarding washing for eating, one may also say that this is the Rambam’s view, as Beis Yosef wrote at the end of siman 141. What is written below in siman 613 is not difficult, for it speaks of Yom Kippur, where they are more stringent regarding washing. The custom is to pour fragrant oil into the water. Magen Avraham and Taz at the end of siman 611 wrote that they act improperly because of creating fragrance. I wrote in siman 658 that this requires study.
[9] They return, etc. In Levush: “and afterward he should wash,” etc. This means after removing the shoes, for if before removing them, he must return and wash because of the removal of the shoes. Sheyarei Knesset HaGedolah and Olas Tamid wrote that with shoes that can be removed without touching, he may wash before removing them, and that such is their practice. Above in siman 2 I challenged this; see there.
And the Leviim pour, etc. When there is no Levi to pour, a firstborn who opened the womb, who also has some sanctity, enters in place of the Leviim; Bach. Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah wrote that if there is also no firstborn, it is better that the kohen wash himself than that an Israelite pour over his hands. If the Levi is a talmid chacham and the kohanim are amei ha’aretz, some say there is no guilt on the Levi if he does not wish to pour, and some say that nevertheless he is obligated to pour. It is proper to be stringent, for even according to the first view he only said there is no guilt, but initially it is good to be stringent. If there is one kohen who is a talmid chacham, according to all opinions he is obligated to pour even for an am ha’aretz.
[11] And they did not drive, etc. And it is true that he was not distracted, but the BH wrote that it is good for the Levite to take his hands first, and it is not necessary to say that he touched his body or his shoes. And even greater than this, the remnants of the Great Knesset wrote that they simply did not want the Levites to take their hands forever when water is poured by the kohanim:
[12] And he blessed al netilas yadayim, etc. Magen Avraham asked: He should have said briefly that one does not bless on this washing, for do we not know that if he did not bless in the morning he blesses on his washing even if he does not wish to perform Nesias Kapayim? End quote. In my humble opinion, he comes to exclude a case where he had no water and cleaned with anything that cleanses, to recite the blessing “al nekiyus yadayim,” as above siman 4, seif 22. In such a case it appears that if he afterward washed with water, he need not bless al netilas yadayim unless he performs Nesias Kapayim; examine carefully.
