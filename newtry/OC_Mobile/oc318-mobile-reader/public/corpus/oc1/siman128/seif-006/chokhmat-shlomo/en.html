Seif 6. Although the kohanim washed their hands in the morning, they wash their hands again until the joint, etc. Note: I was asked whether the washing of hands for Nesias Kapayim requires a vessel, like washing for eating, or not. I answered that at first glance it would seem to require a vessel, like washing for a meal, since it resembles sanctifying the hands for the Temple service, which required a vessel from the laver. This also appears from the language of the Rambam cited in Beis Yosef, siman 205, who wrote: How so? A kohen who did not wash his hands may not perform Nesias Kapayim, but washes his hands until the joint, as they sanctify for the service. Since he wrote ‘as they sanctify for the service,’ this implies the law of washing for the service, which requires a vessel. But according to this it is difficult: how did the Beis Yosef then write that if he washed his hands in the morning he need not wash them again, since in the morning no vessel is required and now a vessel would be required? One cannot say that he is speaking of where he washed in the morning with a vessel, for then why did the Beis Yosef first write that this implies that when his hands are impure, such as when he did not wash in the morning, he must wash? It can be found even where he washed in the morning but not with a vessel. Rather he should have said: if he washed properly in the morning from a vessel he need not wash. Since he did not write so, it would prove that he holds no vessel is required. But if so, his proof from the service is astonishing, for there he sanctifies properly from the laver in the morning. Also Tosafot’s question against those who hold the morning washing suffices is difficult; why not answer that for words of Torah a vessel is not needed, whereas for Nesias Kapayim a vessel is needed? This proves that here too a vessel is not required. Yet one may say that it depends on this dispute: those who hold morning washing suffices hold no vessel is required, while those who hold it does not suffice hold a vessel is required. For now the practical law requires study, and it is astonishing that the early and later authorities did not note this. The view that morning washing suffices is not a proof, for perhaps the Sages enacted that if his hands have a presumption of purity through the morning washing, since then no vessel is required, they did not enact another washing for Nesias Kapayim; but if he did not wash and his hands are impure, they enacted washing for Nesias Kapayim like the Temple service, with a vessel. Similar to Chagigah, where terumah requires intent and chullin does not, but immersion for chullin that establishes him for chullin helps even for maaser. So too here one may say that according to all opinions a vessel is required when washing now. In any event, the words of Beis Yosef require study; examine this.
