Water. If there is no Levi present, a bechor who is peter rechem, who is holy, enters in place of the Levi — Beit Yosef. Magen Avraham wrote: specifically a bechor to the mother; but a bechor to the father alone we do not find holiness upon him nowadays. If there are no bechorim either, it is good that they wash by themselves over the hand of a Yisrael — as Knesset HaGedolah. Gaon Maharar David Oppenheim elaborates in his book Nish'al David volume 2 siman 98 whether a kohen may attend a kohen. If the Levi is a chacham and the kohanim amei ha-aretz, if he wishes he need not give water on their hands but they wash themselves — Mahari HaLevi siman 39, Magen Avraham, Rabbeinu Shimshon HaLevi siman 69 and yud; he wrote it is measure of piety for a Levi talmid chacham to pour water by kohanim even if they are amei ha-aretz; and if the Levi sees the hour requires granting latitude in his hand, certainly it is forbidden to belittle the honor of Torah — see there. If there are kohanim who are talmidei chachamim and amei ha-aretz, both — he may not pour on the talmid chacham nor on the am ha-aretz, but pours by all of them — Mahari HaLevi siman 61, and Pri Chadash wrote he is liable death if he squeezes water on an am ha-aretz even if some are talmidei chachamim, and all the more if all are amei ha-aretz — see there; see Knesset HaGedolah who brought opinions that even if all are amei ha-aretz the Levi talmid chacham is obligated to pour water on their hands — see there. See responsum Darchei Noam siman 12 and Chavos Ya'ir siman 205. Forbidden to put on Yom Tov in water something that produces scent — Magen Avraham. (And Eliyahu Rabbah permits, since the oil itself is mixed in the water it does not produce scent — see there at length where he brings proof.)

Their hands. However, where the Levi had diverted his attention, it is proper that the Levi wash first, and we need not say where he touched his body and his shoes — Beit Yosef. And in Knesset HaGedolah he wrote the simple custom among us is that Levites also wash their hands first — see there.
