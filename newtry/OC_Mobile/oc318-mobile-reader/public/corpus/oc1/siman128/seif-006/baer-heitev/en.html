Water. And if there is no Levi present [to pour], a firstborn who is a peter rechem, who is holy, pours [the water], entering in place of the Levi (Bach). And the Magen Avraham wrote that this refers specifically to a firstborn to the mother; but a firstborn to the father alone — we find no holiness in him nowadays. And if there is also no firstborn present, it is best that they [the kohanim] wash by themselves rather than from the hand of an [ordinary] Yisrael (Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah). And the Gaon Moreinu HaRav Rabbi David Oppenheim discusses at length in his manuscript work "Nishal David," siman 98, whether it is permitted for a kohen to serve [pour water for] a kohen. And if the Levi is a scholar and the kohanim are unlearned, if he wishes he need not pour water on their hands; rather they wash by themselves (Mahari Halevi, siman 39; and Magen Avraham). And the Rash Halevi, subparagraphs 9 and 10, wrote that it is a measure of piety for a Levi who is a Torah scholar to pour water for kohanim even though they are unlearned; and if the Levi sees that the moment calls for standing on ceremony, the choice is in his hand, for certainly it is forbidden to demean the honor of the Torah — see there. And if there are kohanim who are Torah scholars and unlearned together, he may not pour [only] on the scholars and not on the unlearned; rather he pours for all of them (Mahari Halevi, subparagraph 41). And the Peri Chadash wrote that he is liable to death if he pours water [only] on the unlearned, even if some of them are scholars, and all the more so if they are all unlearned — see there. And see the Kenesses HaGedolah, who cited opinions that even if they are all unlearned, a Levi who is a Torah scholar is obligated to pour water on their hands — see there. And see the Responsa Darkei Noam, siman 12, and the Chavos Yair, siman 205. It is forbidden [on Yom Tov] to put into the water anything that generates a fragrance (Magen Avraham). (And in the Sefer Eliyah Rabbah he permits it, since the oil itself is mixed into the water so it does not generate a fragrance — see there at length, where he brings proof for his words.)
Their hands. However, in a case where the Levi was distracted, it is proper that the Levi wash [his own hands] first; and it goes without saying [that this applies] where he touched his body or his shoes (Bach). And in the Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah it is written that it is a widespread custom among us for the Levi'im too to wash their hands first — see there.
