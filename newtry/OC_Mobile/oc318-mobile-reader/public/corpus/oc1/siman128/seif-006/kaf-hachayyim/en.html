35) [Seif 6] "Even though the kohanim washed their hands," etc. This is stated in Sotah 39a: Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi said that any kohen who did not wash his hands may not recite the Priestly Blessing, as it is written, "Lift your hands in holiness and bless Hashem." Beit Yosef writes that Rambam's words imply that washing is required specifically when the kohen's hands are impure, such as when he did not wash in the morning. If he washed his hands in the morning and knows of no impurity that came upon them, he need not wash again. He writes that this was the practice in Egypt, where a kohen did not wash his hands at the time of the Priestly Blessing but relied upon the washing he had performed in the morning. Orchot Chaim likewise writes in the name of R' Avraham son of Rambam that this was the practical ruling before his father Rambam and the other Torah teachers, that every kohen who is standing in prayer proceeds to the Priestly Blessing and relies upon the washing of his hands for Shema and prayer. Beit Yosef cites this. Tosafos in Sotah also cite, in the name of Rashi, what his teacher told him, but they write that this was copied from a student's gloss. Rashi's own language does not appear that way, for his explanation, "who did not wash his hands before ascending the duchan," implies washing immediately beforehand. Tosafos further write that both our Talmud and the Yerushalmi imply that even if he washed his hands in the morning he must wash again close to the Priestly Blessing, and should not delay longer than the time required to walk 22 amot. Beit Yosef cites this and writes that Tur agrees with Tosafos. This is also the view of the Mechaber here in Shulchan Aruch, and so write Levush, Bach, Pri Chadash, letter 6, Shulchan Aruch HaRav, letter 8, Chesed LaAlafim, letter 3, and Ben Ish Chai, Parashat Tetzaveh, letter 7. This also appears from the Zohar cited below in letter 40. Such is the custom here in Jerusalem, following the Mechaber and those who agree with him. Only where it is not possible, such as a prayer leader who is standing in prayer and for whom washing would constitute an interruption, or a similar case, may one rely upon Rambam. See the following letter. 36) There, "even though the kohanim washed their hands in the morning," etc. If he washed his hands in the morning, even only through the ends of the finger joints, and has no water with which to wash for the duchan, Rambam and those who agree with him are sufficient authorities to rely upon, and he may recite the Priestly Blessing, provided that he did not divert his attention from his hands. Olat Tamid, letter 12; Eliyah Rabbah, letter 8; Chayei Adam, rule 32, letter 8; Mishnah Berurah, letter 20. But if he did not wash even in the morning, he may not recite the Priestly Blessing. Cleaning the hands by other means suffices for prayer, but for the duchan water is required. Olat Tamid and Eliyah Rabbah there, and this also appears to be Pri Chadash's view. See below, letter 39. 37) Orchot Chaim writes in the name of Baal HaTamid: "We have not found anyone who wrote that one must wash his feet, but this is the practice in these lands, and Jewish custom is Torah." Beit Yosef cites this. Pri Chadash writes that perhaps they derived this by analogy to the sanctification of the kohanim, regarding which it is written, "they shall wash their hands and their feet." Nowadays this is not practiced, and correctly so, because the verse here says "your hands" and does not say "your feet." The language of the Zohar likewise says only that he must sanctify his hands. Beit Yosef also writes that we have never seen anyone practice washing the feet, and Rama writes in Darchei Moshe, letter 6, that this is not the custom in our lands either. See what we wrote above in siman 4, letter 5. 38) There, "even though the kohanim washed their hands in the morning," etc. The Egyptian custom was that at Shacharit a kohen did not wash again before the Priestly Blessing, relying upon the morning washing, as stated above. Nevertheless, according to the ruling of the Mechaber and the authorities who agree with him, one should wash again immediately before ascending to the duchan. Where the Priestly Blessing is recited again later in the day, the same principle applies: if the kohen's hands have remained guarded from impurity there are authorities upon whom to rely in a case of necessity, but ideally he should wash again close to the blessing. The essential point is that the washing be connected to the Priestly Blessing and that no diversion of attention or impurity intervene. 39) The washing for the Priestly Blessing requires water. Mere cleaning of the hands does not suffice when water is available, because the Sages required an act of washing for the sanctification of the hands before the duchan. In a case of necessity where water is unavailable, the authorities discussed above may be relied upon only when the kohen washed properly in the morning and did not divert his attention from his hands. If he did not wash in the morning, cleaning alone does not permit him to recite the Priestly Blessing. See the authorities cited above and the discussion in the following letter concerning the Levi pouring the water.
