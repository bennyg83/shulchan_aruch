A verse. Tosafos in the chapter Eilu Ne'emarin writes that although one who saw a dream says Ribbono shel Olam, the Sages permitted it because of danger, lest his dream require a remedy. Terumas HaDeshen writes that Ribbono shel Olam is said specifically while the kohanim prolong the final kaf of Veyishmerecha, the final kaf of Vichuneka, and the final lamed of Shalom.
To say them. So write Me'il Tzedakah, siman 95, Bach, Olas Tamid, Peri Chadash, and Knesses HaGedolah. Magen Avraham and Taz write that one who says the verses should say them while the chazzan prompts the word. I have seen the scandalous practice of the multitude shouting the verses loudly and then again shouting from Yevarechecha up to the current word. Because of their voices they cannot hear the kohanim. This is improper and should be abolished. I was asked whether one may read the weekly portion twice in Scripture and once in translation while the kohanim bless the people. I answered that it is forbidden. Even according to the view permitting such reading while the chazzan reads the Torah portion from the Sefer Torah, it is forbidden during Birchas Kohanim. Knesses HaGedolah and Peri Chadash.
