And some say, etc. The Levush writes: the shaliach tzibbur says, and the entire congregation with him quietly, ‘Our G-d and G-d of our fathers,’ etc. Lechem Chamudos, page 42, wrote: I do not know from where this comes. Perhaps because the Rama wrote according to the opinion that they say it, etc. But it appears to me that it is a scribal error and it should say ‘that he says it,’ for afterward he wrote everything in the singular. So too Malbushei Yom Tov wrote, but concluded: I later saw that it is somewhat implied in Tashbetz, who wrote that when the kohanim ascend the duchan one says ‘Our G-d,’ etc., quietly, and the chazzan says aloud ‘Kohanim,’ and ‘and Your holy people,’ etc., quietly. This implies somewhat that the beginning is not referring to the chazzan. I am astonished, for I examined Tashbetz, siman 198, and the word chazzan is not mentioned at all; rather, his language is ‘and he says aloud,’ etc. So too is the language of the Tur, and so it is implied in Agudah, chapter 2 of Megillah. I also found explicit in Responsa Maharam Mintz, siman 81: the chazzan should say quietly, while the congregation answers Amen, ‘Our G-d,’ etc., until ‘Kohanim,’ which he says aloud. Thus, specifically the chazzan says it.
