Two, he calls. If one of them is a minor, he does not call "Kohanim" (Mabit, siman 64). Two kohanim who hate one another are permitted to ascend together, and one cannot say to the other, "You ascend at Shacharis and I at Mussaf," or the reverse, because he can say, "I wish to bless at both" (Maharam Mintz, siman 12).
And some say that they say it. That means that only the chazzan says it, and not the congregation (Lechem Chamudos), and so it appears from the Hagahos Maimoniyos, unlike the Levush.
Toward the people. If the ark is fixed on another side, nevertheless the faces of the kohanim should be toward the chazzan (Rabbi Shimon Kahn in the responsa of the Beis Yosef). But I saw in Constantinople that the ark was fixed to the north side and the kohanim stood there; and in many places there are many kohanim, with some standing by the southern wall and some by the northern wall. Also, in some places the ark is fixed in the eastern wall and the bimah faces another direction, so that the faces of the kohanim are not toward the chazzan.
He does not call. Nevertheless, he recites the blessing, even though he is not obligated to ascend, as written in se'if 2 (Maharam Mintz, siman 12). A single kohen ascends by rabbinic law (Tosafos, Menachos, folio 44; see the Tur).
