33) If there are two. The verse says, 'Say to them,' meaning that the kohanim are to be told to bless Israel; the plural 'them' implies at least two.
34) Kohanim. This is not considered an interruption in the prayer, just as the prayer leader's prompting them word by word is not an interruption, because the Priestly Blessing is a need of the prayer.
35) He should not say 'Our God and God of our fathers.' This formula was instituted only when the kohanim do not bless personally. Since they themselves are blessing, why should the prayer leader say it?
36) He resumes and says 'with Your holy people.' This refers to the prayer leader. The custom in some places for the kohanim to say 'with Your holy people' is an error.
37) They turn their faces, etc. The Gemara teaches that the Priestly Blessing must be given face to face. The kohanim need not face specifically toward the prayer leader. If the sanctuary is situated in the north and the platform before which the prayer leader stands is in the east, the kohanim may stand before the sanctuary in the north, facing south toward the congregation. Magen Avraham in Knesses HaGedolah's name. Some say it is better for the kohanim to stand in the east even if the sanctuary is elsewhere, with the congregation standing opposite them face to face. Peri Chadash, cited by Peri Megadim. Two kohanim who hate one another, even if each vowed not to benefit from the other, may ascend together. Neither can tell the other, 'You ascend at Shacharis and I will ascend at Mussaf,' or the reverse, because the other may answer, 'I wish to bless at both.' But if the congregation hates a kohen or he hates the congregation, reciting the Priestly Blessing endangers him. He should therefore leave before Retzeh if he cannot overcome his inclination and remove the hatred from his heart. For this reason, the blessing formula says, 'to bless His people Israel with love.'
38) If there is one, etc. Even if one additional kohen is present but is a minor under thirteen, the prayer leader does not call 'Kohanim.' Mabit, siman 64. Peri Chadash questions this. Under our custom, however, where the prayer leader recites 'Our God' and raises his voice only for 'Kohanim,' it is of no consequence, because this is not an interruption and is part of the prayer. For this reason, Eliyah Rabbah writes that even if no minor kohen is present and there is only one adult kohen, the prayer leader should not be rebuked if he calls 'Kohanim' aloud, because it is not an interruption.
39) He turns his face. He raises his hands and also recites the preliminary blessing, even though under what was written above in se'if 2 he is not obligated to ascend. Magen Avraham in Maharam Mintz's name. All the more so according to Maharlach and Taz, se'if katan 3, who hold that a lone kohen is obligated by Torah law to ascend even without being called; he certainly recites the blessing.
