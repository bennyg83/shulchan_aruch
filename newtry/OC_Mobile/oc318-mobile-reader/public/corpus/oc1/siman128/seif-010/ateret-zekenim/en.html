When there are two kohanim, the chazzan calls, 'Kohanim.' If there is only one kohen, the chazzan does not call, 'Kohanim'; rather, the kohen turns his face on his own, even if a minor kohen is with him. See what Mahararm MiLublin wrote. Some write that the custom is to call even to a single kohen, as cited by the Beit Yosef in the name of the Semak and Semag. Some say the passage quietly until the word 'Kohanim.' Rashi and the Rambam likewise wrote this, and this is the custom, unlike the Ran in the chapter HaKorei, who wrote that before they move their feet he calls to them, 'Kohanim.' They recite the blessing, 'Who sanctified us with the sanctity of Aharon and commanded us to bless His people Israel with love.' Regarding the phrase, 'and commanded us to bless with love,' where do we find that HaKadosh Baruch Hu commanded that Israel be blessed with love? It appears that this follows Rashi's explanation of the full spelling in the verse, 'So shall you say to them': Do not bless them hastily or in agitation, but with concentration and a whole heart. Consider this carefully.
