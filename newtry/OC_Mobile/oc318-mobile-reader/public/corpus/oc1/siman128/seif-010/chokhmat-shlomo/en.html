(Seif 43) After seven days of mourning he raises his hands; and within seven days of mourning he left the synagogue at the time they call the Kohanim. NB: see Magen Avraham, who wrote "all the more so we," etc.; and it appears that even if it happened that the law of aninut did not apply to him — for example, he has no intention to bury him today — nevertheless he should not raise his hands, since he is not in joy, as Magen Avraham wrote; and this depends on the two reasons: according to Shach's reason, since he was not an onen, he raises his hands; and if it is according to Magen Avraham's reason, he should not raise his hands. And behold, according to Shach, who would have permitted in such a case to raise his hands — I found a practical solution in an incident that came before me in one house where there was a dead person and there was a Kohen, and he had a brother; and the incident was on Yom Tov, and the synagogue was close to the house, in such a way that the Kohanim could not raise their hands there. It appeared that the brother, the Kohen, was permitted to enter that synagogue and raise his hands there, since there are views like Shulchan Aruch whether relatives are permitted to become impure not for the sake of burial; and we are accustomed that it is forbidden to become impure in any case — it is a doubt; and also Shach's view in siman 371 is that impurity of a tefach adjacent is only rabbinic; and Magen Avraham challenged him that it is from the Torah. In any case, Penei Yehoshua in Kerisos, chapter 3, and in the first chapter of Sukkah, brought not according to Magen Avraham's view; and from any doubt one does not emerge [and see in my novellae to Yoreh De'ah, siman 372, what is written similarly]. If so, there was a double doubt: perhaps according to those who hold it is permitted to become impure not for the sake of burial; and if you say according to those who hold it is forbidden to become impure — nevertheless, perhaps adjacent impurity is only rabbinic, according to Shach and the above-mentioned Penei Yehoshua; and again, regarding rabbinic matters we are lenient to rely on those who hold it is permitted not for the sake of burial — a rabbinic doubt is resolved leniently, and it becomes a double doubt, as the poskim write regarding blood of eggs; and that is, he was an onen — nevertheless, if he has no intention to bury him today, or on Shabbat in places where they raise their hands on Shabbat, the brother of the dead is permitted to raise his hands in that nearby synagogue, and others are forbidden to raise their hands. However, according to Magen Avraham's reason, it is forbidden to raise his hands in any case, because he is not in joy. However, regarding blood of eggs itself it is difficult, for behold there is also a double doubt in the reverse: perhaps it was in a place that forbids it; and if you say it was not — perhaps the halachah is like the one who holds blood of eggs is from the Torah; therefore one should be concerned for stringency that it was in a place that forbids it. And here too the same question applies; and it is uncertain; and understand well. And behold, what is written above, that because of a double doubt it is permitted to enter the synagogue to raise his hands — one should not challenge: if so, even for a discretionary matter it would be permitted to enter the nearby house; but this is not so, for we hold one does not create a rabbinic doubt lekhatchilah, or a double doubt. But that is specifically for a discretionary matter; but for a mitzvah matter, it appears it is permitted to create a rabbinic doubt lekhatchilah, for the reason that one does not create a rabbinic doubt or double doubt lekhatchilah is because it is like canceling a prohibition lekhatchilah; and just as one does not cancel a prohibition lekhatchilah, so one does not create a double doubt lekhatchilah. If so, since for a mitzvah matter it is permitted to cancel a prohibition lekhatchilah, as is written in Hilchot Sukkah, siman 626 — regarding a mitzvah, the prohibition of "one does not cancel" is not relevant — if so, it is also permitted to create a rabbinic doubt and double doubt lekhatchilah; and therefore for a mitzvah matter it is permitted to gather from what we said. But for a discretionary matter it is forbidden, for one does not create double doubt and rabbinic doubt lekhatchilah; and similarly I wrote in my novellae to Yoreh De'ah, siman 201, in a responsum; see there. And this is correct in my opinion. One may also say that there is a double doubt in a matter that has permissive views that do not help; but for a mitzvah matter, "it has permissive views" is not relevant, for tomorrow there will no longer be a mitzvah matter; and understand well.
