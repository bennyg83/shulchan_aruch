His hands. Together with other kohanim, he may raise his hands according to all opinions, for he is no worse than a minor. Magen Avraham, Olas Tamid, Peri Chadash, and most poskim. All this concerns an unmarried man who has never married. If he is married but his wife is not with him, he may raise his hands even alone. Knesses HaGedolah, Olas Tamid, and Magen Avraham.

In the joy of Yom Tov. Beis Yosef, citing Agur and Hagahos Maimoniyos, writes that kohanim customarily immerse before duchaning, and daily immersion would be difficult; therefore they duchan only on Yom Tov. Magen Avraham writes that for this reason, when Yom Tov falls on Shabbos they should not raise their hands, because the Sages did not wish to cancel the conjugal obligation stated in the Torah. Since Taz's time, kohanim have customarily refrained from relations on Yom Tov night lest they again become impure through an emission at night. Although they could immerse in the morning, they would remain tevulei yom. Some, however, refrain from relations on Yom Tov but do not immerse before Yom Tov. This is an error: If they are concerned for an emission, they should have immersed beforehand, since that impurity is already present. If it is his wife's immersion night, he should have relations and immerse in the morning. Magen Avraham and Taz. Beis Yosef writes: Fortunate are the people of Eretz Yisrael and the entire kingdom of Egypt, who raise their hands every day without immersing. This is the proper practice everywhere.
