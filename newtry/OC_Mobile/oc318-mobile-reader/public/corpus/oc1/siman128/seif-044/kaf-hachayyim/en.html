44) Taz 511:8 writes: I saw communities pour scented oil into water on Yom Tov for washing the kohanim's hands before the platform. They act improperly, because this creates fragrance in the water. Even if poured before Yom Tov, the kohanim create fragrance on Yom Tov when it transfers to them; they desire and intend this, so the practice must be stopped. Magen Avraham there, se'if katan 11, and here, se'if katan 8, agrees. Leket HaKemach, part 2, siman 20, likewise forbids washing face and beard with rosewater on Shabbos because it creates fragrance in the beard hair, and hair resembles clothing. Eliyah Rabbah here, os 8, says that because the oil itself is mixed with the water, this is no more creating fragrance than smelling the oil; it may be poured either into the water or onto the hands. Chacham Tzvi, siman 92, likewise disputes Taz and Magen Avraham and holds that creating fragrance does not apply to human flesh, foods, or drinks. Ginas Veradim 3:16 permits applying it to hands, face, and beard, but agrees with Taz and Magen Avraham that rosewater may not be added to water. Nechpah BaKesef, siman 4, defends the custom of adding rosewater to wine on Shabbos and Yom Tov and permits it plainly. He is stringent only about adding fragrance to water intended for washing; if it was added before Shabbos, washing is permitted. Mahari Ayash agrees at the end of Shevet Yehudah. Chida cites them in Birkei Yosef 511:5 and Machazik Berachah there, os 5. Likkutei Peri Chadash 511 says any intent to create fragrance is forbidden. Batei Kehunah, part 1, siman 293, likewise appears to forbid intentional creation of fragrance on a person or object, permitting it when unintended. Responsa Shoel UMeishiv, second edition, part 2, siman 7, discusses the dispute and rules like Taz. Shelah's summary of Yom Tov laws and Shulchan Aruch HaRav at the end of siman 511 rule likewise. Therefore, practically, anything intended to create fragrance is forbidden; for example, putting rosewater into washing water, or washing one's face, hands, and certainly hair with rosewater itself; because the prohibiting authorities are the majority. It is permitted to add it to foods and drinks to sweeten them, or to pour it into one's hand in order to smell the rosewater itself, as some communities do at celebrations so each person can recite the fragrance blessing, since he does not intend to perfume his hand. Rav Pe'alim, part 2, siman 51, rules accordingly.
