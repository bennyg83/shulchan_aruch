A Kohen may perform the priestly blessing even if he is unmarried. {Rama: Some say he should not perform the blessing, since one who lives without a wife lives without joy, and the one who blesses must be in a state of joy [Mordechai, chapter "One who reads while standing"]. The custom is that he does perform the blessing even though he is unmarried; nevertheless, one who wishes not to perform the blessing should not be rebuked, provided he is not in the synagogue at the moment they call "Kohanim" or tell them to wash their hands. The custom in all these countries is not to perform the priestly blessing except on Yom Tov, because then they are in a state of Yom Tov joy, and "one of good heart shall bless" — unlike other days, even on Shabbat, when they are preoccupied with concerns about their livelihood and suspension of their work. And even on Yom Tov they perform the blessing only during Musaf prayer, when they leave the synagogue and rejoice in the Yom Tov joy. [His own words.] For every Shacharit and Musaf where the blessing is not performed, the prayer leader says "Our God and the God of our fathers, etc." as above at the end of siman 127. On Yom Kippur the blessing is performed as on Yom Tov; there are places where they perform it at Ne'ilah, and there are places where they perform it even at Shacharit.}
