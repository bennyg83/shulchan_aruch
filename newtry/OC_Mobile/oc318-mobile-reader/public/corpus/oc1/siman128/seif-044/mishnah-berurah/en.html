(160) That he does not lift his hands — however a married man whose wife is not with him — permitted per all.

(161) And they practiced that he lifts hands — even if he is the only kohen there.

(162) Even though he is not married — for only regarding mourning we are stringent that one dwells in pain; not so here — although not joyful, nevertheless not dwelling in pain.

(163) When they call kohanim — truly he should leave at berachah Retzah as stated several times; but also at berachat kohanim he should not be in synagogue because of disgrace, as above se'if 64.

(164) In all these countries, etc. — and in Eretz Yisrael and all Egypt they practice lifting hands every day; Poskim praised their custom in this.

(165) But on Yom Tov because, etc. — whether it falls on a weekday or on Shabbat. And there are places where the custom is not to perform nesi'at kafayim even on Yom Tov when it falls on Shabbat; but this custom is not the main principle at all, as many Acharonim wrote. Kohanim practiced on their own to immerse on Yom Tov because of nesi'at kafayim for the next day; and in any case a person must purify himself on the festival, and nevertheless this does not prevent b'dieved.

(166) Immersed in the joy of Yom Tov — and on Yom Kippur as well they perform nesi'at kafayim, for there is in it the joy of forgiveness and pardon.

(167) And regarding the cancellation of their work — the language is garbled, and Heaven forbid that holy Yisrael should grieve on Shabbat over the cancellation of their work on the holy day. And the correct reading is that it should say "and because of the cancellation of their work," and this reason applies to what is above — that they do not perform nesi'at kafayim on other days; and likewise Darkei Moshe divided it — he wrote the reason according to those occupied with reflections, etc., for what was stated above that they do not perform nesi'at kafayim on Shabbat; and on weekdays he wrote the reason is because of canceling work — see there.
