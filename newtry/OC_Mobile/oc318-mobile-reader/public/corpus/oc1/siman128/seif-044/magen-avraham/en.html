Only on Yom Tov. The Mateh Ephraim wrote that it is a poor custom, and in some places the practice is that when Shabbos falls on Yom Tov there is no priestly blessing. I do not know the reason for this. Perhaps it is because they say, "Master of the world," etc., and this involves the prohibition of supplication on Shabbos, unlike Yom Tov, where they are not so particular, as written in the Agudah, that it is not said on Shabbos. Afterward I found in the book Pisron Chalomot that he wrote that he found in a responsum of the Geonim that one should not say the "Master" on Shabbos unless he is fasting on Shabbos for a dream; he disagrees with them and writes that if it is permitted to fast, all the more so one may say the "Master," end quote. But it appears to me that the words of the Geonim are firm and enduring. Granted, a fast certainly nullifies the dream like fire consumes flax, and it is like danger to life; this is not so regarding the "Master." And as written in Yoreh Deah at the end of siman 125, we require the remedy to be known; see there. Therefore one should act as the Geonim wrote, for we do not have the strength to disagree with them based on reasoning alone. One may also say that specifically dreams for which one fasts on Shabbos also permit saying the "Master," unlike when one is not fasting. So too the Shelah wrote that on that day one may confess his sins and cry. The Beis Yosef wrote in the name of the Agur and the Hagahos Maimoniyos that the custom of the kohanim is to immerse for the priestly blessing, and it is difficult for them to immerse every day; therefore there is no priestly blessing except on Yom Tov, end quote. It appears to me that for this reason, when Yom Tov falls on Shabbos there is no priestly blessing, for they did not wish to nullify the conjugal duty stated in the Torah. The Agudah wrote similarly on what is written in siman 613, se'if 11. It appears to me that for this reason the kohanim were accustomed not to be with their wives on the night of Yom Tov, so that they not become impure again on Yom Tov through a seminal emission. Although it is possible to immerse in the morning, nevertheless they would be tevul yom, and furthermore, they were not accustomed to immerse on Yom Tov, as written in siman 326, se'if 7. But some practice not to be with their wives and also do not immerse on erev Yom Tov, and they do not act properly. It appears to me that if it is the night of immersion, he should be with her and immerse in the morning; and see what I wrote in siman 581, se'if 4.
