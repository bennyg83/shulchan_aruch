They are not permitted to begin ‘Yevarechecha.’ This was written according to his view, for he ruled in seif 13 that the chazzan does not prompt the word ‘Yevarechecha’; therefore it is necessary to warn the kohanim about beginning ‘Yevarechecha.’ But according to us, where the chazzan also first prompts the word ‘Yevarechecha,’ this warning is directed at the chazzan, that he should not begin prompting the word ‘Yevarechecha’ before the congregation has finished Amen. The kohanim need no warning, for in any case they wait for the prompter to first read ‘Yevarechecha.’ Beis Yosef likewise explained the Tur’s statement that the kohanim do not begin another blessing until Amen has ended from the mouth of the congregation as referring to the prompter. He also explained that it refers to the blessing ‘we have done what You decreed upon us,’ etc., as the Rama concludes here, that the kohanim should not begin to say ‘Ribono shel olam,’ etc. My teacher and father-in-law asked on this, since above it was already ruled that the kohanim wait until the shaliach tzibbur begins Sim Shalom and only then turn their faces and say ‘Ribon,’ etc. It appears to me to answer that often the chazzan begins Sim Shalom while a minority of people are still answering Amen, and we do not find this forbidden. Therefore the warning was to the kohanim not to say ‘we have done what You decreed upon us,’ etc., until the entire congregation has finished Amen, for the answering of Amen belongs to the blessing and is like an extended blessing. Therefore one cannot say ‘we have done what You decreed’ until the act is finished, as long as some are still answering.
