They are not. See Taz. Although he writes 'the entire congregation,' he was not satisfied by Magen Avraham, letter 28. Beis Yosef's question speaks of a majority, but once he cited Tosafos he was satisfied; Rama therefore did not comment here. Taz follows his approach in letter 12, like Perishah and Tosafos' first answer: The prompter may not say Kohanim until Amen has ended from the entire congregation, and all the more the chazzan must wait for everyone before Yevarechecha. Levush, like Rama in se'if 13, writes that the prompter does not say Yevarechecha until Amen has ended from the congregation, omitting 'all' like Magen Avraham, letter 28, because the prompter need not wait for the minority. Regarding Rama's gloss, Levush corrects the wording so that the chazzan begins Sim Shalom after the congregation answers Amen; then the kohanim begin Ribbono shel Olam when the chazzan opens Sim Shalom. Taz explains that before Sim Shalom the chazzan need not wait for the minority, as in siman 124:9. Although one might distinguish that no waiting is needed there but here he must wait until the kohanim finish Shalom, since a minority still prolonging Amen remains within Birchas Kohanim, Taz holds that here too the entire congregation must finish. The kohanim cannot say, 'We have done what You decreed,' because they bless even the minority; even one person; see Taz, letter 23, and Magen Avraham, letter 38. Until the minority finishes Amen, they have not completed the blessing. Magen Avraham disagrees from beginning to end in letter 28. See there; with Hashem's help this will be explained there.
