72) He should not recite the Priestly Blessing. He might be unable to concentrate sufficiently to resume at Sim Shalom, because fear of the congregation confuses him. Even if he is confident he will not become confused, the Sages did not permit him to leave his place and ascend when other kohanim are present and the Priestly Blessing will not be canceled.
73) His hands. Rather, he remains standing while another person who is not a kohen prompts the kohanim, as stated in se'if 22.
74) But if they told him, etc. This means that they told him during Retzeh or earlier. If they told him after Retzeh ended, he may not ascend, as stated in se'if 8. Magen Avraham and other later authorities. See above, se'if katan 9, for Eliyah Rabbah's view in Maharam Mintz's name.
75) For he violates a positive command. The later authorities therefore incline that even if he is not confident he can resume the prayer, he should ascend. Concern for confusing the prayer, whose essential obligation is rabbinic, does not justify violating a Torah-level positive command. Especially nowadays, when people pray from a siddur, he should certainly ascend.
76) Confident that he can resume. For us, who pray from a siddur, he is considered confident that he can resume his prayer. Nevertheless, when other kohanim are present, he should not move his feet, because even for one who is confident, permission was granted only where no other kohen is present.
(77) A little in avodah — to go toward the duchan side; and the reason because of that which is stated above in seif 8 — see there.
78) He ascends the platform. Meaning that he moves fully from his place and stands on the platform face to face with the congregation, as required.
79) He recites the Priestly Blessing and another prompts him. He first recites the blessing 'Who sanctified us, etc.,' and another person then prompts him word by word. It cannot mean that the other person first calls 'Kohanim,' because he is the only kohen present, and above in se'if 10 we ruled that 'Kohanim' is not called for one kohen.
80) It is preferable. This refers to the prayer leader who is confident he can resume. Even then, the prayer leader himself descends afterward and finishes Sim Shalom only if the prompter did not intend to hear the entire repetition from beginning to end, and certainly if he spoke during it. The prompter then cannot finish Sim Shalom because he missed the preceding blessings. If he listened from beginning to end, however, hearing is equivalent to reciting, so it is preferable that he himself finish Sim Shalom. This avoids interrupting the prayer while the prayer leader walks from the platform back to his place before the ark. The prayer leader must likewise intend to hear the prompter's Sim Shalom; otherwise, his own prayer would extend only from the beginning through the words preceding Sim Shalom.
81) That the prompter finish Sim Shalom. Gra's view in his commentary is that it is always better for the prayer leader himself to finish. Derech HaChaim, Chayei Adam, and other later authorities likewise state without qualification that the cantor finishes and do not mention the Shulchan Aruch's alternative.
