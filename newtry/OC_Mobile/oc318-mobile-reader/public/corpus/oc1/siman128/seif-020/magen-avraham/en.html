Assured that he will return. For us, who pray from the siddur, he is assured that he will return to his prayer; nevertheless, when there are other kohanim, he should not uproot his feet (Lechem Chamudos). The Bach wrote: Our custom now is that when the chazzan is a kohen and there are other kohanim, a Yisrael who was concentrating on the prayer stands and begins "Our God," etc., until "with peace," and the chazzan returns and says Kaddish, end quote. Their words imply that when there are other kohanim he should not raise his hands, as Maharam wrote. In the responsa of Maharil, siman 179, he wrote: Although there would appear to be some room to distinguish, since nowadays they prompt from writing and he will not err so much, nevertheless who would come after the king, Maharam, end quote. Now the custom is that he raises his hands even if another kohen is there, and I do not know from where they derived this. In Nachalas Tzvi it is written that they rely on the fact that we pray from the siddur. This is surprising, for all the Geonim I cited did not wish to rely on this. Perhaps they hold as the Bach wrote in siman 585 in the name of Maharshal, that according to the Tur there, who permits the chazzan to blow shofar, the same applies here, that he may raise his hands even if other kohanim are there. But to me this is very forced, for the Tur ruled plainly here like Maharam. Rather, the main point is as written above. Therefore it appears to me that in a place with no established custom, he should not raise his hands if there are other kohanim, in accordance with the opinion of all the Geonim.
