And if the prompter had intention for the prayer leader's prayer from beginning to end. However, even for the prompter alone it is good that he have intention in his heart for the Amidah from beginning to end. And it is difficult — for one who spoke during the time the prayer leader was praying (Mordechai) cannot prompt. And even if the prayer leader returns afterward to the prayer and says "Grant peace," it is good to listen to the entire prayer (Beit Yosef and Mahari). But in Hagahot Maimoniyot it implies: if the prayer leader says "Grant peace," we have no concern regarding this — only if the prayer leader does not say "Grant peace," then one must be careful that another not stand in his place to prompt and say "Grant peace" — only one who has intention for the entire prayer with the prayer leader. And it appears that we deal with a case where the prayer leader is not assured that he will return to his prayer; for if so, the prayer leader himself should return and finish the prayer. And we must say: that which we say that if he is not assured that he will return to his prayer he does not lift his hands — that is specifically when he himself finishes the prayer; but if there is another who finishes the prayer, he may lift his hands (Beit Yosef). And it does not appear so from the plain sense of our Mishnah; rather, whoever is not assured that the prayer leader will return to his prayer is not permitted to ascend the duchan. And there is no remedy that the prompter have intention from beginning to end while the prompter finishes "Grant peace," since he is not assured — and so too my teacher in Bach. And there are those who say now that since they pray from the siddur, there is no concern for confusion of mind of one who lifts his hands even where there are other priests who call "Kohanim" afterward (Levush). Two priests who hate one another — even so they are obligated to ascend the duchan together, even if there are no priests there except those two; and even if they have vowed benefit from one another, they are permitted to perform a mitzvah together, for mitzvot were not given for enjoyment. And there is one who says that three who vowed benefit from one another do not combine for a zimmun — see Yoreh De'ah siman 217, essay 27, and in siman 221 seif 13, and in Orach Chayyim siman 485 seif 5, and in siman 199 seif 11. And one who does not wish to ascend with his fellow — one cannot force his fellow to split with him the lifting of hands, to say to him "You ascend at Shacharit and I at Musaf," for his fellow can say "I do not wish to nullify my mitzvah, and I will ascend the duchan every time" — and it is not all within your power to nullify my mitzvah because of your foolishness (responsa of Maharam Mintz, siman 12, section 4). But a priest who hates the congregation, or the congregation hates him — he shall not ascend the duchan; and this priest has great danger when he ascends the duchan (Zohar, Parashat Nasso 134b).
