Specifically. See Taz. Eliyah Rabbah, letter 60, objects at length: The difficulty is stronger, for why does Tur say that one who is thirteen and has two pubic hairs may not duchan alone today, when today's practice is only occasional? Moreover, the phrase 'with other kohanim' is difficult, since even regularly he is permitted with others. To me, Taz's words are clear. Tosafos in Megillah notes three statements: One implies that a minor without two hairs may not duchan, but one with them may; another says that once a child knows how to raise his hands he may duchan even without two hairs; a third requires a full beard, implying that two hairs do not suffice. There must be three categories. A true minor may not duchan alone even occasionally; one with two hairs may duchan alone occasionally; and a true minor may duchan with other kohanim, all the more one with two hairs. Perhaps he may even do so regularly with others, though this is not compelled. To duchan regularly alone, he must have a full beard. Another reconciliation is possible: Alone, even occasionally, he may not duchan until his beard is full, because otherwise it dishonors the congregation. The statement implying that two hairs suffice concerns duchaning regularly with others; a true minor is permitted with others only occasionally for training, not regularly every day. The statement allowing a minor with others concerns an occasional case, while the statement requiring a full beard concerns duchaning alone even occasionally. This resolves all three passages and vindicates Tur. He therefore writes that both a true minor and one with two hairs may never duchan alone, even occasionally. With others, both may do so occasionally; regularly, a true minor is forbidden even with others. Today there is no regular duchaning, so Tur equates them and permits even a minor with others.
