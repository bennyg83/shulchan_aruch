Lechem Chamudos and Bach imply that when other kohanim are present, the chazzan may not duchan even if he is certain he can return. Bach writes that if there are no other kohanim and the chazzan leaves his place, the prompter should recite Sim Shalom; even when other kohanim are present and the chazzan remains in place, another person prompts and the custom is that this prompter recites Sim Shalom. This makes sense only if the chazzan may not duchan when other kohanim are present. Taz disputes the custom and holds that when other kohanim are present the chazzan himself should recite Sim Shalom, noting that this was the earlier practice in Bach's presence without protest.
Maharam rules that the chazzan may not answer Amen even when he is certain he will not become confused. Maharil writes that today, when the chazzan prompts from a siddur, there is no concern of confusion and he may answer. I do not have Maharil's responsum available. The authorities did not wish to rely on this, for Maharil wrote explicitly that one should not rely on it regarding Amen, and the same applies to duchaning. Other great authorities follow Maharam. Maharam himself did not permit the chazzan to duchan when other kohanim were present, even with assurance, although in their era they already prayed from siddurim; only in Talmudic times did they pray orally.
Tur there permits the chazzan to blow the shofar if he is certain he will not become confused, even when another person is available.
One might therefore say that the custom relies upon Tur, who must disagree with Maharam. This is very forced, for it makes Tur contradict himself. The principal distinction is that blowing the shofar is permitted even when another is available because the chazzan remains in his place, whereas duchaning requires him to move. He may not move when other kohanim are present, even if he is certain he will not become confused. The custom therefore has no basis.
