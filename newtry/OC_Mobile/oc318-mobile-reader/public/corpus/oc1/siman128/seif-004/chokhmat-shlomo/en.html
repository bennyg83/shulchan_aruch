(Seif 22) They strive that the mikra reader be an Israelite, etc. NB: see Taz in the name of Rambam, who derived this law from the verse "say to them," which implies he is not of them, etc.; see there. And behold, in my view it is very surprising from Shas, folio 38, where it says there: R' Chiyya taught: we hold a Kohen calls "Kohanim" and an Israelite does not call "Kohanim," as it is said "say to them" — from their own. If so, we see that on the contrary it is explicit that "say to them" specifically means from their own; and Abbaye concludes there that the halachah is not like R' Chiyya — that is, only that we do not require it to be specifically from their own; but from where do we derive the reverse, that specifically he is not from their own? And from the Torah we should innovate that they should divide into opposite views: for R' Chiyya the explanation of "say to them" will be "from their own," and for Abbaye the intent will be literally the opposite, that "say to them" and he is not of them; and it is uncertain in this; and understand well.
