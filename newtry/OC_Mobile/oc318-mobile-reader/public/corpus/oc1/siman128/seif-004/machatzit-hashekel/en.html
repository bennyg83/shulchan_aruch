When the kohanim, etc. This is from Mordechai. See Beis Yosef at the end of the siman for the reason.
Bach asks why Tur writes, 'Any kohen who has no impediment,' rather than simply saying that any kohen who does not ascend violates the command. It is obvious that one with an impediment cannot ascend. He answers that even when the impediment is only rabbinic, although one might think the Torah command should override it, he does not violate the command. The chazzan's call is not directed to kohanim whom the Sages disqualified, and it is therefore as though he was not called.
Beis Yosef adds, 'if he is in the synagogue or if they told him to ascend.' This implies that even if he was explicitly called, he does not violate the command when he has a rabbinic impediment. This must be the case, for otherwise Bach's question remains.
But the Sages prevented him. Tosafos on Shabbos 4a discusses one who placed bread in an oven close to nightfall: May he remove it on Shabbos, despite the rabbinic prohibition, before it bakes and he incurs stoning? Even if the Sages did not permit removal, he would surely ignore their prohibition to avoid stoning. Tosafos answers that once the Sages prohibited removing it, he no longer incurs liability for the baking.
In siman 191 the Sages permitted workers to abbreviate Birchas Hamazon so as not to cause the employer a loss of labor. Although Birchas Hamazon is a Torah obligation, the Sages have authority to suspend a Torah law passively.
