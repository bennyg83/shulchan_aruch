There — and they did not see the words of Tosafot, etc. And per Tosafot in that passage in Gittin — they forbid him not to benefit from his invalid wife, and when he specifies the law they will not permit him; unlike Rashba and Shulchan Aruch, who hold one must specify the reason. If so, the court that forbids him forbids him in general not to marry a divorcée — for there is no concern at release that he will need to explain the reason to them; therefore they were concerned lest he come before a sage who holds like Tosafot, and if so when he does not explain the reason he will permit him plainly, for he will tell him the language of the vow — not to marry a divorcée — and he will not know that because he is a kohen he vowed thus; therefore they enacted that they forbid him in the presence of many. And although it was possible to enact that they forbid him not to benefit from his invalid wife, nevertheless since they needed to enact something regarding the vow — how would it be better to forbid him in the presence of many, for even one who comes to a sage who holds one need not specify cannot permit; but regarding a widow who does not need to enact the vow at all — they did not need to add that she forbid in the presence of many for this alone, lest one err that one need not specify, and one not say the language to her alone — we are not concerned, since all poskim ruled one must specify; unlike a kohen, since in any case one must enact some correction regarding the vow — better is this correction that he forbid in the presence of many, whereby we escape all concerns:
