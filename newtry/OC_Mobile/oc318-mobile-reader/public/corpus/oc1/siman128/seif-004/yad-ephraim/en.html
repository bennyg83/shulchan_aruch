There: They did not consider the words of Tosafos, etc. According to Tosafos in Gittin, they impose upon him a vow that he will not derive benefit from his wife who is prohibited to him; when he specifies the relevant circumstance, the sage will not release the vow. Rashba and Shulchan Aruch, by contrast, maintain that the cause of the vow must be specified. Accordingly, if the court merely imposes an unspecified vow upon him not to marry a divorcee, there should be no concern that it will be released, because he would have to explain the cause to the sage. Nevertheless, they feared that he might approach a sage who follows Tosafos and does not require specification. If he does not explain the cause, that sage will readily release the vow, for the man will merely state that he vowed not to marry a divorcee, and the sage will not know that he vowed because he is a kohen. They therefore instituted that the vow be taken publicly. Although they could instead have instituted that he vow not to derive benefit from a woman forbidden to him, once it was necessary to establish some form of vow, a public vow was preferable, since even a sage who holds that the cause need not be specified cannot release it. Regarding a widow, however, there was no need to institute a vow at all. They therefore did not add a requirement that she vow publicly merely because of the isolated concern that she might encounter a sage who does not require specification. One should not say that this concern alone should still have been taken into account, since all the poskim rule that specification is required. Regarding the kohen, by contrast, some corrective enactment concerning the vow was necessary in any event; the superior enactment was therefore to require a public vow, which removes every concern.
