And another should read for him, etc. The Levush implies that if he is confident he must himself complete Sim Shalom, and so it is implied in Beis Yosef. Nachalas Tzvi challenged the Levush, for Beis Yosef cited Agur that Rashi and Maharam do not dispute: according to Rashi the shaliach tzibbur completes Sim Shalom, meaning where the prompter did not have intent, and Maharam speaks where the prompter had intent. He did not distinguish that Rashi speaks when he is confident and Maharam when he is not confident. Thus even when he is confident the prompter completes Sim Shalom, and if he is not confident the remedy of another completing does not help. His words are astonishing, for would Beis Yosef contradict himself immediately and not write in the language of dispute? Rather, Rashi cannot be explained as speaking where he is confident, for Rashi explained so at the beginning of the Mishnah in Ein Omdin, and the beginning speaks where he is not confident; therefore it was necessary to distinguish between whether he had intent or not. So appears to me to be the view of the Levush. However, now Lechem Chamudos wrote that the shaliach tzibbur should always complete Sim Shalom, since he prays from a siddur, and so it is implied in Hagahos Maimoniyos.
