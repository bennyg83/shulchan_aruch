He should not. See Taz, who agrees that assurance does not help here. In se'if 20 Shulchan Aruch permits it so that duchaning not be entirely canceled; otherwise he does not. Although one must answer Amen as in siman 216, here he is not obligated, especially when other kohanim are present. It is proper to fulfill a positive commandment, but not where the mitzvah will not be canceled entirely. Peri Chadash rules simply that an assured chazzan answers Amen, following his approach in se'if 20. We have only Shulchan Aruch's view, and halachah is not derived from Midrash. Taz writes that the chazzan answers Amen after 'with love'; Levush likewise implies this. Kesef Mishneh, Hilchos Tefillah 14:5, is uncertain, for perhaps the Sages made no distinction. Whether an individual who has reached HaTov Shimcha in his Amidah may answer Amen after 'with love' and the like depends on the Bach; Taz dispute: Bach would require him to have begun earlier; according to Taz it is proper. See Eliyah Rabbah, letter 38. In any event, after Shalom the chazzan should not answer Amen because of confusion, since he must say Sim Shalom. With Hashem's help I shall write in siman 215 that if two people recited blessings over fruit, one may not answer Amen to the other's blessing before tasting. Responsa Panim Meiros, part 2, siman 5, writes likewise and proves it from Tur, siman 59. There is no need to elaborate here.
