He should not. See Taz, who agreed that assurance does not help here. In seif 20 the Mechaber wrote that he should not be prevented entirely, but otherwise not. Although he is obligated to answer Amen, as in siman 216, here he is not obligated, and certainly if there are other kohanim. Although it is proper to fulfill a positive commandment whenever it is not entirely nullified, etc., Peri Chadash rules plainly that he answers if he is assured, following his view in seif 20. We have only the view of the Mechaber, and one does not derive halacha from Midrash. What the Taz wrote, that he answers Amen after ‘with love,’ is also implied in the Levush. See Kesef Mishneh, Hilchos Tefillah 14:5, who was uncertain about this, for perhaps the Sages did not distinguish. Know that an individual standing in prayer who reached ‘haTov shimcha,’ etc., whether he may answer Amen after ‘with love’ and the like, depends on the dispute of Bach and Taz. According to Bach he should have preceded; according to Taz it is proper. See Eliyah Rabbah, letter 38. In any event, after ‘Shalom’ the shaliach tzibbur should not answer Amen because of confusion, for he must say Sim Shalom.
