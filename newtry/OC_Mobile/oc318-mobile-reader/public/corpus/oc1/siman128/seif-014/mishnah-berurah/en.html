(50) One does not bless etc. — all this is from the Torah for we derive all this from verses: in holy tongue as written 'so shall you bless' in this language; and standing as written 'to serve Him and to bless in His name' — what service is standing as written 'to stand to serve' so too blessing; and nesiut kafim as written 'and Aharon raised his hands toward the people and blessed them'; and loud voice as written 'say to them' like a person saying to his fellow. Further they derive in the Gemara there must be face to face; and most latter authorities and almost all wrote all these are impediments and even b'dieved they do not fulfill otherwise. In all these it is good he go outside before Retzeh when he cannot bless properly [latter authorities].
51) And standing. The kohanim must stand, but the congregation may sit, provided they remain face to face. Magen Avraham. The custom is for everyone to stand. This is also implied by Meiri on Sotah, chapter 7, and Megillah, chapter 3, and by Eshkol, laws of the Priestly Blessing, who writes that everyone must stand before them with awe and gravity. A weak kohen who can stand only by leaning may not recite the Priestly Blessing, because leaning is equivalent to sitting. Later authorities.
52) And raising the hands. One whose hands tremble and who cannot raise them may not recite the Priestly Blessing; this requirement invalidates even after the fact. It does not help to make supports tied to his hat and insert his hands so they remain raised, because then they are supported, while actual raising of the hands is required. Noda BiYehudah and Magen Gibborim, cited in Shaarei Teshuvah. If he can raise them briefly, however, he should raise them while pronouncing Yevarechecha, lower them during the melody between words, and raise them again while pronouncing each word, as Responsa Ksav Sofer, siman 13, writes.
53) And in a loud voice. This means a medium voice, excluding a whisper. Sometimes an actually loud voice is required; for example, when the congregation is large; because the entire congregation must hear, as Sifrei states. Peri Chadash. It therefore appears obvious that one whose voice is hoarse and who can speak only in a whisper cannot recite the Priestly Blessing; it is best that he leave before Retzeh.
