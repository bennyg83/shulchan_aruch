[29] In the holy tongue, etc. In Sotah, folio 38, they learned by a gezeirah shavah from Har Gerizim that it requires the holy tongue; standing is learned by a gezeirah shavah from “these shall stand to bless”; and in a loud voice, as it is written, “say to them,” like a person speaking to his fellow. Ateres Tzvi wrote that the Levush wrote other derivations, because he did not see the source of the law in Shas; end quote. In my humble opinion, the Levush saw two proofs, for he also saw what is written in the Tur and Sifrei, and he relied on the verse “So shall you bless.” The Beis Yosef wrote: I do not know why our master wrote that in Sifrei it is supported, for in our Shas too they support it from verses; end quote. The Levush answers that this is what the Tur meant: in Sifrei they attach it to the verse “So shall you bless” itself, and not from another verse learned from our Shas. So too it implies in the Perishah, who wrote regarding these words of the Beis Yosef as follows: It is possible that in Sifrei it supports it from the body of “So shall you bless,” that Hashem spoke with Moshe in the holy tongue, standing, and in a loud voice, and said to Moshe, “So shall you bless,” except that Nesias Kapayim is difficult, for it is not alluded to in the word “so”; end quote. In my humble opinion, it is possible that the Holy One, blessed is He, showed him Nesias Kapayim and said, “So shall you bless,” as Rashi explains regarding “This month shall be for you” and “This they shall give, everyone who passes,” etc. So it appears to me from the words of Rambam, chapter 17, unlike the Kesef Mishneh and Lechem Mishneh; see there and examine carefully. Levush wrote regarding standing, that above there is no sitting, etc. This is difficult to me, for “there is no sitting” refers to the heavenly host, but regarding the Holy One, blessed is He, it is written, “I saw Hashem sitting on a throne,” and “God, the King, sits,” as we say in the chapter Ein Dorshin regarding the incident of Acher. Granted, according to the Perishah's explanation, he means that at that time the Holy One, blessed is He, stood. A proof for this appears to me from Megillah, folio 21, which says: From where do we know that Torah is taught standing? As it is written, “And you stand here with Me,” so to speak, even the Holy One, blessed is He, was standing. But on the Levush this is difficult. Perhaps his intent is that since there is no sitting above, necessarily Moshe stood when He said to him, “So shall you bless.” Yet this is difficult, for it is written, “and I sat on the mountain,” and in Megillah it says that difficult matters were taught while sitting. Why, if there is no sitting above? Rather, one must say that the reason is that Moshe was below ten handbreadths, as stated in Sukkah, folio 5. Or it means that flesh and blood is different, for one cannot err regarding him as divine. Alternatively, permission was given to him for the honor of Torah, as we find regarding Metatron, that permission was given to him to sit and write the merits of Israel. In a similar manner, my father and master, the rabbi, may his light shine, explained the Midrash on Ruth, “At that time Michael will stand”: Is there sitting above? Rather, he was silenced from teaching advocacy for Israel. Meaning, since he was silenced, he has no permission to sit and must stand. Tosafot, folio 38, s.v. harei, wrote that it appears to Ri that a kohen who blessed while sitting has done nothing, for blessing is compared to the service, and one who sits invalidates the service; therefore here too he must go back and bless while standing; end quote. Knesset HaGedolah wrote that the same applies to the holy tongue and the other requirements, that they are indispensable. Olas Tamid wrote: Nevertheless, it appears that it is good for the kohen to go outside when he cannot bless according to the law. It is possible that if the congregation sits facing the kohanim, there is nothing wrong, for specifically the kohen is warned; and so wrote Magen Avraham. See above, siman 23.
