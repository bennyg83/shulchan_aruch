11) [Se'if 2] Every kohen who has none of, etc. A kohen may not contract impurity in order to recite the Priestly Blessing. Tosafos, Sotah 38b, in the name of the Yerushalmi, Nazir, chapter Kohen Gadol; cited by Eliyah Rabbah, os 3, and Mishbetzos Zahav, os 3. Mareh HaPanim on Nazir explains that contracting impurity violates both a positive and a negative command, and one positive command cannot override both. Pesach HaDevir, os 1, cites him. Eliyah Rabbah writes that even if the kohen has already washed his hands to ascend and then hears that impurity is present, he must leave the synagogue. So too Har HaMoriah, os 2: Even if told to ascend, he does not contract impurity for the blessing. Mishbetzos Zahav says that if the impurity is in an adjoining house he need not leave, because transmission in that case is rabbinic while the Priestly Blessing is biblical, even when he knows he must bless. See our words above, siman 126, os 13, where we wrote that according to some, if an opening one tefach wide connects them, the impurity is biblical. If he knows or is informed of it, he must therefore leave.
11) A synagogue stands in a courtyard surrounded by projecting roofs and porticoes. A corpse, Heaven preserve us, lies in one house, all of whose doors are locked. Since the rule that impurity is destined ultimately to leave through an entrance is rabbinic, a kohen may enter the synagogue to recite the Priestly Blessing, because he enters for a mitzvah. Responsa Ramaz, siman 15. In siman 16 he writes that he sent this ruling to Rabbi Shmuel Aboab, who agreed in practice. It is cited by Ikarei HaDat, siman 5, os 58, and Beis Menuchah, os 2. See Responsa Devar Shmuel, siman 223, which cites Ramaz.
11) Impurity was present in a house adjoining the synagogue and entered through the women's-section window. The sages of Eretz Yisrael ruled on Shabbos that the window, which measured a tefach by a tefach, could be closed with wool. The wool was suitable for lying upon and therefore not muktzeh, and closing it did not violate building, because adding to an existing structure is permitted. They likewise permitted closing it with a wooden lattice. This was both for the congregation's honor, so they would not pray where impurity entered through the window, and because they should not be left without the Priestly Blessing recited with love. Leket HaKemach, Yoreh Deah, page 123c; Shaliach Tzibbur, page 140a; Beis Menuchah, os 3.
11) From Rabbi Leket HaKemach's statement that the sages of Eretz Yisrael ordered the window closed on Shabbos because the congregation should not be left without the Priestly Blessing, it follows that if repair had been impossible, the kohanim could not have blessed. They may not remain in the synagogue because impurity enters through the window, and the mitzvah of the Priestly Blessing does not override impurity. This also follows from Devar Shmuel, siman 204, and Taz, Yoreh Deah 371:3. So writes Heichal HaKodesh in Shaliach Tzibbur there and Beis Menuchah, os 4. See Heichal HaKodesh's lengthy discussion there and our words above, siman 126, os 13.
11) Regarding the Priestly Blessing outside the Temple in Temple times, where the explicit Divine Name was not pronounced, and likewise today: Keren Orah, in his novellae to Sotah 38, discusses whether it is biblical or rabbinic and concludes that it is biblical. This follows from Rambam's unqualified language in Hilchos Tefillah; Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi's statement that every kohen who does not ascend violates three positive commands; the Yerushalmi cited by Tosafos there asking whether a kohen may contract impurity for the blessing; and the entire discussion in Kesubos, chapter HaIshah Shenisarmlah, about using the Priestly Blessing as evidence of lineage. Yalkut likewise cites Sifrei Zuta interpreting 'in every place where I cause My Name to be mentioned' as including outside the Temple. Peri Megadim appears to agree in Eshel Avraham, os 1, and Mishbetzos Zahav, os 3. Although Mor Uketziah repeatedly writes here that the blessing outside the Temple in Temple times, and likewise today, is only rabbinic and adduces several proofs, the great scholar Pesach HaDevir, os 4, already addresses his words. He proves clearly from many Talmudic passages and from leading early and later commentators and poskim that it remains a biblical positive command today. Mor Uketziah himself did not maintain his position, for Responsa She'eilas Yaavetz, part 1, siman 54, calls it a biblical positive command today. Pesach HaDevir's addenda, page 217d, notes that Mabit states explicitly in Kiryas Sefer, chapter 14 of Hilchos Tefillah, that it is biblical outside the Temple as well. So rule Maharshach in the newly printed Binyan Shlomo, siman 10, and his brother in Reishis Bikkurim, page 10b. Sedei Chemed, part 3, Maareches Divrei Chachamim, siman 64, Orach Chaim, os 3, cites them. This also appears from Maran's words here and Shulchan Aruch HaRav, os 3.
