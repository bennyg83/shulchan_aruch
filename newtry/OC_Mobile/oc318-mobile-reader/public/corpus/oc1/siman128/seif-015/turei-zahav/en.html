It is preferable, etc. In Beis Yosef he brought Hagahos Maimoniyos in the name of Maharam, who wrote this law, that the prompter should say Sim Shalom and have intent for what the shaliach tzibbur said from the beginning. He wrote that this speaks where the shaliach tzibbur is not assured, for otherwise the shaliach tzibbur himself would finish Sim Shalom. He asked: if so, why did the tanna say that he may perform Nesias Kapayim only if he is assured? It is possible even if he is not assured, where another, the prompter, finishes Sim Shalom. He answered that this indeed helps, but the tanna of the Mishnah did not discuss where another finishes the prayer, only where he himself finishes. But here in Shulchan Aruch it implies that another finishing does not help so that he need not be assured; rather, even where he is assured, this is mentioned, that if he wants another to finish, the other must hear from beginning to end. In this it is preferable. One cannot say that ‘preferable’ refers to one who is not assured, meaning that one who is not assured is preferable to one who is assured and finishes himself; this is obvious nonsense. How can what is not mentioned in the Mishnah be preferable to what is mentioned? Also, in Beis Yosef, when he rules this as preferable when the prompter finishes, he brings Rashi’s view, and that refers to where he is assured; see there. His words in Beis Yosef astonish me: why did they say in the Mishnah that he should not perform Nesias Kapayim because of confusion, when we easily have a way that he will not come to confusion? His words here are correct. One should not ask why in truth he should not perform Nesias Kapayim even if not assured, since the concern is his return to prayer and another can do it. Certainly the tanna speaks only where he himself finishes, and from this we learn that another finishing does not suffice, for he must have intent to hear from the shaliach tzibbur, and because of confusion he will not be able to listen. Therefore his words here are primary: he specifically must be assured, and then it is preferable if another prompts and says Sim Shalom, unlike what the Levush wrote, that even if he is not assured this suffices.
