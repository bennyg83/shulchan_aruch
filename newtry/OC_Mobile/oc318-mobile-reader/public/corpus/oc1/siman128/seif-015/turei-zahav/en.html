It is better, etc. In Beit Yosef the Beit Yosef brought Maggid Mishneh in the name of Maharam who wrote that the reader says "Shema" and intends what the prayer leader began with "Shema"; and he wrote on this that it refers when he is not assured — for otherwise the prayer leader himself would finish Shema. And he challenged this: if so why did the tanna say he may not raise his hands except when assured — we find even when not assured, such as when another finishes Shema; and answer: if so it helps only that the tanna of the mishnah does not discuss when another finishes prayer but when he himself finishes; but here in Shulchan Aruch it implies that after another finishes it does not help regarding not needing to be assured, but even when assured remember this: if he wishes another to finish afterward he must hear from beginning to end; and in this "it is better" refers to one who is not assured — meaning this one who is not assured is better than one who is assured and finishes himself — which is obvious; how could what is not mentioned in the mishnah be better than what is mentioned. And further in Beit Yosef when he decides that it is better when the reader finishes he brings Rashi's view — and that refers when assured, see there. And I wonder at his words in Beit Yosef why they said in the mishnah he may not raise his hands because of confusion when we have an easy way that he not come to confusion; and his words here are correct. And one may say: why indeed may he not raise his hands even when not assured, since the concern is returning to his prayer; and perhaps when another finishes certainly the tanna does not discuss except when he himself finishes — from this we learn that finishing by another does not suffice, for he must intend to hear from beginning to end; and from this "it is better" is understood.
