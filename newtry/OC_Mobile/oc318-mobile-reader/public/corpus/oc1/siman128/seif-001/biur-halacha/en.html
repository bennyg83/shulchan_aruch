Sefer Chareidim writes that it is a positive commandment for a kohen to bless the Jewish people, as it is stated, "So shall you bless the children of Israel." The Jews who stand silently facing the kohanim and direct their hearts to receive their blessing as the word of Hashem are also included in the mitzvah. The later authorities cite this statement.
There is no raising of the hands, etc. The reason is that this is a sacred matter. Ran writes, however, that only Kaddish, Kedushah, and Barchu are technically called sacred matters. The requirement of ten for Bircas Kohanim is derived by a verbal analogy. Here it is written, "So shall you bless the children of Israel," and elsewhere it is written, "I shall be sanctified among the children of Israel." Even according to Ran, however, this derivation is only an asmachta, and the requirement is D'Rabbanan. See Peri Megadim and Chayei Adam at the beginning of rule 32.
With fewer than ten. This applies even if ten were present in the synagogue when the chazzan began the repetition and some later left. Although, as ruled above in siman 55, seif 2, the entire prayer may then be completed, Bircas Kohanim may not be performed because it is an independent matter that the Sages incorporated into prayer. If Bircas Kohanim itself began with ten and some then left, it may be completed. This is stated by Ran in Megillah, cited by Beit Yosef in siman 55, and by Knesses HaGedolah, cited in Baer Heitev. Knesses HaGedolah further writes that whenever the congregation and the chazzan say the prayer together aloud, etc. His meaning follows what we wrote above in siman 124, Biur Halacha, under the heading "quietly," in the name of Peri Megadim. Therefore, I did not copy this law into Mishnah Berurah, because it is inapplicable under our custom.
A non-kohen may not, etc. See Peri Megadim, who agrees with Peri Chadash that a chalal who ascends the platform violates a positive commandment like a non-kohen, and even if he ascended, he must descend. A tumtum or androgynos should not ascend the platform. It appears to me that such a person should leave the synagogue before Retzei.
For a non-kohen violates a positive commandment. See what we wrote in Mishnah Berurah. It appears to me that all this applies specifically according to the ruling that mitzvos require intent. This raises an apparent difficulty regarding the common practice of one person, whether a kohen or a non-kohen, blessing another when escorting him with the words, "May Hashem bless you," etc. Although such a blessing is given outside the time of prayer, the Yerushalmi in the fourth chapter of Taanis states that we do not find Bircas Kohanim apart from prayer. Yet this is certainly only a Rabbinic enactment that fixed it within prayer, and according to Torah law the mitzvah does not depend upon prayer at all. Indeed, according to most authorities prayer itself is D'Rabbanan. Consequently, since under Torah law the mitzvah can be fulfilled through an ordinary blessing even outside prayer, and the Torah said, "You," meaning kohanim, "and not non-kohanim," how may a non-kohen bless another person using this formula? The universal custom therefore supports the ruling that mitzvos require intent. Alternatively, the custom may follow Bach, who holds that a non-kohen violates the positive commandment only when spreading his hands. Another possibility is that since the Sages enacted that Bircas Kohanim not be performed outside prayer, anyone who recites these verses outside prayer, whether a kohen or a non-kohen, is regarded as expressly intending not to fulfill the mitzvah of Bircas Kohanim, and it is therefore permitted.
