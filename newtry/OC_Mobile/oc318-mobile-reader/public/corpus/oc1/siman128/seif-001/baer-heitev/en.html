Ten. It is stated in Yerushalmi, chapter "HaKore et haMegillah," "standing" — they do not lift their hands with fewer than ten. They began with ten and some of them left — the remainder finish, therefore. And I wonder why the codifiers omitted this law — as Knesset HaGedolah. Wherever there are ten in the synagogue who do not pray the repetition except the congregation and the prayer leader together in a loud voice, and the priests complete their prayer before the congregation reaches Retzeh — they may ascend the duchan to lift their hands; and even if the prayer leader calls them, there is no interruption here, for it is required for prayer; and for ease of the matter, he should call after he finishes his prayer before Retzeh, as Knesset HaGedolah. Further, there he stammered to say that Birkat Kohanim is only in a place where there is a Torah scroll, see there. And Peri Chadash wrote that for Birkat Kohanim we do not require that there be a Torah scroll there, but wherever ten pray there is Birkat Kohanim, and that thus is the custom, see there, seif 10; and so too Eliyahu Rabbah. One who came and said he is a priest is believed to lift his hands — Rama in Orach Chayyim siman 3, seif 1; and see what I wrote there.
