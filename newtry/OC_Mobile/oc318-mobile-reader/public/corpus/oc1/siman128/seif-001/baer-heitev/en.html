From ten. It is stated in the Yerushalmi, chapter "One who reads the Megillah standing": They do not raise their hands [for the priestly blessing] with fewer than ten; if they began with ten and some of them left, [those remaining] complete [the blessing] — end quote. I wonder why the poskim omitted this ruling (Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah). Wherever there are ten in the synagogue who do not pray the repetition [aloud], but rather the congregation and the chazzan [say it] together aloud, and the kohanim complete their prayer before the congregation reaches "Retzeh," they may ascend the duchan to raise their hands; and even if the chazzan calls to them, there is no interruption here, for it is a need of the prayer. For good measure he should call them after he has finished his own prayer, before "Retzeh" (Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah). Further there, he was hesitant to say that there is no priestly blessing except in a place where there is a Sefer Torah — see there. But the Peri Chadash wrote that for the priestly blessing we do not require that there be a Sefer Torah; rather, in any place where ten pray there is a priestly blessing, and that this is the practice — see there, se'if 10; and so wrote the Olas Tamid. One who comes and says that he is a kohen is believed to raise his hands (Rama in Even HaEzer, siman 3, se'if 1); and see what I wrote there.
