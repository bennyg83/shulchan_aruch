1) There is no, etc. Its law in every respect is as explained above in siman 55 regarding Kaddish. See Mishnah Berurah there, se'if katan 33, where we cited Peri Megadim's doubt whether a sleeping person counts toward the quorum. Some say the Priestly Blessing is recited only where a Torah scroll is present, but most later authorities, indeed nearly all of them, disagree. Some say that one who arrives and says, 'I am a kohen,' is believed for purposes of reciting the Priestly Blessing, and this is the custom. Rama, Even HaEzer 3:1, in the gloss.
2) Toward the quorum. The kohanim are themselves included in the blessing, as the verse says, 'And I will bless them,' meaning the kohanim. Even if most of the quorum are kohanim, it is valid, provided at least one Israelite is present to answer Amen after the Priestly Blessing. Regarding a synagogue consisting entirely of kohanim, see below, se'if katan 25.
3) A non-kohen may not. The verse says, 'So shall you bless, etc.'; you, and not non-kohanim. A prohibition inferred from a positive command has the status of a positive command. Bach writes that this applies specifically when he raises his hands, even if he omitted the preliminary blessing 'Who sanctified us, etc.' If he recites the Priestly Blessing without raising his hands, Bach holds that he does not violate the positive command, while Peri Megadim implies that some prohibition exists in every case. Magen Gibborim writes that the Torah's exclusion 'you, and not non-kohanim' applies only when he intends to fulfill the mitzvah. If he has no mitzvah intent and ascends merely to avoid contradicting companions who mistakenly believe he is a kohen and tell him to ascend, there is plainly no violation of the positive command; see there. It appears to me that this is true only under our ruling above, siman 60:4, that mitzvos require intent. Under the contrary view, it would be permitted only if he explicitly intends not to fulfill the mitzvah, or does not intend to bestow a blessing at all. See Biur Halachah.
4) Even, etc. Meaning: Even if we assume that a non-kohen does not violate the positive command when he blesses together with kohanim, because 'you, and not non-kohanim' excludes only one who blesses alone, he is nevertheless forbidden to do so because his preliminary blessing, 'Who sanctified us, etc.,' is a blessing in vain. The Torah commanded only kohanim to bless Israel.
5) A non-kohen who ascends. Tosafos means that the only apparent prohibition is the blessing in vain, since the Torah commanded the kohanim to bless Israel.
6) And perhaps together with, etc. Meaning: Ri says that in this case the non-kohen does not violate the positive command, but only the prohibition of a blessing in vain. As practical halachah, the later authorities agree that no distinction should be made; in every case he violates the positive command.
