Seif 1 in the gloss: A non-kohen may not raise his hands even with other kohanim — see Magen Avraham what he wrote to settle the Rama's difficulty that R' Yehudah holds per his method that women may perform semicha as optional. They showed me in Etz Chaim siman 3 wondering on Magen Avraham: if so why did Tosafot write perhaps because of a blessing in vain, after all Tosafot wrote in Rosh Hashana that women bless on positive time-bound commandments — so it would not be a blessing in vain — see there. In my opinion Magen Avraham's words are sound: for a mitzvah that is by action such as shofar, tefillin, or lulav — though he is not commanded, nevertheless he performs a mitzvah and there is an act, therefore at least he does an act and may bless. But Birkat Kohanim is only speech and the main intention is to bless Israel — that Israel be blessed — and if the blessing takes effect it is a mitzvah; but if the blessing does not take effect there is no mitzvah; since in any case non-kohanim have no mitzvah to bless, they may bless but the blessing does not take effect and it remains a doubt whether the blessing takes effect — so they did nothing and how is a blessing relevant when it is nothing — and this fits well. Further one may say this is like Rashi in Berakhot 20a s.v. she'at: there is a difference regarding impurity of kohen and nazir that uproots with hands — there it is different because this mitzvah was not stated for a dead mitzva as it was not stated for relatives, etc. — I explained this in my youth and later found it in my notes in his novellae on a dead mitzva where the exclusion in the place of the prohibition is not considered overridden but the prohibition was not stated in that place and it is full permission like relatives where permission is explicit; but for Pesach and other matters where the exclusion was not stated in the place of the mitzva, only that human dignity overrides — that is mere override and is not overridden by a positive commandment — see there [the author's son said truth in his mouth intending Ramban in Sha'ar HaGemul and see responsa Atzei Chayyim Orach Chayyim from Leipnik who expanded on this]. If so here too one may say: in Shulchan Aruch a positive time-bound commandment is stated simply implying males and females; only from elsewhere we derive women are exempt from positive time-bound commandments — therefore exempt, nevertheless they may bless since it applies and "and you shall command" applies because the body of the mitzvah is stated simply; but for Birkat Kohanim since in its place it says "you" and not non-kohanim, since in its place it excludes non-kohanim, "and you shall command" does not apply and it is well a blessing in vain — and Magen Avraham well compares to semicha: there too it says "the sons of Israel shall lean" and not daughters, yet some hold women may lean optionally — so too for non-kohanim it is optional but a blessing does not apply. What is in Rosh Hashana 32 regarding not hindering minors — women hinder — the Gemara compares to the dispute of R' Yosi and R' Shimon whether women may lean optionally, even though not comparable as above — there regarding performing the body of the mitzvah there is concern whether they do it; the concern is only from "do not add" — no reason to distinguish whether the exclusion was stated in its place; especially per those who hold women may lean optionally — a fortiori where diminished in its place permitted from Torah in what is not diminished in its place permitted; and per those who forbid leaning, since the reason is "do not add" — what difference whether in its place or not. And if so from where would this baraita that women hinder come — and similarly in Mishna Berurah Pesachim 27 regarding "this and that caused." But for blessing, "and you shall command" — there is reason to distinguish: if diminished in its place itself, "and you shall command" does not apply; if not diminished in its place it applies — and this fits; examine.
