Se'if 1, gloss: A non-kohen may not raise his hands, even together with other kohanim. See Magen Avraham's resolution of Rema's question: Rabbi Yose follows his own view that women may optionally perform semichah. Atzei Arazim 3 objects: Tosafos suggests concern for a blessing in vain, yet Tosafos in Rosh Hashanah permits women to bless over time-bound positive mitzvos. Magen Avraham is correct. With an active mitzvah such as shofar, tefillin, or lulav, although a woman is not commanded she still performs a mitzvah-act and may bless. Birkas Kohanim, however, is only speech whose purpose is to bless Israel. If the blessing takes effect it is a mitzvah; if a non-kohen's blessing does not take effect, he has done nothing. Since non-kohanim are not commanded and their blessing may not take effect, how can a blessing be recited over a null act? Alternatively, Rashi, Berachos 20a, explains that a kohen or nazir becoming impure for a meis mitzvah is not an active override because their prohibition was never stated regarding a meis mitzvah, just as it was not stated regarding relatives. I explained in youth (and later found in Pnei Yehoshua) that when the exclusion is written beside the prohibition it is complete permission; when human dignity overrides an independently stated command, it is only a rejection and cannot override through action. The author's son notes that this accords with Ramban in Shaar HaGemul and Responsa Atzei Chaim. Similarly, ordinary positive commands are phrased generally for males and females; women's exemption from time-bound commands is learned elsewhere, so they can still say 'Who commanded us.' Birkas Kohanim itself says 'you' (kohanim and not non-kohanim) so one excluded there cannot say 'Who commanded us.' Magen Avraham aptly compares semichah, whose own verse says 'the sons of Israel,' not daughters; Rabbi Yose nevertheless permits women to perform it voluntarily, though without a blessing here. Rosh Hashanah 32a compares restraining women from shofar to the dispute over voluntary semichah. Regarding performance and bal tosif, there is no reason to distinguish whether the exclusion is local; if women may perform even where locally excluded, certainly they may where excluded only elsewhere. The prohibiting view likewise invokes bal tosif in either case, and no other tanna fits the baraisa. For the blessing formula, however, the distinction is clear: a local exclusion prevents saying 'Who commanded us,' whereas an external exemption does not. See my work on Orach Chaim from 5616, page 126a, for further discussion and resolution of Atzei Arazim's objection.
