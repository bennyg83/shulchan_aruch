He drank a revi'is. Rabbi Yerucham wrote: A drunkard, it appears to me, should not raise his hands specifically when he reached the drunkenness of Lot, end quote. The Beis Yosef disagreed with him; see there. It appears to me that Rabbi Yerucham's words are clear and correct, for in the Gemara we derive the one who blesses from a nazirite, who is forbidden wine. If so, other beverages are permitted, as with a nazirite. The Rambam too wrote: Six matters prevent the priestly blessing, etc., wine, etc.; if he drank a revi'is of wine, etc. Even regarding Temple service, there is no death liability for other beverages, as the Rambam wrote. If so, regarding the priestly blessing there is no prohibition at all with other beverages, unlike the Olas Tamid, who himself contradicted his words in s.k. 90; see there, unless he is as drunk as Lot, for then he is exempt from all mitzvos. It implies that even if he cannot speak before the king, he may raise his hands, for this is not comparable to prayer. I do not understand what the Beis Yosef wrote, that Rabbi Yerucham compares it to prayer, for that is not so; see there. It appears to me that if he drank wine from the press, he is disqualified, for it is forbidden to a nazirite and it is also forbidden for the Temple service. Therefore the Rambam did not mention it here. See siman 99, se'if 2; the same applies here.
