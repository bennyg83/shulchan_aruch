137) A revi'is of wine. We compare the Priestly Blessing to the Temple service, where in such a case the kohen is liable.
138) A little water. Meaning the wine was not undiluted, but was mixed with a small amount of water.
139) Permitted. In such a case one is not liable in the Temple, though an ordinary prohibition remains. Since the Priestly Blessing is less stringent than the Temple service, it is permitted initially.
140) Although it is diluted, etc. In such a case one is liable in the Temple service.
141) Until it wears off, etc. See siman 99:2, which applies here as well. All this concerns wine. Regarding other intoxicating drinks, Magen Avraham permits the Priestly Blessing even when the kohen is so intoxicated that he cannot speak before a king; a state in which his prayer is an abomination under siman 99; unless he reaches Lot's intoxication, when he is considered mentally incompetent. Mateh Yehudah agrees. Many later authorities disagree and hold that intoxication from any drink disqualifies once he cannot speak before a king. Magen Avraham also forbids freshly pressed wine for the Priestly Blessing; several later authorities disagree because it does not intoxicate.
