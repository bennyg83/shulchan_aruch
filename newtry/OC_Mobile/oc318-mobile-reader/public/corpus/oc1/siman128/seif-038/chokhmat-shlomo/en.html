Se'if 38: If he drank a revi'is of wine at once, he may not raise his hands, etc. Rambam, Hilchos Issurei Mizbe'ach 1:16, writes that the rule for entering from the altar inward and leaving there is the same: one who drinks a revi'is between the Ulam and altar, or tears his garments there, and leaves, is flogged. Raavad objects: on Rambam's reading, he need not drink between the Ulam and altar; Toras Kohanim's inclusion of exit means that even if he drank outside and entered the Heichal he is liable; during entry there must be no drunkenness in the Heichal or at the altar, with death also applicable. Kesef Mishneh explains Raavad as objecting that Rambam requires bringing wine inside to drink, asking who would bring it there. This is astonishing: one could have brought ordinary wine unlawfully or drunk libation wine, incurring both misuse and lashes for intoxication. Raavad instead means that if Toras Kohanim teaches liability for exit, the Tosefta need not be placed where he drank inside; it could concern drinking outside and receiving two sets of lashes, for entry and exit. That is the plain meaning. Rambam can be defended. Toras Kohanim derives exit from 'you and your sons with you.' 'With you' means similar to you, like the Sages' exposition of 'with you' regarding Noach. Aharon was then inside, as Parashas Shemini says, 'Do not leave the entrance of the Tent of Meeting'; the verse hints that if they then drank wine they would be liable, proving liability upon exit. This cannot teach two liabilities for entry and exit, since Aharon had not entered intoxicated and would not be 'similar to you.' It must concern drinking inside and liability on exit. Kesef Mishneh may share this reading but explain Raavad's objection differently. Ordinarily, 'if you grasp much, you grasp nothing,' so it is preferable to apply the inclusion to one who drank inside and incur one liability rather than to drinking outside and incur two. Kesef Mishneh explains that Raavad sees bringing wine inside as uncommon, making it more reasonable to derive two liabilities in the common case of drinking outside than one liability in the uncommon case. This resolves the matter well.
