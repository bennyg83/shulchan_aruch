To answer Amen, etc. Lest his mind become confused and he not know which blessing he must prompt, the second or third verse; Tur. Knesset HaGedolah wrote in the name of Lechem Chamudos that if he is assured he will not become confused he answers Amen, for so it is found in Midrash Rabbah, parashas Ki Savo. He did not see Tosafot Yeshanim, chapter Ein Omdin, where he retracted, for we do not learn halacha from the Midrash. This also escaped Olas Tamid; see there. Nevertheless, it appears to me that for us, who pray from a siddur, there is room to permit, as I will write in seif 21. However, from Responsa Maharil, siman 179, it implies that even for us it is forbidden. All this is with the shaliach tzibbur, but for an individual standing when they perform Nesias Kapayim, it is implied from Bach that it is forbidden because it is an interruption, for only for the shaliach tzibbur is it not an interruption.
