One who has a blemish, etc. — See Taz, who wrote that all those who are not [fit for] nesi'at kapayim need to leave the synagogue at the time of the platform, lest they say of him "he is the son of a divorcee"; and Eliyah Rabbah is ambiguous about this, and likewise in Peri Megadim; and they rejected his proofs; and likewise in Sefer Nahar Shalom he disagrees with him, that all these are recognizable in their blemish and people will not come to err; and therefore the poskim concealed [the matter] and did not require him to leave; and so is implied in Magen Avraham, seif kaf-dalet; see there. But because not all are expert in this law, it is possible that it is better that they go outside, per the Taz's view. Peri Chadash wrote: all those of whom we say "he shall not go up — if he went up he shall not descend"; and see in Peri Megadim in Mishbetzot Zahav, siman 39.

And likewise a blind person in one of his eyes — It is obvious that specifically if his blindness is evident and people will come to look at him; but if his blindness is not evident to people — as is common with types of blind people whose eyes are like the eyes of other people, only that the light has been taken from them — it is permitted.

One who goes incidentally, etc. — See Mishna Berurah, and see Magen Avraham who brought Bach's opinion that disagrees with this. Behold, Levush, Eretz Tzvi, Peri Chadash, and other later authorities all agreed with Shulchan Aruch, and Chayei Adam wrote something like a ruling on this: that if he was in the synagogue at the time they call the Kohanim, and he also left before Retzeh, he may ascend.
