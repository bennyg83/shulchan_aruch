A blemish. All those who do not raise their hands should leave the synagogue during duchaning, lest people say that the person is the son of a divorcee. Taz. The Gemara states that one whose garments do not properly cover him may not raise his hands; see siman 53:13. One suffering from intestinal illness should not raise his hands. Peri Chadash. Sefer HaMiktzo'os says in the name of Peri Chadash that a kohen whose wife is a niddah may not ascend the duchan while she remains in that state. Peri Chadash writes that these words should not be publicized, because they are not essential law; leave Israel alone, for it is preferable that they err unwittingly rather than deliberately.
In his city. Taz asks why such a kohen is not disqualified, since duchaning is compared to Temple service. He overlooked an explicit Gemara in Taanis 26b: One might think that just as a blemished person may not serve, a blemished kohen may not bless; therefore Scripture compares him to a nazir. See Magen Avraham, se'if katan 54, and Taz, se'if katan 35. This requires examination. Shevus Yaakov, part 2, siman 1; Yad Aharon.
