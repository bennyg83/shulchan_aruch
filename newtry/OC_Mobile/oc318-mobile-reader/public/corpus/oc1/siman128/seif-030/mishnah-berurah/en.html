(108) That they are white-flecked — this refers also to his nose, one speckled with fine white dots. And those speckled with fine dots [called sommer-frankenlein] — if that is common there, even if not the majority of the townspeople do so, he may lift his hands.

(109) In it — for it is something astonishing, and for this reason they will divert their attention from the kohen to hear the berachah.

(110) Market stalls — that is, like our pozmakhot.

(111) And if he was one who crushes [garlic], etc. — for the reason blood on his face disqualifies his hands and feet, and in all these cases it is because we fear they will gaze at him; but one who crushes [garlic] they will not gaze at him because it is no novelty in their eyes. It is stated in the Gemara that a puffaḥ may not lift his hands, for it is disgraceful to the congregation — what is puffaḥ is explained above siman 53 se'if 13 see there. Peri Chadash wrote that one with intestinal illness may not lift his hands; nevertheless it appears proper that he leave before Retzah.

(112) Walks only occasionally, etc. — for people are not accustomed to carry on commerce with him and are not habituated to his blemish — see Bi'ur Halachah.

(113) Rather, etc. — for since he at any rate comes to settle there for some time, people are accustomed to trade with him and become habituated to his blemish within thirty days.
