108) They have white blotches. This refers to his face as well and means speckled with tiny white spots. One with fine freckles, called zomer shprenkel, may recite the Priestly Blessing if they are common there, even if most local residents do not have them.
109) On it. It is something unusual, so people will be distracted from concentrating on the blessing.
110) Leg coverings. Meaning something like our stockings.
111) If he is familiar, etc. A deformity of face, hands, or feet, and all similar conditions, disqualifies because people may stare. When he is familiar, they do not stare because his condition is no novelty to them. The Gemara states that one whose clothing does not properly cover him may not recite the Priestly Blessing, because this degrades the congregation. Such a person's definition is explained above in siman 53:13. Peri Chadash writes that one with an intestinal illness may not recite it; it appears best that he leave before Retzeh.
112) Visits temporarily, etc. People do not ordinarily conduct business and interact with him, so they are not accustomed to his deformity. See Biur Halachah.
113) Rather, etc. Since he has come to settle there for some period, people ordinarily conduct business and interact with him, becoming accustomed to his deformity within thirty days.
