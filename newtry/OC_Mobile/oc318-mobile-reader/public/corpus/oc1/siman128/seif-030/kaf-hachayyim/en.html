30) There: Wearing shoes. Tur's words prove that shoes should be removed before washing the hands. Accordingly, they must be removed outside the synagogue. So writes Mateh Moshe, siman 178. Our present custom, however, is to leave the synagogue to wash and remove the shoes only upon ascending. The differing customs apparently reflect differing footwear. In the Ottoman lands, shoes are not tied and can be slipped off without touching them, so the kohanim wash first and remove them while ascending. In European lands, shoes must be tied and cannot be removed without touching them, so they must be removed outside before washing; otherwise the kohanim would have to wash again after removal. Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur, os 4. He adds that our custom of removing shoes upon ascending is nevertheless improper, because the shoes remain visibly below the platform before the entire congregation throughout the blessing. Modest people hide them beneath the benches before ascending. I saw Rabbi David HaKohen leave his shoes outside the synagogue, and believe I likewise saw Rabbi Yosef HaKohen do so. Olas Tamid, os 9; Eliyah Rabbah, os 6; Peri Chadash, os 5; Shaliach Tzibbur there, page 141a; and Beis Menuchah, os 16-17, cite this. The rule depends on local circumstances. Where shoes left outside are not likely to be stolen, leave them there; where theft is a concern, conceal them under the synagogue benches. If tied shoes must be touched during removal, remove them before washing to avoid another washing afterward. Untied shoes that can be slipped off without contact may be removed after washing. See our words above, siman 4, os 69, and below, se'if 17, in the gloss.
