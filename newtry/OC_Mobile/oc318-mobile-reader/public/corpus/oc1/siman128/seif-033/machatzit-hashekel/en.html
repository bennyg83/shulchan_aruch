Seif katan 33, it is preferable, but nevertheless he must, etc. The Bach wrote this to answer the difficulty of Beis Yosef, who asked why the Mishnah taught that only if he is assured may he perform Nesias Kapayim, since even if he is not assured there is a remedy, namely, that the prompter have intent throughout the entire prayer and that the prompter complete Sim Shalom. Beis Yosef wrote that perhaps the Mishnah speaks only where the shaliach tzibbur himself wishes to complete Sim Shalom. The Bach answered differently: when the prompter completes Sim Shalom, he must have intent for the entire prayer; otherwise it is improper for the prayer to be in pieces, with the shaliach tzibbur praying until Sim Shalom and the prompter praying Sim Shalom. Since he had intent for the whole prayer, listening is like answering, and it is as if the prompter prayed the entire prayer. Similarly, where the prompter completes Sim Shalom, the shaliach tzibbur must have intent for the blessings of Sim Shalom; otherwise the shaliach tzibbur did not pray all eighteen blessings, only until Sim Shalom, and this too is improper. Therefore, even though the prompter can complete Sim Shalom, nevertheless the shaliach tzibbur must be assured that he can have intent for the Sim Shalom that the prompter says. But in Beis Yosef it implies otherwise, since he did not answer as the Bach wrote.
