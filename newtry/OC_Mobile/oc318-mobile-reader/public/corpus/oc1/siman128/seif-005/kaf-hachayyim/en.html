27) [Se'if 5] The kohanim may not ascend the platform wearing shoes. This is one of Rabban Yochanan ben Zakkai's nine enactments, as stated in Rosh Hashanah 31b and Sotah 40a. Sotah explains the reason: A sandal strap might break, causing him to sit and tie it while his colleagues bless, and observers may say he is the son of a divorcee or chalutzah. Beis Yosef; Levush; Olas Tamid, os 9. Beis Yosef writes that even shoes and sandals without straps are forbidden because the Sages made no distinction and enacted a general prohibition covering every shoe and sandal. So write Levush; Olas Tamid there; Eliyah Rabbah, os 6; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, os 6; and Chayei Adam 32:10.
28) There: They may not ascend, etc., wearing shoes. On Tishah BeAv and Yom Kippur as well, when cloth footwear is worn, it must be removed. Panim Meiros, part 2, siman 28; Zera Emes, siman 14; Machazik Berachah, os 7; Shaliach Tzibbur, page 144b; Chesed LaAlafim, os 2; Ben Ish Chai, Tetzaveh, os 6.
29) There: Wearing shoes. Kohanim may not ascend wearing felt shoes called filtri. Those who suffer greatly from the cold may wear them, provided these are not the shoes in which they go outdoors, but are put on only when ascending. This allowance applies specifically to felt; leather is forbidden even if not worn outside. Knesses Yechezkel, siman 11; Ikarei HaDat 5:29.
30) There: Wearing shoes. Tur's words prove that shoes should be removed before washing the hands. Accordingly, they must be removed outside the synagogue. So writes Mateh Moshe, siman 178. Our present custom, however, is to leave the synagogue to wash and remove the shoes only upon ascending. The differing customs apparently reflect differing footwear. In the Ottoman lands, shoes are not tied and can be slipped off without touching them, so the kohanim wash first and remove them while ascending. In European lands, shoes must be tied and cannot be removed without touching them, so they must be removed outside before washing; otherwise the kohanim would have to wash again after removal. Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur, os 4. He adds that our custom of removing shoes upon ascending is nevertheless improper, because the shoes remain visibly below the platform before the entire congregation throughout the blessing. Modest people hide them beneath the benches before ascending. I saw Rabbi David HaKohen leave his shoes outside the synagogue, and believe I likewise saw Rabbi Yosef HaKohen do so. Olas Tamid, os 9; Eliyah Rabbah, os 6; Peri Chadash, os 5; Shaliach Tzibbur there, page 141a; and Beis Menuchah, os 16-17, cite this. The rule depends on local circumstances. Where shoes left outside are not likely to be stolen, leave them there; where theft is a concern, conceal them under the synagogue benches. If tied shoes must be touched during removal, remove them before washing to avoid another washing afterward. Untied shoes that can be slipped off without contact may be removed after washing. See our words above, siman 4, os 69, and below, se'if 17, in the gloss.
31) There: But leg coverings are permitted. Maran's view in Beis Yosef is that every kind of leg covering is permitted, even if it has straps and is tied near the knee, provided it is not leather. So write Olas Tamid, os 14 and os 11, and Shulchan Aruch HaRav, os 6. Olas Tamid says that one who tries to be stringent and goes entirely barefoot without cloth leg coverings acts improperly in places where people do not go barefoot before dignitaries. Chagigah likewise says it is improper to bare one's leg before one's teacher, implying that one should not go barefoot in a synagogue or study hall. Mishnah Berurah, os 18, cites him. See above, siman 95, se'if 5, and our words there.
32) There, gloss: Some are stringent if they are leather, etc. Peri Chadash, os 5, writes that Maran holds in Beis Yosef that leather leg coverings are forbidden even without straps and no risk of breakage, because the Sages made no distinction. The custom is nevertheless lenient. Birkei Yosef, os 3, cites him and notes that an unpublished responsum of Radbaz, siman 2264, says the same. Radbaz adds that when it is not cold and the other kohanim are barefoot, he too should stand barefoot. This appears to apply where people stand barefoot before dignitaries; elsewhere, he should wear cloth coverings, as stated in the preceding os.
33) Peri Chadash writes that although we just permitted leather leg coverings, this applies when shoes are worn over them. If one walks through the market wearing only the leg coverings, he must remove them before ascending out of respect for the congregation. Eshel Avraham, os 5, and Mishnah Berurah, os 18, cite him.
34) There, gloss: Some are stringent if they are leather, etc. This applies specifically when they reach only to the knee and are tied with a strap or ribbon. If they reach the thighs and are trousers, they are permitted. Cloth covered with leather is permitted in every case. Magen Avraham, se'if katan 5; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, os 6; Chayei Adam 32:10; Mishnah Berurah, os 17. Although siman 554:16 forbids cloth footwear covered with leather on Tishah BeAv, there the prohibition is due to comfort: Anything protecting the foot may not be worn, and a leather covering protects it. Here the concern is confusion with shoes, and leather-covered cloth will not be confused with them. Machatzis HaShekel; Peri Megadim, Eshel Avraham, os 5; and Levushei Serad on Magen Avraham 554:16. This resolves Eliyah Rabbah's question in os 7.
