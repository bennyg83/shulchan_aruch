15) Wearing shoes. A sandal strap might break, leaving him embarrassed as people mock him for an untied sandal. He may sit to tie it while his companions bless, and people may say he is the son of a divorcee or chalutzah and therefore withdrew and sat down. Even shoes without straps are forbidden, because the Sages made no distinction. The shoes should be concealed rather than left exposed in the synagogue, out of respect. He should remove them before washing his hands. If he can remove them afterward without touching them, however, he may remove them after washing. Later authorities.
16) But leg coverings. These are long footwear reaching the knees, near the lower leg. They are permitted because the rationale above does not apply. Although they too sometimes have straps near the knee, we do not fear that he will sit to tie them, because even when untied they are not so embarrassing.
17) If they are leather. The stringent authorities consider them a type of sandal or shoe, and the Sages did not distinguish between those with and without straps. Nevertheless, leg coverings joined to trousers and reaching the thighs are permitted according to all views, because they are entirely outside the decree. Cloth leg coverings are likewise permitted under this view even when covered with leather.
18) Some places customarily rule leniently. Nevertheless, our boots, called shtivel, which are ordinarily worn through street mud, should not be permitted, out of respect for the congregation. Peri Chadash; see there. For this reason, our reed overshoes called kalasin are likewise improper. The later authorities write that it is not proper to ascend completely barefoot, because nowadays going barefoot before dignitaries is degrading. One should wear cloth stockings, and this is the custom.
