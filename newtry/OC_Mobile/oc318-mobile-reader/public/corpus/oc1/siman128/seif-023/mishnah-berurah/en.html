88) As one stands in prayer. For they are praying that Hashem bless Israel. Levush.
89) They should not look at them. Meaning neither at the kohanim's faces nor their hands. The reason is likewise to avoid distraction from the blessing; all the more should they not look elsewhere. Strictly speaking, only prolonged gazing that may cause distraction is forbidden; a brief glance is permitted. Only in the Temple era, when the kohanim pronounced the explicit Divine Name and the Shechinah rested upon their hands, was even a brief glance forbidden. This is not so today. Nevertheless, as a remembrance of the Temple, the custom even now is not to look at them at all.
90) The kohanim also, etc. This too is because of distraction, as explained above.
91) Therefore the custom is, etc. This custom is more correct. Eliyah Rabbah, Gra, and other later authorities.
92) Their hands outside the tallis. Darkei Moshe adds that the congregation too customarily covers their faces with talleisim so they cannot look at the kohanim's hands.
