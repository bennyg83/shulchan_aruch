While the priests bless the people, etc., their faces shall be toward the priests; and also the people receiving the blessings shall intend to receive the blessings and not look at them, so they do not come to distraction of mind (Yerushalmi). But the reason is not that their eyes are dim, for this is explained in the chapter Ein Dorshin specifically while the Temple stands (Maharam and see below siman 130). One who is behind the priests need not leave his place nor push his fellow from his place that he bought — all the more one who has no place in the synagogue at all must stand in the synagogue courtyard (my teacher in Bach). And so it appears from Rashi that one is not forced, etc., because they can come before the priests to be blessed face to face. And they say Ribon haOlamim when the priests sing the twenty of "May the Omnipresent bless you" and "May the Omnipresent guard you" and the thirty of peace, for there they finished that entire blessing (Maharam).
