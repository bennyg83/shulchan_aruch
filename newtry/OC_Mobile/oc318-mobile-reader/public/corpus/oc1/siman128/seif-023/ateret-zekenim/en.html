While the kohanim bless the people, etc., the people's faces should be directed toward the kohanim. Those receiving the blessings should also intend to receive them and should not gaze at the kohanim, so that they not become distracted, as stated in the Yerushalmi. The reason is not that their eyes will become dim, for the Gemara in the chapter Ein Dorshin explains that this applied specifically when the Beis HaMikdash stood, as stated by Mahararm MiLublin. See below, siman 130. One who stands behind the kohanim need not leave his place and displace another person from a place that belongs to him. This is certainly true of one who has no place at all in the beis haknesses and must stand in its courtyard, as my teacher in the Bach ruled. This is also implied by Rashi's explanation of the phrase, 'those who are not prevented,' since they can come before the kohanim and be blessed face to face. The prayer, 'Master of the worlds,' is said while the kohanim sing the final letter of Veyishmerecha, Vichuneka, and Shalom, because at those points they have completed the respective blessing, as stated by Mahararm MiLublin. A kohen may not add the words, 'May Hashem increase you.'
