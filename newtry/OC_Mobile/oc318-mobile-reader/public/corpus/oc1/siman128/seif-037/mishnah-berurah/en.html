134) An apostate to idolatry, etc. Whether unintentionally or intentionally, and even after repentance, he is disqualified, derived from the Temple service, for which he is unfit: 'But the priests of the high places shall not ascend Hashem's altar in Jerusalem, etc.' One who merely promised to convert and then retracted is not disqualified according to any view. The later authorities write that even one who converted to the faith of the Ishmaelites, who do not practice idolatry, is nevertheless called an apostate and may not recite the Priestly Blessing. Likewise, one who publicly desecrates Shabbos is regarded as a non-Jew for this purpose and may not recite it.
135) Some say that if he repented, etc. This applies even if he intentionally practiced idolatry.
136) According to all views he recites the Priestly Blessing. Although Rambam, author of the first view, explicitly writes that even one coerced to practice idolatry may not recite it, the present case concerns one who did not yet perform an idolatrous act but merely converted; that is, professed idolatry and accepted it as divine. Although intentional profession alone is treated like actual idolatry for capital liability, regarding coercion we are lenient for the Priestly Blessing because he performed no act.
