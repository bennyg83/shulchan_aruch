An apostate. Even if he did not worship idolatry; and if he did worship idolatry, even under duress, he does not raise his hands according to those who are stringent (in Bedek HaBayis, from precision in the Rambam's wording). So they practice there in his place (Radbaz, part 2, siman 219). However, if he promised to apostatize and retracted, he is not disqualified (ibid., 25). Rabbi Levi ibn Chaviv wrote that "apostate" means that he said, "You are my god," and Rabbi Aharon Sasson likewise wrote in siman 156. To me it appears that what the Rambam wrote, that he converted, refers to the religion of the Ishmaelites, who do not worship idolatry; even so, since he changed his religion, he should not raise his hands. Although the text says that he converted to idolatry, that is the language of the printers, as is known. The Bach wrote: An uncircumcised man whose brothers died because of circumcision is disqualified from the Temple service, and this requires study, for the poskim did not write that he is disqualified from raising his hands because it is compared to the service, end quote. His words require study, for we say explicitly in the last chapter of Taanis that it is compared only leniently, that he is permitted with grape pits, but not stringently; for one with a physical blemish is fit to raise his hands, and many disqualifications listed in chapter 2 of Zevachim are fit for raising hands. So too the Semag wrote explicitly at the beginning of mitzvah 20 that an apostate is fit to raise his hands, for it was compared to the Temple service only regarding standing, as stated in Sotah and Taanis. Even according to the Rambam and his group, who disqualify an apostate, that is because the reason an apostate is disqualified from service is that they penalized him, as stated in Avodah Zarah, chapter 4; if so, here too they penalized him to be disqualified from raising his hands, and the same applies to one who marries women in a prohibited way. But where there is no penalty, he agrees that they are fit to raise their hands, for it is an explicit Talmudic teaching. What the Rambam wrote regarding a drunk, that the blessing was compared to the Temple service, means this: just as in the Temple service, if he interrupted it by putting even any amount of water into the wine, he is exempt, so too here; for if it were derived from a nazirite, I would say that even any amount, and even diluted, is forbidden. He therefore teaches us that we derive it from the Temple service. In this way his words are proper, according to the words of the Gemara; examine it and you will find it. And even if he intentionally did not circumcise himself, he is fit, as written in se'if 39 in the gloss.
