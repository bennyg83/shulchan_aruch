Apostate (mumar). Even if he did not worship idolatry — and if he worshipped idolatry, even if he was compelled — he is not disqualified from nesi'at kafayim according to those who are stringent (s.v. from precision in the Rambam's wording); and thus they practice in that locale (Radbaz chelek 2 siman 219). However, if he undertook to apostatize and returned, he is not disqualified (there siman 25). And R' Levi Chaviv wrote that mumar means one who said to me "you [are not a kohen]" — and likewise R' Eliyahu Sasson siman 256. And it appears to me that what the Rambam wrote "who apostatized" refers to the religion of the Ishmaelites, who do not worship idolatry; even so, since he changed his religion he may not lift his hands — and even though he wrote "who apostatized to idolatry," that is the printers' language, as is known. The Bach wrote: an uncircumcised one whose brother died from circumcision is disqualified from avodah — and it requires study, for the Poskim did not write he is disqualified from nesi'at kafayim, for it is compared to avodah, end of his words. And his words require study, for we explicitly say in the last chapter of Ta'anit that it is compared only leniently — that one may [bless] with a split lip — but not stringently, for a ba'al mum is fit for nesi'at kafayim; and also the four disqualifications listed in chapter 2 of Zevachim are fit for nesi'at kafayim; and likewise Semag explicitly at the beginning of the mitzvah wrote that a mumar is fit for nesi'at kafayim, for it is not compared to avodah except regarding standing, as stated in Sota and Ta'anit, therefore. And even for the Rambam and his followers who disqualify a mumar — this is because the reason a mumar is unfit for avodah is that we penalized them, as stated in the first chapter — if so, likewise we penalize that he is unfit for nesi'at kafayim; and likewise one who marries women in transgression. But where there is no penalty they concede they are fit for nesi'at kafayim, for it is Talmudic. And what the Rambam wrote regarding a drunkard — because a berachah was compared to avodah, meaning to say: just as with avodah, if the immersion was interrupted by any amount of water poured in he is exempt; likewise here — if from a nazir it would be that even any amount of diluted [wine] is forbidden, it teaches us that we derive from avodah; and with this his words stand properly like the Gemara's words — investigate and forget. And even if he did not circumcise himself deliberately he is fit, as the Shulchan Aruch wrote in Hagahot.
