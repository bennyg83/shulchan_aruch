And if he was compelled. — See Mishna Berurah; and we did not copy the opinion of Peri Chadash and Maharam Di Boton [cited in Knesset HaGedolah], who forbid even without an act, only in bowing and confessing alone — one, because it is not so relevant for us, since we hold like the second view that repentance helps, and one who was compelled is certainly considered as having repented and stood upright; and further, the main law of one compelled, even with an act, is not clear, as wondered in responsa Lechem Rav — where did the Rambam get this matter? Also regarding unwitting action it is not so clear to forbid in bowing and confessing alone, even though the Rambam wrote explicitly to forbid in this, whether unwittingly or intentionally; his words are difficult, as Lechem Mishneh wondered from the passage in Menachos 109, which proves there that regarding Temple service itself it is not forbidden unwittingly except in throwing and the like, and not in bowing and confessing alone; see there. An uncircumcised Kohen whose brothers died because of circumcision — Magen Avraham wrote that he raises his hands, even per the first view in this seif, and he is included in what Rama wrote in the gloss in siman 39, that other transgressions, etc., and its source there is from the Rambam, who is the first view here; and Bach is uncertain about this, since we compare blessing to service in Rambam's view, perhaps the same applies regarding an uncircumcised Kohen, who is also disqualified for Temple service; and see Choshen Mishpat, who sided in law like Bach, at least per Rambam; and so is the view of Peri Chadash, and so sides Eliyah Rabbah; and from Peri Chadash and Eliyah Rabbah it is further implied that an uncircumcised Kohen is disqualified even per the second view, that we do not compare blessing to service, and for the reason that perhaps he is similar to one sitting who is disqualified, or to one who marries women in transgression, who is disqualified according to all; see there. And in my humble opinion it appears that regarding an uncircumcised Kohen who was not circumcised because of compulsion, whose brothers died because of circumcision, one should be lenient, at least for us, since we hold that an apostate to idolatry who repented is fit for raising the hands, and for the reason that we do not compare blessing to service; see Or Zarua and Shevut Yaakov, where this view is explained at length, and what emerges from their words is that since their sanctity was not removed and they are forbidden in a divorcee and a harlot and the like — if so, behold their sanctity remains upon them regarding raising the hands; and similarly one may say in our case, for it would not occur to one to say that an uncircumcised Kohen is permitted in a divorcee — behold, the sanctity of the Kohen is upon him, and he raises the hands according to this approach. And what Peri Chadash brought as proof from one who marries women in transgression — the cases are not comparable, for there he is a willful sinner, therefore we penalize him and we do not give him the privileges of the priesthood as long as he himself profanes it [and you should know that since he divorced her and forbade benefit to her, he returns to his rank]; but that is not so here, where he was under complete compulsion, and he is no worse, at least, than an apostate who repented, who for us raises his hands. And what Eliyah Rabbah wanted to be stringent about him, because he is unfit due to his foreskin to eat kodashim, and therefore he is worse even than a blemished person — this is not so, for a tamei person proves otherwise, as Peri Megadim wrote. Also known is Rabbeinu Tam's view, that an uncircumcised Kohen disqualified for service means one who did not circumcise himself intentionally; but one whose brothers died because of circumcision is fit even for service, and even though many disagree with him — see Ramban, beginning of Parashat HaErel, Rashba, and Ritva there — nevertheless one should combine his view, at least for raising the hands, since according to most poskim blessing was compared to service, only that it is rabbinic. However, regarding an uncircumcised Kohen who did not circumcise himself intentionally, it is proper to be stringent, in Peri Chadash's words, that he should not raise his hands until he is circumcised, like one who marries women in transgression.
