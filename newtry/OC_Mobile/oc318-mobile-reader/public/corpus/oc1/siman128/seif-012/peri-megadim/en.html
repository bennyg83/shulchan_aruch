Until. See Taz, who disagrees with Magen Avraham, letter 28, and follows Perishah that here too one waits for the minority. Tosafos on Sotah 39b, s.v. ad, gives two answers. According to the first, even a kohen cannot hear the word Kohanim while Amen is being answered, because the two sounds are dissimilar; the rule that a cherished sound receives attention applies when the two sounds are alike. According to the second, kohanim can attend even to dissimilar sounds. The kohen merely may not begin Yevarechecha until Amen has ended from the entire congregation, because those prolonging Amen will not hear Yevarechecha. Tosafos appears to adopt the latter answer, like Magen Avraham here, letter 28. Taz adopts the first. Thus the chazzan certainly may not begin Yevarechecha until the entire congregation has finished Amen, because the kohanim cannot hear two dissimilar sounds and may err. He must even wait before saying Kohanim, although there is less obligation for the kohanim to hear that word; to fulfill 'when they tell them,' it would suffice that two kohanim were told to wash. Nevertheless the chazzan waits for the minority. This is Shulchan Aruch's phrase 'from the congregation.' In siman 124:9, 'from the majority of the congregation' is explained as though it said the entire congregation. Beis Yosef's question in letter 13 therefore cannot be resolved like Magen Avraham, letter 28. Magen Avraham adopts Tosafos' second answer and accordingly writes what he does. Peri Chadash notes that trumpeters do not hear the shofar. One may answer that this is unlike those prolonging Amen, for Amen requires mental concentration, whereas ordinary hearing remains possible: Even the trumpet blowers can direct their attention to hear the shofar.
