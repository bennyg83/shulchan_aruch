The end of a blessing. I do not know what he is saying, for "to you" is not the end of a blessing, and nevertheless they turn their faces. It appears to me that the reason is because all these words are in the second person; therefore they turn themselves so that they bless everyone.
And when they prolong the melody. At the end of the word, but not while they are saying the word, for then one must be silent, as written in se'if 26. Although with the word "shalom" it is impossible to wait until they finish the word, since they prolong the melody before they complete the final mem of "shalom," nevertheless whatever can be corrected and done according to halachah, we do. And if you say: Since they prolong the melody, the recitation does not help, for he must say it while they are blessing. One may answer that for this matter it is not yet considered the conclusion of the blessing, as we find with Shema Yisrael, where one prolongs the dalet of Echad; even though he has already completed the word, it is still called accepting the yoke of the kingdom of Heaven (Terumas HaDeshen, siman 27).
And the prompter. This means: the chazzan who is praying obviously should not say the "Master," for it would be an interruption in the prayer. But even another prompter should not say the "Master," because of confusion (there). See what I wrote in se'if 19. When there is no kohen there, one does not say "And may it be pleasing" (Rabbi Binyamin of Lublin, siman 30).

For we find in the Gemara, chapter HaZeroa, that Rava had a kohen servant, and also in Bava Kamma, folio 20, it states that Rami bar Chama served Rav Chisda, who was a kohen; see there. Rabbi Yerucham wrote, Nesiv 2, part 3: A kohen must do something once a year to maintain himself in the priesthood, either to take the gifts or to spread his hands; so it is clear in Chullin, chapter HaZeroa, end quote. According to what Tosafos wrote there, that because on the eve of Yom Tov they slaughtered many animals, and if he did not take they would say that he was not a kohen, therefore he took, it implies that otherwise he need not maintain himself as a kohen. But in Tur Yoreh Deah, se'if 1, it implies as Rabbi Yerucham wrote. In the Gemara it asks: Let him spread his hands. It answers: his time prevented him. Rashi explained that he had a fixed time to learn at that hour. The Rif explained that he had an intestinal illness and could not hold himself that long.
