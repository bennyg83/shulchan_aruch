168) They turn during them, etc. The custom is intended to spread the blessing to all those standing at the kohanim's sides.
169) They prolong the melody, etc. Eliyah Rabbah cites Responsa Beis Yaakov: The custom of singing veyishmer without the final chaf and then adding the chaf, and doing the same with vichuneka, is erroneous. Only the completed word's ending may be prolonged, because before completion the word's meaning is not expressed at all. Cantors likewise must take care not to split words in two.
170) For each of them, etc. This also explains the Mechaber's words. Although the Priestly Blessing consists of three verses, each verse contains two distinct blessings: 'May Hashem bless you' is one, and 'protect you' a second; 'May Hashem illuminate His countenance toward you' is one, and 'favor you' a second; 'May Hashem raise His countenance toward you' is one, and 'grant you peace' a second. Levush. Magen Avraham says the kohanim turn their faces during these words because they all address the listener directly.
171) And during, etc. The text should read 'during,' and this is the reading in old Shulchan Aruch editions.
172) During the melody of the words, etc. Meaning while they prolong the sound of the word's final letter, not while pronouncing the word itself. During its pronunciation, one must remain silent and concentrate, as stated in se'if 26.
173) The prompter. It is obvious that a prayer leader reciting the repetition should not say Ribono, because that would interrupt the prayer. Even when the prayer leader is a kohen and someone else prompts, the prompter should not say Ribono, lest his concentration become confused and he be unable to resume prompting. After prompting the word shalom, however, he may say Ribono, because confusion is no longer a concern: He does not conclude Sim Shalom; the prayer leader does, as explained above in se'if 22 and Mishnah Berurah there. Later authorities. Responsa Maharam Lublin writes that when no kohanim ascend during Mussaf, the custom is not to say Veserav.
174) It resembles misappropriating consecrated property. The verse says, 'You shall sanctify him, for he offers the bread of your God.' Even now, when we have no offerings, he retains his sanctity. For this reason, he is forbidden to marry a divorcee and to become impure through corpses.
175) Unless he waives it. If he waives his honor, it is permitted. Priestly honor; such as receiving the first opportunity to open, taking a choice portion first, and similar honors we must accord kohanim; was given for their benefit. He may therefore waive it and permit an Israelite to make use of him. Some say he can permit such use only when he receives some benefit, such as payment, or even when serving a distinguished person without pay because he desires to serve him and benefits from doing so. If he receives no benefit, he cannot waive it: Although honor can be waived, being used is degrading. It is proper to be stringent initially. One must certainly avoid using kohanim for degrading services. Magen Gibborim.
