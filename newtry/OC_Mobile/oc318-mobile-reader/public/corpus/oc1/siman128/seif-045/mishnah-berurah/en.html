(168) They turn with them, etc. — and the reason they practiced thus is so that the berachah should spread to all the people standing at their sides.

(169) That they prolong in the tune, etc. — Ateret Zekenim wrote in the name of the responsum of Beit Yaakov: what is customary to sing — the recitation of v'yishmarecha without the kaf, and afterward the kaf; and likewise what they do with v'yichanecha is an error, for one prolongs only at the end, for before finishing the intent of the word is not conveyed at all; and likewise cantors should be careful not to divide words in two.

(3) (Ke) because each one of them, etc. - he also commented on the words of the author and the author of the DA'G, the 33 blessings of the wine in the blessing of the priests MM Each blessing by itself has two blessings in it, that is, may the the Master bless you, blessing 1, and preserve you, a second blessing, Yar, etc. To you is one blessing, and to you is a blessing 2, Jesus, the the Master, etc. All these boxes are present:

(171) And at the time, etc. — it should read "at the time," and so it is in old Shulchan Aruch editions.

(172) In the tune of the words, etc. — that is, when they prolong in the tune the utterance of the last letter of the word; but not when saying the word, for then one must be silent and have intent, as written in siman 126.

(173) And the makri — meaning the shatz who prays; obviously he should not say Ribono shel Olam, for that is an interruption in prayer; but even when the shatz is a kohen and another is the makri, likewise the makri should not say Ribono shel Olam, lest his mind be disturbed and he be unable to return and call out. However, after he has called before them the word shalom, he may then say Ribono shel Olam, for there is no concern of confusion, since he does not finish the berachah of "place peace" — only the shatz does, as Mishna Berurah wrote above in se'if 22 [Acharonim]. Maharam Lublin wrote in his responsum: when there are no kohanim at Mussaf to ascend the duchan, the custom is not to say v'te'arev.

(7) (Ked) Faded as above in consecration - for it is said that he consecrates because he sacrifices the bread of your the Omnipresent and even now that we do not have sacrifices in his sanctity he stands and for this reason it is forbidden to divorce and defile the dead:

(175) If he did not waive this — but if he waived, it is permitted; for kevod haKehuna [such as opening first or taking a nice portion first, and the like from things we are obligated to honor them] is given them only for their benefit; therefore it is in his power to waive and give permission to Yisrael to use it. And some say he cannot give permission to use it unless he has some benefit — such as for payment, or even gratis to an important person whom he desires to serve and enjoys this; but if he has no benefit from this he cannot waive, for although for kavod he may waive, use is a matter of disgrace; and it is good to be stringent l'chatchila. And to use them for contemptible service — certainly one must be careful [Magen Giborim].
