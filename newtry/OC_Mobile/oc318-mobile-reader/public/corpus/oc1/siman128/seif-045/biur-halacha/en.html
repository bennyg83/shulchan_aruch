And at the time they lengthen with the melody of the words, etc. — See Mishna Berurah; and in our countries the congregation is accustomed to say Ribbon after the reading of the mikra, the last word of each verse, and the Kohanim are silent then or sing; and after the congregation finishes Ribbon, the Kohanim finish the last word. This custom seemingly has some reason, so that afterward the congregation can listen well to the blessing from the Kohanim; however, one may question this somewhat, for there is an interruption between the reading of the mikra and the Kohanim's recitation. And granted, when they sing, it is possible that this too is included in the beginning of reciting the word [see above in siman 124, Magen Avraham, seif kaf-daled, and one may distinguish]; but when they are silent, what can be said? And see above in seif 8, "and the Kohanim, etc.," until the word is finished from the mouth of the mikra — and this is from Shas, Sotah, and it is implied from this language that after the mikra is finished they must immediately answer; and so in Shulchan Aruch, seif 13, "and they answer after him for each word" — it implies they must immediately answer. And in truth, it is not explained in several early authorities that the reason for reading the mikra is so they will not err, and when the matter is drawn out it is possible that an error will occur; therefore, at least it is proper for the congregation not to prolong greatly in their recitation. And see above in siman 65, Mishna Berurah, seif kaf-daled, what we wrote further on this matter.

It is forbidden to make use of a Kohen, etc. — And a Kohen with a Kohen, it is possible that it is permitted [Yeshuos Yaakov, and so wrote Magen Giborim in the name of Pelaah]; and there are also those who side to be lenient regarding a Kohen with an am ha'aretz. Nevertheless, in demeaning service it is certainly proper to be careful; see there.
