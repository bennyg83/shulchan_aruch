And the chazzan. See Taz, who writes that here it is not preferable, etc.; above in seif katan 20 it is preferable because the shaliach tzibbur is occupied by the awe of the congregation, but here, on the contrary, the shaliach tzibbur should say it, and it is not proper for another who is not standing in the place of the shaliach tzibbur, etc. See Magen Avraham, letter 31, who brought the words of the Bach. Chemed Moshe wrote that when there is only one kohen, if there is someone expert in the prayer, why should the kohen be shaliach tzibbur and be prevented from Nesias Kapayim? He also wrote that when the shaliach tzibbur is a kohen, he should not say ‘Elokeinu,’ and the prompter says only the word ‘Kohanim,’ not ‘Our G-d and G-d of our fathers’; see there. See Peri Chadash in the name of Mishpat Tzedek, siman 29, that if the shaliach tzibbur is a kohen and there are no kohanim, he should not say ‘Our G-d and G-d of our fathers,’ only an Israelite should say it; see there. If there are kohanim, one cannot say ‘Our G-d and G-d of our fathers’ according to the opinion in seif 10. I do not know, for if so, on a weekday too, when the shaliach tzibbur is a kohen, he should not say ‘Our G-d and G-d of our fathers, bless us,’ etc.; rather, the kohanim are also blessed.
