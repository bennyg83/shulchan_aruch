And the chazzan stands and is silent. Meaning, until the beginning of Sim Shalom, which the shaliach tzibbur says. So wrote Beis Yosef in the name of the Maharam in Hagahos Maimoniyos and the Mordechai. It seems that here it is not preferable for the prompter to finish Sim Shalom, for above, where the chazzan left his place and another came in his place and prompted, it is preferable that the other also say Sim Shalom. But here, where the chazzan stands in his place and merely is silent, and another stands next to him and prompts, how could that other say Sim Shalom when he is not in the chazzan’s place but only standing beside him? This is unacceptable. Although my teacher and father-in-law wrote that our custom is that the other prompter finishes Sim Shalom and the chazzan says Kaddish, I did not see this anywhere. On the contrary, many times in my youth I was the prompter beside a shaliach tzibbur who was a kohen, and the shaliach tzibbur said Sim Shalom, and my teacher and father-in-law was in that synagogue. This is the primary practice, as it appears to me.
