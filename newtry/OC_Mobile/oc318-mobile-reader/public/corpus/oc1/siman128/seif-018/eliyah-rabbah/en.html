[36] To call the kohanim until Amen finishes etc. — so it is in the Talmud there. Olat Tamid wrote and there is here a slight contradiction to our custom that we say Elokeinu — end his words. I saw in Tosafot Berakhot daf 34 s.v. lo etc. that they wrote explicitly our custom was not in Talmud times for the reason mentioned above; and accordingly Levush who wrote in seif 10 in general like customs that say Elokeinu — he should not have written 'this time' at all; nevertheless in responsa Maharam Mintz that I brought above that the prayer leader says Elokeinu while the congregation answers Amen — I learned that even though he says Elokeinu, nevertheless sometimes the prayer leader reaches the kohanim before the entire congregation finishes answering Amen; on this perhaps the Talmud warns not to call kohanim until all finish; and so it appears in the body of the responsa — see there and examine; and examination is needed on it from the aforementioned Tosafot words.
From the mouth of the reader, etc. The congregation does not answer Amen to the blessing ‘with His sanctity’ until the blessing is completed from the mouth of the kohanim, and the shaliach tzibbur does not begin, etc. The same applies to the words ‘ya’er’ and ‘yisa’: the shaliach tzibbur waits until Amen is finished from the mouth of the congregation. So it is implied in Beis Yosef and so wrote Mateh Moshe. Magen Avraham distinguishes between the reader, who need not wait for everyone, and the kohanim, who must wait. Olas Tamid also inferred from Shulchan Aruch that the shaliach tzibbur calls ‘Kohanim,’ ‘Yevarechecha,’ and ‘Sim Shalom’ even if the answering of Amen has not ended from the mouth of the entire congregation, and the majority suffices. Shulchan Aruch in siman 124, seif 9, says this regarding Amen to the repetition of the shaliach tzibbur, but the kohanim should not begin ‘Yevarechecha’ or ‘Ribono shel olam’ until Amen has ended from the mouth of the entire congregation. He concludes that practically, the majority also suffices, as with other blessings of prayer. I am astonished why it was not ruled this way, for Tosafot in Elu Ne’emarin 39 wrote so, cited by Beis Yosef. It appears that this refers only to ‘Ribono,’ which resembles prayer, but regarding ‘Yevarechecha,’ which the kohanim say and which the entire congregation must hear especially, he agrees that they must wait until it has ended from the mouth of the entire congregation.
