Modim. It does not say here, 'from the entire congregation.' This teaches that the prompter may begin even before the entire congregation has finished Amen; a majority suffices. Olas Tamid and Magen Avraham.
Amen. Here it says, 'from the entire congregation,' teaching that the kohanim must wait until everyone has finished Amen. This differs from siman 167, where one need not wait for those who prolong Amen excessively. Birchas Kohanim must be heard by everyone, so the kohanim must wait. Although the prompter need not wait for those who prolong Amen, the kohanim do. This resolves Beis Yosef's question: Since the kohanim do not say a word until the chazzan first prompts it, what is meant by waiting until Amen has ended from the congregation? Olas Tamid and Magen Avraham. See Eliyah Rabbah, who discusses this at length.
The congregation. Bach asks that the kohanim may not begin until the chazzan begins Sim Shalom, as stated in se'if 15. Olas Tamid and Taz answer that if some prolong Amen and the chazzan has already begun Sim Shalom, the kohanim must nevertheless wait for them. This is incorrect. On the contrary, the chazzan must wait so that they hear the blessing, as I wrote in se'ifim ketanim 4 and 17. The kohanim, however, need not wait for them before saying Ribbono shel Olam, which is unlike Birchas Kohanim. See Magen Avraham.
