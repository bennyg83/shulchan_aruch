Modim. He did not write here "from the mouth of the entire congregation" to teach that the prompter may begin even though the entire congregation has not yet finished Amen — only a majority of the congregation suffices — Eliyahu Rabbah and Magen Avraham.

Amen. Here it is written "from the mouth of the entire congregation" to teach that the priests must wait until Amen finishes from the mouth of all. And there is one who says, as written in siman 167, that one need not wait for those who prolong excessively — for Birkat Kohanim is different, for it requires that all hear; therefore they must wait. And even though the prompter need not wait for those who prolong, the priests must wait. And with this the difficulty of Beit Yosef is resolved — what he wrote: behold, the priests do not say the word until the prayer leader prompts it first; if so, what is "until Amen finishes from the mouth of the congregation," etc., see there; and it is difficult — Eliyahu Rabbah and Magen Avraham, see there (and see in Eliyahu Rabbah who extended on this).

The congregation. Bach challenged: behold, he is not permitted to begin until the prayer leader begins "Grant peace," as written in seif 15. And one answers: if there are those who prolong in Amen and the prayer leader began "Grant peace," even so the priests must wait for them — and thus Eliyahu Rabbah and Taz explained, see there. This is not so — on the contrary, the reverse is more reasonable: the prayer leader must wait for them so that they hear the blessing, as I wrote in se'if katan 14 and conclusion 17, see there; but the priests need not wait for them with "Master of the worlds," etc., for it is not comparable to Birkat Kohanim — Magen Avraham.
