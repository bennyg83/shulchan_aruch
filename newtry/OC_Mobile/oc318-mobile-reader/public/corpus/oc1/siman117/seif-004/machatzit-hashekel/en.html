Seif katan 4, those who require, etc. This requires study, etc. Meaning, the basis of this ruling is Responsa Rosh, where the Rosh expresses wonder that we do not request rain from the seventh of Marcheshvan as the residents of Eretz Yisrael do. We follow Babylonia only in disputes of law between the scholars of the West and the Babylonian Talmud, or between residents outside Eretz Yisrael and those in Eretz Yisrael. This matter, however, depends on the natural conditions of the lands. In Eretz Yisrael rain was needed immediately from the seventh of Marcheshvan, so they requested it then. Babylonia did not need rain until sixty days after the tekufah, so they waited until then. Since these lands require rain in Marcheshvan, it would have been proper for them to follow Eretz Yisrael and request it from the seventh of Marcheshvan. The Rosh writes that his view was not accepted. Based on this, the Beit Yosef writes that although initially we do not follow the Rosh, since he himself states that his view was not accepted, nevertheless an individual who mistakenly mentioned rain during the summer need not repeat the Amidah except as a voluntary prayer. Accordingly, even without a drought, an individual who mistakenly requested rain from the seventh of Marcheshvan onward should not need to repeat the Amidah. Yet the wording of the Shulchan Aruch implies that this applies only when there is a drought.
During harvest time, etc. At that time rain is certainly an adverse sign, as stated in I Samuel 12: “Is it not wheat harvest today? I shall call to Hashem and He will send thunder and rain, and you shall know and see that your wickedness is great, which you have done in the eyes of Hashem by asking for a king.”
