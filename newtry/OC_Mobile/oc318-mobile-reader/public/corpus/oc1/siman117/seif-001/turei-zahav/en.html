And in Eretz Yisrael, etc. — because there one needs rain more, since it is higher than all lands; unlike the children of exile, and we follow the children of exile of Babylon. The Rosh already wondered about this: if these countries need rain after planting — that is, from half of Tishrei onward — why should they say it in its season? Granted, a matter that depends on obligation and exemption, prohibition and permission, we follow Bnei Bavel; but in this, why should we follow Bnei Bavel, who sit by abundant waters and do not need rain until 60 days after the equinox, unlike other countries, etc. And he expanded on this as Beit Yosef wrote. Beit Yosef concludes in the name of Mahari"i: since the Rosh wrote there that even though he is alone he may ask, because the matter is needs of the many, only so that it not appear like factions — it appears that if an individual erred and asked for rain at a time when the law was to ask for dew, he should not return, since the truth appears per the Rosh's words, and even an individual has a place to pray, except for the reason mentioned where he prayed bedieved — this concern does not apply, and it appears he should not return; requires investigation, end quote. And per Beit Yosef: since the Rosh's words were not accepted and the whole world did not practice thus, it is a passage not like the Rosh; therefore one who erred and asked for rain must return, even in a land where all, in general, need rain in the summer season. Nevertheless, since the Rav Mahari"i cast doubt upon us — who will be lightheaded against him — therefore it is fitting to leave doubt and return and pray as a nedavah, etc., end quote Beit Yosef. And thereby the words of the Shulchan Aruch are explained with proper explanation.
