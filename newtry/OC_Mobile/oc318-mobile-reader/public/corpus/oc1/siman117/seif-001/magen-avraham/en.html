There are therefore always two full days between the equinox and the beginning of the request. Until the Minchah prayer. Tur writes until Mussaf. Beit Yosef’s wording is imprecise, since the request for rain is not relevant in the Yom Tov prayer. Bach writes that the practical difference concerns one who mistakenly began a weekday Shemoneh Esrei on Yom Tov and remembered during Barech Aleinu. He completes that blessing, as stated in siman 268, and must then say “Grant dew and rain.” This requires study. Strictly according to law, the mention of rain should cease immediately at Maariv. We delay until Mussaf only so that different groups should not follow conflicting practices, as stated in siman 114. That reason is inapplicable to the request for rain, as explained above. Therefore, even an individual should not request rain at Maariv. This is explicit in Taanis 4b, which asks, “Is there a request for rain on Yom Tov?” See there. As explained above, the festivals emerge during the season of dew, because rain is not beneficial then. It appears to me that the same law applies when the first night for requesting rain falls on Friday night. If an individual mistakenly recites the weekday Shemoneh Esrei, he should not mention dew and rain, since the congregation has not yet begun requesting it. Tur also appears to imply this. Darkei Moshe gives another answer to Beit Yosef’s question. He writes that our custom is to recite the piyyutim concerning dew during Mussaf on Pesach, thereby informing the congregation that they should cease mentioning rain and should pray for dew, in accordance with the Sephardic custom. In Bircas HaShanim one should say, “Satisfy us from Your goodness,” and not “from its goodness,” because that is the established text of the prayer. I have never heard or seen anyone say “from its goodness,” and one should not alter the formulation established by the Sages. Responsa Rosh, klal 4, siman 20. Maharshal writes likewise.
