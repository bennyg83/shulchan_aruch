The years. One should say, “Satisfy us from Your goodness,” and not, “from its goodness,” as ruled by the Rosh and Maharshal. See Magen Avraham. The Taz wrote that when one is traveling he should say, “from Your goodness,” but when he is in the city he should say, “from its goodness”; see there for the reason. The Siddur of the Arizal has the version, “from its goodness.”
The sixtieth. Meaning, in the Maariv prayer with which the sixtieth day begins.
The sixtieth. Meaning, the day on which the tekufah falls is counted among the sixty days, even if the tekufah falls at midday or later, provided that it occurs somewhat before nightfall. There are always two days between the tekufah and the beginning of the request. Thus, if the tekufah occurs on Sunday, the request begins in Maariv belonging to Wednesday.
And in Eretz Yisrael. This is because rain is needed there more, since it is higher than all other lands. One who left Eretz Yisrael for outside the Land, if his wife and children remain in Eretz Yisrael, requests rain according to the practice of Eretz Yisrael even if he does not intend to return during the rainy season, as ruled by Maharikash and Halachos Ketanos, part 1, siman 74. I heard from Rabbi A. Yitzchaki, of blessed memory, who heard from Rabbi Yaakov Molcho, of blessed memory, that Rabbi Z. Gota, of blessed memory, and several other great authorities disagreed with Maharikash and wrote that he requests rain according to the practice outside Eretz Yisrael. This is likewise ruled in Responsa Devar Shmuel, siman 323. Peri Chadash ruled that residents of Eretz Yisrael who traveled outside the Land request rain according to the practice of Eretz Yisrael as long as they intend to return during that year. If they do not intend to return until after a year or two, as is common with emissaries from Eretz Yisrael, then even if their wives and children remain there, they request rain according to the practice outside Eretz Yisrael; see there.
