1) [Seif 1] The blessing of the years, etc. In Ashkenaz the custom is to say 'Bareich aleinu' both in summer and in winter, except that they change the request [for rain] according to the season: in the rainy season they say 'and give dew and rain for a blessing,' and in the summer season 'and give a blessing.' The Sephardim have the custom to say in summer 'Bareicheinu' and in winter 'Bareich aleinu,' as stated in the Tur and the Beis Yosef. And in Shaar HaKavanos it is written in the name of the Arizal, of blessed memory, that one should say in summer 'Bareicheinu' and in winter 'Bareich aleinu,' and it was cited above in siman 48; see there. So too wrote the Birkei Yosef, ot 1, in the name of the disciples of the Arizal, of blessed memory, and the Shalmei Tzibbur, folio 129b, and the Beis Oved, ot 1, cited it. And it is astonishing that he wrote it in the name of the disciples and not in the name of the Arizal, for in many places he cites from Shaar HaKavanos, which was arranged by Maharshu [R' Shmuel Vital], of blessed memory. See our words above in siman 114, ot 6.
2) There: The blessing of the years, etc. One should not say 'and satisfy us from its goodness' (mituvah) but rather 'from Your goodness' (mituvcha), for it refers to the Holy One, blessed be He, and not to the year; and likewise throughout the entire berachah. Rosh in his responsa, klal 4, siman 20; and so too wrote the Maharshal in his responsa, siman 64. Knesses HaGedolah cited it in the Hagahos HaTur and wrote that such is the custom; see there. Magen Avraham, s.k. 2. So too wrote the Kesher of the Shelah in the laws of the Amidah, and wrote that one should not be concerned with the acronym of 'ha'adamah, v'sabeinu, mituvah,' which forms a Name of Havayah; rather one should say 'mituvcha.' End of his words. So too wrote the Eliyah Rabbah, ot 1, and wrote that it is not as found in the siddur in the name of the Shelah to say 'mituvah'; see there. And as for what the Ateres Zekeinim wrote, ot 1, that on a journey one should say 'mituvcha,' since then the acronym forms one Name, while in the city one should say 'mituvah,' since then the acronym forms the Name Havayah, we have already written in the name of the Kesher [of the Shelah] that one should not be concerned with the acronym; therefore one should not differentiate the city from the journey, but should always say specifically 'mituvcha.' And as for what the Baer Heitev wrote, s.k. 1, that in the siddur of the Arizal, of blessed memory, it is written 'mituvah,' this is not found in the writings of the Arizal, of blessed memory, and also in Shaar HaKavanos, in the text of the tefillah, it is not written so, and it was already cited above in siman 48; see there. Likewise in the siddur of the Rashash, of blessed memory, which is arranged according to the kavanos of the Arizal, of blessed memory, it is written 'mituvcha.' One must therefore say that this [reading] is not from the Arizal, of blessed memory, but rather some student wrote it and attributed it in the name of the Arizal, of blessed memory; therefore one should not rely upon it, and one must say 'v'sabeinu mituvcha.' And as for what the Abudarham wrote, 'uvareich shenoseinu' (our years) in the plural, it appears to be a scribal error, for immediately afterward one says 'us'hi acharisah' (and let its end), which is singular; therefore the essential reading is to say 'uvareich shenaseinu' without a vav, and the nun vocalized with a kamatz. Shalmei Tzibbur, folio 129a, and the Shaarei Teshuvah cited it, ot 1, and wrote that even in the Ashkenazic version, in which one does not say 'us'hi acharisah,' one should nonetheless not change to say 'shenoseinu,' but rather 'shenaseinu' with a kamatz under the nun; and such is the custom. End of his words. And when one wishes to pray for a livelihood, whether it is better to say it in this berachah or in [the berachah of] Shomea Tefillah, will be explained below in siman 119, with Heaven's help.
3) There: And we begin to request rain, etc. The Tashbetz, volume 3, siman 123, wrote in the name of his teacher, of blessed memory, that it is a tradition in the hands of the Geonim, of blessed memory, that the 'sixtieth [day] of the season' (tekufah) is reckoned according to the season of Shmuel and not according to the season of Rav Adda; see there. So too wrote the Maamar Mordechai, ot 3, that we reckon according to the season of Rav Shmuel, and so it is in all the sefarim of the authorities of our times who composed [works] on the matter of the molados, that they reckon according to the season of Rav Shmuel, and such is the practice throughout the world; see there. And see in the Yeshuos Yaakov, in a responsum from his grandson, R' Tzvi Hirsch, of blessed memory, who elaborated on why we reckon [the request for rain] according to the season of Rav Shmuel, but the festivals according to the season of Rav Adda; see there. Olas Tamid, ot 2.
4) There: In the Maariv prayer of the sixtieth day, etc. For example, if the season (tekufah) falls on the first day [Sunday], the request [for rain] is begun in the Maariv prayer belonging to the fourth day [Wednesday]; it turns out that there are always two days between the season and the request. Magen Avraham, s.k. 1. So too wrote the Eliyah Rabbah, ot 3, in the name of Mateh Moshe, and gave as a mnemonic 'v'ad ya'aleh,' etc. And one who forgot and did not pray Minchah on the day before the request, or who forgot and requested [rain] at Minchah before the day of the request, whose law is to repeat, and did not remember until the Maariv prayer, this was explained above in siman 108, ot 6; see there.
5) There: In the Maariv prayer of the sixtieth day, etc. When the time for the request in the blessing of the years arrives, it is good that the shamash announce on the preceding day, at all the prayers, in this wording: 'On this night is the request,' just as he announces the season (tekufah), because the time of the request is not a fixed time like [the times for] 'morid hageshem' or 'morid hatal,' which are always on Pesach and Sukkos. Orchos Yosher, chapter 2. So too wrote the sefer Moed L'Chol Chai, siman 27, ot 4, that it is fitting for every moreh tzedek to issue an announcement about saying 'Bareich aleinu' in the Amidah a day or two before the night when the sixtieth day of the season falls. The Yafeh LaLev cited it, ot 2.
