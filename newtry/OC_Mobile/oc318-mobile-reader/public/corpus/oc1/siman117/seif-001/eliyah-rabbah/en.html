The blessing of the years. In Eliyah Zuta I wrote that our text should be 'from Your goodness,' referring to the Holy One, blessed be He, and not 'from its goodness,' referring to the year. So writes Magen Avraham in the name of a responsum of the Rosh, and so write Maharshal, Mateh Moshe, and Kitzur Shelah, unlike what is printed in a siddur in the name of the Shelah, which has 'from its goodness.'
[2] In the Maariv prayer, etc. One counts fifty-nine days. A mnemonic is “tazal katal amarta,” meaning that after counting the numerical value of “katal,” one says “Grant dew and rain.” If the sixtieth day falls on Shabbos, they begin on Motzaei Shabbos, since “Grant dew and rain” is not recited on Shabbos. So writes Mateh Moshe. This somewhat implies that even one who mistakenly began the weekday blessing of Barech Aleinu on Shabbos should not say “Grant dew and rain,” since the congregation has not yet begun saying it. Magen Avraham writes likewise, though this is not conclusive. It appears to me that in our lands he should say it, since after the fact one need not repeat the Amidah if he began from the night of the seventh of Marcheshvan, as stated below in seif katan 3.
[3] And the day of the tekufah, etc., is included, etc. This applies even if the tekufah occurs after midday. Mateh Moshe gives the following rule for determining the day on which the request begins: count four days from the weekday on which the tekufah of Tishrei fell, and on that fourth weekday in Kislev the request begins. Thus, if the tekufah fell on Sunday, the request begins on Wednesday, and so in every case. The mnemonic is, “And a mist shall rise,” etc. He further writes that after the fact, if one mistakenly said “Grant dew and rain” from the night of the seventh of Marcheshvan onward, even in our lands he need not repeat the Amidah. There is no essential reason to distinguish our lands from Eretz Yisrael. Babylonia was low-lying, whereas these lands need water like Eretz Yisrael. See Eliyahu Zuta.
Until the Mussaf prayer, etc. The Tur writes until the Mussaf prayer. They asked that requesting rain is not relevant in the festival prayer. The Bach answers that the Tur teaches that if on the morning of Pesach one mistakenly began the weekday Amidah and only realized in the middle of Birkat HaShanim, he completes that blessing and includes 'Grant dew and rain,' because the request does not cease until Mussaf. This also follows from the Tur's wording, 'as long as rain is mentioned,' indicating that whenever rain is still mentioned, the request is made where possible. One should not challenge this from Taanit 4, where the Gemara asks, 'Is there a request on Yom Tov?' There the discussion concerns the chazzan's prayer, where it is not plausible that he would mistakenly recite the weekday prayer through Birkat HaShanim, since the congregation would certainly remind him before that point, as above in siman 114, seif 2. I later saw that Magen Avraham rejected the Bach based on that Gemara, but in my humble opinion there is no difficulty, as I have explained.
