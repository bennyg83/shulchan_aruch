In the Maariv prayer — Chayei Adam wrote in the name of Peri Chadash that bedieved, if one prayed and forgot to ask for rain, we count from the evening; and therefore, since the tekufah of Tishrei never falls except at three hours or at nine hours, night or day, one never needs to repeat the first Maariv prayer, since sixty days have not yet been completed from the evening upward. And likewise, if the tekufah falls during the day, one need not repeat even in the morning prayer for one who prays before three hours of the day. And when it falls at nine hours of the day and one prays Minchah before nine hours, one also need not repeat — thus far. And Peri Megadim brought the Peri Chadash and wrote on it: "And I do not know whether to rely on him in this, for the halacha is not like Abaye Ezer in this matter." And in any case one is required to pray with a stipulation, as in siman 107, paragraph 1; see there. And in Machzik Berachah he ruled explicitly not like Peri Chadash. And also Be'er Heitev Choshen did not mention any distinction at all between the first day and other days. And also from the Gra's commentary it seems plainly that he holds that the halacha is not like Rabbeinu Efraim in this.

Until the Minchah prayer — And if one erred on Maariv of the first night of Pesach and prayed the weekday Shemoneh Esreh and remembered after beginning Birkas "Boneh Yerushalayim," the law is that he must finish that entire berachah as written in siman 268; he does not say "Tal u'Matar," since the congregation also does not say it. And if the case occurred on Shabbat and he erred and prayed of weekday and began Birkas "Boneh Yerushalayim," he likewise does not say "Tal u'Matar," since the congregation has not yet begun, and the individual is always dragged after the congregation [Magen Avraham].
