If he did not request rain, etc. At first glance it appears that the same applies if one omitted from any other blessing something essential to that blessing, such as omitting the matter of the ingathering of exiles from the blessing "Teka be-shofar," which is named for that in Megillah 13; he too may complete it in the blessing of Shome'a Tefillah. A proof is from Tosafot, Berakhot 29a, s.v. "mipnei," who excluded only matters of praise, such as the seasonal mention, Havdalah, and other praises that are not prayer and request. This case is different. Even regarding Havdalah itself, the Magen Avraham rules in siman 294 s.k. 3 that if one knows he will not have a cup on which to recite Havdalah and forgot to say Havdalah in Chonen HaDa'at, he should say it in Shome'a Tefillah so that he will not need to repeat. The same should apply here. What is stated below in siman 119:3, that if one erred in one of the middle blessings he returns to the beginning of that blessing, may refer to where he remembered after Shome'a Tefillah. This is evident because that ruling also includes one who forgot to say tal u-matar in Birkat HaShanim, and there he certainly does not return to the beginning of the blessing, but asks in Shome'a Tefillah. Rather, regarding tal u-matar the Mechaber's rule can apply only where he remembered after Shome'a Tefillah; perhaps the same applies here. The Mechaber did not spell this out because he wished to include also a skipped blessing, and when one skipped a blessing there is no difference whether he remembered before or after Shome'a Tefillah, for he may not include it there. Although the Peri Chadash is lenient even in that case, the Chayei Adam already challenged him. "Error" also includes adding something that spoils the blessing, such as saying tal u-matar in the summer and the like; in such a case there is certainly no repair in Shome'a Tefillah. But regarding an omission, perhaps he can complete it in Shome'a Tefillah as above.
Know that according to the Gra above in siman 59:1, who holds that the essential matter depends on the closing formula, he has fulfilled his obligation and does not even need completion; my question is only according to the Shulchan Aruch there. I later found in Peri Megadim, siman 119, Mishbetzot Zahav 3, that he too disagrees with the Peri Chadash, and regarding our doubt he writes that if one skipped words that are part of the blessing's theme, he cannot complete them in Shome'a Tefillah. His reason is that request and Havdalah are not fixed permanent wording, and therefore a remedy was enacted for them, unlike this; but the Peri Megadim did not bring a proof for this reasoning.
As though uprooted. See Mishnah Berurah. So wrote the Magen Avraham in siman 422, and so ruled all the later authorities, unlike the Chayei Adam, who wrote that actual uprooting of the feet is required. What the Nishmat Adam wrote, that the Yerushalmi cited by the Magen Avraham is not found, is not so; it is in Berakhot chapter 5 after the Mishnah of "Modim, modim," and the Rashba also cites it in his novellae in chapter Tefillat HaShachar. With the help of Heaven we also found in Talmidei Rabbenu Yonah there, s.v. "lo amran ela," explicit support for the Magen Avraham. The proof of the Nishmat Adam from Rashi in Berakhot can be rejected, for perhaps Rashi follows Rav Amram, cited in Tur siman 422, who rules that the matter depends only on uprooting one's feet, or follows the first formulation in the Gemara.
