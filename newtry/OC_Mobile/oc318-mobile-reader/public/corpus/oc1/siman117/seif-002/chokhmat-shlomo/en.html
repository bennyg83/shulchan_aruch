(Seif 4) If one did not ask for rain in the rainy season they make him return, etc. NB: Behold I saw in Ba'er Heitev, who brought in the name of Leket Yosher: in the law, if one continued in the Shabbat prayer and began in "Atah Chonen" and finished in Birkas HaShanim, and if he remembered it was Shabbat and it was summer days and he asked for rain — he returns until he says Birkas HaShanim properly and then goes to Shabbat, etc.; thus far. And behold I say: let the matter be silenced and not said, for lechatchilah one should not pray weekday except if one began — then he completes that berachah and no more. If so, it would work out if he had already begun that berachah; but if he had not yet begun, certainly he has no [permission] to begin. If so, in this, since he asked rain in it, it is as if he did not pray that berachah and must begin anew; and if he comes to begin anew, behold today is Shabbat and he has no [permission] to begin weekday. And it appears this law would depend on what the poskim dispute in siman 108: if one prayed on Shabbat and did not remember it is Shabbat, or on Rosh Chodesh and did not remember Yaaleh VeYavo — whether he is required to return and pray weekday or not. There are those who hold: since at night also one does not mention, why should he return and pray? And that is: although he did not pray properly, nevertheless he is not like one who did not pray at all, only considered as one who prayed but not properly, and from that this would not be corrected. But others hold that he must return and pray weekday, from the force that they hold he is like one who did not pray at all, and therefore must return and pray with stipulation. And therefore here too: if one asked for rain in Birkas HaShanim, for those who hold that one who did not act according to law is like one who did not pray — here too it is as if he did not pray Birkas HaShanim at all; and since he did not pray it at all, behold if he remembered at the beginning before he began it, he has no [permission] to begin except in the Shabbat [prayer]. And therefore here too he has no [obligation] to return and say Birkas HaShanim, only begin with Shabbat. But according to those who hold that although not properly, nevertheless he is like one who prayed — if so here too he is as one who began Birkas HaShanim only did not finish; and since he began it, he is required to finish that berachah; therefore he is required to return and say Birkas HaShanim and afterwards begin Shabbat. However for practical halacha it appears all agree here that he is like one who did not pray, for there regarding Shabbat, since by law even on Shabbat one may pray weekday, etc., therefore it is not relevant to say he is like one who did not pray even though he did not remember Shabbat; and likewise Rosh Chodesh without remembering Rosh Chodesh — since in any case in kum ve'aseh he did not spoil his prayer, only in shev ve'al taaseh; therefore he is not considered in omitting the mention like one who did not pray. But here, where he remembered in action in kum ve'aseh something improper, certainly he is like one who did not pray, and it is as if he had not begun it yet, and he is not required to begin anew. And so it seems the essential practical halacha: safek berachos lehakel, and particularly in a matter whose time is not today, only from the force that he began it — and in this there is doubt, he is not required to begin anew. So it seems to my understanding correct and clear. And the law of Leket Yosher is astounding; let the matter be silenced and not said. And the body of the book Leket Yosher is not in my hands to examine its source in this; perhaps God will grant it reaches my hands and I will explain the matter further, with God's help; examine carefully.
