Seif 2. Individuals who require rain, etc., do not request it in Bircas HaShanim, but in Shome'a Tefillah, etc. Note: See the Taz, who derives proof from Rabbi's response, “They are considered like individuals, and therefore request it in Shome'a Tefillah,” and necessarily understands that even in Shome'a Tefillah they retain the status of individuals. I say that even if this inference is accepted, the Gemara there proves the opposite. The Gemara concludes by asking, “What is the practical ruling?” and rules that the request is made in Shome'a Tefillah, but it does not repeat Rabbi's phrase, “They are considered like individuals and request it in Shome'a Tefillah.” Why did it depart from Rabbi's wording? Necessarily, since the Gemara concludes that there is a dispute, and Rabbi, as a Tanna, may disagree, one opinion holds that they have the full status of a congregation and mention rain in Bircas HaShanim, whereas Rabbi holds the opposite, namely that even in Shome'a Tefillah they are treated as individuals, as the Taz explains. The Gemara therefore rules in accordance with both positions: it follows Rabbi in requiring the request to be made in Shome'a Tefillah, but regarding audible recitation it follows the other opinion, allowing the request to be said aloud because they retain the status of a congregation. It therefore omitted the words, “They are considered like individuals,” and stated only that the halachah follows Rabbi with respect to Shome'a Tefillah, but not with respect to their being considered individuals. This is correct; examine it carefully.
