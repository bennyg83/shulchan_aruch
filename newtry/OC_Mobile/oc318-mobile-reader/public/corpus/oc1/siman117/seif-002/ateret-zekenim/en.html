Individuals who need rain, or one entire land such as all of Spain or all of Ashkenaz in its entirety, are considered like individuals, and on Shabbat. And it appears that according to this reasoning, all the more so when we need to ask for rain, heaven forbid, in Cheshvan on Shabbat when we need to — for even at the tekufah of Tammuz, which is a bad omen for rain, they ask on Shabbat when they need rain; all the more so in this case, namely in the rainy season. However, I received that one must be careful not to ask for rain at all outside the time the Sages established, even on Shabbat, but rather to appease before God, blessed be He, through fasting and selichot with the thirteen [Divine] attributes, and they recite verses and psalms of rain — but one does not ask "and give dew and rain" (my teacher in Beit Chadash). And I heard that one of the great authorities did so: when there was a withholding of rain in the summer days, he asked publicly "and give dew and rain" on Shabbat, and he was gathered to his people in that year; they attributed the matter to having troubled Heaven.
