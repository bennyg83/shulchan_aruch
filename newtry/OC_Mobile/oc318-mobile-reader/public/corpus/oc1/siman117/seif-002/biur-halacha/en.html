And however, if, etc. — The reason for this law follows the responsum of the Rosh, who wondered why we do not ask on 7 Cheshvan like the people of Eretz Yisrael; although we follow the sons of Babylonia, that refers to a law among the laws in which the people of the West argue with the Babylonian Talmud, or the people of chutz la'aretz with the people of Eretz Yisrael — but this depends on the nature of the lands: in Eretz Yisrael they needed rain immediately on 7 Cheshvan and therefore ask on 7 Cheshvan, and the sons of Babylonia did not need rain until sixty days after the tekufah; therefore they did not ask until that time. And if so, in lands that need rain in Cheshvan it would be fitting that they practice like the people of Eretz Yisrael to ask on 7 Cheshvan. And likewise, if the custom of lands that need rain also in the summer days [such as Ashkenaz, where the Rosh mentioned that it needs rain from Pesach until Shavuos], all their law in prayer is like winter days in Eretz Yisrael. And the Rosh wrote that his words were not accepted before the sages of his generation; and based on this Beit Yosef wrote that although lechatchilah we do not rule like him, since it spread among the dispersed of Israel not like him, nevertheless bedieved if an individual erred and mentioned at the [proper] time, etc., he is not required to return to pray except as a nedavah — and with this the words of Shulchan Aruch are explained, and what he wrote "if in a land," etc., means to exclude if some towns need rain — for whatever does not have the name of a land, they are considered like individuals, even per the Rosh; so it is proven in the Rosh's responsum and brought in Beit Yosef. And even bedieved if one asked for rain in Birkas HaShanim he returns — and it is astounding about Peri Chadash who ruled that one city that needs rain likewise bedieved need not return; behold there is proof from the case of Ninveh to the contrary, unless we force it and say that what the Gemara ruled in Shomea Tefillah is only lechatchilah, but bedieved even in Birkas HaShanim; and this requires study.

Those who need rain — It seems somewhat from this wording that specifically when rain is withheld; but otherwise we make him return even though rain in that place is not a sign of curse. And as seems from the Rosh's responsum, from which is the source of this law — lands that need rain between Pesach and Shavuos, like Ashkenaz and the like — even in the general case their law is like winter days in Eretz Yisrael; and although lechatchilah we do not rule like him, at least regarding bedieved one need not return; and this requires study [Machatzit HaShekel and Machaneh Efraim]. And see in Zecher LeAvraham, who wrote not to enter into a safek berachos. He further wrote there that the same applies for us in our places, that we need rain between Pesach and Shavuos — that if one erred and asked then for rain, one need not return, for as it appears all those places near and far need rain and it is as the land of Ashkenaz and Spain as the Rosh wrote; see there. And from the words of Be'er Heitev Choshen and Chayei Adam it seems they hold that specifically when there was withholding of rain; but otherwise one must return and pray. And it seems to me this is a safek in the law; therefore one should return and pray and stipulate: if I have not fulfilled my prayer obligation until now it should be for obligation, and if I have fulfilled it, for nedavah — as above in siman 108, seif 11, and as the Gra wrote in Biur in this siman; and there that safek in the law is like a safek whether one prayed, in siman 107, seif 1; see there in Magen Avraham. And the same in all this: if one erred and asked for rain from 7 Marcheshvan onward in diaspora lands that need rain at the start of winter, one returns and prays and stipulates as stated. And all from this reason: for in Berach Yosef and Shailat Yaavetz they ruled that bedieved one need not return to pray, like Radbaz; and it is explained there in Radbaz that since in that place it is rain season, even though there is no present need for rain, one need not repeat the prayer on account of this, since in any case it is not a sign of curse — and this is like the Magen Avraham's view above. And from Be'er Heitev Choshen and Chayei Adam and some other Acharonim it seems they did not adopt this reasoning for practical halacha unless there is explicit withholding of rain; therefore it appears one should act as we said above.

One who erred in it alone, etc. — It seems plain that the same applies if the entire congregation or shaliach tzibbur erred and asked tal u'matar — likewise he is not required to return, for the reason is as Maharia wrote: that truly the Rosh's words appear [correct] and one may rely on him at least regarding bedieved. And if so, what difference is there whether individual, shaliach tzibbur, or congregation? And that which he specified "individual" is to include even if only one individual asked tal u'matar not following the custom of the entire congregation there — still, perhaps [one need not return], since it is the need of the many; and so it seems in Levush. And it is astounding about Peri Megadim and Be'er Heitev Choshen who wrote that specifically an individual, but a shaliach tzibbur returns — they have no source for this. And further it seems plainly that he specified "individual" because in the congregation, even as nedavah one cannot return to pray, as above in siman 107, seif 3 — but there is no obligation even in the congregation, and it appears the same for shaliach tzibbur. Also in what Peri Megadim wrote in Mishbetzos Zahav, se'if katan 2, that specifically if he erred, but if he acted intentionally he returns to the beginning, as above siman 114 — in our matter we cannot say this at all, for the reason is because one may rely bedieved on the Rosh; and behold according to him certainly even if intentional he does not return, for such is the nusach of the berachah.

And he asked for rain, etc. — It seems that regarding rain he did not err, but conducted himself like other people and did not mention — and nevertheless he is not obligated by law to return and pray. And seemingly it is difficult, for the reason is perhaps the halacha is like the Rosh, that the time of asking extends there longer; and if so, the time of mention also extends there for him, as explained explicitly in the Rosh's responsum there, that both are equal in this. And if so he should return for not having said rain, for he wrote there that in such lands they would have the law of returning "Morid HaGeshem" from Shavuos onward, like Eretz Yisrael from Pesach onward; see there. And it seems there they are entirely equal; and if so, just as in Eretz Yisrael if one did not mention until Pesach they make him return, so too here until Shavuos — and if so necessarily he did not fulfill this prayer. And seemingly from this there would be proof for Peri Megadim who wrote in siman 114, ot vav, that according to Shulchan Aruch in mention, even one entire land that needs rain, nevertheless they make him return, and he does not hold this at all like the Rosh; and see there in Biur Halacha what I wrote about this. Or perhaps Shulchan Aruch speaks in what he wrote "and however," etc., to his custom — the Sephardic custom that practices saying "tal" in summer days, as above siman 114, seif 3, and it was ruled there in siman 65 that if one mentioned "tal" instead of rain they do not make him return — but this is somewhat strained, since he begins with two [lands], Spain and Ashkenaz, and concludes with one.
