2) There — Birkat HaShanim, etc. And one should not say "u've'achalnu mituvah" but rather "mituvkha," for it refers to the Holy One, blessed be He, and not to the year; and so throughout the blessing — the Rosh in responsa, general 4, siman 20; and so Maharshal in responsa siman 64. And Knesset HaGedolah in the hagahot of Taz wrote that such is the custom, see there. Magen Avraham seif katan 2. And so Kitzur Shnei Luchot HaBrit in the laws of Shemoneh Esreh. He wrote that one should not pay attention to the sefer Torah layout facing "ha'adamah" and "u've'achalnu" "mituvah," which is the tetragrammaton — rather one says only "mituvkha," end quote. And so Eliyah Rabba letter 1 wrote, and this is unlike what is found in the siddur in the name of Shlah to say "mituvah," see there. And what Eliyah Rabba wrote in letter 1 — on the road one says "mituvkha" because then in the sefer Torah layout there is the name YHVH; and in the city one says "mituvah" because then there is the tetragrammaton in the sefer Torah layout — we already wrote in the name of Kitzur Shnei Luchot HaBrit that one should not pay attention to the sefer Torah layout, and therefore one should not change between city and road but should always say "mituvkha" specifically. And what Ba'eir Heitev wrote in seif katan 1 — that in the siddur of the Ari z"l it is written "mituvah" — this is not found in the writings of the Ari z"l, and also in Sha'ar HaKavanot in the prayer text it is not written; and we already wrote above in siman 48, see there. And likewise in the siddur of Rabbi Shalom Sharabi z"l, arranged following the kavanot of the Ari z"l, it is written "mituvkha"; if so, it must be that this is not from the Ari z"l but rather some student wrote it and titled it in the name of the Ari z"l, and therefore one should not rely on this; and one must say "u've'achalnu mituvkha." And what Radbaz wrote — "u'varekh shnotenu" in plural language — it appears to be a scribal error, for immediately afterward one says "u't'hi acharitah," which is singular language; and therefore the essential thing is to say "u'varekh shnatenu" without vav, and the nun should be kamatz. Shulchan Aruch page 129 side a, and they cited Sha'arei Teshuvah letter 1, and he wrote that even in the Ashkenaz version where they do not say "u't'hi acharitah," nevertheless one should not change to say "shnotenu" but only "shnatenu" with kamatz on the nun — and such is the custom, end quote. And when one wishes to pray for livelihood — whether it is better to say it in this blessing or on Shabbat and Yom Tov — it will be explained below in siman 119 b'ezrat Heaven.
