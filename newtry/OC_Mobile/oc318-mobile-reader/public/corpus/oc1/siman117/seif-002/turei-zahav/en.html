Rather in Shemoneh Esrei. It seems to me that what is permitted for the congregation to pray in Shemoneh Esrei is when they pray in a whisper; but the prayer leader should not say it in a loud voice, even in Shemoneh Esrei — as it says in the end of chapter 1 of Taanit: the people of Nineveh sent to Rabbi [Chanina]: such as we, who even in the season of Tammuz need rain — how shall we act? As individuals who are like us, or as multitudes who are like us? As individuals who are like us, in Shemoneh Esrei, or as multitudes who are like us? And in Birkat HaShanim he sent them: as individuals who are dead — and Rashi explained: and in Shemoneh Esrei we say the request for rain as an individual who asks his needs. For what we say, that the request belongs in Birkat HaShanim even for an individual, is because it is the congregation's season; but in another matter that applies to an individual and not to the congregation — as here, where the season of Tammuz is not the congregation's season for requesting — in Shemoneh Esrei one mentions it, not in Birkat HaShanim, end quote. Behold, we have no power to make communal prayer from this, even for a whole land, if the prayer leader says it in a loud voice when repeating his prayer — he would thereby make communal prayer from it; and this is more severe than an individual who says it in Birkat HaShanim in this regard, for the Sages were strict not to make communal prayer from this except in its season. And I saw in Moharach, of blessed memory, who wrote: I received that one must be careful not to ask for rain at all out of season, even in Shemoneh Esrei, except one appeases through fasts and selichot, etc. And I heard that two great ones acted thus at a time of withholding of rain in the summer season — to ask in the congregation and give dew and rain in Shemoneh Esrei — and each was gathered to his people in that year, and they attributed the matter to having troubled Heaven, end quote. If there is substance to this kabbalah, it seems straightforward that he speaks of the prayer leader's prayer, and not because of troubling Heaven — for this matter is found only in that incident of Rava in chapter 3 of Taanit, page 24, who was nearly not punished for requesting rain in Tammuz not for need, as Rashi explained there — namely, that he wished to show the king that he had power to pray for a miracle, as is stated there; but in a place where there is need for rain there is no reason or scent of reason to prevent a request for rain in Shemoneh Esrei, as is proper in siman 119, that a person asks his needs in Shemoneh Esrei; and see what is written there. Heaven forbid that a person be punished for this — unless the prayer leader mentions it in the repetition of the prayer; this is not correct for the reason I mentioned, that then communal prayer is made. And it seems one may bring proof from Rabbi's language, who answered the people of Nineveh: as individuals who are dead, and in Shemoneh Esrei — why did he mention and in Shemoneh Esrei? He could have said briefly as individuals who are dead, for that was the question; rather it appears he hinted to them that even in Shemoneh Esrei they should not mention this in the congregation — that is, in the prayer leader's prayer; therefore he said as individuals who are dead, and in Shemoneh Esrei — that even in Shemoneh Esrei you should be specifically as individuals; as seems correct to me.
