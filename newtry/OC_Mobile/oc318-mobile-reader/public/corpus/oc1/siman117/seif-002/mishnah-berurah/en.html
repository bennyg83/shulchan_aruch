(8) One does not request for him — and even though it is stated below in siman 119 that if he needed livelihood he says it in the Blessing of the Years, since livelihood is needed for all and has no harm to anyone; but rain has harm to other lands.
(9) On Shabbat and Yom Tov — and it appears therefore all the more that we should request rain on Shabbat and Yom Tov after the seventh of Marcheshvan, or between Pesach and Shavuot, in places that need it; for even in the period of Tamuz, which are a sign of curse, they request on Shabbat and Yom Tov. The Taz wrote: that the congregation may pray on Shabbat and Yom Tov means when praying silently; but the chazzan should not say it aloud, even on Shabbat and Yom Tov. And our custom today is that the chazzan says on Shabbat and Yom Tov, but he does not say "and give dew and rain" alone, rather in verses and other wordings — and it is already printed in siddurim. And on Shabbat, Rosh Chodesh, and Yom Tov they mention the thirteen attributes and say psalms of rain, as in siddurim.
(10) Returns and prays — speaks when he finished his entire prayer; then he returns and prays as voluntary from the beginning of the prayer. And if he remembered before finishing, it is not applicable to say he returns to the Blessing of the Years and prays as voluntary; rather, he finishes from the beginning his entire prayer, and afterward returns and prays as voluntary [Mordechai].
(11) General — see in Biur Halacha that if there was no withholding of rain and he requested rain in our lands from Pesach until Shavuot, he must by law return and pray as voluntary.
