(8) One does not request it for him. Although it is stated below in siman 119 that if one needs livelihood he may request it in the blessing of the years, livelihood is different, for it is needed by everyone and causes no harm to anyone. Rain, however, can harm other lands.
(9) In Shomea Tefillah. It appears that, according to this, we should certainly request rain in Shomea Tefillah after the seventh of Marcheshvan, or between Pesach and Shavuos, in places that require it. After all, even during the summer season, when rain is considered a sign of curse, they request it in Shomea Tefillah. Taz writes that the congregation's permission to pray for it in Shomea Tefillah applies when they pray silently, but the chazzan should not say it aloud, even in Shomea Tefillah. Our present custom is that the chazzan does say it in Shomea Tefillah, but he does not say only, “Give dew and rain.” Rather, he uses verses and other formulations, as printed in the siddurim. On Shabbos, Rosh Chodesh, and Yom Tov, they mention the Thirteen Attributes and recite Tehillim concerning rain, as stated in the siddurim.
(10) He repeats the prayer. This refers to one who has completed his entire prayer. He should then repeat the prayer from the beginning as a voluntary prayer. If he remembered before completing it, he cannot return to the blessing of the years and continue as a voluntary prayer. Rather, he should first complete the entire prayer, and afterward repeat it as a voluntary prayer. Maamar Mordechai.
(11) At all. See Biur Halachah, which states that if there was no drought and one requested rain in our countries between Pesach and Shavuos, strictly speaking he must repeat the prayer as a voluntary prayer.
