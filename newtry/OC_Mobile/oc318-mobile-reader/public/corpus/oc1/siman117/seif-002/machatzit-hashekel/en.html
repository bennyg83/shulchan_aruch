Seif katan 2, until the prayer, etc. The Bach wrote, etc., and he must say it, etc. Meaning, if one erred in Shacharis and prayed the weekday Amidah until Barech Aleinu, he must say “Grant dew and rain” within Barech Aleinu.
To mention, meaning “He causes the wind to blow,” etc.
And regarding the request, etc. As stated above at the beginning of siman 114. In any event, not everyone begins on the same day. Although we are presently discussing cessation, and in fact everyone ceases on the same day, just as uniformity is not essential regarding the beginning, so too it is not essential regarding cessation. At least with respect to the request we find that one person requests and another does not, namely at the beginning, as stated above in siman 114.
Accordingly, even if at the end one person requests and another does not, this does not constitute forbidden factionalism. His primary reliance is also upon the statement of Chazal.
If so, even an individual should not request it at Maariv, etc. Meaning, we see that were it not for the concern of forming factions, it would have been proper to begin mentioning rain at night. For this reason, with respect to the request, where this concern does not apply at the beginning, they begin at night. The same should apply to cessation: where there is no concern of factions, it is proper to cease at night. Since the congregation actually prays the Yom Tov Amidah at Maariv and does not request rain, an individual who mistakenly prays the weekday Amidah should likewise not request it. On the contrary, by not requesting rain he does not form a faction, because the congregation also did not request it. This is explicit in Taanis 4a. The Mishnah states that Rabbi Yehudah says: On the first day of Pesach, the one who leads Shacharis mentions rain, while the latter one, namely the one who leads Mussaf, no longer mentions it. On 4b the Gemara challenges this from a Baraisa in which Rabbi Yehudah states that rain is requested until Pesach has passed. The Gemara initially assumes that mention and request cease at the same time. Rav Yosef explains that “until Pesach has passed” means until the chazzan who leads Shacharis on the first day of Pesach has passed before the ark. Abaye objects: Is there a request for rain on Yom Tov? The Baraisa discusses requesting rain, yet neither at Maariv nor at Shacharis is the request made because the Yom Tov Amidah contains no such request. According to the Bach, the Gemara could have answered that the practical difference concerns an individual who mistakenly prayed the weekday Amidah through Barech Aleinu and would need to request rain. Since it did not answer this way, the Bach's ruling is necessarily incorrect. The text “as stated above, that they went out, etc.” contains a scribal error. It should read: Moreover, according to what the Beit Yosef stated above in siman 114 in the name of the Yerushalmi, Rabbi Avin said in the name of Rabbi Yochanan that Rabbi Yehudah's reason is so that the festivals should conclude with dew, because dew is beneficial to the world. Meaning, even though on Yom Tov rain is not an adverse sign once they have left the sukkah, and the comparison to a servant whose master pours the cup in his face no longer applies, nevertheless a person's heart rejoices more when the sky is clear and rain does not fall. The Rosh explains that this gives the reason why we do not begin mentioning rain at Maariv of Shemini Atzeres. The earlier Yerushalmi explanation, that not everyone attends shul at Maariv, suffices only for the cessation at Mussaf on the first day of Pesach, since before Yom Tov people are occupied with preparing matzah and maror. On the night of Shemini Atzeres, however, Chol HaMoed preceded it and people are not occupied, so everyone attends shul and rain should have been mentioned at Maariv. Therefore the Yerushalmi gives the additional reason that the festivals should conclude with dew, showing that they did not wish even to mention rain. How, then, could the Bach rule that an individual who erred should request “Grant dew and rain”? This also appears from the Tur. Although an individual might otherwise have been fit to mention or request rain, because the congregation did not do so he follows the congregation. The Tur cites Raavyah that the sixty days after the tekufah must be complete twenty-four-hour periods. Therefore, if the tekufah fell three hours into the day and the congregation prays on the sixtieth day before those three hours, they do not request rain. Even if an individual prays after those three hours, when the time for the request has already arrived, since the congregation did not request it earlier, he follows the congregation and does not request it. Although we do not follow Raavyah regarding the need for complete twenty-four-hour periods, no one disputes the principle that an individual follows the congregation in such a case. The same applies here.
And in Darchei Moshe he wrote another answer, etc. Meaning, how can the Bach's ruling be rejected when proof for it might be derived from the Tur's resolution of the Beit Yosef's question? Therefore he notes that Darchei Moshe offered a different answer.
In order to publicize, etc. Meaning, because there is no announcement that the congregation is ceasing, as Magen Avraham writes in siman 114, seif katan 5, the recitation of the liturgical poems requesting blessed dew, as stated there in seif katan 4, publicizes that they are ceasing to request rain.
This follows the Sephardic custom of mentioning dew throughout the summer, as stated above in siman 114. We Ashkenazim, at least at the beginning of summer, namely during Mussaf of the first day of Pesach, pray for blessed dew. Since until the recital of the dew poems it is not publicized that the congregation is ceasing to mention and request rain, the Tur's wording, “until Mussaf,” is understandable, for until then the time of the request appears to continue.
One should say, etc. “From Your goodness,” and not “from its goodness.” The Maharshal explains that satisfaction does not come from the goodness of the land, which is a separate blessing. As Rashi explains on “You shall eat your bread to satisfaction” in Parashas Bechukosai, this is an additional blessing: one eats a little and it is blessed within his stomach.
