(א) (Endnote 2) "Until the prayer," etc. — and the Bach wrote etc., and he should say etc. — meaning: if one erred in Shacharit and prayed weekday prayer until "Barekh aleinu," he should say within the blessing "Barekh aleinu" "v'ten tal u'matar."

(ב) From mentioning — meaning "Who makes the wind blow," etc.

(ג) "And in requesting," etc. — as said above, siman 114; and among us not everyone begins on day one. And even though now for the matter of ceasing they are established — and indeed everyone ceases on day one — nevertheless, just as there is no strictness regarding beginning, so too regarding ceasing, for in any case we find in requesting that this one asks and that one does not ask — that is, at the beginning, as above in siman 114.

(ד) And if so, if in the end one was asking and one was not asking, there is no strictness. Also, his main reliance is on what our Sages said.

(ה) "If so, even an individual does not ask in Maariv," etc. — meaning: for it appears that if not for concern of groups [with differing practices], it would be proper to mention from the evening; and what is the reason that in requesting this is not applicable at the beginning? Therefore they begin in the evening. And if so, the same applies to ceasing — where there is no concern of groups, it would be proper to cease from the evening. And since in truth the congregation prays Maariv as Yom Tov prayer and does not ask in the prayer, therefore even an individual who erred and prayed weekday prayer does not ask; on the contrary, in this that he does not ask there are not groups of groups, for the congregation too did not ask. And so explicitly in Taanit 4, from that which we learned at the beginning of Taanit — Rabbi Yehuda says: one who passes before the ark on the first Yom Tov of Passover and prays Shacharit mentions "Who makes the wind blow," and the last one — that is, the one praying Musaf — again does not mention. And on 4b it challenges: and it was taught — Rabbi Eliezer [says]: until when do we ask for rains? That is, "v'ten tal u'matar" — until Passover passes; and it might seem that mention and requesting have the same time of ceasing. And R' Yosef answers: what is "until Passover passes" that the baraita teaches? Until the prayer leader who prays Shacharit on the first Yom Tov of Passover passes. And Abaye challenges: requesting on Yom Tov — does such a thing exist? And the baraita deals with requesting! And how could he say to him "until the prayer leader who prays Shacharit passes" — behold, already in the evening and Shacharit they did not ask, and in Yom Tov prayer there is no requesting. And per the Bach's words it should have said for example: until the prayer leader who prays Shacharit passes — if an individual erred and prayed weekday until "Barekh aleinu" he must ask — or that the Bach's words do not exist. And as said above, "that the festivals have passed," etc. — there is a scribal error here and it should read as follows. And further, according to what Mahari wrote in siman 114 in the name of the Yerushalmi — R' Avin in the name of R' Yochanan: the reason of R' Yehuda is so that the festivals have passed and dew is nullified, because dew is good for the world (and meaning: rain on Yom Tov — even though they have already left the sukkah and it is not the reason they said in Sukkah that rain on the festival is a sign of curse, like a slave who pours a cup for his master and spills it on his face — nevertheless a person's heart is happier if the sky is clear and rains do not fall); if from those rains would fall. And the Rosh explained that he comes to give a reason why one does not begin to ask in Maariv of Shabbat and Yom Tov — for it seems the reason the Yerushalmi gave before this is because not everyone is in the synagogue at Maariv; and in what Magen Avraham wrote in siman 114 — it is not sufficient except for ceasing, which they cease at Musaf of the first Yom Tov of Passover, for in the evening not everyone is in the synagogue because on the eve of Yom Tov people are busy with work for the needs of Yom Tov and matzah and maror; unlike the night of Shabbat and Yom Tov that was before it, which was the eve of the Festival for the Diaspora, and people are not busy — if so, on the night of Shabbat and Yom Tov everyone enters the synagogue and it would have been proper to mention in Maariv of Shabbat and Yom Tov "Who makes the wind blow"; and for this he gave the reason that the festivals have passed and dew [is nullified], and even to mention rain they did not want. And if so, how did the Bach write that an individual who erred asked "v'ten tal u'matar"? And so in the Tur. Meaning: even though the individual would have been worthy to mention or ask, nevertheless since the congregation did not ask, the individual is drawn after the congregation — for the Tur wrote in the name of Ravyah that these sixty days after the tekufah require hour-for-hour calculation; therefore if the tekufah fell three hours into the day, if the congregation prays on the sixtieth day before three hours into the day they do not ask, and if afterward an individual prays after three hours — even though then it is already the time for requesting — nevertheless, since the congregation that prayed before three hours did not ask "v'ten tal u'matar," also the individual praying after three hours is drawn after the congregation and does not ask. And it is not that we rule like Ravyah and require hour-for-hour; nevertheless, in this that the individual is drawn after the congregation in such a case there is no dispute — and the same applies here.

(ו) "And in Darkei Moshe he wrote another answer," etc. — meaning: how is it possible to reject the Bach's words? For one can say proof from the Tur's words to resolve Mahari's difficulty above; and for this he wrote "behold, Darkei Moshe [gives] another answer."

(ז) "In order to inform," etc. — meaning: since they do not proclaim that they cease, as Magen Avraham wrote in siman 114 seif katan 5; therefore through saying the piyyutim that request dew for a blessing, as Magen Avraham wrote in siman 114 seif katan 4 — from this it becomes known that they cease to ask for rain.

(ח) As the custom of Spain — they mention throughout all the summer days, as above in siman 114; and we Ashkenazim, at least at the beginning of the summer days — that is, in the Musaf prayer of the first day of Passover we pray for dew for a blessing. And since until that time of saying the piyyutim, dew is not publicized that they cease to mention and ask — it is not the Tur's language "until Musaf," for until that time it appears that the time for requesting still continues.

(ט) "One should say," etc., "mituvkha" and not "mituvah." And the reason — Maharshal wrote that satiation is not from the goodness of the land, which is a separate blessing; and as Rashi explained on the verse in parashat Bechukotai: "and you shall eat your bread to satiety" — that is a separate blessing; one eats a little and is blessed in his innards.
