Seif katan 2, until the prayer, etc. Bach writes, etc., and that must be said, etc. Meaning, if one erred at Shacharit and recited the weekday Amidah until the blessing Barech Aleinu, he should say within Barech Aleinu, 'and give dew and rain.' 'To mention' means saying 'Mashiv haruach,' etc. Regarding requesting rain, etc., as explained above at the beginning of siman 114. In any event, not everyone begins on the first day. The words 'and as stated above, that they departed,' etc., contain a scribal error. The correct text is: Furthermore, according to what Beit Yosef wrote above in siman 114 in the name of the Yerushalmi, Rabbi Avin said in the name of Rabbi Yochanan that Rabbi Yehudah's reason is that the festivals should depart with dew because dew is beneficial. Even after leaving the sukkah, rainfall on Yom Tov diminishes people's joy, for the heart is happier when the sky is clear and rain is not falling. Rosh explains that this comes to answer why we do not begin requesting rain at Maariv of Shemini Atzeret. The earlier reason in the Yerushalmi, that not everyone is present in synagogue at night, suffices regarding the cessation of the mention at Mussaf on the first day of Pesach because on the preceding evening people are occupied with preparing for Yom Tov, matzah, and maror. On the night of Shemini Atzeret, however, Chol HaMoed preceded it and people are not occupied in this way, so everyone comes to synagogue. One might therefore have expected them to begin at Maariv. The Yerushalmi explains that the festivals should depart with dew, and they did not even wish to mention rain then. This makes Bach's statement that an individual should begin earlier difficult. Nevertheless, the individual follows the congregation. Tur writes in the name of Ra'avyah that the 60 days after the seasonal cycle require complete 24-hour periods. Thus if the tekufah occurred 3 hours into the day, and the congregation prays on the 60th day before those 3 hours have passed, they do not request rain. If an individual later prays after the 3-hour point, when the time of requesting rain has technically arrived, he still follows the congregation, which did not request rain, and does not say 'Veten tal umatar.' Although we do not rule like Ra'avyah regarding the requirement of complete 24-hour periods, there is no disagreement with the principle that in a case of this sort the individual follows the congregation. The same applies here. Darchei Moshe gives another answer, etc. Meaning, how can Bach's position be rejected when proof for it might be drawn from Tur's resolution of Beit Yosef's question? He therefore notes that Darchei Moshe gave another resolution. In order to publicize, etc. Meaning, because there is no announcement that the congregation is ceasing, as Magen Avraham writes in siman 114, seif katan 5, the liturgical poems requesting blessed dew publicize that they are ceasing to request rain. This follows the Sephardic custom of mentioning dew throughout the summer. We Ashkenazim at least at the beginning of summer, during Mussaf on the first day of Pesach, pray for blessed dew. Until those poems are recited it has not yet been publicized that the congregation has ceased mentioning and requesting rain. Tur's wording, 'until Mussaf,' is therefore understandable, because until that point the period of requesting rain appears to continue. One should say 'from Your goodness,' not 'from its goodness.' Maharshal explains that satiation does not come from the goodness of the land, which is a separate blessing. As Rashi explains the verse 'You shall eat your bread to satisfaction' in Parashat Bechukotai, this is an additional blessing: one eats a small amount and it becomes blessed within his stomach.
