(4) With Rashi's commentary; For it explains Scripture as the Targum does, and even more. But reading another vernacular translation that explains only the individual words does not fulfill the requirement in place of Targum, because the Targum explains many matters that cannot be understood from the Scripture alone.
(5) Is considered like Targum; Thus, one fulfills the obligation by reading Scripture twice and Rashi's commentary once; verses on which Rashi has no commentary should be read three times. One who is unable to understand Rashi should read a Torah commentary in the contemporary Yiddish language, such as Tz'enah Ur'enah or the like, which explains the portion according to Rashi and the other Sages, founded upon the Talmud.
(6) Targum and also Rashi; Targum has the advantage that it was given at Sinai and explains every word, while Rashi has the advantage of explaining the subject through the Midrashim of Chazal more than the Targum does. In truth, everyone should study the weekly portion with Rashi in addition to Targum, for many Torah portions (especially in Vayikra) cannot be understood at all through Targum alone. One who presently has only a Chumash should read Scripture twice and recite the Targum when it becomes available. He should read in order, not an earlier verse after a later one, and initially he should read Scripture twice before Targum, not Targum first. If possible, it is very commendable not to interrupt shnayim mikra v'echad Targum for anything; meticulous people act thus, and a conscientious person should do so [Knesses HaGedolah in the name of Seder HaYom; Shaarei Efraim].
