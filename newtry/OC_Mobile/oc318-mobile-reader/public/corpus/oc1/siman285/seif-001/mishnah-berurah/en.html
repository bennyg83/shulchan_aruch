(1) To read for himself each week; That is, he should neither read it before that week nor delay afterward, for he must complete his portions together with the congregation. Whoever completes his portions together with the congregation is granted length of days and years [Gemara].
(2) Twice Scripture; He should not read Scripture once and Targum once while intending to hear the second reading from the shaliach tzibbur; rather, he must read Scripture twice besides what he hears from the shaliach tzibbur, unless he also reads it aloud then. Magen Avraham 8 cites Lechem Chamudos that, after the fact, one reading may be fulfilled by hearing the shaliach tzibbur, though some later authorities are stringent even after the fact. Regarding the method, some say to read every verse twice followed by its Targum; others say to read each open or closed passage twice and then its Targum. Magen Avraham and Shaarei Teshuvah favor the first view. Maaseh Rav records that the Vilna Gaon recited the Targum after every open or closed passage, or wherever there appeared to be a greater break in the subject. Either practice is valid. One who knows the cantillation and vowels by heart should ideally read from a Torah scroll itself.
(3) And Divon; Although it has no Targum; the same applies to Reuven, Shimon, and the like, which must be read three times. Some are stringent that for Ataros and Divon, which do have a Jerusalem Targum, that Targum should be read.
