Twice the verse and once the translation, etc. Meaning each verse twice and then its Targum. It is the optimal mitzvah to read each parashah unit, verse and translation, before beginning the next. Every petuchah or setumah, and even a pesik in the middle of a verse such as "after the plague" or Etzion Gever in Devarim, counts as a unit for this purpose, as Shelah writes. Lechem Chamudot, seif katan 41, infers from the masculine wording "two" that the instruction concerns each verse; if it referred to a parashah it should use the feminine. See Maharshal's answer there. The Arizal and Magen Avraham also say to proceed verse by verse. Magen Avraham adds that the final verse should be said again after the Targum so as to conclude with Torah, meaning it is said before the Targum and repeated after it, as the Tur at the beginning of siman 284 implies. Seder HaYom writes that one who has no Chumash with Targum should read the verses twice and, upon reaching a settled place, read the Targum later. He should not read later verses before earlier ones, but preserve the order.
[Levush] So that he will be proficient, etc. Shelah, page 131, writes that one should direct heart and eyes to contemplate the mitzvot, ethical teachings, and character traits emerging from the verses. The Arizal did not chant the Targum with trop, only the Hebrew text, as a gloss to Shelah records. Olat Shabbat, citing the kabbalists, writes that it is good not to interrupt at all but to complete the entire reading in one sitting. Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah cites Mateh Moshe siman 444 as giving a reason: Torah was given three times, at Sinai, in Ohel Moed, and in Mishneh Torah. He asks why then we do not read the Hebrew three times, suggesting the reason accounts only for three total readings, while Targum has a separate rationale corresponding to the meturgeman. I am surprised, because Mateh Moshe itself explains: first at Sinai, second in Ohel Moed, third as "explained well" in Mishneh Torah; therefore we read the text twice and the third time Targum, corresponding to that explanation. The reason for Targum is thus explicit: it explains the text as Mishneh Torah was explained well. The later authorities cite Radbaz that one expert in the trop by heart has a mitzvah to read from a sefer Torah. Taz writes that a God-fearing person should read at least once from a sefer Torah, while Perishah holds a sefer Torah is required only when reading publicly for others.
[3] [Levush] His days are lengthened, etc. It was necessary to say "days" even regarding an elderly person who has already advanced in years. It further appears to me that lengthening and shortening of days may be understood as referring to good and evil respectively (Ma'adanei Melech, p. 5). Even those who are weary and embittered in spirit, who loathe their lives, are obligated in reading the Torah portion twice and the Targum once. A person should not read the Torah twice and the Targum once in order to receive the reward of long life, but should read for the sake of the One Who commanded it, and long life will follow of itself (Sefer Chasidim 301). However, Tosafot on Rosh Hashanah 4a states that for a Jew this is permitted, because he does not regret his earlier good deeds.
[4] Atarot and Divon, etc. Even though these are names of idolatrous places (Rabbeinu Bachya).
[5] [Levush] But in the vernacular, etc. This applies specifically to one who can understand Rashi's commentary. One who is not capable of that should read Tze'enah Ure'enah or a similar work in the vernacular, so that he will understand the subject of the parashah (Taz).
