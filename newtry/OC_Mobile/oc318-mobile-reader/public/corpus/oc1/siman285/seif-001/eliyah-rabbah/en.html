[1] Twice Scripture and once translation, etc. Explanation: this after that and afterward translation; and it is a mitzvah from the preferred to read in each parashah Scripture and translation on it before beginning another; and wherever there is a parashah, whether open or closed, and even when there is a break in the middle of a verse such as "and it was after the plague" and "in Zion a man" — the parashah of Devarim is called a parashah for this matter (Shelah). And Lechem Chamudos s.k. 41 deduced from that he said "twice" masculine language — that he should read on every verse and verse; for if it referred to a parashah it should have said "twice" feminine. And what Rashal answered — see there (and Olah Tamid did not look in Lechem Chamudos and Gan Nata erred in the view of Rashal); and likewise wrote the Ari and Magen Avraham; and he concluded the last verse they say after the translation in order to finish in Torah — until here; explanation that they said before translation and also after translation; and likewise it implies in Tur beginning siman 284.
