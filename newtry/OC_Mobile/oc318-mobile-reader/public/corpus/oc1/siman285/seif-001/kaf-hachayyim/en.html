1) [Seif 1] To read for himself, etc. Rav Huna bar Yehudah said in the name of Rabbi Ami: A person should always complete his parshiyos with the congregation, reading the Scripture twice and the translation once, even Ataros and Divon, because anyone who completes his parshiyos with the congregation has his days and years prolonged. Berachos 8a; Tur. The initial letters of VeEleh Shemos allude to the phrase, 'A person is obligated to complete the parashah, reading the Scripture twice and the translation once.' Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 1.
2) There: He is obligated to read for himself, etc. This teaches that one should not count what he heard from the chazzan as one reading of the Scripture and add only one further reading and the translation. Rather, he must personally read the Scripture twice and the translation once. This is Rambam's wording. Lechem Chamudos writes that Rambam stated this only as the preferred practice, but that does not appear correct. Rather, he states the actual law. Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef, paragraph 3, writes the same; see there. Olas Tamid, paragraph 1. Mahari of Bruna, siman 103, similarly writes that he heard in the name of Mahari Weil that one who listens to the Torah reading from the chazzan does not thereby fulfill even one of his two required readings of the Scripture, but must begin again and personally read the Scripture twice and the translation once; see there.
3) There: The Scripture twice, etc. Shelah writes that one should read each parashah twice and then its translation. Maharshal writes the same, and this is also implied by Megillah 4. Lechem Chamudos, siman 41, however, rules that one should read each verse twice and then its translation. Hagahas Yafeh Nof writes the same in the name of the Arizal and Seder HaYom, as do the kabbalistic writings. This is also implied by siman 145, because this was the custom when the Torah was translated publicly. The final verse should be recited once more after its translation, so that one concludes with Torah. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1. Shaar HaKavanos, folio 12a, similarly states that the Arizal's practice was to go to the synagogue or his study hall immediately after completing Shacharis on Friday. If a valid Sefer Torah was present, he would take it out and read the parashah from it, reading each verse twice and the translation once. He himself read the Scripture from the Sefer Torah, while a student read the translation to him from a Targum, and he repeated it after the student. He followed this procedure for every individual verse until completing the parashah. He did not act like certain people who display pretension by reading the entire parashah once, repeating the entire Scripture a second time, and then reading all the translation together as a third reading. Rather, he read every verse individually, twice in the Scripture and once in translation. He exerted himself to read it on Friday and said that this is the mystical meaning of the verse, 'And it shall be on the sixth day, and they shall prepare that which they bring.' Only when an extremely serious circumstance beyond his control arose would he read the parashah twice in the Scripture and once in translation after completing Shacharis on Shabbos, before the morning meal, as Rabbeinu HaKadosh instructed his son. He did not act like those who read it during the Shabbos morning prayer. These are his words. See below, paragraphs 7 and 25.
4) Maggid Meisharim records in Yeshayah, siman 66, that the maggid said to Maran: Is it proper in your eyes that you read the parashah twice in the Scripture and once in translation hurriedly, like one raking coals, merely to remove the burden from yourself? You should remember the statement of Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar, 'Do not make your prayer a fixed burden,' etc. Rather, you must read the parashah calmly, perceive its textual difficulties, and resolve them. Consult the commentators regarding anything you cannot resolve. These are his words.
4) Sefer Chassidim, siman 301, writes that a person should read the Scripture twice and the translation once for the sake of the One Who said, 'So that the Torah of Hashem shall be in your mouth,' and it is written, 'In your mouth and in your heart, to perform it,' etc., and, 'Teach it to the Children of Israel; place it in their mouths.' Nevertheless, the Holy One, blessed is He, will prolong his days and years. He should not rely upon this condition, as it is stated, 'Let it be known to you, O king, that we will not serve your god,' and, 'You shall be wholehearted with Hashem your God.' Therefore, before reading the weekly parashah twice in the Scripture and once in translation, he should say: 'For the sake of the unification of the Holy One, blessed is He, and His Shechinah, etc., I hereby come to read the weekly parashah twice in the Scripture and once in translation, to rectify its root in the supernal realm. May the pleasantness, etc.' See further our words above in siman 132, paragraph 6, regarding the unification one should intend before reciting the daily verses. The same intention should be made on Friday before reading the parashah twice in the Scripture and once in translation; see there.
5) When reading the Ten Commandments, one must take care to read them twice in the Scripture and once in translation according to the cantillation used for the ordinary division into verses, in accordance with Responsa Masas Binyamin, siman 6, and Shtei Yados. To avoid error, he should consult the Targum in a Mikraos Gedolos, which contains only the cantillation for the ordinary division into verses, the form prescribed for an individual's reading. Regarding the words Lo Sirtzach, Lo Sinaf, Lo Signov, and Lo Saaneh, however, one must take care to read them together twice in the Scripture and then once in translation, because they all constitute one verse, as Masas Binyamin writes there. One may not divide a verse into sections and read each section twice in the Scripture and once in translation. Rather, he must first read the entire verse twice in the Scripture and then once in translation. Beis David, siman 111; see there.
6) One should not read the Scripture once, then the translation, and afterward the Scripture again. Rather, he should read the Scripture twice and then the translation once. Lechem Chamudos, paragraph 41; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur, paragraph 2. Shelah, folio 131, similarly writes that the translation must be recited last, after the two readings of the Scripture, and not between them. This is the implication of the Gemara's expression, 'the Scripture twice and the translation once,' and is also demonstrated by the Zohar and the words of the kabbalists; see there. Mateh Yehudah, paragraph 1, cites this.
7) A person who has a Sefer Torah in his home and also has a Chumash should act as follows. If he is proficient in reading a Sefer Torah with the correct vowels, cantillation, and verse endings, he should read the two Scripture readings from the Sefer Torah, which possesses greater sanctity. This is the principal purpose of the mitzvah of a Sefer Torah: to read from it. If he is not proficient, it is preferable that he read the Scripture twice and the translation once from a Chumash rather than read incorrectly from a Sefer Torah and thereby strip the word sefer of its meaning. Radbaz in the new responsa printed in Furth, siman 529, cited by Knesses HaGedolah; Machazik Berachah, paragraph 2. Even if he is proficient and reads from a Sefer Torah, he should not recite the blessings over Torah reading, because they were instituted only for a public reading, as stated above in siman 139, seif 8; see there.
