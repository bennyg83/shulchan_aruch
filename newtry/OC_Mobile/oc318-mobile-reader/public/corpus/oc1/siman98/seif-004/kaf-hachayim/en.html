(ד) Chida wrote in Simchat HaRegel p. 17 that the prayers arranged for the order of Kadesh and Rachtzah according to the Ari's intentions should not be said even by kabbalists — for besides printing errors, Ramaz objected to one who voices intentions in the course of prayer, etc. — see there. See also in his book Yosef Ometz siman 44, where he brought from Iggerot HaRamaz siman 6 p. 4a, who wrote, end quote: The matter of the prayers arranged by Maharashab, may his memory be blessed, is not right in my eyes at all: first, many intentions depend only on thought and not on verbal expression; second, the essence of intention is at the moment of performing the mitzvah, such as at the moment of tekias shofar — so how and with what will he precede to arouse the upper forces and lights before performing the main act for them? Third, there are many names within the prayers — whichever way: if he mentions them with his mouth his sin is too great to bear, and if only in thought then what benefit is prayer in speech, and the essence — the names — would be in thought alone; this and another matter, etc., end quote. I also heard from Maharash Sharabi before Iggerot HaRamaz was printed that he has more than these reasons Ramaz wrote, with clear proofs from the foundation of our teacher the Ari's teachings. I also saw a responsum manuscript of the Rav Kemaherava, who agreed through Maharar Yavanon to forbid this in the course of prayer, etc., and they expanded on the damages that follow from this, end quote. Be'er Heitev in Zecher LeChaim vol. 3, Laws of Pesach end of notes 107–108.

(ב) And he wrote in the Zohar (Parashat Vayakhel p. 213b), end quote: While his mouth and lips are still moving his heart intends and his desire ascends on high to unify all, etc. He wrote in the margin gloss, end quote: It implies that the entire essence of intention is while it still stirs on his lips and he speaks the prayer with his mouth — not in silence at all, end quote. If so, from this too it implies that intention must be specifically at the time he performs the mitzvah or says prayer — not before and not after. Therefore those who intend according to the Ari's words prolong words that contain intention so that the intention be at the time he says those words.
