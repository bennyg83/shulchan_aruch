[4] And that it should not appear upon him as a burden, etc. The explanation is that lekhatchilah we require both — speech in a manner of supplication, meaning that he speaks gently like a poor person who seeks mercy for himself, and that it not appear upon him as a burden — meaning that he should not pray hastily like one who prays out of obligation and seeks to be released from it. But bedieved speech in a manner of supplication alone suffices. And if he transgressed and did not speak in a manner of supplication, even if it does not appear upon him as a burden, his prayer is not prayer and he must return and pray — and so it is implied in Beit Yosef, Bach, and Lechem Chamudos.
