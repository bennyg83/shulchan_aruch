And standing, similar to the Temple service. The Tur writes, “and aligning the feet like kohanim during the Temple service.” This is difficult, for above, in siman 95, we concluded that one must stand like the angels, not like the kohanim, deriving it from the verse “one straight leg” written concerning angels. Beit Yosef already discussed this there but did not succeed in resolving it. It appears to me that when the kohanim walked toward the altar, they certainly placed heel beside toe, as it is written, “You shall not ascend by steps, etc.” One opinion derives the law of prayer from this. If so, the kohanim also stood this way during the service itself, since prayer corresponds to the Temple service in every respect. According to the opinion that derives the law of prayer from “one straight leg,” however, the feet must be aligned side by side. It follows that the kohanim likewise stood this way during their service, because prayer corresponds to the Temple service. You must therefore say that during the service the kohanim did not position their feet as they did while ascending the altar. Accordingly, the Tur writes here that one aligns his feet like the kohanim during the service, for the alignment of the kohanim during the service and the alignment of one praying are certainly one matter according to all opinions. Each opinion understands the kohanim's service according to its own view. The Tur's purpose here is only to mention the respects in which prayer resembles the Temple service. This appears correct and clear.
And there should not be an intervening object, etc. The Tur also writes, “and between him and the ground.” Beit Yosef asks from where he derived this and therefore omits it here in the Shulchan Aruch. It appears to me that the Tur plainly refers to an object three tefachim high. He compares it to an intervening object between oneself and the wall, which applies only when it is ten tefachim high, as explained above, since that is an obstruction opposite one's body. Likewise, an obstruction beneath one's feet constitutes a separation when it is three tefachim high, while anything lower than three tefachim is regarded as attached to the ground. Although even an object less than three tefachim high separates from the Temple floor, the principle of being attached to the ground is inapplicable there, since it is forbidden to add even the slightest amount to the floor, as stated at the beginning of the chapter Kisui HaDam. The cases are comparable only when the object is not regarded as attached to the ground: ten tefachim between oneself and the wall, and three tefachim beneath one's feet. Such an object is like an intervening object upon the Temple floor, which is forbidden even in the slightest amount. One cannot object that above the Tur explains the prohibition of standing three tefachim high by saying that one should not display elevation before Hashem, as it is written, “From the depths I call You.” The answer is that the Tur's purpose here is only to derive the laws of prayer from the laws of the Temple service. For this reason he lists all the respects in which they are alike, and therefore includes this matter of an intervening object even though it has an independent reason. The Tur never intended to prohibit standing upon something lower than three tefachim, even as an ideal enhancement.
