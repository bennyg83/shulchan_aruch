And standing like service. Tur wrote "and equalizing the feet like kohanim during service" — but it is difficult, for above siman 95 we brought that like angels is required, not like kohanim, which is derived from "straight leg" written regarding angels; Beit Yosef already wrote on this there and could not resolve it. It seems to me: certainly when kohanim walked to the altar it was heel beside toe, as written "do not go up by steps," etc., and from this one opinion derives for prayer; if so, during service too kohanim stood thus, for prayer is in place of service in every respect. But that opinion which derives prayer from "straight leg" implies one must equalize foot beside foot; automatically during service too kohanim were thus, for prayer is in place of service. Therefore you must say that during their service kohanim's feet were not as when ascending the altar; therefore Tur wrote here equalizing feet like kohanim during service — for certainly equaling kohanim in service is one matter with equaling the worshipper for all opinions: for the one who holds as in priestly service, and for the one who holds as in priestly service. He did not come here except to mention in what prayer equals service — as above, correct and clear.

And nothing should separate, etc. Tur also wrote "between him and the ground." Beit Yosef asked from where he learned this; therefore Shulchan Aruch omitted it here. It seems simple that it refers to height of three tefachim, for he speaks of interposition like between him and the wall — specifically at height of ten tefachim as above, i.e., interposition against his body; likewise interposition under his feet where there is a gap of three tefachim height — less than three is like ground contact. Even though for flooring interposition there is a gap even under three tefachim, there "ground contact" does not apply because it is forbidden to add on the flooring even a hairbreadth, as in the beginning of Kisuy HaDam; but they are equal regarding whether it is not called ground contact — such as at ten tefachim height between him and the wall, and under his feet at measure of three, it is like flooring interposition even at less. One could say: above he gave reason to forbid at three tefachim height because there is no height before the Omnipresent, as said "from the depths I called You" — one could say our Tur did not intend here except to learn prayer laws from service laws; for this reason he counted all matters that are equal, therefore he counted this matter of interposition that are equal, although this has a reason otherwise as above — correct. But it never entered Tur's mind to forbid standing on less than three tefachim even for optimal mitzvah performance.
