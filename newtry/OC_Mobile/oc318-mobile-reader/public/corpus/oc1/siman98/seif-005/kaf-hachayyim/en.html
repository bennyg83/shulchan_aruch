5) There: He should remove all thoughts, etc. Or HaYashar, chapter 14, writes that one who does not concentrate during prayer associates with the spirit of impurity, and Satan joins them. Woe to him and his soul for the harm he causes himself. This applies to everyone and all the more to a shaliach tzibbur. Expositors of allusions taught that a shaliach tzibbur approaching the lectern should intend that the initials of teivah (lectern) form "straight prayer with concentration of the heart." Rabbi Chaim Palagi cites this in Kaf HaChaim, siman 11, paragraph 5. Chomas Anach on Mishlei, siman 27, writes that the test of whether a person is righteous is whether he concentrates well during prayer without any other thought. Kaf HaChaim there cites him. Batei Knessiyos writes that one who prays with heartfelt concentration is saved from chibut hakever. Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 2, and Kaf HaChaim there cite him. Sefer HaMiddos, entry "Money," part 2, paragraph 10, writes that one who fulfills "Let your fellow's money be as precious to you as your own" thereby merits praying with heartfelt concentration. Yafeh LaLev, paragraph 2, cites this.
