1) [Se'if 1] One who prays must concentrate in his heart upon the meaning of the words, etc. Rambam writes at the end of Moreh Nevuchim regarding prayer: If, while moving your lips in prayer and turning your face toward the wall, you think about your purchases and sales, do not consider yourself to have prayed. At that time you are closer to those concerning whom it is stated, "They are close with their mouths but distant in their innermost parts." Darkei Moshe, paragraph 1, cites him. The holy Zohar, Parashas Metzora, folio 55a, states: What is meant by "without deceitful lips"? We have learned that every word of prayer a person utters ascends, pierces the heavens, and enters its assigned place. There the word is examined to determine whether it is proper. If it is proper, it is brought before the holy King to accomplish His will; if not, it is cast outside and another spirit is aroused through that word. Etz Chaim, gate 40, chapter 2, writes (as cited above in siman 60, paragraph 7) that prayer without concentration is like a body without a soul. Although the Rav speaks of concentration upon the Divine Names and Sefiros, concentration upon the meaning of the words also makes an impression. Moreover, if one does not concentrate upon their meaning but thinks other thoughts, the prayer does not emerge pure and clean; rather, it is entirely filled with rust, good and evil intermixed, because an alien thought was placed within it. This is the opposite of what one must do: give it the aspect of a soul and raise it like a fully adorned bride before the One Who created everything and examines the hidden chambers of the heart. See what we wrote above in siman 95, paragraph 11, and below, paragraph 5.
2) There: One must concentrate in his heart upon the meaning of the words, etc. One must also be careful with the words, letters, and vowel points, pronouncing them properly and in their correct form without omitting anything. Siddur Nehora HaShalem, chapter 13, folio 14b, writes: A person must therefore be careful throughout prayer, habituating himself until it becomes natural to utter proper words in their entirety, without omitting anything. The holy Zohar teaches that many thousands and myriads of holy, supernal, and hidden worlds depend upon every letter and every point. The holy Zohar, Parashas Acharei, folio 73a, states: Come and see; the first word of Torah taught to children is alef-beis, etc. It follows that when a person properly utters a word, he moves all the worlds that depend upon its letters. If he does not direct his mind and heart to articulating it properly, he substitutes another word that does not belong to the order of prayer and, Heaven forfend, causes great defects in the holy worlds dependent upon those letters. The holy Zohar repeatedly teaches that an arousal below produces an arousal above. He should therefore habituate himself to this. At the beginning of that training he should follow Darkei Moshe and pronounce the words as deliberately as though counting coins, until he is certain that he will not stumble; he may then say the words somewhat faster. See there for the frightening account of a renowned pious man of an earlier generation whom they wished to punish severely after his death because he had not been careful to pronounce the letters and vowels properly. See also what we wrote above in siman 5, with Hashem's help. A person must therefore constantly direct his heart and mind whenever he utters letters and words in Torah study or prayer, so that he loses no letter at the beginning or end of a word. Some letters are swallowed at the beginnings of words, some in the middle, and some at the end. People's pronunciation also differs; each person has certain letters that he does not articulate well and must watch particularly. Haste necessarily causes letters to be lost without his realizing it. He must be especially careful when uttering one of the holy Names, pronouncing it with trembling, holiness, purity, and great concentration. One who comes to purify himself is assisted from Heaven. May Hashem enable us and all Israel to perform His will and serve Him wholeheartedly.
3) The holy Zohar writes in Parashas Beshalach, folio 57a: Whoever comes to unify the holy Name but does not concentrate upon it with heart, desire, and fear, so that those above and below will be blessed through it, has his prayer cast outside, and all proclaim evil concerning him. The Holy One, blessed be He, applies to him, "When you come to appear before Me," etc. Magen Avraham cites this at the beginning of the siman and writes that one who knows that he cannot fulfill this should have no kabbalistic intentions regarding Names and unifications; rather, he should pray according to the plain meaning, understanding the words with heartfelt concentration. Halachah Berurah, paragraph 1; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1. Certainly, one who does not properly know the order of the kabbalistic intentions is forbidden to employ them, as the Zohar states at the end of Parashas Terumah, folio 178a: What is meant by "Those who despise Me shall be treated lightly"? This is one who does not know how to unify the holy Name, bind the bond of faith, draw influence to the required place, and honor his Master's Name. It would have been better for him not to have been created; all the more so one who does not concentrate when answering Amen. Chida likewise writes in Machazik Berachah, siman 274, paragraph 2: One who has no knowledge of Kabbalah should not utter, mention, or even contemplate the secrets written in the Arizal's siddur or other abridgments without understanding them; he is more likely to cause harm. May their Master forgive those who pray from the Arizal's siddurim without knowing or understanding: if they intend something, they err greatly, and if not, their conduct is forbidden because of ostentation and deception. Chida cites this in Kesher Gudal, siman 12, paragraphs 12 and 13. Eliyah Rabbah writes that recently Divine Names and unifications have been printed and everyone handles them; the sages of the generation must protest. Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1, cites him. One who properly knows the order of intentions from teachers and books, however, and directs the entire order of prayer with joy and trembling, without any alien thought, has immeasurable reward, as stated in the Zohar in Beshalach and Terumah. Rabbi Chaim Vital writes likewise in Shaar HaKavanos, end of discourse 4 among the discourses of the night, folio 54d. See also what we wrote above in siman 61, paragraph 28, and Responsa Rav Pe'alim, volume 3, Sod Yesharim, siman 13. Consider this carefully.
4) Chida writes in Simchas HaRegel, folio 17, that the prayers arranged for the order of Kadesh and Urchatz according to the Arizal's intentions should not be recited, even by kabbalists. Besides printing errors, Ramaz protested against expressing kabbalistic intentions in the form of prayers; see there. See also Chida's Yosef Ometz, siman 44, citing Iggeros HaRamaz, siman 6, folio 4a: The prayers composed by Rabbi Shabbetai are entirely improper in my eyes. First, many intentions depend solely on thought and not verbal expression. Moreover, the principal intention must occur at the moment the mitzvah is performed, such as at the instant the shofar is blown; how, then, can one arouse the supernal powers and lights before performing the act that is essential to them? Furthermore, these prayers contain many Divine Names. If he pronounces them, his sin is too great to bear; if he merely thinks of them, what purpose is served by verbally reciting the prayer, when its essential element (the Names) remains in thought? There are additional objections as well. I also heard from Rabbi Shalom Sharabi, before Iggeros HaRamaz was printed, that beyond Ramaz's reasons he possessed clear proofs from the foundations of the Arizal's teachings. I also saw a manuscript responsum by Rabbi Binyamin Abulafia, endorsed by Rabbi Yonah Navon, opposing this prayer format. They discuss at length the spiritual harms that result. Zechor LeAvraham, volume 3, laws of Pesach, end of paragraphs 107 and 108, cites this.
4) The holy Zohar writes in Parashas Vayakhel, folio 213b: While his mouth and lips murmur, his heart should concentrate and his desire ascend ever higher to unify everything, etc. A marginal gloss there states: This implies that the principal concentration occurs while his lips are moving and he speaks the prayer with his mouth, not while entirely silent. This likewise implies that the intention must occur specifically while one performs the mitzvah or recites the prayer, not before or afterward. Those who employ the Arizal's intentions therefore prolong words that contain an intention, so that the intention occurs while they utter those words.
5) There: He should remove all thoughts, etc. Or HaYashar, chapter 14, writes that one who does not concentrate during prayer associates with the spirit of impurity, and Satan joins them. Woe to him and his soul for the harm he causes himself. This applies to everyone and all the more to a shaliach tzibbur. Expositors of allusions taught that a shaliach tzibbur approaching the lectern should intend that the initials of teivah (lectern) form "straight prayer with concentration of the heart." Rabbi Chaim Palagi cites this in Kaf HaChaim, siman 11, paragraph 5. Chomas Anach on Mishlei, siman 27, writes that the test of whether a person is righteous is whether he concentrates well during prayer without any other thought. Kaf HaChaim there cites him. Batei Knessiyos writes that one who prays with heartfelt concentration is saved from chibut hakever. Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 2, and Kaf HaChaim there cite him. Sefer HaMiddos, entry "Money," part 2, paragraph 10, writes that one who fulfills "Let your fellow's money be as precious to you as your own" thereby merits praying with heartfelt concentration. Yafeh LaLev, paragraph 2, cites this.
