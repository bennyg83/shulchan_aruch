[1] He must concentrate, etc. Sefer HaGan writes that to dispel an improper thought during prayer, one should say three times, “Pi,” and afterward spit three times. He should not spit fully, but gently, with his tongue between his lips while spitting. This may be the meaning of the verse, “May the words of my mouth and the meditation of my heart be acceptable before You.” Magen Avraham, however, disagrees, because this constitutes an interruption. Kitzur Shelah gives the following remedy for removing an extraneous thought: before prayer one should pass his right hand over his forehead three times, saying each time, “Create for me a pure heart, God, and renew a steadfast spirit within me.” If an extraneous thought comes to him during prayer, he should pause briefly, pass his right hand over his forehead, and contemplate the verse just cited. Sefer Chassidim, siman 158, writes: Do not hasten to bring your words out of your mouth. If you need livelihood, do not direct your heart only to the blessing concerning livelihood, namely Bircas HaShanim; and if someone is ill, do not direct it only to the blessing for the sick. In Heaven they may say of you, “This person thinks he needs only this.” Therefore, concentrate on all the blessings and do not direct your heart only to the requests, for then accusers may say above that his prayer is unworthy of acceptance, since he is unconcerned with Hashem's honor and concentrates only on what his own desire seeks, not on praise. A person should not contemplate Divine Names or mystical unifications unless he knows that he is capable of doing so and can direct his heart with inner desire and awe, as stated in Zohar, Beshalach 115. Magen Avraham writes: “He should pray according to the plain meaning, understanding the words.” Recently, printed texts containing Divine Names and mystical unifications have appeared and everyone handles them. The sages of the generation should protest. See seif katan 1, and see below, siman 232, what I wrote in the name of Sefer Chassidim.
