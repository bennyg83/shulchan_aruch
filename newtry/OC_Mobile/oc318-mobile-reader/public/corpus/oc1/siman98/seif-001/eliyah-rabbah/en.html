[1] One must have intent, etc. He wrote in Sefer HaGan that to nullify a bad thought during prayer one should say three times "pi," and afterward spit three times; he should not spit fully but only downward, and the tongue should be between the lips at the time of spitting — until here. And it is possible that this is the explanation of the verse "May the words of my mouth and the meditation of my heart be acceptable before You." However, Magen Avraham disagrees, for it constitutes an interruption. In brief, Shelah wrote a segulah for intent to remove foreign thoughts: before prayer he should pass his right hand three times over his forehead and each time say "Create for me, O God, a pure heart, and renew within me a steadfast spirit." And similarly, if a foreign thought comes to him in the middle of prayer, he should be silent a little and pass his right hand over his forehead and meditate on the verse mentioned above. Sefer Chassidim wrote (siman 578): Do not hurry to bring forth your words from your mouth; and if you need livelihood, do not set your heart only on that blessing — on the blessing of years — or if he has a sick person, only on the blessing for the sick, because they say about you above: This so-and-so thinks he needs only this. Therefore have intent in all the blessings, and do not set your heart only on requests, for then above there will be accusers saying it is not fitting to accept his prayer, that in the honor of the Omnipresent he is not concerned to ask with intent what his soul desires and not in praise. A person should not have intent at all in divine names and unifications except one who knows in himself that he is capable of them and that he can intend with heart, will, and awe, as written in Zohar Beshalach 115. And this is the language of Magen Avraham: he should pray in the simple sense to understand the matters — until here. And recently divine names and unifications have been printed, and everyone handles them; the sages of the generation must protest. See siman 91.
