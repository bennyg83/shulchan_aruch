And he must contemplate, etc. As stated: “One may not stand, etc.,” and “the early pious men would wait one hour.”
And before praying he should contemplate, etc. There: “They would wait, etc., in order that, etc.” Rabbeinu Yonah writes that this does not mean during prayer, for then it should have said “in prayer.” Rather, it means that their hearts should be whole with the Omnipresent, contemplating the exaltedness of Hashem, etc., and removing, etc. Concerning this they said there, “A mnemonic for the matter is: Prepare, etc.” This refers to purifying a person from the pleasures of this world, for which one requires assistance from the Omnipresent, as it is written, “Prepare their heart toward You.” This is why they called it a mnemonic rather than a proof, since the plain meaning of the verse does not concern concentration in prayer. The end of the verse, however, “You will incline Your ear, etc.,” indicates this.
And it is forbidden, etc., in order that, etc. See Zohar, part I, 132b: “Come and see, for they have established that one should not pray, etc., because, etc.; he should not place, etc.” See there.
