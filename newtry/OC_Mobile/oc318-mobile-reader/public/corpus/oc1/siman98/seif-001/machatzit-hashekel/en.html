Sefer HaGan writes, etc. “It does not appear proper to me to do this during the Amidah, etc.” Eliyah Rabbah cites Kitzur Shelah as follows: A tested remedy for dispelling an extraneous thought is that before prayer one should pass his right hand over his forehead three times and say each time, “Create for me a pure heart, God, and renew a steadfast spirit within me.” Similarly, if an extraneous thought comes to him during prayer, he should pause briefly, pass his right hand over his forehead, and contemplate the verse just cited.
Seif katan 1: His children, etc. This refers to those who bring children to the synagogue, namely children who have not yet reached the age of education. The reason is that the children play and jump in the synagogue, desecrate its sanctity, and disturb the concentration of those praying. Moreover, even when they grow old they will not abandon the bad habit in which they were trained in childhood, namely treating the sanctity of the synagogue with contempt. Once they have reached the age of education, however, the opposite is true: he should bring the child with him to the synagogue and teach him the ways of life, to sit with awe and fear. He should not allow him to leave his place and should urge him to answer Amen, Kaddish, and Kedushah. See there, at the end of the laws of prayer and Torah reading.
