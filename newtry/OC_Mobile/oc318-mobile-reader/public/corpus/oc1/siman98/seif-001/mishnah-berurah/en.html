(1) Meaning of the words — A person should not intend in names and unifications but pray simply to understand the words with heart intention, unless he is versed in the secrets of God and knows to intend with heart, desire, and awe — otherwise, heaven forbid, he causes much damage. See Magen Avraham in the name of the Zohar and in Rashal's responsum siman 98, who wrote at length and testified about Rashi that after he learned the secrets of Kabbalah he said he prays like a newborn infant. See Penei Yehoshua on the first chapter of Avodah Zarah, who wrote that these intentions explained here in Shulchan Aruch cannot be intended during prayer but only before; in prayer one must intend the meaning of the words — see there.

(2) "He should be silent," etc. — In Sefer HaGan he wrote to cancel a bad thought during prayer: say three times "pei pei" (initial letters of "Paltei Yosef," who overcame the impulse of their hearts, as in Sanhedrin 21b), then spit three times — not a full spit, only gently, tongue between lips when spitting — and certainly the thought will depart. Magen Avraham wrote on this that it does not seem right to do this within the Amidah, for it is an interruption, and who knows if it is a tested remedy. In Eliyah Rabbah he brought in the name of Kitzur Shelah, end quote: A segulah to remove foreign thoughts — before prayer pass the right hand three times over the forehead and each time say "A pure heart create for me, O God, and a steadfast spirit renew within me"; likewise if a foreign thought comes during prayer, be silent a little, pass the right hand over the forehead, and meditate on that verse, end quote.

(3) His young sons — Shelah protests against those who bring children to beit haknesset, meaning small ones who have not reached education, because children play and dance in beit haknesset, profane its sanctity, and confuse worshippers' minds; moreover when they grow old they will not leave the bad conduct trained in youth to act wildly and despise beit haknesset sanctity. But when they reach education, on the contrary bring him to beit haknesset, teach him to sit in awe and fear, not let him move from his place, and urge him to answer amen, Kaddish, and Kedushah — see there at the end of topics of prayer and Torah reading. See Tanna Devei Eliyahu ch. 1, ch. 13, on the great punishment for a father who lets his son answer vain words and prayers in beit haknesset.
