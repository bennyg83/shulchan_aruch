For his business. See Baer Heitev. Birkei Yosef writes in the name of Rabbi Yaakov Tzemach that when his teacher, of blessed memory, was traveling and people greeted him, he would examine the place. If it was pure and clean, he replied, “Peace be upon you.” If it was filthy, he replied with another good blessing.
Incidental. See Baer Heitev. See Magen Avraham concerning one who does not state a teaching in the name of its author and violates a prohibition; the source is marked as the first chapter of Nedarim. See what I wrote in Beis Ephraim on Treifos, in the introduction to part 2, that the source is Tanchuma, Parashas Bamidbar. Later, Noda B'Yehudah, second edition, was printed, and he wrote likewise in the concluding notes; see there. See Responsa Beis Ephraim, Orach Chayim, siman 15. See also what he writes in the name of Yam Shel Shlomo, that in Talmud study one should not say beitzim but bei'im. It appears that nowadays, when bei'im has come to denote the organs below and carries a more indecent implication, it is proper to say beitzim. See there regarding establishing fixed times for Torah, where Rashi explains that because a person must engage in worldly occupation, etc. The author of Haflaah explained that kava, “established,” is related to “so-and-so robbed me,” meaning that even one deeply occupied with business must steal away time from it for Torah study. These are wise words. See the introduction to Responsa Beis Ephraim, Orach Chayim, for a fine and acceptable explanation of this matter.
