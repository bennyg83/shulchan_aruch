(א) He should go to his business — and a person's conduct must all be on the middle path: he should not be excessively stingy nor excessively spendthrift; and likewise in all traits — only pride and anger he should distance from them to the ultimate extreme; and as the Tanna said: be very lowly of spirit; and likewise regarding anger the Sages warned greatly about it.

(ב) Primary — rather he should do only for his livelihood; but even in this itself he must be careful of the impulse's temptation, which tempts him that all day requires striving for this livelihood. And the main thing is that he reflect upon himself what is the true necessity without which it is impossible — then he can maintain that his work is temporary and his Torah primary.

(ג) In his hand — for nothing prevents Heaven from saving whether through much or through little, and He will send him blessing through the work of his hands.

(ד) With integrity — meaning that nothing of theft or fraud should be found in his business; and regarding idolatry as well they ask a person at the time of judgment, as they said. Behold, there are several frequent mitzvot, positive and negative, incumbent upon a person to perform and to be careful of at all times, which are not brought in the Shulchan Aruch; and several of them the Magen Avraham brought from the words of the Rambam and the other Rishonim; and likewise several good practices mentioned in Chazal — therefore I will not refrain from copying some of the matters here as well. The Rambam wrote (ch. 6 of Hilchot De'ot halachah 2): It is a positive mitzvah to cleave to Torah scholars and their disciples in order to learn from their deeds, as it says "and to Him you shall cleave"; and is it possible for a person to cleave to the Divine Presence? Rather, thus the Sages explained explicitly: this mitzvah — cleave to Torah scholars and their disciples; therefore a person must strive to marry the daughter of a Torah scholar, marry his daughter to a Torah scholar, eat and drink with Torah scholars, do business with Torah scholars, and join them in every kind of attachment, as it says "and to Him you shall cleave," end of his words. And Chazal said: whoever enjoys a meal at which a Torah scholar is present is as though he enjoys the radiance of the Divine Presence (Berachot 64). Further, he wrote in ch. 6 halachah 3: It is a mitzvah upon every person to love every individual of Israel as himself, as it says "and you shall love your neighbor as yourself"; therefore one must speak his praise [and before his enemies it is forbidden lest they come to speak his disgrace — there in ch. 7 of Hilchot De'ot], and pity his property as he pities his own property, and desire his honor as he desires his own honor; therefore one who glorifies himself through his fellow's disgrace has no share in the World to Come, end of his words. And specifically "your neighbor" in Torah and mitzvot; but a wicked person whom one saw transgress a sin well known in Israel and who did not accept rebuke — it is a mitzvah to hate him [Orach Chayim 113]. Love of the convert who converted — two positive mitzvot, as it says "and you shall love the convert"; also a mitzvah in ch. 10, "and you shall love the convert." One who hates an individual of Israel in his heart transgresses a negative commandment, as it says "you shall not hate your brother in your heart"; but one who strikes his fellow or insults him does not transgress this negative commandment — rather he transgresses because of "he shall not add to strike him," and in insulting he transgresses because of "you shall not wrong one another"; and if he shamed him with words he also transgresses because of "you shall not bear sin because of him." The Rambam wrote in Sefer HaMitzvot mitzvah 205: included in the mitzvah of rebuke is that we rebuke one another — when a person among us sins against another we should not bear a grudge in our hearts and not reckon it to him as a sin, but we are commanded to rebuke him in speech until nothing remains in the soul; nevertheless one should not speak harshly until he shames him, as it says "you shall not bear sin because of him," but rather rebuke him in private, calmly, and with soft language [and the main laws of rebuke we will explain later, siman 606 and siman 608]. A person must be careful with orphans and widows even if they are very wealthy; and whoever provokes them or angers them or causes them pain or rules over them harshly or causes loss to their property transgresses a negative commandment, and all the more so one who strikes them or curses them, as it says "every widow and orphan you shall not afflict." In what case? When he feeds them for his own need; but when he feeds them the rabbi in order to teach them Torah or crafts or to lead them on a straight path — it is permitted; nevertheless he should not treat them with the ordinary conduct of people, but should make a distinction for them and conduct them with calmness and great mercy — one orphan of the father and one orphan of the mother. Until when are they called orphans for this matter? Until they no longer need a person to rely upon him and to support and care for them, but do all their needs themselves like all other adults. One who gossips about his fellow transgresses a negative commandment, as it says "you shall not go as a talebearer among your people." What is a talebearer? One who carries words and goes from this one to that one and says: so-and-so said such-and-such; I heard about so-and-so such-and-such — even if it is true, behold this destroys the world. There is a sin greater than this by far, included in this negative commandment — lashon hara; that is, one who speaks his fellow's disgrace even though he spoke truth [but one who speaks falsehood is called one who puts out a bad name upon his fellow]. One who speaks matters that, if heard person from person, cause harm to his fellow in body or property, or even to distress him or frighten him — behold this is lashon hara [and in this negative commandment many laws are found, explained at length in several places in the words of the Rishonim; one who wishes to stand upon all the details of the laws should see in Sefer Chafetz Chaim, for there all the particulars are explained at length]. That one should not hold fast to dispute, as it says "and you shall not be like Korach and his congregation" [Semag negative commandment 157]. One who takes revenge from his fellow transgresses a negative commandment, as it says "you shall not take revenge and you shall not bear a grudge." How so? He said to him: lend me your axe — he did not wish to; tomorrow he said to him: lend me your sickle — he said to him: I will not lend you, in the manner that you did not lend me — this is revenge [and Rabbeinu Eliezer of Metz wrote in his siman and thus: not specifically borrowing vessels, for it does not write "vessels" in the verse, but even other property that are not vessels — we learn that Israel are warned not to withhold doing charity and kindness with money because he did not do so with him, for this is revenge, etc., see there]; and if he said to him: here you are, I lend you, but I am not like you — this is bearing a grudge; rather he should erase the matter from his heart and not mention it at all, end of his words of the Rambam. Further, in mitzvah 73 — to confess before Heaven from every sin a person commits, as it says "and they shall confess their sins," etc. In the section of negative commandments siman 195 — not to eat and drink in the manner of a glutton and drunkard in the days of youth, as it says "you shall not eat upon the blood," meaning you shall not eat food that brings to shedding blood — eating of a rebellious and wayward son for whom one is liable to death [and even though after the days of youth one is not liable by Torah law to death for such eating, nevertheless we can see how very repulsive and ugly the matter is, from that which is written "and they shall say to the elders of his city, this our son," etc., "glutton and drunkard," and it is said in Scripture "do not be among winebibbers, among gluttonous eaters of meat," and to the Ramban all this is included in the positive mitzvah "you shall be holy," see there]. A person should not bring forth a disgraceful word from his mouth [Pesachim 3]. Positive mitzvah 299 — not to place a stumbling block before the blind; that is, not to give him advice that is not fitting for him, as it says "before the blind you shall not place a stumbling block." He should beware of flattery and scoffing, and likewise distance himself from falsehood to the ultimate extreme [see Shevuot 31]; however, for the sake of peace it is permitted to change [and see in the Magen Avraham what he writes in the name of the Shlah, and it requires study]. In three matters it is permitted to change — in a mattress, in a bed, and in hospitality [Choshen Mishpat 262, see there]. It is stated in Sotah at the end of ch. 7: when Agrippas the king read "you shall not set a foreigner over you," etc., his eyes flowed with tears because he was not of the seed of Israel; they said to him: our brother, you are; and at that hour Israel became liable for destruction because they flattered Agrippas — for even though it was not in their power to protest, nevertheless they should have been silent and not upheld him in this; and this is the punishment of one who flatters in a matter of sin out of fear of him and is not concerned for fear of Heaven. Nevertheless, if he fears lest he kill him, it is permitted to tell him: you acted well, even if he transgressed a sin. They said, our Sages, on the verse "in righteousness you shall judge your fellow" — judge your fellow toward merit; see Shabbat daf 127, and one who suspects the innocent is lashed on his body [Yoma 19]. Idle speech of profanities transgresses a positive commandment — meaning a disgraceful matter and lightheadedness, as it says "and you shall speak of them," and not in idle words; and a negative commandment derived from a positive commandment is a positive commandment [Yoma 19]. The Rambam wrote in Sefer HaMitzvot mitzvah 8: we are commanded to resemble Him, may He be exalted, as far as we are able — "and you shall walk in His ways"; and this commandment was repeated: "to walk in all His ways," and it was explained [Sotah 14]: just as the Holy One, blessed be He, is called gracious, so you be gracious; just as He is called merciful, so you be merciful, etc.; and this matter was repeated in another expression: "after Heaven your Heaven you shall walk," and it was explained that the meaning is resemblance in His good deeds and the noble traits described through them, end of his words. And there are many more frequent mitzvot, positive and negative, mentioned in all four books of the Shulchan Aruch, and one who examines will find each in its place; and there are very many that were not brought in the Shulchan Aruch and are found in the books of enumeration of mitzvot — the Rambam, Semag, and Chinuch [and especially in Sefer Chareidim, for he gathered from all the Rishonim who preceded him the mitzvot practiced in action in this time] — and it is very proper that every person learn them and be expert in them, and thereby be able to fulfill them; and as they said on the verse "and you shall see it and remember all the commandments of Heaven and perform them" — remembrance brings to action; for if he does not know whether it is a mitzvah at all, what will he remember to fulfill?
