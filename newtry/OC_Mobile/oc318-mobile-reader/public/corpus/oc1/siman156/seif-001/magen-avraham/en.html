(א) See Hilchot De'ot of the Rambam; the general principle is that a person should walk the middle path — he should not be excessively stingy nor excessively spendthrift; only pride and anger he should distance from himself to the ultimate extreme. We say in the Gemara (Berachot ch. 6): six things are unbecoming for a Torah scholar: (1) his work is primary — meaning he should do only enough for his livelihood; and we say in the Gemara that many acted like Rabbi Yishmael and succeeded (see there). See in Hagahot Maimoniyot ch. 3 of Hilchot Talmud Torah regarding the matter that Torah study is good together with derech eretz.

(ב) "He will be obligated to him for an oath." See responsa of R. Aharon Sasson siman 95, that sometimes it is forbidden to accept it. The Rambam wrote ch. 6 of Hilchot De'ot: positive mitzvah to cleave to Torah scholars and their disciples in order to learn from their deeds, as it says "and to Him you shall cleave"; mitzvah upon every person to love every individual of Israel as himself, as it says "and you shall love your neighbor as yourself" — and specifically your neighbor in Torah and mitzvot; but a wicked person who does not accept rebuke — it is a mitzvah to hate him [Orach Chayim 113] [Hagahot Maimoniyot]; and it is implied there in Tosafot that nevertheless it is a mitzvah to argue with him in order to subdue his impulse so they do not come to complete hatred; and the Nekudot Yisrael ruled the opposite of this, and likewise Tosafot in Bava Metzia, and likewise the Rama in Choshen Mishpat siman 472 seif 11 like him. One who glorifies himself through his fellow's disgrace has no share in the World to Come. Love of the convert who converted — two positive mitzvot, as it says "and you shall love the convert." One who hates an individual of Israel in his heart transgresses a negative commandment, as it says "you shall not hate your brother in your heart"; but one who strikes his fellow or insults him — even though he is not permitted — does not transgress because of "you shall not hate" [nevertheless he transgresses because of "he shall not add to strike him"]. When a person sins against another, he should not hate him and be silent, but should say to him: why did you do such-and-such [see siman 606 and siman 608 regarding rebuke]; nevertheless he should not speak harshly until he shames him, as it says "you shall not bear sin because of him" — for one who whitens his fellow's face in public has no share in the World to Come; rather rebuke him in private, calmly, and with soft language; and if he does not repent, a second or third time they shame him in public. The Ra'avan wrote: even though they expound in Bava Metzia ch. 2 "you shall surely rebuke" — even a disciple to a rabbi, that is when he accepts rebuke (meaning when rebuked), but to his fellow even if he does not accept [Hagahot Maimoniyot]. One who suspects the innocent is lashed on his body [Yoma daf 19]. It is forbidden to appoint an Israelite even if he does not intend to appoint but only to cast lots, even for a matter of mitzvah — rather they extend their fingers and count their fingers [Yoma daf 22]. One does not appoint a communal administrator over the public unless a basket of creeping things hangs behind him [there]. That which is permitted to change for the sake of peace — that is in a matter that already passed and not for the future [Shlah siman 426]. In three matters it is permitted to change — in a mattress, in a bed, and in hospitality; see Choshen Mishpat end siman 262. The Rosh wrote at the end of Shevi'it: one who went to a place where they do not know him and they think he studied many years and honor him greatly — he must tell them: I did not study so much, see in the Yerushalmi. A guest should not praise the host lest many guests increase upon him, as is stated in Berachot. If he heard a ruling and it seems to him that the halachah is so — it is permitted to say it in the name of a great person so that they accept it from him [Eruvin daf 51 and Pesachim daf 112]; and at the end of tractate Kallah it is stated: one who says a matter in a sage's name without concluding it from him causes the Divine Presence to depart; and likewise Berachot daf 27, see there in Terumat HaDeshen, and it requires study. Whoever does not say a matter in the name of its speaker transgresses a negative commandment [Nedarim ch. 1]. A person must be careful with orphans and widows even if they are very wealthy; and whoever provokes them or angers them or causes them pain or rules over them harshly or causes loss to their property transgresses a negative commandment, and all the more so one who strikes them or curses them, as it says "every widow and orphan you shall not afflict." In what case? When he feeds them for his own need; but when he feeds them the rabbi in order to teach them Torah or crafts or to lead them on a straight path — it is permitted; nevertheless he should not treat them with the ordinary conduct of people, but should make a distinction for them and conduct them with calmness and great mercy — one orphan of the father and one orphan of the mother. Until when are they called orphans for this matter? Until they no longer need a person to rely upon him and to support and care for them, but do all their needs themselves like all other adults. One who gossips about his fellow transgresses a negative commandment, as it says "you shall not go as a talebearer among your people." What is a talebearer? One who carries words and goes from this one to that one and says: so-and-so said such-and-such; I heard about so-and-so such-and-such — even if it is true, behold this destroys the world. There is a sin greater than this — lashon hara; that is, one who speaks his fellow's disgrace even though he spoke truth (such as if he said: where is there a fire except in the house of so-and-so, where meat and fish are always found — even this is lashon hara; and such as if he brought him out through lashon hara); but one who speaks falsehood is called one who puts out a bad name upon his fellow. But a habitual speaker of lashon hara — one who sits and says: so-and-so does such-and-such, such were his fathers, I heard about him such-and-such (in the Yerushalmi it is permitted to speak lashon hara about disputants — why? "and I will come after you and fulfill your words" [Hagahot Maimoniyot]); and specifically to a king who is proper like Nathan to David [Hagahot Semak]; and one who accepts it is worse than the speaker [and if he sees recognizable matters he is permitted to believe and accept, as they said in the chapter "with what" regarding David, see there] [Hagahot Maimoniyot]. Dust of lashon hara — how so? That he said: who will tell so-and-so that he should be as he is now; or that he should say: be silent, I do not wish to inform what happened and what was — and similar matters; and all who speak of their fellow's goodness before his enemies — behold this is dust of lashon hara, for he causes them to speak his disgrace (but before his lovers it is permitted) [Shlah]; however Rashi explained: he should not speak more than necessary of his goodness, for from speaking and enumerating his traits it is impossible that he not come to his disgrace [Hagahot Maimoniyot], and it requires study, for in Bava Batra daf 164 it is stated that he would say: how beautiful is this writing, and R. Shimon said to him: I did not write it but Yehudah the tailor wrote it, and he said to him: cease from lashon hara. And likewise one who speaks lashon hara through jesting and through lightheadedness — all of it is that he does not speak in hatred, as Shlomo said: "like one who brandishes arrows, firebrands, and death," etc., and he said: "am I not playing?" And likewise one who speaks lashon hara through trickery: (1) one who tells before his fellow, or not in his presence; (2) one who tells matters that cause, if heard person from person, harm to his fellow in body or property, or even to distress him or frighten him — behold this is lashon hara [and see Shabbat daf 33b in the incident of R. Shimon bar Yochai]. And if these matters were said before three, the matter is already heard and known; and if one of the three told the matter a second time, there is no lashon hara in it, provided he does not intend to spread the report and reveal it more. Ch. 1 of Yoma: from where [do we know] one who says to his fellow a matter that is in heaven will not be said until he says to him "go say," etc.; and if the speaker warns him not to say — even if they said it before two, there is lashon hara, as they said: PZBH there were disciples, etc. [Hagahot Maimoniyot]; and in Bava Batra daf 39 Tosafot explained: any curse that is said before three — there is no evil speech for the speaker, for it is as though he said it before its owner, for there is no lashon hara, see there as is proven in the Gemara. And Tosafot wrote in Arakhin ch. 6: such as in this case where he says "fire in the house of so-and-so," for it can be understood that he did not say it as evil speech; but if he said a disparaging word about his fellow, even if it was in his presence, there is evil speech. One who takes revenge from his fellow transgresses a negative commandment, as it says "you shall not take revenge and you shall not bear a grudge." How so? He said to him: lend me your axe — he did not wish to; tomorrow he said to him: lend me your sickle — he said to him: I will not lend you, in the manner that you did not lend me — this is revenge; and if he said to him: here you are, I lend you, but I am not like you — this is bearing a grudge; rather he should erase the matter from his heart and not mention it at all, end of his words of the Rambam. They said in Yoma: any Torah scholar who does not take revenge and bear a grudge like a snake — when they touch his honor he is not a Torah scholar; nevertheless he himself should not take revenge, but if others take revenge he should be silent; and if they appease him he must forgive; and every person must be careful in this. The Rambam wrote in enumeration of mitzvot mitzvah 73 — to confess before Heaven from every sin a person commits, as it says "and they shall confess their sins," etc. In the section of negative commandments siman 195 — not to eat and drink in the manner of a glutton and drunkard, as it says "this our son is gluttonous and drunkard." Positive mitzvah 299 — not to place a stumbling block before the blind, as it says "before the blind you shall not place a stumbling block"; and may the Place guide him on a true and straight path. He should beware of flattery and scoffing as is known, and also from anger. This one who examines his assets every day finds a perutah. One should divide his money: a third in land, a third in merchandise, a third ready at hand. "And you shall love your neighbor as yourself" — judge your fellow toward merit, see Shabbat daf 127. A person should not bring forth a disgraceful word from his mouth; therefore he should not say in the Talmud "beitzim" but rather "beyim" [Yeshuot Yaakov Bava Kamma ch. 4]. One should not kill a louse in the presence of his fellow [Chagigah ch. 1] [Massekhet Derech Eretz ch. 3]. The homeowner enters first and the guest leaves first. The zealous precede to mitzvot and do not precede excessively from morning [Pesachim daf 4]: one who calls Abraham "Avram" transgresses a positive and a negative commandment [beginning of Berachot]. One who enjoys a wedding feast and does not gladden the groom transgresses five voices [Berachot ch. 1]. Idle speech of profanities transgresses a positive commandment — meaning a disgraceful matter and lightheadedness [Yoma]. A person should not say: I have more than is fitting for me according to my deeds and my service before Heaven — for if so he says the Holy One, blessed be He, is lenient; rather he should conclude with "except the merit of ancestors assists me" [Yeshuot Yaakov Bava Kamma ch. 5]; and it appears to me that Heaven gives reward more abundantly, but He does not waive sin and prolongs anger. Shabbat daf 31: when they bring a person to judgment they ask him: did you deal faithfully, establish times for Torah, engage in procreation, anticipate salvation (per the prophets), delve in wisdom, understand a matter from within a matter; and even so, if fear of Heaven is his treasure — yes, if not — no; and see in Midrash Mishlei where the rabbi expanded greatly what they ask a person whether he learned the measure of a cubit, etc.; in our many sins we do not know how far, and may Heaven grant wisdom. And Rashi: because a person must occupy himself in derech eretz — for if there is no derech eretz there is no Torah — it was necessary to establish times for Torah in a fixed measure so he does not spend the whole day on derech eretz, end of his words; it is implied that one who does not need this is obligated "and you shall meditate on it day and night" — b'dieved siman 246. Kiddushin daf 30b: a person is obligated to teach his son a craft or engage in commerce; and if he does not do so it is as though he teaches him banditry. Shabbat daf 129: whoever let blood and has nothing to eat — take a bad dinar and go to the shopkeeper and taste with each one wine; and afterward when he wishes to buy, the shopkeeper will not take it until he tastes the measure of a revi'it. And even though in our bloodletting there is not so much concern, nevertheless one can learn from this for other sick people. Sotah end ch. 7: when Agrippas the king read "you shall not set a foreigner over you," etc., his eyes flowed with tears because he was not of the seed of Israel; they said to him: our brother, you are; and at that hour Israel became liable for destruction because they flattered Agrippas — for even though it was not in their power to protest, nevertheless they should have been silent and not upheld him in this; and this is the punishment of one who flatters in a matter of sin out of fear of him and is not concerned for fear of the Holy One, blessed be He. Nevertheless, if he fears lest he kill him, it is permitted to tell him: you acted well, even if he transgressed a sin [Tosafot there]. Berachot daf 7: a wicked person whom the hour smiles upon — do not provoke him. Shabbat daf 33: they said — women are lightheaded; lest they distress them and they reveal us — they went and hid in a cave; it is implied that it is permitted to flee when there is danger to lives even though thereby he causes distress to his fellow; and it is implied that he is obligated to bear distress so that his fellow not be killed needlessly — end of Berachot. All who force the hour — the hour forces him; and all who are pushed aside before the hour — the hour is pushed aside before him. All who enjoy a meal at which a Torah scholar is present is as though he enjoys the radiance of the Divine Presence [Shabbat daf 30]. In words of Torah answer a fool according to his folly; in worldly matters do not answer — as that one who said to R. Ashi: your wife committed adultery with me. There [Shabbat daf 32]: a person should not stand in a place of danger. Rav would not pass on a bridge where there was an idolater, saying: perhaps judgment will be decreed upon him and I will be caught with him. Shmuel would pass only on a bridge where there was an idolater, saying: Satan does not dominate two nations; and if so, regarding an apostate Israelite or a wicked person — for the whole world one should not pass with him.
