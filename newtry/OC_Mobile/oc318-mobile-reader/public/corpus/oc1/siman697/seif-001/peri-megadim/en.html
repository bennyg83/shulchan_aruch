And they are stated. See the Taz, and see the Rosh and the Beit Yosef here. But mourning, according to everyone, is observed in the first Adar; see Eliyah Rabbah. And according to what Tosafos wrote in the first chapter of Megillah, that there is no difference, meaning that joy applies in the first Adar, nevertheless there is no positive commandment in it such that a public positive commandment would override the mourning of an individual. Labor is also permitted in the first Adar even nowadays; see Magen Avraham. If one explicitly vowed to fast on Purim Katan, one can say that the vow takes effect, for it is only a mere custom. But if he vowed generally to fast on Mondays and Thursdays of the entire year, one can say that his intent was except for Purim Katan; this requires some study. As for why it states, There is no difference between the fourteenth in the first Adar and the second, and it specifically states the fourteenth: according to the Rif this is well understood, and according to Tosafos one can say that it states the fourteenth because of most people. Eulogizing and fasting, however, are forbidden on both, as it concludes here.
