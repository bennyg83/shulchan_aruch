The first Adar. According to all opinions, Al HaNissim is not recited on Purim Katan because the Megillah is not read. If one recited it, he need not repeat the prayer.
Ya’ancha. Neither Lamnatzeiach Ya’ancha nor the supplication that follows Tachanun is recited on either day of Purim Katan, because their laws are identical. Regarding the chapter of Tehillim “A song of Asaf,” customs differ. In some places it is omitted whenever Tachanun is omitted, while elsewhere it is omitted only on days when Hallel is recited. If Purim Katan falls on Shabbos, Tzidkascha is omitted. One who experienced a miracle in Adar and accepted an annual day of feasting and joy observes it in the first Adar if the event occurred in an ordinary year, and in the second Adar if it occurred in a leap year. A meal held to commemorate a miracle is a seudas mitzvah, because every meal held in remembrance of the wonders of Hashem is a seudas mitzvah.
And fasting. Mourning customs apply in the first Adar according to all opinions. If one explicitly vowed to fast on Purim Katan, the vow may take effect because the prohibition against fasting is only customary. If he accepted fasting every Monday and Thursday throughout the year without qualification, it may be assumed that he intended to exclude Purim Katan.
On the fourteenth. This does not apply on the fifteenth, even in walled cities.
Nevertheless, he should increase, etc. Hagahos Tashbetz writes that one should increase the meal. Rabbeinu Yechiel of Paris customarily prepared a larger meal and invited others. This is the meaning of the Rama’s conclusion, “A good-hearted person feasts continually,” namely that it is proper to increase the meal in honor of the miracle commemorated at this time.
