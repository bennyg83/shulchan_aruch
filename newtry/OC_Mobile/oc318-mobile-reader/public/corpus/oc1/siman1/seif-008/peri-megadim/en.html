“A verse.” — **Taz:** **Eshel Avraham** 14, citing **Toldos Chayim**: this should be said before Parashas Korbanos, and Parashas Korbanos before Eizehu Makoman. Parashas Korbanos before Eizehu Makoman is correct; afterward the explanation—verse, then Mishnah, then the verse “He shall slaughter on the flank” (Vayikra 1:18); **Beit Yosef** has “slaughter before Me” (a scribal error); we say it after Parashas Tamid. **It is appropriate** to recite **Tehillim** before prayer—they are called zemiros to cut down accusers—**Levush**, **Eshel Avraham** 14. **Pesukei Dezimrah** are Tehillim from “A praise of David” through “Bless Hashem forever”; accordingly a Torah scholar should learn Torah first—see there in the name of the **Shelah** that Tehillim should be said slowly, not snatching verses: “answerers” who snatch the leader’s words, or a leader who snatches answerers’ words, is not blessing but, Heaven forbid, reviling; likewise with the Song at the Sea verse by verse, snatching is wrong. What the **Taz** wrote—that one says “May our lips compensate” in place of the Tamid service—is a scribal wording matter, for the Tamid was a lamb—**Eshel Avraham** note 4 on this.
