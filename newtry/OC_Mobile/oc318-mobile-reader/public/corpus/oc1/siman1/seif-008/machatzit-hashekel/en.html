Subsection 8 — olah, etc. I was asked, etc.: the advocate — meaning the advocate compares the chatas to an advocate and the olah to a gift. If one sins against the king, it is not fitting to bring him a gift before his sin is forgiven, for he will not accept it; first an advocate who appeases the king is needed, then the gift. So whenever he has not brought the chatas and sin is in his hand, he cannot bring a gift — namely olah — until he already brought the chatas and was atoned. Explanation as stated in the Gemara there on that page: chatas atones for a positive command from a frozen state — meaning the main obligation of chatas is if one transgressed a negative command whose intentional violation carries kares; for an inadvertent violation one brings chatas. If one brings chatas to atone for a negative command whose intentional violation carries kares when he is already liable for olah — e.g., he transgressed a positive command or a negative command linked to a positive command for which olah comes (and likewise for sinful thoughts and arrogance) — that chatas brought for the negative command also atones for the positive command, but only from a frozen state. Rashi explains: it is not complete atonement; the Targum: "and the iron floated and the bronze congealed." But olah atones for a positive command from a fixed state — complete atonement. Therefore Tosafos challenged: how can you say olah is a gift when it comes after chatas — behold, for a positive command it atones more than chatas?

And he explained this — Magen Avraham wrote: even so, relative to chatas the olah is called a gift, for before bringing the chatas the matter was sin of a negative command whose intentional violation carries kares, and also sin of a positive command; even if he brings olah, a debt of a negative command whose intentional violation carries kares remains. Therefore it is better that he bring the chatas first and achieve complete atonement for the negative command; and for the positive command at least atonement from a frozen state. And although some iniquity of a positive command may remain on him — for he did not achieve complete atonement — nevertheless the olah is again called a gift. But in our recitation, etc., and it is like reading Torah; if so, reciting Parashas Chatas is doubtful — perhaps it does not atone at all for the negative command, for he is not liable, and automatically also not for the positive command, for it is like reading Torah — for a mere positive command it is forbidden to bring chatas. Olah atones for a positive command, as we say in the Gemara on that page. Meaning: perhaps he is not liable for olah either; and although olah comes as a nedavah — nevertheless, since it is doubtful like chatas whether it atones, he should precede chatas, for the main precedence depends on what atones more. Therefore he had to say there is no person not liable for olah; if so, olah certainly atones and chatas is doubtful whether it atones, as above; therefore there is more reason to precede olah to chatas.
