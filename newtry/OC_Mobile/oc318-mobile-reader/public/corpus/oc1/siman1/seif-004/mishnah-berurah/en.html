(12) Without intention — but if the one who says much also has intention, the one who says much is better. If the one who says little has constraint and cannot prolong, or he estimates that if he prolongs he will not be able to have intention, and he shortens his supplications and says them with intention — he is considered before the Holy One, blessed be He, like one who has leisure and prolongs his supplications with intention. On this we learned: "Whether one increases or decreases, provided his heart is directed toward Heaven." The same applies to Talmud Torah, for everything depends before the Holy One, blessed be He, on whether one does all that is in his power. One who is a Torah scholar and has a heart to understand and learn may refrain from saying many supplications and requests printed in siddurim; it is better that he study in their place. A person should set a fixed time every day to study books of mussar, whether a little or much, for whoever is greater than his fellow — his inclination is greater. The spice against the evil inclination is rebuke from the statements of Chazal.
