A little [supplication] is good, etc. Our Rabbi wrote: whether one increases or decreases, provided his heart is directed in his supplications — for a little with intention is better than much without intention. There is a difficulty either way: if it deals with one who increases with intention, why is he equated with one who decreases? If without intention, why did he then say "a little with intention is better"? He begins by equating them; in the Gemara end of Menachos they say: whether one increases or decreases, provided his heart is directed for the sake of Heaven. It appears based on what we find in the first chapter of Shabbos 10a: Rava saw Rav Hamnuna prolonging his prayers and said, "They abandon eternal life and engage in temporal life" — he held that Torah time is separate, etc. Similarly they said well: one who increases in prayer is like Rav Hamnuna, who held Torah time is separate; one who decreases is like Rava, whose intent is to engage in Torah. Therefore he concludes well: "provided his heart is directed to Heaven" — meaning in the matter where he shortens prayer it is for the matter. Accordingly our Rabbi said: sometimes abundance of prayer prevents one from having intention; then it is better to shorten and intend. Therefore "whether one increases or decreases" — so that he have intention. He concludes "provided" — meaning the reduction is so his heart be directed to Heaven; better to say little with intention than much without. That is why he shortens — so he may have intention, as above — correct.
