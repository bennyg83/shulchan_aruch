[4] The measure of his learning, etc. Shelah wrote page 124: if he does not wish to sleep he should wash his hands even while remaining lying down; but it is forbidden to be awake on his bed lying down unless he meditates on words of Torah. Bach wrote siman 4 — this is his wording: Tola'as Yaakov wrote in the Zohar that one who walks in the morning four amos without washing hands is liable for death. One may wonder why Beit Yosef did not write this; it seems that since the majority cannot be careful about this, it is better they be inadvertent than intentional — therefore they did not write it; so far his wording — but this is strained. Also investigation is needed regarding why people are lenient in this, even Torah scholars; likewise Levush at the beginning of siman 25: immediately when he rises from his bed he should turn aside and wash his hands, etc. — and ordinary people do not have their privy within four amos of their bed, implying leniency. It also seems so to me from Levush siman 8 seif 11: if he wishes to wrap himself before washing so as not to walk four amos without tzitzis, etc. — easy to understand. My brother-in-law our teacher R' Yaakov said they rely on what Shach wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 344 in the name of Mordechai: the entire room in which the deceased lies is considered like four amos — so far. One difficulty: they are more lenient than going outside that room. I also wonder at Mordechai: in Berachos 25a Rava said the halachah is not like R' Shimon ben Elazar who said the whole house is like four amos — yet we establish below siman 79 regarding excrement in a room, and it implies there that "whole house" means the whole room; likewise Likutei Maharil wrote as a rule throughout the Talmud: wherever "house" is mentioned it means room. Rather it seems to me to resolve Mordechai's words: at the beginning of Berachos it says one speaks before the deceased only words of the deceased and four amos are not mentioned — therefore he explained that in the whole room it is forbidden, for it is considered like within four amos; the reason as Beit Yosef wrote there — it appears as despair of tending to him since one speaks before the deceased. But where four amos are mentioned, it certainly seems forbidden even in that room outside four amos. Incidentally I will mention briefly what I wrote in my book Eliyah Rabbah to Yoreh De'ah there to settle Beit Yosef's wonder on Mordechai — his wording: why did he rely on the first wording that the whole room is forbidden? In my humble opinion: because Mordechai reverses the Talmud's wording. In the first wording, even in mundane matters it is forbidden to speak before the deceased; if so the reason is not mockery of the pauper but that it appears as despair — therefore forbidden in the whole room. But in the latter wording, forbidden only in words of Torah because of mockery of the pauper — if so forbidden only within four amos as we say a dead person occupies four amos for reading Shema, and one who walks with tzitzis and tefillin in cemeteries in siman 366 there. This seems to me Tosafos's explanation in Berachos 3a s.v. ein, in what he wrote "specifically in his four amos"; understand. Again I found in Damasek Eliezer [of Apt] page 226 [1a] on Tola'as Yaakov cited above — his wording: perhaps specifically in their time as regarding uncovering and pairs — so far. With this the Bach's wonder why Beit Yosef did not write is resolved; yet the God-fearing should be strict and do as mussar books wrote: a person should prepare two vessels, one with water and one empty near his bed; Etz Chaim wrote it is good the empty vessel be broken, for it rules the other side. Sefer Mekor Chaim wrote one should not touch garments or anything until washing hands. Seder Hayom wrote: upon rising he should immediately say "I give thanks before You, living and enduring King, that You restored my soul to me with great compassion — Your faithfulness," and this does not require washing hands even if they were soiled, since he mentions neither Name nor epithet — so far; see end of siman 5 what I wrote there.
