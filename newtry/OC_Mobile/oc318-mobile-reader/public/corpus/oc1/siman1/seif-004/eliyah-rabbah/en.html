[D] “Measure of his sleep …” Shelah p. 124: if he does not wish to sleep he washes hands even while lying down; but it is forbidden to be awake on his bed lying unless thinking Torah. Bach siman 4 cites Tololas Yaakov from Zohar: walking four cubits in the morning without washing is liable for death—why did Beis Yosef not write? Apparently most cannot be careful—better unintentional than intentional—therefore they omitted—his words are strained. Needs study why people are lenient even learned Jews; Levush siman 25 start: immediately upon rising one relieves himself and washes… ordinary people’s facilities are not within four amos of bed—implies leniency. Also Levush siman 8 seif 11: wrapping tallis before washing to avoid four amos without tzitzis—easy to understand. My brother-in-law Rav Yaakov relied on Shach Y.D. §344 citing Mordechai: entire room where body lies counts as four amos—but leniency beyond that room is unclear; Mordechai Berachos 25a Rava: halacha not like Rabbi Shimon—the whole house is four amos—but we hold below siman 79 regarding feces in room… implies specifically room… Likutei Maharil: wherever Gemara says “house” means room. I resolve Mordechai: Berachos opening discusses speaking before the dead only funeral matters—four amos not mentioned—therefore entire room forbidden like within four amos; reason as Beis Yosef—appears hopeless when speaking before dead; where four amos explicit—outside four amos forbidden even in room. Incidentally I summarized in Eliyah Rabbah Y.D. resolving Beis Yosef’s wonder at Mordechai—why tie to first version forbidding entire room—because Mordechai switches Gemara’s wording: first version even mundane speech forbidden before dead—not mockery of poor; appears hopeless—entire room; second version Torah words forbidden because mockery—only within four amos like Kerias Shema near corpse; walking in cemetery with tzitzis/tefillin siman 366 there. This explains Tosafos Berachos 3a s.v. ein… “specifically his four amos”—investigate. I found Damasek Eliezer p. 126a on Tololas Yaakov: perhaps in their time like demons/pairs—so Bach’s wonder why Beis Yosef omitted—people rely; nevertheless God-fearing should be stringent per musar sefarim: prepare two vessels by bed… Etz Chaim: empty vessel should be chipped—it rules the Other Side.
