(Magen Avraham, section 4) The night is twelve hours — meaning that after twelve hours of night it is considered day in winter, and in summer it is the reverse; and this is regarding midnight, that after six hours from the start of the night it is the time of midnight. And the Arizal in Tanya Shlomo — his view is not thus — see there. And see what is written in siman 47. And see in Bechor Shor, Berachos, who wrote in the name of Lekutei HaAri that the time of midnight, whether in summer or winter, is in the middle of the darkness of the night; and he, z"l, agreed with him, unlike Shaarei Tziyon. But in Shalmei Tzibbur he brought in the name of the rabbi, the thousandfold sage R. Shalom Mizrachi, author of Erech Shai, z"l, who wrote that at the equator day and night are equal, and the time is six hours after twelve hours of night — which is two hours after midday, when the sun is at the zenith for each person; then it is certainly midday everywhere at the equator and in all climates. As is known, at the equator day and night are equal in summer and winter; and from when evening shadows begin to tilt until the completion of twelve hours — then it is certainly true midnight, for in winter the night is twelve hours from the start of the day. And from its end, like two-thirds additions approximately at the end of the night, as mentioned in the Zohar, Vayakhel, etc. And likewise was the time of the tamid offering of bein ha'arbayim — from when evening shadows tilt, whether in winter or summer; and although in summer from when evening shadows tilt until sunset there are more than six hours, that excess time beyond six hours is from the start of the coming night; and likewise in winter the reverse — so that after six hours of the twelve hours of night, as above, then it is midnight, etc. And immediately one should prevail like a lion to rise from his sleep and be very careful not to pass true midnight while he sleeps, for then he tastes the taste of death, etc. And even if he slept after midnight, after he says the blessings and tikkun chatzot there is no harm, and the spirit of impurity does not rule over him, nor does he taste the taste of death after he awakens at midnight to draw down the mochin to Zeir Anpin, etc. — see there, for he extended further in pleasant words. And likewise wrote the gaon, author of Shevut Yaakov; and see in Shaarei Teshuvah on this in the name of other responsa.
