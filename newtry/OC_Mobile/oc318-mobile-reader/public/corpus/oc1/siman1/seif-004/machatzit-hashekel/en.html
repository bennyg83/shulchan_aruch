S.K. 4 — "one who rises early," etc. It should read "falling on the face" (nefilas apayim) — see siman 131:3, where Rabbeinu Yonah wrote there is no nefilas apayim at night; nevertheless on the nights of the watch, since it is close to day, they are accustomed to fall. Magen Avraham cites Rekanati and this is his language: Rekanati wrote to extend in Selichos until the day is fixed, and then they fall on the face — end of his words. So that Rekanati's words here do not contradict each other, one must say he also intended this here; but Rekanati's wording here does not imply so.

And in our Gemara, the first explanation — it is in Berachos 3a: a tanna taught, R' Eliezer says three watches comprise the night, etc., and a sign for the matter: first watch — a donkey brays; second — dogs bark; third — a baby nurses from its mother's breast and a woman speaks with her husband. It challenges: what did he count? If the beginning of the first watch — why a sign? It is the way of the world. If the end of the last watch — why a sign? It is day. It answers: he counted the end of the first watch and the beginning of the last and the middle of the middle watch. And Abaye says: he counted all the ends of the watches; the last is not needed, for it is day. Practical difference: for one who sits in a dark house and does not know the time for Kerias Shema — a baby nursing, a woman speaking with her husband, etc. — to rise and read Shema — end of his words. Therefore the first explanation, which gave a sign for the middle of the middle watch — the practical difference is that then it is a time of favor to pray, namely chatzos per the Zohar. And although Rashba in his novellae wrote the practical difference is for kodshim, that the time of eating is until chatzos, as in the first mishnah of Berachos — this is because Rashba did not have the Zohar before him, for it had not yet been revealed in his days; therefore he had to say it refers to kodshim. But now that the Zohar is revealed and explains that chatzos halailah is a time of favor to pray for the destruction — it is better to align our Gemara with the Zohar and also explain R' Eliezer's words on the middle of the middle watch regarding chatzos prayer. So too R' Eliezer's wording teaches, concluding: and on every watch Hashem sits and roars, etc. And for this reason the Rosh wrote there on R' Eliezer's words: it befits every God-fearing person to be pained and worried at that hour and pour out supplications on the destruction of the Temple, as written in seif 3. The second explanation does not read so, for he did not give a sign for chatzos, for he counts the end of the watch. And although we say in Yevamos 71a: the Sages taught — all those years Israel was in the desert (although a north wind did not blow for them, as stated there — nevertheless) there was no day that a north wind did not blow for them at chatzos halailah, as it says, And it was at chatzos halailah and Hashem struck, etc. What does the verse teach? This informs us that chatzos is a time of favor. Rashi explains: since chatzos halailah is a time of favor for striking the firstborn, it is also a time of favor for the north wind — end of his words. Nevertheless, we still have not heard that then is a time of favor for prayer, especially for the destruction of the Temple. See in Yevamos in Maharsha in chiddushim, who wrote: it appears to me the intent is that chatzos halailah — the spirit of that hour is good for the wounded; nevertheless Hashem struck, etc., at that hour, for Hashem can change nature — end of his words.

We always reckon the night, etc. — although for prayer, etc. Shaarei Tziyon wrote thus, and it is implied from Shaarei Tziyon and from Magen Avraham below siman 233: both summer and winter we reckon the beginning of night from tzeis hakochavim, and all twelve hours from the hours of equality; afterward it is night; and even in winter when the night is longer, the extra hours at the end of night are counted as day. If so, chatzos halailah is not equal summer to winter — in summer its time is after our chatzos halailah, and in winter before chatzos. The later authorities expanded on this. But in the responsum of Shevut Yaakov siman 1 he challenged at length and concluded: from the Zohar it is implied, as we say at the end of night — the hours added in winter belong to day; the same at the beginning of night — the hours added in winter from day — why distinguish beginning of night from end? Rather we reckon from the middle of the day when the sun stands in the middle of the sky; afterward six hours are called day, summer and winter; at the beginning of the seventh hour night begins and lasts twelve hours from the hours of equality; afterward it is day, summer and winter. According to this, chatzos halailah is equal summer and winter — namely twelve hours after chatzos hayom when the sun stands in the middle of the sky. He wrote see there. But one who wishes to mourn in three watches should reckon from tzeis hakochavim until amud hashachar, as implied in our Gemara specifically for one who mourns at chatzos per the Zohar; if so, for the time of chatzos too one should follow it. Not so the watch whose custom follows our Talmud — one follows their times as evident from the words of the Talmud — see above.
