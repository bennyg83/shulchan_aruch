And finally — see Baer Heitev; and see Yad Ephraim, who writes on this in the name of Teshuvos Shav and in Shalmei Tzibbur. See Shevut Yaakov siman 1, who wrote that for rising at midnight one should reckon twelve hours from midday — when the sun is at the zenith for each person — and twelve hours after that, i.e. half the celestial sphere; that is the time of midnight, when a north wind blows (this is also Shalmei Tzibbur's view). One may also be careful about the equinox hour; a conscientious person may be stringent on himself and be precise even about hours from the start of the day or the very start of the night. But for the time of Kerias Shema and prayer it depends on actual day and night. One who wishes to pray at the hours when the watches change, as the Tur writes, should reckon the night from tzeis hakochavim until alos hashachar as three watches, summer and winter — see there. Eshel Avraham brought some of this; see responsa Devar Shmuel simanim 213 and 221; see Yad Ephraim end of siman 58 and Even HaShoham siman 23: people who vowed to rise at midnight to pray and plead for mercy — their intent is halachic midnight. One should not reckon midnight as six hours after night entry, long or short nights; it has been explained that this is not the view of Shelah and Shaarei Tziyon, and that appears to be the main view. Shevut Yaakov vol. 3 siman 44 wrote that midnight prayer should not be done publicly but in private — "walk modestly"; therefore when a few exceptional individuals do so in synagogue with ten, there is concern for yohara if the whole congregation does not do so — see there. But nowadays the world is accustomed to do so and there is no yohara in any case; so too Birkei Yosef on Shelah's words: "In our ears we heard, in our eyes we saw" — in many towns the custom spread contrary to what was written. He also wrote there: a midnight group with a fixed room near the synagogue — the community leaders want them to say it in synagogue so there are also ten at the start; that is proper. But if some youths among them sometimes engage in idle talk and do not restrain themselves from speaking even in synagogue, better they keep their fixed place in the room. He wrote in Iggeres HaRamaz (acronym: our rabbi R. Moshe Zacuto) that tikkun should not be done at the start of the night even in summer, nor at the start of the second watch, but from midnight onward; sleep should come at the start of the night so one can rise at least half an hour before daylight. In pressing circumstances, even after alos there is some basis for tikkun — see there. In siman 15 there he wrote: where they add selichos on erev Rosh Hashana and Yom Kippur at the watch and also do nefilas apayim, one who regularly does tikkun chatzot all year should do it on erev Rosh Hashana too — see there. See siman 16 there: one accustomed to tikkun chatzot who rises at the watch when the congregation already says selichos and lacks time for both — he should say tikkun chatzot per the Ari's order, which is a greater principle than selichos. Birkei Yosef cites Mahar"a Azulai (author of Chesed LeAvraham): after completing tikkun chatzot he says: "I believe with perfect faith in the consolation of Zion; our God in His mercy will comfort us and renew His kingdom speedily" — see there.
