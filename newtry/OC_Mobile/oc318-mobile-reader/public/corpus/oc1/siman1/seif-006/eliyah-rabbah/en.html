[6] Parashas HaAkedah, etc. — so that one remember before the Holy One, blessed be He, the merit of the Patriarchs, and remember that he must surrender his soul to the Holy One, blessed be He; and Parashas HaMan — that he believe all his sustenance comes from the providence of the Holy One, blessed be He. Sefer HaKavanos writes that one should say it before Parashas HaKorbanos, and one may say it even on Shabbat. The Shelah wrote on page 202 that on Shabbat and Yom Tov one should not say the korbanos parshiyos except the tamid offering and what accompanies it — musafim and ketores — for these are offered on Shabbat and Yom Tov; but the rest of Parashas HaKorbanos one should not say at all, even if his intent is like one reading Torah — up to here. In Eliyahu Zuta I wrote in the name of Lechem Chamudos, page 84: anyone who has a heart to understand and learn — it is good for him to learn, and the fixed prayers suffice for him; and in place of supplications and korbanos he should learn — up to here. In brief, the Shelah cited my words and challenged: behold, in the Talmud time for Torah is separate and time for prayer is separate — and this is not precise, for there it refers to the eighteen blessings and Kerias Shema. It is also difficult that he contradicts himself, for in the laws of netilas yadayim he ruled like my words. I also found likewise in Peskei Tosafos: one who is not busy with his affairs and is not a baal Torah — one should not prevent him from saying every day Maamados, words of appeasement, and other verses printed in siddurim; but one who is busy with his affairs should be prevented from saying them; and one who is a baal Torah — it is better for him to study in their place — up to here. They also wrote in Peskei Tosafos: it is forbidden to say Maamados and supplications during piyutim of zemerah or while the chazan repeats the prayer — up to here. The grammarians also wrote: if one does not say Maamados every day, he should at least say in the morning immediately after Parashas HaTamid the psalm Shir shel yom; and even on Shabbat he should say immediately after "Uveyom haShabbos" the psalm Shir leyom haShabbos. Afterward one says "Atah hu shehiktiru," etc., and "And God spoke to Moshe," etc., Pitum haKetores as printed in Maamados — then one need not say it in Musaf even on Shabbat and Yom Tov — up to here. I also saw in Taz siman 132 to say it before prayer, except that he was precise that Parashas HaTamid corresponds to the blood of the tamid and prayer corresponds to the limbs of the tamid — for otherwise, why two? Therefore he wrote to say the ketores after the tamid so that one reads between blood and limbs. I wonder, for this is not precise at all, for at the time they actually offered the tamid they would pray afterward, as stated in Sukkah 53; and Rabbeinu Yonah at the beginning of the chapter Tefillas haShachar explains that in prayer the essence is opposite the blood. In any case, for practical law it is correct — not for this reason, but because he found it so in the responsum of Menachem Azariah siman 14; see there his reason. Seder Hayom writes: I saw a custom to say explicitly the song the Levites would say in the Temple — on Sunday "Ledavid mizmor," etc., and likewise they begin on the other days of the week — up to here. But in the responsum of Menachem Azariah siman 25 it is explained that one should not say thus; see there the law of these songs. Levush Orah, Parashas Ve'etchanan: the reason we do not say Tachanun on Shabbat and Yom Tov is the supplication that breaks a person's heart; but a request that God give him abundant gifts is permitted — and this is why they arranged requests in Maamados for Shabbat.
