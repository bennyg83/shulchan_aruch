The passage of the Binding, etc. It is recited in order to recall before Hashem the merit of the Patriarchs, and to remember that one is obligated to surrender his life to Hashem. The passage of the manna is recited so that one should believe that all his sustenance comes through Divine providence from Hashem. Sefer HaKavanos writes that it should be recited before the passage of the offerings, and that it should be recited even on Shabbos. Shelah, page 202, writes that on Shabbos and Yom Tov one should recite in the offerings only the daily offering and everything connected with it, the additional offerings, and the incense, because these were offered on Shabbos and Yom Tov. All the other passages of the offerings should not be recited at all, even if one intends merely to read Torah. In Eliyah Zuta I wrote in the name of Lechem Chamudos, page 84, that one who has the capacity to understand and learn would do better to learn Torah, and the established prayers suffice for him. In place of additional supplications and the passages of the offerings, he should study Torah. Kitzur Shelah cites my words and questions them from the Gemara’s statement that the time for Torah study is distinct from the time for prayer. He was not precise, because that passage refers to Shemoneh Esrei and Shema. It is also difficult that he contradicts himself, because in the laws of washing the hands he rules in accordance with my position. I also found this in Piskei Tosafos: One who is not burdened with business and is not learned should not refrain from reciting every day the Maamados, passages of appeasement, and the other verses printed in the siddurim. One who is burdened with business, however, should refrain from reciting them, and one who is learned would do better to study Torah in their place. Piskei Tosafos further writes that it is forbidden to recite Maamados and supplications during Pesukei DeZimrah or while the chazzan repeats Shemoneh Esrei. The grammarians further write that one who does not recite the Maamados every day should at least recite at Shacharis, immediately after the passage of the daily offering, the chapter of Tehillim of the day. Even on Shabbos, immediately after “And on the Shabbos day,” he should recite “A chapter of Tehillim, a song for the Shabbos day.” Afterward they should recite “You are the One before Whom they burned,” etc., followed by “Hashem said to Moshe,” etc., and the formulation of Pitum HaKetores as printed in the Maamados. He then need not repeat it at Mussaf, even on Shabbos or Yom Tov. I likewise saw in Taz, siman 132, that it should be recited before prayer. Taz reasons that the passage of the daily offering corresponds to its blood, while the prayer corresponds to its limbs. Otherwise, why would both be necessary? He therefore writes that the incense should be recited after the daily offering, so that it is read between the blood and the limbs. I am astonished, because this inference is not sound. Even when the daily offering itself was actually brought, the people afterward prayed, as stated in Sukkah 53. Moreover, Rabbeinu Yonah at the beginning of the chapter Tefillas HaShachar explains that the principal correspondence of prayer is to the blood. Nevertheless, the practical ruling is correct, though not for Taz’s reason. I found it stated in Responsa Menachem Azaryah, siman 14. See there for his reasoning. Seder HaYom writes: I have seen a custom to say explicitly, “The song that the Leviim recited in the Beis HaMikdash on the first day was ‘A chapter of Tehillim of David,’” and similarly to introduce the other days of the week. Responsa Menachem Azaryah, siman 25, however, indicates that one should not say this. See there for the laws of these songs. Levush HaOrah, parashas VaEschanan, writes that the omission of supplications on Shabbos and Yom Tov applies only to a supplication that breaks a person’s heart. A request that the Holy One, blessed be He, grant him abundant gifts is permitted. This is why requests were arranged within the Maamados of Shabbos. See also siman 40, siman 260.
