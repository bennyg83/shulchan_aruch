Like a lion. See Be'er Heitev and Taz, who writes that the principal strength must be directed against the evil inclination, and therefore says "like a lion," etc. It appears that this follows the account in Yoma 69b, where the evil inclination emerged from the Holy of Holies like a fiery lion cub. Accordingly, he says that one should strengthen himself like a lion against it.
The dawn. See Be'er Heitev. Birkei Yosef writes that this statement does not appear in the editions of the Zohar before us. It does appear, however, on folio 10 of a manuscript Zohar, which states that for this one incurs death at the hands of Heaven. He writes that one must wash his hands even if he does not intend to rise. If the water is far away and no one can bring it to him, some conscientious people customarily walk in increments of less than four amos. This does not appear correct to me; it is preferable to run, so as not to prolong the presence of the evil spirit resting on his hands. Responsa Shevus Yaakov, volume 3, siman 8, writes that people are not careful to avoid walking four amos before washing. One can say that they rely upon Rashba's view in Berachos that an entire house is considered like four amos. It is obvious that this may be relied upon only in pressing circumstances. See Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 4, and below, siman 4, for what I wrote concerning this.
"I have set Hashem before me." See Be'er Heitev. I saw a writing on this subject by the pious and godly Rabbi Zalman of Lokvar concerning the custom of making illustrated parchment menorahs for placement in siddurim, inscribing upon them "I have set Hashem," the four-letter Name, and other Divine Names. Such a menorah was called a "Shivisi." Its purpose was to remind a person not to engage in idle talk during prayer, out of awe of the Name before his eyes. He cites his uncle, my ancestor, the gaon and author of Tevuos Shor, who wittily said, "Set it aside; even without the Shivisi, I have silenced myself"; meaning that a person can guard himself and remain silent even without the Shivisi. He protested the practice strongly, because most people do not protect these sheets properly and they are cast about at the head of every street. This resembles the account in Rosh Hashanah 18b, where the day on which mention of the Divine Name was removed from legal documents was made a festival. One should therefore gather strength to abolish this custom. He discusses the matter at length and pleasantly. It appears that there is no concern with large parchment menorahs affixed in a synagogue before the lectern, or elsewhere beneath a glass panel. His objection concerns only those placed in siddurim, for the reason stated above and because they generally lead to erasure of the Name. We see with our own eyes that they are lost and erased, especially when Names are written upon them improperly. The one who warns and the one who heeds the warning will shine like the radiance of the firmament.
Those who mock. See Be'er Heitev. Regarding what he writes about "walking modestly" as well, see Sukkah 49b and what my brother, the gaon Rabbi Ephraim Zalman, may his light shine, wrote concerning this in Yad Ephraim.
With alacrity. See Yad Ephraim to siman 4, who writes that the words "bechemlah" ("with compassion") should be followed by an esnachta; see there.
