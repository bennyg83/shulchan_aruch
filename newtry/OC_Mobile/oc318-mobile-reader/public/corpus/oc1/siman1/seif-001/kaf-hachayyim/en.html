1) [Seif 1] He should strengthen himself like a lion, etc. He should be the one who awakens the dawn. Shlah writes of the mystical practice of joining day and night through Torah study or prayer, both in the morning and in the evening. Magen Avraham, se'if katan 1; Eliyah Rabbah, ot 1; Kisei Eliyahu, ot 1; and Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ot 8.
2) There, in the gloss: 'I have set Hashem before me always,' etc. He should constantly picture the Name Havayah before his eyes. This is of great benefit for fear of Hashem and also for refining the soul. Shaar Ruach HaKodesh, Yichud 21.
3) There, in the gloss: 'And he should not be ashamed before people,' etc. In any event, he should not quarrel with them. Beit Yosef; Magen Avraham, se'if katan 2.
4) There, in the gloss: 'And immediately when he awakens,' etc. This is not literal: he should wait briefly and not rise suddenly immediately after sleep. Magen Avraham, se'if katan 3; Eliyah Rabbah there. Seder HaYom writes that upon rising he should say: 'Modeh ani lefanecha, Melech chai v'kayam, shehechezarta bi nishmati b'chemlah, rabbah emunatecha.' He need not wash his hands for this, even if they are soiled, because he mentions neither G-d's Name nor a Divine title. Shalmei Tzibbur, page 17d; Chayei Adam, rule 1, end of ot 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ot 5; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, siman 1, ot 2. Although Chida writes in Kesher Gudal, siman 1, ot 1, that some say Modeh Ani requires washing the hands, since most poskim write that it does not, that is how we rule.
5) He should say this text whether he is still lying down or sitting. Shulchan Aruch HaRav there. He should pause slightly between the word 'b'chemlah' and the word 'rabbah.' Kitzur Shulchan Aruch there. It is forbidden, however, to articulate words of Torah while one's hands are soiled. Eliyah Zuta; Shalmei Tzibbur there; Shulchan Aruch HaRav there. See below, siman 4, ot 110.
6) Immediately upon awakening from sleep, one should wash his hands properly if he does not intend to resume sleeping, even if he remains lying down. Eliyah Rabbah, ot 4, in the name of Shlah; see Taz. Birkei Yosef, ot 1, writes likewise in the name of Derech Chayyim.
