Let him strengthen himself like a lion. The main heroism is against the Evil Inclination — they said "Who is a hero?" etc. He said "like a lion," for a lion's nature is not to fear any creature, as it says "From their roaring he will not cower" — so too a person should not be overcome by fear of his inclination, even though it is mightier than he.

That he himself should awaken, etc. Apparently this matter is not connected [to the previous clause] but is alacrity on its own. The point is: there is no comparison between one who performs a mitzvah through another's arousal and one who does so himself. Accordingly it appears [correct] what our Rabbi the Tur wrote: "I awaken the dawn and the dawn does not awaken me" — why is this at the end? It seems we find that a person who sanctifies himself from below is sanctified from above; so too regarding arousal there are two kinds — first arousal from below, then heavenly arousal comes to assist. David praised himself for being on such a level that his arousal from below was so great he needed no assistance from above — therefore he said "I awaken the dawn," like sanctifying Israel, as written regarding "the hind of dawn"; that level is only David and his like. But we and our like should hope to achieve arousal from below so that arousal from above may follow. Therefore the Mechaber mentioned only that one strengthen himself — at minimum let him awaken the dawn, even if he does not merit the second arousal we mentioned.
