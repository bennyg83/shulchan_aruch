"Let him prevail," etc. — Taz: It is known that matter and form are rivals; the reflexive hispa'el "yisgaber" means a person must strengthen his soul over his matter, for "the inclination of man's heart is evil from his youth" (Bereishis 8:21) — so he would vanquish and be vanquished within himself — and this is his lifelong struggle, for every day the evil inclination incites him and he must stand guard over his soul against it, as it is said, "From their roaring he will not cower" (Yeshayahu 31:4). See also Shmuel I 2:9: "He guards the footsteps of His pious ones, but the wicked are silenced in darkness." The Rama there concludes "Not by might shall a man prevail" — meaning one needs help from Heaven; only the person must begin. See also Iyov 15:25, "He strove against Shaddai" — the same reflexive form. There is a hishta'avas parallel to hishta'ares (as with his'ateir, etc.). The evil inclination is surely mightier than man — "Who can contend with One mightier than he?" — yet a person must be bold in his mind; therefore Scripture stresses "like a lion" rather than "like a leopard": a lion does not cower before shepherds, and however many people he knows are stronger than he, his nature is not to cower — so too a person must strengthen himself and be bold by nature. Hence the difficulty: why "bold as a leopard"? Because boldness toward a mocker is not such heroism, for he thinks the mocker is a human like himself; but toward the evil inclination, which is a mighty angel, one who overcomes it is called a hero — "Who is the hero?" The Rama resolved this well: the first two clauses correspond to positive commandments, the last two to negative commandments — see there.
