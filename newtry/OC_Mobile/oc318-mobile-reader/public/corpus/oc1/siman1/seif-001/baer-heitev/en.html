The dawn. In the Shelah he wrote the secret of joining day and night in Torah or in prayer, whether in the morning or in the evening. And immediately when he awakens from his sleep he should wash his hands, and thereby he is saved from pronouncing the Name in vain. And all the more so if he does not wish to sleep, even though he remains lying down, as the Shelah wrote. In Sefer Tola'at Yaakov it is stated in the Zohar: one who walks in the morning four amos without having washed his hands is liable to death, end quote. And the Bach wondered about our Rabbi the Beit Yosef that he did not write this. And it seems that because most people cannot be careful about this, it is better that they be unwitting violators than intentional violators; therefore he did not write it, and the remainder of the authorities likewise, and similarly another matter. And see Yad Aharon, siman 4, in the glosses on the Tur.

"I have set Hashem before me always." He should picture the Tetragrammaton always before his eyes like this: Y-H-V-H, and this is the secret of "I have set Hashem before me always," and this is of great benefit for the matter of fear, as the Ari, of blessed memory, wrote.

With alacrity. It is not comparable; rather he should pause a little and not stand up suddenly (Gittin 70a). Seder HaYom writes: upon rising he should say, "I give thanks before You, living and eternal King, that You have restored my soul to me with great compassion; great is Your faithfulness." Washing the hands is not required for this even if his hands are dirty, because he does not mention the Name or a substitute; nevertheless, for study it appears to be forbidden (Eliyahu Zuta). And after washing the hands he should say the seventy-two-letter Name: Ana b'koach.
