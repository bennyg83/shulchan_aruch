The dawn. The Shelah wrote a secret: to join day and night through Torah or prayer, both in the morning and in the evening. Immediately upon waking from his sleep he should wash his hands, and thereby he will be spared from a nocturnal emission; and all the more so if he does not wish to go back to sleep but remains lying down (Shelah). The Sefer Tola'as Yaakov wrote that it is stated in the Zohar that one who walks four amos in the morning without having washed his hands is liable to death — end quote. The Bach wondered at Rabbeinu Beis Yosef for not recording this. It appears that the reason is that most people cannot be careful about it, and it is better that they act inadvertently rather than deliberately; for this reason the Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah did not record it, and others wrote likewise. See also Yad Aharon, siman 4, in his gloss on the Tur.
I have set Hashem before me. One should constantly envision the Name of Havayah before his eyes, in the form of its four letters, Yud-Hei-Vav-Hei; and this is the secret meaning of "I have set Hashem before me always," and it is of great benefit for [attaining] the awe [of Heaven] — the Arizal, of blessed memory.

With alacrity. This is not meant precisely; rather, he should pause a little and not stand up suddenly (Gittin, folio 70). The Seder HaYom wrote that upon rising he should say, "I give thanks before You, living and enduring King, for You have restored my soul within me with great compassion; abundant is Your faithfulness." This does not require washing the hands, even if his hands are soiled, since it mentions neither the Name nor an appellation of it; nevertheless, for Torah study it appears to be forbidden (Eliyahu Zuta). And after washing the hands he should recite the forty-two-letter Name, "Ana b'Koach."
