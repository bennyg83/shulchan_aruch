That he should awaken the dawn - It is very proper to be careful to say, before all three prayers, the request of thanksgiving explained in Yerushalmi, chapter Tefillat HaShachar. In Shacharit: "I give thanks before You, Hashem my God and God of my fathers, that You brought me out from darkness to light." At Mincha: "I give thanks..." that just as You privileged me to see the sun in the east, so You privileged me to behold it in the west. At Maariv: "May it be Your will..." that just as I was in darkness and You brought me out to light, so may You bring me out from darkness to light.
This is a great principle in the Torah, etc. - One who wishes to fulfill "I have set Hashem before me" properly should be zealous to fulfill what we will write in the name of Sefer HaChinuch. Because of their great preciousness, several great authorities copied these words in their books. This is the language of the Chinuch, in his introduction and in mitzvot 25, 26, 417, 418, 432, and 387: Six mitzvot are constantly obligatory; they never cease from a person even for one moment throughout his life. At every time and every moment that he thinks about them, he fulfills a positive commandment, and there is no limit to the reward for these mitzvot. They are as follows.
1. To believe that there is one God in the world, who brought all existing things into being, and that from His desire and will comes everything that exists now, existed before, and will exist forever; and that He took us out of Egypt and gave us the Torah. This is the positive commandment written, "I am Hashem your God who took you out..." Its meaning is: know and believe that the world has a God who supervises it, for I took you out of the land of Egypt. 2. Not to believe in any god besides Him, as it says, "You shall have no other gods before Me." Even if one admits that the Holy One, blessed is He, rules over everything, but imagines that He entrusted governance of the world to an angel or star, this is acknowledgement of idolatry and one violates "You shall have no other gods before Me." Rather, one must believe that the Holy One, blessed is He, Himself in His glory supervises all worlds, and no created being has power to do anything without His will; therefore He is called God of gods. 3. To affirm His unity, as it says, "Hear, Israel, Hashem is our God, Hashem is One." Its meaning is: hear, Israel, and know that Hashem, who brought everything into being by His will, and who is our God supervising all worlds, is Hashem alone without any partnership. 4. To love the Omnipresent, blessed is He, as it says, "You shall love Hashem your God..." How does a person attain love? Through Torah, as it says in Sifrei: since it says "You shall love," I would not know how to love the Omnipresent; therefore Scripture says, "These words that I command you today shall be upon your heart," for through this you recognize the One who spoke and the world came into being. That is, through contemplation of Torah, one recognizes the greatness of the Holy One, blessed is He, which has no measure or end, and love necessarily settles in his heart. The matter of the mitzvah is that a person should strive to place all his aim and thought after the love of Hashem, and should evaluate in his heart that all that exists in the world, wealth, children, and honor, is as nothing against His blessed love; and he should constantly toil in seeking the wisdom of Torah so that he may attain knowledge of Hashem. One who fixes his thought on material matters and the vanities of the world not for the sake of Heaven, but only to enjoy himself and attain honor, has neglected this positive commandment, and his punishment is great. 5. To have the fear of Hashem upon his face constantly so that he not sin; concerning this it says, "Hashem your God you shall fear." When a matter of transgression comes before a person, he is obligated to arouse his spirit and set in his heart at that moment that the Holy One, blessed is He, supervises all human actions, even if their deeds are in darkness, and that He repays them according to the evil of the deed, as it is written, "If a man hides in hidden places, will I not see him? says Hashem..." 6. Not to stray after the thought of the heart and the sight of the eyes, as it says, "Do not stray after your heart..." The Sages said: "after your heart" refers to heresy, and "after your eyes" refers to immorality. Included in heresy are all foreign thoughts that oppose Torah knowledge; included in immorality is one who pursues worldly desires without directing them at all to a good purpose, meaning that he does not do them so that he may remain healthy and be able to labor in the service of his Creator, but his intent is always to multiply great pleasures for himself.
All this I have gathered briefly from his pleasant language; these words are healing salve for the eyes, and fortunate is one who fulfills them properly. See Chayei Adam, rule 1, who also discusses them at length.
And he should not be embarrassed, etc. - See Mishnah Berurah in the name of Beit Yosef. Know that Beit Yosef speaks only of a mitzvah that one performs for himself, where people mock him; then certainly he need not be concerned at all for their mockery, and he should not quarrel with them. But if he is in a place where heretics rise against the Torah and wish to enact measures in communal affairs through which they will turn the people away from the will of Hashem, and he opened with peace and his words were not heard, Beit Yosef said nothing about such a case. It is a mitzvah to hate them, to contend with them, and to frustrate their counsel in every way possible. King David, peace be upon him, said: "Do I not hate those who hate You, Hashem? And against those who rise against You I contend. I hate them with ultimate hatred..."
