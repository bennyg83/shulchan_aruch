That he should awaken the dawn — it is very proper to be careful to say first of all the three prayers of gratitude to God explained in the Jerusalem Talmud, chapter Tefillas HaShachar — namely: at Shacharis, "Modeh ani lefanecha, the Name, God of my fathers, shehotzeisani me'afeila le'orah." At Minchah: "Modeh ani," etc. — that just as You privileged me to see when the sun was in the east, so You privileged me to see it in the west. At Arvis: "Yehi ratzon," etc. — just as I was in darkness and You brought me out to light, so bring me out from darkness to light.

This is a great principle in the Torah, etc. — One who wishes to fulfill “I have set…” properly should hasten to fulfill what is written in Sefer HaChinuch; and because of the greatness of their belovedness, several great authorities copied them in their books. This is the language of the Chinuch [in its introduction and in mitzvos 25, 26, 17, 18, 32, 87]: Six mitzvos whose obligation is constant shall not cease from a person even for one moment all his days; and every time and every moment that he thinks about them he fulfills a positive commandment, and there is no end to the reward for mitzvos. They are: (a) To believe there is one God in the world who created all existences, and His desire and will is everything that is now and was and will be forever; and that He brought us out of Egypt and gave us the Torah — this is the positive commandment written “I am God, your God who brought you out,” etc.; meaning: know and believe there is a God who providentially cares, for He brought you out of the land of Egypt. (b) Not to believe in any god besides Him — “you shall have no other gods before Me”; even if he acknowledges the Holy One, blessed be He, rules over all but imagines in his thoughts that He entrusted governance of the world to an angel or a star — this is idolatry and violates “you shall have no other gods before Me”; rather he must believe the Holy One, blessed be He, Himself in His glory providentially cares over all worlds, and no creature has power to do anything contrary to His will; therefore the Holy One, blessed be He, is called God of gods. (c) To unify Him — “Hear, O Israel: the Name is our God, the Name is One”; meaning: Hear, O Israel, and know that God who created everything by His will and is our God who cares over all worlds is one God without any partnership. (d) To love the Place, blessed be He — “and you shall love God your God,” etc. How does a person reach love? Through Torah, as stated in Sifrei: since it says “and you shall love” — I do not know how to love the Place — therefore it says “and these words… upon your heart today”; meaning, through contemplating Torah he recognizes the greatness of the Holy One, blessed be He, which has no comparison and no end, and love settles in his heart necessarily. The substance of the mitzvah: a person should set all his direction and all his thoughts after love of God; and he should reckon in his heart that everything in the world — wealth, children, honor — is as nothing against His love; and he should always toil seeking Torah wisdom to attain knowledge of Him. One who fixes his thought on material matters and vanities of the world not for God's sake but only to enjoy and gain honor has nullified this positive commandment and its punishment is great. (e) To have fear of Heaven upon one’s face always so as not to sin — on this it says “God your God you shall fear”; whoever has a matter of sin come to his hand must awaken his spirit and put to his heart at that moment that the Holy One, blessed be He, watches all human actions even if in secret, and repays them vengeance for the evil of the deed, as written “if a man hides in secret places I will not see him, says God,” etc. (f) Not to stray after heart thoughts and sight of eyes — “you shall not turn after your hearts,” etc.; the Sages said: after your hearts — this is heresy; after your eyes — this is licentiousness. Heresy includes all alien thoughts opposite the Torah’s view. Licentiousness includes one who pursues worldly lusts without directing them to any good intent — not doing them so that he will stand healthy and strive in his Creator’s service, but his intent is always to increase great pleasures for his soul. I collected this from his pleasant language briefly; they are illuminating to the eyes, and fortunate is one who fulfills them properly. See Chayei Adam, klal 1, which also extended on them.

And he should not be ashamed, etc. — See Mishna Berurah in the name of Beit Yosef. Know that Beit Yosef spoke only about a mitzvah one does for himself and people mock him — then certainly he should not heed their mockery and not quarrel with them. But if he stands where there are heretics rebelling against Torah who want to make regulations in city matters and thereby divert the people from God’s will, and he opens with peace and his words are not heard — in such a case Beit Yosef did not speak at all. It is a mitzvah to hate them, quarrel with them, and frustrate their counsel however possible. And David said, “I hate those who hate You… complete hatred I hate them,” etc.
