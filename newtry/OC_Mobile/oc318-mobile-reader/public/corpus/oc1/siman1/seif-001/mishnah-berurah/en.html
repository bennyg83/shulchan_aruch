(1) To serve his Creator — for man was created for this purpose, as Scripture says: "Everyone who is called by My name, whom I created for My glory" (Isaiah 43:7), etc. Even if his inclination misleads him in winter to say: How can you stand in the morning, for the cold is great; or misleads him in summer to say: How can you rise from your bed when you have not yet had your fill of sleep? — he should strengthen himself against it and not listen; and he should think in his soul: If he had to rise to serve a king of flesh and blood, how careful and zealous he would be to rise early and prepare himself for his service; all the more so and a fortiori before the King of kings, the Holy One, blessed be He.

(2) At dawn — In the Shelah it is written as a secret: to join day and night in Torah or in prayer, both morning and evening. As soon as he awakens from sleep and does not wish to sleep further, he should wash his hands, even while still lying down. All the more so he should not walk four cubits without washing his hands. One must be very careful about this. The holy Zohar greatly emphasizes the punishment for this, because one retains the spirit of impurity upon oneself. Nevertheless, Heaven forbid that one transgress any prohibition on account of lack of hand-washing — such as holding back from urinating on account of it, or using water that a friend prepared for himself, unless it is clear to him that he will immediately give other water in its place. Some err in this. If it happens that the water is far and no one can bring it to him, some people of good disposition are accustomed to walk less than four cubits at a time. Shaarei Teshuvah wrote on this that it is not proper; rather it is preferable that they go quickly, so as not to retain an evil spirit on their hands. Some say that for this purpose we say the entire house is considered like four cubits; but one should not rely on this except in a time of need. If it happens when he rises at night that he does not have water to wash his hands three times properly to remove the spirit of impurity — even so, Heaven forbid that he cancel words of Torah for this reason until daybreak; rather he should wash a little, or clean his hands with whatever cleanses, and bless and study, as required by the Talmud and the poskim.

(3) When the congregation prays — that is, even though the time for prayer will not pass, nevertheless it is a mitzvah [to pray] with the congregation. See below at the end of seif katan 9.

(4) The righteous — that is, he should always picture in his soul how he stands before Heaven, blessed be He, for the Holy One, blessed be He, fills the whole earth with His glory. They wrote in the name of the Ari, may his memory be a blessing, that one should always picture the Tetragrammaton before his eyes with the vowels of yirah (fear), thus: yod-heh-vav-heh; and this is the secret of "I have set Heaven before me always," and this is of great benefit for fear of Heaven. Some were accustomed for this reason to make menorahs of illustrated parchment to place in siddurim, and they would write on them "I have set Heaven," etc., with the four-letter Name and other names; such a menorah was called "Shiviti." The reason was so that one would remember not to engage in idle conversation during prayer out of awe of the Name. Shaarei Teshuvah wrote in the name of Tevuos Shor that one should gird himself with strength to abolish the custom, because for the most part they do not preserve the parchment properly, and they are spilled in public streets, and also many times the Name comes to be erased. It appears that regarding large menorahs illustrated on parchment that are fixed in the synagogue before the amud under a glass panel — the above reason does not apply; we have no concern with this, end of his words. Those who fix one on the amud without a panel — one should protest against them, for through the candles fixed on the amud, the Name comes to be erased, Heaven forbid, in most cases.

(5) Because of people — in any case he should not quarrel with them, because the trait of brazenness is very unseemly, and it is not fitting to use it at all, even in divine service, for he will acquire in his soul a habit of being brazen even when not in divine service (Beis Yosef). See Biur Halacha.

(6) Those who mock him — likewise if he is an average person standing in the place of great ones, he should not be ashamed of them to learn and perform the mitzvah. But if he can perform the mitzvah not in their presence, that is better. However, before average people who will learn from him, one can say it is better to do it in their presence, so they will learn from him to do as he does. But let his heart be directed for the sake of Heaven, and not to boast, Heaven forbid.

(7) Also in private conduct — meaning, even when he is at home in private, his conduct should also be there his Heaven, as he concludes and proceeds.

(8) With alacrity — not necessarily; rather he should wait a little and not stand up suddenly, for this harms the body [Gittin 70a]. It is good to say immediately upon rising: "I give thanks before You, living and enduring King, that You have restored my soul to me in compassion; great is Your faithfulness." The word "in compassion" should be with a pause (etnachta), and "great is Your faithfulness" without a pause. This is from the verse "They are new every morning" (Lamentations 3:23), etc., that the Holy One, blessed be He, fulfills His faithfulness to return the souls entrusted to Him in the morning. This does not require washing the hands, even if his hands are soiled, because this formula mentions neither a Name nor an epithet. Nevertheless, for study it appears that it is forbidden before he has washed his hands, and in any case it is forbidden before the blessing on the Torah. See above at the end of note 2, seif 3 [edition].
