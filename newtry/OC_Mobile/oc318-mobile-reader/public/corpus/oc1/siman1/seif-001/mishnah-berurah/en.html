(1) To serve his Creator. For this is why man was created, as Scripture states, “Everyone who is called by My Name, and whom I created for My glory,” etc. Even if his evil inclination entices him in winter, saying, “How will you rise in the morning, for the cold is severe?” or entices him in summer, saying, “How will you rise from your bed when you have not yet had enough sleep?” he should overcome it and not listen. He should think to himself: If he had to rise to serve a mortal king, how careful and diligent he would be to rise early and prepare himself for that service. All the more so, many times over, before the King of kings, the Holy One, blessed is He.
(2) The dawn. The Shelah writes of a mystical practice to connect day and night through Torah or prayer, both in the morning and in the evening. As soon as one awakens and does not intend to return to sleep, he should wash his hands, even while he remains lying down. All the more so, he should not walk four amos without washing his hands, and one must be exceedingly careful about this. The holy Zohar greatly emphasizes the punishment for delaying, because one leaves the spirit of impurity upon himself. Nevertheless, God forbid that one violate any prohibition because he has not washed his hands. For example, he must not restrain himself from urinating, nor take water that another prepared for himself unless he is certain that he will immediately replace it. Some people stumble in this matter. If the water is far away and no one can bring it to him, some meticulous people have the practice of walking in increments of less than four amos. Shaarei Teshuvah writes that this does not appear correct, and that it is preferable to walk quickly so as not to leave the evil spirit upon his hands. Some say that for this purpose the entire house is considered like four amos, but one should rely on this only in pressing circumstances. If one rises during the night and lacks enough water to wash his hands three times properly and remove the spirit of impurity, God forbid that he refrain from Torah study until daylight. Rather, he should wash with a small amount of water, or clean his hands with any cleansing substance, then recite the blessing and study, in accordance with the Gemara and the poskim.
(3) When the congregation prays. Even if the time for prayer will not pass, it is nevertheless a mitzvah to pray with the congregation. See below, at the end of s.k. 9.
(4) The righteous. He should constantly picture himself as standing before Hashem, for the Holy One, blessed is He, fills the entire earth with His glory. It is written in the name of the Arizal that one should constantly visualize the Name Yud-Hei-Vav-Hei before his eyes with the vocalization associated with awe. This is the mystical meaning of, “I have placed Hashem before me always,” and it is of great benefit in attaining awe. Some therefore had the practice of making illustrated parchment plaques to place in siddurim, writing upon them, “I have placed Hashem,” together with the four-letter Name and other sacred Names. Such a plaque was called a Shivisi. Its purpose was to remind a person not to engage in idle speech during prayer, out of awe of Hashem. Shaarei Teshuvah writes in the name of Tevuot Shor that one should exert himself to abolish this custom, because these parchments are generally not guarded properly, are cast about publicly, and often lead to erasure of the Divine Name. It appears, however, that there is no objection to large parchment plaques affixed in the synagogue before the lectern beneath a glass panel, because the preceding concern does not apply. End quote. Those who affix them to the lectern without a glass panel should be protested, because the candles placed there frequently cause erasure of the Divine Name, God forbid.
(5) Because of people. In any event, he should not quarrel with them, because brazenness is an exceedingly disgraceful trait. It is not proper to use it at all, even in serving Hashem, because he may acquire a disposition toward brazenness even outside the service of Hashem. This is from the Beit Yosef. See Biur Halacha.
(6) Those who mock him. Likewise, if an ordinary person is among prominent people, he should not be embarrassed to study or perform a mitzvah in their presence. Still, when he can perform the mitzvah outside their presence, that is preferable. In the presence of ordinary people who may learn from him, however, it may be better to perform it openly so that they learn to act as he does. He must direct his heart for the sake of Heaven and not, God forbid, to display himself.
(7) Also to walk modestly. Meaning, even when he is privately within his home, his conduct should still be with Hashem his God, as the Mechaber continues.
(8) With alacrity. This is not literal. One should pause briefly and not rise suddenly, because doing so is harmful to the body, as stated in Gittin, folio 70. It is proper to say immediately upon rising: “I give thanks before You, living and enduring King, for You have mercifully restored my soul within me; great is Your faithfulness.” One should pause after “mercifully,” and say “great is Your faithfulness” without a break between those words. This is based on the verse, “They are new every morning,” because the Holy One, blessed is He, faithfully restores in the morning the souls entrusted to Him. Washing the hands is not required before saying this, even when the hands are unclean, because the formula contains neither a Divine Name nor a descriptive Divine title. It nevertheless appears forbidden to study before washing the hands, and in any event Torah study is forbidden before Birkat HaTorah. See above, at the end of s.k. 2.
