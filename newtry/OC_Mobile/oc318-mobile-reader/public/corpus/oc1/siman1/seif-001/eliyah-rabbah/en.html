[1] He shall strengthen himself like a lion, etc. The Acharonim wrote in the name of the Zohar: A person should not say, "I am lying on my bed and learning," but must stand and put on his garments because of readiness to greet your God, Israel — and if possible he should stand on his feet, so that he not come to sleep; this is very good. They also wrote that one should join morning and evening, day and night, in Torah or prayer. Reishis Chochmah, Shelah, and other mussar books extended on the greatness of the mitzvah of rising at chatzos and the punishment of one who nullifies it — see there. They also wrote: one who stands at chatzos to study should engage in oral Torah. The time of chatzos — Shaarei Tzion understands in the name of the Zohar that whether in summer when nights are short or in winter when nights are long, midnight is six hours from the very beginning of the night. So it is reasonable that night always begins for everyone from the very beginning of the night, as written "and it was evening and it was morning." In Sefer Derech Chochmah page 46 it seems he understood that night begins in summer some hours before nightfall; therefore he challenged: how do we pray Minchah in summer within six hours before midnight? And why is melacha not forbidden in summer on Friday evening from six hours before night? He was not precise. It is further explained in the Zohar and Shaarei Tzion that in winter, after twelve hours from the beginning of the night, even though it is still night, it is considered actual day both for chatzos and for watches. The difficulty: if so we would permit praying Shacharis before daylight, and in summer one would not pray Shacharis until after twelve hours from the beginning of the night. In my view prayer law is different, for prayer was established for set times — Shacharis because we merit to see the day, as will be explained below; therefore we require that it be day for us. Perhaps there are other hidden reasons. In any case I hold we may prove from the Talmud at the beginning of Berachos, which argues with the Zohar and holds that we count the night in summer and winter as seasonal hours, and chatzos is always literally half the night — from its challenge: if the end of a watch is counted, the end of the last watch is a sign — why do I need daytime? So far. Per the Zohar nothing is difficult, for we need a sign for long nights. Also what does the Talmud challenge — it is written "at chatzos I rise," and it is written "my eyes precede the watches" — behold, even if he rose at chatzos, that is after six hours from the beginning of the night, nevertheless there are two watches until day in long nights; understand. Lechem Chamudos wrote in chapter HaRo'eh: even though he must rise quickly without laziness, nevertheless he should wait a little and not stand suddenly immediately after sleep, for the master said at the end of chapter Mefanin: five things are close to death — one who eats and stands, drinks and stands, sleeps and stands, expectorates blood and stands, uses his bed and stands — rather he should wait a little and then rise.
