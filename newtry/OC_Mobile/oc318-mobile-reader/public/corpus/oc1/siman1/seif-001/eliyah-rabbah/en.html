[A] Later authorities cite Zohar: one should not say “I lie on my bed and will study Torah”; one must stand and dress appropriately—“prepare to meet your God, Israel”—and if possible rise on one’s feet, so as not to fall asleep—very good. They also paired morning and evening, day and night, in Torah or prayer. Reshis Chochmah, Shelah, and other musar works elaborate the mitzvah of rising at halachic midnight and punishment for neglect—see there. One who rises at midnight for study should engage in Oral Torah (Shaarei Tzion citing Zohar). Chatzos: Shaarei Tzion cites Zohar—whether summer short nights or winter long—from twelve seasonal hours, Chatzos six hours after actual nightfall; thus night always begins for everyone from true nightfall—“there was evening and there was morning.” Derech Chachmah p. 46 seems to understand summer night beginning some hours before nightfall—hence his question how we pray Minchah in summer within six hours before Chatzos, and why melachah is not forbidden erev Shabbat summer six hours before nightfall—his reasoning is unclear. Zohar and Shaarei Tzion: in winter after twelve hours from nightfall it still belongs to the day for Chatzos and watches. If so we should permit Shacharis before daylight yet in summer delay Shacharis until twelve hours after nightfall—I answer prayer follows instituted times; Shacharis corresponds to seeing daylight as explained later; perhaps hidden reasons exist. In any case Gemara Berachos opening argues with Zohar and holds we reckon night with seasonal hours always—Chatzos is literally half the night—from the Gemara’s question about end of watch… See there. If Zohar—no difficulty—we need a sign for long nights; what does Gemara challenge “Chatzos I arise” vs “my eyes anticipate the watches”? Even rising at Chatzos after six hours from nightfall there are two watches until day on long nights—investigate. Lechem Chamudos, chapter “HaRo’eh”: even if one must rise promptly without laziness, pause briefly and do not stand suddenly after sleep—for Master said end of chapter “One removes five things close to death: ate and rose, drank and rose, slept and rose…” — pause a moment then rise.
