[2] In the morning, etc. Shomrim LaBoker must be recited before daylight, even in the summer. If it is already close to sunrise, he should first recite the kinnot, then begin Shomrim LaBoker, and omit the kinnot that appear within it. So wrote Rabbeinu Chananel; see Responsa Be'er Eishek, siman 124. Knesses HaGedolah writes in siman 619: Regarding those people who rise early for the synagogue every day but doze during prayer, and my own eyes have seen sleep overtake them even during Krias Shema, of them I apply the verse, “Woe to those who rise early in the morning.” Therefore, the good and proper course is that, if they can also concentrate upon their prayers, they should rise during the pre-dawn watch. But if, by rising early on Shabbos, they will come to sleep during prayer, it is better that one concentrate upon his prayer than that he rise early in the morning. See below, siman 151, seif 2, that it is forbidden to sleep in a synagogue even temporarily.

It is forbidden, however, to remain awake while lying upon his bed unless he is reflecting upon words of Torah. Bach, siman 4, writes as follows: Sefer Tola'as Yaakov states in the name of the Zohar that one who walks four amos in the morning without washing his hands is liable to death. It is surprising that Beit Yosef did not record this. It appears that, since the overwhelming majority are unable to be careful about it, and it is better that they remain inadvertent rather than become deliberate transgressors, he omitted it. These are his words, but the explanation is strained. It also requires study why the public, including those knowledgeable in halachah, is lenient. Levush writes at the beginning of siman 25 that immediately upon rising from bed one should relieve himself and wash his hands. Since an ordinary person's privy is not within four amos of his bed, this implies grounds for leniency. The same appears from Levush, siman 8, seif 11, who writes that one may wrap himself in tzitzis before washing so that he will not walk four amos without tzitzis. The implication is readily understood. My brother-in-law, the distinguished Rabbi Yaakov, said that people relied upon Shach, Yoreh Deah 344, who cites Mordechai that the entire room in which a deceased person lies is regarded as four amos. This does not appear correct, first because people are even more lenient and leave that room, and second because Mordechai itself is difficult. In Berachos 25a Rava states that the halachah does not follow Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar, who says that an entire house is regarded as four amos. We likewise rule below, siman 79, regarding excrement in a room, and the discussion there implies that “the entire house” means the entire room. Likutei Maharil similarly writes that throughout the Gemara the word “house” commonly means a room. It appears to me that Mordechai may be resolved as follows. At the beginning of Berachos the Gemara states that in the presence of the dead one may speak only of matters relating to the dead, without mentioning four amos. Mordechai therefore explains that the prohibition applies throughout the room, which is treated like the deceased person's four amos. The reason is as Beit Yosef explains there: when one speaks of other matters in the deceased person's presence, he appears to have despaired of attending to the burial. Where the Gemara expressly limits a prohibition to four amos, however, the prohibition applies only within four amos, even in the same room. Incidentally, I shall briefly mention what I wrote in Eliyah Rabbah on Yoreh Deah there, resolving Beit Yosef's question why Mordechai connected the rule that the whole room is prohibited specifically to the first version of the Gemara. Mordechai reverses the order of the Gemara's versions. According to the first version, even ordinary conversation is forbidden before the deceased. The prohibition is therefore not based on mocking the helpless, but upon the appearance that one has abandoned attending to him, and it applies throughout the room. According to the second version, only words of Torah are forbidden because of mocking the helpless, and the prohibition therefore applies only within four amos, like Krias Shema near a corpse and walking in a cemetery while wearing visible tzitzis or tefillin, discussed there in siman 366. This appears to be the meaning of Tosafos on Berachos 3b, s.v. Ein, who write, “specifically within his four amos.” Examine this carefully. I later found in Damesek Eliezer of Apta, page 326a, regarding the above words of Tola'as Yaakov: perhaps this applied specifically in their times, like the laws of uncovered liquids and pairs. This resolves Bach's question why Beit Yosef did not record it, and upon this the public relies. Nevertheless, a God-fearing person should be stringent and follow the mussar works, preparing beside his bed two vessels, one containing water and one empty. Etz Chaim writes that it is preferable for the empty vessel to be chipped, for it then represents dominion over the Other Side.
