[9] The korbanos, etc. For it is written, And this is the law of the sin-offering — why do I need "and this"? Rather, that their reading is like their offering. Rashi explained: from that it says "law" (torah) and does not say "statute" (chukah) he derives — and it does not seem that the Torah wished to make them a mere statute, only a law of Torah that has a revealed reason (Levush). In Eliyahu Zuta I discussed Rashi's words — he holds, as Mizrachi wrote on Parashas Chukas, that everything where it is not written "Torah" but only "chukah" alone still has a reason. If there is a difficulty: the Talmud in Menachos 19a challenges on what we say — wherever it says chukah and torah it is only to be indispensable. It challenges: but korbanos, where it says torah, are not indispensable. It answers: torah requires chukah; chukah does not require torah. If so, for this reason chukah was not written — it was not said to be indispensable. In Eliyahu Zuta I expanded; see there. My master, my father the rabbi n"r"w, explained Rashi's words: he learns from the word torah; but he asked on Rashi — perhaps we require torah to learn that it is indispensable — and he answers: from that chukah is not written, meaning that from torah alone we do not learn indispensability, only from chukah — and this is a sharp point.
