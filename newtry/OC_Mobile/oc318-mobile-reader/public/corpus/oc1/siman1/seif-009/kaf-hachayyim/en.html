9) On Shabbos night, if one was not awake precisely at midnight and midnight passed while he slept, there is room to question whether the external forces clothe themselves in him and he tastes the taste of death, or whether they do not, since the external forces have no grasp on Shabbos, as explained in Shaar HaKavanot, the Discourses of Shabbos, and Shaar HaMitzvot, Parashat Beshalach and Parashat Behar Sinai. Emet LeYaakov, entry tav, ot 74, likewise writes: 'It follows that on Shabbos, the good within kelipat nogah (its vessels) ascends above, etc.' See there. If so, they do not clothe themselves in the lower human being either. I later saw in Shemen Sasson, part 3, discourse 1 on the changes in the prayers, ot 8, that there is no need to rise at midnight on holy Shabbos night, because 'night will shine like day' and there is no fear of the external forces; see there. He writes likewise in Shemen Sasson, part 4, called Petach Einayim, in the discourse on Tikkun Chatzot. Nevertheless, even on the nights of Shabbos and Yom Tov one should endeavor to rise at midnight so as not to break his habit, and also for the reason we wrote in siman 238, ot 3; see there.
