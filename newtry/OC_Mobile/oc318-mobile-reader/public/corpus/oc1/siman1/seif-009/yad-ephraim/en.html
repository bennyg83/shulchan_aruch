Se'if katan 12. Some emend the text to read: 'Regarding the Minchah prayer, everyone agrees that the tamid precedes the incense, as stated in Yoma; but the passage of the meal offering can be recited after the incense.' In truth, if this is the emendation, the words 'unlike Olat Shabbat' are imprecise. Olat Shabbat itself writes this in disagreement with the Shulchan Aruch, which places the tamid and then the incense. Olat Shabbat writes: 'It appears to me that this is not the order; rather, the tamid is followed by the meal offering and then the incense.' Magen Avraham should therefore have said: 'Olat Shabbat writes that the meal offering should precede, etc., but this does not appear correct, for Abaye, etc.' The phrase 'unlike Olat Shabbat' implies that it follows from Magen Avraham's preceding words that Olat Shabbat is wrong, as is Magen Avraham's usual style. It appears to me as follows. Olat Shabbat's words require explanation: why does he place the passage of the meal offering before the incense? His reference to Rambam's Hilchot Temidin supplies nothing, and no one disputes the explicit Talmudic rule that burning the incense preceded the meal offering. It appears that Olat Shabbat also holds that our recitation of the tamid passage corresponds to the limbs. Although the incense preceded the limbs, we nevertheless recite the tamid passage first because it is a type of sacrifice. Since in any case we do not recite the passages in the Temple's sacrificial order, the meal-offering passage may be placed beside the tamid passage, because the meal offering also belongs to the category of offerings, unlike the incense. Although the incense preceded the meal offering, we are already not following the order; otherwise the incense would also have to be recited before the tamid passage, as Hagahot Maimoniyot indeed states. According to Magen Avraham, however, reciting the tamid passage corresponds to the tamid's blood. The recitation consequently follows the sacrificial order, and it is obvious that the order may not be changed: the incense must precede the meal offering. After Magen Avraham concludes that the passage corresponds to the tamid's blood, he writes that at Minchah everyone agrees the tamid comes first, because Yoma states that the afternoon incense came between the limbs and the libations. In any event, the recitation must follow the order, unlike Olat Shabbat, who places the meal offering before the incense because he holds that the tamid passage corresponds to the limbs and that the recitation does not follow the order. This is incorrect, for Abaye arranged it as follows: the tamid's blood preceded the incense, the incense preceded the limbs, and the limbs preceded the meal offering. Whichever way one turns, if the passage corresponds to the limbs, the incense should also precede the tamid passage; one cannot recite them out of order. The atonement is in fact effected by the blood, so our recitation properly follows the order. Understand this carefully. What remains difficult in my humble opinion is that Magen Avraham and Olat Shabbat discuss the meal offering brought with the morning tamid. What separate meal-offering passage is there? The passage of the libation offering in Parashat Shelach has no relevance to the tamid's meal offering; see there and what my revered grandfather writes in Tevuot Shor. The tamid's meal offering is recited within the tamid passage itself: 'and a tenth of an ephah of fine flour as a meal offering.' See the Talmud in Yoma there and Rashi there. It is obviously inconceivable that one should interrupt the passage before 'and a tenth of an ephah' and insert the incense passage in the middle. This certainly cannot be correct and requires further study.
