Section 12 — There are emendators who, in the Minchah prayer, all agree that the tamid precedes the ketores, as stated in Yoma. But the parashah of the minchah offering they would place after the ketores, as it should read. And truly, if one emends thus he is not precise in what he wrote “not like Eliyah Rabbah” — for Eliyah Rabbah truly likewise disagrees with the words of the Shulchan Aruch that wrote tamid and then ketores; and regarding this Eliyah Rabbah wrote, and it seems to me the order is not thus but tamid and then minchah and then ketores — Eliyah Rabbah. And thus Magen Avraham should have said. And in Eliyah Rabbah he wrote that minchah should precede… and it does not appear so, for Abaye… and the language “not like Eliyah Rabbah” implies it continues his prior words, from which it is proven “not like Eliyah Rabbah,” and thus in most places in Magen Avraham. And in my humble opinion to explain — one truly needs to add spice to Eliyah Rabbah’s words: why does he precede the parashah of the minchah offering to the ketores? And what Eliyah Rabbah wrote — see in Rambam, Laws of Temidim — I did not find a matter. And certainly none dispute that offering the ketores was before the minchah, and this is fixed Talmud. And it seems Eliyah Rabbah holds also that what we say, the parashah of the tamid, is against the limbs; and although the ketores precedes the limbs, nevertheless we say the parashah of the tamid first, since it is a type of sacrifice. And if so, since in any event we do not say in the order of sacrifice in the Beis HaMikdash, automatically one should adjoin the parashah of the minchah offering to the parashah of the tamid, since the minchah offering is also related to sacrifice, unlike the ketores. And although the ketores precedes the minchah offering, nevertheless since in any event we do not say in order — for otherwise one would have to say it before the tamid also, as truly the gloss of Mahari ibn Nun. And according to what Magen Avraham wrote, that reciting the parashah is against the blood of the tamid — automatically the recitation is in the order of sacrifice. And if so it is obvious one should not change the order, and one should say ketores before minchah. Therefore, after Magen Avraham decided it is against the blood of the tamid, he wrote that regarding the minchah offering all agree the tamid precedes, because it states in Yoma that the afternoon ketores was between the limbs and the libations. In any case the recitation must be in order. And not like Eliyah Rabbah, who wrote minchah before ketores — and we were because he holds the parashah of the tamid is against the limbs and the recitation not in order — as Abaye arranged… the blood of the tamid before the ketores, the ketores to the limbs, the limbs to the minchah offering. And in any case, if the parashah is against the limbs, one should precede the ketores before the parashah of the tamid also. But nevertheless one should not say not in order — for then truly atonement is in the blood, and our recitation is properly in order. Understand. And what is difficult here — in my humble opinion, since Magen Avraham and Eliyah Rabbah speak of the minchah offering that they would offer with the morning tamid — what parashah of a minchah offering is here? For the parashah of the minchas nesechim in Shelach lecha is not a matter for the tamid minchah offering at all — Eliyah Rabbah. And in what the Arizal wrote in Tanya Shlomo, and regarding the tamid minchah offering — the recitation is in that same verse of the tamid offering itself that says “and a tenth of an ephah of fine flour for a minchah offering”; see the Talmud in Yoma there and Rashi there. And it is obvious it is unthinkable that he would stop in the parashah before “and a tenth of an ephah” and say the parashah of the ketores in the middle — this certainly is not. Requires study.
