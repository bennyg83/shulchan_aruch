Seif katan 10. See Baer Heitev. Shevus Yaakov, volume 2, writes that those accustomed to recite the passages of the offerings every day may also recite the passages of the sin offering and guilt offering on the day after Yom Kippur. The formula “May it be Your will that this be considered as though I offered a sin offering” should not be recited without qualification, whereas the corresponding passage for a guilt offering may be recited throughout the year. See the works cited there concerning one whose heart troubles him lest he have sinned. He may recite the scriptural passage as Torah study, but should not make a definite declaration that he is obligated to bring an offering. Rather, he should condition the prayer upon actually having incurred such an obligation. This avoids the appearance of bringing a non-obligatory sin offering while still allowing the passage to awaken repentance.
