Parashas HaAkedah — to remember the merit of the Patriarchs every day, and also to subdue one's inclination as Yitzchak surrendered his soul; Parashas HaMan — so one believe all his sustenance comes through divine providence; Parashas HaKorbanos — as we say at the end of Menachos: "This is the law of the chatas" — whoever engages in the law of chatas is as if he offered a chatas, etc.

And specifically as an individual, etc. — for initially they would read it publicly, as stated in the chapter on Tamid; and we say in the end of the first chapter of Berachos that they abolished [public reading] because of the heretics' resentment — they would say the rest of the Torah is not true — and that was in public; but for an individual there is no such resentment.
