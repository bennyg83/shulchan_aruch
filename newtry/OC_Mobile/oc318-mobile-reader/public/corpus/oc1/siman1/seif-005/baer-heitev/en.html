The passage of the Akeidah. It should be said before the passages of the offerings. The Akeidah and the manna may be recited even on Shabbos. Olas Tamid; Shtei Yados; Soles Belulah; Knesses HaGedolah. Rabbeinu Bachya on Parashas Tzav writes that merely reciting the passage is not enough; one must understand its explanation and recognize the wonders of Hashem. The statement that anyone who says Ashrei three times daily is assured a share in the World to Come has the same meaning.
And peace offerings. After the olah and shelamim passages, one should recite the passage of libations, since no such offering is brought without libations; a chatas and asham, however, have no libations. Magen Avraham asks why Beis Yosef places the olah before the chatas, when a chatas precedes an olah. His answer implies that one who knows he is liable to bring a chatas should recite the chatas passage first. Other poskim imply that the olah passage is always first.
In public. This is forbidden because sectarians claim that only the Ten Commandments constitute Torah. Accordingly, especially nowadays they should not be written on a public prayer sheet. Lechem Chamudos; Magen Avraham; Knesses HaGedolah; Responsa Beis Yaakov 155. Maharsha maintains that Rashba prohibited establishing them between the blessings like Krias Shema, but permits their public recitation before Baruch She'amar. We rule, however, that public recitation is entirely forbidden. An individual may recite them only before or after prayer, not among the blessings. Olas Tamid; Responsa Shevus Yaakov II:44.
