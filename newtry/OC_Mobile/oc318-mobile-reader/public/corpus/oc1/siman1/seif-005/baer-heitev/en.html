The passage of the Akedah. Before the passage of the offerings, and he may say the passage of the Akedah and the passage of the manna even on Shabbat. Olas Tamid, and likewise Baal Shetei Yadot and Solet Belulah and Knesset HaGedolah, see there. Bachye wrote on parashas Tzav that saying it alone is not enough unless he explains the passage and recognizes the wonders of Hashem; and likewise he explained what is written: whoever says Tehillah leDavid three times every day is assured that he is a child of the World to Come.

And peace-offerings. After olah and shelamim he should say the passage of the libations, for there is no sacrifice without libations. But chatat and asham do not require libations. Magen Avraham questioned why the Beit Yosef wrote to say olah, etc., then chatat, etc., for we rule that chatat precedes olah. He reconciled it, and from his answer he derived that when one knows he became liable for a chatat he should say chatat first, see there. And from the words of other poskim it appears that one should always say olah before chatat.

In public. Because of the heretics who said there is no Torah except this one, especially in our time; therefore they do not write them on the special communal prayer book. Levush Chadash, Magen Avraham, as Knesset HaGedolah wrote. And see Beit Yaakov, siman 255. Maharsha wrote that the Rashba forbids saying it in public only to establish it among the blessings like Kerias Shema, but before Baruch she'amar it is permitted even in public. We hold to forbid it in public entirely, and for an individual specifically before or after prayer; but to establish it within the blessings is forbidden even for an individual (Olas Tamid). And see Shevut Yaakov, part 2, siman 44.
