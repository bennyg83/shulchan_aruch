The passage of the olah extends from the beginning of Vayikra through “and if his offering is from the flock.” The passage of the minchah extends from “When a soul” through “you shall offer.” If one has time, he should also read in parashat Tzav from “This is the law of the minchah” through “shall become holy.” Shulchan Melachim. A kohen should add the verse, “Every minchah of a kohen shall be entirely burned.” The passage of the peace-offering extends from “If his offering is a peace-offering” through “and if it is from the flock”; in parashat Tzav, from “This is the law of the peace-offering” through “in the wilderness of Sinai.” This also includes the passage of the thanksgiving-offering. Afterward comes the passage of the libations in parashat Shelach, from “The Omnipresent spoke,” at “When you come,” through “with you.” The passage of the sin-offering in Vayikra extends from “If one soul sins” through “and he shall be forgiven.” If one has time, he should also read in parashat Tzav from “This is the law of the sin-offering” through “shall be burned in fire.” Shulchan Melachim. The passage of the guilt-offering in parashat Tzav extends from “This is the law of the guilt-offering” through “it is a guilt-offering.” If one has time, he should also read its passage in Vayikra. Ibid. One should also recite the passage of the variable offering, which is brought for an uttered oath, from “If a soul sins” through “it shall belong to the kohen like the minchah.” Magen Avraham. See Pri Megadim, who is uncertain whether the passage of the libations must be recited three times; for the olah, thanksgiving-offering, and peace-offering, each of which requires libations; or whether one recitation suffices. Arachot Chayim inclines to the latter view. It is proper to recite all these passages before prayer and in the synagogue. Arachot Chayim in the name of the Zohar. One must not, however, thereby miss the time for Keriat Shema or even communal prayer. One should confess before reciting the passages of the offerings. Arachot Chayim in the name of Eliyahu Rabbah and Pri Megadim. The confession should be recited while standing. In our time a work entitled Avodah Tamah has been printed, explaining every detail of the recitation of the offerings. It also copies from Rambam the order of offering that is needed for this purpose. The greatness of this mitzvah is already explained in the sacred works. How good it is for one who follows this practice first to study these matters from the Gemara or Rambam's works, so that he will subsequently understand what he says. It will then be considered as though he actually offered a sacrifice. Likewise, regarding what we recite each day, “Abaye arranged,” etc., and the preparation of the incense, it is a mitzvah to examine and understand what one is saying. See the extensive discussion by the author of Shelah in his glosses to Sefer Yesh Nochalin.
