And the parashah of the olah — from the beginning of Vayikra until "and if from the flock." The parashah of the minchah — from "and a soul" until "you shall offer." [If he has leisure he should also read in parashas Tzav from "and this is the law of the minchah" until "it shall be holy" (Shulchan Aruch).] The kohen adds the verse "and every minchah of a kohen shall be entirely burned." The parashah of shelamim — from "and if a shelamim offering" until "and if from the flock," and in parashas Tzav from "and this is the law of the shelamim" until "in the wilderness of Sinai" — which is also the parashah of thanksgiving; and afterward the parashah of libations in parashas Shelach from "and Heaven spoke" "when you come" until "to you." The parashah of chatas in Vayikra from "and if one soul sins" until "and it shall be forgiven him" (if he has leisure he should also read in parashas Tzav "and this is the law of the chatas" until "in fire it shall be burned" (Shulchan Aruch)). The parashah of asham in parashas Tzav from "and this is the law of the asham" until "it is an asham" (if he has leisure he should also read in Vayikra) (ibid.). And the parashah of olah and descending offering as well, which comes for a false oath — from "and if a soul sins" until "and it shall be for the kohen like a minchah" (Magen Avraham). See Peri Megadim, who is uncertain whether one must say the parashah of libations three times for olah, thanksgiving, and shelamim, since each requires libations, or whether for reading once suffices; and Erech HaShulchan leans this way. It is good to say all these parshiyos before prayer and in the synagogue (Erech HaShulchan in the name of the Zohar), provided he not thereby delay the time of Shema or even communal prayer. One must confess before reading the parashah of the offerings (Erech HaShulchan in the name of Arizal and Peri Megadim), and the confession should be standing. In our days a book Avodah Tamah has been printed explaining all matters of reciting the offerings in every detail; it also copied from the Rambam the order of offering that was needed for this. The greatness of this mitzvah is already explained in books; how good for one who practices this to learn these matters first from the Gemara or books of the Rambam so that afterward he understands what he says — and thereby it is considered for him as if he actually offered a korban. Likewise what we say daily: "Abaye would arrange," etc.; and matters of preparing the ketores — it is a mitzvah to look and understand what he says. See what the author of Shelah extended in his glosses on Sefer Yesh Nochalin.
