(13) Parashas HaAkedah — before the parshiyos of the sacrifices. One may say Parashas HaAkedah and Parashas HaMan even on Shabbos. Recitation alone is not enough; rather he should reflect on what he says and recognize Heaven's wonders. So too what they said in the Gemara: "Whoever says Tehillah leDavid three times every day is assured that he is a child of the World to Come" — likewise in this manner. The reason for saying all this: Parashas HaAkedah — to remember the merit of the Patriarchs every day, and also to subdue his inclination, as Yitzchak surrendered his soul. Parashas HaMan — so that he believe all his sustenance comes through individual divine providence, as written: "He who gathered much did not have too much, and he who gathered little did not have too little" — to teach that increasing effort is of no benefit. It is stated in Yerushalmi Berachos: whoever says Parashas HaMan is assured his sustenance will not diminish. The Ten Commandments — so that he remember every day the standing at Mount Sinai and strengthen his faith in Heaven. Parashas HaKorbanos — as we say in tractate Menachos: "This is the law of the chatas" — whoever engages in the law of chatas is as if he offered a chatas, etc.

(14) And shelamim — the same applies to Parashas Todah, for it too is included among shelamim. After olah, shelamim, and todah one says Parashas Nesachim, for there is no sacrifice without libations. But chatas and asham do not require libations.

(15) And chatas — see Magen Avraham, who wrote that if one knows he became liable for a chatas, he should say Parashas Chatas first. From the words of the other poskim it does not appear so.

(16) With the congregation — because of heretics who say there is no Torah except this [portion]. Especially in our time. Therefore they also do not write it on a pamphlet designated for the congregation. Some wrote that it is not forbidden to say it with the congregation, but only to fix it among the blessings like Kerias Shema; but before Baruch SheAmar it is permitted even with the congregation. We hold to forbid it with the congregation entirely. For an individual — specifically not within prayer; but to fix it within the blessings is forbidden even for an individual [Eshel Avraham and Ateres Zekenim].
