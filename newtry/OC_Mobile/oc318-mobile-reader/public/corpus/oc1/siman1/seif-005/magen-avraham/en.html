“The Binding.” Bachye, Parashas Tzav, writes that mere recitation is not enough; one must explain the passage and recognize Heaven’s wonders. So too he explains why one who recites Tehillah leDavid three times is assured of a share in the World to Come.

“Olah.” After olah, shelamim, and todah one says Parashas Nesachim—it is in the order Shelach lecha—for there is no sacrifice without libations; but chatas and asham do not require libations. Parashas Tzav es Aharon does not apply to olah but to the tamid offering, as stated in siman 69; and one says Parashas Vayikra, “If a soul shall sin,” at the end of Vayikra, which deals with a vain oath, witness oath, definite asham, and doubtful asham. I was asked: why did the Tur and Rabbeinu Yerucham in Shulchan Aruch list “olah … chatas,” for we hold (Zevachim ch. 10) that chatas precedes olah, and likewise Rashi, Parashas Tazria, as explained in Rosh. It appears to me: in Zevachim 7a, why does chatas come before olah? It is likened to an advocate who enters before the king—the advocate enters, the gift follows him—see there; Tosafos wrote that olah is the gift, even though chatas atones more, etc.—see there. The Gemara explains that chatas atones for deliberate violation of a positive command, and olah for failure to perform a positive command; nevertheless, when chatas is offered first, atonement has already been effected for kares-related and positive-command lapses, so olah is then a gift. But in our recitation the Tur wrote that one should not say “Yehi ratzon” after Parashas Chatas, lest one not be liable for a chatas and it be like reading Torah without obligation; and olah atones for a positive command, as the Gemara says: there is no Jew who is not obligated in some positive precept—so one says olah before it “atones,” as in Zevachim: there is no Jew without some positive obligation. But if one knows he is liable for chatas, he says chatas first.

“In public.” Because of heretics who say there is no Torah but this one—and especially in our time—therefore we do not write it on a pamphlet designated for the community (Taz 69).
