It is good to recite the order of the sacrifices specifically by day; only from the mishnah of Eizehu Mekoman may one recite before daybreak. And one should say the Ten Commandments in a loud voice to arouse the intention that one should juxtapose the Ten Commandments with Baruch she'amar, because there are ten instances of "baruch" until "Blessed are You, the Name, Father of mercy," corresponding to the Ten Commandments. And one should say the "Anokhi" of parashas Yisro in the morning and the "Anokhi" of parashas Ve'etchanan in the evening after the bedtime Shema (R' Yitzchak Luria).
