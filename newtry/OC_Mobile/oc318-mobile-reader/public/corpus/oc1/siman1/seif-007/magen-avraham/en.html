Yehi Ratzon. After the passage of a chatas one should not recite this prayer, because a chatas is not brought voluntarily, but only when one inadvertently violated a prohibition whose deliberate violation carries kares. Tur. Beit Yosef appears to maintain that one should likewise not recite it after the passage of an asham talui, because we rule like the Sages that an asham talui cannot be brought voluntarily. It is brought only when a piece of forbidden fat and a piece of permitted fat lay before him, he ate one, and he does not know which one. See Yoreh Deah, siman 5. The Tur writes that after the passage of an asham one should say Yehi Ratzon. Beit Yosef cites Maharai and Maharich, who write that an asham may also be brought in a manner resembling a voluntary offering, for Nedarim states that the pious would undertake nezirus in order to bring a korban. It would therefore appear that it can come like a voluntary offering. Their language plainly differs from that of Olas Shabbos. I am greatly astonished at these geonim. Nedarim 10a states that they undertook nezirus in order to bring a chatas; no asham is mentioned. Accordingly, Yehi Ratzon should be recited after a chatas, not after an asham. Moreover, Scripture explicitly states that a nazir brings a chatas, not an asham, unless he became tamei. Such an asham cannot be called voluntary, and is comparable to all chataos and ashamos brought because of sin. One cannot answer that the asham of a nazir differs because it can result from deliberate defilement, and thus a person can undertake nezirus, deliberately become tamei, and bring an asham, unlike other offerings brought only for inadvertent sin. If so, there would be no need to derive the principle from nezirus, because an asham for a designated maidservant, an asham for withholding property, and a variable offering are likewise brought for deliberate sins. Moreover, Heaven forbid that righteous people would deliberately become tamei and violate a negative commandment. In any event, a chatas should be considered capable of arising through a voluntary undertaking, since a nazir who remains tahor brings one. In the year 5428 I asked the great gaon Rabbi Yitzchak, av beis din of Poznan, and he argued with great acuity and breadth that a nazir's chatas does not come for atonement and therefore cannot be recited for another sin. A nazir's asham, by contrast, does come for atonement, and when its passage is recited it can atone for another sin. This does not appear consistent with Nedarim, where the commentator explains that the pious feared that they might sin and wished to bring an offering for atonement, and therefore undertook nezirus. This indicates that they attained atonement through the chatas. This is not the place to elaborate; see Rambam, Hilchos Nezirus, chapter 1. Bach writes in the name of Maharshal that after the passage of a chatas one should say, “If I became liable for a chatas, may it be Your will that this recitation be regarded as though I offered it.” This is difficult, because Shabbos 72b expressly teaches that a chatas requires prior knowledge, as the verse states, “or his sin becomes known to him.” Although Rashi at the end of 71a appears to imply otherwise, the conclusion requires prior knowledge, and Rashi's words require resolution. A conditional prayer is therefore ineffective if the person does not know that he sinned. It therefore appears proper that after the passage of an asham one should say, “Master of the Universe, if I became liable for an asham, may it be Your will,” because an asham does not require prior knowledge. After the passage of a chatas he should say nothing. In this manner the Tur's ruling accords with halachah. Shelah writes that on Shabbos and Yom Tov one should not say Yehi Ratzon, because a voluntary offering is not brought on those days. There is no harm, however, in reading the passages as Torah. A Torah scholar should preferably study the portion relevant to that day. See siman 107, seif 1. On Erev Pesach, Chol HaMoed Pesach, and Erev Yom Kippur, one should not recite the passage of the todah; see siman 51.
