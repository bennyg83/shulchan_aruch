“Maharam.” But after Parashas Chatas one should not say “Yehi ratzon” thus, because chatas does not come as a voluntary offering except when one inadvertently violated something whose deliberate violation carries kares (Tur). Rabbeinu Yerucham’s view seems to be that even after asham talui one should not say so, for we rule like the Sages that it does not come voluntarily except when a piece of fat and a piece of lean were before him, he ate one, and does not know which—and see Yoreh Deah siman 5. The Tur wrote that after asham one says “Yehi ratzon,” and Beis Yosef cites Mahar”i and Mahar”i”ch that asham too can come voluntarily, since Nedarim says the pious would vow nezirus to bring a korban—so it would seem it should apply like a nedavah—his words imply not like the Magen Avraham. I was greatly astonished at the geonim: in Nedarim 10a they volunteered nezirus to bring a chatas, and asham is not mentioned there—so “Yehi ratzon” would fit chatas, not asham. Moreover Scripture is explicit that a nazir brings chatas, not asham, unless he became tamei—then it is not called a nedavah and is like all chataos and ashamos for sin. One might answer that nazir’s asham is for deliberate sin (Kerisus ch. 2), so one could vow nezirus, become tamei deliberately, and bring asham—unlike other korbanos that come only for inadvertence—but that fails, for asham of a defamed maidservant, asham of deposit, and chatas of one who must bring sliding-scale offerings also come for deliberate sin; Heaven forbid that the righteous become tamei on purpose and violate a negative command; and in any case chatas is not called “coming voluntarily” when a tahor nazir brings it. I asked the great gaon R. Yitzchok, av beis din of Poznan, 5368; he argued sharply and learnedly that nazir’s chatas is not for atonement, so one cannot say it over another sin—unlike nazir’s asham, which is for atonement, and saying it would imply atonement for another sin—and Nedarim with the commentator’s explanation does not read that way: they feared sin and wished to bring a korban for atonement and therefore vowed nezirus—hence they were atoned by that chatas; no room to expand—see Rambam, Nezirus ch. 1. Bach cites Shlah that after chatas one says “If I became liable for chatas, may it be accepted,” etc.—but I find this difficult, for Shabbos 72b is clear that chatas requires prior knowledge, as it says “or his sin becomes known to him”—and though Rashi end of 71a suggests no prior knowledge from “this is a ram,” etc., the halachah requires knowledge and Rashi needs resolution—so a condition does not help if he does not know he sinned. Therefore my practice: after Parashas Asham say “Master of the Universe, if I became liable for asham, may it be accepted,” etc., for asham does not require prior knowledge; after chatas say nothing. Thus the Tur aligns with halachah. Shelah writes that on Shabbos and Yom Tov one does not say “Yehi ratzon,” for voluntary korban is not brought then; the parshiyos may still be said like Torah reading, and if he is a scholar it is better he study Yoma—see siman 107:1 and Magen Avraham and Eliyah Rabbah; therefore one does not say Parashas Todah—see Magen Avraham there.
