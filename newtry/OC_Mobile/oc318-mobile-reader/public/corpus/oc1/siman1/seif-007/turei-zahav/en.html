That they come as voluntary offerings — unlike chatas, which comes only if one violated a matter requiring chatas; the same for asham — although we find asham talui, and that a person can bring himself to nezirus and afterward bring asham — nevertheless an ordinary asham does not come voluntarily. I saw in the name of Maharshal that one may say "Yehi ratzon" even after Parashas Chatas and Asham, only in conditional language — "May it be Your will: if I violated a matter requiring chatas, let this be considered as if I offered a chatas." In my humble opinion this is not so, for we find in many places in the Gemara that one brings an olah conditionally — if obligated it is an olah, if not it is a voluntary olah, as in the chapter "A man who betrothes," in the passage "We learned there: an animal found beyond Migdal Eder," etc. — but we do not find this for chatas, since chatas does not come voluntarily: how can one say "if I am not liable for chatas let this be a nedavah"? A chatas act cannot be a nedavah at all — a nedavah act is entirely different from a chatas act. So here too: if he is liable for chatas and the reading counts as chatas — as if he actually performed chatas per its law — how can he say "if I am not liable let it be nedavah," when that reading was already done as the law of chatas in all details read in Torah? Therefore one should not say it. I also saw a convincing answer to those who say [Yehi ratzon] before Parashas HaTamid, Mishnayos Eizehu Makoman, and "May our lips compensate" — in my opinion one should protest, for this implies about everything said afterward; also chatas laws are mentioned there, and one cannot offer "peace" bulls as nedavah lest he not be liable and it be like offering non-sacred animals in the Temple — unless he says "May our lips compensate" in place of the tamid service, as above.
