As a voluntary offering. The Mechaber's wording implies that after the chatas and asham passages one does not say the yehi ratzon, because these offerings cannot be voluntary; this applies even to asham talui, as in Yoreh De'ah 5 and Shach 7. Bach and Nachalas Tzvi cite Maharshal that one should use a conditional formula. Magen Avraham and Taz question this. Magen Avraham concludes that after the asham passage one says the condition, because an asham does not require prior definite knowledge, but after chatas one says nothing. Olas Tamid, Eliyahu Zuta, Bnei Chayei, and Yad Aharon all follow Maharshal: one says, 'If I am liable to bring a chatas, may this be considered in its place; otherwise, may it be considered Torah study.' See Zevach Shelamim 1. Emunas Shmuel 53 cites Shelah that on Shabbos and Yom Tov one should not say the yehi ratzon, since a voluntary offering is not brought then, though the passages may be recited as Torah study; a Torah scholar should preferably study the daily portion. Soles Belulah says not to recite the other offerings even as Torah study. Be'er Heitev copied that earlier, though the author does not know its reason.
