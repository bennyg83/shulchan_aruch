“When he concludes.” After the passages of the chattat and asham offerings, he certainly should not recite the prayer that the reading be regarded as an offering, because the recitation takes the place of an offering and he may not be liable to bring a chattat or asham. Nevertheless, he should recite the passages of the chattat and asham even though he cannot say the Yehi Ratzon afterward. Reading those passages provides some atonement if he committed a sin for which he would be liable to bring those offerings. Even if he does not know whether he sinned, he should recite them conditionally: if he sinned, the reading atones for him; if he did not, he receives reward for studying Torah. After the passage of an asham that may be offered voluntarily, such as an asham talui or the asham of a nazir, he may recite the prayer. Beit Yosef. Some say he may also form a condition after the passages of a definite chattat and asham, saying: “Master of the worlds, if I am liable to bring a chattat, may it be Your will that this be accepted,” etc. Maharshal and my teacher in Bach. Before Eizehu Mekoman one should not say, “So will we render bulls with our lips,” because this cannot properly refer to a chattat: perhaps he is not liable to bring one, and it would resemble bringing a non-sacred animal into the Temple courtyard. Rather, he should say, “So will we render bulls in place of the service of the Tamid.” One who regularly reads the passages mentioned above or Tehillim must first recite Birkat HaTorah. It is good to recite Tehillim specifically before prayer and not after prayer. Maharam MiLublin. Before leaving the entrance of his home in the morning, he should recite the verse “Hear, Israel.” A kabbalistic tradition in the name of Rabbi Yehudah HaChasid. It is good to give or designate one small coin for charity before every prayer and to say, “As for me, through righteousness I shall behold Your face.” He should intend that this coin redeems his prayer from every accuser. Charity rises in place of the accuser, who becomes satisfied with his portion like the goat sent away on Yom Kippur. When giving the coin, he should have in mind the combined divine name Y-A-H-D-O-N-H-Y: the giver corresponds to the name Hashem in the mystical attribute of yesod, the recipient corresponds to the name Adonai, and through the act the two names are unified. He should say aloud, “For the sake of the unification of the Holy One, blessed is He, and His Shechinah,” because supernal holiness is aroused through speech and action, as explained in parashat Bechukotai. He should give charity generously, for charity has many virtues. Totze'ot Chayim. One who rises from study to pray must wash his hands before praying. Maharil. When he enters the synagogue, before anything else he should bow toward the holy ark until all the vertebrae of his spine move. He should recall his lowly condition and the greatness of his Creator, as expressed by the verse, “I shall bow toward Your holy sanctuary in awe of You”; that is, with great awe aroused by reverence. Emek HaBerachah, page 26. He should begin with the verse, “As for me, through Your abundant kindness,” and not with Mah Tovu, which was said by Bilam. The verse “As for me, may my prayer to You, Hashem, be at a favorable time” should likewise be omitted, because it is to be said only in the place established by the Sages, namely, during Minchah on Shabbat, in accordance with the Midrash cited by the Tur. Maharshal, siman 64; Shevil Emunah, path 4.
