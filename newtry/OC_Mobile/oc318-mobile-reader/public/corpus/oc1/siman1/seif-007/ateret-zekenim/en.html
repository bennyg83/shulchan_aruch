When he finishes: After the passages of chatat and asham he certainly should not say Yehi ratzon, because the statement is in the place of the offerings and perhaps he is not liable for a chatat and asham; nevertheless he may recite the passages of chatat and asham even though he cannot say Yehi ratzon afterward, since reciting the passage of chatat and asham atones somewhat if he sinned a sin for which one is liable to bring a chatat and asham. Even if he does not know that he sinned, he may read it out of doubt, for if he sinned it atones for him, and if he did not sin they give him reward as one who reads in the Torah. But after a voluntary asham, such as a outstanding asham or a nazir's asham, he may say it (Beit Yosef). And some say that he may intend to say it even after a definite asham and chatat, only that he should say it conditionally: "Master of the universe, if I am liable for a sin, may it be Your will that it be accepted," etc. (Maharshal and my teacher in the Bach). One should not say before Eizehu Mekoman, "May the offering of our lips be accepted," regarding chatat, because it is not applicable, lest he is not liable for a chatat and it would be like bringing non-sacred animals into the Temple courtyard. Rather he should say thus: "May the offering of our lips be accepted in place of the continual offering service." One accustomed to recite the passages mentioned above or Tehillim must first bless Birkas HaTorah, and it is good to say Tehillim specifically before prayer and not after prayer (Maharam of Rothenburg). And before leaving his doorway in the morning he should say the verse Shema Yisrael (Kabbalah in the name of R' Yehuda heChasid). It is good to give or set aside one perutah before every prayer for charity and to say, "I will behold Your face in righteousness." He should intend with that perutah that his prayer is redeemed from every prosecutor, for charity ascends in the place of a prosecutor and he rejoices in his portion like the matter of the goat of Yom Kippur. He should intend when giving the perutah in the name Y-H-V-H, which is the Giver in the secret of Y-D-V-D, and the receiver in the name Adonai, and unify the Name mentioned, and say with his mouth for the unification of the Holy One, blessed be He, and His Shechinah, for the supernal holiness is aroused by speech and action, as explained in parashas Bechukosai. He should increase giving charity, for charity has many virtues (Toledot Chaim). One who stands from study to prayer must wash his hands before praying (Maharil). When he comes to the house of prayer, before anything else he should bow to the holy ark until all the joints of his spine crack, and remember in his heart the lowliness of his station and the greatness of his Creator, as David said, "I will bow toward Your holy Temple in fear of You," meaning in great fear with great arousal (Emek HaBrachah, page 26). He should begin with the verse, "But I, through Your abundant kindness," and not "How goodly," which Balaam said; and also the verse, "But I, with prayer to You, Heaven, at a time of favor," should be skipped, for it is said only where the Sages established, namely on Shabbat at Minchah per the midrash, as the Tur wrote (Maharshal, siman 64, Shvil Emunah, Netiv Dalet).
