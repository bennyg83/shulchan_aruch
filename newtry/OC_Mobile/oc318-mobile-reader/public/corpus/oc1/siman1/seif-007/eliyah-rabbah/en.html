[7] The Ten Commandments, etc. In Eliyahu Zuta I wrote: Rashbatz was accustomed to recite them before Baruch She'amar because there are ten instances of "baruch" up to "Baruch Atah HaEl." His grandfather (meaning Rashbatz's grandfather; Gan Neta erred in my words — see there) was accustomed to say the first parashah of Anochi in the morning and the second in Parashat Ve'etchanan in the evening after Kerias Shema upon his bed — up to here. The students of the Ari z"l wrote that he would say every day four remembrances: of the giving of the Torah, of wiping out Amalek, of Miriam who became a metzora — as written, Remember what Hashem your God did to Miriam, etc. — and also remembrance of the Exodus from Egypt — up to here. The Shelah wrote on page 128: what is found in our siddurim — the arranged petition after the 72 verses concluding with Baruch Atah Hashem shomei'a tefillah, and likewise some other petitions printed elsewhere — is an error, for this blessing is not mentioned in the Talmud and poskim, and one who recites it recites a blessing in vain. Rather one should say: Blessed are You Who hears prayer — without mentioning the Name — up to here. See below siman 46 seif 7 and Taz end of siman 131.
