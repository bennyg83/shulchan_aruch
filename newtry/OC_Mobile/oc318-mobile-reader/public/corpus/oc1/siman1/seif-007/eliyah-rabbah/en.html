[7] The Ten Commandments, etc. In Eliyahu Zuta I wrote that Maharshal was accustomed to recite them before Baruch She'amar, because the word “blessed” appears ten times from the beginning until “Blessed are You, God.” His grandfather, meaning Maharshal's grandfather, was accustomed to recite the first passage of the Ten Commandments in the morning and the second passage, in Parashas Va'eschanan, at night after reciting Krias Shema upon his bed. Gan Nata misunderstood my words concerning his grandfather; see there. The students of the Arizal write that he recited four remembrances every day: the giving of the Torah, the eradication of Amalek, the remembrance of Miriam becoming a metzora, as it is written, “Remember what Hashem your God did to Miriam,” and the remembrance of the Exodus from Egypt. Shelah, page 128, writes that the petition arranged after the seventy-two verses in our siddurim, which concludes with the blessing, “Blessed are You, Hashem, Who hears prayer,” is printed in error. The same applies to several other supplications and petitions printed elsewhere with such conclusions. This blessing is not mentioned in the Gemara or poskim, and one who recites it pronounces a blessing in vain. Rather, he should say, “Blessed are You, Who hears prayer,” without mentioning the Divine Name. See below, siman 46, seif 7, and Taz at the end of siman 131.
