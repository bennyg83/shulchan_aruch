"They [these portions]." — Taz: The Mechaber's view is that after Asham one does not recite [the passage], for it is not brought as a nedavah; the halacha is not like Rabbi Elazar in Kerisos (25a) but like the Rabbis — an Asham Taluy is not a nedavah, nor a Nazir's Asham — Magen Avraham 11 on Maharshal; Bach resolved it in Eshel Avraham 13 as a conditional — "let it be as if he read Torah" — and Tashbetz agreed, responsum 9. Eshel Avraham wrote there: if it is known that he sinned, he should first say "I have sinned, I have perverted, I have rebelled," and afterward recite Parashas Chatas and Asham and Yehi Ratzon; and it is true as Rambam wrote in Hilchos Teshuvah that a sacrifice without confession does not atone, as the Torah says, and confession is "I have sinned," etc., there ch. 1–2, and confession standing — see Tur, Magen Avraham 4. What he wrote about "May our lips compensate" applies likewise to Asham — exclude it from the category — the Darishah wrote one should say Yehi Ratzon "as if I had offered," etc., after Eizehu Makoman, and so his teacher would say; see siman 50, Bach on incense, etc.
