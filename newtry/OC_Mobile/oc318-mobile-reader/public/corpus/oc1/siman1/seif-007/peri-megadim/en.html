That they are, etc. See Taz. The Mechaber's view is that after the passage of an asham one should not recite Yehi Ratzon, because an asham cannot be brought voluntarily. The halachah does not follow Rabbi Eliezer in Kerisos 25a, but the Sages, who maintain that an asham talui cannot be brought voluntarily. Regarding the asham of a nazir, see Magen Avraham, seif katan 11, and what he writes concerning Maharshal. Bach resolves the matter by saying the prayer conditionally. Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 13, writes that one may stipulate that, if he is not obligated, the recitation should be regarded merely as reading Torah. Tevuos Shor, seif katan 9, agrees. Eliyah Rabbah further writes there that if a person knows that he sinned, he should first say, “I have sinned, I have acted perversely, I have rebelled,” and afterward recite the passages of the chatas and asham and the Yehi Ratzon. This is correct, for Rambam writes in the first chapter of Hilchos Teshuvah that an offering without confession does not atone, as the Torah states. The confession is, “I have sinned,” etc. See there, chapters 1 and 2. Confession must be recited while standing; see siman 607 and Magen Avraham, seif katan 4. Regarding the phrase, “and let our lips compensate for bulls,” the same principle applies to an asham, which should not be excluded. Derishah writes that after Eizehu Mekoman one should say, “May it be Your will that it be regarded as though I offered,” and so his teacher was accustomed to say. See siman 50 and Bach concerning the incense, etc.
