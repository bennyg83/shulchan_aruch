(18) They come as voluntary offerings — but after Parashas Chatas one should not say so, because chatas does not come as a voluntary offering unless one violated a matter for which one is liable for chatas. The same for asham. For asham talui we hold it does not come as a voluntary offering. The latter authorities wrote in the name of Shlah that one may say "Yehi ratzon" even after chatas and asham, only in conditional language, namely: "May it be Your will before You: if I violated a matter for which one is liable for chatas, let this be considered as if I offered a chatas"; and if not, it is like reading Torah. In this manner one says likewise after Parashas Asham. But Magen Avraham raised a difficulty: how can one say "if I became liable for chatas"? For we hold regarding chatas that it requires prior knowledge that one sinned! Therefore Magen Avraham concluded: regarding chatas one should not say "Yehi ratzon" at all; only regarding definite asham and asham talui one may say this "Yehi ratzon" conditionally. But if his heart troubles him lest he sinned and he had knowledge that he sinned and forgot, he may also say "Yehi ratzon" after chatas conditionally [Shaarei Teshuvah].
