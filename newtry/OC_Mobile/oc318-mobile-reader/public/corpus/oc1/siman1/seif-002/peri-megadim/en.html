“Let him be.” — **Taz:** It is difficult for him: it should read “Let him awaken the dawn,” for the **Tur** writes, “Let not your inclination overcome you (right shin)—how will you stand a great cold or if you are not sated from your sleep—and it argues with you—not [to rise] before dawn, for in summer [the debate is] after dawn and in winter thus.” “Let him awaken the dawn” is a separate idea—that a person should accustom himself to light foods and the like and not sleep much, for in sleep there is no “prevailing.” Therefore it is answered homiletically: the word shachar is expounded in the **Zohar**, Vayikra 11d, on Krias Shema—see there. Every person must first awaken himself, and then Heaven helps, for “not by his strength alone shall a man prevail,” as said; **David** praised himself that afterward he needed no help from Heaven—rather he “awakens the dawn,” giving arousal, joy, and abundance above, and Krias Shema does not awaken him to help even afterward. Accordingly it all fits one theme: he must prevail through his own arousal—“let him awaken”—not that arousal begins from Heaven; yet ultimately arousal needs help from above, for not everyone is David. Hence the **Mechaber** omitted “and Krias Shema does not awaken him.” A printer thought the initials kuf-lamed-yud stood for “to the holiness of Israel,” which is an error—it should mean, as it were, “to Knesses Yisrael,” or it was written kuf-lamed-yud “to Knesses Yisrael,” and the printer reversed it—understand. “I will awaken the dawn” (Tehillim 57:9; 108:3)—he rose at midnight; according to the homily it works—see **Rif** Berachos ch. 1, **Ein Yaakov**. What the **Rema** wrote in the gloss—even so he should not delay when the congregation prays—means that even if prayer time has not passed, it is still a mitzvah to [pray] with the congregation; and he changed the **Tur**’s wording from “the time of prayer that is the appointed time for every living being.”
