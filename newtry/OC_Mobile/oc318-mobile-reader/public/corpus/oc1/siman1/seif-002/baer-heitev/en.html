And at the end of the night. This is the time of "watchers for the morning," even in summer. If he is close to sunrise he should say the kinah first and afterward begin "watchers for the morning," which is the way of wisdom. Regarding the midnight prayer, see Sefer HaKavanot and the Zohar in Vayakhel, which implies that we always count the night as twelve hours, whether in summer or winter, although for prayer it is not so; see siman 233, Magen Avraham, and see Olas Tamid. The later authorities received that "where I lay down" until "when I arise" means from the oaths that they say in Ruth: "where I lay down" until the twelfth, "when I arise," he need not rise to study at night. Some say from the fifteenth of Av until the fifteenth of Nisan he should rise; and some say the time of sleep is eight hours, and the verse "I would have slept, then I would have been at rest" applies to him, and everything is according to what kind of person he is. The Ari wrote that one should recite mishnayot before everything, and thereby he merits the soul, for mishnah shares letters with neshamah. If he merits true wisdom, that time is especially auspicious.
