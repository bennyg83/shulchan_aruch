If it is close to sunrise, he should first recite the lament and afterward begin “those who watch for the morning.” Derech Chochmah. Regarding Tikkun Chatzos, see Sefer HaKavanos and the Zohar, Parashas Vayakhel. It appears there that the night is always calculated as twelve hours, whether in summer or winter, although this is not so regarding the times of prayer. See siman 233, Magen Avraham, and see Olas Tamid. The later authorities received a tradition concerning the period “from lying down until arising.” Meaning, from Shavuos, when Rus is read and the word “lie down” appears, until Tishah B’Av, when “arise” appears, one need not rise at night to study. Some say that one should rise from the fifteenth of Av until the fifteenth of Iyar. Others say that the proper amount of sleep is eight hours, alluded to by the verse, “I lay down, then I shall rest.” Everything depends upon the individual. The Arizal writes that a set amount of Mishnayos should be studied before anything else. Through this one merits a neshamah, for the letters of Mishnah are the same as those of neshamah. If one has merited the wisdom of truth, that time is especially auspicious for its study.
