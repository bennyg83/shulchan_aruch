“One who rises early.” The Kabbalists expanded at length on midnight prayer; Rekanti, Parashas Bereishis, wrote as follows: Some say the time for pleading mercy is the end of the night; I rule that at midnight it is fitting to plead for mercy upon the Community of Israel and her children, etc., and at the end of the night one should ask for one’s personal needs—and this request should be with prostration (nefilas apayim)—see siman 131:2. In our Gemara, the first explanation, which gave a sign for the middle of the “middle”—i.e., midnight—implies that it is a time of Divine favor; the second explanation does not read that way. See Zohar, Vayakhel, p. 242: we always reckon the night as twelve hours, summer and winter, even though for prayer it is not so, as stated in siman 233; likewise Shaarei Tziyun.
