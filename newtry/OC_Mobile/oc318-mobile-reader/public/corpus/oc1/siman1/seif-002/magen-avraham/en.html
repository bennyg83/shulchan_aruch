One who rises early. The kabbalists discuss Tikkun Chatzos at length. Rekanati on Parashas Bereishis writes: “Some say that the time for requesting mercy is at the end of the night. I rule that at midnight it is proper to request mercy for the Congregation of Israel and her children, and at the end of the night he should request his personal needs. This latter request requires nefilas apayim.” See siman 131, seif 2. According to the first answer in our Gemara, which gives a sign for the middle of the middle watch, namely midnight, it appears that midnight is a time of favor. The second answer does not imply this. See Zohar, Vayakhel, page 342, that the night is always reckoned as twelve hours, in both summer and winter, even though the rule is different regarding prayer times, as stated in siman 233. Shaarei Zion writes likewise.
