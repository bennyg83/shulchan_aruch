The dawn. See Be'er Heitev. Birkei Yosef writes that this statement does not appear in the editions of the Zohar before us. It does appear, however, on folio 10 of a manuscript Zohar, which states that for this one incurs death at the hands of Heaven. He writes that one must wash his hands even if he does not intend to rise. If the water is far away and no one can bring it to him, some conscientious people customarily walk in increments of less than four amos. This does not appear correct to me; it is preferable to run, so as not to prolong the presence of the evil spirit resting on his hands. Responsa Shevus Yaakov, volume 3, siman 8, writes that people are not careful to avoid walking four amos before washing. One can say that they rely upon Rashba's view in Berachos that an entire house is considered like four amos. It is obvious that this may be relied upon only in pressing circumstances. See Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 4, and below, siman 4, for what I wrote concerning this.
