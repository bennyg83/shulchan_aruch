"I have set Heaven before me always" — see Baer Heitev. I saw in my writings in Dubno, the rabbi, the pious divine R. Zalman Lokover, z"l, about what they used to do: menorahs of parchment with drawings to place in siddurim, and they write on them "Shivisi Heaven," etc., four letters and other names; and such a menorah was called by the name Shivisi. The reason is so that one would remember not to speak idle talk during prayer, from the moment Heaven is before his eyes. He wrote on this in the name of his uncle, the Arizal, the gaon, author of Tevuos Shor, who said in his approbation metaphorically: if one did not say Shivisi and was silent — meaning he could restrain himself even without Shivisi — he was very indignant about this, because mostly they do not preserve it properly, and it would be spilled at every crossroads. And as they said in Rosh Hashana 18b, that for Batlit adkreta from a triya they made a festival — therefore here too it is fitting to gird strength to abolish the custom. He extended in pleasant words. And it appears that regarding large menorahs drawn on parchment that they fix in the synagogue before the pillar or other places where they fix it under a glass plate — we have no concern with this, and he was not strict only on those placed in siddurim for the reason above; and also mostly it comes to erasure of the Name, as our eyes see they go to loss and erasure, and in particular they write on them names not per halacha. May the warners and the heedful be enlightened like the brightness of the sky.
