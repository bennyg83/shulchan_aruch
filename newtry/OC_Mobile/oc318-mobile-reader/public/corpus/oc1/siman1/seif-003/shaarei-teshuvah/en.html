"I have set Hashem before me." See Be'er Heitev. I saw a writing on this subject by the pious and godly Rabbi Zalman of Lokvar concerning the custom of making illustrated parchment menorahs for placement in siddurim, inscribing upon them "I have set Hashem," the four-letter Name, and other Divine Names. Such a menorah was called a "Shivisi." Its purpose was to remind a person not to engage in idle talk during prayer, out of awe of the Name before his eyes. He cites his uncle, my ancestor, the gaon and author of Tevuos Shor, who wittily said, "Set it aside; even without the Shivisi, I have silenced myself"; meaning that a person can guard himself and remain silent even without the Shivisi. He protested the practice strongly, because most people do not protect these sheets properly and they are cast about at the head of every street. This resembles the account in Rosh Hashanah 18b, where the day on which mention of the Divine Name was removed from legal documents was made a festival. One should therefore gather strength to abolish this custom. He discusses the matter at length and pleasantly. It appears that there is no concern with large parchment menorahs affixed in a synagogue before the lectern, or elsewhere beneath a glass panel. His objection concerns only those placed in siddurim, for the reason stated above and because they generally lead to erasure of the Name. We see with our own eyes that they are lost and erased, especially when Names are written upon them improperly. The one who warns and the one who heeds the warning will shine like the radiance of the firmament.
