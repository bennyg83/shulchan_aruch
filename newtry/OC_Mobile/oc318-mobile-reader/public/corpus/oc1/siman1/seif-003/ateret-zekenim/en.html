“It is proper.” Every person should share Rachel's sorrow and weep each night over the exile of the Shechinah, which corresponds to Rachel, and over the destruction of the Temple, as indicated by the verse, “Arise, cry out in the night”; see there. Afterward he should study Torah until dawn. If he cannot, he should perform the tikkun, sleep, and then rise before dawn. Lechem Min HaShamayim. Being among the first ten people in the synagogue effects a great tikkun, and the first person to arrive at the synagogue attains the level of a righteous person. Zohar, parashat Terumah. A person must accept upon himself the positive mitzvah “You shall love your fellow as yourself,” because this causes his prayer to be joined with all Israel. This applies especially to love among companions. Every person should include himself with the group, as one companion among the others. If a companion is in distress, he should pray for him; if the companion is ill or suffering, Heaven forbid, he should share his pain. One must also recite every detail of the confessions even if he personally did not commit the sin. The confession must therefore be in the plural (“we have sinned”) and not “I have sinned,” because all Israel constitutes one body in the mystical pattern of Adam. This is the secret of the mutual responsibility of souls. Sefer HaKavanot of the Arizal.
