It is proper. Each person must share in Rachel's sorrow and weep every night over the exile of the Shechinah, which is in the aspect of Rachel, and over the destruction of the Beit HaMikdash, as it is written, "Arise, cry out in the night," see there; and afterward he should engage in Torah until the pillar of dawn rises. If he cannot, he should perform tikkun and sleep, and afterward rise before the pillar of dawn (Lechem min haShamayim). Whoever is among the first ten in the synagogue has a great tikkun, and whoever is first in the house of prayer is on the level of a tzaddik (Zohar, parashas Terumah). He must accept upon himself the positive mitzvah of "and you shall love your neighbor as yourself," for thereby his prayer will be included with all Israel, and in particular the love of companions; and each person must include himself, for he is one companion among the companions. If a companion is in distress he should pray for him, or if he is ill and in pain, God forbid, he should share in his sorrow. He must also say all the details of the confessions; although he has no particular sin, he must nevertheless say in the rabbinic wording, "We have sinned" and not "I have sinned," for all Israel is one body in the secret of Adam, for this is the sum of the guarantee of souls (Sefer HaKavanot of the Ari).
