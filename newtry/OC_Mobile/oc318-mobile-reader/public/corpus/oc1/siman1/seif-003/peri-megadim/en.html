“Good.” — **Taz:** **Magen Avraham** 7—indeed so for the **Taz** as well: one who shortens cannot increase, like a poor man with a sacrifice; but the words “provided he intends for Heaven” (Shamayim) on their face address one who adds, for it is obvious that one who shortens must intend. For the **Taz** it fits better on “depends” (smichah): the meaning is “provided that what he shortens is for Heaven so that he can intend well”—hence **Netilas Yadayim** tastes bad when there is abundance without intention. One must examine what “a little good” is, which implies that much without intention is also somewhat good, and “this is better”—for “unintentionally you did not call Me, Yaakov” (Yeshayahu 43:22). For the **Taz** it fits the **Tur**’s wording, but not the **Mechaber**’s—understand.
