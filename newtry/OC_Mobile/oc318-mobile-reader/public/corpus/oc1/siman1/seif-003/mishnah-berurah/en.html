(9) It is fitting for everyone, etc. — and the Kabbalists greatly expanded on the greatness of rising at midnight, for it is great. The order of conduct per the Ari's writings, z"l, has already been printed in siddurim. The time of midnight is always exactly the middle of the night in every place, even on long or short nights. It is twelve hours after midday. The time to mourn the destruction is better a little before midnight; from midnight onward he should engage in Torah; at the end of the night he should ask for his needs. Some say then [one recites] "They who stand watch for the morning." If he is close to sunrise, he should say only the psalms and skip the kinos, and afterward say "They who stand watch for the morning." After tikkun chatzot, a measure of Mishnayos comes before everything. If he has merited the wisdom of truth, that time is very auspicious. The latter authorities accepted: from "Lie down" until "Arise" — that is, from Shavuos when they say "Lie down" until Tisha BeAv when they say "Arise" — one need not rise at night. Some say: from 15 Av until 15 Iyar one should rise. Those who practice according to Kabbalah hold that one must always rise. A group that joined together to say tikkun chatzot in public — one should not prevent them; on the contrary, "in the multitude of people is the King's glory." It is better that they say the tikkun in the synagogue than in a room adjacent to it, for a synagogue is holier; also they should be among the first ten. One must be especially very careful about idle conversation during their rising at night. One who cannot rise before daylight, because he is weak by nature, or who knows that if he rises at the watch he will sleep during prayer — it is better that he sleep as much as needed. Nevertheless he should be very careful to rise an hour, or at least half an hour, before the shamash calls people to the synagogue, so that he can prepare himself to pray with the congregation and with cleanliness.

(10) That he be distressed — but Torah and prayer should be with joy.

(11) The destruction of the Temple — the Shelah wrote that at every meal one should say "By the rivers of Babylon"; on Shabbos, and likewise on days when tachanun is not said, one should say "A song of ascents: When Heaven restores the captivity of Zion," etc. The main thing is that he know what he is saying; after heartfelt intent, the words follow.
