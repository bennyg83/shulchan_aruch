(9) It is proper for every person, etc. The kabbalists wrote at great length about the immense virtue of rising at midnight. The appropriate order of conduct, based on the writings of the Arizal, has already been printed in the siddurim. Midnight is always the exact midpoint of the night in every location, whether the nights are long or short, and it is twelve seasonal hours after midday. The most fitting time to mourn the destruction is shortly before midnight. From midnight onward one should engage in Torah, and near the end of the night he should request his needs. Some then recite Shomrim LaBoker. If sunrise is near, he should recite only the psalms, omit the lamentations, and afterward say Shomrim LaBoker. After Tikkun Chatzos, study of Mishnah takes precedence over every other subject. If one has merited knowledge of the hidden Torah, that time is especially suited for it. The later authorities received a tradition that from the period of “Lie down” until “Arise,” meaning from Shavuos, when the haftarah includes “Lie down,” until Tisha B'Av, when it includes “Arise,” one need not rise during the night. Others say that one should rise from the fifteenth of Av until the fifteenth of Iyar. Those who follow kabbalistic practice hold that one should rise throughout the year. A group that gathers to recite Tikkun Chatzos communally should not be prevented. On the contrary, “In a multitude of people is the glory of the King.” It is preferable that they recite the Tikkun in the synagogue rather than in an adjoining room, because the synagogue has greater sanctity and they will also be among the first ten. One must be especially careful to avoid mundane conversation while awake at night. A person who cannot rise before dawn because he is naturally weak, or who knows that rising during the night watch will cause him to sleep during prayer, should instead sleep as much as he needs. Nevertheless, he should be very careful to rise one hour, or at least half an hour, before the attendant calls the congregation to the synagogue, so that he can prepare himself to pray with the congregation in cleanliness.
(10) That he should grieve. Torah study and prayer, however, should be performed with joy.
(11) The destruction of the Beis HaMikdash. The Shelah writes that at every meal one should recite “By the rivers of Babylon.” On Shabbos, and on days when Tachanun is not recited, one should say “A Song of Ascents, when Hashem returns,” etc. The essential matter is to understand what one is saying, for the words follow the intent of the heart.
