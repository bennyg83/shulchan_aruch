(There subparagraph 11.) The Tur writes: after Asham one says Yehi Ratzon. See Niddah 70a—because one brings kodshim to an invalid location—and see Rashi; this applies only when sacrifices were actually brought and leftovers remained. For mere recitation, the Sages too agree one may stipulate on an asham conditionally—if not, it is a nedavah. With this the Tur’s words are settled; many great scholars stumbled on the question that asham does not come as nedavah. What seems implied from Tosafos Kiddushin 55a s.v. dilma—that one cannot stipulate—offers no proof: Tosafos’ remark that voluntary libations are for individuals only rebuts a baraisa against Shmuel, who holds thus in Zevachim 91a; but for practical halachah we do not follow Shmuel—voluntary libations also use basins; likewise Rambam Hilchos Maaseh HaKorbanos 16:14—see there. Yet Rambam Hilchos Pesulei HaMukdashin 6:18 is puzzling: he writes that if one finds a male two-year-old ram there is no remedy—perhaps it is an asham whose owner has not yet atoned—cannot one bring it conditionally as Tosafos asked? Needs study.
