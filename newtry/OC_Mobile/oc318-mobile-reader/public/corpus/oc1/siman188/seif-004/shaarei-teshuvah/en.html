In Shulchan Aruch in the gloss: and some say we recite etc.—see Ateret Zekenim; and see Beit Yosef who wrote because it is written "I will return to Yerushalayim in mercy" etc. Seif 7 in Shulchan Aruch: if he erred etc. And Rabbeinu Yonah wrote: if he forgot retzeh and began to say Baruch Atah and remembered and finished lamdeni chukekha—even per what Ritva wrote in his novellae per the opinion of some poskim that l'chatchila one should not finish lamdeni chukekha but should finish boneh Yerushalayim and say Baruch asher natan etc.—nevertheless here since he already said lamdeni chukekha b'dieved all agree he may say retzeh and conclude afterward boneh Yerushalayim; but if he finished boneh Yerushalayim Amen and began Baruch Atah with intent to say the fourth berachah he must return to the head per Rambam and Rosh that we rule like them—for when he finished the d'oraisa berachot etc.; and even if he said lamdeni chukekha it is like supplications and he returns to the head—see there in name of Mahari Malko: if he fell asleep in the middle of birkat hamazon and does not know which berachah he is on he returns to the head of birkat hamazon—see there. (There) he says Baruch etc. and it implies he does not say Name and malchut—see Acharonim and Eshel Avraham that his opinion is to mention Name and malchut on Rosh Hashanah. And in Rabbeinu Yonah he wrote one should be concerned for Maran's opinion in Beit Yosef and Mordechai that he rules like them and one may say that also Rif and Rambam hold so—and doubt of berachot to be lenient—see there. (There) in the gloss: and perhaps one may distinguish—see Ba'er Yaakov daf 12 who wrote to distinguish that on Rosh Chodesh for Darkei HaEmunah he does not return on account of yaaleh veyavo unlike Al HaNissim that some say he returns (even in birkat hamazon on Chanukah see siman 682; and per Rashi's opinion this is in birkat hamazon of Purim—see Taz siman 695); therefore one may say within the other haRachaman etc.—see there. (There) and he does not conclude with that of Rosh Chodesh—see Magen Avraham that his opinion is he concludes with that of Rosh Chodesh and he left it requiring study; and Levush and Levushei Serad brought them; and Taz also wrote like Magen Avraham; and see Eshel Avraham and Rabbeinu Yonah and Yad Ephraim what they wrote on this.
