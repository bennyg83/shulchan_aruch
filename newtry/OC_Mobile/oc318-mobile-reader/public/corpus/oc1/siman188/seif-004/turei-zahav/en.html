Opens with Racham etc. In the Gemara he said regarding this and on Shabbat one opens with Nacham and concludes with Nacham—Rashi explained one need not change in the opening; and Tur concluded that Racham also means comfort; per this he did not need to inform us there is no change except that Racham is language of supplication and one does not say supplication on Shabbat but must say Nachamenu; and so in Tosafot in name of R' Yitzchak—it teaches us Racham means comfort; but in Semag it implies it teaches one must say Baruch Atah Heaven mekadesh HaShabbat as in prayer; and some begin on Shabbat with Racham and conclude menachem Tzion b'vinyan Yerushalayim—and this is not correct for the conclusion must resemble the opening; and even though Racham is like Nacham, nevertheless since he changes on Shabbat he reveals his mind that there is a distinction.
