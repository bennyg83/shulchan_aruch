9) [Seif 4] The text of this blessing, etc. The Tur writes that one concludes, 'Blessed are You, Hashem, Who rebuilds Yerushalayim.' Mordechai at the end of Berachos writes that Maharam would say, 'Who in His mercy rebuilds Yerushalayim.' Kol Bo and Orchos Chaim, however, write concerning Maharam's text that it is imprecise, because Yerushalayim will be rebuilt only through justice, as it is written, 'Tziyon shall be redeemed through justice.' Beis Yosef cites their words and writes that if one said, 'in His mercy,' he is not required to repeat the blessing; see there. We find that Maran, of blessed memory, agrees with the Tur, Kol Bo, and Orchos Chaim that one should not say, 'in His mercy,' but only, 'Who rebuilds Yerushalayim.' Such are his words here in the Shulchan Aruch. R' Yaakov Castro writes the same, cited by Birkei Yosef, paragraph 1; see there. Simchas HaRegel writes the same in his siddur; see there, as do Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 4; Maaseh Rav, paragraph 88; Beis Menuchah, paragraph 5; Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 8; and Ben Ish Chai, parashas Chukas, paragraph 16. Rema, of blessed memory, however, writes in Darkei Moshe, paragraph 1, that the custom follows Maharam. The verse, 'shall be redeemed through justice,' means only that if the Jewish people judge truthfully between one person and another, then Tziyon will be redeemed, as indicated by, 'and those who return to her through righteousness.' Such are his words here in the gloss to the Shulchan Aruch. Levush, seif 2, Mateh Moshe, siman 325, Bach, Ateres Zekeinim, paragraph 1, and Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef, paragraph 1, rule likewise. The latter writes that this is primary and that this is his practice; see there. See further below, paragraph 13, and understand this.
10) There: He begins it with, 'Have mercy,' etc. Or, 'Comfort us,' etc. Nowadays, both on weekdays and Shabbos, the practice is to say, 'Have mercy,' and in the conclusion to say, 'Blessed are You, Hashem, Who in His mercy rebuilds Yerushalayim.' One should not alter this at all to say, 'Who comforts Tziyon through the rebuilding of Yerushalayim,' because this too may constitute a conclusion with two matters. Maharshal writes that he received this tradition from his teachers. Bach; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef, paragraph 6. The words of the Arizal, of blessed memory, likewise appear to indicate no distinction in the text of this blessing between weekdays and Shabbos. See Shaar HaMitzvos, parashas Eikev, and Shaar HaKavanos, folios 61 and 72b; see there. The siddur of Rashash, of blessed memory, likewise begins the blessing with, 'Have mercy,' and concludes, 'Who rebuilds Yerushalayim,' both on weekdays and Shabbos. This is the common practice. See below, paragraph 12.
