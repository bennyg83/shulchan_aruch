(7) Or Nachameinu—that is Nachameinu Heaven Elokeinu b'Yerushalayim irecha uv'Tzion mishkan kevodecha uv'malchut beit David meshichecha uv'veit hagedol vehakadosh etc.
(8) And concludes with it etc. or Menachem—from this language it implies that even if he opened with Rachem he may conclude with Menachem even though the conclusion is not literally of the same kind as the opening—we have no concern, for in any case the subject is one; but some Acharonim are strict about this and hold it must be literally of the same kind as the opening.
(9) And one should not change etc.—meaning if he says on weekdays Rachem he says on Shabbat also Rachem; and even though it is forbidden to request one's needs on Shabbat—here it is different because the formula of berachah is always so; and also all the HaRachaman one may say on Shabbat even though they are not in the formula of berachah the Sages enacted—for since everyone is accustomed to say them every time they bless in birkat hamazon they have become for them like a formula of berachah, and this is not because of requesting one's needs on Shabbat.
(10) One text—and the Gra wrote that the essence is like Rif: on weekdays one says Rachem na as our custom, and on Shabbat Nachameinu etc. and concludes Menachem etc.; and there are several Rishonim who hold so.
(11) That they say boneh berachamav etc.—because it is written "I will return to Yerushalayim in mercy" etc.; and also since he began at the beginning of the berachah with Rachem he also concludes with berachamim so the conclusion will be literally of the same kind as the opening; and in any case for all it is not disqualifying at all—and see in Ma'aseh Rav.
