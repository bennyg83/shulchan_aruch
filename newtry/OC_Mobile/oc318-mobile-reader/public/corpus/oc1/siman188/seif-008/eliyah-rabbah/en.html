And Yom Tov for joy, etc. Lechem Chamudot and Levush Yom Tov in Shulchan Aruch—Levush omitted le'amo Yisrael here; but Rosh and Tur wrote, until here his language. And I found like Shulchan Aruch and Levush in Kol Bo daf 43 and R' David Abudraham. However in Tosafot daf 43 and Semag and Beit Yosef I saw this language: Blessed are You Heaven Elokeinu Melech haOlam Who gave Shabbatot for rest and Yamim Tovim le'amo Yisrael for a sign and covenant and for joy, etc.—and this is more correct, for le'amo Yisrael applies to both; and so Baal Halakhot Gedolot daf 13. Lechem Chamudot also wrote on what Rosh wrote: for joy and simchah and zecheron—this is his language: I already wrote that some do not say zecheron, until here his language. His words are wondrous—for the poskim who wrote so did not write so except on Yom Tov alone, but not on Shabbat and Yom Tov; and one must say it appears to him there is no reason to distinguish between Yom Tov alone and Shabbat and Yom Tov; but in Bach I found a reason: since Shabbat is memory of creation and Yom Tov memory of Exodus from Egypt, therefore they add to say zecheron—which applies to both Shabbat and Yom Tov—see there. I also examined all poskim and did not find anyone who disputes Shabbat and Yom Tov; on the contrary Tur and Kol Bo dispute on Yom Tov alone—even so they wrote on Shabbat and Yom Tov that they say zecheron. Knesset HaGedolah on Rosh Hashanah: concludes Mekadesh Yisrael veYom Hazikaron; but Magen Avraham wrote he does not conclude like Rosh Chodesh—for some say it is a mitzvah to fast on Rosh Hashanah, and therefore one need not sit in his fast in his fast—siman 597. To me it appears that in any case one who eats on Rosh Hashanah is obligated to eat bread per all, and it is like other Yom Tov; and moreover even on Rosh Chodesh some conclude; and since many poskim hold it is forbidden to fast on Rosh Hashanah, certainly one should rely on them. And it appears that if he opened haTov vehaMetiv he returns to the beginning like other Yom Tov; however a sick person who ate on Yom Kippur and forgot yaaleh veyavo says Blessed are You Heaven Elokeinu Melech haOlam Who gave holy days to Israel—the day of Yom Kippur—and he does not conclude, for it is like Rosh Chodesh and they do not return him.
