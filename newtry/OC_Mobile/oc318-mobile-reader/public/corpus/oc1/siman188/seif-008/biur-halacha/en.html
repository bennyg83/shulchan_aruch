Third meal, etc.—but the first two meals certainly require bread—for all hold, as written: and you shall call Shabbat a delight, and there is no delight without bread [Rashba in his novellae]; and in Tosafot Rabbenu Yehuda on Berakhot it is stated that the same applies on Yom Tov, as written regarding it simchah, and this is not without bread.

On Shabbat its law is like Rosh Chodesh—see Mishna Berurah what he wrote: if he has not yet begun berachat haTov vehaMetiv he says Blessed Who gave, like Rosh Chodesh—and this is Magen Avraham s.k.16—see there; and it implies regarding conclusion too it is equal to Rosh Chodesh that he does not conclude, and he follows his reasoning in s.k.17 that wherever b'dieved we do not return him, likewise he does not conclude if he remembered before he opened haTov vehaMetiv; however per what we wrote there in name of Nehar Shalom, this is not dependent at all—for regarding Rosh Chodesh the Talmud doubts concluding because the day is not considered significant enough to fix a berachah in birkat hamazon with opening and conclusion; if so, here too in our matter: though we are strict not to return him if he remembered after opening haTov vehaMetiv, nevertheless Shabbat is certainly significant, and when he remembered before opening haTov vehaMetiv he must say Blessed Who gave, etc., with opening and conclusion—and especially in the third meal, for from the outset the Mechaber ruled siman 491 seif 5 that its obligation is specifically with bread; afterward I found in Nehar Shalom in this seif that he also sides with our words.
