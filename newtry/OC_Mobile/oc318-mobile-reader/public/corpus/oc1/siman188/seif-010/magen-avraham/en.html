He mentions that of Shabbat. And it is not comparable to one who did not pray Minchah on Shabbat who prays in Maariv two weekday [Amidot]—for there Minchah already passed and he does not pray Maariv except as makeup, and the two prayers are the prayer of Maariv; and his law is to distinguish in the first—and how will he pray that of Shabbat? But in birkat hamazon he comes upon the meal with which he ate on Shabbat; and since he has not yet prayed Maariv of weekday he must mention a kind of the event in birkat hamazon—end of Maharil in responsum siman 57/56. It implies that if he already prayed Maariv he again does not mention that of Shabbat even if he prayed intentionally; and so in five simchot for Maharam; and so in Maariv siman 695 seif 3; and even though per the reason that we follow the beginning of the meal—if so even if he prayed Maariv he should mention—one must say because it appears contradictory; and as written seif 30; and if so even if he prayed intentionally he does not mention—see there siman 419.

And likewise for Rosh Chodesh. Shelah ruled that in these, since there is no mitzvah in their addition, he should not mention in Maariv; and so in responsa of Rama siman 132 that some practice so; and we have only the words of Rabbeinu Yonah; therefore if Rosh Chodesh fell on Maariv he says that of Shabbat and not that of Rosh Chodesh—for we follow the beginning of the meal, as written siman 271 seif 6 in the gloss (Bach)—and see there not like the first explanation that wrote because of Tosafot Shabbat that does not exist—for also on Rosh Chodesh he ruled so; and what he wrote as a compromise that an hour and a quarter within the night is from Tosafot Shabbat is not precise—for specifically at night we say so since for R' Yehuda he divides from Minchah onward it is night for the matter of Maariv—what is not so in Maariv where a slight amount suffices as written siman 293; and so Ran explicitly ch. 2 of Shabbat—see there; and so is obvious: if there were a mitzvah in its addition the grammarians would be careful in the matter as at night; and see there siman 271 that it is in Rosh and Tosefta that if he ate also at night bread he must mention that of Rosh Chodesh; nevertheless it requires study—for if he mentions both it appears contradictory—for one who prayed Maariv; and if so which of them to reject? One must say he mentions that of Rosh Chodesh and not that of Shabbat—for how many great ones hold he never mentions that of Shabbat because we follow the present; and if he ate at night—all agree he mentions also that of now as stated explicitly in Tosefta; therefore he mentions that of Rosh Chodesh, as Shelah for his method; and a fortiori in Maariv for Yom Tov; but in Maariv for Hanukkah he does not mention that of Hanukkah—for it is only optional to mention, as stated above.
