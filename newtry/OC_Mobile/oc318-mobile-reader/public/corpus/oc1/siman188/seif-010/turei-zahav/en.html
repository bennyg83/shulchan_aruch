He mentions Shabbat. In Beit Yosef he brought in name of responsum of Rosh: one should not mention Shabbat on a weekday just as Minchah of Shabbat—if he did not pray it he prays two weekday [Amidot] in Maariv for one does not mention Shabbat on a weekday even though he became obligated in this prayer on Shabbat; and likewise members of a group who were reclining and the day ended—for R' Yosi in Tosefta Berakhot differs in words of R' Yosi and mentions Shabbat in birkat hamazon even though it was a weekday meal since he blesses on Shabbat he must mention Shabbat; so too even though the back was a Shabbat meal since he blesses on a weekday he should not mention Shabbat—end of his words. But in Hagahot Maimoniyot he wrote in name of Rambam one should say Al HaNissim—for we find Rav prayed Minchah of Shabbat on erev Shabbat; and in name of Ashkenazic responsum which is responsum of Maharil Beit Yosef wrote that per this reasoning Minchah of Shabbat is not difficult—for Rosh brought that time of Minchah prayer passed and he does not pray Maariv except as makeup—and how will he pray Shabbat? And his law is to distinguish in the first; but in birkat hamazon he comes upon the meal with which he ate on Shabbat; and since he has not yet prayed weekday Maariv he must mention a kind of the event in birkat hamazon; and so is the simple custom to mention Al HaNissim in such a case; and so on Motza'ei Shabbat when their meal extended until night—end of Beit Yosef; and in responsum of Maharil itself siman 56 he wrote afterward: however there is responsum of Rosh that one should not mention a kind of the event on the night Motza'ei the day even if he began while still day—end of his words. It implies Motza'ei Shabbat first is because he had not yet seen responsum of Rosh at the beginning of his writing; or he wrote first his own reasoning; and even though in Beit Yosef's citation he mentioned the difficulty from Minchah of Shabbat that Rosh brought—it implies also at the beginning the pilpul was in Rosh's mind that these two words Rosh brought are not in responsum of Maharil but Beit Yosef's language that he inserted within the words of the responsum; and thereby it is settled what one may say: why did he write one should say Al HaNissim at night—and the version "for Minchah of Shabbat." And still difficult from Tosefta that Rosh brings that one must mention Shabbat in birkat hamazon; and when his meal extended until Shabbat—it implies we do not follow the beginning; and why shall we go after Al HaNissim following the beginning when both are in birkat hamazon? And it appears at first glance in truth Maharil retracted at the end when he brought responsum of Rosh—and this is for the reason I wrote; but however we did not find our hands and feet in this—for Motza'ei Shabbat truly from that Tosefta Rosh mentioned we do not follow the beginning; and why do we practice saying Al HaNissim on Motza'ei Purim and likewise on Motza'ei Shabbat we say retzei—and do we have power to divide against Tosefta? And it appears to me all is settled: certainly we properly follow the beginning for this matter—not to diminish what belongs to the beginning such as retzei and Al HaNissim if additional holiness came upon him at the time of concluding the meal—certainly he also says a kind of that holiness; and this is not canceling following the beginning; therefore he properly mentions Shabbat when a meal that began on a weekday extended; and on Minchah of Shabbat and Yom Tov on Sunday when the meal extended until night—we follow the beginning and he is obligated to say retzei and afterward he also says yaaleh veyavo; and if you say this contradicts—for there is no contradiction: first he says that of Shabbat and afterward that of Yom Tov—just as on Motza'ei Shabbat that fell on Yom Tov one says first kiddush belonging to Yom Tov and afterward havdalah belonging after Shabbat before Yom Tov fell—for in the end there are upon him two kedushot; so too in birkat hamazon he mentions that of Shabbat that passed and that of Yom Tov that now comes; and the same on Rosh Chodesh that fell on Motza'ei Shabbat—he says retzei and yaaleh veyavo; and Maharshal wrote: if Yom Tov fell on Sunday and the Motza'ei Shabbat meal extended until night he says only retzei—for we follow the beginning of the meal; difficult from Tosefta I mentioned that if a weekday meal extended into Shabbat he mentions Shabbat—and the same Rosh Chodesh; and in Sefer Amek Berachah I saw he wrote in name of the pious Maharash of Lublin: on Rosh Chodesh on Motza'ei Shabbat he says yaaleh veyavo and not retzei—and difficult from Al HaNissim that we say it at night and retzei that we say on Motza'ei Shabbat—for certainly kedushat Rosh Chodesh does not help here to uproot from him the obligation that reaches him on account of Shabbat; therefore I say: neither this word nor that—but in this one says both as written; and the principle in this: if he extends a weekday meal within Rosh Chodesh or within Shabbat—for one who holds he must [mention] in birkat hamazon as written siman 271 seif 6—he mentions Shabbat in birkat hamazon as Shulchan Aruch wrote there; and even though Rama wrote there the essence is he does not mention because we follow the beginning—behold we see Tosefta is its refutation; but one may say the explanation of his words is as stated—it will be explained there that eating is forbidden; but on Rosh Chodesh it appears Rama concedes; and if Rosh Chodesh or Yom Tov fell on Motza'ei Shabbat he mentions both; and all the more if it was Rosh Chodesh or Yom Tov on Shabbat and he extended within the night—he mentions both; and thereby all is settled, with God's help, as appears to my humble opinion: In the formula Avinu ro'einu zonenenu Tur brought Yerushalmi: what is one to say—Avinu ro'einu zonenenu on Shabbat? For it is forbidden to request one's needs on Shabbat. He said: the formula of berachot is so. And Beit Yosef wrote on this: from here it implies one may say zonenenu with shuruk in the meter of shovenu Elokei yisheinu; and as Abudraham wrote in name of Ibn Ezra that now it is not a Name-title but he prays before Him may He sustain us; and it appears so one may explain parnasenu chalkeilenu that he prays He will sustain us and provide for us; and also ro'einu may be explained per this as "pasture us" with missing vav and shin for resh; and in winter days I heard from grammarians they say so—but in all books it is written ro'einu with vav—end of his words. And in my humble opinion they are not all words of prayer—for if so it would be lip-service but superfluous with many doublings of language in this request; rather properly two of them are the Name-title and two others language of request—that is, we need Him may He sustain us with necessities such as bread and more permissible such as fruits; and both are included in berachat Borei Nefashot as Tur wrote siman 207; and the same here we say Avinu ro'einu zonenenu—that is with cholam, and both are the Name-title that You have power to pasture us with necessities and with luxuries; we request on these two matters; and on this he says language of request also—doubling parnasenu vechalkelenu; so it appears to my correct humble opinion.
