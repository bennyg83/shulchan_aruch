10) There: He begins it with, 'Have mercy,' etc. Or, 'Comfort us,' etc. Nowadays, both on weekdays and Shabbos, the practice is to say, 'Have mercy,' and in the conclusion to say, 'Blessed are You, Hashem, Who in His mercy rebuilds Yerushalayim.' One should not alter this at all to say, 'Who comforts Tziyon through the rebuilding of Yerushalayim,' because this too may constitute a conclusion with two matters. Maharshal writes that he received this tradition from his teachers. Bach; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef, paragraph 6. The words of the Arizal, of blessed memory, likewise appear to indicate no distinction in the text of this blessing between weekdays and Shabbos. See Shaar HaMitzvos, parashas Eikev, and Shaar HaKavanos, folios 61 and 72b; see there. The siddur of Rashash, of blessed memory, likewise begins the blessing with, 'Have mercy,' and concludes, 'Who rebuilds Yerushalayim,' both on weekdays and Shabbos. This is the common practice.
