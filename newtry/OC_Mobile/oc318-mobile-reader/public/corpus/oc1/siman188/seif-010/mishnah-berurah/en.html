(32) For we follow etc.—and since the beginning of the meal was while still day he was already obligated to mention a kind of the event—and therefore even if much time passed at night his obligation was not uprooted; and the Acharonim wrote that if he prayed Maariv before birkat hamazon he again does not mention that of Shabbat in birkat hamazon—for it appears contradictory.
(33) After the beginning of the meal—and therefore even if Rosh Chodesh fell on Maariv he mentions in birkat hamazon that of Shabbat alone and not that of Rosh Chodesh—and specifically when he finished his meal while still day; but if he ate bread also at night and has upon him obligation to mention also that of Rosh Chodesh—and both cannot be mentioned for it is two that contradict—how will he say on this Shabbat day and afterward say on this Rosh Chodesh day—for Rosh Chodesh is on one day? If so it is better to mention that of Rosh Chodesh—for this has obligation for all, unlike mention of Shabbat where there is dispute among Rishonim whether he is obligated to mention at all when he blesses on Maariv (and even though we rule here in Shulchan Aruch that we follow the beginning and he is obligated to mention Shabbat on Maariv—nevertheless here which is a place of constraint it is better to push off mention of Shabbat because of mention of Rosh Chodesh which is obligation for all). However on Maariv for Chanukah and Purim—even if he finished his meal at night he does not mention that of Chanukah and Purim, only Shabbat alone—for anyway mention of a kind of the event of Chanukah and Purim is only optional as above in siman 187 seif 4—so Magen Avraham; and many other Acharonim; and some say that if Yom Tov or Rosh Chodesh fell on Maariv he mentions Retzei and also yaaleh veyavo—for we follow the beginning of the meal and also the hour in which he blesses, and we are not strict about what appears contradictory—for in Retzei he was obligated from the hour the meal began by day, and afterward when time passed and he did not bless and night of Yom Tov or Rosh Chodesh arrived—an additional obligation was added upon him to remember a kind of the event of the hour in which he blesses.
(34) And likewise for Rosh Chodesh etc.—meaning if he ate on them and delayed birkat hamazon until evening he must mention—for we follow the time of the beginning of the meal—and he did not pray Maariv, as stated; and see below in siman 419.
