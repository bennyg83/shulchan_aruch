Blessed are You Hashem: even though if he had mentioned them in their place there would not be a blessing formula with Name and kingship, because when he mentions them in the adjacent blessing they are included in the blessing that has Name and kingship in its opening and conclusion; opening and conclusion of the first includes all; but since he finished Boneh Yerushalayim and says it as a blessing by itself it is not adjacent to its companion since it is only incidental [Beit Yosef].
Gave Shabbosos for rest: see Mishnah Berurah what Taz wrote regarding if he does not know the formula of this blessing, etc.; in my humble opinion much needs study per what Shulchan Aruch ruled above siman 114 seif 6: if he forgot to say Mashiv HaRuach and remembered before Atah Kadosh he says Mashiv HaRuach, and likewise Yaaleh VeYavo when he remembered before he began Modim, and as below siman 422 seif 1; the reason in all this: since he has not yet begun another blessing it is not yet called the end of a blessing; so here too in our matter: though the Rabbis enacted initially to say the formula Blessed Who gave Shabbosos, etc., for thus is preferable as we wrote initially; but where he does not know the formula why should he not say here Retzei when it is Shabbos or Yaaleh VeYavo when it is Yom Tov, since he has not yet begun the blessing after it and is still like one engaged in the Rachem blessing? If you wish to distinguish and say Bircas HaMazon is different since the Rabbis consider the end from when he finished Boneh Yerushalayim - nevertheless where he blesses for the essence of the event a separate blessing they were lenient not to return to the beginning when he remembered, as long as he has not begun blessing of HaTov VeHaMeitiv; they brought proof for their ruling from our matter that the enactment helps before HaTov VeHaMeitiv - apparently the end of blessing is not considered before he began the blessing after it; if you come to force a distinction as we wrote, there is no source for their ruling. Indeed this ruling of Shulchan Aruch in siman 114 and below siman 422 is not a clear matter - for many Rishonim dispute - see above in Biur Halacha. Nevertheless per Shulchan Aruch's opinion above, requires study in practice.
Until he began, etc: see Mishnah Berurah what we wrote in name of Chayei Adam; from the last Acharonim it appears that once he began this blessing there is no remedy and he returns to the beginning, unlike Chayei Adam; it appears to me they hold that what we say in the Gemara - that when he opened HaTov VeHaMeitiv he returns to the beginning - is because thereby it is evident he already diverted his mind from the third blessing; so appears truly in Yerushalmi chapter Sheloshah she'achlu - see there Penei Moshe; so explained Or Zaru'a explicitly in Laws of Meal siman 140 - see there; per this what helps that he finish Blessed Who gave rest? - for when he said Blessed are You Hashem he already considered a fourth blessing and thereby already diverted from the third blessing; therefore the law of Chayei Adam requires study.
To return, etc: if he fell asleep in Bircas HaMazon and does not know which blessing he is on, he returns to the beginning of Bircas HaMazon unless he knows with certainty that he said one of the blessings - in which case he returns only to the place where he is in doubt [Birkhei Yosef].
To the beginning of Bircas HaMazon: here Raavad and Rashba and Rabbeinu Yonah hold he returns to Boneh Yerushalayim since he is still standing in blessing of HaTov VeHaMeitiv [and if he finished HaTov VeHaMeitiv as well, in any case he returns to the beginning of Bircas HaMazon]; nevertheless the unqualified ruling is to return to the head because Rambam and Rashi and Rosh uphold one method that he returns to the head. And though I found in Rokeach also that his opinion is like Raavad, nevertheless one should not depart from the Mechaber's ruling - for I searched and found another band of Rishonim who all uphold this method: they are Ra'ah in his novellae on the Rif, Or Zaru'a, Rabbeinu Yerucham, Tosafos Rabbeinu Yehuda on Berakhot, Shitah Mekubetzes, Migdal Oz, and Riaz. The reason for this method Magen Avraham copied in seif katan8: the reasoning of Rosh that all three blessings are considered as one blessing - and thereby yielded a new law that not only in this matter but wherever he erred [such as he did not mention covenant and Torah in blessing of haAretz or kingship beit David in Boneh Yerushalayim] he returns to the beginning of Bircas HaMazon; see Peri Megadim who doubts this greatly. Rashi gave a different reason in this matter - only to the same blessing in which he erred; and likewise Rambam who wrote he returns to the head - perhaps his reason is also like Rashi's reasoning - see there; and I also found in Nehar Shalom a challenge to Magen Avraham on this. And here truly all the Rishonim who hold this method of returning to the head that I mentioned above, except Rosh and Rabbeinu Yerucham his disciple - they all did not mention this reasoning that three blessings are considered as one; and the necessity that compelled Rosh to innovate this law was to answer Raavad's difficulty on this method - for it is no worse than one who says supplications after prayer, which is not yet called uprooting; and for this reason he had to innovate that though it is not called uprooting, nevertheless he returns to the head because all three blessings are like one long blessing; but we do not need this, as Peri Megadim and Nehar Shalom wrote for Rashi's opinion; and also see Ra'ah's novellae on Rambam's Laws of the Rif where he also disputes Raavad and answers his difficulty with other explanations. And it is that it is not comparable at all to one accustomed to say supplications after prayer - for supplications are like a long prayer; but here one cannot say HaTov VeHaMeitiv is like a long blessing - on the contrary here they fixed Amen between them and made it consolation at the end of blessings, and opened HaTov VeHaMeitiv as a blessing - behold they made it a blessing entirely by itself; so too Migdal Oz and Shitah Mekubetzes Berakhot - see there; per this we do not need at all Rosh's reasoning that three blessings are considered as one; on the contrary it appears they do not hold this reasoning at all - for if they held it he would not have needed his answer. The conclusion from our words: for Rashi and Ra'ah it is proven they do not hold like Rosh's reasoning; and also Raavad and Rashba and Rabbeinu Yonah and Rokeach dispute the Mechaber's essential ruling and hold that even if he erred in Boneh Yerushalayim and opened HaTov VeHaMeitiv he does not return to the head but only to blessing of Rachem; and though it is hard to rely on them since most Rishonim ruled to return to the beginning of Bircas HaMazon as the Mechaber's opinion as I wrote above, nevertheless this would be specifically when he already finished the blessings of Torah and opened HaTov VeHaMeitiv - and from the reason that it is considered like uprooting legs in prayer he returns to the beginning of prayer, as Rashi and Ra'ah wrote; but not when standing in the middle of Bircas HaMazon and remembered he erred that he should return to the head from the reason that three blessings are considered as one - which was never mentioned even by holders of this method; therefore Magen Avraham's ruling requires study in practice.
