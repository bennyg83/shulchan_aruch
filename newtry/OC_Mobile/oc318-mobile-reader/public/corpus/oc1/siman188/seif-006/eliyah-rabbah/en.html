Blessed are You Heaven Mekadesh, etc.—it implies they do not conclude with malchut but only Blessed are You Heaven and not Elokeinu Melech haOlam; and so in Lechem Chamudot daf 61 he wondered on what is written that they conclude with malchut—for where do we find malchut in the conclusion? And in Knesset HaGedolah he challenged Lechem Chamudot: we have not seen, etc.; but I found in Kesef Mishneh ch. 2 Laws of Berakhot on what Rambam ruled that we do not conclude on this Rosh Chodesh—this is his language; and it appears from our master's words that in others he concludes with Name and malchut; and so the opinion of most commentators; and so it implies from his doubt on Rosh Chodesh—thus in others it is obvious to him that he concludes, until here his language. But his words are wondrous—for I examined all poskim, Rishonim and Acharonim, and there is none who mentioned malchut in the conclusion except Rabbenu Yonah. And it appears to my humble opinion: after I examined Rif and Semag and Kol Bo who wrote the formula of the berachah Blessed Who gave... Blessed are You Heaven Mekadesh haShabbat—and so in some versions of Rambam; and since they did not mention Name in the opening, it appears to them that one does not mention in the opening at all, but in the conclusion; and it appears to them that the same law applies—that they conclude with malchut since they did not open with it; and perhaps this is also Rabbenu Yonah's opinion who explained his words on the words of Rif—examine and requires study. In any case, all other poskim mentioned Name and malchut in the opening, and in the conclusion Name and not malchut, as Levush and Shulchan Aruch wrote, and this is primary. And incidentally I will explain the words of Kesef Mishneh and remove from it the wondrous wonder that occurred to Knesset HaGedolah—that though there is no conclusion with Name and malchut, nevertheless the doubt of conclusion on Rosh Chodesh stands—see there where he expanded. And it appears to my humble opinion: first add two more difficulties on Kesef Mishneh: one, behold the doubt of Rosh Chodesh is explicit in the Talmud Berakhot daf 49; and moreover for Rambam there is no doubt—he ruled Stam that he does not conclude; therefore it appears to me that this is his explanation: it appears to him that if the conclusion were without Name and malchut, then even though the Talmud is in doubt, nevertheless we would rule to say them from doubt; but if it is with Name and malchut, their doubt is stringent—not to take Heaven's Name in vain, as it implies in Levush end of siman 8; and if so, this is what he says: from his doubt on Rosh Chodesh he means to say he rules from doubt regarding Name—that he does not conclude; if so, perforce all conclusion is with Name and malchut—so it appears to me, examine. Maharshal wrote from Rosh: and if he does not know this berachah he returns to the head of birkat hamazon; but if he does not know berachat Rosh Chodesh he does not return. Taz wrote: if he knows the beginning and end, even though he does not know the rest of the formula properly, he says it and need not return, until here. And it is clarified from this: in every place he erred in birkat hamazon he returns to the head of birkat hamazon, except if he erred in the fourth berachah he does not return but only to haTov vehaMetiv; and likewise Magen Avraham wrote; and see above siman 483 s.k.14.
