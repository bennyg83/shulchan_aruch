On Rosh Hashanah one does not say it, and Your word our King, etc.—for it suffices for us with mention of remembrance alone; and it is not comparable to the haftarah where there the berachah was enacted for the obligation of the day's sanctity, and also there must be a kind of conclusion adjacent to the conclusion; but in birkat hamazon the essence of the day's sanctity is yaaleh veyavo, and this was enacted specifically for one who forgot, and there is a kind of conclusion adjacent to the conclusion as on other Yom Tov and Shabbat (responsum of Maharil—see siman 597); and it appears to me on Rosh Hashanah one does not say it for fasting and joy; nevertheless it requires study—for it appears that we do not return him, for some say it is a mitzvah to fast on Rosh Hashanah; and one must say we do not concern ourselves for this opinion at all; and see siman 597 that we rely on this opinion even leniently that one need not sit a fast upon a fast; therefore it appears to me that on Rosh Hashanah he says without conclusion like Rosh Chodesh; and it is obvious that a sick person who ate on Yom Kippur and forgot yaaleh veyavo—they do not return him; and if he remembered before haTov vehaMetiv he says Blessed Who gave holy days to Israel—the day of Yom Kippur—and he does not conclude, for it is like Rosh Chodesh as stated—not like what is written in Kol Bo that he concludes.

To the head of birkat hamazon. For all three berachot are considered as one (Rosh); therefore wherever he erred in them he returns to the beginning as written siman 114 seif 6; but if he erred in the fourth berachah he does not return except to haTov vehaMetiv.
