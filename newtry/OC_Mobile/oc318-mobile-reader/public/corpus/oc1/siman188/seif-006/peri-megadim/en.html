And it appears he says within haRachaman even though Rambam wrote in Hagah that yaaleh veyavo is different from Al HaNissim—nevertheless in Responsa he implies the essence of the third meal is specifically with bread and raises somewhat when he mentions within haRachaman. And Magen Avraham s.k. 11. And fourth meal its law is also like Rosh Chodesh as Tosafot Berakhot 49b s.v. iy ba'i—Maharil brought them Magen Avraham s.k. 17. And fifth meal—if its law is like Rosh Chodesh or one may say specifically the fourth, since there is dispute of R' Chidka on Shabbat 118a and we say the halachah is not like him—and the same for several meals their law is like Rosh Chodesh at least; and on Yom Tov one meal at night and one by day he returns—for the rest of their laws are like Rosh Chodesh. Orach Chayim letter tet-zayin: And know that in Shulchan Aruch he wrote on Shabbat and Yom Tov Baruch Atah etc. and concludes with Name alone and it is a long berachah; but for Rosh Chodesh it is written Baruch etc. and he did not mention Name and malchut—and so it appears for if not behold on Rosh Chodesh he does not return—how will he say a berachah in vain with Name and malchut? But in Levush he wrote that also on Rosh Chodesh one says Baruch Atah etc.—and it requires study. Orach Chayim letter yod-vav. And siman 422 in Magen Avraham 1—so wrote that if he finished the berachah he does not say Name—if so here on Rosh Chodesh when he does not return and nevertheless says Name—the reason is it is not considered an interruption for berachot of Torah were already completed (and even if we say for asking in presence of one who fears and honor its law is like prayer as siman 483 seif 8, and even berachat haTov vehaMetiv lest they treat lightly until haRachaman etc.—nevertheless there is no interruption to mention that of Rosh Chodesh here). And see Tur and no need to expand. And from all this it appears to be lenient in doubt of berachot and say Baruch shenatan Rosh Chodesh without Name and malchut—and in our novellae we wrote somewhat on this. And it will be explained in Magen Avraham further in s.k. 8.
