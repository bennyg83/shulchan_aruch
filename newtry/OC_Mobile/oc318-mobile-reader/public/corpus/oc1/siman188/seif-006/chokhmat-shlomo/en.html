There, seif 6. If one erred and did not mention Shabbos. Note: I am uncertain about one who recited Bircas HaMazon on Shabbos and does not remember whether he mentioned Shabbos. Must he repeat it or not? The basis of the doubt is what is written in Shulchan Aruch, Hilchos Rosh Chodesh, siman 422, that if one is uncertain whether he mentioned Yaaleh VeYavo on Rosh Chodesh, he need not repeat the Amidah, because thirty days do not pass without his mentioning it. Magen Avraham writes there that, according to this, if one is uncertain whether he recited the Shabbos Amidah, he likewise need not repeat it. He later rejects this, however, because Shabbos has an independent blessing. According to this, in Bircas HaMazon on Shabbos, where there is no independent blessing and the matter is only an insertion, like Yaaleh VeYavo on Rosh Chodesh in the Amidah, we may presume that he mentioned it, since he does so every Shabbos. Even according to Taz, who disagrees there, and according to Magen Avraham, one may say that their ruling applies specifically to something recited only once every thirty days, such as Rosh Chodesh. Moreover, Rosh Chodesh is not so conspicuous, and it is possible that the fact that it was Rosh Chodesh slipped his mind. Shabbos, however, is highly conspicuous, as it is said elsewhere that the awe of Shabbos is upon him. The same reasoning should apply here. Therefore, in such a case one may say that there is a presumption that he mentioned Shabbos. Examine this carefully. The practical ruling presently requires study.
