(16) And did not mention Shabbat—even if he is in doubt whether he mentioned or not we rely that presumably he certainly did not mention.
(17) He says Baruch Atah etc.—and if he does not know the formula of this berachah he returns to the head; and if he knows the beginning and end even though he does not know the rest of the formula in the middle properly he says it and need not return to the head [Taz and Shach]—and see what we wrote in Biur Halachah.
(18) Yamim tovim l'Yisrael—it should read l'amo Yisrael.
(19) L'sason ul'simcha—and on Rosh Hashanah one does not say l'sason ul'simcha; also one does not say uDevarekha Malkeinu etc.; and this is its formula: Baruch Atah Heaven Elokeinu Melech HaOlam asher natan yamim tovim l'amo Yisrael et yom hazikaron hazeh, and he does not conclude in it as on Rosh Chodesh—and this is Magen Avraham's opinion who disputes Kol Bo that compares Rosh Hashanah to other Yom Tov; but many Acharonim agreed with Kol Bo—and therefore one must conclude Baruch Atah Heaven mekadesh Yisrael veyom hazikaron; and if he forgot and began haTov vehaMetiv—also Magen Avraham's opinion is he again does not return as on Rosh Chodesh, and Ateret Zekenim disputes him and holds he returns. And on Yom Kippur regarding a sick person who must eat and must say yaaleh veyavo—as below siman 618 seif 10: if he opened in haTov vehaMetiv and remembered he did not say yaaleh veyavo he need not return as on Rosh Chodesh; and if he remembered before he began berachat haTov vehaMetiv—some say he must say Baruch shenatan yamim kedoshim l'Yisrael et yom haKippurim hazeh without conclusion; and some say the Sages did not enact any berachah formula at all to complete the mention of yaaleh veyavo, and whoever did not say it in its place again does not return—and so is reasonable.
(20) Yom Tov on Shabbat he says etc.—and it deals with one who forgot and did not say both Retzei and yaaleh veyavo—therefore he must include both; but if he forgot Shabbat alone he says only asher natan Shabbatot limnucha; and likewise if he forgot Yom Tov alone he says asher natan yamim tovim l'amo Yisrael etc. [Acharonim].
(21) And all these berachot etc.—meaning the openings of the berachot.
(22) Before he began haTov etc.—meaning he is standing after berachat Boneh Yerushalayim; and if he remembered after he said the word Baruch Atah it is simple he returns to say Retzei veHachalitzeinu or yaaleh veyavo and says afterward uvneh Yerushalayim etc.; and if he remembered after he already said Baruch Atah Heaven it is proper he finish immediately lamdeni chukekha so the Name not be in vain—and it is still as if he did not finish the berachah and he returns to Retzei.
(23) Until he began haTov etc.—Chayei Adam wrote: meaning he said Baruch Atah Heaven Elokeinu Melech HaOlam—this is called the beginning of this berachah; but if he said only Baruch Atah etc. he finishes asher natan Shabbatot limnucha etc. and afterward returns and says berachat haTov vehaMetiv—and see in Biur Halachah.
(24) To the head of birkat hamazon—for since he already finished the order of Torah-level berachot and diverted his mind from them and opened in haTov vehaMetiv—it is like uprooting feet in the prayer of eighteen that he returns to the head if he erred in anything; and if he erred in the fourth berachah such as he did not say in it Name and malchut—even though he already finished it—he does not return except to haTov vehaMetiv which is a berachah by itself [Magen Avraham and Shach].
