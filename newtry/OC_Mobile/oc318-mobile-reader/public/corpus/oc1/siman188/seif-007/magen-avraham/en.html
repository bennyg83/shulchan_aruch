He is not obligated to eat bread. For he may eat fruits; but on Shabbat and Yom Tov he is obligated to eat bread—and from the Stam of Rabbeinu Yonah it implies he holds that even on all the days and nights he must return, for he is obligated to eat two meals, one by day and one by night. And that which we say on Pesach only the first night is obligatory—that is on the other days one may eat rich matzah; and also on Sukkot one may eat a kezayit of bread, and in the sukkah he is not obligated until more than a kebeitzah—as Ran wrote in the old chapter; and Terumat haDeshen ch. 3 wrote that on Shabbat and Yom Tov there is no d'oraisa obligation except because there is no kiddush except in the place of a meal he needs bread—and see Ra'avad seif 5 that it does not imply so—and see siman 168 seif 7 what is written:

And its law is like Rosh Chodesh. This is Rambam's opinion, and as written siman 582 seif 2—see there siman 270.

And one should not say it in vain. It requires study—for they say tachanun all day which has mention of names—why should this be less? For specifically a berachah in vain is forbidden—and it requires study.

He does not return. Even though it does not suffice without eating bread—nevertheless because of this minority they did not enact to return him—meaning specifically on Yom Kippur that falls on Shabbat when one prays four prayers anyway they enacted to mention also that of Shabbat—but to return on account of that they did not enact, since one never returns on account of Rosh Chodesh; and also the obligation of eating on that day is because of Shabbat [Tur]—and if so there is never an obligation of mention to mention in Rosh Chodesh, and it is like one who reads haftarah on Shabbat Rosh Chodesh who does not mention that of Rosh Chodesh as written ch. 4 seif 2.

He forgot that of Shabbat. From this word it implies that even if he forgot that of Shabbat alone nevertheless he must also include Rosh Chodesh with Shabbat; and in Tur he wrote if he forgot also that of Shabbat etc.—it implies if he forgot that of Shabbat alone he does not mention Rosh Chodesh in the berachah—and so is reasonable; however when he opened in haTov vehaMetiv that he returns to the head—all agree he mentions that of Rosh Chodesh, for in all of birkat hamazon he mentions that of Rosh Chodesh—similar to Yom Kippur which is a day obligated in four prayers; and it appears to me that on Chanukah on Shabbat when he mentioned that of Chanukah and not that of Shabbat he returns to the head and does not mention that of Chanukah—for one is not obligated to mention that of Chanukah as is in the Gemara.

And he does not conclude. I did not find this reason; and also it requires study why he should not conclude with that of Rosh Chodesh—for even on Rosh Chodesh on weekdays some say he concludes, except because of doubt of berachah in vain one does not conclude—and here since anyway he concludes with that of Shabbat what harm is there if he also says and Roshei Chodashim—and it requires study—and see siman 188.
