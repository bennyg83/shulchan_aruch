(s.k.7) Which, etc. And some explain for the haftarah—meaning in any case they say uDevarekha emet; but Malkeinu also in the haftarah one may not, as written siman 582; and it is possible Maharil did not hold so.

And this was enacted specifically for one who forgot—so it should read—meaning specifically if it happened he forgot—therefore it suffices with saying yom hazikaron. And it appears on Rosh Hashanah one does not say it for fasting and joy as explained in siman 582: on Rosh Hashanah one does not say festivals for joy, etc.

Nevertheless it requires study—for it appears to me we do not return him—from Maharil's words it implies he holds if he already began haTov vehaMetiv we return him—for where they do not return, it is because eating bread on that day is not obligatory; so too he does not conclude if he says asher natan, as we hold on Rosh Chodesh; and since Maharil wrote and there is a kind of conclusion adjacent to the conclusion—perforce one must say he concludes; if so he also holds we return him; and on this Magen Avraham challenged why.

And one must say we do not follow this opinion—meaning since we hold it is a mitzvah to eat on Rosh Hashanah as written in siman 596—if so even for returning we do not follow the opinion that holds it is a mitzvah to fast.

For one need not sit a fast upon a fast—if he fasted on Rosh Hashanah because we rely on those who say it is a mitzvah to fast on Rosh Hashanah.

All the more for the matter of doubt berachah one must be concerned for this opinion—and it would be fitting to say we do not return him; and also he should not conclude if he says asher natan, etc.
