If one erred and did not mention Rosh Chodesh in Birkat HaMazon, whether by day or by night, he says, 'Blessed is He Who gave New Moons to His people Israel as a remembrance,' and does not conclude it with a blessing formula, provided that he remembered before beginning HaTov VeHaMeitiv. But if he did not remember until after beginning HaTov VeHaMeitiv, he does not return, because he is not obligated to eat bread in a manner that requires Birkat HaMazon. And Chol HaMoed has the same law as Rosh Chodesh. {Rama: And it is possible that nevertheless one should say it among the other HaRachaman passages, as explained above at the end of siman 187 regarding Al HaNissim. And perhaps there is a distinction, because Ya'aleh VeYavo contains Divine Names and one should not recite them in vain. So it appears to me, and this is the custom.} If Rosh Chodesh falls on Shabbos and he mentioned Shabbos but not Rosh Chodesh, and he did not remember until after beginning HaTov VeHaMeitiv, he does not return. And if he forgot Shabbos as well and remembered before beginning HaTov VeHaMeitiv, he combines Rosh Chodesh with Shabbos and says, 'Who gave Shabbosos for rest and New Moons for remembrance.' And if he already began HaTov VeHaMeitiv, he returns to the beginning of Birkat HaMazon and mentions both Shabbos and Rosh Chodesh. And there is an authority who says that if he forgot both Shabbos and Rosh Chodesh and remembered before beginning HaTov VeHaMeitiv, he says, 'Who gave Shabbosos for rest and New Moons for remembrance,' and concludes with the concluding formula for Shabbos, and does not conclude with that of Rosh Chodesh.
