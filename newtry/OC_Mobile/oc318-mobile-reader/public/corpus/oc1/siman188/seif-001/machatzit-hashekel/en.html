s.k.1 And they will treat lightly, etc. Bach wrote that he holds, etc.—Magen Avraham's words in name of Bach refer to Rama's gloss that wrote to settle our custom that they answer in a loud voice. And Bach wrote another settlement for our custom: specifically in the time of the Talmud when they exempted workers from berachat haTov vehaMetiv as stated in siman 191; therefore they said in Berakhot daf 45b on that which is taught: one who answers amen after his berachot is praiseworthy—we establish it in the baraita in Boneh Yerushalayim (for on other berachot if he answers amen on his own berachah it is repugnant, as stated siman 215—see there the reason); and we say that Abaye answered this amen in a loud voice so the workers would hear—for when they finished birkat hamazon d'oraisa and he begins berachat haTov vehaMetiv from which they are exempt, they will go to their melacha; and R' Eliezer on the contrary answered this amen in a whisper so they would not treat haTov vehaMetiv lightly; and Bach holds specifically in their days—for if he answers aloud the intent is so the workers hear and go to their melacha because birkat hamazon d'oraisa is already finished—there is concern that also other people will treat haTov vehaMetiv lightly. But in this time when also workers bless haTov vehaMetiv, as stated in siman 191—there is no concern of treating lightly.
