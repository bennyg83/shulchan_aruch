Seif 1: After he concluded, etc.—HaTov is not d'oraisa, etc. NB: Behold the Sages said they enacted HaTov for the slain of Betar who did not rot, and HaMetiv that they were given burial; per their words it appears to say another explanation in this, with God's help: behold death is sometimes evil and sometimes good, as the Midrash on Bereishit says: "very good"—this is death, etc.; and behold they say here that what causes the dead to rot is from sins, and if there is no sin he does not rot; death on account of sin is for evil, and death not on account of sin but only to elevate him in the upper world—this is for good; therefore initially they thought their killing was for evil; but when they saw they did not rot they understood it was not on account of sin—they gave thanks retroactively for their killing, for it was for good—they sanctified God's name; may their merit protect us, amen.
