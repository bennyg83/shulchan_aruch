(s.k.3) The formula, etc. Beit Yosef beRachamim. Kol Bo and Or Zaru'a wrote that one should say Boneh beRachamav Yerushalayim—because it is not built except in judgment, as written in Yeshayahu ch. 1: Zion shall be redeemed through justice. But Mordechai in name of Maharam wrote one should say beRachamav, as written in Zekharyah: and I will return to Yerushalayim in mercy—my house shall be built in it (and one must say for him: that which is written Zion in justice is on account of doers of justice therein, as Rashi wrote there—but nevertheless it will be in mercy even though their merit is not sufficient to redeem); therefore Mahari Giinzburg wrote the formula of the reader mentioned—that the word beiti is between the word beRachamim and the word yivaneh; therefore one can say Boneh Yerushalayim beRachamim that the word Yerushalayim be between Boneh and beRachamim—even though in the reader beRachamim was mentioned first and at the end the word building—nevertheless here we have no concern, since it is impossible to fix the language of the berachah in this language.

Beit Yosef wrote one should say zonenenu, etc. His proof from Yerushalmi that Tur brought is brought in Magen Avraham seif 7 here: thus it is taught—it is forbidden for a person to request his needs on Shabbat; Rav Zeira asked Rabbah: what is the law of saying Avinu ro'einu zonenenu on Shabbat? He said: the formula of berachot is so—end of his words. From this it is proven that zonenenu is said in language of request; therefore Rav Zeira was in doubt whether he may say it on Shabbat—for it is requesting his needs; but if it were thought it is said in language of praise and praise zonenenu with cholam—then in what was Rav Zeira in doubt whether he may say it on Shabbat? Therefore Rabbeinu Yonah wrote: since zonenenu is language of prayer, likewise ro'einu is language of prayer with missing vav and shin for resh. However Taz s.k.7 challenged: per this the language is doubled four times—ro'einu zonenenu parnasenu vechalkelenu; therefore Taz explained: two are the Name-title—that is ro'einu zonenenu; and two—that is parnasenu vechalkelenu—are in language of request, for we need two matters: one—things necessary for human life like bread and water; two—things added for human enjoyment like several kinds of fruits and delights of this world; therefore two titles are on these two matters, and likewise two requests—that is parnasenu vechalkelenu—that we request on these two matters; per this what Rav Zeira asked in Yerushalmi was not his question except because of parnasenu vechalkelenu which are request—even though Rav Zeira did not specify in his question except what is the law of saying ro'einu zonenenu—one can say he cited the beginning of the statement, but his intent is the end of the statement—that is parnasenu vechalkelenu.
