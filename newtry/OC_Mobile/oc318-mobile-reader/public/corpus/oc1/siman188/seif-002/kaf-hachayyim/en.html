2) [Seif 2] Gloss: It appears that this applies specifically when one recites the blessing alone, etc. Rema, of blessed memory, writes this distinction in Darkei Moshe, paragraph 2, to justify the custom of responding with this Amen aloud; see there. According to Maran, of blessed memory, however, there is no distinction. Whether one recites the blessing for himself or to discharge others, he must say it quietly. Bach writes the same; see there. Darkei Moshe gives another reason for the custom of saying it aloud: specifically in their times, when laborers returned to their work, there was concern that they might treat the blessing lightly, but this does not apply now; see there. Bach writes the same, cited by Magen Avraham, paragraph 1. Bach nevertheless concludes that it is proper to say it gently, not excessively loudly, because there remains concern for treating the blessing lightly; see there. Taz, paragraph 1, likewise writes that one should not protest against those whose practice is not to say it quietly; see there.
