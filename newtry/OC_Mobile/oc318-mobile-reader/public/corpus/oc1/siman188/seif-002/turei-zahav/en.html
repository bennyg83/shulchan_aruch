And it appears specifically when blessing alone etc. He wrote this to settle our custom that we do not say it in a whisper; and Rashi in responsum expanded in siman 44 to uphold our custom; and it appears to me to settle it in this way: as it says in the Gemara—Abaye answered him aloud so the workers would hear and rise to their work; Rav Ashi answered in a whisper so they would not treat haTov vehaMetiv lightly—meaning they would sense there is a conclusion of Boneh Yerushalayim; and Rif and Rambam did not bring this—it appears to me they explain that Abaye and Rav Ashi do not disagree in law but each had a reason for his custom. What Abaye "answered aloud" does not mean he raised his voice more than other berachot—for there is no benefit, for even if he says it in the voice of other berachot the workers will hear he answers Amen, for he ruled above that the rest of berachot require a loud voice; rather he means he said it aloud like other berachot—unlike in a whisper like Rav Ashi; and Rav Ashi said in a whisper from concern of contempt—thus each had a sound reason for his custom; therefore one should not protest those who practice like Abaye; and one should not say it in a whisper specifically, as stated.
