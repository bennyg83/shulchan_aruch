(2) And it seems specifically etc.—and some reconcile the custom for another reason: they were not concerned about treating the fourth berachah lightly except in their days when the workers were going to their melacha when they hear Amen after Boneh Yerushalayim (for the Sages did not obligate them in the fourth berachah because of the loss of the employer's melacha)—therefore he had to answer Amen quietly so the workers would not hear and recognize that the fourth berachah is not from the Torah and come to treat it lightly even when not in a place of loss of the employer's melacha; but now that the workers also bless four berachot as written in siman 191 there is no concern for this; and per this reason whether when blessing with zimun or when blessing alone he may answer Amen in a loud voice, and it seems the custom is so. He should not say Boneh Yerushalayim Amen in one breath—only wait a bit after the word Yerushalayim and then say Amen so it does not imply that Amen is also from the conclusion of the berachah.
