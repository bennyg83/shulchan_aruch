(s.k.2) Not so, etc. Tosafot also (daf 49a), etc. in Boneh Yerushalayim; and Maharsha wrote there that they mean saying Amen ro'einu, etc.—see there; therefore one must say that he holds that which the Talmud says there that one should not equate malchut beit David to malchut shamayim—that is specifically not to say uMalchutekha malchut beit David, etc.—for that would be a comparison; but if he mentions malchut shamayim not in the place of malchut beit David—even though he mentioned malchut beit David in that berachah—there is no need for it and it is not called a comparison; and one must say for Tosafot: even though we say in the Gemara there that they say in berachat haTov three malchuyot as explained siman 289, and we say there one reason: because the hands of berachat haTov vehaMetiv are not considered adjacent to its companion—for every berachah adjacent to its companion does not need in the opening Name and malchut)—for after a time they enacted it in Yavneh for the slain of Betar and therefore it needs to begin with Name and malchut; and one malchut they enacted in it because of berachat Boneh Yerushalayim and one because of berachat haAretz—that is berachat Nodeh lekha; for in berachat Boneh Yerushalayim it is fitting to mention therein malchut since he mentions therein malchut beit David; and also in berachat haAretz Tosafot wrote it is fitting to say therein malchut even though for us it is a berachah adjacent to its companion—nevertheless initially it was not enacted as an adjacent berachah—for berachat haZan Moses enacted and berachat haAretz Joshua enacted and berachat Boneh Yerushalayim David and Solomon enacted; but since for us that we say in order it is adjacent, it is not fitting to begin it with Name and malchut—nevertheless there must be malchut in them; therefore they enacted corresponding to them malchut in haTov vehaMetiv. And they say: for this reason they did not mention in Boneh Yerushalayim itself malchut—because one may not equate malchut beit David to malchut shamayim, as written in Shulchan Aruch; if so for those accustomed to say in Boneh Yerushalayim Amen ro'einu, etc. and mention therein malchut—why did they need to say malchut in haTov vehaMetiv because of berachat Boneh Yerushalayim? One must say: since they do not say malchut shamayim in its place—that is at the beginning of the berachah or adjacent to malchut beit David—if not because one may not equate malchut beit David to malchut shamayim there is not so much recognition; and this is not honor for Heaven; and it is preferable when they added malchut elsewhere in another berachah—for there is more recognition.

All the more in yaaleh veyavo, etc.—for Amen, etc.—is that it is not adjacent to malchut beit David; nevertheless in that matter it is; but yaaleh veyavo is another matter; if so even for Mordechai who disputes Tosafot and holds one should not say Amen, etc. as written in Shulchan Aruch—nevertheless in yaaleh veyavo which is a matter by itself one can say Mordechai also concedes.
