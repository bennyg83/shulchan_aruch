(s.k.5) Or, etc.—we hold like the Sages, etc.—for we say in Berakhot daf 48b: on Shabbat he begins berachat Racham and says kedushat hayom (that is retzeh) in the middle of Boneh Yerushalayim; if he wished to say it in Racham he says it; if he wished in berachat haAretz he says it; if he wished in berachat haTov vehaMetiv he says it; and the Sages say he does not say it except in Racham alone. And he challenges: the Sages are the first Tanna; and he answers: b'dieved there is a difference between them—for the first Tanna if b'dieved he said it in another berachah he fulfilled, and for the Sages he did not fulfill.

Rosh ch. 7 Bava Metzia daf 92a: we learned—a worker may eat cucumbers (for the Torah permitted him to eat from his work that he does) even for a dinar (meaning even if his eating is more than his wages); R' Eliezer ben Chisma says a worker should not eat more than his wages; and the Sages permit—but they teach a person not to be gluttonous and thereby close the door before him (meaning lest no person wish to hire him). And the Talmud challenges: the Sages are the first Tanna; and he answers: teaching there is a difference between them—for the first Tanna does not need teaching and the Rabbis say teaching is needed—and so Rosh: the law is like the Sages who teach, for the Sages are taught in plural language.

And likewise in Niddah: we learned—one who miscarries on day 41 of her pregnancy (for before then it was mere water) sits [in uncleanness] for a male and for a female—meaning the stringency of both lest it was female and she would have fourteen days of tumah and days of taharah like a male until completion of forty days alone; and R' Yishmael holds she sits only for a male, for he holds formation of the female is at eighty days; and since she miscarried on day 41, perforce there is no room to suspect female; and the Sages say she sits for male and female, for they hold also formation of the female is at forty days like a male. And he challenges: the Sages are the first Tanna; and if you say the mishnah should be Stam like the first Tanna to inform us the law is like him—obvious that individual and many—the law is like the many; and if not we know the law is like the first Tanna; and he answers: it was thought the law is like R' Yishmael even though he is individual against many because his reason is sound—until here the content of the Gemara's words. Behold because R' Yishmael's reason is sound it was thought the law is like him against the first Tanna; but since it teaches the Sages hold like the first Tanna—from this we know the law is not like R' Yishmael even though his reason is sound; if so it is proven the Sages are preferable to the first Tanna except there is no complete proof; therefore he wrote: and so it implies one can reject—that there the reason is not because we do not hold like R' Yishmael because the Sages are preferable to the first Tanna, but from an extra mishnah he infers—for otherwise why are the words of the Sages "the first Tanna" as he challenged there; but rather R' Yehudah HaNasi wished to establish it like the first Tanna, and a little implies so from the extra mishnah we hear it for him and not that the Sages are preferable—and understand.

But in Maamar Maimon, etc.—we learned there five [types of] blood are tamei in a woman, etc. Beit Shammai: even like water of thirty days is tamei; and Beit Hillel purify the green; Akavya ben Mahalalel declares tamei and the Sages purify. And the Talmud challenges: whether on Beit Hillel or on the Sages—Akavya is the first Tanna, for the first Tanna counts five types of blood implying no more; and we know that between like water of thirty and green is pure; and he answers: on both there is a difference between them—for the first Tanna holds five bloods are certainly tamei and like thirty and green are tamei from doubt—that is, we suspend; and if it touched terumah we neither eat nor burn; and from Beit Hillel we hear like thirty is completely pure; and from the Sages of Akavya we hear green is completely pure. Rosh wrote: the law is like the Sages of Akavya that green is completely pure—not like the first Tanna who holds we suspend; just as we do not hold like the first Tanna in what he holds we suspend in water of thirty—for certainly we hold like Beit Hillel who holds completely pure—so too in green that the first Tanna holds we suspend we do not hold like him; and so Maamar Maimon: this that Rosh needed proof we do not hold like the first Tanna against the Sages—because Rosh wrote in ch. Makom sheNahagu, etc.; and that which we learned there in ch. Makom sheNahagu they would do melacha on erev Pesach until noon—they do; and where they practiced not to do—they do not; and on daf 55 we learned: and the Sages in Yehudah would do, etc. until noon; and in the Galil they would not do at night (before erev Pesach—Beit Shammai forbid and Beit Hillel permit); and he challenges: initially it taught custom (since we learned makom sheNahagu, etc.) and afterward it taught prohibition (and Maharsha explained like Tosafot and so Rosh holds—since Beit Shammai and Beit Hillel dispute, in custom there is no dispute—we look how we practice); and he answers: the first part is R' Meir who holds in a baraita it is custom, and the latter part comes according to R' Yosi who holds in a baraita it is forbidden; and Rosh wrote: even though we hold R' Meir and R' Yosi, the law is like R' Yosi in this that Stam mishnah of that chapter is preferable to the words of the Sages—end of Rosh.

And it requires study—for Rosh's words contradict each other: per the words of Maamar Maimon Rosh holds the first Tanna is preferable to the Sages, and in Bava Metzia in this [case of] the worker Rosh wrote the Sages are preferable to the first Tanna.

And it is possible that in Bava Metzia he relied, etc.—meaning always the first Tanna is preferable to the Sages; and in this worker where he rules like the Sages Rosh did not rely on this alone that the Sages are taught in plural language, but relied primarily on another reason Rosh wrote—that their reason is sound like the Sages who teach (and see there per this Rosh's words that he wrote afterward there should be difficult); and it appears to me that wherever, etc. we hold like the Sages not like Maamar Maimon; if so one can say in Bava Metzia in the worker Rosh ruled like the Sages; if so likewise for the matter of birkat hamazon we hold like the Sages that even b'dieved he did not fulfill.

But in ch. Makom sheNahagu, etc.—it is called a complete Stam and is not called the first Tanna; and Stam mishnah is preferable to the Sages; and so Rosh inferred the language in ch. Makom sheNahagu that Stam mishnah of that chapter is preferable, etc.

And in Niddah he brings, etc.—meaning per this that the Sages are preferable to the first Tanna—the difficulty of Maamar Maimon is challenged: why did Rosh need in Niddah proof that we hold like the Sages against the first Tanna.
