(א) (s.k. 6) Near, etc. Magen Avraham deduced from that he wrote "near the fire" and not "facing the fire" as in the previous seif "facing the hearth" — rather here it deals when he is close to the fire in a place where yad soledes bo applies; and these are Rabbeinu Yehuda's words in his long book, who gave a reason for Rosh's words that one must be careful not to warm his hands, etc., that it deals in a place where yad soledes bo applies and on account of the prohibition of cooking; accordingly Rambam too concedes it is forbidden; if so, what is Magen Avraham's s.k. 5; therefore Rabbeinu Yehuda wrote in seif 5 "and some say" — it must be that Magen Avraham holds from Rabbeinu Yehuda's language "and some say" that Shulchan Aruch retracted and holds Rosh forbids even where yad soledes bo does not apply, on account of washing, as Magen Avraham explained here:

(ב) And it appears to me he holds, etc., even to wash his hands in them is forbidden; and he holds that even in such a case where there is no full washing, and Rambam permitted as in s.k. 5 — Rosh forbids also in this:

(ג) And so in the gemara: we taught Ra'avad says they wash the minor on the third day of his circumcision when it falls on Shabbat, for on the third day the infant is endangered by washing, and the Sages forbid; and they asked: is this washing entire-body washing or only circumcision washing; and they sought to resolve that certainly entire-body washing is meant, for if only circumcision washing — what is the Sages' reason to forbid, for he holds there is no danger; nevertheless he is not worse than putting water on a wound on Shabbat, which is permitted; and they rejected: on a wound it was permitted only in water heated from erev Shabbat, and here it deals with water heated on Shabbat; therefore the Sages forbade even circumcision washing alone; therefore certainly entire-body washing is meant in water heated on Shabbat permissibly, such as to warm or another way; for if the Sages forbade heating water on Shabbat for circumcision washing — their dispute would be whether heating water for washing is permitted; for if heated permissibly, even the Sages agree washing is permitted; if so why teach "Ra'avad says they wash," etc. — it should have said "they heat water," etc., for in washing even the Sages agree; rather certainly even if heated permissibly the Sages forbade circumcision washing; hence in water heated on Shabbat even washing one limb is forbidden, and even Ra'avad agrees, and there he permitted only on account of danger:

(ד) And so in Mordekhai who wrote because he heats the water upon him — it cannot deal where yad soledes bo applies and on account of cooking prohibition; why did he write "because he heats" — do we not rule warming is not its cooking; rather in a place where he can come to yad soledes bo, even to warm is forbidden lest he leave it until yad soledes bo applies, as written in siman 318 seif 14; and so it should have said "lest he leave it until his hand reaches yad soledes bo"; or the reason is on account of washing — if so, what he wrote "because he heats," etc., and thereby it becomes like washing, and so in Rosh on the braita that teaches "and he shall not warm himself facing the hearth" because he warms the water upon him — Rosh wrote therefore a person must be careful after netilat yadayim not to warm, etc.; it also implies the reason is on account of heating water, and where there is no cooking prohibition — nevertheless the reason is on account of washing:

(ה) And so what is written on Yom Tov, etc., and nevertheless forbidden to wash in them — meaning his entire body; but to wash face, hands, and feet is permitted even to heat the water in any case; it is proven that even if the water was heated permissibly — nevertheless forbidden to wash his entire body in them; even though in water heated from erev Yom Tov it is permitted to wash the entire body — nevertheless if heated on Yom Tov, even if heated permissibly, forbidden to wash his entire body in them:

(ו) And from this on Shabbat for face, hands, and feet too it is permitted to wash in water heated from erev Shabbat; nevertheless in water heated on Shabbat, even if heated permissibly to wash in them face, hands, and feet — entire-body washing on Yom Tov and washing face, hands, and feet on Shabbat are equivalent:
