(א) (s.k. 8) Permitted, etc., on account of wringing, etc. — even though in Beitzah 18a they said that according to all a person may immerse on Shabbat; and what is the reason — even for us who rule immersion at its time is not a mitzvah — nevertheless on account of tza'ar or mitzvat onah it is permitted and they were not concerned for these distant concerns; but when he could immerse before, the custom was stringent; and so Terumat HaDeshen wrote to be stringent, but for another reason:

(ב) Even though even Mordekhai, etc. — meaning we do not rule like Mordekhai, but in cold water it is permitted as written in seif 1; therefore he wrote "even for Mordekhai" — specifically in a vessel, because the observer thinks he washes in hot water; unlike in a river where everyone sees it is cold:

(ג) Nevertheless the custom is to forbid — for whatever reason; and it is possible on account of the concerns Maharil wrote — not on account of wringing alone but on account of those reasons to forbid washing alone; therefore even to immerse is forbidden, for it appears as fixing — meaning in the gemara they said: although a vessel that became impure is forbidden to immerse on Shabbat and Yom Tov because it is like fixing, a person may immerse because he appears as cooling himself even in rainy season and even in foul water; nevertheless sometimes he washes when his body is soiled with mud and excrement and washes to remove mud and excrement upon him — therefore he does not appear as fixing; if so, in our time where the custom is careful not to wash even in cold, as above — if a person immerses everyone knows it is for immersion and he will appear as fixing, and forbidden; and so in Yoreh De'ah siman 199 in name of Kol Bo, etc., on account of wringing hair — even though Rabbeinu Yehuda wrote there is no concern for this, for we rule there is no wringing in hair; therefore his intent is only not to push off mitzvah of immersion at its time; for even though Rambam wrote chapter 9 there is no wringing in hair from the Torah and therefore one is not liable for wringing hair — nevertheless its d'rabbanan prohibition exists, as written in siman 330; nevertheless he did not push off mitzvah of immersion at its time; but washing is certainly fit to forbid for this reason; and therefore immersion of mitzvah is also forbidden, for it appears as fixing — one cannot say he appears as cooling himself, for it is forbidden to immerse on account of wringing hair; since it is d'rabbanan it is not precise that even d'rabbanan there is none, for we rule immersion of a ba'al keri is voided; rather it is a general stringency, as written in siman 323 regarding immersing a new vessel on Shabbat — according to one who says vessel immersion is d'rabbanan, for one opinion it is permitted to immerse on Shabbat like immersing a vessel with impurity of the womb; since it is d'rabbanan, permitted to immerse on Shabbat and it is not called fixing; and the same here:

(ד) And in Agur, etc., that in a river it is forbidden — refers to above according to Terumat HaDeshen who wrote that in a river all permit; also in Beit Yosef in name of Kol Bo, etc. — these are duplicated words, for he already wrote thus above:

(ה) And it appears to me a mumar, etc. — for R' Yehuda, etc. — this is R' Yosi who holds he is not a convert until he circumcises and immerses; therefore even if he circumcised in our presence we do not immerse him on Shabbat because it is like fixing; but R' Yehuda holds one suffices — either circumcision or immersion, and in one his law is like an Israelite; therefore in any case both circumcision and immersion are needed, nevertheless in one of them it suffices for his law to be like an Israelite that his sanctified things are sanctified and he does not make yayin nesech, and for all matters he is like an Israelite; therefore if he circumcised in our presence we immerse him on Shabbat, for it is not like fixing; and we rule like R' Yosi; nevertheless from R' Yehuda we learn regarding immersion of a mumar who returned to Judaism that he requires immersion, as written at the end of siman 268 (and Taz siman 267 s.k. 5 wrote he also needs to shave) — meaning as a general stringency to liken him to a convert who converted; but from the law's side he need not at all, as the novellae wrote in Yevamos, for he is a full Israelite who was a wicked Israelite; and even though the novellae wrote d'rabbanan he needs immersion — not precise, for we do not find anywhere in the Talmud that a mumar who repented in responsa requires immersion; rather it is a stringency; and if so, he is no worse than a convert for R' Yehuda, who is permitted to immerse on Shabbat and it is not like fixing; and in Sefer Tashbetz he questioned this — all this is fine for the reason explained in the Talmud regarding a convert; but according to Rambam, and so Rabbeinu Yehuda cited there in Shulchan Aruch, who holds per the conclusion one need not the reason for prohibiting a convert's immersion on Shabbat as fixing, but he requires three for a convert's immersion as it is written regarding him "mishpat" and requires three like regarding mishpat — therefore we do not judge on Shabbat; if so this reason applies also to a mumar who repented, for we also require three at the time of immersion, as Rama wrote there; and in my humble opinion it is difficult: from the reason of mishpat he should not need Magen Avraham's reason to permit a mumar's immersion, for regarding a mumar "mishpat" is not written; and what we require three for his immersion is only for likeness like a convert; if so, certainly permitted on Shabbat — only nevertheless forbidden because it is fixing — where does this go, Magen Avraham wrote it is not fixing, as above:
