A person is permitted, etc. — see in Magen Avraham who brought in name of Maharil and Terumat HaDeshen to be stringent in this; and at the end of his words he concluded nevertheless it appears permitted to immerse for keri since it is d'rabbanan — meaning we say in Beitzah 18: one who became impure with impurity of the womb — they immerse it on Yom Tov, and the reason is since the obligation of immersion is only d'rabbanan it does not appear as fixing; and for the flow of the matter he wrote thus, that today they have voided immersion entirely, as above in siman 88 and similarly for the daily offering [and it is possible Magen Avraham's intent is even according to those stringent who hold they did not void it for prayer]; and it is difficult, for it is not comparable at all — there with impurity of the womb he is not impure from the Torah, unlike here where he is impure from the Torah and through immersion he becomes pure — this itself appears as fixing. And know that it implies in Beitzah 17b in the mishnah, since it was taught anonymously, it deals with all impurity such as impurity of sheretz and zavim, etc., that for them they did not forbid reading Torah, as above in siman 88; and nevertheless it implies there in Beitzah 18 in the gemara that if not because he appears as cooling himself a person would be forbidden to immerse himself on Shabbat because he appears as fixing; therefore regarding impurity of keri, which is a primary impurity [gloss: and it is possible to say he holds this is specifically in early generations when impurity and purity were practiced, therefore fixing through immersion for all impure so they can use pure things through immersion — unlike today when impurity and purity are not practiced, and fixing from learning Torah is not, for even without immersion it is permitted, as above; and aside from a niddah woman, for through immersion she becomes permitted to her husband — therefore it appears as fixing in our time, and he does not appear as cooling himself. And one should not challenge: if so, fully impure vessels from the Torah should also be permitted to immerse today, and Shulchan Aruch ruled above in siman 261 seif 1 we do not immerse vessels and foods impure — it deals as Rashi explains [and so Ra'avad in his mishnah commentary] that regarding new vessels the Mechaber's view leans more to those who permit even on Shabbat itself, and all the more bein ha-shemashot, as implied in siman 323 seif 7] — there it is different, for it is something requiring a court and needs another court to permit it; therefore even though the reason was voided the decree they decreed on immersing vessels was not voided, unlike immersing a person, for they did not decree then at all because he appears as cooling himself; and now that we refrain from bathing it is not recognizable as cooling himself and it is recognizable it is for immersion — nevertheless it does not appear as fixing through immersion today when impurity and purity are not practiced, end gloss]. I again found in Biur HaGra who wrote: a person, etc., see Magen Avraham; and the essence is as Beit Yosef wrote in name of Kol Bo and Terumat HaDeshen who forbid; and so Magen Avraham in siman 128 s.k. 70 — see there, end of his words; and it appears his intent is what we wrote, and he disagrees with Magen Avraham who wrote immersion for keri is permitted according to all; and Gra concludes on this: and the essence is as the Beit Yosef wrote, etc., meaning according to Terumat HaDeshen's reason that in our time since they practice stringency in bathing, it appears as fixing through immersion; again one should not distinguish between immersion for keri and other impurity, since ultimately he becomes pure through immersion and it appears as fixing; and this is what Gra concluded; and so Magen Avraham in siman 128 s.k. 70 — see there, meaning he wrote explicitly that therefore priests did not practice on Yom Tov to be intimate with their wives so they could marry them tomorrow with purity in their hands, because they did not practice immersing on Yom Tov — see there; behold even for immersion of keri one should not immerse; nevertheless Magen Avraham's words are difficult to me, for there it deals with one who became impure on Yom Tov itself, and in this even for immersing vessels it is permitted and they did not decree because it appears as fixing, as stated in Beitzah 18; and keri impurity should not be stricter than immersing vessels that is permitted; and it is possible to say there, because impurity and purity were practiced and they were careful in purity, therefore they permitted immersing and using in purity; unlike today; and along this line one finds in Tosafos 18b s.v. kol; and perhaps Magen Avraham's intent there is different. And the outcome regarding immersion for keri: Magen Avraham contradicts himself — there he writes the custom is to be stringent, and here he is lenient; and Gra's view is as Magen Avraham wrote above in siman 128, and most Acharonim are lenient — behold they are Mateh Ephraim, Olat Shabbat, Tosafot Shabbat, Shulchan Aruch, etc.; meaning even if he could immerse from erev Shabbat and Yom Tov — nevertheless certainly we should not protest one who practices leniency; and all the more if he became impure on Shabbat and Yom Tov itself; and more so it appears to me that even Gra, who brought proof from Magen Avraham's words above in siman 128, brought it only as proof there is no distinction between immersion for keri and other immersion; but regarding the law itself, if he became impure on Shabbat and Yom Tov itself, possibly he too agrees one should not be stringent at all, as implied in Gra's commentaries in Yoreh De'ah siman 197 — see there; and so the custom has spread to permit; only he should be very careful not to come to wringing. And know further that if he is entirely pure and wants to immerse Shabbat morning for added sanctity — there is no concern of fixing from the law's side; proof from what they said there, in the gemara 19a: they immerse from one corner to another; see Rashi there; and so in Sefer Tosafot Shabbat siman 264 from the scribes; only according to Maharil's words that women properly practice not immersing not at their time on account of concern of wringing — it appears such immersion too should be prevented; and sufficient for us what we are lenient to immerse on Shabbat where he needs immersion:
