(א) (s.k. 5) As one who washes his entire body. Thus Rambam — meaning Shulchan Aruch's language is Rambam's language:

(ב) Therefore he wrote it is permitted to anoint, etc. — the language is not precise, for Rambam did not write explicitly that it is permitted to anoint his hands, etc.; rather from that he wrote "not like washing his entire body" it implies specifically in such a case it is forbidden, but for his face, hands, and feet in such a case it is permitted; therefore Magen Avraham gave the reason that he holds it is not full washing, as he deduced from Rambam's language; and thus "as one who washes his entire body" with the kaf of comparison implies he is not literally washing; but if it were literal washing — even his face, hands, and feet would be forbidden, as Magen Avraham wrote in the next s.k. that specifically in water heated from erev Shabbat it is permitted to wash face, hands, and feet; but in water heated on Shabbat, even if heated permissibly — forbidden even to wash one limb, except that here it is not literal washing and therefore permitted to wash face, hands, and feet; therefore Rabbeinu Yehuda wrote in seif 5 "and some say" — meaning Rambam disagrees and permits; and see in s.k. 6:
