1) [Seif 1] It is forbidden to bathe, etc. The reason is that the Sages decreed lest one come to violate a prohibition punishable by stoning by heating water on Shabbos. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 1; Magen Avraham, paragraph 1; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 1.
2) There: It is forbidden to bathe one's entire body, etc. The same applies to most of the body. Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef; Magen Avraham, paragraph 2; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 1; Eshel Avraham, paragraph 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1.
3) There: Even with water heated before Shabbos. Although Tur equates Yom Tov with Shabbos in this respect, see below in Shulchan Aruch, siman 511, seif 2, which permits bathing on Yom Tov with water heated before Yom Tov; see there.
4) There: Even with water heated before Shabbos. One who is suffering, even though his entire body is not ill, may possibly bathe. Chiddushei Rabbi Akiva Eiger.
5) There: Whether the water is in a utensil or in the ground. The author proceeds from the less novel to the more novel case. Water in a utensil is plainly forbidden, because an observer may say it was heated that Shabbos, since a utensil does not ordinarily retain heat for so long, and may consequently come to permit heating water on Shabbos. Even water in the ground, however, such as in a bathhouse pool that ordinarily retains its heat for an entire day or longer, was nevertheless prohibited by the Sages as a decree. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 2.
6) There: Even pouring the water over his body, etc. Bathing means placing one's body or limbs into water and washing there. Rinsing means pouring water over one's body or limbs, which is not the ordinary manner of bathing, yet it is nevertheless forbidden. Ran explains that after bathers perspire, they ordinarily take water in a utensil and pour it over themselves. People may therefore suspect that the water was heated that day. The author's words imply that even rinsing one limb after another is forbidden when he ultimately rinses the entire body. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 3. Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 1, similarly writes that rinsing the entire body one limb at a time is forbidden; see there. Women who immerse on Friday night during winter must be warned that when they leave the spring and customarily pour hot water over themselves, they should pour it over no more than half the body. The same applies to the father of a child who immerses on Shabbos morning before a bris. Ben Ish Chai, Parashas Tetzaveh, paragraph 10. See below, paragraph 32.
7) There: Even pouring the water over his body, etc. The same prohibition applies to washing a corpse, even when there is concern that it will decay, and even to having a non-Jew wash it, unless one agreed upon payment with the non-Jew before Shabbos. Radakh, House 22, chamber 1; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur; Olas Shabbos, paragraph 4. Bigdei Yesha questions Radakh from the explicit Mishnah that one may anoint and wash a corpse provided one does not move any of its limbs. Pischei HaDevir, paragraph 1, has already resolved this: the Mishnah discusses cold water, whereas Radakh forbids hot water. On the contrary, he writes that the words of Bigdei Yesha themselves require resolution; see there.
8) There: But one may wash his face, etc. If the water was heated on Shabbos, one may not use it even to wash his face, hands, and feet. Magen Avraham, paragraph 6. See below, paragraph 18.
9) There, gloss: Or other limbs, etc. Mishnah Berurah, siman 5, writes: It appears to me that women do not customarily begin wearing white garments on Shabbos or Yom Tov. Perhaps this is because they do not know how to take proper care regarding washing, distinguishing between hot water heated before Shabbos or Yom Tov and water heated on Shabbos or Yom Tov. Where no custom exists, it may be permitted, provided she does not wash with a garment, lest she come to squeeze it. These are his words. Magen Avraham, paragraph 3; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1; Eshel Avraham, paragraph 3; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1; Chayei Adam, klal 70, paragraph 1. See above, siman 301, seif 48. Whether one may immerse in hot water on Shabbos will be explained below in paragraph 32.
10) There: But in the hot springs of Tiberias it is permitted, etc. It is forbidden to bathe on Shabbos for medicinal purposes in pools and bathhouses resembling the hot springs of Tiberias. Responsa Mayim Rabbim, Orach Chaim, simanim 25, 26, and 27; see there at length. Birkei Yosef, paragraph 1; Shaarei Teshuvah. See also below, siman 328, paragraphs 247 and 249.
11) There: But in the hot springs of Tiberias it is permitted, etc. Beis Yosef writes that one may even bathe within them, as the ordinary meaning of bathing is to enter the water. Mordechai, however, forbids entering even cold water to bathe and permits only rinsing oneself by pouring water over the limbs. The ruling here does not follow Mordechai. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 3. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1, likewise rules that one may bathe within them.
12) There: But not in a utensil, etc. When the water is in a utensil, it is not apparent whether it was heated by fire or comes from the hot springs of Tiberias. Beis Yosef. One may consequently confuse the cases and bathe in water heated by fire. Levush.
