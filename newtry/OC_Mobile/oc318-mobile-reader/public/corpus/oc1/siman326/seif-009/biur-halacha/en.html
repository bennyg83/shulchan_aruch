With things that do not dissolve hair — see in Tur who listed many things whose source is the gemara; and the same for all similar if he knows they certainly do not dissolve; and it appears regarding species whose nature he does not know and perhaps they certainly dissolve — one should forbid washing with them. And Rambam in chapter 22 specified all things mentioned there, in the gemara for permission and did not mention natron and sand, though they are mentioned at the beginning of the sugya — permitted according to R' Shimon; and so in chapter 1 of Hilchot Shabbat he mentioned permitted to wash hands in fruit dust and did not mention anything of natron and sand; it appears Rambam holds natron and sand are forbidden and requires a reason; and see in Lechem Mishneh who also wondered and answered Rambam holds according to R' Yishmael in Nazir who said he may not rub his head with earth, that even natron and sand are forbidden as tza'ar ba'alei chayyim, and he ruled like him because there the gemara explains his words; and what they permit with vessels even in natron and sand, as above in siman 323 and so explicitly in Rif, Rosh, and all poskim — meaning vessels are not so harsh and it is not tza'ar ba'alei chayyim; requires study, for Rambam left the stam mishnah in chapter Bameh Madlikin that implies the stam mishnah includes even natron and sand, and we say 81a on it that the halacha follows this stam mishnah and he ruled like R' Yishmael; and further according to Lechem Mishneh's reasoning, what does the gemara in Bameh Madlikin challenge from this mishnah — it should have said the braita forbidding rubbing one's hair holds like R' Yishmael that it is tza'ar ba'alei chayyim; and also from R' Chananel's explanation there it implies that according to what we rule like R' Shimon, even natron and sand are permitted to rub the hair; and so in Hagahot Asher"i he wrote there that by law it is permitted even in natron and sand; nevertheless at the end he wrote there it is difficult for him to permit, perhaps on account of R' Yishmael's reason — requires study for practical halacha:
