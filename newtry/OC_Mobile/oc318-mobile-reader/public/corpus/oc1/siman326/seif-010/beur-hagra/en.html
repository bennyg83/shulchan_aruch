(א) Seif 10 "permitted to wash." Tosefta:

(ב) "And forbidden." But in Tosefta it also permits in salt; and Beit Yosef explains it deals with one who bathes and does not scrub; but in Tosefta they reconcile them together with morsen; and likewise for those who explain they do not crush lest they squeeze fruits, as stated siman 318 seif 16 — permitted in salt; and Tosefta supports them:

(ג) "Or in other fat." Implied from his words that on chol it is permitted; and likewise Tosafos end of chapter 4 of Niddah in the name of Rabbeinu Tam that it is permitted. And his words are strained — for if so on Yom Kippur forbidden only oil specifically; but in Mordechai chapter 5 of Shabbos and Sefer HaTerumah and Rashba they forbid explicitly like on Yom Kippur, that both are karet; and likewise terumah and lashes for rest — for on Yom Kippur it is due to affliction, as from what is stated chapter 9 of Shabbos: from where do we know anointing is like drinking on Yom Kippur, as stated, etc. — therefore because it resembles drinking; and likewise in beginning of chapter 4 of Niddah; except Tosafos and Mordechai and Sefer HaTerumah and Rashba wrote it is an asmakhta and is only rabbinic — they made it like drinking; and therefore in a place of pain they permitted, as stated end of Yoma: one who has lice on his head may anoint in his usual way on Yom Kippur, etc.; but not in a place without pain it is forbidden; and see Tosafos there in Yoma; and likewise on Rosh Chodesh and all prohibitions:
