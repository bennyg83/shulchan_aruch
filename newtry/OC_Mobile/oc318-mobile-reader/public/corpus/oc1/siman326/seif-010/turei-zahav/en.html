And forbidden to wash his hands in salt, etc. Also in siman 320 Tur and Shulchan Aruch wrote thus. And it is astonishing, for in scrubbing in natron it is certainly psik reisha, for it is impossible to be careful from kneading when he puts water on dry natron; nevertheless permitted, as Beit Yosef wrote in name of Mordekhai that kneading as an unusual manner is permitted; and in salt and natron it is forbidden on account that they hold it is nolad — why should nolad, which is d'rabbanan, be stricter than that which helps as an unusual manner in kneading, which is a toldah of kneading that is a primary melacha. And one may say that in salt and natron he wants them to melt and be absorbed in his hand to do their action — therefore forbidden when he does nolad through his rubbing; unlike in kneading natron where he does not want them to knead, and in this he also wants what we say in chapter "With what may one insulate" regarding all vessels one may rub them, as above in siman 323, that it is difficult when one scrubs a vessel to which fat adhered — then the fat melts through his hand; rather certainly on account that he does not intend for this it is not forbidden on account of nolad; therefore in truth to scrub vessels in salt is forbidden, as written at the end of siman 323:
