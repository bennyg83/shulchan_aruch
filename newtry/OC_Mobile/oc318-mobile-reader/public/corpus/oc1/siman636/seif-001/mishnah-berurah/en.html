(1) An old sukkah - This means the old roofing; but regarding the walls, there is no concern according to all opinions.
(2) That he made before they entered, etc. - For if it was within thirty days, since one studies the laws of the festival thirty days before the festival, it is presumed to have been made for the sake of the festival.
(3) Thirty days - This is not precise, for the same applies even if it was made at the beginning of the year: it is valid, since even if he made it not for the sake of the festival but only for shade, it is also valid, as above in siman 635.
(4) Provided that he renews, etc. - This is at the outset and for the mitzvah alone, so people not come to permit even one made from the outset as a dwelling, which is Torah-invalid, as above in siman 635. All the more so regarding the ganbach and rakbash sukkot mentioned in siman 635, it is certainly a mitzvah to renew something in it for the sake of the festival.
(5) Now in its body - Meaning he should renew, before Yom Tov, in the body of the roofing, namely by placing in it a little roofing, one square handbreadth by one handbreadth, as below.
(6) And if the renewal is over all of it, etc. - Meaning that it runs along the entire length of the sukkah or along its entire width, it suffices even if its length or width is only a minimal amount.
(7) Even from the beginning of the year, etc. - A sukkah that stands from year to year, although he originally made it for the sake of the festival, since the festival passed, that making has lapsed, as the Magen Avraham wrote in siman 638, small paragraph 2. Although it is valid, because it was originally made for shade, the act that was for the sake of the festival has lapsed, and therefore he must renew something in it.
