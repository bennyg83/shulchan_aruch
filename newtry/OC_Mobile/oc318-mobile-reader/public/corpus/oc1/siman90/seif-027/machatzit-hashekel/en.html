(s.k. 27) Permitted to spit and throw it behind him — as is written in siman 97 seif 2; see Derech Moshe in the name of Or Zaru'a — meaning: there in siman 97 Shulchan Aruch wrote that it is forbidden to spit (meaning at the time of the eighteen blessings); and if he cannot avoid spitting, he swallows it in his garment; and if he is a guest, he throws it behind him — it implies that not at the time of the eighteen blessings everything is permitted. However, Derech Moshe in siman 97 explained in the name of Or Zaru'a, and these are his words: Or Zaru'a wrote that even not at the time of prayer it is forbidden to spit before him or to his right, only behind him or to his left — until here. And Or Zaru'a certainly does not argue on the Gemara that we say 24b: Rabbi Yehudah was standing in prayer and spit happened to him — he swallowed it, etc.; it implies that not at the time of prayer it is permitted. And it is strained to say Rabbi Yehudah deals with one praying in his house, but in a synagogue even not at the time of prayer it is forbidden; if so, Or Zaru'a should have said that in a synagogue even not at the time of prayer he swallows it, etc. Perforce one must say Or Zaru'a holds the stringency of prayer is that one must specifically swallow it in his garment and he was not permitted to throw it behind him except when he is a guest; but not at the time of prayer it is permitted for everyone to throw it behind him and he need not swallow it, even if he is not a guest. And on Shabbat it is forbidden to rub, as explained below siman 316 seif 11; but it is permitted to press it with his foot, even spittle and not only urine — and see there in Magen Avraham s.k. 25.
