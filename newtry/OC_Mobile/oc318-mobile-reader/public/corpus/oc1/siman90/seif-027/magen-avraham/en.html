He should be silent, etc. This is not comparable to what is written in siman 78, that he need not interrupt. There the interruption would be great, for he would need to remove his clothing and put on other clothing. Here, however, silence is not called an interruption, and moving from his place is also not so much an interruption, as written in siman 104, seif 3. See the Levush, who wrote that above it is called compulsion because the water is dripping from him; in the Gemara it implies that the man is rejected, as written in siman 65; see there.
