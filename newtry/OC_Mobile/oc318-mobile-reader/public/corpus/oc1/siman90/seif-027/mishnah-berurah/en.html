(83) In prayer—and likewise in Kerias Shema and other matters of holiness, as written above; and he cited prayer to include more cases, for here too one must walk before him four amos, and we do not say that since he is already standing in prayer there is no need to distance.

(84) He should remain silent, etc.—for all this is not called an interruption; but it is forbidden to interrupt with speech to bring him water, since the prohibition of urine is only rabbinic; and some say that there is no need to distance at all in this; therefore even if others are standing within the four amos of the urine and praying, he should not interrupt with speech on their account to inform them. And if a child fouled with feces in the synagogue in the middle of prayer and others are standing there and praying and do not know of this—if it is impossible to inform them in another way except by speech, it is permitted to interrupt with speech to save them from a Torah prohibition, for it is better that he perform a light prohibition, namely the rabbinic prohibition of interrupting prayer, than that others perform a Torah prohibition of praying in a soiled place. And this was already explained above in siman 79 in Mishna Berurah and Biur Halacha regarding the manners that are Torah law in this. And specifically if he has nothing with which to cover; but if he has something with which to cover, whether feces or urine, it is forbidden to interrupt and even to move from his place if the bad smell does not reach him.

(85) And it is better—Chayei Adam wrote: if he fears that perhaps he will delay in order to finish all of it and he cannot distance four amos, such as that he cannot pass in front of the one praying—he may rely on the Rashba who holds that it is permitted to pray since he is already standing in prayer, and the prohibition of urine is rabbinic.

(86) And he will need to return—see above in siman 98 in Mishna Berurah seif 5, that in the blessing of Kerias Shema if this occurred, and likewise in all blessings one need not return to the beginning.
