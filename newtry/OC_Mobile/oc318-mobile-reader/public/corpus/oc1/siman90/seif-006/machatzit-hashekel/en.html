Seif katan 6, not, etc. To converse in the field. We say that this means to pray, for we expound that conversation means prayer.
As we say, Yitzchak called it a field; this is the correct reading. Meaning, in truth it was enclosed by partitions, and the verse calls it a field because it was Mount Moriah, which Yitzchak called a field. See there at the end, that he knew the Second Temple built in his merit would be destroyed, as it says, 'Zion will be plowed like a field.'
According to the second answer, the reason is not brazenness but that passersby will interrupt him. Why then does the Gemara call him brazen?
It appears to me to uphold, etc. He will not be disturbed, etc. This is brazenness, that he is so proud. Although the Magen Avraham brought the language of the second answer of Tosafot as saying that the reason is that passersby will not interrupt him, if so why does the Magen Avraham say that the reason is pride and brazenness? In truth, however, this is not Tosafot's language. Tosafot writes that one can say the valley here means a valley where people regularly pass and travelers go. The Taz quotes it this way. The Magen Avraham earlier copied Tosafot according to the Beit Yosef's understanding, that the reason is that passersby should not interrupt him. But the Magen Avraham's explanation follows Tosafot's actual language.
