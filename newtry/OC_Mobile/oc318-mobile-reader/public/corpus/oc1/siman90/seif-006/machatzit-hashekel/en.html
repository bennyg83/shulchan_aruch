(Note 6) Not, etc. — to converse in the field. And we say that it means to pray, for we expound: "conversation" means only prayer.

As they say — Rabbi Yitzchak called it a field, as it ought to read. That is, in truth there were partitions enclosing it there, and that which is written "in the field" is because there was Mount Moriah, and Yitzchak called Mount Moriah "field" (and see there at the end that he knew the Second Temple built through his merit would be destroyed, as it is written "Zion shall be plowed as a field").

Under the second explanation, the reason is not on account of impudence but that passersby might interrupt him. And why did the Gemara say "he is impudent toward Me," etc.?

And in my view, to uphold, etc. — he will not be interrupted, etc., and this is impudence in that he boasts so greatly. And even though the Beit Yosef brought the words of Magen Avraham for the second explanation, that Tosafos wrote, and this is its language: alternatively, the reason is so that passersby will not interrupt him; if so, there is no need for what Magen Avraham wrote, that the reason is on account of pride and impudence. But in truth this is not the language of Tosafos; rather, this is the language of Tosafos. And one may explain that the passage here deals with a valley, a place where wayfarers are accustomed to pass and travelers, end quote. And so it is brought in the Taz, except that Magen Avraham above cited the words of Tosafos as Beit Yosef understood them — that the words of Tosafos are the reason so that passersby will not interrupt him. And the explanation of Magen Avraham matches the true language of Tosafos.
