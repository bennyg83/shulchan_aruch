(f) If he does not turn [his face], etc. It is permitted to pray there in a time of pressure (Levush). And Malbishei Yom Tov wrote: this is not like all the commentators — for according to Rashi in this case it is certainly permitted lekhatchilah, namely that which we say [one who] turns his face to the synagogue — we have nothing in that [to forbid]. And according to R' Yitzchak it also appears it is not forbidden in this case, since his back is not behind the synagogue and he prays toward the side where the congregation prays — up to here. And one might wonder — in Lechem Chamudos p. 5 he wrote on him that his words are correct. One must say: that which we say in the Gemara "we have nothing in that" — means he is not called wicked; but in truth it is better to enter the synagogue. And according to this, the same law applies on the sides, even if he turned his face to the synagogue or to the east; and if so, this is comparable to one who prays in his house and does not enter the synagogue. And behold, Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Hagahos Maimoniyos and Semak — this is his wording: one should be stringent like the words of both; and if so, those standing outside the synagogue to the east or west should not turn their face to the east or west, for if so it would be like a doubtful case according to one of the explanations; rather they should turn their face to the north or south — up to here. And they wondered with great wonder, and the Bach, Lechem Chamudos, and Nachalas Tzvi upheld that according to both explanations it would not be a safek if one stands to the west and his face to the east — see there. And until I greatly debated with the above-mentioned great ones, I toiled and found — thank the Omnipresent — that in Hagahos Semak end of siman 12 he wrote before this his wording: "Behind the synagogue — because we make the opening of the synagogue in the west, and for those praying opposite the west it is reversed" — up to here his wording; and afterward he wrote the matters mentioned above. And if so, it appears to my humble opinion clear and simple: that which he wrote "and if so, those standing outside the synagogue to the east or west" — refers to the above, and that is for us who pray to the east: if one stands behind the east he should not turn his face to the east, in accordance with the stringency of both explanations. And similarly for those praying in the west, or one standing behind the west — he should not turn his face to the east or west; but if he stands in the west and his face to the east — he admits it is permitted. And there is no doubt that such is the explanation in Hagahos Maimoniyos; and now it is not in my possession; and similarly I found explained in the Agudah, chapter Oshein Pesin.
