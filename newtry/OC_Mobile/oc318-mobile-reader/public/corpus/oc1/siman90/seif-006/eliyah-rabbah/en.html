If he does not turn, etc. The Levush says that in pressing circumstances one may pray there. Malbushei Yom Tov writes that this is unlike all the commentators, for according to Rashi such a case is certainly permitted initially, and this is what the Gemara says, that if he turns his face toward the synagogue we have no concern. According to Ri as well, it seems not to be forbidden in this case, since his back is not toward the back of the synagogue and he prays in the direction in which the congregation prays. This is surprising, for Lechem Chamudos wrote that his words are correct. One must say that when the Gemara says we have no concern, it means that he is not called wicked, but in truth it is better that he enter the synagogue. Accordingly the same applies at the sides, even if he turns his face toward the synagogue or eastward, for this is comparable to one who prays at home and does not enter the synagogue. The Beit Yosef cites Hagahos Maimoniyos and Semak that one should be stringent like both explanations, and therefore those standing outside the synagogue to the east or west should not turn their faces east or west, for then according to one explanation it is improper; rather, they should turn north or south. The Bach, Lechem Chamudos, and Nachalas Tzvi wondered at this, for according to both explanations one standing on the west with his face eastward is not improper. After laboring over it, I found, thank G-d, that Hagahos Semak first wrote that behind the synagogue refers to our custom of making the entrance on the west, and for those who pray westward the rule is reversed. Then he wrote the words cited above. It is therefore clear that his words about those standing east or west refer to our case, where we pray eastward, that one standing behind the east side should not turn eastward as a stringency for both explanations, and similarly those who pray westward or stand behind the west should not turn east or west. But if one stands on the west with his face eastward, he agrees that it is permitted. No doubt this is the explanation in Hagahos Maimoniyos, and I found it explicit in Agudah, chapter Osin Pasin.
