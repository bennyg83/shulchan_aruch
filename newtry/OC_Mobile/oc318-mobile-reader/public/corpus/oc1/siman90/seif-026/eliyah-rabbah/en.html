(z) And a person is not an interposition, etc. Beit Yosef brings proof from that which the Gemara says: a man should not pray behind his rabbi — thus, behind another person we have nothing [to forbid]. The Bach wrote: it can be rejected — for behind one's rabbi there is a prohibition; but behind another person there is no prohibition; however, one should be careful lekhatchilah on account of interposition — up to here. And one must say: this is the opinion of "some say" that a person constitutes an interposition. But one might wonder: how does he resolve the Gemara p. 27 that says R' Yirmiyah would pray behind his rabbi because of "talmid chaver" — behold, even lekhatchilah he would pray behind a person? Therefore it appears to me to resolve: he holds it is not literally "behind" he is speaking, but he meant to say near his rabbi; and on account of "behind his rabbi" — that he does not stand with his rabbi as his equal. And that is what Rashi explained there: not opposite his rabbi with his rabbi — and he appears as if they are equal; and not behind his rabbi, for that is like arrogance (yuhara). For ostensibly literally behind is not yuhara — rather his explanation is that he also stands with his rabbi, only he does not stand equal to him but is drawn back behind him — which in any case is also yuhara, for in any event he stands at his side. But literally behind — always constitutes interposition. And Tosafos there did not understand Rashi's explanation thus, and therefore wrote on him: "and some say" it appears as if bowing to his rabbi — up to here. And in my humble opinion Rashi intended well not to explain thus, for the explanation is not literally behind him but as we said.
