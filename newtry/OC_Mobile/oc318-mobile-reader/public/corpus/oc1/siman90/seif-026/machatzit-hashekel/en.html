(s.k. 26) When he goes out, etc. — it is a mitzvah to run so that he return quickly; and he was precise about this from what we say: Rabbi Chama bar Rabbi Chanina said: one who leaves a synagogue should not take a large step; and above Abaye said: it is not stated except for leaving; but for entering it is a mitzvah to hurry, etc. And the author challenged in Avodah Berurah: what does Abaye teach us — behold it states "one who leaves"; it implies specifically leaving; and it is pleasing to him that Abaye's intent is: it is not stated except for leaving — if his intent is only to leave, then it is forbidden; but for entering, meaning that his leaving and running is in order to enter — then on the contrary it is a mitzvah to run. And Levushei Chamudot rejected his proof, for one may say: if from the precision that it says "one who leaves," it implies specifically in leaving there is prohibition, but for entering there is no prohibition; nevertheless we have not heard that there is a mitzvah to run to enter — it teaches us that for entering there is even a mitzvah; and it is reasonable that Levushei Chamudot forbids running even when leaving, even if his intent is to enter, on account of marit ayin, as Magen Avraham wrote in his name.

And further, etc. — there is a distinction between two reasons: for the first reason — if he leaves when the congregation also leaves and runs in order to enter the beit midrash, it would be forbidden; but for the second reason, even in such a case it is permitted and a mitzvah, as is written in the name of Shelah.
