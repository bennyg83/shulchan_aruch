Seif katan 26, when he leaves, etc. It is a mitzvah to run so he will return quickly. This is inferred from the statement: one who leaves the synagogue should not take large steps; Abaye said this applies only to leaving, but to entering it is a mitzvah to run. The author in Ein Yaakov asked what Abaye teaches, since the wording already says 'one who leaves,' implying specifically leaving. He answers that Abaye means that the prohibition applies only when his intent is merely to leave, but if his leaving and running is in order to enter again, it is a mitzvah to run. Lechem Chamudos rejects the proof, saying that from the wording 'one who leaves' we would know only that leaving is prohibited and entering is not prohibited, but we would not know that there is a mitzvah to run when entering; this is what Abaye teaches.
And further, etc. The practical difference between the two reasons is that according to the first reason, if he leaves when the congregation is also leaving and runs to enter the study hall, it would be forbidden; but according to the second reason, even in such a case it is permitted and a mitzvah, as written in the name of the Shelah.
