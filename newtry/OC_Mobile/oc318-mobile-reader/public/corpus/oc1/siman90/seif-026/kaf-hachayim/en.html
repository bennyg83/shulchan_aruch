(26) [Seif 5] One should not pray in an open place, etc.—for Rav Kahana said: brazen toward me is one who prays in a valley. And Rashi explained "in a valley"—in a valley: when one is in a modest place, awe of the King falls upon him and his heart is broken, so far. Tosafos challenged: but it is written "And Isaac went out to meditate in the field." And they answered: there it deals with Mount Moriah, as we say in Pesachim (ch. 8): not like Isaac who called it a field, etc.; or alternatively, the valley here deals with a valley where people are accustomed to pass through and travelers go, so far—and the implication is that he is called brazen only if he prays in a valley where people pass and travelers go, for because of this awe of the King does not fall upon him; but if he prays in a valley where people do not pass, awe of the King falls upon him there too and he is not called brazen. And see Beit Yosef, who challenged this answer of Tosafos. And for what Eliyah Rabbah wrote, and likewise we lean toward Taz seder katan 2, see there. And likewise Eliyah Rabbah note 4—that in Agudah he ruled like Tosafos' second answer, that in a field where no one passes or rests it is permitted, and it is fitting to rely on this, so far. And what Hafla'ah wrote note 6 s.v. further—he analyzed from the Tanna of the house of Eliyahu that in a valley, even if there are no passers or resters, one should not pray because of the ways of idolaters—and not like Tosafos—is not mandatory; it may be said this applies specifically if he goes initially to pray there even though he has another place more modest than it, or if he goes to pray there regularly; but incidentally, or if he has no place more modest than it, he may pray there if there are no passers or resters, like the words of Tosafos.

(26) And what Tosafos wrote in answer 1—that there it deals with Mount Moriah—Bach wrote, meaning it was called by the name "field" but was not open, rather surrounded by partitions. And likewise Taz seder katan 2. But Mateh Yehudah note 2 explained that Mount Moriah is different, for it is the site of the Temple and a place of atonement—Adam was created from it, there Noah built the altar and offered his sacrifice, and there Abraham built the altar and bound Isaac his son, as Rambam wrote chapter 2 of Hilchos Beit HaBechirah, see there. Therefore, even though it was open, since it is a holy place and this is none other than the house of the Omnipresent, and the Shekhinah of our strength has never moved from there forever—therefore awe of the Shekhinah is upon the worshiper there and his heart is certainly broken, unlike another open place where it is greatly open and forbidden because it is brazenness toward Heaven and he cannot have intent at all, so far. And likewise explained Nahar Shalom note 20 and 22, Hafla'ah note 6—and he wrote that so too wrote the rabbi Yitzchak Yeranen, and so it is in Zohar Parashat Bo 194b, see there.
