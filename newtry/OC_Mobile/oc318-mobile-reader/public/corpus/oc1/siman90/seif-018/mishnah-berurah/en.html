Provided that he prays with ten. Even if in the synagogue there is 'in a multitude of people is the King's glory,' the study hall is preferable. This applies to one who learns there, so that he not interrupt his learning to go to the synagogue; otherwise, a multitude of people is preferable, as the Peri Megadim wrote. It appears obvious that this is specifically a study hall fixed for an individual to learn, whose sanctity is not so fixed, as implied by the Magen Avraham in siman 153. But a study hall fixed for public Torah study certainly has greater sanctity than a synagogue, and it is better to pray there with ten than in a synagogue with a larger crowd, even for one who does not learn there at all, as the Gemara states that Hashem loves the gates marked in halacha more than synagogues. So it implies from several later authorities.
And he is not interrupted, etc. Meaning, except for necessary matters for his body and learning. Therefore he should not go to the synagogue even if there are not ten there, because he loses from his learning during the walk. See the Beur HaGra, who wrote that in this case, where his Torah is his occupation, even according to the first opinion ten are not required. All the more so one teaching Torah publicly with students should not interrupt his learning to go to the synagogue.
That they should not learn, etc. For they will not judge him favorably by attributing his praying alone to his learning; rather, they will say that he is not concerned for prayer, will make a kal vachomer for themselves, and will not be concerned for prayer at all.
At the time the congregation. And likewise during Torah reading; see siman 146. Ma'amar Mordechai wrote that if he already prayed and is standing there only to answer Kaddishim and Kedushos from those who recite a shortened service or repeat the whole prayer, he may occupy himself with Torah and need not be concerned. This is also evident from the language of the gloss, which speaks specifically of separating from the congregation.
