(55) "Provided he prays with ten"—and even if in the beit knesset there is a majority of people, nevertheless the beit midrash is preferable; and this applies to one who studies there, so that he not be interrupted from his study to go to the beit knesset. But otherwise a majority of people is preferable, so Peri Megadim. It appears straightforward that this is specifically in a beit midrash established for an individual's study, whose holiness is not so established, as implied by Magen Avraham in siman 153, seif 1, that here we deal with this; but a beit midrash established for many to study Torah—certainly its holiness is greater than a beit knesset, and it is better to pray there with ten than in a beit knesset that has a majority of people, even for one who does not study there at all, as stated in the Gemara that the the Omnipresent loves gates marked with halachah more than synagogues, etc.; and so is implied by several later authorities.

(56) "And he is not interrupted" etc.—meaning only for necessary matters for his bodily needs and his study; therefore he need not go to the beit knesset even if there is no ten there, because he would be interrupted from his study at the time of going. See in Biur haGra who wrote that in this manner, when his Torah is his occupation, even per the first view we do not require ten; and all the more so for one who studies Torah publicly with students—he need not interrupt his study to go to synagogue.

(57) "That they not learn" etc.—for they will not judge him favorably to attribute it to his Torah that he prays alone for his study; they will only say he is not concerned for prayer, and they will judge themselves by a fortiori and not be concerned for prayer at all.

(58) At the time the congregation—and similarly at the time of Kaddish and Kedushah; see siman 146. Maamar Mordechai wrote that if he already prayed and is not standing there except to answer Kaddish and Kedushah from those who spread Shema or repeat the entire prayer, he engages in Torah and is not concerned; and so is proven from the wording of the gloss, that we deal specifically with one who separates from the congregation, etc.; see there.
