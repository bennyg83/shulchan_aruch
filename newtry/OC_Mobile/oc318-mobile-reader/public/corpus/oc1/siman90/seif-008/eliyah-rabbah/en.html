(h) But if he is praying, etc. Sheyarei Knesset HaGedolah wrote: I saw in Constantinople many communities — their courtyards are to the side of the east, their faces toward the east, and their backs toward the eastern wall in the synagogue; and it appears he holds that a courtyard is like a house — up to here. And from Rav Hai Gaon in Beit Yosef it does not imply thus; and perhaps Rav Hai Gaon holds that even in a house it is forbidden, and we do not rule like him — only that Beit Yosef did not write them as a dispute. And what the Levush wrote "or in a courtyard," etc. — so is explained in Beit Yosef: specifically when he stands in an open space and it is clear from all around him — that is forbidden; and therefore I wonder on Magen Avraham who wrote that in the courtyard of the synagogue it is forbidden. And it is possible he holds that specifically a courtyard that is not designated for the synagogue is permitted; but this is not implied in Beit Yosef and Levush. And similarly it is logical that it is comparable to a courtyard — even if you challenge from where he gets this.
