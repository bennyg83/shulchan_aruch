But if he is praying, etc. Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah wrote: I saw in Constantinople many communities whose courtyards are to the east, and they pray facing east with their backs to the eastern wall of the synagogue. It appears that they hold that a courtyard is like a house. From Rav Hai cited by the Beit Yosef it does not seem so; perhaps Rav Hai holds that even in a house it is forbidden, and we do not rule like him, though the Beit Yosef did not present them as disagreeing. What the Levush writes, 'or in a courtyard,' is explicit in the Beit Yosef, that only when he stands in an open area free on all sides is it forbidden. I am therefore surprised at the Magen Avraham, who wrote that the synagogue courtyard is forbidden. Perhaps he holds that only a courtyard not designated for the synagogue is permitted, but this does not imply from the Beit Yosef and Levush, and logic also compares it to a courtyard; though if you ask how he knows this, it requires thought.
