1) [Se'if 1] One who prays should not stand, etc. It is explicit in a baraisa that there is no haughtiness before the Omnipresent; that is, one must present himself as lowly, submissive, and humbled, as we learned: One may stand to pray only in a state of solemnity. Pri Chadash 1. Levush writes that the prayer of one who lowers and humbles himself is more readily accepted. Sefer Chassidim 773 writes that if, while a person is praying, the joy of loving Hashem suddenly enters his heart because Hashem desires to fulfill his will, this is the meaning of, 'How beautiful and how pleasant is love amid delights,' and it is written, 'Then you shall delight in Hashem,' etc.; see there.
2) There, in the gloss: Even if they are not three handbreadths high. Beis Yosef in the name of Mahari Abuhav. Beis Yosef explains in his name that the concern is distraction: he worries that he may fall and will not concentrate on his prayer. Perishah 1, however, writes that below three handbreadths there is no fear during prayer. Rather, the meaning is that one may not pray upon a bed or any utensil, even below three handbreadths, because it constitutes an interposition, since prayer must resemble the Temple service; in the service, a utensil between the person and the ground would constitute an interposition, as Tur writes below in siman 98. It then says that even an elevated place consisting of natural ground, where interposition is inapplicable, is forbidden. This prohibition applies only when it is three handbreadths high; below three handbreadths it is considered attached to the ground and there is no elevation. See there. See the next paragraph and below, siman 98:26, with G-d's help.
3) There, in the gloss: Even if they are not three handbreadths high, etc. Levush and Solel Boneh in Makor Chaim 1 write likewise. Bach, however, writes that a bed, chair, or bench is forbidden only when three handbreadths high, but below three handbreadths is permitted. He concludes that this is the ruling we adopt, and that Shulchan Aruch rules so, unlike Rama's gloss; see there. Taz 1 also appears to hold this view. Maamar Mordechai 1, however, disputes Bach and writes that one should not be lenient at all. Regarding Bach's assertion that Shulchan Aruch rules this way, he writes that there is no indication of this. Maran may well agree with Mahari Abuhav, whom he did not dispute in Beis Yosef. In Shulchan Aruch 1 he copied only the language of the Tosefta, while in se'if 2 he states that the prescribed height of an elevated place is three handbreadths, etc. This implies that the three-handbreadth measure applies only to an elevated place. See there regarding Mahari Abuhav's words. He further writes that Rambam can also be understood in accordance with Mahari Abuhav and that Taz's words are not conclusive; see there. Lechem Chamudos, Berachos 1:57, likewise forbids a bed, chair, or bench even below three handbreadths, as do Eliyah Rabbah 1, who writes that this is implied by Maharil, Hilchos Tefillah; Erekh HaShulchan 1; Chayei Adam 22:4; Shalmei Tzibbur, page 109a; and Siddur Beis Yaakov, laws of the Amidah 20. See also below, siman 98:26.
3) There: Even if they are not three handbreadths high. One unaccustomed to wearing shuaikis may not pray in them, because he is distracted by the possibility of falling. Pri Chadash 1; Yad Aharon, Hagahos Beis Yosef. One should likewise take care not to stand upon anything slippery, lest concern that he may fall distract him. This applies even to an unsteady board if it distracts him. The same applies if he wears kabkab footwear that distracts one who is unaccustomed to it, and to similar cases. Ben Ish Chai, Yisro 3.
4) There: Nor upon an elevated place. One should likewise not stand upon cushions to pray, because he appears haughty. Levush 619:1, cited by Magen Avraham 614:3. Machatzis HaShekel writes there that even if they are not three handbreadths high, he appears haughty; see there. One should similarly take care not to stand on anything that appears haughty. It is permitted, however, to stand upon grasses spread in the synagogue or the like, or upon a mat. Chayei Adam, ibid.
5) Regarding the practice of praying upon a sofa - beds made of wood or stone that divide half the house like a platform and are fixed to the floor and wall - it appears that they are considered like the ground. They do not have the status of a bed with respect to placing food beneath them, nor with respect to a mourner's obligation to sit only upon the ground. When people assemble in a house to pray, however, and all or most are below, it appears improper for individuals to stand above unless circumstances leave no alternative. Halachos Ketanos 2:122. Yad Aharon cites this in Hagahos HaTur and writes: 'I see no concern in this, and I have seen several great authorities who were not concerned.' Siddur Beis Yaakov, ibid., paragraph 21, cites this and concludes that this is the primary ruling.
6) There: Nor upon an elevated place. If one transgressed and prayed upon an elevated place, he need not pray again. Pri Megadim, Mishbetzos Zahav 1, in Rambam's name, Hilchos Tefillah 5:1, 6, and 7; see there. Chayei Adam writes likewise at the beginning of the siman.
7) There: Unless he was elderly, etc. This means that it is difficult for him to descend from the bed. Magen Avraham 2. That is, he is standing upon the bed. It may also mean that it is difficult for him to stand on the ground without a cushion. If he cannot stand, however, he may pray while seated or lying upon the bed. Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham 2. See below, siman 94:6 and 9, and our comments there, with G-d's help.
8) There: Or he intended to make his voice heard by the congregation. In all these cases, whether he is ill or elderly, or is a shaliach tzibbur who must make his voice heard by the congregation, he may pray even upon a bed, chair, or bench, as Beis Yosef writes. This is permitted even according to Perishah, whose explanation of the prohibition upon a bed, chair, or bench because of an interposition we cited above in paragraph 2. One may say that his ruling applies specifically where another option exists, but under constrained or unavoidable circumstances it is permitted, as Rambam writes in Hilchos Tefillah 5:1. See Eliyah Rabbah 1.
9) There: Unless he was elderly, etc. This implies that even if the place is ten handbreadths high or more, it is permitted, according to the circumstances.
