In Magen Avraham, se'if katan 30: It is as though he sold him. The prohibition of lo sechanem does not apply, as explained there. Ran could not have answered that this is a psik reisha that he does not desire; he does not intend to free the slave from service, but only to include him in the quorum; because a psik reisha is treated as full intent. The fact that he does not desire the result merely makes it comparable to a melacha not needed for its own purpose, a category that does not apply to other prohibitions. However, as I wrote in my novellae to Shabbos, Rashi on 75a distinguishes between a psik reisha about which one is indifferent and one whose result he actively does not desire. Where he is indifferent, it is treated as intent and only classified as a melacha not needed for its own purpose. Where he does not desire the result, it is treated as unintentional and R' Shimon permits it even for other prohibitions that do not carry death liability. See Ran's novellae at the beginning of the chapter HaNechenakin. Ran could therefore have answered that it is permitted as an undesired psik reisha.
