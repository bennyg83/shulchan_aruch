(1) "And even" etc.—because when he stands on them he is distracted and fears lest he fall and cannot concentrate in his prayer. Therefore what they permitted above for an elderly or sick person—even though there is no issue of height—nevertheless would be forbidden to stand because of concern; the reason must be that the Sages did not trouble him and permitted him to pray on them in whatever way suits him, sitting or standing. Or one can say that there we deal with a case where there is no concern.

(2) They are not three handbreadths high—the Bach and Taz permit, but Eliyah Rabbah rules like the Shulchan Aruch.

(3) "And not on" etc.—because there is no height before Hashem, as it says "From the depths I called You, Hashem."

(4) Elderly or sick—for whom it is difficult to descend from the bed.

(5) To make himself heard to the congregation—for he may stand on a high place, even on a chair or bench, and this is not [subject to] height. Some later authorities wrote that where there is concern, even to make himself heard to the congregation it is forbidden. Magen Avraham wrote that now the practice is that the place where the shaliach tzibbur stands is lower than the rest of the beit knesset, because of "From the depths I called You," and therefore the shaliach tzibbur is called in all places "descends before the ark."
