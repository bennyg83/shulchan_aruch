Even so, etc. Because when he stands on them he is distracted and fears lest he fall, and he will not be able to concentrate in his prayer. According to this, what is permitted nearby for an elderly or sick person, although there is no haughtiness there, should nevertheless be forbidden because of fear. One must say that the Sages did not burden him, and permitted him to pray on them in whatever way is better for him, sitting or standing. Alternatively, it speaks there of cases where there is no fear.
They are not three high. The Bach and Taz permit, but the Eliyah Rabbah rules like the Shulchan Aruch.
And not on top of, etc. Because there is no haughtiness before the Omnipresent, as it says, 'Out of the depths I called You, Hashem.'
An elderly or sick person. For whom it is difficult to descend from the bed.
To make himself heard to the congregation. He may stand on a high place, and even on a chair or bench, and this is not haughtiness. Some later authorities wrote that where there is fear and distraction, even to make himself heard to the congregation is forbidden. The Magen Avraham wrote that nowadays the place where the shaliach tzibbur stands is lower than the rest of the synagogue because of 'Out of the depths I called You, Hashem'; therefore the shaliach tzibbur is everywhere called one who descends before the ark.
