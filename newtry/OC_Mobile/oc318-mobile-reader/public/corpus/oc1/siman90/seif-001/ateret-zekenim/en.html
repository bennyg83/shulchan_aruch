One who prays should not stand on a bed nor on a chair, etc. That is, a bed, chair, and bench — even if they are not three handbreadths high, as the Rama wrote — because of distraction, as he is distracted lest he fall and not concentrate in his prayer. But other things must be three handbreadths high. And some say it is forbidden only on something ten handbreadths high (Beit Yosef, R' Yitzchak in the name of Orach Chayyim). And one should be stringent: even at three handbreadths it is forbidden to pray upon them (Beit Yosef in the name of the Rambam). And some say that even a bed, chair, and bench are forbidden only if three handbreadths high, but less than three handbreadths is permitted, for there is no distraction at less than three (my teacher the Bach). And the reason is that one should not pray in a high place, as it is said, "From the depths I called to You, O Lord," etc. Therefore the custom is that the place where the chazan stands to pray is a low place in the beit hakneset (Agudah and above, siman 98).
