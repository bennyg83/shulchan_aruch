Even if they are not three high, etc. The Bach and Nachalas Tzvi disagree, holding that if they are not three handbreadths high it is permitted, like a low place, since the reason for a chair or bench is only haughtiness; see there. The Taz likewise challenged this from the statement that one who enters to visit a sick person should not sit on a bed or on a high place. If even less than three is considered haughtiness when sitting on a chair before the Shechinah, the reason of haughtiness should have been stated here regarding standing as well. In my humble opinion this is not difficult, for sitting on even a small chair involves haughtiness, unlike standing. Also, the Gemara itself can be read as applying the reason of haughtiness to the whole matter, and Maharil, Hilchos Tefillah, implies that it is forbidden. Moreover, the Derishah wrote that standing on an implement is forbidden because it interrupts between him and the ground, and if so, even less than three is certainly forbidden, as the Levush writes below in siman 98. Even the Bach, who disagrees there regarding interruption, concludes that it is a mitzvah to be exact that nothing intervene; see there. What the Taz wrote at length on the Beit Yosef there, I did not grasp, for one who examines the Beit Yosef will see that this is indeed his explanation. It appears that when the Levush at the end of this seif permits for the shaliach tzibbur, he means on a high place, but standing on an implement is forbidden even for the shaliach tzibbur for the reasons mentioned. A high place permitted for a shaliach tzibbur appears to be permitted even if ten handbreadths high, and so it implies from Kol Bo.
