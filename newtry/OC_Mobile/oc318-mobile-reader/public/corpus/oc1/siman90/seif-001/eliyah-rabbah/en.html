(a) And even if they are not three handbreadths high, etc. The Bach and Nachalas Tzvi disagree that if they are not three handbreadths high it is permitted as in a low place, for also on a chair and bench the reason is only on account of height — see there. And similarly the Taz challenged from that which we say: one who enters to visit a sick person should not sit on top of a bed, etc., and on a high place, etc.; and if so, even less than three would be considered height when sitting on a chair before the Divine Presence. If so, he should have explained here also regarding standing that the reason is on account of height — up to here. And in my humble opinion this is not difficult at all, for he holds that in sitting there is height on a small chair but not in standing. And moreover, in the Gemara it is truly possible to explain thus that he cited the reason of height for all of it. And similarly in Maharil Laws of Prayer it seems to me it is forbidden. And moreover, thus wrote the Darishah that the reason it is forbidden on an object is on account of an interposition between him and the ground; and if so, certainly even less than three is forbidden; and so wrote the Levush later siman 98. Also the Bach, who disagrees later on the law of interposition — nevertheless he concludes that it is a mitzvah to be careful that there not be an interposing object — see there. And what the Taz expounded at length on Beit Yosef there — I have not grasped his final opinion, for in truth the explanation is so in Beit Yosef for one who examines. And it appears that what the Levush said at the end of this seif that it is permitted for the prayer leader — that is in a high place; but on top of an object, even for the prayer leader, it is forbidden for the reasons mentioned. And a high place that is permitted for the prayer leader — it appears even at a height of ten it is permitted, and so is implied in Kolbo.
