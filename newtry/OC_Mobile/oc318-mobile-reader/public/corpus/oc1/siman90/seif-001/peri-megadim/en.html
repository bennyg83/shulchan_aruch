And even. See Taz. Mahari Abuhav holds that regarding vessels, because of fear of falling, even a height of less than three tefachim is forbidden. Even to make himself heard by the congregation is forbidden. Only upon ground or the like elevated three tefachim, where there is no fear, is it permitted to make himself heard. See Nechalat Tzvi; according to what I wrote, this is understandable. The discussion of visiting the sick in Shabbos 12b is based upon a chair's not being nullified even when less than three tefachim high and upon its elevation. The same should apply here because of elevation, and standing upon a chair to make oneself heard should likewise be permitted. Yet one cannot simply say that a chair certainly possesses elevation; a minimum measure is necessarily required, namely three tefachim. Why did the teaching not merely say, 'He should not stand in a high place'? One might have thought that a vessel constitutes an independent domain and is permitted; it therefore teaches otherwise. See our novellae to Berachos 10b: R' Yehoshua ben Levi says, 'One should not stand in a high place'; the supporting baraita states, 'One should not stand upon a chair, etc., or in a high place.' Since R' Yehoshua ben Levi mentions only a high place, it apparently follows that a chair too is prohibited because of elevation, in accordance with Taz. See Bach. One should investigate the law if a person transgressed and prayed in a high place. In siman 71, seif 7, one who prayed in a cemetery did not fulfill his obligation. We have likewise expressed uncertainty regarding a rabbinic prohibition; see siman 76, seif 8, and siman 185. Must he repeat the prayer here or not? See siman 98, seif 2. Tur's statement that nothing should intervene between him and the ground is not indispensable in the sense that one must repeat the prayer; rather, the rule is an ideal enhancement of the mitzvah. See Perishah here, who writes that in siman 98 Tur derived the prohibition against standing on a chair from here because it is an interposition, so that even less than three tefachim is forbidden. See Taz, siman 98, se'if katan 2; it is implied there that a height of three tefachim is also problematic because of interposition. If so, how is standing there to make oneself heard permitted? At first glance, naturally elevated ground, such as a hill or hollow, involves no interposition. A chair, however, should indeed be forbidden even when less than three tefachim high. Yet this follows Tur's own approach here, under which a chair is forbidden because of elevation only when three tefachim high. Granted, it also constitutes an interposition, and therefore according to one who is concerned for Tur's ruling in siman 98, standing upon a vessel even less than three tefachim high to make oneself heard should be forbidden. On the other hand, within three tefachim even a vessel may not constitute an interposition, because it is considered like solid ground. Flooring is different: since one may not add to the Temple floor, a vessel upon it certainly constitutes an interposition. Rambam, chapter 5 of Hilchot Tefillah, halacha 1, implies that transgressing this rule does not invalidate the prayer.
The humble writer says: Since I have several thoughts here, I decided to set them out. First: regarding Taz's statement that only a high place should be forbidden, perhaps Mahari Abuhav intended to include all the cases. The text should then say, 'He should not stand in a high place; upon a bed, chair, or bench.' It need not repeat the word 'not,' and it appropriately places 'a high place' first. Second: regarding the proof from visiting the sick in Shabbos 12b, it is true that Rif, Rambam in chapter 14 of Hilchot Avel, halacha 6, and Rosh have the same reading as here. The Mechaber in Yoreh De'ah 335, seif 3, however, omits 'a high place' and mentions only a bed, chair, and bench, following Ran's explanation that the concern is being above the sick person's head. When the sick person lies upon a bed, one may sit upon a chair. Thus, the prohibited elevation there can be less than three tefachim: any position above the sick person's head is forbidden. Third: Tur presents a difficulty. Why was elevation prohibited when it is permitted in order to make oneself heard? And why should interposition not be relevant, which would forbid it even to make oneself heard? Fourth: regarding adding to the Temple floor, Zevachim 25a derives the rule from the priestly garments; see there. Zevachim 112 does not list interposition among the differences between a public altar and an individual altar. The tanna may have omitted some matters; see Olat Tamid. Interposition is learned from the priestly garments, whereas an altar outside the Temple has no priestly garments. This is not the place for that discussion. Fifth: why does the Mechaber in seif 2 specify a place of four amos? A place ten tefachim high and four tefachim wide constitutes another domain regarding words of Torah, as stated in siman 79, seif 2, and Magen Avraham, ot 5; all the more so should it here. Why is it not treated as an independent domain? This can be answered. With God's help, see what I write further on Magen Avraham concerning this. See below, siman 345, seif 5: regarding Shabbos, a vessel less than three tefachim high is considered like solid ground. Nevertheless, one sees that the rule there cannot be imported here, as proven by a high place ten tefachim high and four wide, as I wrote here. This is not the place to elaborate.
