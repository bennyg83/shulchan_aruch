And even so—see Taz: Mahari holds regarding vessels that on account of concern [of falling], less than three tefachim is also forbidden, and even to make himself heard to the congregation it is forbidden—only a high place of three tefachim on the ground and the like, where there is no concern, is permitted to make himself heard (see Netziv and what Maharshal wrote). And on Shabbos in Bikkur Cholim 12b—the reason regarding a chair is that even less than three tefachim is not nullified, and on account of height likewise here on account of height it is permitted to make himself heard on a chair as well; but it is impossible to say regarding a chair "all the more so" there would also be height—for perforce a measure is required, and that is three tefachim. And that which [the mishnah] only said "one shall not stand in a high place alone"—this means a vessel independently constitutes a separate domain and is permitted, as he teaches us. And see in our novellae Berachos 10b: Rabbah bar Chanah—"one shall not stand in a high place"; the Tosefta—"one shall not stand on a chair" etc., and not in a high place—and Rabbah bar Chanah holds "high place" alone, from which it appears that a chair too is on account of height, like the Taz, and so it appears to be correct. And there is room to inquire: if one transgressed and prayed in a high place, what is the law—for in siman 71 seif 7 in a synagogue he does not fulfill [his obligation] and we were uncertain about a rabbinic [matter] there as well; see siman 76 seif 8 and Kaf HaChaim. If so, whether one returns and prays here or not. And see siman 98 sec. 2—what Tur wrote, that one should not press between himself and the ground—is not for invalidation but a preferred mitzvah; there is no explanation of invalidation to return and pray, but rather the law is invalidation only [when lacking] a preferred mitzvah. And see Perishah here that Tur in sec. 98 learned from here regarding standing on a chair on account of interposition, and even less than three tefachim is forbidden; and Taz in 98b—and it appears there regarding height of three [handbreadths] on account of interposition as well. And accordingly, how is making himself heard permitted? And seemingly ground in a high place is a hill and depression—for there is no interposition—and a chair likewise even less than three tefachim is forbidden; rather one goes according to his view here, that for Tur a chair too specifically [requires] height of three tefachim on account of height, and if so there is interposition in it, and to make himself heard—for one who is concerned for Tur in sec. 98—even less than three tefachim in a vessel is forbidden on account of interposition, for loose ground is considered like ground even in a vessel; but pavement is different, for it is forbidden to add onto it—all the more so interposition through a vessel. And in R' Meir ch. 45 Mishnah Torah ch. 8—it would appear that transgression does not invalidate.
