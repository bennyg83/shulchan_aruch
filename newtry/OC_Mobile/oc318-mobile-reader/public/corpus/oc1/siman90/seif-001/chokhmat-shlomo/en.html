Seif 1 — One who prays, etc. NB: the law of prayer — whether there is a Torah obligation in it altogether or it is only rabbinic — the Rambam and Ramban disagree on this. And it appears to my humble opinion that now there is clear proof that its essence is from the Torah, from chapter 3 of Sukkah (38b), where R' Zeira said there: always as you said initially; and that which was difficult for you — this from the Torah, this rabbinic — the musaf of Yom Tov, two are rabbinic, etc. And it is still difficult: why does he challenge? Behold it is proven in Eruvin chapter 3 that there is a distinction in a matter whose essence is from the Torah or not; and likewise in tractate Mikvaos regarding one who went down to immerse, etc. And if so, here too why does he challenge? Behold the lulav in any case its essence is from the Torah, for on the first day of Yom Tov its essence is from the Torah, and likewise on the other days in the Temple; but prayer its essence is rabbinic — therefore if they began they do not interrupt; and perforce it is proven that prayer too is essentially from the Torah per the Rambam's view, and it comes out well; examine closely.
