And even if they are not three tefachim high. This is from Mahari"a in Beit Yosef, and his proof from what the baraita teaches: one may not stand on a bed, etc., and afterward it says and not on a high place — and he did not combine them in one clause. When it says not on a high place, it seems he comes to teach us that a bed, bench, and vessels are forbidden even without three tefachim of height, because one must be concerned about disturbance lest he fall and not have proper intent in his prayer. I wondered about this, for in the first chapter of Shabbos the Rif and Rosh cite similarly regarding one who enters to visit the sick — he should not sit on a bed, etc., nor on a high place — and there it is not fitting to say so. And if your soul wishes to say that there too on a bed, etc., is forbidden even without three tefachim, for reasons of honor of the Divine Presence by the sick — for he appears as though elevated when sitting on a chair, etc. — if so he should have explained here too regarding standing that the reason is due to height. But according to my humble opinion there is no proof here for Mahari"a from what the tanna did not combine them in one statement, for if he had said only on a high place I would have said that on vessels is permitted because they are considered a domain of their own, as said afterward in enclosed partitions where it is permitted — it teaches us that not so. And if so we would not have needed to give a measure for sitting on a chair, but nevertheless one must give a measure, for certainly on a chair of any height it is not forbidden — therefore certainly its measure is at least three tefachim.
