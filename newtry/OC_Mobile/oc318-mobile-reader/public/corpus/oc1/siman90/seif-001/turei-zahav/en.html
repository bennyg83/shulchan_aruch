Even if they are not three tefachim high. This is from Mahari Abuhav, cited by Beit Yosef. His proof is that the Baraisa first teaches that one should not stand upon a bed and the like, and afterward says, 'nor upon a high place.' Since the Tanna did not combine them into one clause by saying merely, 'not upon a high place,' it appears that he intended to teach that a bed, bench, or other utensil is forbidden even when it is not three tefachim high, because one may be preoccupied with fear of falling and fail to concentrate on his prayer. I am astonished by this, for in the first chapter of Shabbos the Rif and Rosh record virtually the same formulation: one who enters to visit the sick should not sit upon a bed and the like, nor upon a high place. There, this explanation is inapplicable. Should you wish to say that there too sitting upon a bed and the like is forbidden even when it is not three tefachim high, out of respect for the Shechinah that rests near the sick person, because sitting on a chair appears haughty, then the reason here regarding standing should likewise have been explained as haughtiness. In my opinion, however, Mahari Abuhav has no proof from the Tanna's failure to combine them into one clause. Had the Tanna said only that one may not stand upon a high place, I might have thought that standing upon utensils is permitted because they constitute an independent domain, as the ruling later permits a place surrounded by partitions. He therefore teaches that it is not permitted. If so, no minimum height would have been needed for sitting upon a chair. Nevertheless, some measure must be specified, for certainly a chair of any minute height is not prohibited. It is therefore clear that its minimum measure is at least three tefachim.
