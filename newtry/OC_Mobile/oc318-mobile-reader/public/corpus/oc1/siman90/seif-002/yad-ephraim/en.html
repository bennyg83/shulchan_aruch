Magen Avraham, se'if katan 36: “And he proves this from Tosafos, but his words are not conclusive,” etc. Many understood Bach's question as identical to Turei Zahav's question and were forced into a strained explanation of Magen Avraham's words. In truth, this is entirely unnecessary, as is clear to one who examines Bach. Bach asks that Tosafos came to state a practical ruling; what difference does it make to the Talmudic law that the beds happened to be made that way? He further asks about Tosafos' words, “A bed apparently is not permanent.” This implies that Tosafos introduced this point, although it is explicit in the Talmud. Bach therefore concludes that the same law applies to our beds. Magen Avraham responds that there is no proof from Tosafos. When Tosafos write, “It apparently is not permanent,” their intent is that the questioner considered it nonpermanent and therefore raised his question. Tosafos may indeed be discussing the beds of their time and did not write this as a practical ruling, but only as an explanation of the questioner: this is how the matter appeared to the questioner. Tosafos hold that an ordinary bed in their time was also designated for sitting, as Beit Yosef writes.
