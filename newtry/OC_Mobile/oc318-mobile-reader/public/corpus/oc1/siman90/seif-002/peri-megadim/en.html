Regarding—in Taz's words, the second view: a leniency in an open valley where passersby are not common is permitted; the first view—only in a modest place where one's heart is humble, so it appears to be correct. And he wrote further that Isaac went to pray, and it is not written in another expression to say he hid himself under one of the bushes; and accordingly on the road when there is a forest one stands there and prays if it does not cause him difficulty in delaying his journey, and it is called a modest place. And from Berachos 3a—that he said he should pray on the road and replied he fears passersby will interrupt—there is no proof for the second view of Tosafos; and as Beit Yosef understood, we need not the first view of a modest place—nevertheless since Elijah told him he should pray on the road, better than entering a ruin, he necessarily replied that he fears passersby, and replied to him "correct." And behold a dark house and an open valley—seemingly both are equal on account of humility; and this is what he said: an open valley is called brazen because one does not humble himself to conceal himself before his Creator—even though "if a man hides in hiding places" etc., see Netziv (what he wrote regarding windows that one's heart should be humble when looking heavenward—for the author one need not receive Jerusalem). And it is difficult why the author omitted that one who prays in an open valley is brazen. A blind person—it makes no difference to him whether a dark house or a window not facing east; but an open valley and a dark house are also good for a blind person in a modest place. On the night of Yom Kippur, for the Maariv prayer one should not close the windows in the synagogue with curtains on account of receiving Jerusalem.
