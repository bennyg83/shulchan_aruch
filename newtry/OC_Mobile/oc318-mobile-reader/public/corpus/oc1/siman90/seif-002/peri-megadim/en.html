In a place. See Taz. According to Tosafos' second answer, there is a leniency in a valley where people are not commonly present, and it is permitted. According to their first answer, however, one must pray specifically in a secluded place so that his heart will be humbled. See Bach. He further wrote that Scripture says, 'Yitzchak went out to converse in the field,' and did not use another expression, to indicate that he concealed himself beneath one of the shrubs. Accordingly, while traveling, if there is a forest, one should stand there and pray, provided the resulting delay to his journey is not difficult for him, for it is considered a secluded place. No proof for Tosafos' second answer, as Beit Yosef understood it, can be brought from Berachos 3a, where Eliyahu asked why he did not pray on the road and he answered that he feared travelers would interrupt him. Even according to the first answer, which requires a secluded place, since Eliyahu told him that it would have been preferable to pray on the road rather than enter a ruin, he necessarily replied that he feared travelers, and Eliyahu answered that he should have recited a short prayer. Now, a dark house and a valley would appear at first glance to be equal in that both promote humility. This is incorrect, for one who prays in a valley is called brazen because he does not humble himself by concealing himself before his Creator, even though 'Should a person hide in concealed places...' See Nezach Yisrael. Regarding what was written concerning windows, that one's heart is humbled when he looks toward heaven, according to the Mechaber one need not face Jerusalem. I do not know why the Mechaber omitted the ruling that one who prays in a valley is brazen. For a blind person, there is no practical difference between a dark house and a window not facing east, but even for a blind person a valley and a dark house are preferable insofar as they are secluded places. On the night of Yom Kippur, during Maariv, one should not close the synagogue windows with boards, because one must face Jerusalem.
