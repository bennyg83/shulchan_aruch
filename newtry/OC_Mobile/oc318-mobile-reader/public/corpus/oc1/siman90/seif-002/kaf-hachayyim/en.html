2) There, in the gloss: Even if they are not three handbreadths high. Beis Yosef in the name of Mahari Abuhav. Beis Yosef explains in his name that the concern is distraction: he worries that he may fall and will not concentrate on his prayer. Perishah 1, however, writes that below three handbreadths there is no fear during prayer. Rather, the meaning is that one may not pray upon a bed or any utensil, even below three handbreadths, because it constitutes an interposition, since prayer must resemble the Temple service; in the service, a utensil between the person and the ground would constitute an interposition, as Tur writes below in siman 98. It then says that even an elevated place consisting of natural ground, where interposition is inapplicable, is forbidden. This prohibition applies only when it is three handbreadths high; below three handbreadths it is considered attached to the ground and there is no elevation. See there.
