10) [Se'if 2] The prescribed height of an elevated place is three handbreadths. The reason is that anything below three handbreadths is considered attached to the ground and is permitted. Beis Yosef; Levush.
11) There: If it was three handbreadths high and measured four amos by four amos, etc. If it has sufficient area to contain a square of four amos by four amos, it is permitted even if its length exceeds its width or it is circular, as Maran rules in Shulchan Aruch, Yoreh Deah 286:13. Although Rosh there holds that it must actually be four amos by four amos, as stated in Beis Yosef there and Shach 23, one may say that here he agrees. On the side where the person stands to pray, however, there must be enough space for him to take three steps. See below, siman 123:3, and our comments there, with G-d's help.
12) There: Likewise, if it was surrounded by partitions, etc. The plain meaning is that the partitions must have the prescribed height, namely ten handbreadths.
13) There: Likewise, if it was surrounded by partitions, etc. This implies that it must be surrounded by partitions on all four sides. Rambam's language in Hilchos Tefillah 5:7 is likewise, 'surrounded by partitions on all its sides.' Beis Yosef and Bach in siman 345 also imply that 'surrounded by partitions' means on four sides; see there. Ben Ish Chai, Yisro 3, however, writes that three partitions suffice, as does Shulchan Tahor HaZahav 4; see Shoshanas HaMelech in the margin there. In my humble opinion, one should not be lenient with only three partitions and no tzuras hapesach on the fourth side, unless some unavoidable circumstance requires him to pray in that place.
14) There: Even if it does not measure four by four, etc. If it is not four amos wide and is not surrounded by partitions, it is forbidden for the attendant or the like to stand there and pray. Nevertheless, one may argue that since this is the attendant's regular place, there is no haughtiness. Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham 3; see there. See se'if 3.
15) There: It is permitted to pray there, etc. Raavad, commenting on Rambam, Hilchos Tefillah 5:7, writes that in all these cases, if one can descend and pray below, he should do so. Although Maran writes there in Kesef Mishneh that even if he can descend he need not do so, one who is stringent and descends in order to satisfy all opinions is worthy of blessing.
