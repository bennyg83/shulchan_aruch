Seif katan 4, or windows. It is stated in the Gemara, etc. Although one needs, etc. This requires explanation: what novelty did he find in the Talmud more than in the Shulchan Aruch, which brings the Gemara's words? Also, his question apparently applies to the Gemara itself. In my humble opinion, words are missing, and it should read: It is stated in the Gemara, end of 34b, and Rashi explains that they cause him to direct his heart, for he looks toward Heaven and his heart is humbled.
And although one needs, etc. This is the question of Mahari Abuhav, cited by the Beit Yosef in siman 95. Mahari Abuhav asked this question on Rashi, and wrote that for this reason Talmidei Rabbeinu Yonah explained that the purpose of windows is that the house be properly ventilated, meaning that this helps concentration. According to his explanation, he need not look toward Heaven, and all is well. But according to Rashi it is difficult. Likewise, according to the reason the Beit Yosef wrote here in Shulchan Aruch, which is the Rambam's language, that the windows face Jerusalem as we find by Daniel, and as stated by Shlomo in his prayer, the reason for the windows is so one knows that his prayer must ascend through Jerusalem. This also does not require looking toward Heaven during prayer, and is therefore not difficult, unlike Rashi. The Beit Yosef answers as the Magen Avraham writes.
