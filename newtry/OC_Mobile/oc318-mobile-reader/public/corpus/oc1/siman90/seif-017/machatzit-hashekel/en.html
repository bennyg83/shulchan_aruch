Seif katan 17, he should aim, etc. And specifically, etc. In the above incident, when he said, I was unable, he was told, let the master gather ten. He replied, the matter is burdensome to me. He was then told, let the master tell the shaliach tzibbur to inform him when the congregation prays, so that he can pray at the time the congregation prays. This implies that if it is possible to gather ten, that is preferable, for he first told him to gather ten before telling him to pray at the time the congregation prays.
And in Semag, etc. According to the first explanation, that 'the matter is burdensome' refers to Rabbi Nachman, the matter depends on the householder; if he has many servants who can gather ten for him, he must gather ten. But according to Semag's explanation, that the burden refers to the ten people, then the burden exists in every case unless they came on their own.
It implies from Avodah Zarah 4 that Rabbi Yosef said one should not pray Mussaf on Rosh Hashanah during the first three hours of the day, because then it is an hour of anger above, lest they examine his deeds and reject his prayer. The Gemara asks that if so, one should also not pray Shacharis in the first three hours. It answers that since the congregation also prays Shacharis then, his prayer is not rejected because he prays at least when the congregation prays. It follows that even when the congregation prays Shacharis, one should not pray Mussaf then to prevent rejection; one must say that this is not considered the time the congregation prays. Tosafot there states that when one prays at the time the congregation prays, that suffices, even if he is not with them.
However, perhaps, etc. This is specifically at a time of anger. Although Tosafot brought proof from the statement in Berachos that a person's prayer is heard only with the congregation, meaning the teaching of Abba Binyamin that a person's prayer is heard only in the synagogue, and Tosafot understand synagogue to mean praying with the congregation, according to the Magen Avraham's words Tosafot's question returns: how can it state absolutely that prayer is heard only with the congregation, when sometimes it is heard even not with them, such as not at a time of anger? Perhaps one can say that even when it is not a time of anger, it is not heard when he does not pray with the congregation unless he prays at least at the time the congregation prays.
See what is written in siman 591. There it discusses when the time of anger is. According to Rashi it is the first hour of the day, according to Tosafot the third hour, and according to the Tur and Shulchan Aruch all of the first three hours. Although they are not all an hour of anger, since the Holy One, blessed is He, is then occupied with Torah and does not act beyond the strict law, one must be concerned that his prayer may be rejected; see there.
