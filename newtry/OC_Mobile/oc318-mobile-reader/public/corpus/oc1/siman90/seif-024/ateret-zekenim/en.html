One should not pray at the side of his teacher, etc. And a distance of four cubits does not help except for one who prays behind his teacher or opposite him. But one who prays and his back is toward his teacher — even as far as his eyes see — is forbidden (Nimukei Yosef, for so is implied in the Rosh and R' Yitzchak). And from the words of the Tur it is not implied thus, but any distance of four cubits is a domain by itself and in every case permitted (my teacher the Bach and above, siman 103). And even in a congregation where one need not distance, nevertheless one must beware to distance from behind his teacher so his teacher can step three steps behind him (Beit Yosef in the name of Ohel Moed). One who comes on the road and is near an inn and fears that if he comes to the inn the innkeeper's sons will confuse him and he cannot pray with intention — if he can withdraw from the road in a manner that it is likely passers-by on the road will not come and interrupt him — it is better to pray on the road than to pray in the inn. But if it is not clear to him that passers-by will not interrupt him, and it is possible to find for him a room or corner in the inn where he can pray there with a free heart and the innkeeper's sons will not confuse him — then it is better to pray in the inn (Maharam of Italy).
