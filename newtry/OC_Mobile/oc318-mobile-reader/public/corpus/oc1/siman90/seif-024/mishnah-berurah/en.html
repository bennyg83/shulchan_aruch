At the side of his rebbe. For it is arrogance, as he shows that he is equal to his rebbe. His rebbe means one from whom most of his wisdom comes, or the gadol hador. The Chayei Adam wrote that it seems to him the same applies to his father.
And not behind his rebbe. For it also appears like arrogance. Some say it appears as if he is bowing to his rebbe in his prayer. Others say it is because he causes distress to his rebbe, for perhaps the rebbe will need to take three steps back while he is praying and will need to wait for him.
And not in front of him. This follows a kal vachomer, for it is certainly disgraceful to his rebbe when he stands with his back toward him.
There is no concern, etc. For everyone knows that this place is designated for the rebbe and that place for the student, and there is no arrogance or appearance of bowing to him.
That it is good to be stringent. Meaning, to be concerned for the third reason above, that it appears he causes distress to his rebbe. According to this, one should be stringent even in public when behind him.
The custom is to be lenient. See Biur Halacha, that nevertheless, initially, when one establishes a place behind his rebbe, it is good to distance four amos and three steps, so as not to distress his rebbe.
Four amos is permitted. For it is another domain. See Peri Megadim, who wrote in the name of the Pri Chadash that in front of him exactly four amos suffices, but behind his rebbe one needs four amos and three steps so that his rebbe can take three steps back and not need to wait for him, since within his four amos it is forbidden to step, as below in siman 102. He further wrote that inside the almemar it is permitted even nearby, for it is another domain.
And see Yoreh Deah, etc. Meaning, there it is explained that a distance of four amos helps even if his rebbe is behind him, and so wrote the later authorities.
