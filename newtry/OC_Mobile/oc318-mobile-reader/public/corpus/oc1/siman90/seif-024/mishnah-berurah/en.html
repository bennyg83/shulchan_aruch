(73) Beside his rabbi — for it is arrogance, as it appears that he is equal to his rabbi; and “his rabbi” means one from whom most of his wisdom comes, or the gadol hador. The Chayei Adam wrote: it seems to me the same applies regarding his father.

(74) And not behind his rabbi — for it also appears as arrogance; and some say it appears as though he bows to his rabbi in his prayer; and some say because he distresses his rabbi, lest he need to take three steps after him while he is still praying and he will need to wait for him.

(75) And not before him — a kal vachomer: this is certainly a degradation to his rabbi, that he stands behind him opposite him.

(76) There is no concern, etc. — for everyone knows that this place is designated for the rabbi and this for the student, and there is no concern of arrogance or as though bowing to him.

(77) That it is good to be stringent — meaning, to be concerned for the third reason mentioned above, because it appears as though he distresses his rabbi; therefore one should be stringent also in the congregation behind him.

(78) The custom to be lenient — see in Biur Halacha that nevertheless it is good lekhatchila, when he fixes a place for himself behind his rabbi, that he distance himself the measure of four amos and three steps, so as not to distress his rabbi.

(79) Four amos permitted — it is another domain; and see in the Peri Megadim, who wrote in the name of the Pri Chadash that before him it suffices with restricted four amos, but behind his rabbi we require four amos and three steps, so that his rabbi can take three steps after him and not need to wait for him; and within his four amos, is it not forbidden to step, as below in siman 102? He also wrote that within the ohel it is permitted even adjacent, for it is another domain.

(80) And see in Yoreh De'ah, etc. — meaning that there it is explained that distance of four amos helps even if his rabbi is behind him; and the later authorities wrote likewise.
