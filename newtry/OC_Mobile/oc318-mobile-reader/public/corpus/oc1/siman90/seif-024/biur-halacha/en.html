Although it is preferable to be stringent, nevertheless the custom is to be lenient. See Eliyah Rabbah, who wrote in the name of the Levush that in practice it is proper to be stringent. Eliyah Rabbah wrote that this appears to apply specifically when one is in front of his teacher, but not when he is behind him. I did not understand this, for the Levush himself subsequently wrote that one should be careful, when standing behind his teacher, to distance himself enough to be able to take three steps without causing distress to his teacher. This means in addition to the four amos, as Or Zarua wrote, and as Eliyah Rabbah himself later stated. In truth, regarding standing in front of one's teacher, I do not know the reason for those who are stringent when praying with the congregation. Regarding standing behind him, however, it is proper to be stringent, because the rationale that he causes distress to his teacher is demonstrated by the Yerushalmi, as Peri Chadash and the Gra wrote. In any event, when initially establishing one's fixed place, it is certainly proper to be careful about this. The Levush implies that even in front of one's teacher it is preferable to be stringent. Perhaps the reason is that it is disrespectful to his teacher when he stands behind him, facing him, even in this manner.
