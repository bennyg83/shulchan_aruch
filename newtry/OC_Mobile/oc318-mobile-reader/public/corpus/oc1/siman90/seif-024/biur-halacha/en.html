And even though it is good to be stringent, nevertheless the custom is to be lenient — see Eliyah Rabbah, who wrote in the name of the Levush that in practice it is proper to be stringent; and Eliyah Rabbah wrote that it appears specifically before him, but not behind him. And I did not understand this, for the Levush himself wrote afterward that one should be careful behind him to distance so that he can take three steps, so as not to distress his teacher — and this is apart from the four cubits, as the Magen Avraham and Eliyah Rabbah themselves wrote afterward. And in truth, before him I do not know the reason of those who are stringent in public; but behind him it is proper to be stringent, for this reason that it distresses his teacher is proven in the Yerushalmi, as Peri Chadash and the Gra wrote. And in any case, when designating a place ab initio, it is certainly proper to be careful in this. And from the Levush it is implied that even before him it is good to be stringent; and it is possible the reason is because it is a disgrace to his teacher when one stands behind him facing him, even in this matter.
