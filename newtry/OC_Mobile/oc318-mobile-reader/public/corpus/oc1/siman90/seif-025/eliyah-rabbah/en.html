(y) A small matter is not significant, etc. Beit Yosef in the name of Avudraham brings proof for this: if so, how do they pray in the synagogue before the benches on which they sit? — up to here. And as for our benches that are ten [handbreadths] high and four wide — one must say we consider them a permanent object like an aron and a box; but this needs examination in the shtender; and see Magen Avraham and Taz. And a bench in a house is not called permanent and is an interposition — and so wrote in the Agudah. And the wording of the Rokeach: a bed, chair, and table that are not permanent constitute an interruption — up to here. And this escaped the Taz — see there. And the law of our bed designated for lying — Beit Yosef wrote it is not an interposition; and the Bach, Taz, and Nachalas Tzvi disagree that it constitutes an interruption and brought proofs; but one who examines will see they can be rejected lightly, and there is no need to elaborate. It appears to me to distinguish: for a bed on which one regularly stands always in one place — it is not an interposition; as opposed to a bed they move from place to place. The Taz wrote: if it is not easy to pray in another manner — such as when they pray with ten in some room and it is impossible for every person to stand without interposition — one should not enter another room on account of this, for there is no prohibition in interposition but rather a mitzvah from the preferred [way]. And in any case he should lower his eyes or pray from within the siddur and not look outside.
