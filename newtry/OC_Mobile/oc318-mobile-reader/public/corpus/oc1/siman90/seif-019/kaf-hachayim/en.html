[Seif 4] One must open doorways, etc. Maran wrote in Kesef Mishneh chapter 5 of Hilchos Tefillah law 6 in the name of Rambam's responsum: synagogues and places designated for congregational prayer need not have windows forced upon them. And what he wrote "one should pray only in a house that has windows" means an individual praying in his home, as Daniel would do, so far. However, per the Zohar, Parashat Pekudei folio 251a, a beit hakneset too needs windows, as it writes: "These windows and these slits all exist for one purpose—all prayers that ascend from below to above—and to look through them to raise them before the Holy One, blessed be He. And on this, any beit hakneset that has no windows is not a place for prayer in it, as is fitting—for this lower beit hakneset corresponds to the upper beit knesset; the upper beit knesset has windows as we said, so too below to above: the Great Assembly has twelve upper windows, so too this lower beit knesset—and all correspond one to the other because worlds exist through these in this manner, and through these the Holy One, blessed be He, elevates His glory in all," so far. And likewise is Maran's view here in Shulchan Aruch—from that he wrote anonymously, and further from what he wrote afterward "and it is good that there be twelve windows in the beit hakneset"—it implies that at first he said one must open doorways, etc., in every place one prays, whether in a house or in a beit hakneset; and it also implies that however many windows one wishes to open may be opened. And afterward he wrote "and it is good that there be twelve windows in the beit hakneset"—meaning that in the beit hakneset one should do so precisely as written in the Zohar. And such are the words of Levush, as explained there. And see Nahar Shalom note 3 and what he wrote on it in Hafla'ah note 4, and understand well.
