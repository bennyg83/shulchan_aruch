It is a mitzvah, etc. As it says, 'Let us know, let us pursue knowing Hashem.' From here we learn that all the more so one must then be careful not to stand in the middle of the road conversing with his fellow about personal matters, for through this it is common to arrive after Barchu and Kedushah. See the Perishah, who wrote that the main running should be when he comes near the synagogue, for there it is recognizable that he does it for the mitzvah of the synagogue. The Peri Megadim wrote that at Shacharis, when he goes with tallis and tefillin in hand, it is always recognizable; nevertheless, in a gentile street he should not run.
To run. Until the entrance of the synagogue. In the synagogue itself it is forbidden to run; rather, he should walk with awe.
On Shabbos, when it is forbidden. Even on a weekday, taking large steps removes one five-hundredth of a person's eyesight. See Peri Megadim.
It is forbidden to run. And not to take large steps, because he shows that the delay in the synagogue seems to him like a burden. This applies when he goes to his affairs. But if he leaves in order to return, it is a mitzvah to run so that he returns quickly, and similarly if he leaves the synagogue for the study hall to learn Torah.
