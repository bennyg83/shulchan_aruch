(40) "Mitzvah" etc.—as it says "Let us know, let us run to know the Lord," etc. From this we learn all the more that one should be careful then not to stand in the middle of the road to converse with a friend about mundane matters, for thereby it often happens that another comes during Barchu and Kedushah. See Perishah who wrote the main running should be when one comes near the beit knesset, where it is evident one does so for the mitzvah of the beit knesset. Peri Megadim wrote that in the morning when one goes with tallit and tefillin in hand, it is always evident; nevertheless on a street of idolaters one should not run.

(41) To run—until the entrance of the beit knesset; but in the beit knesset itself it is forbidden to run, rather one should walk with awe.

(42) "On Shabbat when it is forbidden"—and even on weekdays one loses one of the thousand parts of a person's eyesight; see Peri Megadim.

(43) It is forbidden to run—and not to take an oversized stride, for he shows himself that delay in the beit knesset is as a burden to him in the manner when one goes to his business; but if he goes out intending to return to a mitzvah he is commanded to run to return quickly; and similarly if he goes out from the beit knesset to the beit midrash to study Torah.
