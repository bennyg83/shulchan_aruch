It is a mitzvah to run. Until the entrance of the synagogue; but in the synagogue itself it is forbidden to run — rather he should walk with awe and fear [Eruvin, Shulchan Aruch].

Shabbat that is forbidden. And even in the sand, A. comes from the light of his eyes:

When he goes out. But if he goes out with intent to return, it is a mitzvah to run so that he may return quickly [the author in Orach Chayyim]. And the Levush is stringent about this on account of concern for onlookers; and in my view there is no concern here, for as long as the congregation has not left the synagogue, presumptively his intent is to return; and furthermore, it is better that they suspect him than that he himself cancel hearing Kedushah or Kaddish. And so too the Shelah, page 74, that one who goes out from the synagogue to the beit midrash should run.
