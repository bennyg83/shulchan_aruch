It is a mitzvah to run. Until the entrance of the synagogue. In the synagogue itself it is forbidden to run; rather, he should walk with awe and fear. Olas Tamid; Shelah.
On Shabbos, when it is forbidden. Even on a weekday, one who takes large steps loses one five-hundredth of his eyesight.
When he leaves. But if he leaves in order to return, it is a mitzvah to run so that he will return quickly. So wrote the author in Ein Yaakov. Lechem Chamudos hesitates because of concern for onlookers, but this does not appear to me, for as long as the congregation has not left the synagogue, presumably he intends to return. Also, it is better that they suspect him than that he himself lose hearing Kedushah or Kaddish. The Shelah likewise wrote that one who leaves the synagogue for the study hall should run.
