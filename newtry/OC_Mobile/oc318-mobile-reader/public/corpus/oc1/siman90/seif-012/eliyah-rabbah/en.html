(l) He is called a bad neighbor, etc. It implies even if there is no minyan in the synagogue — for it states anonymously "one who has a synagogue [nearby]." And further proof — behold Daniel prayed individually; nevertheless he prayed in a place of song and prayer — so wrote Mateh Moshe p. 37. And apparently from Daniel we would not learn that he is called a bad neighbor if he does not go to the synagogue; and one must say he does not bring proof from Daniel, but rather that there is an obligation even for an individual to pray in the synagogue. And the reason he is called a bad neighbor — the Perishah wrote: bad to the creatures who dwell with him in the city, since he causes that the Divine Presence does not rest upon them; and therefore he is also called a bad neighbor to the Holy One, blessed be He — in the manner of "those You hate, O Hashem, I hate" (Tehillim). Also one can say that a bad neighbor does not enjoy his fellow's neighborhood; so he shows he does not enjoy that the Divine Presence dwell with him. It is stated in the chapter HaPo'alim [Bava Metzia 107a] that if there are two synagogues — it is a mitzvah to go to the distant one, for he has reward for the steps.
