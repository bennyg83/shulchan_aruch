He must go there. If he can come there while the day is still long and he will not need to go alone, as written in seif 17; and this is a fortiori [Levush]. It is stated in the Gemara in the chapter "Three who ate" that Rabbi Eliezer freed his slave to complete ten, even though he transgresses a positive commandment when he frees his slave — for the positive commandment of the many is different; and so too Tosafos in the first chapter of Shabbos. And the Rosh wrote: a positive commandment of the many, as it is written "and I shall be sanctified among the children of Israel" — that is, a positive commandment of sanctifying Hashem's name with ten in public; and even if it is a rabbinic matter, such as hearing Kedushah and Barchu, for which we do not find a Biblical root, nevertheless it overrides the positive commandment of an individual — for it is not reasonable to say that it deals with a Biblical positive commandment, such as reading Parshas Zachor, which is from the Torah, end quote. And in the chapter "These are shaved" it is written that specifically where the many prevail, such as here, it is called a positive commandment of the many. And the Ran in the chapter "The sender" wrote that "you shall serve them forever" is a full negative commandment; or one may say that since he is not acting for the benefit of the slave, there is no positive commandment at all and it is like selling him to him, end quote. And it requires investigation — for if so, what does the Gemara challenge from "a mitzvah that comes through transgression"? For according to his words there is no transgression at all. And see Orach Chayyim 267 seif 9 and Beit Yosef there.
