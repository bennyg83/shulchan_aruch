And he wants to lodge there. The same applies in the morning: if along the road before him, within four mil, there is a place where people pray with ten, he must wait until he arrives there, provided that through this the time of Shema and prayer will not pass.
If before him. Specifically on the road ahead of him, where he will need to go there anyway. But to the sides, its law is like behind him.
To go there. In a manner that he will not need thereby to walk alone. It also speaks where it is still broad daylight, so that even if he goes there he can arrive while the sun is still on the earth; otherwise he is not obligated, for a person should always enter while it is good. See Peri Megadim, who wrote that on the road, if he finds ten to pray Minchah half an hour after midday, he may do so, although otherwise this is not initially proper as stated in siman 233; here it is permitted.
And behind him, etc. See the previous seif katan. One sitting at home has the law of one for whom it is behind him. Therefore one who lives in a settlement within a mil of a place where people pray with ten must go every morning to pray with ten, but not at night, since he need not walk on the road at night for a minyan. This seif is an open rebuke to those people who are in the city and are lazy about going to the synagogue to pray Minchah and Maariv.
