The congregation. Even though he can pray in his home on Yom Kippur, nevertheless "in a multitude of people is the King's glory" — Magen Avraham. Even if there are many sinners in the congregation he should not refrain from praying with the congregation, as Knesset HaGedolah writes. One who was imprisoned and could not pray with ten and pleaded before the minister, who only allowed one day and no more — which day should he choose? The Radbaz ruled vol. 4 siman 13 that he should not delay the mitzvah and should immediately pray with ten on Yom Kippur. Chacham Tzvi in his responsum siman 106 challenged him from the chapter HaTekhelet (Menachos 49), where we have a doubt about a congregation lacking tamidin and musafin — which takes precedence — and we establish it regarding musafin of today and tomorrow's tamidin: is the frequent preferred, or the sacred? And if per the Radbaz's view, even when the frequent is preferred musafin would precede on the grounds that we do not postpone mitzvot on Yom Kippur — in Laket HaKemach page 11 he reconciles that there is a difference between an individual and a congregation, for a congregation does not die, but for an individual where it is a mitzvah we do not delay on Yom Kippur. The author Yad Aharon answers that regarding that which is frequent and sacred, if he offered the frequent he can no longer offer the sacred, and likewise in reverse; therefore we are in doubt who is deferred before whom. But here he does not lose the mitzvah entirely, for if he waits until Yom Kippur he fulfills the mitzvah completely; therefore we do not postpone on Yom Kippur. And see the responsum of Yad Eliyahu siman 42. One who travels on Shabbat and Yom Tov at dawn to pray in a minyan of ten, because most of the congregation delays their prayer, and in that minyan they also pray musaf immediately after shacharis, and there is time afterward to go to the synagogue — it is better to pray musaf with the congregation in the synagogue, for "in a multitude of people is the King's glory." And it seems not like the Radbaz ruled there, where there is a long time between them and there are concerns and reasons; but for one day we have no concern — Yad Eliyahu siman 42.

Compelled — meaning his strength was weakened even though he is not ill — Magen Avraham. If he is compelled regarding money, that due to his exertion to pray with the congregation he will suffer monetary loss, Yad Eliyahu ruled in his responsum siman 7 that he is not obligated by law and may pray individually at home or in the synagogue when there is no congregation. Possibly due to loss of income he is somewhat obligated by law to pray with the congregation, for there is a difference between loss of income and loss from his pocket on Yom Kippur. Whether one may travel on a small or large ship on Shabbat to complete a minyan and pray with the congregation — see the Radbaz vol. 1 siman 87, and in the responsum of Chavat Yair siman (112) [115], and in the responsum of Avodat HaGershuni siman 123, and in the responsum of Maharam of Rothenburg siman 1 letter 2.

They pray. And specifically if it is burdensome for him to gather ten — Magen Avraham. It implies in Eiruvin 4b that if one prays musaf at the time the congregation prays shacharis it is not called "at the time the congregation prays"; see Tosafos there.

In settlements. It seems proper in summer days when the congregation delays shacharis and then in the settlement it is forbidden even to study Torah, as written in siman 89; therefore it is better that he pray immediately at sunrise, and for arvit he must wait until the stars emerge — Magen Avraham.
