Seif katan 9, and it is the reverse, etc. For the main point, etc., is that in their days the synagogue entrance was in the western wall, and they prayed facing east. According to this, one who stands outside on the entrance side, namely the west, with his back to the synagogue and his face to the opposite side, so that his face is westward, certainly appears like a denier according to all views, for there are two defects: his back is to the synagogue, and he turns his face to a direction different from the congregation. When he stands on the west and turns toward the synagogue, namely eastward, there are two advantages: his face is toward the synagogue and toward the congregation's direction. That is certainly permitted by all. The dispute is only when he stands on the east of the synagogue.
