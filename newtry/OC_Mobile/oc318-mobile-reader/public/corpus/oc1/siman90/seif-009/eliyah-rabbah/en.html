Sefer Chasidim, siman 772, writes not to sit in prayer with a wicked person, because he thinks improper thoughts and the Shechinah withdraws from him; see also siman 773. But the Shelah writes that if the righteous person prays with intent, the wicked person will not cause his prayer to be rejected. Know that even if one has ten at home, he should strive to pray in the synagogue, as it implies from Kenesses HaGedolah in the name of the Rambam, and from seif 18 regarding the sanctity of the study hall. However, when one sees that in a synagogue with a large crowd there is much confusion and no one hears either the Torah reading or the prayer, Responsa Radbaz, siman 18, writes that it is preferable to pray with ten at home. What the Shulchan Aruch writes, 'if he is compelled,' means that his strength is weak even if he is not sick, and this is specifically where it is burdensome to gather ten. Responsa Beis Yaakov, siman 70, writes that one should fine people who refrain from going to synagogue because they are engaged in Torah or earning money, and the wealthy should be fined more. Though his proof from siman 108 can be rejected, for there the time of prayer passes, nevertheless we see that a mitzvah is not set aside for money, and this too is a mitzvah. One must weigh the loss of the mitzvah against its reward. This is especially so for a Torah scholar, for even if he is learning, there is suspicion and chillul Hashem, as explained in seif 18. Magen Avraham writes that from Avodah Zarah 4 it implies that one who prays Mussaf when the congregation prays Shacharis is not considered to be praying at the time the congregation prays; see below, end of siman 236. He further writes that in summer, when the congregation delays Shacharis, one who lives in a settlement is forbidden even to learn, as explained in siman 89 and siman 235; therefore it is better that he pray immediately at sunrise.
