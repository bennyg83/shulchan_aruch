To pray in the synagogue. Even if he has ten at home, he should make the effort to pray in the synagogue. See seif 18 in the Mishnah Berurah, seif katan 54.
With the congregation. Because the Holy One, blessed is He, does not despise the prayer of the congregation. Even if there are sinners among them, he should not refrain from praying with them. If he has two synagogues, and one has a larger crowd, it is a greater mitzvah to pray there. The later authorities wrote that if in the crowded synagogue there is much confusion and one cannot hear either the prayer or the Torah reading, it is better to pray at home with ten. Between praying alone in the synagogue and praying at home with ten, prayer with the congregation is preferable, and even if he can hear Kaddish and Barchu, nevertheless prayer with the congregation is preferable. Peri Megadim. The essence of prayer with the congregation is the Shemoneh Esrei, meaning that ten adult men pray together, not as the masses think, that the essence of praying with ten is only to hear Kaddish, Kedushah, and Barchu. Therefore a person is obligated to hurry to synagogue so that he reaches the Shemoneh Esrei with the congregation. Chayei Adam. One who goes early on Shabbos and Yom Tov to pray with a minyan because most of the congregation delays prayer, and that minyan prays Mussaf immediately after Shacharis, while there is later time to go to the synagogue, it is better to pray Mussaf with the congregation in the synagogue because 'in a multitude of people is the King's glory.' This is specifically if his fixed place for prayer is in the synagogue, but if his place is not fixed there, it is better to pray the whole prayer in one place. Responsa Radbaz writes that a prisoner whom the officer allowed one day to pray with the congregation in a minyan, whichever day he chooses, should pray that day immediately and not delay the mitzvah to wait for Yom Kippur or Purim. See Chayei Adam and Nishmas Adam, who discuss many details in this matter.
Compelled. Meaning that his strength is weakened even though he is not sick. If it is financial compulsion, so that by trying to pray with the congregation he will suffer a loss, he may pray at home alone or in the synagogue without a congregation. But to prevent loss of profit, he should not refrain from prayer with the congregation, for there is a difference between loss of profit and loss from his capital. Shem Yaakov relates of the gaon Rabbi Zalman Mirels, Av Beis Din of Hamburg, that once he went to synagogue wrapped in tallis and tefillin as usual, and someone met him who had precious stones to sell and wanted him to come home with him. The gaon answered that he should wait until he returned from the synagogue. Meanwhile the seller sold them to someone else, who profited several thousand Reichsthaler. The gaon rejoiced greatly that he had cast aside great money for the sake of prayer with the congregation. Magen Gibborim. Eliyah Rabbah wrote in the name of Responsa Beis Yosef that one should fine people who refrain from going to synagogue because they are engaged in Torah or earning money. The wealthy should be fined more; and a Torah scholar, even if learning, is subject to suspicion and chillul Hashem, as written in seif 18.
He should aim to pray, etc. This is specifically if it is burdensome for him to gather ten. From Avodah Zarah 4b it implies that if one prays Mussaf while the congregation prays Shacharis, this is not called the time when the congregation prays. If he prays with them in the synagogue, it is called the congregation's prayer.
They pray Shacharis. In the summer, when the congregation delays Shacharis, one who lives in a settlement is forbidden even to learn, as written in siman 89, seif 6, and siman 232, lest he be drawn after his learning and the time for prayer pass. If so, he would lose a long time; therefore it is better that he pray immediately at sunrise. All the more so today, when in large communities they pray immediately from the light of morning, so it is always at the time the congregation prays.
At the time the congregation. Meaning, at the time when Jewish communities pray. In Ashkenaz, where they customarily pray Maariv early, as written in siman 235, an individual living in a village or on the road must wait until nightfall.
Nevertheless, etc. Because it is a place fixed for sanctity, and his prayer is more accepted there. It is obvious that if he is a Torah scholar and there is concern for chillul Hashem if he comes to the synagogue after the prayer, he should pray at home.
