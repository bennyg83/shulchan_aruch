(27) To pray in the beit knesset—and even if he has ten at home, he should strive for the beit knesset; and see siman 108 in Mishna Berurah note 54.

(28) With the congregation—for the Holy One, blessed be He, does not despise the congregation's prayer, and even if there were sinners among them he should not refrain from praying with them. If he has two synagogues and one has a larger congregation, he is obligated to pray there more. The later authorities wrote that if the beit knesset of the larger congregation has much confusion and a person cannot hear neither prayer nor Torah reading, it is better to pray at home with ten. Between praying alone in the beit knesset and at home with ten, communal prayer is preferable; and even if he can hear Kaddish and Barchu, nevertheless communal prayer is preferable [Peri Megadim]. The essence of prayer with a congregation is the Shemoneh Esreh—that ten adult men pray together—and not as the masses think, that the main thing of praying with ten is only to hear Kaddish, Kedushah, and Barchu; therefore they are not strict that there be ten in the beit knesset—and this is an error; therefore it is an obligation on a person to hurry to come to the beit knesset so that he arrive to pray Shemoneh Esreh with the congregation [Chayei Adam]. One who goes on Shabbat and Yom Tov early to pray in a minyan of ten because most of the congregation delay their prayer, and in that minyan they also pray Musaf immediately after Shacharit and there is time afterward to go to the beit knesset—it is better to go to pray Musaf with the congregation in the beit knesset, for in a large congregation is the splendor of the King—so too in responsa Yad Eliyahu siman 42; and specifically if his designated place for prayer is in the beit knesset; but if his place is not designated to pray in the beit knesset, it is better that he pray the entire prayer in one place. Radbaz wrote in responsa regarding one held by the prince who gave him permission one day to pray with the congregation in a minyan—whichever day he wishes, he should pray that day immediately and not delay the mitzvah to wait for Yom Kippur or Purim; and see Chayei Adam klal 68 and Neshmas Adam, who elaborated on several details in this matter.

(29) Compelled—that is, his strength was weakened even though he is not sick. And if he is compelled by money—that because of his effort to pray with the congregation he would incur a loss—he may pray at home alone or in the beit knesset without a congregation; but because of loss of profit he should not refrain from praying with the congregation, for there is a difference between loss of profit and loss from his pocket; and so too in the name of Yaakov; and he testified about the gaon Rabbi Zalman Mirlesh, av bet din of the holy community Hamburg, that once he went to the beit knesset wrapped in tallis and tefillin as was his way, and a man met him who had to sell precious stones and wanted him to go with him to his house; the gaon answered that he should wait until he came from the beit knesset—and meanwhile he sold them to another, and the other profited on them several thousand rubles; and the gaon rejoiced with great joy that he cast aside much money for communal prayer [Magen Gibborim]. And so too in Eliyah Rabbah in the name of responsa Beit Yosef that one should fine people who prevent going to the beit knesset because they are engaged in Torah or because they earn money; and the rich should be fined more; and a Torah scholar—even though engaged in study—nevertheless there is suspicion and desecration of Hashem's name, as written in siman 108.

(30) He should intend to pray, etc.—and specifically if it is difficult for him to gather ten. It is implied in Eruvin 4b that if he prays Musaf at the hour when the congregation prays Shacharit, it is not called "at the hour when the congregation prays"; and if he prays with them in the beit knesset, it is called communal prayer.

(31) They should pray Shacharit—and in summer days when the congregation delays Shacharit and he lives in a settled place, it is forbidden even to study, as written in siman 89 seif 6 and siman 232, lest he prolong his study and the time for prayer pass; and then a long time will be lost—therefore it is better that he pray immediately at sunrise; and all the more so as today in large congregations they pray immediately when morning brightens—and then he is always at the hour when the congregation prays.

(32) At the time when the congregation—meaning at the hour when congregations of Israel pray. And in Ashkenaz where they are accustomed to recite Maariv early, as written in siman 235—a individual who lives in a village or on the road must wait until the stars emerge.

(33) Nevertheless, etc.—because it is a permanent place for holiness and one's prayer is accepted there more. It is plain that if he is a talmid chacham and there is concern for chillul Hashem when another person comes after the prayer in the beit knesset, he should pray in his home.
