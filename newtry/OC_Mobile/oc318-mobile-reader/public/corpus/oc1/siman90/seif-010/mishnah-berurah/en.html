(34) With the congregation it is forbidden—even though in the previous seif it writes that even in his house he may intend for the hour when the congregation prays, that is so his prayer will be accepted, but there is no prohibition if he precedes the congregation's prayer. But when he stands with the congregation and precedes, there is also a prohibition. See Orach Chaim who wrote the reason—because he is as one who disparages the congregation. It is implied in the Gemara that even if he wishes to do this for Torah study, such as having a fixed lesson to study Torah with someone. Bach wrote that if he needs to hurry his prayer, such as one going on the road, he may leave the beit knesset and pray at home; and it is possible that for Torah study too it is permitted, and so is implied in Maamar Mordechai—but there it speaks of Musaf prayer, and perhaps the same applies to Shacharit; see below in siman 108.

(35) To precede his prayer—even if he wishes to go out outside the beit knesset, so Magen Avraham in the name of Rivash. But in Maamar Mordechai it writes there is no proof from Rivash's words, and he concludes there is no prohibition in preceding his prayer, only he does not act properly, for he still causes loss to the congregation's prayer.

(36) "The time is passing"—it implies that then even in the beit knesset it is permitted; and similarly is proven from the wording of Terumat HaDeshen, and the Bach ruled that specifically when the time is so short that while he goes to his house the time for prayer will pass; but if there is time he should go to his house and pray alone with intention. It is stated in the Gemara that if his heart was weak he may pray at home before the congregation, but in the beit knesset even this is forbidden; and see siman 114, seif 2, that if he is sick or anus, even in the beit knesset it is permitted—so too the Bach and Perishah there.
