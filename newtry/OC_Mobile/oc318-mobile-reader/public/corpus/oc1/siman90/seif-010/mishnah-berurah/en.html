With the congregation it is forbidden. Although in the previous seif he wrote that even at home one should aim for the time when the congregation prays, that is so his prayer be heard; there is no prohibition if he prays before the congregation. But when he stands with the congregation and prays before them, there is also a prohibition. See Orchos Chaim, who writes that the reason is that he appears to disgrace the congregation. It implies from the Gemara that this is so even if he wants to do this for Torah study, such as if he has a fixed Torah lesson with someone. The Bach wrote that if he needs to hurry his prayer because he is setting out on a journey, he should leave the synagogue and pray at home. Perhaps for Torah study also it is permitted to do so, and so it implies from Ma'amar Mordechai, though there he speaks of Mussaf; perhaps the same applies to Shacharis. See below, seif 18.
To precede his prayer. Even if he wants to leave the synagogue, so wrote the Magen Avraham in the name of Rivash. But Ma'amar Mordechai wrote that there is no proof from Rivash, and concludes that there is no prohibition of preceding his prayer, though he does not act properly, since he loses prayer with the congregation.
The time is passing. This implies that then it is permitted even in the synagogue, and so the language of Talmidei Rabbeinu Yonah proves. The Bach ruled that this is only if the time is so short that while he goes home the time for prayer will pass; but if there is time, he should go home and pray alone. It is stated in the Gemara that if his heart is weak he may pray at home before the congregation, but in the synagogue even this is forbidden. See siman 114, seif 2, that if he is sick or compelled, even in the synagogue it is permitted; so wrote the Bach and Perishah there.
