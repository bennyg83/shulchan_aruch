Workers doing melacha for the baal habayis may pray atop the olive tree and atop the fig tree, and this is not [subject to the rule] that one should not stand on a high place and pray. For since they ascended to do their melacha, it is as if one ascended to an upper story. But for other trees they must descend. The reason the olive differs from other trees is that it has many more branches than other trees, and there is great effort in ascending and descending, and they would be interrupted from their melacha; therefore they said one may pray there. But for other trees, where there is no interruption [from work], they must descend. The baal habayis, even atop the olive or fig tree, must descend to pray, for he is not subject to melacha—he is on his own property. If they were lenient for workers because of interruption of melacha, they were not lenient for the baal habayis.
