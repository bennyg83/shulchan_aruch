(3) There, in the gloss, {Rama: and even if they are not three tefachim high}, etc. And likewise Levush, end of Knesset HaGedolah siman 98 note 1. But Bach wrote that on a bed, chair, and bench too it is not forbidden for height unless three tefachim; but less than three tefachim is permitted—and he wrote, and so we rule, and likewise in Shulchan Aruch—and not like the rabbi in the gloss, see there. And likewise is the view of Taz seder katan 1. However, Maamar Mordechai note 1 disagreed with Bach and wrote that one should not be lenient at all; and what Bach wrote, and likewise in Shulchan Aruch, he wrote on it that there is no source for this, and it may well be said that Maran agrees with Mahari"a—for in Beit Yosef he did not disagree with him, and in Shulchan Aruch seif 1 he only copied the language of the Tosifta, and in seif 2 he wrote the measure of a high place that they said three tefachim, etc., and it implies that this measure of three tefachim does not refer to a high place but to the words of Mahari"a, see there. And see there Eid, who wrote that Rambam's words too may be explained like Mahari"a, and Taz's words are not mandatory, see there. And likewise is the view of Levush Chadash in the first chapter of Berachos note 57—to forbid on a bed, chair, and bench even in less than three tefachim, see there. And likewise Eliyah Rabbah note 1, and he wrote that so is in Maharil laws of prayer, see there. And likewise Chayei Adam general 22 note 4, Shulchan Aruch HaRav 109:1, Siddur Beit Yaakov in laws of the Eighteen Blessings note 24—and see further below siman 98 note 26.

(3) There, {Rama: and even if they are not three tefachim high}—one who is not accustomed to stilts may not pray on them, for he is distracted lest he fall. Pri Chadash note 1. Eliyah Rabbah in Hagahot Beit Yosef. And likewise one must be careful not to stand on something slippery, because he is distracted lest he fall; and even on a board that sways in a way that distracts him. And likewise if he wears a kapota and beketshe that distract one who is not accustomed to them—and the like—Ben Aharon, Parashat Yitro note 3.
