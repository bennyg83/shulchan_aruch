26) [Se'if 5] One should not pray in an open place, etc. Rav Kahana said: One who prays in a valley is brazen in my eyes. Rashi explains that when one is in a private place, awe of the King rests upon him and his heart is broken. Tosafos ask that it is written, 'Yitzchak went out to meditate in the field.' They answer that this concerned Mount Moriah, as Pesachim 88a states, 'Not like Yitzchak, who called it a field,' etc. Alternatively, the valley here means a valley where people and travelers regularly pass. Their intent is that one is called brazen only if he prays in a valley through which people and travelers pass, for this prevents awe of the King from resting upon him. If he prays in a valley where people do not pass, awe of the King rests upon him there too, and he is not called brazen. See Beis Yosef's question on this answer of Tosafos; according to our explanation it is resolved. Taz 2 also inclines this way; see there. Eliyah Rabbah 4 likewise writes that Agudah rules in accordance with Tosafos' second answer, permitting a field without passersby, and that one may rely upon it. Yafeh LaLev 6 argues from Tanna Devei Eliyahu that one should not pray in a valley even without passersby because this follows the ways of idolaters, unlike Tosafos. This is not conclusive. One may say that this applies specifically if he initially goes there to pray even though he has another, more private place available, or if he regularly goes there to pray. An isolated occasion, however, or a case where no more private place is available, is permitted when there are no passersby, in accordance with Tosafos.
26) Regarding Tosafos' first answer, that the verse concerns Mount Moriah, Bach explains that although it was called a field, it was not open but surrounded by partitions. Taz 2 writes likewise. Matteh Yosef 2, however, explains that Mount Moriah is different because it is the site of the Beis HaMikdash and the place of atonement. Adam HaRishon was created from it; Noach built the altar there and offered his sacrifice; and Avraham Avinu likewise built the altar there and bound his son Yitzchak, as Rambam writes in Hilchos Beis HaBechirah, chapter 2. Therefore, although it was open, since it was a sacred place - 'this is none other than the House of God' - and the presence of our mighty Shechinah never departed from there, the awe of the Shechinah certainly rests upon one who prays there and his heart is broken. This differs from another place that is mostly open, which is forbidden because it is brazen toward Heaven and one cannot concentrate at all. Nehar Shalom 20 explains likewise, as do Yafeh LaLev 6 and Yitzchak Yeranen, page 12, column 4. So too Zohar, Bo 39b; see there.
27) There: Such as in a field. It is obvious that travelers may pray in a field. Magen Avraham 6; Shulchan Aruch HaRav 5; Shalmei Tzibbur, page 109b; Kesher Gudal 7:10; Siddur Beis Yaakov, laws of the Amidah 23. They must nevertheless pray off the road, in a place where passersby will not interrupt them. Siddur Beis Yaakov, ibid. Before beginning, they must also take exceptional care to look in every direction and ensure that no excrement or filth is present, so that their prayer not be an abomination. Yafeh LaLev, end of paragraph 6.
