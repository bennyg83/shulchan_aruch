(10) One should not pray—and in the Gemara it states that brazen is one who passes over this; and Peri Megadim wrote that therefore if one happened to find a house without windows and an open valley, it is better to pray in the house without windows.

(11) In an open place—and behold, for one resolution of Tosafos: if he stands at the side of the road in a place where he is not afraid that passersby will interrupt him, it is permitted; but in Beit Yosef he objects to their words. And behold, even though Magen Avraham and other later authorities reconcile the words of Tosafos, nevertheless they wrote that in the Zohar it implies one must pray specifically in a house. And passersby—for all opinions it is permitted to pray in a field; nevertheless when there are trees there it is better that he stand among them and pray if it does not cause him difficulty in delaying his journey, for it is somewhat a modest place thereby; and when he is in his home he should not rely on this.

(12) As in a field—and in a place that is enclosed by partitions, even though it is not roofed, it is fine.
