Such as. See Taz, who discussed this at length, and see what I wrote on Magen Avraham, seif katan 36, concerning this. Regarding his distinction based on whether the object serves a prayer-related purpose, Magen Avraham rejected this; see there. Regarding his suggestion that one close his eyes, that remedy applies where the reason is that one should not look at the object. Below, however, in siman 98, the Mechaber gives the reason that it constitutes a barrier between him and the wall. The same should apply to an object interposed between him and the wall, in which case closing one's eyes would not help. It may be answered that within four amos it is forbidden, whereas beyond four amos it is a separate domain and is permitted. See Magen Avraham, seif katan 36. This also resolves the Mechaber's statement in seif 22 that one should be careful not to pray behind another person, while the rule not to pray behind one's teacher is likewise an actual prohibition. Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 27, asked how Rabbi Yirmiyah prayed behind Rav ab initio; see Berachos 27b. It may be answered that Rabbi Yirmiyah was praying just beyond four amos, but had not distanced himself by the measure needed for three steps. See Magen Avraham, seif katan 38, and understand this.
