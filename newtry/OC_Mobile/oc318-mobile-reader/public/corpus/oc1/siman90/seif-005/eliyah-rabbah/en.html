Because when he, etc. According to this, even in a field where no one passes by it is forbidden, like the first answer of Tosafot at the end of chapter Ein Omdin. But I saw in the Agudah that he rules like the second answer of Tosafot, that in a field where no one passes by it is permitted, and one may rely on him. The Levush wrote that although it says that Yitzchak went out to pray in the field, there it was different because it was Mount Moriah, where his heart was humbled because of the Akeidah. He must be explaining Tosafot there that way. The Bach, however, explains that Mount Moriah is called a field, but in truth it was enclosed by partitions. He also explains that Yitzchak prayed among trees, and therefore it says 'to converse,' an expression related to shrubs and trees.
