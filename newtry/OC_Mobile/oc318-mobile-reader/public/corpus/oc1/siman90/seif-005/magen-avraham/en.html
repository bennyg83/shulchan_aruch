One should not pray, etc. Tosafos raised a difficulty: the verse writes "And Isaac went out to pray in the field" — and one could explain that "field" means the Temple Mount, as we say Abraham called it a field. Alternatively, the reason is so passers-by on the road would not interrupt him, and Isaac prayed in a valley in a place where passers-by are not common — so far. And Rabbeinu Yonah rejected the second explanation because we say in the Gemara "It is impertinent of one who prays in a valley" — and on the second view there is no reason of impertinence; therefore Beit Yosef explained the reason as written here in the Shulchan Aruch. And it seems to me we can uphold Tosafos' explanation, and this is what he means: when one prays in a valley he is impudent, showing by himself that even if all passers-by pass by him he will not be nullified from his intention. See Zohar Beshalach p. 105 — he raises this question there, and it seems from there that one should pray specifically in a house; and it is obvious that passers-by pray in the field.
