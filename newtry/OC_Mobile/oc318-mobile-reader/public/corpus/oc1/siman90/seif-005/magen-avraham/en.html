One should not pray, etc. Tosafot asked: does the verse not say, “Yitzchak went out to converse in the field”? One can answer that the field means Mount Moriah, as we say that Avraham called it a field. Alternatively, the reason is that passersby should not interrupt him, and Yitzchak prayed in a valley in a place where passersby were not common. This is their wording. The Beis Yosef rejected the second explanation, because we say in the Gemara, “One who prays in a valley is insolent before Me,” and according to the second answer the reason is not insolence. Therefore the Beis Yosef explained the reason as written here in the Shulchan Aruch. To me it appears that Tosafot’s explanation can be upheld, and this is what it means: when one prays in a valley, he is insolent, for he shows about himself that even if all passersby pass before him, his concentration will not be disturbed. See the Zohar, Beshalach, page 105, which asks this question there, and it appears there that one should pray specifically in a house. It is obvious that travelers pray in the field.
