There one must open doorways or windows, etc. Rashi explained that windows cause one to direct his heart to look toward the heavens and his heart is humbled. Tur wrote: and therefore they must be open toward the direction in which he prays—and Beit Yosef brought it. And he wrote that likewise Rambam in chapter 5 of Hilchos Tefillah. And one must open doorways or windows opposite Jerusalem in order to pray opposite them. And the disciples of Rabbeinu Yonah wrote: it is possible to say the reason is that through seeing light his mind settles more and he can have proper intent—and Beit Yosef brought it, see there. And see further below siman 95 note 8. However, per what was written in the previous note in the name of the Zohar—it is not because of settling the mind nor because of looking, but the reason is mystical. And the practical difference is that even a blind person must pray in a beit hakneset that has windows. And see further in Zohar Parashat Beshalach folio 59b in the name of Ramak, who wrote: "And that synagogue should have windows, as it is written 'and windows open'—like above; and one who gazes from the windows peeks from the slits." And if you ask: even in a field, so that the spirit may ascend—is it not so? For we need a house and there is none—to find a house below like the pattern of the house above, upper dwelling to lower dwelling; and further, that prayer and that spirit must ascend and go out from the crookedness in a straight path to Jerusalem, etc., see there—it implies that per the Zohar too we require windows opposite Jerusalem.
