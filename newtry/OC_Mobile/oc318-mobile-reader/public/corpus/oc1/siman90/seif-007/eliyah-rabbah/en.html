(g) In other directions, etc. See above that the same law applies: if he turns his face to the east and his side is toward the wall of the synagogue — no concern; but in the east and west, even if he turns his face to his side or to the south — one should be uncertain, etc., and be stringent — Levush. Lechem Chamudos wrote: I do not know why he does not rely on the words of Rav Hai Gaon that Beit Yosef brought, who permitted explicitly — up to here. And it appears to my humble opinion he is uncertain about what Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Hagahos Maimoniyos that there are commentators who forbid even on the sides; and even though Beit Yosef rejected [this] with both hands, I saw in Bedek HaBayis of his that he erased the rejection — see there. And moreover, Rav Hai Gaon deals with courtyards on the side of the east, and it is plausible to him to be lenient in this.
