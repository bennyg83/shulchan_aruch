On the other sides, etc. See above, that the same applies if he turns his face eastward and his side is toward the synagogue wall, there is no concern. But when he is east or west of it, even if he turns his face to the side or southward, there is room for doubt and one should be stringent, as the Levush writes. Lechem Chamudos asks why he should not rely on Rav Hai, cited by the Beit Yosef, who explicitly permits. In my humble opinion, the Levush is uncertain because the Beit Yosef cited Hagahos Maimoniyos that some explain that even the sides are forbidden. Although the Beit Yosef rejected this fully, I saw in his Bedek HaBayis that he erased the rejection. Also, Rav Hai speaks of courtyards on the east side and holds leniently in that case.
