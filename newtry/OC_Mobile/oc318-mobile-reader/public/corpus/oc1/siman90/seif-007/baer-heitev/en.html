Synagogue. See Responsa Yad Eliyahu, siman 7, who concludes that what was said about one who prays behind a synagogue applies specifically when the congregation is praying the Shemoneh Esrei and he too is praying the Shemoneh Esrei; see there.
The congregation. Meaning, it speaks of one praying on the side where the entrance is open, namely the west. Therefore, if he turns his face toward the synagogue, he is praying in the same direction as the congregation, and is not called wicked. But if he turns his face away from the synagogue, he is praying opposite the congregation inside, and is called wicked, for it appears like two authorities, Heaven forbid. According to this explanation, if he stands behind the synagogue on the eastern side and turns away from the synagogue while praying eastward like the congregation, he is also not called wicked. See Eliyah Rabbah on this.
Opposite. This is Rabbeinu Yitzchak's view, cited in Tosafot there. He explains 'behind the synagogue' as behind the eastern wall, where there are no entrances. When one stands behind that wall with his back to the wall, even though his face is eastward, the direction in which the whole congregation prays, he is nevertheless called wicked. But when he turns his face toward the synagogue, since his face is toward the eastern wall, even though he stands outside and is found praying westward while the congregation faces east, we are not concerned, because the whole congregation faces the eastern wall where the ark is, and his face is also toward that wall.
Both. He should pray only on the west side and turn his face toward the synagogue.
To be stringent, etc. Although he is not praying in the direction in which the congregation prays, since he is not praying opposite them, we have no concern. Talmidei Rabbeinu Yonah; Beit Yosef.
Toward the synagogue. The same applies to the courtyard. So wrote the Kenesses HaGedolah: I have seen many communities whose courtyards are on the east side, and they pray with their faces eastward and their backs toward the eastern wall of the synagogue. It appears that they hold that a courtyard is like a house, where the Rambam permits praying toward the east even though one's back is toward the synagogue. See Lechem Chamudos, letter 83.
