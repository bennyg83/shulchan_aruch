(In the synagogue.) See in the responsum of Yad Eliyahu siman 7, who raised that it appears that what they said regarding one who prays behind the synagogue applies only specifically when the congregation prays Shemoneh Esreh and he too prays Shemoneh Esreh — see there.

(the congregation). That is, it is discussed regarding one who prays on that side where the opening is open there — namely on the west side; therefore if he turns his face toward the beit hakneset, he is found to pray toward the same side that the congregation prays. And therefore he is not called wicked. But if he turns his face away from the beit hakneset, he is found praying opposite the congregation that prays in the beit hakneset — he is called wicked, as it appears like two authorities, heaven forbid. And according to this explanation, if one stands behind the beit hakneset on the east side and turns away from the beit hakneset and prays toward the east as the congregation prays, he too is not called wicked. (And see in Sefer Eliyah Rabbah on this.)

(in the opposite direction). This is the view of R' Yitzchak brought in Tosafos there, who explains that behind the beit hakneset means behind the eastern wall where there are no openings; and when one stands behind that wall and his back is toward the wall, even though his face is toward the east and he is found praying toward the direction in which the whole congregation prays — nevertheless he is called wicked. And even if he turns his face toward the synagogue, since his face is toward the eastern wall, even though he stands outside and is found praying toward the west while the whole congregation faces the east — we have no concern, since the whole congregation faces the eastern wall where the Sanctuary is, and his face too is toward that wall — there is no concern.

Both of them — that he should pray only in the west and turn his face toward the synagogue.

To be stringent, etc. — even though he is not praying toward the direction the congregation prays, nevertheless since he is not praying in the opposite direction we have no concern. Terumat HaDeshen, Beit Yosef.

The synagogue. And the same applies in the ezra; thus Knesset HaGedolah — I saw many congregations whose ezra is toward the east, and they pray with their faces toward the east and their backs toward the eastern wall in the synagogue; and it appears they hold that the ezra is like a house where the Rambam permitted praying toward the east even though one's back faces the synagogue, etc. And see Levush Chadash sign 83.
