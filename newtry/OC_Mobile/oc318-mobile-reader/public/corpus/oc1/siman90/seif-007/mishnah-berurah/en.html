(15) Behind the beit knesset—in responsa Yad Eliyahu siman 7 he wrote that it appears to me that what they said regarding one who prays behind the beit knesset applies only when the congregation is praying the Shemoneh Esreh and he too prays Shemoneh Esreh—see there; and in Maamar Mordechai he disagrees with him.

(16) The congregation—that is, we deal with one who prays on the side where the door is open, namely the west side; therefore if he turns his face toward the beit knesset, he is found praying toward the same side as the congregation prays, and therefore he is not called wicked; but if he turns his face away from the beit knesset, he is found praying opposite the congregation—for they pray toward the east and he toward the west—and therefore he is called wicked, for he appears as two domains, heaven forbid; and according to this explanation, if he stands behind the beit knesset on the east side and turns away from the beit knesset and prays toward the east as the congregation prays, he is also not called wicked.

(17) In the opposite direction—this is the view of R' Yehuda as brought in Tosafos there, who explain "behind the beit knesset" means behind the eastern wall where there are no openings; and when he stands behind that wall with his back toward the wall, even though his face is toward the east and he is found praying toward the direction in which the whole congregation prays, nevertheless he is called wicked, for he is among those of whom it is said "and their backs toward the Temple of the Lord"; but if he turns his face toward the synagogue, even though he is found praying toward the west and the whole congregation faces east, we have no concern, since the whole congregation prays toward the eastern wall where the Temple was, and his face too is toward that wall—there is no concern.

(18) Both of them—that he should not pray except in the west and turn his face toward the beit knesset; and even though this is permitted according to all, nevertheless it is only permitted under duress; otherwise one is obligated to pray in the beit knesset, as written in seif 9.

(19) In other sides—that is, north or south; and we deal with one who cannot turn his face east, such as when feces is before him as full as his eyes can see; if not so, it is even better that he turn his face east; nevertheless even in all this it is only permitted under duress, as written in the previous note.

(20) That he turn his face—so that his back should not be against the beit knesset; and even though he is not yet praying toward the direction in which the congregation prays, nevertheless since he is not praying in the opposite direction, we have no concern—and all this when standing in the north of the beit knesset or its south; but when standing in its east or west, it is forbidden even if he wishes to tilt himself to his north or south.
