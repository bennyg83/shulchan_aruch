Regarding what he writes, 'Even if there are many sinners, etc.,' Rambam's wording does not say this; rather, it states, 'Even if there were sinners among them, etc.' My ancestor Maharsha writes in his Chiddushei Aggadot to Keritot, concerning the statement that any fast that does not include the sinners of Israel, etc., as demonstrated by the galbanum, etc., that there must be ten people apart from the sinner, just as the incense contained ten spices apart from the galbanum; see there. I later saw that Chemed LeAvraham writes likewise in Ein HaKoreh, nahar 46. He writes there that this applies specifically when there are ten righteous people and one wicked person, or twenty righteous people and two wicked people; nine righteous people, however, do not have the power, etc. Regarding what Ba'er Heitev writes from Responsa Tashbetz and Responsa Chacham Tzvi, see Shaagat Aryeh and She'elat Yaavetz, siman 18. See Shalmei Tzibur in the name of Radvaz, volume 1, who writes: 'But now that I see that a large crowd produces much confusion and no person hears the prayer or the Torah reading, I prefer praying in the study hall with ten together with the students, even though there is no large crowd, etc.' See there. See Responsa Mahari HaLevi, brother of the Taz, siman 13, regarding Abaye's statement, 'At first I would pray, etc.'; see there.
