He is called a bad neighbor, etc. This implies even if there is no minyan in the synagogue, for the teaching states simply, one who has a synagogue. There is further proof, for Daniel prayed alone yet prayed in a place of song and prayer, as Matteh Moshe writes. At first glance Daniel does not prove that he is called a bad neighbor if he does not go to synagogue; rather, he cites Daniel only to show that even an individual is obligated to pray in the synagogue. The Perishah explains that he is called a bad neighbor to the people living with him in the city, because he causes the Shechinah not to rest upon them. Therefore he is also called a bad neighbor toward the Holy One, blessed is He, in the manner of 'Those who hate You, Hashem, I hate.' One can also say that a bad neighbor does not benefit from his neighbor's closeness; so too, this person shows that he does not benefit from the Shechinah dwelling with him. In Bava Metzia 107a it says that if there are two synagogues, it is a mitzvah to go to the farther one, for he has reward for the steps.
