And a chest. The Beit Yosef wrote that the same applies to our beds made for sleeping, which are considered fixed. The Bach disagrees and holds that it is not considered fixed, because sometimes they are taken and placed elsewhere, and he proves this from Tosafot; see there. The Magen Avraham refutes his proof; see there. The Pri Chadash agrees with the Bach, unlike the Magen Avraham; see there. See Taz, who asks on the Beit Yosef why he did not answer simply like the questioner's understanding, that it speaks of a bed made for sleeping. So wrote the Pri Chadash; see there. I do not understand, for if so, what was he praising himself about, 'all my days I was distressed,' meaning that he was careful and guarded? What special caution is there, since it would have been better to pray by the wall? This requires understanding.
And its width is four. This requires study, for the practice is to pray in front of the shtender on which the siddurim are placed, even though it is ten high and four wide. Magen Avraham; see there. The Taz wrote that anything needed for prayer in the synagogue is not subject to the law of an intervening object. Therefore the custom is that each person has a shtender, although they are ten high and four wide. For this reason it seems to me that a table at home is also not considered an interruption for prayer, because the table is needed for prayer, since he places the book on it. Nevertheless, even regarding an item that would interrupt between him and the wall, if it is not easy to pray otherwise, such as when ten pray in a room and not everyone can stand without something intervening before the wall, he certainly should not delay prayer because of this and go to another room to pray, for this is only the preferred mitzvah and there is no prohibition when necessary because of cramped space. Nevertheless, he should close his eyes or pray from the siddur and not look outside, so that he not lose concentration. Thus the Taz.
