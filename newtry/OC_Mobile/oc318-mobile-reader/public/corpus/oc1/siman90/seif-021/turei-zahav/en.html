Such as an ark and chest, etc. In the Gemara it states: a baraita — Abba Benjamin says: on two matters I was distressed all my days — regarding my prayer that it be before my bed, etc. What is before my bed? Shall we say literally before my bed? But Rav said: from where do we learn for one who prays that nothing should interpose between him and the wall, etc.? Rather, that my prayer be adjacent to my bed. Rashi explained: that I not do melacha from when I rise from my bed until I pray, etc. And Tosafos wrote that nothing should interpose; but a fixed matter such as an ark and chest is not this interposition. But a bed seems it is not fixed. And Beit Yosef seems that this is specifically a bed in the time of the sages of the Talmud that was for sitting; but our bed, which is for lying down, is fixed. And it is difficult, for he resolves that my prayer be adjacent to my bed means after he rises from his bed in the morning — and if so why establish it in this and not according to its plain sense as the questioner's view, dealing with a bed for lying? And one may say that in the time of the Talmud there was one bed for lying and sitting — therefore it was not fixed since one must move it at mealtime. This is not so, for in the chapter HaMekabel it states regarding arranging a debtor — they give him two beds, one for eating and one for lying. If so, why not establish the resolution according to its plain sense on a bed for lying? Therefore according to my humble opinion this distinction is not correct, and every bed was interposition in this. And regarding the distinction Tosafos made between ark and chest and bed — one must understand what difference there is between them, for the synagogue chest too is sometimes moved, as we find in tractate Taanis that at a rain fast they would take the chest out to the city street. Rather it seems that these matters are needed for prayer in synagogue, and therefore interposition does not apply to them. And therefore it is customary in all synagogues that each person has a stand before him on which prayer books are placed, and they are not considered interposition at all even though they are ten tefachim high and four wide — as explained. Not so with a bed and other vessels in which prayer is not needed — therefore this is not called fixed for prayer and is interposition. And for this reason it seems to me that a table in the house is also not considered interposition for prayer, for the table is needed for prayer since one places the book on it. Nevertheless it seems that even regarding something that interposes between him and the wall, if one cannot easily pray otherwise — such as when sometimes ten pray in some room and not every person can stand without interposition to the wall — certainly he should not delay prayer for this to go to another room to pray, for the reason for prohibiting such interposition is lest intent be nullified, as Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Rambam in this matter; and this is only a preferred practice and there is no prohibition when it is necessary because of pressure of place. And nevertheless it seems a remedy for this that he close his eyes at the time of prayer, as written in seif 23, or pray from within the prayer book and not look outside and not bring about nullification of intent, as above.
