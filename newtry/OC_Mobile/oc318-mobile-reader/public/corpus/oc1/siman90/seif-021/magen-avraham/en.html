A cabinet and a chest. The Beit Yosef wrote that the same applies to our bed made for sleeping, which is called fixed. The Bach disagrees and holds that it is not called fixed, because sometimes it is taken and placed elsewhere, and he proves this from Tosafot. His words are not compelling, for Tosafot refers to the questioner, who thought that a bed is not called fixed, and it speaks of their beds, as the Beit Yosef wrote. It requires study that the custom is to pray before the shtender on which the siddurim are placed, though it is ten high and four wide. One cannot say it is because it is needed for prayer, for a table and chest are also needed for prayer, and yet they are permitted only when fixed. Nor can one say it is because it is not four wide below, for a bed too is presumably not four wide below, and the case is not of one closed below. See siman 87.
