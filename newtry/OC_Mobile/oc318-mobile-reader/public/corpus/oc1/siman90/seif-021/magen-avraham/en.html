Ark and chest. And like Beit Yosef, and the same applies to our bed made for sleeping — it is called fixed; and the Bach disagrees and holds it is not called fixed because sometimes they take it and set it elsewhere; and he proves this from Tosafos — but his words are not compelling, for Tosafos refer to the questioner; it seems to the questioner that a bed is not called fixed, and it deals with their bed as the Ramban wrote. And there is a difficulty: they practice praying before the shtender on which they place siddurim and it is ten handbreadths high and four wide; and I said because prayer requires it — for a table and chest also are needed for prayer and nevertheless it is permitted only when fixed; and I said because it is not four handbreadths wide from below — and a bed too is not four wide below, for presumably we do not deal with one sealed below — see ch. 7, and in Sha'agat Aryeh in Mordechai it is written: specifically something ten high and four wide, but benches and the like are not considered; for the essence is so one's eyes will not scatter here and there and one cannot concentrate; and with a small thing this does not happen as long as one does not turn one's face to the wall — and it appears from the elders who say so. Therefore when standing in the middle one need not be concerned if something interrupts; and in poskim it does not seem so — see siman 98 seif 14; and in Rashba responsa siman 96 it seems a chest does not interrupt since it is a synagogue utensil — so far.
