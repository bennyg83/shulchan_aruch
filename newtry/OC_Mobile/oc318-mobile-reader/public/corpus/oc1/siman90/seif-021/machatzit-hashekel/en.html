(s.k. 21) And the language of Tosafot does not imply thus — see in Beit Yosef, who explained his language. It is stated in the Gemara, Berachot 28b: Rav Avya read for him a portion and did not come to the derashah of Rav Yosef; the next day when he came before Rabbi Yosef, Abaye sought to appease Rabbi Yosef so there would not be resentment in his heart against Rav Avya. Abaye said to Rav Avya: why did the Master not come yesterday to the derashah? He said: I was weak-hearted and could not. He said: why did you not taste anything and come? He said: I did not think, etc., it is forbidden for a person to taste anything before praying Musaf (and Rav Yosef's derashah was before Musaf). He said: you should have prayed Musaf individually, then tasted something and come. He said: Rav Avya did not think, etc., it is forbidden to precede his prayer to the congregation's prayer. Abaye said: with the congregation we learned specifically that it is forbidden to precede, until here. And what we establish: even for an individual — meaning when he wishes to leave the synagogue — nevertheless it is forbidden to precede, as Magen Avraham wrote in s.k. 19 in the name of Rivash — meaning when she is healthy.

But in a synagogue it is forbidden. Meaning: even when he was weak-hearted — for Rav Avya answered Abaye thus, that it is forbidden to precede, etc., even though he was weak-hearted; and on this he answered: specifically with the congregation we learned — it implies even in such a case of weak-heartedness; but when he is not weak-hearted, even for an individual it is forbidden, as stated above. And it deals with Yom Kippur 2, that if he is sick, etc., that he wrote there that it is forbidden to mention rain in the last Musaf of the last day of Sukkot until the prayer leader announces; therefore even if he is sick or under duress he may not precede his prayer to the congregation's prayer, because it is forbidden to mention, etc.; and so in Tur in the name of Avi HaEzri; and Rabbi Yosef wrote: when he does not pray with the congregation, we must inform us; but if with the congregation, all days of the year too it is forbidden to precede his prayer to the congregation's prayer — until here. But Bach wrote there: because on other days of the year too it is forbidden to precede, etc.; therefore he said that here it deals even if he is sick or under duress, for on other days of the year one may precede, etc.; but here it is forbidden because it is forbidden to mention, etc. — until here.
