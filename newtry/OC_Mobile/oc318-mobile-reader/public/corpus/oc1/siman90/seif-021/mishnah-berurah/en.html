That nothing intervene. As it says, 'Chizkiyahu turned his face to the wall and prayed.' Nevertheless, this is only the preferred form of the mitzvah and there is no prohibition when needed because of cramped space. Therefore, if it is not easy to pray another way, such as when ten pray in some room and not everyone can stand without an intervening object before the wall, he should not delay prayer for this by going to another room. Nevertheless, he should close his eyes or pray from the siddur and not look outside, so that the intervening object before him not cause loss of concentration.
Between him and the wall. Even if he is far from the wall and stands in the middle of the synagogue, one should be careful that nothing intervene between him and the wall. The Peri Megadim inclines to say that if the intervening object is outside four amos from him, it is another domain and permitted; so ruled Magen Gibborim.
And a chest. Our beds made for sleeping, which are not usually moved from place to place, are called fixed objects and do not intervene. Some disagree, and it is good to be stringent when possible.
Only with a large object. See the Taz, that anything needed for prayer, even if very large, is not considered an interruption, such as the table at home on which the siddur is placed. Therefore the custom in synagogues is that each person has a small table, called a shtender, on which the siddurim are placed, even though it is ten high and four wide. So wrote other later authorities.
And its width is four. Even if it is four wide only above and not below.
It is not considered an interruption. The Pri Chadash disagrees, and Ma'amar Mordechai also wrote that this does not seem to follow from the Rambam's responsum cited in the Beit Yosef. Nevertheless, benches in the synagogue are not considered an interruption according to all views, because they are fixed; see the Pri Chadash there.
