(64) Between him and the wall — and even if he is far from the wall and stands in the middle of the synagogue, he should likewise be careful that nothing interposes between him and the wall; and the Peri Megadim leans to say that if the interposition is outside four amos from him, it is another domain and permitted; and the Magen Giborim ruled likewise.

(65) And a platform—and our beds made for lying down, which are not normally moved from place to place, are called fixed objects and do not interpose. Some disagree, and it is good to be stringent when possible.

(66) Only a large object — see in the Taz that any object needed for prayer, even if it is very large, is not considered an interruption, such as the table in the house on which they place the siddur; therefore they practice in the synagogue that before each person there is a small table [called a shtender] on which they place siddurim, even though it is ten tefachim high and four wide; and other later authorities likewise.

(67) And four tefachim wide — even if it is not four tefachim wide only above and not below.
