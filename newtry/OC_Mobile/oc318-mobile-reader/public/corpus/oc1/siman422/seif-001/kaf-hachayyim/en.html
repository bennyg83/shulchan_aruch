1) [Se'if 1] And one recites Ya'aleh V'Yavo, etc. In the text of Ya'aleh V'Yavo we say, 'Remember us, Havayah our God, on it for good, and take note of us on it for blessing.' The common practice is to pause between 'Remember us, Havayah our God, on it for good' and 'and take note of us on it for blessing' in order to answer Amen. Tola'as Yaakov, however, writes that 'remember us' refers to the masculine aspect and 'take note of us' to the feminine aspect, and one should therefore not interrupt between them. Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur, subsection 1; Olas Shabbos, subsection 1. Abudraham, however, implies that one should pause. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1.
2) There. And one recites Ya'aleh V'Yavo, etc. In some places the custom is to recite Ya'aleh V'Yavo aloud, like Atah Chonantanu and V'Sodieinu, in order to remind the congregation that it is Rosh Chodesh and to alert unlearned people who are unfamiliar with it. This is a proper custom, and there is no need for the alternative practice of interrupting before the beginning of the Amidah so that the attendant announces, 'Rosh Chodesh.' Reciting the chapter Barchi Nafshi before Ma'ariv and within Pesukei D'Zimrah also provides a reminder, through the association of one remembrance with another, not to forget to mention Rosh Chodesh. Ruach Chaim, subsection 1. However, regarding the statement about reciting it within Pesukei D'Zimrah, our custom is not to interrupt between the chapters of Tehillim to recite Barchi Nafshi. The custom of the pious members of Beis El, may their Rock and Redeemer protect them, who follow the Arizal, is likewise not to interrupt between the chapters of Tehillim on festivals to recite the chapter associated with the festival. Similarly, on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur they add no chapter but recite only the chapters of Tehillim associated with Shabbos. Regarding the statement that it is unnecessary for the attendant to interrupt and announce, etc., this was already explained above in siman 236, se'if 2, and in our words there in subsections 16 and 17. Consult them there.
3) There. At Ma'ariv, one is not required to repeat, etc. If one omitted Ya'aleh V'Yavo at Minchah and did not remember until Ma'ariv, one recites the Ma'ariv Amidah twice, as stated above in siman 108, se'if 11. See our words there in subsections 52, 54, and 55 regarding the laws relevant to this ruling.
4) There. At Ma'ariv, one is not required to repeat. Radvaz writes in Responsa, siman 561, that even where one need not repeat the Amidah, such as at Ma'ariv of Rosh Chodesh, if one remembers before beginning Modim, one does not repeat the blessing but recites Ya'aleh V'Yavo there, between the blessings. Maran, however, rules in siman 294, se'if 4, that immediately upon completing the blessing, one may not go back. This implies that one does not mention it at all, and this is the primary ruling. Eliyah Rabbah, siman 114, subsection 7, and Birkei Yosef, subsection 1, agree. Ma'amar Mordechai, subsection 5, and Shalmei Tzibbur, folio 220c, write likewise. See above, siman 294, subsection 17.
5) There. At Ma'ariv, one is not required to repeat. Once one has said, 'Baruch Atah Havayah,' even if one has not completed the words, 'Who restores His Presence to Tziyon,' one does not go back. Rather, one completes the blessing and recites Modim. Ma'amar Mordechai, subsection 4; Shalmei Tzibbur, there, folio 220d; Shaarei Teshuvah, subsection 2. See below, subsection 11.
6) There. At Ma'ariv, one is not required to repeat. If one did not recite Ma'ariv on Rosh Chodesh and recited the Shacharis Amidah twice but omitted Ya'aleh V'Yavo from the second Amidah, one need not repeat it. The same applies when Rosh Chodesh lasts two days and one did not recite Minchah on the first day, then omitted Rosh Chodesh from the makeup Amidah on the second night: one need not repeat it. Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur. We likewise wrote this above in siman 108, subsections 39 and 40. See there. See also the gloss there to se'if 9 and our words there, with the help of Heaven.
7) There. But if one omitted it at Shacharis, etc. If one omitted Ya'aleh V'Yavo and remembered after reciting the first three blessings while intending to recite Mussaf, it is obvious that one should complete the Shacharis Amidah, even though the first three blessings were recited with the intention of saying Mussaf. Responsa Zera Emes, Orach Chaim, siman 47; Machazik Berachah, subsection 1. Nehar Shalom in siman 424, Ma'amar Mordechai in siman 423, subsection 3, Shalmei Tzibbur, folio 222b, and Shaarei Teshuvah in this siman, subsection 3, write likewise.
