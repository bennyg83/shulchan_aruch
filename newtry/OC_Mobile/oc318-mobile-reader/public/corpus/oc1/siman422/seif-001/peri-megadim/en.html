And one who examines Yerushalmi chapter "three who ate" — Rav and Shmuel disagree in Birkat HaMazon regarding one who did not mention Shabbat: Rav said he returns and Shmuel said he does not return. Shimon bar Abba in the name of R' Yochanan: if in doubt whether he mentioned Rosh Chodesh — he does not return. He found a tanna who argues: a day that has musaf — one mentions; and if not, he returns. A day that has no musaf — Chanukah and Purim — he does not return. And Beit Yosef explained: if Shimon bar Abba deals with prayer — the tanna argues that doubt is like certainty regarding what one is accustomed to mention, as written in Yerushalmi in tractate Taanit. Or Shimon bar Abba deals with Birkat HaMazon similar to Shabbat there — and doubt is like certainly he did not mention what one is accustomed to mention; and R' Yochanan who said regarding Rosh Chodesh "if in doubt, etc." — certainly he does not return; and he holds: if in doubt regarding Shabbat he returns like Rav; and the tanna argues even on Rosh Chodesh; and on chol hamoed in Birkat HaMazon he returns — and therefore we do not hold like the tanna who argues but like our Gemara: on Rosh Chodesh he does not return — deals with siman 108 page 2 in the explanation of Maharar Eliyahu z"l there — that it is according to Beit Yosef's explanation; but for Darkei Moshe the halacha is like R' Yochanan in doubt on Rosh Chodesh — since he is not accustomed thirty days he does not return in doubt in prayer which is d'rabbanan; or when he prayed Musaf first per Rambam z"l — and the tanna who argues is not against R' Yochanan but against Shmuel who said on Shabbat in Birkat HaMazon he does not return even though he is obligated to eat bread; and the tanna said on Rosh Chodesh and chol hamoed in prayer he returns — for the obligation is the same: bread on Shabbat is obligatory — so too one may explain according to Perishah and Derishah — see there.
