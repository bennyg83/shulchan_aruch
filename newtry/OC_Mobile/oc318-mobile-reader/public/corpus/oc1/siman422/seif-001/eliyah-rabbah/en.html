Yaaleh VeYavo, etc. Avudraham writes in the laws of Pesach: Had we said only “may it rise,” I would have thought it should rise but not come. We therefore say “and come.” Had we said only “and come,” etc. Had we said “and be accepted,” I might have thought it should be accepted but not heard in the world in which we seek acceptance. Therefore, etc. Had we said “and be heard,” I might have thought only once. We therefore say “and be remembered.” Had we said “and be remembered,” I might have thought twice, but not forever. We therefore say “and be recalled” forever. Thus all these expressions are necessary. There are eight, corresponding to the eight levels mentioned in Atah Bechartanu. How so? Corresponding to “and Your great Name,” we say “may it rise,” meaning rise upward. Corresponding to “upon us You called,” we say “and come upon us.” Corresponding to “and You drew us near, our King, to Your service,” we say “and reach,” referring to the time of drawing near and service. Corresponding to “and You sanctified us,” we say “and be seen,” referring to holiness, because through our sins holiness is not visible among us. Corresponding to “and You exalted us,” we say “and be accepted,” meaning that He should exalt us. Corresponding to “and You desired us,” we say “and be heard,” because You desire us. Corresponding to “You loved us,” we say “and be remembered,” referring to the remembrance from ancient times. Corresponding to “You chose us,” we say “and be recalled,” referring to the choice by which He chose Avram, etc. Five remembrances are mentioned, corresponding to the five times Israel is mentioned in one verse, “I have given the Leviim,” etc., because the Holy One, blessed be He, desires to mention Israel at every moment. Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah writes that people ordinarily pause between “Remember us, Hashem our God, on it for good” and “and recall us on it for blessing” in order to answer amen. Tolaas Yaakov, however, writes that “remember us” refers to the male and “recall us” to the female, and therefore one should not interrupt. Avudraham writes that “Remember us,” etc., is based upon “Remember me, my God, for good,” while “recall us,” etc., is based upon “I have surely remembered you,” and “save us,” etc., is based upon “Save us, God of our salvation.” This implies that one may interrupt. See siman 236, seif 2, that at Maariv the shamash announces Yaaleh VeYavo between redemption and prayer. Nevertheless, one may not interrupt between Shema and its blessings.
