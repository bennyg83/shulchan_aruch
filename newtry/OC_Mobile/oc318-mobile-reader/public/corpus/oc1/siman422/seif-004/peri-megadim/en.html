Hallel is recited standing. See Taz. If one recited it sitting, he fulfilled his obligation. A proof may be brought from Hallel on Pesach night, and also from Sefiras HaOmer, as in siman 489, Magen Avraham, seif katan 3. This appears correct. See Magen Avraham, seif katan 11, and Taz there, seif katan 141. See Peri Chadash, who writes that Hallel may be recited in any language even if one does not understand it. See Magen Avraham, siman 62, seif katan 1, and what is written there. Peri Chadash gives another reason that Hallel is recited standing: it is testimony that Hashem performed miracles and wonders for us, and testimony must be given while standing. See there. Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 1, writes that the expressions zachreinu and ufkadeinu may be used for both masculine and feminine subjects. See there. The custom is to answer amen to the phrases zachreinu and ufkadeinu lechaim. The attendant announces Yaaleh VeYavo at Maariv between Kaddish and the Amidah. See siman 236.
