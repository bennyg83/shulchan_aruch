Standing. Except on Pesach night. Beit Yosef in the name of Shibbolei HaLeket. It is forbidden to lean upon a pillar or wall, because leaning is not considered standing, as Tur writes in siman 141 and Tosafos writes in Sotah 40a. I am greatly astonished by Rama in Choshen Mishpat, siman 17, who writes in the name of Rivash that regarding testimony, standing while leaning is considered standing. Tosafos and Tur prove from Zevachim that leaning is not standing. It is obvious that Rama saw Beit Yosef at the end of siman 28 of Choshen Mishpat, who writes in the name of Rivash: If testimony was accepted while the witnesses stood, the act is valid. If they were leaning upon a pillar, this is even permitted initially, because leaning is not sitting. Rama understood the clause about leaning as referring to the witnesses. This is incorrect, because the passage began with the judges. There is plainly a textual error. The correct reading is as Beit Yosef records in Shulchan Aruch, that leaning is like sitting. It refers to the judges, meaning that if the judges leaned upon a pillar, this is permitted because leaning counts as sitting. This appears completely clear. Shevuos also implies this, where one sitting is compared to someone untying his shoes. Although there leaning is also treated like standing, that case is different because only an expression of honor is required. Where actual standing is required, one may not lean. Tosafos writes this in Zevachim 19a. Although Tosafos writes there on 19b that despite the prohibition against sitting in the Temple courtyard, leaning is permitted, this does not prove that leaning is not sitting. There, only actual sitting is prohibited because of the honor of the place. Regarding judges, however, leaning is considered sitting.
