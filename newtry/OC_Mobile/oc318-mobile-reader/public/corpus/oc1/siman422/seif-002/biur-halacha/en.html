(א) And we recite, etc. — it is stated in the Gemara Megillah 20 that Hallel's time is the whole day [except Hallel of Passover night]; and it is stated in She'elat Yaavetz that Hallel applies to Shacharit prayer; therefore one should not taste until after Hallel — unless for one whose heart is weak, for whom it is permitted; and even as a matter of piety there is none. And regarding reciting Hallel in a mourner's house within seven days — see above siman 131 in Mishna Berurah s.k. 20.

(ב) Hallel — Rabbeinu Yerucham wrote in Hallel: even though he fulfilled his obligation whether as an individual or as a congregation — specifically when he is not expert and does not know how to answer; but if he knows how to answer, if he fulfilled he does not fulfill another's obligation unless he answers after him "Halleluyah" — end of his words. And Magen Avraham wrote that his words' plain meaning is obvious; and so is explained explicitly in the Gemara at the end of chapter 3 of Rosh Hashanah in Hallel and in Megillah — even though he fulfilled he fulfills [another] [and Netiv Chayim wrote that in chapter 3 of Rosh Hashanah it was said only regarding blessings and not regarding Hallel — and the end of the chapter escaped him]; and what he concludes that all this is when he is not expert is from the Gemara Sukkah 38: "Greater is recitation" — and he answers after him "Halleluyah" — thus he must at least answer "Halleluyah"; and he holds it is to impede; and if so, in the Gemara Rosh Hashanah that concludes and teaches anonymously "even though he fulfilled he fulfills" and does not require answering — it deals with one who cannot answer [and one may ask somewhat: how is it possible he cannot answer the word "Halleluyah"? Perhaps he does not know in which place to answer this word]. And what Rabbeinu Yerucham wrote "he fulfilled" is not precise — for the same applies even if he did not yet fulfill himself: he cannot fulfill another if the other can answer unless he answers; and so Eshel Avraham and Peri Chadash wrote — specifically when he fulfilled must he answer; but if he has not yet fulfilled himself and blesses for himself and for his fellow, even if his fellow did not answer anything he has fulfilled. And they support this from the sugya of "three who ate" [and see in Peri Chadash there that he also disagrees on the fundamental law of Rabbeinu Yerucham — per him even when expert one can fulfill; and so above siman 273 in Biur Halacha; and see Ritva Sukkah 38]. And it is further explained in the Gemara Sukkah 38 and in poskim that women are exempt from Hallel because it is a time-bound positive commandment [except Hallel of Passover nights, for they are obligated because they too were in that miracle — so Tosafot there]; therefore they cannot fulfill men unless the men answer them word for word [and it concludes in the baraita there: a curse should come upon a man who needed his wife to recite for him]. And behold it seems from Magen Avraham that this law applies also on Rosh Chodesh; and in my humble opinion this is not clear — for this applies only to say on days when they complete Hallel, for upon men there is obligation from the Gemara "women cannot fulfill them" even where they practiced this mitzva from before — if only from custom alone and not from obligation; but on days when they do not complete Hallel, for men too it is only from custom; and in such a place women too practiced this mitzva — what difference between these and those? And perhaps Magen Avraham's intent is where women had not held this mitzva until now — that even from custom they have no obligation; and now a woman wants to recite and fulfill — requires study. And know that it is plain that in any case she herself may recite Hallel and bless even though she is not obligated — as in every time-bound positive commandment that meticulous women practice upon themselves and bless; and it further seems reasonable that also in Rosh Chodesh Hallel per our custom that we bless on Hallel — a woman too may bless like men, as Tosafot wrote in Erchin 10 [and we copied their words in Shaarei Tzion letter 5]; and in Siddur Yaavetz he wrote that women should not bless on Rosh Chodesh Hallel — and we do not practice like him.
