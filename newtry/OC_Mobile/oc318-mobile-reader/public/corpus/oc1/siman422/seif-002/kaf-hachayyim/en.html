And one recites Ya'aleh V'Yavo, etc. In some places the custom is to recite Ya'aleh V'Yavo aloud, like Atah Chonantanu and V'Sodieinu, in order to remind the congregation that it is Rosh Chodesh and to alert unlearned people who are unfamiliar with it. This is a proper custom, and there is no need for the alternative practice of interrupting before the beginning of the Amidah so that the attendant announces, 'Rosh Chodesh.' Reciting the chapter Barchi Nafshi before Ma'ariv and within Pesukei D'Zimrah also provides a reminder, through the association of one remembrance with another, not to forget to mention Rosh Chodesh. Ruach Chaim, subsection 1. However, regarding the statement about reciting it within Pesukei D'Zimrah, our custom is not to interrupt between the chapters of Tehillim to recite Barchi Nafshi. The custom of the pious members of Beis El, may their Rock and Redeemer protect them, who follow the Arizal, is likewise not to interrupt between the chapters of Tehillim on festivals to recite the chapter associated with the festival. Similarly, on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur they add no chapter but recite only the chapters of Tehillim associated with Shabbos. Regarding the statement that it is unnecessary for the attendant to interrupt and announce, etc., this was already explained above in siman 236, se'if 2, and in our words there in subsections 16 and 17. Consult them there.
