(12) And Hallel is recited with omissions. The entire Hallel is not recited because Scripture states, "The song shall be for you as on the night when a festival is sanctified." We expound that a day sanctified as a festival, meaning that melacha is forbidden, requires song, while a day not sanctified as a festival does not require song. Arachin 10a. Our ancestors nevertheless customarily recited it, and in order to make it recognizable that its recitation is not required by law, passages are omitted. The widespread custom is to omit from "Not to us" until "Hashem remembered us," and from "I love, for Hashem hears" until "What shall I return." Some omit different passages. Rambam. See Eliyah Rabbah for the reason for our custom.
(13) He need not repeat it. This is b'dieved, because the expression "completes" does not necessarily refer specifically to one who recites the entire Hallel. In the language of the Sages, "completes" sometimes means simply "recites."
(14) Some say that even the congregation, etc. The essential recitation of Hallel on Rosh Chodesh is only a custom and not an obligation by law, as stated above, and one does not recite a blessing over a custom.
(15) Some say that an individual also, etc. Even though its recitation is only a custom, we find many practices that are only customary and over which a blessing is nevertheless recited.
(16) Nevertheless, a person should be careful, etc. This is in order to satisfy the opinion of the poskim who hold that an individual does not recite a blessing over it. The later authorities therefore write that if one arrives at the synagogue near the time of Hallel, he should first recite Hallel with the congregation and afterward pray. They further write that if he is in the middle of Pesukei DeZimrah, he should interrupt to recite Hallel with the congregation, and this is not considered an improper interruption, for it is no worse than the psalms added on Shabbos. However, he should not recite a blessing over Hallel, either before or after it. He fulfills the need for blessings through Baruch SheAmar, which he already recited at the beginning, and Yishtabach at the end. All this applies to Hallel on Rosh Chodesh, regarding which some poskim hold that no blessing is recited. On days when the entire Hallel is recited, however, when all agree that he is obligated to recite a blessing, he should not recite it in the middle of Pesukei DeZimrah, because he would thereby lose its blessings.
(17) He says it to two people, etc. For example, when he says "Give thanks," they should likewise respond after him, "Give thanks," and when he says "Please," they should respond after him, "Please."
(18) The custom is to do this with "Give thanks" but not with "Please." This practice is more applicable to "Give thanks," because he says "Give thanks," which implies that he is addressing others. The later authorities write that there is no practical difference in this respect between Rosh Chodesh and days on which the entire Hallel is recited. In every case he should recite it before two people. When Rama writes that it is then considered like a group, he means that on Rosh Chodesh there is an additional reason for doing so, because he thereby also somewhat satisfies the opinion of the poskim who require a congregation for the blessing over Hallel. Nevertheless, they write that this entire practice is only ideal. If two people are not present, he need not seek them out.
