(There seif 2) And we recite Hallel with skips, etc. Gloss: behold in the Gemara Erchin [10b] we say: Rosh Chodesh is called moed — let us say; and it answers: because it is not forbidden in melacha, as written: "this song shall be yours as on a night of consecrating a festival, a night of being consecrated for a festival requires song, and what is not consecrated for a festival does not require song" — see there. And behold I was asked by a certain sage from what is written in Pesachim 69 on the second Passover that it does not require Hallel in its eating, as written: "this night shall be for you as a night of consecrating, a festival night consecrated for a festival requires song; what is not consecrated for a festival — also requires song"; and it challenges: if so, in its performance too; and it answers: night we exclude, day we do not exclude, etc.; and if so on Rosh Chodesh whose Hallel time is by day — why do we exclude it from "consecrated festival night"? End of his question. And this is what I answered: behold to my humble opinion I will explain to him the matter — for we have two times to say Hallel: one — if it is the day of the miracle even though it is not a festival, such as Chanukah; or it is a moed day and thus even though it is not the time of the miracle. Therefore Rosh Chodesh — where there is no time of the miracle, only it is a matter of doubt from the fact it is called moed — therefore in this one should not learn it from Passover, which is the time of the miracle, only it is a matter of doubt to compare it to a festival since it is called moed; therefore the Gemara answers that from a festival one should not learn it — because it is forbidden in melacha. But from where do we know this itself — that Hallel depends on melacha? Therefore the Gemara brings proof from that verse "as on a night of consecrating a festival"; and automatically it is proved from there that Hallel depends on melacha; and therefore there is no proof from moed — since there it is forbidden in melacha; and since from moed there is no proof, again a verse is not needed to exclude — for it is reasonable not to say it, for from the Torah it is not said "it" since it is not the time of the miracle nor moed. But on the second Passover — since it is from the matter of the miracle of the two makeups of the first; and in any case one who sees the place of the miracle and remembrance of the miracle is like the day of the miracle — therefore on the second Passover it is reasonable to say Hallel even though it is not a festival, like Chanukah; and for this we need a verse to exclude that we not say Hallel on it; and since a verse is needed to exclude — therefore here it says well: night we exclude, day we do not exclude. And so it is correct, with Heaven's help; examine. There in Hagahah: and there are those who say also an individual blesses on it, etc. Gloss: behold thus is Tosafot's view that one blesses on Rosh Chodesh Hallel. And behold they proved thus from what we say in tractate Megillah: Rav came at the beginning of the month, heard them reciting Hallel, wanted to stop them; when he heard they skip by leaps he said: evidently the custom of their ancestors is in their hands, etc. — see there what Tosafot questioned: he should have known from what they did not bless, etc. And in my humble opinion it seems there is to resolve: behold they said in the Gemara: whoever recites Hallel every day — behold this is blaspheming and reviling, etc.; and see Magen Avraham in the laws of Rosh Chodesh. And per this — if we say on Rosh Chodesh there is no obligation to recite Hallel — if so it is forbidden to recite Hallel as praise, not as request; for if he blasphemes and reviles. But it seems that blaspheming and reviling is only when one recites and completes all Hallel; but when he skips in the middle of Hallel — then he is not blaspheming and reviling, etc. And behold we hold: a custom that is against the law — we are not concerned for the custom and do not follow it. And therefore at first too Rav knew when he saw they did not bless — he knew it was from custom; but nevertheless he thought to stop them because he thought they would recite all Hallel — and then he would be blaspheming and reviling; and it is against the law — we do not follow this custom; but when he saw they skip by leaps — then he is not blaspheming and reviling; and further they may go after the custom since it is not against the law. So it appears correct to me. And there is proof for the above distinction from what they do not complete Hallel on the seventh day of Passover because of "the act of My hands," etc.; and we say half Hallel — understand and examine.
