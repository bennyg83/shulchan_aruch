And they recite Hallel. Shehecheyanu is not recited over Hallel because some months are deficient, and the recitation then occurs within thirty days. Avudraham. This requires study, because even when the month is full there are two days of Rosh Chodesh, and Hallel is recited on the thirtieth day. Perhaps he holds that since the second day is primary, the first day resembles reading from the Torah when one is not obligated in the matter. The basic premise, however, is difficult. Shehecheyanu was not instituted for something that occurs regularly. Berachos 37 states that it is recited specifically for something that comes after half a year. See what I wrote in siman 225. Women are exempt from every form of Hallel because it is a time bound positive mitzvah. They therefore cannot discharge others, unless the men repeat every word after them. A curse should come upon one who did not learn and must be led in this way. If he did learn, he degrades his Creator by appointing such agents. See Tosafos, Berachos 20b, and the Mishnah in the third chapter of Sukkah. See what I wrote in siman 479. Bedek HaBayis cites Rabbeinu Yerucham, netiv 13: Although one has already fulfilled his obligation, he may discharge either an individual or a congregation. This applies specifically where the listener is not proficient and does not know how to respond. If he knows how to respond, one who already fulfilled his obligation cannot discharge him unless the listener answers Halleluyah after him. Bedek HaBayis asks where this appears in the Gemara. I am astonished that Beit Yosef, for whom the paths of the Talmud were illuminated, asked this. The first part is an explicit passage in the third chapter of Rosh Hashanah, and the latter part is the Mishnah in the third chapter of Sukkah. Rabbeinu Yerucham’s formulation, “If he already fulfilled his obligation, he cannot discharge him,” is not precise. The same applies even if he has not yet fulfilled his own obligation. The listener must answer Halleluyah. The reason he mentioned one who already fulfilled his obligation is that when the reader has not yet fulfilled his own obligation, there is no concern regarding the reader, since he reads for himself. If he has already fulfilled it, he may not read again for his fellow unless the fellow responds Halleluyah. It must be that the reader recites the blessing. Mere reading is not prohibited. Perhaps even reading is prohibited, because Shabbos, chapter 6, says that one who recites Hallel every day is a blasphemer. It may therefore be forbidden to recite it as song except at times established by the Sages. This requires study in Shiltei HaGibborim, third chapter of Berachos, in the name of Riaz.
To recite it with the congregation. If one arrives at the synagogue close to Hallel, he should first recite Hallel with the congregation and afterward pray. Lechem Chamudos. Perhaps if he is in the middle of Pesukei DeZimrah, he may interrupt to recite Hallel with the congregation. This is no worse than the extra Tehillim recited on Shabbos. Nevertheless, it appears to me that in such a case he should not recite a blessing over Hallel, either before or after it. Since he already recited Baruch SheAmar and will recite Yishtabach, how can he recite two additional blessings? A similar principle is stated regarding the Haggadah. He should therefore do this only for the Hallel of Rosh Chodesh, because many poskim hold that no blessing is recited over it. On days when the complete Hallel is recited, he should not recite it in the middle of Pesukei DeZimrah.
He says to two people, etc. Lechem Chamudos writes that this applies only on days when the incomplete Hallel is recited, because of doubt concerning the blessing. The common practice to do this even on days when the complete Hallel is recited is mistaken. It appears that he did not see Agur and Shibbolei HaLeket, who cite Midrash Shocher Tov that one should not say Hodu unless two people are present. The reason is: to whom is he saying “Give thanks”? Tur writes similarly in siman 479. The established custom is to seek two people on Pesach night, although there is no doubt regarding the blessing. Mordechai explains that it is a mitzvah to answer the opening phrases. See Yalkut Tehillim, siman 112. Agur asks why this differs from other passages of Hodu recited by an individual, such as in the Great Hallel. Perhaps here, because it is recited as song, others must answer, as we find in the Song at the Sea. See citations 81, 100, 140.
