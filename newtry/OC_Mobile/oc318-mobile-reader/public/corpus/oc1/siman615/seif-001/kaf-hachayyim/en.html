1) [Seif 1] Marital relations are forbidden on Yom Kippur. This is derived from Scripture, Yoma 77, for it is considered an affliction. Tur and Beit Yosef.
2) Ibid. And it is forbidden to touch one's wife, etc. The Taz, s.k. 1, distinguished between the cases, ruling that at night even touching must be forbidden, because marital relations ordinarily occur at night, but during the day there is no prohibition except sleeping in one bed, not touching. He wrote that this also appears from the Agudah, which states that during the day one need not be careful, see there. However, Eliyah Rabbah, sec. 1, wrote regarding these words of the Taz that they are not compelling. Regarding what the Taz wrote, that this appears from the Agudah, Eliyah Rabbah wrote that it seems he examined only the brief citation in the Beit Yosef and not the Agudah itself in the chapter Makom Shenahagu, where it is explicitly explained that touching is forbidden even during the day. This is likewise explained in the chapter Yom HaKippurim, and the words of Maharil also escaped his attention, see there. Birkei Yosef, sec. 1, likewise challenged the aforementioned words of the Taz, and also Ateres Zekeinim, who similarly cited from the novellae of the Agudah. He wrote that the view of the Shulchan Aruch is to forbid touching both by day and by night, and that this is also the view of the Agudah and Maharil, as Eliyah Rabbah wrote, see there. So too wrote Magen Avraham, s.k. 1; Mishbetzos Zahav, sec. 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, sec. 1; Chayei Adam, rule 145, sec. 22; Derech HaChaim, sec. 8; Mateh Ephraim, sec. 1; Mishna Berurah, sec. 1. See Ben Ish Chai, parashas Vayeilech, sec. 15, who wrote that for some need one may rely upon those who permit it during the day, see there. Nevertheless, the words of the aforementioned poskim imply that it is forbidden under all circumstances, and examine carefully.
