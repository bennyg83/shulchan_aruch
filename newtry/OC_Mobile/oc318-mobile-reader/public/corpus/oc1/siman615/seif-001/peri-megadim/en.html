And it is forbidden to touch. See the Taz. See siman 554 in the Magen Avraham, letter 19, in the name of the Darkei Moshe; perhaps the same applies to touching, only the Shulchan Aruch there did not mention touching. Meaning, Tishah B'Av is rabbinic, whereas Yom Kippur is according to the Torah and is worse. There he is lenient by day, while here he forbids even by day because it is from the Torah. See Eliyah Rabbah. In the Levush he wrote the opposite; see there. What was written there, that he forbids touching lest he come to thoughts and emission, is difficult: why not say lest he come to have relations with her, as on Tishah B'Av? See the Taz, who also implies that by day touching is permitted. Perhaps on Tishah B'Av it is permitted by day because Yisrael are holy, unlike Yom Kippur, where perhaps he will come to thoughts and emission and will have to worry, and it is more severe.
