Seif 1: Yom Kippur is forbidden in marital relations, etc. NB: Beit Yosef brings thus in the name of Mordechai. In my view it requires study from Yerushalmi ch. 8 of Yoma, also brought in Beit Yosef this siman, which asks: washing is forbidden — marital relations all the more so; it should resolve where they do not immerse or before Ezra instituted immersion for those who had seminal emission — end quote. The intent is as Beit Yosef and Kaf HaChayim: the question is that anyway relations are forbidden needing immersion to pray and washing is forbidden — why ask? There is a difference: if forbidden from the act itself biblically, also forbidden to sleep with her and touch her by rabbinic decree lest it lead to Torah violation; if only due to immersion concern it is rabbinic and prayer is rabbinic — a decree on a decree — and one could touch her; perforce on Yom Kippur one may touch her. When studying this I saw in Kaf HaChayim Shirurei Korban who asked: perhaps the Mishnah teaches that marital relations are forbidden near sunset after he prayed and need not pray again and can immerse at night — he says it is forced to say from forbidding relations by day; in my view it is not so forced — Yerushalmi's question is in two ways: marital pleasure exceeds washing and shoe-wearing, so relations should be forbidden all the more; one could say relations is superior because of conjugal obligation mitzvah — therefore it asks well: washing forbidden, so cannot have relations due to immersion prohibition; conjugal obligation is only at night not day — so at night forbidden from washing, near sunset no mitzvah — kal vachomer from other afflictions — why teach relations? This is Yerushalmi's intent. Also question on Yerushalmi: perhaps Mishnah follows R' Yosi who holds immersion in its time is a mitzvah — washing forbidden only if not in time, but in time mitzvah permitted on Yom Kippur as in our Gemara — if he has relations it is immersion in time and one might think permitted — therefore it teaches forbidden from the act itself. One cannot say immersion in time only if accidental where positive command overrides Yom Kippur prohibition, but if willingly — negligent positive command does not override — R' Yosi agrees forbidden; or say difference if obligated in conjugal mitzvah it is a real mitzvah (Pesachim ch. 6 mistaken mitzvah) — not negligent — immersion in time mitzvah — might think permitted — why ask? Requires study — examine well.
