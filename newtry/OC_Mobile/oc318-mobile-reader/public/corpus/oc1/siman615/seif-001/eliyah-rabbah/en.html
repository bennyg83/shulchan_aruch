[א] [Levush] And even to touch, etc. In Shulchan Aruch he wrote the opposite order: first not to touch and then not to sleep. Taz challenged: it is obvious that if touching is forbidden, all the more sleeping on a bed; it appears to me therefore Levush was precise and wrote the opposite. In any case what Taz was precise about here, that Shulchan Aruch permits touching by day — it is not clear to learn this permission from weak inference. And further difficult: in Yoreh De'ah siman 195 he also wrote first the law of touching and then the law of sleeping. Therefore it appears sleeping is also a stringency, even if they sleep each in his garment and do not touch each other — it is forbidden. And what Taz wrote is his wording; and likewise in Agudah on Yom Kippur night it is forbidden to touch, etc., implying by day it is permitted — up to here his words. It seems he only looked at Beit Yosef who brought it briefly and not at Agudah itself, chapter Makom Shenahagu, which deals explicitly with lighting a candle; but regarding touching it is explained forbidden even by day, and likewise explained in chapter Yom Kippur. Also omitted were the words of Maharil: in Agudah he forbids touching both by day and by night and one should not speak much with her, for it is more severe than niddah.
