And it is forbidden to touch his wife. In siman 554, regarding Tishah B'Av, he did not mention a prohibition of touching, only the prohibition of sleeping with her in one bed. In truth, they are equal, as the Beis Yosef wrote in the name of Hagahos Mordechai regarding sleeping in a bed here; only Sefer Agudah brought the prohibition of touching on Yom Kippur and did not mention Tishah B'Av. It further appears to be precise here in the Shulchan Aruch, which wrote, 'and so it is forbidden to sleep,' etc., which is entirely superfluous: regarding another man's wife, where it is forbidden to sleep in one bed but touching is permitted, once he wrote first the prohibition of touching, it is obvious that one bed is forbidden. In my humble opinion, one should distinguish: on Tishah B'Av the Shulchan Aruch did not forbid touching because on that day she goes in degraded clothing and he will not desire her so much; this is unlike Yom Kippur, when she goes in clean clothing. On Yom Kippur too there is a distinction: at night one should forbid even touching, because marital relations are common at night, but by day the only prohibition is sleeping in one bed, not touching, especially since the awe of the day is upon him, as written at the end of siman 612. This is unlike sleeping, when awe is not upon him; therefore one must be careful even by day not to be in one bed. Therefore here he first wrote the prohibition of touching, which applies specifically at night, and so too in Agudah: 'the night of Yom Kippur, it is forbidden to touch,' etc., implying that by day one need not be careful. Afterward the Shulchan Aruch wrote the prohibition of sleeping in a bed, which applies even by day. So it seems to me regarding the view of the Shulchan Aruch, and it is correct.
