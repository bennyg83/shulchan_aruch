In the blessing. In Pithchei Tefillah (seif 68, siman 485): "kohanim im" — the word kohanim is not attached to the word im (as written siman 128, seif 10 in the gloss), for we establish halachah that kohanim are agents of the Merciful One; rather the meaning is that the kohanim are called "your holy nation." Even though a nation in general is not called [holy], as said in the chapter HaZeroa, "your holy nation" is called — as said in chapter 4 of Yoma. It is written in Zohar Chadash, parashat Nasso: when he says "May Heaven bless you," he should look toward the side of the Sanctuary; "and keep you," he should look toward his right side; "May Heaven illuminate," toward the Sanctuary; "His face toward you," etc.; "and favor you," he should look toward the left side to unite it in the right. It is good to say at the time of saying Birkat Kohanim: "Please draw near the salvation of those who hope for You," etc. And this is a name of 22 [letters] (Tzemach Tzedek, parashat Nasso, and Shelah).

Until "and I, etc." — and we only say until "peace."

"So may it be Your will." It implies they should not answer except at the end. And some practice answering between each verse, ken yehi ratzon; but those who at the beginning say only "yehi ratzon" err, for then there is no meaning to what "yehi ratzon" refers to (Bach).
