(Seif kaf bet) In one bowing, etc. — [because of] "Heaven straightens the bent," as above, siman 113, seif zayin.

It seemed to me his intent, etc. — He comes to reconcile that even at the beginning of a blessing one must straighten up at the Name; for at the beginning of the eighteen [blessings], i.e., the beginning of the blessing of the Fathers, when one bows one must straighten up at the Name. And likewise the Bach raised [this difficulty]. And also he comes to give a reason for the distinction: that it is necessarily to straighten up so it appear he did not bow, because of the enactments of the Sages; and therefore he straightens up when he fulfills the enactments of the Sages.

Therefore he should straighten up at the Name — he meant to say: Since otherwise he would need to straighten up, therefore it is preferable especially when he straightens up at the Name because of "Heaven straightens the bent," rather than straightening up afterward; but [to straighten up] purely because of "Heaven straightens the bent" alone — if not that otherwise he would need to straighten up, he would not need to straighten up.

And the same applies at the beginning of the blessing. Where he straightens up immediately, this is because of the enactment of the Sages that one must straighten up after bowing; if so, likewise it is preferable that the straightening up be at the Name.

And there he meant to say: at the end of Modim D'Rabbanan there is no Name, for they do not conclude with the Name.

Even though there is a Name at the beginning, we have no concern — so far. He meant to say: Since one need not straighten up until the end of Modim D'Rabbanan from the side of the enactments of the Sages, therefore purely because of "Heaven straightens the bent" alone one need not straighten up, as explained. And if so, what is written in Piskei Mahari"a, and one need not be strict about "Heaven straightens the bent," etc., but only at the end of a blessing — he meant to say: if this were in Modim at the end of a blessing, there would be a Name.

In the name of Rashal, that he should straighten up slightly. The Bach wrote that he ruled to act like Mahari"a, that the whole [recitation] should be in one bowing, and to show straightening up when he mentions Heaven because of "Heaven straightens the bent"; therefore he straightens up slightly, and at the end in bowing — as the rabbi Beit Yosef wrote in the Shulchan Aruch.
