And the congregation does not answer Amen after him but rather ken yehi ratzon. And some wrote that one should not answer ken yehi ratzon except one by one at the end; and if he wishes he may answer ken yehi ratzon on each berachah and berachah, but the essence is that he should answer nothing except ken yehi ratzon at the end. And the custom is to say the priestly blessing even for an individual praying at a time when it is appropriate for nesiat kapayim; and some wrote that even on a private fast day one says it at Minchah, and likewise on a communal fast the congregation says it—all individually even if they did not fast with them. And Maharam was astonished at this, for it implies that the individual does not say it even when he prayed with the congregation but rather the prayer leader—and he did not distinguish between Shacharit and the other prayers.
