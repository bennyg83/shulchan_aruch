(6) If there are no [kohanim] there, etc. — see below siman 128, seif 10 in the gloss, that he wrote our custom is even when kohanim are present the prayer leader says "Our God and God of our fathers," etc., until "your holy nation" — see there.

(7) The prayer leader says, etc. — and after the fact, if he did not say it, they do not send him back; and see in Biur Halacha.

(8) In the blessing — in Pithchei Tefillah: "kohanim im kedoshim" — the word kohanim is not attached to "im kedoshim" to say they are kohanim im kedoshim, for we establish that kohanim are agents of the Merciful One and not our agents; rather the meaning is that the kohanim are called "your holy nation." When he says "May you be blessed, Heaven," he should look toward the side of the Sanctuary; "and keep you," toward his right side; "May Heaven illuminate," toward the Sanctuary; "His face toward you," etc.; "and favor you," toward his left side to unite it in the right — Zohar, parashat Nasso.

(9) "And I will bless them" — and our custom today is not to say except until "peace." Magen Avraham, and so it implies from the explanation of the Gra.

(10) And the congregation, etc. — for Amen applies only when answering after a kohen who blesses; unlike the prayer leader, who only speaks by way of request that the Almighty bless us with the blessing the kohanim bless.

(11) "So may it be Your will" — meaning after the end of the third blessing, for it is all one subject that the prayer leader arranges in his prayer what the kohanim say. And some answer ken yehi ratzon after each blessing.

(12) At a time when it is appropriate — meaning at Shacharit and Musaf, and not at Minchah; and on a public fast also at Minchah — and the reason, because they say Birkat Kohanim, and an individual who prays alone says Shalom Rav [Levush and Eliyah Rabbah].

(13) But otherwise, etc. — and if he said Sim Shalom or at Shacharit Shalom Rav, he has fulfilled. And in the siddur of the Ari z"l it is written always Sim Shalom; and see in Biur Halacha.
