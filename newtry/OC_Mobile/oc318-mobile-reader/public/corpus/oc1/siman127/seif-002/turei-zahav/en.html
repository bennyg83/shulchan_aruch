The wording here implies that 'Ken yehi ratzon' is said only after the conclusion of Birkat Kohanim. The widespread custom, however, is to say 'Yehi ratzon' after the first 2 blessings and 'Ken yehi ratzon' after the third. A reason may be offered: without peace, nothing is complete, and the third blessing is peace, which completes the principal blessing. Therefore 'Ken yehi ratzon' is said there to indicate that this is the culmination of the blessing. A copy from Sefer Etz Chaim composed by the divine sage Maharam Kalpiras, a disciple of the holy divine sage Rabbi Yitzchak Luria, regarding matters of prayer: After the passage of the Tamid one should recite Pittum HaKetoret. In 'Yayin Kafrisin,' the yud is pronounced with a tzere. The tallis should cover the head tefillin. Whenever one says Ashrei, he should afterward say 'And we shall bless God,' etc. He would not remove his tallis and tefillin until after Aleinu. After Aleinu one should say, 'Hashem shall be King over all the earth; on that day Hashem shall be One and His Name One.' One should have in mind that the verse begins with a vav and ends with a dalet, which together equal 10, corresponding to the 10 sefirot. This is what I saw there. The baraita, 'Our Rabbis taught: Nothing precedes the morning Tamid,' etc., is in Pesachim, chapter Tamid Nishchat, page 59. Tosafos write there that Rabbeinu Yitzchak holds that the word 'lamps' should not be included, because the teaching lists only matters burned upon the altar, regarding which the Torah was particular that they come afterward, as the verse says, 'and he shall burn upon it the fats of the peace offerings.' When reciting the first verse of Shema, 'Hashem is our God, Hashem is One,' one should have in mind the 4 letters of the Divine Name. When saying 'Blessed is the Name of His glorious kingdom,' one should have in mind the 4 letters of the Name of lordship. When saying 'forever and ever,' one should have in mind the combined Name formed from those 2 Divine Names. When saying 'May His great Name be exalted,' one should have in mind that He, blessed is He, rules over the 4 worlds of Atzilut, Beriah, Yetzirah, and Asiyah. 'Le'alam,' in the singular, corresponds to Atzilut, where there is great unity. 'Ul'almei' corresponds to Beriah; 'almaya' corresponds to Yetzirah; and 'b'alma' corresponds to Asiyah. When saying 'Yitbarach,' one should bow, since the word contains the root associated with bending the knee, and one also bows at 'Lecha.' In the wording 'He is appeased with mercy and reconciled through supplications,' a new edition printed 'and enticed through supplications.' This is incorrect, because Ramban writes in Parashat Mishpatim, regarding 'If a man entices,' that enticement refers to false words.
