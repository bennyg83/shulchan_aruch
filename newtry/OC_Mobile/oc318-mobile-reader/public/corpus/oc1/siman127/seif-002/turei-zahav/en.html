It appears from here that one should not say, “May it be His will,” except after the completion of Birkat Kohanim. The common practice, however, is to say, “May it be His will,” after the first two blessings, and after the third to say, “So may it be His will.” A reason may be given: if there is no peace, there is nothing, and the third blessing is peace and is the completion of the principal blessing. Therefore they say, “So may it be His will,” there, to indicate that this is the essence of the blessing. Copy from the book Etz Chaim, composed by the divine Maharam Kalphiras, a disciple of the divine, holy of holies, Maharari Luria, of blessed memory, concerning prayer. First: After the passage of the Tamid one should recite Pittum HaKetoret. In “Cyprus wine,” the yud is vocalized with a tzeirei. One should not say “a quarter of a kav,” but only “a quarter,” for this is the unstated meaning in the Gemara. One should not say that the incense precedes the limbs. After the incense one should say “Hashem Tzevakot” three times and then “May it be pleasing to Hashem.” The bowls of frankincense precede the afternoon Tamid. In the phrase “upon it complete all the offerings,” the word “complete” should be pronounced with a tzeirei beneath the lamed. Afterward one should say Ana Bekoach. The wording should be: “Please, with the power of the greatness of Your right hand, release the bound,” in one breath, namely the Name formed by the initials Avgitatz. The same applies to the other Names, namely Kera Satan, Neged Yachash, Betar Tzitag, Chakav Tana, Yagal Pazak, and Shaku Tzayit. Each verse should be recited with a melody, like one reading from the Torah. Likewise, when saying “and two verses,” one should say, “and here are two verses,” etc. One must look at the tzitzit every day, as it is written, “And you shall see it.” This is of great benefit to the soul, and sin will not come about through him. The tallit should cover the tefillin of the head. Whenever one says Ashrei, he should afterward say, “And we shall bless God,” etc. He would not remove his tallit and tefillin until after Aleinu. After Aleinu one should say, “Hashem shall be King over all the earth; on that day Hashem shall be One and His Name One.” One should have in mind that it begins with a vav and ends with a dalet, which together equal ten, corresponding to the ten sefirot. This is what I saw there. The Baraita, “Our Rabbis taught: Nothing precedes the morning Tamid,” etc., is in the chapter Tamid Nishchat, folio 59. Tosafot write there that Rabbeinu Yitzchak holds that the word “lamps” should not be included, because the teaching lists only matters of burning on the altar, regarding which the Torah was particular that they come afterward, as it is written, “and he shall burn upon it the fats of the peace offerings.” End quote. When reciting the first verse, “Hashem is our God, Hashem is One,” one should have in mind the four letters of the Name Yud Hei Vav Hei. When saying, “Blessed is the Name of His glorious kingdom,” one should have in mind the four letters of the Name of lordship. When saying, “forever and ever,” one should have in mind the combined Name Yud Alef Hei Dalet Vav Nun Hei Yud. When saying, “May His great Name be exalted,” one should have in mind that He, blessed is He, rules over the four worlds of Atzilut, Beriah, Yetzirah, and Asiyah. “Le'alam,” in the singular, corresponds to the world of Atzilut, where there is great unity. “Ul'almei” corresponds to the world of Beriah. When saying “almaya,” one should have in mind the world of Yetzirah, and “b'alma” corresponds to the world of Asiyah. When saying “Yitbarach,” one should bow, because the word contains the root meaning knee, and at “Lecha” one bows. In the wording “He is appeased with mercy and reconciled through supplications,” a new edition printed “and enticed through supplications.” This is incorrect, for the Ramban writes on Parashat Mishpatim, regarding the verse “If a man entices,” that enticement concerns false words.
