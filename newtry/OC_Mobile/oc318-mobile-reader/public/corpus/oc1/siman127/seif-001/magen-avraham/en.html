And they should not bow [too much]. He explains that they bow as the law of other bowings, as written siman 113, seif 5 [Kesef Mishnah in Beit Yosef]. And the Bach explains that in Modim that one says with the prayer leader he should not bow but only nod his head slightly, and the world did not practice thus.

In one bowing. And one need not be strict about "Heaven straightens the bent" except at the end of a blessing — you will know from Birkat Kohanim and Yom Kippur: the meticulous do not practice straightening up except at the end of a blessing (there). It seemed to me his intent: at the end of the blessing that one must necessarily straighten up as the Sages enacted, therefore he straightens up at the Name; and the same applies at the beginning of the blessing where he straightens up immediately. But here, where he does not straighten up until he finishes the whole blessing — and there [in Modim D'Rabbanan] there is no [concluding] Name, even though there is a Name [at the beginning] we have no concern. And further there is proof from a king who prayed the entire prayer in bowing, even though there are several Names in it. And the Bach wrote in the name of Rashal that he should straighten up slightly when he says the Name until its end, and then he bows — so far. And the world practices as Rama wrote.
