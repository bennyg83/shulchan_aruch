And they should not bow too much. Beit Yosef brought many decisors who hold that in all bowings there is this warning; and with this it is settled for me why they did not give here a limit for how far is the bowing here, so we know what is called too much. Rather it appears that the law of bowing was mentioned above, siman 103, and here they warned that they should not bow more than that measure. And that they cited here the Jerusalem [Talmud] — because here is the main bowing that is mentioned explicitly in the word Modim, as explained. And the Tur challenged the Rambam, who wrote thus from "too much" in the Jerusalem [Talmud]: an incident occurred with one who bowed too much and they removed him — Rabbi [implies] he speaks specifically of the prayer leader, for removal applies only to the prayer leader; and I do not understand the challenge, for to amplify he said thus in the Jerusalem [Talmud] that there is here so great a transgression that they removed him so he not be prayer leader because of this. If so, every person must be careful not to bow too much — as explained, this is correct. And see what is difficult from here on that [matter] in siman 113.

And concludes "Blessed," etc. — because in our Gemara there is no conclusion at all, and in the Jerusalem [Talmud] it is stated that there is a conclusion, "Blessed are You, God of acknowledgments"; therefore one must conclude, but without the Name.

Also at the end — for it is stated in the Gemara: Rava concludes like R. Akiva in Modim, beginning and end; and Ra'avad explained this refers to Modim D'Rabbanan. And it is further stated in the Jerusalem [Talmud]: R. Zeira said, and only in Modim; and Beit Yosef explained this means to say one need not bow except at the word Modim; and therefore he wrote here it is good to be concerned to be stringent also at the end.
