{Rama: He wrote in Shiurei Knesset HaGedolah siman 128 in hagahot Beit Yosef ot 26 that he practiced not to say uva letzion in a mourner's home, and his reason because the mourner does not lift his hands—therefore one does not say uva letzion with him. And in my humble opinion it is astonishing—for if so, according to Rama's words in siman 128 seif 43 that all twelve months a mourner does not lift his hands, then they should not say uva letzion with him, and likewise when the mourner prays in synagogue before the amud he should not say birchot kohanim. Therefore it seems to me not like this—for what difference if the mourner does not lift his hands? If called to the Torah he is obligated to ascend even if not yet ascended; if called to ascend he is obligated, and if he does not ascend he violates a positive commandment as explained in Magen Avraham there sk 65. So it does not detract from Minchah on Yom Kippur where even though there is no nesiat kapayim they still say uva letzion, since if he ascended he does not descend, as explained in Rama's hagahah at the end of siman 129.}
