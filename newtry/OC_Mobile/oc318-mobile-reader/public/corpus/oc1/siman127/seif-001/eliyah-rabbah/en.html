They bow, etc. They say Modim DeRabbanan because it is not the way of a servant to acknowledge his master and say 'You are my master' through an agent, since the master could deny the agency and say, 'I did not send him.' Rather, every person must personally accept the yoke of the Kingdom of Heaven. The rest of prayer, however, consists of requests, and one may seek his needs through an agent, because everyone desires his welfare and would not deny having sent the agent. The Levush writes that while the congregation says Modim DeRabbanan, the chazzan also bows and continues praying. This means that the chazzan need not wait until the congregation finishes Modim DeRabbanan; he proceeds with the prayer while they recite it. Indeed, the Beit Yosef, Bach, and later authorities write that the congregation should say Modim DeRabbanan slowly enough to finish when the chazzan completes the blessing of thanksgiving, so that they appear to bow with him at both the beginning and the end. I am surprised, however, because Responsa HaRosh, klal 4, end of siman 19, writes that the chazzan should prolong Barechu so that the congregation can say the additional praise, and gives Modim as an analogy: the chazzan prolongs it so the congregation can say Modim DeRabbanan. Abudarham, folio 28, writes likewise. This implies that while the chazzan prolongs the words 'Modim anachnu lach,' the congregation completes Modim DeRabbanan. The Shulchan Aruch and Levush rule similarly in siman 57 regarding Barechu; see there.
They should not bow excessively, etc. It is all one bow, etc. Nevertheless, the Bach and Nachalat Tzvi explain that this means only a slight nod of the head and no more. So writes Kesef Mishneh at the end of chapter 9 of Hilchot Tefillah, and so it is ruled in Piskei Tosafot. They are sufficiently authoritative to rely upon, unlike the later authorities who disagree. This applies specifically to the congregation, whose bow is only so that they not appear to deny the acknowledgment. The chazzan, however, bows according to the full law of bowing.
