(1) They bow, etc. And they say Modim D'Rabbanan, etc.—for it is not the way of a servant to thank his master and say "my the Master, You" through an agent who can deny him and say "I did not send him"; but every person must accept upon himself the yoke of the kingdom of Heaven with his own mouth. But in the rest of the prayer, which is request, one may plead his needs through an agent—for every person desires his benefit and will not deny and say "I did not send him." He wrote in Levush: and while the congregation says Modim D'Rabbanan the prayer leader also bows and prays before them—up to here. Meaning the prayer leader need not wait until the congregation finishes Modim D'Rabbanan, but prays before them while the congregation says. Greater than this wrote Beit Yosef and Bach and later authorities that one should say Modim D'Rabbanan little by little so he finishes with the prayer leader when he finishes the berachah of Hodayah, and it appears as if he bows with him at beginning and end—up to here. But I am astonished—for in responsa of the Rosh klal 4 end of siman 19 he wrote that the chazzan should extend in Barchu so the congregation says Veyitaleh veyishtabeach, etc., and a sign to the matter is Modim—that the chazzan extends in it so the congregation says Modim D'Rabbanan—up to here his language. And so wrote Abudraham page 28—it implies while the prayer leader extends in the word Modim anachnu lach the congregation finishes Modim D'Rabbanan. And so ruled Shulchan Aruch and Levush in siman 57 in Barchu—see there.
