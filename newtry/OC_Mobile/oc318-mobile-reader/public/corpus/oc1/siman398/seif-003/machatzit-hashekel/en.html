Paragraph 3: From the place, etc.; or there no longer remains, etc. The Gemara on folio 55a states in the name of Rav Huna: Regarding a city shaped like an arc, if the distance between its two ends is less than four thousand amos; the boundary of one end overlaps the boundary of the other, so it is considered one city and measured from the chord, as though the intervening area were filled with houses; its boundary is measured from the chord. Otherwise, each person's boundary is measured from the doorway of his house along the arc. The Gemara then asks how great the distance between the chord and the arc may be. Rabbah bar Rav Huna says two thousand amos. Rashi explains that if it is more than two thousand, a person exhausts his boundary upon leaving his house before reaching the chord, so the boundary cannot be extended from the chord. Rava, son of Rabbah bar Rav Huna, permits even more than two thousand amos. Abaye says that the halachah follows Rava, son of Rabbah bar Rav Huna, because one could walk around through the houses. Rashi explains: if he wishes, he can reach the chord by following the houses around the arc until one of its ends, and from there walk to the other end, since their boundaries overlap, there being less than four thousand amos between the two ends of the arc. We rule like Rava, son of Rabbah bar Rav Huna. Tosafos, s.v. "less than," writes that since according to him it suffices if the boundary overlaps from only one side, it appears that he would likewise hold that if the distance between the chord and arc is only two thousand amos, the boundary is measured from the chord even if the two ends of the arc are more than four thousand amos apart. One can reach the other end by way of the chord, since the chord lies within the arc's boundary. That is, although one cannot walk from one end of the arc to the other by way of the chord, he can walk upward through the houses to the midpoint of the arc and from there through the empty area of the arc to the chord, since they are no more than two thousand amos apart. Rema cites this law in his gloss. The same therefore applies here: although the distance from the arc to the chord is more than two thousand amos, the measurement begins at the point where the city narrows so that only two thousand amos remain from the arc to that point. This is so even if the city is more than four thousand amos wide there; the boundary is measured from the point of narrowing as though the city extended only that far, as Rema writes when the width at the narrowing is less than four thousand amos.
