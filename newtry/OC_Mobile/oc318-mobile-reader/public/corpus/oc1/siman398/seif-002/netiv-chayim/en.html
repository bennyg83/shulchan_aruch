(On Magen Avraham, seif katan 12) He writes that the Tur’s unqualified ruling requires study. Note that he erred in this, for what basis did he have for deriving this implication? Granted, those who live in the middle of an arc may measure their techum beyond either end of the arc. The two ends of the arc belong to a single city, although they appear like two cities. Since the intervening area is enclosed within the arc, the whole is treated as one city. The residents of the middle can also reach both ends by passing alongside the houses, and they are therefore treated like the residents of the two ends for purposes of measuring their techum from there. Three villages, however, are not treated as a single city merely because their techumin overlap. Even two cities are not treated as one unless they are no more than 141⅓ amos apart. The reason that three villages can sometimes be treated as one is that each pair of adjacent villages may measure its techum from the second village on its side. We treat them as one by conceptually filling the empty space between them on account of the third village. Once you permit this through the principle of filling the intervening space, however, a resident cannot also measure his techum from the village on his other side. Were he to measure from the village on his left because the third village on his right enables us to treat the intervening space as filled, he would thereby lose the techum that extends from the village on his right. The Tur was therefore correct not to distinguish between the cases.
