And two thousand amos beyond it. According to the Rama in seif 4, one first adds seventy amos and a fraction, and then measures the two thousand amos.
It is treated as closed. One may walk from one end of the city to the other, and the techum is measured outward from there. As long as the breach is no wider than two city extensions, the area remains one city. Beyond that, it is treated as two separate towns.
But a single house. Whether it stands alone or lies more than seventy amos and a fraction from the city, it does not have the law of a city. Two towns in such a case may combine up to one hundred forty-one and one-third amos, but a single house cannot. If there are three courtyards with two houses in each courtyard, it is considered a town for purposes of adding a city extension, as Rabbi Akiva Eiger writes.
