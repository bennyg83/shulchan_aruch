"4 amot by 4 amot." See Magen Avraham, who writes that if a structure does not have a width of 4 amot, no matter how long it is, it is not called a house. I did not copy this ruling because the later authorities challenged it and hold that the matter depends upon the dispute cited in Yoreh De'ah, siman 286, seif 13, and Shach there, regarding mezuzah. "And storehouses." The same law applies to horse stables that contain a residential dwelling. [Gemara] "A roof and plastered ceiling." Gra writes that the word "plastered ceiling" should not be in the text, because it is absent from both the Gemara and the codifiers. Regarding houses that project outward one after another, the measuring and squaring of the city depends upon the placement of those houses in relation to the city's main body. Where they project opposite the central portion of the city there is greater room to include them within the city's square. Rashba's novellae support such a distinction, and Rambam and Ra'avad may also agree. Where houses project only from 1 side, the positions attributed to Ra'ah and Ra'avad, as cited by Ritva, indicate that the city is squared by drawing the appropriate imaginary lines around those projections. This is also discussed by Gaon Yaakov and Yad Ephraim. The reading that would produce a diagonal extension of less than 4,000 amot is therefore not decisive. The city must instead be squared afterward in accordance with the method of the Rishonim cited by the later authorities, as explained in Mishnah Berurah.
