If a house is within 70 amot and a remainder of the city, and a second house is within 70 amot and a remainder of the first house, and a third house is within that distance of the second, and so on even for a journey of several days, all are considered 1 city. When measuring the techum, one measures from outside the last house, provided that this residential house is at least 4 amot by 4 amot. Likewise, a synagogue that contains a dwelling for its attendants, a house of idolatry that contains a dwelling for its priests, storehouses that contain a dwelling, a bridge or grave containing a dwelling, 3 walls without a roof that enclose an area of 4 by 4 amot, a house built in the sea, 2 walls with a roof and ceiling over them, and a cave with a structure over its entrance containing a dwelling, all join the city if they are within 70 amot and a remainder. From the outermost such house we regard it as though a line were stretched across the face of the entire city, and the 2,000 amot are measured outside that line. {Rama: If there are 2 such houses opposite the city, their law is like a city shaped as an arch. [Tur]} But 2 walls without a roof, even if people live between them; a bridge or grave; a synagogue, house of idolatry, or storehouse without a dwelling; a pit, cistern, cave, dovecote, or house on a ship, and the like, do not join the city.
