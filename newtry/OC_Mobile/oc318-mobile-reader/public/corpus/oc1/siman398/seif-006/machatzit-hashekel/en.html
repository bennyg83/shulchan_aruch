Paragraph 6: Four amos by four amos, etc. Even if it is very long, it is not called a house. Tractate Sukkah 3a states: A house that does not contain four amos by four amos is exempt from mezuzah, one may not place an eruv in it, and it is not treated as an extension of a city, etc. Regarding mezuzah, Rambam holds that if the house is long and narrow but contains sufficient total area to form a square of four amos by four amos, it is obligated in mezuzah. Beis Yosef rules without qualification like Rambam in Yoreh Deah, siman 286. According to Rambam, the same law should apply to all the matters listed in that baraisa. Magen Avraham in siman 366, paragraph 6, similarly cites Ralbach regarding a house in which an eruv is deposited: it is sufficient if its area can form a square of four amos by four amos. Magen Avraham then writes, "See Yoreh Deah, siman 286, se'if 13," intending to disagree, for Shach there writes that one should be stringent in accordance with the Rosh, who requires an actual square of four amos by four amos. The same stringency should apply here: it must actually be square in order to count as an extension of the city, since these laws are equivalent. Magen Avraham concludes, "See siman 345." This concerns a separate matter. In paragraph 10 there, and likewise at the end of the siman, Magen Avraham writes that for an area four tefachim by four tefachim enclosed by walls to constitute a private domain, the empty interior must measure four by four tefachim without including the thickness of the walls. At the end of that siman he infers this from the wording, "there are not four tefachim between them," implying that the empty interior must be four tefachim. The same follows here from the wording, "they contain four amos by four amos": the empty interior, excluding the thickness of the walls, must measure four amos by four amos.
