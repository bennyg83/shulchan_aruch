Seif 8: besides the width, etc. It should be placed after the word "less," one-third.
And some say that we do not. This follows the first explanation in Tosafot there, s.v. hatam, that specifically if there are four thousand amot between the two outer villages then we do not measure, etc., as stated there in the Gemara, for on this the question and answer were: there, it has no, etc. But with an arched city we measure even to the middle. What Tosafot and the Rosh wrote there, "and so too the residents of the head of the arch," etc., Maharsha already wrote that this is what it means: because of filling in it is forbidden, and one needs the reason that he goes by way of houses. At first glance one could ask: what is the question, "but Rav Huna said: a city," etc.? Within two thousand it is permitted even at four thousand, because it is absorbed on one side. Rather, this is the explanation: with an arch, absorption on one side is acceptable because there are houses, but here absorption on one side is insufficient, as he answered there, "there," etc.; it must be absorbed from two sides. He answered there, etc. Maharshal wrote that Tosafot in the first answer holds that the question is according to Rabbah bar Rav Huna, for otherwise the Gemara asks nothing with "but Rav Huna said," etc., as above, but according to Rabbah bar Rav Huna, who requires absorption on two sides. This is what Tosafot wrote in the second answer, "and one may further say that there," etc.; in truth the Gemara's answer is so, and it is according to Rabbah bar Rav Huna. His words are correct. Although this is not according to the halakhah, the Rosh wrote it because of the Gemara's case of three villages and to explain the Gemara's interpretation. See Magen Avraham, seif katan 12. What the Magen Avraham wrote in seif katan 4 did not examine Maharsha on this Tosafot, and as written in Tosafot 55a, s.v. pachot. The Tur also wrote the latter language of Tosafot stringently, and so wrote the Rosh, but the Rama omitted it.
