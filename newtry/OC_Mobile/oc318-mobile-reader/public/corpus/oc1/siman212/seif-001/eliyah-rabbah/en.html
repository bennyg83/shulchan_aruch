The principal item is mixed in, and whatever is the majority is principal. If the mixture contains one of the five species, we follow it, provided there is actual substance of it. But if it was diluted with water until very thin, one recites Shehakol, according to Magen Avraham. See above, siman 208.
Salted fish, etc. This is the wording of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch. Bach wonders, because the Gemara speaks of one eating the fruits of Ginosar, where the salty food comes to restore the heart weakened by the fruits' sweetness and the bread eaten with it is subordinate. But if one eats salted fish with bread, the fish should not exempt the bread. Lechem Chamudot similarly wonders that only where one must eat the salty food because he previously ate something harmful is the bread exempt. I examined Sefer Adam VeChavah, page 143, which writes: "A salty food, such as the fruits of Ginosar, with bread accompanying it, he blesses over the salty item because it is primary relative to the fruits, but not another salty item." This implies that he understands the fruits of Ginosar themselves to be salty and that one eats bread with them. This also appears to me to be Rabbenu Yonah's meaning and so appears from Taz, unlike Bach's strained explanation. It seems to me that this is also the Tur's view, and that "fruits of Ginosar" is not exclusive; the same applies to very salty fish. But this is only where he eats the bread merely so the saltiness will not harm his throat. Those who establish a meal on salted herring together with bread certainly treat the bread as primary.
