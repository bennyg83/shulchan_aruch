1) [Seif 1] Whatever is primary, etc. Whatever constitutes the majority is primary, as stated in siman 208, seif 10. Whenever a mixture contains one of the five grain species, we follow the grain, as stated there in seif 2, provided that it has substance. If it was mixed with water until it became thin, one recites Shehakol, as stated there in seif 6. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1; Yad Aharon, Hagahos HaTur; Chayei Adam, rule 56, paragraph 1. See above, siman 204, paragraph 60.
2) There: He recites a blessing over the primary item and covers the subordinate item. This implies that it applies specifically if, when reciting the blessing, he intended to eat the subordinate item and intended to cover it. If he did not intend this at the time of the blessing because he thought he would not need it, and afterward needed it, he must recite a blessing. Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 1; Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 4. See below, paragraph 6. Likewise, if he first recited a blessing over the subordinate item and did not intend to cover the primary item, he must recite a blessing again over the primary item if their blessings differ. If their blessings are identical, however, he need not recite another blessing as long as some of the subordinate item remains before him, as stated above in siman 211, paragraph 24; see there. See Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 1, and understand this.
2) There: Both from the concluding blessing. If one ate less than the requisite measure of the primary item but ate the requisite measure of the subordinate item, regarding whether he must recite a concluding blessing, see Responsa Meshivas Nefesh, siman 5, which concludes that the bread combines with the fish. It is considered as though he ate the requisite measure of fish, and he recites Borei Nefashos; see there. See above, siman 210, paragraph 3.
