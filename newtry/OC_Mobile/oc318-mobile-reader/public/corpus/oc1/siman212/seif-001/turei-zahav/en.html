One who eats salted fish, etc. In the mishnah it states: they brought before him salted food first and bread with it — he blesses on the salted food and exempts the bread, for bread is secondary to it; and is there anything where salted food is primary and bread secondary? R' Acha taught: it was stated regarding one who eats fruits of Ginosar. Tosafot explained that the salted item is primary because it comes to restore the heart weakened from the sweetness of the fruits, and what follows is secondary and does not come except because of the salted item; and you will ask: since fruits are primary and salted food secondary, let him bless on the fruits and exempt all of them; and one may say it discusses that he did not eat the fruits in that session; or alternatively when he ate the fruits the salted food and bread were not yet there, for he did not know his heart would weaken from the sweetness of the fruit — end of their words; and one may say on the tanna itself: why did he take specifically such a case and not say simply and exempt by the blessing on fruits the salted food and bread? It appears it teaches one law — namely we find that a person eats food not for the sake of eating that thing itself but for something he already ate that pains him from sweetness or sharpness — nevertheless it is secondary; nevertheless he must bless, for perforce the tanna did not exempt here except that it was before him at the time of blessing on the eating he already ate, or he was thinking of it when he blessed on the previous eating that he would need to eat afterward for repair; and Rabbenu Yona did not explain thus, rather he explained: one may say the fruits of Ginosar are very sweet and are not eaten except with much salt so the sweetness not harm him, and they eat them with bread — and bread is found secondary to the fruits; therefore he blesses on the fruits and exempts the bread — end of his words; his intent is that the salted item mentioned in the mishnah is not a separate thing but the fruits themselves are called salted — meaning they are salted much and they eat them as those who eat sharp foods call cucumber or pepper that they salt at the time of eating. And it appears for the law Rabbenu Yona agrees to what is said in the name of Tosafot that if one eats something salty by itself after fruits there is a distinction as written — namely if he ate it in that session that he eats the fruits, or it was in his mind at the time of blessing to eat afterward something salty — then he is exempt from blessing on the salted item even though it was not before him initially; but if he ate it afterward in another session and the first session was nullified, or he did not think of the salted item at the time of blessing on the fruits — he is obligated to bless also on the salted item as explained here; and everything is well explained.

So that it not harm his throat. I found in the name of Rashal who challenged Tur on this, for in the Gemara it implies specifically if he eats the salty item after fruits of Ginosar — then bread is secondary to the salty item; but if there are no fruits and he eats salted fish with bread — bread is primary; and why did Tur write regarding salted fish even without fruits first that bread is secondary to the salted item? And one may answer that in the Gemara it discusses one who has no desire at all to eat the salty item except because of eating fruits first; and Tur and Shulchan Aruch discuss one who has desire to eat salted fish, only he fears it will harm his throat — therefore he eats a little bread with it — therefore bread is secondary; and nevertheless certainly if he eats salted fish called herring with bread — certainly bread is primary, for this is his main meal and herring is only relish for the bread; and therefore he specified "so it not harm his throat" — to teach that he did not intend at all to eat bread for itself; on the contrary he would have preferred to eat the fish without bread if possible.

And some say if the secondary is dear to him, etc. Beit Yosef in Issur Zarua — and this is his language: but if the radish is primary and not dear, he blesses on the olive and returns and blesses on the radish; and increasing blessings is preferable, and one should not abolish blessings in such a manner, end of his words. And this refers to what is said in the Gemara chapter 44: he blesses on the radish and exempts the olive — and we establish it when the radish is primary; and Rashi explained that because of it he began eating and he does not eat the olive except to remove the sharpness of the radish — the olive is secondary, end of his words; and on this Issur Zarua said specifically when the radish is dear and primary. And one may say from what Tosafot wrote in Menachot daf 36 s.v. Shach bein tefillah, etc.: since he can exempt himself from a second blessing, it is called a blessing that is not needed — there it discusses that he need not speak between the blessings, for he will cause an unnecessary blessing; and it appears he wrote one should not abolish blessings in such a manner to resolve what is said regarding equal blessings in siman 211 that he blesses on one and exempts the second — and here he could be exempted with one blessing; therefore he said such a manner — his intent: here there are two blessings that are not equal — this borei peri haAdamah and that borei peri haEtz; and since each has an advantage — for the radish has advantage in that it is primary, and for the olive in that it is dear — therefore he blesses on each; and it implies here that each is as fit for it; and this "if the secondary is dear" — specifically if he eats it by itself — here he said he should bless on it first and eat a little of it and afterward bless on the primary and eat it. And this appears to me to prove from chapter 44 daf 35: on olive oil we bless borei peri haEtz; and we challenge: if he drinks it as is, does harm come to him? Rather he eats it by way of bread — it would be bread primary and oil secondary, etc.; and it is strained to establish it by way of anigrinon — and why not establish it with bread and the oil is dear to him? Rather certainly in this "dear" does not help, since he eats it with bread together; and here it helps only by itself, as above.

Or he eats the primary. Meaning: and afterward the secondary in order to sweeten — such as he ate first radish and afterward olive; or he ate sweet fruits first, and likewise fruits of Ginosar that we mentioned.

That he eats pits of caraway. It appears regarding caraway and the like that are to sweeten the drink — complete exemption does not apply to them, for everything one brings within the time one is engaged in drinking is called before the drink — for he did not come to sweeten what he already drank, only what he will drink afterward; through this it will draw desire to drink afterward — automatically it is called before the drink and is not exempted by the blessing he blessed before the first drink, for they do not come to be secondary to what he already drank, for it is of no benefit to him; and this is not similar to olive that one eats after radish which is for benefit on what he already ate and is secondary to it — this appears simple.

He blesses on the food first. For it is not possible that afterward the primary exempt him retroactively from its blessing, for he already enjoyed without a blessing — so too in Terumat HaDeshen there; and it is difficult from that which chapter 44 daf 43: we bless on smell when its fragrance rises — and he is not yet present and does not enjoy; and they answer: since his mind is to enjoy — likewise here one may say: since his mind is to drink wine and he eats first to sweeten the drink — he may bless immediately on the wine and eat the pits and drink; and one may say there is an interruption between blessing and drinking — for in HaMotzi there is speech interruption between blessing and eating, and nevertheless if he said "bring salt" or relish it is not an interruption, since it is for the need of eating; likewise this eating is for the need of drinking and is not an interruption; and one may say that here there is another enjoyment from the drink — therefore there is interruption.

Only shehakol. Beit Yosef wrote on this and it appears to me per what our rabbi wrote in siman 210 in the name of Rosh that because of lack of required quantity another blessing does not apply — likewise because of sweetening the drink his blessing should not change, end of his words. And Rama wrote on this that his words do not exist; rather since it is secondary to another thing and the primary enjoyment is not from that thing, one does not bless on it its proper blessing but must bless on it so as not to enjoy from this world without a blessing; and when he blesses shehakol on it he has fulfilled; and some say at least from the time he enjoys from the food which is his primary enjoyment he must bless on it its proper blessing, end of his words; and this distinction is not sufficient per Beit Yosef's view, for also in lack of required quantity one is not called enjoying properly from the primary enjoyment — for whatever does not have the required quantity, the enjoyment is not complete; if so, what difference whether enjoyment is incomplete because of lack of quantity or because it is secondary to another thing — in both there is some enjoyment in any case. And in Levush he wrote: some say regarding lack of required quantity — there is no obligation of blessing except that because of lack of required quantity we come to establish for it another blessing; on this Rosh said that because of lack of required quantity another blessing does not apply, rather he should not bless at all; but here it is simple he must bless, only he can exempt it with the primary — only because of not enjoying from this world without a blessing; and he already enjoyed before eating the primary — the mind gives that he bless first not because of itself but because of the primary he wants to eat afterward, for he does not desire it except for the primary — in this Rosh agrees that because of its secondary status his blessing changes, end of his words. This too is not sufficient, for there too one may say it is forbidden to enjoy without a blessing — if so, for less than the required quantity we should have said at least he bless with God's name; nevertheless Rosh said: since it is a thing whose blessing is a three-word formula, God's name is not applicable in it at all even for less than the required quantity; likewise here there should be no change of blessing from what is in it — rather one of two: either he does not bless at all or he blesses its proper blessing. And per my humble opinion, to resolve in this manner: there it discusses the final blessing and we find no general rule there, rather each kind by itself — therefore every change there is called a change, and this is not correct; unlike here which discusses the first blessing in which there is a general rule that on everything one says shehakol he has fulfilled — only because of importance in some of them they added l'chatchila to specify them with special blessings; and therefore whatever special blessing does not apply well in its particular detail — in this one relies on the primary in any case entering the general rule and blesses shehakol, which is also its blessing as explained — correct; and in any case here it discusses that the secondary is not dear to him — for otherwise he would need to bless on it its special blessing as explained above nearby.
