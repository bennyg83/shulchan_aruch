The primary is mixed. And whatever is the majority is primary, as written siman 208 seif 7; and whatever has from the five species — we follow after them, as written there seif 2, provided there is substantial quantity in them; but if he mixed them in water until it is soft, he blesses shehakol — see there seif 6 and see end of siman 204.

For example, such as one eats. Meaning: it is difficult either way — if he is not hungry, why does he eat bread? And if he is hungry, bread is primary; therefore "for example," etc., means he is not hungry, only that he ate something sweet and eats salted fish to remove the sweetness and eats bread with it so it not harm his throat; and since his primary eating is not for the sake of bread, he does not bless on it (Ri). And see in the gloss; and you will bless on sweet fruits and exempt the salted fish and bread — and one may say that when he ate the fruits the salted fish and bread were not yet there, for he did not know his heart would weaken from the sweetness; or alternatively it discusses that he did not eat the fruits in that session (Tosafot) — meaning he changed his place, not like Nitzachon and Bach; and siman 178 implies from their words that if he knew his heart would weaken, even though salted fish was not before him, he is exempted by the blessing on the fruits; and likewise siman 206 seif 5; and Bach's language for explanation 1: he eats fruits also at the hour he eats salted fish and bread, only he already blessed on the fruits when salted fish and bread were not yet there, etc.; and there is some contradiction in his words from what he challenged in siman 177 seif 4; also what he wrote to explain Ri — it appears, etc., except the Talmud discusses there that the fruits themselves scatter much salt on them and he eats bread with them — therefore he blesses on the fruits — see there. And Shelah wrote: one who eats bread to sweeten the drink blesses on the beverage and exempts the bread if his mind was explicitly on it or his custom is usually to eat bread in the middle of drinking — it is as if his mind was on it; and otherwise he must bless, end of his words; and Baal HaMaor regarding things that come within the meal — bread exempts them; and you will ask: what does it teach us? It is the mishnah "whatever is primary," etc.; and one may say it teaches that even if his mind was not on it at the time he blessed on bread, one might say he needs a blessing — it teaches: since they are secondary they do not need a blessing, end of his words. One must say: specifically bread, for there is kevi'at seudah and his mind is on all they will bring him, as written in siman 177 sk 7; but regarding other primary and secondary we require that his mind be on it, as written above; for otherwise Rif should have said the law from the mishnah "whatever is primary," etc., and taught us even if his mind was not on it — rather perforce we say thus only regarding bread; and if his mind was not on it he blesses shehakol on the bread, as written in the gloss.

The secondary is dear. This is Rabbenu Yonah's language: Agur in the name of Issur Zarua — this applies when the radish is primary and dear; but if the radish is primary and not dear, he blesses on the olive and returns and blesses on the radish; and increasing blessings is preferable, and one should not abolish blessings in such a manner, end of his words; and it requires study what he wrote "and not dear" — it implies that even when both are equal he blesses on the olive; and it requires study why, for behold the radish is primary; and on your question what he wrote "and increasing blessings is preferable," etc. — on the contrary we hold it is forbidden to increase, as written siman 215; and it is difficult what they say on daf 41: R' Yehuda blesses on the olive, etc., and why does R' Yehuda need, etc. — perhaps nevertheless R' Yehuda holds where there is a seven-species kind he blesses on the seven-species kind, as Issur Zarua holds that he blesses on the dear one; and that which the mishnah teaches he blesses on the radish — it discusses when the radish is dear; therefore it appears the explanation of Issur Zarua applies when the radish is primary and dear — meaning as Rashi wrote that because of it he began eating and he does not eat the olive except to remove the sharpness of the radish — the olive is secondary; meaning his heart weakened and he desires to eat radish which is his primary eating, as they say in the Gemara regarding fruits of Ginosar. But if the radish is primary and not dear, etc. — meaning his heart did not weaken, only that he eats radish with olive; even though ostensibly the olive is secondary to the radish, nevertheless the radish is not primary — perhaps the olive is primary, for he eats both together and the radish is not dearer to him than the olive; and if so he blesses on the olive borei peri haEtz and afterward blesses on the radish borei peri haAdamah which is still a seven-species kind; one may say that in borei peri haEtz it is preferable; and one may say he should bless first on the radish and exempt the olive, for we hold if he blessed on tree fruit borei peri haEtz he fulfilled — increasing blessings is preferable; it is forbidden to do so l'chatchila, as written siman 206; see in the Gemara and in Rashi that Knei Paz's words and so in Agur who brought the baraita's language — see there; and likewise siman 177 we hold things that come within the meal — bread exempts them, since bread is primary even though presumably they are dearer than bread; and likewise in Rashba's novellae daf 40 in the name of the Yerushalmi: even if the secondary is dear, he blesses on the primary. One who soaks after his eating a little bread in sharp wine and eats it to digest the food — it appears he should bless on the wine, for his intent is not to eat bread, only on the wine; but because it is his custom to drink it he soaks bread in it — it is like oil that I wrote in siman 202 seif 4 that he blesses on the oil; nevertheless if he eats before his eating bread soaked in wine, certainly his intent is also to settle the heart and it is bread primary and he blesses HaMotzi; and see Tosafot daf 41 s.v. Halachta, etc., and in Rosh there. Shelah wrote it is good to refrain from eating bread at the time of drinking, for who can distinguish whether he eats it to fill his stomach — then he must bless HaMotzi — or he eats it to sweeten the drink; and he wrote that when he eats it to sweeten the drink he washes hands without a blessing; and in Rama's responsum he wrote washing is not required; and likewise Mateh Moshe in the name of Rashal; and see siman 178.

Only shehakol since, etc. It appears to me Issur Zarua holds specifically here where it is secondary to drinking other beverages and it would be fit to bless shehakol on the beverage and exempt this — but because he eats the secondary first he blesses on it; therefore he blesses on it as the blessing of the primary. But when the primary's blessing is something else, he does not bless shehakol on it. But in Terumat HaDeshen he understood that even if one wants to drink wine he blesses shehakol on the pits, since they are secondary and lost their own blessing, and blesses shehakol and afterward blesses on the wine borei peri haGefen — and it requires study from where this is; and also Rabbenu Yonah disagrees with him; therefore it appears one should not rely on this ruling — see siman 174 seif 7.
