If the primary is mixed — see in Mishna Berurah and in Derech HaChayim letter 18 [regarding fried foods] — he wrote: if one eats two foods that are mixed in one bowl, if his primary intent is on one he blesses on it and exempts the other even if it is the lesser amount; and in an unspecified case, whatever is the majority is primary — see there. But if in the mixture there is even a small amount from the five species that he mixed in to eat, he blesses borei minei mezonot and exempts the other — end of his words. However, in Chayei Adam general letter 51 seif 13 and general letter 54 seif 9 he sides with the view that where each species is recognizable and distinct, he blesses on each species and we do not follow the majority nor the five species of grain; and for practical law it appears safek berachot lehakel and he should bless only per the majority's blessing. And likewise in a stew in which five species of grain are mixed, he blesses only borei minei mezonot [and also on al haMichyah at the end if there is an olive's bulk of grain within k'dei achilat peras] — and so it appears in Peri Megadim in the introduction on the matter of blessings of enjoyment letter 11, that even where each is recognizable by itself we still follow the majority and likewise after the five species even if they are the minority, and he blesses only one blessing — and so I heard teachers in the name of one gaon. And one who wishes to be concerned for Chayei Adam's view should mash the fruit of the ground and mix it with pieces of dough or groats on the five species of grain and eat them together and bless borei minei mezonot on both species together. And likewise regarding other species that are not of the five species of grain — if the majority is of one species and the second species that is the minority is distinct by itself, such as if he cooked apples together with a few potatoes and the like — he should mash the minority and bless per the majority's blessing.

And he does not bless on it only shehakol — see in Magen Avraham what he explained as Ateret Zekenim's reason, and he wrote: but in Terumat HaDeshen he understood that Ateret Zekenim discusses, etc. And behold the essence of the matter is explained in Darkei Moshe: since it is secondary to something else and the primary enjoyment is not from that thing on which he blesses with its proper blessing, but he must bless so as not to enjoy the world without a blessing — and when he blesses shehakol on it, behold he has blessed, etc. And at first glance one may say Rama follows his method in siman 204 seif 11 in the gloss where he wrote: and any medicinal paste that healthy people do not regularly use except for medicine — they bless shehakol on it; and this is from Ra'ah brought there in Beit Yosef; and the reason: since the thing is designated only for medicine and not to enjoy from it, its specific blessing does not apply, and only shehakol is blessed, for even though he does not intend to enjoy, nevertheless he enjoys. And likewise in our matter one may say: since he does not intend to enjoy from the secondary but only to sweeten so the primary not harm him, its specific blessing does not apply, only shehakol; however in truth there is to distinguish — there healthy people do not use it at all and therefore it lost its blessing, unlike here where it is food of healthy people, only that he took it now as secondary — it is preferable more so. And in any case it appears to me that according to what most poskim agreed there on this law, not like Ra'ah, and as explained in the words of the acharonim there — that even if he stands for medicine and intends for medicine, since he nevertheless enjoys he blesses its proper blessing — all the more so in our matter which is no less, that even though he does not intend to eat the secondary for itself he blesses its proper blessing. And see in Biur HaGra who also questions the essence of Ateret Zekenim's law and wrote that it is wondrous.
