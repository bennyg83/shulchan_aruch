One who eats. Taz: Berachot 44a — it teaches the tanna's challenge of Tosafot and their answer: even though one eats one thing because of what pains us — if it was not in one session or was not in his mind on it, its proper blessing [applies]. And you will ask: from where do we know this? Perhaps salted food is shehakol and on all of them shehakol — b'dieved he fulfilled; and regarding secondary, even l'chatchila likewise, unlike tree fruit borei peri haEtz which does not exempt bread and fruit. And one may say we learned (Mishna Berurah 1): if he blessed on bread he exempted crumbs — and here it teaches us that relish too is exempt, as Tosafot wrote — behold even a blessing that does not exempt, such is the law; and all the more so when their blessings are equal and the secondary is a seven-species kind — he blesses on the primary, siman 211 seif 1 and Levush here. And if he blessed on the secondary and did not explicitly intend on the primary — he must return and bless — similar to what Rabbenu Yonah wrote siman 211 seif 5 in the gloss that it is not logical he exempt by dragging — see there. And what if both were before him at the time of blessing, or the secondary was not before him and he knows he will need it because his way is that his heart weakens, etc. — he blesses on the primary and exempts the secondary, and even if both were before him — it requires study whether he knew his heart would weaken; and also this specifically in that session — but if that session was nullified and afterward he ate the secondary, he reconsidered — and this is Tosafot's language there s.v. One who eats: the two answers do not dispute each other; and see Nitzachon, Bach's explanation — for explanation 1 even if he knew his heart would weaken, etc., it is true thus; but the session was nullified and he reconsidered — understand this; and Eliyah Rabbah letter 4 separates it — it appears as Taz wrote, and there is no need to elaborate, and it requires study. And still it is difficult: bread exempts crumbs — for I am the main object of the meal — see there; and the early ones disputed this; and perhaps this is the rule "whatever is primary," etc., which proves that even borei peri haEtz and borei peri haAdamah and the like — he blesses on the primary, etc.; and see letter 4 of this matter in Taz and Magen Avraham 2; and if so, it will be explained there.
