(2) Salted fish, etc. Thus is the language of Tur and Shulchan Aruch. And Bach wondered: the Talmud there says regarding one who eats fruits of Ginosar — it implies that the salted item comes to restore the heart weakened from the sweetness of the fruits, and the bread afterward is secondary; but when one eats salted fish and bread with it he does not exempt, and likewise Lechem Chamudot wondered — specifically when he must eat the salty item because he already ate something that harms him is he exempt for bread — see there. And I looked in Sefer Adam VaChavah daf 143 — he wrote this language: salted such as fruits of Ginosar and bread with them — he blesses on the salted item which is primary relative to the fruits, and not another salted item — end of his words. It implies he explained the Talmud that the fruits of Ginosar themselves are salty, and he eats bread with them; and so it appears to me as the explanation of Rabbenu Yona, and so it implies in Taz, and not like Bach who extended to force his explanation — see there and think well. And it appears to me that such is Tur's view and it is reasonable to him that fruits of Ginosar are not specifically so, but the same law applies to salted fish that is very salty — and specifically when he does not eat bread except so his throat not be harmed; but those who set a meal to eat salted fish called herring with bread — certainly bread is primary.
