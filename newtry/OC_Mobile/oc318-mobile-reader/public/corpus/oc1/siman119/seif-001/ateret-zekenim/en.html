If he wished to add, etc.—if he adds it on behalf of all Israel, etc. It appears that for an individual's needs one may ask in plural language if he wishes, and this is preferable; proof comes from the wayfarer's prayer. However, when he speaks in plural language it must be specifically at the end of the blessing. There are four distinctions in the matter: (1) In the blessing of Shema Koleinu one may ask for all his needs in any matter he wishes. (2) If he comes to say at the end of each blessing something akin to that blessing, he may say it even if his needs are not literally those of the blessing, since he says it in plural language; but in singular language it is forbidden, since he is not saying it on behalf of all Israel—and specifically at the end of each blessing, but not in the middle of the blessings, for asking for the needs of the many in the middle appears like adding to the formula that Chazal instituted. (3) If he asks for his actual needs, such as for a sick person he has, and the like, he may say it in singular language and even in the middle of the blessing, but not in plural language, lest it appear like adding to the formula that Chazal instituted. (4) At the end of the prayer, even before "May the words of my mouth be acceptable," he may say it as he wishes—whether in singular or plural language, whether for his actual needs or for communal needs (Rabbeinu Yonah, chapter 1 of Eruvin).
