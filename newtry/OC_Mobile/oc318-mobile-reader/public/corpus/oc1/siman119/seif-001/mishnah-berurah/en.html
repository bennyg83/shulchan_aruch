(א) If one had a sick person — see in Peri Megadim that he wrote that specifically if he had one; and likewise regarding livelihood — specifically if he needs a livelihood. But one is not permitted to seek mercy in a middle blessing for the future — that he should not become ill, and that he should not lack bread, and the like. Not so in Shomea Tefila, where even for the future one is permitted to ask.

(ב) Mercy — see Pri Etz Chayim, who brings from the Zohar, parshat Vayishlach: Save me, please, etc. — that in prayer one must elaborate words as fitting; see there. One who asks mercy for his fellow need not mention his name, as it says please heal her now and he did not mention Miriam's name — and this is when the sick person is before him; but when not before him, he must mention his name.

(ג) He should begin with the blessing — for one must make the main part from the coin that the sages coined, and one's request should be subsidiary and not the reverse. And Peri Megadim wrote that one must precede and say a complete phrase from the theme of the blessing — for example, if one wishes to ask that God give him wisdom, he should precede and say You grant man understanding, but not the word You alone; and similarly in all blessings.

(ד) His needs — it is good to confess in Shemoneh Esreh and say, "I have sinned, I have transgressed, I have acted wickedly." And to ask for one's sustenance even if one is wealthy. And if one has a recent sin, one should mention it explicitly in the prayer before him, and one should say the prayer with humility and at minimum in a voice of weeping, and accept upon oneself not to do such a thing again knowingly; and if not, then they prosecute him from above, Heaven forbid — and see what was written in siman 122, section 8, in the name of Chayei Adam.

(ה) On behalf of all Israel — and the same applies on behalf of the public.

(ו) And not in singular language — since he is saying it on behalf of the many.

(ז) At the end of the blessing — see in Peri Megadim, siman 122, in Mishbetzos Zahav, who sides with saying that specifically before he said "Blessed are You," but afterwards it appears it is forbidden (and see there that this matter is not clear to him).

(ח) And not in the middle — for when one asks for public needs in the middle of a blessing, it appears as though one is adding to the coin that the sages coined. In what case does this apply? To an individual; but in a congregation they are permitted — and therefore they say selichot in the middle of the blessing "Forgive us," and similarly in the middle blessing of Yom Kippur.

(ט) And not in plural language — for although they permitted him to ask even in the middle of a blessing when he needs it per the hour, if he also says it in plural language it will appear as though he is adding to the coin that the sages coined — and see in siman 566, section 4, that it is good to be careful ab initio in all this, as Rabbeinu Yona said.

(י) Shomea Tefila — and Bach wrote that according to R' Yitzchak, Shemoneh Esreh is similar to the other blessings regarding this matter, that in the middle it is forbidden to ask in plural language.

(יא) Before "yehi ratzon," etc. — see below in siman 122 in Mishna Berurah, section 7, what is written there.
