[1] And in "Who hears prayer," etc.—"for You hear prayer, every mouth." Eighty-five in gematria is "word" (R' David Abudraham). Seder HaYom wrote that one should confess in "Who hears prayer" and say: "Please, the Master—I have sinned, I have transgressed, I have acted wickedly; forgive my sin, pardon my iniquities, and atone for my transgressions—for all that I have sinned, transgressed, and acted wickedly before You from the day I came upon the earth until this day." And if he has some sin committed that day, he mentions it in the previous prayer service with him—Shacharis, Minchah, or Maariv. It appears to me that if he confesses in "Forgive us," he should not confess except in plural language; but in the blessing of "Who hears prayer" he may say it even in singular language—up to here. It is good to ask there for his sustenance even if he is wealthy (the writers). Abudraham wrote: many people read "Who hears our prayer and our supplication," and one should not consider oneself so righteous; rather it is correct to say: "Who hears prayers and supplications, You."
