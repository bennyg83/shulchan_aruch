And in Shomea Tefillah, etc., for You hear the prayer of every mouth. The numerical value of the word for 'mouth' is the same as that of milah, circumcision, as Rabbi David Abudarham notes. The Seder HaYom writes that one should confess in Shomea Tefillah and say: 'Please, Hashem, I have sinned, acted perversely, and transgressed. Forgive my sins, pardon my iniquities, and atone for my transgressions, for all in which I have sinned, acted perversely, and transgressed before You from the day I came upon the earth until this day.' If he has committed some particular new sin that day, he should mention it in the prayer nearest to that event, whether Shacharit, Minchah, or Maariv. It appears to me that if he confesses in Selach Lanu he should do so only in the plural, whereas in Shomea Tefillah he may confess even in the singular. It is proper to ask there for one's sustenance even if one is wealthy, as stated in the writings. Abudarham writes that common people say 'You hear our prayers and supplications,' but one should not presume so much upon being among the righteous. Rather, it is proper to say, 'You are the One who hears prayers and supplications.'
[2] [Levushei Serad] Provided that he says it in the singular, etc. This means when he says it in the middle of the blessing, but at the end of the blessing he says it in the plural. It is explicit that this applies specifically when he has a sick person or requires sustenance, but one should not add requests in the blessings regarding future needs; rather, he should say them in Shomea Tefillah. See siman 557, seif 2, regarding Nachem, and see Beit Yosef. The Lechem Chamudos, folio 42, writes: He must actually have such a need, and may not add a request merely in general when neither he nor another person for whom he prays requires it. One who prays for another person in his absence must mention his name, as Maharil wrote. He further records that the community decreed a fast and recited Selichos because Maharil became ill.
