(טז) Only Refa'einu — meaning that he finished the blessing Refa'einu and said "Blessed are You, the Master"; but if he remembered before he sealed it — even if he said "Blessed are You," since he has not yet said the Name, he returns and says Aneinu with its closing formula and afterwards returns and says the blessing Refa'einu. And all this is with a prayer leader, whose fixed place to say Aneinu is between Go'el and Rofeh; but with an individual who includes Aneinu in the blessing Shomea Tefila — if he forgot to say Aneinu, once he has finished Shomea Tefila, even if he did not open with Ratzah, he need not return, as is the law for everything that one does not return for. And the reason for the difference is that there it is different because it is included within the blessing, and once he finished the blessing, if he returns there would be a blessing in vain; not so here, where they fixed it as an independent blessing — it is not annulled unless he would need to bless the blessing Refa'einu again on account of this; and as long as he has not said the Name of "Blessed are You," there is no blessing in vain.
(יז) It — meaning that by his returning to say Aneinu he will necessarily need to say afterwards the blessing Refa'einu again so that the blessing of the years will be in order after Refa'einu, and it is a blessing in vain, for one does not return on account of Aneinu, as written in siman 565. And if he forgot and said Aneinu before Re'eh Na — see Magen Avraham, who holds that he should say Aneinu another time after Re'eh Na; and so wrote Divrei Chamudos and Peri Megadim remains in doubt on this law; and in the book Magen Giborim he elaborated on this and concludes for practical halacha likewise that one should not return and say Aneinu again; and if he wishes, he may say it in Shomea Tefila as an individual.
(יח) Aneinu in Shomea Tefila — the same applies if the ten fasting people were not in the synagogue and they came after the blessing Refa'einu — he should say Aneinu in Shomea Tefila as an individual. And if the prayer leader began to pray aloud and there were then in the synagogue ten who were fasting, and afterwards some of them left before the prayer leader began Aneinu — he should not say Aneinu, and it is better that he say it then in Shomea Tefila; and if he already began Aneinu and they left, he finishes.
(יט) As an individual — see Divrei Chamudos, who rules that he should not seal it with a blessing but only in Shomea Tefila like any other individual generally; and so it is correct that with this he fulfills according to all, see Magen Avraham. And if he also forgot in Shomea Tefila — even if he remembered before he began Ratzah, he should not say it again but should say it after he finishes "beshalom," before "Elohai netzor," and without a closing formula [Divrei Chamudos and Shaarei Tzedek, see there].
