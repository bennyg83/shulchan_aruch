And he did not say Aneinu, etc. — Explanation: Since we already rule that on account of Aneinu we do not return him, any time he remembers and can return and say it and it will not be a blessing in vain, he returns. Nevertheless, if he already finished Refa'einu and remembered — if he were to return to say Aneinu, he would need afterwards to say Refa'einu a second time with its blessing, and it would be a blessing in vain. But if he had not yet said the conclusion of the blessing of Refa'einu, and only remembered before saying Blessed are You, Healer, etc. — he may return and say Aneinu and afterwards say Refa'einu, for there is no blessing in vain here. And the reason it mentions here the prayer leader is because an individual says it in Shomea Tefila; and if one forgot until after one finished Shomea Tefila, even though one had not yet begun Ratzah, one says Aneinu there, since we do not return on account of Aneinu, as Beit Yosef wrote in siman 114, seif 6. And even though regarding the prayer leader they said that even if he began Refa'einu — this is when he did not finish it — this is because the prayer leader says it as a separate blessing; not so an individual, who includes it in Shomea Tefila — it has no relevance after finishing Shomea Tefila. And some explain regarding "He Who makes the wind blow" in siman 114 that we return on account of it; and so too in siman 294. And Mahariach, may his memory be blessed, wrote here that an individual may say Aneinu between Shomea Tefila and Ratzah; and he did not analyze precisely, for he himself wrote in siman 114 like Beit Yosef, that there is a distinction in this between something we return for and something we do not.
