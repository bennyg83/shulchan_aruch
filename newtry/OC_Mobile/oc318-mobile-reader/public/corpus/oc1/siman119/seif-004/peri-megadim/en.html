And it is not — Eshel Avraham: The difference between Mashiv HaRuach and Aneinu of the prayer leader and Aneinu of an individual is thus: Mashiv HaRuach has its essence within the blessing, and therefore when he remembers between blessings or within the blessing it is considered, as the Levush wrote in siman 114 and Magen Avraham (8), or they were lenient in this; but if he began only "You" alone, he has interrupted and must return. Not so with Aneinu of the prayer leader, whose essence is between blessings — therefore, when he did not say "Blessed are You, Healer," etc., there is no blessing in vain and he says Aneinu between blessings. Not so with Aneinu of Shemoneh Esreh of an individual — from things that one does not return for; if he finished Shemoneh Esreh he has not fulfilled. And see Prisha.

Eliyah Rabbah wrote, sign 6: If the prayer leader said Aneinu before Re'eh Na, he returns and says it afterwards — see there. I am in doubt, for although the eighteen [blessings] were fixed in order according to Rashbam's explanation and in the order of creation, and one may not interrupt between them — but here Aneinu is in order as in siman 566; perhaps ex post facto it does not invalidate so severely, and it would seem to say that he should return to say Selach Lanu and Re'eh Na in order — but this too is not [correct], and it requires further study. Knesset HaGedolah wrote: A prayer leader who stood in prayer and there were not ten in the synagogue who were fasting, and he said Refa'einu — when their judgment came (when the fasters arrived), he forgot — see there in Eliyah Rabbah; and this means he should say it in Shomea Tefila afterwards. The words of the Levush here are doubtful, and in 567, seif 1, it requires further study; and they already raised this, and Eliyah Rabbah [wrote] here. And Magen Avraham, sign 7, and Eliyah Rabbah there will be explained.

Pri Chadash wrote in seif 3 that even if one skipped an entire blessing, one can include it in Shomea Tefila; and proof from Tosafos, Berachos 29a, s.v. mippenei, etc. But I, the poor one, do not see proof from there, for certainly dew and rain and the like, which are not said at all times and are not in the fixed formula of the blessing — only a question whether, if one forgot, one says it in Shomea Tefila, which includes everything; therefore it was enacted thus. Not so a complete blessing: even if one erred a little in a blessing and skipped some words and afterwards said the blessing that follows it — one has no remedy in Shomea Tefila, for one lacks the count of the fixed blessings. And what Tosafos wrote regarding havdalah — it was not a complete blessing; and what they challenge there, to include in Shemoneh Esreh — although "Grant us understanding," each word is in place of a blessing, nevertheless dew and rain are not from the fixed [blessings]. And see siman 110 regarding the law of "Grant us understanding," and whether ex post facto, if one prayed "Grant us understanding" at home, one has fulfilled. And what Pri Chadash wrote in seif 7 regarding the blessing Refa'einu — there is there an omission of the word "not" — see there; and Taz and Magen Avraham, sign 27, ruled that if one did not conclude with Blessed are You, Healer, one returns and says Aneinu; and so is the law — see there.
