Heal us. That is, he concluded the blessing of "Heal us," and if he returns and says "Answer us," he would have to say "Heal us" again, for it must be in order—and it would be a blessing in vain, for one does not go back on account of "Answer us." But if he did not conclude "Heal us," he says "Answer us" and then "Heal us." The reason it mentions here the prayer leader is because an individual says it in Shema Koleinu; and if he forgot until after he finished Shema Koleinu, even though he had not yet begun, he may say "Answer us" there, since one does not go back on account of "Answer us"—unlike the prayer leader, who says it as a separate blessing. Taz and Magen Avraham and the later authorities, not like Rabbeinu Tam.

As an individual. And he concludes with "Blessed are You, the Master, Who answers in time of trouble" and Shema Koleinu. Hagahot Maimoniyot and Bach; Magen Avraham—not like Aruch HaTurei Zahav. And see in Knesset HaGedolah (and in Eliyah Rabbah he ruled like Aruch HaTurei Zahav that he concludes only in Shema Koleinu alone as an individual, and so in the responsa of Rashal); and if he concluded in Shema Koleinu alone he has fulfilled his obligation. Magen Avraham. The same applies if there were not ten fasting in the synagogue and others came after the blessing of "Heal us"—he says it in Shema Koleinu. Shagas Aryeh. And if he forgot even in Shema Koleinu, he says it as a separate blessing after "Grant peace." Rashal; Magen Avraham. And see in the Tur, siman 565, seif 11, that even for an individual the law is so; and although we do not rule thus for an individual, nevertheless for the prayer leader one may rule thus. Magen Avraham. And Knesset HaGedolah wrote in the name of Shagas Aryeh that he says it during the service if he remembered; and the Rama ruled that his law is like that of an individual, and so wrote Aruch HaTurei Zahav. And if he said "We have answered" before "See, please," he must say "Answer us" again after "See, please." Magen Avraham; see there.
