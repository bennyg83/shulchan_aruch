"Only Refa'einu" — meaning that he concluded the blessing of Refa'einu; and if he returns and says Aneinu, he will need to say Refa'einu a second time, since they must be in order, and it would be a blessing in vain — for we do not return on account of Aneinu. But if he did not conclude Refa'einu, he says Aneinu and afterwards Refa'einu (Maaseh, siman 112; Levush; Bach). And in the Levush there is a printing error here.

Aneinu in Shomea Tefila — and he concludes with Blessed are You, Who answers in time of trouble, Shomea Tefila (Hagahot Maimoni, chapter 1; Bach). And it is not a double conclusion — for Rashi explained in Taanis, folio 13, that the conclusion of the blessing of Shomea Tefila refers to Aneinu and all its matters. Therefore it seems that if he concluded with Shomea Tefila alone, he has fulfilled according to Rashi; and so is found in the responsum of Rashal, siman 64. The same applies if there were not ten fasting in the synagogue and they came after Refa'einu — he says it in Shomea Tefila (Shulchan Gavoah). And if he forgot also in Shomea Tefila, he says a separate blessing after Shalom [Rashal in his responsum]. And see Tur, siman 565, section 11, that even for an individual the law is thus; and so in the Hagahah — and Nihu that we do not hold thus for an individual. Nevertheless, for the prayer leader there is room to rule according to their words that it is not out of order, since the order of blessings has already concluded; and thus he wrote in the name of Shulchan Gavoah that he says it at Avodah if he remembered. And if he erred and said Aneinu before Re'eh Na — it requires investigation whether he should return and say Aneinu after Re'eh Na, as with other blessings; or perhaps, since even if he did not say it at all we do not return him, the same applies if he did not say it in its place; or perhaps it must be in order, as written that if one says Aneinu he must afterwards say Refa'einu a second time — all the more so here, where he said Re'eh Na not in its place. And so is the main point.
