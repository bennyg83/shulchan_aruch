Per Rabbeinu Yerucham, etc. In the Gemara it is found, first chapter of Eruvin: It was taught, R' Eliezer — A person should always ask for his needs and afterwards pray, as it is said, "A prayer for the poor when he wraps himself and pours out his speech before the the Master" — and "speech" means only prayer, etc. R' Yehoshua — A person should always pray and afterwards ask for his needs, as it is said, "I pour out before Him my speech; my distress I declare before Him," etc. And the sages say: Neither as this one says nor as this one says, but rather a person asks for his needs in Shomea Tefila. R' Yehuda said in the name of Rav Shmuel bar Sheilat in the name of Rav: Even though they said a person asks for his needs in Shomea Tefila — if he has a sick person in his house, he says it in the blessing for the sick; and if he needs a livelihood, he says it in the blessing of the years. R' Yehoshua ben Levi said: Even though they said a person asks for his needs in Shomea Tefila — if he comes to say at the end of each blessing something akin to that blessing, he says it. R' Chiya bar Ashi said Rav said: Even though they said a person asks for his needs in Shomea Tefila — if he comes to say after his prayer, even in the order of yehi ratzon, he says it. And Rabbeinu Yerucham wrote that this law divides into four parts. (1) In the blessing of Shomea Tefila one may ask for one's needs in any matter one wishes, for the blessing of Shomea Tefila includes all needs; therefore it was enacted. (2) If one comes to say at the end of each blessing something akin to that blessing, one may say it — and even if not for one's actual needs it is permitted, since one says it in plural language; however, in singular language it is forbidden to say it, since one is not saying it except on behalf of all Israel, and it would be an interruption. And specifically when one can say it in plural language at the end of each blessing — for since one has already completed what the sages enacted for a full obligation, one need not be concerned about what one adds afterwards; but in the middle of the blessing, no — for since one asks for the needs of the many in the middle of the blessing, it appears like adding to the formula that the sages coined. (3) If one asks for one's actual needs — such as for a sick person in one's house that he be healed — one may say it in singular language and even in the middle of the blessing; and this is what we say, "If he has a sick person in his house," etc. And he did not mention here at the end of the blessing, but the blessing for the sick in general — meaning that even in the middle of the blessing one may ask for all one's needs, since one needs it for the moment; and this is when one says it in singular language, so it should not appear like adding to the formula. (4) At the end of the prayer — whether before yehi ratzon or after yehi ratzon — one may extend as one wishes, whether in singular language or plural language, whether for one's actual needs or for communal needs — end quote. And as it appears, the distinction between the first law, which is Shomea Tefila, and the fourth law, which is after the prayer of eighteen [blessings], is this: in Shomea Tefila the individual prays for all his needs, and in this there is no distinction between the middle and the end; not so in the rest of the blessings, as Rabbeinu Yerucham wrote above. But for communal needs, even if he has no need at all, the individual has no [reason] to mention them at all; and it appears he learned this from the words "a person asks for his needs in Shomea Tefila" — implying his needs specifically. And what he wrote "in any matter he wishes" means in every place he wishes, whether in the middle or at the end; but after the prayer one may pray even for communal needs even if one has no need for them. Or alternatively, the distinction is that in Shomea Tefila one may say whatever one desires, only that one should not extend; but after the [eighteen blessings] one may extend. And so too Beit Yosef in the name of the Mordechai — this distinction between after the prayer and the other blessings; and this is the main point. If so, there is a difficulty on what the Rav wrote here in Shulchan Aruch, that the blessing of Shomea Tefila and after the [eighteen blessings] are equal for every matter — if so, there are only three laws here, and Rabbeinu Yerucham wrote that there are four laws. And per the latter explanation it is fine: in Shulchan Aruch he did not mention at all the matter of extending; therefore he equated them, and this is correct. And what we say selichos in the blessing Selach Lanu in plural language in the middle of the blessing — this is the opposite of what Rabbeinu Yonah wrote in the second law. One may say the congregation is different, etc.; so too Beit Yosef.
