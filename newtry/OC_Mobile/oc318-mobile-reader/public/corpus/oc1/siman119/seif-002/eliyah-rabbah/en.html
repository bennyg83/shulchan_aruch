[2] [Levush:] "And this is when he says it in singular language," etc. The explanation is: when he says it in the middle of the blessing; but at the end of the blessing he says it in plural language. And it explicitly implies that specifically when he has a sick person or needs livelihood; but to pray for the future one should not add in the blessings—rather he says it in "Who hears prayer." And see siman 597 regarding the law of consolation in seif 2 and in Beit Yosef. This is the wording of Lechem Chamudos, page 42: there must be such a need for him, and not that he adds gratuitously without his needing it, or after others have prayed for him—up to here. One who prays for his fellow not in his presence must mention his name—so wrote Maharil. Furthermore, that the community decreed a fast and said selichos because someone was sick—from Maharil.
