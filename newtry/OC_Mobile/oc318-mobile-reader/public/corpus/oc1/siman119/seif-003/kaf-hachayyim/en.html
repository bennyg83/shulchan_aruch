3) There: If he needed a livelihood, he requests it in the blessing of the years. But in Shaar HaKavanos, the sixth discourse on the Amidah, it is written that he should ask in Shomea Tefillah, and this is its language there: It is fitting to ask concerning one's sustenance in the berachah of Shomea Tefillah, to show that he trusts in Him, blessed be He, in Whose hand is sustenance for every living thing, in the manner of the prayer of Rav Yeiva Sava every day before his eating, as mentioned in parashas Beshalach (folio 62b). End quote. So too is written there in the text of the tefillah, folio 51c, and in Pri Etz Chaim, Shaar HaAmidah, chapter 19. And it is implied that even if he is wealthy he must ask concerning his sustenance, in order to show that he trusts in Him, blessed be He. So too wrote the Magen Avraham, s.k. 1; Eliyah Rabbah, ot 1; Siddur Beis Oved, ot 3. And in the Birkei Yosef, ot 2, he wrote in the name of the disciples of the Arizal, of blessed memory, that one should pray in his thoughts for livelihood during the blessing of the years, and ask for his sustenance explicitly in Shema Koleinu; and the Shalmei Tzibbur cited it, folio 128c, and Zechor L'Avraham, ot tav. That is, when he says 'bareich aleinu es hashanah hazos' he should pray in his thoughts that Hashem, blessed be He, grant him a good livelihood, and likewise in the summer when he says 'Bareicheinu,' etc.; and afterward, in Shomea Tefillah, he asks explicitly.
3) And he wrote further there in Shaar HaKavanos that it is very fitting for a person, when saying the silent tefillah, to say viduy (confession) in Shomea Tefillah, as mentioned in the Zohar, parashas Balak, folio 195b, and this is its language: Afterward, to place himself among the devout in Shomea Tefillah, to detail his sins. So too is written in the text of the tefillah there, and this is its language: When he reaches the words 'umilfanecha Malkeinu reikam al teshiveinu' [and from before You, our King, do not turn us away empty], he should say viduy over his sins briefly: Master of the world, I have sinned, I have transgressed, I have rebelled before You; may it be Your will that You pardon, forgive, and atone for me all that I have sinned, transgressed, and rebelled before You from the day I was created until this day. And if he wishes, he may confess at length, in detail. End quote. So too is written in Pri Etz Chaim there, and so wrote the Eliyah Rabbah there in the name of the Seder HaYom. And the Eliyah Rabbah wrote further there in the name of the aforementioned that if he has some newly incurred sin from that day, he mentions it in the prayer nearest to him, Shacharis, Minchah, or Maariv.
3) And in the siddur of the Rashash, of blessed memory, is written the order of the viduy and the request for livelihood in the berachah of Shomea Tefillah, after 'shomea tefillos v'sachanunim atah,' at greater length, and this is its language: Please, Hashem, we have sinned, we have transgressed, we have rebelled; we and our fathers and the members of our households. We have violated the Written Torah and the Oral Torah; we have neglected and failed to fulfill the 248 positive commandments and their branches, and those of the words of the Prophets and of the Scribes; and we have transgressed the 365 negative commandments and their branches, and those of the words of the Prophets and of the Scribes; and we have turned away from Your good commandments and laws, and it has not profited us. And You are righteous in all that has come upon us, for You have acted with truth and we have acted wickedly. And now, behold, we return in complete repentance, and regret with complete regret all that we have sinned, transgressed, and rebelled, and abandon them with complete abandonment. And may it be Your will, Hashem our God and God of our fathers, that You pardon and forgive all our transgressions, and open for us the gates of repentance, and extend Your right hand to receive us in complete repentance, for Your right hand is extended to receive those who return.
3) And may it be Your will, Hashem our God and God of our fathers, that You prepare for us and send us, today and on each and every day, sustenance and a good livelihood, bread to eat and a garment to wear, with ease and not with distress, with abundance and not with constriction, with honor and not with disgrace, in permitted ways and not in forbidden ways; a livelihood in which there is no shame or humiliation, a livelihood through which I will be able to engage in Your holy, pure, and perfect Torah for its own sake, a livelihood that will not bring me to need the gifts of flesh and blood nor their loans, for by Your Name sustain me graciously, and from the treasury of Your free gift sustain me and provide for me through the great Name Dikarnosa, which is appointed over livelihood. For You give each and every man his needs and each and every body sufficient for its sustenance, as it is written, 'The eyes of all look hopefully to You, and You give them their food in its proper time.' And even though we have sinned and our sustenance has been withheld, in Your kindness we trust and in Your favor do good to us, as it is written, 'Hashem desires those who fear Him, those who hope for His kindness'; and such are Your ways, to give life to the good and to the wicked, as it is written, 'You open Your hand and satisfy every living thing with favor.' 'And from before You, our King, do not turn us away empty,' etc. End quote. Accordingly, one who has time during the silent tefillah should say this order that is written in the siddur of the Rashash, of blessed memory; and if he has no time, he should say briefly, as written in Shaar HaKavanos, 'Master of the world, I have sinned,' etc., as mentioned above, and afterward say: May it be Your will, Hashem our God and God of our fathers, that You prepare and send us, today and on each and every day, sustenance and a good livelihood, bread to eat and a garment to wear, with ease and not with distress, with abundance and not with constriction, with honor and not with disgrace, in permitted ways and not in forbidden ways, for Your service and for the study of Your Torah, through the great Name Dikarnosa, which is appointed over livelihood, as it is written, 'The eyes of all look hopefully to You,' etc., 'You open Your hand,' etc. (And he should intend the Name only in his heart, and also intend that it equals in gematria the Name Chatach [the Name of sustenance formed by the final letters of 'poseiach es yadecha'], which with its three letters and its vowel points equals seventy-two.) And every person should write this text on paper in order to say it within the tefillah. And see the Zohar, parashas Pinchas, folio 224b, from which it emerges that one should not say the prayer for sustenance in the Minchah prayer; so I heard from the perfect and comprehensive sage, our teacher R' Avraham ben Asher, may Hashem protect him. Shalmei Tzibbur, folio 132d; Shaarei Teshuvah, ot 3; and the Ruach Chaim cited it, ot 1, and wrote that if he has merit in his hand, it will be accepted; see there.
3) And the Shalmei Tzibbur wrote further there, and this is its language: It is good that he say this prayer with submission and a broken heart, with a bowed head, and with a tear if it is possible for him; and if it is not possible for him, he should say it in a weeping voice, and he should accept upon himself not to return again to act thus of his own will; for if not, they prosecute against him, saying: So-and-so, who confessed, has returned brazenly to his former ways; and then his punishment is all the greater, God forbid, as written there in the Zohar. End quote. Shaarei Teshuvah there.
