(א) There — if one needed a livelihood, one asks for it in the blessing of the years. And in Shaar HaKavanot, drush 6 on the Amidah, he wrote that one should ask in Shomea Tefila, and this is what he said there: it is fitting to ask for one's sustenance in the blessing of Shomea Tefila, to show that one trusts in Him, blessed be He, in Whose hand is sustenance for all living things — in the manner of the prayer of Rav Yi'eva Saba every day before his eating, as cited in parshat Beshalach (folio 62b). So too there in Nusach HaTefillah, folio 91b. Pri Etz Chayim, Shaar HaAmidah, chapter 19 — and it implies that even if he is wealthy he must ask for his sustenance, in order to show that he trusts in Him, blessed be He. So too Magen Avraham, s"k 1; Eliyah Rabbah, ot 1; Siddur Beit Yosef, ot 3; and in Berachot Yosef, ot 2 — he wrote in the name of the disciples of the Ari, may his memory be for a blessing, that one should pray in his thought about livelihood in the blessing of the years, and ask for his sustenance explicitly in Shomea Kolenu. And in Beit David, Shulchan Hatamim, folio 128b; Zekan Aharon, ot 9 — and this is when he says Bless us, O the Master our Heaven, with the year — thus he should pray in his thought that Heaven, may He be blessed, give him good livelihood; and likewise in summer when he says Bless us, etc.; and afterwards in Shomea Tefila he asks explicitly.

(ב) And he wrote — Aruch HaShulchan, in Shaar HaKavanot — that it is very fitting for a person that when he says the whispered prayer he should say confession in Shomea Tefila, as cited in the Zohar, parshat Balak, folio 195b, and this is what it says: after his whispered prayer he enters into Shomea Tefila to detail his sins. So too in Nusach HaTefillah there, and this is what it says: when he reaches the words from before You, our King, do not return us empty, etc., he should say confession for his sins in a short way: Master of the Universe, I have sinned, I have transgressed, I have acted wickedly before You; may it be Your will that You pardon, forgive, and atone for me for all that I have sinned, transgressed, and acted wickedly before You from the day I was created until this day. And if he wishes, he may confess at length in detail — so far his words. So too in Pri Etz Chayim there; so too Eliyah Rabbah there, citing Seder HaYom. And Aruch HaShulchan, the elder, wrote in the name of the above that if he has some fresh sin on that day, he mentions it in the previous Amidah with him — whether Shacharit, Minchah, or Maariv.

(ג) And in the Siddur of the Rashash, may his memory be for a blessing, he wrote the order of confession and asking for livelihood in the blessing of Shomea Tefila — after Who hears prayer and supplication, You — at greater length, and this is what he says: Please, O the Master, we have sinned, we have transgressed, we have acted wickedly, we have rebelled — we and our fathers and the people of our households. We have transgressed against the Written Torah and against the Oral Torah, and we have nullified and not fulfilled the 248 positive commandments and their ramifications, and the 365 negative commandments and their ramifications, and the 248 organs and 365 sinews, and we have turned away from Your good commandments and they were not worthwhile for us; and You are righteous concerning all that has come upon us, for You have acted truthfully and we have acted wickedly. And now we have returned in complete repentance and regret everything we have sinned, transgressed, and acted wickedly — complete regret and abandoning them with complete abandonment. And may it be will before You, O the Master our Heaven and Heaven of our fathers, that You pardon and forgive all our transgressions, and open for us the gates of repentance, and spread forth Your right hand to receive us in complete repentance, for Your right hand is extended to receive penitents.

(ד) And may it be will before You, O the Master our Heaven and Heaven of our fathers, that You prepare for us and send us today and every day sustenance and good livelihood — bread to eat and clothing to wear, in comfort and not in distress, in abundance and not in restriction, in honor and not in disgrace, permissibly and not forbiddenly — livelihood in which there is no shame or disgrace; livelihood through which I may engage in Your holy, pure, and perfect Torah for its own sake; livelihood that will not require me to depend on gifts from flesh and blood, nor on borrowing from them, for in Your Name I rely; and from the treasury of the gift of grace sustain me and provide for me through the great name Dikarnosa, who is appointed over livelihood. For You give every person according to his lack, and to every nation according to its sustenance, as it is written: The eyes of all look to You, and You give them their food in its time. And although we have sinned and withheld our sustenance from ourselves, in Your kindness we have trusted and in Your good will You have done good to us, as it is written: The the Master desires those who fear Him, those who hope for His kindness, and so are Your attributes to give life to the good and the bad, as it is written: You open Your hand and satisfy the desire of every living thing. And from before You, our King, empty, etc. — so far his words. If so, one who has leisure in the whispered prayer should say this order written in the Siddur of the Rashash, may his memory be for a blessing; and if he does not have leisure, he should say it briefly as written in Shaar HaKavanot: Master of the Universe, I have sinned, etc., as above. And afterwards he should say: May it be will before You, O the Master our Heaven and Heaven of our fathers, that You prepare and send us today and every day sustenance and good livelihood — bread to eat and clothing to wear, in comfort and not in distress, in abundance and not in restriction, in honor and not in disgrace, permissibly and not forbiddenly — for Your service and for study of Your Torah, through the name Dikarnosa the great, who is appointed over livelihood, as it is written: The eyes of all look to You, etc., You open Your hand, etc. (And he should intend the Name only in his heart, and also intend that it is the gematria of chatak together with its three letters and vowel points, gematria 72.) And so on. Every person should write this formula on paper in order to say it within the prayer. And see the Zohar, parshat Pinchas, folio 224b, from which it is explained that one should not say prayer for sustenance in the Minchah prayer — thus I heard from the complete and comprehensive sage, Maharar Avraham ben Asher, may his light shine; Shulchan Hatamim, folio 132b, seif 3, and he brought Ruach Chayim, ot 1, and wrote that if one has merit in his hand it will be accepted — see Aruch HaShulchan.

(ה) And he wrote — Aruch HaShulchan, Shulchan Hatamim — and this is what he says: it is good that one say this prayer with humility and brokenheartedness, with bowed head and tears if possible for him; and if it is not possible for him, he should say it in a crying voice. And he should accept upon himself not to return again knowingly to do such a thing; and if not, then accusers rise against him to say that so-and-so who confessed has returned to brazenness to his first rebellion, and then his punishment is greater, God forbid, as written in that Zohar passage — so far his words.
