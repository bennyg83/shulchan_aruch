"If he skipped or erred," etc.—the matter of error: it appears to me that it is included in two ways. (1) That he did not complete the blessing properly, such as that he did not conclude it properly, or that he omitted something that is essential to the blessing—such as that he did not say "Who makes the dew descend" in the rainy season, or that he omitted to mention the ingathering of exiles in the blessing of "Sound the shofar," whose essence is built upon that—even if he concluded properly (as stated in Megillah 13), and the like; and as if he omitted "Who forms light" in the blessing of "Who forms light," where the author ruled above in siman 59, seif 1, that he has not fulfilled his obligation [although Magen Avraham wrote in siman 114, seif kaf-tet, such as if he erred in their conclusions—but if he erred mid-blessing it is of no consequence; it appears to me that Magen Avraham's intent there is that he omitted some matter that is not essential to the subject of the blessing; but a matter that is essential to the blessing is like the conclusion, and this is proven from what Magen Avraham concluded there—that there are many places with variant texts in the prayer, from which we see that he deals with a matter of wording alone, but not where he omitted the subject of the blessing. And did not Magen Avraham copy in siman 64 the words of Kesef Mishnah there? And see there in Rambam and Kesef Mishnah that the subject of the blessing is indispensable. And did he not direct one to look in siman 59? And the meaning is that there it is proven that if he began with "Who forms light," which is the essential subject of the blessing, even if mid-blessing he said something unrelated to the blessing he has fulfilled his obligation—unlike if he omitted the essential subject of the blessing, in which case he has not fulfilled his obligation, even according to Magen Avraham; and so wrote Peri Megadim in Mishbetzos Zahav, ot gimel. And see what we inclined to above in siman 117, seif 5, on this matter. (2) If he said something that spoils the blessing, such as that he said "Who makes the dew descend" in the summer days, and the like. Now all this is per the author's view above in siman 59; but per the Gra there, that even if he did not mention at all at the beginning "Who forms light," since he concluded properly he has fulfilled his obligation after the fact—it appears that the same applies here in the first manner: we follow the conclusion and he has fulfilled his obligation, since in it he mentions the ingathering of exiles. And see in Biur Halacha there, where we wrote that the view of Rashba and Ramban is also like that of the Gra.
