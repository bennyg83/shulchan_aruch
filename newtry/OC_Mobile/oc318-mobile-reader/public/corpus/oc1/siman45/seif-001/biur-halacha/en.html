It is forbidden to enter a cemetery, etc. — See Pri Megadim, who wrote that there are three views on the matter: the Bach holds that in a cemetery too, it is forbidden only within four amot of a grave; but outside four amot of a grave, even if one stands in the middle of the cemetery, it is permitted — as long as there is no grave within four amot around him. And the Taz explained that by Torah law there is no prohibition except within four amot of a grave; and the Tur ruled stringently as a protective fence outside four amot of a grave for anyone standing in a cemetery — that is, in a place designated for graves; but outside the cemetery one need not be stringent at all according to him, as he explained in Orach Chayyim that one does not make a fence for a fence. And the Magen Avraham explained that within four amot of the cemetery is also forbidden; and according to his words it is difficult to combine them and teach, "It is forbidden to enter within four amot of a cemetery or of a corpse." But in Machatzit HaShekel on siman 71 he explained that the Magen Avraham holds here as the Taz, that it is not forbidden from the place where the cemetery began — that is, the land designated for graves — and outward, even one amah, even if there is no intervening partition; only that in this he argues with the Taz and holds that from the place of the cemetery inward — such as when there is a partition around the cemetery, or even if there is no partition at all but it was designated for graves — it is forbidden by Torah law even far from the graves; and with this the difficulty of the Pri Megadim on the Magen Avraham is removed. And the Magen Avraham who wrote in our matter that it is forbidden even within four amot of the cemetery — that is, if the graves begin immediately from the place where the cemetery began; and that which the Mechaber did not mention the prohibition only in a cemetery is because he comes to include in this that from the place where the land of the cemetery began and inward it is forbidden even outside four amot from the graves. And know also that the Gra holds in siman 23 in his Biur that within four amot of a cemetery is forbidden; and he also hinted to look in the Magen Avraham in our matter — it also appears that he explains the Magen Avraham as the Machatzit HaShekel above, and he also deals specifically in this manner; for he pointed there to the source for what the Mechaber wrote there, that within four amot of a grave is forbidden — and you know that there is no strain at all to explain thus, for the Taz himself, who is not stringent at all outside the cemetery, also wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 282, note 3, that within four amot of a cemetery is forbidden; therefore he deals in the manner the Machatzit HaShekel wrote. The conclusion from this is that from land designated for graves and outward there is certainly no prohibition at all, even one amah, as long as there are four amot from the grave. However, even in what the Magen Avraham ruled stringently in the cemetery itself by Torah law outside four amot of a grave, there is room to examine this; for the Bach and the Urim veTumim stand in one view to be lenient, and the Taz also holds like them from the outset of the law; and the Pri Megadim challenged him that according to his words land designated for graves is forbidden even very far from graves — if so, how did they practice saying Kaddish in a cemetery? See Yoreh De'ah siman 376; and so he wrote in Sefer Nahar Shalom that it is an excessive stringency to be stringent outside four amot; if so, we would need to be stringent over all land designated for graves even many amot distant; but from the place of the graves and inward, even if it happened that there are more than four amot distant from the grave, Nahar Shalom wrote there that there is room to be stringent in this.
