(1) [Seif 1] It is forbidden to enter a cemetery or within four amot of a corpse, etc. It is implied that in a cemetery it is forbidden even outside four amot from the grave—for if not, combine the cases and say: "It is forbidden to go within four amot of a grave or of a corpse"—Taz, note 1. And likewise the Magen Avraham, note 1, that even within four amot of a cemetery is forbidden; and he wrote that so it is in Yoreh De'ah, siman 282, seif 4, and siman 366, seif 3, and siman 376, seif 4; and to this the Beit Yosef intended in siman 23, and such is also the intent of the Gemara—thus far; see there. And see the Machatzit HaShekel. However, the Bach challenged that in the Gemara it appears that it is forbidden only within four amot of a corpse, but outside four amot is permitted—and likewise the Beit Yosef in siman 23; and he answered that in a cemetery too it is forbidden only within four amot of a grave—see there. And likewise Shaarei Teshuvah, letter 1. And the Taz there answered that the Tur holds that certainly the essence of the prohibition is within four amot of a grave; rather, as a protective decree one should not enter a cemetery with tefillin on his head, lest he come within four amot without realizing it—see there. And Maamar Mordechai, letter 1, answered: because an unspecified cemetery is full of graves, and presumably when he is there he is near a grave—therefore Maran ruled to forbid in general—see there. Thus there are four disputes in the matter: the Bach and Shaarei Teshuvah hold that even in a cemetery it is forbidden only within four amot of a grave. The Taz holds that not entering a cemetery with tefillin is only a protective decree, but it is not forbidden by law. Maamar Mordechai holds that it is forbidden to enter because it is full of graves. The Magen Avraham holds that even within four amot of a cemetery it is forbidden to enter. And since so, one should rule stringently and not enter a cemetery with tefillin on his head even outside four amot of a grave. And likewise Razav, letter 1, and these are his words: a cemetery that is surrounded by a partition—one should rule stringently not to enter it at all with tefillin on his head, even very far from the graves, lest he come within four amot of some grave unknowingly; but outside the partition it is permitted even near it, although he is within four amot of the graves, because the partition interrupts and he does not appear to be mocking the poor (lo'eg larash)—thus far. And see Yoreh De'ah, siman 366, seif 6. And ground designated for a cemetery—such as when one made a partition around it and the like, and there are graves within it—for the entire four amot from the designated ground it is forbidden, even very far from the graves—Levush; Pitchei Olam, letter 1. And see further in our words to siman 23 what we wrote on this, with God's help.
