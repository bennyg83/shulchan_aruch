(Seif katan 1) In a cemetery, and even within, etc. “This is what he intended,” etc. Magen Avraham intends to resolve Bach's question, which Taz also asks. Since in a cemetery the prohibition apparently applies even beyond four amos from a grave, why did the Mechaber not combine the clauses and write: “It is forbidden to enter within four amos of a grave or of a dead person while wearing tefillin”? In siman 23, Beit Yosef writes in his commentary on Tur: “Know that when the Gemara says a person may not walk in a cemetery with tefillin upon his head, the prohibition applies specifically within four amos, but beyond four amos it is permitted.” His words therefore appear contradictory. Magen Avraham answers that the prohibition here beyond four amos refers to one who stands inside the cemetery itself. The distinction in siman 23 permitting beyond four amos refers to one standing outside the cemetery. Even outside the cemetery he must remain at least four amos from it, specifically where no wall surrounds the cemetery, as stated explicitly in Yoreh Deah, siman 367. Beit Yosef's statement there, that beyond four amos it is permitted, refers to this case. Here, Shulchan Aruch did not mention that outside the cemetery one must remain four amos away, because he considered it obvious. His discussion in siman 26 indicates that a cemetery is more severe than a dead person. Since he writes that one must remain four amos from a dead person, all the more so from a cemetery.
