(Note 1) In a cemetery, and even within, etc. — And to this he intended, etc.; his intent is to resolve the difficulty of the Bach; and so the Taz challenged, for if so, in a cemetery even outside four amot from the grave is forbidden — for otherwise we would need specifically within four amot of a grave to combine them and teach, "It is forbidden to enter within four amot of a grave or of a corpse" with tefillin, etc. And in siman 23 the Rabbi wrote in his book on the Tur, and these are his words: and know that in the Gemara we say a person should not walk in a cemetery with tefillin on his head — specifically within four amot is forbidden, but outside four amot is permitted — end of his words; behold, his words contradict each other; and to this the Magen Avraham answered that what he forbade here even outside four amot is when he is within the cemetery; and that which he distinguished above in siman 23 that outside four amot is permitted is outside the cemetery; for even from outside one must in any case distance four amot from the cemetery — and this is specifically when there are no partitions for the cemetery, as stated explicitly in Yoreh De'ah siman 367 — see there; and as the Rabbi wrote in Yoreh De'ah, that outside four amot is permitted, and here he did not mention this in the Shulchan Aruch that outside the cemetery one must distance four amot, because he holds the matter is obvious, as appears from his words in his work in siman 26, that he is more stringent than a corpse; and since he wrote that from a corpse one must distance four amot, all the more so from a cemetery outside.
