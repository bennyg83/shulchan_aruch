(1) In a cemetery — and even within four amot of the place where the graves begin is also forbidden if there is no intervening partition between them; and that which he did not combine them and say, "It is forbidden to walk within four amot of a grave or of a corpse" — the Magen Avraham's view is that he comes to hint that from the place of the cemetery and inward it is forbidden even far from the grave; and there are those who are lenient in this. And see Biur Halacha that in the middle of a cemetery where there are graves around, it appears that one should be stringent even far from the grave; unlike at the end of the cemetery where there are no graves but only land designated for graves — one need not be stringent by Torah law when far from the grave; nevertheless it is proper not to enter at all with tefillin on his head whoever is inside the cemetery partition, lest he unknowingly come within four amot of some grave.

(2) Within four amot, etc. — and the Eitz Yosef wrote that the entire room in which the dead person lies is considered as four amot of a corpse; and Magen Giborim argues with him and wrote that it applies only to four amot — see there. And even the grave of a minor who has not yet reached the age of mitzvot — nevertheless one should not enter there with tefillin on his head because of lo'eg larash.

(3) Permitted — and the straps too must be covered; therefore, although it is permitted to enter with shel yad alone since it is covered, one must be careful with the strap on his finger that it too be covered [Taz].
