Or. See Taz. There are three opinions. Bach holds that in a cemetery too, only the area within four amos of a grave is forbidden. Beyond four amos from a grave it is permitted even if one stands in the middle of the cemetery, provided no grave is within four amos. The reference to four amos of a dead person teaches a novelty. One might have thought that while a body is being taken for burial the prohibition extends beyond four amos. Taz explains that by strict law the prohibition applies only within four amos of a grave. Tur nevertheless imposed a safeguard and prohibited being anywhere in the cemetery beyond four amos from a grave. Magen Avraham explains that even within four amos of the cemetery itself is forbidden. This also appears from Yoreh Deah, to which he refers. According to Magen Avraham, it is difficult why the Mechaber did not combine the clauses and write: “It is forbidden to enter within four amos of a cemetery or of a dead person.” Taz too requires explanation. What source did Tur have for creating this safeguard when the Gemara specifically permits beyond four amos from a grave?
