Or — Eitz Yosef. Three views on the matter: the Bach holds that in a cemetery too, within four amot of a grave is what is forbidden; but outside four amot of a grave, even if one stands in the middle of the cemetery, it is permitted — as long as there is no grave within four amot around him. And within four amot of a corpse he states a greater matter — that is, a corpse being taken out for burial; even outside four amot is forbidden. And the Taz explained that by Torah law there is prohibition only within four amot of a grave; and the Tur ruled stringently as a protective fence outside four amot of a grave for anyone standing in a cemetery. But the Magen Avraham explained that within four amot of a cemetery is also forbidden, and so in Yoreh De'ah, as he showed a place for this; and according to his words it is difficult to combine them and teach, "It is forbidden to enter within four amot of a cemetery or of a corpse." It is also difficult for the Taz — from where does the Tur have this fence? — since in the Gemara it is specifically within four amot of a grave; but outside four amot of a grave is permitted.
