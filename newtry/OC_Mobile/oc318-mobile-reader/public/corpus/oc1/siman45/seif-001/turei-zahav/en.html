Or within four amos of a dead person. This implies that in a cemetery it is forbidden even beyond four amos from a grave. Otherwise, the Mechaber should have combined the clauses and written: “It is forbidden to walk within four amos of a grave or a dead person.” Yet at the beginning of the chapter Mi SheMeiso the Gemara says that within four amos is forbidden but beyond four amos is permitted, meaning for reciting Krias Shema. Beit Yosef likewise writes in siman 23: “Know that when the Gemara says a person may not walk in a cemetery with tefillin upon his head, the prohibition applies specifically within four amos, but beyond four amos it is permitted.” This creates a difficulty in Tur and Shulchan Aruch's wording here. It appears to me that Tur holds that the essential prohibition certainly applies only within four amos of a grave. Nevertheless, as a safeguard, one should not enter a cemetery at all while wearing tefillin, lest he unknowingly approach within four amos of a grave. This resembles the Nazirite teaching: “Go around, go around; do not approach the vineyard.” If, however, he happened to enter a cemetery while remaining beyond four amos from every grave, he is certainly obligated to recite Krias Shema. The prohibition against entering is only an added stringency. This appears correct to me.
