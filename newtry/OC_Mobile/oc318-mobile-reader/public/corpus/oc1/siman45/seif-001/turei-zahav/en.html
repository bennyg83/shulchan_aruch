Or within four amot of a corpse. It is implied that in a cemetery it is forbidden even outside four amot from the grave; for if not so, let him combine them and say, "It is forbidden to walk within four amot of a grave or of a corpse." And in the beginning of the chapter "One whose dead [relative] is before him" we say: within four amot is forbidden, outside four amot is permitted — meaning to recite Shema; and so the Beit Yosef in siman 23, and these are his words: and know that in the Gemara we say a person should not walk in a cemetery with tefillin on his head — specifically within four amot is forbidden, but outside four amot is permitted — if so, there is a difficulty on the words of the Tur and the Shulchan Aruch here. And it appears to me that the Tur holds that the main prohibition is certainly within four amot of a grave; but because of a protective fence one should not enter a cemetery with tefillin on his head, lest he unknowingly come within four amot of a grave; and as written in Nazir, "go around, go around, do not approach the vineyard"; but nevertheless, if it happened that he went to a cemetery and he is outside four amot, he is certainly obligated to recite Shema — for they spoke only to be stringent that he should not enter at all, as appears to me.
