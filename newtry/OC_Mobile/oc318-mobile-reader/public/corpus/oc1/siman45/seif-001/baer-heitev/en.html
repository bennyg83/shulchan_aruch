Corpse. It is implied that in a cemetery it is forbidden even outside four amot from the grave; for if not so, let him combine them and say, "It is forbidden to walk within four amot of a grave or of a corpse." And at the beginning of the chapter "One whose dead [relative] is before him" we say: within four amot is forbidden, outside four amot is permitted — meaning to recite Shema. And the Taz wrote that the Tur holds that the main prohibition is certainly within four amot of the grave; but because of a protective fence one should not enter a cemetery with tefillin on his head, lest he unknowingly come within four amot of a grave. But nevertheless, if it happened that he went to a cemetery and he is outside four amot from a grave, he is certainly obligated to recite Shema. And so too the Bach; and the Urim veTumim that the prohibition is precisely within four amot of the grave regarding Shema. And see the Taz in siman 71, note 5, and the words of the Magen Avraham here require further study; see siman 78, note 4 in Magen Avraham and Yoreh De'ah siman 282, seif 4, and siman 366, seif 3, and examine carefully. Even the grave of a minor who has not yet reached the age of mitzvot — nevertheless it is forbidden to put on tefillin and recite Shema beside him. Maharitz in his chiddushim on the chapter "What is considered a bite," folio 12 amud dalet; see Eitz Yosef, who wrote that the entire room in which the dead person lies is considered as four amot — see there.
