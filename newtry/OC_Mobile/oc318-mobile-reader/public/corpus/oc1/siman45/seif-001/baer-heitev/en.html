Yet at the beginning of the chapter Mi SheMeiso the Gemara says that within four amos it is forbidden, but beyond four amos it is permitted to recite Krias Shema. Taz writes that Tur holds that the essential prohibition indeed applies only within four amos of a grave. Nevertheless, as a safeguard one should not enter a cemetery while wearing tefillin, lest he unknowingly come within four amos of a grave. If, however, he happened to enter a cemetery and remains beyond four amos from every grave, he is certainly obligated to recite Krias Shema. See there. Bach and Olas Tamid likewise write that the prohibition applies specifically within four amos of a grave. See Taz in siman 71, seif katan 5. Magen Avraham's words here require study. See Magen Avraham in siman 78, seif katan 4, and Yoreh Deah, siman 282, seif 4, and siman 367, seif 3. Even beside the grave of a minor who never reached the age of mitzvos, it is forbidden to put on tefillin or recite Krias Shema. Maharit Tzahalon in his novellae to the chapter Eizehu Neshech, page 12, column 4. See Ateres Zekeinim, who writes that the entire room in which the deceased lies is considered like four amos.
