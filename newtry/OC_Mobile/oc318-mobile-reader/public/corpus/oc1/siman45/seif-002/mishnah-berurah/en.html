(4) The outer room — their practice was that after donning the robe in the middle room they would go to the outer room and finish dressing.

(5) And some of them are naked — some say that if now no naked person is there, it is permitted to put on tefillin there and bless; and some forbid, since the place is designated for this — the law of a bathhouse applies to it in part. And the immersion room — it appears from the Taz in siman 84 that the law of a middle room applies to everything, and only the blessing of immersion may be recited there — see his reasoning. And from the Magen Avraham in this siman it appears that when no naked person is there, it is permitted to put on tefillin there and bless; for only in a bathhouse did they rule stringently even when no person is there, because its uncleanness is greater than the steam of hot water that people use in it — unlike a mikveh [and if they also pour hot water in it, there is room to examine]. But if a naked person is there, it is forbidden to enter with tefillin and sacred writings, for it is forbidden to stand before God naked; and see below in siman 84 in Mishna Berurah on this matter.

(6) And in the inner room — in this all agree that even when no naked person is there, because its uncleanness is great, it is like a permanent privy.
