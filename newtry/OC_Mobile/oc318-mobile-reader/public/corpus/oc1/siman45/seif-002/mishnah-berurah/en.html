(4) The outer room. Their practice was to put on a robe in the middle room, then proceed to the outer room and complete dressing there.
(5) And some are unclothed. Some authorities say that if no person is presently unclothed there, one may put on tefillin and recite a blessing there. Others prohibit it, because the location is designated for undressing and therefore possesses the law of a bathhouse at least in part. Taz in siman 84 implies that a mikveh room has the law of the middle bathhouse room in every respect, except that the blessing upon immersion may be recited there. See there for his reason. Magen Avraham here implies that when no unclothed person is present, one may put on tefillin and recite a blessing in the mikveh room. The Sages were stringent specifically in a bathhouse even when no one is present because its filth is greater from the hot-water steam used there. This does not apply to a mikveh. If hot water is also poured there, the matter requires consideration. When an unclothed person is present, however, it is forbidden to enter wearing tefillin or carrying sacred writings, because one may not stand unclothed before the Divine Name. See below in siman 84, Mishnah Berurah, where this matter is discussed.
(6) And in the inner room. Everyone agrees that it is forbidden there even when no unclothed person is present, because its filth is extensive and it resembles a lavatory.
