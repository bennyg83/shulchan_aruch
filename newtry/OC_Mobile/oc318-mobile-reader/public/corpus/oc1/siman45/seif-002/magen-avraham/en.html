In a bathhouse. Rabbeinu Yeruham wrote in his book, part 2, section 2: a story that someone embroidered a verse and recited it on his tallit, etc.; the Rambam wrote, etc., that one who does so brings verses of the Torah to contempt, for the tzitzit do not have the sanctity of the body; therefore he brings them into a privy and into the immersion room, etc. — how can he bring scriptures of the Torah into a place of filth and contempt? — end of his words. It is implied that it is forbidden to enter wearing tefillin into a place where people immerse; and in Kol Bo, chapter 3 of Kriat Shema, he wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Menachem: there is an opinion that since the prohibition of a bathhouse is because people stand there naked, the same applies to those mikvaot where women immerse — it is forbidden to bless inside them. And it is not reasonable that the main prohibition of a bathhouse is because of uncleanness and the steam in it through using it with hot water; but cold is permitted — and this requires study; for regarding a mezuzah, the immersion room is like a bathhouse — end of his words. And in Yoreh De'ah siman 200 it states that women bless in a mikveh; but it appears to me that nevertheless it is forbidden to enter inside with sacred writings, for it is forbidden to stand before God naked, as stated in Shabbos folio 120; and for this reason one is exempt from a mezuzah. And when no person is there it is permitted; for only in a bathhouse did they rule stringently even when no person is there, but not in a mikveh, as above.
