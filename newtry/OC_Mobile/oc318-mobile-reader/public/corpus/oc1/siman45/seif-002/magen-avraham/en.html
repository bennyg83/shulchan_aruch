In a bathhouse. Rabbeinu Yerucham, netiv 2, part 2, records an incident in which a person embroidered the verse “And He said” upon his tallis. Rambam wrote that one who does so causes Torah verses to be treated disrespectfully, because tzitzis possess no intrinsic sanctity. He will therefore bring them into a lavatory and a place of immersion, thereby bringing sacred writings into a filthy and degrading place. This implies that one may not enter a place of immersion while wearing tefillin. Kesef Mishneh, chapter 3 of Hilchos Krias Shema, cites Rabbeinu Manoach that some say: since a bathhouse is forbidden because people stand there unclothed, the same applies to mikvaos in which women immerse. It is forbidden to recite a blessing there. Rabbeinu Manoach rejects this and explains that the principal bathhouse prohibition is due to its filth and steam from hot-water use. A cold-water mikveh is therefore permitted. This requires study, because regarding mezuzah a room used for immersion has the law of a bathhouse and is exempt. In Yoreh Deah, siman 200, the law states that women recite the blessing in the mikveh. It appears to me, however, that it is still forbidden to enter the mikveh carrying sacred writings, because one may not stand unclothed before the Divine Name, as stated in Shabbos 120. For this reason such a room is exempt from mezuzah. When no person is present, however, it is permitted. The Sages were stringent concerning a bathhouse even when no person is present because of its filth, but not concerning a mikveh. This appears correct.
