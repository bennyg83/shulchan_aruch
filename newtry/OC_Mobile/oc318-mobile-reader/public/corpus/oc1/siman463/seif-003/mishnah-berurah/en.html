(6) Karmel that is singed, etc. Although the grain was already roasted in fire, so that its flour is comparable to baked flour, and we rule that baked flour does not become chametz, it is nevertheless forbidden because of the concern stated by the Mechaber.
(7) And it is found to become chametz, etc. If they transgressed and placed it into food, the food is forbidden for benefit and may not be retained, because this is a doubt concerning a Torah prohibition.
(8) When it is cooked. Even if the flour itself was roasted in an oven, it should not be cooked in water, lest it not have been roasted thoroughly and come to leaven. Some permit cooking roasted flour, because the heat of the fire penetrates flour more effectively than whole kernels. Several acharonim agree that one may rely on them b'dieved. Thus, if one transgressed and made a cooked dish from it, the dish may be permitted for benefit or retained until after Pesach. If flour is found inside a baked matzah, the matzah may not be placed into gravy, because that flour was certainly not baked thoroughly, since the matzah separated it from the fire. If it was placed into the gravy, the entire dish is forbidden even for benefit.
(9) Meaning, they place flour, etc. This explanation follows the Tur. The Beit Yosef, however, quotes the Rambam's phrase “new pots,” meaning that when new pots are first used, flour and water are cooked in them to harden and strengthen the vessel.
