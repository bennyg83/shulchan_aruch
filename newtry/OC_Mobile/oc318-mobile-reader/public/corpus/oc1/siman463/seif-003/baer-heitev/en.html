Well. Rambam is concerned about this specifically regarding roasted kernels. Regarding roasted flour, however, Rambam rules in chapter 5 that it may initially be cooked. Nevertheless, since the Mechaber concludes here, “except for baked matzah,” it follows that he holds roasted flour is forbidden in every case. Magen Avraham writes: It requires study whether, after the fact, if it was placed into food, the food becomes forbidden because of this concern. Rashi permits it initially. A practical difference arises concerning matzah that was kneaded with flour and baked, as explained at the end of siman 459, and was afterward placed into gravy. Nevertheless, it appears to me that regarding matzah one should be stringent, because the matzah separates between the flour and the fire. Chok Yaakov writes that roasted kernels should be forbidden even after the fact. Regarding roasted flour, however, since according to Rambam it is permitted even initially, one may rely upon him after the fact to permit benefit from it or to retain it until after Pesach.
