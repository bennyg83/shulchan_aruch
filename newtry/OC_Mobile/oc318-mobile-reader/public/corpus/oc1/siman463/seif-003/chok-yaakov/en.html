And specifically regarding carmel [fresh roasted grain] the Rambam is concerned about this; but regarding roasted flour, the Rambam ruled in chapter 5 that it is permitted to cook lechatchilah. And it is possible that this is also Rashi's view on folio 40b regarding that Rava wiped his pot with chasisi: Rashi explained, kimcha d'abhshuna (roasted flour); and with this is resolved what Tosafos ask there. However, the Mechaber who concluded here "rather baked matzah, etc." implies that he holds that in every case the roasted is forbidden, like the view of Tosafos; and so he wrote in Beis Yosef in siman 461 in the name of Rabbeinu Yeruham. Even though the Rambam also wrote in the same language as the Mechaber, "rather baked matzah, etc.," nevertheless he wrote explicitly that roasted flour is permitted, which is not so in the Mechaber's language. And see Magen Avraham who wrote, and these are his words: And this requires further study, if it fell after the fact into a food, whether they forbid because of this concern, for behold Rashi permits lechatchilah, and so Beis Yosef wrote in the name of the Rambam; and nevertheless, regarding matzah kneaded with flour and baked, and afterwards they put it into the cooked dish, one should be stringent, since the matzah intervenes between the flour and the fire; end quote. Now, what is implied from the brevity of his language, that even regarding roasted carmel, according to the Rambam and Rashi it is permitted lechatchilah: this is certainly not so, for they do not disagree with the anonymous ruling of the Gemara that forbids; and so the Rambam wrote explicitly that it is forbidden; and if so, it is a Torah-level doubt and forbidden after the fact. But regarding roasted flour, since according to their view it is permitted even lechatchilah, it is possible to rely [on this] after the fact and permit benefit from it, or to keep it until after Pesach; so it appears to me.
