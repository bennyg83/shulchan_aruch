Rather, baked matzah. In the Tur it is written: And in the Gemara it says that even with baked matzah it is forbidden where there are servants, etc. And Beis Yosef wrote that this is not a difficulty, for in the Gemara there are two versions, and the Rif and the Rosh ruled like the latter version; and certainly it would not have crossed the Tur's mind to raise a difficulty from one version against one who rules like the second version. Rather, it appears that his intent is: since according to the first version Rava said that it is not proper to permit where there are servants, namely the house of the Exilarch, for there is concern of confusing [it] with flour; and according to the latter version we say that they did so for Rava himself, and there servants were not common; if so, why should we say that he does not hold of distinguishing between where there are servants and where there are not, and that there is no difference of law between them, only concerning with whom the incident occurred? And as long as it is not proven that we should create a dispute, it is certainly preferable to say so. And one may also say that [the Tur] refers to lentils and other kinds of kitniyos, which are truly flour and resemble flour of the five species more than baked matzah does; and the Tur says that even with baked matzah there is a stringency where there are servants; and although there is a dispute about this, nevertheless regarding lentil flour it is obvious that one must be concerned about this; and so we hold that all flours of kinds of kitniyos and lentils are certainly forbidden for us, for even when they are whole they are forbidden because they may be confused with grain, as is stated in siman 453. All the more so regarding these, so it appears to me according to my humble opinion.
