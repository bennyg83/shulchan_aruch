Rather see the Taz; see Pesachim 40b, and see there. And the Taz wrote that the Tur raised a proper difficulty, for it is possible that as a matter of practical law there is no dispute between the first version and the latter version, only that where servants are common the act is forbidden, and for Rava, servants were not common in his place. And what he wrote regarding lentils: that is, it is possible that where servants are widespread, everyone agrees that lentil flour is forbidden to cook, even according to the one who permits baked matzah. And see in the Tur what he wrote: and the Rif and my master my father the Rosh, of blessed memory, wrote it without specification regarding baked matzah; and the Rif explained chasisi as baked matzah, and the Rosh explained it as lentil flour, like the Aruch. And what he wrote that for us flour of kitniyos is forbidden, see siman 453; and it appears that lentil flour, if it fell into a food, and all the more so whole kitniyos, the same applies to flour: whenever the majority is from a permitted food, see siman 453. There are those who do not eat baked matzah except on the last day of Yom Tov of Pesach; the reason is that they were concerned lest one come to confuse [it], or perhaps there is flour in them that was not baked; and as for soaking ground matzah in cold water, according to the first reason there is no prohibition, and according to the second reason one may say [there is]; and the world relies on the Mechaber and cooks baked matzah; and the Rav as well, since he was silent, agrees with him; see Pri Chadash, and see Magen Avraham [note] 5; and with God's help it will be explained further.
