Seif 3—one may not cook in them except baked matzah returned and ground. Note: Tur forbids even baked matzah; see Taz explaining Tur's view; see my Passover laws ch. 7 reconciling Beit Yosef—that from Gemara Rava himself inferred dispute. Now it fits Tur's view: Rava himself did so, meaning he himself had no error concern and held that through others who do not know it is forbidden and leads to contempt; but through great ones of Israel it is permitted, also a recognition since he himself does unusual action—recognition and no confusion concern. On this I add to Taz on Tur's intent: not to forbid specifically where workers are common, rather to forbid in every case since R' Yochanan said Rav: wherever Sages forbade for marit ayin, even in inner chambers it is forbidden; though some hold d'rabbanan permitted in inner chambers—see Magen Avraham siman 301—nevertheless in d'oraisa certainly forbidden in inner chambers; here with chametz being d'oraisa, since Rava forbids where workers are common, we forbid everywhere there are no workers, for wherever we fear marit ayin even in inner chambers it is forbidden. But necessarily Rava does not hold like R' Yochanan bar Rav, for many amoraim in Beitza ch. 1 do not hold like Rav; so Rava permits where workers uncommon or he himself does it. But for us who rule like Rav and poskim wrote what we rule is only from Rava onward; before, halacha followed earlier ones—therefore forbidden in every case. This is Tur's intent—reasonable—and I found proper reason for those who do not eat knaidelach; seemingly without reason, but per above one should heed Tur who forbids with Gemara and poskim support—requires study. In my responsum to KK Posen I wrote that even in pressing need one may permit legumes as earlier responsum; nevertheless Turkish wheat called weitzen is certainly forbidden to make flour and bread or cooked dish for Passover—lest confusion, as Taz here—all legume flour forbidden, permitted only whole like pasta and rice, especially called weitzen (wheat in Hebrew)—confusion and marit ayin; hesitant to permit—requires study.
