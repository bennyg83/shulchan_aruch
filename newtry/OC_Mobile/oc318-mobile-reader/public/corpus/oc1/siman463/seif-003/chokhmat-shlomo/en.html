Se'if 3. One may cook with them only baked matzah that they returned and ground. Marginal note: In the Tur he forbids even baked matzah; and see in the Taz what he wrote to explain the Tur's view; and see in my work on the laws of Pesach, this siman, what I wrote to resolve the Beis Yosef's view, that from the language of the Gemara "Rava himself" one may deduce that it is a dispute. And at present it appears that the Tur's view fits well: for what the Gemara says "Rava himself, etc." means that Rava himself would do so; that is, regarding him himself there is no concern of error, and he holds that through others who do not know, it is forbidden and it comes to belittling; but through great ones of Israel it is permitted to do, and there is also a distinctive marker from this itself: since he himself does this, which is not his usual practice, it constitutes a distinctive marker, and there is no concern of confusion. Behold, according to this I add further to the Taz's words regarding the Tur's intent: that his intent is not to forbid only specifically where servants are common, but rather his intent is to forbid in every case; since R' Yehuda said in the name of Rav: Anywhere that the Sages forbade because of appearance [mar'is ayin], it is forbidden even in the innermost chambers; and although there are opinions that regarding a rabbinic [prohibition] it is permitted in the innermost chambers, see Magen Avraham siman 301, nevertheless regarding a Torah-level [matter] it certainly teaches us that it is forbidden in the innermost chambers; and if so, here, since regarding chametz the prohibition is Torah-level, and since Rava forbids where servants are common, he should have forbidden even where there are no servants, for wherever we are concerned about appearances, it is forbidden even in the innermost chambers. But necessarily Rava holds not like R' Yehuda in the name of Rav, for there are several Amoraim in the first chapter of Beitza who do not hold like Rav; and if so, Rava too would hold so, and therefore he permits where servants are not common, or when he himself does it. But for us, who hold like Rav, and for the reason that the posekim wrote, and what we hold like the later authorities applies only from Rava onward, but beforehand the law follows the earlier ones, therefore in every case it is forbidden. And this is the Tur's intent: since in the Gemara he forbids where servants are common, therefore for us one must forbid in every case; this appears to be the Tur's view. And with this I have found a proper reason for those who practice not to eat knaidlach. And at first glance it lacks a reason; but according to what I have now written, it is proper to be concerned for the Tur's view that forbids, which has support in the Gemara and the posekim; study this carefully. And behold, in my responsa to the holy community of Pomorin I wrote that even though in a time of urgent need one may permit a type of kitniyos, as I wrote in a responsa previously, nevertheless the kokrizes called Turkish wheat: this is certainly forbidden to make flour from them and to make from them something like bread or a cooked dish for Pesach, for there is concern of confusion in this, since it appears like flour; and as is apparent from the Taz's language here, that all flour of kinds of kitniyos is forbidden, and one should permit only when it is in its natural state, such as beans and peas, and not when flour has been made from them; and all the more so since they are called in the vernacular "wheat," which is wheat in the Holy Tongue, for there is in this concern of confusion and appearances; and Heaven forbid to mention permitting this; study this carefully. And see in Mor U'Ketzi'ah and in the book Ikrei HaDat, who bring that the Chacham Tzvi also cried out against those who forbid kinds of kitniyos; and therefore at least one should be lenient in a time of urgent need; study this carefully.
