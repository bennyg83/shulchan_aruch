From oil and salt—see Mishna Berurah that even our salt, etc. It is evident per Ran whom the later authorities cited that in every manner it is permitted; so too Gra. Now I found in Maharitva's novellae on Pesachim, necessary for our topic: there vitika is permitted and we establish it with oil and salt, and because fruit juice does not leaven—one says this is with salt quarried from the mountain; but salt made from water is like water and forbidden. Another says even quarried salt dissolves to water, but here since salt is minimal for flavoring they were not concerned. Others explained in every salt case; here when flour and oil were cooked first then salt was added it is like baked and does not come to leaven—this seems correct to Maharitva—end. What Ran wrote is the second view; we can say others agree that with little salt it is permitted in every case like Ran, only they did not want to establish specifically with little salt. One need not challenge Ran why the Gemara needed to say with water and salt—say both with oil and salt; rather one case is much salt therefore forbidden. One can also say for Maharitva why not establish with oil and salt together—perhaps the custom is to add salt at the end. For practical law it appears good l'chatchila to follow Maharitva—to add salt at the end after the food is cooked:
