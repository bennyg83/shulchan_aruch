In siman 462, seif 3, the word “honey” should read “wine,” and in seif 6, even the smallest quantity of water mixed with honey is forbidden. The distinction drawn there between a mixture made before Pesach and one made during Pesach requires some study. See Magen Avraham, siman 462, seif katan 6, and the gloss there. Apparently, the intent is to distinguish between water that became nullified a long time before kneading and was transformed into fruit juice, and water mixed into fruit juice in even the smallest quantity immediately before kneading. See Responsa Tzemach Tzedek, siman 122, Peri Chadash, and Taz, seif katan 15, concerning this. Magen Avraham there, however, distinguishes between grape juice that subsequently fermented and other cases, and Peri Chadash writes likewise. In practice, one should not permit eating unless the water was a small quantity, was nullified in sixty, and the mixture later fermented, as in siman 462, seif 3. It is possible that even without a long interval this is permitted in such a case. But where no change occurred, one should not be lenient even with a small quantity of water and a long interval, except perhaps in a case of very great loss. Mead, in which water is the principal ingredient, should be forbidden for drinking. As to retaining it or deriving benefit from it, one should not rebuke a person who is lenient in a case of great loss, as Magen Avraham explains at length in siman 462, seif katan 6.
