[13] The fallen (novelet) and they, etc. So Rashi explained in the Talmud Berakhot 40, and Rambam, R' Yitzchak, and Bertenora explained that they are fruits that fell from the trees unripe before they were cooked; and it appears both are true; and so is implied in Kolbo 98, who wrote: fruits that were burned and fell before their cooking — one blesses shehakol — end of his language, meaning either they were burned or they fell — easy to understand. And behold in Demai chapter 1 Rambam explained on fallen dates of the palm: those that fall from the palm when the wind blows, and it is also mostly hefker — end of his words. And his explanation appears to me even if it falls after they were cooked — and as he concludes the reason "mostly hefker" — meaning he explained here like the one who says "dates of wind"; but in Berakhot where the tanna taught fallen fruits anonymously, the explanation is like the one who says "cooked like myrtle," as the Talmud concludes. And I saw in Taz at the beginning of siman 204 that he wrote: since the Talmud in the latter language says "cooked" — for the one who says dates of wind, this is, etc., and for the one who says cooked like myrtle it is difficult — therefore Tur and Rabbeinu Yerucham ruled like the one who says dates of wind, see there. And I did not descend to the end of his view, for one who examines the Talmud will see this refers to fallen dates mentioned in Demai; but regarding fallen fruits taught anonymously by the tanna in Berakhot it concludes it is cooked like myrtle; and so I found explicitly in Ba'al Halachot Gedolot — this is his language: fallen fruits that the tanna taught here — cooked like myrtle. And behold what I explained in Rambam, that he explained dates of wind after they were cooked, appears proved in the Talmud: for if the explanation of dates of wind is before they were cooked, what does it challenge for the one who says dates of wind — what species of curse is this, behold this is a species of curse that fell before ripening; also the Talmud challenges "Borei peri haEtz" — he needs to bless, implying it deals after they were cooked. And Bertenora wrote in Demai: fallen dates — dates that do not cook on the tree, and one picks them and places one on another so they cook; and some say fallen dates of the palm that the wind cast before their cooking — end of his words. Behold the first explanation is taken from Rosh, who explained thus the one who says cooked like myrtle; and if so, for blessing too one blesses shehakol, as explained in the Talmud there; and per this explanation Bertenora explained in Berakhot and the first explanation he explained in Demai are all one: everything not cooked is a sign of curse, and the same for unripe that fell from trees that one cooks to ripen — and so is implied at the beginning of the first chapter of Orlah; and therefore Bach and Perishah did not mention Rosh's explanation in the law of blessing shehakol — understand; and so implies Lechem Mishnah in the chapter "How does one bless." And all this is to exclude from Sefer Be'er Avraham, who did not delve into the depth of the matters — one who wishes may study it, and I did not wish to extend. However one can wonder on Bertenora: behold the latter explanation, perforce like the one who says dates of wind; for if not, why did he also bring the explanation of dates of wind; and so is implied in Rosh — and if so one can wonder: behold it deals even if they fell after they were cooked, as I clarified above from the Talmud; and it is close to me that he followed Rambam's explanation afterward, and he holds Rambam's words in Berakhot and Demai are one. And it is not so, but in Berakhot he explained per the one who says cooked like myrtle, and in Demai per the one who says dates of wind — meaning the wind dried them after they were cooked.
