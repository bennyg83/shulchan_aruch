(note 6) Some say, etc.: and s.v. etc., that Rama's gloss is the view of Tur. And he derives it from what we learned in Shevi'it and is brought in Berakhot daf 36b: From when may one not prune trees in the shemittah year? (Rashi explains: the Merciful One said regarding shevi'it "for food" and not for loss, and since it already has the name of fruit it is forbidden to prune the tree, for one destroys it.) Beit Hillel say: carobs from when they take root, and vines from when they are grafted (and it says there that this means the size of a white bean), and all trees from when they produce. And Tur learned: and this is Rama's view in his gloss: that the law of berakha is from shevi'it, and at that time when it is called fruit for shevi'it it is called fruit for berakha. Therefore he disagrees with the Mechaber, who wrote anonymously "and all other trees from when they produce," etc., but he singled out carobs and olives from the general rule. And therefore Rabbeinu Yerucham in Berakhot wrote that one cannot learn, etc., for behold carobs even from when they take root are bitter and not fit for eating; therefore one blesses upon them borei peri haEtz, etc., as above. And so is printed in Sefer Netiv Chayim (and know that from "therefore," etc., onward is not in Beit Hillel's book but is the language of Magen Avraham to explain Beit Hillel's words). And his intent is: how is it possible to give a measure for berakha like in shevi'it: namely carobs from when they take root and likewise olives from when they bud? For certainly whenever the fruit is bitter one does not bless upon it, as the Mechaber wrote, provided it is not bitter, etc.; and carobs, for the most part, even after they take root are bitter; nevertheless for berakha it does not depend on taking root or budding but only on whether it is not bitter. And "all trees": their time is from when they produce, meaning when they are not bitter, as Rabbeinu Yerucham wrote; and if carobs are found before they take root that are not bitter, one blesses upon them borei peri haEtz. And he wrote that so appears to be the view of Rif, etc., that they summarized their words that other trees are from when they produce and did not exclude carobs and olives from the general rule; it must be that which they gave a measure for boser as a white bean is not derived from shevi'it but from orlah.
