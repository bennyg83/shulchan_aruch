6) There: Borei Pri HaEtz. A fruit grows in great abundance in Eretz HaKodesh, so much so that people use it as fencing around gardens and orchards. It is called the fig of Adam, and in Arabic sabras. The fruit's peel and the leaves are covered with thorns. It is planted by placing one leaf in the earth, which becomes covered with leaves on both sides. When the fruit is taken, leaves and fruit grow there the following year. One recites Borei Pri HaEtz and Shehecheyanu over it. According to the Geonim, whenever fruit emerges from branches it is a tree; according to Rabbeinu Yonah, everything depends upon whether it must be sown each year. Pri HaAdamah, volume 1, folios 24a and 25a. Kiryas Melech Rav, volume 1, siman 10, agrees; see there. Birkei Yosef, paragraph 1; Ikarei HaDinim, siman 10, paragraph 71; Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 1, cite this. If it is known that it has not completed the years of orlah, it is forbidden. Darkei Moshe, Hagahos HaTur, paragraph 1. One likewise recites Borei Pri HaEtz over the pine nuts of Chevron and Borei Nefashos afterward. Pri HaAdamah there; Chesed LaAlafim there.
