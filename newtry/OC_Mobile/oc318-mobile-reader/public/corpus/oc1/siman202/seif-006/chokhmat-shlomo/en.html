There, seif 6. The caper bush, etc. Note: A Torah scholar asked me about the words of Rabbi Ovadiah of Bartenura in Orlah, chapter 1, mishnah 9. He writes there that Rabbi Yose follows his own reasoning, for he says that the blossoming fruit is prohibited because it is considered fruit, etc., and that the halachah does not follow him, since he also prohibits the immature dates. The question was that this appears to contradict the Gemara in Berachos 36b, which states explicitly that regarding other trees the Sages agree with Rabbi Yose. At first glance this is indeed difficult. Rash also cites the Yerushalmi, which accords with Rabbi Ovadiah of Bartenura, and it is surprising that he does not cite our Gemara at all. I answered that, in my humble opinion, this may be explained according to the continuation of the Gemara in Berachos: “The halachah follows Mar bar Rav Ashi, who discarded the caper berries and ate the husks; since with respect to orlah they are not fruit, with respect to the blessing they are likewise not fruit.” Rosh does not have this reading and therefore rules that they are fruit, but Rif does have this text; see the commentators in the name of Maharshal. According to this, Rash and Rabbi Ovadiah of Bartenura had this reading in the Gemara and understood that the conclusion rejects the earlier statement that the Sages agree concerning other fruits. Rather, they hold as the Gemara initially stated, that even regarding other trees the immature growth is not considered fruit. As for the earlier challenge from the mishnah concerning Shemittah, one may answer that Shemittah is not comparable to orlah. Rabbi Yose agrees that for Shemittah the blossom is not fruit, because Shemittah is more lenient, while for the more stringent prohibition of orlah even the blossom is considered fruit. This distinction concerns the blossom. Regarding other trees, however, Shemittah and orlah are equal, and therefore the mishnah says, “from when they produce.” According to the Sages, if it is not fruit for orlah, it is likewise not fruit for Shemittah, whether the blossom or the immature growth of other trees. The blessing follows the law of orlah. Since we rule according to the Gemara’s conclusion, which follows Mar bar Rav Ashi, the Sages hold that in all trees such growth is not fruit. Rabbi Ovadiah of Bartenura therefore correctly writes that Rabbi Yose follows his own reasoning and that the halachah does not follow him. He rules in accordance with the Yerushalmi and also in accordance with the conclusion of our Gemara. This appears to me to resolve their position; examine it carefully.
