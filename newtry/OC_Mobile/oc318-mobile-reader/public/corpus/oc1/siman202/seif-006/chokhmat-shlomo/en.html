There seif 6 — caper, etc. N.B. I was asked by a certain sage about the words of Ra'avad in tractate Orlah chapter 1 mishnah 9, where he wrote that the anonymous view there — R' Yosi according to his reasoning, who said the samadar is forbidden because it is fruit, etc., and the halacha is not like him, for he forbids in capers — end of his words. And I was asked that this contradicts the Gemara in chapter 6 of Berakhot, where we say explicitly 36b regarding other trees that the rabbis agree with R' Yosi, see there. And ostensibly it is truly wondrous. And behold Rosh also brought the Yerushalmi, which is like Ra'avad's view — and it is wondrous that he did not bring at all our Talmud. And I answered that it appears in my humble opinion: per what we read there in Berakhot afterward, "and the halacha is like Mar bar Rav Ashi, who threw the capers and ate the peels" — and from the aspect of orlah it is not fruit, and for blessing too it is not fruit. And behold Rosh does not read it, and rules that something is fruit; but Rif reads it properly — and see in the explicit [commentators] in the name of Maharshal. And therefore it appears that Rosh and Ra'avad read in the Talmud and hold not like what it says initially that the rabbis agree on other fruits, but hold as it says initially that also on other trees it is not fruit; and what it challenged above from the mishnah of Shevi'it — one can say shevi'it is not comparable to orlah, and R' Yosi admits that for shevi'it the samadar is not fruit, for shevi'it is lenient; but orlah, which is strict, the samadar is also fruit. And this is regarding samadar; but on other trees shevi'it and orlah are equal — therefore it says "from when they bring forth"; but per the rabbis, if regarding orlah it is not fruit, also for shevi'it it is not fruit — neither samadar nor other trees — and blessing is similar to orlah; and since we hold like the conclusion of the Talmud that says "and the halacha is like Mar bar Rav Ashi," he holds that per the rabbis on all trees it is not fruit. Therefore it is well that he wrote "R' Yosi according to his reasoning," and the halacha is not like him; and he rules like the Yerushalmi and like the conclusion of the Talmud as well — and it comes out well thus, it appears in my humble opinion, to settle their views — understand well:
