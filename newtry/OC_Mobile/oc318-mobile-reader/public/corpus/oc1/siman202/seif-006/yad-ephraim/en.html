Seif katan 17. Borei Peri HaAdamah, because it is not the principal fruit. This is the view of the Rif and Rambam. Tosafos and the Rosh disagree, for no proof may be brought from orlah outside Eretz Yisrael, since in that area one follows every lenient opinion. They therefore rule that one should recite Borei Peri HaEtz. Mordechai likewise rules this way; see there, and see also the Aruch, entry tzelaf. The explanation that the caper shell is merely a guardian of the fruit requires study. What relevance does being a guardian have to the law of blessings, as Taz and Pnei Yehoshua write regarding orange peel? Shaagas Aryeh, siman 23, explains the Rif's reasoning: if one eats caper shells outside Eretz Yisrael and recites Borei Peri HaEtz, he adopts two contradictory positions. See there. Maadanei Melech already raised this point. In the aforementioned kuntres, however, I wrote that there is no proof from this, for the Rosh at the beginning of Berachos writes regarding prayer that we sometimes follow contradictory leniencies, since the Sages were lenient in prayer. The same should apply here, where the rule is to follow every lenient opinion. I brought proof from the Gemara's question, “Let the Master act in accordance with Beis Shammai,” and from the discussion on 40a concerning species of thornbushes, as well as from the law of hops, which are not considered kilayim yet require Borei Peri HaAdamah. I also discussed at length there, and in a responsum, the law of palm shoots.
