(1) Borei: and not haborei [Magen Avraham]:
(2) Borei Peri HaEtz: and although their growth is from the ground, they did not wish to exempt them with Borei Peri HaAdamah like other fruits of the land, since they are most important, and they established a special blessing to mention praise of the Omnipresent Who created such important fruits:
(3) That it is made into condiment, etc.: Chayei Adam wrote similarly regarding what they call [vermit wine] which is mar: also one should bless upon it Borei Peri HaGefen; but in Pri Megadim Orach Chaim letter 2 he is doubtful in this, and wrote because the palate does not enjoy it except for healing, perhaps that even though Mishnah Berurah it is not fit to bless upon it:
(4) If the majority is wine: seemingly if it deals with diluted wine and raisin wine of ours that are ordinarily diluted with much water, even if beer was the minority the power of the wine was nullified by the mixture per what we hold below in siman 204 seif 5 in the Rama gloss: see there; and perhaps it deals with when the minority beer also does not rise to six parts against the wine, and in this manner there is still the name of wine upon it, as below in siman 204 seif 5 cited: see there; or it deals with undiluted wine, and so is implied in Biur HaGra:
(5) And if the majority is beer: see in Mishnah Berurah; and see in Pri Megadim that he challenged from these words of Rama upon what Magen Avraham wrote in siman 204 note 96 that wine in beverages: even per the words of Taz: in any case requires six against it to be nullified, and per his view even six do not help: see there; and here we say that by majority it is nullified [and what he wished to answer that here specifically when it mixed by itself and not with intent to dilute: it does not appear so in Tashbetz siman 87 from which is the source of this law]; and I saw in the words of Gra Zal in siman 204 and likewise in Chayei Adam general 55 note 3 that they omitted entirely the words of Magen Avraham there and brought to law as Rama wrote in name of Tashbetz in this siman. However, in my humble opinion it appears there is a distinction here, for not all beverages are equal for blessing: if the way of the world is to mix those beverages in wine to improve its taste, then even if they are majority against the wine [provided they are not six against it] they also do not nullify the wine, and even if the taste of the wine changed thereby from what it was before: and like that case of condiment explained in Yerushalmi that is one-third honey and one-third spices: behold the wine is minority relative to the spices and honey, and nevertheless they bless Borei Peri HaGefen; and the reason as Rashba wrote in responsum and Tashbetz brought: behold its importance is in it, and it is not applicable here to say the wine is ancillary, for on the contrary they are ancillary relative to the wine to improve its taste; and the words of Rama deal with beer and the like that the way of the world is not to mix them in wine in order to improve its taste, for although it appears beer strengthens the power of the wine, nevertheless the way of the world is not to mix for this in order to improve it: therefore it is not ancillary relative to the wine, and thus the matter depends on majority:
(6) One blesses Shehakol: see in Mishnah Berurah that Gra disagrees with this ruling and his view is one blesses Borei Peri HaGefen, for mixing wine in beer equals mixing wine in water, and we do not follow majority here; however, several latter authorities brought Rama's words as halakhic ruling [and Gra's difficulty from what they said "all wine that is not aged," etc.: Pri Megadim and Shulchan Aruch Harav resolve that specifically in water they said it is the way of dilution, and therefore it was not nullified, and not in other beverages; and what he further wrote that the mishnah does not deal at all with mixture, etc.: Magen Giborim answered that for the law we hold that even in mixture we say the primary exempts the ancillary, as below siman 212 seif 1; and likewise he challenged his words in Sefer Damasek Eliezer: see there what he wrote in his answer. And the essential point is that Gra holds that regarding wine, even if beer is majority we do not consider it primary, for he compares it to water in which wine is diluted]: and therefore for the first blessing certainly it is correct initially to bless Shehakol [and if he blessed Borei Peri HaGefen he fulfilled]; and for the concluding blessing it appears he should take another revi'it of wine and exempt this too with the concluding blessing; however, per what is explained in siman 204 in the latter authorities there regarding mixing wine in water, in any case it depends on the strength of the wine, and even if the water is less than the measure explained there to nullify the wine, nevertheless one does not bless Borei Peri HaGefen unless there is in it taste of wine and the way of the world is to drink this wine in such a mixture: it appears the same applies in mixture with beer if it is weak wine and the way of the world is not to dilute even with majority water; also in mixture with beer one blesses Shehakol and afterward Borei Nefashos, even per the view of Gra, for from Gra it appears beer and water share one law, and all the more if the taste of wine was lost one blesses Shehakol for all opinions, as written in Mishnah Berurah:
