(note 1) Borei and not HaBorei — for all berakhot one may say in past tense, for Heaven already created; and Borei for everyone is past tense, while HaBorei — Rabbi Nachman and the Rabbis dispute on daf 38: Rabbi Nachman holds it is future tense; and the Rabbis hold that even HaBorei is past tense, and we rule like the Rabbis; nevertheless it is better to say Borei, which is past tense for everyone, and one should not use the yud of the dispute. And that which we say "hamotzi lechem" the taste, as in Yerushalmi — so as not to mix the letters of two mems of olam at the end and mem of hamotzi — one mem is swallowed by the other in articulation. And that which we say from a person's berakhot it is recognizable if he is a Torah scholar and fit to show innovation in his berakhot — therefore more so one should say HaBorei to teach that the halachah is like the Rabbis, as Levush wrote; but if he shows this innovation in the berakha on hamotzi on Shabbat — see Taz siman 167.
