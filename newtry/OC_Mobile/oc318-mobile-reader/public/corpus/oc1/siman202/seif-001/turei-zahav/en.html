And if the majority is beer, etc. It implies that we do not follow after taste but after the majority; and one can ask from what is written regarding challah, for it is said in Yoreh De'ah siman 324: one who makes a dough from wheat and rice — if there is taste of grain it is obligated in challah even though the majority is rice; and likewise one should bless on what one enjoys from the taste of wine — so what does the majority of beer help? And one can answer that grain is one of the five species that have extra significance, as Rosh wrote — we do not follow after the majority in them, for if so taste would be unnecessary; but regarding bread, nevertheless Rosh wrote that even if one mixed with them much honey, etc. — it is found that we do not follow after taste; but it appears to me that there there is a type of obligation and a type of exemption, and the exempt does not have power to exempt the obligated — not so here, where there is an obligation of blessing for beer and an obligation of blessing for wine; therefore we follow after the primary, as we hold: whenever there is a primary and with it something secondary, one blesses on the primary and exempts the secondary; and for this reason in every dish into which they put fat, even much — nevertheless one does not bless on the fat even though it gave much taste:
