[1] We read: "One who wishes to be pious should fulfill the matters of blessings." And ostensibly it is wondrous, for it implies one who did not fulfill them is called pious — but he is not called wicked either, for it is written "a companion to a destructive man." Rather he wanted to say that one should not rely on the general rule he stated about shehakol, but should learn the matters of blessings to bless on every kind of blessing that applies to it. And he wrote in the writings of the Ari z"l: one who exempts himself with the blessing shehakol — behold this is in the category of "a pit that its breakers cursed it," end of his words. Sefer Chassidim siman 852: a person who has one nut in his hand should not bless and afterward break the nut, lest the pit wormed or rotted and it be found he blessed in vain — end of his words. And he wrote in Shelah page 93: specifically a nut, for if there was a worm in it it is not fit for anything and is in vain, unlike other fruits that even if they spoiled and wormed inside he did not bless in vain on account of the outer shell upon which the blessing fell as well; therefore it is better to bless on the fruit when it is whole, for all matters of blessings — whole is better (Yaakov Levi). And I will add that breaking a nut is an interruption between blessing and eating, similar to cutting bread in Hamotzi — end of his words. And per this, even if he has many nuts before him he must break before the blessing; but per Sefer Chassidim's reason — lest it worm — if so with many nuts he need not break them, for we are not concerned they all worm; therefore Sefer Chassidim was precise and wrote that he has one nut, etc., implying that with many he blesses and afterward breaks. And one can distinguish between the law of Hamotzi, where he truly cuts nothing; and in truth the reason is because he cuts a little in the bread the cut is not recognizable — and if it were recognizable that he cut he would not be permitted to cut even on Shabbat he does not cut at all, because it is not a complete interruption, as explained in siman 167; and it is better to interrupt with this and bless on the whole. However in blanched flour he wrote: one who wishes to peel the fruit should peel and afterward bless, so as not to interrupt between blessing and eating — end of his words — and one can distinguish. Further he wrote that a person should not bless on food or drink that is very hot or very cold, for there is concern for interruption; and therefore the pious and men of deed fill the cup for Kiddush before netilat yadayim — end of his words. Further he wrote: every person should beware to clarify the fruit clean and beautiful, that it not be wormy, to bless on it — because he must remove the pit so as not to come to doubt in the concluding blessing because it is a beriah — end of his words. And it is not clear why he should not bless on it when it is whole — behold he can afterward take the pit from it and not eat it; however if he wished to eat it with the pit he should break it beforehand, as will be explained in siman 210. Bach wrote at end of siman 206: one who blessed on food and after he blessed the food became repulsive in his eyes — he should eat some of it even though it rotted so that the blessing not be in vain; and likewise on a nut he does not bless until he sees the pit inside — end of his words. Matateh Moshe wrote: one thirsty to drink water should not bless and afterward pour, but pour and afterward bless; and Kenesset HaGedolah wrote: see the reason because of interruption — end of his words. And it appears more to me the reason is because of disgrace to the blessing; and see below the reason for pouring Havdalah. Further Matateh Moshe wrote in the name of Sefer Chassidim: if he blessed on water and heard there is a dead person in the city, he should pour a little of the water and drink the rest — end of his language; but Kenesset HaGedolah siman 204 copied Sefer Chassidim's words in this language: drink a little of the water and pour the rest — end of his words, and he did not mention Matateh Moshe's words; and so I saw in Sefer Chassidim itself siman 854: drink first and afterward pour. And Kenesset HaGedolah wrote: also some say in pouring water that the angel of death casts into the water a drop of poison of death — nevertheless drink a little, and a guard of a mitzvah knows no evil; and since he drinks a little, where there is no blessing in vain, pour the rest:
