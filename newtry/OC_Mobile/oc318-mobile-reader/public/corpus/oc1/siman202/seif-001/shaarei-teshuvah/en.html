With borei peri haEtz — Baer Heitev; and see in Ateret Zekenim that he wrote another reason — because the breaking is an interruption, similar to cutting bread in the blessing of HaMotzi — that if one wants to peel the fruit, he should peel first and afterward bless; and therefore if there are many nuts that one need not worry they have all become wormy, one need not break first; but one should distinguish between that of cutting bread and peeling fruit — for one can say breaking is not so much an interruption — see there. And for my humble view one can say that even if you say it is not so much an interruption, nevertheless one should not do so l'chatchila; for also the reason to bless first on the whole is not present here, since the peel is not seen at all after the fruit — we follow, and the blessing is on the kernel inside that is fit for eating, and behold it is in its wholeness. He further wrote, in the name of Shelah that one should not bless on food or drink that is very hot or very cold, lest there be concern for interruption — see there. And it appears that if one knows the dish lacks salt or spices and is not flavored without them, he should put into it before the blessing so that he bless on something seasoned and prepared for the meal properly — unless he needs to taste to know how much salt and spices to put in; then he should bless first; and even though tasting does not require a blessing, nevertheless he should not do so lest he swallow a bit — see siman 210; therefore one should bless first. And see Bach siman 206 — one who blessed on a food item and after he blessed it became repulsive in his eyes — he should eat a bit even though it spoiled, so the blessing not be in vain — see there. And it appears that if it became repulsive in his eyes and his soul is weary of eating it, he should not eat — for one who eats things his soul is weary of violates "you shall not make yourselves detestable," as explained in Yoreh De'ah siman 110; and in Taz there it implies it is a d'oraisa prohibition; and see in the words of R' Alexander Ziskind in Smag siman 13 that he wrote two views on this — see there; and Bach deals with one who is not so repulsive in his eyes, only that he does not wish to eat it because of slight rot — somewhat repulsive even in what remains fit for food; therefore he should force himself to eat so his blessing not be in vain. And he wrote in Ateret Zekenim in the name of Mateh Moshe — one who wants to drink water that he pours out a bit — he should pour first and afterward bless; and Kenesset HaGedolah wrote because of interruption; and it is more plausible because of disrespect to the blessing — see in the laws of Shabbat regarding pouring the Havdalah cup; and see OC end of siman 206 in Smak — if one heard that someone died in the city or that the period falls and he blessed on water — see there.
