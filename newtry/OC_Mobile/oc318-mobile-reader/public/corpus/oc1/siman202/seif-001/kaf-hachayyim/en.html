1) [Seif 1] Over all tree fruits, one recites, etc. R' Chaim Vital, of blessed memory, writes in Shaar Ruach HaKodesh, folio 9b: My teacher, of blessed memory, told me that a person's principal attainment of Ruach HaKodesh depends upon his intent and care with every blessing over enjoyment. Through these blessings, the power of the husks attached to physical foods, which cling through them to the person who eats the food, is nullified. Through reciting the blessings over them with intent, he removes those husks from the foods, refines his own physical substance, and becomes pure and fit to receive holiness. He strongly warned us about this. Shaar HaMitzvos, parashas Eikev, writes the same; see there. See further below, paragraph 24.
2) There: Over all tree fruits, one recites, etc. One should take care to recite the blessing over a clean, beautiful fruit that is not worm-infested, because of the verse, 'This is my God, and I will beautify Him.' Or Chadash; Beis Menuchah, paragraph 7; Yafeh LaLev, paragraph 2.
3) One who has a fruit before him and recited Borei Pri HaEtz over it, with no other fruit before him, and afterward a more beautiful fruit arrived, should eat only the fruit over which he recited the blessing. Sefer Chassidim, siman 847; Yafeh LaLev there. Before the blessing one should intend that through this blessing the power of the husks attached to those fruits is nullified, as stated above in paragraph 1.
4) A person holding one nut should not recite the blessing and then break the nut, lest its kernel be worm-infested or rotten, causing his blessing to have been in vain. Sefer Chassidim, siman 849. Knesses HaGedolah cites this in Hagahos HaTur and writes that the same applies to every fruit about which there is reason to fear that it is unfit to eat. If, however, it is beautiful and clean and there is no concern that it is unfit, it is a mitzvah to recite the blessing while it is whole. One need not be concerned about the delay while cutting it between the blessing and eating, as stated below in siman 206, paragraph 21; see there. A fruit that requires peeling should be peeled before the blessing to avoid an interruption. Even after the peel is removed, if the fruit itself remains unbroken, it is considered whole. Therefore, when peeling a fruit, one should take care not to break it, so that the blessing is recited while it is whole. Likewise, when opening a fruit to inspect it for worms, one should take care to close it again if possible, so that it appears whole at the time of the blessing. See below, siman 206, seif 3, and our words there, with the help of Heaven.
5) There: One recites, etc. Every blessing must have a past-tense meaning, such as, 'everything came into being through His word,' rather than, 'everything will come into being through His word.' Likewise, 'Who creates the fruit' means, 'Who created,' like, 'Who forms mountains and creates wind.' Similarly, HaMotzi, 'Who brings forth bread from the earth,' means, 'Who brought forth.' Riaz, cited by Shiltei Gibborim at the end of the chapter Keitzad Mevarchin; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur. See above, siman 167, paragraph 22.
6) There: Borei Pri HaEtz. A fruit grows in great abundance in Eretz HaKodesh, so much so that people use it as fencing around gardens and orchards. It is called the fig of Adam, and in Arabic sabras. The fruit's peel and the leaves are covered with thorns. It is planted by placing one leaf in the earth, which becomes covered with leaves on both sides. When the fruit is taken, leaves and fruit grow there the following year. One recites Borei Pri HaEtz and Shehecheyanu over it. According to the Geonim, whenever fruit emerges from branches it is a tree; according to Rabbeinu Yonah, everything depends upon whether it must be sown each year. Pri HaAdamah, volume 1, folios 24a and 25a. Kiryas Melech Rav, volume 1, siman 10, agrees; see there. Birkei Yosef, paragraph 1; Ikarei HaDinim, siman 10, paragraph 71; Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 1, cite this. If it is known that it has not completed the years of orlah, it is forbidden. Darkei Moshe, Hagahos HaTur, paragraph 1. One likewise recites Borei Pri HaEtz over the pine nuts of Chevron and Borei Nefashos afterward. Pri HaAdamah there; Chesed LaAlafim there.
7) There: Borei Pri HaEtz. If instead of 'Who creates the fruit' he said, 'Who forms the fruit,' or instead of, 'fruit of the ground,' he said, 'fruit of the earth,' although he was not permitted to alter the text, he fulfilled his obligation. The rule that one who changes the formulation established by the Sages for blessings does not fulfill his obligation does not apply here. Petach HaDevir; see there. Sedei Chemed, Asifas Dinim, section Berachos, siman 1, paragraph 35, cites this; Orach Chaim, paragraph 2.
8) If he said, 'Borei Pri HaEtz, Blessed are You, Hashem, our God, King of the universe,' or said, 'Pri HaEtz, Blessed are You, Hashem, our God, King of the universe, Who created it,' Beis Yaakov writes in handwritten novellae that he fulfilled his obligation. Petach HaDevir, paragraph 2, cites this and comments that if he said, 'Borei Pri HaEtz, Blessed are You, Hashem, our God, King of the universe,' it is obvious that he did not fulfill his obligation, because the words are unintelligible and the blessing's beginning cannot be connected to its end. Besides failing to fulfill his obligation with such a broken phrase, he nearly pronounces the Name of Heaven in vain. If, however, he said, 'Pri HaEtz, Blessed are You, Hashem, our God, King of the universe, Who created it,' the blessing makes clear that he blesses the Holy One, blessed is He, and the primary conclusion is that he fulfilled his obligation; see there. Sedei Chemed there and Orach Chaim there cite this.
9) If one recited Borei Minei Mezonos over tree fruit or ground produce, although all foods are called nourishment, since 'nourishment' is a general term encompassing everything, this is ineffective for the purpose of a blessing, except for dates and wine. If one recited Borei Minei Mezonos over them, he fulfilled his obligation. Beis Yaakov writes this in a manuscript, cited by Petach HaDevir, paragraph 12; Sedei Chemed there, paragraph 36; Orach Chaim there. Chayei Adam, rule 58, paragraph 3, however, writes that if one recited Borei Minei Mezonos over any item, he fulfilled his obligation, except for water and salt; see there. Ben Ish Chai, parashas Balak, paragraph 13, writes the same. See above, siman 167, paragraph 75. This will be explained further below in siman 206, with the help of Heaven.
10) There: Except for wine, etc. Regarding the reason, see our words above in siman 167, paragraph 25.
11) There: Borei Pri HaGafen. One does not say, 'HaBorei.' Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur; Magen Avraham, paragraph 1. We wrote the same above in paragraph 5; see there. If one recited Borei Pri HaEtz over wine, Magen Avraham, siman 208, paragraph 22, writes that he did not fulfill his obligation; see there. Rema MiPano, however, writes in Alfasi Zuta on the chapter Keitzad Mevarchin that if one recited Borei Pri HaEtz or Borei Pri HaAdamah over wine, after the fact he fulfilled his obligation; see there. We write the same below in siman 208, paragraph 77, in the name of several authorities; see there.
12) There: Whether raw or cooked, etc. The Tur writes this, and Beis Yosef states that Tosafos and Rosh write the same in the chapter HaMocher Peiros, contrary to Rashi and Rashbam, who write that one recites Shehakol over cooked wine. Ran in the chapter Arvei Pesachim agrees with Tosafos and Rosh, and Semak likewise concurs. Maggid Mishneh in chapter 29 writes that Ramban and Rashba hold the same view. Mordechai in the chapter Keitzad Mevarchin, Hagahos Maimoniyos in chapter 8 of Hilchos Berachos, Terumas HaDeshen, siman 30, Responsa attributed to Ramban, siman 190, and Shibolei HaLeket likewise conclude this way, since it is explicit in the Yerushalmi and we do not find that the Bavli disagrees. All this is explained in Beis Yosef; see there. Bach rules accordingly and writes that this is the practice. Machazik Berachah, paragraph 1, agrees and challenges Mor U'Ketziah, who rules to recite Shehakol in accordance with Rashi; see there. Erekh HaShulchan, paragraph 1, rules likewise, contrary to Panim Meiros, volume 3, siman 21; see there. Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, siman 204, paragraph 6; Chayei Adam, rule 55, paragraph 1; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, siman 49, paragraph 1. Although we wrote above in siman 17, paragraph 7, that in cases of doubt concerning blessings we rule leniently even against the majority and even against Maran, of blessed memory, that applies when the dispute is whether a blessing is required at all, in which case one does not recite it because of doubt. If all agree that a blessing is required and dispute only which text to recite, we follow the majority like other laws. This is especially true here, since the item is truly fruit of the vine and the blessing is not false. Therefore, one should follow the majority and the consensus of the later authorities.
13) There: Whether cooked. Although cooked wine cannot become prohibited as wine handled by a non-Jew, there is no distinction regarding its blessing. Magen Avraham, paragraph 2.
14) There: Whether cooked. Over cooked wine that thickened and became a food, one recites Borei Pri HaGafen. Beis Yehudah, Orach Chaim section, siman 61; Yad Aharon, second edition, Hagahos HaTur, paragraph 1.
15) Regarding distilled wine called aguardiente, Mor U'Ketziah writes that it is superior to cooked wine, one recites Borei Pri HaGafen over it, and contact by a non-Jew prohibits it; see there. Machazik Berachah, paragraph 2, however, challenges him and writes that Tashbetz, Responsa, volume 3, siman 291, and Radbaz, early responsa, siman 299, explicitly permit distilled wine of our type after contact by a non-Jew. Regarding its blessing, it is extremely clear that one recites Shehakol, since it has changed entirely and has a new appearance, aroma, and taste. He writes that this is the universal practice; see there.
16) Over arrope, one recites Shehakol, and this is obvious. Beis Yosef in the name of Orchos Chaim. Arrope is made by pressing grapes and, after pressing, placing a certain white earth into the grape juice for an hour or longer, so that the wine emerging from them clarifies quickly and retains its sweetness. It is then heated until yad soledes bo. Sherbet is made similarly. My view agrees that one should recite Shehakol over it, and I believe this is the practice in Constantinople. Knesses HaGedolah, Responsa, volume 2, siman 1; Yad Aharon there, Hagahos Beis Yosef, paragraph 1. Birkei Yosef, paragraph 2, likewise writes in the name of R' Avraham Azulai that over arrope, which is congealed grape juice, one recites Shehakol. The same applies to cooked grapes called trankopit and to kamardin made from apricots; one recites Shehakol; see there. See below, siman 204, paragraph 63. Know that some people of deeds do not eat the kamardin sold in the market, because it is known that market producers prepare a large quantity together, including worm-infested fruit. Flying insects from the air or crawling insects from the ground may also form in it. They eat it only if it is soaked in water and strained.
17) Over fermenting wine, one recites Borei Pri HaGafen, for the Sages said that a person may squeeze a cluster of grapes and recite Kiddush over it, and Kiddush may be recited only over wine. Beis Yosef in the name of Rashba; Levush; Ateres Zekeinim. See siman 272, seif 2. Likewise, over wine that flows by itself, even without pressing, one recites Borei Pri HaGafen. Knesses HaGedolah, seif 8; Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1, writes the same.
18) There: Into which honey is placed, etc. Even if it consists of one-third honey, one-third spices, and one-third wine, the wine is nevertheless primary, since the other ingredients are added only to improve and flavor it. Tashbetz, volume 1, siman 86; Misgeres HaShulchan on Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, siman 49, paragraph 2; Mishnah Berurah, paragraph 6.
