12) There: Whether raw or cooked, etc. The Tur writes this, and Beis Yosef states that Tosafos and Rosh write the same in the chapter HaMocher Peiros, contrary to Rashi and Rashbam, who write that one recites Shehakol over cooked wine. Ran in the chapter Arvei Pesachim agrees with Tosafos and Rosh, and Semak likewise concurs. Maggid Mishneh in chapter 29 writes that Ramban and Rashba hold the same view. Mordechai in the chapter Keitzad Mevarchin, Hagahos Maimoniyos in chapter 8 of Hilchos Berachos, Terumas HaDeshen, siman 30, Responsa attributed to Ramban, siman 190, and Shibolei HaLeket likewise conclude this way, since it is explicit in the Yerushalmi and we do not find that the Bavli disagrees. All this is explained in Beis Yosef; see there. Bach rules accordingly and writes that this is the practice. Machazik Berachah, paragraph 1, agrees and challenges Mor U'Ketziah, who rules to recite Shehakol in accordance with Rashi; see there. Erekh HaShulchan, paragraph 1, rules likewise, contrary to Panim Meiros, volume 3, siman 21; see there. Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, siman 204, paragraph 6; Chayei Adam, rule 55, paragraph 1; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, siman 49, paragraph 1. Although we wrote above in siman 17, paragraph 7, that in cases of doubt concerning blessings we rule leniently even against the majority and even against Maran, of blessed memory, that applies when the dispute is whether a blessing is required at all, in which case one does not recite it because of doubt. If all agree that a blessing is required and dispute only which text to recite, we follow the majority like other laws. This is especially true here, since the item is truly fruit of the vine and the blessing is not false. Therefore, one should follow the majority and the consensus of the later authorities.
