(12) There — whether raw or cooked, etc. — so too Tur, and Beit Yosef wrote that so Tosafot and the Rosh in the chapter "One who sells fruits," and not like Rashi and the Rosh who wrote that one blesses upon it shehakol; and so is the view of the Ran in the chapter "Ayin P' — like the view of Tosafot and the Rosh; and so agreed Semak. And Rav HaMagid in Perek 29 wrote that such is the view of Ramban and Rashba. And so too Mordekhai in chapter 25 and Hagahot Maimoni in chapter 8 of the five [orders of] Berakhot. And Terumat HaDeshen siman 30 and in responsum to Ramban siman 199; and so he concluded in She'erit Yosef — since it is explicit in the Yerushalmi and we do not find in our Talmud that disagrees with it in this, as explained all this in Beit Yosef — see there. And so too Bach, and he wrote that such is the custom. And so agreed the Mechaber note 1, and he challenged the Magen Avraham who ruled to bless shehakol like the view of Rashi — see there. And so too Erekh HaShulchan note 1. And he wrote — and not like R' Panim Me'irot volume 3 siman 21 — see there. Maamar Mordekhai note 2. Razah siman 204 note 6. Chayei Adam general 55 note 1. Kitzur Shulchan Aruch siman 49 note 1. And it appears that although we wrote above siman 172 note 7 that we follow the individual against the majority and even against Maran z"l — if the dispute is whether one must bless or not, we say one does not bless out of doubt; but if for everyone one must bless, only the dispute is whether one blesses thus or thus — then we follow the majority of opinions like other laws, and especially in a case such as this where the truth is it is fruit of the vine and he does not lie in his blessing — one should follow the majority of opinions and the agreement of the latter authorities.
