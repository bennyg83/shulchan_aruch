And if there is no way, etc. — requires study from below siman 208 seif 4 "one who eats raw grain," etc., and this law emerges from there from what is in the Gemara 37 "one who chews wheat," etc.; and perhaps for this reason Rambam changed in chapter 3 of Berakhot halachah 2 and did not cite the braita mentioned as is his way everywhere, but rather wrote "one who eats grain that is cooked" — to inform us that specifically cooked, but not raw; and because it is not his way to eat raw — unlike the matter there. And perhaps one can also answer for Shulchan Aruch's ruling on types of grain: since it is fit for food it is considered more — and see in Magen Giborim what he wrote in the name of Shevut Yaakov on this:

To eat them alive, etc. / one eats them when they are alive, etc. — it deals with one who is fit to eat at least through pressing; for otherwise even shehakol one does not bless on it, as above at end of seif 2; and as is found below as well regarding dry peppers in Taz:

One blesses shehakol — and it is not comparable to above in seif 2, where it is clear from Shulchan Aruch that if they are fit to eat even only through pressing one blesses the blessing appropriate to that species; it appears to me because there it does not deal with fruit that in its maturity would be fit to eat raw for every person — therefore we say that even now in its smallness, from when it sprouted until it is fit to eat even only through pressing, it has the status of fruit; unlike here, where even in its maturity it is not fit to eat for most people when raw — upon it there is no status of fruit at all; and see in Levush what he answered on this — and in my humble opinion I wrote as above:
