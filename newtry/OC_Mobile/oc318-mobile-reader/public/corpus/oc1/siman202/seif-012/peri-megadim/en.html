Soft walnut — Taz. Magen Avraham emended "and they are." The lay author who writes on pharmacological matters said: I have a bit of knowledge and I am now enumerating them with God's help.

Here in these countries they compound a root called Tetirzelli kalmazin — they do not sow it, only it grows in water and has long [leaves] on it, and the root has a good smell, and they cut pieces and dry them and compound in honey or sugar — appears shehakol as written in Peri Megadim 61 and Taz and Magen Avraham 18 — wild herbs that are not sown are not significant and one blesses shehakol, and likewise this; and so too by honey it is significant — the blessing is obvious that honey is primary for this matter and shehakol for these are herbs and not fruit. And those fruits that they compound while still raw, like green plums in sugar and the like, small citrons — if very raw and impossible to eat them thus — its law is explained here in seif 62 Magen Avraham 5: very raw and fixed by fire — shehakol, for they were not planted for this and are not fruit at all (even though it is the majority as stated); and if they are somewhat fit for eating only that their fruit is not finished — explained in seif 62 borei peri haEtz; if so, regarding green plums somewhat fit for eating under duress one can say borei peri haEtz like other trees; and "Dear to soft almonds" Peri Megadim 61 Magen Avraham — only the shell is primary and therefore shehakol. And soft walnut here appears from Levush that it is not fit at all for eating without honey — therefore shehakol for it was not planted for this, as we said. And from both, or if one does not eat green plums and small citrons compounded in honey, or to bless borei peri haEtz — if they are not fit at all under duress, there is in them gentile cooking — see Yoreh De'ah 113 Taz 13; nuts with their shells — see there they brought Ateret Zekenim Rabbi Gr"a note 3, Magen Avraham Rosh Chodesh 4 — dried ginger is permitted; such a case is forbidden unless eaten under duress — then one should bless borei peri haEtz like other trees when they remove, etc., in seif 61.

And radish and tamcha that are called retiich and kriin if fried in honey — according to Taz in Rosh Chodesh note 3, a general rule applies as if there is a distinction between borei peri haEtz and borei peri haAdamah — whatever grows on a tree, even though it changed in cooking in water for the worse and one blesses shehakol even for borei peri haEtz; nevertheless when fixed by honey again borei peri haEtz; not so borei peri haAdamah when changed for the worse, and in honey is fixed shehakol; and myariyim and ribiin change for the better (in water) borei peri haAdamah, in honey see there. And to me, the poor one, this requires further study — what is written from Mar'ish 3 regarding dried ginger borei peri haAdamah with honey in seif 67 and Peri Megadim seif 61 and roses in honey borei peri haAdamah — Magen Avraham 23 that all these presumably change for the worse in water and before cooking are not fit for anything — when fixed by honey borei peri haAdamah, and it is strained to distinguish between them; but Magen Avraham in Rosh Chodesh note 8 supported this well and according to him one need not distinguish between borei peri haEtz and borei peri haAdamah, and Ateret Zekenim Rosh Chodesh note 4 likewise unlike Taz. And if so regarding kriin there is room to question — perhaps the law of wild herbs applies that it is not sown and is not so significant as stated; not so radish that is sown — one can say to bless borei peri haAdamah by honey, and this requires further study. And a compound called tzukad'a — some say it is cucumber peel — one should bless shehakol; orange peel — many opinions shehakol, and here even for Taz Peri Megadim 15 orange peel borei peri haAdamah — one can say this changed for the worse in water, and this requires further study also. And regarding blessing on scent in Tetirzelli — one can say herbs in spices for they are not fruit, and it will be explained in the five scent blessings. And still this requires further study; and regarding netilat yadayim in compounds — siman 158 Taz and Magen Avraham 7 see there. And what is written regarding pri — but in sugar they are not one of the seven beverages and according to all no netilat yadayim needed; and siman 205 I wrote a note for Taz regarding roses and ginger see there.
