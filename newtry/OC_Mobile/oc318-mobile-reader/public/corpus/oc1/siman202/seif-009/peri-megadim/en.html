And the Rosh. See Taz. The Rosh was not uncertain. The Tur writes in siman 205 that fruit liquid produced by cooking is different and superior, so the Rosh clearly distinguished. Taz's conclusion can be framed as follows: where it is not usual to cook or crush the fruit, one recites Shehakol, following Rashba; where it is usual to cook, one recites Borei Peri HaEitz; but where it is usual to squeeze, he did not decide, because even when it is usual, the juice may still be regarded as sweat. Taz asks why cooked-vegetable liquid receives the vegetable's blessing when, in seif 1, where wine is mixed with beer, one follows the majority rather than taste. Here too, the water is the majority and only the vegetable taste remains, so one should recite Shehakol. He answers that water is not significant and one follows the taste, whereas when one important beverage is mixed with another, one follows the majority. This also explains the gloss in siman 204:5 and Magen Avraham and Taz there, that with fewer than six parts water one does not follow the majority because water is involved, whereas with another important beverage one does. According to what I wrote above, however, one may distinguish between an accidental mixture and one intentionally prepared for its taste, without distinguishing water from other beverages. Taz in siman 205:5 derives from this that vegetables cooked in vinegar or another beverage cause the liquid to retain Shehakol, because that liquid is significant and one follows the majority, not the taste, even when meat is also present.
