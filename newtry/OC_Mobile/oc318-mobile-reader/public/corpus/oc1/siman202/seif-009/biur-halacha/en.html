That one cooked and burned them, etc.: see in Mishnah Berurah in what he wrote in the name of some commentators who explained that it is a type of dates that do not cook on the tree and they place them in earth or straw to warm, etc. And know that from the words of Lechem Chamudot on the mishnayot brought in Avodat HaRokeach it is clear that this deals with one who did not bury in earth: for if he buried in earth and it cooked, it would be like other tree fruits and one blesses Borei Peri HaEtz. However per this: why does the Gemara call them "cooked like myrtle"? Behold for "cooked like myrtle" one blesses Borei Peri HaEtz: and it is strained to say the Gemara's intent is that it stands to become cooked like myrtle; and further proof can be brought from R' Chananel's explanation brought in Tosafot R' Yehuda and in Or Zotart, who wrote that they are fruits not fit for eating at all and they keep them in earth to cook: and therefore per this initially one must say one does not bless on them at all, since they are not fit for eating even through pressing; and therefore what the Gemara said that one blesses Shehakol is after fixing them through warming in earth and the like. And I found in Avodat HaRokeach siman 204 in the name of Perishah that he too holds thus for practical law, that after they were kept in earth nevertheless one blesses only Shehakol; however afterward I saw in Magen Giborim that he brought in the name of responsa Panim Me'irot part 1 siman 65, and he too sides thus, that cooking through warming in earth or straw and the like helps to bless Borei Peri HaEtz: but he did not bring proof for this, see there. And for practice: requires study. And know further that Rambam in his commentary on the mishnah explained on fallen fruits that fell from the tree unripe before they were cooked: and so Terumat HaDeshen: "fruits that fell before their cooking" [and in Ashkol he also wrote similarly: "that were stored or fell before they were cooked"]; and Tur in siman 204 wrote on fallen fruits of a type of dates that do not cook on the tree: and it implies from this specifically a bad type of dates that cannot cook on the tree; but plain dates that were stored or fell before their cooking did not change their blessing for this reason, and he goes according to his reasoning in siman 202 seif 2 that all other trees are considered their fruit from when they bring forth even though cooking is not yet finished at all: and we can say therefore the Mechaber omitted inside the view of Rambam and Terumat HaDeshen here, because he goes according to his reasoning in siman 202, who ruled on all other trees from when they bring forth it is considered fruit: and what difference to me whether they were stored themselves or fell on their own [I again found in Beit Meir that he too wrote therefore the Beit Yosef wondered on Riaz, who wrote that fallen fruits means those whose cooking was not finished, because he goes according to his reasoning]; however per what we noted there that this law is not clear from the outset: if they fell on their own before cooking, it is also possible one blesses Shehakol: afterward I found in Nishmat Adam that he also raised from the above seif:
One blesses Shehakol: know that it is clear in the Gemara to the conclusion that dates that fall from the tree due to wind are superior fruit and one blesses on them Borei Peri HaEtz; and so R' Chananel, brought in Or Zotart [and Rashi's words in Alfasi on the mishnah there are not precise, see there]; and so the Gra; and if they fell before they were cooked: see what we wrote earlier, that this depends on views.
