The tips, etc. Tur in the name of Rabbeinu Menachem of Merseilles. Although the Tur disagrees and holds that one recites Borei Peri HaAdamah, as with unripe grapes, the Shulchan Aruch follows Rabbeinu Menachem. See Magen Avraham. Bach writes likewise and explains that they are no better than nevelos, as stated later.
And likewise, etc. Mishnah there, 40b.
For they are, etc. Gemara there: “According to the second version, this is understandable,” etc.; “but regarding nevelos,” etc.
That ripen, etc. Rashi there. Rambam, Rabbeinu Yonah, Rash, in the first chapter of Orlah and the first chapter of Demai, the Rosh there, and all the commentators, however, explain that they do not ripen on the tree. The Tur writes likewise at the beginning of siman 204. Beit Yosef overlooked this when he challenged Rabbeinu Yerucham at the beginning of this siman. This is the view of Rabbeinu Menachem regarding the tips of grapes, and also the view of Rambam. See seif 15. The Aruch likewise explains that pagin are fruits that do not become fit for eating, and they are buried in earth to ripen. Bach in siman 204, cited by Magen Avraham there, seif katan 2, erred in this matter, for he understood Rambam and the Tur as referring to dates blown down by the wind. This is a major error, for the Gemara says that according to all opinions one recites Borei Peri HaEtz. Were his explanation correct, the Gemara should have said that the same law applies before they ripen. Rather, it is as explained above and as stated in Bava Metzia 74a, “lacking burial,” etc., and there 89b, “he may not bury it in the ground,” etc. Beit Yosef in Yoreh Deah 294:1 writes, “and the dates,” etc., but this appears nowhere. See there and what is stated there.
