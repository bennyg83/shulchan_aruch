(48) Grape ends (sofei anavim) — meaning grapes found on vines in winter days that never ripen and from which they make vinegar.
(49) That they cooked them and they withered, etc. — meaning that through burning of heat they became cooked and dried and changed to spoilage [Rashi]; and other commentators explained that withered ones (noveilot) means a type of dates that do not ripen on the tree and are not fit for eating except one plucks them and warms them in earth and they ripen, or one places them in bundles or in straw to warm until they cook.
(50) One blesses shehakol — and afterward Borei Nefashot; and this is not comparable to seif 2 regarding boser, for even though it has not reached full ripeness one blesses on it borei peri haAdamah; and from what is written regarding grape ends that one blesses on them shehakol — one can say that case is different, for there they are superior grapes only that they have not yet finished ripening; not so here, where they are spoiled, for they stand only for vinegar and are not superior to liquids that come from them, whose blessing is only shehakol; and this is also the reason of those who explain that on withered dates one blesses shehakol, for they are inferior to other dates that never ripen on the tree.
