[Levush] And it is shehakol, etc. — in Magen Avraham he wondered from below siman 205 seif 2 that one blesses on mei silka borei peri haAdamah — up to here. And I wonder more about Bach who wrote also in the name of BeHaG that one blesses shehakol, and I found in the chapter "How does one bless" in this manner that he wrote one blesses borei peri haAdamah. And it appears in my humble opinion that Shulchan Aruch did not write that one blesses shehakol except when there is much oil — for then in any case the taste of vinegar is nullified even though vinegar is primary, and it is no worse than vegetables cooked in meat in siman 205 — and not as implied from Bach and Nachalat Tzvi that one blesses shehakol even when there is much oil in it. And if one puts in it a little oil and drinks it for healing, Bach wrote that for everyone one blesses borei peri haEtz and he erases the word "much" that Tur wrote, and all the latter authorities followed him. And in my humble opinion this requires study, for in the Talmud [Berakhot] daf 36 it challenges: if he drinks it by means of vinegar, if so vinegar is primary; and it answers: here with what are we dealing? With one who is concerned for his throat — Rashi explained — and he must put in it much oil, for then oil is primary and vinegar is ancillary — up to here. It implies that healing is specifically by means of much oil and therefore oil is primary, and so it implies in Tashbetz siman 322. And regarding one who does not drink it for healing — even with much oil vinegar would be primary — it is possible that Rashi holds like R' Yosef who wrote in Tur that with much oil always oil is primary; or one may say that since it is impossible to drink without vinegar and he drinks it on account of vinegar, vinegar is primary. Or also, since there is no way to put much oil if he is not doing it for healing, his mind is voided relative to every person, as I explained somewhat [note 18]. Also it appears to me proof for this — for if not, why did the Talmud bring up the baraita "One who is concerned for his throat," etc. — does it wish to bring proof that oil is medicine for the throat? Rather it cites on account of what the tanna said "he puts much oil," etc. — examine this well. And Taz wrote that since there is much oil in it, even though vinegar is most of it, oil is primary since there is also a significant amount of it. And incidentally I raised a difficulty in the Talmud there, for it challenges: it is obvious — lest I say one blesses borei peri etz zayit like wine that changed also for the better, as the Talmud said there "from it we learn" borei peri haEtz in the conclusion of the sugya there — and one may resolve with difficulty.
