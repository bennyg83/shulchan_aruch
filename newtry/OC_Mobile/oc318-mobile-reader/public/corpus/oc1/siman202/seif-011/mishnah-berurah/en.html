(54) He blesses shehakol on them — this discusses when one drinks the water by itself; but if one eats the fruits themselves and also drinks the water, it is reasonable that according to everyone he need not bless on them neither an initial blessing nor a concluding blessing, for they become secondary to the fruit; and if his main intent is for the water and he ate some of the fruits initially and blessed borei peri haEtz — requires further study whether he should bless again on the liquid shehakol, for per the Rosh he has already fulfilled with borei peri haEtz and therefore he should bless first a different blessing shehakol.
(55) To the Rosh — this refers to the previous seif where he brought a dispute on this and says that in practice it is settled that we hold like the first view to bless shehakol, for one fulfills thereby; also per the Rosh's view, for in any case on all things if he said shehakol he fulfilled; and in the concluding blessing as well there is no practical difference for all fruits, for their concluding blessing is only Borei Nefashot; but for fruits of the seven species such as raisins and figs upon which one blesses at the end the abbreviated form of three — here there is room to doubt how we should act: if the water has the status of fruit one should bless on it the abbreviated form of three at the end, and if they do not have the status of fruit in themselves one should bless on them only Borei Nefashot; and in these one does not exempt the other even b'dieved.
(56) But within the meal — for within the meal with HaMotzi it exempts the blessing after them, whether it would be the abbreviated form of three or Borei Nefashot.
(57) And if he drew off, etc. — explanation: that which we equated raisins to figs is only when the raisins were mixed with the broth, for there was still broth and soup on them; but when one drew off the water from the raisins it becomes full wine and one blesses borei peri haGefen on them; and Magen Avraham's view is not that through drawing it becomes wine, but even when mixed — whenever one did not intend to eat the raisins but only to draw off and drink the water — even if he drank from the vessel while still mixed together, he has the status of wine; and Magen Giborim explains it in the plain sense, that specifically when he drew off; but the case is when they have not yet lain three days, and therefore they do not have the status of wine for the matter of the blessing borei peri haGefen unless he drew them off and separated them from the raisins.
(58) And one blessing, etc. — that is, on the vine and on borei peri haGefen; and see in the latter authorities that even if three were not placed and four came — the reason is that since the raisins are good, the power of the fruit standing within them gives a strong taste.
(59) That if one treads them — meaning, without soaking — if juice came out from them; then if he soaked them it becomes full wine.
(60) No juice at all, etc. — meaning, and one blesses on them shehakol according to everyone; and even though the water was somewhat sweetened, it is only mere sweat.
