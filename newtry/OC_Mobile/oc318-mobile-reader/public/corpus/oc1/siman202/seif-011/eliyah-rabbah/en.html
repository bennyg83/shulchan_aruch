[11] [Levush] because I do not reject, etc. Malbushei Yom Tov wrote it is similar, only those who disagree: also on dates they disagree and he holds one should bless only Shehakol; and so is in Beit Yosef, and he proved thus from Rashi's explanation and Tur; and see siman 204 seif 11, and siman 207 seif 4: end of his words: and so Lechem Chamudot and Nachalat Tzvi challenged Levush. And in my humble opinion Levush explained well, for he delved into Tur's words, because it is difficult for him on Tur, who wrote first: dates that one crushed somewhat and are not completely mashed: Borei Peri HaEtz, and these are Rashi's words, which imply that when completely mashed one blesses Shehakol; and afterward he wrote per Rambam's words: dates that one mashed and made from them dough, etc.: Borei Peri HaEtz, even though Bach answered and wrote: this is his language: and Tur did not bring Rashi's view here because it seemed to him his words are rejected before Rambam and R' Yitzchak: end of his words. It did not seem to Levush to answer thus, for he found much difficulty on Bach: one, that Tur did not prove that R' Yitzchak holds like Rambam, but wrote "and it is possible that R' Yitzchak too admits"; and further, Tur's language that concluded "important like the essence of the fruit" per everyone is clear that Rashi too admits to Rambam, for otherwise the language "per everyone" would not fit well: understand. And further, in the abridged rulings of Rosh and Rabbeinu Yerucham I saw they ruled like Rashi. Therefore it seemed to Levush that Rambam and Rashi do not disagree: Rambam deals when they were not crushed so much that they became like a beverage and their appearance changed completely; but in pavedli that changed completely Rambam admits to Rashi's explanation, and Rashi deals with this; and so I found in Derishah, who wrote that Rambam speaks only when they did not dissolve and changed from the category of food, and also the appearance of a date remains somewhat. And unlike Beit Yosef, who wrote that Rambam disagrees with Rashi's explanation: end of his words.
