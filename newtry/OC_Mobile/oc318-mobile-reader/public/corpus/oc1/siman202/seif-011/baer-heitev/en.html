According to the Rosh. For if one recited Shehakol over any of them, he fulfilled his obligation. If, however, he first ate the fruit and recited Borei Peri HaEtz, it requires study whether he should then recite Shehakol separately over the broth. He should therefore first drink another beverage over which he can recite Shehakol. Magen Avraham. The brine of pickled produce, such as cucumbers or sauerkraut, requires only Shehakol, since they were not pickled for the purpose of extracting their liquid. Taz.
Was drawn off. Beit Yosef writes: This applies specifically when the raisins remain mixed with the water; if the water was drawn away from the raisins, it is wine. Rama explains his words as follows: When the raisins remain mixed with the water and one both eats the raisins and drinks the water, the liquid is like the broth of any other fruit. But if it was drawn off, meaning that his intention was not to eat the raisins at all but only to extract wine from them, then even if he drank directly from the vessel containing the raisins, he recites Borei Peri HaGefen. Physical drawing-off is not actually required. This is also implied by all the poskim who discuss raisin wine without distinguishing whether it was drawn off. Magen Avraham; see there. Taz likewise writes that after three days it is fully considered wine even if it was not drawn off. See there.
