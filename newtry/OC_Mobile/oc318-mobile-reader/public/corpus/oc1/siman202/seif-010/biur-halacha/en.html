Concerning that water, etc. This implies that one recites the appropriate blessing over the fruits themselves. This is also evident from Magen Avraham in the name of Rashba. It must therefore refer to fruits that are good both raw and cooked, for otherwise one recites only Shehakol even over the fruits themselves, as explained below in seif 12; see Mishnah Berurah there. Even so, regarding the liquid, we do not say that their liquid shares the fruit's blessing unless most consumption of those fruits is through cooking. This is how the words of the Shulchan Aruch must be understood. There is clear proof for this, since apples are described in seif 12 as good both raw and cooked, yet Rashba explicitly writes that one recites Shehakol over their liquid because they are ordinarily eaten raw and not boiled. Necessarily, regarding their liquid, most of the fruit must specifically be intended for that use. It is possible that Rashba agrees with the Rosh where the ordinary practice is equally to eat the fruit cooked and raw, and that he disputes him only where the fruit is good cooked but is ordinarily eaten only raw. See Rashba's novellae to the entire sugya of vegetable broth, which somewhat imply this. Know further that even according to the Rosh, who holds that one always recites Borei Peri HaEtz over their liquid, this means only that most consumption need not be through cooking. Nevertheless, the fruits must at least be good both raw and cooked. If they are good only raw, then even over the fruits themselves one recites only Shehakol when eating them cooked, as explained immediately below in seif 12 in Mishnah Berurah. Accordingly, it is clear that according to all opinions one recites only Shehakol over water in which apples were soaked, known as apple kvass. The apples themselves are not ordinarily eaten after soaking, since soaking spoils them, and one recites Shehakol over them. Their liquid cannot be superior to the fruit itself. Apart from this reason, there is another reason why its blessing is Shehakol; see Magen Avraham, siman 205, seif 2. Chayei Adam rules likewise.
