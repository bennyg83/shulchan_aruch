Shehakol. This is not comparable to vegetable broth discussed in siman 205, seif 2. Since those vegetables are ordinarily eaten after cooking, their cooking liquid is treated like the vegetables themselves. Fruits, however, are not ordinarily cooked or pressed, but eaten as they are; therefore, their liquid is not treated like the fruit itself. Beit Yosef in the name of Rashba. Regarding dried fruits, however, such as apples, pears, cherries, and plums, all agree that one recites Borei Peri HaEtz, since they are ordinarily cooked rather than eaten as they are. This is my practice and is the principal ruling. Bach and Taz. Olas Tamid likewise rules that when most of a fruit is eaten cooked, its cooking liquid is certainly treated like the fruit itself: if it is a fruit of the ground, one recites Borei Peri HaAdamah, and if it is a tree fruit, one recites Borei Peri HaEtz. Magen Avraham remains uncertain about this. Over coffee and tea one recites Shehakol. Perach Mateh Aharon, part 1, siman 40. Responsa Shevus Yaakov, part 2, siman 5, likewise rules that Shehakol is recited over coffee and tea. Halachos Ketanos, siman 9, and Responsa Panim Meiros, siman 95, however, write that strictly speaking one should recite Borei Peri HaAdamah, although the custom is to recite Shehakol. See there. See also Bnei Chayei and the authority who follows him, and see siman 204, seif 7, regarding what is written there.
