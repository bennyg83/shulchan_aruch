(51) Fruits that were soaked — from the outset, for in this people regularly give taste in water.

(52) Rather shehakol — for it is only mere sweat as above seif 8, and it is not comparable to mei silka and all boiled fruits upon which one blesses borei peri haEtz, and as below siman 207 seif 2 — because there most eating of those vegetables is through boiling; not so here with fruits whose way is not to boil or pickle them but to eat them as they are; therefore we do not say their boiling and pickling should be like them. However, regarding fruits whose majority of eating is through cooking or pickling — one who boils or pickles them, they are like them and one blesses on them borei peri haEtz according to all; and all this is regarding fruits whose planting from the outset was with intent to eat them cooked or pickled; but regarding fruits whose way is to eat them raw, only that some dry them and afterward cook them — one never blesses on their sauce except shehakol, even though the way of all of them is to cook when dry; nevertheless at the beginning of their planting they were not planted with intent to dry them but to eat them raw. And regarding dried plums or cherries Chayei Adam wrote: in countries where many grow there, presumably the majority plant with intent to dry them; if so, the berakha of their sauce is borei peri haEtz.

(53) That if one tastes, etc. — meaning when tasting and sensing that there is taste of fruit in the cooked or soaked water — one blesses borei peri haEtz, and we do not say it is only weak taste and mere sweat as we say in seif 8 regarding squeezing fruits, for more taste of fruit enters the water through cooking than if one squeezed the fruit itself when raw. And for halachah the latter authorities ruled l'chatchila one should bless shehakol, and b'dieved if he blessed borei peri haEtz he fulfilled; and see in siman 207 seif 2 what applies to our matter.
