(51) Fruits that he soaked: For a full twenty-four-hour period, since in that amount of time they ordinarily impart flavor to the water.
(52) Only Shehakol: Because the liquid is regarded merely as the fruit’s moisture, as explained above in seif 8. This is unlike beet water and the liquid of other cooked vegetables, over which one recites Borei Peri HaAdamah, as explained below in siman 205, seif 2. There, most consumption of those vegetables is through cooking, whereas fruits are ordinarily not cooked or pickled but eaten as they are. We therefore do not say that the liquid produced by cooking or pickling has the same blessing as the fruit. With fruits whose usual mode of consumption is through cooking or pickling, however, the liquid produced by cooking or pickling has the same blessing as the fruit, and according to all opinions one recites Borei Peri HaEtz over it. This applies only to fruits that were originally planted with the intention that they be eaten cooked or pickled. If fruits are ordinarily eaten fresh, but some are dried and then cooked, one always recites only Shehakol over their liquid. Although all such dried fruits are cooked, they were not originally planted with the intention that they be dried, but rather to be eaten fresh. Regarding dried plums or cherries, Chayei Adam writes that in countries where they grow in abundance, they are presumably planted mostly with the intention that they be dried. Accordingly, one recites Borei Peri HaEtz over their liquid.
(53) If the flavor entered, etc.: Meaning, when one tastes and senses the flavor of the fruit in the cooked or soaked water, he recites Borei Peri HaEtz. We do not say that it is merely a weak flavor and fruit moisture, as stated in seif 8 regarding squeezed fruit juice, because cooking draws more of the fruit’s flavor into the water than squeezing the fresh fruit itself. As a matter of practical halachah, the later authorities rule that initially one should recite Shehakol. After the fact, if he recited Borei Peri HaEtz, he has fulfilled his obligation. See siman 205, seif 2, for what is relevant to this matter.
