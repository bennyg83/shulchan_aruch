Even though the flavor of the fruit entered it, etc. This is the Rashba's view as cited by Beit Yosef. He explains that since these fruits are normally eaten whole, and are not normally cooked or squeezed, their liquid does not retain their blessing. But something normally cooked or squeezed has liquid that shares its status. Accordingly, the broth of dried plums requires Borei Peri HaEtz, because they are normally cooked.
And the Rosh wrote that it is possible, etc. The Rosh writes: “Although it contains only the broth and the flavor of the vegetables, one recites upon it the same blessing as upon the vegetables themselves. This is unlike fruit juice, which we call mere moisture, because that liquid does not contain the fruit's flavor. It is possible that if one cooked fruit and the fruit's flavor entered the water, one recites Borei Peri HaEtz upon it.” Beit Yosef concludes that the liquid in which vegetables were cooked has the same law as the vegetables, whether they were cooked in water or merely soaked in it, provided that they imparted flavor to the water. Since the Rosh uses the phrase “it is possible,” it would appear at first that he was uncertain, in which case one should recite Shehakol to avoid doubt. Yet the Tur states this ruling unequivocally in siman 205, that on water in which vegetables were cooked one recites the same blessing as upon the vegetables. It therefore appears that according to the Tur there is no doubt here. Although the Rosh writes “it is possible,” he did not mean to express uncertainty about the law itself. This may be explained from the approach of Tosafos, who write that the water of cooked beets has the same law as the beets and requires Borei Peri HaAdamah, even though it contains only water and vegetable flavor. Although we stated earlier that fruit juice is mere moisture, Tosafos writes that perhaps one may distinguish between them, but does not explain the distinction. The Rosh therefore writes that perhaps this is the distinction: ordinary fruit juice lacks the flavor of the fruit, whereas cooked water contains that flavor. The law itself is not in doubt, for the water of cooked vegetables certainly has the same law as the vegetables, meaning that water flavored by fruit has the same law as the fruit. In practice, one should recite Shehakol on liquids from foods not normally cooked or crushed, but Borei Peri HaEtz on liquids from fruits normally cooked. One should not object from what I wrote in this siman, that we follow the majority rather than the flavor, because here the other component is merely water and has no significance against the fruit's flavor. So it appears to me. See also what I wrote in siman 205, seif 2.
