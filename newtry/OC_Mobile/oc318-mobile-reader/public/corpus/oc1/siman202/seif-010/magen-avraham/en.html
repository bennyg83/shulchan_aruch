Rather shehakol. And Levush wrote regarding water that rises in siman 205 seif 2 that those waters — since most of their eating is by boiling — the water of their boiling is considered like them; not so regarding fruits that are not customarily boiled or squeezed but eaten in their raw state — regarding those we do not say their water should be like them (Beit Yosef, Rashba). However, regarding dry fruits such as apples and pears, berries, and plums — meaning that one blesses borei peri haEtz on their water, for they are not customarily eaten except by boiling them and not in their raw state — and such is my practice, and such is the essence (Bach). And it requires further study, for it implies that Rashba's reason is because they are customarily boiled from the outset they were planted with intent to boil them; and if so, fruits that were not planted with this intent, even if they later dried, it would not help; and so too Levush wrote that specifically fruits whose majority of eating is by cooking one blesses borei peri haEtz (Eastern communities). And so too wrote in the name of Rashal.

Taste of the fruit. For more of the taste of the fruit enters the water through cooking than when one squeezes the fruit itself.
