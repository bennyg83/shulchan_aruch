There is room to question what was written, that raisins are normally used this way and the Rashba agrees, as in seif 11, and there is no doubt. This follows his position in letter 8 regarding dried plums, and so too the Bach. However, according to what Magen Avraham wrote in letter 22, that this applies specifically where the majority of the fruit is planted for such use, dried plums are different. Raisins are not planted with the intention that they be soaked, but rather that they be pressed. This also appears from Levush, seif 11, and Eliyah Rabbah, letter 15, who writes that there is no difficulty from raisins. As for what I wrote above regarding raisin wine, Magen Avraham in letter 27 addresses this and explains it differently; with Hashem's help, it will be explained there. Regarding soaking fruit, Olas Tamid wrote that it requires no less than twenty-four hours. Eliyah Rabbah, letter 14, likewise notes that even in the laws of prohibitions, we rule that only soaking for twenty-four hours is equivalent to cooking. Therefore, regarding fruits normally used in this manner that were soaked for twenty-four hours, there is no doubt that one recites Borei Peri HaEtz. However, if they were pickled in vinegar for the time it would take them to boil on the fire, even if this is their usual use, one may say that the blessing is Shehakol, for perhaps vinegar does not create the status of pickling in so short a time. See Shach, Yoreh Deah 105:2, and Magen Avraham, like the Taz, in siman 205, letter 5, who considers this a case in which one follows the majority, as I wrote in letter 9. But if they were pickled in strongly salted brine for the time it would take them to boil on the fire, and this is their normal use, the blessing is certainly Borei Peri HaEtz. If this is not their normal use, it is the dispute between the Rashba and the Rosh whether pickling is equivalent to cooking, as I wrote. What was written regarding cucumber brine may be what Rambam wrote in chapter 8 of Hilchos Berachos, halachah 4, namely, that they were cooked in order to extract their liquid. See Magen Avraham in siman 205, letter 6.
Now, the borscht beverage made in Russia, and also in our region, from bran and flour, requires the blessing Shehakol, since the blessing on wheat flour is Shehakol, as stated in siman 208, seif 5. As for that which is made from red beets, or what is called borscht, one who drinks it cooked in this manner should seemingly recite Borei Peri HaAdamah, like the liquid of vegetables in siman 205, when this is their normal use. Since pickling is comparable to cooking, one might rely on what Magen Avraham wrote there in letter 6. Nevertheless, in such a case one should recite Shehakol. The same applies to dried plums if the liquid is separated from them; one may say that according to all opinions the blessing is Shehakol. See what I will write, with Hashem's help, in Magen Avraham, letter 22, concerning this.
