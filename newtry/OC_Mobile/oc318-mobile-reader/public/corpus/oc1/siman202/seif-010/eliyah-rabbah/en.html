And a great wonder: in the Gemara there it concludes "this and this" to exempt from tithes, and so ruled Rambam chapter 1 of Hilchot Ma'aser, and Shulchan Aruch Yoreh De'ah siman 331 seif 74. And more wondrous: behold Rosh there wrote explicitly exempt from tithe, only that he distinguishes between tithe and blessing, that for blessing it is considered more since one enjoys from them. And from this matter it became clear to me Rosh's words and to remove the two wonders that stood: one that Adnei Melech wondered: if so, what does the Gemara challenge from one who said one eats caper peels orlah, and juxtapose on caper peels one says Borei Peri HaEtz: behold one can distinguish. And further he wondered: behold Ba'al Halachot Gedolot wrote: from the aspect of orlah it is not fruit, and for blessing too it is not fruit: if so there is no distinction for Ba'al Halachot Gedolot. And in truth it is a great difficulty, and so I found in Rashba's novellae daf 27 that he proved from the power of this difficulty that one may not distinguish between tithe and orlah and blessing; but I learned from there that specifically regarding the blessing Borei Peri HaEtz one may not distinguish, but regarding Borei Peri HaAdamah one may distinguish. And if so it appears in my humble opinion that Rosh's intent is to bless Borei Peri HaAdamah on bitter almonds, for it is comparable to caper leaves on which one blesses Borei Peri HaAdamah even though exempt from tithe: unlike what he wrote before him that one blesses Shehakol, meaning it does not refer at all to Ba'al Halachot Gedolot's words, who wrote before him that one blesses Borei Peri HaEtz. So it appears clear to me in Rosh's and Tur's words, and all Acharonim who did not address this left me room. However one must answer Ba'al Halachot Gedolot's view: behold we say in the Gemara "this and this" to exempt: and he wanted to say that possibly the explanation of "this and this" is that large almonds, whether bitter or sweet, are exempt, but small ones are liable: opposite Rashi's explanation that it refers to small ones. And with this it is also settled what Adnei Melech found difficult on Rosh, who challenged Rashi's explanation of exempt and liable that it implies it deals with one matter; therefore the Gemara explains "this and this" refers to bitter small and sweet large: and he challenged: behold also per Rashi's explanation it is difficult on Ba'al Halachot Gedolot. And per what I wrote it is settled that per Rashi's explanation nothing is difficult on Ba'al Halachot Gedolot, for one can explain the opposite in the Gemara: understand well. However one can wonder that Rashba contradicts himself, for I wrote above that he wrote one cannot distinguish on Borei Peri HaEtz between tithe and orlah: if so how did he rule there like Ba'al Halachot Gedolot to bless on small bitter almonds Borei Peri HaEtz; he himself explained in his novellae in the first chapter of Chullin "this and this" to exempt like Rosh's explanation. And until the wonder increased I would say that specifically regarding caper peels we say there is no distinction between blessing and tithe and orlah since it is finished: but regarding small almonds that are not finished there is a distinction, for Ba'al Halachot Gedolot and Rashba there refer to caper peels: but this is strained to distinguish thus. Further I found in Tashbetz siman 323 that he wrote on Ba'al Halachot Gedolot's words: however in Chullin we say "this and this" to exempt: end of his words, implying he rejects this and disagrees with Ba'al Halachot Gedolot; and so in Sefer Tzidah LaDerekh and Avudraham I found they wrote: this is their language: some say Borei Peri HaEtz and some say Shehakol: end of their words. Also Rosh's words themselves are not decisive for one who examines; and since great Acharonim did not decide, it is fit to bless from doubt Shehakol.
