In Taz note 5. And it appears that Rosh did not intend except to prove, etc. — his words are puzzling, for it is simple that the honey mentioned in the Torah is date honey, for dates are not written at all in the verse; and nevertheless they consider it among the seven species because Scripture writes "honey," and that is date honey. And so we say that for measures we say honey is like a large date — see Rashi there. Also figs and pomegranates are written explicitly in Scripture, and since afterward it counts honey — if so, that is date honey. The intent of Rosh is simple, that he meant to say: granted, if dates were written in Scripture we would properly say that the honey that comes out is like other beverages that come out from fruits; but since Scripture writes "honey," automatically we say that the honey too is among the seven species; and therefore Scripture took "honey" to reveal that the honey too is included, and all the more so the dates themselves. However it is still difficult: what is the difference between this and what comes out from figs, pomegranates, and apples, where we say it is mere perspiration? If so, the honey mentioned in the Torah refers to the dates themselves — for this Rosh said: I would say that there, where the beverage is not gathered within them until it comes out by itself, it is mere perspiration; unlike date honey that oozes by itself from dates, which is among the seven species and one blesses on it borei peri haEtz. And likewise Ma'adanei Melekh explained the words of Rosh briefly. And what Taz wrote regarding figs — that it does not come out by itself except by pounding and it is not in the category of perspiration said regarding honey — I saw in the book Nezirut Shimshon to the Gaon R' Shimshon Hasid, who challenged him at the end of Ketubot, for there it states regarding honey that oozes from figs, as it is written — meaning zavat milk and honey; and apparently from there it should challenge also Rosh himself, for fig honey too oozes by itself. One must say that certainly the honey written in Scripture refers to date honey, for figs are counted explicitly among the seven species and it is not mere perspiration, since its way is to come out by itself; and even though sometimes figs too ooze by themselves, nevertheless their way is not so much like date honey, that it should be like the fruit itself. That which it says there — meaning that it says zavat milk and honey — it speaks by way of mere hint; it says thus to sharpen the language, because he saw that the honey was mixed with goat milk as appears there — see there. But the primary honey mentioned in the Torah is date honey — this requires further study. And see in Yerushalmi Bikkurim, which derives from what is written "and when the thing breaks forth," etc., that honey is dates and not actual honey — see there; and they brought it in Mishneh Torah chapter 3 of Bikkurim. And that is why it is obligated in tithes; and in Tosafot it is explained in several places that if tithe of fruits is rabbinic, tithe of figs is also rabbinic.
