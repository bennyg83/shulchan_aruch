(note 8) With bread, etc.: even, etc., and a little bread: some say the oil is primary. And this is because of the importance of bread: it is not nullified relative to oil even if he needs it for medicine.
And it is difficult from what is written regarding one who eats fish, etc., in siman 212: meaning one who ate sweet things and it pained his heart, and he eats salted fish to remove the sweetness; and so that the salted fish not harm him he eats bread with it. We hold that one blesses on the fish, for it is primary, and exempts the bread; thus even bread that is considered nullified relative to the primary: and the same law applies here.
And because of this difficulty he means what Bach and Maharil wrote as obvious.
And that which he did not establish, etc.: meaning on daf 35b: R' Yehudah said in the name of Shmuel: on olive oil one blesses borei peri haEtz, and he challenged: how so? If he drinks it plain: behold it harms him and he does not bless at all; and if he eats it by bread: bread is primary, and we hold that one blesses on the primary, etc.; and if he drinks it by anigran (meaning mei silka): anigran is primary, and we hold, etc. And it answers: actually, when drinking by anigran (this is not stated explicitly in the Talmud, but this explanation is necessary), such as one who is concerned for his throat whose remedy is oil but plain it harms him and he drinks it by anigran: then for him the oil is primary, and therefore he blesses on the oil borei peri haEtz: up to here the Gemara. And if so, it should be difficult why he retracted from what he said initially that he eats it by bread, and it answers: by anigran for medicine it should have said as he said initially that he eats it by bread and is concerned for his throat and eats it for medicine: then it would be oil primary; and it can be inferred from this like Bach, that if he eats it by bread, even for medicine, nevertheless the oil is not primary.
This is because, etc.: meaning this is not proof, for the Talmud stated the ordinary manner of the matter, and proof from that it specified anigran, etc.: meaning that even according to Bach, who holds that bread does not become secondary to oil because of the importance of bread, but other beverages certainly become secondary to oil when drinking for throat medicine; if so, why did the Talmud specify anigran: it should have said anonymously that one drinks by beverages, which speaks of one concerned for his throat? Rather one must say it stated the ordinary manner of the matter. And likewise if he drinks it, etc.: and see above note 34.
