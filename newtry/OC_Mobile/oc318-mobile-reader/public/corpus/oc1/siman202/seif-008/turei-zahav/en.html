Honey that flows from dates. Beit Yosef cites the Rosh regarding the view of the Halachos Gedolos: “It is possible that the Gaon's reason is that Scripture says ‘honey’ and does not say ‘dates.’ The verse therefore refers to honey that flows by itself from dates, which is included among the seven species, and one recites Borei Peri HaEtz upon it. It differs from liquid extracted from pomegranates and apples, which emerges only through crushing and squeezing. Honey that flows naturally from dates is the honey mentioned in the Torah.” At first glance, this implies a distinction between date liquid that flows naturally and that which emerges through crushing or squeezing, and Beit Yosef understood it this way. He therefore wrote that the Tur deliberately states that even on honey flowing from dates one recites Shehakol, and all the more so when it emerges through crushing. This is astonishing, for crushing, and certainly squeezing, extracts more of the actual fruit than does natural seepage, which is merely moisture. Rather, the Rosh intended only to prove that the honey mentioned in the Torah is date honey, because dates naturally exude liquid, and Scripture describes the Land as flowing with milk and honey. Other fruits, such as figs and pomegranates, do not exude liquid naturally; their liquid emerges only through crushing or squeezing, which is not included in the term “flowing” used concerning honey. The Tur here mentioned the simpler case, namely natural seepage, while he discusses squeezing in siman 205. See what I wrote there in seif 3.
