Date syrup (zav) from dates. Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Rosh, who wrote on Hagahot Maimoniyot — and it is possible that the Gaon's reason is because in Scripture it is written "honey" and dates are not written; if so, the verse speaks of honey that oozes by itself from dates, and it is among the seven species and one blesses on it borei peri haEtz. I would say regarding what comes out from pomegranates and apples, for these come out by pounding and squeezing; but date honey that oozes by itself from dates — that is the honey mentioned in the Torah. End of his words. Apparently it implies that there is a distinction in dates between what comes out by itself and what comes out by pounding and squeezing; and so understood Rav Beya — therefore he wrote, and it appears that our master deduced to write "and on zav from them shehakol," to say that even when it oozes by itself one blesses shehakol, and all the more so what comes out by pounding. End of his words. In truth it is a puzzling matter, for certainly more comes out by squeezing, and all the more so by pounding, than what comes out by oozing alone — for there it was only mere perspiration, unlike pounding and squeezing where the body of the fruit comes out from it. It appears that Rosh did not intend except to prove that the honey mentioned in the Torah is from dates — for from dates it comes out by zava, and regarding honey Scripture writes the language of zavat milk and honey, unlike other things such as figs and pomegranates that do not come out by themselves except by pounding and squeezing; and this is not in the category of zava that is said regarding honey. Tur took here the simpler case — that is, oozing by itself and squeezing, as written in siman 205; see there seif 63.
