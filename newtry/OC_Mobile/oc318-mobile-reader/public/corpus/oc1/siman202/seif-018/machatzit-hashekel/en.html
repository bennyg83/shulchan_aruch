(note 18) And some say to bless, etc. — it appears specifically in berries (godgedania) that were cooked and completely dissolved, etc.; but dates that were completely mashed — not so, etc., for we say daf 38 trimah remains in its matter — bless on it its berakha as if whole; and Rashi explained somewhat crushed and not completely broken — end of his words; and Tur and Rabbeinu Yosef and Tosefet Shabbat siman 69 deduced if completely mashed they are not in their matter and one blesses shehakol; and Rabbeinu Yosef ruled like Rambam: dates one mashes by hand and makes dough, etc. — implying even if completely mashed nevertheless borei peri haEtz; and in Tosefet Shabbat the question regarding godgedania of Meinzlin that they cook and completely dissolve and mix spices and honey — how to bless; and he answered after bringing Rashi's words implying regarding dates if dissolved completely shehakol — all the more so godgedania where they mix spices, etc. (and he did not cite Rambam's view there); but in Shulchan Aruch which wrote therefore likewise in plums etc. — does not imply the case where spices were mixed and it is like dates; and afterward he brought "some say to bless shehakol" which is Tosefet Shabbat's view. And Magen Avraham wrote: it appears Rama ruled to bring the view of "some say" for povidla but not for dates, and so it seems from Levush's words. The distinction: regarding godgedania Magen Avraham wrote they were cooked and completely dissolved, i.e., through cooking which is the way of povidla to cook and thereby dissolve; but regarding dates he wrote they were completely mashed and did not write cooked and dissolved, for the way is not to cook them but they are mashed immediately; and so Rambam's language the Mechaber cited: dates one mashes by hand, etc. Therefore regarding godgedania since they dissolved through cooking their form was nullified and they are not recognizable at all as fruit — therefore they lost their berakha and one blesses shehakol. Not so dates that were not cooked — even though completely mashed by hand until dough — nevertheless the impression of their form is recognizable that they are dates; therefore they remain in their matter to bless borei peri haEtz; and so Levush gave this reason — see there.

And likewise siman 204, which wrote that even if they are very pressed — meaning preserves or rose hips or fruits that are spiced in honey, the honey is secondary, and even if very pressed one blesses upon them with the blessing of the fruits; thus even if very pressed they remain in their substance (b'milteiheyhu kayamim). And Rama did not disagree with him except that there it speaks of when their form is still recognizable, as Magen Avraham wrote there, citing Shelah — see there.

And also in Taz it is possible that he concedes regarding dates. Even though his proof for berries is from Rashi's words regarding dates as above, one can say his intent is that Rashi, even regarding dates that were completely mashed, holds to bless shehakol; nevertheless we rule like him regarding berries since it is preferable, or for the reason stated above, or because they mix spices and honey in them as above, or because possibly dates are more commonly mashed than berries, and as he distinguished earlier between berries and spices — even though Taz did not bring the view of Rambam at all.

And also regarding berries that were cooked, etc. — meaning that even regarding berries it is not clear to Taz to bless shehakol; if so, one can say that regarding dates he concedes to bless borei peri haEtz.

And in Rokeach, etc. — pressed and cooked — see that he holds that even if cooked and completely dissolved, nevertheless one blesses borei peri haEtz; if so, at least when not cooked one should bless borei peri haEtz. And Taz wrote to bless also on berries borei peri haEtz.
