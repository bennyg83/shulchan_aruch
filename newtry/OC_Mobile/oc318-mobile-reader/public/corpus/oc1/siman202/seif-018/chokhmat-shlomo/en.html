There, seif 18. Over pepper and ginger when they are moist, one recites Borei Peri HaAdamah. Note: The source of this law is Berachos 35b, where Rava states that over pepper one recites Borei Peri HaAdamah. The Gemara challenges this from Rava’s statement that one who chews pepper on Yom Kippur is exempt, and answers that the present case concerns moist pepper. This is difficult according to Tosafos in Shevuos 22b, as explained by Mahari Ben Lev and Maharshal, that according to Rabbi Shimon, who imposes lashes for any amount, one is liable even for eating something in an unusual manner. This shows that eating in an unusual manner is at least regarded as consuming some amount. Why, then, does the Gemara challenge Rava? It could have answered that on Yom Kippur, which requires a specific measure, one who chews pepper is exempt because this is an unusual manner of eating and the requisite measure is absent. A blessing, however, is recited even over the smallest amount, and therefore a blessing is required. This is exceedingly difficult; examine it carefully.
