On sugar — it is Rambam's language, and Tur challenged him that it is not comparable to date honey, for dates stand in their essence for that purpose, unlike here where the reeds stand in their essence for squeezing sugar; if so they are like olives and grapes whose fruit that emerges from them is like them — and Beit Yosef wrote on this that they are matters of taste, but l'chumra it is good to be concerned for Rambam's view to bless on all shehakol, for through this one exits according to all opinions; however in many places he retracted and wrote that Tur never saw those sugar reeds; and in truth in the land of Egypt where those reeds grow they are sold by thousands to suck them in the mouth — and therefore their essence is not specifically to squeeze and make sugar from them; and therefore even if one squeezed them they are no better than other fruit waters that are only perspiration [so is the gist of his intent, unlike Taz, and Acharonim already challenged him] — and so Radbaz challenged Tur in this challenge; but in truth if this is the essence of Rambam's reason, then sugar itself is only sauce like other fruit waters; but per this Rambam should have ruled to bless borei peri haEtz or borei peri haAdamah on the reeds themselves when one sucks them — and Rambam's plain language implies the reeds are no better than the sugar itself; and further on the essence of the matter certainly Tur's words are correct, that the essence of the fruit of these reeds is the sugar made from them, which is an important thing, and we do not concern ourselves that thousands suck the tree itself in the mouth, for that is only slight pleasure and a subsidiary matter — and certainly the essence of their planting is for the sugar; and so is explained in truth in BeHaG itself: "sugar reed — certainly it is a tree, and when people plant it they certainly also plant with intent for sugar," etc. [and what he also wrote plainly deals with what he said at first that certainly it is a tree, and on this he says planting too is essentially for sugar] — and so Ashkol, that planting is with intent for sugar; and so Riaz in his laws as a simple matter that they plant only with intent for sugar; and so is also the Gra's view in his commentary, who rejected Kessef Mishneh's words; but Rambam's reason is in truth very simple in my opinion, for in BeHaG itself it is found that he retracted from his first view, and thus: "sugar and sugar reeds — borei peri haAdamah, and not because he holds the reed is not a tree, but rather it seemed correct to them that although it is a tree, since fruit does not emerge from it and we do not eat fruit from it, we are not told to bless borei peri haEtz but borei peri haAdamah, like the case of safflower blossom," end of BeHaG [and these words escaped Radbaz, who wrote their dispute is because some do not change their trunk except every two years and some change every three years, see there] — and Rambam holds that certainly the reasoning is like the second view [and for those who wrote that all Geonim hold its blessing is borei peri haAdamah] but he holds it is not fruit at all, and from the reason of this BeHaG, and one should not bless even borei peri haAdamah; and it is not comparable to safflower blossom, for at least they eat them — unlike here where one cannot eat it except to suck the sweet taste; and in such a case we do not find in the Talmud to bless on it a fruit blessing — and this is Rambam's intent, that this is not called fruit; and therefore this law does not apply at all to boiled vegetables whose water that emerges from them is like them, and likewise to water of soaked fruits per Rosh in seif 10, that in all of them there is fruit — unlike here, which is not fruit at all but a tree that one cannot eat except that it has sweet taste; and therefore one does not bless on sugar, and all the more on the tree itself, but shehakol. However per this our sugar made from vegetables called beets — and even if we say it does not depend on water of boiled vegetables that the water is like them, for this is possible specifically in cooking boiled vegetables and not in making something new from the boiling as in our case — nevertheless it is no worse than other fruit waters that emerged through cooking, for per Rosh one blesses as on fruits, and Rashba too admits, for he disagrees only because these fruits' way is not to extract beverages but to eat them as they are; and in our case all know they plant thousands of fields only for this, and also the vegetables are a special species called sweet beets that stand essentially only to extract sugar from them — therefore one should bless on the sugar borei peri haAdamah as on the beets themselves, and we have no reason here to bless shehakol except per the second reasoning Rambam mentioned, because they change through fire and Rambam holds they did not enact on this a fruit blessing that emerged from it, since it is not recognizable at all to the eye of a person from which fruit or vegetable this thing emerged, for behold it is now like a piece of salt, and one does not bless on such except shehakol. And behold for practical law: although many Rishonim [BeHaG in his first view, R' Chananel, Tosafot, Rosh, Mordechai, Tur, and Rambam] hold one should bless borei peri haEtz on sugar made from reeds, and so too several Acharonim [Bach, Taz, and Gra] that fundamentally the law is like BeHaG and his supporters mentioned — nevertheless in practice one should certainly conduct l'chatchila like Shulchan Aruch, who ruled like Rambam to bless shehakol, for through this one exits according to all, as Beit Yosef and Bach wrote, and so several Acharonim transmitted for practice. And even on our sugar made from beets the world also practices to bless shehakol, and it appears they relied on Rambam's view and his reasoning because it changes through fire; but b'dieved if one blessed borei peri haAdamah on our sugar or on reed sugar, whether borei peri haEtz or borei peri haAdamah, he fulfilled [and so implies Levush, Avodat HaRokeach, and the Gra]. For behold the Rishonim mentioned who hold to bless borei peri haEtz on reed sugar, and there are more Rishonim who hold one blesses on them borei peri haAdamah, namely Terumat HaDeshen and Riaz in his laws, see there — and all agree on it; and Rambam too wrote that such is the view of all Geonim [only that he disagrees with them]; and in any case if one blessed borei peri haEtz or borei peri haAdamah he fulfilled. And it is astonishing on Taz, who advised to exit from doubt by taking borei peri haEtz and intending to exempt with his blessing the sugar — and it is wondrous why he was not concerned for the view of all these great ones who hold its blessing is only borei peri haAdamah, and per this even b'dieved he did not fulfill per all these great ones; and it would have been better for him to advise even per his view, that fundamentally like Tur, to bless borei peri haAdamah, through which one fulfills b'dieved even per Rosh and Tur — and so Avodat HaRokeach rejected Taz's words. And know that regarding sucking sugar reeds there are also views among poskim whether shehakol or borei peri haEtz or borei peri haAdamah — and therefore l'chatchila one should bless shehakol like Shulchan Aruch, and b'dieved if one blessed borei peri haEtz or borei peri haAdamah he fulfilled:
