And because, etc. As stated on 40a: “If one recited a blessing over fruits,” etc. Rosh. See Tosafos there, s.v. Rabbi Yehudah, and see seif 8 in the gloss.
Provided that, etc. As stated there concerning pepper and ginger.
And some say, etc. There: “Beis Hillel say,” etc. The Rosh, however, writes: “And all other trees, from when they produce fruit.” In his explanation, he writes that one cannot derive this law of blessings from the law of Shemittah, for carobs remain bitter even after their fruit clusters form and are not fit to eat. This is the meaning of the Shulchan Aruch’s words, “provided that,” etc. His words are difficult, for if so, all the more so when they merely begin to emerge they are still inedible, since that stage precedes the formation of the fruit clusters. It appears that the Rosh also agrees that carobs are judged from when their clusters form, but he mentioned only the other trees, following the concluding words of Beis Hillel: “And all other trees, from when they produce fruit.” See Rosh, chapter 6 of Berachos, seif 5. The fundamental ruling nevertheless requires study. What connection does this have to Shemittah? Even regarding Shemittah, one may not eat until the produce has become food, meaning when it reaches the stage of liability for maaser, at which point it is considered fruit. Shemittah itself, however, is not dependent upon the item’s being a fruit. It appears more correct that the law should depend upon the time of liability for maaser, when it is considered fruit, even regarding the blessing.
