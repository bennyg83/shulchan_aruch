"the reason" that one cannot learn from shevi'it for berakha, for carobs even from when they ripen they are bitter and not fit for eating — and thus he wrote in Shulchan Aruch "and only," etc. His words are rejected, for if so, all the more so from when it brings forth he does not yet eat, since it is before the shiur of "from when they ripen"; and it seems that Rash also holds thus regarding carobs from when they ripen, only that he did not mention except other trees as the end of the words of BeHaG "and other trees from when they bring forth" — see there in Rash chapter 6 of Berakhot note 65; but it requires further study on the fundamental law, for what connection is this to shevi'it, for even in shevi'it it is forbidden to eat until it becomes food, namely that they arrive at the season of tithes, for then it is fruit; but *shevi'it does not depend on fruit, and it appears more that it depends on the time of liability for tithes, when it is fruit even for berakha:
