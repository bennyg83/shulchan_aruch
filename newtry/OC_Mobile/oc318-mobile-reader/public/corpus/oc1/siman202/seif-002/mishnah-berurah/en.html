(11) To double the white — in its growth.

(12) borei peri haAdamah — for anything that grows on a tree and is not the main fruit — borei peri haAdamah [Tur].

(13) And since it is not known, etc. — for there are several kinds, large and small.

(14) One always blesses borei peri haAdamah, etc. — because b'dieved if he blessed borei peri haAdamah on borei peri haEtz he fulfilled, as below in siman 206. Nevertheless in such a case one should not eat from them an amount that obligates a concluding blessing except within a meal, since it is doubtful whether he should bless after them the abbreviated form of three or Borei Nefashot [see above and Magen Avraham].

(15) Very large — and if according to appearance it has already finished ripening, there is no distinction at all in its size, and in any case one blesses on it borei peri haEtz; and likewise if their pits are visible from outside, certainly it is close to ripening and one blesses on them borei peri haEtz according to everyone.

(16) From when they produce fruit — even though they are still very small.

(17) Unripe (afutz) — sour.

(18) Until it is unfit, etc. — it is clear from this that if they can in any case be eaten under duress, even though they are sour and bitter as is the way of fruits in their small stage, nevertheless one blesses on them their appropriate blessing; and Chayei Adam and likewise in Ollalot Ephraim questioned this law, and their view is that in such a case one does not bless except shehakol unless one cooked them or sweetened them by fire — for then one blesses on it borei peri haEtz since it is fruit in essence, only that it requires fixing.

(19) One does not bless on it at all — for it is considered like mere wood, and even if one fixes it by fire one does not bless on it except shehakol, as the law of pits that one sweetens below in seif 3. Chayei Adam wrote that therefore kinds of fruits that they spice and fry in honey and the like while still very small — if they were not fit for eating before their cooking at all — one does not bless on them except shehakol after their cooking; and see in Biur Halacha what we wrote regarding apples.

(20) Until one sees in them, etc. — for then it is considered fruit; but all this is also specifically if they were not very bitter, as stated above.

(21) One blesses, etc. — and likewise Magen Avraham and the Gra agreed; and see in Biur Gra that he is even more stringent in this matter, as we wrote in Biur Halacha.

(22) borei peri haAdamah — that is, even though they are sweet, they are still not the essence of the fruit.
