Seif katan 4. Borei Peri HaAdamah, because anything whose blessing, etc. In Even HaEzer he writes that one should eat it only during a meal because of uncertainty concerning the blessing MeEin Shalosh. Nevertheless, regarding a single grape whose size is less than the requisite measure, the doubt exists only because perhaps the halachah follows the Yerushalmi that one must recite an after-blessing upon an entire creation, as I wrote above in siman 210. In such a case there is a double uncertainty, and one may therefore be lenient even initially. At first glance one could say independently that since the fruit has not completed its development, it is not considered an entire creation even according to the Yerushalmi. This, however, is incorrect, as I explain below there. See Peri Megadim, siman 65, who questions the Shulchan Aruch's ruling that unripe grapes are considered fruit once they reach the size of a white bean, while other tree fruits are considered fruit from when they emerge. He argues that the prohibition against cutting them proves this only regarding orlah. In my view, since they are called fruit regarding orlah, they are likewise fruit regarding blessings. During Shemittah, with respect to other fruits that are not normally eaten before ripening, eating them earlier constitutes destruction. Nevertheless, if they are made fit by some preparation, they do not lose their proper blessing. What he cites from Lechem Chamudos, that when sweetened one recites Shehakol, refers to fruit that is extremely bitter or astringent and wholly unfit for consumption. Ordinary tree fruit, however, is edible with difficulty once it emerges, and through sweetening it becomes normally edible. Whenever one eats the actual fruit for which the tree was planted, such as unripe grapes, the argument that the tree was not planted for this purpose does not apply, as the Rashba writes in responsum 428. Sweetening it through fire therefore does not diminish its blessing. I wrote at length about these laws in my kuntres Olelos Ephraim, and have abbreviated here.
