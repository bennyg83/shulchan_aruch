Note 4 — borei peri haAdamah: every thing whose blessing, etc. And in Even haEzer he wrote that one should not eat except within the meal due to doubt of the blessing abbreviated form of three — see there. Nevertheless it is possible regarding a segment of grape that has no measure, only due to doubt that perhaps the law is like the Yerushalmi that after cutting one is obligated to bless, as stated above siman 211 — if so, in such a case there is a double doubt and one may be lenient even l'chatchila. And apparently, if not for this, one could say that since the fruit has not finished ripening it is not called cutting, even per the Yerushalmi; but this is as stated below there. And see in Peri Megadim siman 65, who wondered on the body of the ruling of Shulchan Aruch in this, that he ruled regarding boser, double white, and other trees from when they are brought forth — that we do not learn from that case, for we do not cut except for the matter of orlah, etc. — see there. It appears to me that since for orlah it is called fruit — if so, it is also fruit for the matter of blessing; and in the shemittah year, where there is no way to eat before the fruit finishes cooking in other fruits, it would be a loss; nevertheless, when sweetened by some thing he did not lose his blessing. That which he wrote in the name of Levush — when sweetened one blesses shehakol — is when it is bitter or extremely unripe and not fit for eating at all; but ordinary trees from when they are brought forth are fit under duress, and through sweetening it becomes a way of eating; and whoever eats from the body of the fruit that was planted on its account — such as boser, for they did not plant for this, etc., as Rashba wrote in his responsum siman 428 — if so, what is sweetened by fire does not reduce its blessing. And in Ohel Avraham Efrayim kontras I wrote at length on these laws and shortened here.
