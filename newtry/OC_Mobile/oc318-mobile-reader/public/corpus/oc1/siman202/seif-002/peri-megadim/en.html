And if — Taz, view of Bach z"l for medicine: even a little oil in anigran — all (Rabbi Yosef Yeres and Beit HaGedolah) agree oil is primary and bless only borei peri haEtz; and if he intends for eating and puts little oil — Rabbi Yosef Yeres agrees anigran is primary; and with much oil more than usual nevertheless (anigran is majority) for Rabbi Yosef Yeres borei peri haEtz and for Beit HaGedolah shehakol; and he deleted what Tur wrote that this is his medicine that he puts much oil — delete the word "much" — see there. And so from the Mechaber's conclusion here; and what Rashi Berachot 36a s.v. one concerned for his throat needs much oil and oil is primary — one must strain for Shabbat prohibition he said nevertheless permitted; and therefore he noted here and not "concerned for his throat"; but Taz holds for medicine too specifically when he puts much oil beyond measure then blesses borei peri haEtz, otherwise not; and there is proof: otherwise why did Rabbi Yosef Yeres not say in the Gemara it cites "concerned" to say even little oil and not as example. And know this great precision in Rabbi Yosef Yeres's words: otherwise whence for Tur to equate the dispute between Rabbi Yosef Yeres and Beit HaGedolah — is it not explained in Shulchan Aruch in the gloss we follow the majority, as Taz wrote letter 1, that wherever there are two berakhot we follow the majority? If so Rabbi Yosef Yeres deals with one who already blessed on anigran or on something shehakol and mixes oil with anigran — in this his reasoning is clear from his words: whatever is not harmful and its taste is sensed from Torah one does not bless borei peri haEtz; on the contrary exemption does not exempt obligation. And Beit HaGedolah deals where two berakhot are before him — shehakol and borei peri haEtz — in this specifically medicine oil is primary and bless only borei peri haEtz; and if sent for medicine we follow the majority and bless shehakol from what he would bless borei peri haEtz. It is difficult for Tur why Rabbi Yosef Yeres needs to strain to say the Gemara cites example, etc. — learn Rabbi Yosef Yeres holds even when two berakhot are before him oil is primary; and Tur agreed to his words. And what Rashba wrote we follow his intention, etc. — possibly to sweeten the matter, for he hinted to Rashba's difficulty and separated why Beit Yosef relied on Rashba also in Beit HaGedolah; and one can say Rashba Beit Yosef brought at the beginning of the siman we follow the majority (see seif 1 in the gloss — Tashbatz and Beit Yosef Rashba); if so Tur's proof is no proof; and if so it emerges from his words z"l innovative matters: (1) for medicine one needs much oil beyond usual; (2) if a little mixed in a majority we follow the majority — when two berakhot are before him — if he already blessed on beer shehakol and afterward mixed with beer and wine taste — bless borei peri haGefen, for exemption does not exempt obligation; (3) that even with intent when mixing, if there is a majority we follow the majority — and this is difficult from siman 204 note 1 Taz as written in letter 1; and to me all this requires further study — see in letter 1 from this. And in our Chiddushim for him letter 1 we expanded; and in Magen Avraham letter 29 it will be explained further; and here one should not expand — see letter 9 and 207; and in Yoreh De'ah it will be explained further.

Again a moment I will speak — for at first glance with much oil and little anigran one could say for everyone borei peri haEtz, even for Beit HaGedolah who says in such a case it is harmful azukei and like wine — appears similar; and see Chokhmat Shlomo in the name of Rav HaGaon R' Avraham Yitzchaki who distinguishes: on weekdays we require intent for medicine, but stam even if concerned for throat it is not proven he acts for medicine; on the contrary in his eyes it is better (even though harmful nevertheless for medicine it is); and on Shabbat one cannot in his eyes even stam also borei peri haEtz — see there. And the Mechaber's wording needs understanding, for in Beit HaGedolah he wrote "if they tied him" — intent for medicine is implied stam not; and in Levush likewise; and he intends for medicine; but the Mechaber wrote stam "concerned for his throat," etc. — possibly even stam like Rambam z"l chapter 8 laws of Berakhot halachah 2; and indeed Rambam concluded "but one not concerned for his throat" and the Mechaber concluded "but not intending for medicine" — an enhancement even if concerned, everything and somewhat contradictory to the precision; and if not intending for medicine but for eating — requires further study.
