Levush and apparently Derishah distinguish between dates mashed by hand, where some form of the fruit remains and therefore, according to Rambam, Borei Peri HaEitz is recited, and fruit preserves whose form is no longer recognizable. The Gemara says that terima is permitted but beer is prohibited with respect to terumah. One might therefore have thought that so long as it remains food, even when its form is entirely unrecognizable, it retains its original status, and that terumah and blessings are equivalent. The Gemara also states that wheat flour receives Borei Peri HaAdamah like olive oil, concluding that there is no further elevation possible. We thus see that even where the form of the fruit is wholly unrecognizable, it retains its status. Likewise, the liquid of cooked vegetables is treated as the vegetables themselves. It must be that those items are not ordinarily crushed, and on the contrary are generally eaten in their original form, so if one changes them he recites Shehakol. Olive oil, wheat flour according to Rabbi Yehudah, and the liquid of cooked vegetables are ordinarily prepared in that form. If so, what proof does Taz bring from ground spices in siman 203:7? Grinding is their normal manner, as Magen Avraham writes in his name in seif katan 18, and as stated in Terumas HaDeshen 29. There it also implies that the reason one should perhaps recite Shehakol on preserves is that honey and spices are mixed in, creating two disadvantages: the fruit's form has entirely changed and its taste has also changed. Where fruit has completely dissolved but its fruit taste remains recognizable, Terumas HaDeshen may agree that Borei Peri HaEitz is recited. Even if one brings proof from cooked fruit according to the Rosh, where Borei Peri HaEitz is recited although this is not its usual manner, one can answer that its taste remains recognizable, unlike preserves mixed with honey and spices. All this concerns the first blessing. The after-blessing is more difficult. If terima, a paste mashed by hand, receives Borei Peri HaEitz, its after-blessing is Me'ein Shalosh; if its first blessing is Shehakol, its after-blessing is Borei Nefashos. Siman 208 explains that Borei Nefashos and Me'ein Shalosh do not exempt one another. One solution is to eat another fruit of the seven species and also an item requiring Shehakol, and thereby cover both after-blessings. If only this terima is available, according to the Tur in siman 209, who holds that Me'ein Shalosh is D'Oraisa, one might recite it because of the doubt. According to us, however, since the matter remains doubtful, one should not recite either Borei Nefashos or Me'ein Shalosh. This requires study.
