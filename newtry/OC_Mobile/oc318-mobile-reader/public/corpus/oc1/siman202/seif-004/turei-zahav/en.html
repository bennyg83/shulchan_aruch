In the Gemara they establish the case of one who blesses on olive oil when he is concerned about his throat for its medicine — that he puts much oil into anigron and swallows. Rashi explained that he must put much oil in it because then the oil is primary and anigron is secondary. They challenge: it is simple — why might one think that since he intends it for medicine he should not bless on them at all? It teaches us: since he has benefit from it he must bless. Rashi explained that there is eating enjoyment aside from the medicine. Tur wrote, and R' Yosef explained: it is not specifically when he is concerned about his throat but rather they took the common case; likewise if he is not concerned about his throat, if he puts much oil into anigron he blesses on it, for he benefits from it. But Hagahot Maimoniyot wrote that he should bless only if it healed his throat, for he intends for medicine and benefits from it; but if there is no healing, even if he puts much oil, the anigron is primary and he has no blessing to bless on the oil. Tur concludes on this that it is more plausible like R' Yosef to bless even when he is not concerned, for since he enjoys — why should he not bless? On the contrary, it is more simple that he must bless when it is not for medicine than when it is for medicine. End of his words. It is clear the intent of Tur that they dispute this: certainly whenever there is a primary and a secondary one blesses only on the primary, but they dispute what is the primary. R' Yosef holds that the oil is primary, and therefore the Gemara took the case of one concerned about his throat — for then he certainly puts much oil, since the main healing is in that — from which, if without medicine he puts much oil, the law is likewise. Hagahot Maimoniyot hold that we follow intention: if it is for medicine, the main thought is on the oil; if it is not for medicine, the main thought is on the anigron — he blesses on the anigron and exempts the oil. Tur ruled here as Hagahot Maimoniyot. To me it is puzzling in the view of our master Tur: how can it be said that the oil is primary against the anigron, which is the majority? And how will the blessing that applies to the anigron — which is brine water and has the blessing shehakol — be exempt because of the blessing on the oil, which is borei peri haEtz? That which we say in the Gemara regarding one who drinks anigron and is concerned about his throat, that he needs a blessing since he benefits — that is in a case where he need not bless on the anigron, which is brine, such as when he does not enjoy that brine at all and they are only for medicine with the oil so that it not harm him, or that he already blessed on that type which is brine — and it turns out that now he lacks only the blessing on the oil. In this he said that he must bless on the oil, since he benefits from it in any case. If so, there is no dispute at all between R' Yosef and Hagahot Maimoniyot: R' Yosef speaks in a matter where he needs a blessing only on the oil that is mixed into the anigron beverage. In this he said that if there is much oil, whether by way of medicine or without medicine, he needs a blessing on the benefit; and therefore R' Yosef said he blesses on it, for he benefits — and he did not say that he benefits from it more than from the anigron, but rather it is certain that we pay no attention to the anigron, which needs no blessing; only the blessing on the oil is discussed. But Hagahot Maimoniyot speak where he needs a blessing on the anigron as well — then we follow the primary, and this depends on intention, that is, whether it is for medicine. It appears that our master Tur would not want to say thus, for if so R' Yosef would not have needed to say that the Gemara took the common case of one concerned about his throat, but would have said that it is specifically when he needs a blessing also on the anigron. Rather it is certain that even when he needs a blessing on it, the oil was primary — in this they dispute Hagahot Maimoniyot. It appears to me his reason: if there is much oil in the anigron, even though the anigron is the majority against the oil, nevertheless the oil is primary since there is also a significant amount from it — then the anigron is secondary to it even though it is more in quantity. Hagahot Maimoniyot hold that nevertheless we follow the majority, and in this goes the ruling of our master Tur in what he wrote that it is better when it is not for medicine than when it is for medicine. Rashba already similarly, whom Beit Yosef brings — that we follow his intention. Mo'ach z"l expanded on this here and concludes to emend in the words of Tur the word "much"; and in truth everything is correct as we remembered.
