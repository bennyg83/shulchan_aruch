4) A person holding one nut should not recite the blessing and then break the nut, lest its kernel be worm-infested or rotten, causing his blessing to have been in vain. Sefer Chassidim, siman 849. Knesses HaGedolah cites this in Hagahos HaTur and writes that the same applies to every fruit about which there is reason to fear that it is unfit to eat. If, however, it is beautiful and clean and there is no concern that it is unfit, it is a mitzvah to recite the blessing while it is whole. One need not be concerned about the delay while cutting it between the blessing and eating, as stated below in siman 206, paragraph 21; see there. A fruit that requires peeling should be peeled before the blessing to avoid an interruption. Even after the peel is removed, if the fruit itself remains unbroken, it is considered whole. Therefore, when peeling a fruit, one should take care not to break it, so that the blessing is recited while it is whole. Likewise, when opening a fruit to inspect it for worms, one should take care to close it again if possible, so that it appears whole at the time of the blessing. See below, siman 206, seif 3, and our words there, with the help of Heaven.
