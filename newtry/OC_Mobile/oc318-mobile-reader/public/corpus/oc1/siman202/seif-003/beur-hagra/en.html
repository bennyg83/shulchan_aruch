Seif 3, And if, etc. Rosh there, as explained above. Rashba, however, disagrees with Tosafos and writes that, on the contrary, pits are not considered fruit, since Scripture had to include them through the phrase “es hatafel,” just as it includes peels. He further writes that no blessing is recited over bitter pits, for trees are not planted with them in mind; if one sweetens them, however, etc., as stated in the Shulchan Aruch. This requires study, for the Shulchan Aruch combines the views of Tosafos and Rashba. See Magen Avraham. It is certain that the statement, “if one sweetens them,” reflects the view of Rashba, who disagrees with Tosafos. The principal view appears to be that of Rashba, for the Mishnayos in chapter 11 of Terumos, in Bikkurim, and in Uktzin all state without qualification that pits are not fruit at all. A further proof is from the law of neta revai. The fact that pits are forbidden as orlah necessarily derives from the scriptural inclusion of “es,” etc. This is therefore the principal ruling.
