This is also Magen Avraham's intent, for he writes that Levush says the same. See Taz in the name of Levush, where this distinction, that they were not planted for this purpose, is explicit, and likewise Beer HaGolah. Magen Avraham's statement that when the pits are bitter they are not fruit means that the trees were not planted for their sake. In truth, however, there was room to argue that even if the tree was not planted with the intention that its pits be sweetened, since one eats the actual principal substance of the fruit, this should not matter, as the Rashba writes regarding unripe grapes, where one eats the very fruit for which the tree was planted. Once we say that the pits themselves are considered part of the fruit, there should again be no distinction. Magen Avraham therefore writes that while bitter they are not considered fruit. He means that bitter pits are not considered the body of the fruit, such that one could say he is eating the fruit for which the tree was planted, as with unripe grapes. There, it is proper to regard it as the fruit itself, for although it is bitter now, it will eventually become sweet. Here, by contrast, although once sweetened one intends to eat the pits and they are then like the fruit itself, while bitter they are not included in the fruit at all, and the tree was not planted for them in any respect. In the aforementioned kuntres I wrote at length and concluded that if they were sweetened and one mistakenly recited Borei Peri HaEtz or Borei Peri HaAdamah, he fulfilled his obligation and should not recite another blessing because of the concern of a blessing in vain. Those blessings have authorities upon whom to rely. But when the pits were bitter and became fit only through fire, it appears that if one mistakenly recited Borei Peri HaEtz or Borei Peri HaAdamah and then remembered, he should say Baruch Shem Kevod Malchuso LeOlam VaEd and recite Shehakol over the pits. Although Abudraham writes otherwise even where they were sweetened by fire, since the Rashba rules that the blessing is Shehakol, the Shulchan Aruch follows him, and all the later authorities agree, this is the principal ruling and one may rely upon it to recite the correct blessing anew.
