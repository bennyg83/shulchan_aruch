There, note 7 — this requires further study regarding Rashba, etc. And see in Ohel Avraham Efrayim, who wrote that the reason is because they were not planted for this, etc. And in truth also Magen Avraham's intent is thus, for he wrote that the Levush likewise said so; and see in Taz in the name of the Levush, where this distinction of "they were not planted," etc., is explained explicitly; and so in Be'er Heitev. And what Magen Avraham wrote — when they are bitter they are not fruit — means that they were not planted for them, etc. In truth there was room to say that even though they were not planted with intent to sweeten them, nevertheless since one eats the body and primary of the fruit we do not care about this that they were not planted, as Rashba wrote in his responsum regarding boser — since one eats the body of the fruit that was planted on its account; and since we said that their pits are considered the body of the fruit again, one should not distinguish in this. Therefore Magen Avraham wrote: when they are bitter they are not fruit — meaning fruits whose pits are bitter we do not consider for them the body of the fruit, to say that this one eats the body of the fruit that was planted on its account, as with boser — for there it is properly applicable to say that it is the body of the fruit, even though it is bitter now and its end is to become sweet; not so here: even though when they are sweetened one's mind is on them and they are like the body of the fruit, nevertheless when they are bitter they are not in the category of fruit and people did not plant them with intent for them at all. And in the aforementioned kontras I expanded and brought that when sweetened b'dieved, if he blessed borei peri haEtz or borei peri haAdamah he fulfilled and he need not return and bless, for there is concern of a blessing in vain; and those blessings that he blessed borei peri haEtz or borei peri haAdamah have upon what to rely, unlike when bitter and sweetened by fire — it appears that if he erred and blessed borei peri haEtz or borei peri haAdamah and remembered, one should say in such a case that he bless on the pits shehakol. And even though in Avodah Zarah it is written that even when sweetened by fire, nevertheless since Rashba and likewise Shulchan Aruch ruled and all the Acharonim agreed to bless shehakol like him — we follow the primary view and one may rely on this to return and bless.
