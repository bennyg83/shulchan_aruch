(23) Sweet — not specifically, but anything that the palate enjoys from it somewhat; and the reason one blesses borei peri haEtz is because they too are part of the parts of the fruit; and many latter authorities disagree on this and hold that one does not bless borei peri haEtz, for they are not like the fruit itself, and one blesses on them only borei peri haAdamah; and such is the agreement of the Gra. And if he blessed borei peri haEtz he fulfilled b'dieved [Derekh HaChaim]; and if one eats the pit after eating the fruit, it is reasonable that according to everyone he is exempt with the borei peri haEtz blessing he blessed on the fruit, since it is secondary to the fruit.

(24) Bitter — meaning that they are not fit for eating even by duress, as stated above at the end of seif 2.

(25) One blesses on them shehakol — many latter authorities questioned this, and their view is that per the Mechaber pits are like the fruit itself — if so, also when bitter and one sweetens them by some thing, one should also bless borei peri haEtz on them, as with bitter almonds explained in seif 5; and some resolve that even per this view pits are not superior to almonds themselves that are the essence of the fruit, unlike pits; therefore if they are bitter it is not called fruit at all and sweetening afterward does not help; however for practical law one should not deviate from the ruling of Shulchan Aruch, for in any case we wrote previously the view of many latter authorities and the Gra among them to disagree on the fundamental law of the Mechaber, and per their view also sweet pits one blesses on them at most only borei peri haAdamah, for they are not fruit; and it follows that when bitter and one sweetens by fire one does not bless on them except shehakol.
