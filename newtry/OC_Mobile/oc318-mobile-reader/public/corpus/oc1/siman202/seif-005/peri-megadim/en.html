Honey. See Taz. The Rosh writes regarding date honey that one recites Shehakol. The Gaon said that this applies only when it was mixed with water, but alone it receives Borei Peri HaEitz. The Rosh writes that this distinction is not correct. Beit Yosef understood the Rosh to hold that squeezing and crushing are inferior even to liquid that flows out by itself, whereas cooking is superior to both. Therefore, according to our ruling here that date honey flowing by itself receives Shehakol, all the more so juice extracted by squeezing or crushing receives Shehakol. By contrast, in seif 10, according to the Rosh, fruit liquid produced through cooking receives Borei Peri HaEitz, while squeezed juice receives Shehakol. This is certainly the Rosh's view, because squeezed and crushed liquid is inferior to cooked liquid. Chiddushei HaRashba, however, implies that squeezing is equivalent to cooking, and he distinguishes between fruits ordinarily cooked or squeezed, whose liquid retains the fruit's blessing, and fruits not ordinarily prepared that way. The Mechaber in seif 10 follows the first opinion without qualification, namely Rashba, yet in siman 205:3 he rules that squeezed fruit juice receives Shehakol. He thus appears to adopt two conflicting approaches. I discussed this at length in my novellae. It is also reasonable that squeezing should be superior to liquid that flows out by itself. Dates, however, naturally exude liquid when they contain much oil, and that exudate is then like the essence of the fruit. This was the Gaon's argument from Scripture's praise of Eretz Yisrael as flowing with honey. When dates lack abundant oil and are squeezed, their juice is not equivalent. In any event, according to the Rosh, neither spontaneous exudation nor squeezing and crushing is equivalent to cooking. According to Rashba, squeezing is equivalent to cooking. Wine and oil, even when they flow by themselves, receive Borei Peri HaGefen and Borei Peri HaEitz. See also the Mishnah in Terumos 11:3, which lists date honey, vinegar made from late grapes, apple wine, and other fruit liquids. Why list these individually? According to the Rosh, this follows his view that squeezing and crushing are not equivalent to cooking. According to Rashba, apple wine is listed because apples are not ordinarily cooked or squeezed; but fruits ordinarily cooked or squeezed yield liquid with the fruit's blessing. Date honey excludes the Gaon's opinion, and vinegar from late grapes excludes the thought that the exudate is the fruit's essence. See Magen Avraham seif katan 20. I discussed this at length in my novellae.
