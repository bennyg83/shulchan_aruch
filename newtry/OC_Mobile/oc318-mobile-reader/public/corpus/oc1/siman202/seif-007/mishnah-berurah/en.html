(40) That one mashes by hand and made, etc. — explanation: even though it was completely crushed thereby, nevertheless one blesses borei peri haEtz, because since the substance still remains, it is fit to bless on them borei peri haEtz; and likewise regarding borei peri haAdamah, such as the view of the sages of the land of Israel that one mashes and makes them like dough.

(41) Called povidla — that they make from berries and other kinds from which they remove the pits and cook them until they are completely dissolved.

(42) And some say to bless on them shehakol — in this the latter authorities disagree: some hold "some say" does not dispute except regarding povidla because they are completely dissolved and their form is not recognizable at all; but in the Mechaber's law that deals with dates that were crushed, where their designation and form are recognizable even when completely crushed — they agree one blesses borei peri haEtz; and some hold "some say" disputes also the Mechaber's law and hold that since they were completely crushed one blesses shehakol. And for practical halachah there is no difference: for even if we say "some say" disputes even dates that were crushed, nevertheless for halachah we rule like the Mechaber's view that where substance remains their berakha did not change; only in povidla where all form was lost and it is not recognizable at all what it is — l'chatchila one blesses shehakol, and such is Rama's ruling that with this berakha one fulfills on everything b'dieved [however for the concluding berakha there is no advice if it happened regarding things that are among the seven species, as in siman 208 seif 13 that Borei Nefashot does not exempt me'ein shalosh and me'ein shalosh does not exempt Borei Nefashot unless he also eats fruit of the seven species and also something whose berakha is Borei Nefashot to exempt this too as well (Peri Megadim); and if he has none, it appears to me he can bless me'ein shalosh, for thus implies Rama's wording that it catches the essence of the law that its berakha is borei peri haEtz].

(43) To bless shehakol — and therefore kinds of berries and cherries and plums and the like that they squeeze and compound in honey and sugar — one blesses on them shehakol, since they are crushed and completely dissolved and lost their form; and b'dieved if he blessed with their appropriate berakha he fulfilled.

(44) Their water — and if the majority way of eating those fruits is by crushing, l'chatchila one blesses its appropriate blessing, whether for the matter of borei peri haEtz or borei peri haAdamah; such as a food cooked from gourds called "kirbes," and likewise ginger and other spices that are ground which one eats with sugar — they bless with borei peri haAdamah; and see below siman 208 seif 8 in the Rama gloss regarding these countries.
