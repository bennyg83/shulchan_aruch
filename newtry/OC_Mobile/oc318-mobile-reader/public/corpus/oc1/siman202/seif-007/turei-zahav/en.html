But if he blessed borei peri haEtz he fulfilled, for thus appears the primary. This primary is a clear primary, and there is no place to come and settle not to bless borei peri haEtz, for what Mahari wrote in Tosafot Harosh siman 29 — since Rashi wrote trimma one blesses borei peri haEtz, meaning pounded somewhat and not completely crushed — implies that when completely crushed they say shehakol. Beit Yosef already reached this at the end of siman 204 from what Rambam wrote, that even when mashed and made like dough one blesses borei peri haEtz. He also brought proof from the words of Tur siman 204 in his dispute with his colleagues, that all agree when the substance of the fruit remains, the fruit is primary, etc. And another proof from what Tur wrote siman 205, that even when cut into thin pieces he did not lose the blessing. Also here Tur wrote explicitly that R' Yosef agrees to the words of Rambam in this, and the precision of Tosafot Harosh on Rashi that we mentioned puzzles me; on the contrary, deduce from the Gemara the opposite: R' Asi said these dates of terumah are permitted to make trimma from them and forbidden to make beer from them — and if one could think that when crushed much its law is not like trimma, Rabbi Asi should have forbidden making beer, for he has already finished all the substance of the dates and there is only taste, as Tosafot wrote in chapter 28 page 38, s.v. this honey; and it would have been a praise that even if there is still substance but it is crushed much it is forbidden — and this is certainly a great proof that wherever the substance still remains there, it is still the fruit primary. From the words of Rashi there does not appear reason to be precise to forbid: for also Tur similarly said dates that are pounded somewhat, etc., and afterward concludes like Rambam that he made dough from them and they bless borei peri haEtz. That which Rashi took "pounded somewhat" appears that he came to explain the meaning of the word trimma — why it is called thus — and explained in this that they pound it somewhat and not entirely; such is the explanation of the word trimma, and it appears that it is like the form of trages that he mentioned in chapter 28 page 37 — Rashi explained that it is when cut one to three, and likewise in Greek they call three trei — and so is explained the intent of Rashi, that trimma means anything pounded somewhat and not completely crushed. End of his words. It implies that the way of explaining the language trimma everywhere is such, and the Talmud that took trimma here is because they were accustomed to make food in this manner from dates — and they took the common case; and likewise when well crushed the law is such. Also in Nedarim siman 57 they say one who vows from dates is permitted in date honey — implies that in pounded dates it is certainly forbidden all while the substance of the dates remains — and likewise here. Also nearby he brings even in the cooking water of fruits, that for Ra'ash one blesses borei peri haEtz if there is taste of the fruit; and even though he wrote there the language "and it is possible," etc., nevertheless here there is substance of the fruit — it is simple that it is clear. I hold that one who blesses l'chatchila borei peri haEtz on dried plums or cherries has no concern at all and did well, since the blessing borei peri haEtz has an advantage over shehakol, since it is a definite blessing as below. Also proof from what is written in seif 13 regarding spices that are ground and mixed with sugar syrup — the syrup is primary and they bless as required for blessing on those spices — even when ground completely they did not lose their blessing. Every day when we cook apples until they are completely crushed we bless on them borei peri haEtz, as written in seif 12, whether cooked, etc. — for certainly they are crushed through cooking — and there is no concern here of a blessing in vain, as I hold.
