(note 7) shehakol requires further study — for Rashba, in his method, etc.: Rashba and Rosh disagree regarding the berakha on pits, for regarding orlah it is explained in the mishnah at the end of the first chapter of Orlah and in Berakhot daf 36b that the law of orlah applies also to pits. And Rosh holds the reason is because it is fruit, and the same applies regarding berakha; but Rashba holds it is precise only regarding orlah that Scripture expanded it, as it is written "its fruit" and we expound to forbid even what is ancillary to its fruit; but it is not called fruit for berakha (and see above note 17). And since he holds that even sweet pits are not fruit, all the more so bitter ones that one sweetens by fire, or etc.; therefore Rashba ruled to bless upon them shehakol. But since the Mechaber ruled like Tosafot and Rosh that sweet pits are fruit, if so, also regarding bitter ones that one sweetens by fire one should bless borei peri haEtz.

And as he wrote in seif 5 regarding almonds — meaning do not think that regarding bitter ones, since initially they were not fit, even if one sweetens them now they are not called fruit; behold we see regarding large bitter almonds that if one sweetens them one blesses borei peri haEtz. And Rosh wrote that it is as if they were sweet from the outset.

And the same law applies here. And one must say: when they are bitter they are not fruit, etc., and so is in Levush. And thus Levush: pits of other fruits — the fruit upon them is the main fruit, and the pits are not the main fruit. Therefore if the pits are not fit for eating without fire, they are not considered fruit even after one sweetens them.

But almonds — the main fruit is the pit (meaning the kernel inside), and the beginning of their planting is for this, to sweeten it by fire, etc. — up to here his words. And one must say that he holds thus even according to Rosh, who considers the pit fruit; nevertheless, since it is not the main fruit, we require that it be fit for eating immediately without sweetening.
