[10] [Levush] one blesses on them, etc. So wrote Tur, and Bach wrote: this is his language: it is found at the beginning of the chapter "In every mixture" and at the end of the first chapter of Chullin regarding tithes, and the same law regarding blessing; and so wrote Rosh at the beginning of the chapter "How does one bless," end of his language. And a great wonder: in the Gemara there it concludes "this and this" to exempt from tithes, and so ruled Rambam chapter 1 of Hilchot Ma'aser, and Shulchan Aruch Yoreh De'ah siman 331 seif 74. And more wondrous: behold Rosh there wrote explicitly exempt from tithe, only that he distinguishes between tithe and blessing, that for blessing it is considered more since one enjoys from them. And from this matter it became clear to me Rosh's words and to remove the two wonders that stood: one that Adnei Melech wondered: if so, what does the Gemara challenge from one who said one eats caper peels orlah, and juxtapose on caper peels one says Borei Peri HaEtz: behold one can distinguish. And further he wondered: behold Ba'al Halachot Gedolot wrote: from the aspect of orlah it is not fruit, and for blessing too it is not fruit: if so there is no distinction for Ba'al Halachot Gedolot. And in truth it is a great difficulty, and so I found in Rashba's novellae daf 27 that he proved from the power of this difficulty that one may not distinguish between tithe and orlah and blessing; but I learned from there that specifically regarding the blessing Borei Peri HaEtz one may not distinguish, but regarding Borei Peri HaAdamah one may distinguish. And if so it appears in my humble opinion that Rosh's intent is to bless Borei Peri HaAdamah on bitter almonds, for it is comparable to caper leaves on which one blesses Borei Peri HaAdamah even though exempt from tithe: unlike what he wrote before him that one blesses Shehakol, meaning it does not refer at all to Ba'al Halachot Gedolot's words, who wrote before him that one blesses Borei Peri HaEtz. So it appears clear to me in Rosh's and Tur's words, and all Acharonim who did not address this left me room. However one must answer Ba'al Halachot Gedolot's view: behold we say in the Gemara "this and this" to exempt: and he wanted to say that possibly the explanation of "this and this" is that large almonds, whether bitter or sweet, are exempt, but small ones are liable: opposite Rashi's explanation that it refers to small ones. And with this it is also settled what Adnei Melech found difficult on Rosh, who challenged Rashi's explanation of exempt and liable that it implies it deals with one matter; therefore the Gemara explains "this and this" refers to bitter small and sweet large: and he challenged: behold also per Rashi's explanation it is difficult on Ba'al Halachot Gedolot. And per what I wrote it is settled that per Rashi's explanation nothing is difficult on Ba'al Halachot Gedolot, for one can explain the opposite in the Gemara: understand well. However one can wonder that Rashba contradicts himself, for I wrote above that he wrote one cannot distinguish on Borei Peri HaEtz between tithe and orlah: if so how did he rule there like Ba'al Halachot Gedolot to bless on small bitter almonds Borei Peri HaEtz; he himself explained in his novellae in the first chapter of Chullin "this and this" to exempt like Rosh's explanation. And until the wonder increased I would say that specifically regarding caper peels we say there is no distinction between blessing and tithe and orlah since it is finished: but regarding small almonds that are not finished there is a distinction, for Ba'al Halachot Gedolot and Rashba there refer to caper peels: but this is strained to distinguish thus. Further I found in Tashbetz siman 323 that he wrote on Ba'al Halachot Gedolot's words: however in Chullin we say "this and this" to exempt: end of his words, implying he rejects this and disagrees with Ba'al Halachot Gedolot; and so in Sefer Tzidah LaDerekh and Avudraham I found they wrote: this is their language: some say Borei Peri HaEtz and some say Shehakol: end of their words. Also Rosh's words themselves are not decisive for one who examines; and since great Acharonim did not decide, it is fit to bless from doubt Shehakol. But it appears to me to decide to bless Borei Peri HaAdamah on small bitter almonds like caper leaves, and this aligns with the plain sense of the Gemara and the reasoning of most poskim: understand and do not extend:
[11] [Levush] because I do not reject, etc. Malbushei Yom Tov wrote it is similar, only those who disagree: also on dates they disagree and he holds one should bless only Shehakol; and so is in Beit Yosef, and he proved thus from Rashi's explanation and Tur; and see siman 204 seif 11, and siman 207 seif 4: end of his words: and so Lechem Chamudot and Nachalat Tzvi challenged Levush. And in my humble opinion Levush explained well, for he delved into Tur's words, because it is difficult for him on Tur, who wrote first: dates that one crushed somewhat and are not completely mashed: Borei Peri HaEtz, and these are Rashi's words, which imply that when completely mashed one blesses Shehakol; and afterward he wrote per Rambam's words: dates that one mashed and made from them dough, etc.: Borei Peri HaEtz, even though Bach answered and wrote: this is his language: and Tur did not bring Rashi's view here because it seemed to him his words are rejected before Rambam and R' Yitzchak: end of his words. It did not seem to Levush to answer thus, for he found much difficulty on Bach: one, that Tur did not prove that R' Yitzchak holds like Rambam, but wrote "and it is possible that R' Yitzchak too admits"; and further, Tur's language that concluded "important like the essence of the fruit" per everyone is clear that Rashi too admits to Rambam, for otherwise the language "per everyone" would not fit well: understand. And further, in the abridged rulings of Rosh and Rabbeinu Yerucham I saw they ruled like Rashi. Therefore it seemed to Levush that Rambam and Rashi do not disagree: Rambam deals when they were not crushed so much that they became like a beverage and their appearance changed completely; but in pavedli that changed completely Rambam admits to Rashi's explanation, and Rashi deals with this; and so I found in Derishah, who wrote that Rambam speaks only when they did not dissolve and changed from the category of food, and also the appearance of a date remains somewhat. And unlike Beit Yosef, who wrote that Rambam disagrees with Rashi's explanation: end of his words. And with this Beit Yosef's wonder on Terumat HaDeshen in siman 204 is also removed: understand, for I abbreviated:
[12] One blesses Shehakol, etc. And Bach wrote: one scrupulous in his deeds should bless on them initially Borei Peri HaAdamah, and if he blessed Borei Peri HaEtz or Shehakol he fulfilled: end of his words. And his words are not clear to me; also in Nachalat Tzvi he contradicts his words. However what Nachalat Tzvi ruled to bless initially Borei Peri HaEtz: from what Beit Yosef wrote siman 204, and Taz too ruled it is not so, for I already wrote Beit Yosef's words do not appear , and Rambam and Rashi do not disagree; also they did not see the abridged rulings of Rosh and Rabbeinu Yerucham that I wrote above that they ruled like Rashi; however on mashed dates one can rule thus, and not for their reasons but as I explained that Rashi too admits; but for pavedli the law is like Levush and Rama's ruling; and what Taz extended on this lightly can be rejected: and one need not extend:
[13] The fallen (novelet) and they, etc. So Rashi explained in the Gemara Berakhot 40, and Rambam, R' Yitzchak, and Bertenora explained that they are fruits that fell from the trees unripe before they were cooked; and it appears both are true; and so is implied in Kolbo 98, who wrote: fruits that were burned and fell before their cooking: one blesses Shehakol: end of his language, meaning either they were burned or they fell: easy to understand. And behold in Demai chapter 1 Rambam explained on fallen dates of the palm: those that fall from the palm when the wind blows, and it is also mostly hefker: end of his words. And his explanation appears to me even if it falls after they were cooked: and as he concludes the reason "mostly hefker": meaning he explained here like the one who says "dates of wind"; but in Berakhot where the tanna taught fallen fruits anonymously, the explanation is like the one who says "cooked like myrtle," as the Gemara concludes. And I saw in Taz at the beginning of siman 204 that he wrote: since the Gemara in the latter language says "cooked": for the one who says dates of wind, this is, etc., and for the one who says cooked like myrtle it is difficult: therefore Tur and Rabbeinu Yerucham ruled like the one who says dates of wind, see there. And I did not descend to the end of his view, for one who examines the Gemara will see this refers to fallen dates mentioned in Demai; but regarding fallen fruits taught anonymously by the tanna in Berakhot it concludes it is cooked like myrtle; and so I found explicitly in Ba'al Halachot Gedolot: this is his language: fallen fruits that the tanna taught here: cooked like myrtle. And behold what I explained in Rambam, that he explained dates of wind after they were cooked, appears proved in the Gemara: for if the explanation of dates of wind is before they were cooked, what does it challenge for the one who says dates of wind: what species of curse is this, behold this is a species of curse that fell before ripening; also the Gemara challenges "Borei Peri HaEtz": he needs to bless, implying it deals after they were cooked. And Bertenora wrote in Demai: fallen dates: dates that do not cook on the tree, and one picks them and places one on another so they cook; and some say fallen dates of the palm that the wind cast before their cooking: end of his words. Behold the first explanation is taken from Rosh, who explained thus the one who says cooked like myrtle; and if so, for blessing too one blesses Shehakol, as explained in the Gemara there; and per this explanation Bertenora explained in Berakhot and the first explanation he explained in Demai are all one: everything not cooked is a sign of curse, and the same for unripe that fell from trees that one cooks to ripen: and so is implied at the beginning of the first chapter of Orlah; and therefore Bach and Perishah did not mention Rosh's explanation in the law of blessing Shehakol: understand; and so implies Lechem Mishnah in the chapter "How does one bless." And all this is to exclude from Sefer Be'er Avraham, who did not delve into the depth of the matters: one who wishes may study it, and I did not wish to extend. However one can wonder on Bertenora: behold the latter explanation, perforce like the one who says dates of wind; for if not, why did he also bring the explanation of dates of wind; and so is implied in Rosh: and if so one can wonder: behold it deals even if they fell after they were cooked, as I clarified above from the Gemara; and it is close to me that he followed Rambam's explanation afterward, and he holds Rambam's words in Berakhot and Demai are one. And it is not so, but in Berakhot he explained per the one who says cooked like myrtle, and in Demai per the one who says dates of wind: meaning the wind dried them after they were cooked.
