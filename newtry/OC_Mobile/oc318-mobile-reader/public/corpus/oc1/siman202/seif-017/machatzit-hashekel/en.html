R' Yishmael: mustard of orlah outside the Land (and orlah outside the Land is rabbinically forbidden); one throws away the avyonot and eats the kaparisim; and we say: evidently kaparisim are not fruit, since they are permitted regarding orlah. And we learned regarding kaparisim one says borei peri haEtz; and we answer: R' Oshaya holds like R' Akiva who disputes R' Eliezer, and holds kaparisim are exempt from ma'aser; evidently they are not fruit. And in Eretz Yisrael he does not hold like him, but outside the Land where the prohibition is only rabbinic he is lenient like R' Akiva.
And they challenge and teach that it is a guard for the fruit and forbidden because of orlah, as it is written "its fruit": "the ancillary to its fruit": and what is that? A guard for the fruit. And they answer that kaparisim are not a guard, for when the fruit reaches ripening the kapar falls. And the Gemara rejects this. And it concludes: what is the reason kaparisim are not a guard: for even if one takes the kapar from the fruit the fruit does not dry up; if so there is no need for the kapar's guarding. And some say for soft almonds: meaning sweet almonds if one eats them when soft, i.e., in their small state when their main eating is then: we rule in siman 204 one blesses shehakol on fruit peel; behold on fruit peel one blesses shehakol.
For there people were not planted with intent, etc.: only to eat them when their fruit finishes and they are large and the inside of the almonds is fit for eating.
Therefore it appears one should bless borei peri haEtz; and Taz in siman 204 note 15 wrote to bless on them borei peri haAdamah and brought proof that Tosafot cited only from here that we bless on pits borei peri haEtz and did not mention peels as well. Or that regarding peel one cannot learn berakha from orlah: meaning peel is not fruit for berakha but nevertheless orlah is forbidden because it is a guard as above, and on a guard for fruit one does not bless Borei, since it is not fruit; unlike pits which are obligated in orlah because they are not a guard: evidently they are fruit, and therefore Tosafot derived from them likewise for berakha to bless borei peri haEtz. At first glance Taz's words are reasonable. And not to make Magen Avraham mistaken on an obvious matter: one can say Magen Avraham inferred from Rosh in laws of orlah that in Berakhot there he wrote per Hagahot L'v Chamisha Assar: we learn pits of pomegranate etc., and pits are obligated in orlah because they are a guard, for we expound "its fruit": "the ancillary to its fruit"; but pits that are not a guard and are obligated in orlah: evidently they are fruit. From here one can say on fruit pits one blesses borei peri haEtz: end of his words; and this supports Taz's words. But Levush there challenged: see what Rosh wrote in laws of orlah: and pits of any fruit are forbidden in orlah, for we expound "its fruit," the ancillary to its fruit: end of his words. Per Levush it seems he holds that even though we expound from "et" to include ancillary to fruit, nevertheless it is called fruit; the amplification comes to include that it should be fruit, and nevertheless his wording here (meaning in Berakhot) does not imply so; and further if so why specify pits: even peels one should bless borei peri haEtz, since pits are included from "et" like peels, and we bless on them borei peri haEtz; if so peels too: yet our teacher did not write we bless borei peri haEtz except on pits: learn that he does not hold pits are included from "et" but it is mere reasoning that they are considered fruit. And as his wording implies here where he wrote "but pits": end Levush. And one can say Magen Avraham seized the essence of Rosh's words in laws of orlah, for otherwise in tractate Berakhot his wording is not so clear; and Rosh would hold pits too are not fruit by reasoning except they are included from "et" being called fruit; if so peels too, as Levush wrote. And thus what Tosafot and Rosh cited pits is not precise but peels too; and in winter days I said proof for this: the Gemara challenges R' Yishmael who said mustard of orlah outside the Land, etc.: since we are lenient like R' Akiva for ma'aser we should say the halachah follows R' Akiva for ma'aser outside the Land which is rabbinic: kaparisim from ma'aser; and automatically we would know for orlah outside the Land as well. And what is the difficulty: for even if we say kaparisim are exempt from ma'aser outside the Land, from that we know they are not fruit; nevertheless they could be forbidden for orlah because they are a guard for fruit, expanded in orlah from "its fruit"; but for ma'aser a guard is exempt from ma'aser; therefore it was necessary to teach that even for orlah they are permitted outside the Land because they are not a guard, as the Gemara concludes. And I recently saw this difficulty printed in Tzelach on Berakhot in the name of HaRav HaGaon R' Avraham part 2 and Maharam Fishels z"l; and per Magen Avraham and as understood from Rosh in laws of orlah: R' Yishmael holds everything included from "its fruit," even peels that are a guard, are expanded and called fruit; if so the question is proper: if we say exempt from ma'aser: evidently not a guard and not expanded from "its fruit," and automatically we know permitted regarding orlah. And know what Taz wrote there on Rosh that he holds one blesses borei peri haEtz even on kaparisim: it is explained there end of first chapter of Orlah in the mishnah: pomegranate peels etc. and pits are obligated in orlah and permitted in revai, and impossible also in revai they should be forbidden, and so challenged Peirush here regarding pits that one blesses borei peri haEtz; and so in Tzelach cited; and per my view in Peirush HaRash: well they questioned what he wrote the reason they are permitted in revai: we learn "fruit" "fruit" you redeem, etc.: evidently they are not fruit; but per R' Akiva in Rittenero: and revai planting revai is not forbidden in benefit etc. in ma'aser sheni and it is not sanctified with sanctity of ma'aser sheni but a thing fit for eating: end of his words; if so it deals with peels and pits that are not fit for eating but dyeing; therefore in orlah which is forbidden in benefit they are forbidden, but revai planting which is permitted in benefit is permitted; and Tosafot and Rosh derive from this pits fit for eating one blesses borei peri haEtz; see in Sefer Ohr Rashi what he wrote regarding m'arnatzan.
