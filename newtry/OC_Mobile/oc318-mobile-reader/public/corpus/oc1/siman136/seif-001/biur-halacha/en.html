On Shabbat and Yom Tov, etc. — and now we shall arrange the obligations that the parnas is required to assign to be read before others, following the enactments and customs that our great rabbis established, and as explained in Levush and Magen Avraham in siman 282 and Shaarei Ephraim. A groom on the day of his wedding. A groom on the Shabbat before the wedding when they sing for him. A youth who becomes bar mitzvah on that Shabbat. A husband whose wife gives birth on Shabbat and goes to synagogue. A groom on the Shabbat after the wedding. Yahrzeit on Shabbat when it is the day his father or mother died. A father of a male child born on the Shabbat before the circumcision — and there are places where they practice that the mohel and sandek are also obligated, and there are those who do not practice thus but only honor them with hagbahah of the Torah scroll. A groom on the day of his wedding — that is, on a weekday when the wedding feast is held on that day, he precedes all obligations, even a youth who becomes bar mitzvah on that day, and all the more so he precedes yahrzeit. A youth who becomes bar mitzvah in that week — if he is an irani (meaning he is a resident of the city), he precedes all obligations since it is the time of his education; and when they call a bar mitzvah youth, his father recites the blessing "Blessed is He who released me from the punishment of this one," as below in siman 225 — except for a groom for whom they sing, who is equal to him and they cast lots; and if the youth who becomes bar mitzvah is not a resident of the city, even though they are obligated to call him, nevertheless he does not override any obligation of the city's residents. The groom on the Shabbat before the wedding is also before all obligations, even if the wedding will not be in that week — for example, if he goes to hold his wedding in another city far from here — he also overrides all obligations; and this applies only to one for whom they sing, but a widower for whom they do not sing is not obligated on the Shabbat before the wedding even if she is a virgin; and in Shaarei Ephraim he wrote that if there are no obligations, he should be called before others. And if the groom had thought the wedding would be in this week and afterward the wedding was postponed to another week — if he wants them to sing for him again, he is obligated to call him then, and if not, he is not obligated. One whose wedding was from Wednesday onward is obligated on the Shabbat after it and overrides yahrzeit and the father of the son before circumcision — and this applies only when he is a bachelor or married a virgin; an elderly man is not obligated. And one whose wife goes to synagogue precedes this groom, even a bachelor who married a virgin; and in Shaarei Ephraim he wrote that even if the wedding was in this week before Wednesday, although he does not override another obligation, nevertheless he should be called before others. A bachelor who married a virgin precedes one who married a widow; a widow precedes a chalutzah; a divorcee. Yahrzeit is an obligation and is overridden because of other obligations, except the obligation of the father of the child before circumcision — and even if the circumcision is on that Shabbat, it is overridden because of yahrzeit, since she will eventually go up on the Shabbat when she goes to synagogue; and if the yahrzeit is not on Shabbat itself but in the week after, the father of the child is not obligated to override, and nevertheless they practice calling him when there is no other obligation. The father of the child on Shabbat when his wife is healthy and goes to synagogue is obligated to call him; but if she is ill and cannot go, he is not obligated until she goes. And if it is the thirtieth day for a male or eightieth for a female, he is obligated even though she does not go, since it is the time of bringing the sacrifice; and the miscarrying woman, her husband is obligated unless she miscarried a form that we are not expert in. On Shabbat when his wife goes to synagogue — meaning whether for a male or female child — he precedes all obligations except the groom on Shabbat for whom they sing and the youth who becomes bar mitzvah, who precede, as stated. They practice calling the shofar blower to ascend to the Torah on Rosh Hashanah; and there are places where the chazzan who prays Musaf on Rosh Hashanah is also obligated to ascend to the Torah on that day when he prays — and this applies only if he blows and prays for free; but if he comes for wages, he is not obligated at all. And there are places where on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur they do not pay attention to obligations except to volunteers with a favorable eye regarding charity. If one bought one aliyah and the parnas remembered that there is an obligation that must be called, he may retract, for it is a mistaken sale. If there are two equal obligations, they cast lots — except if one is a Torah scholar, he precedes. One who was ill and recovered, etc., who must recite the blessing of Hagomel — he should be called before others, since they practice blessing at the time of Torah reading; nevertheless he does not override any obligation, since by law he can recite the blessing with ten even without ascending to the Torah scroll — he should do so and not override the obligation because of him. They practice calling one who is about to travel and will delay after Shabbat, or one coming from the road; and likewise they practice sharing honor with a distinguished guest and calling him — and this is good and proper; but they do not override any obligation from the mentioned obligations; and plainly that in a place where the custom is to sell aliyot and the money falls to charity — for those who are not among the obligations but only because of custom — one should not lose charity because of them; and nevertheless it depends on the parnas's discretion; and it appears from the Acharonim that even in a place where they practice selling all aliyot and not paying attention even to obligations — nevertheless a groom on the Shabbat before the wedding and likewise a youth who becomes bar mitzvah — they do not override them. And it appears to me that also the rest of the above obligations should not be overridden at all, since it is an ancient custom; rather, at minimum they should give them from the additional aliyot. And the rest of the details of all these matters, see in Shaarei Ephraim.
