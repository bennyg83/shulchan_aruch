And after them, Torah scholars, etc. Then come the sons of Torah scholars whose fathers hold communal office, and afterward the communal leaders, etc., as the Tur writes. The Levush writes that in these lands, where mitzvah honors are sold, the purchaser may call whomever he wishes, provided that each person is called according to his honor. The Shach in Choshen Mishpat siman 382 cites Maharshal, Perek HaChovel, siman 60, that if someone purchased the third, sixth, or seventh aliyah and another person preempted him, the latter must pay ten gold pieces if the purchaser seized the money. The Shach adds that Maharshal's words are not conclusive. Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah understood Maharshal as speaking even if the money was seized, but he overlooked the Shach just cited. Maharshal there also explains that the same applies where Reuven was called to the Torah and Shimon preempted him and recited the blessing. All this applies where Shimon prevented Reuven from answering Amen, or recited the blessing quietly. If Reuven could answer Amen, Shimon is exempt. It seems that Maharshal's ten gold pieces means ten for each blessing, which would total twenty. So writes the Perishah in Yoreh Deah siman 28. Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah emends Maharshal's text to say twenty, but this is unnecessary. See Responsa Nachalat Shivah, siman 38, where all the above sources were apparently overlooked; the proof cited there from Tosafot Chullin 87 is surprising, and there is no need to elaborate. Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah writes that where there are many bridegrooms and all are Yisraelim, one marrying a virgin precedes one marrying a widow; one marrying a widow precedes one marrying a chalutzah; and one marrying a chalutzah precedes one marrying a divorcee, provided they are all equal in Torah knowledge. See below, end of siman 282, concerning priorities among those obligated to receive aliyot. In Eliyah Zuta I recorded regulations instituted here in Prague by the gaon Maharal. I saw the following order: first, a bridegroom on his wedding day or on the Shabbos after the wedding; then a bar mitzvah boy; then two shoshvinim; then the sandak; then the mohel; then the husband of a woman who gave birth; then one observing a yahrzeit. This requires study whether the shoshvinim have priority even on the Shabbos after the wedding, especially since the Levush in siman 282 does not mention shoshvinim. Magen Avraham there writes that if the woman who gave birth is ill and cannot come to the synagogue, her husband has no obligation until she does come. Nevertheless, at forty days after the birth of a male or eighty days after the birth of a female, he has an obligation, because that is the time for bringing the offering. If his wife died, he no longer has that obligation. The husband of a woman who miscarried has an obligation unless she miscarried a form whose halachic status we cannot determine; then he does not displace another obligated person, since perhaps it was not a fetus. Responsa Nachalat Tzvi, siman 38, writes that even in a place where it is customary to call a communal leader, one observing yahrzeit precedes him, and in such a matter a person may take the law into his own hands because the loss cannot be recovered. Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah further writes that in places where individuals own Sifrei Torah and, when they have a celebration such as a week of a son's birth, a wedding, or the honor of lighting, they take out their own Sefer Torah for the day's reading, the following rules apply when two celebrations coincide and both have Sifrei Torah. A bridegroom precedes one with the lighting honor; the father of a newborn precedes the bridegroom; a baal brit precedes the father of the newborn. If there are two fathers, two baalei brit, or two bridegrooms, a Torah scholar who is a halachic decisor precedes a Torah scholar who is sharp in analysis but not a decisor; a Torah scholar precedes an unlearned kohen; a kohen precedes a Levi; and a Levi precedes a Yisrael. If two fathers are otherwise equal, the one whose child was born first takes precedence, since he acquired the mitzvah first. The custom in this city is likewise that the child born first is circumcised first. Between bridegrooms or those with the lighting honor, or even between fathers of children born at the same time, they cast lots. One who owns a Sefer Torah that he personally purchased and had written precedes one who inherited a Sefer Torah from his ancestors. One who has a Sefer Torah in this congregation precedes one whose Sefer Torah is elsewhere and who wishes to bring it for that Shabbos. The Levush's statement that each person should be called according to his honor is a significant indication, as Lechem Rav concludes, and Responsa Maharam Mintz, siman 6, implies the same. If the gabbai refuses to call one of the scholars out of spite, the communal officers should enact a regulation and remove him from his position. See there at length.
