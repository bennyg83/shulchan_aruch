[1] After Torah scholars come the sons of Torah scholars whose fathers are appointed over the community, and after them the communal leaders. This is the order stated by the Tur. Levush writes that in places where mitzvah honors are sold, the purchaser may call whomever he wishes, but each person should nevertheless be called according to his honor. Shach, Choshen Mishpat siman 382, cites Maharshal, Perek HaChovel, siman 60, that if one purchased the third, sixth, or seventh aliyah and another person preempted him, the latter is liable for 10 gold coins if the purchaser seized them; Shach writes that this is not compelling. Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah did not see the words of Shach. Maharshal discusses a case where Reuven was called to the Torah and Shimon preempted him and recited the blessings. He is liable only if Reuven was unable to answer Amen to Shimon's blessing or to recite the blessing quietly for himself. If he could answer Amen, Shimon is exempt. It appears that where liability applies, it is 10 gold coins for each blessing, thus 20 in all, as Perishah writes in Yoreh De'ah siman 28. There is therefore no need to alter Maharshal's text. Responsa Nachalat Shivah, siman 38, did not see these sources, and the proof it brings from Tosafos, Chullin 87a, requires consideration. Where several bridegrooms are present and all are Yisraelim, precedence is given, when their Torah standing is equal, in the following order: one marrying a virgin, then one marrying a widow, then one marrying a chalutzah, then one marrying a divorcee. See siman 282 regarding other rules of precedence among those obligated to receive an aliyah. The enactments attributed to Maharal of Prague list among those with a special claim: a bridegroom on the wedding day or on the Shabbos following the wedding, a bar mitzvah boy, the 2 shoshvinim, a sandak, a mohel, the husband of a woman who gave birth, and one observing a yahrzeit. The status of the shoshvinim after the wedding requires consideration, since Levush in siman 282 does not mention it. Magen Avraham writes that if a woman who gave birth is ill and cannot come to the synagogue, her husband has no special obligation to receive an aliyah until she comes. At 40 days after the birth of a male, or 80 days after the birth of a female, he has an obligation because of the offering associated with childbirth. If his wife died, he has no such obligation. A husband whose wife miscarried has a claim unless there is uncertainty whether a recognizable fetal form had developed. Nachalat Tzvi, siman 38, writes that a yahrzeit takes precedence over a communal leader, and that one may take steps to preserve this right because once the day passes the opportunity cannot be recovered. Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah records the following order in cases of competing honors connected with Sifrei Torah and communal mitzvot: a bridegroom precedes one holding the lighting honor; the father of a newborn precedes the bridegroom; a baal brit precedes the father of the newborn. Where 2 fathers, 2 baalei brit, or 2 bridegrooms are otherwise equal, a Torah scholar who is a halachic decisor precedes a Torah scholar who is sharp but is not a decisor; a Torah scholar precedes an unlearned kohen; a kohen precedes a Levi; and a Levi precedes a Yisrael. Between 2 fathers of equal standing, the one whose child was born first takes precedence, since his mitzvah came first. The custom of that city was likewise that the child born first receives the brit milah first. Between equal bridegrooms, equal holders of the lighting honor, or fathers whose children were born at the same time, they cast lots. One whose Sefer Torah he personally purchased and had written precedes one who inherited a Sefer Torah. One whose Sefer Torah is regularly in that congregation precedes one whose Sefer Torah is elsewhere and who wishes to bring it there for that Shabbos. Levush's statement that each person should be called according to his honor strongly supports these rules, as Lechem Rav concludes, and Responsa Maharam Mintz, siman 6, implies likewise. If a gabbai refuses to call a Torah scholar out of spite, the communal officers should enact a regulation and remove him from his position.
