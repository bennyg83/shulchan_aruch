One who sleeps See the Taz and the Beit Yosef, that the Tur's wording is abbreviated. For this reason, with a bed there is certainly a roof above the one lying beneath it, one tefach wide or more, and ten tefachim high, meaning from the roof over the sleeper down to the ground. Otherwise, even though the bed as a whole is ten tefachim high, it is valid. In letter 1 and in the Bach the dispute is mentioned. It implies that according to the Rif, Rambam, and Rosh, one who sleeps under the bed is invalid by Torah law like sukkah beneath a sukkah, for this also applies to invalid s'chach: the bed is invalid because it was not made for shade but to lie upon, and even if it was set up for shade, it is rabbinically invalid when it is four tefachim distant because of the decree of a ceiling. According to the Razah, it is rabbinic, because it appears that he is standing in the shade of an ohel and not in the shade of the sukkah. A practical difference is on the first night, if he has no place except under the bed; see Tosafot and the Yerushalmi regarding Tavi. See siman 629, Magen Avraham 22, and the general introduction: with something rabbinically invalid, if he has no other, he performs the mitzvah and by Torah law fulfills it, and perhaps even blesses, because in such a case they did not decree. There we wrote that one can distinguish between a decree and something that appears as marit ayin or the like. Also on the second night, he would not recite shehecheyanu. According to the one who says the invalidity is Torah-level, in such a case he does not sit under the bed on the first night, and on the second night he recites the time blessing. However, even according to the Rif, Rambam, and Rosh one can say that it is not Torah-level invalidity but rabbinic, because it resembles sukkah beneath a sukkah. The Bach did not write this, and it requires examination in practice. One who studies the Milchamot will see that sukkah beneath a sukkah is when the upper s'chach is invalid s'chach that is not for shade, for there is a lower one; and according to Shmuel in Sukkah 10a, why do I need ten tefachim between them? We rule like him in siman 628. In the chapter HeYashen, Rabbi Yehudah follows his view that a temporary ohel does not come and nullify a fixed one. This requires examination. See the Ramban about this, and Rashi on the Mishnah.
