And if it is not for beauty, etc. - This wording is also found in the Tur, and it derives from the words of the Rosh; see there. It means that it is not specifically because of falling leaves that it is invalid, but even not because of falling leaves, anything not for beautification is invalid, for it is not nullified to the sukkah. See the Biur HaGra, who includes in this one who spread the sheet in order to dry it, which is the need of the garment, that it is invalid. This requires examination, for according to this the Tur contradicts himself: in this siman he rules that for the need of the garment it is invalid, while at the end of siman 629 it is proven that he holds that for the need of the garment it is permitted, as the Darchei Moshe wrote there. Regarding the Shulchan Aruch itself, although he too copied the Tur's wording at the end of siman 629 and his intent there is certainly to be lenient even at the time of eating, as we proved there, it is not difficult for me. Perhaps his words there refer only according to the some say view that he first brought, which is Rabbeinu Tam's approach, and according to that view it is certainly permitted for the need of the garment, as the later authorities wrote, and not according to the first opinion mentioned there at the beginning. But the Tur, who mentions there only this view, is difficult. I found in the Bach here that he wrote: see below at the end of siman 629, where our teacher holds that it is not specifically for beauty, but the same applies when he placed it for a person's need, to protect him, or for another need; it has the law of beautification. These are his words. It is proven from him that when the Tur wrote and if it is not for beauty, he means that it was not for any need at all, and this is invalid by law. But where it is for the garment's need, it is indeed permitted, as he wrote explicitly at the end of siman 629; see there. But according to the Gra's words, this requires examination. Perhaps one can answer with difficulty according to what the Gra wrote, that the Mechaber in siman 626:1, where he wrote that in all this there is no difference between whether the tree preceded or the sukkah preceded, ruled plainly like the first opinion and unlike Rabbeinu Tam. This wording is also written by the Tur there; see there. According to this it works well: the Tur in siman 627 follows what he copied as practical law in siman 626, unlike Rabbeinu Tam, that there is no advantage in the sukkah having preceded, since the sheet had more shade than sun. According to that, protecting from the sun and leaves is forbidden, and he holds that the same applies for the garment's need. But here, where he copied Rabbeinu Tam's approach inside, he concludes that for the garment's need it is also permitted. The Beit Yosef already wondered about the main matter: why in siman 626 he copied the law unlike Rabbeinu Tam, while here he copied Rabbeinu Tam's approach. See the Bach and the glosses of Maharlach.
