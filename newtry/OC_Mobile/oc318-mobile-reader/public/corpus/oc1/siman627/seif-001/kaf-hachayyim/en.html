1) [Seif 1] Rama's gloss: One must sit in the open space of the sukkah. So wrote the Tur: Just as we require that the sukkah be beneath the open sky, with no other s'chach above it, so too we require that the person be in the open space of the sukkah, with no other covering interposed between him and the sukkah, end quote. The Bach wrote that the Tur first said, Just as, etc., in order to explain why the Sages forbade sleeping beneath a bed, etc. He stated that it is like a sukkah beneath another sukkah, in which one does not fulfill his Torah obligation even after the fact. So too wrote the Rif and the Rosh in the chapter HaYashen, and the Rambam in his Commentary to the Mishnah and in his code. However, the Razah wrote that the reason is that when it is ten tefachim high it constitutes a tent, and the person is not standing in the shade of the sukkah but in the shade of the tent formed by the bed. According to him, the prohibition is rabbinic, but according to the Torah one fulfills his obligation. The Ramban rejected this and said that, if so, the same should apply even where it is not ten tefachim high, since it constitutes a tent with regard to other matters. Rather, the reason is that this tent comes and nullifies the s'chach of the sukkah, since it is significant, and the Sages derived this rule from the law of a sukkah within a sukkah, end quote. Nevertheless, the Mishbetzos Zahav, sec. 2, wrote that it is possible that even according to the Rif, Rambam, and Rosh, it is not invalid according to the Torah, but only rabbinically, see there. It follows that this matter is subject to a dispute. A practical difference concerns one who can only sleep beneath a bed or beneath an invalid canopy. It is preferable for him to sleep beneath the bed, etc., rather than inside the house, since according to one opinion he fulfills the Torah obligation.
2) Ibid. One who sleeps beneath a bed, etc. For the primary forms of dwelling in the sukkah are eating, drinking, and sleeping. Rashi, Sukkah 20b. The ruling mentions sleeping because, in any event, it is not the normal manner to eat or drink beneath a bed or beneath a canopy. See below, siman 639, seif 2.
3) Ibid. If it is ten tefachim high, etc. In seif 2, two conditions are mentioned: it must have a roof one tefach wide and be ten tefachim high. Only then is it considered an independent tent. The same applies here regarding a bed. It was unnecessary to mention a roof here, because every bed certainly has a width of one tefach, and it serves as a roof for one who lies beneath it. Taz, s.k. 2.
4) Ibid. If it is ten tefachim high, etc. Even if the sides of the bed are extremely narrow and do not have even a width of one tefach, it nevertheless has the status of a tent. This is implied by Shulchan Aruch HaRav, sec. 1, and so wrote the Mishna Berurah, sec. 3. Therefore, one must take care not to sleep beneath a table that is ten tefachim high. Ben Ish Chai, Parashas Haazinu, sec. 1.
