In Magen Avraham, se'if katan 1: If it is not high, etc., and you will see that there is a scribal error in his words. In my humble opinion this requires examination. Even if we emend the text to read, and also the sheets do not reach, etc., nevertheless there the reason for the prohibition is that the bed is like a roof in the middle of a canopy, and it is forbidden to stretch it out because under the bed there would be an ohel. But here, where he sleeps under the canopy and not under the bed, what do the sheets matter? However, it seems that the Agudah holds that whenever the partitions reach the ground, even though within less than three tefachim there is not a width of one tefach near the top, it is nevertheless an ohel. This also seems from the Agudah's words in Shabbos, chapter Tolin, where he wrote: or he should not make the roof one tefach wide within three tefachim, and also the walls should not reach the ground; if he makes it this way, he may sleep under it in the sukkah, as written, etc. There is a scribal error here, and it must read: and also the sheets do not reach there, as I wrote above. His words imply that both are required: the roof should not be one tefach wide, and additionally the walls should not reach the ground. But from the words of Tosafot and the other poskim it does not seem so. According to the version in Agudah in Sukkah, where he wrote: if it has no roof, such as where it is not one tefach wide within three of its roof, and also the sheets that reach the ground do not have a partition of one tefach, even though it is ten high it is permitted to sleep in it, etc., it would appear that the text should read: and also the sheets do not reach the ground; there is no partition of one tefach. In our Magen Avraham the text reads, that they do not have a partition, implying it refers to the sheets, but this is a scribal error and should read, that it does not have, referring to the canopy. So it is in the Bach. Meaning, since the roof has no tefach, automatically even if the sheets reach the ground, nevertheless there is no partition of one tefach here, because sloping tents are not treated as tents. But the Agudah's words in Shabbos require examination. See Mekor Chayim, where it should read Agudah. See the Bach in siman 315, and you will see, etc.; but this is not so. Afterwards I saw in the Machatzit HaShekel that he seems to have understood that what the Magen Avraham meant by siman 315 is what is written in the Shulchan Aruch: and if it is lowered from the bed, etc. But this is not so, as I wrote above, for that is not relevant here, and he himself wrote this. He explained the Magen Avraham's intent there as meaning that it is explicit there that reaching the ground is better; see there in se'if 3 and in the Taz there.
