(א) Magen Avraham s.v. the taste, etc. As is written in siman 553: he wrote there that if he knows that on motza'ei Shabbat night he will not have wine — and in such a case Magen Avraham wrote there — he is permitted to make havdalah on Shabbat from the afternoon onward. And if so here too on motza'ei Shabbat he cannot make havdalah since it is forbidden to drink the havdalah cup — he should make havdalah by day from the afternoon onward, for there is no greater compulsion than this:

(ב) And if so it implies, etc., that it is like the next day. Meaning the Tur in siman 553 brought in the name of Ramban that from when he began eating the interrupting meal bathing is forbidden; but in eating and putting on shoes and other matters he is not forbidden until darkening, for his acceptance does not help since it is still day. And Tisha B'Av has no addition, but in bathing nevertheless it is forbidden, since he benefits from the bathing afterward and then is his main benefit and it appears as bathing for the sake of Tisha B'Av. However the Rosh disagrees with him and we rule like the Rosh. Nevertheless here it is proven (for these matters Magen Avraham wrote, since he made havdalah he accepted Tisha B'Av upon himself — they are the words of the Tur in the name of Ramban) that from the afternoon onward the Ramban agrees, etc., and even in eating it is forbidden for it is like the next day itself; and even though Tisha B'Av has no additions — for the Ramban wrote he accepted it upon himself and it is forbidden to drink. And so too Beit Yosef siman 26, and so he wrote: Ramban wrote in Sefer Torah HaAdam, etc.; but one may say that the acceptance forbids him at this time which is from sunset, and from the afternoon onward since he can make it an addition on Torah days (meaning Yom Kippur) he can also make it on rabbinic days since he wished to add it upon himself and make it like the day itself — end of his words. Not so with circumcision, etc., meaning even though it is possible that several years in succession there will be circumcision on every Tisha B'Av and since they give to children — lest he come to tie himself up; nevertheless since it is not a fixed matter, for it is possible that in several years there will not be circumcision on Tisha B'Av, the Sages were not concerned:

(ג) See in Yoreh De'ah siman 341 and siman 396 in Taz who juxtaposed the words of Shulchan Aruch there with each other: the Rosh in chapter Mi SheMeto brought a dispute that R' Yehuda was an onen on motza'ei Shabbat and was not permitted to make havdalah; and the next day after burial he also did not make havdalah and gave a reason like one who girded on the first day of Yom Tov who is exempt from the festival offering, and if his girding spread to the second day nevertheless he is exempt since it was not fit on the first day — also on the second day which is make-up of the first he is exempt. And here too since day one is make-up for havdalah which should have been done on motza'ei Shabbat, and since he was then an onen and exempt he has no make-up:

(ד) And Maharam disagreed with him that he is obligated to make havdalah after burial. And Rav Bet Yosef ruled in siman 341 like Maharam that if he was an onen on motza'ei Shabbat he is obligated to make havdalah after burial; and in siman 396 Rav Bet Yosef ruled regarding a minor who reached majority within seven days that they disagree — Maharam and Rosh — whether he is obligated to mourn: the Rosh exempts for the reason stated that it is like girding that spread. And Maharam obligates him, for he goes after his reason that he obligates him also in havdalah and does not learn from this case of girding. And Rav Bet Yosef ruled in siman 396 that a minor who reached majority within seven days is exempt from mourning like the Rosh — and this contradicts what he wrote in siman 341 regarding havdalah, one reason for both. And this question is difficult here too, that he contradicts himself in what he ruled that one should make havdalah at motza'ei Tisha B'Av with what he ruled in Yoreh De'ah siman 396 regarding a minor who reached majority that he is exempt. And in the resolution that Taz wrote there, here too it is resolved: Rav Bet Yosef ruled from Maharam Tiktin that a minor who reached majority is exempt, for the Sages placed the main obligation at the time of main bitterness, meaning when the stone was sealed; and from there onward he mourns and continues until the end of seven. And this minor who was exempt at the beginning of bitterness — when the obligation then began — the obligation did not apply to him again, not because it is make-up. Not so havdalah: initially the Sages enacted that one begin at motza'ei Shabbat until the end of day three, and not that from day one onward it is make-up for motza'ei Shabbat; and also motza'ei Shabbat is not the main time — therefore even though he was an onen on motza'ei Shabbat he is obligated to make havdalah after burial at present, and the same may be said here on Tisha B'Av:

(ה) Nevertheless the words of Rav Bet Yosef are settled on this; but in any case the words of the Rosh require study, that he ruled regarding an onen on motza'ei Shabbat like the view of R' Yehuda that he does not make havdalah after burial. And here the Tur wrote in the name of the Rosh that he agreed to make havdalah at motza'ei Tisha B'Av. Afterward I examined the Rosh end of Taanit and he wrote: and it is not similar, for there itself it is deferred — an onen is exempt from all mitzvot and it is like girding; but here itself he is obligated and time causes that he cannot make havdalah, and when time passes he will make havdalah — end of his words, and so it is:
