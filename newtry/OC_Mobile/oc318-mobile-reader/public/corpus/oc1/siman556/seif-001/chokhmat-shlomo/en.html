Seif 1: on Tisha B'Av one says in Boneh Yerushalayim "nechamah," etc. Note: behold what the Rama wrote in the name of Maharil that one who ate on Tisha B'Av should say nechamah in Birkat HaMazon; and on this the Gaon Mahar'a of Vilna wondered, for on days when there is no musaf there is no mention in Birkat HaMazon. And on this a certain scholar wrote to me that one should say the reason of Maharil is because it is fitting to mention the blessing of mourners in Birkat HaMazon as the law is for a mourner, meaning. And I answered him that his words are not correct, for behold the Rosh wrote explicitly in the first chapter of Kesubos that for us, since we have no row and no open space, one does not say "Baruch Menachem Avelim" in Birkat HaMazon. His intent is specifically in former times when they would comfort the mourner and it was a seudat mitzvah established specifically for the mourner that mention is relevant in Birkat HaMazon; but in our time when we do not have this, mention in Birkat HaMazon is not relevant. Therefore in the blessing of mourners in our time it was not enacted except after all of Birkat HaMazon has already finished — then since he can add afterward whatever he wishes he may mention this; and also not in the language of supplications but behold it is like a justification of judgment and prayer like other "HaRachaman" — he is a mourner forbidden to say this within the blessings of Birkat HaMazon itself. And on Tisha B'Av Maharil ruled to mention this within the third blessing and to say it in the language of nechamah — not so with other mourners, for he made from this an obligation of mention like on Yom Kippur; therefore the Gaon Mahar'a of Vilna wondered well.
