He makes Havdalah. See Baer Heitev. And he wrote in Birkei Yosef in the name of Responsa Kenesses HaGedolah, part 2: a sick person who eats on Tisha B'Av should make Havdalah immediately on Motzaei Shabbos. And the Beis Yosef there is somewhat inclined that he can also discharge his household members with this Havdalah; see there. And see Gan HaMelech, siman 144, whose view is that ideally one does not make Havdalah over the cup on Motzaei Tisha B'Av, except if he forgot to make Havdalah in the prayer on the night of Tisha B'Av (and it appears to me that [this means] also that on Motzaei Tisha B'Av he did not make Havdalah in the prayer; for if not, that alone would have sufficed according to him). And in a case of this sort, it is better that he make Havdalah over the cup than that he return to pray the eighteen blessings; see there. But the custom is not so; rather, as written in the Shulchan Aruch, to make Havdalah on Motzaei Tisha B'Av over the cup, and they do not make Havdalah at all in the prayer on Motzaei Tisha B'Av, since he made Havdalah yesterday in the prayer. And it appears that even if he forgot yesterday to make Havdalah in the prayer, he need not make Havdalah now in the prayer, for its time has passed, and he should not interrupt now in the prayer; and with what they make Havdalah now over the cup, that suffices. And see there in Gan HaMelech, who disagrees with Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah, who permits smelling [spices] on Tisha B'Av, whereas he wrote that it is forbidden because of pleasure, etc. And from what the Magen Avraham wrote in the name of the Levush, it is implied that specifically on Motzaei Shabbos it is forbidden, because it is pleasure to restore the additional soul; but on other days, not; see there.
