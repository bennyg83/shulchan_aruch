(א) [Levush] that if he makes havdalah, etc. So wrote the Levush siman 559 seif 5, and he considered it a Shabbat offering there that he wrote thus from his own reasoning and therefore disagreed with him and did not examine that he took them from the Rosh end of Taanit and Terumat HaDeshen and Beit Yosef. And what he challenged from siman 553 that even if he makes havdalah by day nevertheless melacha is forbidden — and the same law that drinking is permitted — up to here; and nothing is difficult, for nevertheless stringently he should be forbidden in drinking — he made himself a prohibition through havdalah; but regarding the prohibition of melacha he cannot remove it through his havdalah since stars have not yet emerged, and in this manner we say in the evening prayers in several places, and in Eliyah Zuta there I expanded on this. Shlah wrote: women who do not know how to pray to make havdalah in prayer — every man must warn them that by day they should not kindle fire to cook for the night's need, or that they should say: Blessed are You, the Omnipresent our God, King of the universe, Who separates between holy and profane.
