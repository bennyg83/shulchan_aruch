[Levush:] That if he makes Havdalah, etc. So wrote the Levush in siman 299, se'if 5. And Olas Shabbos thought there that he wrote this from his own reasoning, and therefore disagreed with him; but he was not precise, for he took them from the Rosh at the end of Taanis, and from Terumas HaDeshen and Beis Yosef. And as for what he challenged from siman 293, that even though one makes Havdalah by day, even so melacha is forbidden, and the same law [applies that nevertheless] drinking is permitted, end quote: this is no difficulty at all. For nevertheless, stringently, that drinking should be forbidden, he imposes an issur upon himself by means of Havdalah; but regarding the prohibition of melacha, he cannot discharge [others] through his Havdalah, since it is not yet nightfall (tzeis hakochavim). And of this sort we say regarding Maariv prayers in several places; and in Eliyah Zuta there I elaborated on this. The Shelah wrote: women who do not know how to pray so as to make Havdalah in the prayer: every man must caution them that during the day they should not make a fire in order to cook for the needs of the night, unless they say, "Blessed are You, Hashem our God, King of the universe, Who distinguishes between holy and mundane."
