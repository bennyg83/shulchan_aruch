But it appears there is no difference: since the Yerushalmi tied the presumption to 30 days, meaning each day is equal to its fellow for establishing this presumption — namely, the regular prayers each day, and the extra ones are not counted. And furthermore, it appears to our view that if he erred or sinned on some day or days in these 30 days and did not pray at all, certainly his presumption was not weakened for this matter that is stated — that it requires specifically the measure of days that he prayed, and he must complete first the days of prayer, and afterward we say in doubt that he is presumed to say as required. For if so, Rabbi Meir would certainly not have been silent about mentioning this and writing "and he" — that he did not lack one prayer all 30 days; rather certainly, since after not mentioning on some day he returned afterward and mentioned, this is within the 30-day presumption. And this is further proven: for regarding requesting dew and rain, if he did not mention until after Shema/listening prayer he must repeat — and if he is also in doubt about 30 days, the matter depends; for we do not find a distinction in this, and behold on Shabbat this request is not there — how would the 30-day presumption help him for this? Rather simply he was not particular about the number of prayers matching as they are each day, but only regarding "what exists each day"; and since he was presumed in most of these days, it is enough, as appears to our view. But a question arises on what Rabbi Meir brought as proof from the habitual ox: we say, if he kept his goring distant he is liable; if he brought his goring close — all the more so here likewise, since after 30 days if he is in doubt he need not repeat — kal vachomer he should need to repeat on one day. But this kal vachomer of goring is in the chapter "How is the festival," page 24, for Rabbi Meir who holds so; but Rabbi Yehuda disagrees there and holds that the habitual ox is only specifically after three days, and three times in one day does not help; and he derives from zavah — Rabbi Yehuda does not hold that only in zavah was it a Scriptural decree, but we derive from it for other places. If so, likewise in prayer regarding mentioning we would say so; and we explicitly rule the law there like Rabbi Yehuda. And in the Tur he brought the view of Rokeach that he does not hold this of Rabbi Meir; and Beit Yosef gave in the name of Mahariya the reason for his words: it is different regarding an ox, which has an evil nature — when this ox does the goring close up it shows its nature is so; unlike if it did so from a distance, we would say it happened by chance. But here the matter depends on habit of the tongue, and the tongue is not so habitual when one does a thing once close up. And the Rosh inclined to Rabbi Meir's view, and it appears as his words — for habit of the tongue is only from the side of nature practiced in man, and behold it is like a goring ox — end quote. And according to what I challenged, I do not know how to rely on Rabbi Meir's view in this.
