We explained above the basis of our custom, which follows the practice of Rav Amram by making an interruption. When an announcement is made, however, the attendant announces it before Mussaf, unlike Rav Amram, who wrote that even when there is an announcement the congregation begins at Minchah. See Beit Yosef, whom I cited above.
Ba'al HaMaor wrote in the first chapter of Taanis an aggadic interpretation of the verse, “Hear, Hashem, righteousness”: this refers to Krias Shema; “attend to my song”: this refers to the blessing of Yotzer; “give ear to my prayer”: this refers to the Mussaf prayer; “let my judgment emerge from before You”: this refers to blowing the shofar during Mussaf, for a person’s mind is not settled until the Mussaf prayer. For this reason, the prayer for rain is recited during Mussaf. See the Yerushalmi and its commentator, who cites this, and see Eliyah Rabbah.
In Parashas Derachim, Derech Hashem, he cites the third chapter of Niddah, which states that Af-Beri, the minister of rain, acts only with permission from the Holy One, blessed is He. He wonders that all things are done only with permission from the Holy One, blessed is He. One may answer that all other matters were initially entrusted to an agent, although His providence continuously governs them, and without Him no existence could endure even for a single moment. Rain is different, for no agent was appointed over it. Rather, when the Holy One, blessed is He, wishes to send rain, He channels the flow through Af-Beri. Mishneh LaMelech answered that rain from the treasury of goodness was not entrusted to an agent, as it is written, “Hashem will open for you His good treasury,” whereas other rains were entrusted to Af-Beri.
