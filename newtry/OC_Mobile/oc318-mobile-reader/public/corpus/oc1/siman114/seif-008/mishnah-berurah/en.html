(35) In the summer days, etc. — because he is accustomed from winter days to say Mashiv haRuach uMorid haGeshem, therefore doubt arose for him perhaps he said now likewise and did not say Morid haTal like other people. But the reverse — the Mechaber could not cite, i.e., that doubt arose for him in rainy days whether he mentioned rain, for the Mechaber discusses the custom of Sephardim who practice saying Morid haTal in summer days, and through this he is exempted even in rainy days, as explained in seif 5. And therefore Rama wrote: and for us who do not mention dew in summer days, automatically there is also reason to be in doubt in rainy days.

(36) Until, etc. — for until this time there is a presumption he said as he was accustomed until now; but from here onward his tongue is already accustomed to say properly.

(37) Thirty days — and although in seif 9 he wrote that suffice with ninety times and in thirty days there is more than that because of Musaf prayer, many Acharonim already answered that thirty days also means the number of the Tamid prayers there are in thirty days, i.e., three times a day. And therefore according to their words we do not need complete thirty days, but once ninety prayers are completed automatically his tongue is accustomed to say properly. And regarding She'alah — that in thirty days there are not ninety prayers because of Shabbos and Yom Tov prayer which have only seven blessings — from this arises a stringency that he must return from doubt even after thirty days until ninety prayers are completed in which his tongue was accustomed to say properly. And from the words of the Gra and several other Acharonim it seems the main [factor] depends only on thirty days whether to be lenient or stringent, and it appears for practical law one should be lenient like both of them, for doubt in berachos is lenient. Taz wrote: if he erred or was negligent on some day or days of these thirty days and did not pray at all, nevertheless his presumption was not weakened thereby and he need not make up with another day — see there. And he wrote in sefer Magen Giborim: all the more so if he prayed as Shomea k'Oneh and the like it counts for him. And see Machatzis haShekel and sefer Maamar Mordechai and Darchei Chayim who all dispute Taz's ruling.

(38) With presumption that he mentioned, etc. — and if it is clear to him that he intended to remember the type of occurrence [regarding the mention, She'alah, Yaaleh veYavo, and the like] within the prayer, and after a long time doubt fell in his heart whether he remembered in the prayer or not — he need not return. And if the doubt arose immediately after the prayer — he should return [Mor uKetziah and Magen Giborim].
