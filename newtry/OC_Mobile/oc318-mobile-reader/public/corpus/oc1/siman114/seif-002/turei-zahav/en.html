Until the shaliach tzibbur announces. From Rama's words it appears that here it should read "until he mentions" — meaning, that the shaliach tzibbur mentions in his prayer "He Who makes the wind blow" — and so in the Yerushalmi in Asheri, and the congregation begins Minchah afterward; and on this Rama concluded well. And some say "before," etc. — automatically the congregation begins in Musaf; see nearby from this. And in the Tur he brought this Yerushalmi and brought on it Ra'avad, who explained the prohibition: one may not mention until the shaliach tzibbur announces "He Who makes the wind blow" and "He Who makes the rain descend," or "He Who causes the dew to descend," so that there should not be confusion between them. Beit Yosef challenged: for it is stated in the Yerushalmi, R. Chaggi said it is forbidden for an individual to mention until the shaliach tzibbur mentions; R. Simon in the name of Ribbi Levi: on the shaliach tzibbur the matter depends — with wonder, R. Mana before R. Chaggi: what is the dispute? He said to him: not this that you said, that on the shaliach tzibbur the matter depends — an individual, if he comes to mention, mentions dew; and surely you said it is forbidden for an individual to mention until the shaliach tzibbur mentions regarding rain — therefore regarding "He Who causes the dew to descend," an individual, if he wishes to mention, may mention even if he did not hear from the shaliach tzibbur. And why did Ra'avad write also regarding dew? It seems to answer: it was difficult for Ra'avad, since the reason is so there should not be confusion between them — what difference to me rain, what difference to me dew? Therefore Ra'avad explained that what R. Chaggi said, "it is forbidden for an individual to mention," is an emphatic way: regarding a congregation praying quietly, it is obvious they should not mention before the shaliach tzibbur, so there should not be confusion between them; but even an individual praying between himself and himself, where there is no concern of confusion, is forbidden. And on this the individual challenged: what is the dispute — meaning, if Ribbi Levi disputes regarding an individual? For he must be dealing with an individual, since he spoke with wonder, "and that on the shaliach tzibbur the matter depends" — learn that one who comes to mention in a place where there is no shaliach tzibbur, i.e., an individual. And on this he replied to him that there is a distinction for an individual between rain and dew; but for a congregation praying quietly there is no distinction, because there is concern of confusion also regarding dew. And this is not like Beit Yosef, who wrote... and it seems that "on the shaliach tzibbur the matter depends" means to say that even the congregation should not mention until the shaliach tzibbur mentions; only that for the congregation there is no distinction, as stated.

Even if he is sick. Meaning: if he is not sick all year, it is forbidden to advance his prayer before the congregation, as stated in siman 90.

He should not advance, etc. According to Rama, who reads in the Shulchan Aruch "until he mentions" — therefore the congregation should not mention until Minchah. Why then did it write that he should not advance his prayer before the congregation's prayer, from which it implies that the congregation mentions in Musaf? It must be said that certainly the shaliach tzibbur that the Shulchan Aruch speaks of is the shamash; and the words "and some say" that Rama wrote are a textual error — it should read, meaning "before," etc. And there is no dispute here at all; Rama only intended to explain that the shaliach tzibbur that the Shulchan Aruch mentioned is the shamash, as above.
