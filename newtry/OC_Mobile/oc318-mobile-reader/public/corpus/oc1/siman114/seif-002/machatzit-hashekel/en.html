(Endnote 2) That he should announce, etc. Know that in the Yerushalmi it states: Rabbi Hagai in the name of Rabbi Pedat said it is forbidden for an individual to mention it until the communal agent mentions it. The Rosh wrote: it seems to me that this refers to why the Yerushalmi asks (brought in Magen Avraham, endnote 1) that they should begin in Shacharit; and on this he said that an individual may not mention until the communal agent mentions — meaning that he should say before the Musaf prayer in a loud voice "Who makes the wind blow and rain fall," and this is impossible in the morning, because he cannot interrupt between the sections — end of his words. According to his words, what the Yerushalmi says, "until the prayer leader mentions," must mean in a loud voice, and that is that he should announce before the prayer. But Raavyah wrote: it is forbidden for an individual, etc., until the prayer leader mentions, whether quietly or in a loud voice — end of his words. And the Bach explained that he holds that what the Yerushalmi says, "forbidden until the prayer leader mentions," is according to its simple meaning: it is forbidden for an individual to mention when the prayer leader has not yet stood to pray Musaf, for he has not yet mentioned it, even quietly; but when the cantor stood and prayed quietly, and they know the prayer leader will mention it quietly, the whole people also mention in the Musaf prayer. The author, who wrote "forbidden until the prayer leader announces" — if we say he holds like the Rosh, that he must announce before the prayer, and therefore the author wrote "until he announces" — then it would be difficult: in what does the Rama disagree, who wrote "and some say that before," etc.? Behold, the author also holds so (and for this reason the Taz emended the words of the Rama; see there). Rather, the Rama understood from the author's words, since he did not write "until he announces before the prayer," and the Rosh's words — therefore like Raavyah he holds that one need not announce before the prayer; rather, the Rabbeynu Yonah explains that he wrote for himself, on the path of what is better, that he should say in a loud voice within the prayer "Who makes the wind blow," etc.; for according to Raavyah, even if he mentions quietly it suffices; nevertheless it is preferable that he announce within the prayer in a loud voice "Who makes the wind blow," etc., so that he not forget anything. But in truth, from the side of the law it suffices even if the prayer leader mentioned quietly.

And what the author wrote in the language "forbidden," etc. — it refers only to the essential law, that it is forbidden for an individual to mention before the prayer leader mentions. But regarding what he wrote "that he should announce," etc. — the word "forbidden" does not refer to this, for what the prayer leader needs to say in a loud voice "Who makes the wind blow," etc., is not from the side of the law, as above. And similarly it appears from the end of the author's words, who wrote "therefore," etc., "because it is forbidden until the prayer leader says," etc. — it implies that with saying it suffices, and that is even quietly (and the Bach inferred from this wording that the author wrote "until he says," for Raavyah holds so; and he challenged the words of the Rabbeynu Yonah against each other, for at first he wrote "until he announces," and that is like the Rosh — but per the words of Magen Avraham, there is no difficulty). Rather, the end of his words that he wrote "until he says" — and meaning: from the side of the essential law he wrote. And on this there is no difficulty in what the Rama wrote, "and some say that before," etc., which is the view of the Rosh. And know that according to Raavyah and the Rabbeynu Yonah, who stand in their view that he holds one does not announce before the prayer — it must be that which they do not begin in Shacharit: according to their words, there is no interruption between the sections, as Magen Avraham wrote, endnote 1. It must be that he holds like the other version in the Yerushalmi: if they mention in Shacharit, one who was not in the synagogue in the evening will think they already began in Maariv; and like other mentions of "Yaaleh veYavo" and the like, the next year he too will mention in Maariv, and there will again be groups. Unlike when he sees that in the morning they do not mention, he will know that in the evening too they did not mention. And Rabbi Hagai did not come to resolve a difficulty, but stated a law. And the Bach wrote that per his view — meaning, per the Rosh and Rama, who stand in their view; unlike Raavyah and the Rabbeynu Yonah, who stand in their view.

And similarly seif 3 in the gloss, who wrote that the people do not stop until Mincha, for they already heard from the prayer leader that he stopped in Musaf; but in Musaf they mention, even though they know the prayer leader stops quietly. And siman 90, seif 9: without this reason, it is also fitting for one in a village to wait until the time when the communities pray, in order to pray at the hour when the congregation prays.
