Seif katan 2, That the chazzan should announce, etc. Know that the Yerushalmi states in the name of Rabbi Chaggai quoting Rabbi Pedas: an individual may not mention it until the chazzan mentions it. The Rosh writes that this refers to the Yerushalmi’s question, cited by Magen Avraham in seif katan 1, why they do not begin at Shacharis. The answer is that an individual may not mention it until the chazzan mentions it, meaning that before Mussaf the chazzan says aloud, “Who causes the wind to blow and the rain to descend.” This cannot be done in the morning because one may not interrupt between redemption and prayer. According to the Rosh, the Yerushalmi’s phrase “until the chazzan mentions it” means aloud, through an announcement before the prayer. Raavyah, however, writes that an individual may not mention it until the chazzan mentions it, whether silently or aloud. Bach explains that Raavyah understands the Yerushalmi literally: an individual may not mention it before the chazzan has begun Mussaf, since the chazzan has not yet mentioned it even silently. Once the chazzan begins the silent Amidah and the congregation knows he will mention it silently, the entire congregation also mentions it in Mussaf. If the Mechaber, who writes that one may not mention it “until the chazzan announces it,” agrees with the Rosh that an announcement before prayer is required, then in what respect does Rama disagree when he writes, “Some say that before,” etc.? The Mechaber would hold the same view. For this reason Taz emends Rama’s words. It must therefore be that Rama understood from the Mechaber’s failure to write “until he announces it before prayer,” in the Rosh’s formulation, that the Mechaber follows Raavyah. No announcement before prayer is required. Beit Yosef added on his own that it is preferable for the chazzan to say aloud during the prayer, “Who causes the wind,” etc., so no one forgets, although according to Raavyah even a silent mention suffices. Strictly speaking, it is enough for the chazzan to mention it silently.
The Mechaber’s use of the word “forbidden,” etc. refers only to the basic rule that an individual may not mention it before the chazzan does. It does not refer to the statement that the chazzan should announce it aloud, for the chazzan’s saying “Who causes the wind,” etc., aloud is not a strict requirement, as explained. This is also implied by the end of the Mechaber’s words: “Therefore,” etc., “because it is forbidden,” etc., “until the chazzan says,” etc. This implies that mere recitation suffices, even silently. Bach likewise infers from the Mechaber’s phrase “until he says” that he follows Raavyah, and asks how Beit Yosef’s statements can be reconciled, since at first he writes “until he announces,” which sounds like the Rosh. According to Magen Avraham’s explanation, however, there is no difficulty. The end of the Mechaber’s statement, “until he says,” refers to the strict law. Rama’s statement, “Some say that before,” etc., which is the Rosh’s view, is therefore understandable. Know that according to Raavyah and Beit Yosef, who follows him and holds that no announcement is made before prayer, one must explain why they do not begin at Shacharis, since according to them there would be no interruption between redemption and prayer. They must follow the Yerushalmi’s other answer: if they mentioned it at Shacharis, one who was absent from the synagogue at night would assume they had already begun at Maariv, like Yaaleh VeYavo and similar insertions, and the following year he would mention it at Maariv, again creating separate groups. When he sees that it is not mentioned at Shacharis, he knows it was not mentioned at Maariv either. Rabbi Chaggai did not come to resolve the question, but merely stated a law. Bach writes that this is according to the Rosh, and Rama follows his view, unlike Raavyah and Beit Yosef, who follow the other view.
This is also implied by the gloss in seif 3, which states that the congregation does not cease mentioning rain until Minchah, after they have heard the chazzan cease during Mussaf. Yet during Mussaf they themselves omit it, although they know the chazzan omits it silently. See siman 90, seif 9, that even apart from this, one living in a village should preferably wait until the time the communities pray, so that he prays when the congregation prays.
