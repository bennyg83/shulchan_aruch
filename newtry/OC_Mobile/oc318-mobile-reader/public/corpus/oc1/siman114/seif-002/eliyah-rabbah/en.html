[2] In the Mussaf prayer, etc. Levush writes that they begin specifically at Mussaf and not at Maariv because not everyone is present in the synagogue at night. They therefore do not begin or cease during Shacharis, lest those absent from the synagogue the previous night think the congregation had already begun at Maariv and err the following year. This is difficult, for Beit Yosef, citing the Yerushalmi, explains that this reason applies only to the first night of Pesach, when people are occupied with work or with matzah and maror. During Chol HaMoed Sukkos, however, work is prohibited and everyone comes to the synagogue at night, as Rabbi David Abudraham also writes. Rather, the reason is so that all the festivals should conclude with dew. The fact that they begin and cease on those days is likewise based on this reason, as Beit Yosef explains from the Yerushalmi. Levush, however, altered the explanation and wrote that it is because a rainy day is difficult. It appears that he wrote this according to our custom, under which we do not say “Who causes the dew to descend” during the summer. Nevertheless, even for this reason, that rain is difficult, the Sages did not establish the change on Yom Tov. It therefore appears to me that this is why we do not begin at Maariv on Shemini Atzeres. Examine carefully. Another reason for mentioning it in Mussaf is the Yerushalmi’s exposition: “Hear my song” refers to Torah; “give ear to my prayer” refers to prayer; “without deceitful lips” refers to Mussaf. What is written immediately afterward? “Let my judgment come forth from before You.” This shows that Mussaf is the primary prayer.
