[2] "In the Musaf prayer," etc. Levush wrote: the fact that they began specifically in Musaf and not in Ma'ariv — because not all the people are present then in the evening; and therefore they do not begin and stop in Shacharit — lest they mislead those who were not yesterday in the synagogue, who say them in Ma'ariv, and err the following year — until here. And it is difficult: for it is explained in Beit Yosef in the name of the Jerusalem Talmud that this reason applies only on the first [day] of Pesach, when they are busy with work or with matzah and maror; but on the intermediate days of Sukkot, when work is forbidden on the intermediate days of the festival, everyone comes to the synagogue in the evening — and similarly R. David Abudraham wrote; rather the reason is so that all the festivals come out with dew [in the formula]. Also what they begin and stop on those days is for this reason, as explained in Beit Yosef in the name of the Jerusalem Talmud — except that Levush changed and wrote the reason because the rainy season was near. And it seems he wrote thus for our custom, that we do not say "Who makes the dew descend" in the hot season; and in any case, for this reason that the rainy season was near, they did not enact it on Yom Tov — it seems to me that therefore they do not begin in the Ma'ariv of Shemini Atzeret; examine well. Another reason for mentioning [rain]: we say in the Jerusalem Talmud: "Hear my cry" — this is Torah; "listen to my prayer" — this is prayer; "without deceit on my lips" — this is Musaf; and what is written after it: "From before You let my judgment go forth" — thus Musaf prayer is primary:
