And there is not — Taz: it is proper to stop in the evening when the festivals begin [mentioning] dew and to stop rain only in the evening, for there everyone is not there and they would be agudot; but with interruption this suffices. And at the beginning there must be tal — on Yom Tov when they are free and in synagogue in the evening Bach explains. However the reason of R' Chiya bar Padda applies also to interruption because one must announce beforehand — and this is for Sephardim who announce in Musaf on the first day of Pesach "Mashiv haRuach uMorid haTal" and they interrupt at [God's] name, and this is impossible in Shacharis because one must juxtapose redemption [to prayer], etc. (And in Maariv, although there is a mitzvah to juxtapose redemption as in siman 236, nevertheless for the sake of the mitzvah it would be proper to announce if not that everyone is not there.) And what he wrote "for a year" — a stumbling block in this: possibly everyone like Semag, even for Sephardim, if he mentioned rain in a place where they do not mention — they return him as in seif 4 and as explained in note 2. And behold Sephardim rule: the congregation in a whisper in Musaf on the first day of Pesach the Shaliach Tzibbur announces before "Mashiv haRuach uMorid haTal." And we Ashkenazim who do not [mention dew] on Yom Tov — thus we cannot announce lest we appear to refuse rain; therefore the Shaliach Tzibbur stops in Musaf and the congregation in Minchah as Rama wrote in the gloss, seif 3. And see Levush, Taz, and Magen Avraham there. And R' Chiya [bar Padda] explains for Bach: reason for the mention on Yom Tov; and in Maariv too they do not interrupt.

And one should see why the shaliach tzibur does not pause during the prayer of the Eighteenth [blessing] in a loud voice on the first day of Pesach and on Yom Tov of Sukkot — one mentions [rain-related phrases] in the Eighteenth [blessing] at Shacharis in the repetition, and the congregation mentions [them] at Musaf. This follows the reason of R. Chagai bar Pedat (in letter 2). For Sephardim it is fitting that one not do so: in the silent prayer neither they nor the shaliach tzibur mention [it]; in the repetition the shaliach tzibur mentions and the congregation did not mention in the silent prayer, and the shaliach tzibur mentions in the repetition — and similarly in the pause. Not so for Ashkenazim; according to us, as the Mishna Berurah wrote in the gloss to seif 3, the shaliach tzibur pauses in the repetition in a loud voice, but in the silent prayer he also mentions [it] at Musaf, as explained in the Levush — see there. Therefore at Shacharis one can do this whether in the pause or at the beginning, as stated. It therefore appears that for us only reason (1) is primary; even he holds that one should mention [it] in the evening. See in Magen Avraham, siman 1, with God's help, from this.
