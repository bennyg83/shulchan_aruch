"He Who makes the wind blow" in the summer days. Meaning: we say in the Gemara that wind and dew are never stopped, but rain is stopped; and in the summer days rain is not good in the harvest season, as stated, "Is it not wheat harvest today? I will call to the heavens and He will give thunder and rain." And if one said "He Who makes the wind blow" in the summer days, they return him — because wind relates to rain, and one might think so. It teaches us that they do not return him, for his statement does not help regarding wind, since in any case it is not stopped. Similarly, if he did not say "He Who makes the wind blow" in the rainy season, one might return him, since wind is then needed — it teaches us they do not return him, for it is not stopped even if he does not say it.

And we Ashkenazim, etc. It appears to be the reason for our custom that there is no need to mention dew, for it is not stopped; and what we mention it in the Maariv Amidah is so that it should be for a blessing — for it is stated in the first chapter of Taanis that there is dew that is not for a blessing; therefore we ask that it should be dew of blessing. But in the mention (insertion) this does not apply. And what we mention wind, even though it is not stopped — since we mention rain, which relates to the blessing "You are mighty," as we learned: "powers of rain" — we also mention wind, which relates to rain.

And they do not stop until Minchah. For in Musaf there is no recognizable sign before the shaliach tzibbur stops in his prayer; and he said: why did they not enact that he announce beforehand "He Who makes the wind blow"? — they would err and say that because of "He Who makes the wind blow" they forbade it. In truth, regarding "He Who makes the wind blow" there is no prohibition to mention, only regarding rain; and therefore he should announce beforehand "He Who makes the rain descend" — if so. But regarding "He Who makes the wind blow" they say as above on this.
