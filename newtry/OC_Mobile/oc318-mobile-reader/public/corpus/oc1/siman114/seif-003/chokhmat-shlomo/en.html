(Seif 9) If on the first day of Pesach one says the blessing "You are mighty" until "Who makes the dew descend" ninety times, etc. NB — the Tur wrote, and this is its language: and Maharam of Rothenburg was accustomed in Shulchan Aruch to say the blessing "You are mighty," etc., and his proof from the chapter "How is the habit" — for we say regarding an ox that is prone [to gore]: "If he distanced its goring it is liable; if he brought its goring near — is it not all the more so?" etc. And Rashba wrote: I have not seen the elderly rabbis of France do so, for the case is not similar to the proof — for there the reason is because it was established to gore; and if it was established with three distant [gorings], all the more so with three near ones; but rain, which was fixed in prayer and the matter depends on habit of speech — we do not say thus, etc. And behold at first glance it seemed to me that Maharam of Rothenburg's intention was for what is said there, in the Gemara: "It was asked — for designation of an ox or for designation of a man? A difference — if three groups of witnesses came in one day: if you say for designation of an ox, it is designated; if you say for designation of a man, it is not designated." They said: "Now he is testifying about him — what is the law?" And it is difficult for me: how could one think it is for designation of a man? How does R. Meir disagree and say a fortiori: "If he distanced its goring it is liable; if he brought its goring near — is it not all the more so?" It works out well if it is for designation of an ox — then the a fortiori is fine; but if it is for designation of a man, this is not an a fortiori — for in three days, with this, remembrance is fixed in his heart that it is established, and he has a guardian; but if he designated on one day, one could say he forgot the matter and it left his heart, and therefore he did not guard it; and necessarily it is proven that even regarding a matter that depends on memory of the heart, one remembers more in near time than in distant time. And from this it is proven that the same applies to "Who makes the wind blow." However, as we analyzed, one may uphold the words of Rashba — and that he made the Gemara difficult: why does it say a difference if three groups came in one day, and not also say the reverse — if three groups came in three days, but all testified about three gorings of one day — for example, that it gored them all in one day — then if it is for designation of an ox it does not help, and if for designation of a man it helps. And behold from this it is proven that there is no doubt at all if it is for designation of a creature alone — it is simply clear to him that we require the gorings to be on three days; only it was asked whether we require designation of a man as well, or designation of an ox alone suffices; and so is the intention of the Gemara — whether for designation of an ox alone, or we require designation of a man as well. If so, the words of R. Meir would be thus: the reason of R. Yehudah, who holds that for designation of a man we require three days, is since he holds that for designation of an ox we require three days — if so, as the judgment of the ox, so the judgment of the owners; and its end judgment is like its beginning judgment; and since its beginning judgment requires three days, its end judgment also requires three days; but if its beginning judgment did not require three days, all the more so at its end for designation of owners we do not require three days. And accordingly the words of R. Meir do not come to him — that it deals with designation of an ox, and he is not uncertain — only in the words of R. Yehudah, whether it deals with designation of an ox alone or designation of a man as well; and if so, we have no proof for the words of Maharam. But accordingly we would have proof to the contrary for the words of Maharam — that he made the Gemara itself difficult: why is it not also asked regarding the reverse? Perhaps one could say that R. Yehudah holds that for designation of a man alone we require three days, but for designation of an ox we do not care at all; and why is the reverse not asked of him as well? And necessarily he cannot ask thus — for then the a fortiori of R. Meir would go away: "If he distanced its goring it is liable; if he brought its goring near — is it not all the more so?" — for regarding designation of a man, which depends on memory, this is not an a fortiori; for a matter done day after day over much time is fixed more in one's heart than what is done much in a short time; and necessarily the a fortiori of R. Meir applies only to designation of an ox; and so for R. Yehudah, designation of an ox certainly requires [three days], and there is no doubt — only whether designation of a man is also required; and from this it is proven like the view of Rashba and not like the view of Maharam of Rothenburg — for according to Maharam it is difficult why he is not also uncertain regarding the reverse, whether designation of an ox alone also suffices, as above; examine well. It also seems, in my humble opinion, to resolve the difficulty of the Acharonim on Maharam of Rothenburg mentioned above — that he brought proof from what R. Meir said: "If he distanced its goring it is liable; if he brought its goring near — is it not all the more so?" — and they wondered at this, for we do not rule like R. Meir but like R. Yehudah; see there. And it seems to me thus: there is [room] to say that the reason for the dispute of R. Meir and R. Yehudah is thus — for at first glance the words of R. Meir are clear: if distance is a presumption, all the more so nearness. But it appears they disagree in the dispute of the poskim whether forgetting is coercion or negligence. And accordingly, behold in chapter 3 of Ketubot we say that regarding plotting witnesses one does not find warning — for if they were warned from the outset they would say "I forgot"; if so here too, since the obligation of the goring ox is as explicit in the verse "and it was testified to its owner and he did not guard it" — he can say "it was that it was testified about him" in any case he forgot afterward that it became established, and therefore he did not guard it; but this works out only if forgetting is coercion; but if forgetting is negligence, then on this very matter he is liable for what he was negligent in. And in plotting witnesses, even if they were negligent, one does not obligate them lashes for negligence — only if they testified with actual intent and not for negligence; but monetary payments one is liable for even negligence. Therefore one could say that here they disagree: certainly regarding establishing the body of the ox to gore, the a fortiori of R. Meir is correct — if he distanced its goring he is liable, all the more so if he brought its goring near. But regarding the owners, who could not say "I forgot" — it is the reverse: if it was testified about him for three days in order, he is not apt to forget; but if all were testified on one day, he is apt to forget and can say "I forgot." Therefore R. Meir holds that forgetting is negligence; therefore the main matter depends on whether the body of the ox was established to gore — and with this the a fortiori is well learned: if he distanced its goring he is liable, if he brought its goring near — is it not all the more so? But R. Yehudah, who holds that forgetting is coercion — if so, even though the ox became a goring ox, nevertheless the owners can say: since it is written "and it was testified to its owner and he did not guard it," they can say "I forgot"; therefore we require that it be over three days, and then with the multiplicity of days it is certainly negligence if he forgot; and with this, for all opinions forgetting is negligence; and therefore over three days it is negligence if he forgot, but on one day it is not negligence if he forgot, and he can say "I forgot." And even if they testified to me over three days — since the goring was on one day, and for testimony we require that one be fit to testify immediately — but if one is not fit to testify immediately, it does not help if one testifies afterward — for its beginning was in disqualification and its end in fitness; similarly here, if it gored them all on one day — if so, if they all testify today it is not considered established; and since they are not fit to testify today, it does not help if they testify afterward over several days. And therefore it comes out well that there, regarding establishment, it depends on the owners and one can say "I forgot"; therefore here three days are preferable to one day. But to establish oneself to say "Who makes the wind blow" — he himself was established to be presumed to say as he was accustomed, and this does not depend on his thought but only on what is accustomed in his mouth — as we hold in a doubt regarding Shema: if one began "so that your days may be multiplied," then one certainly finishes; and it would be if hearing that one holds the chain and goes — if so we see that we rely on what one is accustomed to in this; and certainly one learns an a fortiori from the ox, which itself is certainly an a fortiori: if with distance it is so, all the more so with nearness. And the words of Maharam of Rothenburg come out well, with God's help; and even if Maharam did not intend this, in any case it is already written in the early authorities in the abridged [commentary] on Ta'anit, siman 25: "the spirit of God fluttered over them"; see there and understand; examine well:
