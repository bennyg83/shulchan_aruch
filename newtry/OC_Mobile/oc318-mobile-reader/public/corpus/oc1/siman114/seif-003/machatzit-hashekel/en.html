Seif katan 3, And likewise regarding dew. Meaning, the Sephardic custom is to say “Who causes the wind to blow and the dew to descend” during the summer. According to our custom, the end of the Mechaber’s statement, that if one omitted dew during the summer he is not required to repeat, is inapplicable, since our initial custom is not to mention dew during summer. It must therefore refer to the Sephardic custom of mentioning dew in summer. The reason is as Magen Avraham writes in seif katan 4. However, Magen Avraham’s statement that the Sephardic custom is to say during summer “Who causes the wind,” etc., requires explanation. If so, why does the Mechaber begin by saying that if one said “Who causes the wind” during summer he is not required to repeat? According to the Sephardic custom, this is the preferred practice. I would have thought the two words “Who causes the wind” are a scribal error, and it should read only that the Sephardic custom is to say “Who causes the dew to descend” during summer, as Beit Yosef and Levush write. Bach, however, also writes that the Sephardic custom is to say “Who causes the wind to blow and the dew to descend” during summer.
