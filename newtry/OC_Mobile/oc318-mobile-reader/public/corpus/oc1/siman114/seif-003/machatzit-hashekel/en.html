(Endnote 3) And similarly regarding dew. Meaning: the custom of Sephardim is to say "Who makes the wind blow and Who makes dew descend" in the summer days — meaning that for us the author's conclusion does not apply, that if they did not mention it in the summer days they do not make him go back; for behold, l'chatchila the custom is so, not to mention dew in the summer days. Rather, it refers to the custom of Spain, who mention dew in the summer days; and the reason is as Magen Avraham wrote in endnote 4. However, what Magen Avraham wrote, that the custom of Spain is to say in the summer days "Who makes the wind blow," etc. — it is difficult: if so, why did the author say at the beginning of his words, "if one said 'Who makes the wind blow' in the summer days, they do not make him go back"? Behold, for the custom of Spain the custom is so l'chatchila. And it would seem that the two words "Who makes the wind blow" are a scribal error, and it should read that the custom of Spain is to say "Who makes dew descend" in the summer days — as the Rabbeynu Yonah wrote; see there, and similarly in the Levush. But the Bach also wrote that the custom of Spain is to say "Who makes the wind blow and Who makes dew descend" in the summer days.
