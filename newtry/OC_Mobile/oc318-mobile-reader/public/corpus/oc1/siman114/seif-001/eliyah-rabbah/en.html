[1] "In the second blessing," etc. The fact that one mentions resurrection of the dead three times in this blessing — R. David Abudraham wrote: once — when a person awakens from his bed, when He restores his soul; twice — "Who revives the dead with mercy" in rains, which is equivalent to resurrection of the dead; three — that we conclude "Blessed are You, the Master, Who revives the dead," and that is resurrection of the dead for the future to come. And with this the difficulties of Tosafot in Bava Metzia 85, in the passage beginning "when he reached," etc., regarding "Who revives the dead," are resolved; see there and examine well. One may also say that we count two more — such as "and maintains His faith to those who sleep in dust," and "He gives life to the dead" on Shabbat — which are resurrection of the dead, for they sleep, as it is written "and many who sleep in the dust of the earth will awaken"; and "King Who causes death and restores life" — there are five; for we find four considered like the dead, and the fifth is actual resurrection of the dead — for we always count praise from below to above. And know that resurrection of the dead corresponds to Isaac at the time of the binding, as R. David Abudraham wrote; and the fact that we mention rain in "You are mighty" — because it is called the might of rains, for every drop and drop descends from a high place, and the wind does not scatter them except to the place the Holy One, blessed be He, desires; and that is the might that has no equal. Matat Moshe wrote: the third [word] of "You are mighty" must be read with a dagesh. In the Tur it is implied that it hints at what is not handed over by an agent in this blessing; and the mnemonic mafteach: rain, livelihood, resurrection of the dead, life. Perishah wrote that life is hinted in "Who releases the bound" — for the child and mother were bound; and for this reason they enacted in the blessing of circumcision "Give thanks to the the Master," etc., that four must give thanks, and one of them [is] one who came out from the house of prisoners; and one should have this intention when saying "Who releases the bound":
