In Magen Avraham, seif katan 2: before the prayer — and the same applies if the shaliach tzibbur already mentioned quietly within the prayer, as should be. And it seems to me that the author, who left the matter unspecified and wrote "until the shaliach tzibbur announces," did not explain that the announcement must be specifically before the prayer. It appears at first glance that he holds like the view of Abbe Ezri, that whenever the shaliach tzibbur mentioned quietly, it is also included in the announcement; and since the shaliach tzibbur and the congregation have already stood to pray and have announced, the matter is already publicized and it is permitted for an individual to mention. And what is written later in the Shulchan Aruch, "but if he knows that the shaliach tzibbur announced," etc. — it does not mean announcement specifically before the prayer, but rather that whether before the prayer or he announced — meaning, that he mentioned quietly — all are included in the announcements of the shaliach tzibbur; only that because it is his way to announce first, therefore he says "even though he did not hear from the shaliach tzibbur." But in truth there is no insistence on announcement specifically beforehand, and as the language of the Shulchan Aruch also implies, which says that it is forbidden to mention until the shaliach tzibbur speaks — implying that speech suffices. And with this the question of the Bach is resolved, see there. And on this Rama says: and some say that the shamash announces before the prayer — and they are a matter that exists in practice, as the Mordechai wrote; and this is like the view of the Rosh and Ra'avad. In their view the Bach wrote that what he mentioned quietly does not help if they did not announce out loud first. And on this the author and Rama disagree: for in the author's view, the shaliach tzibbur's quiet mention is also like a full announcement — for otherwise he would have had to explain that they must announce out loud before the prayer; rather, whenever the shaliach tzibbur stood to pray quietly, afterward any individual may mention. And Rama's view is that it is forbidden; see the Bach and understand well. And if we do not interpret Magen Avraham as stated, it appears that he means: since the author left unspecified that the shaliach tzibbur should announce, it means that whenever the individual heard from the shaliach tzibbur that he said "He Who makes the wind blow" in the quiet prayer — such as if he raised his voice somewhat — it suffices so. And nevertheless it fits well why they did not enact that one mention in Shacharis quietly within the prayer: furthermore it does not apply that one must adjoin redemption to prayer, as stated in seif katan 1 — for in this it does not apply to enact that he say out loud in the quiet prayer, for the sages did not wish to enact in this abolition of the congregation's prayer intention; only that he must announce. But rather, if there is one who heard from the shaliach tzibbur that he said "He Who makes the wind blow" from the quiet prayer, or his presumption that the shaliach tzibbur already said it — it is like the shaliach tzibbur announced. But ab initio one should not enact that the congregation wait until the shaliach tzibbur reaches after "He Who makes the wind blow," or that he say "He Who makes the wind blow" in the quiet prayer out loud. And one should not interpret Magen Avraham's intent that per the author he must say in the quiet prayer out loud, and nevertheless one should not do so in Shacharis because of the first reason that the Taz wrote in seif katan 1 — that he should think they mentioned from the evening; for Magen Avraham did not mention in seif katan 1 only the second reason that the Taz brought in the name of the Rosh. And also because no posek ruled that the shaliach tzibbur should say in the quiet prayer out loud; and in Beit Yosef too he did not mention anything of this, only brought the Rosh's words that he should say before the prayer out loud; and so too Ravyah, that it is forbidden for an individual, etc., until the shaliach tzibbur mentions — whether quietly or out loud, etc., see there; and there his explanation is that even in the shaliach tzibbur's quiet mention it suffices if the congregation waited until he mentions quietly — see the Bach.
