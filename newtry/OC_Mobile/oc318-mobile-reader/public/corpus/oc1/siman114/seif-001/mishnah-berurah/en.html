(1) In the second blessing. Because it contains resurrection of the dead, and rain sustains life just as resurrection restores life. (2) In the Mussaf prayer, etc. It would have been appropriate to begin mentioning rain on the first Yom Tov of Sukkot, when judgment concerning water takes place. However, rain is a sign of curse during Sukkot because one cannot sit in the sukkah while it rains, so rain is not mentioned until the 7 days of dwelling in the sukkah have passed. Strictly speaking, one should begin immediately on the night of the final Yom Tov, but at Maariv not everyone is in the synagogue, and the congregation would become divided, with some mentioning rain and others not. Why not begin at Shacharit? Some write that an individual may not mention rain until the prayer leader or attendant announces 'Morid hageshem' aloud before the prayer, as explained below, and this cannot be announced at Shacharit because redemption must be joined to the Amidah. See Taz for another reason. If one erred and mentioned 'Mashiv haruach umorid hageshem' at Maariv of Shemini Atzeret or at Shacharit, he does not repeat the prayer. (3) Until the Mussaf prayer. We likewise do not cease mentioning rain from the preceding evening so that the congregation will not be divided. When they cease at Mussaf, everyone knows because the prayer leader or attendant announces 'Morid hatal' before Mussaf, and the announcement of dew signals that the mention of rain is ending. This applies to the Sephardic custom of saying 'Morid hatal' during the summer. In our countries, where 'Morid hatal' is not said in summer, this wording cannot be announced. Nor is it proper to announce explicitly that they should stop saying 'Morid hageshem,' because that appears as though one rejects rain, in keeping with the statement that one does not pray for an excess of goodness. Rama therefore rules in seif 3 that the custom is to continue mentioning rain until Minchah, after everyone has heard the prayer leader omit it in Mussaf. During the silent Mussaf, however, both the congregation and the prayer leader still mention rain, so that the congregation will not be divided. If one erred at Maariv of Pesach, or at Shacharit or Mussaf, and did not mention 'Mashiv haruach umorid hageshem,' all agree that he does not repeat.
