Seif 1: "We begin," etc. NB — it is difficult for me: why did the poskim omit the law explicit in the Gemara Ta'anit 3a — what is written there: "even if one said 'Who causes the wind to pass and makes the dew fly,' we do not make him go back" — and why did the poskim omit this law? It requires study now; examine well. And behold regarding this law — that we begin to say in the second blessing "Who makes the wind blow and Who makes the rain fall" in the Musaf prayer of the last Yom Tov of the festival, etc. — I was asked regarding one who prayed Musaf on Shemini Atzeret and did not mention "Who makes the wind blow," and did not hear from the prayer leader that he announced; he also did not know at all that the prayer leader had announced — whether he is obligated to return and pray or not. And I answered that it seems clear he is obligated to return and pray, since thus the Sages enacted to begin in Musaf; and as the Rama, Taz, and Magen Avraham wrote — that even the Mechaber's intention is thus to begin in Musaf; and if so, one is obligated to hear and remember; and if one did not mention [rain], even though one did not hear, one is obligated to return and pray. And you should know that if there were a view that after the fact one does not return and pray — behold we hold that every time of constraint is considered after the fact; so why did he write in Shulchan Aruch that even if one was sick or under duress one should not advance one's prayer before the congregation? And since one is under duress or sick — you have no greater time of constraint than this — and one would pray without "Who makes the wind blow," which would be like after the fact; but certainly that even after the fact one returns and prays, and must wait for the announcement and mention [rain]. And again I examined in Pri Megadim in Mishbetzot, and from the flow of his language too it seems to me that one returns and prays in such a case; and if one mentioned [rain] before hearing from the prayer leader, one also returns for that reason itself; and so I wrote in siman 108 in the omissions and novellae there. And so it is; and the main point, in my humble opinion; examine well:
