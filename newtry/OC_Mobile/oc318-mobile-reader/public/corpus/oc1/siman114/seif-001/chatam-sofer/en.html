In the Mechaber, seif 9: "ninety times." NB — see responsa of Chatam Sofer Orach Chaim siman 20 [there it is said that in the Jerusalem Talmud it is found in the first chapter of Ta'anit: one who prays and is uncertain what he mentioned — R. Yehudah said: all thirty days there is a presumption that what he is accustomed to he mentions, etc.; and Hagahot Maimoniyot wrote in the name of Maharam of Rothenburg that one who wishes not to return on account of doubt should say so many times "You are mighty" until "Who sustains the living," as there are in thirty days; and he did not write ninety times, because one could say that the words of R. Yehudah are built on the statement of the Gemara in Hagigah 9b — "one who repeats his chapter one hundred times is not like one who repeats his chapter one hundred times and one" — and it applies on Pesach and abroad [even though R. Yehudah was in the Land of Israel; and see Shabbat 9a in Tosafot in the passage beginning "For us," that his custom was to teach halakhah to students from abroad]; and if so it would be one hundred times and one, for in thirty days there are ninety prayers — Shacharit, Minchah, and Ma'ariv; subtract from them two Ma'ariv and Shacharit of the first day of Pesach, for one does not mention [rain] on it except from Musaf onward — there remain eighty-eight times; and eight Musaf prayers of the days of Pesach; and three more Musaf prayers of three Shabbats with their Musaf prayers; and two days of Rosh Chodesh Iyar — thirteen; and combined with the above eighty-eight it comes to one hundred and one prayers. And in the Tur he wrote ninety times; and as Magen Avraham wrote in sk 12 — and perhaps he intended what was said. And so I teach and for the outset one should say one hundred and one times. However, one who said only ninety times and again became uncertain whether he mentioned [rain] — I have no authority to rule that he returns and prays, against the implication of the Gemara and the Rama there:
