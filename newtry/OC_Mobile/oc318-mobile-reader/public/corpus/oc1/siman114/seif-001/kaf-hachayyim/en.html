1) [Seif 1] We begin to say in the second berachah 'He causes the wind to blow,' etc. The reason is that just as the resurrection of the dead is life to the world, so too rain is life to the world. Tur; Levush; Taz, s.k. 1; Olas Tamid, ot 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ot 1.
2) There: We begin to say in the second berachah, etc. The reason it does not open with 'Baruch' is that it is adjacent to the one before it. Tur. And so it is with all the berachos until the end of the tefillah. Levush; Ateres Zekeinim, ot 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav there. See above at the beginning of siman 113.
3) One should read 'Atah Gibor' with a hard (dageshed) gimmel. Mateh Moshe, siman 125, in the name of the Tzioni; Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah in the Hagahos HaTur, ot 1; Olas Tamid there; Ateres Zekeinim there; Eliyah Rabbah, ot 1; Shalmei Tzibbur, folio 115d; Kaf HaChaim, siman 15, ot 22; and in the sefer Einei HaEidah, folio 28a, who wrote that in the berachah of 'Atah Gibor' one should have in mind the Name Agla [formed by the initial letters of 'Atah Gibor L'olam Ado-nai'], for it is a segulah for success and for favor; see there. Ruach Chaim, ot 3; Ben Ish Chai, parashas Beshalach, ot 13.
4) There: In the Musaf prayer of the last Yom Tov of the festival, etc. This means that one begins to mention [rain] in the Musaf prayer of the first day of the last Yom Tov of the festival [Shemini Atzeres], and one does not stop until the Shacharis prayer of the first day of Pesach; and the Shacharis prayer is included among those in which one mentions it. Tur; Levush.
5) There: In the Musaf prayer of the last Yom Tov, etc. The reason one does not begin at Maariv is that not everyone is in the synagogue, and they would form separate groups, this one mentioning [rain] and that one not mentioning it. And the reason one does not begin at Shacharis is so that a person should not think that they had mentioned it from the evening, and the following year mention it in the evening. There is yet another reason why one does not begin at Shacharis: for Rabbi Chaggai bar Padas said that it is forbidden for an individual to mention [rain] until the shaliach tzibbur mentions it; that is, the shaliach tzibbur must say 'Mashiv haruach' aloud before the tefillah, and this is impossible at Shacharis, since one must juxtapose [the berachah of] redemption to the tefillah. So wrote the Rosh according to the Yerushalmi, and the Beis Yosef cited it. Derishah, ot 1; Taz, s.k. 2; Magen Avraham, s.k. 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav there. The Yerushalmi further explains another reason: Rabbi Avin said in the name of Rabbi Yochanan that the reason of Rabbi Yehudah is so that the festivals depart with dew, because dew is beneficial to the world. The Rosh explained that this reason applies to the mentioning [of rain] and not to the stopping of it, for on Yom Tov everyone comes to the synagogue in the evening, since one does not perform melacha on Chol HaMoed; therefore the first reason is not sufficient, and so he differentiates, in order that dew be mentioned on the festivals also at Maariv and Shacharis. End of his words. The Beis Yosef and the Derishah cited it there. See the following ot and ot 7.
6) In some countries where they have the custom to mention rain on the day of Hoshana Rabbah, they do not act properly; however, if on Hoshana Rabbah they announce 'Mashiv haruach,' etc., in order that they begin to mention it on the night of Shemini Atzeres, there is some support for their custom. Radbaz, volume 1, siman 240; Knesses HaGedolah in the Hagahos HaTur; Magen Avraham, s.k. 1. However, from the words of the Rav [the Arizal], of blessed memory, in Shaar HaKavanos, in the ninth discourse on Sukkos, and in Pri Etz Chaim, Shaar HaLulav, chapter 8, it appears that the time for saying 'Mashiv haruach umorid hageshem' begins specifically from the Musaf prayer of Shemini Atzeres onward, and not before then, as seen there, for a reason rooted in the hidden [teachings]. See further the following ot.
7) On the night of the last Yom Tov of the festival, if one mentioned rain in place of dew, the Gan HaMelech, siman 146, wrote that he does not repeat [the tefillah], and Yad Aharon cited it in the Hagahos HaTur. However, the Erech HaShulchan, ot 1, wrote that this is only according to the first reason that the Rosh wrote in the name of the Yerushalmi, that it would have been fitting to mention [rain] at Maariv were it not in order that the congregation not become divided; but according to the second reason that the Rosh wrote in the name of the Yerushalmi, that the festivals should depart with dew, if one mentioned rain on the night of the last Yom Tov of the festival he must repeat. And even on the first night of Pesach, when one does not stop [mentioning rain] at Maariv so that there not be [divided] groups, it is possible that if he did not mention rain he must repeat, since the enactment of the Sages was to mention it only at Musaf, so that Maariv and Shacharis are like the other days, in which one repeats, for it is a deviation from the fixed formula; and it is not comparable to that case in siman 117; see there. However, the Shalmei Tzibbur, folio 121c, ruled like the sefer Gan HaMelech that he does not repeat. So too ruled the Chida in Kesher Gudal, siman 14, ot 14; the Siddur Beis Oved, in the laws of dew and rain, ot 6; and so wrote the sefer Derech HaChaim, ot 3, that if one erred and mentioned 'Mashiv haruach,' etc., at Maariv of Shemini Atzeres or at Shacharis, he does not repeat. Mishnah Berurah, ot 2. And likewise, if one erred at Maariv of Pesach, or at Shacharis, or at Musaf (that is, for Ashkenazim, who say 'Mashiv haruach,' etc., at Musaf, as stated in seif 3 in the gloss) and did not mention 'Mashiv haruach,' he does not repeat. Derech HaChaim, ot 8; Mishnah Berurah, ot 3.
