1) [Seif 1] We begin to say in the second blessing, 'Mashiv haruach,' etc. The reason is that just as resurrection of the dead gives enduring life, so rain sustains life continually. Tur; Levush; Taz, seif katan 1; Olat Tamid, letter 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, letter 1. 2) There, we begin to say in the second blessing, etc. It does not open with 'Blessed' because it is adjacent to the preceding blessing. Tur. The same applies to all the blessings through the end of the Amidah. Levush; Ateret Zekenim, letter 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav there. See above at the beginning of siman 113. 3) One should pronounce 'Atah Gibor' with a dagesh in the gimel. Mateh Moshe, siman 125, in the name of Tziyoni; Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahot HaTur, letter 1; Olat Tamid there; Ateret Zekenim there; Eliyah Rabbah, letter 1; Shalmei Tzibbur, page 115d; Kaf HaChayyim, siman 15, letter 22. Sefer Einei HaEdah, page 28a, writes that during the blessing Atah Gibor one should have in mind the Divine Name represented by the initials AGLA, as a segulah for success and favor; see there. Ruach Chaim, letter 3; Ben Ish Chai, Parashat Beshalach, letter 13. 4) There, in the Mussaf prayer of the final Yom Tov of Sukkot, etc. Meaning, one begins mentioning rain in Mussaf on the first day of the final Yom Tov of Sukkot and continues through Shacharit on the first day of Pesach, inclusive. Tur; Levush. 5) There, in the Mussaf prayer of the final Yom Tov, etc. We do not begin at Maariv because not everyone is in the synagogue, and the congregation would become divided, with some mentioning rain and others not. We do not begin at Shacharit so that no one will suppose that it had already been mentioned the previous evening and then, in the following year, begin at night. There is another reason: Rabbi Chaggai bar Padas said that an individual may not mention rain until the prayer leader does so. This means that the prayer leader must announce 'Mashiv haruach' aloud before the prayer, which cannot be done at Shacharit because redemption must be joined immediately to the Amidah. So writes the Rosh based on the Yerushalmi, cited by Beit Yosef; Derishah, letter 1; Taz, seif katan 2; Magen Avraham, seif katan 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav there. The Yerushalmi gives another reason. Rabbi Avin said in the name of Rabbi Yochanan that Rabbi Yehudah's reason is that the festivals should depart with dew, because dew is beneficial. Rosh explains that this reason concerns beginning the mention of rain rather than ceasing it, because on Yom Tov everyone comes to synagogue in the evening and the first reason is therefore insufficient. Thus they arrange matters so that dew is still mentioned during the festival at Maariv and Shacharit. Beit Yosef and Derishah cite this. See the next letter and letter 7. 6) In some countries the custom is to mention rain on Hoshana Rabbah. They do not act properly. If, however, they announce 'Mashiv haruach,' etc. on Hoshana Rabbah so that people begin at night on Shemini Atzeret, there is some basis for the custom. Radbaz, volume 1, siman 240; Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahot HaTur; Magen Avraham, seif katan 1. From the words of the Arizal in Shaar HaKavanot, discourse 9 on Sukkot, and Pri Etz Chaim, Shaar HaLulav, chapter 8, it appears that the proper time for saying 'Mashiv haruach umorid hageshem' begins specifically with Mussaf of Shemini Atzeret and not before, for a kabbalistic reason explained there. See the next letter. 7) On the night of the final Yom Tov of Sukkot, if one mentioned rain in place of dew, Gan HaMelech, siman 146, rules that he does not repeat the Amidah, and Yad Aharon cites him. Erech HaShulchan, letter 1, writes that this is only according to the first reason of the Rosh in the name of the Yerushalmi, that it would have been fitting to mention rain at Maariv were it not for the concern that the congregation would become divided. According to the second reason, however, that the festivals should depart with dew, one who mentioned rain on the night of the final Yom Tov must repeat. Even on the first night of Pesach, when we do not cease at Maariv in order to avoid division, it is possible that one who failed to mention rain must repeat, since the enactment was not to cease until Mussaf. Maariv and Shacharit therefore retain the law of the preceding days, and changing the fixed formula requires repetition. Nevertheless, Shalmei Tzibbur, page 121c, rules like Gan HaMelech that he does not repeat. So rule Chida in Kesher Gudal, siman 14, letter 14; Siddur Beit Oved, laws of dew and rain, letter 6; and Derech HaChaim, letter 3, that if one mistakenly said 'Mashiv haruach,' etc. at Maariv of Shemini Atzeret or at Shacharit, he does not repeat. Mishnah Berurah, letter 2. Likewise, if one erred at Maariv of Pesach, Shacharit, or Mussaf, for Ashkenazim who still mention rain in Mussaf as stated in seif 3 in the Rama, and omitted 'Mashiv haruach,' he does not repeat. Derech HaChaim, letter 8; Mishnah Berurah, letter 3.
