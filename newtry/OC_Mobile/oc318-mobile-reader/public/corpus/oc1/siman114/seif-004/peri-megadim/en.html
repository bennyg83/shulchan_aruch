Until [the phrase "until"]. Ben Zion elaborated on what [the versions differ about], and some say; therefore the Taz explained that the girsa here is "until the shaliach tzibur mentions" (attune de-din ke-din). In the Yerushalmi that the Asheri brought it is written: R. Chagai bar Pedat said, until the shaliach tzibur mentions. There are three explanations on this for one who wishes to study; in Beit Yosef and Bach the Rosh explained as if he said "until he announces" — meaning before Musaf the shaliach tzibur or the shamash announces, and the congregation mentions at Musaf. Rav Amram — they brought the Rosh — that the shaliach tzibur mentions in the repetition and the congregation at Minchah (as for us in the pause in seif 3). The explanation of "mentions" would be literal. Abba Ebi Ezri — for the Bach's explanation — is a third view: one does not announce before Musaf, only in Musaf silently, since once the shaliach tzibur has already mentioned it silently the congregation may also mention silently. Accordingly, before the Mishna Berurah the Mechaber's girsa was "forbidden until the shaliach tzibur mentions," like the Yerushalmi's version and Rav Amram's explanation; on this he wrote "and some say it is not so, but like the Rosh — he announces before Musaf and the congregation mentions silently." In letter 6 he challenged this: if so, how did the Mechaber bring "therefore it is forbidden to precede" — those are the words of Abba Ebi Ezri; for Rav Amram it does not apply. Therefore he emended that "and some say" should be deleted. The Levush wrote that for this reason (aguda) it is forbidden to mention until the shaliach tzibur or shamash announces — see there — and omitted "and some say." But Magen Avraham, siman 2, appears to explain that the Mechaber means "until the shaliach tzibur announces," specifically not the shamash — that is Abba Ebi Ezri's explanation (as the Beit Yosef understood plainly): the shaliach tzibur in the silent prayer says aloud "Who causes the wind to blow and Who causes the rain to fall," the congregation hears and they also mention silently after hearing from the shaliach tzibur. The Mishna Berurah emended that it is not so; rather before Musaf the shaliach tzibur or shamash announces. With God's help this will be explained further in Magen Avraham.

He further brought the Beit Yosef's difficulty on the Tur and Ra'avad: in the Yerushalmi it is explained that dew is nullified in summer days (and likewise for us, the pause) — if an individual wishes, he may mention (or pause) before the shaliach tzibur. This is R. Chagai bar Pedat's second [reason] — only the mention of rain, because there it is chol hamo'ed and [people] are present in the synagogue, as the Rosh wrote — see there. The Taz distinguishes between an individual and the congregation; the practical ruling will be explained in Magen Avraham, siman 2, with God's help.
