"And one returns to the beginning of the blessing" — and the Gra ruled like the Rambam, that in this case too one returns to the beginning [of the Amidah]:

"And if one concluded the blessing," etc. — and it is not similar to that [case] in seif 6, where specifically if one began the blessing after it — for here, since one mentioned what is not fit to mention, it detracts; and similarly Levush, Beit Yosef, and Ohr Zarua ruled that in this case too, specifically if one began the blessing after it; otherwise it is as though one still stands in that blessing and returns to the beginning of the blessing. And Peri Megadim brought their dispute and concluded "it requires study," and a doubt regarding blessings is resolved leniently; but Darkhei Chaim, Chayei Adam, Shulchan Shlomo, and Shulchan Atzei Shittim ruled like the unstated ruling of Shulchan Aruch; and so for practice, since in any case the view of the Gra is like the Rambam — that even if one remembered in the middle of a blessing one returns to the beginning — and also there are several great poskim who disagree with R. Aviyah, whom Shulchan Aruch ruled in seif 6 like his view — that one says it without a closing formula before beginning the blessing after it, and holds that since one finished that blessing it is as though one began another blessing — therefore in any case in our matter, where perhaps R. Aviyah also holds like the other poskim, one should not deviate from the unstated ruling of Shulchan Aruch: that after one finished the blessing, even if one has not yet begun the blessing after it, one returns to the beginning of the prayer. And afterward I found, with God's help, in Maamar Mordechai, that he ruled like Shulchan Aruch for all these reasons:

"And even in a place that needs [rain]," etc. — see in Peri Megadim that he wrote that even an entire land like Ashkenaz and our [lands] — nevertheless it is worse than asking [for rain in the wrong place]; see there his reason; and similarly Chayei Adam wrote that even if there was a withholding of rain — and as I copied in Mishnah Berurah — and in truth I did not understand their words; for behold the reason of Shulchan Aruch there regarding asking [for rain] is that perhaps the halakhah is like the Rosh, whose reason is plausible — see in Beit Yosef there [Mahari Abuhav, Taz, and Gra]; and in the responsum of the Rosh it is explained explicitly there that the same applies regarding mentioning [rain]; and proof from there from 4b in the conclusion of the Gemara that asking [for rain] does not extend more than mentioning [rain]; and as the Gra noted there in siman 117, seif 1, in the passage beginning "And they ask [for rain]"; see there. And from Shulchan Aruch that wrote "even in a place," etc. — it is not proof, for perhaps it deals with certain towns; for in that case even for the Rosh one does not mention rain, for regarding asking too one does not ask for rain in the blessing of the years in that [case] — as he brought proof there, in the responsum from that [case] of the people of Nineveh. And in this manner the Rambam also wrote: that in places that need rain in the hot season, one asks in Shema Kolenu — as Beit Yosef brought all this in his name there in siman 117. But [regarding] one entire land that holds like the Rosh for after the fact — that if one changed the prayer formula and fixed asking [for rain] in the blessing of the years, one has fulfilled — and therefore holds that this is not called a change from the currency that the Sages established, for each country and country its time is fixed according to its custom — if so, it seems the same applies regarding mentioning [rain]: one has fulfilled after the fact. And see in Beit Yosef what he wrote in the name of Terumat HaDeshen — it also seems to deal with a matter where the law is in Shomea Tefillah, but not where the law is in the blessing of the years. And even if you say that Terumat HaDeshen does not hold like the Rosh regarding asking, and the law is always according to him in Shema Kolenu — in any case there is no proof from the Mechaber for the words of Peri Megadim and Chayei Adam; and on the contrary, the opposite seems from the words of that responsum of the Rosh, and as we rule like him for after the fact. And if so, accordingly in our countries that need rain between Pesach and Shavuot, it would seem fit for us to rule after the fact that one does not return if one mentioned rain; but since in any case Magen Avraham is uncertain in siman 117, sk 4, that perhaps Shulchan Aruch is uncertain to rule like the Rosh only when there is a withholding of rain — it seems that this [leniency] does not apply; and proof for this also seems from the words of the Rama below in seif 9 in the gloss — for behold in our countries it was [dry], and nevertheless he requires returning if one certainly mentioned [rain]; but when there is a withholding of rain and one prayed and mentioned rain, it requires study whether one must return and pray [and all the more so if one also asked for rain — for otherwise one certainly has not fulfilled after the fact]. And it is more plausible that one should pray as a voluntary [offering] in any case in our countries; and see in siman 117 in Biur Halacha in the passage beginning "And one asks for rain":

"We make him go back" — see in Chiddushei R. Akiva Eiger, who tends to say that on Shabbat night, if one forgot to say "Who makes the wind blow," we do not make him go back — for it is no worse than if one prayed only the seven-blessing [Amidah], in which case one has fulfilled after the fact even though rain was not mentioned there; and he concluded "it requires study":
