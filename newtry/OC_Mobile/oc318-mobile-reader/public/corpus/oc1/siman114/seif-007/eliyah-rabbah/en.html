[7] If he completed, etc. The entire passage is regarded as one extended conclusion, and this is not considered concluding with two themes, since rain is equivalent to resurrection. According to this, the insertion is effective only if he says it without interruption. According to the reason I wrote above, however, even if he paused briefly between “Who causes the wind to blow” and “Blessed are You, Hashem, Who revives the dead,” he may still say it. The Tur writes similarly regarding requesting rain: if he remembered before beginning “Sound the great shofar,” he says “Grant dew and rain” there, because rain is included in the blessing of the years. This explains siman 188, where on Shabbos and Yom Tov, if he remembered before beginning HaTov VeHaMeitiv, he must say the special blessing “Who granted,” etc., because those themes are not included in the preceding blessing. Levush. This implies that the insertion is allowed only for omissions requiring repetition. For omissions that do not require repetition, such as Yaaleh VeYavo on Rosh Chodesh night, Al HaNissim, or Aneinu, he does not insert them once he has completed their blessing, even before beginning the next blessing. Beit Yosef, Bach, Taz, and Magen Avraham write this, and Bach writes likewise in siman 682. This is difficult, for in siman 119 Bach himself rules regarding Aneinu that as long as one has not begun Retzei, he may say it. This requires study. I saw that Maadanei Melech, page 35, and Piskei Tosafos disagree at length with Beit Yosef and hold that for every omission not requiring repetition, one may insert it until beginning the next blessing. This requires study regarding the later authorities. There is also clear proof from the Tur in siman 188 in the name of Rabbi Yosef. See there. I later found that Chiddushei Rashba to the chapter Tefillas HaShachar forbids this, and writes that Tosafos likewise forbid it because it constitutes an interruption. This also appears to me from the Rosh and Mordechai there, who explain the matter in terms of interruption. I therefore say that in this matter it is preferable to refrain from acting.
