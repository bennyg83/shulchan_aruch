But for the reason I wrote above, even if he paused a little between "Who makes the wind blow" and "Blessed are You, the Master, Who revives the dead," he also says it. The Tur wrote, and similarly in a question: if he remembered before he began to blow the shofar, he says "there" and "and give dew and rain," for rain is included in the blessing of the years. And now it works out in siman 488 regarding Shabbat and Yom Tov, that if he remembered before he began "hatov vehametiv" he must say "Blessed is He Who gave," etc., for those are not included in the blessing before it (Levush). It implies in matters where we make one go back; but in matters where we do not make one go back, such as "Yaaleh veYavo" on the night of Rosh Chodesh and "Al haNissim" — and our topic: even if he did not begin the blessing after it, he does not mention it — and similarly wrote the Beit Yosef, Bach, Taz, and Magen Avraham; and the Bach wrote so in siman 492. It is difficult, for in siman 119 the Bach himself ruled in our topic that whoever did not begin may say it; and study is needed, for I saw in Maadanei Melech, page 35, and Piskei Tosafot disagree with the Beit Yosef at length, that in all matters where we do not make one go back, if he did not begin, etc., he may say it — and study is needed on the later authorities. There is also clear proof from the Tur, siman 488, in the name of Rabbi Yosef; see there. Again I found in the novelties of the Rashba, chapter "Prayer of the Morning," that he forbids it, and the Tosafot wrote so, that it is an interruption; and so it seems to me in the Rosh and Mordechai there, from the fact that they wrote the reason is because of interruption; and I say in this that "sit and do not act" is preferable.
