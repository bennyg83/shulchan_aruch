(7) On the night of the last Yom Tov of the festival: if he mentioned rain instead of dew, Gan HaMelech, siman 146, wrote that he does not go back, and Yeshuot Yaakov brought it in Hagahat Tzioni. However, Erech Hashulchan, sign 1, wrote that this is specifically per the first reason that the Rosh wrote in the name of the Yerushalmi, that it would have been fitting to mention it in Maariv were it not so that the congregation would get mixed up; but per the second reason that the Rosh wrote in the name of the Yerushalmi — so that the festivals should go out with dew — if he mentioned rain on the night of the last Yom Tov of the festival, he must go back. And even on the night of the first Yom Tov of Pesach, where they do not stop in Maariv so there should not be groups — it is possible that if he did not mention rain he must go back, since the enactment of the Sages is not to mention it except in Musaf; it would be that Maariv and Shacharit are like other days, where one goes back because it is a change of nature, and it is not comparable to that case in siman 117; see there. However, the Shlah, page 121 side 3, ruled like Gan HaMelech that he does not go back; and similarly the Chida in Kissei Rachamim, siman 14, sign 14; the siddur of Beit Yosef in the laws of dew and rain, sign 6; and similarly in Derech Chayim, sign 3: if he erred and mentioned "Who makes the wind blow," etc., in Maariv of Shemini Atzeret or in Shacharit, he does not go back — Mishnah Berurah, sign 2. And similarly if he erred in Maariv of Pesach or in Shacharit or in Musaf (that is, for Ashkenazim who say in Musaf "Who makes the wind blow," etc., as written in seif 3 in the gloss) and did not mention "Who makes the wind blow," he does not go back — Chayei Adam, sign 8; Mishnah Berurah, sign 3.
