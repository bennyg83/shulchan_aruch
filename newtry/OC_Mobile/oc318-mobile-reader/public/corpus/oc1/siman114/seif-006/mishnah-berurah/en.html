(28) And he began—even the word "Atah" alone; and the same applies if he began to say "Nekadesh."
(29) In whatever place he remembers—for the Sages did not fix a place within the blessing, but said generally one mentions the might of rains in techiyat ha-metim; only they practiced saying before "michalkel chayim," for that is sustenance and rains are also sustenance; therefore in whatever place he remembers it suffices. And it is simple that if he remembered after he said "vene'eman attah lehachayot metim," he must return and say "vene'eman attah lehachayot metim," for we require a subject resembling the closing near the closing.
(30) And even if he finished—and likewise if he remembered after he said the Name, he should finish "mechayeh hametim" and immediately say Mashiv ha-ruach and morid ha-geshem.
(31) Rather he says — and it is proper lekhatchilah that he say it immediately, so he not wait until after kedei dibbur.
(32) Without a closing — and again he says "You are holy," and the same applies if he forgot Yaaleh veYavo on Rosh Chodesh in Shacharis and Minchah and remembered after he finished the blessing of Mechayeh haMetim, which says [God's name] in its place, and he begins Modim. And as explained below in siman 422, seif 1: anything that did not begin the blessing after it is not called the complete end of this blessing for all matters that require returning him, even though it is called an ending for matters that do not require returning him, such as Havdalah in Chonen haDa'at, Yaaleh veYavo in Maariv of Rosh Chodesh, Al haNissim on Chanukah and Purim, and all similar cases where one does not say them when he finished the blessing even though he did not begin the blessing after it. And the end of a blessing is called immediately when he said the name of the blessing. And there are authorities who disagree and say that even for matters that require returning him, immediately upon finishing the blessing it is as if he began another blessing; and in any event it seems for practical law that if he remembered after he said [God's] name he should not finish Mechayeh haMetim but should finish "Teach me Your statutes" and it will be as reading a verse alone, and his law is as if he still stands in the middle of a blessing and returns to Mashiv haRuach, and similarly in all matters that require returning him, as I explained in Biur Halacha there.
(33) The first [blessings] — he mentioned the first [blessings] because the discussion concerns them, and the same applies to the last three. And the reason is that the first three have one theme — to arrange praise of the Omnipresent before requesting one's needs, like a servant who arranges praise before his master before requesting reward from him. And the last three are like a servant who received reward from his master, praises him, and departs.
(34) He returns to the beginning — and specifically if he erred in their closings, signing this blessing in the theme of another blessing, or signed with "HaKel haKadosh" and did not remember until after kedei dibbur, for he cannot again fix his error since returning is not effective except within kedei dibbur. And all similar cases — any matter that if it occurred in another blessing he would need to return to the beginning of that blessing — therefore in the first three and last [blessings] he returns to the beginning since they are considered as one. But if he erred in the middle it does not hold him up. And see above in siman 68, note 1 in Mishna Berurah where we explained at length.
