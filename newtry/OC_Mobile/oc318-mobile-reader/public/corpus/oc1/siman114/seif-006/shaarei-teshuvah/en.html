The same — Beer Heitev. See in Shulchan Atzei Shalom that if it happened he did not return to the beginning of the blessing but [only] to "You are mighty…" and finished his blessing — we do not return him. It is explained in Ritva's novelae on Taanis: where one returns to the beginning of the first blessing, he does not return to "O the Master, open my lips." He also wrote that if he erred and said "Who causes the rain to fall" in summer days and did not remember until he said "Blessed are You" — he finishes "Teach me Your statutes" and again returns to pray from the beginning of the blessing. It appears that even if he wants the "Blessed are You" he said to count as the place of the beginning of the blessing and to say "Our Heaven and Heaven of our fathers," etc. — it did not help him, since his saying "Blessed are You" was directed toward finishing "Who revives the dead." See above siman 59 and 87; therefore he needed to finish "We have learned Your statutes" and then return to the beginning — see Likutei. He also wrote further in Shulchan Atzei Shalom there: in one who erred and mentioned rain in summer days and did not remember until "the holy Heaven" — whose ruling is to return to the beginning — he may not rely on intending with the shaliach tzibur's repetition; he must return immediately to the beginning, and then he can finish his prayer before the shaliach tzibur begins "Kadosh." Even for one who holds below siman 124 that one may rely on intending with the shaliach tzibur — nevertheless one can say that if he erred in the first three [blessings], which are considered one, it is as if he did not pray at all, and therefore the shaliach tzibur does not discharge him. He wavered in the matter here and there and it is weak in his hand — see there. According to his explanation it appears that post facto, if he acted and intended with the shaliach tzibur's repetition, he need not return and pray. Nevertheless it is possible he should repeat and pray as a voluntary prayer in which he also innovates something, since one can say that what he intended with the shaliach tzibur counted for him. Also, that he does not now say "Who causes the rain to fall" — this is not a new matter.
