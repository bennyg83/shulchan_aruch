"At the place where one remembered" — Peri Megadim wrote that regarding asking for rain: if one forgot and remembered before finishing the blessing, one returns and says "and give dew and rain" and concludes there in order. And from the words of Shulchan Shlomo it is implied that one may also say dew and rain after "and satisfy us from Your goodness" if one wishes. But regarding "on the miracles" on Chanukah and Purim — if one forgot and remembered before finishing the blessing "the Good One — Your Name," it is implied from the words of both of them that one returns and says afterward "and for all of them may Your Name be blessed," etc., in order. The Acharonim wrote that if one remembered in a place where the matter is not finished — such as immediately after saying "and maintains His faith" — one finishes from "to the sleepers in dust" onward, and all similar cases:

"Without a closing formula" — this law in practice, in my humble opinion, requires much study; for behold the source of this law stems from R. Aviyah, whom the Rosh brought in chapter 1 of Ta'anit and the Mordechai there; and see in Beit Yosef that Rashba disagrees with this reasoning and rules like Rabbenu Yonah, who holds that since one finished that blessing it is as though one began another and one returns to the beginning. And behold even though Beit Yosef, Magen Avraham, and other Acharonim hold that for halakhah we follow the view of Shulchan Aruch, who holds that since the Rosh brought the view of R. Aviyah for halakhah, so we follow — nevertheless one difficulty remains: for Ohr Zarua remains in doubt regarding the words of the Mordechai, who wrote in his name that one says it without a closing formula — which contradicts what he wrote in his name in the chapter "Prayer of the Morning," that according to what he wrote there the law would be that one must begin from "Who makes the wind blow" and finish until the end of the blessing [and what Peri Megadim wanted to answer for some of his difficulty has no place at all for one who examines the Agudah itself, which wrote there explicitly that R. Aviyah ruled like our Talmud and not like the Jerusalem Talmud; and also that the Mordechai deals with the evening of Rosh Chodesh]. A second [difficulty]: for in Biur HaGra a difficulty also remains on the ruling of Shulchan Aruch [and see in Tzelach, who also challenged R. Aviyah from what he said there regarding Avodah — "one returns to Avodah" — and answered somewhat]. And it is implied that he also holds like Rashba; and apart from this I found several great Rishonim who hold not like R. Aviyah — namely Ra'avyah, brought in Sha'agat Aryeh in the chapter "Prayer of the Morning"; and also in Sefer Berakhah Meshuleshet in Tosafot R. Yehudah he-Hasid in the name of his teacher; and similarly in Hagahot Semak he wrote explicitly that even if one remembered before beginning "Ata Kadosh," one returns to the beginning; and from the Rambam too it is implied that one always returns to the beginning, as brought in Beit Yosef; and similarly in Kitzur Shulchan Aruch he also ruled that one returns to the beginning — see in the gloss there at the end of his book — and the law requires study. And behold even though to decide against Shulchan Aruch is very difficult after that many, many great Acharonim uphold the method of Shulchan Aruch and strained themselves to resolve all the difficulties on this method — nevertheless it seems to me that if after saying "Elokeinu" one remembered that one forgot "Who makes the wind blow," one should not finish the blessing but should conclude "teach me Your statutes" so that it is like reading a verse alone, and return to "Who makes the wind blow" and finish in order; and the same applies to "May our eyes behold" and similar — if one forgot and remembered after saying "Elokeinu," one finishes "teach me Your statutes" and afterward says "May our eyes behold Your return to Zion," etc., in order. And regarding dew and rain as well — accordingly it seems that if one forgot to mention it in the middle and finished the blessing of the years, even though one did not begin Tekiah — nevertheless one should not again say here "and give dew and rain," for one cannot say it in Shomea Tefillah, and one has fulfilled according to all opinions:

"And in every place one erred in them" — and what was ruled in seif 4, that if one mentioned rain one returns only to the beginning of the blessing — the Acharonim wrote that they hold that since one has not yet finished the blessing, this is not yet a complete error such that one must return to the beginning on account of it; rather we only require a simple return — that one repeat the blessing and not say "and"; and see above in siman 104, sk 21, in Mishnah Berurah in what he wrote there, in the name of Chayei Adam, that the same applies regarding [mentioning] "He causes the wind to blow" or mentioning the event of other days in prayer — all of these where one has not finished that blessing need only return to its beginning:
