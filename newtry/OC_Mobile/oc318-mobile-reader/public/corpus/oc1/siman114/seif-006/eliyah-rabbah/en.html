[6] He completed, etc. This applies even if he did not yet begin “You are holy,” etc. Although seif 7 states that as long as he has not begun “You are holy,” he may say “Who causes the wind to blow,” it appears that there the reason is, as Levush writes, that it is considered one extended conclusion. Here, however, he has already completed the conclusion and the blessing has ended, so he must return to its beginning. Bach writes, however, that here too it refers specifically to a case where he already began “You are holy,” as in the later ruling. He apparently understands that there, once he began “You are holy,” the previous blessing is considered fully ended, whereas here, as long as he has not begun “You are holy,” he returns only to “You are mighty.” The Tur and Shulchan Aruch speak without qualification, and the later authorities did not note this dispute. As a practical ruling, Bach’s view appears correct to me.
