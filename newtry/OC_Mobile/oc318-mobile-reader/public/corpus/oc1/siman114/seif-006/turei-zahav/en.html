Without a concluding blessing. In the Mordechai, first chapter of Taanis, may his memory be blessed, and Ravyah ruled that if he did not say "He Who makes the wind blow," etc., and remembered between "He revives the dead" and "You are holy," he need not return at all; rather he says "He Who makes the wind blow" and "He Who makes the rain descend" without a concluding blessing, and again says "You are holy." And the same law applies in the request of Yaaleh v'Yavo where he had not begun the blessing that follows it — as stated in chapter 3 of "that ate": regarding one who erred and did not mention Rosh Chodesh, he says "Baruch Who gave Rosh Chodesh," etc., and concludes: "I do not know whether to conclude or not; and since it is a doubt, one does not conclude out of doubt" — end quote. And as Beit Yosef: this applies only if he mentioned Rosh Chodesh in the festival blessing, where we are in doubt in the Gemara whether to conclude; therefore from doubt one does not conclude. And Ravyah thinks the same applies to prayer. One might say: since on Shabbat and Yom Tov one concludes "Baruch Who gave," etc., and concludes so — it should have been stated regarding prayer, for prayer is obligatory; we compare it to the festival blessing of Shabbat and Yom Tov, not to Rosh Chodesh... as stated in chapter 3 of "that ate." And it is possible, etc. — end quote. The Rav, may his memory be blessed, did not analyze here as needed regarding what was challenged to him: why does Ravyah compare prayer to Rosh Chodesh and not to Shabbat and Yom Tov? And thus he entered a great forced explanation of what he wrote, "and it is possible," etc., which is a matter that has no place; and truth will show his path, for there is no question here at all. For the Mordechai in the name of Ravyah did not come here to bring proof from Rosh Chodesh regarding not concluding here in prayer — this was never and will never be: who would invent from his heart that one should conclude here with "Baruch," and we have eighteen blessings, not nineteen? And if there were nineteen here, furthermore a concluding blessing does not apply here — how would he conclude, and in what formula would he conclude? Rather it is simple that Ravyah came here only to answer a difficulty: namely, that first he brings proof that any case where one did not begin the blessing that follows it is not called a conclusion, as we find regarding "hatov vehametiv" on Rosh Chodesh; and so that the question should not be difficult — for there is no comparison to there, where one is concluding, as we find regarding Shabbat and Yom Tov; but here a concluding blessing does not apply. And since a concluding blessing does not apply here, we do not find to say here that one should say "He Who makes the wind blow," etc., before "You are holy" — for perhaps the sages did not give permission to mention before beginning the second blessing, except where a concluding blessing is possible. Therefore he says that regarding Rosh Chodesh there is doubt in the Gemara, chapter 3 of "that ate," page 49, in Rav's words, whether he says it with a concluding blessing or without — automatically we rule that he says it without a concluding blessing; therefore there is relevance to say that one says it without a concluding blessing. If so, likewise regarding "He Who makes the wind blow," even though there is no concluding blessing there at all, there is relevance to say "He Who makes the wind blow" before "You are holy." And Levush on Moed, may his memory be blessed: I saw that he wrote that this which the Mordechai wrote and concludes "I do not know whether to conclude," etc., is proof for Ravyah that he was in doubt about "He Who makes the wind blow" whether to conclude or not, and on this he said: from doubt one does not conclude. And he wrote against Beit Yosef and left him an orphan — he did not analyze this at all, for the Mordechai already decided that one may say "He Who makes the wind blow" without a concluding blessing — how would he be in doubt afterward? And furthermore, since even regarding Rosh Chodesh there is doubt in the Gemara and it was not resolved, what proof does he bring from that doubt to Ravyah's doubt? And furthermore, what doubt would he have regarding "He Who makes the wind blow" — how does a concluding blessing apply here? Would he return to say again in "Blessed are You... Who revives the dead," or say a new formula in "Blessed are You... Who makes rain descend"? The matter is clear, as stated in seif dalet in explanation of the Mordechai's words: the doubt is only regarding the festival blessing, as Beit Yosef stated — and he is a man whom the Master helps. Only what he understood, that the Mordechai came to bring proof from Rosh Chodesh regarding that a concluding blessing is not needed — this is not so; proof is not needed for this; rather he came to answer a difficulty as stated. But the main proof he brought only regarding that one does not return before beginning the blessing that follows it — and this is correct. And what the Rav said here "without a concluding blessing" is not for any need, for certainly a concluding blessing does not apply here at all. In Beit Yosef he proved that we do not say: since he finished the blessing and did not begin the other, he says what he skipped — this applies to something they return him for; but for something they do not return him for, as soon as he finished the blessing one may say it even though he did not begin the other. Beit Yosef wrote: Rabbi Meir — why did they not enact a separate blessing in the place where one mentions, as in the festival blessing? If one forgot, he recites one blessing of Shabbat; the Rambam says because there was a pause in your hand; and some explain for the festival blessing that there he had already finished the blessing of "hatov vehametiv," which they enacted in Yavneh. And in the Levush he wrote that "He Who makes the wind blow" and "He Who makes the rain descend" that one says before "You are holy" is like a concluding blessing at length — and this means as if he says in "Blessed are You... Who revives the dead" both "He Who makes the wind blow" and "He Who makes the rain descend," even though he did not mention rain within the blessing; it is included in "Who sustains the living with kindness," for the main sustenance is through rain; and this is not "one does not conclude with two," etc. And even in "Who causes dew and rain to descend" it is so: if he did not say it and finished the blessing and remembered before he began to blow the shofar, he says "and grant dew and rain" before blowing — likewise the reason is because the request for rain is included in "Bless our year," etc., and it is as if he says in "Blessed are You... Who blesses the years" "and grant dew and rain," etc. — end quote. And he extended further regarding Yaaleh v'Yavo from matter to matter, and these words are not fitting for one who said them — for how is it possible to say "Who blesses the years and grant dew and rain," when "grant dew" is future tense of request, and he should have said "Who blesses the years and gives dew and rain," which is also mentioning His praise? All the more so in Yaaleh v'Yavo. And furthermore, nearby he brings Rabbi Yehuda's words on this, and also "Who performs miracles" — the same law would apply for those who hold like Ravyah; but certainly this is an invented matter from the heart to join this to the previous blessing. According to his words it would have to be without any pause until the end of the previous blessing — and this is undoubtedly not so; rather even if he waited a bit after finishing the blessing and remembered before he said the blessing that follows it, he says it at that time. And this is simple; no pilpul is given, in my humble opinion.
