(19) Twilight, etc.: He specified twilight because after the fact, if he blessed during twilight he has fulfilled, as above; and all the more so if someone asked him after the stars appear, he must be careful not to answer "today such-and-such."
(20) Today such-and-such: And even in a vernacular language. And if he did not say "today," we have no concern about it, because the essence of the mitzvah of counting is that he say "today such-and-such."
(21) He cannot, etc.: For even though he did not say "of the omer," he has fulfilled after the fact, as above in s.k. 8.
(22) To go back and count with a blessing: For he has already fulfilled the counting with this according to the view that mitzvos do not require intent; and even though we hold that they require intent, and this one did not intend to fulfill the mitzvah with this, regarding the blessing one must be stringent and be concerned for that view, and he should not bless over the counting that he will count afterward. And there is another counsel: that he intend explicitly not to fulfill the counting with this. The later authorities wrote that this speaks where he was asked before he reached the weeks; but if he was asked at the time he reached the weeks, even if he said to him "today such-and-such," it is nothing as long as he did not mention weeks, and he must go back and count with a blessing.
(23) And when the time arrives: That is to say, the appearance of the stars; and even if they have already prayed Shema and the Amidah, for otherwise even without this it is forbidden because of Shema and the Amidah. And the later authorities agreed that according to what we ruled above in siman 235 to be stringent from a half hour before the time, so too here we should be stringent not to eat from the half hour adjacent to the counting and onward [even if he already prayed early]; but in a place where the custom is that the shamash calls to count the counting, one should not be stringent beforehand.
(24) To eat: And the same applies to other activities, as above in siman 232 and 235.
(25) He stops and counts: That is, according to the view that counting nowadays is according to the Torah, as indicated in the gloss; and if so, for us who hold that counting nowadays is rabbinic, he does not stop, as above in siman 232 regarding Minchah. And some later authorities wrote that therefore the Rama here in our matter was concerned to be stringent like that view that it is according to the Torah, because it is something with no trouble at all to pause a little and count.
(26) Before the time arrived: And according to what we wrote above, that from a half hour adjacent to the time the prohibition begins, the Rama's law would apply when he began eating more than a half hour before the time.
(27) He need not interrupt: Even according to the view that counting nowadays is according to the Torah, since there is still time left to count.
