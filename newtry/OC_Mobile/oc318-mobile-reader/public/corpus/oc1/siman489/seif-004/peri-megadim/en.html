One week. See Taz, Parashas Vayeitzei; and in masculine count the letter hei is a mnemonic of inverted other words; see Beis Shmuel, Even HaEzer 126, letter 9. Therefore "seven weeks" (Devarim 16) is masculine; which is not so for "seven complete sabbaths" (Vayikra 23): Shabbos is feminine language, like "those who desecrate it," etc., "it is holy" (Shemos 31); and "sabbaths" refers there to the day of Shabbos, calling the week thus. And that which is written "one who does," etc., melachah: see Tosafos Kesubos 5a; and siman 292 [2] in Taz. And one may say it refers to "oyam" (Shemos 31); and see Rashi on the Chumash: "a week of this," and it refers to her; and so wrote Rashbam of blessed memory and Ibn Ezra and in the Targumim. Nevertheless "week" with a sheva mobile, not "week" with a kamatz; and he said two things; see there: namely, a week of this, and also the word "week" here means sevenths of days, etc. And I built per the rule of the letter hei in masculine count: a kamatz is a mnemonic, for the hei does not rest in the middle, etc.: "eight" for masculine, the nun with a kamatz; and for feminine, the nun with a segol; "eight" amos. And in other matters the opposite: "sister fair" with a kamatz, "a fair man" with a segol. And what the Mechaber wrote "four days": see Even HaEzer until ten days, and from there onward "day," in siman 126; and see Eliyah Rabbah [6]; and so is the custom to say "eleven days."
