To count again with a blessing. Although he did not say “baOmer,” he fulfilled his obligation after the fact, as Magen Avraham writes. Tur, Rokeach, and Maharil do not require saying “baOmer” even initially. He therefore need not count again with a blessing. Although we rule that mitzvos require intent, as explained in siman 60 and in Beit Yosef's citation from Rashba, one still should not bless again here, as Magen Avraham writes in seif katan 14. One may also say that this is considered intent, since he at least intends to state the number of the Omer count, and the mitzvah consists merely of recounting it. Taz's explanation at the end of seif katan 6 is extremely difficult, for the wording of Shulchan Aruch implies the opposite. This requires study.
When the time arrives, it is forbidden to eat until he counts. Nevertheless, he must first recite Maariv and Krias Shema at their proper time, because they are more frequent and therefore precede counting. This is also implied by Piskei Tosafos cited above in seif katan 13. Shelah writes that where a group prays Maariv at the proper time, it is fitting to join them during the days of the count, for one mitzvah then leads to another: Krias Shema at its proper time and counting with the congregation. This also explains why Rama did not state that eating is forbidden from half an hour before the time, as explained in simanim 232 and 235. That statement was unnecessary, for half an hour beforehand eating is already forbidden because of prayer and Krias Shema at their proper time. Rama needed to teach only that even if one already prayed and recited Krias Shema at the proper time, he still may not eat once the time for counting has arrived. This appears simple and clear, and Magen Avraham needlessly labored to draw various distinctions in order to answer this question. The later authorities write that Maharshal did not eat the evening meal during the days of the count until after nightfall, so that he could count first.
He interrupts and counts. This is because some hold that counting is according to the Torah. Although in seif 6 Rama accepts Mechaber's ruling that one does not recite another blessing, that is because Ran writes that most poskim hold that counting in our time is D'Rabbanan, as explained by Beit Yosef. Rama nevertheless rules stringently here that one should interrupt. What loss is there in interrupting briefly? In the later case, however, there is a concern of reciting a blessing in vain. Nachalas Tzvi leaves Rama's words unresolved, but according to what I have written there is no difficulty.
