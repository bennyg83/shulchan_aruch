For if he says to him "today such-and-such," etc. See Taz, who brought proofs that this is as one who intends not to fulfill; and behold the later authorities rejected his proofs [see Eliyah Rabbah, Maamar Mordechai, and Nahar Shalom]. Nevertheless regarding the essential law there is much to study: for the view of the Gra is that this se'if applies only according to the view that mitzvos do not require intent; and so too is the view of Peri Chadash, that for us who hold that mitzvos require intent he must go back and bless [and he does not hold like the Radbaz to distinguish between Torah-level and rabbinic; and he also does not hold the answers of Chok Yaakov in s.k. 15 and of Magen Avraham in s.k. 8]. And further it appears to my humble opinion that if this was during twilight and this answering person is always accustomed to bless specifically at the appearance of the stars, it is reasonable that in such a manner it is as intending explicitly not to fulfill, and he is permitted afterward to go back and count with a blessing. And for practice this requires further study.
He cannot go back and count with a blessing. But without a blessing he certainly must go back and count; so it is proven from Magen Avraham in s.k. 8, and it is reasonable like him, for nevertheless there is a dispute whether mitzvos require intent; and all the more so during twilight, for the view of Eliyah Rabbah is that even ordinarily, when he counts in the manner of counting, he should afterward go back and count without a blessing; and all the more so here. And what is written regarding before twilight: it appears to me obvious that even if it was after plag haMinchah and the answering person had already prayed Ma'ariv, nevertheless one should not be stringent in such a case, and he may afterward go back and count with a blessing when the time of counting arrives; for this view of after plag is rejected according to the essential law, and one need not be stringent in such a case.
