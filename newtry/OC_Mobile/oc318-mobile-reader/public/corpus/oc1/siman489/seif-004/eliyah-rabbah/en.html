[4] The days and the weeks, etc. In Eliyahu Zuta I brought the words of Rabbeinu Yerucham, folio 44: It is difficult why they do not recite two blessings, one for days and one for weeks, for they are two mitzvos like tefillin of the hand and of the head. And further, why do they say "which are one week"; we should have said "today is one week." And it appears to me that because it is written "seven weeks," etc., it turns out that the counting of weeks was written only in connection with the omer, but the counting of days is not written in connection with the omer; thus days are according to the Torah even nowadays, but the counting of weeks when there is no omer is only a remembrance of the Temple, and for something that is only a remembrance of the Temple one does not recite a blessing over it, just as with the wrapping of Pesach; therefore they say "which are one week," for this is not an actual counting. And if we were counting actually as in the time of the omer, it would appear that the blessing would also apply to the weeks, end quote. And Beis Yosef and all the later authorities noted this novel distinction. And according to this it is proper to rule at least that if he forgot to count the days, even though he counted the weeks, he must go back and bless. However, it appears that this is specifically at the end of each and every week; but in the middle of the weeks, since there are poskim that one need not mention even l'chatchila anything but "today is one week and one day," even though we do not hold thus, nevertheless one should not go back and bless. And below in s.k. 14 this will be further clarified.
