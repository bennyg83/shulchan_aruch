Levush: One does not recite Shehecheyanu, etc. Avudraham explains that from Pesach until Shavuos the world is in distress concerning the grain and the trees. The Holy One, blessed be He, therefore commanded us to count these days so that we remember the world’s distress, return to Him wholeheartedly, and plead before Him to have mercy upon the grain. Another reason for the counting is that every member of Israel is occupied during the harvest season. He therefore commanded them to count so that they would know when Shavuos would arrive. There is also an allusion to the seventh millennium, which will be entirely Shabbos. Accordingly, the seventh day of Pesach has the character of Atzeres, Shavuos follows seven weeks, and the eighth day of Sukkos alludes to the World to Come and to the great Jubilee. Rabbeinu Yerucham. I found in Tzeidah LaDerech that the ancients worshipped the seven planets as gods. Bilam therefore offered seven altars. Each of the seven possesses seven forms of dominion, totaling forty nine. The Torah therefore commanded us to count and then gave the Torah on the fiftieth day, commanding us not to worship them. At the giving of the Torah, the Holy One, blessed be He, therefore tore open the seven heavens and opened the seven earths so that all would know that there is no God besides Him.
