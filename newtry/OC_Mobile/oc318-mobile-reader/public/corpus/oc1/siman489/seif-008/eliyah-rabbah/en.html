[8] [Levush] "One does not recite Shehecheyanu," etc. Abudraham explains that from Pesach until Shavuot the world is in distress concerning the grain and the trees. God therefore commanded us to count these days so that we remember that distress, return to Him wholeheartedly, and plead before Him to have mercy upon the grain. Another reason for the counting is that each member of Israel is occupied during the harvest season, and He therefore commanded them to count so that they would know when Shavuot arrives. They also alluded through this to the seventh millennium, which is entirely Shabbos. Therefore there is an Atzeret on the seventh day of Pesach, Shavuot comes after 7 weeks, and the eighth day of Sukkot alludes toward the World-to-Come and to the great Jubilee. Rabbeinu Yerucham. In Tzeidah LaDerech I found that the ancients treated the 7 planets as deities. Bilam therefore offered 7 altars, and each of the 7 has 7 forms of dominion, totaling 49. The Torah therefore commanded the counting and gave the Torah on the 50th day, commanding that they not be worshiped. At Matan Torah God tore open the 7 heavens and opened the 7 earths so that all would know that there is no God besides Him.
