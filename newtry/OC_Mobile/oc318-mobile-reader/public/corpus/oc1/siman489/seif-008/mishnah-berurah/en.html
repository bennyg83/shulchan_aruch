(35) He forgot to bless, etc.: That is to say, that he did not count and did not remember until the counting after it. And the same applies if it became known to him that yesterday he erred in the count and counted a different counting: his law is as one who did not count at all.
(36) He counts on the other days: According to the view of many poskim that the countings of the days do not impede one another, and each and every day is a mitzvah by itself.
(37) Without a blessing: To be concerned for the view that we require seven complete weeks, and this is lacking, for one day is missing. And it is proper in this that he hear the blessing from the chazzan or from one of those who bless, and answer amen with intent to fulfill, and afterward count [Eliyah Rabbah and Peri Chadash].
(38) He should count on the other days with a blessing: For there is a double doubt: perhaps he did not skip at all; and even if you say he skipped, perhaps the law follows those poskim that each day is a mitzvah by itself. And the same applies in any matter whose law is to go back and count without a blessing because of a doubt: if he did not go back and count, he should count the remaining nights with a blessing [later authorities]. And therefore if he erred in the days and did not err in the weeks, or erred in the weeks and did not err in the days, whose law is to go back and count without a blessing, as above in s.k. 7: if he did not go back and count, nevertheless he counts the other days with a blessing; and see Biur Halacha.
