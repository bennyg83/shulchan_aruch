He counts on the other days. That is, he holds that the days do not impede one another. And even though it is written "they shall be complete," R' Yitzchak ibn Giat wrote in the name of R' Hai Gaon that he fulfills completeness in weeks: that is, in each complete week, when he says the week's reckoning he fulfills "seven weeks shall be complete." And he further answered there: where one day was forgotten, on the second night he should count two countings: for example, if he forgot the first counting, on the second counting he should say "yesterday was one of the omer, and this day is two of the omer"; and since he also counted yesterday's, he has not left the category of "they shall be complete"; see there. And so it is brought with some variation in Shibbolei HaLeket in the name of a responsum of the Geonim; see there in siman 234. However, other poskim did not mention this view, and wrote that this "they shall be complete" refers to each day that he began to count from evening, as we expound in Menachos, folio 66, and in Megillah, folio 20; but not that all the days impede one another [see Tosafos Menachos and Tosafos Megillah, and Shibbolei HaLeket in the name of R' Yeshayah, and Or Zarua, siman 329]. And in Eliyah Rabbah I found that he wrote it is good to be concerned for the words of Rav Hai Gaon in his second answer, and to count also yesterday's counting together, and without a blessing, as above. And other later authorities did not mention this matter.
Without a blessing. Nahar Shalom wrote: one whose deceased lies before him on one of the days of the omer should count by day after the burial of his deceased without a blessing; and it is not similar to prayer, where one does not pray as a makeup, as stated in Yoreh De'ah, siman 341, since at its essential time he was exempt; here, according to the view that one counts it even by day, it is not as a makeup for the night, but rather that his obligation continues until the end of a full day, as implied from the Rambam, chapter 7 of Temidin uMusafin; and it is similar to havdalah, where he makes havdalah on Sunday after the burial of his deceased, for its essential time is until the fourth day. And according to this, since he counts by day he can count the other days with a blessing. However, if his burial continued the whole day such that he had no opportunity to count after the burial of his deceased, he should not again count on the coming days with a blessing: what difference is there if he forgot to count or was exempt from counting; in either case it was not complete; see there. Now what he decided, that according to the one who obligates counting by day it is not for the reason of makeup, is not so clear; see Or Zarua, siman 329, what he wrote in the name of Avi HaEzri. And regarding the essential law, what seemed decided to him that an onen is exempt from counting: the Noda BiYehuda, Mahadura Kama, volume Orach Chaim, siman 27, is uncertain about this, based on what Maharshal wrote in a responsum, siman 70, that an onen is not exempt from all things; and he gathers there some reasonings by which one may say that he is also not exempt from counting; and primarily because in this he is not preoccupied and is not diverted for this moment from attending to his deceased; and he also combines the view of some poskim that an onen is exempt but is permitted to fulfill. And based on all this, his view is that he should count while his deceased lies before him without a blessing, and then he can count on all the days with a blessing, since without this there are poskim who do not require completeness at all; see there. And the Gaon R' Akiva Eiger copied it in his glosses.
He should count on the other days with a blessing. See Mishnah Berurah what he wrote regarding if he erred in the days, etc., and it is from the words of the Taz. And even though he cited only erring in the days and not in the weeks, it is obvious that the same applies, and all the more so if he erred in the weeks and not in the days, for days impede more than weeks according to all, as above in Mishnah Berurah s.k. 7; and so wrote Peri Megadim. However, according to reasoning it would appear that the Taz speaks only of the remaining days of the week, where it is not an impediment that he also mention the days, as the later authorities agreed [see Chok Yaakov s.k. 8]; and therefore one may say that the same applies if he erred in it, for it is no worse than not mentioning at all; which is not so on a day that completes the weeks, where according to the law he must mention also days and it is an impediment, as above in s.k. 7: if so, it is obvious that similarly if he erred in the reckoning of the days, his counting is nothing and is as if he skipped entirely, and he counts the other days without a blessing. And it is possible that the Taz's view is that even on days that complete the weeks one may also be lenient regarding the coming countings, since according to several poskim even without this the days do not impede one another; and if so, at least one may be lenient in a case like this where in any case he mentioned one of the numbers, either days or weeks. And see Eliyah Rabbah, who also questioned the essential law of the Taz; and for practice this requires further study. And Peri Megadim also spoke about this, and there are printing errors in his words, and I could not understand his view.
