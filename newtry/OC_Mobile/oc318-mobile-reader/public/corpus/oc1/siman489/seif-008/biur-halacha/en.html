(א) He counts on other days — meaning he holds days do not impede each other; though written "temimot tehiyena," R. Yitzchak Gi'at wrote in the name of R. Hai Gaon: he fulfills temimot in weeks — in each complete week when saying the week's count he fulfills "seven weeks temimot tehiyena"; he also answered there: if one day was lost, count on the second night two countings — e.g. if he forgot the first count, on the second count say "yesterday was one in the omer, and today is two in the omer" — since he also counted yesterday's, he is not excluded from temimot tehiyena — see there, and so in Shulchan Aruch with variation in Shibbolei Leket in the name of responsa of Geonim — see there siman 234; however other poskim did not mention this view and wrote "temimot tehiyena" refers to each day he began counting from evening, as we expound in Menachot 66 and Megillah 20, not that all days impede each other [see Tosafot Menachot and Megillah and Shulchan Aruch in the name of R. Yeshaya and Or Zaru'a siman 329]; in Eshel Avraham I found he wrote it is good to be concerned for R. Hai Gaon's second answer and count yesterday's count together. And without berachah — as above. Other Acharonim did not mention this.

Without blessing — Nahar Shalom wrote one whose deceased lies before him on one of the omer days counts by day after burial without blessing; and it is not similar to prayer one does not pray as makeup as in Yoreh De'ah siman 341 since essentially he was exempt; here per those who count it even by day it is not makeup for night but his obligation extends until end of the festival as implied Rambam chapter 7 Laws of Festivals; and similar to havdalah one makes havdalah on day 1 after burial of deceased whose essential time is until day 4; therefore one who counts by day can count remaining days with blessing; however if burial extended all day such that he had no leisure to count after burial — he should not count further days with blessing — what difference if he forgot to count or deceased prevented counting — either way not temimot — see there; what he decided for one obligated to count by day is not from makeup reason — see Or Zaru'a siman 329 what is written in name of Abba ha-Ezri; in essence of law what is ruled an onen is exempt — Rav Nuv'ei Mahadura Kama 8 Orach Chayim 27 is uncertain per Maharshal responsum 70 that not from all matters is onen exempt; he gathers some reasons one may say also from counting not exempt; primarily because he is not preoccupied and not nullified for this moment from engaging with deceased; he combines also view of some poskim onen exempt but permitted to fulfill; per all this his view he counts while deceased before him without blessing and again can count all days with blessing since without this some poskim do not require temimot at all — see there; Rav Akiva Eiger copied in his glosses

He counts on remaining days with blessing — see Mishna Berurah what is written regarding if he erred in days etc. — Taz's words; although he only cited erred in days and not weeks — it is obvious likewise and all the more erred in weeks and not days since days impede more than weeks for all — as in Mishna Berurah s.k. 7; so Peri Megadim; nevertheless per reasoning it appeared Taz deals only with remaining weekdays that do not impede that he also mention days as Acharonim agreed [see Chayei Adam s.k. 8]; therefore one may say likewise if he erred in it — no worse than not mentioning at all; unlike day completing weeks where by law he must mention days and it impedes as s.k. 7; if so obvious likewise if he erred in counting days his counting is nothing and is as if entirely skipped and counts remaining days without blessing; possible Taz's view even on days completing weeks one may be lenient for upcoming countings since for many poskim days do not impede each other — if so at least one may be lenient when he mentioned one of the numbers — days or weeks; see Avodat Yom Tov who also questioned Taz's essential law — requires study for practical law; Peri Megadim also spoke on this and there are printing errors in his words and I could not understand his view
