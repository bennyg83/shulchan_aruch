While it is still day. See Mishnah Berurah; and it implies from them that from plag haMinchah according to all he cannot count. And know that there are many later authorities [Levush, Eliyah Rabbah, Chok Yaakov, Maamar Mordechai, and Nahar Shalom] who explain "while it is still day" as from plag haMinchah and onward; and what it says "he counts with them" is because although according to the essential law, so long as it is not night it is not the time of counting, as above in the previous se'if, nevertheless there are places that were accustomed to be lenient in this because they were used to praying Ma'ariv before dark, and they feared that if they did not count then in the congregation and each went home they would forget because of preoccupation and not count, and the essential practice of counting would be nullified. And therefore under pressure they relied on whoever says that regarding counting nowadays, which is only a remembrance of the Temple according to most poskim, one should not be more stringent in it than regarding Shema and prayer; and since they consider this the beginning of night and recite Shema and pray Ma'ariv, so too we should consider it night regarding counting. And it says that even one who is a Torah scholar, if his heart strikes him lest he be preoccupied and forget to count privately, may count with the congregation, but he should not bless then in any case, for according to the essential law it is not then the time of counting; only that we do not protest the custom of those places, and so that they not be entirely nullified from this mitzvah. And when the time arrives at night he should bless and count, and it is not a blessing in vain, for the first counting is nothing according to the essential law, as above.
And even if he answered amen, etc. That is to say: their custom was that the chazzan would bless and discharge the whole congregation with his blessing, and the counting each one would count for himself. And you might say: since he answered amen together with the congregation, he certainly intended to fulfill with the blessing over the counting that he counted, and automatically it is proven that he intended to fulfill the mitzvah with the counting; it teaches us that we do not say so, since he intended explicitly not to fulfill.
And he should bless and count at night. See Mishnah Berurah. Peri Megadim wrote: on erev Shabbos in a congregation that delays it is better, and not like those who advance and say it is already night, and a moment earlier say it is a long day to accept Shabbos; and I call upon them "darkness for light," and that light in a small moment to darkness. Therefore it is proper to refrain from this, etc.; see there.
