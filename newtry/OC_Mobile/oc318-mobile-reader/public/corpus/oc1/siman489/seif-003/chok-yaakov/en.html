The congregation recites the blessing while it is still day, although according to all opinions the time for counting begins no earlier than bein hashemashos. They nevertheless bless before nightfall in order to fulfill the obligation of unlearned people who do not count at night, as Levush writes. They rely on the same basis used for Maariv and Krias Shema when recited before nightfall, namely that sunset is considered night, as explained in siman 235; see Magen Avraham there, seif katan 3. The same applies to counting, which is D'Rabbanan. I found this stated explicitly in Piskei Tosafos to Menachos, siman 176: some say that once Maariv has been recited, since it is night regarding Krias Shema and prayer, it is also night regarding the Omer. The congregation therefore relies on this opinion, but one who wishes to be stringent for himself should count at the proper time. This resolves the contradiction that Rabbi Shlomo Levi raises in his responsa and answers with difficulty. Taz likewise strains to reinterpret the poskim, but according to what I have written everything is correct and straightforward. Our custom is not to count with the congregation while it is still day, even on Erev Shabbos or Erev Yom Tov, unless it is at least bein hashemashos, as stated below in seif katan 12 in the name of Bach.
