3) There. The counting begins, etc. Shehecheyanu is not recited over counting the omer because nowadays it is only a remembrance of the Beis HaMikdash. Furthermore, Shehecheyanu is found only with a mitzvah that provides some benefit, such as taking the lulav, which brings joy; sounding the shofar, which brings remembrance before our Father in Heaven; reading the Megillah, because the Merciful One had compassion upon us and redeemed us; and redeeming the firstborn son, where the father recites Shehecheyanu because the child has passed beyond doubtful viability, since a person who survives thirty days is not considered nonviable. Counting the omer, however, recalls no benefit, but only the sorrow of the destruction of the House we desire. Ran at the end of Pesachim in the name of Baal HaMaor. See there. Responsa Rashba, siman 126, writes likewise. Levush explains that the counting is for the sake of Yom Tov, so the Shehecheyanu recited for the festival includes it. See there for another explanation. The later authorities give additional reasons, but there is no need to elaborate.
