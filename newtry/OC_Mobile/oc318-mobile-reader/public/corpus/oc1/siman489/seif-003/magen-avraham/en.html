One who prays with the congregation, etc. These are the words of the Rashba, siman 235: One who said this in order to count with the congregation while it was still day attributed empty things to our master, for whoever counted while it was still day has done nothing at all; on the contrary, it is like testifying to what is not so. And if he fears he may forget the counting and rely on this counting, as with the evening Shema, then he has already fulfilled the counting, and when he returns and blesses it is a blessing in vain, end quote. And so it appears from the language of Avudraham, who concluded: but the Rambam wrote that if he counted and did not bless, he has fulfilled; see Beis Yosef. Nevertheless he holds that his remedy is his ruin, for perhaps he fulfilled with the first, and it is again forbidden to bless; and if so, it requires further study why the Beis Yosef wrote [as he did]. And it appears to me that he follows his own view, for he ruled in siman 60, se'if 4, that mitzvos require intent; and if so, he should intend in his heart: if I remember at night, I do not intend to fulfill with this counting. And so it appears from the language of the Rama, who wrote "if his intent was not to fulfill." And the Hagahos Maimoniyos wrote that some permit counting on erev Shabbos while it is still day after the evening prayer; and the Bach wrote that we do not hold thus. And it appears to me that it is permitted to count after the stars appear even if he has not yet prayed Ma'ariv, even on Motza'ei Shabbos, for it is night for all matters, and even to make kiddush and havdalah is permitted before the prayer, and one mentions Retzeh in Birkas HaMazon, as is written in siman 271 and 293 and siman 267. And in Sefer Chanukas HaBayis he wondered about the custom to count before havdalah, for it is still Shabbos; see there. And it appears to me that there is nothing to this, for in truth Shabbos has already passed and gone; only that they are forbidden in melachah until he makes havdalah, as it appears to me.
His intent was not to fulfill. It is difficult, for even with unspecified [intent] he has not fulfilled, for we require that he intend to fulfill, as is written in siman 60, se'if 4. And perhaps since it is forbidden to recite a blessing in vain, if it was unspecified it is forbidden to bless a second time, since some say mitzvos do not require intent, as it appears to me.
