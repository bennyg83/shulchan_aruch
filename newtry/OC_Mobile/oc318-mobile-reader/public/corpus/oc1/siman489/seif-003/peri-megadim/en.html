Today. See Taz. And see Magen Avraham [5]: the lamed and the beis with a kamatz because of the guttural letter. And they say "May it be [His] will that the Temple be rebuilt"; see Tosafos Megillah 20b, s.v. "All night," and the Levush: now they only mention a remembrance of the Temple. Counting nowadays is rabbinic according to most poskim; they request "May it be [His] will," etc., and "may the omer be done as its mitzvah." Which is not so for lulav of seven days as a remembrance of the Temple: one does not say "May it be [His] will," for we fulfill an act as in the Temple; and regarding wrapping as a remembrance of the Temple one does not bless. Even if it were certain that the law follows Hillel, nevertheless after the fact he fulfilled with each one alone, and each already ate alone, lest rabbinic maror push aside Torah-level matzah; again one does not bless; and all the more so when it is doubtful whether the law follows Hillel. And see Eliyah Rabbah, letter 4; and God willing in Magen Avraham I will explain further.
