And there is missing language here, and it should read: most commentators wrote that nowadays it is rabbinic; therefore it is better to count at twilight so that completeness be certain, and a rabbinic doubt is to leniency; which was not so in Temple times, when one needed the appearance of the stars for a Torah doubt. And the Ran wrote that it is not proper to create a rabbinic doubt l'chatchila (and see siman 466 in Magen Avraham [2]). And if "complete," one should not be more stringent nowadays than according to the Torah, where of necessity one counts at the appearance of the stars. And see Tosafos Menachos 66a, s.v. "Remembrance," and Tosafos Kerisos 17b, s.v. "From the latter clause," that twilight is considered somewhat established; and in Lechem Mishneh, chapter 8 of Shogagos, law 2. And one may distinguish between them; and for law, l'chatchila he should not count until the stars appear; and if he counts at twilight he should afterward count without a blessing. See Kesef Mishneh, chapter 7 of Chametz uMatzah, law 8, regarding the four cups: a rabbinic doubt does not apply since there is no trouble; the same here; and at the beginning of the chapter "Nishaluhu," etc. And God willing in Magen Avraham [6][7] I will explain further. And on erev Shabbos in a congregation that delays, it is better, and not like those who advance and say it is already night, and a moment earlier they say it is a long day to accept Shabbos; and I call upon them "darkness for light," and that light in a small moment becomes darkness. Therefore it is proper to refrain from this; and holy Israel hear and accept rebuke and discipline. Therefore for Kabbalas Shabbos they advance, and for counting they delay.
