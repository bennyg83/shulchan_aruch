(s.k. 5) Today: He should not say "that today." And in Chok Yaakov he wrote that even one who says "that today" is not mistaken; see there.
Masculine language, and therefore he should say "one week" and not "one" [feminine]. And likewise "two weeks," and not "two" [feminine]; three, four, five, six, seven: all with a hei at the end of the word. But "eight," for both masculine and feminine there is a hei at the end, and the difference is only in the vowel.
And Beis Shmuel wrote in Even HaEzer, siman 126, s.k. 9, in the name of the Levush: for masculine the nun with a kamatz, and for feminine with a segol; and in the name of Tosefos Yom Tov in Mishnah Taharos, chapter 5, he wrote the opposite: for feminine with a kamatz, like "ro'eh hi."
See Even HaEzer.
And he further wrote there that until ten he should say "days," and from eleven onward he should say "day."
And so wrote Chok Yaakov here in the name of Shibbolei HaLeket, that so is the elegance of the Holy Tongue; and after the fact this does not impede; see there in Even HaEzer. And whether he says "of the omer" or "to the omer," there is a dispute, and whoever follows one master has fulfilled, etc.
In Yoma, folio 55, when the Kohen Gadol was counting the sprinklings on Yom Kippur, R' Meir and R' Yehudah dispute. R' Meir holds he would count thus: one; one and one; one and two; etc. And R' Yehudah holds he would count: one; one and one; one, two; and one; etc. And we say they do not dispute; each master according to his locale, etc. And Rashi explained that in R' Meir's locale they would count the smaller first, and in R' Yehudah's locale they would count the larger first.
