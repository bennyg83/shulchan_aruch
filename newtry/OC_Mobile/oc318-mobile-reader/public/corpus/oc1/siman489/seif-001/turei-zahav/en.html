(א) They begin to count. Even though regarding yovel it is also written "you shall count for yourself," and likewise regarding zavah "she shall count" — the Rabbis learned that this "you shall count" regarding the omer is an actual count, unlike other countings — so Ran.

(ב) And mitzvah for each one. For it is written "you shall count for yourselves" — the count must be for each one. Standing — for the sages taught: "when grain rises you shall begin to count" — do not read "when grain rises" but "when you rise."

(ג) Today one day in the Omer. Such is our custom; but in Ran end of Pesachim that Beit Yosef cites, he mentions "la'omer" with lamed, and our custom appears more correct, for on day one counted at the beginning of night 16 it means days of the omer that will be brought tomorrow on day 16; on later days too "days of the omer" fits all, unlike "la'omer" on night 16 which would mean what was already brought, while "ba'omer" refers only to days of the omer — one cannot explain it on the future offering, for the bet implies within; with lamed one could explain the future offering. One should not say only "today" without "day."

(ד) One week. And not "one" in feminine, as Rashi explained regarding "a full week" — "week" is masculine language.
