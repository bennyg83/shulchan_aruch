After the evening prayer. He wrote in Sefer Mor uKetzi'ah a reason that they count after the prayer: because in earlier generations the custom was to pray Ma'ariv before night, and at the conclusion of Ma'ariv night began, so they were not counting at the beginning of the night according to law. But in Chok Yaakov, s.k. 16, he wrote that according to the law one must give precedence to Shema and the Amidah, which are frequent.
To count the omer. Behold, the view of the Rambam and the Chinuch is that it applies according to the Torah even now; but the view of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch and several poskim is that nowadays it is only a remembrance of the Temple when they offered the omer; and so is the approach of the poskim in this siman. But in truth the Rambam too is not alone in his view, for the view of Rabbeinu Yeshayah is likewise; see Shibbolei HaLeket at the beginning of siman 234; and so too is the view of R' Binyamin there; see there at the end of the siman. And so too is the view of Ravyah, brought in Or Zarua, siman 329, and it implies there that Or Zarua also agrees with him in his law; see there. And likewise from the words of Rav Yehudai and Rav Amram and R' Yitzchak ibn Giat, who agreed to their words, brought in the Itur: that if he did not count from the evening he has lost [it], for we are particular about completeness: it implies ostensibly that it is according to the Torah and not a mere remembrance, from the fact that the Itur pushed aside their words with the reason that counting the omer for us is not according to the Torah, since now there is neither bringing nor offering, and one should not be exacting about completeness: implying that for them it is according to the Torah. [However, one may say somewhat that they hold that even though it is only a remembrance of the Temple, the Sages made it like according to the Torah, specifically regarding completeness.] And according to this there is great support for our custom that we are careful not to count until the stars appear, since according to several great authorities it is according to the Torah. Afterward I found something like this also in Eliyah Rabbah, only that he did not mention all these great ones that we wrote.
And it is a mitzvah upon each one, etc. See Mishnah Berurah, where I wrote of the dispute of the later authorities whether we say in this that one who hears is as one who answers; and it is a dispute of the Levush and Chok Yaakov with Peri Chadash: according to Levush and Chok Yaakov, just as regarding lulav we expound "and you shall take for yourselves" for each and every one, so too here each and every one must himself count; and Peri Chadash is lenient in this, and likewise the view of Berkei Yosef tends. And in my humble opinion this matter is a dispute among the early authorities: for Rashi explained there in Menachos 65b on what the Gemara says "for each and every one," that each one is obligated to count; and in Chiddushei HaRashba he clarified his words: that if the Torah's intent were for the congregation alone and not for each individual, it should have said only "and you shall count" and been silent; and from its saying "for you," it implies it comes to say for each one; see there. Thus the mitzvah applies to each individual separately. And against this, from the language of the early R' Yitzchak ibn Giat it implies that one can discharge his fellow in this; for these are his words there in the laws of counting: And we thus received from our teachers a support for counting the omer while standing, from what is written "from the time the sickle is first put to the standing grain": why need Scripture say "to the standing grain"? To teach you: while standing, and upright. And on Motza'ei Shabbos, when after the evening prayer they say "Vehi Noam" and Kedushah deSidra while sitting, the early ones were accustomed that the chazzan bless standing and the whole congregation answer amen with intent, so as not to trouble the congregation to go back and stand, end quote. And so wrote the author of Orchos Chaim in the name of R' Hai Gaon; see there. And it implies from this that they are discharged by his counting; for if their intent is that they are discharged only in the blessing alone, then in any case they would need to stand for the counting itself, which must be standing, as above. [And in my humble opinion one should study even according to Rashi's explanation above: where he is not expert to count himself, perhaps his fellow can discharge him; and it is no worse than what we hold regarding Birkas HaMazon, that according to all one cannot discharge his fellow unless he himself ate, because it is written "and you shall eat and be satisfied," whoever ate should bless; and even so we hold that a scribe blesses and a boor is discharged; and perhaps the same applies here; and this requires further study.] Thus we have clarified that this matter is a dispute of the authorities; and therefore l'chatchila certainly each one must count himself; but after the fact, if he heard from his fellow and intended to fulfill, he should go back and count without a blessing; and so wrote Peri Megadim.
He counts onward. See the later authorities who disputed if he counted with initials, for example saying "today is 2 days" and the like, whether he has fulfilled; and Chok Yaakov wrote: since according to most poskim the essence of counting nowadays is only rabbinic, one should not be stringent from doubt, and he should go back and count without a blessing. And know that in any case if he blessed only in his heart he certainly has not fulfilled at all, for thought is not like speech; and so Peri Chadash wrote explicitly in se'if 7. And see above, siman 62, se'if 4, in Mishnah Berurah and Biur Halacha there, and examine carefully.
