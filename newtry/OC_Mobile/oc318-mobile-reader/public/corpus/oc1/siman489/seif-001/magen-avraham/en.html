(א) Even if the first day falls on Shabbat they say on the second night the ma'arivim of the second because of prioritizing the omer (Levush) — implies if seventh day of Passover falls on Shabbat they say on the last day ma'arivim of the seventh — see siman 642: in Pozna they do not practice so, but gemara end Sukkah implies the last is always postponed — see there. They begin to count. Kesef Mishnah ch. 6 Sukkah questioned how we bless on counting on second Yom Tov without concern for Yom Tov degradation, as we do not bless on sukkah on Shemini Atzeret — answered: there one says on the cup "this Yom Tov day" and blessing on sukkah would contradict; not so here. Or since we are expert in fixing the month, by law no need to sit in sukkah on Shemini Atzeret therefore no berachah; here by law one must count today therefore we bless — end quote. Women exempt — positive time-bound (Rambam; Zohar Tzav 119); however already made obligatory. Magen Avraham.

(ב) To count for himself. If he wishes to hear berachah from shaliach tzibbur and count for himself — all berachot even if expert one fulfills through shaliach tzibbur except Birkat Hamazon and Shema and prayer (Rashba responsa 126); here too he must count himself, as gemara "you shall count" — count for each one, as they said regarding lulav "you shall take" — taking for each one. Beit Yosef in Rashba's name: one fulfills through shaliach tzibbur's count — requires study; perhaps since we hold shome'a ke'oneh it is as if he counted himself. Simple one may count in any language he understands; if he does not understand Holy Tongue and counted in Holy Tongue he did not fulfill, for he does not know what he says — not counting.

(ג) Standing. If he counted seated he fulfilled (Rambam).

(ד) And weeks. It appears if he counted days alone and not weeks he need not return, as gemara for those who hold counting rabbinic; Beit Yosef in most poskim we hold counting today rabbinic; if he counted weeks alone he also fulfilled as Tur wrote.

(ה) Today. He should not say "that today," for it implies giving reason for what was counted above; rather "today day..." Weeks and days are masculine, as written "seven weeks." Always put smaller number first — see Even HaEzer siman 126; however Yoma 55a: all per local custom; in our land even in secular tongue we mention smaller number first. Some say Psalm Elohim yichaneinu after counting.
