Even if the first day falls on Shabbos, on the second night they say the Ma'ariv passages belonging to the second, because of giving precedence to the counting (Levush). This implies that if the seventh of Pesach falls on Shabbos, on the last day they say the Ma'ariv passages belonging to the seventh; see what I wrote in siman 642, that in Posen they do not practice thus; but in the Gemara at the end of Sukkah it implies that the last is always deferred; see there. They begin to count. The Kesef Mishneh in chapter 6 of Sukkah asked: how do they recite a blessing over the counting on the second day of Yom Tov, and we are not concerned about degrading Yom Tov, for for that reason they do not recite a blessing over the sukkah on Shemini? And one may say that there they say over the cup "this day of Shemini Atzeres," and if he would bless over the sukkah they would contradict each other, which is not so here. Alternatively, since we are expert in the fixing of the month, then according to the law one need not sit in the sukkah on Shemini, therefore they do not bless; but here it is the opposite, for according to the law one must count today, therefore they bless, end quote. Women are exempt from counting, for it is a positive time-bound mitzvah (Rambam; and so wrote in the Zohar, Tetzaveh, folio 319); however, they have already assumed it upon themselves as an obligation.
To count for himself. And if he wishes, he hears the blessing from the chazzan and counts for himself, for all blessings, even if he is expert, he is discharged by the chazzan, except for Birkas HaMazon, Shema, and the Amidah (Rashba in a responsum, siman 126); see siman 59, se'if 4. Thus he holds that in any case he must count himself, as is written in the Gemara: "and you shall count," that there be a counting for each and every one, in the manner they said regarding lulav, "and you shall take," that there be a taking for each and every one. And in Beis Yosef it is written in the name of the Rashba that one is discharged by the chazzan's countings; see there; and this requires further study. And perhaps since we hold that one who hears is as one who answers, it is as if he himself counted. And it is obvious that one may count in any language, but specifically in a language he understands; and if he does not understand the Holy Tongue and counted in the Holy Tongue, he has not fulfilled, for he did not know what he was saying, and this is not a counting, as it appears to me.
Standing. And if he counted while sitting, he has fulfilled (Rambam).
And the weeks. It appears to me that if he counted the days alone and did not count the weeks, he need not go back, as is in the Gemara according to the view that counting is rabbinic; and Beis Yosef wrote in the name of most poskim that we hold counting nowadays is rabbinic. And if he counted weeks alone he has also fulfilled, as the Tur wrote.
Today. He should not say "that today," etc., for that implies giving a reason for what is above it; rather he should say "today is day," etc. Weeks and days are masculine language, as it is written "seven weeks." He should always say the smaller number first, for example "one and twenty days"; see Even HaEzer, siman 126. However, in Yoma, folio 55, it is stated that everything follows the custom of the locale in their reckoning; and in our lands, even in the vernacular, they mention the smaller number first. Some are accustomed to say the chapter of Tehillim "May God be gracious to us" after the counting.
