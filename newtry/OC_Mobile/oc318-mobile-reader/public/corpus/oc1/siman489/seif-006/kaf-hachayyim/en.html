The counting begins, etc. I have seen meticulous people who, on the second night of Yom Tov, conduct the Seder and recite the blessing over counting the omer only after completing the entire Seder. They give a sound mystical reason, and their words appear correct. Yet the law forbids eating before counting, and throughout the long Seder there is a strong possibility of forgetting to count. Birkei Yosef, subsection 5. Nahar Shalom of Rashash, page 25b in the new edition, however, writes that according to the mystical teaching as well, those outside Eretz Yisrael must recite the blessing over counting immediately after Maariv on the second night of Yom Tov. Counting the omer is a Torah positive commandment, whereas conducting the Seder on this night outside Eretz Yisrael is rabbinic. Each has its own distinct spiritual aspect. Therefore, the blessing over the omer must be recited immediately after Maariv of the second Yom Tov and not delayed until after the Seder, contrary to some mekubalim. Delaying it would give the external forces a hold. See there. Machazik Berachah, subsection 2, cites this. See there.
