He counts. See Taz; and see Magen Avraham [7]. Now the Taz holds that Avudraham wrote that one may say: if he counts without a blessing he has fulfilled according to the Torah, and therefore from the rabbis he has not fulfilled in counting unless he blessed first, for so they enacted to bless over mitzvos. And it fits that he reads with the congregation at twilight without a blessing, lest he forget at night: at least he has fulfilled according to the Torah; and if he remembers he counts at night with a blessing. And according to what he wrote afterward, that according to the Rambam he has fulfilled even rabbinically without a blessing, and so is the law in all mitzvos that blessings do not impede: necessarily he counts in the congregation and intends not to fulfill at all with this counting; and what benefit is there in counting with the congregation? And it does not please him that he counts conditionally: if I remember, this will not be for the mitzvah, etc.; for in such a case he holds it is possible he has not fulfilled at all; and see Magen Avraham. And what the Taz wrote, that the congregation reads at doubtful darkness at twilight: see letter 5. And regarding them after the fact it is because of the unlearned who are also in the synagogue and may forget at night to count; but otherwise one should not create a rabbinic doubt for leniency, as I wrote in letter 5. And God willing in Magen Avraham [7] this will be further explained; see there.
