Or the reverse: they are four, and he opened, etc., and erred and concluded with five, etc. See Mishnah Berurah, that the later authorities wondered about this. Now the Taz answered that the Mechaber's intent is not that he concluded regarding the counting itself, but regarding the blessing: that is to say, at the beginning of the blessing when he said "Blessed are You, Hashem our God, King of the universe," he calculated with intent that afterward he would count four; and only at the time of concluding the blessing it seemed to him that today is the fifth day of the omer, and he finished the blessing with intent to count five of the omer: he does not go back and bless, for in this we follow the opening. And in the first law that the Mechaber depicted it is also in such a manner, and there the reason is that we follow the closing; and because it is a rabbinic matter we follow both for leniency, and he does not go back and bless. And this law the later authorities copied for practical law (see Derech HaChaim and Eliyah Rabbah). But the Taz derived from this a new law: that where the opening and the closing were with intent to count the fifth day, and after he finished the blessing it became known to him that today is the fourth day, he goes back and blesses; and regarding this law several later authorities did not agree with him; see Mishnah Berurah and Shaar HaTziyun.
