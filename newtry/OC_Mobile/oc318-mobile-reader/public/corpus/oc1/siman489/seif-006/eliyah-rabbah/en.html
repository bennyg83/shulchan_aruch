[6] Fourteen, etc. The smaller number comes first; and in places where they count the larger number first, they count according to the custom of their place (later authorities). And Shiyarei Keneses HaGedolah discussed this at length. And in our lands, even in the vernacular, they count the smaller number first. It is permitted to count in any language one understands; and if one does not understand the Holy Tongue, he should not count in the Holy Tongue (Magen Avraham). Shibbolei HaLeket wrote: until ten he says "days," and from eleven and upward they say "day," and this is the elegant style of the Holy Tongue (Emek Berachah). Some are accustomed to say the psalm "May God be gracious to us" after the counting. Maharil wrote: when he reaches forty-nine he should say "which are seven weeks."
