He does not repeat the blessing. Bach and other later authorities object to this ruling from simanim 59 and 209, where one does not fulfill the obligation when he spoke falsely, as the poskim rule in siman 209 against Rambam. To avoid treating the words of the early authorities as entirely mistaken, it appears that the cases are not comparable. There, one held a cup of beer and concluded with the blessing for wine, or concluded Maariv with “Who forms light”; he thus did not bless at all over the object or matter he intended. Here, however, he blessed with the intention of counting the present day, and when he concluded he said, “Today is such and such.” His intent therefore related to the day on which he stood; he merely erred in calculating its number. The principal intent of his blessing and counting was directed to the correct day, as indicated by the word “today.” There, by contrast, he concluded with an entirely different species or subject. Here the error is evident from the formulation itself. Therefore, one should not place himself in a possible case of a blessing in vain. Beit Yosef, whose rulings we follow, was uncertain about this, and Rama did not gloss upon his ruling.
