See Mishnah Berurah: if he counted during twilight he has fulfilled; so wrote Magen Avraham and other later authorities; and this matter is mentioned in Tosafos Menachos, folio 66, s.v. "Remembrance." And when is it called twilight? See siman 261: the view of Shulchan Aruch there is that twilight is three-quarters of a mil before the stars appear, which is about a quarter of an hour; and before that it is definitely day. And see there in Biur Halacha, s.v. "And the measure," at the end of the discussion there: that even according to those who hold that twilight begins immediately after sunset, that is regarding Shabbos, where we take stringently like R' Yehudah; but to be lenient, even in rabbinic matters, it requires further study whether we can be lenient and say two leniencies: one, that it is twilight like R' Yehudah, and also that a rabbinic doubt is to leniency, since according to R' Yosi it is definitely day the whole time from sunset until close to the stars appearing, as we say in the Gemara Shabbos 34: when R' Yehudah's twilight ends, R' Yosi's twilight begins; and this requires further study.
