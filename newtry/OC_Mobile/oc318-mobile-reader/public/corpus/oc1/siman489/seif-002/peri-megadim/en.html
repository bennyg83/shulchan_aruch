And one may say: "and you shall count" implies all Israel; and "for you," that one who is not able [to count himself] by hearing from the chazzan; which is not so for Yovel, where it is written "and you shall count for yourself" to the court. Also regarding Yovel we hold that even the court has no mitzvah to count. Peri Chadash, se'if 8, brought a question: one who forgot one day to count, may he be chazzan and count with a blessing to discharge others, and he too fulfills? He concluded that he cannot, for he is not obligated in the matter; and proof from Megillah: a city person does not discharge a metropolis person; and so he wrote in siman 688 and 689; see there. But he may hear the blessing from another and intend to fulfill through his blessing; see there. Now this is certainly so: although it is a matter of mitzvah, whoever is not obligated in that mitzvah does not discharge others of their obligation; and one should not say from the reason of arevus he discharges; and specifically a deaf-mute, imbecile, and minor: arevus does not apply to them. For in siman 589, se'if 2, a deaf person who speaks and does not hear, although it is a matter of mitzvah, does not discharge others with shofar of their obligation; see Beis Yosef there; and see Rosh Hashana 29a. And see the Rosh on Berachos 20b, who asked: if women's Birkas HaMazon is rabbinic, she does not discharge men of their obligation; and what differs from one who ate an olive-bulk who discharges others? And he answered: there it is from the reason of arevus, and a woman is not included in arevus. And resolve: even if a woman is included in arevus, she is not obligated in Birkas HaMazon of the Torah, therefore arevus does not apply to her. And one may say that here too he says arevus does not apply to her, for she is not obligated in this mitzvah. And indeed women regarding kiddush of the day according to the Torah discharge men with kiddush, for arevus applies; and Parashas Nitzavim, see Rashi there. And so too a man for a woman: even though he has fulfilled, he discharges, for they are included in arevus; and see what I wrote in siman 271, se'if 2, and 273, and in other places we wrote about this. And in the general introduction: also an adult for a minor who is of education age, one may say that if he has fulfilled he does not discharge them, for arevus does not apply; and kiddush and Birkas HaMazon are different, for the Sages enacted to discharge his sons and household even though he has fulfilled, for they rely on him, or he is called obligated since upon him is to educate them. And so too a woman in such a case, who is not obligated to educate her son; see siman 343, Magen Avraham [1].
However. One may say it is not similar to a city person who does not discharge a metropolis person, who is called not obligated; which is not so for this one, who was obligated but did not count on the first night: nevertheless he is called obligated in the mitzvah. Also it is a double doubt: perhaps the law is that if he skipped the first night he counts and is obligated; and even if you say he is not obligated, perhaps the law follows Rashi that a city person discharges a metropolis person; and see Magen Avraham, siman 493, letter vav, that the Tur in his hints took as main the first answer of the Rosh, and like Rashi of blessed memory that a city person discharges a metropolis person; and with a double doubt he blesses, as is fitting, as Peri Chadash himself wrote here: if it is doubtful whether he skipped, he counts with a blessing. And one may say there that it is a dispute of the authorities from one source; and also perhaps Rashi would agree that he does not discharge, and it was a leniency they were lenient in the villages; see there. And see Seven Views of the Rashba of blessed memory, Yevamos 14a. Now certainly if there is someone from whom to hear the blessing, he should certainly do as Peri Chadash wrote. And if not, etc., and he has a minor son who has reached education age and can bless, etc., see siman 186 in Magen Avraham: whether one rabbinic [obligation] discharges another rabbinic. And if his father recites to his minor son who has reached education age word by word, in such a case one may say it is fine, like a minor and a woman who recite to an adult word by word, and the son.

And it is not similar to prayer and kiddush, where the enactment was fixed and did not move from its place; which is not so for making some [new] enactment: whence do we know? Also in siman 124 the Tur wrote simply that he repeats the Amidah; and at the beginning of siman 269 he wrote that one should not make kiddush now in the synagogue; and at the beginning of siman 268, the blessing me'ein sheva; see there. And see Beis Yosef there: it implies one should not distinguish between them. And according to the Tur one may say in 124 it is because of Kedushah; and for the Mechaber it is difficult; and one should not lengthen. And God willing in se'if 7 and 8 I will explain further.
