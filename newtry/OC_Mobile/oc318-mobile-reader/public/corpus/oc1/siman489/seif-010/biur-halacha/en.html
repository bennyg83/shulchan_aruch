Even in our time — see Mishna Berurah what is written we cannot protest those who are lenient — so many Acharonim; they sided finding reason for world custom as Mishna Berurah wrote; nevertheless since it is known many who are careful regarding any prohibition in other prohibitions are also lenient here — I saw the reason is they think whoever wants to be careful must be careful in all stringencies of guards and wages and also in purging vessels — difficult to be careful in these countries; therefore each relies on world custom to be entirely lenient; but in my opinion it is not proper because difficulty in all stringencies should not make entirely lenient; behold in flour it is easy to be careful as today found everywhere wheat flour sown in Cheshvan (and I heard from acquaintances good wheat flour sown in Poland are all sown in Cheshvan and those from Volhynia majority are old from those sown in Cheshvan); also from presumption possibly majority are old; and in beer several Acharonim side to leniency — see Penei Yehoshua and Mishkenot Yaakov and other Acharonim; also beer is not certainly new for even in winter months beer is sometimes made from old barley malt (and although many great Acharonim are stringent also on beer as explained Yoreh De'ah and Acharonim there — lenient have whom to rely on); in doubt we have Or Zaru'a who wrote one may rely in pressing circumstance on those who hold new grain d'rabbanan and it is rabbinic doubt; likewise in purging vessels and lees there are sides to leniency; but in the groats themselves of barley and oats in our country they are certainly new in winter; although we cannot protest world custom to be lenient as Acharonim wrote — proper and correct to be stringent for oneself at least in this; behold for great Rishonim it is d'oraisa — Rif, Rosh, Rambam, Semag, Ittur, Mordechai, in name of Ravyah, Hagahot Maimoniyot, Ritva; and even Or Zaru'a who sides d'rabbanan in chutz la-aretz did not rely to be lenient except in doubt — then one may rely in pressing circumstance on those who hold d'rabbanan and it is double doubt; but not certainly new; Terumat HaDeshen also wrote thus; Peskei Tosafot Menachot wrote of gentiles' grain it is d'rabbanan — if so at least d'rabbanan; nevertheless forbidden; we have no lenient ones except R' Baruch Baal HaTosafot who wrote to be lenient in distant places abroad; Ma'aleh Tzedek in name of R' Avigdor Cohen in name of Rivash wrote to be lenient entirely on gentiles' grain as brought in Bach — they are individual against all these authorities; also several lenient Acharonim did not wish to be lenient except in doubt not certainly; Shulchan Aruch here and Yoreh De'ah sealed like poskim it is d'oraisa in all respects; many Acharonim also grasped thus for essence; therefore certainly proper and correct to be concerned for all this and separate at least from certainly new as above;
