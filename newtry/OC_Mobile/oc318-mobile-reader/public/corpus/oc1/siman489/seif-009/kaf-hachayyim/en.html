The sacred Zohar, parashas Tetzaveh, page 183a, writes likewise, as does Shaar HaKavanos, page 85c. Magen Avraham, subsection 1. Magen Avraham writes there, however, that women have already accepted it upon themselves as an obligation. Chok Yaakov, subsection 3; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 2; Chok Yosef, subsection 3; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 2, write likewise. Nevertheless, Maharshal Shaar Aryeh in manuscript and Mahari Lingo in manuscript objected to women counting. Birkei Yosef, subsection 22, cites them and writes that if women follow Rabbeinu Tam by reciting a blessing over the lulav and similar mitzvos, they should follow their usual practice with counting as well. Shulchan Aruch HaRav writes there that there is no concern of a prohibition when they recite the blessing, although they are exempt. Sefer Shulchan Shlomo, however, writes that in any event they should not recite the blessing, because they will certainly make a mistake on one day, and most of them do not understand the meaning of the words. See there. Mishnah Berurah, subsection 3, cites this. All this follows the Ashkenazic custom, under which women recite blessings over time-bound positive commandments, as stated below, siman 589, se'if 6, in the gloss. See there. Sephardic women, however, may not recite the blessing in any event if they wish to fulfill the mitzvah of counting the omer. If possible, they should hear the blessing from men, with both parties intending that the women fulfill their obligation, and then the women should count.
