Se'if 9. One should count before they recite [havdalah]. Its source is in Terumat HaDeshen. And in my humble opinion there is reason to discuss for practical law, for one may say that Terumat HaDeshen holds like the poskim that havdalah is rabbinic; but according to the rulings of the Rama above in siman 271, se'if 5, that one interrupts in the middle of a meal for havdalah, he necessarily holds that havdalah is according to the Torah, as the Magen Avraham wrote at the beginning of siman 239. (And what the Rama wrote at the end of siman 296, that two should not make havdalah each for himself, that is l'chatchila to be concerned stringently for those who hold havdalah is rabbinic and women are exempt; but according to the essential law they make havdalah for themselves, for havdalah is according to the Torah, since "remember" means at its entrance and at its departure, and accordingly women are obligated, for whoever is included in guarding is [included in remembering].) One may say that havdalah comes first. And even though on every Motza'ei Shabbos counting comes before [havdalah] because taking the day out is preferable to the rationale of giving precedence to a Torah-level [obligation], and this is proven from yaknehaz: even though kiddush of Yom Tov is rabbinic, as the Magen Avraham wrote at the beginning of siman 271, nevertheless one may say that on Motza'ei Shabbos and Yom Tov regarding the counting, that in kiddush there is bringing the day in and in havdalah taking the day out, they stand on the law to give precedence to the Torah-level [obligation]. And in truth, what is written regarding an ordinary Motza'ei Shabbos also has no proof to put counting before havdalah from yaknehaz, for there there are two advantages: one, bringing the day in of kiddush, and one, taking the day out of havdalah; therefore they give precedence to rabbinic kiddush before Torah-level havdalah. And even though the Rama did not gloss thus, and the Mechaber too appears to hold that havdalah is according to the Torah from his words at the beginning of siman 291, which imply for law that women are obligated in havdalah, that is because the Rama ruled here in se'if 4 that one breaks from his eating for the counting and is concerned for those who hold that counting nowadays is according to the Torah; therefore he holds here like Terumat HaDeshen that they count first. But according to what the Magen Avraham wrote in this siman, s.k. 6, that if he erred and counted at twilight he has fulfilled, since we are not concerned for the poskim that he has not fulfilled, and it is proper to count after nightfall without a blessing, for it is no trouble at all, for he holds that the essential counting nowadays is rabbinic: if so, a strong proof is needed to put rabbinic counting before Torah-level havdalah, since against taking the day out there is also bringing the day in; and this requires further study.
