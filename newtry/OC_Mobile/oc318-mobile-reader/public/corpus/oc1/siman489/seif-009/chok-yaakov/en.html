Before Havdalah. This is after Kaddish Tiskabel, as stated by Abudraham. On Motzaei Shabbos as well, counting is performed immediately after Kaddish Tiskabel. Although our custom is to recite VeYiten Lecha before Havdalah, counting is nevertheless performed immediately after Kaddish Tiskabel, before VeYiten Lecha, because once the prayer has concluded through Kaddish the mitzvah of counting takes effect. This is the custom. Abudraham and the books of custom say “before Havdalah” because their custom was to recite Havdalah immediately after Kaddish Tiskabel, unlike our custom. Although counting is performed before Havdalah while it is still Shabbos, it is still Shabbos only regarding melachah; the day of Shabbos itself has already passed. So writes Magen Avraham in seif katan 6. In addition, Havdalah has already been recited in the prayer.
When the final Yom Tov of Pesach falls, etc. On the second day of Pesach, Kiddush is not recited in the beis midrash at all, as explained in siman 487. At home, one always counts before Kiddush because eating is forbidden before counting. This is Magen Avraham's intent, though his text contains a scribal error. Taz writes that on the night of the final Yom Tov one should always count before Kiddush, for it is preferable to extend the sanctity of the seventh day, which is according to the Torah, rather than the sanctity of the final Yom Tov, which is D'Rabbanan. This is contrary to Rama's implication that counting is always performed after Kiddush unless the final Yom Tov falls on Motzaei Shabbos. The custom follows Rama. This may be for the reasons explained above in seif katan 2.
