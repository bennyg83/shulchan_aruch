(s.k. 7) One who prays, etc. And so it appears from the language of Avudraham, from whom the law mentioned in Shulchan Aruch [comes], who wrote first: Machzor Vitry wrote: one who prays with the congregation while it is still day counts with them without a blessing; he says to himself: if I remember at night in my house I will go back and bless properly, so I will not have blessed in vain; and if I forget, behold I have counted days and weeks for the mitzvah. But the Rambam wrote: if he counted, etc.; and so is the main [view], end quote.
See Beis Yosef: it implies that he holds as it should read; and if so, it requires further study, etc., for Avudraham himself agreed with the Rambam and holds that it is not proper to do so.
"I do not intend to fulfill": and that is the unspecified case, that he has not fulfilled; and the same applies even if Beis Yosef held that intent is not required: he must still intend not to fulfill, for according to all he has not fulfilled even if intent is not required. Only that Magen Avraham correctly wrote that Beis Yosef holds that [mitzvos] require intent. Also Beis Yosef in siman 589 brought in the name of the Ra'ah that according to the view that intent is not required, even if he said explicitly that he does not wish to fulfill, nevertheless he fulfills; but we do not hold like him; and see Beis Yosef, siman 475.
On erev Shabbos while it is still day: since he has already made it night regarding Shabbos, the same applies regarding the counting. And Chok Yaakov brought it in the name of Piskei Tosafos on Menachos, that even on a weekday if he prayed Ma'ariv he may count, for just as it is night regarding Shema and prayer, so too regarding counting. And he wrote that on this the congregations who count while it is still day rely, as Magen Avraham wrote in the name of the Rashba and Avudraham, and not like the Taz who wrote that it speaks of twilight. But we do not hold in this like Piskei Tosafos; see there.
