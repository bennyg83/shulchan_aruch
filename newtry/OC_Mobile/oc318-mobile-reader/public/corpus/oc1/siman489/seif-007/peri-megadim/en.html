For if. See Taz. Now the Mechaber in siman 60, se'if 4, ruled that mitzvos require intent according to the Torah; and regarding rabbinic, one may say intent is not required; see Magen Avraham there [3]. And counting nowadays is rabbinic according to most poskim; see letter 9; if so, one may say intent is not required. And this is what the Rama wrote in se'if 3: "if his intent was not to fulfill," implying that with unspecified [intent] he has fulfilled, for intent is not required for rabbinic. Nevertheless, this one who answers "today such-and-such" presumably only answers the asker something, but his intent is not to fulfill. Nevertheless, the proof he brings from Birkas HaMazon in siman 284 is difficult, for that is according to the Torah; and from lulav in siman 658 it is fine, for on the other days it is rabbinic. And see Eliyah Rabbah: since the adult does not hold the lulav; see there. And see Magen Avraham, letters 8 and 9. And it implies for law that after the fact, if he said "today such-and-such," he afterward counts with a blessing, for unspecified is as his interpretation that he intends not to fulfill. And see Peri Chadash and Chok Yaakov [15]: the Mechaber's language "he cannot go back and count with a blessing" implies that it is forbidden to bless, as Magen Avraham wrote in letter 5: even though the law is that mitzvos require intent, a doubtful blessing is to leniency, and the stringency of not taking [Hashem's Name in vain], as in siman 216; see there. And if he said the days and not the weeks, ostensibly there is a double doubt to bless: perhaps mitzvos require intent, and perhaps he has not fulfilled with days alone; see Magen Avraham [4] and Peri Chadash here; and this requires further study.
