1) [Se'if 1] When Rosh Chodesh is observed for two days, etc. The reason months are arranged with one full month of thirty days and one deficient month of twenty-nine days is that from the molad of one month to the molad of the next there are twenty-nine days, twelve hours, and 793 chalakim of an hour, while an hour contains 1,080 chalakim. Since months are counted only in complete days, as it is written, 'Until a month of days,' one month is made deficient with twenty-nine days and another full with thirty days, so that the calculation balances and the molad occurs on Rosh Chodesh. The remaining 793 chalakim from each month eventually accumulate into an additional day, and that year is then made complete, meaning that seven months are made full. Since part of the thirtieth day belongs to the coming month and the moon is frequently renewed on that day, it is made Rosh Chodesh. Rosh Chodesh is therefore observed for two days: day thirty completes the preceding month, and day thirty-one begins the coming month. All this is explained at length by the Tur, Levush, and Rambam in Hilchos Kiddush HaChodesh, chapter 8. See there. Even when months were sanctified on the basis of sighting, Rosh Chodesh was sometimes observed for two days. Thus, regarding Yehonasan it is written, 'And it was on the following day, the second day of the new moon.' Peri Chadash, end of this siman. Know that the moon is concealed for twenty-four hours. In Eretz Yisrael it is concealed for eighteen hours before the molad and six hours afterward; for the residents of Bavel, six hours before the molad and eighteen hours afterward. Rosh Hashanah 20b. See Rashi's explanation there.
