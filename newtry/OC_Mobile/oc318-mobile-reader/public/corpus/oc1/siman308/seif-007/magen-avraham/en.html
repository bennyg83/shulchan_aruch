That broke on a weekday. The same if it broke on Shabbat, as seif 6 — only he mentioned on a weekday because of the end: if he threw it to the ash heap while still day; if he threw it on Shabbat — permitted to move since at bein hashemashot it had the status of a vessel (Maggid Mishneh) — end of his words; Darkei Moshe; and so the Bach, unlike Rabbeinu Yerucham who wrote that if it broke on Shabbat it is forbidden to move unless fit for its original function — difficult to invent distinctions not found in Talmud and poskim; however what the Bach wrote explicitly on Tosafot requires study, for Tosafot there are as their plain sense; Rava holds nolad is forbidden and Tosafot explained accordingly; for practical law there is no distinction — even on Rosh HaShanah permitted unless he threw it to the ash heap while still day.

That has no relevance of a vessel. Such as an iron lamp and a board [Maggid Mishneh ch. 4 of Eruvin], and likewise a wooden chip as below, and a wick is called a vessel as the Beit Yosef wrote siman 314 in the name of Terumat HaDeshen — see siman 301; and so Tosafot Shabbat daf 47: a whole wick is called a vessel, and wick fragments are called pebbles; if so, permitted to move a whole wick for the need of its use and its place, as seif 3. And it appears to me from here people are accustomed that one may move a whole candle of tallow or wax; nevertheless they err, for it is not permitted except when he needs it for a permitted matter, as seif 3. The Beit Yosef wrote: the son of the Rashbatz wrote regarding a place where they practice that the first time a man brings his son to synagogue he brings a wax candle in his hand — even on Shabbat there is no prohibition of moving, since from erev Shabbat he intended to bring it, for thought releases muktzeh, as seif 20 — end of his words; and it does not appear so to me, for the case is not comparable to the proof, for there they are fit to use on Shabbat, unlike a wax candle — end of his words. And it does not appear so to me — the reason of those places, such as lest the child take it in his hand there be a prohibition; nevertheless one should be strict — see siman 309 and siman 343.
