A piece of earthenware that broke on a weekday from a vessel and is fit to cover a utensil with may be moved, even in a place where no utensils are found there to cover with it; and if he threw it to the ash heap intentionally, it is forbidden to move it, since it ceased being a utensil.And even if it is suitable for a poor person, since its owners designated it — even though they are wealthy — muktzeh for a rich person is muktzeh for a poor person, as will be explained at the end of the siman (Ran, chapter BeMah Ishah); and specifically a piece of earthenware, because it comes from broken utensils; but a thing that has no aspect of utensil, such as pebbles or stones — even if they are fit to cover a utensil with — it is forbidden to move them. {Rama: For every thing that is not a utensil at all, it is forbidden to move it even for its body, all the more so for its place (Mordechai, chapter 1 of Yom Tov):) ודוקא חתיכ' חרס משום דאתיא משברי כלים אבל דבר שאין בו שייכו' כלי כגון צרורות או אבנים אע"פ שראוים לכסות בהם כלי אסור לטלטלם: הגה דכל דבר שאינו כלי כלל אסור לטלטלו אפי' לצורך גופו כל שכן לצורך מקומו (מרדכי פרק קמא דיום טוב.
