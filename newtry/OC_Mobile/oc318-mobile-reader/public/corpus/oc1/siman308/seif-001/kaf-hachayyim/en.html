1) [Seif 1] All utensils may be moved on Shabbos, etc. This siman and those following it through the end of siman 312 explain the laws of muktzeh. Rambam writes at the end of chapter 24 that moving muktzeh is forbidden so that moving objects on Shabbos will not resemble moving them during the week. Raavad writes there that moving muktzeh is forbidden because of carrying. These are their words in brief. See Beis Yosef, who cites them at length. Bach writes that it appears even Rambam agrees that the prohibition of muktzeh is because of carrying. Rambam wished only to explain why the law forbidding muktzeh immediately follows the law governing one's manner of walking. Just as the Navi warned that one's manner of walking on Shabbos should not resemble that of the week, so too moving objects on Shabbos should not resemble moving them during the week; see there.
2) The general categories of muktzeh articles that may not be moved are divided into six parts. First, a utensil set aside because of potential financial loss, meaning a utensil that one is particular not to damage, such as a shechitah knife, bris milah knife, or bookbinder's knife. Second, a utensil whose designated use is forbidden. Third, anything that is neither a utensil nor human or animal food, such as stones, money, reeds, wood, beams, earth, sand, a corpse, living creatures, and figs or raisins placed where they dry, and similar unusable items. This is muktzeh because of its inherent nature. Fourth, a utensil whose designated use is permitted upon which an inherently muktzeh item rests, such as a barrel with a stone upon it or a cushion with money upon it. Even if the muktzeh item is later removed, since it rested upon the utensil during twilight, the utensil was set aside during twilight and remains set aside throughout the day. Fifth, something that was attached to the ground or needed to be trapped during twilight. Sixth, something set aside because of its mitzvah, such as sukkah wood and decorations. Beis Yosef; Levush Serad. Some of these categories nevertheless have grounds for permission, as each will be explained in its place.
3) There: All utensils may be moved, etc. This means even a very large and heavy utensil, as stated in seif 2, and even a utensil whose designated use is forbidden when it is needed for a permitted use or its place, as stated in seif 3. The purposes for which such utensils may be moved will be explained below. Without any need, however, even a utensil whose designated use is permitted may not be moved, as stated below in seif 4; see there.
4) There: Apart from muktzeh because of potential financial loss, etc. Since one is particular about such utensils, he assigns them a fixed place and does not move them from it. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 2.
5) There: Such as a shechitah knife, etc. The same applies to a perforated utensil used to fumigate vessels and sprinkle water upon them, a mortuary knife, and a carpenter's adze. Rabbeinu Yerucham; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2. This also appears in Beis Yosef.
6) There: Or for bris milah. This discusses when it is not needed for the bris. When moved for the bris, it is not muktzeh, and while it remains in his hand he may wash it. Nevertheless, doing so is forbidden because it is not needed for Shabbos. Rabbi Moshe of Trani, volume 3, simanim 84 and 89; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef; Magen Avraham, siman 331, paragraph 5.
7) Rabbeinu Yerucham writes in the name of Rambam, section 11, volume 2: One may move the instrument and return it after the bris, because once he moved it permissibly, he may return it wherever he wishes, although he has no other child to circumcise. These are his words. Beis Yosef cites this in Yoreh De'ah, siman 266. Darkei Moshe writes there that Maharil is stringent and requires the mohel to drop the knife from his hand immediately after the bris because it is muktzeh. The same applies to all bris supplies and on Yom Tov. Darkei Moshe continues: Rabbeinu Yerucham's words appear correct to me, because since the instrument was not set aside during twilight, it may be moved wherever he wishes; see there. Rema rules this way there in seif 2. Maharshal similarly writes in Responsa, siman 40, that one may move and put away the knife, because anything prepared during twilight does not become muktzeh even if its permitted use subsequently ends; see there. Taz there in siman 266, paragraph 1, however, challenges Rema and Maharshal and writes that after the bris the knife may be moved only to put it away in the room where the bris was performed, but no farther. Even before the bris, it may not be moved needlessly when not required for the bris; see there. He writes the same in Orach Chaim, siman 310, paragraph 3. Magen Avraham, siman 331, paragraph 5, writes that one should be stringent and not release it from his hand until he brings it home and puts it away; see there. Eliyah Rabbah there, paragraph 5, cites this and writes that accordingly, when there is only one mohel and he must put down the knife to perform peri'ah, it may not afterward be moved. It is therefore proper for him to hand it to another person; see there. Nekudos HaKesef there in siman 266, however, resolves Taz's question and appears to rule like Rema and Maharshal. Nevertheless, he writes that Rema apparently permitted moving it only to put it away within an enclosed courtyard with an eruv, but not needlessly; see there. Eliyah Rabbah there likewise rejects the questions of Taz and Magen Avraham and concludes leniently like Rema and Nekudos HaKesef; see there. Responsa Rav Pe'alim, volume 3, siman 18, also rules like Rabbeinu Yerucham; see there. See further below, paragraph 55.
8) Permission to move bris supplies and the knife after the bris was granted only to the mohel when a bris occurs on Shabbos, because for him it is considered not to have been set aside. It also appears possible to be lenient for those assisting with the bris, according to the custom in some communities of auctioning the honor of carrying the bris supplies: one person holds a silver tray containing all the instruments and hands the mohel whatever he needs. One can say that these were not set aside for him either. It is also possible that specifically for the mohel, the knife is muktzeh because of potential financial loss and may not be moved after he fulfills the mitzvah, whereas for others it is like an ordinary knife. Although Beis Yosef in Orach Chaim, siman 308, writes in the name of Ran that an item regarded as muktzeh by the wealthy is also muktzeh for the poor, Tosafos in the chapter Mefanin, beginning with the words 'Since it is fit for a Kohen,' writes that an item regarded as muktzeh by the wealthy is not muktzeh for the poor, or even for the wealthy when in a poor person's house. Ikarei HaDat, siman 14, paragraph 52, in the name of Pachad Yitzchak, entry shin.
9) There: Even when needed for its place or for a permitted use. Toras HaBayis, section 12, volume 3, writes the same, contrary to Mordechai at the end of the chapter Kirah, who permits it. Darkei Moshe, paragraph 1. Nezirus Shimshon writes that it should at least be permitted when needed for its place; see there. Orach Chaim, paragraph 1, cites this. We nevertheless maintain the view of Maran and Rema that it is forbidden both when needed for a permitted use and when needed for its place. It appears to me, however, that if during the week one demonstrated that he is not particular about the utensil, such as occasionally using it for another purpose when necessary, for example cutting fruit and the like, it may be moved on Shabbos when needed for a permitted use or its place. I afterward saw that Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 4, writes the same. See below, paragraph 14.
10) There: Even when needed for its place, etc. It is certainly permitted to move it indirectly. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 3.
11) There, gloss: It is forbidden to move it. This means that the bookbinder's knife itself may not be moved. One might have thought that because it is inserted among other knives, he is not particular about it; the ruling teaches that it is forbidden. The sheath together with the knives, however, may be moved, because it was made as a base for both forbidden and permitted items, as stated at the end of siman 310; see there. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1.
12) There, gloss: It is forbidden to move it. It may not be moved in its present state. Rather, one must shake out only the forbidden item. If it is more valuable than the permitted knives, even shaking is forbidden, in accordance with the law of a base for forbidden and permitted items in siman 310; see there. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 4.
13) There: A perfumer's hammer, etc. A goldsmith's hammer, however, is permitted, as stated in seif 3. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 5.
14) There: About which one is particular, etc. In a place where people are not particular, it is permitted, as stated in seif 3. Magen Avraham, paragraph 2. It is possible that Magen Avraham's phrase 'in a place where people are not particular' is not precise. Even if people in that place are particular, a particular person who is not particular about it may use the leniency. Machatzis HaShekel. This is also implied by Eshel Avraham, paragraph 2.
15) There, gloss: And he is particular about them. If he is not particular about them, they may be moved although they were set aside for merchandise. Beis Yosef; Levush; Magen Avraham, paragraph 3. Any expensive item is forbidden even if it was not placed in storage. An inexpensive item is forbidden only if it was placed in storage and one is particular about it. Magen Avraham there; Machatzis HaShekel; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 6.
16) There, gloss: And he is particular about them. This applies even to dining utensils, such as bowls, glassware, and knives. It appears to me that this applies specifically to a merchant who does not ordinarily lend utensils. One who ordinarily lends new utensils does not render them muktzeh even after placing them in storage, because he is not particular about them. Chayei Adam, klal 65, paragraph 3, section beginning 'lest.'
