[1] [Levush] And additionally when he examines, etc. At the beginning of siman 301 Levush gives only the first reason. See Bach there.
[2] Such as, etc. Likewise a perforated utensil used to fumigate vessels while sprinkling water through it, a slaughterer's large knife, and a carpenter's adze (Rabbenu Yerucham).
[3] For circumcision, etc., even when they are inserted, etc. Magen Avraham explains that the circumcision knives themselves remain prohibited to move. One might have thought that since they are inserted with other knives the owner is not particular about them, so we are taught that they remain prohibited. The sheath together with the knives, however, may be moved because it serves as a base for both prohibited and permitted items. Maharil's wording, "we do not permit muktzeh merely because of a 'since' argument," does not fully imply this. See the end of siman 310 and seif 5 there, and seif katan 9.
[4] A spice hammer, etc. Perishah writes that for the same reason it would be prohibited to drink from a mortar; see Derishah. Derishah questions whether there is greater room to permit our metal spice hammer, which can easily be washed even if it becomes dirty, and reports that his teacher permitted cracking nuts with it. The text apparently must read "spice hammer" rather than "blacksmith's hammer." This also resolves Beit Yosef's difficulty from Rambam, and Shiltei Gibborim writes similarly. As to the basic law, if it is evident that the owner is not particular about the utensil, it is certainly permitted, as Levush concludes regarding a utensil, etc. The same applies to a slaughtering knife and similar items mentioned above if the owner is not particular. Shiltei Gibborim, chapter 8, writes that blank paper is muktzeh. One must explain that it is muktzeh because of monetary loss, since it is designated for writing and the owner is particular about it (Magen Avraham). At first glance blank paper is muktzeh by its very nature like a stone, for the category of monetary-loss muktzeh applies particularly to utensils. It appears to me, however, that since paper can be used to cover the opening of a flask it has the status of a utensil. I later found Recanati, siman 95, stating that a get may be moved because one can study the laws of gittin from it, and similarly any scraps suitable to cover a flask, except paper specifically designated for writing. I am surprised by Responsa Rosh, rule 22, and Agudah, chapter Kol HaKelim, which write that a goldsmith's hammer and spice hammer are prohibited, contrary to the Gemara and Rosh's own ruling that a goldsmith's hammer is permitted. Rabbenu Yerucham, netiv 12, likewise cites that responsum of Rosh. This requires study.
