Behold, before we explain this siman I will precede with a small introduction. Behold, the prohibition of moving muktzeh that the Sages fenced in — Rambam wrote in ch. 24 and this is his language: the Sages forbade moving some things on Shabbat as one does on a weekday; and why did they touch this prohibition? They said: if the prophets warned and commanded that your walking on Shabbat not be like your walking on a weekday, and your Shabbat speech not like weekday speech, as it says "and speak a matter" — a fortiori there should not be moving on Shabbat like moving on a weekday, so that the day not appear as a weekday in his eyes and he come to lift and repair vessels from corner to corner or from house to house, or to store stones and the like; for behold he is idle and sits in his house and seeks something to occupy himself, and he ends up not resting and the reason stated in the Torah "so that he may rest" is nullified; and also when he visits and moves vessels whose function is for prohibition, he may occupy himself somewhat and come to melacha; and also because some people are not craftsmen but idle all their days, such as strollers and corner-sitters whose days are all resting from melacha — if it were permitted to walk and speak and move like other days, they would not rest a rest recognizable to all; therefore resting in these matters is equal rest for every person; and for these reasons they touched the prohibition of moving and forbade a person to move on Shabbat except vessels he needs, as will be explained — end of his words. Raavad gave a reason that it is a fence for carrying out: if we permit moving everything, one will come through that also to carry from reshut ha-yachid to reshut ha-rabbim. Know: from here until end of seif 12 four categories of muktzeh are explained. Category one: muktzeh because of chisaron kis — a vessel a person is particular about lest it be damaged or spoiled. Category two: something that is not a vessel and not human food or animal food, such as stones, reeds, coins, wood, beams, earth, sand, a corpse, gentile food, and dried figs and raisins placed where they dry — and anything similar that is not fit and is called muktzeh because of its essence. Category three: a vessel whose function is for prohibition in seif 3. Category four: a vessel whose function is for permission with something muktzeh on it, and even if removed on Shabbat, since at bein hashemashot it was muktzeh for bein hashemashot it is muktzeh for the whole day [and there are two more categories: muktzeh something that was attached or lacking preparation at bein hashemashot — explained in Yom Tov laws; second, muktzeh because of a mitzvah such as sukkah wood and decorations — explained in Sukkah laws]. Explanation of these laws from here until end of siman 12 — so too the Beit Yosef; therefore one who examines these simanim will understand which muktzeh category each law is.
