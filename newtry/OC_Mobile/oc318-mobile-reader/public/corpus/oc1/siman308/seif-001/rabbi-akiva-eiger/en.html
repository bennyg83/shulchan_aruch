Seif 1. One use is forbidden to move. If on Shabbos he wants no longer to keep the knife for circumcision but to keep it like other knives for all purposes, one can say that its set-aside status has thereby departed and it is permitted, according to what we rule that on Shabbos we follow Rabbi Shimon and do not say that since it was set aside at twilight it remains set aside. However, if so, it should be permitted for the need of its place even while he keeps it for circumcision, since he has a permitted possibility of nullifying its set-aside status. See what I wrote above in siman 209 on Magen Avraham s.k. 12 in the gloss, and nearby on Magen Avraham s.k. 19 in the gloss. Yet from the words of Tosafot at the beginning of Beitzah, s.v. ein mevakin, it appears that it is muktzeh because of monetary loss. For now, when he wants to split it for wood, he nullifies its being set aside for building, and nevertheless even according to Rabbi Meir it is forbidden. This requires great study.
