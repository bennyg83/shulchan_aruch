Forbidden to sit. For palm branches — some stand for sitting and some for wood; here they were forbidden only because of his thought that he cut them for burning, and therefore thought of sitting removes thought of burning; but a row of stones has nothing standing for sitting, therefore an act is required to prove they are for sitting (Maggid Mishneh ch. 25); if so, with our wood too an act is required, as siman 321 and 322. What Or Zarua wrote from siman 309 seif 4 — he did not understand, for there it is permitted only to tilt and shake — see siman 322.

And some say the law of stones, etc. Darkei Moshe wrote in Mordechai ch. 4: he wrote there is no distinction; on the contrary, some say specifically with palm branches they disagree, but stones are permitted because they do not move; somewhat implied in Ran ch. 17, and so one may explain the Rosh — end of his words; see siman 325 seif 11; requires study how one can say they do not disagree about stones, for they explicitly disagree in the Gemara daf 125; therefore it appears to me: certainly where they do not move — certainly permitted to sit on them for all, for muktzeh is not forbidden by touch, as Ran plainly and Rashi daf 50: palm branches require tying if not tied — forbidden to move tomorrow; and so daf 125: arrange stones so we need not touch them tomorrow, for designation does not help a stone to become a vessel — end of Rashi; behold permitted to sit on them even though muktzeh, except with palm branches that when one sits he moves them and therefore forbidden; but heavy stones — permitted to sit for all. Rambam deals with stones not so large that when one sits he moves them, therefore forbidden unless he acted from yesterday (Maggid Mishneh); he holds when arranged it is an act; what Darkei Moshe wrote that the Rosh also deals with sitting without moving — but to move them requires an act, as the Rosh in responsa and siman 322. Nevertheless requires study: in siman 311 seif 8 they say one shakes the straw with his body and may lie on it — all the more to sit; requires study; and so in Eruvin daf 37b; therefore it appears to me even Rambam who requires arranging today so as not to need to touch tomorrow — as Rashi — but permitted to sit without arranging.
