44) There: And sacred writings, etc. The same applies to cups, bowls, plates, a fig knife, a pot skimmer, and a small knife that is usually on the table. Shiltei Gibborim. Rambam's commentary to the Mishnah in the chapter Kol HaKelim, however, somewhat implies that cups and bowls are like sacred writings. Kol Bo, page 33, also implies this. Tosafos on page 123, however, implies like Shiltei Gibborim, and the matter requires examination. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 14. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraphs 16 and 17, rules like Rambam and Kol Bo, that cups, bowls, and a knife are not like sacred writings but like any utensil whose designated use is permitted. They may be moved only for their own use, but not entirely without need; see there. Chayei Adam, klal for Parashas Mikeitz, paragraph 1, also rules this way. He therefore writes that a person sitting at a table upon which there are cups, bowls, a knife, and a fork may not move any of these utensils about needlessly, for demons cause hands to become meddlesome; see there.
