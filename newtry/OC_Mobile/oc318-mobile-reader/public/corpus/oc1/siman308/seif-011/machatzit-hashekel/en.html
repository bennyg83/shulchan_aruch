And shofar implies that tefillin, etc. Since they are divided into three clauses, sacred writings, tefillin, and shofar, they must differ in law. Sacred writings may be moved even for no need; tefillin may be moved specifically for a need, even from the sun to the shade; and a shofar is permitted only for the need of its body or its place.
They do not require a sign. That is to say, for this reason Shabbos is not a time for tefillin, because it is written regarding tefillin, “and it shall be a sign upon your hand,” and on Shabbos and Yom Tov we already have a sign, namely Shabbos and Yom Tov themselves, as explained above in siman 31. Therefore we do not need the sign of tefillin, as Tosafot wrote at the end of the first chapter of Beitzah. The Mishnah there teaches that on Yom Tov one may send garments, whether sewn or not sewn, etc., but not a nailed sandal, since it is forbidden to wear it on Shabbos, for the Sages prohibited it because an accident once occurred through it, as stated in Shabbos 60a. The rule is that anything from which one derives use on Yom Tov may be sent on Yom Tov. Rav Sheshes permitted sending tefillin, and Abaye challenged him from that rule. The Gemara answered that the words “on Yom Tov” in the Mishnah refer to the latter clause, meaning, anything used on a weekday may be sent on Yom Tov. Tosafot wrote that this comes to include only tefillin, but not a nailed sandal, for if one were permitted to send it, he might come to wear it; unlike tefillin, where even if one puts them on there is no prohibition. See Shitah Mekubetzes, for according to this one must explain the Mishnah as anything used on a weekday, meaning its primary use is on a weekday, although on Yom Tov it is also somewhat fit. On Chol HaMoed one who puts on tefillin is liable to death, for he appears to belittle the sign of Chol HaMoed by showing that he needs the sign of tefillin. See there.
And also Tosafot in Shabbos, etc. That is to say, aside from the words of the Zohar, even from our Gemara it is evident from Tosafot in Shabbos not like Tosafot at the end of the first chapter of Beitzah. If so, why is there greater reason to rely on Tosafot in Beitzah than on Tosafot in Shabbos, who prohibit it?
Rather, they hold, etc. Tosafot there, s.v. not with a nailed sandal, wrote that even according to the Rabbis of Rabbi Eliezer, who permit moving a nailed sandal for the need of its body or its place, it is forbidden to send it on Yom Tov. Although sending is for the joy of Yom Tov and is considered like a need of its body, since a mishap came through the nailed sandal there is no joy of Yom Tov. From where did Tosafot know that an object whose primary use is for a prohibited labor may be sent on Yom Tov? It must be that since we explicitly find that Rav Sheshes permitted sending tefillin, and they hold that tefillin are called an object whose primary use is for a prohibited labor, the same applies to all such objects. Thus Tosafot hold that it is forbidden to put on tefillin on Shabbos and Yom Tov.
For otherwise, according to him, why do we not put them on, etc. This is an additional proof to what was written above. According to what was ruled above in siman 31, that it is forbidden to put on tefillin on Chol HaMoed, all the more so it is forbidden on Shabbos. Besides the fact that this follows logically, he also brought a proof, for if it were permitted to put them on on Shabbos, why do we not put them on? Even if there is no obligation of tefillin on Shabbos, one who puts them on would receive reward like one who is not commanded but performs the mitzvah, as women do with time-bound positive commandments, where according to some they may even recite a blessing, as above in siman 13. Nevertheless it is forbidden to put them on on Shabbos.
And what the Bach inferred from the teaching “he should not go out,” etc. This refers to the Mishnah that says one should not go out with tefillin, and if he did go out he is not liable to bring a chatas. The Gemara on folio 61 concludes that both the first clause and the latter clause can follow either the opinion that Shabbos is a time for tefillin or the opinion that Shabbos is not a time for tefillin. Even according to the view that it is a time for tefillin, initially he may not go out lest he need the bathroom, remove them, and come to carry them four amos in the public domain. And the latter clause, that if he went out he is not liable, can follow the view that Shabbos is not a time for tefillin, for nevertheless they are not a burden since they are worn in the manner of a garment. From this the Bach inferred that since the Gemara establishes the Mishnah according to all views, even according to the view that Shabbos is not a time for tefillin, and nevertheless it teaches only that he may not go out, this implies that at home he may put them on. These are his words.
For anything the Sages prohibited one to go out with into the public domain is also prohibited to go out with in a courtyard. The same would apply even when sitting in one place, that it is prohibited. This follows Rav and not Rabbi Anani bar Sasson.
However, with tefillin Rav agrees. As Tosafot wrote, since one is obligated to handle them, there is no concern lest he forget and go out with them into the public domain. Therefore Rashi wrote that he holds Shabbos is not a time for tefillin and one is not obligated to handle them. This is understandable according to Rav, that even in a courtyard they are forbidden lest he forget and go out with them into the public domain, and likewise even sitting in one place is forbidden.
Nevertheless it is like an object, etc. That is to say, according to the Zohar, which says that one who puts them on on Shabbos is liable to death, they are certainly like an object whose primary use is for a prohibited labor, and they are permitted only for the need of their body or their place, like a shofar. Even according to our Gemara, since we rule that Shabbos is not a time for tefillin, they are like an object whose primary use is for a prohibited labor. One might have thought that since there is no intrinsic prohibition in putting them on, only a decree lest he go out, they are called an object whose primary use is permitted, since on account of the tefillin themselves there is no prohibition in putting them on. The Magen Avraham therefore wrote that this is not so; their primary use is called prohibited.
And so it appears from Tosafot in Shabbos, folio 46. The Gemara there says that a lamp in which naphtha was lit, though it is foul-smelling, may nevertheless be moved because it is fit to cover a vessel. The Gemara asks that if so, all pebbles in a courtyard should be permitted to move, for they too are fit to cover vessels. It answers that specifically a naphtha lamp, which has the status of a vessel, benefits from the reasoning that it is fit to cover a vessel, whereas pebbles, which do not have the status of a vessel, do not benefit from being fit to cover a vessel. It was taught that necklaces, nose-rings, and rings are like all vessels that may be moved in a courtyard, and Ulla said that the reason is that they have the status of a vessel. Tosafot wrote that Ulla inferred that the baraisa must follow Rav, who forbids going out even in a courtyard, from the fact that it says that they may be moved in a courtyard, implying only moving is permitted. Tosafot did not ask that they should be like an object whose primary use is for a prohibited labor, permitted for the need of its body or place, and therefore Ulla needed to say that it is because they have the status of a vessel. Thus Tosafot wrote that those ornaments with which it is forbidden to go out in a courtyard lest she forget and go out to the public domain are called objects whose primary use is for a prohibited labor, and the same applies to tefillin.
And see siman 303, seif 18. That is to say, according to the authorities there who rule like Rabbi Anani bar Sasson that anything with which one may not go out to the public domain is nevertheless permitted in a courtyard, tefillin too would be permitted in a courtyard, apart from the view of the Zohar. If so, they may certainly be moved, for they are like an object whose primary use is permitted.
