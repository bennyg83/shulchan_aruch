Seif 11. One removes its point. The Rashba’s view in Chiddushim is that this applies specifically when it was removed before Shabbos, but if it was removed on Shabbos one should permit it, since the pieces perform a type of their original work and were prepared incidentally to their original object. He compares it to remnants of mats in the following seif. In my humble opinion, since we rule regarding remnants of mats that if one threw them into the refuse heap before Shabbos they are forbidden, this implies that if he threw them into the refuse heap on Shabbos they are permitted, and thus they are better than a needle. See the Rashba, who indeed holds that if he threw them into the refuse heap on Shabbos they are forbidden, for they are exactly like a needle. But we distinguish that a needle is worse, because it no longer stands at all for its original work. If so, one cannot learn from one to the other at all, and this requires study.
