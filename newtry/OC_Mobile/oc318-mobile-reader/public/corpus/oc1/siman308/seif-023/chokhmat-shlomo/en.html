There, seif 23: It is permitted to cut a branch from a palm tree while it is still day, etc. NB: The Magen Avraham wrote that this is because he designated it for that purpose. The Korban HaEdah, in Shirei Korban, Sukkah chapter 3, challenged him from what is said there: the Rabbis say that an aravah may be waved for a sick person on Shabbat. This proves that even without designating it for that purpose it is permitted, and he left the matter as requiring study. In my humble opinion this is not difficult at all. With a branch, the matter depends on a person's intent; therefore if he did not designate it for this purpose it is muktzeh. But before the Sages forbade going out with a lulav on Shabbat, it was permitted to go out with it even on Shabbat, and if so it was also then permitted to wave it for a sick person. Therefore, although afterward the Sages enacted that it not be taken on Shabbat, they did not forbid waving it for a sick person, and it remains as it was originally. The above-mentioned rabbi himself concluded, "and one may resolve it"; perhaps his intent was as above, and it is clear.
