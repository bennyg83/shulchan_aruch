19) [Seif 3] A utensil whose designated use is forbidden. This means that it is designated for an activity forbidden on Shabbos. Magen Avraham, paragraph 5. Even if the activity has not yet been performed with it, but it is new and was prepared and stands ready for that purpose, such as a new mortar, it is nevertheless classified by its sole intended forbidden use, although mere designation ordinarily has no effect. A use forbidden even rabbinically suffices, as demonstrated by a shofar. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 5. See there further regarding a nutcracker called knak-holtz, a receptacle with a wooden piece attached by a screw into which nuts are placed for cracking. He discusses it at length and ultimately inclines toward leniency; see there.
20) There: It is permitted to move it. An object that is not a utensil, such as stones, money, reeds, beams, and similar items, may not be moved even when needed for a permitted use or its place. Beis Yosef; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 8.
21) There: It is permitted to move it. Even a utensil that is muktzeh because it is repulsive may be moved when needed for a permitted use or its place. Responsa Rashba, siman 456; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur. See below, seifim 34 and 35, and above, siman 279, seif 6.
22) A utensil whose designated uses include both forbidden and permitted activities may be moved to protect it from sun or shade. Shiltei Gibborim, chapter Kol HaKelim; Knesses HaGedolah there; Olas Shabbos, paragraph 6. If it is fit for a permitted activity but is not designated for it, it is called a utensil whose designated use is forbidden. Olas Shabbos there. When another utensil is available, one should not move a utensil whose designated use is forbidden even when needed for a permitted use or its place. So write the later authorities.
23) There, gloss: If it was used for a forbidden purpose during twilight, etc. This means that a forbidden item rested upon it, making it a base for a forbidden item. If one used a hammer to perform forbidden labor during twilight, it is obvious that the hammer may nevertheless be moved on Shabbos when needed for a permitted use or its place. Magen Avraham, paragraph 6; Eshel Avraham.
24) There, gloss: See above, siman 279. And our words there, with the help of Heaven.
25) There: Such as a goldsmith's or blacksmith's hammer. The ruling proceeds from the greater novelty to the lesser. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 7. Tur states them in the reverse order, and this is also Rosh's wording.
26) There: And place it wherever, etc. Since it is already in his hand, he may move it farther. It appears to me that even if he forgot and picked it up, he may continue moving it. Magen Avraham, paragraph 7. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 11, however, writes that Magen Avraham's ruling is not compelling; see there. See Even HaOzer, siman 266, who writes that this applies specifically to a utensil whose designated use is forbidden. Since it retains the status of a utensil and may at least be moved when needed for its place, once it comes into his hand he may move it wherever he wishes. Complete muktzeh, however, must be put down immediately when Shabbos arrives, even if it came into his hand before Shabbos and he forgot; see there. Shaarei Teshuvah, paragraph 6, cites this. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 13, similarly writes that some forbid this regarding complete muktzeh and that one should account for their view except where loss would result. These are his words.
27) It appears obvious that although one may hold muktzeh in his hand as long as he wishes, he may not transfer it from one hand to the other, because this is a new act of moving. Tosefes Shabbos in the introduction to the siman.
28) There: Wherever he wishes. This implies even from one room to another. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 7, writes the same in the name of Tosafos and Rosh. In the name of Rashi and Ran, however, he writes that it apparently applies only within the same room; see there. Shabbos, chapter Mefanin, states that if a person has muktzeh grain, he may make a path through it with his feet when entering and leaving, because this is unusual movement for a permitted need, namely his passage. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 11, writes that according to Rosh, who maintains that moving an item with one's body is more lenient than other indirect movement and is permitted even for the muktzeh item's benefit, the Gemara mentions entering and leaving only as the ordinary case, because a person normally does so for passage. The same act is permitted for the muktzeh item's benefit; see there. See below, siman 311, seif 8. Accordingly, if a muktzeh article lies on the ground, one may push it with his feet to prevent a loss. Mishnah Berurah, paragraph 13.
29) There: But to protect it from sun or shade, etc. If he has in mind to use it, he may move it even when his principal intent is to protect it from sun or shade. Magen Avraham, paragraph 8, in the name of Yerushalmi, Beitzah, chapter 5; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 12.
30) There, gloss: But merely touching it, etc. Rema follows his own view at the beginning of siman 513 that touching muktzeh is not forbidden even when done for the muktzeh item's benefit. According to the Shulchan Aruch in siman 310, seif 6, however, it is permitted only when touching serves the permitted item's benefit. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 13.
31) There, gloss: A synagogue menorah, etc. If the menorah is suspended, according to Rema at the beginning of siman 513 it may not be touched, lest the contact cause it to sway and thereby move it. According to Beis Yosef in siman 310, it may possibly be permitted because we do not consider this concern. If a permitted object rests upon it, one may certainly remove that object, because even if the menorah sways slightly, this is indirect movement for a permitted item's benefit. If an oil lamp is burning upon it, however, it is forbidden, lest contact cause it to sway and tilt, as stated in siman 265; see there. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 14.
32) There, gloss: It is likewise permitted to take, etc. This applies although it will sway slightly, as stated immediately above. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 15.
33) There, gloss: By blowing, etc. This resembles moving through one's body, as implied by Maharil cited by Beis Yosef at the end of this siman; see there. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 16. See below, paragraph 253.
34) There, gloss: By blowing, etc. It appears that according to all opinions, blowing a feather with one's breath does not involve muktzeh. This is more indirect than unusual movement and is not considered movement at all. Orach Chaim, paragraph 3, in the name of Eshel Avraham from a sacred manuscript.
