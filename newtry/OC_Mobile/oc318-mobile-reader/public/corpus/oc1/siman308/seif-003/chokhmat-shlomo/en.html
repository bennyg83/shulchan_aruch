Seif 3: And he is permitted to take it from there and place it in whatever place he wishes. Note: the reason is explained in Magen Avraham that for any muktzeh matter, as long as it is in his hand he is permitted to carry it further; and see Chiddushei Levi Orach Chayim siman 391, where I wanted to say that it is permitted to give it also to a fellow, and the fellow may carry it to whatever place he wants, since he is permitted to move it, he is also permitted to give it to a second person. And there appears to be proof for this from the Yerushalmi at the end of chapter 3 of Sukkah that I brought there, which says there: Such was the practice of the people of Jerusalem, etc. — he goes to the synagogue and his lulav is in his hand, etc., to lift his hands and read in the Torah; he gave it to a fellow, he placed it on the ground — it is forbidden to move it; thus it is explicitly stated that if he places it on the ground it is forbidden, and necessarily while it is still in his hand it is permitted to move it; and it is expressly stated that while he carries his hands and goes up to the Torah it is permitted to give it also to a fellow, even though if he were to place it on the ground it would be forbidden to move it. If so, the same applies to all muktzeh, and so it is. Also, we wrote there that this is only if he took it in a permitted way or accidentally; but if he deliberately took it improperly, then it is forbidden to move it further if he regretted — he must only remove it from his hand; see there, and so it is, and correct in my opinion; examine. Again, afterward I saw in Sefer Tosefet Shabbat siman 266 that it is permitted to give it to a fellow; and in my opinion it appears to decide as I wrote. Also, one can say that Tosefet is not dealing except when he moved it deliberately and not accidentally and in a permitted way; and so it is; examine.

There, seif 23: It is permitted to cut a branch from a palm tree on a weekday, etc. Note: Magen Avraham wrote that this is since he designated it for that; and Knesset HaGedolah challenged in Shirrei Korban chapter 3 of Sukkah from what the Rabbis say there that a willow branch is permitted to wave with for a sick person on Shabbat — it is proven that even without designating it, it is permitted; he left it requiring further study. In my humble opinion, there is room for distinction: regarding a branch, the matter depends on the person's thought; therefore, if he did not designate it for that, it is muktzeh. But regarding a lulav — before Chazal forbade going out with it on Shabbat, it was permitted to go out with it even on Shabbat; if so, then it was also permitted to wave with it for a sick person; therefore, when they enacted not to take it on Shabbat, nevertheless to wave with it for a sick person they did not forbid it, and it remained as at first. The rabbi mentioned above himself concluded "and it is possible to resolve," and it is possible that his intent is as I said; examine.
