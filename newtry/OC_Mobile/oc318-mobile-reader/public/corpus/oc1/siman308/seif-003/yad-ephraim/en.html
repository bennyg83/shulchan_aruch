There: And there is to resolve that this is the meaning, etc.; and they are concerned lest he go out on Rosh Hashana — as it should be; and it is possible to explain also what he wrote above, the proof from Tosafot, Shabbat, according to what he wrote above, the proof from what Tosafot wrote that they permit melacha forbidden — namely, tefillin; on this he wrote to resolve the words of Tosafot in Menachot, and also Shabbat: the reason it is forbidden is lest he go out on Rosh Hashana; and nevertheless it is properly called melacha forbidden, even though from the aspect of leaving tefillin on Shabbat there is no prohibition if he wants to leave them; nevertheless, since it is forbidden lest he go out on Rosh Hashana, it is called melacha forbidden. Except that it requires study, since Tosafot in Shabbat are not dealing except regarding Yom Tov — there is no hotzaah on Yom Tov, only not for any need at all; and with tefillin, if he wants to leave them, it is somewhat a need. See Beit Yosef siman 316 in the name of Baal HaHashlamah, who wrote that he wants to learn Torah in them, or wants to leave them to guard his soul, and not in the status of obligation, end of his words. And in Shabbat 61 we say that if he goes out, he is not liable for a chatat, even according to the one who says it is not the time of tefillin, because it is done in the manner of a garment; thus on Yom Tov it is possible that l'chatchila it is permitted if from this reason — lest he go out on Rosh Hashana with them — the matter comes. Therefore, one must explain the words of Magen Avraham: nevertheless it is like a utensil, etc. — plainly he states the law, that even for this reason — lest he go out on Rosh Hashana — it is forbidden to leave them on Rosh Hashana, and it is like a utensil whose melacha is forbidden. Again I saw that one can say his intent: Bach deduces that in the house it is permitted; therefore he holds that tefillin are melacha permitted; and on this he wrote that Tosafot in Menachot wrote that even in the house it is forbidden, and there is to resolve their reason — lest he go out on Rosh Hashana; and difficult is Bach's deduction there, regarding liability for chatat, where he says that even if he goes out with them on Rosh Hashana he is not liable for chatat, because it is derech garment; but nevertheless, also in the house there is a prohibition, since it is not the time of tefillin — no need for touching, and lest he go out; and nevertheless it is called melacha forbidden, unlike Bach. And what he wrote, "so it is implied from Tosafot 46" — meaning there, Ula said: it is clear that utensils that it is forbidden to adorn oneself with, lest one go out on Rosh Hashana with them, are melacha forbidden, and it is forbidden to move them except for its body or place; see there.
