Fit for raw eating. Tur wrote raw meat even unsalted entirely, and "tafuach" meaning spoiled — permitted to move since fit for dogs; Beit Yosef wrote fit for raw eating as here. One must say this reason applies only to tafuach; but unsalted meat is not for dogs, and although we rule siman 324 that what is prepared for a person is prepared for dogs — that is if he wants to cut it actually for dogs; here he does not want it except for a person, we do not consider it for dogs. Tosafot wrote the reason: unsalted meat for R' Yehuda is animal food, and that is because Tosafot afterward wrote all Israel are princes and we do not rule so, as explained in Beit Yosef siman 308 above. Difficult: why permitted? And further difficult on Beit Yosef and Shulchan Aruch who mentioned the reason of meat fit for raw eating that is human food — Gemara: R' Chiya holds raw meat forbidden to move; we challenge from bar Oza who permitted from heat to shade; answered bar Oza is different, fit for raw eating — implies unsalted not fit for raw eating. Maharach wrote on Beit Yosef and Shulchan Aruch their words are imprecise and escaped him Rambam ch. 26 who wrote thus: raw meat whether unsalted or salted may be moved because fit for a person — end quote, certainly raw eating. And it appears to answer for permission of muktzeh, being fit alone does not suffice but we require he holds it for that; but for prohibition of moving what is not fit for him, being fit suffices, as said in ch. Kirah: no muktzeh for R' Shimon in a fit thing. Therefore for R' Chiya we say so in the Gemara — he holds like R' Yehuda and requires holding for that purpose for muktzeh permission, not fit alone; therefore he distinguishes bar Oza from other meat though other raw meat is fit for eating in many places and siman 319 in Tur; rather certainly people do not hold it so much for that as bar Oza, therefore other meat is muktzeh and forbidden for R' Yehuda; but for R' Shimon whom we rule with, since anyway fit for a cup, it is fit and no muktzeh prohibition — therefore Rambam properly wrote fit for a person; afterward Beit Yosef and Shulchan Aruch followed Rambam. If so what they forbade with bland fish in the baraita as here afterward is because not fit even for dogs as Tosafot to the first answer; but Rashi explained bland fish not fit at all, and for dogs he does not intend to throw — end quote; and in the Gemara we say stam like R' Shimon; but it appears from Tosafot they do not read so in the Gemara, for they wrote in second version the baraita comes for R' Yehuda; if so Rashi also appears to hold so and explained the reason thus as Tosafot second version; if so for R' Shimon fit alone suffices, and therefore unsalted meat permitted, all settled, with God's help.
