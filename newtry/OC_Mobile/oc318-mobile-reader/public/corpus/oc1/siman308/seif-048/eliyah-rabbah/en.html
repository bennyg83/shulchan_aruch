Even on a weekday, etc. This requires study, for the Rosh wrote in the name of Tosafot that thought for weekday use forbids until he thinks of it for Shabbos, although the Rosh disagrees with them by his own reasoning. If so, it would be proper for us to rule like Tosafot. I also saw that Rabbeinu Yerucham, nesiv 12, ruled like Tosafot, even though he was a student of the Rosh and generally followed him. This is also clear from the responsum of the Rashba, siman 757, and in the Ran in the chapter Bameh Tomnin regarding the responsum of the Rashba. See there and examine carefully. Furthermore, the Ran wrote in the name of the Ramban that thought does not help entirely, for when Rav Asi said that if one sat on it, even though he did not think about it, it is permitted, specifically sitting is required. Although the Ran wrote that the Ramban’s words did not appear correct to him, nevertheless why should we be lenient even with thought for weekday use? Furthermore, Shiltei Giborim in the chapter Bameh Tomnin brought the words of the Ramban. Even more difficult to me is that in the responsa of the Rosh, klal 22, it is clear that he ruled like the Ramban, that thought does not help, and this contradicts what he wrote in his rulings.
