2) The general categories of muktzeh articles that may not be moved are divided into six parts. First, a utensil set aside because of potential financial loss, meaning a utensil that one is particular not to damage, such as a shechitah knife, bris milah knife, or bookbinder's knife. Second, a utensil whose designated use is forbidden. Third, anything that is neither a utensil nor human or animal food, such as stones, money, reeds, wood, beams, earth, sand, a corpse, living creatures, and figs or raisins placed where they dry, and similar unusable items. This is muktzeh because of its inherent nature. Fourth, a utensil whose designated use is permitted upon which an inherently muktzeh item rests, such as a barrel with a stone upon it or a cushion with money upon it. Even if the muktzeh item is later removed, since it rested upon the utensil during twilight, the utensil was set aside during twilight and remains set aside throughout the day. Fifth, something that was attached to the ground or needed to be trapped during twilight. Sixth, something set aside because of its mitzvah, such as sukkah wood and decorations. Beis Yosef; Levush Serad. Some of these categories nevertheless have grounds for permission, as each will be explained in its place.
