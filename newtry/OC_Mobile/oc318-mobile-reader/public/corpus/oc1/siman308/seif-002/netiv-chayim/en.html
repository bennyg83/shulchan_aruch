(S.k. 60) And therefore, in our wood. Note: requires study — he attributed the view of the Maharam Meiri to the view of the Shulchan Aruch, and this is impossible to say; for in siman 311, seif 8, he wrote that stam straw on a bed is muktzeh for kindling; nevertheless, one sways it with his body, and if he placed a pillow or cushion that sways it, even by hand it helps — he sits; and all the more so thought, even stam muktzeh for kindling. On the Maharam Meiri there is room for distinction, that he wrote thus in understanding the Rambam, and in his book this was not mentioned — that stam straw is muktzeh for kindling; and one can say that it stands for kindling and for lying down, and as thus they were not forbidden except from his thought that they cut them for kindling, and therefore the thought of lying down removes it from the thought of kindling, like branches. But the Shulchan Aruch, who copied the language of the Tur — it is impossible to say thus. Therefore it appears that the Tur holds that in this layer of stones, thought alone is not sufficient, like placing a stone on the mouth of a barrel, since in the chapter Notel the gemara compares these to one another. And if you ask: what is the difference between branches and this layer? One can say, as Tosafot, chapter Kol HaKelim, s.v. ve'azdi, leaned their reasons — a layer is more fit for sitting than stones; see there. To this intent the Shulchan Aruch here intended; examine.
