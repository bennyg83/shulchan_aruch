However, even if before Shabbos he stated that he does not wish to keep it as a brit milah knife but only as an ordinary knife, one can say that this does not help. In siman 310, seif 7, in Rama's gloss regarding a money purse, designation is not removed unless he performs an act, such as opening it at the bottom and thereby removing it from its special designation. There the purse is muktzeh machmat chisaron kis, and it appears that one cannot cancel its designation before Shabbos merely by speech, but only through an act. As for what Magen Avraham writes in siman 308, seif katan 40, regarding palm branches, that since they stand for firewood just as they can stand for sitting, the thought of using them for sitting can remove the thought that had designated them for burning: one must say that a money purse is different. Since he designated it for money and actually placed money into it, there was an act, and a mere thought cannot undo that act. Palm branches are different because scraping or preparing them is not considered an act proving designation for burning, since that preparation may also be needed for sitting. Accordingly, regarding a brit milah knife that was actually used for brit milah, and likewise a slaughtering knife that was actually used for slaughter, one can say that verbal declaration alone does not cancel the special designation unless there is an act. All this requires further study for practical halacha.
