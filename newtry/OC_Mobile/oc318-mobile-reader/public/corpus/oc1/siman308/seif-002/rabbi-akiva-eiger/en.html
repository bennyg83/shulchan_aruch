However, even if on erev Shabbat he said that he does not want to keep it as a circumcision knife but as other knives, one can say that it does not help; for in siman 310, seif 7, in the gloss regarding a purse of money — not unless he did an action with it, that he opened it from below and removed it from the status of being set aside. And there, with a purse, it is muktzeh of monetary loss; and it is implied that speech alone on erev Shabbat does not help, only an action. What Magen Avraham wrote, siman 308 s.k. 60, regarding branches — since they stand for wood as for sitting as for wood, only he nullified his thought in fencing them for wood; thought of sitting removes it from thought of burning. It must be said that with a purse, since he designated it for money and left money in it, it is an action, and thought does not nullify it. Branches are different, for scraping is not called an action — for sitting also needs scraping, and the action is not proven upon it for burning. Thus with a circumcision knife that he circumcised with, and a slaughter knife that he slaughtered with — one can say that speech does not nullify its set-aside status unless by action. All this requires study for practical law.
