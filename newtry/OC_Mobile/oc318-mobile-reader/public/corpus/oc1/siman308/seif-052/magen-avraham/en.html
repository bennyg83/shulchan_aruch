One does not move. For it was already designated by the wealthy owner's intent and does not require being muktzeh for everyone; and we say: because the loaf forbade itself by vow it became muktzeh (Ran ch. 3). Shulchan Aruch wrote on this: Tosafot daf 46 wrote: specifically for the woman it is forbidden, but for others permitted; and so it appears to me — in many places we find muktzeh for one person is not muktzeh for everyone, etc.; and in Tosafot ch. Mefakin (Shabbat daf 127) they wrote: where the thing is fit for everyone but its prohibition rests upon it, such as terumah in an Israelite's hand — permitted for all to move; but something muktzeh because of degradation, such as rags with three in them that are muktzeh for the wealthy — nevertheless permitted for the poor to move, and even for wealthy people in a poor person's house — end of his words; requires study, for Tosafot's intent is also: if it belongs to a wealthy person, even the poor may not move it, since it is muktzeh because of degradation — everything follows the owner's intent; only here they disagree with the others and hold muktzeh because of prohibition resting on it is permitted for all; and also on daf 46 Tosafot wrote: where she vowed benefit from a loaf, even she may move it since it is fit for others, unless she forbade it to the whole world — and so below siman 325 seif 68; see siman 310 seif 62; Shulchan Aruch's words require study.
