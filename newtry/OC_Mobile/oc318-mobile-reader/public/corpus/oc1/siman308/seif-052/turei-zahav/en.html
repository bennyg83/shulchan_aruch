And even paupers, etc. To explain this we bring the passage in ch. Kirah: R' Yosi said R' Romanus told me R' permitted taking a coal pan with its ashes in which there were coals — explained: incense remains from the fire, fit to smell, and through incense he moves the pan with ashes. Abaye: are coals in R' Yehuda's house considered significant? He was rich! You say fit for paupers? We learned: garments of paupers for paupers, of the wealthy for the wealthy, but of paupers for the wealthy — not a garment in their regard; here too coals are not significant in R' Yehuda's regard. Ran wrote: from this we learn muktzeh for the wealthy is muktzeh even for paupers, for already designated in the wealthy owner's mind, and we do not require designation for all; it implies for all paupers in the world it is muktzeh, for we follow initially who was owner — he said even for paupers, not paupers in the house. In ch. Mefinin (Shabbos 127) Israel may move terumah since fit for a kohen — Tosafot: although in Kirah we say garments of paupers for the wealthy not — here all agree it is fit, only the prohibition is tithe upon it. That which we say in ch. Kol HaKelim thin sheets without three by three handbreadths may be moved — for paupers, or even for wealthy in a pauper's house — end quote. This depends on the house: wealthy outside — muktzeh for them, for not fit for them; but this wealthy one dwelling in a pauper's house has something fit since fit in his house; unlike if designated for the wealthy — as thrown to the ash heap, seif 7 certainly we do not say if another takes from the ash heap it is fit for him; therefore here muktzeh for all paupers even outside the wealthy man's house. One must say so: if you say for other paupers outside permitted, what did the Gemara challenge on R' Romanus whom R' permitted — perhaps R' Romanus was outside R' Yehuda's house, what do we care about R' Yehuda's wealth? Rather since the pan was in R' Yehuda's house it was forbidden to all even outside; so Ran inferred muktzeh for wealthy is muktzeh for paupers; Shulchan Aruch wrote stam muktzeh for paupers — all paupers. The rule: what is fit for paupers and is in a pauper's house is muktzeh for the wealthy since not fit for them; but whoever is in a pauper's house follows the house even for a wealthy man; but muktzeh for the wealthy in his house is forbidden to all paupers as already thrown to the ash heap — so appears clear to me; Maharach wrote from those Tosafot that muktzeh for wealthy in his house is permitted for paupers outside — in my humble opinion what I wrote is plain:
