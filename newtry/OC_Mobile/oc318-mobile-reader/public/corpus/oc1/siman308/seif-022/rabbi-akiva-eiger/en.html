Seif 11: One takes the point. The view of the Rashba in Chullin is that specifically if it was taken intentionally. But on Shabbat one should permit, since they make something similar to its melacha, and they were prepared incidentally via their "fathers"; he compares it to mat remnants in the seif that follows. In my opinion, since he ruled in mat remnants that if he threw them to the ash heap intentionally — it is implied that if he threw them to the ash heap on Shabbat, it is permitted — behold, it is better than a needle. (See the Rashba, who truly holds that throwing them to the ash heap on Shabbat is forbidden — truly like a needle.) We distinguish: a needle is worse, for it does not stand at all for its melacha; thus one cannot learn one from the other at all; requires study.
Seif 22: But in anything whose manner is such. One who designates a part of paper to wipe with — if it is good paper that stands for writing, he must designate it for that forever, and it helps for all, since it is not a matter of action; but with coarse paper, designation for one Shabbat suffices — Magen Avraham below, siman 312 s.k. 2, and Tosafot Shabbat there, etc.
