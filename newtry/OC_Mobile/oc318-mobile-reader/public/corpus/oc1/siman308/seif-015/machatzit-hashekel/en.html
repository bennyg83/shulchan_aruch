To cover with it, and according to the view that nolad is forbidden, it must be fit for its original use. In Eliyah Rabbah and Tosefes Shabbos they challenged him, for the Mishnah in Shabbos 124b teaches that all utensils that may be moved on Shabbos may also have their broken pieces moved, provided that they can perform some sort of work, such as pieces of a kneading trough to cover something. Rabbi Yehudah says, provided that they can perform a kind of their original work, such as pieces of a kneading trough to pour porridge into. Rav Yehudah said in the name of Shmuel that they disagree when the vessel broke on Shabbos, for the first tanna holds it was prepared, and Rabbi Yehudah holds it is nolad. But if they broke before Shabbos, all agree that it is enough that they can perform some sort of work, since they were already prepared for another use during the week. Most Rishonim ruled like the first tanna. It appears from the Beis Yosef that their dispute is whether this is nolad or not; for the first tanna it is prepared, and for Rabbi Yehudah it is nolad, and so the wording of the Gemara implies. Since we rule like the first tanna, it is not nolad. How then did the Magen Avraham write that according to the view that nolad is forbidden, they must perform a kind of their original work? The Bach and Taz also understand that according to the first tanna it is not nolad. In my humble opinion, the Magen Avraham holds that although the Beis Yosef understood this way, in the Shulchan Aruch he retracted, and this also seemed logical to the Magen Avraham. The reason of the first tanna, who is Rabbi Shimon, is that he does not accept nolad. When the Gemara says that he holds it is prepared, it means that he holds nolad is permitted, and therefore it is considered prepared and standing, because Rabbi Shimon does not accept muktzeh and holds that a person’s mind is on whatever is fit for him. With this, the words of Tosafot cited by the Magen Avraham in s.k. 17 are also well understood.
And the same applies if a non-Jew makes a vessel, etc. Although we rule that something belonging to a non-Jew does not require preparation, nevertheless nolad is forbidden even when it belongs to a non-Jew, as the Magen Avraham wrote in siman 506, s.k. 3, unlike the Levush there.
