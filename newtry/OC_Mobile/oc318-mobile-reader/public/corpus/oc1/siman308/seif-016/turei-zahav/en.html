Forbidden to move it. The reason in the Gemara: lest he drive in — Rashi: when he attaches a leg he drives it in firmly; from this reason Terumat HaDeshen ruled regarding a bench whose leg slipped [forbidden] lest he drive it in. It appears from his words there is room to be lenient with a bench since fit to lean on another bench, only he wrote to be stringent. And it appears to me specifically when slipped he forbids this, for possible to attach and the prohibition is only if he drives in, therefore they forbade even without driving lest he drive; but if a leg broke there is no lest he drive, only need to make another leg, therefore no concern; and Rama's "even if broken from beforehand" is not precise but means slipped. And further it appears: if the leg already slipped and that leg is gone entirely and the bench stands so without a leg, no prohibition to lean it on another bench, for here lest he drive does not apply, as appears to me in this rabbinic stringency, and to lean toward leniency where there is no lest he drive concern, especially where need at a Shabbat feast as Beit Yosef shortly regarding mats:
