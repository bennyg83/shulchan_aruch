This is difficult to me, for from the words of Shiltei Shabbos below in siman 310, seif 18, it is clear that moving with one’s body is permitted even for the need of something prohibited, and the Rama there did not disagree. If so, here too we should say that it is not considered moving at all, and even for a prohibited need it should be permitted. In the source of the law, in Responsa Mahari Weil, siman 222, he compares it to the case of straw on a bed, which one moves with his body.
Seif 5. An object whose primary use is for prohibited labor. The same would appear to apply to an object that is muktzeh because of monetary loss, that it is permitted even though it has no permissibility on its own for the need of its body or place. The source of this law is the case of a mortar that contains garlic. At the beginning of the chapter Kol HaKeilim, the Gemara wanted to prove from that baraisa that an object whose primary use is for prohibited labor is forbidden even for the need of its body or place. Thus, even though it has no permissibility at all, nevertheless it may be moved incidentally to the garlic. According to this, the same applies to a lamp that was lit on Shabbos; it may be moved by means of a loaf or child. So the Baal HaMaor brought in the name of one sage.
