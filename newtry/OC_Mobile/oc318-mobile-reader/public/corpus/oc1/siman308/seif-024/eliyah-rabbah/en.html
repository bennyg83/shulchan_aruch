The Magen Avraham wrote that this requires study, for the Ran in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah prohibited only going out with them, because it is not evident that he designated them for that use and it appears like carrying on Shabbos; but regarding moving them, all agree that thought suffices, as with palm branches in seif 20. However, he overlooked the responsum of the Rashba, siman 756, who wrote that to move a stone to crack nuts one must use it before Shabbos, as with tufts of wool and wool combings, so that there be a recognizable act. Thus it is explicit that even regarding moving, an act is required. This is also clear in the Ran in the chapter Bameh Tomnin, as he wrote regarding the Rashba, and the Beis Yosef brought it briefly. See there. The Ran in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah refers to the Gemara, which is discussing going out with them, and therefore he also mentioned the law of carrying, and this is clear.
[50] It is prohibited to sit, etc. Magen Avraham writes that Olat Shabbat misunderstood siman 307, where only tilting and shaking are permitted. His words are surprising, because Beit Yosef makes clear that according to the view requiring that stones be arranged, even tilting and shaking are prohibited, as Shiltei Gibborim, p. 125, explains according to Beit Yosef here and in siman 309. In my humble opinion there is no difficulty in any event. Below the case concerns one who intends to remove the item on Shabbos, which Sefer HaTerumah permits. Although Beit Yosef writes that the other authorities do not imply so, he nevertheless cites that opinion below as "some say." This is clear.
[51] [Levush] And some say their law is, etc. This is primary (Magen Avraham). Levush apparently omitted it because he saw Beit Yosef's objection to Maggid Mishneh: there must be a distinction between palm branches and stones, because Rav Asi himself, who says regarding palm branches that sitting on them is sufficient even without prior designation, says regarding building stones, "Go out and rub them smooth." Beit Yosef, however, apparently did not see Tosafot on 125b, who raises this question and answers that the text there reads Rav Ashi. See Maharsha. Tosafot's second answer accords with Beit Yosef, distinguishing palm branches from stones. Yet this creates some difficulty with the Gemara on 126b, which challenges a rule about a reed and utensil covers from palm branches. Perhaps those too differ, especially according to Maggid Mishneh's understanding of Rambam. It also appears to me that Rav Asi's instruction "rub them smooth" was because the stones were soiled with mud, and he required an act for that reason; mere thought or sitting on them would otherwise suffice. This resolves Tosafot's further question how Rav Asi follows his own position. Alternatively, Rav Asi may be stating the law according to Rabbi Yochanan there, and Rabbi Yochanan follows his own view at the end of chapter Kol HaKelim, disagreeing with Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel that thought alone suffices. Mordechai and Agudah in chapter Bameh Tomnin likewise imply that branches and stones share the same rule. Large stones that cannot readily be moved may be sat upon in any case, while small stones require an act of rubbing them smooth; otherwise one may not sit upon them because he will certainly come to arrange them on Shabbos. This removes Magen Avraham's difficulties.
