35) [Seif 4] A utensil whose designated use is permitted, etc. This means one designated for a permitted activity, although it is also fit for a forbidden activity. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 9. The same applies when it is designated for both forbidden and permitted activities. Magen Avraham, paragraph 9, in the name of Shiltei Gibborim. We already wrote this above in paragraph 22; see there. If most of its use is forbidden, we follow the majority, based on the principle that all utensils follow their principal use. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 9. A pot designated for cooking and used only occasionally for water or fruit is a utensil whose designated use is forbidden, unless it contains a cooked dish. Eshel Avraham there in the name of Chiddushei Rashba. Nevertheless, it appears that even after its food was emptied, if it stands in the house, it may be moved and removed outside because it is like a chamber pot. See Shabbos 124 regarding a bowl. This is also implied by Meiri, with the help of Heaven.
36) A scribe's writing case is a utensil containing a receptacle for ink and a receptacle for paper and documents. Some wish to say that it is a utensil whose designated use is forbidden. Radbaz, however, writes in the new responsa, volume 2, siman 674, that this is not a utensil designated for forbidden use, but a utensil made both for permitted and forbidden uses. On this basis he ruled concerning a public gathering assembled to supervise communal affairs, where documents that they needed to read lay in a scribe's writing case, that they could move the case, bring it before them, and remove the documents; see there. Yad Aharon; Mishnah Berurah in Shaar HaTziyun, letter 1. See below, siman 310, seif 8, and examine it carefully.
37) There: It is permitted to move it, etc. For this reason, Responsa Beis Yehudah, siman 207, permits bringing in on Shabbos water drawn for kneading the matzos. Nevertheless, he concludes that it is preferable to drink a little of it on Shabbos or leave it in a place from which it will not need to be moved, because minimizing exertion is preferable; see there.
38) There: But not without a need, etc. Shelah, page 136, warned that even those who fear Heaven do not pay attention to this. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 9.
39) There: But not without a need, etc. It appears that they forbade this only when it will not be needed at all that day. If he will use it that day and carries it with him so it will be ready when needed, it is permitted. Taz, paragraph 2. Accordingly, after Minchah, when people do not ordinarily eat again, it would be forbidden, as stated in siman 323, seif 6, regarding washing utensils. If, however, he carries them with him so they will not be stolen, it is certainly permitted. Machatzis HaShekel, paragraph 9.
40) There: And sacred writings, etc. This means books that one is permitted to read, unlike the writings of non-Jews. Magen Avraham, paragraph 10; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 19; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 17.
41) Shiltei Gibborim writes in chapter 8 that blank paper is muktzeh because of potential financial loss, since it is intended for writing and one is particular about it. Magen Avraham there; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 8; Tosefes Shabbos there; Eshel Avraham, paragraph 10; Machatzis HaShekel, paragraph 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 6.
42) Matzah designated for the mitzvah on Erev Pesach that falls on Shabbos does not have the status of food. Eshel Avraham there. Beis Yosef likewise writes in siman 444 that mitzvah matzah may not be moved on Erev Pesach that falls on Shabbos because it is rabbinically forbidden to eat on Erev Pesach and is therefore muktzeh. Other matzah may be given to birds or to a non-Jew, whereas one does not wish to give this matzah away. Pri Megadim cites this here, paragraph 4, and writes that accordingly, those who send the guarded matzos prepared for others for the Seder night on this Shabbos act improperly, because they move muktzeh on Shabbos; see there. However, regarding his inference from Mordechai that even other matzos are muktzeh because they are not fit to be eaten that Shabbos, it appears that they are fit for young children who do not understand the account told at night, as stated in siman 471, seif 2, in the gloss; see there. Since they are fit for children, they are not muktzeh. Mitzvah matzah, however, is needed for the Seder night and one is reluctant to give it to a child, so it is muktzeh. Accordingly, it appears that if he has much more mitzvah matzah than he needs for the Seder night and is not reluctant to give some to a child, it is not muktzeh.
43) The same applies to animal food: it does not have the same status as human food. Eshel Avraham there. It appears that he means animal food does not share the rule of human food, which may be moved even without any need. It may, however, be moved when needed, as stated below in seifim 28 and 29; see there.
44) There: And sacred writings, etc. The same applies to cups, bowls, plates, a fig knife, a pot skimmer, and a small knife that is usually on the table. Shiltei Gibborim. Rambam's commentary to the Mishnah in the chapter Kol HaKelim, however, somewhat implies that cups and bowls are like sacred writings. Kol Bo, page 33, also implies this. Tosafos on page 123, however, implies like Shiltei Gibborim, and the matter requires examination. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 14. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraphs 16 and 17, rules like Rambam and Kol Bo, that cups, bowls, and a knife are not like sacred writings but like any utensil whose designated use is permitted. They may be moved only for their own use, but not entirely without need; see there. Chayei Adam, klal for Parashas Mikeitz, paragraph 1, also rules this way. He therefore writes that a person sitting at a table upon which there are cups, bowls, a knife, and a fork may not move any of these utensils about needlessly, for demons cause hands to become meddlesome; see there.
45) There: And foods, etc. Muktzeh foods, such as untithed produce, food attached to the ground, and similar items, may not be moved even when needed for a permitted use or their place, as the Maggid Mishneh writes in chapter 4 of Eruvin. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 20.
46) There, gloss: And tefillin may be moved only for a need. This means for any need, even when they are not needed for their own use or their place, like a utensil whose designated use is permitted, as Beis Yosef writes. Beis Yosef, however, cites a work called Mikdash that permits moving them only when needed for their own use or their place; see there. This also appears to be the view of Magen Avraham, paragraph 11; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 11; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 21; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 19; and Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 3. Chesed LaAlafim writes that one should initially take care to remove the tallis from its bag before Shabbos so it will not be together with the tefillin. If he forgot to remove it before Shabbos and, when removing the tallis on Shabbos, the tefillin came out with it, he should take them with the intent of sitting in their place and then sit there, for moving them when their place is needed is permitted, as stated above.
47) Mishbetzos Zahav writes in paragraph 3 that according to siman 301, seif 42, when tefillin are in an unprotected place one brings them in pair by pair. It follows that there is at least no Torah prohibition in wearing tefillin on Shabbos, and the Sages permitted it because of the disgrace to the tefillin. The same applies here: the possibility that they will be stolen is like a disgrace, so rabbinic moving is permitted. This is the substance of his words. Shulchan Aruch HaRav there likewise implies that when non-Jewish thieves might take them, creating a disgrace, it is permitted to put them away so they will not be stolen.
48) Whether one may move Megillas Esther on Shabbos: Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef here, paragraph 4, and in siman 688, Hagahos Beis Yosef, paragraph 2, is uncertain; see there. Eliyah Rabbah here, paragraph 10, writes simply that it is permitted, for it is like sacred writings. Pri Chadash, siman 688, paragraph 6, forbids it. Mateh Yehudah, however, writes concerning those words of Pri Chadash: It appears that according to his reasoning, this prohibition applies only on the Shabbos on which the fifteenth falls, when there is an obligation to read. On other Shabbosos of the year, when there is no obligation, Rabbah's decree does not apply. Why should reading it be forbidden, and why should it not have the same law as reading from the Torah, since it was given to be written? Since reading it is not forbidden, moving it is certainly not forbidden. It is unreasonable that because reading is forbidden on one Shabbos, it should be forbidden on every Shabbos. He later saw that Knesses HaGedolah inclined to permit it, and the widespread custom likewise appears lenient; see there. Shaliach Tzibbur cites him in Shaar HaChag, page 326a. Shaarei Teshuvah, siman 693, paragraph 2, writes that bringing it to the synagogue before nightfall should be prevented because of preparing on Shabbos for the weekday, as explained in siman 668. One should be stringent not to bring it through a minor, but wait until nightfall, or bring it discreetly to the synagogue before day ends and study it there a little by way of learning. It then does not appear so much like preparation; see there.
49) Regarding whether a get may be moved, see Even HaEzer at the end of siman 136. Piskei Recanati, siman 95, and Shiltei Gibborim in the name of Semag permit moving a get because many laws may be learned from it. Maharshal, chapter 5, siman 6, forbids moving it. It appears that one may be lenient when there is a need, since the matter is rabbinic and the view of those who permit is reasonable. All this concerns a get that has not yet been given to the woman and is prepared to be given, so it is not fit to cover the opening of a bottle. If the woman has already received it, it is fit to cover a bottle opening and may be moved. Rashba writes the same in his Chiddushim to Gittin. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 13. See above, siman 307, paragraph 119, and examine it carefully.
50) There, gloss: And a shofar may not be moved, etc. The reason is explained in Hagahos Ashri: a shofar is a utensil whose designated use is forbidden. Or Zarua writes that specifically a shofar, which is a utensil, may be moved when needed for its own use or its place. Trumpets, however, are not utensils and may not be moved even when needed for their own use or their place. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 13. It appears to me that this follows Rabbi Yehudah, but according to our ruling like Rabbi Shimon it is permitted. Magen Avraham, paragraph 12; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 11; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 22; Eshel Avraham, paragraph 12; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 20. The same applies to other musical instruments: they may be moved only when needed for their own use or their place, like any utensil whose designated use is forbidden, since blowing or playing a musical instrument on Shabbos is forbidden, as stated in simanim 588 and 339. Shulchan Aruch HaRav there. See also the end of siman 588 and our words there, with the help of Heaven.
51) There, gloss: Only when needed for its own use, etc. The same should apply to a lulav, allowing it to be moved when needed for its own use or its place. Taz, siman 658, paragraph 3; Eliyah Rabbah there, paragraph 1. Tosefes Shabbos here, paragraph 22, however, questions Taz: How could he write this simply without considering Rema there, who writes that it is like a stone, based on Ran and the Maggid Mishneh? This implies that it may not be moved even when needed for its own use, like a stone. The reason appears simple: it is like muktzeh because of potential financial loss, since people are presumably particular not to use it for anything lest that use disqualify it. It therefore cannot be compared to a shofar. These are his words. Chemed Moshe there, paragraph 2, likewise writes that Taz was imprecise, and Machatzis HaShekel there, paragraph 2, agrees; see there. Mishbetzos Zahav there, paragraph 3; Erekh HaShulchan there, paragraph 2; and Chayei Adam, klal 66, paragraph 13, likewise challenge him. The consensus of the later authorities is that a lulav may not be moved on Shabbos even when needed for its own use or its place, because it has no status of a utensil at all. Regarding an esrog, see siman 658, seif 2, in the gloss, and our words there, with the help of Heaven.
