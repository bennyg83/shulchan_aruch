And they practice to spread grasses. And if it falls on Sunday, they spread them at the entry of Shabbos (Maharil); but the custom is that they spread them on Yom Tov; so wrote the Magen Avraham; and they also practice to set up trees in the synagogue as a memorial that they are judged regarding the fruits of the tree. And the later authorities wrote in the name of the Shenei Luchos HaBeris: regarding what they practice to distribute grasses in the synagogue, they should not distribute until after the Shemoneh Esreh prayer, so that one can recite a blessing, for it is forbidden to interrupt from Baruch She'amar until after Shemoneh Esreh, end quote. And in truth they practice to distribute beforehand, and the reason is possibly that they rely on [the rule] that between the chapters we hold one may ask out of respect and respond to any person, and all the more so it is permitted to recite a blessing over fragrance between the chapters.
Everywhere to eat. And the Maharil wrote that it is forbidden to fast on Shavuot a dream-fast, like that of Mar the son of Ravina who would sit in a fast the whole year: that is for a dream-fast alone, except for Erev Yom Kippur and Purim and Shavuot, because "if not for this day"; and so wrote the Beis Yosef in siman 288; and so is the agreement of the Magen Avraham below in siman 604, s.k. 1; and not like the Ateres Shalom who is uncertain about this.
Food. And the Kol Bo wrote in siman 52, and these are his words: they also practiced to eat honey and milk on the festival of Shavuot because of the Torah, which is likened to honey and milk, as it is said: "Honey and milk under your tongue"; and they practiced in all Yisrael to put saffron in a cake, and the reason is because it gladdens the heart; they also practiced that the women make on Shavuot a long bread with four heads; and it appears to say that this custom is found as a memorial for the two breads offered on Atzeres, or because of the constellation of Twins that serves in Sivan, end quote.
Milk. See below in siman 505: that milk milked on Yom Tov by a non-Jew should be treated as forbidden, and one should not change; but on the second day of Yom Tov there is room to permit it; and so is the agreement of the Taz and the Magen Avraham below, not like the Maharshal who permitted; and so ruled in responsa Chut HaShani, siman 31, and in responsa Nachalas Shivah, question 36; and he concludes: nevertheless, if I were in a place where they practice permission, I would not protest against them.
And afterward a meat meal. In my sefer Maginei Eretz, klal 76, s.k. 5, I wrote in the name of the Kol Bo that there are who eat cheese at the meal of Atzeres even though there is no interruption of a measure of six hours; nevertheless on Shavuot they rely to fulfill the custom, and also he desires to eat in the morning, [for] the food has already been digested; but he should clean between his teeth. I also wrote there in the name of Zichron Moshe that there is a reason to permit on Shavuot to eat dairy food and meat in one meal; and nevertheless one who guards his soul should distance himself from them; and so is the agreement of the later authorities, that even on the festival of Shavuot one must be careful in everything as throughout the year; and see there in Yoreh De'ah, siman 89.
Two breads. One for a meat meal; and so too he needs to have two tablecloths; and in Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah he wrote, and these are his words: and the meticulous ones merit two tables.
