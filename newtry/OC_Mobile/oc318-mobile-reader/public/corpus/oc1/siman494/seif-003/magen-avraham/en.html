It is forbidden to fast. Because when Atzeres fell on Shabbos, the day of slaughtering the offerings was after Shabbos; and see what I wrote at the end of siman 429. Women are permitted in melacha on Isru Chag (Sheyarei Kenesses HaGedolah in the Hagahos Mordechai, chapter 2 of Shabbos).
From the beginning of Rosh Chodesh Sivan. For immediately on the second of Sivan Moshe said to them to sanctify them for the Torah, as is stated in chapter 8 of Shabbos. The Geman wrote at the end of [section] 15: three days before Shavuot they do not fast, for they are the days of hagbalah; which implies that on the second of Sivan it is permitted to fast; but the world practiced not to fast for the reason I wrote above.
To spread grasses. And if Shavuot falls on Sunday, one spreads them at the entry of Shabbos (Maharil), for he holds that since they are not fit for animal food it is forbidden to move them on Shabbos and Yom Tov; see siman 308, se'if 28. And it is possible to say that since he intended upon them from before nightfall, it is permitted to move them, as is written in siman 308, se'if 8; and so they practiced to spread them on Yom Tov. And it is possible that even the Maharil came only to exclude that one should not spread them on Shabbos when his intent is for the need of Yom Tov, for Shabbos does not prepare for Yom Tov. They practice to set up trees in the synagogue and in the houses; and it appears to me that the reason is so that they will remember that on Atzeres they are judged regarding the fruits of the tree, and they will pray for them.
Milk. There are many reasons; and he wrote the reason that is stated in the Zohar, that those seven weeks were for Yisrael seven clean days, like a woman purifying from her niddah; and it is known that blood curdles and becomes milk; and that is from judgment to mercy; and the custom of our fathers is Torah; but one must be careful not to come to a prohibition; and see in Yoreh De'ah, siman 89, that one need not interrupt with Birkas HaMazon if he is not eating hard cheese; and he should be careful to take another tablecloth.
Like two cooked dishes. That is to say: just as on Pesach they make a memorial for the offering, so too on Shavuot.
And they need to bring, etc. For it is forbidden to eat meat and milk from one bread; and therefore they practiced to bake it with butter, for then he will certainly need to bring another bread to eat with meat; and he should be careful to heat the oven well, as is written in siman 461; and he also needs a new peel, as is written in siman 451, se'if 19; and not like some women who flip it to its other side, for it is absorbed from one side to the other from the grease; see in Yoreh De'ah, siman 108, se'if 3.
A memorial for the two breads. And if so, they should be of wheat, like the two breads. He wrote in the Shenei Luchos HaBeris that they should not distribute fragrant grasses in the synagogue until after the Shemoneh Esreh prayer, so that they can recite a blessing over the fragrance; for it is forbidden to interrupt from Baruch She'amar until after Shemoneh Esreh.
