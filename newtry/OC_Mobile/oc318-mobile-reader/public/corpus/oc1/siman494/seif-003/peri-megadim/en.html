(ב) They delay etc. Taz in responsum Ma'aseh Binyamin at the end of the book, law 4 in Orach Chayyim, that they wait for kiddush until nightfall; and nevertheless it appears that for Ma'ariv too they delay as Taz wrote, for in mourning and niddah some say that if the congregation prayed Ma'ariv they already made it night etc.; if so, here there is a requirement for completeness of 49 days, and when he prays Ma'ariv and mentions in the prayer the day of Shavuot from the day before, behold one day is missing from 49. And see Mishnah Berurah there, in the name of his teacher Maharash the Second. And see Eliyah Rabbah in Magen Avraham 1 and Eliyah Rabbah 3; and we find to learn from Pesach and Sukkot on the first night, and on Shavuot on the first night in kiddush they wait until nightfall, for kiddush is only in the place of a meal and it is forbidden to eat before nightfall — see siman 429 in Shulchan Aruch, similar to matzah as it requires "in the evening you shall eat" — see siman 472 in Taz and Magen Avraham. And one can say that kiddush is only in the place of a meal, similar to counting in prayer, in Magen Avraham note 14, that in his house he counts and afterward makes kiddush. And kiddush on Shemini Atzeret see siman 568 in Taz to Maharshal that he waits until ninety-five, for otherwise he would need to bless "to sit in the sukkah," and Taz disagrees — see there and it will be explained. And to pray Ma'ariv he can from the day before on Pesach and Sukkot, not so on Shavuot — to Taz they delay Ma'ariv as well so there shall be completeness on the second night because of the safek of the day; such is an error, for one does not count on the first day.
