They delay, etc. See Taz: in responsa Mas'at Binyamin at the end of the sefer, law 4 in Orach Chaim, that they wait for Kiddush until night; and nevertheless it appears that for Ma'ariv too they wait, as the Taz, of blessed memory, wrote: for in mourning and niddah there are those who say that if the congregation prayed Ma'ariv they have already made it night, etc.; if so, here we require completeness of 49 days, and when he prays Ma'ariv and mentions in the prayer the day of Shavuot while it is still daytime, behold something is missing from the 49 days. And see responsa Mas'at Binyamin there in the name of his teacher Moreinu HaRav Rabbi Shmuel the Second, of blessed memory; see there. And see siman 271 in the Magen Avraham, 1, and see Eliyah Rabbah 3; see there. And we find that we learn from Pesach and Sukkos on the first night, and on Shavuot on the first night: for Kiddush they wait until nightfall, for there is no Kiddush except in the place of a meal, and it is forbidden to eat before nightfall; see siman 639 in the Shulchan Aruch; similar to matzah, which requires "in the evening you shall eat"; and see siman 472 in the Taz and Magen Avraham. And one can say: for there is no Kiddush except in the place of a meal, similar to the counting in [siman] 489 in the Magen Avraham, letter 14: that in his house he counts and afterward makes Kiddush. And Kiddush on Shemini Atzeres: see siman 668 in the Taz regarding the Maharshal, of blessed memory, that he waits until nightfall when the stars appear, for otherwise he would need to recite the blessing to sit in the sukkah; and the Taz disagrees; see there; and with God's help it will be explained there. And to pray Ma'ariv one may while it is still daytime on Pesach and Sukkos; not so on Shavuot: according to the Taz they delay Ma'ariv as well so that there shall be completeness on the second night because of the doubt of the day; such as this is an error, for one does not count on the first day.
