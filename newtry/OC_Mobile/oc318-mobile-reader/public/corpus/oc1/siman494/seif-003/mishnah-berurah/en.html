(6) On the night after: Because when Atzeres fell on Shabbos, the day of slaughtering the offerings was after Shabbos, and it is like a kind of Yom Tov [Magen Avraham]; and according to this reason, on Isru Chag after Sukkos it is permitted to fast; however, from the words of the Gra it is implied that there too one should not fast; and so it is implied in the Beis Yosef.
(7) And one does not say Tachanun: And they also do not fast on them.
(8) From the beginning of Rosh Chodesh Sivan: For immediately on the second of Sivan Moshe said to them to sanctify them for the Torah, as it is written: "If you will surely heed My voice and keep My covenant," etc.; and afterward the three days of hagbalah begin.
(9) On Shavuot: That is, whether one spreads them on Erev Yom Tov or on Yom Tov itself; and even if those grasses are not fit for animal food, since he intended upon them from before nightfall it is permitted to move them, as is written in siman 308, se'if 20; and especially if they are fragrant grasses. However, if Shavuot falls on Sunday, one should not spread them on Shabbos, since his intent is for the need of Yom Tov, and Shabbos does not prepare for Yom Tov; rather he should spread them on Yom Tov itself or on Erev Shabbos.
(10) A memorial for the joy of Matan Torah: For there were grasses around Mount Sinai, as it is written: "the flock and the cattle shall not graze," etc. The later authorities wrote: in a place where they practice to distribute fragrant grasses in the synagogue, they should not distribute from Baruch She'amar until after the Shemoneh Esreh prayer, so that one can recite a blessing over them, for in between it is forbidden to interrupt. They practice to set up trees in the synagogue and in the houses as a memorial that on Atzeres they are judged regarding the fruits of the tree [Magen Avraham]; and the Gra canceled this custom because now it is a statute of the nations to set up trees on their festival.
(11) In several places to eat: And whether it is permitted to fast a dream-fast on Shavuot, see below in siman 604 in the Magen Avraham and in what we wrote there in the Mishnah Berurah.
(12) Dairy foods: See Magen Avraham; and I further heard in the name of one great authority who said a proper reason for this: for at the time they stood at Mount Sinai and received the Torah [for in the Ten Commandments all parts of the Torah were revealed to them through this, as Rav Saadiah Gaon wrote that in the Ten Commandments the entire Torah is included], and they went down from the mountain to their homes, they did not find what to eat immediately except dairy foods; for meat requires much preparation: to slaughter with a checked knife as Hashem commanded, and to remove the cheilev strands and the blood, and to rinse and salt, and to cook in new vessels, for the vessels they had beforehand, in which they had cooked during that day-and-night period, became forbidden to them; therefore they chose for the moment dairy foods, and we make a memorial for this.
(13) On the first day, etc.: Also in some places they practice to eat honey and milk because of the Torah, which is likened to honey and milk, as it is written: "Honey and milk under your tongue," etc.
(14) Like the two cooked dishes, etc., so they eat, etc.: That is to say: just as on Pesach they make a memorial for the offering, so we must make on Shavuot a memorial for the two breads that they would bring; and therefore they eat dairy foods and afterward a meat meal, and they need to bring with them two breads, for it is forbidden to eat meat and milk from one bread; and in this there is a memorial for the two breads.
(15) Two breads, etc.: And therefore they practiced to bake one bread with butter, for then he will certainly need to bring another bread to eat with meat; and he should then be careful to heat the oven well between bread and bread; and he also needs a new peel; not like some women who flip it to the other side, for it is absorbed from one side to the other from the grease.
(16) On the table: And he should be careful to take another tablecloth when he wants to eat meat. And one need not interrupt with Birkas HaMazon if he is not eating hard cheese; rather he should wipe his mouth well and rinse, as is stated in Yoreh De'ah, siman 89.
(17) A memorial for the two breads: And if so, they should be of wheat, like the two breads. The Peri Megadim wrote: one must be careful regarding meat and dairy foods in all that one is careful throughout the year, as explained in Yoreh De'ah, simanim 88 and 89, and that their reward not go out in their loss. He further wrote: in the formula of the haftarah blessing, "over the Torah and over the Avodah," etc., "this day of the festival of Shavuot"; but they do not mention to say "mikra kodesh," etc. And they mention souls and say Av HaRachamim on the second day; and in every place where they read "All the firstborn" they mention souls, for it contains "the gift of the hand," and they vow tzedakah. When Shavuot falls on Friday-and-Shabbos they do not say Chapters [of Avos]; and when Shavuot falls on Motzaei Shabbos they say Chapters at Mincha on Shabbos.
