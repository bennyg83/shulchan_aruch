It is difficult for me: how do we say on Shavuot "the day of the giving of our Torah," when we hold like R' Yosi, who said that the Torah was given on the seventh of Sivan; for we hold that one must separate for six onos, as is written in Yoreh De'ah, siman 196 (and it seems to me that I saw this question in some posek); and for us, Shavuot is always on the sixth of Sivan. And in truth the Rambam, chapter 5 of Hilchos Avos HaTumah, ruled that one need separate only three onos; and the Kesef Mishneh wrote on the version of the Rambam and the Rashba that the Sages hold three onos, and he ruled like them; see there. And if so, our custom is according to the Rambam; and what is written in Yoreh De'ah like R' Yosi is for stringency. And regarding the further difficulty, that the Torah was given on the 51st day of the counting, for they left Egypt on Thursday and the Torah was given on Shabbos: the "Ten Sayings" already answered that it comes to hint to us the second day of Yom Tov of the Diaspora; and it is possible that this is what is meant by "Moshe added one day of his own accord," and this is not its place. On the night of Shavuot they do not make Kiddush of the cup until nightfall when the stars appear, for it is written "they shall be complete" (see Taz and Mas'at Binyamin). It is stated in the Zohar that the early chassidim would stay awake all night and occupy themselves in Torah; and already most of those who learn have practiced to do so; and it is possible to give a reason according to its plain sense, because Yisrael were sleeping all night and the Holy One, blessed be He, had to awaken them, as is stated in the Midrash; therefore we must correct this. And see siman 4, s.k. 13: that in the morning one should not recite the blessing on washing the hands, and also one should not recite a blessing over a tallis katan, as is written in siman 8. And regarding Birkas HaTorah I am in doubt: for in siman 47, se'if 12, he wrote that even if he learned at night, etc., he need not recite a blessing so long as he has not slept; which implies that if he stayed awake all night he need not recite a blessing on the following day; and so it is implied in the Gemara, chapter 4 of Sukkah: a sukkah, whose nights are not separated from its days, one need recite a blessing over the sukkah only once all seven [days]; and see end of siman 639. However, it is difficult: for we hold that sleep by day is not an interruption even if it is fixed sleep; if so, one can say that also at night sleep is not an interruption, only that from the outset our intention is to exempt only for one day and one night, and when he rises in the morning before dawn, it is then accounted as the following day. Know this: for according to Rabbeinu Tam one need not recite a blessing before dawn, for he is exempted by yesterday's blessing until daybreak; if so, for us too one can say that since they practice to recite Birkas HaTorah all day, therefore from the outset his intention was to exempt only for day and night, and therefore he must recite Birkas HaTorah when day breaks; and so it appears as the main [ruling]; but one who wishes to fulfill his obligation amid doubt should hear Birkas HaTorah from another and intend to fulfill his obligation. And see siman 235, that one must read Krias Shema after nightfall when the stars appear, since he does not read it upon his bed. And it is stated in the [Ari's] writings that if the night of immersion falls on Shavuot, he should have relations. Mas'at Binyamin wrote in siman 6: on Shavuot they read with the upper cantillation that is on the Ten Commandments, and on Shabbos Parashas Yisro they read with the lower cantillation, and then "You shall not murder" is the end of a verse and one reads the tzadi with a kamatz; and the same applies to other ends of verses or an esnachta; and when they read with the upper cantillation, then one reads "You shall not murder" with the tzadi with a pattach. And in the Chizkuni on Parashas Yisro he wrote that on Atzeres, which resembles Matan Torah, and they translate the dibros, they read each dibbur of "You shall have no" and the dibbur of "Remember" with large cantillations to make each one a verse, and the dibros of "You shall not murder" with small cantillations; but on Shabbos Yisro they read "You shall have no" and "Remember" with small cantillations, and "You shall not murder," etc., with large cantillations to make them one verse, for we do not find a verse in the Torah of two words; and also in "I am," etc., there is a large cantillation; see there. And in the sefer Or Torah he wrote that an individual reads the lower, and in a congregation he reads the upper; and so wrote the annotator in Ein Yaakov on the Yerushalmi, chapter 7 of Shekalim; see there. All the firstborn. And if it falls on Shabbos, they begin "Aser te'aser."
And Hashem is in His Sanctuary. And in these lands they begin the prayer of Chavakuk.
