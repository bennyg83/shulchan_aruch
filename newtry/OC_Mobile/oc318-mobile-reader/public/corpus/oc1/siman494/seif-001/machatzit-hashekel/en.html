Magen Avraham wrote: It is difficult, etc. Do we not hold like R' Yosi, etc.? We say in Shabbos, folio 86a: Our Rabbis taught: a woman who emits seed on the third day after relations is pure, the words of R' Elazar ben Azaryah; R' Yishmael says: on the third day she is impure, and on the fourth day she is pure; R' Akiva requires five onos (an onah is a day or a night), and afterward, specifically if she emitted after five onos, she is pure. And we say that they disagree in the dispute of the Rabbanan and R' Yosi: the Rabbanan hold that the Torah was given on the sixth of Sivan, and R' Yosi holds that the Torah was given on the seventh of Sivan. And Rava said: everyone holds that on Rosh Chodesh they came to the wilderness of Sinai, and everyone holds that the Torah was given on Shabbos; when they disagree is about the fixing of the month. R' Yosi holds that Rosh Chodesh was on Sunday, and on Sunday he said nothing to them because of the weakness of the journey; on Monday he said to them: "And you shall be for Me a kingdom of kohanim"; on Tuesday he said to them the mitzvah of hagbalah; and on Wednesday they did the separation. And the Rabbanan hold that Rosh Chodesh Sivan was on Monday, and he said nothing to them [then] because of the weakness of the journey; and on Tuesday he said to them: "And you shall be for Me a kingdom of kohanim"; and on Wednesday he said to them the mitzvah of hagbalah; and on Thursday they did the separation. And everyone holds that on Friday night they immersed, and not by day, so that these should not be going to immerse while those are going to receive the Torah. And R' Elazar ben Azaryah holds like the Rabbanan, that on Thursday they did the separation, and since they immersed on Friday night, which was the third day from the separation, therefore one who emits on the third day is pure. And R' Yishmael holds like R' Yosi, that on Wednesday they did the separation, and since they immersed on Friday night, which was the fourth day from the separation, therefore specifically one who emits on the fourth day is pure. And R' Akiva also holds like R' Yosi, only he holds like R' Elazar ben Azaryah that Moshe ascended at dawn and descended at dawn; if so, on Wednesday at dawn he descended and told them the mitzvah of separation; and he holds that for this reason he warned them in the morning to separate from their wives so that there would be five onos until Friday night, which is the time of her immersion. And we say there on 86b: R' Chiyya bar Abba said in the name of R' Yochanan: these are the words of R' Yosi and R' Akiva; but the Sages say we require six complete onos. And Rashi explained that they hold like R' Yosi that on Wednesday they did the separation, and they hold that he descended at dawn and told them the mitzvah of separation on the morning of Wednesday, and they hold that they immersed on Shabbos day; if so, there were six onos. And we hold in Yoreh De'ah that we require six onos: because emitting seed voids one day, therefore she must wait from wearing whites even if she ceased in purity until the fifth day from relations, and the fifth day counts for her toward the count of seven clean days (and there are who say that even the fifth day does not count for her; see there the reason), for the first four days are within six onos, and we are concerned lest she emit seed and not sense it and void one day; see there. If so, necessarily he holds like R' Yosi that on Wednesday they did the separation and the Torah was given on the seventh of Sivan; and the Sages hold three onos, and the reason is that they hold like the Rabbanan that on Thursday they did the separation, and they hold that he descended at dawn and they immersed on Friday night, and there are only three onos. In Yoreh De'ah [we rule] like R' Yosi, that we require six onos.
For they left Egypt on Thursday; if so, they began to count on Friday night; if so, 49 days, that is seven weeks, were completed on Thursday of the week; and since the Torah was given on Shabbos, two days were added onto the 49 days, Friday and Shabbos day, totaling 51 days; and we say "on the fiftieth day, the day of the giving of our Torah." And even though according to what the Magen Avraham wrote before this, that we hold like the Rabbanan that on Thursday they did the separation, if so they left on Friday; for so it is stated there on folio 87b: Come and hear regarding the Rabbanan: Nisan in which Yisrael left, etc., and that day was Thursday; and since the fifteenth of Nisan was Thursday, if so Rosh Chodesh Iyar was Shabbos, for Nisan was full; and Rosh Chodesh Sivan was on Sunday, for Iyar was defective; and this is difficult for the Rabbanan who hold that Rosh Chodesh Sivan was Monday. And they answer: the Rabbanan said to him: Iyar of that year was full, and Rosh Chodesh Sivan was on Monday. And he objects: but a baraita teaches that Iyar was defective; for a baraita teaches: Nisan in which they left, etc., and that day was Thursday; Nisan was completed and Rosh Chodesh Iyar was on Shabbos; Iyar was defective and Rosh Chodesh Sivan was on Sunday. And they answer: whose is this? It is R' Yosi. And on folio 88a he objects against R' Yosi from what a baraita teaches in Seder Olam: Nisan in which Yisrael left, etc., and that day was Erev Shabbos; and since Rosh Chodesh Nisan was on Erev Shabbos, Rosh Chodesh Iyar was on Sunday, and Rosh Chodesh Sivan was on Monday; and this is difficult for R' Yosi. And they answer: whose is this? It is the Rabbanan. Until here is the sugya. Thus according to the Rabbanan, whom we hold like, they left on Erev Shabbos. And one must say that the Magen Avraham's question is according to what Tosafos wrote on folio 87b, s.v. "And that day," etc.; and so wrote the Tur in siman 430: that for this reason they call it Shabbos HaGadol, for then when they left, the tenth of Nisan fell on Shabbos, and the mitzvah of the Pesach of Egypt was to take [the lamb] on the tenth of the month, and they took it on Shabbos day and tied it to the legs of their beds, an abomination of Egypt, and they were saved from the hand of Egypt, or from the wars of the firstborns with the Egyptians, as Tosafos wrote; and because the miracle was done on Shabbos, therefore they call it Shabbos HaGadol, after the great miracle; see there. And since the tenth of Nisan fell on Shabbos, necessarily the fifteenth of Nisan fell on Thursday. And even though we hold like the Rabbanan, necessarily according to our custom and according to Tosafos and the Tur, who hold that when the Gemara answers on the baraita of folio 87b "whose is this? It is R' Yosi," since it did not say "rather, whose is this? It is R' Yosi," it implies that it did not retract from the first answer, and the first baraita that it brought, which teaches that the fifteenth of Nisan fell on Thursday, can also come according to the Rabbanan, and they hold that Iyar was full; only the second baraita, which teaches explicitly that Iyar was defective, must come according to R' Yosi; and one must say that the first baraita and Seder Olam disagree according to the Rabbanan whether Iyar was full or defective; and we hold like the Rabbanan and like the first baraita, that they left on Thursday and Iyar was full: so one must say according to the Tur and our custom. And see what Chok Yaakov wrote above in siman 430; and with this the Magen Avraham's questions for our custom are resolved.
On the night of Shavuot they do not make Kiddush, etc. It implies that for Ma'ariv one may pray while it is still daytime; and so it is proven from what he wrote afterward, that one must say Krias Shema after nightfall when the stars appear, since he does not read it upon his bed. And in the Taz he wrote that for Ma'ariv too one should not begin until night. And regarding what the Taz wrote concerning the interruption in saying Akdamus, see responsa Shaarei Ephraim who upholds the custom; and see Chok Yaakov and Eliyah Rabbah.
They would stay awake all night. See in Chok Yaakov the custom of the Gaon, our master, Moreinu HaRav Rabbi Shmuel, of blessed memory; and see above in siman 4, se'if 13, as it should read.
In the morning one should not recite a blessing, etc. For washing is required without a blessing, for there is a dispute of the poskim on this, and a doubt regarding blessings is [resolved] leniently.
As is written in siman 8, s.k. 24, for there is also a dispute. And there the Magen Avraham wrote that his intent should be to exempt it with the blessing of a tallis gadol.
In the Gemara, chapter 4 of Sukkah, folio 45b: R' Yehudah said in the name of Shmuel: lulav seven [days], meaning one must recite a blessing over it every day of the seven days of Sukkos; sukkah one day: one need recite a blessing over the sukkah only on the first day alone. And he gives a reason: for lulav the nights are separated from the days, for the mitzvah of lulav is specifically by day; if so, each day is a mitzvah unto itself. But a sukkah, whose nights are not separated, for even at night one is obligated in the sukkah, and it is like one long day, and the blessing of the first day exempts. And although we do not hold so, but rather even for a sukkah one must recite a blessing every time he enters it, as is stated there on folio 46a, or at the time of eating, as we hold in siman 639: that is because once he left it there was a distraction of mind; but regarding Torah, whose mitzvah is all day and night, as it is written "and you shall meditate on it," etc., there is no [concern] that he distracted his mind for this reason: even if he interrupted his learning, when he returns to learn he need not recite a blessing, as is written above in siman 47, se'if 10; and see there in the Magen Avraham and Taz.
And since regarding Torah there is no [issue] of distraction of mind, the reasoning of the Gemara regarding a sukkah remains: that here too the nights are not separated from the days, and he is exempted by the blessing that he recited yesterday.
Unless he slept. For sleep is an interruption.
Sleep by day is not, etc. And as is written in siman 47, se'if 11. And if so, even if he slept at night, why must he recite a blessing when he awakens? Therefore one must say that our intention is to exempt only for day and night. If so, the same applies if he stayed awake all night: he must recite [a blessing] when day breaks.
And so it appears as the main [ruling], even though above in siman 47, s.k. 12, it appears that he was in doubt; one must say that after he raised the question he later resolved it for himself.
Mas'at Binyamin wrote, etc. They read with the upper cantillation, etc.; and he gave a reason: what we find in several of the Ten Commandments verses of two kinds of cantillation is one reason: whether we divide the verses as in the rest of the Torah in order to know the beginning of a matter and its end; and if so, "You shall not murder, You shall not commit adultery," etc., until "your fellow" is all one verse, for "You shall not murder" alone is not the end of a verse, for we do not find in the Torah a verse of two words, as the Magen Avraham wrote below in the name of the Chizkuni. And according to that division of verses, the lower cantillation belongs to them, as is explained there; see there. But when our desire is to divide the verses into dibros, so that each dibbur is a verse by itself, therefore one must say that "You shall not murder" is the end of one verse, for it is a complete dibbur; and so too each and every dibbur, except for "I am" and "You shall have no": if they divide the dibros they make them one verse, since those two dibros we heard from the Mouth of the Almighty; and so they make them one verse to teach that specifically these alone are equal in their level in that we heard them from His mouth, may He be blessed. And according to this one must vocalize them with the cantillations called the upper cantillation. And therefore on Shavuot, when the Torah was given, they read them to divide into dibros, and therefore they read with the upper cantillation; and then "You shall not murder" is the end of a verse and one reads the tzadi with a kamatz. This applies to Shavuot when they read with the upper cantillation. And what he wrote afterward: "And when one reads with the upper cantillation, then one reads 'You shall not murder' with the tzadi with a pattach" is a printer's error, and it should say: "And when one reads with the lower cantillation." And the reason is: for there, when one reads with the upper cantillation, that is according to the division of the dibros, then "You shall not murder" is the end of a verse, and the end of a verse is with a kamatz; and the same applies in place of an esnachta, since it is a pause of a clause. And therefore also the tav of "tirtzach" is dageshed, even though it came after the aleph of "lo" and after the letters aleph-vav-hei-yud it should be soft; nevertheless, since "tirtzach" is the end of a verse, there is under "lo" the cantillation tipcha, and it is one of the cantillations that cancel [the softening of] aleph-hei-vav-yud; therefore the tav is dageshed; and so too for the other tavs: "tinaf," "tignov." But when they read the lower cantillation, then "You shall not murder" is in the middle, and there is also no esnachta there; therefore its law is with a pattach under the tzadi, and the tav is soft since it came after the letters aleph-hei-vav-yud; see there.
