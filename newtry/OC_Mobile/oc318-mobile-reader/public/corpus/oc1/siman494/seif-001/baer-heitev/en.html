Shavuot. See Magen Avraham in what he wrote, that according to everyone the Torah was given on the 51st day of the counting: why did the Holy One, blessed be He, fix the festival of Shavuot for the generations on the 50th day of the counting; see there; and see Chok Yaakov. And the Maharsha in Chiddushei Aggados, chapter 1 of Avodah Zarah, in the sugya of heaven and earth touching in their testimony, etc., also asked this question, and answers there that the Holy One, blessed be He, intended thereby to teach that fear of sin precedes wisdom in rank and in time; see there. And according to this I said that this is the intent of Rav Yosef, that on the day of Atzeres they would prepare for him a three-year-old calf; he said: if not for this day that causes it, how many Yosefs are there in the marketplace, end quote; see Pesachim 68a. It is fitting to examine it carefully a great deal. First: what is this language that he says "if not for this day that causes," which implies that the day causes it; he should have said "if not for Matan Torah." And further: if not for Matan Torah, He would return the world to tohu and vohu, as we say that the Holy One, blessed be He, stipulated with the act of Creation: if Yisrael accept the Torah, well; and if not, I will return them to tohu and vohu. And further: after Rav Yosef was more humble than any person, as we say at the end of Sotah: when Rebbi died, humility ceased; Rav Yosef said to him, do not teach "humility," for there is I; see there; and here he boasted that because of the giving of the Torah he is superior to other people; this is not within the bounds of humility. And according to what I wrote, his intention was as follows in what he said "if not for this day," etc.: for it is known what Chazal said, that humility brings to fear of sin. And if so, Rav Yosef, who was on a level that he was humble, was also on a level that his fear of sin preceded his wisdom. And this is what he said: on the day of Atzeres, meaning on the 50th day, they would prepare for him a three-year-old calf, and he said: if not for this day that causes it, meaning if the day of Atzeres were not on this day but on the 51st day of the counting, it would not be proven that fear of sin precedes wisdom in rank and in time, and I would have no distinction at all, and how many Yosefs are there in the marketplace; but now that the day of Atzeres is on the 50th day and the giving of the Torah was on the 51st day, the question is difficult and one must answer as the Maharsha wrote; and examine it carefully. And the later authorities wrote that they delay praying Ma'ariv at the entry of Shavuot so that the days of the counting shall be complete.
The third. Mas'at Binyamin wrote in siman 6: on Shavuot they read with the upper cantillation that is on the Ten Commandments; and on Shabbos Parashas Yisro they read with the lower cantillation, and then "You shall not murder" is the end of a verse and one reads the tzadi with a kamatz; and the same applies to other ends of verses or an esnachta; and when they read with the upper cantillation, then one reads "You shall not murder" with the tzadi with a pattach; see there; and see Halachos Ketanos at the end of the sefer. And in the sefer Or Torah he wrote that an individual reads the lower, and in a congregation he reads the upper; and so wrote the annotator in Ein Yaakov on the Yerushalmi, chapter 7 of Shekalim; see there. The Taz wrote, and these are his words: regarding what they practice in these lands to read the first verse and afterward begin Akdamus, one must wonder how they are permitted to interrupt, etc.; and I heard recently that distinguished rabbis instituted to sing Akdamus before the kohen begins to recite the blessing over the Torah reading, and so it is proper to practice in all congregations; and likewise regarding Yatziv Pisgam that they say on the second day after the first verse of the haftarah, it is proper to practice so, end quote of the Taz. But in Teshuvos Shaarei Ephraim, Orach Chaim, siman 10, he discussed at length to uphold the custom, and that no person has the power to change the custom; and so agreed the Chok Yaakov; see there (and so agreed in the sefer Eliyah Rabbah; and see there regarding saying Arkin according to what is explained).
In the Merkavah. And they practiced that specifically a great and wise person reads the haftarah; and Or Zarua wrote that they say it standing.
