(1) On the 50th day of Sefiras HaOmer, etc.: And they delay praying Ma'ariv at the entry of Shavuot until nightfall when the stars appear, so that the days of the counting shall be 49 complete days. It is stated in the Zohar that the early chassidim would stay awake all night and occupy themselves in Torah; and already most of those who learn have practiced to do so; and it is stated in the Shulchan Aruch of the Arizal, of blessed memory: know that anyone who on that night did not sleep at all whatsoever and was occupied in Torah is assured that he will complete his year and that no harm will befall him. And the reason the Magen Avraham wrote according to its plain sense is that Yisrael were sleeping all night and the Holy One, blessed be He, had to awaken them to receive the Torah [as is stated in the Midrash]; therefore we must correct this. And one must caution those who stay awake all night not to recite the blessing on washing the hands in the morning except after he has relieved himself before prayer, and then he recites the blessing on washing the hands and Asher Yatzar, as is explained above in siman 4, s.k. 30; see there. And regarding a blessing over a tallis katan there are disagreements among the poskim; therefore he should see to intend to exempt it with the blessing that he recites over a tallis gadol, as is explained above in siman 8 in the Mishnah Berurah, s.k. 42. And regarding Birkas HaTorah, see above in siman 47 in the Mishnah Berurah, s.k. 28. And regarding the blessing Elokai Neshamah and the blessing who removes sleep, see in siman 46 in the Mishnah Berurah, s.k. 24. It is proper to be careful according to Kabbalah not to have relations on the night of Shavuot unless it is the night of immersion; and see what we wrote above in siman 240 in the Mishnah Berurah, s.k. 7, and in the Beur Halacha there.
(2) And they read on the first, etc.: And the custom is to say Akdamus; and several later authorities agreed to say this before the kohen begins to recite the blessing over the Torah; and so is the custom nowadays in many communities. However, Yatziv Pisgam that they say on the second day at the time of reading the haftarah has spread in some places to say it after the first verse of the haftarah.
(3) From the third month: And they read the dibros with the upper cantillation; and see in the Beur Halacha, where I explained at length.
(4) In the Merkavah of Yechezkel: And in some places they practice that a great and wise person reads this haftarah; and there is a support for the matter: "and not in Maaseh Merkavah unless he was a wise person and understands of his own knowledge." And there are also who practice that anyone who reads it quietly with the maftir also says it standing, out of respect for it.
