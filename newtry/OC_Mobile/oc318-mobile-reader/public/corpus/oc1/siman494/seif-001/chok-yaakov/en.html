On the fiftieth day of Sefiras HaOmer. And the later authorities wrote that they delay in the Ma'ariv prayer so that the days of the counting shall be complete. And regarding what the Magen Avraham asks from Yoreh De'ah, siman 196, see my sefer Toras HaShelamim what I wrote there. And regarding what the Magen Avraham further asks in the name of Aserah Maamaros: since on Thursday the children of Yisrael left Egypt, as is stated in Shabbos, and on Friday they began to count the counting, it turns out that the fiftieth day is on Friday, and according to everyone the Torah was given on Shabbos, so it turns out that on the 51st day the Torah was given; so why do they make the festival of Shavuot on the fiftieth day; and he answered that it comes to hint to us the second day of Yom Tov of the Diaspora, end quote. This answer is puzzling: for if so, the second day would be the main Yom Tov, which is astonishing; and for the people of Eretz Yisrael who make only the first day, what is there to say. And see what I wrote on this question above in siman 430, s.k. 2; and it appears to me that from the outset there is no difficulty at all, as the Rivash wrote in siman 96: that the verse did not attach the festival of Shavuot to the day of Matan Torah but to the fiftieth day of the Omer; only that in this time, when we know the fixed calendar of the months, and Nisan is always full and Iyar defective, it turns out that the completion of fifty days is the sixth of Sivan; therefore we say "the day of the giving of our Torah." And so is explained explicitly in the first chapter of Rosh Hashanah, folio 6b: for a baraita of Rav Shemayah: Atzeres is sometimes on the fifth of Sivan, sometimes on the sixth, sometimes on the seventh; how so? If both are full, Nisan and Iyar, the fifth; if one is full and one defective, the sixth; thus it is explicit that when they would sanctify by sight, sometimes the festival of Shavuot would fall on the fifth of Sivan even though the Torah had still not been given according to everyone except on Friday like the Sages or on the seventh like R' Yosi; rather, certainly there is no practical difference at all in this; and see what I wrote above in siman 430 in the name of Seder Olam, and examine it carefully. Further the later authorities wrote not to sleep at night but only to occupy oneself in Torah, and the reward is great; and this custom has already spread in the eyes of the masses of the people to learn together with ten according to the tikkun and to say Kaddish, and they immerse when day breaks, and some immerse on the eve of the festival. And in the house of my master, father-in-law, the Gaon, they did not practice so to learn with ten, but each and every one learns individually, for the main tikkun was instituted only before common people who do not know how to learn; therefore they say the tikkun. And regarding Birkas HaTorah, see in the Magen Avraham; and see above in siman 46, se'if 12; and see siman 293; and in the Kol Bo, siman 74, how they deal with the child when they initiate him on the festival of Shavuot to learn Torah, and it is a custom of the ancients, and a person should not change from the custom; so wrote the Rokeach; and see there. And the later authorities further wrote that one should forbid marital relations unless it is the night of her immersion.
It is the festival of Shavuot. In the Maharil at the end of the laws of Shavuot he wrote, and these are his words: the Maharil would not recite Kiddush Levanah on any Yom Tov that falls on Motzaei Shabbos, for example the festival of Shavuot that falls on Sunday or Monday; and he gave a reason: just as there are techumim below, so there are techumim above, and one should not greet the Presence on Yom Tov outside the techum; however, he testified about his distinguished teacher that he was not particular and would sanctify it even on Yom Tov on Motzaei Shabbos, end quote; and it is brought in the Taz above at the beginning of siman 426, and he concludes: and so is the main [ruling], to sanctify it even on Yom Tov; therefore the Rama did not bring it, end quote of the Taz. And without careful attention he wrote this, and what is written there in the Shulchan Aruch, se'if 2, in the Rama's gloss, escaped his notice: explicitly that they do not recite Kiddush [Levanah] on Yom Tov; and see in responsa Mas'as Moshe, siman 78, who also ruled that one should not recite Kiddush on the night of Yom Tov and Shabbos, and he wrote there the reason that the honor of Shabbos the Queen and the sanctity of Yom Tov are preferable to greeting the face of the moon, and one does not mix one joy with another.
On the first, from "In the month." The Taz wrote, and these are his words: regarding what they practice in these lands to read the first verse and afterward begin Akdamus, one must wonder greatly how they are permitted to interrupt, etc.; and I heard recently that distinguished rabbis instituted to sing Akdamus before the kohen begins to recite the blessing over the Torah reading, and so it is proper to practice in all congregations; and likewise regarding Yatziv Pisgam, until here his words. However in responsa Shaarei Ephraim, Orach Chaim section, siman 10, he discussed at length to uphold this custom of the early authorities, which is a custom of the veterans, and that no person has the power to change the custom, and he has something upon which to rely; see there where he discussed at length; and so they have always practiced here from years of old; and recently they wanted to change the custom because of the words of the Taz, and they did not succeed in changing the custom; and see what I wrote above in siman 489, s.k. 11, regarding changing customs.
