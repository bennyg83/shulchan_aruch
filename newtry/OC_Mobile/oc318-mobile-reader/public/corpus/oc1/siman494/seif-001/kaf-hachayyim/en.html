1) [Se'if 1] On the fiftieth day of the counting of the omer, etc. The beginning of Maariv at the onset of Shavuos is delayed so that the days of the counting will be complete. Taz, at the beginning of the siman; Chok Yaakov, subsection 1. Some later authorities write likewise. Shelah, page 179b, however, writes that one need be careful only regarding Kiddush. Reciting the prayer while it is still daytime is of no consequence, for Rabbi Oshaya recited the Motzaei Shabbos prayer on Shabbos even though it was still fully Shabbos. See there. Therefore, one who cannot delay the prayer should nevertheless not recite Kiddush until nightfall, as written in the following subsection.
2) On the first night of Shavuos, one waits to recite Kiddush until it is actually night. If he recites Kiddush before nightfall, part of the forty-ninth day of the counting will be lacking, while the verse states, 'Seven complete weeks shall they be.' Masas Binyamin, at the end of the work, Hagahos Orach Chaim, siman 7; Sheyarei Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur, subsection 1; Olas Shabbos, subsection 1; Magen Avraham, subsection 1; Pri Chadash; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 3; Chok Yosef, subsection 1; Shulchan Gavoah, subsection 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 2.
3) There. On the fiftieth day of the counting of the omer, etc. Rivash, siman 96, writes that Scripture did not make Shavuos dependent upon the day on which the Torah was given, but upon the fiftieth day of the omer. It is not necessary that this be precisely the day on which the Torah was given, neither earlier nor later. Now, however, according to our calculation, in which Nissan is full and Iyar is deficient, it will always occur in this manner, because the fifty days conclude on the sixth of Sivan. For this reason, we refer in the prayer to the season of the giving of our Torah. See there. Pri Chadash; Chok Yaakov, subsection 1; Chok Yosef, subsection 3; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1.
