And they read see Taz; and see Eliyah Rabbah, Chok Yaakov, and Eliyah Rabbah 5; and in the Levush [it is written] that he should read it after the first verse; and in many places they practice as the Taz wrote, that they say Akdamus before the first verse and before the first blessing; but Yatziv Pisgam on the second day they practice to say after the first verse of the haftarah; and see siman 140; see there. And it appears that nevertheless they open the Sefer Torah beforehand and cover it with the mantle of the Sefer Torah, and the chazzan says Akdamus before the kohen recites the blessing.
See Levush: on the second day they read Ruth; and see siman 490 in the Magen Avraham, 7 and 8; and I do not know a proper reason why specifically on the second day they read Ruth, not on the first day. And they do not recite a blessing over the reading of a megillah; see Taz there. And they mention souls and say Av HaRachamim on the second day; and every place where they read "All the firstborn" they mention souls, for it contains "the gift of the hand," and they vow tzedakah. The haftarah of the first day is in Yechezkel; see Eliyah Rabbah, letter 6, that it is proper to say it specifically standing; see there; and it is implied that for another haftarah one may sit; and this requires some further study. See siman 282 in the Magen Avraham, 12: that a minor does not go up for maftir on the first day of Shavuot. In the formula of the haftarah blessing: "over the Torah and over the Avodah," etc., "this day of the festival of Shavuot"; but they do not mention to say "mikra kodesh," etc., even though in prayer they say "mikra kodesh," as in siman 488 in the Taz and Magen Avraham, letter 2; there the reason is that it is a mitzvah to gather in the house of Hashem in public and to sanctify the day in prayer, which is not so in the haftarah; see there. And I saw in some versions in siddurim that "mikra kodesh" is written in them in the haftarah in the blessing over the Avodah, and we do not practice so, as aforesaid. And in a printed Chumash with the Ramban, of blessed memory, the Wilhermsdorf printing of the Rif, and in the Mantua printing, I found likewise that one does not say "mikra kodesh" in the haftarah; however, in a Chumash printed in Amsterdam with Rashi and Ashkenazi Judeo-German it is written "this day of rest, a festival, mikra kodesh"; see there; and perhaps it is the Sephardi version; and even this I do not know why, as I wrote, for why should they mention in the haftarah "mikra kodesh" more than other matters; and for us it is obvious that one does not say "mikra kodesh" in the haftarah at all. One must be careful regarding meat and dairy foods in all that one is careful throughout the year, as explained in Yoreh De'ah, simanim 88 and 89, and that their reward not go out in their loss; and in our many sins in this bitter exile, whatever fence one can fence more is better; and all the more so what is by law and a custom that they already accepted is like a vow.
