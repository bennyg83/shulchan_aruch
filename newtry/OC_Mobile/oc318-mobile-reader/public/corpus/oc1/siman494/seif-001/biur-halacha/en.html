"From 'In the third month.'" See Mishnah Berurah; I will explain somewhat. There are 2 systems of cantillation for the Aseret HaDibrot, commonly called the upper and lower cantillation. Their difference follows 2 distinct ways of dividing the text into verses. The first system is arranged according to the written parashiyot, so that each dibbur is treated as its own unit. From Anochi through Lo Tisa is written as 1 closed parashah and constitutes 1 dibbur, and therefore is read as 1 verse. Likewise, from Zachor through Lo Tirtzach is treated as the unit of that dibbur. From Lo Tirtzach through Lo Tachmod, however, the text is written as 4 separate closed parashiyot, corresponding to 4 separate dibrot, and therefore each is treated as a separate verse. The cantillation and pronunciation accordingly reflect where the verse endings fall. Letters that receive dagesh or become soft, and vowels that vary because of a sof pasuk, follow that division. The second system is arranged according to the ordinary reading division of the verses. In that reading, Lo Tirtzach through Lo Tachmod forms a single verse and one may not make a complete stop in its middle, even when reading privately. It cannot be said that Lo Tirtzach alone forms an independent verse, because there is no verse in the Torah containing fewer than 3 words. By contrast, the passages from Anochi through Lo Tisa and from Zachor through Lo Tirtzach are divided into several verses in the ordinary reading. Therefore the lower cantillation both joins the clauses that belong to 1 ordinary verse and separates the places where the ordinary reading has verse endings. This is the substance of the 2 melodies. On Shavuot the custom is to use the upper cantillation, treating each dibbur as a distinct verse in accordance with the manner in which the Aseret HaDibrot were given. In Parashat Yitro and Parashat Vaetchanan during the regular yearly reading, the common custom is to use the lower cantillation. Some communities, however, are accustomed to use the upper cantillation also there when reading publicly, while an individual reading for himself uses the lower cantillation. The practical pronunciation of the several words that change according to pause or continuation follows whichever cantillation is being used. Thus where a word concludes a verse in 1 system it receives the pronunciation appropriate to a stop, while in the other system it is pronounced as a continuing word. This is the general explanation of the 2 systems.
