They delay beginning Maariv at the entrance of Shavuot so that the days of counting will be complete.
(1) And on the first day they read from "In the third month." Regarding the custom in these countries to read the first verse and afterward begin Akdamut Milin, etc., there is much to wonder: how are they permitted to interrupt the reading? Even speaking words of Torah is forbidden, as written in siman 146, and all the permissions mentioned there do not apply here; all the more so this praise, which is not connected to the reading at all. Why should we interrupt? I recently heard that distinguished rabbis instituted singing Akdamut before the kohen begins the blessing over the Torah reading, and this is proper to practice in all communities. So too with Yetziv Pitgam, which is said on the second day after the first verse of the haftarah, it is proper to practice this way, except that the haftarah is not as severe as Torah reading.
