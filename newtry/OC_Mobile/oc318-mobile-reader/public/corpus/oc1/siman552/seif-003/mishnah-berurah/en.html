(8) In two pots. Meaning that one has a thick batter and one has a thin batter; but if both are equal, there is no insistence on cooking in two pots.
(9) It is called two cooked dishes. Meaning that one person cannot eat both of them; but Reuven may eat one cooked dish and Shimon one.
(10) Whose manner is thus. Meaning that most times they put onions and eggs in peas, as is the manner to fry dough and eggs; but fish it is forbidden to fry with eggs, since most times they cook them without eggs [Magen Avraham; and his intent is even for those places that did not practice to forbid plain fish]. And lebibos filled with cheese are called two cooked dishes; and likewise braided noodles with cheese, since most times they do not do so, they are two cooked dishes.
(11) It is called a cooked dish for this matter. For even though regarding cooking by a non-Jew there is no status of cooked dish upon it, such as milk that was cooked and the like, for our matters it is forbidden; for the main reason they forbade two cooked dishes is that in a multiplicity of cooked dishes there is honor and pleasure.
(12) And there is no difference between roasted and cooked. Such as roasted eggs and roasted fruits; upon all of them there is the status of cooked dish; and that is what he wrote below "when they are raw," to exclude when they were cooked or roasted.
