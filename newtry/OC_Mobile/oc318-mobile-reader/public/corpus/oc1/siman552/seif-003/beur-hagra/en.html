Se'if 3. Even, etc. For it too is called two kinds, as written in chapter 7 of Bava Metzia, sixty kinds of porridge, and see Rashi there; and likewise in several places; but if they are equal there is certainly no insistence; and see Magen Avraham; and so it is in the Gemara there: if he was accustomed to eat two cooked dishes he should eat one kind; and from Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel we learn for the first Tanna.
And likewise there are, etc. So wrote the Geonim and the poskim; and so wrote Rashi in the mishnah; see there. And they learned from eruv tavshilin. And the Ramban disagreed with them and said that regarding eruv tavshilin they were lenient; and that is what "one should be stringent" means. And it appears to me that by law it is truly forbidden; they were lenient regarding eruv tavshilin, for they said there "and they agree regarding fish and egg upon it," etc., which implies even though they are one cooked dish, nevertheless they agree, etc.; for otherwise what is "and they agree," since by law they are two cooked dishes? But regarding two kinds in one pot it was not mentioned in the mishnah because of its great obviousness; and likewise in the beraisa that argues regarding fish and egg; but two kinds even in one pot are not [a dispute]; and that is what "unless it is," etc., means: even though regarding eruv tavshilin they are two cooked dishes.
And a cooked dish, etc. As regarding eruv tavshilin, that they said in Yerushalmi Nedarim chapter 6, halachah 1: any food that is eaten as it is raw has no law of cooking by a non-Jew, and one fulfills with it for eruv tavshilin; and so it is in the Gemara at the beginning of chapter 2 of Yom Tov; and even the Ramban agrees in this; see there.
And there is no difference, etc. As written regarding eruv tavshilin: this cooked dish, roasted, even, etc.; and there, if he roasted them, etc.; and likewise regarding vows: one who vows from a cooked dish, and even when cooked, by Torah law even roasted, as written "and they shall cook the Pesach," rather that regarding vows, etc.
