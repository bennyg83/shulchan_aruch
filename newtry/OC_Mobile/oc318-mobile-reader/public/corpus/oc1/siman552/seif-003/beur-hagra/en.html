(2) And so there is [such a view]. So wrote the Geonim and the poskim; and so Rashi in the mishnah — see there. And they learned from eruv tavshilin. And Ramban disagreed with them and said in eruv tavshilin they were lenient; and his words mean one should be stringent. It appears to me that by law it is truly forbidden; they were lenient in eruv tavshilin where they said there "fish and egg on it," etc., which implies: even though they are one dish, nevertheless they are equivalent — for otherwise what is "and equivalent," since by law they are two dishes? Rather with two kinds in one pot it was not mentioned in the mishnah due to its obvious simplicity; and so in the baraita that argues on fish and egg — but two kinds even in one pot are not implied; and his words mean "unless it is," etc. — even though on Yom Tov they are two dishes.
