(א) Comment 7: On the ground regarding an onen etc. And for us etc. And comment 8 one need not etc. that the meal should be humble etc. He expanded the intermingling in these two comments. It should read as it is in Terumat HaDeshen.

(2) Regarding the ground — and one need not remove shoes, etc., for mourning does not apply until night; and sitting on the ground is so the meal should be lowly — and for us lowliness is recognized only on the ground. Machatzit Hashekel concluded: but removing a shoe is unrelated to the meal — neither for honor nor disgrace. Furthermore it appears this meal is like aninut meal before mourning begins, as the wording implies (Taanit 30b) R' Yehuda bar Elai: he sat between oven and stove like one dead before him — that is aninut; and regarding aninut we learned: he does not recline and eat, does not eat meat, does not drink wine — exactly like our seudah hamafseket; and we are not accustomed to reclining. But removing a shoe is unnecessary for an onen, as Tosafot Ketubot 4a proves explicitly — end quote. Therefore permitted to sit after the meal, etc. — permitted for both reasons above: aninut applies only at meal time requiring sitting on ground, and likewise for onen.
