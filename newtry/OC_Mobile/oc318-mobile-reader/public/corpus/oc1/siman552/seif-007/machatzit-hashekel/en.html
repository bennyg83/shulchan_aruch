(s.k. 7) On the ground of an onen, etc. And for us, etc.; and s.k. 8, and he need not, etc., that there is not, etc., that the meal be lowly, etc. He expanded the intermixing in these two se'if katan paragraphs. It should read as it is in Terumat HaDeshen.
On the ground, and he need not remove, etc. For mourning does not apply until night; and that they sit on the ground is so that the meal be lowly; and for us the lowliness is not recognized except on the ground. He concluded in Terumat HaDeshen: But removing a shoe is unrelated at all to the meal, neither to honor it nor to degrade it. Furthermore it appears that this meal is comparable to an aninut meal, which is before mourning; and so the language of the sefer implies (in tractate Taanis folio 30b) regarding R' Yehudah bar Elai, who said he would sit between the oven and the stove and it seemed to him as if his dead lay before him; and that is aninut. And regarding aninut we learned: he does not recline and eat, and he does not eat meat, and he does not drink wine; and that is exactly like the seudah hamafseket for us. And aside from this we are not accustomed to reclining. But removing a shoe an onen need not, as is proven explicitly in Tosafos, first chapter of Kesubos, etc., end of his words. Therefore it is permitted to sit after the meal, etc.; this is permitted for both reasons above: for also by the reason of aninut, specifically at the time of the meal one needs sitting on the ground, and so it is for an onen.
