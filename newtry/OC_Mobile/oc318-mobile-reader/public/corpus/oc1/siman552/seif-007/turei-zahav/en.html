In the Tur he brought the view of Rabeinu Yerucham that what the Gemara says on erev Tisha B'Av one shall not eat meat that is whole — the reason is because in shelamim it is written joy, and since the time of shelamim has passed there is no element of joy. We also say regarding the rebellious son that he is liable only on meat that is whole in which there is joy. Rava continues and says if he ate poultry meat he does not become a rebellious son and it teaches accordingly. And we learn in the first chapter of Chagigah: Yisrael may vow 18 [log] in vows etc., but not on fowl because there is no joy in them. From all these it implies the same applies to the interrupting meal — it is permitted. And the Tur wrote on this: it appears that even though there is no joy in it, it is forbidden — not because of joy alone did they forbid meat, but in order to increase [restriction]; but know that a mourner after [the mitzvah meal] one gives him wine, as they say wine was created only to comfort mourners, that it is a mitzvah to comfort them to forget their pain — and here they forbade wine so that if he drinks he will forget, and it is a mitzvah to remember the destruction of the Temple and grieve over it; therefore for this reason fowl is forbidden like meat, end of his words. And that they permitted salted meat that is more than the measure of shelamim — the reason is its flavor is nullified and there is no good food. And it would appear one could say what Rabeinu Yerucham said, that the rebellious son is exempt if he eats meat that is not whole, i.e., after two days and one night — for in the chapter of the rebellious son they say Rabba and Rav Yosef both say: if he ate salty meat he does not become a rebellious son. We learned there on erev Tisha B'Av one shall not eat meat etc., but one eats salty meat — how much? R' Chanina bar Kahana said: as long as it is whole. Here what? There on Tisha B'Av it is because of joy; as long as it is whole there is also joy in it. Here regarding the rebellious son it is because of prolonging, and in any amount he does not prolong — Rashi explains: from when a night passes its flavor is weakened and it is not considered whole. per the conclusion regarding the rebellious son he is exempt even in less than the measure of shelamim, and one must say Rabeinu Yerucham explains the Gemara that there is no conclusion here but everything is from the words of the questioners who said here what — shall we say there the reason is because of joy etc., and regarding the rebellious son too do we follow joy and then he continues after it, or shall we not say so but regarding the rebellious son we do not follow joy but only prolonging alone — and the question was not resolved; therefore Rabeinu Yerucham cited only the simple matter without doubt that beyond the measure of shelamim it is obvious he is exempt, and the reason is because there is no joy in it — and we learn that on erev Tisha B'Av this is permitted; so it appears to me plainly. And Maharshal strained needlessly to reconcile the Gemara according to Rabeinu Yerucham, and it is astonishing on the Tur who explained the reason on Tisha B'Av is not because of joy but because of nullification of flavor — whereas in this Gemara they say in explanation that the reason on Tisha B'Av is because of joy and not because of nullification of flavor, for nullification of flavor is even on one night as stated regarding the rebellious son on one side; and per the Tur's explanation it should have been permitted on Tisha B'Av even within the measure of one night since the flavor is nullified. And one may say the Tur explains that this is what it means there on Tisha B'Av: the reason is also because of joy — meaning there are two nullifications of flavor: one is great nullification, i.e., after the measure of shelamim, and the second is small nullification which is even on one night; and on this the Gemara says there because of joy — meaning on Tisha B'Av there is no prohibition [permit] except after the measure of shelamim, and there are two aspects — that there is no joy there and there is great nullification; but with one aspect alone it is forbidden, which is not so regarding the rebellious son — we do not follow joy at all but only flavor alone; therefore it is permitted even with small nullification. It follows that whenever there is one aspect — joy alone — even though there is small nullification of flavor, we are stringent since there is at least flavor and there is joy; therefore it is forbidden on Tisha B'Av within the measure of shelamim; and here with poultry meat there is flavor and no joy — we are also stringent, and there is no permit except where there is no joy and no flavor, i.e., meat after the measure of shelamim where there is great nullification — the Tur's words are correct. And even though there is no practical difference for us since all meat is forbidden to us, nevertheless there is a difference: per the Tur's reason we also follow flavor and there is concern lest he drink and forget the destruction of the Temple; therefore it is forbidden in the interrupting meal even drinking honey water called mead or sharp wine — even though there is no joy in them, nevertheless since they intoxicate there is concern, and this is forbidden per the Tur as appears to me; and so I saw in responsa Rashal siman 31 he forbids even beer for one not accustomed to beer at every meal or who is a weak person. (1) And one need not remove his shoes. And that we require sitting on the ground is not from the reason of mourning but that the meal be poor and lowly, for humiliation is recognized only in this — for according to us all year we do not eat reclining, and that meal is like aninus before burial of the dead when there is not yet mourning.
