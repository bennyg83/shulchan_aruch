In the Tur he brought the view of Ravyah, that what the Gemara said on erev Tisha B'Av that one should not eat meat that is like shelamim, the reason is that regarding shelamim "joy" is written, and since the time of shelamim has already passed for them, there is no joy in it; and we also say regarding a stubborn and rebellious son that he is liable only for meat that is like shelamim, in which there is joy; and it continues after that: And Rava said, if he ate poultry he does not become a stubborn and rebellious son, etc. And we recite in the first chapter of Chagigah: Yisrael pour a hin of oil for vows, etc., but not for birds, because there is no joy in them. From all these it is implied that likewise at the seudah hamafseket it is permitted. And the Tur wrote on this: It appears that even though there is no joy in it, it is forbidden; for it is not for joy alone that they forbade meat, but in order to increase mourning. Know this, for behold for a mourner afterward there is a mitzvah to give him wine to drink, for we say wine was created only to comfort mourners, for there is a mitzvah to comfort him so that he forget his sorrow; and here they forbade wine, for if he drinks he will forget, and it is a mitzvah that he remember the destruction of the Temple and grieve over it; therefore for this reason even poultry is forbidden, like meat, end quote. And that they permitted salted meat that is more than the measure of shelamim, the reason is that its taste is nullified and there is no good food in it. And at first glance one may say regarding what Ravyah wrote, that a stubborn and rebellious son is exempt if he eats meat that is not like shelamim, that is after two days and one night; for in the chapter of the stubborn and rebellious son we say: Rabbah and Rav Yosef both say, if he ate salted meat he does not become a stubborn and rebellious son. We learned there: On erev Tisha B'Av one should not eat meat, etc., but he may eat salted meat; until when? Said R' Chanina bar Kahana: As long as it is like shelamim. Here what? There on Tisha B'Av it is because of joy; as long as it is like shelamim there is also joy in it. Here regarding a stubborn and rebellious son it is because of drawing after [appetite], and with anything [after a night] he is not drawn after. Rashi explains: Once a night has passed, its taste is dulled and it is not significant. This implies according to the conclusion that a stubborn and rebellious son is exempt even with less than the measure of shelamim. And one must say that Ravyah explains the Gemara that there is no conclusion here; rather all is from the questioner's words who said "here what," whether we should say that there the reason is joy, etc., and regarding a stubborn and rebellious son we also follow joy, for then he is drawn after it, or we should not say so, but regarding a stubborn and rebellious son we do not follow joy but only drawing after; and the question was not resolved. Therefore Ravyah mentioned only the simple matter without doubt, that with more than the measure of shelamim he is certainly exempt, and the reason is that there is no joy in it; and we learn from this that on erev Tisha B'Av that is permitted. So it appears to me simply. And my father-in-law, of blessed memory, strained needlessly to resolve the Gemara according to Ravyah. And there is a difficulty on the Tur, who explained the reason on Tisha B'Av not as joy but as nullification of taste, while in this Gemara we say explicitly that the reason is joy on Tisha B'Av and not nullification of taste; for nullification of taste is even after one night, as written regarding a stubborn and rebellious son according to one side. And according to the Tur's explanation one would permit on Tisha B'Av even at the measure of a night as well, since the taste is nullified. And one may say that the Tur explains that this is what it means: there on Tisha B'Av the reason is also because of joy; meaning there are two nullifications of taste: one is a great nullification, namely after the measure of shelamim, and the second is a small nullification, which is even after one night. And on this the Gemara said "there because of joy," meaning that on Tisha B'Av there is no prohibition [permission?] except after the measure of shelamim, and there are two advantages: namely that there is no joy, and there is a great nullification; but with one advantage it is forbidden; whereas regarding a stubborn and rebellious son we do not follow joy at all but only taste alone, and therefore it is permitted even with a small nullification. It turns out that wherever there is one side, namely joy alone, even though there is a small nullification of taste, we follow stringency, since there is at least taste and there is joy; therefore it is forbidden on Tisha B'Av within the measure of shelamim. And here with poultry there is taste and no joy; we also follow stringency, and there is no permission except where there is no joy and no taste, namely meat after the measure of shelamim where there is a great nullification. Thus the Tur's words are correct. And even though there is no practical difference for us, for all meat is forbidden to us, nevertheless there is a difference: according to the Tur's reason, that we also follow taste and we require that he not drink and forget the destruction of the Temple, therefore even drinking mead called "med" or distilled spirits is forbidden at the seudah hamafseket; even though there is no joy in them, nevertheless since they intoxicate there is a concern, and this is certainly forbidden according to the Tur, as it appears to me. And so I saw in Responsa Maharshal, siman 31, that he forbids even beer, unless one is accustomed at every meal to beer, or he is a weak person.

And that we require sitting on the ground is not for the reason of mourning, but so that the meal be poor and lowly; for the lowliness is recognized only in this, for for us all year we do not eat reclining, and that meal is like aninut before burial of the dead, when mourning has not yet begun.
