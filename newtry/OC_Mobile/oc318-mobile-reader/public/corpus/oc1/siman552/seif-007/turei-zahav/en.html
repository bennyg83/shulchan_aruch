In the Tur he brought the view of Raavyah, that what the Gemara says regarding erev Tishah BeAv, that one may not eat meat that is like shelamim, is because regarding shelamim the verse says joy; once the time of shelamim has passed, there is no joy in it. We also say regarding a ben sorer u-moreh that he is liable only for meat that is like shelamim, which has joy and draws him after it. Rava said: if he ate poultry he does not become a ben sorer u-moreh, etc. We also read in the first chapter of Chagigah that Israelites fulfill their vow-offerings, etc., but not with birds, because they have no joy. From all these it implies that the same applies to the final meal before the fast, that poultry should be permitted. The Tur wrote on this: it appears that even though it has no joy, it is forbidden, for they prohibited meat not only because of joy but to increase mourning. Know this: after a mourner buries the dead there is a mitzvah to give him wine, for we say wine was created only to comfort mourners, since it is a mitzvah to comfort him and make him forget his pain; here, however, they prohibited wine, for if he drinks he will forget, and the mitzvah is that he remember the destruction of the Temple and be pained over it. Therefore for this reason even poultry is forbidden like meat; end quote. The reason salted meat older than the period of shelamim was permitted is that its taste is nullified and it is no longer a fine food. At first glance one may ask on Raavyah's statement that a ben sorer is exempt if he eats meat not like shelamim, namely after two days and one night, for in Perek Ben Sorer Rabbah and Rav Yosef both say that if he ate salted meat he does not become a ben sorer; and the Mishnah teaches that on erev Tishah BeAv one may not eat meat, etc., but may eat salted meat. How long? R. Chanina bar Kahana said: as long as it is like shelamim. The Gemara asks: what is the law here? There, on Tishah BeAv, the issue is joy, and as long as it is like shelamim it still has joy; here, with ben sorer, the issue is being drawn after it, and with any amount he is not drawn. Rashi explains that once a night has passed its taste fades and it is not significant. This implies that according to the conclusion a ben sorer is exempt even in less than the period of shelamim. One must say that Raavyah understands the Gemara not as a conclusion but all as the questioner's words: perhaps there the reason is joy, etc., and regarding ben sorer too we follow joy, for then he is drawn after it; or perhaps not, but regarding ben sorer we follow being drawn alone. The question was not resolved. Therefore Raavyah stated only the certain matter, that beyond the period of shelamim he is certainly exempt, the reason being that it lacks joy, and one may infer that on erev Tishah BeAv it is permitted. This appears simple to me, and my father-in-law z"l unnecessarily strained to reconcile the Gemara according to Raavyah. I have a difficulty with the Tur, who explained that the reason on Tishah BeAv is not joy but loss of taste, whereas in this Gemara it explicitly says the reason on Tishah BeAv is joy and not loss of taste, for loss of taste applies even after one night, as stated regarding ben sorer according to one side. According to the Tur's explanation, one should permit on Tishah BeAv even after one night, since the taste is nullified. One may answer that the Tur explains the Gemara as follows: there on Tishah BeAv the reason is also because of joy. There are two levels of loss of taste: a great loss, after the period of shelamim, and a small loss, even after one night. The Gemara says that there, on Tishah BeAv, it is because of joy, meaning permission comes only after the period of shelamim, where there are two favorable factors: no joy and great loss of taste. With only one favorable factor it remains forbidden. By contrast, with ben sorer we do not follow joy at all, only taste, and therefore even a small loss permits. Thus whenever there is one factor, joy alone though there is small loss of taste, we are stringent because it still has taste and joy, and it is forbidden on Tishah BeAv within the period of shelamim. Here with poultry there is taste but no joy, and we also are stringent. Permission exists only where there is neither joy nor taste, namely meat after the period of shelamim, where there is great loss of taste. The Tur's words are thus correct. Although this has no practical difference for us, since all meat is forbidden, there is still a practical difference: according to the Tur's reason, that we also follow taste and require that he not drink and forget the destruction of the Temple, the final meal before the fast forbids even drinking mead or liquor. Although they have no joy, since they intoxicate there is concern, and this is certainly forbidden according to the Tur, as appears to my humble mind. So too I saw in Responsa Maharshal, siman 31, that he forbids even beer for one who is not accustomed to beer at every meal, or who is a weak person.
(1) And he need not remove his shoes. The reason we require sitting on the ground is not because of mourning, but so that the meal be poor and lowly; the lowliness is recognized only through this, for for us, all year we do not eat reclining. That meal is like aninut before burial of the dead, when mourning has not yet begun.
