He wrote: And so wrote the Hagahos Asheri at the end of Taanis, and these are his words: Even though from the halachah it is implied that it is permitted to eat at the seudah hamafseket salted meat or poultry and fish, etc., nevertheless it is not proper to do so, and there is also a prohibition because of "do not forsake the Torah of your mother," end quote. And this requires further study, for it is obvious that fish meat is not included in "meat," and I did not find in any of the later authorities that he wrote fish; and also Moreinu HaRav the Rama in Darkei Moshe, when he transcribed the words of the Hagahos Asheri, did not bring fish. And it is possible that he means that every choice food is forbidden to eat at the seudah hamafseket, just as the Tur forbids every food that is in the manner of honor; and this is the reason that one should not eat two cooked dishes according to the Ri'tz Gei'as, even a dish of lentils. The Rambam wrote: In our days we never ate even a dish of lentils on erev Tisha B'Av unless it fell on Shabbos; see there. Rather, it is proper to be stringent like R' Yehudah bar Oshaiah, to whom they would bring dry bread with salt, and he would drink a pitcher of water. Therefore one must say that for this reason the Hagahos Asheri forbids a fish dish at the seudah hamafseket, because it is a food of honor that is fit for a king's table; and now in our time fish are more a food of honor than beef and poultry.
