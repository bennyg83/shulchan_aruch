(s.k. 4) That which is customary thus — one may say most times, etc.; but fish is forbidden, etc., as Tur wrote and everything similar. Many question: behold Magen Avraham wrote in small seif 2 fish are forbidden even without frying. In my humble opinion this must be per what Tur wrote — was the plain meaning not mentioned in Tur more than in Shulchan Aruch, and Shulchan Aruch wrote Tur's language? Rather Magen Avraham's intent is to settle Bach's question on Tur and disagree for the law: Tur wrote and first brought dispute whether two kinds in one pot are permitted; on this he wrote: it appears even per the stringent, something customary all year such as peas with onions and eggs is permitted — end quote. Bach brought Ramban who wrote some are stringent to forbid two kinds in one pot, even fish with egg on it cutting kaplot under the fish because they are called two dishes for eruv (i.e. Beitza 15b — eruv tavshilin) — Beit Shammai say two dishes required, Beit Hillel one dish, and they agree fish with egg are two dishes — end quote; Beit Hillel did not disagree that fish with egg are two dishes (see 17b); Ramban holds not so — it is light for eruv, not for Tisha B'Av — end quote; likewise Ran end Taanit. Bach wrote: from these words you understand our Rabbi's "it appears even per stringent, customary all year, permitted" — is not so, for fish with egg are customary all year yet some forbid; nevertheless practically Ramban may be relied upon who rejected the stringent — end quote Bach. Bach questioned Tur permitting for all a customary thing when there are stringent; in law Bach permitted fish with egg. But Magen Avraham holds Tur's words are precise for all; why Tur cited peas with onions and eggs often cooked together — therefore permitted for all; Tur omitted fish with eggs as not universal per Ramban and Ran. Reason: usually fish cooked without eggs — can be stringent; thus Bach's question on Tur is settled; in law Magen Avraham disagrees with Bach relying on Ramban to permit fish and egg — Magen Avraham holds since Tur cited peas and onions and omitted fish with eggs, he does not hold like Ramban but this too depends on dispute whether two kinds in one pot permitted; fish with eggs not customary all year are two kinds — if we are stringent on two kinds in one pot, fish with egg forbidden when not customary; but for fish with egg no difference for us who are stringent as in gloss — difference only as Magen Avraham concluded and everything similar; likewise lebibos etc. — anything not usually so even if sometimes done is called two kinds and forbidden.
