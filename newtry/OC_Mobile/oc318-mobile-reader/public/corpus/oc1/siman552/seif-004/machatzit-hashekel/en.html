(s.k. 4) Whose manner is thus; one may say most times, etc. But fish is forbidden, etc., as written in the Tur, and everything similar. Many ask: behold Magen Avraham wrote in se'if katan 2 that fish are forbidden even without frying. And in my humble opinion this requires clarification in what he wrote "as written in the Tur": was the plain meaning of the matter not mentioned in the Tur's words more than in the Shulchan Aruch? And in the Shulchan Aruch he wrote the Tur's language. Rather Magen Avraham's intent is to settle the Beis Yosef's question on the Tur and to disagree with him regarding the law. For the Tur wrote and first brought the dispute whether two kinds in one pot are permitted or not, and on this he wrote, and these are his words: And it appears that even according to the stringent, something whose manner is thus all year, such as peas in which they put onions and eggs, is permitted, end quote. And the Beis Yosef brought in the name of the Ramban who wrote, and these are his words: And there are those who are stringent to forbid two kinds in one pot, and even fish and egg upon it, and he cuts a leek under the fish, because they are called two cooked dishes regarding eruv (that is to say, for we say in tractate Beitzah folio 15b, beginning of the chapter) and he makes a cooked dish from erev Yom Tov and relies on it for Shabbos (and that is eruv tavshilin, to cook from Yom Tov to Shabbos). Beis Shammai say we require two cooked dishes for eruv tavshilin, and Beis Hillel say one cooked dish, and they agree regarding fish and egg upon it that they are two cooked dishes, end of their words. And in this Beis Hillel did not disagree with Beis Shammai that fish and egg upon it are two cooked dishes (and see folio 17b); and the Ramban holds that it is not so, for it is light regarding eruv and it is not regarding Tisha B'Av, end quote. And so wrote the Ran at the end of Taanis. And the Beis Yosef wrote, and these are his words: And from these words it will be clarified for you that what our Rabbi wrote, "And it appears that even according to the stringent, something whose manner is thus all year, etc., is permitted," is not so, for behold fish and egg upon it, its manner is thus all year, and even so there is one who forbids. Nevertheless regarding practice the Ramban is enough to rely upon, for he rejected the stringent view, end quote of the Beis Yosef. Behold the Beis Yosef asked on the Tur who permitted for everyone something whose manner is thus, when there are stringent [voices]; and for the law the Beis Yosef permitted fish and egg upon it. But Magen Avraham's view is that the Tur's words are precise for everyone, and for this reason he mentioned peas with eggs, for most times they put onions and eggs in them, therefore it is permitted for everyone; and the Tur omitted fish with eggs, for that indeed is not everyone's view, as the Ramban and Ran wrote that some say. And the reason is that most times they cook fish without eggs; therefore one may indeed be stringent. And with this the Beis Yosef's aforementioned question on the Tur is settled; and regarding law as well Magen Avraham disagrees with the Beis Yosef who relied on the Ramban to permit fish and egg; and Magen Avraham holds that since the Tur mentioned peas and onions and omitted fish with eggs, it implies that he does not hold like the Ramban, but that this too depends on the dispute whether two kinds in one pot are permitted; for fish with eggs, since their manner is not thus all year, they are two kinds; and if so, according to that which we are stringent regarding two kinds in one pot, the same applies to fish and egg, since their manner is not thus, it is forbidden; rather regarding fish and egg there is no practical difference for us who are stringent as written in the Hagahos Asheri; rather the difference is as Magen Avraham concluded, "and everything similar"; and likewise regarding lebibos, etc., that every thing whose manner is not thus most times, even though sometimes they do so, is called two kinds and is forbidden.
And Shelah wrote it is forbidden to make lebibos, etc. This is proof for Magen Avraham's words. For the lebibos are made from dough, and the cheese filling is in two things, for the dough is a food by itself, and most times they eat the dough without cheese filling; and the cheese is also a food by itself. And even though it is eaten when it is raw, nevertheless when it is cooked it is called a cooked dish as written in the Shulchan Aruch; and it is like two kinds in one pot, since most times their manner is not thus, and it is forbidden. And according to this it is not specifically cheese filling that is forbidden; the same applies if the lebibos were filled with something else, such as what they call powidla [plum jam], which is also forbidden, since also in this its manner is not thus, for most times they cook the dough by way of other dishes, and as it is without any filling at all.
