(1) [Seif 1] On erev Tisha B'Av one should not eat, etc., in order to increase mourning and to remember the destruction of the Beis HaMikdash and grieve over it. Beis David siman 327. Mishna Berurah os 1, and this is from the words of the Tur.
(2) Ibid. At the seudah ha'mafsekes [final meal before the fast], etc., this means that he does not intend to eat another fixed meal after it, as stated below in seif 9, see there.
(3) Ibid. One should not eat meat, etc., this is by the strict law of the Talmud, whereas from the standpoint of custom even beforehand it is forbidden, as stated above in siman 551 seif 9, see there. The practical difference is that even in a place where they did not adopt the custom of prohibition during the aforementioned days, at the seudah ha'mafsekes it is forbidden by the strict law of the Talmud. So too wrote the Levush.
(4) Ibid. One should not eat meat, etc. And similarly a cooked dish in which meat was cooked is forbidden, for it is superior to salted meat. Mateh Yehuda on siman 551 seif 10, Erech HaShulchan os 1. See above siman 551 os 142.
(5) Ibid. And one should not drink wine. The beverage made from raisins, it is possible that it is permitted to drink at the seudah ha'mafsekes. Beis David siman 328. But Maharash Florentin in his work Beis HaRo'eh p. 31 forbade it. Birke Yosef os 1.
(6) Ibid. And one should not drink wine. The same applies to beer, Agudah. But the custom is not to follow this. Darkei Moshe os 3. However, Maharshal in his responsa siman 31 wrote to forbid it in accordance with the Agudah, because it is considered the "wine of the land" [chamar medinah]; but he wrote that all this applies to one for whom it is possible, but one who is accustomed to drinking only beer, or if he is a weak person, is certainly permitted to drink beer even at the seudah ha'mafsekes, see there. The Knesset HaGedolah cited his words in the glosses. Taz at the beginning of the siman, Magen Avraham s.k. 1. Eliyah Rabbah os 6, Yad Aharon in the glosses. However, the Knesset HaGedolah in the glosses os 1 wrote to be strict even for a weak person, because if he were to permit it most people would make themselves out to be weak in order to drink beer, see there. However, the Eliyah Rabbah there wrote to question the words of the Knesset HaGedolah, and his view is to be lenient like the Maharshal, see there. Therefore it seems that it all depends on the person, and even where there is great need one should not permit drinking more than a small amount, enough to be able to eat the meal and no more, since it is an intoxicating substance and we are concerned that he will come to divert his attention from the grief over the destruction. See below os 8.
(7) The Eliyah Rabbah wrote there that it is obvious that a cup for birkas ha'mazon [Grace after Meals] (i.e., of beer) is permitted even according to the Maharshal. So too wrote the Mish'meres Zahav at the beginning of the siman. This follows the view of those who hold that birkas ha'mazon requires a cup even for an individual, as stated above at the beginning of siman 182. For at the seudah ha'mafsekes three people should not sit together [to form a zimun], as stated below in seif 8, see there. Accordingly, based on what was stated above in siman 182 os 1 in the name of the Zohar and the Arizal that birkas ha'mazon for an individual does not require a cup, only with three [forming a zimun], one should not be lenient to drink on account of the cup of birkas ha'mazon.
(8) The Taz wrote there that it is forbidden at the seudah ha'mafsekes even to drink mead [honey water], which is called me"d, or whiskey, for even though there is no element of joy in them, since they are intoxicating there is concern, see there. The Eliyah Rabbah cited his words there. Mish'meres Zahav ibid. Machatzis HaShekel s.k. 1.
(9) Ibid. And one should not eat two cooked dishes. The Zera Avraham in his responsa on Orach Chayyim siman 78 wrote that it is forbidden to drink coffee or tea at the seudah ha'mafsekes, because it is considered a cooked dish and is forbidden on account of two cooked dishes, unless it is a case of illness, see there. However, the Machberes os 2 wrote to prove that there is no issue of a cooked dish in this, and also there is no element of luxury or delight in coffee, for recently it has become extremely widespread and even the poor are accustomed to it. But water cooked with cane and coriander seed with sugar, which is a delight and the practice of the wealthy, that is not permitted except for illness, for one who has a stomach complaint, as written by Maharash Sha'ar Aryeh, see there. The Zechor L'Avraham os 9 and the Sha'arei Teshuvah cited his words.
(10) The two cooked dishes that were forbidden at the seudah ha'mafsekes, there is no distinction between something that is eaten raw and something that is not eaten raw; similarly, there is no distinction between roasted and cooked; similarly, there is no distinction whether one eats them as a cooked dish or as fruit for dessert. Rather, in all cases it is forbidden to eat roasted fruit as dessert at the seudah ha'mafsekes. The great Rav Maharam ibn Chaviv, author of Get Pashut, in his manuscript responsa siman 12. Birke Yosef os 12. Zechor L'Avraham ibid. Sha'arei Teshuvah os 3. Even if one cooked dates and made them into honey, or cooked sugar and made it into a drink, it is also considered a cooked dish. Ben Ish Chai Parshas Devarim os 19. Similarly, if one cooked cheese it has the status of a cooked dish and it is forbidden to eat it with another cooked dish. Tur. Levush. Ben Ish Chai ibid. Similarly, if one cooked the milk it has the status of a cooked dish. Levush. See below os 13.
(11) I have heard that some rabbis permitted eating salad at the seudah ha'mafsekes, that is, raw vegetables with oil and vinegar, and after the salad to eat one cooked dish. Although there are grounds to permit this, since it is not comparable to the radish and salted food that the Mordechai forbade, nor to the cucumbers pickled in vinegar that the Knesset HaGedolah forbade, for here, the salad in these cities is a main part of the meal, and it comes not merely to stimulate the appetite but also to provide sustenance from it, nevertheless, it appears to me that it is proper to be strict. Machberes os 2, Zechor L'Avraham ibid. See below os 15.
(12) To add the juice of unripe grapes or vinegar to the cooked dish that one eats at the seudah ha'mafsekes is certainly permitted. Zera Avraham in his responsa on Orach Chayyim siman 79. The Machberes os 3 cited his words and wrote that this is obvious. Zechor L'Avraham ibid. Sha'arei Teshuvah.
