A fixed meal. Not like those whose belly is the belly of the wicked, who eat meat and drink wine and become drunk, and afterward eat incidental food in order to interrupt with an eating that has no meat and wine (Tur, Ramban). And even though in our country they do not eat meat, nevertheless there is a prohibition when they eat two cooked dishes; and the Levush and Bach and Shelah cry out against this custom; and one who is concerned for the custom that the Rama wrote should increase in the meal before midday.
That it not harm them, etc. And it appears to me another reason: because in the time of the Second Temple, Tisha B'Av was a festival day and they increased in the meal; even now they have not moved from it, to be a remembrance that it will be turned for them into joy and gladness; see in Beis Yosef at the end of siman 554.
From Midrash Eichah. Folio 23a it is stated there that after they eat and drink and become drunk at the meal of Tisha B'Av they sit and read kinchos (Darkei Moshe); and on folio 75 it is stated: Rav, when he would eat all the foods, would take one charcoal loaf and put ash on it and say, this is the meal of Tisha B'Av; but in the Yerushalmi the reading is: Rav, when he would eat all his needs, would take, etc., and say, this is the main meal of Tisha B'Av; if so one may say that he would not eat more than one cooked dish; and also the Midrash one may explain thus; and what it wrote "they become drunk" means that they drink all their need; therefore one should be careful that even before Minchah he eat only one cooked dish; and so wrote in Darkei Moshe that the scrupulous separate themselves; and even though after Minchah he eats eggs, the two cooked dishes do not combine, since he interrupted with a gift [of prayer], as above.
