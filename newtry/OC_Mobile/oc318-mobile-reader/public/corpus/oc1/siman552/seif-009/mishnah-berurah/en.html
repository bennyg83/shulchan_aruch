(20) And his intention is to eat a fixed meal after it. But if afterward he eats only incidental eating, it is not considered a meal at all; and if so the previous meal is the main meal, and it is forbidden to eat two cooked dishes in it.
(21) And afterward they pray Minchah, etc. In Darkei Moshe in the name of Maharil it is stated that they go to the beis midrash and pray Minchah, and presumably he also recited Birkas HaMazon at first.
(22) To increase a bit, etc. And many of the later authorities are not at ease with this: to increase at first in several kinds of cooked dishes and to delight in them, and this is his main meal, and after Minchah when he is not hungry and not thirsty at all he eats as a remembrance of seudah hamafseket, and it is only like incidental eating; and therefore there are among them who say that before Minchah he should eat only one cooked dish, and after Minchah he should eat lentils or eggs as above; and even though this too is in the category of a cooked dish, they do not combine with each other. And Eliyah Rabbah wrote to justify the custom somewhat, and these are his words: I say that so long as he intends for the sake of Heaven with the aforementioned intention [namely that the fast not harm him], permission is in his hand; but in any case every person should see that he not satiate himself more than needed, and the wise man's eyes are in his head, so that he can eat after Minchah the seudah hamafseket that it not be like coarse eating and incidental, end quote. And at the seudah hamafseket he may eat bread with a cooked dish, and they do not combine with each other since he recited Birkas HaMazon and also prayed Minchah in the middle.
