All of this is at the seudah hamafseket, etc. The Rambam wrote: In our days we never ate even a dish of lentils on erev Tisha B'Av; and the Tur brought this, and afterward wrote "and all of this is at the seudah hamafseket," etc., implying that the Rambam's language refers specifically to the seudah hamafseket. And I wonder what the novelty is: if he already ate good foods at the seudah hamafseket, he did not eat a cooked dish? One must say that he did not eat twice, as we do, eating before Minchah a fixed meal and after Minchah prayer a seudah hamafseket; rather he ate only one meal, and it was the seudah hamafseket; and at that meal which was his fixed meal he did not even eat a dish of lentils; but before that, namely before midday, he ate many cooked dishes. And these are the words of the Tur in the name of the Ramban: And it is reasonable regarding the meal with which one interrupts, that he has no intention to eat a fixed meal after it afterward, even though he intends to eat incidental food afterward; not like those whose belly is the belly of the wicked, who eat meat and drink wine and become drunk, and afterward eat incidental food in order to interrupt with an eating that has no meat and wine, end quote. And Maharshal wrote in Responsa, siman 30, that that the Ramban mentioned meat and wine is not precisely because of meat and wine that he calls them the belly of the wicked; rather the Ramban deals according to the Gemara when meat and wine were permitted before the seudah hamafseket; nevertheless it is the belly of the wicked when they eat delicacies to full need and then eat incidental food afterward; and the same applies to other eatings that they make of kinds of fried foods as for the festival of Shavuos in our country, etc.; and I say their iniquity is too great to bear, especially for those who know Torah, end quote. And so my father-in-law, of blessed memory, concluded that every God-fearing person should not eat after midday a meal with many cooked dishes, for what they eat afterward as the seudah hamafseket with fruit on the ground is incidental eating; and a conscientious person who does not wish to change the custom that the glosses of the Shulchan Aruch wrote, should increase in the meal before midday, end quote.
