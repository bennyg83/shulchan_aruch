Se'if 11. One who accepted, etc. Because of danger; and see above siman 624, se'if 5 in the gloss.
Or he should borrow, etc. For they hold that it is not called "this day" except if the day causes it, like the day his father died and the like, as written in the Hagahos Asheri, first chapter of Shabbos; most poskim disagree; and as written above in siman 568, se'if 2 in the gloss. He brought in Terumat HaDeshen a proof from what is written in the first chapter of Taanis: an individual who accepted upon himself Monday and Thursday of the entire year, and a festival occurred in them, etc., and they did not say that he should borrow and repay; and that is what is written in the gloss, "And the Geonim," etc.; and they brought a proof from what is written "and they fast by hours"; and this requires further study, for we say "and that is when he has not tasted," etc. And it appears that the Geonim relied on the Yerushalmi of Nedarim, chapter 9, halachah 1: a matter of R' Yose said they fast by hours, for R' Yose said, "Behold I am in my fast until I finish my chapter," until I finish my teaching; a matter of R' Yonah said they fast by hours; R' Yonah was in Tyre and heard that the son of R' Yose had died; even though he ate cheese and drank water, he raised a fast the whole of that day; a matter of Rav said they fast by hours, for Rav said a person may borrow his fast and repay; Shmuel said to him, And is it a vow?, etc.; and see the Rosh and Mordechai. Nevertheless even according to the Yerushalmi, specifically when he did not accept a fast except by hours, as the Rosh wrote; and so the plain meaning of the Yerushalmi implies; and likewise what is written "a person may borrow," etc., that he must fast another day; and nevertheless regarding fulfillment of his vow that he accepted to fast today, for this a fast of hours counts for him; and it is possible that the Geonim also deal with such a case; and so wrote the Taz; and this requires further study. And even according to Ravyah's explanation who explained "when he has not tasted," etc., that is within the measure of hours that he accepted upon himself; but beforehand, even if he ate, nevertheless it is not counted as a full-day fast that he accepted; and as written in the Gemara: They challenged: Men of, etc. And it appears that the Geonim explain specifically regarding a dream-fast and the like; but whenever he accepted only to afflict himself, it is permitted to eat before evening because of a mitzvah and the like; and as written there: They challenged: Men of, etc.; come and hear, for R' Elazar ben Tzadok said, etc.
