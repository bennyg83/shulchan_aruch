And the Geonim wrote, etc. It appears that they hold that one may not borrow the fast and repay, for the reason we will write nearby, namely to eat immediately on this day and repay afterward. And these are the words of Terumat HaDeshen, siman 275, in the name of Or Zarua: An individual who accepted upon himself a Monday-Thursday fast all the days of the year, and erev Tisha B'Av occurred on Monday: what should he do? To feast twice is forbidden because of his vow; rather he feasts once before the sun sets, for we hold that one may fast by hours, and he prays Minchah the fast prayer, and afterward he feasts, end quote. And there is a difficulty: since he accepted upon himself a plain fast, he must complete it as written in siman 562, se'if 3; if so, how may he eat before sunset? It is well regarding a dream-fast and a yahrzeit, which do not depend on his acceptance; which is not so here, where it depends on what he accepted upon himself, and he is obligated to complete, and whoever does not complete is as if he did not fast at all. And what he wrote that we hold that one may fast by hours: that is not so; that is if he accepted explicitly by hours, but if he accepted plainly, that is a full day, no. One must say that they hold that each day separately needs acceptance, and it teaches us that on this day before Tisha B'Av he should accept the fast explicitly that he will not complete; but this is not so, for whenever he accepted upon himself the whole year, the Rama wrote in siman 562, se'if 8, that one acceptance suffices; and if so he is obligated in every fast to complete it. And it appears that the Geonim intended that in any case he become obligated to repay by fasting another day. And proof from the words of the Rosh in the first chapter of Taanis, who brings that a person may borrow his fast and repay, and wrote on this in the name of Ravyah: And I saw my teachers who would require permitting the vow of his fast with three [judges], for since he said "behold I am fasting tomorrow," it is like a vow; and it appears that since he must repay, the vow is not nullified; rather by estimation of intent we say that his intent was to fast one day and not to fix one day as obligatory. And also from the Yerushalmi there is proof, that something Rav said implies one may fast by hours, for Rav said a person may borrow his fast and repay; and according to our Talmud, that every fast upon which the sun has not set is not a fast, it is impossible to explain except that what he fasted part of the day is called a fast for this purpose, that he need not seek annulment of his vow, for what he fasted part of the day is called a fast, end quote. And in the concise rulings of the Rosh he also wrote, and these are his words: And a person may borrow his fast and repay after he has fasted part of the day, end quote. If so, the Geonim's view is likewise on erev Tisha B'Av that falls on Monday, that he is obligated to fast because of the vow at least a fast of hours, and afterward he borrows the fast and pays on another full day after Tisha B'Av; for only in this manner, that he fasted part of the day, can he afterward make a loan; and that is that they permitted borrowing only when he is in pain, and then he need not fast at all that day; and here when he is not in pain, his permission is after he has fasted part of the day. Also in Terumat HaDeshen, siman 275, he concludes, and these are his words: And reason inclines in our case, namely that he vowed to fast all year Monday-Thursday, that one may not borrow the fast except for duress or real pain or for a meal of mitzvah that applies to them; and even so he must fast part of the day a short time more than he is accustomed, end quote. Even though according to the beginning of his words it does not imply as I wrote, nevertheless according to his conclusion it appears so; rather his view is to be stringent even when he has pain, that nevertheless he should wait a bit; and automatically when he has no pain he should wait until close to sunset, and afterward repay another day. Nevertheless it appears that the Geonim's view is as I wrote, as it appears to me in this; and see what I wrote in siman 568; and as I recalled, so it is also in Mordechai, first chapter of Taanis, on this, and these are his words: However he is obligated to repay it, for he accepted upon himself a full day.
