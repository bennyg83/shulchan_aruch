1) [Se'if 1] If one erred or was prevented by circumstances, etc. See Taz, paragraph 1, and what we wrote above in siman 93, paragraph 15, with Hashem's help.
2) There: The first counts for the present obligation and the second as compensation, etc. Levush explains that the frequent takes precedence over the infrequent, a principle derived from Scripture. Maadanei Yom Tov, chapter Tefillas HaShachar, paragraph 400, questions this and gives another reason: otherwise the enactment may be undermined, and a person may come to pray the compensation even when it is not yet time for the second prayer. Since one who puts the compensation first must repeat it, he will not negligently pray it after its own time but before the time of the second prayer. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1, challenges this reason as well and explains that the compensatory prayer is accepted only together with the coming prayer; if one prays the compensation before the coming prayer, it is not accepted because its time has passed. With Hashem's help, it appears to me that a mystical reason can be given from Shaar HaKavanos, discourse on the changing prayers, folio 59b. In Shacharis the mochin are drawn into the inner vessels, at Minchah into the middle vessels, and at Maariv into the outer vessels. If one first prays the compensatory prayer, it is inappropriate because the present spiritual aspect is different. If he first prays the prayer corresponding to the present aspect, the mochin of that aspect are drawn down; all the more should the mochin of the preceding aspect then be drawn. This opens the way for him also to pray the missed prayer, so the mochin will be drawn in completeness.
3) There: If he reversed them, he did not fulfill his obligation, etc. This means that he intended the first prayer as compensation; he did not fulfill his obligation. Darkei Moshe, paragraph 1; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1; Siddur Beis Oved, laws of compensation after Maariv, paragraph 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1; Ben Ish Chai, Parashas Mishpatim, paragraph 9; Nahar Shalom, paragraph 2. Some say, however, that he need not repeat the prayer unless he explicitly revealed in his prayer that the second was the present obligation and the first compensation; for example, on Motzaei Shabbos, if he recited Havdalah in the second but not the first. If he merely reversed them in thought, he fulfilled his obligation. Taz, paragraph 2; Magen Avraham, paragraph 2; Soles Belulah, paragraph 2; Chayei Adam, rule 27, paragraph 4. Chida in Kesher Gudal, siman 22, paragraph 11, appears to agree. Shulchan Aruch HaRav cites this view and writes that one should take account of it by introducing a novel request when repeating the prayer. All opinions then agree there is no concern of a blessing in vain, as stated in siman 107, se'if 1. Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham, paragraph 2, writes likewise. If he prayed without any intention which prayer was obligatory and which compensatory; for example, he knew only that a compensation must be prayed but not which comes first; it appears that he need not repeat. His presumed intent is to follow the law, and since the law assigns the first prayer to the present obligation, we say that this is how the prayers were accepted before our Father in Heaven. Pri Megadim remains uncertain whether one who is unsure if he prayed Shacharis may compensate after its time at Minchah. We already addressed this above in siman 107, paragraph 2; consult that discussion.
4) There: He must repeat and pray it. He repeats only the compensatory prayer. The order will then be correct: the present obligatory prayer followed by the compensatory prayer.
