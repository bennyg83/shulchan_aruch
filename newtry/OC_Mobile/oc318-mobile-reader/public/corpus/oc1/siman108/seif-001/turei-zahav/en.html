Or he was compelled, etc. Yoreh De'ah siman 341 wrote in seif 2: one for whom a relative died on Shabbos at Maariv does not pray, and in the morning need not pray twice to complete the evening prayer, since its time has passed; and it is not comparable to one who forgot and did not pray Maariv, who prays Shacharis twice, since at night he was not obligated to pray—end quote. And he wrote there in Derishah, and these are his words: From here it would appear the same for one occupied with communal needs and the like at the time of prayer who is exempt from praying, as Tur wrote siman 93; and from the occupation the time of that prayer passed—he also need not complete it at the time of the prayer after it by praying two, one for tashlumin, since at the time of the occupation too he was exempt from prayer, as with mourning—for here, what difference to me whether compulsion of mourning or occupation of a mitzvah; and in my opinion all the more so, for in communal needs too he performs the service of God—end quote. And I wonder if these words came from the mouth of that righteous one, for whoever is under compulsion is called exempt from prayer; yet here we state explicitly that if compelled he prays twice. And further, Terumat HaDeshen wrote siman 5 regarding one who was preoccupied before the official on account of a debt and could not be dismissed except with monetary loss, and in the midst the prayer time passed—which is called compulsion even though on account of money—and he prays afterward twice: behold explicitly, even though he was exempt at that hour of prayer on account of preoccupation that is called compulsion; and likewise too exempt on account of preoccupation with a mitzvah of communal needs—for the entire main reason of that responsum of Terumat HaDeshen is because it is considered like preoccupation with a mitzvah; and if so, the opinion of the author of Derishah is hidden on this, and there is no comparison to the exemption of mourning—for there it is possible for him to pray, for he sits idle, only the Sages exempted him; therefore tashlumin is not applicable there, for that is not called compulsion but exemption; unlike here, where there is no exemption upon him but there is compulsion upon him that he could not pray for lack of leisure; and it is like one who is ill who cannot pray or one who is drunk—all this is called compulsion since he himself is subject to obligation; and for this certainly they enacted tashlumin—as appears straightforward to my humble opinion.
