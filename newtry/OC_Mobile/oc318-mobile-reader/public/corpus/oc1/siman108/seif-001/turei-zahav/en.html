Or was prevented, etc. Yoreh Deah, siman 341, seif 2, rules that one whose relative died on Shabbos does not pray Maariv at Motzaei Shabbos, and in the morning need not pray twice to compensate for Maariv, because its time passed while he was not obligated. This differs from one who forgot Maariv, who prays Shacharis twice. The Derishah inferred that the same applies to one occupied with communal needs or a similar mitzvah during the time of prayer, who was exempt from prayer until its time passed. He too would not compensate at the next prayer, since during that activity he was exempt, just as an onen is exempt. Indeed, communal work is itself service of Hashem. I am astonished that such words could have issued from that righteous scholar. Every unavoidable prevention exempts a person at that moment, yet the Gemara states explicitly that one who was prevented must pray twice. Moreover, Terumas HaDeshen, responsum 5, discusses one detained by an official over a debt who could not leave without financial loss until the time for prayer passed. He is considered prevented and afterward prays twice, even though he was exempt at the time because of his preoccupation. The same applies when he is prevented by a mitzvah such as communal needs. The exemption of an onen is different. He is physically able to pray and is sitting idle, but the Sages exempted him. There is therefore no compensation, because this is not called unavoidable prevention but a formal exemption. One occupied with communal needs has no formal exemption from the obligation itself. Rather, he is obligated but prevented from praying because he has no opportunity, like one who is ill and cannot pray or one who is intoxicated. Such cases are considered unavoidable prevention, and compensation was enacted for them. This appears clear.
