Erred or was compelled, etc. — erred means he thought he had prayed and afterward remembered he had not prayed, or he forgot to pray, as below in seif 8. Peri Megadim wrote: I was uncertain — perhaps he prayed Shacharit or not, and the time for Shacharit passed — whether he makes it up in Minchah or not, for perhaps they did not enact make-up after its time passed except when he certainly did not pray, not in a case of doubt:

And if he inverted — see Mishna Berurah that we were silent on the law like the Mechaber, and so the Gra in his commentary — when he copied the source on Avodah Zarah he did not note at all the difficulty of Magen Avraham on this; apparently he does not hold like Magen Avraham. And know that it is obvious that specifically if he intended explicitly for a make-up prayer; but if he prayed both without specification, according to all he fulfilled his obligation. And I was uncertain — if he began to pray it as a make-up prayer and remembered in the middle and completed it properly, that is, for the sake of obligation — whether this helps; and my uncertainty is — since we hold that all the blessings of the Amidah were stated in order, as below in siman 119, perhaps through this it is considered one matter and negating the intent would help; and even though regarding several matters we find only the first three are considered as one, perhaps regarding negating intent alone it is different; and apparently there is proof from what was stated above in siman 107 seif 1 — if he began to pray, etc., he stops even in the middle of a blessing; and it is stated in the rishonim the reason — since he began with intent of obligation he cannot finish it as a voluntary prayer; and if so, here too negating his intent does not help and he must stop it in the middle, or perhaps regarding make-up prayers the Sages were lenient — and this requires further study:
