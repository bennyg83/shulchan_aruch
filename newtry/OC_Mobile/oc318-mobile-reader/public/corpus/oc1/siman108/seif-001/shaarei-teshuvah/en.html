Birkei Yosef writes that one who was prevented by circumstances beyond his control from praying Maariv, and at Shacharis prayed silently for his current obligation, then intended during the chazzan’s repetition to fulfill the compensatory Maariv prayer, has fulfilled his obligation. This is explained in siman 124, where one who forgot Yaaleh VeYavo or a similar insertion may intend to fulfill his obligation through the chazzan’s repetition. The same applies here. Although the chazzan intends to discharge the Shacharis obligation of one who is not proficient, while this listener intends the repetition as compensation for Maariv, this presents no difficulty, since the blessings are identical. This accords with what Magen Avraham cites here from the responsum of Radbaz, that the chazzan himself fulfills his obligation through the repetition, and the same applies to one who intends to fulfill his obligation through the chazzan’s prayer. Although siman 124 discusses specifically one who omitted an insertion, while a proficient person ordinarily does not fulfill the prayer itself through the chazzan’s repetition, one who relies on the chazzan because he missed the prayer through circumstances beyond his control is comparable to one who forgot a required insertion. Moreover, this is after the fact, since he already intended during the chazzan’s repetition that it serve as compensation. There is also proof from siman 268, where the Shulchan Aruch rules that one who forgot to say Atah Kidashta fulfills his obligation by hearing the abridged seven-faceted blessing. Beit Yosef explains there that this is because Maariv is voluntary. All the more so here, where he heard the entire prayer, does it count as compensation for Maariv, which is voluntary. See there.
