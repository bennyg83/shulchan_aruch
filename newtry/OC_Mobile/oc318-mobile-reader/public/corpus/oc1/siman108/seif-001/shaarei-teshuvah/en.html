Twice. Ba'er Heitev. And Be'er HaGolah wrote: One who was compelled and did not pray Maariv, and at Shacharis prayed quietly for his obligation, and at the repetition of the prayer of the shaliach tzibbur intends to fulfill through it the Maariv prayer as tashlumin—he has fulfilled, as explained in siman 124, that if one forgot Yaaleh VeYavo and the like one may intend at the repetition of the shaliach tzibbur, etc.; and similarly one like him. And even though the shaliach tzibbur intends to discharge one who is not expert in the Shacharis prayer and he intends for Maariv, we have no concern, since the blessings are mutually comparable; and as Magen Avraham wrote here from the responsum of Radbaz that the shaliach tzibbur fulfills through the repetition; and the same applies to one who intends for the prayer of the shaliach tzibbur. And even though in siman 124 it speaks specifically without mentioning, but the prayer itself—the expert does not fulfill through the repetition of the shaliach tzibbur—nevertheless, one who relies on the shaliach tzibbur who did not pray on account of his compulsion when he forgot and did not mention what he must—it is comparable; and also that it is after the fact, for he already intended at the repetition of the shaliach tzibbur for tashlumin. And proof from siman 288: one who forgot and did not mention "Atah kadosh," that Shulchan Aruch rules he fulfills through hearing a blessing akin to seven. And Beit Yosef wrote there the reason: because the Maariv prayer is optional; if so, all the more so here, where he heard the entire prayer and since he fulfilled through it tashlumin for Maariv, which is optional—see there.
