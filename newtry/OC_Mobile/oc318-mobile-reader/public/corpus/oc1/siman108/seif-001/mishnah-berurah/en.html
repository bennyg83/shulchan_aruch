(1) He erred — the intent is to exclude intentional [omission], as below in seif 7. One who did not pray because one's mind was not properly settled on it, even though one acted improperly in this, as above in siman 101 seif 1 and siman 98 seif 2 — nevertheless this is not in the category of intentional [omission], and teshuvah (make-up) helps him.

(2) Or was compelled — such as he was ill or drunk, as below in seif 8. Regarding an onen for whom prayer time passed until he buried his dead, see above in siman 71 seif 1 and in Mishnah Berurah there. One who at prayer time had no water to wash one's hands, and thereby prayer time passed — this too is compulsion; one must afterward pray twice. Ab initio it is not proper to annul prayer because of water, as above in siman 92 seif 4 and in Mishnah Berurah — see there. If one was occupied with communal needs, see above at the end of siman 93 in Mishnah Berurah; and see in Pri Megadim in this siman that he wrote it is proper that here too one pray afterward to complete the prayer one lacked, but one prays it as a voluntary prayer and innovates something in it.

(3) And did not pray the morning prayer — regarding Shema, whether it has teshuvah, see above at the end of siman 58 and what is written there in Mishnah Berurah; see there further that the blessings of Shema have no teshuvah according to all opinions — that is, even if one wished to recite them when reading Shema again with its blessings, for the Sages instituted teshuvah only for prayer, which is supplication for mercy.

(4) The afternoon prayer, etc. — when one is the prayer leader, one fulfills teshuvah by repeating the prayer aloud and having intent in it for oneself as teshuvah, and also to discharge one who is not expert.

(5) Twice — it is not enough to intend to hear the prayer leader and fulfill through it for oneself as teshuvah, whether for evening or morning, for they did not institute that one fulfill through the prayer leader's repetition except specifically for one who is not expert; nevertheless, after the fact one fulfills if this was for completing the evening prayer. And although in siman 124 the halachah is that even after the fact one who is not expert does not fulfill through the prayer leader's repetition — nevertheless here, since it is teshuvah for the evening prayer which is optional, it is lenient.

(6) The first is the afternoon prayer — for one must precede the prayer that is the obligation of the hour to the teshuvah prayer.

(7) And if one reversed — meaning, it was in one's mind that the first would be for teshuvah — one does not fulfill, for the Sages did not institute teshuvah except while engaged in the obligatory prayer, not before. Some are uncertain whether one need not return and pray unless one revealed one's intent explicitly in one's prayer that the second is for obligation and the first for teshuvah — such as on Shabbat night when one made havdalah in the second and not in the first, as will be explained. For the law, the Peri Chadash and Pri Megadim wrote that we have only the Mechaber's words, and the Daat Torah so ruled; nevertheless it is better when returning to pray that one stipulate and say: if I am not obligated to pray a second time, I pray this as a voluntary prayer.

(8) One does not fulfill — whether one reversed intentionally or unintentionally.

(9) And must return, etc. — but if one prayed the second for make-up and the first as obligation, and erred in the obligatory one and did not say tal umatar in a way requiring return, and one did not remember this until after the second prayer — nevertheless the second counts as make-up, and one prays afterward as obligation. And although the obligatory prayer was in error, we do not say it is as one who did not pray at all regarding this matter, because when one prayed the second for make-up it was still prayer time. The same applies whenever one prayed the first prayer as obligation and erred in it by forgetting or adding in a way requiring return and repeat; Magen Giborim wrote all this if one explicitly intended the second for make-up. But if one did not intend, it is better that the second count as obligation and one pray afterward for make-up, for thereby one fulfills according to all opinions.
