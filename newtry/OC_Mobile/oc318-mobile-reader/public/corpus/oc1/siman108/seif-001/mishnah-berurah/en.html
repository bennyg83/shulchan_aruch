Erred. His intent is to exclude one who intentionally neglected prayer, as below in seif 7. One who did not pray because his mind was not properly composed, although he acted improperly in this, as above in siman 101, seif 1, and siman 98, seif 2, nevertheless this is not included in intentional neglect, and makeup prayer helps for him.
Or was under compulsion. For example, he was sick or drunk, as below in seif 8. Regarding an onein whose time for prayer passed before he buried his dead, see above in siman 71, seif 1, and the Mishnah Berurah there. If at the time of prayer he had no water with which to wash his hands and because of this the time of prayer passed, this too is included in compulsion, and afterward he must pray two prayers. Initially, however, it is not proper to annul prayer because of water, as above in siman 92, seif 4, and in the Mishnah Berurah there. If he was occupied with communal needs, see above at the end of siman 93 in the Mishnah Berurah, and see the Peri Megadim in this siman, who wrote that it is proper in this case also to pray afterward to make up the prayer he missed; but he should pray it as a voluntary prayer and introduce something new into it.
And did not pray Shacharis. Regarding Krias Shema, whether it has makeup, see above at the end of siman 58 and what was written there in the Mishnah Berurah. See there further that the blessings of Krias Shema have no makeup according to all opinions; that is, even if he wishes to bless them when he reads Krias Shema again with its blessings, for Chazal instituted makeup only for prayer, which is a request for mercy.
Minchah, etc. When he is the shaliach tzibbur, he fulfills the makeup prayer through his repetition of the prayer aloud, provided that he intends it for himself as makeup and also to discharge one who is not expert.
Two. It is not enough for him to intend to hear from the shaliach tzibbur and fulfill for himself the makeup prayer, whether for Ma'ariv or Shacharis, for they instituted that one fulfill through the shaliach tzibbur's repetition only specifically for one who is not expert. Nevertheless, after the fact he fulfills if this was for making up Ma'ariv, for although in siman 124 we hold that even after the fact an expert does not fulfill through the repetition of the shaliach tzibbur, here, since it is makeup for Ma'ariv, which is voluntary, it is lighter.
The first is Minchah. For he must give precedence to the prayer that is the obligation of the present time over the makeup prayer.
And if he reversed. Meaning that he had in mind that the first prayer should be for makeup, he has not fulfilled his obligation, for the Sages instituted makeup only during involvement with the obligatory prayer, not before it. Some question this and say that he need not go back and pray unless he explicitly revealed in his prayer that the second was for the obligation and the first for makeup, such as on Motzaei Shabbos, where he made Havdalah in the second but not in the first, as will be explained. As a matter of law, the Pri Chadash and Peri Megadim wrote that we have only the words of the Mechaber, and the Derech HaChaim also recorded this as halacha. Nevertheless, it is better that when he repeats the prayer he stipulate and say: if I am not obligated to pray a second time, I pray it as a voluntary prayer.
He has not fulfilled. Whether he reversed them intentionally or inadvertently.
And he must go back, etc. If, however, he prayed the second prayer for makeup and the first for the obligation, and he erred in the obligatory prayer by not saying Tal U'Matar in a case where he must go back, and he did not realize this until after the second prayer, nevertheless the second prayer counts as the makeup prayer, and afterward he prays for the obligation. Although the obligatory prayer was in error, we do not say that it is as though he had not prayed at all for this purpose, since in any event, when he prayed the second prayer for makeup, it was a time of prayer. The same applies in any similar case where he prayed the first prayer for the obligation and erred in it by omitting or adding something in a way that requires him to go back and pray. The Magen Gibborim wrote that all this is if he explicitly intended the second as makeup, but if he did not intend, it is better that the second count for the obligation and that he then pray afterward for makeup, for in this way he satisfies all opinions.
