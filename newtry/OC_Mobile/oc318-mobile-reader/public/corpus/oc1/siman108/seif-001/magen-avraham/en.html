He prays Minchah. And when he is prayer leader he fulfills [his obligation] through what he repeats the prayer in a loud voice [Radbaz siman 361, 25].

And if the reverse. Explanation: he had in mind that the first would be for make-up — he does not fulfill [Darkei Moshe and so Beit Yosef]. It requires examination, since in the Gemara they conclude with a difficulty; however Rosh wrote, thus Rav Hai Gaon: one could say, since he did not make havdalah in the first and made havdalah in the second, he revealed his mind that he prayed the first as make-up and does not fulfill; however, if he did not make havdalah in one of them, or made havdalah in both, he fulfills, for there is no revelation of mind here that the first is for make-up. So too from what they challenge to say: since he did not make havdalah in the first, it is as one who did not pray — why do we return him? It implies that as one who prayed he fulfills both, even though he did not make havdalah in the first and made havdalah in the second — the first counted for obligation and the second for make-up, end quote. It is clear we require revelation of mind specifically; but with thought alone he fulfills. Were it not for Darkei Moshe's words, one could say here too it deals with one who revealed his mind, such as he prayed first without Ashrei and afterward said Ashrei and [the Amidah of] eighteen [blessings] as they say in Minchah; nevertheless it requires examination, for this is not called revelation of mind except when he revealed his mind within the prayer, such as not making havdalah. So too in Chiddushei HaRashba: if he made havdalah in both, even though he intended the second for the obligation of its time, he fulfills — we say perhaps he erred and made havdalah; if so, it is not called revelation of mind, see there.
