He prays Minchah. When he is the chazzan, he fulfills his obligation through his repetition of the Amidah aloud. Radbaz, responsum 361; Knesses HaGedolah.
And if he reversed the order. This means that he intended the first prayer as compensation, and therefore did not fulfill his obligation. Darkei Moshe, and this is also implied by Beit Yosef. This requires study, since the Gemara concludes with a difficulty. The Rosh, however, writes: Rav Hai Gaon wrote that one may say that because he did not recite Havdalah in the first prayer but did so in the second, he revealed that he had prayed the first as compensation, and he did not fulfill his obligation. If, however, he omitted Havdalah from both, or recited it in both, he fulfilled his obligation, because there is no indication that the first was intended as compensation. This is also implied by the Gemara’s question: “Does this mean that because he did not recite Havdalah in the first, it is considered as though he did not pray, so that we require him to repeat it?” This implies that if it is considered as though he did pray, he fulfills both obligations, even though he omitted Havdalah from the first and recited it in the second. The first then counts for the current obligation and the second as compensation. Thus, it is clear that an overt indication is specifically required, whereas intention alone does not invalidate the order. Were it not for the words of Darkei Moshe, one might explain that here too he revealed his intention, such as by first praying without Ashrei and afterward saying Ashrei and the Amidah, in the manner done at Minchah. Nevertheless, this requires study, because such conduct is not considered an overt indication. An indication exists only where it is revealed within the prayer itself, such as by omitting Havdalah. This is also implied in Chochmas Manoach, which states that if he recited Havdalah in both prayers, even though he intended the second for the current obligation, he fulfilled his obligation, because we may say that he mistakenly recited Havdalah. Accordingly, this is not considered an overt indication. See there.
