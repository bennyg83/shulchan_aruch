(Marginal note 4) He should say, etc. — for otherwise why stop with Ashrei? The reason for saying Ashrei is as written in siman 105: where one prays two prayers, afterward he must wait between them; since he must wait, it is better that he say Ashrei in that waiting time so he stand in prayer from words of Torah. One cannot say Tachanun immediately after the first prayer — he need not say Ashrei, for in any case he stopped with Tachanun. Levushei Serad wrote that after Tachanun, etc., one must adjoin Tachanun to the prayer it belongs to and not interrupt between prayer and Tachanun, as Eliyah Rabbah wrote in siman 131. That which he wrote one should stop with Ashrei and stopping with Tachanun does not suffice — Tachanun is not a stopping matter, for there are many days they do not say Tachanun; therefore it implies on days they say Tachanun it suffices to stop with Tachanun. He should say Ashrei and Lamenatzeiach and Uva LeTzion after the second prayer — not as the implication of Sefer Orach Chayyim.
