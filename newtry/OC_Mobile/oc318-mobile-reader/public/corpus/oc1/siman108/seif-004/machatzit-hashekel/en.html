(Seif katan 4) He should say, etc. For otherwise, why would he need to interrupt with Ashrei? The reason for saying Ashrei is as written in siman 105, that when one prays two prayers one after the other, he must wait between them; and since he must wait, it is better that he say Ashrei during the time that he waits, so that he will stand in prayer from words of Torah. One cannot say that if he says Tachanun immediately after the first prayer he no longer needs to say Ashrei, since in any event he interrupted with Tachanun. Lechem Chamudos wrote that after Tachanun, etc., for Tachanun should be joined to the prayer to which it belongs, and one should not interrupt between the prayer and Tachanun, as written at the beginning of siman 131. The reason he wrote that one should interrupt with Ashrei and that an interruption of Tachanun does not suffice is that it is not a fixed matter, since there are many days when Tachanun is not said. According to this, it implies that on days when Tachanun is said, it suffices to interrupt with Tachanun, and he should say Ashrei, Lamnatzeiach, and Uva Letziyon after the second prayer, unlike the implication of the Eliyah Rabbah.
