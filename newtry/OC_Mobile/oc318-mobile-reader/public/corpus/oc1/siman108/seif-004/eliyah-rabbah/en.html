(4) That there is no time, etc. — On the morrow after midnight before the time of Minchah arrives, and likewise if he did not pray Minchah he completes at the time of Maariv prayer and not all night after the time of Maariv prayer ends (Levush). Malbushai Yom Tov wrote: and when its time comes, and behold he waited a long time after praying Maariv — since he is not engaged in prayer there is no time here for make-up prayers — up to here. In my humble opinion it is possible the Levush comes to exclude after midnight, for the Sages enacted its law before midnight as below siman 235 regarding Kerias Shema, and for us regarding prayer it follows after Kerias Shema; or it deals with excluding after dawn rises as below siman 235 seif 4, and it requires examination. Also what Malbushai Yom Tov wrote that if he waited a long time, etc. — how much is the measure of waiting; I saw in Lechem Chamudos page 30 his wording that if he left the matter of prayer and went on his way he does not complete — up to here, implying that as soon as he left the matter of prayer he does not complete. In Perishah I found his wording: he should not delay except the measure of walking four amos or saying Ashrei, and immediately pray the make-up prayer — up to here. But the main law I wrote in Eliyahu Zuta requires examination, that they deduced thus from Rashba that Beit Yosef brought, who wrote "and he is engaged in his prayer," etc. In my humble opinion it is possible it is not precise wording, but all the time of prayer he is engaged in prayer we call it, meaning he finished; but not in the time of prayer, etc. — implying the main matter depends on the time of prayer; so too it seems from the words of Shulchan Aruch and Levush, and so is explained in Beit Yosef siman 89 who explained Rashba's words thus — see there and examine well. So too it seems to me in Hagahah Semag siman 11 and Tashbetz siman 223 who wrote: if he forgot and did not pray Minchah until morning he does not pray Minchah since the time of two prayers passed — up to here. It implies that even if he prayed Maariv at the beginning of the night he may pray Minchah until morning; nevertheless one should be concerned and stringent to pray it as a voluntary prayer.**** END BLOCK ****
