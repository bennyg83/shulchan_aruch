Section 12 — One who errs and mentions an occurrence from other days, etc. NB: I was asked during Chanukah about someone who said "on the miracles" in the blessing of Avodah before "ve-Tachazinah," and he had already shifted his feet. I answered that he must go back, for it is obvious both for the Shulchan Aruch and for the Taz that they do not disagree except regarding whether at least the substance of the mention is fitting in that place — then it would be akin to the blessing itself and it would not be an interruption in the body of the prayer formula, but rather it is not its time today and the delay is from another side; in this the Shulchan Aruch holds it is not an interruption and the Taz disagrees. But if he mentions something that is not fitting in that blessing, even if it is its time today, nevertheless what of it — since it is not fitting in that blessing it is an interruption in the body of the prayer formula. And some say if he already shifted his feet, in such a case for him it would be mere conversation and is an interruption, as the Taz wrote here and above (siman 104) that wherever one shifted his feet he must go back. Also if he did not shift his feet, it seems he should go back, for on the contrary if it were not its time today at all it would only be mere conversation and that would suffice to go back as he wishes. But here, since it is its time today and he mentioned it not in its place, it is worse, per what we say in tractate Zevachim (2a) that a suitable type destroys it but an unsuitable type does not destroy it — so too here, since it is its time today, to remember it not in its place one must go back to the beginning in every respect. However, since we have no clear proof, he should have intent and pray as a voluntary prayer out of doubt. But if he shifted his feet he certainly goes back, even if it happened so in Maariv prayer, as with any other complete error that one goes back even for Maariv, and as if he did not mention Yaaleh ve-Yavo on Yom Tov with Choshen Mishpat — and so it is correct, examine well. See in the Taz what he argues on the Mechaber's ruling, and see in my novellae on the laws of Chanukah siman 577 in my work Orach Chayyim from the year 5664 — a great proof for the Taz's view from the Gemara, and what I explained there why to reject Rabbeinu Yonah's proof, etc., with God's help examine well.**** END BLOCK ****
