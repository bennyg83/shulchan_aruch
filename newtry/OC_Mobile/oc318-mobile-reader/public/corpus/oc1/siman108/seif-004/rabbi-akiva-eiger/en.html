(Magen Avraham siman kuf 11) That it is decided for him, as Beit Yosef wrote in Choshen Mishpat. In my humble opinion I did not find thus in Choshen Mishpat, and the matter is simple that in truth it is not decided for him, for he is under compulsion. The law is explained clearly in Ran perek 3 of Shevuos 46. And the proof from Agudah — it seems to me to reject: for sale of a house is concluded immediately. Rather the Merciful One benefited the seller as if the buyer stipulated with him: this field I bought from you — if you give me money until the 15th, it is sold to you from now; and they acquired from him on this condition. And so is explained in chiddushim of Rashba perek 7 of Gittin that in such a case a claim of compulsion does not help, for in the end he did not transfer title to him except that he will give him the money. But if he does not give him, even though he is under compulsion, he does not transfer his property to him for this, as Shach wrote Choshen Mishpat siman 21 — compulsion the Merciful One exempts, but does not sustain him; understand well. And in Ran's words there I wonder at what he wrote that gittin is different — for even "you are forgiven" does not help — see there; behold also in monetary law it is so, for he compared it to house sale. And explained Ran and Rashba perek 7 Gittin — when one transfers his object on condition he give him 200 zuz and gave them under duress — it depends on two versions there — uncertain; not the place here.
