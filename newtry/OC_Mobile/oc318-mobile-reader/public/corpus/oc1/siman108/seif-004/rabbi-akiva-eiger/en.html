Se'if 4: “For the adjacent prayer.” Tzelach is uncertain whether the Musaf prayer is also considered adjacent, so that on Shabbat one could pray Musaf immediately after midday and then pray Shacharit as compensation.
Magen Avraham, seif katan 11. That the house becomes absolutely his, as Beit Yosef writes in Choshen Mishpat. In my humble opinion, I did not find this in Choshen Mishpat, and it is clear that in truth the house does not become absolutely his, since he was prevented by circumstances beyond his control. The law is stated explicitly by Ran in chapter 3 of Shevuos, page 26. It appears to me that the proof of the Agudah can be rejected. With houses in a walled city, the sale is completed immediately. The Torah merely granted the seller a right as though the buyer had stipulated with him: The field I purchased from you shall be sold back to you retroactively if you give me the money within twelve months. A formal act of acquisition was made from him subject to this condition. Rashba likewise writes explicitly in his novellae to chapter 7 of Gittin that in such a case a claim of coercion is ineffective. Ultimately, he transferred the house only on condition that the seller give him the money. If the seller does not give it, even because of circumstances beyond his control, the buyer does not thereby transfer his own property to him. This accords with Shach, Choshen Mishpat siman 21, that the Merciful One exempts a person under coercion, but does not confer a benefit upon him through it. Examine this carefully. I am surprised by Ran there, who writes that gittin are different because even saying, Let the money be forgiven to you, is ineffective; see there. The same is true in monetary law, since he compares it to houses in a walled city. Ran and Rashba explicitly write in chapter 7 of Gittin that where one transfers an object on condition that the other give him two hundred zuz, and the latter gives the money against his will, the law depends on the two formulations discussed there. This requires study, but this is not the place for it.
