Question. On Yom Kippur, if one forgot Minchah until Ma'ariv, what is the law regarding making it up at Ma'ariv? Also, if he forgot Ne'ilah, what is the law? And what if he intentionally omitted Ne'ilah, or forgot both Minchah and Ne'ilah until night?
Answer. If one erred with Minchah of Yom Kippur, he prays two weekday Ma'ariv prayers, as in seif 10. Do not say that two prayer-times have passed, for Minchah's time is the whole day. The proof is from Tosafot 26a, s.v. Miba'ya, who say that Mussaf has no makeup at night; why not simply say that two prayer-times, Mussaf and Minchah, have passed? Since they hold that makeup is only during the time of the adjacent prayer, necessarily, because its time is all day, two times have not passed. See the Beit Yosef, who wrote that although the Rashba holds that there is makeup for two prayers, etc. See there. This is his meaning: the Rashba also wrote that there is no korban at night, as cited by the Taz, letter 4. On this it is difficult: according to the Rashba, night is not a sufficient reason, for let him wait until Shacharis. To this he answered that nevertheless they gave the reason of night, for whenever it is possible immediately it can also be done later; but when it is impossible immediately, since at night there is no korban, they did not institute it further. If so, since the time of Minchah is until dark, two prayer-times have not passed, for Minchah and Ne'ilah share one time. See siman 233, where some say Minchah is until plag, and Ne'ilah is when the sun is at the tops of the trees; if so, at first glance two times have passed. Nevertheless, after the fact he fulfills until night, and at Ma'ariv it is the adjacent prayer. Certainly if he forgot Ne'ilah, he makes it up at night with two weekday prayers. If he forgot both, one may also say that he makes them up at Ma'ariv, even though in seif 4 there is no makeup, for the main reason there is that two times have passed, whereas here two times have not passed. If he intentionally omitted Ne'ilah, he prays voluntarily if he wishes and need not introduce something new, but he should not stipulate that it be obligatory and be obligated to do so. Although Rav and Rabbi Yochanan dispute this, see siman 623; the halacha is that Ne'ilah is only by day, and if so he is exempt, and it is only a voluntary permission. If he forgot Minchah and Ne'ilah, it is reasonable that he should pray the obligatory prayer, then Ne'ilah, and then Minchah, in order, as in seif 4. Perhaps, since the time of both is the same, the frequent one comes first; nevertheless Ne'ilah is later for us. See siman 233.
See. Tosafot, Berachos 26a, s.v. Ta'ah, wrote that we have not found that they instituted makeup for two prayers; and regarding intentional neglect, even for the adjacent prayer. This seems to mean that once the time of two prayers passed, he is called negligent, for he did not remember during two times, as with the law in Beitzah of one year being negligent. According to this, just as with intentional neglect, where he rules here that it is voluntary without an innovation, why did he rule in seif 4 that an innovation is required?
