(Seif katan 5) He should say, etc. But the kabbalist, etc., held that one should not say Ashrei after Mincha. If so, according to this, the same applies when one erred and did not pray Shacharis and then prays Mincha twice: he should not say Ashrei after the Mincha prayer. However, the Bach wrote that the words of Rabbi Menachem Recanati refer to one who prays Maariv twice, because he holds that when one prays Mincha twice, even without the words of Rabbi Menachem, he need not say Ashrei between one prayer and the next. For he holds that the main reason for saying Ashrei before prayer is to stand in prayer from words of Torah and joy, but for the reason explained in siman 105, it suffices to wait the time it takes to walk four cubits. Therefore, when one prays Mincha twice, since he already said Ashrei before the first prayer, his recitation counts also for the second prayer; see there. This is unlike the Kol Bo, who holds that even when one prays Mincha twice, he should say Ashrei between one prayer and the next. See the Taz.
Ashrei at Maariv, meaning for the sake of the Maariv prayer. But after Mincha there is no prohibition to say it. If so, when he prays Maariv twice, one may say Ashrei between the two prayers, since he does not say Ashrei for the sake of Maariv, but for the sake of the Mincha prayer that he is now making up, and this is proper. See Beit Yosef, siman 234, in the name of the Mahari, of blessed memory.
It is written in the writings of Mahari, siman 59, that if the time for Mincha passed and he wants to pray Maariv, he does not say Ashrei, since the time of Mincha has already passed; if so, the day has ended and gone, and we have not found a makeup for Ashrei. However, he wrote that the Rivash disagrees; see there.
And the practical difference is for one who comes late, etc. For now there is no practical difference between the Bach's understanding of the words of Recanati and the Magen Avraham's understanding, since according to both, when one prays Maariv twice he does not say Ashrei between one prayer and the next as an obligation. According to the Bach, the reason is according to sod, and according to the Magen Avraham, the reason is as Mahari wrote, that there is no makeup for Ashrei.
And not as an obligation, it is permitted even according to the Bach, as the Magen Avraham wrote at the end of this seif katan according to the Zohar. If so, there is no practical difference except for one who comes late to the synagogue, etc. The reason the Magen Avraham did not say that there is a practical difference where one prays Mincha twice is perhaps that he means a practical difference even according to the Bach, who in any event holds that even without the reason of Recanati one should not say Ashrei when praying Mincha twice between one prayer and the next, as above. Or it is possible that the Magen Avraham himself also agrees with the Bach on this, unlike the Levush and the Taz, and therefore there is no practical difference except in the case of one who comes late.
It implies that after the Mincha prayer one should not say Ashrei, etc., and this accords with the Bach.
It appears to me that Yom Kippur is different. He comes to justify the practice according to the Zohar, that on Yom Kippur we say Ashrei after Mincha, because it is a time of favor throughout the day. But one who comes late, etc., should not say Ashrei after Mincha with the intention of fulfilling an obligation.
