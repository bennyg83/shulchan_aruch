(Marginal note 5) He should say, etc. — but the kabbalists, etc., not to say Ashrei after Minchah; if so, likewise one who erred and did not pray Shacharit, when he prays two Minchah prayers — he should not say Ashrei after the Minchah prayer; rather Bach wrote the words of R' Moshe of Recanati: one who prays two Maariv prayers — because he holds that when praying two Minchah prayers, without R' Moshe of Recanati's words, one need not say Ashrei between prayer and prayer; for he holds the main reason for saying Ashrei before prayer is to stand in prayer from words of Torah and joy. But for the reason brought in siman 105 it suffices with waiting the measure of four cubits' walk; if so, when praying two Minchah prayers, since he already said Ashrei before the first prayer, his saying also applies to the second prayer, see there — not as Levush, who holds that even when praying two Minchah prayers one should say Ashrei between prayer and prayer; see Taz.

Ashrei in Maariv — and that is for the sake of Maariv prayer; but after Minchah prayer there is no prohibition to say it. If so, when praying two Maariv prayers he should say Ashrei between the two prayers, since he does not say Ashrei for Maariv prayer but for Minchah prayer he is completing now — it is fine; and see Beit Yosef siman 234 in the name of Maharari, may he rest in Eden.

He wrote in the writings of Maharari siman 59: if Minchah time passed and he wants to pray Maariv — he does not say Ashrei, since Minchah time already passed; if so, the day is finished and gone, and we do not find make-up for Ashrei, end quote. However, he wrote that Rivash disagrees, see there.

And there is no difference for one who is late, etc. — for now there is no difference between Bach's understanding of Recanati's words and Magen Avraham's understanding, for in both views, when praying two Maariv prayers he says Ashrei between prayer and prayer for the sake of obligation — only that for Bach the reason is by mystical teaching (sod), and for Magen Avraham the reason is as Maharari wrote, that there is no make-up for Ashrei.

And not for the sake of obligation — also for Bach it is permitted, as Magen Avraham wrote in this marginal note, per the Zohar; if so, there is no difference except for one who is late coming to synagogue, etc. And that Magen Avraham did not mention a difference regarding one praying two Minchah prayers — perhaps meaning: a difference even according to Bach, who holds in any case, without Recanati's reason, he says Ashrei when praying two Minchah prayers between prayer and prayer as above; or perhaps Magen Avraham himself also holds this like Bach, not like Levush and Taz; therefore there is no difference except for one who is late.

It implies that after the Minchah prayer one should not say [Ashrei], etc., and according to Bach.

And it seems to me that Yom Kippur differs, to explain the custom per the Zohar's reasoning that on Yom Kippur they say Ashrei after Minchah, since it is His will all day; but one who is late, etc., should not say Ashrei after Minchah with intent of obligation.
