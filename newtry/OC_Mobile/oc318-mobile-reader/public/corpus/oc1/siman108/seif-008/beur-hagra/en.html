Seif 8, one who did not, etc. In such a case he is considered under compulsion, as follows from Bava Kamma 23a regarding liability for the four categories of damages. Nimukei Yosef writes there in the name of the Ramah that this is unlike the case stated there on 26b, where one recognized the object and then forgot it, for which he is exempt from the four categories because he is under compulsion. There, there was not even initial negligence, since at the outset, while he could be careful, he was not negligent, and afterward his forgetting was beyond his control, as stated regarding an oath in the third chapter of Nedarim and the third chapter of Shevuos. From here we derive the law of one who failed to pray, etc., and likewise one who took an oath, etc., and forgot, etc., as stated in Yoreh Deah 232:12 in the gloss. Although the Agudah disagrees and brings proof from Gittin 74b to 75a, where the Mishnah teaches, etc., arguing that if so he should be considered under compulsion, but since he had time initially he is not considered under compulsion, see the Agudah there. His view is likewise recorded in the gloss there. The Taz there and the Magen Avraham here have already rejected this proof. See also siman 527:7 and siman 538:1.
