(8) And he need not innovate, etc. — Olas Tamid and Bach disagree and ruled he must innovate something — up to here. I examined Bach siman 234 and saw he disagrees only regarding intentional [omission of] Shacharis or Maariv, but for Minchah he concedes no innovation is needed, because since he was busy with his affairs at Minchah time he is considered somewhat as ones; also for Shacharis and Maariv in my view the words of Shulchan Aruch and Levush, because the main foundation of Bach who deduced thus is that Tur brought the Gaon's words regarding Minchah prayer — evidently the Gaon does not rely on the words of the Gemara but on Minchah specifically. In my humble opinion the Gemara teaches us that even though we hold if he erred at Minchah and prays Maariv twice we do not say his day passed (end of chapter), nevertheless it is a greater matter than forgetting Shacharis and Maariv, especially when intentional; so too it seems in Nachalas Tzvi there, and it also fits what the Gemara says "a crooked thing cannot be made straight," etc. — this one who annulled Kerias Shema of Shacharis and Maariv and they did not mention Minchah — examine well. One may also say that because of Kerias Shema he mentioned Shacharis and Maariv, and Bach elaborated to deduce from this to disagree with Shulchan Aruch; in my humble opinion as I wrote, and furthermore from the Gaon's wording that he himself brought in the name of Rosh regarding "crooked," etc., it is implied it refers to the Gemara, and the Gemara mentioned Shacharis and Maariv; so too I found explained in Rashba's novellae that it refers to the Gemara; therefore it seems to me the main ruling is as Shulchan Aruch ruled here. My master my father wrote an incident: one came to the synagogue near Maariv and went down before the Ark and prayed, and had not yet prayed Minchah; it seems to me this is not similar to intentional, for behold Minchah time still exists; and on Rosh Chodesh he mentions Yaaleh ve-Yavo in the second since Rosh Chodesh was already accepted upon him — up to here. I believe one may rely on the Levush's reasoning in seif 9 here, since it is still day and he did not mention Yaaleh ve-Yavo.**** END BLOCK ****
