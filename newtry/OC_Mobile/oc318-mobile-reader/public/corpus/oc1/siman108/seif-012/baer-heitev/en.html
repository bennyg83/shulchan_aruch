There, the time was certainly appropriate for Havdalah, except that this person recited Havdalah a second time unnecessarily. Here, by contrast, the insertion has no relevance to that time, such as saying during the weekdays something belonging to Pesach, and there can be no greater interruption than this. This ruling requires study. Responsa Halachos Ketanos, part 1, siman 260, likewise disagrees and rules that he must repeat the prayer; see there. It is possible to explain that the ruling concerns a compensatory prayer, such as one who did not pray Minchah on Rosh Chodesh, Shabbos, or another festival and therefore prays Maariv twice. In that case, if he mentioned Rosh Chodesh, Shabbos, or the event of another festival in the compensatory prayer, it is not an interruption, since this prayer comes to compensate for the preceding day.
