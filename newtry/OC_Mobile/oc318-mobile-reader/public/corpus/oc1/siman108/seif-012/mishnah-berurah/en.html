(37) Not at its time — such as mentioning Yaaleh VeYavo not on Rosh Chodesh and Chol HaMoed, or adding Shabbat and Yom Tov on a weekday, and the like, and one remembered after finishing the blessing or the entire prayer.

(38) It is not an interruption — that one would need thereby to return to the beginning or to the start of the blessing in which one stopped; most later authorities disagree with this ruling and hold it is no better than if one interrupted with speech in the middle of prayer, where Shulchan Aruch ruled above in siman 104 seif 6 that one returns to the beginning of the blessing, and in the first three and last three blessings one returns to the beginning of the three; see above there where we explained all details of the laws of speech. Also Daat Torah and Chayei Adam ruled the law as they do. See in Daat Torah what he added: this applies if one mentioned something not relevant to that day — such as saying "remember us" etc. and "inscribe us" etc. when it is not the time of inscription today, or saying Yaaleh VeYavo until after this Rosh Chodesh day, which is an absolute lie that today is not Rosh Chodesh; therefore one's law is as speech in the middle of prayer, as above. But if one did not speak falsely — such as saying "remember us for life" without saying "and inscribe us," or havdalah in Chonen HaDaat, which mentions what God graced one with — a heart to understand and distinguish; this is relevant all year to give thanks to God who apportioned one a heart to understand and knowledge to know; or saying Yaaleh VeYavo until this Rosh Chodesh day, which is not false but only an addition in one's prayer — and although it is forbidden to add in the first three and last three blessings, nevertheless speech it is not. See in Chayei Adam general rule 25: whether regarding inadvertent speech in prayer or mentioning something not at its time — after the fact, if one did not return to the beginning of the blessing, since one finished the blessing, even though one added something irrelevant, one may not return; all the more if one already completed one's prayer — see there his reason in Nishmat Adam.
