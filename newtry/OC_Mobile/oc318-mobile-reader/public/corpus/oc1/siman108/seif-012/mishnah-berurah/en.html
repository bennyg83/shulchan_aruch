Not in its proper time. For example, he mentioned Ya'aleh VeYavo when it was not Rosh Chodesh or Chol HaMoed, or he added the mention of Shabbos or Yom Tov on a weekday, and the like, and he remembered after finishing the blessing or the entire prayer.
It is not considered an interruption. Meaning that because of this he need not return to the beginning or to the start of the blessing in which he interrupted. Most later authorities disagree with this ruling and hold that this is no better than if he interrupted with speech in the middle of prayer, where the Shulchan Aruch ruled above in siman 104, seif 6, that he must return to the beginning of the blessing, and in the first three or last three blessings he returns to the beginning of the three. See above there, where we explained all the details of the laws of speech. Derech HaChaim and Chayei Adam also ruled in practice like them. See Derech HaChaim, who added that this applies if he mentioned something not relevant to that day, such as saying Zochreinu, etc., or Vechasveinu, etc., when it is not a time of writing, or saying Ya'aleh VeYavo until the phrase on this day of Rosh Chodesh, which is an absolute falsehood because today is not Rosh Chodesh; then its law is like speaking in the middle of prayer, as above. But if he did not say a falsehood, such as saying Zochreinu LeChaim without saying Vechasveinu, or saying Havdalah in Chonen HaDaas, where he mentions that Hashem has graciously given him a heart to understand and distinguish, which is relevant the whole year as thanks to Hashem for granting him understanding and knowledge, or if he said Ya'aleh VeYavo only until before on this day of Rosh Chodesh, then it is not false but merely an addition in his prayer. Although it is forbidden to add in the first three and last three blessings, nevertheless it is not considered speech. See Chayei Adam, klal 25, who wrote both regarding inadvertent speech in prayer and regarding mentioning something at the wrong time, that after the fact, if he did not go back to the beginning of the blessing, once he finished the blessing, although he added something not relevant to it, he may not go back; all the more so if he already completed his prayer. See there his reason in Nishmas Adam.
