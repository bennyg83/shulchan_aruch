(Marginal note 12) 'Of Shabbat,' etc. — from what was written in siman 623, seif 3, which implies, etc.; see there in Magen Avraham, who proved thus.

Because it is [still] day, etc. — meaning: although on Shabbat there is no Ne'ilah prayer, nevertheless since Yom Kippur fell on Shabbat and that Shabbat was obligated in four prayers — namely, to pray in addition to the three fixed prayers Ne'ilah — therefore if he did not mention 'of Shabbat' in Ne'ilah they make him repeat.

And in Tur, etc., thus: and that which we say, etc. — meaning: he wrote in the name of R' Yehuda that if he forgot and did not pray, he mentions Yaaleh VeYavo in the Minchah of Rosh Chodesh, for he has no [reason] to pray on Motzei Rosh Chodesh two Maariv prayers, for he gains nothing, since in Maariv too he will not mention Yaaleh VeYavo; and the same law is explained here in seif 11; therefore the Tur [wrote] 'and that which we say.'

If so, here all the more so he fulfills after the fact — meaning: Magen Avraham holds, in his reasoning, that it is more fitting that the prayer one prays as make-up resemble the prayer he should have prayed then in its time, rather than that it now come as make-up from what resembles the prayer whose time is now. If so, learn a fortiori: if one did not pray Shabbat Minchah, when he prays at Motzei Shabbat as make-up he prays weekday Maariv — Tur wrote it is called similar to Shabbat prayer, since by law one must pray Maariv; if so, what he prays at Motzei Shabbat — weekday Maariv as make-up for Shabbat Minchah — he gains and it is as if he prayed Shabbat prayer. All the more so in our case here, where he did not pray Erev Shabbat Minchah — then he should truly have prayed Maariv and not Shabbat [prayer]. Rather, since he prays as make-up on Shabbat he must pray a prayer resembling the day's prayer; nevertheless, if he prayed Maariv and did not mention Shabbat he fulfills, for weekday Maariv too resembles Shabbat prayer, since by law one must pray Maariv on Shabbat, etc.; and all the more so as explained above.

Since it comes as make-up for Maariv — one might say: in Maariv itself, if he forgot Yaaleh VeYavo they do not make him repeat, as written below siman 422.

For one could say that even at night, etc. — thus it should read: he did not examine well, for in Berachos, chapter Tefillas HaShachar, R' Anan said in the name of Rav: if he erred and did not mention Rosh Chodesh in Maariv they do not make him repeat, because the court sanctifies the month only by day. Ameimar said: Rav's statement is reasonable in a full month (Rashi: when the previous month was full they make Rosh Chodesh two days; therefore he can say tomorrow that tomorrow's Maariv is fully Rosh Chodesh more than the first), but in a deficient month they make him repeat. R' Eliezer said to Ameimar: since Rav stated a reason (that they do not sanctify the month at night, end Rashi) — what difference to me deficient, what difference to me full? Rather, there is no difference.

And Rabbeinu Yerucham understood from R' Eliezer's 'what difference to me deficient,' etc. — rather, no difference at all exists between them; on this Magen Avraham wrote that R' Eliezer's intent is only that there is no difference between deficient and full month on the first night; also in a deficient month they do not make him repeat even if he does not say it in Maariv for tomorrow — not as Ameimar's reasoning. But from the second night of a full month this was not mentioned in the Gemara; here one could say like the other view that Rabbeinu Yerucham brought in siman 422, that they make him repeat since they already sanctified it by day; and Tosafos mentioned this reasoning in the passage beginning 'and because there is no,' etc. — although they concluded and it did not seem to divide, they did not divide on this except as reasoning, but did not write it is the inverse of the Gemara's words; therefore it must be as Magen Avraham wrote.

That he did not bring the proofs I brought — meaning: perhaps if he had concluded these proofs Magen Avraham brought, he would have conceded to Magen Avraham's view.
