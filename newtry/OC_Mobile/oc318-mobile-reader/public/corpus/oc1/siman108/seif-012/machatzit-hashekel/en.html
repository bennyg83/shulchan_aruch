(Seif katan 12) Of Shabbos, etc. From what is written in siman 623, seif 3, which implies, etc.; see the Magen Avraham there, who proved this.
Because it is day, etc. Meaning, although on Shabbos there is no Ne'ilah prayer, nevertheless, since Yom Kippur fell on Shabbos, that Shabbos became obligated in four prayers, namely, in addition to the three fixed prayers, the Ne'ilah prayer. Therefore, if he did not mention Shabbos in Ne'ilah, we make him go back.
And in the Tur, etc. This is his language: and that which we say, etc. This means that he wrote in the name of the Ri that if one forgot and did not pray Ya'aleh Veyavo at Mincha of Rosh Chodesh, he should not pray Maariv twice after Rosh Chodesh, for he gains nothing, since also in Maariv he will not mention Ya'aleh Veyavo. This is the law explained here in seif 11, and regarding this the Tur wrote, and that which we say.
If so, here all the more so he fulfills after the fact. This means that the Magen Avraham holds by logic that the prayer he prays as a makeup should more properly resemble the prayer that he should have prayed then, in its proper time, for which this prayer now comes as a makeup, than resemble the prayer whose time is now. If so, he derives by a kal vachomer: if one did not pray Mincha of Shabbos and, when praying on Motzaei Shabbos as makeup, he prays the weekday eighteen blessings, and the Tur wrote that this is considered similar to the Shabbos prayer, since by law he should pray the eighteen blessings, etc., then what he prays on Motzaei Shabbos as the eighteen blessings is considered the makeup for the Shabbos prayer, for he gains something and it is as though he prayed the Shabbos prayer. All the more so in the present case, where he did not pray Mincha on Erev Shabbos, for then he truly should have prayed the eighteen blessings and not mention Shabbos. Rather, since he is praying the makeup on Shabbos, he must pray a prayer resembling the prayer of the day. Nevertheless, if he prayed the eighteen blessings and did not mention Shabbos, he fulfilled, for the eighteen-blessing prayer also resembles the Shabbos prayer, since by law one ought to pray the eighteen blessings on Shabbos, etc. This is all the more so, as explained.
Since it comes as a makeup for Maariv, this is not so, for in Maariv itself, if he forgot Ya'aleh Veyavo we do not make him go back, as written below in siman 422.
For one can say that even on the night, etc. So it should read. He did not examine it well, for in Berachos, at the end of chapter Tefillas HaShachar, Rav Anan said in the name of Rav: if one erred and did not mention Rosh Chodesh at Maariv, we do not make him go back, because beis din sanctifies the month only by day. Ameimar said: Rav's words are reasonable in a full month. Rashi explains: when the previous month is full, Rosh Chodesh is observed for two days; therefore he can say it tomorrow, for tomorrow night's Maariv is a more complete Rosh Chodesh than the first. But in a deficient month, we make him go back. Rav Ashi said to Ameimar: since Rav stated the reason, namely, that they do not sanctify the month at night, what difference is there to me whether it is deficient or full? Rather, there is no distinction.
And the Beit Yosef understood from Rav Ashi's statement, what difference is there to me whether it is deficient, etc. Rather, there is no distinction at all between them. Regarding this, the Magen Avraham wrote that Rav Ashi's intent is only that there is no distinction between a deficient month and a full month on the first night, and even in a deficient month we do not make him go back, even though he will not say it at Maariv of the following night, unlike what Ameimar thought. But regarding the second night of a full month, this was not mentioned in the Gemara. In this, one can say like that opinion brought by the Beit Yosef in siman 422, that we do make him go back, since they already sanctified it by day. Tosafot mentioned this reasoning there, s.v. u'lefi she'ein, although they concluded that it does not seem correct to distinguish. They disagreed with this only by reasoning, but they did not write that it contradicts the words of the Gemara. Therefore one must say as the Magen Avraham wrote.
