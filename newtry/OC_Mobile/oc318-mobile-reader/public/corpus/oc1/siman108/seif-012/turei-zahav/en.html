The matter of the rest of the days, etc. This Beit Yosef learned in the name of Orach Chayyim in the name of HaRav Rabbi Yitzchak from what we state before this, that if he made Havdalah in both he has fulfilled—and I do not understand this proof for this ruling, for there certainly the time of Havdalah is at that time, only that this man made Havdalah a second time without need, since he already made Havdalah in the first prayer—therefore it was not an interruption, for he did not interrupt in anything; and if he had not prayed first, this would have been an obligation upon him; unlike something that has no relevance at that time, which certainly was an interruption. And further, Havdalah is relevant all year, for at the time of Havdalah we say even what does not belong to the time of Havdalah of that hour—such as on Motza'ei Yom Tov he mentions Shabbos, as we say the order of Havdalah lists; likewise he counts in "Atah chonantanu" from what He graced His people, and this too is from the grace He divided for them to distinguish between thing and thing—only they enacted to mention this at the time of Havdalah; therefore there is no interruption here. Unlike one who says on weekdays what belongs to Pesach—you have no greater interruption than this; and further proof from siman 94 seif 4—see there; and this ruling requires study. And in any case it appears that if he interrupted not on topic, it is not better than if one erred in prayer mentioned in siman 104—likewise in what he mentioned not akin to the matter. And further proof from siman 588: Beit Yosef brought the words of the Rabbi the Maggid regarding the order of tekios, if he interrupted in an unnecessary sound—it was an interruption; and these are his words: that although waiting does not invalidate, interruption in a shofar sound that is unfit invalidates—end quote; and likewise there in Shulchan Aruch.
