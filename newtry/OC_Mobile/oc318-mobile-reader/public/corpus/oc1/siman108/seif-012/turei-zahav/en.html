An occasion from other days, etc. Beit Yosef cites Orchos Chaim in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah, who derives this from the law that one who recited Havdalah in both prayers fulfilled his obligation. I do not understand the proof. Havdalah belongs to that time. He repeated it unnecessarily only because he had already recited it in the first prayer, and therefore it is not an interruption. Had he not prayed the first prayer, Havdalah would have been obligatory. Mentioning something that has no connection to the present time is certainly an interruption. Moreover, Havdalah is conceptually relevant throughout the year, for in Havdalah we enumerate distinctions, even distinctions not uniquely related to that particular Motzaei Shabbos. Ata Chonantanu similarly praises the understanding Hashem granted His people to distinguish between categories, although the Sages fixed its recitation at the time of Havdalah. It therefore does not interrupt. Mentioning Pesach on an ordinary weekday, by contrast, is a clear interruption. There is further proof from siman 94, seif 4. This ruling requires study. In any event, speech unrelated to the prayer cannot be better than ordinary conversation during the Amidah, discussed in siman 104. Likewise, in siman 588 Beit Yosef cites the Maggid Mishneh concerning shofar sounds: although a pause does not invalidate, an unnecessary shofar sound does invalidate. Shulchan Aruch rules accordingly.
