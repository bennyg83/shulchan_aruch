Seif 12. One who mistakenly mentions an occasion from another day, etc. Note: During Chanukah I was asked about one who said Al HaNissim in the blessing of Avodah, before Vesechezena, and had already stepped back. I answered that he must repeat the Amidah. Both according to Shulchan Aruch and according to the Taz, their dispute concerns a mention whose substance belongs in that location, so that it is related to the blessing and is not inherently an interruption, but is inappropriate only because today is not the proper time. Shulchan Aruch holds that this is not an interruption, while the Taz disagrees. Where the inserted matter does not belong in that blessing, however, even if it is appropriate to the day, it interrupts the actual text of the prayer. Certainly, if he already stepped back, even ordinary speech would require repetition, as the Taz writes here and above in siman 104. It appears that even if he has not stepped back he must repeat. Indeed, if the mention were wholly irrelevant to the day, it would be no more than ordinary speech, and he could return to Retzei. Here, however, because it is appropriate to the day but was inserted in the wrong blessing, it is worse. This resembles the principle in Zevachim 2a that something of the same category can invalidate more severely than something of a different category. Therefore, mentioning it in the wrong place requires returning to the beginning in all cases. Since there is no conclusive proof, if he has not stepped back he should intend the repeated prayer as voluntary because of the doubt. If he has stepped back, he must certainly repeat, even if this occurred at Maariv, just as with any complete error, such as omitting Yaaleh Veyavo on Yom Tov or Chol HaMoed. See the Taz's disagreement with the Mechaber, and my novellae to the laws of Chanukah, siman 677, in my Orach Chaim work of 5604, where I brought strong proof for the Taz from the Gemara and explained how he rejects Rabbeinu Yonah's proof.
