(10) Two of Shabbos, etc. — Taz wrote, and in my view even after the fact if he prayed the second as weekday he did not fulfill, for there is no prayer on Shabbos without mentioning Shabbos — up to here. But Magen Avraham wrote he fulfills after the fact, and brought proof from Tur siman 422; this is his wording: that which we say if one did not pray Minchah on Shabbos he prays on Motzei Shabbos two weekday [Amidahs], because on Shabbos too by law he must pray eighteen blessings, only because of honor of Shabbos the Sages did not burden him — if so, eighteen blessings of Shabbos also apply — up to here his wording; if so here all the more so he fulfills after the fact — up to here Magen Avraham's words. His words are not correct here, for even though it was fitting to pray eighteen blessings on Shabbos, nevertheless they certainly also had to mention Shabbos in it as below siman 268, and as is implied from Taz; and Tur says thus: lest we say it is not an obligation to complete on Motzei Shabbos since eighteen is not relevant to Shabbos prayer — therefore he said eighteen-blessing prayer applies on Shabbos. But for another reason one may rule he fulfills, since it is for weekday make-up prayers he fulfills after the fact; so too in She'eirei Knesset HaGedolah siman 422 he is uncertain and elaborated greatly on this reasoning whether we follow the main prayers or the make-up prayers, and concludes it is reasonable he fulfills after the fact, and such is the law when he forgot on the night of Rosh Chodesh Maariv and forgot in the second Shacharis prayer to mention — he fulfilled, since if he had not mentioned in Maariv he would have fulfilled. Magen Avraham disagrees with him, since the reason we do not make him repeat Maariv is there is no sanctification of the month at night, and here it is daytime — up to here. This is not a valid challenge, for this prayer still comes from Maariv make-up prayers where one does not repeat. It seems to me since this doubt is a matter that depends on reasoning and there is no resolution among early poskim, and Taz did not have this reasoning in mind and it is obvious to him he did not fulfill — therefore it is correct to go back and pray as a voluntary prayer with innovation; and if there are two days of Rosh Chodesh and he forgot Minchah on the first day and completes at night and forgot to mention in the second, it is simple to She'eirei Knesset HaGedolah there that he fulfilled. So too in my view, for if he forgot Minchah of Shabbos or Rosh Chodesh he completes at Maariv and does not mention Shabbos or Rosh Chodesh at all — evidently we follow the present time.
