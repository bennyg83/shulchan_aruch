(Marginal note 10) If he acted intentionally, etc. — for Minchah time still applies, meaning: it deals with places where they pray Maariv voluntarily; if so, even though he prayed Maariv, nevertheless it is still day, and therefore one who acted intentionally has no make-up prayer — namely, only when its time has passed to complete it in the adjacent prayer; not so here, where its time still applies.

As was written in siman 30: if he prayed Maariv voluntarily he may no longer put on tefillin, for he made it night in his prayer, and night is not the time for tefillin. So too in siman 293, seif 66: a woman who lit the Shabbat candle may not pray Minchah, for she already made it Shabbat in her lighting — tefillin and Shabbat differ, since he made it night; if so, were he to put on tefillin or pray Minchah he would do two contradictory things. Not so here: even if you say it is night, nevertheless he may pray Minchah as make-up; if so, there are not two contradictions. And although here, one who acted intentionally has no make-up and therefore prays because Minchah time still applies — and there are two contradictions — meaning: we require the contradiction to be evident, and here the contradiction is not evident, for who knows he acted intentionally? Perhaps he erred and prays as make-up, even though that is not true.

That he holds it is permitted, etc. — and he says it is permitted. He wrote above in siman 104, marginal note 6, in the name of Taz, that this is called inadvertent.

And it seems to him regarding Rosh Chodesh, etc. — as Magen Avraham wrote in siman 419, thus it should read. And know that even according to Magen Avraham, since it is still day he does not pray as make-up but as a prayer in its time; nevertheless, in my humble opinion he must adjoin it to Maariv so that it appear as make-up, for otherwise there are two contradictions — for then the contradiction is evident, that he does not pray as make-up but as a prayer in its time, yet he has already made it night.
