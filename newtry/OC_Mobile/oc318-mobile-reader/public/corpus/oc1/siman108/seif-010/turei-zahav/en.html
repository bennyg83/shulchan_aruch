The first did not count for him. The Rif writes that he revealed that the latter prayer was the current obligation, and therefore he must pray again so that the current obligation precede compensation. This is difficult. His intent is known to Heaven, and if he says that he intended the first as the current obligation but merely forgot Havdalah, why should he repeat, since Havdalah may be said over a cup? The Rif means that even if he claims the first was current, the fact that the second certainly had the form of the current obligation means that he has two current prayers and no compensation. The revealed intent concerns the second prayer, not the first. When the Tur says that he revealed that the first was compensation and the second current, it means that the character of the second leads us to presume that the first was compensation. If he explicitly says otherwise, he is believed, though he must still pray a third time because no prayer remains as compensation. Accordingly, when one missed Minchah on the eve of Rosh Chodesh and prays two Rosh Chodesh prayers, if he mentioned Yaaleh Veyavo in the second but not the first, the ordinary presumption is that the second was current. But if he explicitly says that the second was intended as compensation and he properly mentioned Rosh Chodesh in it, he need not repeat. We may accept that omission in the first was accidental and that he intended it as the current obligation. This differs from Shabbos, where reciting Havdalah in the second is objectively improper if it was compensation. His claim about his intent cannot overcome the fact that the second prayer itself bears the form of the current obligation. Levush explains the priority of the current prayer by the rule that the frequent precedes the infrequent. This is incorrect, because if that were the reason, reversing them would not require repetition, just as praying Minchah before Mussaf does not invalidate Mussaf after the fact. The superior status of the current obligation is distinct, as the Gemara says that a mitzvah is especially beloved at its proper time. This is stronger than frequency. The time of the missed prayer has already passed, and compensation was enacted only after discharging the present obligation.
