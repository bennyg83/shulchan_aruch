The first did not count for him. In the words of Rif: he revealed his intent—for the second is the obligation of its hour; therefore we require returning the latter prayers in order to advance the obligation of its hour first—end quote. And seemingly difficult—what relevance has revelation of intent here, for before Heaven it is revealed what was in his heart? And if he says he did not intend in the first for tashlumin but for the obligation of its hour, only he forgot Havdalah and need not return because he can say on the cup why he should now return to pray; rather Rif's intent is: even though he says he intended in the first for the obligation of its hour, since we see that in any case in the second he certainly intended for the obligation of its hour, automatically there are two obligations of its hour—if so there are no tashlumin here. From this we find that the revelation of intent is about the second and not the first; and even though Tur wrote siman 292 that he revealed his intent that he intended in the first for tashlumin and in the second on account of his obligation he also intended—as said, from revelation of intent of the second we say presumptively he intended in the first for tashlumin; and if he says it is not so we believe him, only he still must pray a third. And accordingly it appears regarding one who forgot to pray Minchah on the eve of Rosh Chodesh, where we said he prays two of Rosh Chodesh: if he remembered Rosh Chodesh in the second and not in the first, that he must return and pray—we do not say how his intent was; rather since he knew he must pray two, we say presumptively: since he remembered in the second and not in the first, we say he certainly intended in the second on account of the obligation of its hour. But if he said explicitly that in the second he intended for tashlumin and remembered Rosh Chodesh as required—he need not return and pray, since he did properly in tashlumin to remember Rosh Chodesh; we say what he did not remember in the first was forgetfulness, and we believe him to say he intended in the first for the obligation of its hour. Unlike Shabbos, where he acts improperly in the second if he intended for tashlumin—for he need not make Havdalah, and he made Havdalah—it does not help him in what we believe him about his intent in the first for obligation, for explicitly it is evident the second was in any case obligation—as explained, it is correct to distinguish in this. And regarding the superiority that the prayer of the obligation of its hour has over tashlumin prayer—Levush wrote siman 422 that it is because it is frequent and frequent precedes infrequent; and I do not find thus, for if you came to learn this rule, after the fact there would not need to return and pray from what was stated regarding Musaf prayer after Minchah, that we say in siman 287 he need not return and pray Musaf even though Minchah has the superiority of frequent; and furthermore we proved thus in Shulchan Aruch that one need not return on account of superiority of frequent in siman 284; rather the superiority of obligation of its hour is a different superiority, as we say in first chapter of Kiddushin: a mitzvah in its time is beloved and preferable to superiority of frequent—for in superiority of frequent, both of them would have had their time now; which is not so here, where the prayer time has already passed that he did not pray, only they enacted tashlumin for it—and that is specifically after the obligation of its hour, as explained.
