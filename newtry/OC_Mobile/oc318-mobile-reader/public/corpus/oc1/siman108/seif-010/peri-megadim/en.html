The first — Taz raised this to his reasoning and in his gloss in note 9; if so, Rosh Chodesh is not truly comparable to havdalah, for there, even if one said the first was for obligation, one is not believed on the second to say it is for teshuvah — why did one make havdalah? Unlike Rosh Chodesh, where by law one mentions in both; if one did not mention Rosh Chodesh in the first and the second mentioned it and says the first was obligation and the second teshuvah — one is believed, unlike the Levush in Rosh Chodesh who is also not believed. One forgot on erev Shabbat and prayed the evening prayer twice and did not mention in the first, and the second mentioned — even for the Levush one does not fulfill, for if one says the first is for Shabbat, behold one did not mention Shabbat and must return twice; and it is possible the Peri Chadash here in seif 1 disagrees with Rabbi Shlomo Gramizon whom he brought in this siman.

And what he wrote — the reason [for] reversal he does not fulfill — Levush here, some say, because of the frequent [prayer]; and even after the fact he does not fulfill — see siman 484. Nevertheless it seems to me that both for Levush and for Taz, even if unintentional and he preceded make-up, he does not fulfill. And it seems intentional; and in our chiddushim we wrote from this.

See Rashi Berachos 26b s.v. mavdil — in the first, and the second for make-up of Shabbat — one does not make havdalah; it implies like Levush that the reason is one does not make havdalah in the second; and the same for Rosh Chodesh in such a case. And one can say Rashi wrote it should not be in the second, for it implies he did not act properly; and one cannot extend.
