The first. See the Taz, who concluded according to his reasoning and emendation in letter 9. If so, Rosh Chodesh is not fully comparable to Havdalah: there, even if he says that the first was for the obligation, he is not believed regarding the second that it was for makeup, for why did he make Havdalah in it? But with Rosh Chodesh, by law he mentions it in both. If he did not mention Rosh Chodesh in the first and did mention it in the second, and says the first was for the obligation and the second for makeup, he is believed. According to the Levush, however, regarding Rosh Chodesh too he is not believed. If he forgot on Friday afternoon and prays two Ma'ariv prayers and did not mention Shabbos in the first but did in the second, even according to the Levush he has not fulfilled, for if he says the first was for Shabbos, he did not mention Shabbos and must repeat both. Perhaps the Pri Chadash would agree here, despite disagreeing with Rabbi Shmuel Narmizan, cited in this siman.
And what he wrote. The reason why if he reversed them he has not fulfilled: the Levush here in seif 1 says it is because the frequent comes first, and even after the fact he has not fulfilled. See siman 684. Nevertheless, it seems to me that both according to the Levush and according to the Taz, even if he erred and put the makeup prayer first, he has not fulfilled, and it is not only in a case of intentional neglect. We wrote about this in our novellae.
See. Rashi, Berachos 26b, s.v. Mavdil baRishonah, who writes that the second is for makeup of Shabbos and he does not make Havdalah. This implies like the Levush, that this is the reason he does not make Havdalah in the second, and the same would apply to Rosh Chodesh in such a case. One may answer that Rashi wrote this because the Gemara should have said that he need not make Havdalah in the second, implying that doing so is not proper. There is no need to elaborate.
