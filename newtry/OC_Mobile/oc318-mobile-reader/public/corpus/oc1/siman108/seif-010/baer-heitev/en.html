In the second. Because Havdalah is not [part of] mercy [requests] but a mitzvah for its hour, one cannot make Havdalah twice one after the other—for if he did once, why would he do again a weekday matter; unlike Shabbos and Rosh Chodesh where one must mention them in all prayers. Magen Avraham and Taz, not like Levush and Eliyah Rabbah.

It did not count for him. For he needed to advance the obligation of its hour at the beginning, and he revealed his intent that he prayed the first for tashlumin; therefore he must return and pray one for tashlumin without Havdalah—and specifically when he did so deliberately. But if he forgot to make Havdalah in the first, he has fulfilled his obligation, for he did not reveal his intent, as written in s.k. 13, Magen Avraham. And in such a case Rama speaks where he wrote: if he did not mention Yaaleh VeYavo in the first and mentioned in the second, he must return, etc.—that is, he revealed his intent that the first would be for tashlumin; and presumably in that which he did not mention Havdalah or Yaaleh VeYavo in the first we say in this he revealed his intent. But if he said explicitly that in the second he intended for tashlumin only that he forgot Havdalah or Rosh Chodesh in the first—even though he mentioned Havdalah or Rosh Chodesh in the second, he has fulfilled his obligation; and likewise Taz and Peri Chadash—see there.
