In the second. Havdalah is not a request for mercy, but a mitzvah belonging to that moment. It is therefore inappropriate to recite Havdalah twice in succession, for once he has made the day weekday, why should he make it weekday a second time? This differs from Shabbos and Rosh Chodesh, which must be mentioned in every prayer. Magen Avraham and Taz, contrary to Levush and Olas Tamid.
It did not count for him. He was required to place the prayer currently obligatory first, but he revealed that his first prayer was the compensatory one. He must therefore pray once more as compensation, without Havdalah. This applies specifically when he acted deliberately. If he merely forgot Havdalah in the first prayer, he has fulfilled his obligation, since he did not reveal contrary intent, as stated in seif katan 13. Magen Avraham. This is the case addressed by Rama when he writes that if one omitted Yaaleh VeYavo in the first prayer and mentioned it in the second, he must pray again. The reason is that he revealed that the first was intended as compensation. Ordinarily, by omitting Havdalah or Yaaleh VeYavo in the first prayer, we say that he revealed this intent. If, however, he expressly stated that he intended the second prayer as compensation, but merely forgot Havdalah or Rosh Chodesh in the first, he has fulfilled his obligation even though he mentioned Havdalah or Rosh Chodesh in the second. Taz and Peri Chadash likewise write; see there.
