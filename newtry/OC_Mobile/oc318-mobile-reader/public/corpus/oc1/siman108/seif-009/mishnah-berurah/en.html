(25) Of Shabbat — even though it came for weekday teshuvah, nevertheless since it is now Shabbat one prays it also as Shabbat. If he erred and prayed the second as weekday, he is exempt from praying. If in this he preceded the weekday [prayer] before the Shabbat one, he does not fulfill according to all opinions, for it is evident he preceded the teshuvah. But if the day is still large [not yet night], only that he accepted Shabbat with mizmor shir… or with Barchu and has not yet prayed Mincha — as explained below in siman 263 seif 95 that one prays Maariv twice — ab initio one must pray two of Shabbat, the first for Maariv and the second for teshuvah of Mincha. After the fact, if he prayed the first for Mincha, and even if he prayed it as weekday, it is possible he fulfills since it is still day.

(26) Twice — and so ruled Magen Avraham, Daat Torah, Chayei Adam, and other later authorities — unlike the Levush and his supporters, who hold one need not mention [yaaleh veyavo] in the second [prayer] of Rosh Chodesh. One who did not pray Maariv on Rosh Chodesh and prayed Shacharis twice and did not mention yaaleh veyavo in the second — need not return and pray, since even if he had prayed Maariv itself and forgot, he need not return; thus teshuvah will not be stricter than if he had prayed in its time. Similarly, if he erred and did not pray Mincha on Rosh Chodesh, and Rosh Chodesh is two days, and he prays Maariv twice and the second is for teshuvah of Mincha — even if he did not mention Rosh Chodesh in it he need not return, since even in the main Maariv prayer he need not return for yaaleh veyavo; kal vachomer for what is now teshuvah.

(27) Must return — for in this case where he did not mention in the first, he revealed his intent that he intended it for teshuvah of erev Rosh Chodesh, which is weekday; and it is already explained at the beginning of the siman that if one preceded teshuvah to the obligatory one he does not fulfill — and likewise with havdalah in the seif after this — therefore he must return and pray that prayer. This is specifically when teshuvah comes because of a prayer that is not of Rosh Chodesh — since he did not mention yaaleh veyavo in the first he revealed his intent that he prayed the first with intent of weekday teshuvah. But if Rosh Chodesh was two days and he did not pray Mincha on the first day of Rosh Chodesh, so he must pray Maariv twice and did not mention yaaleh veyavo in the first — he need not return and pray, for there is no proof that he prayed it for teshuvah. Further, the later authorities wrote what Rama wrote — that if he did not mention in the first and mentioned in the second he must return — this is when unspecified; but if he explicitly intended the first for obligation and the second for completion, he fulfills.
