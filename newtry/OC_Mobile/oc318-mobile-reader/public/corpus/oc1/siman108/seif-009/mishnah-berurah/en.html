Of Shabbos. Although it comes as makeup for a weekday prayer, nevertheless since now it is Shabbos, he prays it as a Shabbos prayer as well. If he erred and prayed the second prayer as a weekday prayer, he is exempt from praying again. If in this case he put the weekday prayer before the Shabbos prayer, he has not fulfilled according to all opinions, for it is evident that he put the makeup prayer first. However, if it is still fully day, but he accepted Shabbos with Mizmor Shir or with Barchu and has not yet prayed Minchah, as explained below in siman 263, seif 15, he should initially pray two Shabbos prayers, the first for Ma'ariv and the second for makeup of Minchah. After the fact, if he prayed the first for Minchah, and even if he prayed it as a weekday prayer, it is possible that he fulfilled, since it is still day.
Two. So ruled the Magen Avraham, Derech HaChaim, Chayei Adam, and the other later authorities, unlike the Levush and those who support him, who hold that one need not mention Rosh Chodesh in the second prayer. If one did not pray Ma'ariv on Rosh Chodesh and prayed two Shacharis prayers but did not mention Ya'aleh VeYavo in the second, he need not go back and pray, since even if he had prayed Ma'ariv itself and forgotten, he would not need to go back; the makeup prayer should not be more severe than if he had prayed it in its proper time. So too, if one erred and did not pray Minchah on Rosh Chodesh, and Rosh Chodesh is two days, and he prays two Ma'ariv prayers, the second as makeup for Minchah, even if he did not mention Rosh Chodesh in it, he need not go back, since even in the principal Ma'ariv prayer one need not go back because of Ya'aleh VeYavo, all the more so now when it is only makeup.
He must go back. For by not mentioning it in the first prayer, he revealed that he intended it as makeup for the eve of Rosh Chodesh, which was a weekday. It was already explained at the beginning of the siman that if he put the makeup prayer before the obligatory prayer, he has not fulfilled, as with Havdalah in the next seif. Therefore he must go back and pray that prayer. This is specifically when the makeup comes because of a prayer that was not of Rosh Chodesh; since he did not mention Ya'aleh VeYavo in the first prayer, he revealed that he prayed the first with intent for makeup of a weekday prayer. But if Rosh Chodesh was two days and he did not pray Minchah on the first day of Rosh Chodesh, so that he must pray two Ma'ariv prayers, and he did not mention Ya'aleh VeYavo in the first, he need not go back and pray, for there is no proof that he prayed it for makeup. The later authorities further wrote that the Rama's ruling, that if he did not mention it in the first but mentioned it in the second he must go back, applies only where his intent was unspecified. If, however, he explicitly intended the first as the obligation and the second as the makeup, he has fulfilled.
