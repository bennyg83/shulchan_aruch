Of. Rosh Chodesh. See the Taz. The Levush emended the text this way: he mentions it in the first and not in the second; and if he mentioned it in the second but not in the first, the second counts, and he compared it to what was stated in all cases. See Eliyah Rabbah, letter 18, in the name of Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah and Perishah, siman 423, regarding the Levush's view that one does not mention Rosh Chodesh in the second. It appears that on Yom Tov it is like Shabbos, that he prays both as Yom Tov prayers, and it is not comparable to Rosh Chodesh, for if one does not mention Ya'aleh VeYavo it is not a denigration; see Levush. Regarding what the Taz wrote to distinguish, in the Darchei Moshe, siman 423, it is explicit that the reading is like the Levush, and he disagrees, for it is not comparable to Havdalah, where one time suffices; here he is obligated to mention it in every prayer, and not because the prayer is mercy. See there. See the Magen Avraham, letter 13, that there is another practical difference between the Taz and the Levush; see there.
