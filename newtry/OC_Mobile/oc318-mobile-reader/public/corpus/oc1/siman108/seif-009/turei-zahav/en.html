Two Rosh Chodesh prayers. In siman 422, Beit Yosef quotes the Kol Bo: one who did not pray Minchah on the eve of Rosh Chodesh prays Maariv twice and mentions Yaaleh Veyavo in the first but not the second. If he mentioned it in the first but not the second, or in the second but not the first, the second counts but the first does not. If he mentioned it in both, he fulfilled his obligation, and if in neither, he fulfilled his obligation. Beit Yosef says this was compared to one who omitted Minchah on Shabbos. The printed text plainly contains errors. It should read: he mentions Yaaleh Veyavo in both. If he mentioned it in the second but not the first, the second counts and the first does not. If he omitted it in both, or mentioned it in the first but not the second, he fulfilled his obligation. Beit Yosef's comparison to the Shabbos case concerns only mentioning it in the second but not the first, which reveals that the first was intended as compensation. Although omission of Yaaleh Veyavo at Maariv of Rosh Chodesh does not require repetition, reversing the order of current obligation and compensation is worse. The requirement to mention Rosh Chodesh in both prayers differs from Havdalah. Havdalah is not a request for mercy but a time-bound mitzvah. Once he has distinguished weekday from Shabbos in the first prayer, there is no reason to do so again. Yaaleh Veyavo, however, is a prayer for mercy, and one may always pray further. Therefore the sanctity of Rosh Chodesh must be mentioned in the second prayer just as the sanctity of Shabbos is. This was evidently the Rama's reading of the Kol Bo. Levush disagrees and distinguishes Shabbos from Rosh Chodesh, relying on his own emendation in siman 422. His proof therefore rests upon his own text. The correct reading shows that there is no distinction between Shabbos and Rosh Chodesh, and the Rama's ruling is sound.
