Of Shabbos. This requires study. If he did not mention Shabbos in the second prayer, must he repeat it? One may bring proof from siman 623:3, which implies that if he did not mention Shabbos in Ne’ilah, he must repeat it, because that day obligated him in four prayers. Perhaps the same applies here. Alternatively, because this is compensation for a weekday prayer, he need not repeat it, since he was not obligated in such a prayer today. The Tur in siman 422 writes: “That which we say that one who did not pray Minchah on Shabbos prays two weekday prayers on Motzaei Shabbos is because, in truth, on Shabbos too he ought to pray eighteen blessings, but the Sages did not burden him because of the honor of Shabbos.” Accordingly, the eighteen blessings also pertain to Shabbos, and here all the more so he should fulfill his obligation after the fact. This requires study. It also requires study regarding one who did not pray Maariv on Rosh Chodesh, prayed Shacharis twice, and omitted Yaaleh VeYavo from the second. Do we say that since it compensates for Maariv he need not repeat it, or is the reason one does not repeat Maariv that the new month is not sanctified at night, whereas here it is daytime? The latter appears correct. Some authorities maintain that even on the second night he must repeat it because the month has already been sanctified. Although Beit Yosef in siman 422 writes that this contradicts the Gemara, he did not examine it carefully. The Gemara says only that even in a deficient month he need not repeat it, and Tosafos mention this reasoning. Although we do not rule that way generally, in our case all authorities would agree that he must repeat it. This appears to me correct. Knesses HaGedolah writes in the name of one sage that he must repeat it. In siman 422 he likewise raises this doubt concerning one who failed to pray Maariv on Rosh Chodesh, prayed Shacharis twice, and omitted Yaaleh VeYavo from the second. He cites a dispute and concludes that he should repeat neither prayer. It appears to me, however, that the ruling stated here is primary. See there, for he did not cite the proofs that I have brought.
