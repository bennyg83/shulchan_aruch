Shabbos. Magen Avraham is uncertain whether, if he did not mention Shabbos in the second prayer, he must repeat it; see there. Knesses HaGedolah cites Rabbi Moshe di Boton that he has not fulfilled his obligation and must pray again, and Taz rules likewise; see there. Olas Tamid, however, rules that after the fact he has fulfilled his obligation, and Knesses HaGedolah there and Peri Chadash likewise rule.
Two. Magen Avraham writes that one who did not pray Maariv on Rosh Chodesh and prayed Shacharis twice must repeat the second prayer if he did not say Yaaleh VeYavo in it; see there. See Maharsha and examine this carefully. Knesses HaGedolah in siman 422 writes that he is not required to repeat it; see there. Peri Chadash likewise rules.
In the second. He thereby revealed that the first prayer was intended as compensation, as in the case of Havdalah in the following seif. This applies specifically when the compensatory prayer is for a prayer that was not on Rosh Chodesh. In that case one may reason, as below regarding Havdalah, that since he omitted Yaaleh VeYavo from the first prayer, he revealed that he prayed the first as compensation. If, however, Rosh Chodesh lasts two days and he did not pray Minchah on the first day of Rosh Chodesh, then when he prays two prayers on the second night of Rosh Chodesh, one for the current obligation and one as compensation, if he omitted Yaaleh VeYavo from the first and mentioned it in the second, it is obvious that he need not pray again. Here one cannot say that he revealed his intent, and he certainly merely erred.
