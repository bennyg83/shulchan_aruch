If he omitted Yaaleh Veyavo in the first prayer but recited it in the second. See the Mishnah Berurah for the reason. Derech HaChaim writes that the same law applies to one who must pray twice on the first day on which Tal U'Matar is begun. For example, he forgot Minchah on the previous day and must pray Maariv twice, mentioning Tal U'Matar in both prayers. If he omitted it in the first prayer but mentioned it in the second, his law is exactly as stated here. He further writes that if he omitted it in the second prayer, he should pray again as a voluntary prayer, stipulating: if I am obligated to pray, let it be for my obligation; otherwise, let it be voluntary. Shaarei Teshuvah writes similarly in the name of the Maggid Mishneh. In the Mishnah Berurah I ruled that he is exempt, because I saw many Acharonim who agree with the Peri Chadash. One who follows either authority has acted properly.
But if he omitted it in both, etc. The reason is that the new month is not sanctified at night, as stated below in siman 422. Regarding the Rama's gloss, the words 'all this is in the Kol Bo except what he wrote concerning two Rosh Chodesh prayers' mean that everything in this gloss appears in the Kol Bo except the ruling that he prays two Rosh Chodesh prayers. On that point the Rama disagrees with the Kol Bo, for the Kol Bo maintains that initially one does not mention Rosh Chodesh in the second prayer. The four words 'in the first but not in the second,' as printed in the Kol Bo, are a scribal error. This also appears from the Darchei Moshe below in siman 422, where he quotes the Kol Bo and then disputes it. All of this differs from the Taz in this siman. I copied this from Maamar Mordechai and Nehar Shalom. There is no practical difference, because the halachah follows the Rama here, as I wrote in the name of the Acharonim in seif katan 26 of the Mishnah Berurah.
