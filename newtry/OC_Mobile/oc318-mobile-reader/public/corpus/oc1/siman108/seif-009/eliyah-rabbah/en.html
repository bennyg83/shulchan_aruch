Two of Shabbos, etc. The Taz wrote: it seems to me that even after the fact, if he prayed the second prayer as a weekday prayer, he has not fulfilled his obligation, for there is no prayer on Shabbos without mention of Shabbos. However, the Magen Avraham wrote that he fulfills his obligation after the fact, and brought proof from the Tur, siman 422, who wrote that when we say that if one did not pray Minchah on Shabbos he prays two weekday prayers on Motzaei Shabbos, this is because on Shabbos too he should really have prayed Shemoneh Esrei, except that out of honor for Shabbos the Sages did not burden him; if so, Shemoneh Esrei is also relevant to Shabbos. The Magen Avraham therefore says that certainly here he fulfills the obligation after the fact. His words are not correct in this, for although it would have been proper to pray Shemoneh Esrei on Shabbos, they certainly would also have needed to mention Shabbos in it, as below in siman 268, as implied by the Taz. Rather, the Tur meant that one should not say there is no obligation to make it up on Motzaei Shabbos because Shemoneh Esrei does not apply to Shabbos; therefore he said that Shemoneh Esrei prayer does apply to Shabbos. Still, for another reason one may rule that he fulfills his obligation, since it is a makeup for a weekday prayer and he fulfills it after the fact. So too, in Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah in siman 422, he was uncertain and discussed at length whether we follow the main prayer or the makeup prayer, and concluded that it is reasonable that he fulfills his obligation after the fact. He wrote that the same law applies when one forgot Ma'ariv on the night of Rosh Chodesh and, in the second Shacharis prayer, forgot to mention Ya'aleh VeYavo, that he fulfilled the obligation, since had he omitted it at Ma'ariv he would have fulfilled his obligation. The Magen Avraham disagrees, because the reason one is not sent back at Ma'ariv is that the sanctification of the month is not done at night, whereas here it is day. This is not a challenge, for this prayer still comes as makeup for Ma'ariv, where one does not go back. It seems to me that since this doubt depends on reasoning and there is no resolution among the early poskim, and according to the Taz this reasoning did not occur to him and it was obvious to him that one does not fulfill the obligation, it is therefore proper to go back and pray as a voluntary prayer with an innovation. If there are two days of Rosh Chodesh and one forgot Minchah on the first day and makes it up at night but forgot to mention Rosh Chodesh in the second prayer, the Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah there clearly holds that he fulfilled the obligation. So it also seems to me, for if one forgot Minchah of Shabbos or Rosh Chodesh he makes it up at Ma'ariv and does not mention Shabbos or Rosh Chodesh at all; clearly we follow the present time. It appears that in all these laws the same applies if he answered Barchu with the congregation before he prayed Minchah, as below in siman 263, seif 15, and so it is implied in Ramban, siman 168.
Of Shabbos, etc. For the sanctity of Shabbos has already taken effect upon him. The Levush wrote that the reason is that it is a denigration of Shabbos, but this is difficult from what I brought above, that even on Shabbos it would always have been proper to pray Shemoneh Esrei; the Taz also wondered about this. It seems to my humble opinion that this is what he means: since in any event one must mention Shabbos, as I explained, therefore one should not pray Shemoneh Esrei because of the burden. And if he prayed Shemoneh Esrei and did not mention Shabbos, that is a denigration of Shabbos, for he adds what is not normally said and does not mention Shabbos; analyze this.
