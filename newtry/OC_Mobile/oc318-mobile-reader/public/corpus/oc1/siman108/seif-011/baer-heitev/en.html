Twice. There is a dispute on this. Tosafos in the name of R' Yehuda hold he need not return for this, for he already prayed the eighteen, only he forgot it was Shabbos; and when he completes and prays the eighteen and does not mention Shabbos, what does he gain by this tashlumin. And the scholars of Provence hold that what he prayed the eighteen without Shabbos is as if he did not pray at all, since he did not do properly; therefore the poskim ruled he should return to pray as nedavah and need not innovate, since there is a rationale that he must return and pray from the side of law. Knesset HaGedolah wrote in siman 494 in the name of Radbaz part 1 siman 176 [161] that if he was compelled and did not pray at Maariv, he prays during the day two prayers, one obligatory and one for tashlumin, and he does not say Attah Chonantanu even though he did not make Havdalah—see there.

Rosh Chodesh. And if at night too it is Rosh Chodesh, according to all he prays, for he gains that he says Yaaleh VeYavo. And the same on the night of the second day of Yom Tov. And the same if he forgot "and grant dew and rain" or "Who causes the wind to blow." Magen Avraham. And it appears simple that if he forgot Yaaleh VeYavo also at night in the compensatory prayer, he need not return—for the month is not sanctified at night, and although it is compensation for Minchah prayer, with this the difficulty of Tosafos will be resolved in what they challenge: why do I need this reason that the month is not sanctified at night—let it be derived that Maariv is optional, etc.—for it matters for the law, as written, and it is wondrous. See Berachos 26a and 44b. And likewise Knesset HaGedolah in siman 422 and Peri Chadash siman 7: if Rosh Chodesh is two days and on the first day he was distracted and did not pray Minchah, when he prays the second night of Rosh Chodesh two prayers—if in the compensatory prayer he forgot and did not mention Yaaleh VeYavo, they do not return him—see there.
