Two. This is disputed. Tosafos, citing Rabbeinu Yitzchak, maintains that he need not repeat the prayer, for he already prayed Shemoneh Esrei and merely omitted the Shabbos insertion. What would he gain by praying another Shemoneh Esrei as compensation without mentioning Shabbos? The scholars of Provence maintain that a Shemoneh Esrei recited without the Shabbos insertion is regarded as though he did not pray at all, since he did not pray according to law. The poskim therefore concluded that he should pray again as a voluntary prayer. He need not introduce a novel request, since there is an opinion that he is legally required to pray again. Knesses HaGedolah writes in siman 294, citing Radbaz, part 1, siman (176) [361], that if one was prevented from praying on Motzaei Shabbos, he prays twice during the day, one for the current obligation and one as compensation, and he does not say Atah Chonantanu even if he has not recited Havdalah; see there.
Rosh Chodesh. If the night is also Rosh Chodesh, all agree that he should pray again, because he gains by saying Yaaleh VeYavo. The same applies on the second night of Yom Tov, and likewise if he omitted VeSein Tal UMatar or Mashiv HaRuach. Magen Avraham. It appears obvious to me that if he also omitted Yaaleh VeYavo at night in the compensatory prayer, he need not repeat it, since the new month is not sanctified at night. This is so even though the prayer compensates for Minchah. This resolves Tosafos' question why the reason that the new month is not sanctified at night is needed, when Maariv is voluntary. The practical difference is as stated. See Berachos 26a and 30b. Knesses HaGedolah in siman 422 and Peri Chadash in this siman likewise rule that if Rosh Chodesh lasts two days and one was occupied and did not pray Minchah on the first day, then when he prays twice on the second night of Rosh Chodesh, he is not required to repeat the compensatory prayer if he forgot Yaaleh VeYavo in it; see there.
