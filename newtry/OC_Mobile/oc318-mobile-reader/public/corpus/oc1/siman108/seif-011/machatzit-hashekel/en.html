(Marginal note 11) That he holds forgetting is called coercion — meaning: we do not say he should have put it in mind lest he forget later and would have had to pray earlier, and then it would not be called coercion. And in Choshen Mishpat siman 382 — meaning: from there flows the law here; these are the words of Noda B'Yehuda in Bava Kamma, chapter Keitzad Haregel, on what was said: a stone was resting in his lap, he forgot it, it fell and caused damage — although he must pay damages, for Scripture includes inadvertent like intentional for damage payments, nevertheless he is exempt from the four things, namely healing, lost wages, shame, and blemish, for regarding those there is no inclusion; therefore inadvertent is exempt, even though he should have put it in mind first lest he forget and it fall and damage. Even so it is called inadvertent; from this Noda B'Yehuda learned the same applies regarding prayer — forgetting is called coercion, as with the four things. We do not say he should have been concerned lest he forget.

Above in siman 527 regarding eruv tavshilin: in inadvertence, if he did not place [the eruv], he may rely on the city's elder who makes eruv for the whole city, but not one who acted intentionally. He also wrote that forgetting is called inadvertent.

And siman 538 — regarding a lost item in Choshen Mishpat. See Even HaEzer siman 232, where he wrote: one who swore or vowed to do something within a year or on a certain day, etc., and if he did not do it immediately because he said he still had leisure — if he forgot or was coerced later and did not do it, some say it is called coercion and some say it is not called coercion, end quote. Magen Avraham challenged: did not Noda B'Yehuda in Keitzad Haregel learn two laws — the law of prayer explained here in Shulchan Aruch and the law of oath mentioned in Yoreh De'ah siman 232 — from the law of the four things, that in both forgetting applies? Thus Noda B'Yehuda equated the two laws; if so, why did the author here omit and not bring the dissenting view that it is not called coercion, as written in Yoreh De'ah siman 232?

So too in Choshen Mishpat siman 21, from the words of Ramban and Rabbeinu Yerucham, who wrote: one from whom they bought that if he does not come on a certain day and swear, his fellow will prevail in his claims and take all he claimed without oath, etc. — the day passed and he did not come, the conditions were fulfilled and he lost his right. If he brought proof he was coerced that day, he is exempt from this acquisition, end quote. He wrote that he brought proof he was coerced that day — implying that the day before that day he was not coerced; even so it is called coercion, and we do not say he should have put it in mind lest coercion occur that day.

And the proof from the Agudah — and he is the one who says it is not called coercion — that he brought in Yoreh De'ah siman 232; Rabbeinu Yerucham brought it in Choshen Mishpat siman 207. It is as we say in Gittin 75a: per the law, one who sells a house in walled cities has a year from his sale to redeem; if the year passed and he did not redeem, it remains permanently with the buyer and does not leave in the Jubilee. We learned: initially the buyer would hide on the last day of the year so the seller could not return his money and redeem it, so it would remain in his hand forever. Hillel the Elder enacted that he leave his money in the chamber and enter his house. One cannot say such a case is called coercion — why did Hillel need an enactment? Rather the law is so: on the last day he was coerced, for the buyer hid himself; since he came on the last day to court wanting to redeem and the court recognizes his coercion, even if the last day passed the court may compel [the buyer] to accept his money and return his house to him. Even so, such a case is not called coercion, for he should have been concerned lest coercion occur on the last day and should have redeemed earlier. He stipulated to redeem within three years, and in a matter that is not asmakhta — such as if he told him: if you do not redeem within three years it is acquired from now, and similar matters that remove asmakhta, as explained there in Choshen Mishpat — coercion occurred that is absolute to him, and we are on the grounds that through coercion one is exempt, for the Merciful One exempts coercion. So too this siman 21 in Choshen Mishpat, where one is obligated on himself that if he does not swear within a set time his fellow is believed without oath and he must pay what he was not obligated to pay — since coercion occurred the Merciful One exempted him and he is not obligated; but to obligate his fellow for what his fellow was not obligated to obligate him now through this coercion — we have not heard. If so, in this case where he lent on his field and stipulated that if he does not redeem within three years it belongs to the lender from now — when the time arrived and he did not redeem, it belonged to the lender retroactively; and by coercion occurring you want to obligate the lender to return his field that he acquired by law — we do not say this. The same applies in a house in walled cities, were it not for Hillel's enactments: by law the buyer need not return, even though the seller is coerced; one is not obligated to the buyer who acquired the house by law through the seller's coercion. So Shach wrote in Choshen Mishpat siman 21; see there that he further divided several legal distinctions. See Yoreh De'ah siman 232 — Taz also rejected the proof from the Agudah.
