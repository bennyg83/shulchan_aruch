Two on Motzaei Shabbos. There is a dispute about this. Tosafot in the name of Ri hold that he need not go back for this, for he already prayed Shemoneh Esrei, only he forgot Shabbos; if he makes it up and prays Shemoneh Esrei without mentioning Shabbos, what will he gain through this makeup? The sages of Provence hold that praying Shemoneh Esrei without Shabbos is as though he did not pray at all, since he did not do it properly. Therefore the poskim ruled that he should go back and pray as a voluntary prayer, and he need not introduce something new, since the view that he must go back and pray by law is itself no small innovation.
As a voluntary prayer. Meaning that he stipulates and says: if I am obligated, this is for my obligation, and if not, it is voluntary. See the Peri Megadim, who says that the same applies to every genuine dispute among major poskim: one prays as a voluntary prayer, provided that it is a real dispute, not an individual against the many or a lesser authority against a greater one. Then its law is like a doubt whether he prayed, above in siman 107, seif 1. On Shabbos and Yom Tov one does not pray a doubtful prayer, for how can he stipulate, since voluntary offerings are not brought on Shabbos, as above in siman 107. If one was under compulsion and did not pray on Motzaei Shabbos, he prays two prayers during the day, one for the obligation and one for makeup, and he does not say Atah Chonantanu even though he has not yet made Havdalah. So wrote Knesses HaGedolah in the name of the Radvaz. The Magen Avraham in siman 294 wrote that he did not know the reason. In Magen Gibborim, he copied the reason from him: even if he has not yet made Havdalah, he need not say Atah Chonantanu, since he is obligated to make Havdalah, and they did not institute makeup for something that has a remedy. Since Atah Chonantanu has a remedy through Havdalah, it has no makeup.
And the same applies if, etc. Meaning that it is also proper to pray a second prayer at Ma'ariv for makeup as a voluntary prayer. This is also because of the above reason, for he gains nothing through this prayer, since he will not say Ya'aleh VeYavo now that Rosh Chodesh has passed, and he already prayed Shemoneh Esrei. But if that night is also Rosh Chodesh, everyone agrees that he prays, for he gains that he says Ya'aleh VeYavo. So wrote the Magen Avraham, and the later authorities agreed with him. The Magen Avraham further wrote that the same applies on the night of the second day of Yom Tov, meaning that if he prayed a weekday prayer at Minchah on the first day of Yom Tov, he must pray at night as makeup according to all opinions, for he gains that he prays the Yom Tov prayer. In Tzelach, he disagreed with this second law, and this is cited in the Chiddushei Rabbi Akiva Eiger; see Machatzis HaShekel and Magen Gibborim.
Ya'aleh VeYavo. He mentioned Ya'aleh VeYavo precisely, for if one forgot Tal U'Matar or Mashiv HaRuach on Shabbos or Rosh Chodesh at Minchah, or forgot another matter that requires him to go back and pray, everyone agrees that he prays two prayers in the evening, for he gains this through the makeup prayer.
Of Rosh Chodesh. If Rosh Chodesh fell on Friday and was only one day, and he forgot to mention Rosh Chodesh at Minchah and remembered only after dark, he should not make it up at all on Friday night, since it accomplishes nothing; he could only pray as voluntary, and voluntary prayers are not prayed on Shabbos. If one did not pray Shacharis on the first day of Pesach or on Shemini Atzeres, when he makes it up at Minchah he prays two identical prayers. If he erred in the makeup prayer and prayed it as it would have been proper to pray at Shacharis, he does not go back. Likewise, if one did not pray Minchah on the day before one begins saying Tal U'Matar and prays two Ma'ariv prayers, he should ask for rain in both; after the fact, if he did not ask in the makeup prayer, he has fulfilled. See Biur Halacha.
