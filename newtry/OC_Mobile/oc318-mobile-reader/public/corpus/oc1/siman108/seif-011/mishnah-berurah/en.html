(32) On Motzei Shabbat, twice — there is dispute: Tosafot in the name of Rabbenu Yitzchak hold one need not return for this, for one already prayed the Eighteen Blessings, only forgetting Shabbat; and when one completes and prays the Eighteen Blessings without mentioning Shabbat, what does one gain in this make-up? The scholars of Provence hold that what one prayed as the Eighteen Blessings without Shabbat is as if one did not pray at all, since one did not act properly; therefore the poskim ruled one returns to pray as a voluntary prayer and need not innovate, since there is reason to require return and prayer from the law — no great innovation is needed for this.

(33) As a voluntary prayer — meaning one stipulates and says: if I am obligated, this fulfills my obligation, and if not, behold it is voluntary. See in Pri Megadim that the same applies to any doubt of a dispute among the great ones — one prays as voluntary, on condition that the doubt be a real dispute, not one individual against many or small against great; then one's law is as the doubt whether one prayed above in siman 107 seif 1. On Shabbat and Yom Tov one does not pray a doubtful prayer — how can one stipulate, for voluntary offerings are not brought on Shabbat, as above in siman 107. If one was compelled and did not pray on Motzei Shabbat, one prays by day two prayers: one as obligation and one as make-up, and one does not say Atah Chonantanu even though one did not make havdalah — so is the custom in the name of Radbaz; Magen Avraham in siman 294 wrote he did not know his reason — see there; Magen Giborim copied his reason: even if one has not yet made havdalah, one need not say Atah Chonantanu, since one is obligated to make havdalah — they did not institute make-up for something that has a remedy; and since Atah Chonantanu has a remedy in havdalah, it has no make-up.

(34) The same applies if, etc. — meaning, it is also proper to pray in the evening a second prayer for completion as a voluntary prayer; this too is for the reason above, because one gains nothing in this prayer — for Yaaleh VeYavo one will not say now, since Rosh Chodesh has passed and one already prayed the Eighteen Blessings. But if at night too it is Rosh Chodesh, according to all one prays, for one gains saying Yaaleh VeYavo — so writes Magen Avraham, and the later authorities agreed. Magen Avraham also wrote the same on the night of the second day of Yom Tov: if one prayed weekday in the afternoon of the first day of Yom Tov, one must pray at night for completion according to all, for one gains praying the Yom Tov [Amidah]; in Tzelach part 7 he ruled the second law, and it is brought in Chiddushei Rabbi Akiva Eiger; see in Machatzit HaShekel and Magen Giborim.

(35) Yaaleh VeYavo — he specified Yaaleh VeYavo precisely: if one forgot tal umatar or mashiv haruach on Shabbat and Rosh Chodesh in the afternoon, or forgot something else requiring return and repeat — according to all one prays in the evening twice, for one gains this in the make-up prayer.

(36) Of Rosh Chodesh — if Rosh Chodesh fell on erev Shabbat and Rosh Chodesh was only one day, and one forgot in the afternoon to mention Rosh Chodesh and remembered after dark — one has no completion at all on Shabbat night, since it accomplishes nothing; one may only pray as voluntary prayer, and voluntary prayer is not prayed on Shabbat. If one did not pray the morning prayer on the first day of Passover or on Shemini Atzeret, when completing in the afternoon one prays equivalent prayers. If one erred in make-up and prayed as one should have prayed in the morning, one does not return. Similarly, if one did not pray the afternoon on the day before they begin saying tal umatar and prays the evening prayer twice — one requests rain in both; and after the fact, if one did not request in the make-up prayer, one has fulfilled — see in Biur Halachah.
