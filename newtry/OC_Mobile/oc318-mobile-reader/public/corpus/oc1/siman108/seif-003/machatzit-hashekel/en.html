(Marginal note 3) Shacharit twice — it seems to me forbidden, etc., for the time of the second prayer has arrived.

In siman 89 and siman 232 — for we rule that Minchah, since it is rabbinic, even if he began [eating] in prohibition he does not stop. If so, above in siman 89, seif 5, they say one must stop for Shacharit — that is because they were stringent and based it on the verse 'do not eat upon the blood,' meaning: do not eat before you prayed for your blood; not so here, where he already prayed Shacharit before eating, and there is no Torah prohibition of 'upon the blood.' However, he wrote in seif 3 that one completes only within prayer time; and in Sefer Orach Chayyim in the name of Perishah: he should not delay except the measure of walking four cubits or saying Ashrei, and immediately pray the make-up prayer, end quote. If so, when engaged in a meal and not stopping, there is no doubt he waits more than this measure — how can he complete after his meal? And in Lechem Chamudos there he wrote from the implication of Rashba's words: if he left the matter of prayer and went away, he again has no reason to complete, end quote. If so, how can one sit to eat after Shacharit — he has removed his hands from prayer; how can he complete after finishing his meal? And it is not Magen Avraham's reasoning that all this is only lechatchilah; rather Rashba's language that Levushei Serad brought implies even after the fact, see there.
