In the Shulchan Aruch, seif 3: Only during the time of prayer, etc. See Nesiv Chaim, who writes that if one did not pray Shacharis, and it was forgotten from him at the time of Minchah prayer, even though he prayed Minchah Gedolah while the time for Minchah still remained for everyone, nevertheless he can no longer compensate for it, since he did not remember during the obligatory prayer immediately following it. The same applies at Maariv: if he prayed at the beginning of the night, although its time extends throughout the night, he nevertheless cannot compensate once he did not remember during the obligatory prayer immediately following it. See there. The word ‘Maariv’ appears there in place of ‘Shacharis,’ but this is a scribal error. Machazik Berachah copied it as written and likewise erred, and it must be corrected as I have written. See Eliyah Rabbah in the name of Malbushei Yom Tov, who explains that this applies when he delayed a long time after Maariv, since once he is no longer engaged in prayer it is no longer a time for compensation. Eliyah Rabbah brings some proofs that compensation is effective throughout the prayer period, but nevertheless concludes that one should be stringent and pray it as a voluntary prayer. See Ateres Zekeinim, who writes that one may not compensate for Shacharis immediately after midday, before the time for Minchah has arrived, etc. This requires study, for according to the strict law the time for Minchah has already arrived after six hours, except that we are not expert in determining it, as stated in siman 233. It may be answered, however, that nevertheless, since the proper time has not yet arrived, it is not called a time of prayer. It may also be answered that if we call it a time of prayer, he would then be required first to pray the obligation of the current time. See what I wrote at the beginning of siman 88, and see Taz and Peri Chadash here and there. The primary ruling derives from the Rashba, and his words imply as the Taz writes. See there.
