For there is no time, etc. The Levush explains: on the next day after midday, before the time of Minchah has arrived; and likewise if one did not pray Minchah, he completes it during the time of Ma'ariv, but not the whole night after the time of Ma'ariv prayer has ended. The Malbushei Yom Tov wrote: What time is there for it? Since he waited a long time after praying Ma'ariv, as he is no longer occupied with prayer, there is no time here for makeup prayer. In my humble opinion, perhaps the Levush meant to exclude after midnight, for the Sages instituted that its law is before midnight, as below in siman 235 regarding Krias Shema; and for us, prayer follows Krias Shema. Or he is speaking to exclude after dawn has risen, as below in siman 235, seif 4; this requires study. Also, regarding what the Malbushei Yom Tov wrote that if he waited a long time, etc., the measure of delay requires much clarification. I saw in Lechem Chamudos, page 30, that if he left the matter of prayer and went away, he may no longer make it up. This implies that immediately when he leaves the matter of prayer, he may not make it up. In the Perishah I found that he wrote that one should not delay more than the time needed to walk four amos or to say Ashrei, and should then immediately pray the makeup prayer. However, I wrote the main law in Eliyahu Zuta that this requires study, for they inferred this from the Rashba cited by the Beit Yosef, who wrote that he is occupied with his prayer, etc. In my humble opinion, perhaps that language is not precise; rather, throughout the time of prayer we call him occupied with prayer. This is why he concludes, but not outside the time of prayer, etc., implying that the main matter depends on the time of prayer. This also appears from the Shulchan Aruch and Levush, and it is explicit in the Beit Yosef, siman 89, who explained the words of the Rashba this way; see there and analyze. So it also seems to me from the gloss of the Semak, siman 11, and Tashbetz, siman 223, who wrote that if he forgot and did not pray Minchah until morning, he should not pray Minchah, since the time of two prayers has passed. This implies that even if he prayed Ma'ariv at the beginning of the night, he may pray Minchah until morning. Nevertheless, one should be concerned and stringent to pray it as a voluntary prayer.
