He should say. Ashrei. See the Taz. In the Semak, brought by the Beit Yosef, it says that if one prays two Shacharis prayers he should say Ashrei before the second prayer, and likewise if he prays two Ma'ariv prayers he should say Ashrei before the second prayer. It omits the case of two Minchah prayers, that he should say Ashrei before the second. The Perishah and Bach understand from this that in such a case he should not say Ashrei before the second prayer. This also appears to be the view of the Mechaber, who changed the order of the Tur. The Tur wrote: if he erred and did not pray Shacharis, or did not pray Ma'ariv, and likewise if he did not pray Minchah, which is a progression of not only this but even that, as the Bach wrote. The Mechaber, however, first wrote one who erred at Minchah and then one who erred at Ma'ariv, in order to attach the saying of Ashrei to Shacharis; and certainly one who erred at Minchah says Ashrei at Ma'ariv, for it relates to Minchah. The Rama wrote and likewise, etc., so that one not mistakenly say that at Shacharis, where there is Ashrei in any event, he should say it before the second Shemoneh Esrei, unlike Ma'ariv. The Taz disagrees with them and holds that one who erred at Shacharis also says Ashrei at Minchah before the second Shemoneh Esrei. There are three reasons for Ashrei: the reason of the Bach and Taz, that one should stand for prayer from words of Torah; another reason, to separate between one prayer and the next, and although the measure of four amos suffices, as in siman 105, nevertheless initially he should say Ashrei; see Darchei Moshe here and Bach. The third reason is that it should be recognizable that it is the Minchah prayer, as the Beit Yosef wrote in siman 234 in the name of the Rivash. According to this, if one erred at Ma'ariv, he says Ashrei at Shacharis, for there is already Ashrei before Lamnatzeach, and he should say it before the second Shemoneh Esrei even though recognition is not relevant; if he erred at Minchah, he says Ashrei at Ma'ariv because of recognition; if he erred at Shacharis, it implies that he does not say Ashrei twice at Minchah, like the Bach and Perishah. The Taz, however, holds that one who erred at Shacharis says Ashrei twice at Minchah, and he explains the need for the Semak's words, that it necessarily mentioned both Ma'ariv and Minchah, and that suffices. Had it said only Ma'ariv, one might think that at Shacharis, where he says Ashrei in any event, he should say it between the two prayers, but when he erred at Minchah, Ashrei is not possible at Ma'ariv. Had it said only Minchah, that he says Ashrei at Ma'ariv, one might think the same applies at Shacharis and that one Ashrei suffices; from the extra wording we learn that at Shacharis he needs two Ashreis, one between the two prayers and one afterward before Lamnatzeach. Had it mentioned only one who erred at Shacharis and says Ashrei twice at Minchah, one might say the second Ashrei is for makeup of whoever says Tehillah three times each day, etc.; and even had it mentioned one who erred at Shacharis and Minchah, we would not know that one who erred at Ma'ariv says Ashrei twice at Shacharis. Therefore the Semak mentioned Minchah and Ma'ariv, and that suffices. From all the poskim it implies that at Shacharis one says Ashrei once, and it counts also for Lamnatzeach. Simply, one can explain the need: had it said that one who erred at Minchah says Ashrei at Ma'ariv, that may be to make up Ashrei or so that it be recognizable, unlike Shacharis. See Perishah and Beit Yosef here and in siman 234. Regarding Tachanun, see Magen Avraham, letter 4.
What. The Mechaber wrote in seif 3 are the words of the Rashba brought by the Beit Yosef here and in siman 89. But the Taz there, letter 3, and the Perishah and other later authorities explained that one makes it up specifically immediately after the prayer of its time, but if it is distant from it, it no longer has makeup. See Eliyah Rabbah, letter 4, who concludes that if one prayed the obligatory prayer and went about his business, and then wishes to make up the prayer he forgot, he should pray it as a voluntary prayer; see there, and this is correct. The rule is that in every doubtful dispute among major poskim, one prays as a voluntary prayer, as in seif 11, meaning that he stipulates and says: if I am obligated, this is an obligation, and if not, it is voluntary, as in siman 107, Magen Avraham letter 1. This is provided that it is a real doubtful dispute, not an individual against the many or a lesser authority against a greater one, for in such a case a doubtful dispute is equivalent to a doubt in the factual event itself. On Shabbos and Yom Tov one does not pray a doubtful prayer, for how can he stipulate, since voluntary offerings are not brought on Shabbos; see siman 107.
I was uncertain. Regarding one who did not pray Ma'ariv, and four hours of the day passed, and he remembered immediately after completing Shacharis at the end of four hours: may he make up Ma'ariv? Since if he had not prayed Shacharis he may pray until midday, as in siman 89, and there it is an obligation, for there are those who say one must pray until midday, perhaps this is still not called that two prayer-times have passed, and he may make up Ma'ariv before midday. I resolved it from what the Levush wrote, that one does not make up Minchah after the time of Ma'ariv has ended for the whole night, meaning after midnight, as the Eliyah Rabbah, letter 4, wrote. If so, certainly here it is called that two prayer-times have passed in such a case. The words of the Eliyah Rabbah are difficult, for in the Rambam, chapter 3 of Hilchos Tefillah, halacha 6, it is explained that the time of Ma'ariv is initially the whole night. His second answer, that it means after dawn rose, appears correct; with Hashem's help this will be explained in siman 235. This is not comparable to what the Nachalas Tzvi wrote, that if one erred and did not pray Shacharis but prayed Mussaf, he makes up Shacharis at Minchah, for Mussaf's time is the whole day. Although after seven hours he is called negligent, as in siman 286, nevertheless its time is the whole day. But Shacharis after four hours does not give him the reward of prayer in its time, as in siman 89. See the Magen Avraham here, letter 6. From this we learn all the more that he may make up Minchah after midnight during the time of Ma'ariv, as stated. So it seems to me.
