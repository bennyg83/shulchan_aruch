He will say Ashrei — Taz. In Semak, Beit Yosef brings it: for Shacharis twice one says Ashrei before the second prayer; and similarly for Maariv twice one says Ashrei before the second prayer. He omitted [the case of] Mincha twice, that one says Ashrei before the second. Perishah and Bach hold thus — one does not say Ashrei in the second (and so is the Mechaber's view, who changed the order of Tur, which wrote: one erred and did not pray Shacharis, or did not pray Maariv, and likewise did not pray Mincha — which is not this-and-that, as Bach wrote; the Mechaber preceded "erred Mincha" and afterward "erred Maariv," only to adjoin saying Ashrei to Shacharis he wrote thus; and all the more if one erred Mincha that one says Ashrei in Maariv, which pertains to Mincha). And Rabbeinu Yonah wrote "and so…" so one not err to say in Shacharis there is Ashrei — one says it before the eighteen [blessings] of the second, unlike Maariv. The Taz disagrees with them and holds: if one erred Shacharis, one says in Mincha too Ashrei before the eighteen of the second. Behold, for Ashrei there are three reasons: Bach's and Taz's reason — to stand amid words of Torah; and there is another reason — to interrupt between prayer and prayer (even though after the fact it is fine as in siman 105, nevertheless ab initio one says Ashrei). It seems to me here Bach has a third reason this time — that it be evident it is Mincha prayer — so too Beit Yosef in siman 234 in the name of R' Yitzchak ibn Shua. Therefore, if one erred Maariv, one says in Shacharis Ashrei, for in any case Ashrei [must be said] before Lamnatzeiach — one says that before the eighteen of the second, even though recognition is not relevant. If one erred Mincha, one says Ashrei in Maariv because of recognition. If one erred Shacharis, it implies one does not say in Mincha once Ashrei, as Bach and Perishah. Behold the Taz holds that if one erred Shacharis, one says in Mincha twice Ashrei, and this requires [explanation] per Semak's words — necessarily he takes both Maariv and Mincha, and that suffices. For if he said only Maariv, we would say in Shacharis in any case Ashrei — one says it between the second prayer; but if one erred Mincha, one cannot [say] Ashrei in Maariv. And if he said Mincha, one says Ashrei in Maariv — we would say the same in Shacharis, for one Ashrei suffices. And from the extra [wording] we hear one needs in Shacharis twice Ashrei — one between the second prayer and one afterward before Lamnatzeiach. And if he took only "erred Shacharis," one says in Mincha twice Ashrei — we would say the second Ashrei is for make-up. "Whoever says Tehillah three times each day…" — and even if he took "erred Shacharis and Mincha," we would not know that if one erred Maariv one says in Shacharis twice Ashrei; therefore Semak takes Mincha and Maariv, and that suffices. And from all the poskim it is implied in Shacharis one says once Ashrei and "go up to Lamnatzeiach" too; and simply one can say the requirement: if he said Mincha, one says in Maariv Ashrei for completion of Ashrei, or that it be evident — unlike Shacharis. See Perishah and Beit Yosef here and siman 234; and regarding Tachanun, Magen Avraham letter 4.

What the Mechaber wrote in seif 3 — these are Rashba's words; Beit Yosef brought them here and in seif 9. But the Taz there 3 and Perishah and other later authorities explained: only immediately after its timely prayer one completes; but not far removed from it — then it has no make-up. Eliyah Rabbah letter 4 concluded: if one prayed the obligation and went to business and wishes to complete the prayer he forgot, he prays as voluntary — see there; and this is correct. The general rule: any doubt of dispute among the great ones — one prays as voluntary, as in seif 11 — meaning one stipulates and says: if I am obligated, behold this is obligation, and if not, voluntary, as in siman 107 in Magen Avraham letter 1. On condition that it be a real doubt of dispute — not one individual against many, nor small against great; for in such a case a doubt of dispute equals a doubt about the act itself. On Shabbat and Yom Tov one does not pray a doubtful prayer — how can one stipulate, for voluntary offerings are not brought on Shabbat — incident 107.

I was uncertain — one who did not pray Maariv and four hours of the day passed, and he remembered immediately when he finished Shacharis at the end of four hours — whether he completes Maariv, since if he had not prayed Shacharis he prays until midnight as in seif 9 (and there the obligation is that some hold one must pray until midnight, as written there); therefore the time of two prayers has not yet passed, and he completes Maariv before midnight. And it is simple to me from what Levush wrote, that one does not complete Mincha after Maariv ends all night — meaning after midnight, as Eliyah Rabbah wrote letter 4; therefore all the more here, where the time of two prayers has passed in such a case. And Eliyah Rabbah's words are difficult — Rambam Hilchot Tefilah 6 will explain Maariv's time is ab initio all night, and the second condition Eliyah Rabbah wrote after alos hashachar — this appears [correct], and there it will be explained in siman 235. And it seems to me per what Nesivos wrote: one erred and did not pray Shacharis and prayed Musaf — he completes Shacharis in Mincha, for Musaf's time is all day, even though he is called negligent after seven [hours], as in siman 286; nevertheless its time is all day, unlike Shacharis after four hours, for reward of prayer in its time they do not give him, as in seif 9 — Magen Avraham here letter 6. And from this we learn all the more that one completes Mincha after midnight of Maariv, as stated — it seems to me.
