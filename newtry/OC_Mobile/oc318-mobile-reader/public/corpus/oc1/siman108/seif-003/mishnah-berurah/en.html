At the time of prayer. Some later authorities wrote that this means Chazal instituted makeup prayer only while he is still occupied with his main prayer, after waiting the time needed to walk four amos or after saying Ashrei in between. However, many later authorities agreed that although initially one must certainly be careful to pray the makeup prayer immediately after the obligatory prayer, after the fact it does not invalidate unless the time of prayer has passed. Thus, if he did not pray Ma'ariv, he prays two Shacharis prayers only until four hours into the day, which is the time for Shacharis prayer, since for this he receives reward for prayer in its time; after four hours he receives only reward for a prayer of mercy, as the Peri Megadim, Magen Gibborim, and Derech HaChaim wrote. The Derech HaChaim added a new point not mentioned in the other later authorities: if he also did not pray Shacharis itself until after four hours, he should then pray two prayers, since he is occupied with prayer. If one forgot to pray Minchah, some say that he prays two Ma'ariv prayers only until midnight, which is the time fit for Ma'ariv initially; see there in Derech HaChaim for what he wrote further as halacha. However, the Peri Megadim wrote that the time for making up Minchah is the whole night until dawn. If one forgot and did not pray Shacharis, it has makeup only from when the time of Minchah begins, namely the half hour after midday, and he must pray Minchah first; the time for makeup continues after the fact until bein hashmashos, even if it was a day with Mussaf and he prayed Mussaf in between. All this I have copied from the later authorities as practical halacha. Initially, however, in all these cases it is more correct that if he did not pray the makeup prayer immediately after the obligatory prayer, he should pray it as a voluntary prayer, meaning that he stipulates and says: if I am obligated, this is for my obligation, and if not, it is voluntary. So agreed the Peri Megadim.
