(15) At the time of prayer — some later authorities wrote that this means the Sages did not institute the completion prayer except while still engaged in one's primary prayer, after pausing for walking four cubits or Ashrei in between. But many later authorities agreed that although ab initio one must certainly be careful to pray the completion prayer immediately after the obligatory prayer, after the fact it does not invalidate except if prayer time passed — that is, if one did not pray the evening prayer, one prays the morning prayer twice only until four hours into the day, which is prayer time for the morning prayer, for one receives for this the reward of prayer in its time, unlike after four hours when one receives only the reward of supplication — so write Pri Megadim and Magen Giborim and Daat Torah; but Daat Torah added something new not mentioned by the other later authorities: if one also did not pray the morning prayer itself until after four hours, one must then pray twice, since one is engaged in prayer. If one forgot to pray the afternoon prayer, some say one prays the evening prayer twice only until midnight, which is the proper time for the evening prayer ab initio; see in Daat Torah what he wrote further on this for the law. Nevertheless Pri Megadim wrote that the time to complete the afternoon prayer is the entire night until dawn. If one forgot and did not pray the morning prayer, it has completion only from when the time of the afternoon prayer begins, which is the half hour after noon; and also if one prayed the afternoon prayer earlier, the time of completion extends after the fact until twilight, and even on a day when there is Musaf and one prayed Musaf in the middle. All this I copied from the later authorities for the law. Nevertheless ab initio it is more proper in all these cases that if one did not pray the completion prayer immediately after the obligatory prayer, one pray it as a voluntary prayer — meaning one stipulates and says: if I am obligated, behold this is for my obligation, and if not, behold it is voluntary; Pri Megadim agreed.
