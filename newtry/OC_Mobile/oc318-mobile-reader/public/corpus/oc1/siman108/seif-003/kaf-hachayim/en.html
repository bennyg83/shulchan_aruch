(3) There, and if he reversed he does not fulfill, etc. — Explanation: if he had in mind the first would be for make-up prayers he does not fulfill. Magen Avraham note 1; Eliyah Rabbah note 1; Siddur Beit Yaakov on laws of make-up prayer after Maariv prayer note 1; Raz note 1; Ben Ish Chai parashas Mishpatim note 9; and so agreed Nahar Shalom note 2. However some say he need not go back and pray unless he revealed his intent explicitly in his prayer that the second is for the sake of obligation and the first for make-up prayers — such as Motzei Shabbos where he made Havdalah in the second and not in the first; but if he reversed in thought alone he fulfills — Taz note 2, Magen Avraham note 2, Shaarei Teshuvah note 2, Chayei Adam general rule 27 note 4, and so is Chida's view in Kaf HaChayyim siman 22 note 11; this reasoning Raz brought there, and wrote one should be concerned for their words to innovate something when returning to pray, for then there is no concern of blessing in vain according to his view, as written in siman 107 seif 1; so too Peri Megadim in Eshel Avraham note 2. However if he prayed plainly and did not intend even in his thought which is for obligation and which for make-up prayers — such as he only knows they pray make-up prayers but does not know which must be obligatory and which make-up — it seems he need not return, for presumably his intent is to act per halacha; and since per halacha the first must be obligatory we say thus it was accepted before our Father in Heaven.
