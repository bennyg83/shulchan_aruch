(Marginal note 2) And if the reverse, etc. — and it requires examination, since in the Gemara they conclude with a difficulty, etc. — meaning: the law here flows from what is said in Berachos 26b: our Rabbis taught — if he erred and did not pray Minchah on Shabbat, he prays on Motzei Shabbat two weekday [prayers]. He makes havdalah in the first and not in the second; if he made havdalah in the second and not in the first, the second counts for him and the first does not (Rashi: the second counts because of Motzei Shabbat prayer, the first does not, for it is nothing — one may not precede a prayer whose time passed to a prayer whose time is now; the second too does not count for Shabbat, since he made havdalah in it he revealed his mind that it is not Shabbat and it is considered Maariv). They challenge: it should follow that since he did not make havdalah in the first, it is as one who did not pray, and we return him; and they raise: if he erred and did not mention, etc., in 'Who grants knowledge' they do not make him repeat because he can say it over the cup. Difficulty, end Gemara quote — from this flows the law here not to precede make-up prayer. Below in seif 10 he rules like the first version, and although in the Gemara they conclude with a difficulty, nevertheless it is difficult according to me, as Rosh wrote in the name of Rav Hai Gaon there: only where the Gemara concludes a refutation we do not rule thus; but where it concludes with a difficulty it is not an upset and we are permitted to resolve the difficulty and rule accordingly. On this Rosh wrote in the name of R' Hillel: one could say, since he did not make havdalah, etc., as Magen Avraham wrote.

So too from what they challenge, etc. — Rosh brings proof for his law: where he made havdalah in both or did not make havdalah in both, he fulfills both, from what they challenge to say, etc., 'as one who did not pray,' etc. — implying that if the law were so that if he did not make havdalah they return him, it would follow from the first version that the first did not count even for make-up prayer, since he preceded make-up. For since the law is where he did not make havdalah they return him, it must be that the first where he did not make havdalah is for make-up — but he raises from another version that even if he did not make havdalah they do not return him, and it counts for Motzei Shabbat prayer; therefore the reasoning is that it is as if he made havdalah in both, and the reasoning is where he made havdalah in both he fulfills both. The same where he did not make havdalah in the first and made havdalah in the second: although the questioner also knew that where he preceded make-up he does not fulfill, nevertheless the reasoning is — namely, only where it is evident the first is for make-up, such as if we said where he did not make havdalah we return him; but if we do not return him, R' Hillel Gaon's reasoning is not concluded — nevertheless there is revelation of mind that the first is make-up; this reasoning is that it does not help, and nevertheless he fulfills both. If so, for the conclusion it must be as Rav Hai Gaon wrote: where there is revelation of mind it does not count; but where he made havdalah in both or did not make havdalah in both he fulfills both, for there is no revelation of mind. Although there is thought from Minchah to distance the dispute between questioner and conclusion — it is not so, for for the questioner even revelation of mind does not help; according to truth revelation of mind helps, but in thought the questioner's reasoning remains that it does not help.

Nevertheless it requires examination regarding this, etc. — meaning: this law too, that revelation of mind through Ashrei is not clear to Magen Avraham and requires examination. So too in Chiddushei HaRashba — meaning: on the body of the law that with thought alone he fulfills. Thus Rashba there: however, if he did not make havdalah in one, or made havdalah in both, they make him repeat, for he did not reveal his mind that the first should not be because of his obligation — perhaps even though he did not make havdalah he erred and they do not make him repeat; so too when he made havdalah, perhaps he erred in the second and unintentionally made havdalah in it, end quote. Why rely on reasoning that perhaps he erred in a place that can be clarified — ask him whether he intentionally did not make havdalah in the first or second or did so by mistake? Even so, even if he did it intentionally so the first would be for make-up, we do not heed his thought since it is not evident from his actions that one can attribute it to error; therefore he fulfills both.
