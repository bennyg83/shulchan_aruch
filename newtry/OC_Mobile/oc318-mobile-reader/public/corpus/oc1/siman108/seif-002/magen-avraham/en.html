[When praying] Shacharis twice — it seems to me it is forbidden to eat before he prays the second [prayer]; and if he began [eating], he does not interrupt — see incident 89 and siman 232.

He should say Ashrei, etc. It implies one may not [say] Tachanun until after the second prayer, for otherwise there would be no need to interrupt with Ashrei — so too writes Levush. And Maharshal writes he should say Tachanun after the first prayer.

He should say Ashrei between [the prayers], etc. But the kabbalist R' Moshe Cordovero warned greatly not to say Ashrei after Minchah [Bach]; and I myself saw in Cordovero, Parashat Vayeira and Parashat Korach, that he wrote not to say Ashrei in Maariv; and in Avkat Rocheil siman 234 in the name of Maharrai — and it seems proper for one who comes late to the synagogue at the hour when the congregation began Minchah that he pray the eighteen [blessings] with them and afterward say Ashrei, unlike Bach; and so too in Hagahot Maimoniyot and in Shulchan Gavo'a siman 422; and so is the widespread custom in synagogues to say Ashrei after Minchah. However, in the Zohar, Parashat Pinchas page 244, it implies that after the Minchah prayer one should not say Ashrei; and it seems to me that Yom Kippur differs, for it is His will all day; nevertheless the Zohar implies that it is permitted to say it, not with intent of obligation.
