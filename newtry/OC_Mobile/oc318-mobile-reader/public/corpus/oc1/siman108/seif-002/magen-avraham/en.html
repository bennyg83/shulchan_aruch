Shacharis twice. It appears to me that he may not eat before praying the second prayer. If he already began eating, he need not interrupt. See siman 89 and siman 232.
He should say Ashrei, etc. This implies that he should not say Tachanun until after the second prayer, for otherwise why would it be necessary to interrupt with Ashrei? Levush writes likewise. Lechem Chamudos, however, writes that he should say Tachanun after the first prayer.
He should say Ashrei between them, etc. The kabbalist Rabbi Menachem Recanati strongly warned not to say Ashrei after Minchah. Bach. I myself saw in Recanati, Parashas Vayeira and Parashas Korach, that he wrote not to say Ashrei at Maariv. See Beit Yosef, siman 234, in the name of Mahari. A practical difference concerns one who arrives late at the synagogue after the congregation has begun Minchah. He should pray the Amidah with them and say Ashrei afterward, unlike Bach. Hagahos Maimoniyos and Sefer Chassidim, siman 812, write likewise. It is also the universal custom on Yom Kippur to say Ashrei after Minchah. The Zohar, Parashas Pinchas, page 424, however, implies that Ashrei should not be said after Minchah. It appears to me that Yom Kippur differs because it is a time of favor throughout the day. Nevertheless, the Zohar there implies that one may say it when not intending it as an obligatory recital.
