5) [Se'if 2] If one erred and did not pray Maariv, he prays Shacharis twice, etc. When the next day's shaliach tzibbur must pray twice, he need not pray three prayers. He prays two: the silent prayer for the day's obligation, and the aloud repetition as his compensation and to discharge one who is not proficient. Radbaz, volume 1, siman 174; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos on the Tur; Olas Tamid, paragraph 5; Magen Avraham, paragraph 1; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 5; Soles Belulah, paragraph 8; Halachah Berurah, paragraph 2; Pri Chadash, paragraph 2, who writes that the law is clear; Kesher Gudal, siman 22, paragraph 14, who writes that this is the principal ruling, unlike the glossator to Shaliach Tzibbur, folio 171b, who questions it; Siddur Beis Oved, paragraph 9; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 6; Chayei Adam there, paragraph 3; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, siman 21, paragraph 9. According to Shaar HaKavanos, discourse 1 concerning the repetition of the Amidah, the repetition is a distinct spiritual aspect and is necessary independently, not merely to discharge one who is not proficient. The shaliach tzibbur must therefore intend in the repetition three things: his compensatory prayer, discharging one who is not proficient, and the spiritual aspect produced by the repetition.
6) If one did not pray Maariv, prayed Shacharis, and intended during the shaliach tzibbur's repetition that it serve as compensation, he fulfilled his obligation. Birkei Yosef, paragraph 1, and Kesher Gudal, siman 22, paragraph 15; Siddur Beis Oved, paragraph 10; Shaarei Teshuvah, paragraph 2; Kaf HaChaim, siman 15, paragraph 56. Erekh HaShulchan, paragraph 1, however, finds this difficult: the listener and speaker must both intend, while the shaliach tzibbur certainly does not intend during the repetition to discharge his compensation. Moreover, a person cannot silently follow the shaliach tzibbur, as Tur writes in siman 59 in the name of the Rosh. If, however, the shaliach tzibbur intended to discharge his compensatory prayer and he silently followed the entire prayer, perhaps even a proficient person fulfills it after the fact. The shaliach tzibbur must therefore intend during the repetition to discharge this person's compensation, to discharge one who is not proficient, and for the spiritual aspect created by the repetition, as stated above. The listener should likewise intend compensation, fulfilling the alternative opinion, and the aspect created by the repetition. This applies only when the shaliach tzibbur prays calmly, word by word, and aloud; otherwise the listener cannot follow him, as stated below in siman 124, paragraph 56.
7) Know that even ten people who did not pray Shacharis or Maariv pray twice, and the repetition suffices for them. It appears this is the correct text; the printed reading "and it does not suffice" is a scribal error. The passage returns to Magen Avraham's ruling in the name of Radbaz that the shaliach tzibbur fulfills his compensation through the repetition, and teaches that the same applies not only to a shaliach tzibbur but to ten people. This is the evident flow of the language. If ten people did not pray Minchah and pray Maariv twice, however, the shaliach tzibbur certainly does not repeat Maariv for the missed Minchah. The repetition was instituted only in the prayer's own time; no repetition of a compensatory prayer was instituted. This is obvious. Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1. They must have the intentions stated in the preceding paragraph.
8) There: He prays Shacharis twice, etc. It is forbidden to eat before praying the second prayer; if he began eating, he does not interrupt. Magen Avraham, paragraph 3; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 8; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 3. See below, paragraph 21.
9) There: He should say Ashrei and then pray Shemoneh Esrei, etc. He should not recite the blessings of the evening Krias Shema. Beis Yosef, citing Semak; Pri Chadash, paragraph 2. See our discussion above in siman 58, paragraph 27, where we write that he should not recite Krias Shema itself either.
10) There: He should say Ashrei and then pray Shemoneh Esrei, etc. Although siman 105 above requires only waiting four amos, that is the strict law; here Shulchan Aruch gives the preferable practice, so that he prays from a state of joy, as stated in siman 93, se'if 2. Prishah's explanation in siman 105 is strained and amounts to an unsupported assertion, as is evident to the reader. Our explanation resolves the matter. See Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 5.
11) Rema MiPano writes in Alfasi Zuta at the beginning of the chapter Tefillas HaShachar: Rabbi Yochanan says that one who erred and did not pray Maariv should pray Shacharis twice; it appears this is only through four hours, no later. One who missed Shacharis prays Minchah twice, and likewise only until plag haminchah, no later. Machazik Berachah, paragraph 2, cites him and writes that the unqualified language of the poskim does not imply this, nor is common practice so. Shaliach Tzibbur, folio 169b; Zechor LeAvraham, volume 1, paragraph 400; and Ruach Chaim, paragraph 2, cite this. Alfasi Zuta means that one who missed Maariv can compensate after Shacharis only if he prays Shacharis within its preferred fixed time, through four hours, as stated in siman 89, se'if 1. If he prays Shacharis after four hours, he cannot then compensate Maariv. Likewise, one who missed Shacharis can compensate after Minchah only if he prays Minchah before plag haminchah, as Zechor LeAvraham explains. Machazik Berachah rejects this because the unqualified words of the poskim do not imply it. Rather, whenever the current prayer may be prayed, the preceding prayer may also be compensated after it; even Shacharis after four hours and Minchah after plag haminchah.
12) Mussaf has no compensation; one does not compensate it, and no other prayer compensates for it. The same applies to Ne'ilah. Neither prayer interrupts the sequence for compensation: the missed prayer may still be compensated from Shacharis to Minchah or from Minchah to Maariv. Rema MiPano there; Machazik Berachah, paragraph 3; Kesher Gudal, siman 22, paragraph 6; Shaliach Tzibbur, folio 169b; Zechor LeAvraham, paragraph 400. See the following paragraph.
