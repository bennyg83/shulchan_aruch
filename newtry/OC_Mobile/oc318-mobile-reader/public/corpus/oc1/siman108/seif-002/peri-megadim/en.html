And if. See the Taz, who questioned the Beit Yosef for learning this from seif 10, for it is not comparable: there he made an actual change, unlike here. See the Rambam, chapter 3, halacha 8 of Hilchos Tefillah, who did not mention that if he reversed them he has not fulfilled; and in chapter 10 the Taz wrote the law of reversal, see there. See the Pri Chadash, who wrote that nevertheless the halacha is that here too he has not fulfilled. Rabbi Shmuel Narmizan wrote: if one did not pray Minchah and prays two Ma'ariv prayers, and did not mention Tal U'Matar in the first, he goes back and prays two, for otherwise he will have put the makeup first. The Pri Chadash disagrees and says that he repeats only one. According to the Levush here, the reason is that the frequent precedes and that this invalidates after the fact; if so, it invalidates here as well. But the Taz below, letter 10, wrote that frequency does not invalidate after the fact; see siman 684, and with Hashem's help it will be explained there. If so, one may say that if he reversed them he has not fulfilled because he violated the enactment of Chazal that the obligation of the present time must be put first, which is not the case here. Still, one may say that even if he reversed them inadvertently he has not fulfilled, and this is also implied by the plain words of the poskim. If so, the same applies here. See Magen Avraham, letter 2.
