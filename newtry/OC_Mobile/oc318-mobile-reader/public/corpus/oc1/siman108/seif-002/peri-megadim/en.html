And if — Taz challenged Beit Yosef, who learned this from seif 10, and it seems there he made a change in the verb, unlike here. And Rambam Hilchot Tefilah 3:8 — if one prayed and did not mention [something required], if he reversed, he does not fulfill. And in Pri Megadim the Taz wrote the law of reversal — see there. Peri Chadash wrote that nevertheless the halachah is that here too he does not fulfill. Maharash of Nemirov wrote: one who did not pray Mincha and prays Maariv twice and did not mention tal umatar in the first — he returns and prays twice, for otherwise he precedes make-up first; and Peri Chadash disagrees and one returns only once — see there. It appears to the Levush here that the reason [is that] the frequent [prayer] comes first and impedes after the fact; if so, it impedes here too. But the Taz below in letter 10 wrote that the frequent [prayer] does not impede after the fact — see incident 484, and there it will be explained. Therefore one can say that if reversed he does not fulfill, for he transgressed the enactment of Chazal that one must precede the obligation of its hour first — unlike here. But one can say that in reversal, even unintentionally, he also does not fulfill, and so is the conclusion of the poskim; therefore the same here — Magen Avraham letter 2.
