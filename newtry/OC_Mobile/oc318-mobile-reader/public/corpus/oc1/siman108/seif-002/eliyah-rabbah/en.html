He should say Ashrei, etc. Regarding Tachanun, the Levush wrote that it is said after the second prayer. There is no proof from the language of the Shulchan Aruch, for perhaps he did not wish to mention Tachanun because it is not fixed, since there are many days on which Tachanun is not said; and it is more appropriate to attach Tachanun to the prayer of its proper time, as below in siman 131. This also appears to be the view of Lechem Chamudos and the later authorities, that after Tachanun and Ashrei he prays Shemoneh Esrei, and afterward Lamnatzeach and Uva LeTziyon. The Levush wrote that if one prays two Minchah prayers, he also says Ashrei between the two prayers. The Malbushei Yom Tov wrote that the Levush wrote this from his own reasoning. I also saw in the Perishah in siman 105 that he inferred from the fact that the Semak mentioned only one who forgot Minchah and Ma'ariv, that if one forgot Shacharis and makes it up at Minchah, he should not say Ashrei. So too wrote the Bach in siman 5. However, in Lechem Chamudos he wrote that the Levush learned this from the law of Minchah made up at Ma'ariv, where the Beit Yosef in siman 234 wrote in the name of the Rivash that one interrupts with Ashrei so that it be recognizable that the prayer is a makeup for Minchah; and even for Shacharis, Pesukei D'Zimrah precedes, whose main praise is Tehillah LeDavid. In my humble opinion, this cannot be learned from there, for specifically at Ma'ariv, when Ashrei is not normally said, it is recognizable; but at Minchah, when Ashrei is said before the prayer, saying Ashrei is not recognizable at all as being for makeup. Therefore it seems to me that the Levush learned this from another reason in the Rivash in siman 140, namely that one should make up Ashrei to fulfill the statement of Chazal that whoever says Tehillah LeDavid three times, etc. This is also what seems to be learned from the writings of Mahari brought by the Beit Yosef there, for he wrote not to say Ashrei only at Ma'ariv, since the day has passed and he did not say it three times; this implies that at Minchah, while the day still remains, one says it. Thus the proof of the Perishah and Bach is also rejected, for the Semak did not need to mention one who forgot Shacharis, since we learn it by a kal vachomer from one who forgot Minchah and makes up Ashrei at Ma'ariv even though the day has passed; certainly one making up Shacharis at Minchah, while the day remains, says it. This is primary, and this is the practice. The words of the Taz on this are astonishing.
He should say Ashrei between, etc. These are the words of the Bach: although the Semak wrote that even when one prays two Ma'ariv prayers, he must interrupt with Ashrei, the kabbalist Rabbi Menachem Recanati warned very strongly not to say Ashrei after Minchah. At first glance this is difficult, for if so, when one prays two Minchah prayers he should not interrupt with Ashrei, since it is after Minchah; although the Bach truly holds this, he should have brought this proof above. It is also difficult for Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah and Olas Tamid, who ruled like the Levush that if one prays two Minchah prayers he interrupts with Ashrei, yet also copied the Bach's words in the name of Recanati in his writing and language. Also, below in siman 234 I wrote that one may pray Shemoneh Esrei at Minchah and then Ashrei; perhaps even when he makes up Shacharis after Minchah, it is considered like Minchah prayer, but this is forced. I later examined and found in Recanati, Parshas Vayeira, that he wrote: However, in the prayer of Ma'ariv one should not say it, for it will appear as separation, etc. It is explicit that he spoke specifically of Ma'ariv. It also seems to me that his words can be explained as agreeing with the Semak and Shulchan Aruch, that when one prays two Ma'ariv prayers he interrupts with Ashrei, since Ashrei belongs to the Minchah prayer that he is making up; and what Recanati wrote, that one should not say it, refers to the Ma'ariv prayer itself, and he came to give the reason why Ashrei is said at Shacharis and Minchah but not at Ma'ariv. The sod that it should not appear like separation may also be understood this way; understand this. See below at the beginning of siman 132. Nevertheless, one should be concerned and careful not to say it when praying two Ma'ariv prayers. Moreover, I wrote above that Mahari ruled this way in his writings, siman 59. I also saw in the responsum of the Rivash there that some objected to saying Ashrei, and even the Rivash himself did not rule as practical halacha that it should be said, but only wrote that one should not protest against those who say it. Also, in Tashbetz simanim 223 and 224 it seems to me that it is not said. Therefore it is proper to rule not to say it when praying two Ma'ariv prayers. In any case, one should wait the time needed to walk four amos, as above in siman 105.
