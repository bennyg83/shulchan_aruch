(2) He says Ashrei, etc. And Tachanun — the Levush wrote that he says it after the second prayer, and from the language of the Shulchan Aruch there is no proof that perhaps he did not want to mention Tachanun because it is not decided for him, for many days we do not say Tachanun, and it is closer to attach Tachanun to the prayer in its time as below siman 131; so too seems the view of Lechem Chamudos and later authorities that after Tachanun and Ashrei one prays eighteen blessings and then Lamnatzeach and Bo l'Tzion. The Levush wrote that if he prays Minchah twice he also says Ashrei between prayer and prayer — up to here. Malbushai Yom Tov: this is his wording, this is his own reasoning — up to here. I also saw in Perishah in siman 105 that he deduced since Semag only mentions when forgetting Minchah and Maariv — evidently for forgetting Shacharis that he makes up at Minchah he does not say Ashrei; so too wrote Bach in siman 5. But Lechem Chamudos wrote his wording: the Levush learned thus from the law of Minchah that he completed at Maariv, that Beit Yosef wrote in siman 234 in the name of Rivash that one interrupts with Ashrei so there should be a sign that the prayer was to complete the Minchah; and even for Shacharis they precede pesukei de-zimra that one interrupts so the main praise is Tehillah le-David — up to here his wording. In my humble opinion one should not learn from this that specifically at Maariv where one does not say Ashrei is there a sign, but at Minchah where Ashrei is said before prayer, saying Ashrei is no sign at all that it is for make-up prayers; therefore it seems the Levush learned thus for another reason — Rivash in siman 146 that one must complete Ashrei to fulfill the statement of Chazal whoever says Tehillah le-David three times, etc. So too it seems to learn from the writings of Mahar'i that Beit Yosef brought there, that he did not write not to say Ashrei except at Maariv since the day passed without saying three times; and it is implied at Minchah while the day still exists one says it. Now the proof of Perishah and Bach is also rejected, for Semag did not need to mention forgetting Shacharis — we learn from kal va-chomer from forgetting Minchah where one completes with Ashrei at Maariv even though the day passed, and all the more so and kal va-chomer when completing at Minchah for Shacharis while the day exists; and so is the main practice.
