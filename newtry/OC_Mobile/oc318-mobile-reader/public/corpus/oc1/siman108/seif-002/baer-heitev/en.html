Maariv. It implies one may not say Tachanun until after the second prayer, for otherwise why would he need to stop with Ashrei. Magen Avraham and Levush. And Levush Chayyim wrote he should say Tachanun after the first prayer, and likewise Eliyah Rabbah—see there.

For prayer. But when he prays Minchah twice because he did not pray Shacharis, he does not say Ashrei in between, for the Maharam of Rothenburg was very careful not to say Ashrei after Minchah. Therefore he waits the time to walk four amos between prayer and prayer when praying Minchah twice (Bach). And Magen Avraham disagrees, and likewise the accepted custom on Yom Kippur to say Ashrei after Minchah. And it is explained for one who is late to come to the synagogue when the congregation has begun Minchah—he prays the eighteen with them and afterward says Ashrei, not like Bach. And likewise Levush, Levush Chayyim, and Shach in Knesset HaGedolah; and see siman 234. However, in the Zohar on Parshas Pinchas it appears that after Minchah prayer one does not say Ashrei, and Magen Avraham wrote; nevertheless it appears in the Zohar that it is permitted to say it not with the intent of obligation—see there. (And in Eliyah Rabbah he raised that when praying Maariv twice he does not say Ashrei, only waits the time to walk four amos; but for Minchah he prays Ashrei in between—see there.)
