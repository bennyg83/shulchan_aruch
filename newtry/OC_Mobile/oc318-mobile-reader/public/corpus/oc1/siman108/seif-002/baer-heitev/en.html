Two Shacharis prayers. It is forbidden to eat before praying the second prayer. If he already began eating, he does not interrupt. Magen Avraham.
Maariv. This implies that one should not say Tachanun until after the second prayer, for otherwise why would he need to interrupt with Ashrei? Magen Avraham and Levush. Lechem Chamudos writes that he should say Tachanun after the first prayer, and Olas Tamid likewise rules; see there.
For the prayer. However, when one prays Minchah twice because he did not pray Shacharis, he should not say Ashrei between them, for Rabbi Menachem of Recanati strongly warned not to say Ashrei after Minchah. He should therefore wait the time needed to walk four amos between the two prayers when praying Minchah twice. Bach. Magen Avraham disagrees, and the widespread custom on Yom Kippur is likewise to say Ashrei after Minchah. A practical difference concerns one who arrives late at the beis haknesses when the congregation has begun Minchah. He should pray Shemoneh Esrei with them and afterward say Ashrei, contrary to the Bach. Levush, Lechem Chamudos, and Sheyarei Knesses HaGedolah likewise rule. See siman 234. Nevertheless, the Zohar in Parashas Pinchas implies that Ashrei should not be said after Minchah. Magen Avraham writes that it nevertheless appears from the Zohar there that one may say it when not intending it as an obligatory recitation; see there. (Eliyah Rabbah concludes that when praying Maariv twice, one should not say Ashrei, but should merely wait the time needed to walk four amos. At Minchah, however, he should say Ashrei between the two prayers; see there.)
