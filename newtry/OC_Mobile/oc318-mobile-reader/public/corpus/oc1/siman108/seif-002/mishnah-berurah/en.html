(10) Morning prayer twice — one may not eat before praying the second, for its time has already arrived; and if one began eating, one does not interrupt — so writes Magen Avraham. But in Machatzit HaShekel and Magen Giborim they ruled that even after the fact one interrupts, because the second prayer must adjoin the first in every way possible.

(11) After one says, etc. — meaning, one should not interrupt ab initio between them in anything, and even Torah study is forbidden, for the teshuvah prayer must be while still engaged in the obligatory prayer. Nevertheless it seems the prayer leader's repetition of the obligatory prayer may be heard first, for this is still called being engaged in prayer; see above siman 47 seif 7 what we wrote there in Mishnah Berurah in the name of Ohr Zarua.

(12) And the Eighteen Blessings — meaning, with Tachanun that one says afterward, for one may not interrupt between the Eighteen Blessings and Tachanun.

(13) One says Ashrei — so as to stand also in the teshuvah prayer from words of Torah consolation, as likewise in siman 93; and also because otherwise one would need to say Ashrei before Lamenatzeiach and Uva LeTzion — one says it now and not afterward. See in the later authorities who agreed that the same applies if one prays the afternoon prayer twice for completion — one also says Ashrei again before the second prayer; all this is only ab initio, but by law one need not interrupt between prayer and prayer, only for walking four cubits, as stated in siman 105.

(14) Between, etc. — to complete through this also the Ashrei one should have said before the afternoon prayer. Some later authorities wrote in the name of kabbalists not to say Ashrei in the evening prayer, and even when it is for the afternoon prayer one should be stringent; Darkei Chaim wrote that whichever way a person conducts himself, he has whom to rely on; but whenever it is still day, one should not refrain from saying Ashrei even after the afternoon prayer. Therefore if one was delayed and came to the synagogue when the congregation had begun the afternoon prayer, one prays with them and says Ashrei afterward. See in Magen Avraham who wrote that from the words of the Zohar it appears that even in this manner one should not say it with intent of obligation, only as one who reads Torah.
