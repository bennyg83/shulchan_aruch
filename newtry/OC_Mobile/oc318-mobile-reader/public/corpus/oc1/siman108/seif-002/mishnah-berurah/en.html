Two Shacharis prayers. One may not eat before praying the second prayer, for its time has already arrived, and if he began he does not interrupt; so wrote the Magen Avraham. But the Machatzis HaShekel and Magen Gibborim ruled that even after the fact he interrupts, because he must join the second prayer to the first as much as possible.
After he says, etc. Meaning that initially one should not interrupt between them with anything, and even learning is forbidden, because the makeup prayer must be while he is still occupied with the obligatory prayer. Nevertheless, it seems to me that one may hear the repetition of the shaliach tzibbur of the obligatory prayer first, for this is still called being occupied with prayer. See above in siman 47, seif 7, what we wrote there in the Mishnah Berurah in the name of the Or Zarua.
And the eighteen blessings. Meaning together with the Tachanun that he says afterward, for one should not interrupt between Shemoneh Esrei and Tachanun.
He should say Ashrei. This is so that in the makeup prayer as well he stands from words of consolation of Torah, as above in siman 93. Also, since in any event he will need to say Ashrei before Lamnatzeach and Uva LeTziyon, he should say it now and not later. See the later authorities, who agreed that the same applies if he prays two Minchah prayers for makeup: he should also say Ashrei again before the second prayer. All of this is only initially, but by strict law no interruption is needed between one prayer and the next other than the time needed to walk four amos, as stated above in siman 105.
Between, etc. This is in order also to make up the Ashrei that he should have said before Minchah. Some later authorities wrote in the name of the kabbalists not to say Ashrei at Ma'ariv, and even though it is for the sake of the Minchah prayer, one should also be stringent in this. The Derech HaChaim wrote that however a person practices in this, he has upon whom to rely. But as long as it is still day, one should not refrain from saying Ashrei, even if it is after Minchah prayer. Therefore, if he was delayed and came to synagogue when the congregation had begun Minchah, he should pray with them and say Ashrei afterward. See the Magen Avraham, who wrote that from the Zohar it appears that even in this case he should not say it with intent as an obligation, but rather as one reading Torah.
