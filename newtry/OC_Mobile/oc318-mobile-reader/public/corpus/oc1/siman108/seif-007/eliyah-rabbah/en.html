And he need not introduce something new, etc. The Olas Tamid wrote that the Bach disagrees and rules that he must introduce something new. I examined the Bach in siman 234 and saw that he disagrees only regarding one who intentionally missed Shacharis or Ma'ariv; but regarding Minchah he agrees that no innovation is needed, because since a person is occupied with his affairs during the time of Minchah, it is considered somewhat like compulsion. Regarding Shacharis and Ma'ariv as well, it seems to me that the words of the Shulchan Aruch and Levush are primary. The main basis of the Bach was that the Tur brought the words of the Gaon regarding Minchah, implying that the Gaon was not referring to the words of the Gemara but specifically to Minchah. In my humble opinion, he teaches a novelty: although we hold that one who erred at Minchah prays two Ma'ariv prayers and we do not say that his day has passed, nevertheless this is a greater novelty than forgetting Shacharis or Ma'ariv, especially intentionally. This is also implied in Nachalas Tzvi, and this also resolves why the Gemara says, that which is crooked cannot be repaired, referring to one who annulled Krias Shema of morning or evening, and did not mention Minchah. Analyze this. Another answer is that it mentioned morning and evening because of Krias Shema, and the Bach elaborated from this to disagree with the Shulchan Aruch. In my humble opinion, as I wrote, and also from the language of the Gaon that he himself brought in the name of the Rosh regarding that which is crooked, it implies that he refers to the Gemara, and the Gemara mentioned morning and evening. I also found this explicit in the Chiddushei Rashba, that he refers to the Gemara. Therefore the primary ruling seems to me to be like the Shulchan Aruch in this. My father wrote of an incident in which someone came to synagogue close to Ma'ariv, descended before the ark, and prayed, though he had not yet prayed Minchah. It seems to me that this is not comparable to intentional neglect, for it was still the time of Minchah. On Rosh Chodesh he should mention Ya'aleh VeYavo in the second prayer, for he has already accepted Rosh Chodesh upon himself. To me it seems that one may rely in this on the reasoning of the Levush in seif 9, since it is still day, not to mention Ya'aleh VeYavo.
