Prayed. An incident occurred in which a man came to the beis haknesses close to Maariv, descended before the amud, and prayed Maariv, although he had not yet prayed Minchah. It appears to me that this is not considered deliberate neglect and that he may compensate for Minchah. It also appears to me that on Rosh Chodesh he should mention Yaaleh VeYavo in the second prayer, since he has already accepted Rosh Chodesh upon himself. Magen Avraham. Peri Chadash writes: It appears to me that the Mechaber's ruling above in siman 76, that one who recited in a place where there was reason to suspect excrement and afterward found excrement must repeat the recitation, applies specifically when the prayer time had not yet passed, or through compensatory prayer. An example is where, upon completing his prayer, he still had time to pray again, but afterward became occupied or was prevented by circumstances beyond his control. If, however, when he prayed in a place where excrement should have been suspected there was no longer enough time afterward to pray another prayer, this is a distortion that cannot be repaired, and he has no compensatory prayer. Just as we consider him negligent and require him to pray again because he did not inspect the place, so too he is considered negligent with respect to having no compensatory prayer. The law of an intoxicated person is different, since he is unfit to pray until the effect of his wine dissipates; see there.
