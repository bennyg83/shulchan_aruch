Intentionally. One who came to synagogue close to Ma'ariv and prayed Ma'ariv while it was still day, though he had not yet prayed Minchah, even though initially he acted improperly, for he should have prayed Shemoneh Esrei for Minchah while the congregation was praying Ma'ariv, nevertheless he is not compared to one who intentionally did not pray, since the time of Minchah had not yet passed. Even if one says that he can no longer pray Minchah at that time because he has already made it night through his Ma'ariv prayer, still it should be no worse than praying two Ma'ariv prayers, the second as makeup for Minchah. If it is Rosh Chodesh, he should mention Ya'aleh VeYavo also in the second prayer, since he already accepted Rosh Chodesh upon himself by mentioning Ya'aleh VeYavo. The same applies if this occurred on Shabbos: the second prayer should also be of Shabbos. The later authorities wrote that if he did not mention Ya'aleh VeYavo in the first prayer, he should not mention it in the second either, since it is still day. One who prayed in a place where there was reason to suspect excrement, and afterward found excrement, from above in siman 76 it is evident that he must go back and pray because he was negligent and should have checked. The same applies here: he is considered negligent and intentional for this matter, so that if, when he found it, the time of prayer had already passed, he no longer has makeup afterward.
And no innovation is needed. The Bach and the Gra ruled that an innovation is required.
