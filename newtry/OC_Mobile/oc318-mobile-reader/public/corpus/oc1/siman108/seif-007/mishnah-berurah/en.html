(21) Intentionally — one who came to the synagogue near evening and prayed the evening prayer willingly while not yet having prayed the afternoon prayer: although ab initio he acted improperly, for he should have prayed the Eighteen Blessings as the afternoon prayer while the congregation was praying the evening prayer — nevertheless one should not compare him to one who acted intentionally and did not pray, since afternoon prayer time had not yet passed. And even if one cannot pray the afternoon prayer at that hour, since one already made it night in the evening prayer — nevertheless one only prays the evening prayer twice, the second for teshuvah of the afternoon prayer; and if it is Rosh Chodesh, one mentions Yaaleh VeYavo also in the second, since one already accepted Rosh Chodesh in what one mentioned in Yaaleh VeYavo; the same if this occurred on Shabbat — one prays the second also as Shabbat. The later authorities wrote that if one did not mention Yaaleh VeYavo in the first, one also does not mention it in the second, since it is still day. One who prayed in a place where one could have relied on excrement and afterward found excrement — it is proven above in siman 76 that one must return and pray, because one was negligent in that one should have checked; the same — negligent and intentional applies to this matter: if at the time one found it prayer time had already passed, one has no teshuvah afterward.

(22) And one does not need innovation — Beit Yosef and the Gra ruled that innovation is required.
