In the order. See Mishnah Berurah, what he wrote in the name of Peri Megadim, that even if he drank in four times from only one cup he has not fulfilled; and in truth this is not reasonable at all: do we require four vessels, or do we require four times? Also from the following se'if the opposite is implied at first glance, for he wrote: if there are many revi'iyos in the jug, they drink from it, so many people according to the measure of the revi'iyos; unless we force the matter to say that there too they fulfill only for one cup, but for four cups he cannot fulfill thereby, for we require four vessels, and for one cup, however, they fulfill even though many people drank from it, since in any case each one drank only one cup. However, all this is forced, and the plain sense of the matter is that a whole company fulfills the mitzvah of four cups when they all drink from one jug; and this is the opposite of the words of Peri Megadim; and this requires further study.
Not in order, he has not fulfilled. See Magen Avraham, who wrote that "in order" means when he says the Haggadah in between, and that is from the words of Rashbam; and it is implied at first glance that when he did not say the Haggadah in between, even if he waited between cup and cup it is called not in order and he has not fulfilled. However, from the wording of the Gemara it is implied that there is a particular concern only when he drinks them at once: that is, either according to Rashi's explanation at one time, or at least according to the explanation of other commentators, even consecutively one after another; but if he waited in between, although he certainly transgressed an prohibition in not attaching the saying of the Haggadah to each cup as the Sages enacted, nevertheless after the fact he has not lost the cups. I found a dispute on this between Beis Yosef and Peri Chadash; see Beis Yosef in siman 484 regarding Birkas HaMazon, and his view there is that after the fact he fulfilled, as we wrote; and Peri Chadash disagrees with him there from this [law here], that when he did not say them in order he has not fulfilled. In truth there is no challenge from this against Beis Yosef, as we wrote; and afterward I found in Maamar Mordechai that he too wrote like our words to settle the words of Beis Yosef; and similarly Nahar Shalom also wrote there; see there. And know that according to what the Shulchan Aruch ruled, that women are also obligated in the four cups like men, therefore they must be careful to say the order of the Haggadah over each and every cup, or at least hear it from their husbands; for otherwise, according to Peri Chadash, even after the fact they have not fulfilled by drinking the cups, for it is as if they drank them at once; and even according to Beis Yosef, who holds they did not lose after the fact the mitzvah of the cups, nevertheless the enactment of the Sages is to drink them on the order of the Haggadah, for all mitzvos practiced that night are practiced also by women, as clarified in se'if 14.
