(ב) Not in order — he did not fulfill — see Magen Avraham who wrote that "in order" means when he recites the Haggadah in between, per Rashbam; and it appears at first glance that when he did not recite the Haggadah in between, even if he waited between cup and cup, it is called not in order and he did not fulfill. However from the wording of the Gemara it appears there is concern only when he drinks them at once — that is, either per Rashi's explanation at one time, or at least per other commentators even in this case they are consecutive; but if he waited in between, although he certainly transgressed what he did not connect recitation of the Haggadah to each cup as the Sages enacted, nevertheless b'dieved he did not lose the cups. I found a dispute on this between Beit Yosef and Peri Chadash — see Beit Yosef in siman 484 regarding Birkat HaMazon, and his view there is that b'dieved he fulfilled, as we wrote; and Peri Chadash disagrees there on him from here that when he did not say in order he did not fulfill. In truth there is no challenge from this to Beit Yosef, as we wrote; and afterward I found in Ma'amar Mordechai that he too wrote as our words to settle Beit Yosef's words, and similarly Nahar Shalom wrote there — see there. And know that according to what Shulchan Aruch ruled that women are also obligated in four cups like men — therefore they must be careful to recite the order of the Haggadah on each and every cup, or at least hear from their husbands; for otherwise per Peri Chadash even b'dieved they did not fulfill in drinking the cups, for it is as if they drank at once; and even per Beit Yosef who holds they did not lose the mitzvah of cups b'dieved — nevertheless the enactment of the Sages is to drink them on the order of the Haggadah, for all mitzvot practiced that night are practiced also by women, as explained in seif 14.
