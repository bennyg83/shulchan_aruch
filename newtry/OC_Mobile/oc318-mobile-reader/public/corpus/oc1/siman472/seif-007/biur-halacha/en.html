He has not fulfilled and must return and eat. It appears that according to the Mechaber, if he finished the whole meal and recited Birkas HaMazon and afterward remembered that there was no heseiba, he must return and recite HaMotzi and Al Achilas Matzah; which is not so according to some authorities, who hold he need not return and recite Al Achilas Matzah. And it is obvious that even though ideally there is a mitzvah to eat two kezaysim with heseiba, as clarified in siman 475, nevertheless after the fact he fulfills with one kezayit.
And it appears to me, if he did not drink the third cup, etc. And the view of the Bach is that for all four cups he should not return and drink, and he should rely on the view of Avi HaEzri; and so wrote Chok Yaakov. However, we have only the words of the Rama, for several later authorities also question even the Rama's ruling on this, and according to them one should not join the view of Avi HaEzri to be lenient in this [see Maamar Mordechai and Peri Chadash]. And in any case we should not be more lenient than the view of the Rama; and such is the view of Magen Avraham, Eliyah Rabbah, the Graz, and other later authorities.
One should not return and drink with heseiba. Derech HaChayim wrote that specifically [this means] returning and filling another cup; but if he has a large cup and did not drink all of it, and a [sufficient] measure remained in it, and he remembered that he drank it without heseiba: if he remembered before he began saying Hallel or the rest of the Haggadah, such that he has not yet diverted his mind from drinking the rest of the cup, he may drink the remainder with heseiba and without a blessing, for it is not like adding, because one cup one may drink several times, and presumably at the time of the blessing his intention was for the whole cup; but if he began saying Hallel or the rest of the Haggadah, it is like one who changed his mind, and if he wishes to drink what remains in the cup he would need to recite a blessing, [and then] it is forbidden to drink it, for it is like adding; end of his words. And behold, his latter words, that since he would need to recite a blessing over what remains in the cup it is like adding, stem from Magen Avraham in siman 479, s.k. 1; and nevertheless for the practical law they are not clear, for thus far we find in the Tur, siman 473, that because of a blessing it appears like adding to the cups only if he drinks another new cup; which is not so for that same cup itself, where addition is not noticeable at all; and so it appears to my humble opinion from Darkei Moshe there in siman 479; see there. And especially since the main reasoning of "adding to the cup" is not clear, for in the Yerushalmi nothing at all is mentioned except the reason of drunkenness; and it is enough for us if we adopt this reasoning regarding a new cup and not leftover cups; and this requires further study.
