As though adding. Meaning, since it is forbidden to drink between the latter cups, he clearly had no intention from the outset to drink again. He must therefore recite a blessing, and it appears as though he is adding to the prescribed cups. With the first cups, however, since it is permitted to drink between them, he had them in mind and need not recite another blessing. It appears to me that nowadays, when it is not customary to drink between the first cups, he is considered to have changed his mind. If he drinks again, he must recite a blessing and it appears as though he is adding. He should therefore not drink again, but rely upon Raavyah. Regarding the second cup, however, he should drink again, for even if he drinks wine during the meal, the second cup exempts it. It appears to me that at the beginning of the reclining he should intend to drink again between the first cups. Then, if he mistakenly drank without reclining, he may drink again without a blessing. These are the words of Magen Avraham. The Bach, in siman 479, rules that if one drank any of the four cups without reclining, he has fulfilled his obligation, and this appears correct. Chok Yaakov. The Agudah writes likewise.
