And it appears to me, if he did not drink, etc. Meaning that for the first two cups there is room to drink between them and it is not like adding; and this distinction is in the Asheri, chapter Arvei Pesachim, except that he concluded on this: however one may say that since he drank not in their proper form, the matter was clarified that it was not of the count of the cups, and what he drinks now is a cup of obligation; and if he drank the fourth cup without heseiba, he must recite Borei Pri HaGafen over a cup he will drink afterward reclining, for when he drank the fourth cup without heseiba and was not careful to recline, he diverted his mind from drinking more; end of his words. And so ruled the Shulchan Aruch in siman 479. And one may say here about the Rama, whichever way you take it: if he holds like this reasoning that the matter was clarified that they were not of the count, why did he write that it appears like adding to the cups, for he is not adding; and if he does not hold so, and therefore the reason is that after the fact he fulfilled without heseiba, for one may rely on Ravyah like the view of some authorities, then why did he give the reason because of adding, which is not a forced reason, for one may say the matter was clarified; he ought to have given a better reason than this, namely that since he already fulfilled with those two cups that he drank without heseiba, it is forbidden for him to drink, as we learned: between the third and the fourth he should not drink, and likewise after the fourth he should not drink, without the reason of adding to the cups, but rather so that he not become drunk, as is written in siman 479. Thus in these words of the Rama there is a contradiction or an abridgment.
And ideally he should recline for the whole meal. It is difficult from what is stated in the Gemara: an attendant who ate a kezayit of matzah while reclining has fulfilled; learn from this that another person has not fulfilled, but rather must recline during the whole meal; and here we say that specifically ideally he should recline during the whole meal. And in the Rambam, chapter 7, he wrote, and these are his words: when does he need heseiba? At the time of eating a kezayit of matzah and drinking these four cups; and the rest of his eating and drinking, if he reclined, this is praiseworthy; and it is difficult: what distinction is there then between an attendant and another? And one should not say that an attendant need not fulfill the mitzvah in its choicest form, for if so it should not have said regarding the attendant that he fulfilled, but rather that he needs only a kezayit. And one may say that there is no distinction between an attendant and another in this, and it mentioned an attendant there only to teach us that even the attendant is obligated in heseiba; and so that you not say how is heseiba possible for him since he must go back and forth, for this it answered that the main obligation for every person is only with a kezayit of matzah, and this the attendant can fulfill properly. And Maharal of Prague wrote to distinguish between an attendant and another, and it was not settled for me except in the way I wrote.
