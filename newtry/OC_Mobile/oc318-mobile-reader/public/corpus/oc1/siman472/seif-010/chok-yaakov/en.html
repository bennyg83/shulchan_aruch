One who does not drink wine. See the responsum of Rabbi Shlomo HaLevi, part Yoreh Deah, that one who swore not to drink wine should drink raisin-water for the four cups. However, where one can drink wine, it is the preferred mitzvah and he is obligated to drink wine. He left requiring study whether raisin-water is also included in his oath; see there. The Taz likewise wrote that in a place where wine is not found, one should drink raisin-water. See the responsum of the Rivash, siman 9, where it is clear that this applies specifically to those raisins that contain moisture when pressed, but if nothing emerges from them except through soaking in water, one does not fulfill with them; see there. So too the Rokeach, siman 283, and Shibolei HaLeket, siman 68, wrote that where wine is not possible, one should soak raisins in water specifically when they have moisture. These are his words. See above, siman 272, and the Knesses HaGedolah and Olas Shabbos there regarding kiddush. And in the Knesses HaGedolah he copied in the name of the responsum of Rabbi Shlomo HaLevi that raisin-water is entirely forbidden, and some later authorities followed him and abridged the quotation. But his words there are clear as I wrote, and there is no dispute at all.
To force himself. It is stated in Nedarim, Perek HaNoder Min HaMevushal, 49, regarding Rabbi Yehudah bar Ilai, that he would not drink wine, yet he drank the four cups and said: I must bind my temples from Pesach until Shavuos. The Beit Yosef wrote likewise in the name of the Yerushalmi.
