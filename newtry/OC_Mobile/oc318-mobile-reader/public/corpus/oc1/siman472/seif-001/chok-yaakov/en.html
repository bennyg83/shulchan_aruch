And even if he is in the beis midrash at night, he should get up. So it is in the Gemara in Pesachim 109, where they said of Rabbi Akiva that in all his days he never said, “The time has come to get up in the beis midrash,” except on the night of Pesach. Thus is the reading in the Rosh and Tosafot. However, in the text before us it says “except on Erev Pesach,” and the Rashbam explains that even by day he said, “The time has come,” etc.; see there for the reason.
For it is a mitzvah to hasten and eat. This is what the Gemara means when it says that they snatch matzah on the nights of Pesach; Rashi there explains likewise, and this is the main explanation. See there, where other explanations are also given for this matter, such as removing the tray, as explained nearby in siman 473, seif 6. The Rambam, chapter 7 of Hilchos Chametz Umatzah, writes: “They snatch matzah from one another so that the children will see and ask.” Perhaps from this the custom spread in these countries to let the children snatch the afikoman, for through this they will not sleep and will be aroused to ask. See also the explanation of the Rashbam there.
Until it becomes dark. See Terumas HaDeshen, siman 137, that even the kiddush cup must be drunk specifically at night, similar to the eating of the Pesach offering, and this is also brought in the Beit Yosef. However, the Maharil in the laws of the Haggadah rejects this reason and writes that the main reason we begin at night is because the first dipping and the rest of the order of the Haggadah must be at night, and perhaps he will not be able to extend the kiddush until night; therefore they begin kiddush specifically at night.
