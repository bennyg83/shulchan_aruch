1) [Se'if 1] In order to eat immediately, etc. This means to begin the Seder and eat immediately, referring to the karpas eaten directly after Kiddush, as stated below in siman 473, se'if 6.
2) There. Even if one is in the beis midrash, one should arise, etc. Pesachim 109a teaches: It was said of Rabbi Akiva that throughout his life he never announced that it was time to leave the beis midrash except on the eves of Pesach and Yom Kippur. On the eve of Pesach he did so for the children, so that they would not fall asleep; on the eve of Yom Kippur, so that people would feed their children. See Rashi and Tosafos there.
3) There. Because it is a mitzvah to hasten to eat, etc. This means to begin the Seder promptly for the children, so that they will not fall asleep. The Torah states, 'You shall tell your child on that day.' Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1. The purpose is that the children should ask, 'Why is this night different?' and one should answer them, 'We were slaves to Pharaoh,' and so forth. Similarly, parashas Va'eschanan states, 'When your child asks you,' and so forth, followed by, 'You shall say to your child: We were slaves to Pharaoh,' and so forth. In this way one fulfills the positive commandment to tell one's child.
4) There. But one should not recite Kiddush until nightfall. Although on other Shabbosos and Yamim Tovim one may add from the weekday onto the sacred day, Pesach differs. The time for eating matzah is only at night, like the Pesach offering, as the verse states, 'They shall eat it with matzos and bitter herbs,' and the Pesach offering is eaten only at night, as the verse states, 'They shall eat the meat on this night.' Tur and Beis Yosef in the name of Rosh at the beginning of the chapter Arvei Pesachim; Levush. Although actual nightfall will arrive before one completes the Haggadah and reaches the eating of matzah and maror, the cup of Kiddush, which is one of the four cups, the eating of the other vegetable, the other changes made so that the children will ask, and certainly the Haggadah itself must also occur when matzah and maror may be eaten. All these practices relate to and derive from the mitzvos of matzah and maror. We expound the verse, 'You shall tell your child,' and so forth, 'Because of this,' meaning only when matzah and maror are placed before you. Terumas HaDeshen, siman 137; Beis Yosef; Levush; Bach; Olas Shabbos, subsection 1; Magen Avraham, subsection 1; Chok Yosef, subsection 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 2. Taz, subsection 1, likewise writes that Kiddush must be recited at night because it comes for the sake of matzah. See there. Derashos Maharil, laws of the Haggadah, however, explains that Kiddush is begun at night because the first dipping and the rest of the Haggadah must be performed at night. Chok Yaakov, subsection 3, cites this. Regarding the practical law, all agree that Kiddush must be recited specifically at night.
5) Regarding whether one may recite Kiddush and eat before nightfall on Friday night, see above, siman 267, se'if 2, and our words there in subsection 6, with the help of Heaven.
6) There. Until nightfall. If one recited Kiddush before nightfall after the fact, one fulfilled the obligation. Shulchan Gavoah, subsection 3, likewise writes that if one performed the mitzvah of telling the story and ate karpas before nightfall, one fulfilled those obligations. Eating matzah and maror, however, is compared to the Pesach offering, and one does not fulfill those obligations even after the fact. Derashos Maharil cited above, however, implies that only the Kiddush obligation was fulfilled, not the obligations of karpas and telling the story. Terumas HaDeshen cited above and those who agree imply that even the Kiddush obligation was not fulfilled. It therefore appears that regarding Kiddush alone, one may say the obligation was fulfilled because of the principle that a doubtful blessing is ruled leniently, and because Kiddush on Shabbos and other Yamim Tovim may be recited before nightfall. Karpas and the mitzvah of telling the story, however, must be repeated after nightfall.
7) If one ate matzah during twilight, one did not fulfill the obligation and must eat again at night. Whether to recite the blessing over eating matzah at night requires consideration. Mishbetzos Zahav, subsection 1. One should therefore intend to fulfill the blessing through another person, and that person should intend to include this individual in the blessing. See above, siman 213, se'if 3, and our words there, with the help of Heaven.
8) There. Until nightfall. This means until the stars emerge. Chayei Adam, rule 130, laws of the Seder, subsection 3; Ben Ish Chai, parashas Tzav, subsection 28; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 5. See below, siman 562, se'if 1.
