Until nightfall. Matzah is compared to the Pesach offering, as it is written, “They shall eat it with matzos and bitter herbs,” and the Pesach offering may be eaten only at night. Kiddush must therefore be recited at a time when matzah may be eaten. The Maharal of Prague writes that on every erev Shabbos and Yom Tov, even when one adds from the weekday onto the sacred day, the meal must at least conclude at night. This is because the word “today” is written three times, and one meal must be eaten at night according to the Torah. If one stops eating before nightfall, he has not fulfilled the obligation, because the eating must specifically take place at night. On erev Pesach, however, Kiddush itself must also be recited at night. It appears to me that the reason is that the mitzvah of eating matzah is included in Kiddush, and Kiddush is recited on its account.
