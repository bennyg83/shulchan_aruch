Until Taz — matzah is written "in the evening you shall eat matzot" — there is no proof, for from seven it is also called evening: "there you shall slaughter the Pesach in the evening," as Mahari wrote in book Mevo HaShemesh; only Pesach is written "and they shall eat the flesh at night," meaning 7:30, and matzah is compared to Pesach as Tosafot and Rosh wrote beginning of Arvei Pesachim. Therefore if he ate matzah at bein hashemashot he did not fulfill; at night he shall eat — and it requires study whether to bless on eating matzah at night — see siman 67 in uncertainty of a biblical verse, Shema — he returns and blesses and reads; it depends on the two explanations there for the separation; and similarly they distinguish between one who is certainly obligated and uncertainty whether he fulfilled the mitzvah properly — he returns and blesses, versus tumtum, etc.; but per what Kessef Mishnah wrote in the name of Rashba, Magen Avraham brought there note 1 — one should not bless here; and I was astonished there; and see responsum Rashba chapter 1, and Shach and Peri Chadash there. And behold in comparing matzah to Pesach one may say per what is written in Turei Zahav in Peri Chadash that it is forbidden to eat matzah d'oraisa on erev Pesach from midday, like Pesach at night and not by day; and even if we say the law is from a verse and so is the law, nevertheless there is a prohibition from a verse to eat matzah at bein hashemashot even without a blessing, and one is also liable for rabbinic lashes; and leniency in rabbinic uncertainty — the reason like a bride without a blessing, etc.; and all the more so here where one does not wait a short time and shows gluttony. And if at bein hashemashot with a blessing — one may say he transgresses "you shall not carry" — see siman 215; and on this it requires study whether he receives rabbinic lashes; and what Taz wrote that kiddush must be at the time fit for matzah — meaning there is no kiddush except at a meal — see siman 489 in Magen Avraham 14. Or as Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Terumat HaDeshen 136, Magen Avraham note 1, that kiddush, one of four cups, must also be at night. And what he wrote in the name of Maharal of Prague — see Tosafot beginning Arvei Pesachim s.v. ad, and Rosh there, and see siman 461 in Taz letter 6, wrote that even if he did not eat a kezayit of bread at night he fulfilled — Tosefta Shabbat d'oraisa — see there; and so Eliyah Rabbah in siman 67 and Magen Avraham 67:1; nevertheless in Mishnah Berurah part 1 of Laws of Yom Tov for Rambam that it is not d'oraisa; and Avi Ezri 461 and Peri Chadash here. And it implies even at bein hashemashot one may say he fulfills at a Shabbat meal; however one may say that on Shabbat eating is d'oraisa, and further rabbinic. And from what is written "and on its account kiddush comes" — it implies there is no kiddush except at a meal; and if so in Magen Avraham note 1 it will be explained further. And what he wrote "and one is d'oraisa" — apparently a mistake; it should say "one is d'oraisa for a positive command" — see siman 488.
