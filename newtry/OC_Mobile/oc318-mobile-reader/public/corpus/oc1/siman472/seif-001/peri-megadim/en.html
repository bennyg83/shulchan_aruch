See Taz, that regarding matzah it is written "in the evening you shall eat matzos": there is no proof, for from "seven" it is also called evening: "there you shall slaughter the Pesach in the evening," as Mahari Kolon wrote in Sefer Mevo HaShemesh; only regarding Pesach it is written "and they shall eat the flesh at night," meaning midnight (chatzos), and matzah is juxtaposed to Pesach as Tosafos and the Rosh wrote at the beginning of Arvei Pesachim. And according to this, if he ate matzah at bein hashemashot he has not fulfilled his obligation, and at night he should eat; and it requires further study whether to recite the blessing Al Achilas Matzah at night; see siman 67 regarding a doubt about Kerias Shema, where he returns and recites a blessing and reads; it depends on the two explanations there of the Perishah; and likewise those who distinguish between a person who is definitely obligated and a doubt whether he performed the mitzvah properly (he returns and recites a blessing) versus a tumtum, etc.; but according to what Kesef Mishneh wrote in the name of the Rashba, which Magen Avraham brought there note 1, one should not recite a blessing here; and see Eliyah Rabbah there; and see Responsa of the Rashba part 1, and Shach and Peri Chadash there. And behold, regarding the juxtaposition of matzah to Pesach one may say as is written in Eshel Avraham on Peri Chadash, that some say it is forbidden to eat matzah according to the Torah on erev Pesach from midday, like Pesach: at night yes, by day no; and even if we say the law is from the Sages and so is the law, nevertheless there is a rabbinic prohibition to eat matzah at bein hashemashot even without a blessing, and lashes of rebellion also apply; and do not say a doubt of a rabbinic matter is lenient, for the reason is like a bride without a blessing, etc.; and all the more so this one, who does not wait a short time and shows gluttony. And if at bein hashemashot with a blessing, one may say he transgresses "You shall not take [the Name in vain]"; see siman 215; and on this it requires further study whether he is liable to lashes of rebellion. And what the Taz wrote, that Kiddush must be at the time fit for matzah, that is because there is no Kiddush except in the place of a meal; see siman 489 in Magen Avraham 14. Or alternatively as Beis Yosef wrote in the name of Terumas HaDeshen 136, on Magen Avraham note 1, that Kiddush, being one of the four cups, must also be at night. And what he wrote in the name of Maharal of Prague, see Tosafos at the beginning of Arvei Pesachim s.v. "ad," and the Rosh there; and see siman 291 in Taz letter 6, who wrote that even if he did not eat a kezayit of bread at night he fulfilled, for the addition to Shabbos is according to the Torah; see there; and so wrote Eliyah Rabbah at the beginning of siman 267; and see Magen Avraham 267:1; and see Maggid Mishneh, chapter 1 of Laws of Resting on the Tenth, to the Rambam of blessed memory, that addition is not according to the Torah; and see Beis Yosef 261 and Peri Chadash here. And it is implied that even at bein hashemashot one may say he has not fulfilled his obligation with a Shabbos meal; however one may say that eating once on Shabbos is according to the Torah and the further [addition] is rabbinic. And from what is written "and for its sake Kiddush comes," it is implied that there is no Kiddush except in the place of a meal; and God willing in Magen Avraham note 1 it will be explained further. And what he wrote "and one is according to the Torah," apparently a scribal error, and it should say "one is according to the Torah"; see Bach; and see siman 188. See siman 494 what is written there.
