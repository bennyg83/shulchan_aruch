And the attendant. This is the language of the Kesef Mishneh, chapter 7: although he is occupied with the needs of the house, he reclines, because one who acquires a Hebrew slave is like one who acquires a master for himself. So too the Maharil wrote in the laws of the Haggadah, that male and female servants are obligated in reclining, and so it is in the Yerushalmi at the beginning of Erev Pesachim. However, in the Semak, siman 144, it is written that a slave, in the presence of his master, does not recline. Perhaps this refers to a Canaanite slave, but not in the presence of his master he requires reclining, for he is obligated in all mitzvos in which women are obligated. Although reclining is a remembrance of freedom and he and his children are enslaved, nevertheless they can be freed. See Yevamos, Perek HaArel, that an uncircumcised man eats matzah and maror, for it is written regarding the Pesach offering, “he shall not eat of it,” but he does eat matzah and maror. The Rambam wrote likewise in chapter 9 of Hilchos Korban Pesach.
