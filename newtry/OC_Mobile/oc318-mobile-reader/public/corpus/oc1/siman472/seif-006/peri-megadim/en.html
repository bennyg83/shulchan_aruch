And ideally. See Taz: in the Rambam of blessed memory, chapter 7 of Laws of Matzah, it is implied that it is praiseworthy and not ideally required; and from the Rama it is implied that ideally he needs the whole meal with heseiba; and see Peri Chadash; and the wording of the Gemara "the attendant," etc., is difficult for both. And see Eliyah Rabbah letter 11, who wrote that the attendant need not have heseiba for afikoman, and also for the four cups he need not; see there; and it is difficult: from where does he have this? And it is proper that the attendant recline for afikoman and for the four cups, but for the whole meal there is no mitzvah upon him ideally; and according to the Taz even the attendant has a mitzvah ideally to recline during the whole meal; and see Chok Yaakov letter 12: a Canaanite slave before his master does not recline; not before him he reclines (and see letter 4); and he derived this from Semak 140, that a slave before his master does not recline, which deals with a Canaanite slave; and an uncircumcised person eats matzah and maror: the Rambam of blessed memory in Laws of Korban Pesach; end of the words of Chok Yaakov. An uncircumcised person is obligated in matzah and maror, and even an apostate regarding circumcision. And a Canaanite slave, one may say it is not his manner to recline, like a divorced woman in se'if 4.
