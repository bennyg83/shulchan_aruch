And l'chatchila Taz — Rambam chapter 7 of Laws of Matzah implies it is praiseworthy and not l'chatchila; and from Rama it implies l'chatchila the entire meal requires reclining; and see Peri Chadash. The wording of the Gemara "the attendant," etc., is difficult for both; and I was astonished note 11 — he wrote the attendant need not recline at afikoman, also not for four cups — see there; and it is difficult from where he learned this, for it is fitting that the attendant recline at afikoman and for four cups; but for the entire meal there is no mitzvah for him l'chatchila. And per Taz even the attendant is obligated l'chatchila to recline the entire meal; and see Magen Avraham note 12. A Canaanite slave in his master's presence does not recline; not in his presence he reclines (and see note 4). He derived this from Semak 140: a slave in his master's presence does not recline — deals with a Canaanite slave; and uncircumcised eats matzah and maror — Rambam in Laws of Korban Pesach — until here Chok Yaakov. Uncircumcised is obligated in matzah and maror, even an apostate regarding circumcision. And a Canaanite slave — one may say it is not his manner to recline, like a divorced woman in siman 472.
