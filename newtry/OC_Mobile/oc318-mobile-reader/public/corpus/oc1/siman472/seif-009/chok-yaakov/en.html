A revi’is. That is, an egg and a half. The Maharil in the laws of the order of the Haggadah wrote: “Each cup must hold almost the fullness of two eggshells, which is a revi’is of a log. What we say, that a revi’is is an egg and a half, means when one measures by placing an egg into water and measuring against the water that comes out. However, when one fills and measures with an eggshell, then it is almost, but not fully, two shells.” These are his words.
After he dilutes it. That is, one part wine to three parts water, with their wines, which were strong. This is not so with our wines. See above, siman 204, seif 8, and siman 272.
And he should drink all of it or most of it. The Maharil wrote: “He should drink the entire revi’is, and if he cannot drink all of it, he should drink at least most of it.” The implication of the poskim is that if the cup holds much more than a revi’is, he need not drink all of it even initially, even according to the “some say” cited by the Mechaber; this means most of it, not all of it. However, the Bach ruled that initially one must drink all of it even from a large cup. The practice is to drink a revi’is or most of it even from a large cup, and go see what the people do, all the more so in countries where wine is expensive. Nevertheless, with a cup of a revi’is over which one recites the after-blessing, one must drink at least a full revi’is, as explained above in siman 190, seif 3. See there in the Taz, seif katan 3, and below in siman 486, Taz seif katan 1.
Without a large interruption. The Magen Avraham wrote that if he delayed longer than the time of eating a pras, even after the fact he did not fulfill the obligation and must drink again. If he delayed the time of drinking a revi’is, then it is a doubt, and regarding the first two cups he should drink again. These are his words. In the Rokeach, siman 283, it is written: “In the Tosefta it says that if he drank them with breaks, he did not fulfill the obligation,” meaning that he paused a long time between them. The Maharil, at the end of the order of the Haggadah, explained that it means specifically not to interrupt with idle words.
