With fine utensils according to his ability. The Maharil wrote in the laws of the Haggadah: “All year it is good to refrain from using fine utensils as a remembrance of the destruction, except on the nights of the Seder, as a remembrance of freedom. Even utensils held by him as collateral from non-Jews may be placed on a special table in order to rejoice at the sight of them.” It is explicit that even on Pesach, in any event, one should not use them, as the Maharil wrote explicitly in the laws of hagalah, but only place them on a special table for beauty alone. This is unlike what the Magen Avraham wrote in the name of the Maharil, that he meant to use them. That is not so. Also, what is implied in the Magen Avraham, that there is a prohibition of theft in using their collateral, is not precise, for there is no prohibition of theft here at all, as is clear in the Gemara at the end of the chapter HaSocher Es HaPoel. Rather, the Maharil was concerned only for deception, and even regarding this concern the Taz and Shach in Yoreh Deah, siman 120, seif 9, challenged the Maharil and permitted it; see there, for their words are clear, unlike the Be’er HaGolah there, who distinguishes in another way.
That he recline with reclining in the manner of freedom. The Bach wrote that a mourner during the entire twelve months should not recline. The Pesach Me’ubin, siman 27, wrote that the Bach follows his own view, for he rules like the Raavyah that in our time reclining is not required. But according to us, who rule that reclining is required, if the mourner is after the seven days, since his mourning has already ceased, he is obligated to recline, and so is the custom. Now, although he followed his own view, the view of the Sephardim and the ruling of the Beit Yosef in seif 7, that after the fact one has not fulfilled the obligation without reclining, unlike the conclusion of the later authorities whom we follow, as I wrote explicitly there in siman 163, nevertheless regarding the law of a mourner his words appear correct to me. For where do we know to be stringent in mourning, which is treated leniently, and to nullify the mitzvah of reclining, something not mentioned at all by the early or later authorities? On the contrary, in the time of the Gemara, when people were accustomed to recline on their beds all year, we find no prohibition for a mourner except that all seven days he overturns his bed, and afterward he reclines on his bed like everyone else. And if the concern is that it appears like a manner of freedom and authority, what of it, since it is a remembrance of the miracle? On the contrary, we find explicitly regarding a mourner at the end of Perek Metzi’as Ishah: from where do we know that a mourner reclines at the head? As it is stated, “And I dwelt like a king,” etc. See also the Mishnah at the beginning of Perek Kohen Gadol: all the people recline on the ground and he reclines on the bench; see there. Although all these proofs can be rejected in several ways, nevertheless since we find no posek who mentions even a trace of prohibition in this for a mourner, one should not invent such a prohibition in mourning, which is treated leniently, from one’s own reasoning without proofs at all. Perhaps it is considered public mourning. Therefore one should not nullify the mitzvah of reclining. See also the end of siman 660 regarding the circuit with the lulav, where the Kol Bo, cited in the Beit Yosef there, concludes: “These words are astonishing, for why should the mourner lose the circuit?” See there in the Taz, who worked to find a reason to justify the custom regarding lulav; here there is no reasoning at all to prohibit. Nevertheless, if he can alter it slightly, he should alter it. In any event, he should wear the kittel, which is a garment of the dead, and this is also the conclusion of the Taz, unlike the Magen Avraham. This appears to me.
