With fine vessels, etc. And even though all year it is good to minimize in this because of remembrance of the Destruction, on the night of Pesach it is a mitzvah to increase, for this is included in the way of freedom; and they said of Maharil that when he had in his hand pledged fine vessels of gentiles he would never use them at any time, only on Pesach was his practice to place them on a special table to rejoice in seeing them.
That he should sit with heseiba. That is to say, his head tilted toward the left on the bed or on the bench, with cushions under his head by the table.
He should recline on the bench. Thus it should read. And toward his left; and if he has no bench and he sits on the ground (as in the lands of the East), he must also recline on the left side [Peri Megadim]. The poskim further wrote that if he leans himself on his fellow's knees, this too is called heseiba under pressure; but not on his own knees, for it appears like one who is anxious.
