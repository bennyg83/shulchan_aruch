9) [Se'if 2] One should arrange the table beautifully, etc. The reason is that the Sages said: In every generation, a person must regard oneself as though one personally left the bondage of Egypt. Bach.
10) There. With beautiful utensils, etc. Throughout the year it is proper to refrain from displaying beautiful utensils, reducing ostentation in remembrance of the destruction, except on the Seder nights. The Torah requires one to display freedom then. Even utensils belonging to non-Jews that one holds as collateral may be placed upon a special table so that one can rejoice upon seeing them. Derashos Maharil, laws of the Haggadah, cited by Chok Yaakov, subsection 4. See there, where his discussion resolves the words of Magen Avraham, subsection 2, and Chok Yosef, subsection 2.
11) There. With beautiful utensils, etc. The cups should also be beautiful and splendid in honor of the day. If one can afford it, one should obtain a silver cup, because silver alludes to chesed. The numerical value of the word cup equals that of Elokim, which represents judgment. When the cup is silver, the judgment is sweetened.
12) There. So that one reclines, etc. It is a mitzvah to conduct oneself with nobility on the night of Pesach. This atones for having acted with authority and pride throughout the year. Orchos Yosher, chapter 11; Yafeh LaLev, volume 2, subsection 2.
13) Orchos Chaim, page 78c, writes in the name of Midrash Rabbah and Midrash Yelamdeinu that reclining has a basis in the Torah. The verse states, 'Elokim led the people around,' teaching that He seated them reclining in the manner of royalty, resting upon their couches. Shulchan Gavoah below in siman 486, subsection 12, writes likewise in the names of Sefer HaManhig and Abudraham. See there. Yafeh LaLev, there.
14) There, gloss. 'One should recline' upon the bench is the correct reading. This is the version in Darkei Moshe, subsection 1, Mordechai, and the novellae of Ritva.
15) There, gloss. One should recline upon the bench. Even a poor person must recline upon the left side. If one has a bench, one should recline upon it. If not, one must recline upon the ground on the left side. Eshel Avraham, subsection 3.
16) There, gloss. One should recline upon the bench. One may also lean upon another person's thigh, as taught in Pesachim 108a, but not upon one's own thigh, because this appears like worry. Derashos Maharil, laws of the Haggadah; Magen Avraham, subsection 3; Chok Yaakov, subsection 7; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 4; Chok Yosef, subsection 3; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 8. One should not rest one's hand upon bread or upon one's own thigh. Sefer Olas Tamid; Pis'chei Olam, subsection 5.
