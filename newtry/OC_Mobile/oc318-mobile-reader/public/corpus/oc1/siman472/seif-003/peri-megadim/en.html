For in this time. See Taz: his view [is that of a mourner]; but within twelve months for a father or mother, or within thirty for other relatives when he did not observe seven days before the festival, he does not recline, but he does wear the kittel; and in Talmidei Rabbeinu Yonah he wrote thus regarding Yom Kippur; and see Eliyah Rabbah 3; and Chok Yaakov letter 5 ruled that he is obligated in heseiba; see there; and see Eliyah Rabbah. And see Chok Yaakov in the name of Pesach Me'ubin: if the mourner after seven days is obligated to recline, it is implied that within seven he is not; and if so it cannot be pictured for other relatives. And possibly even within seven, once he observed one hour before the festival he nullifies the decree of seven, and thereafter he is not obligated in overturning the bed; even in the time of the Gemara he reclines; and see siman 548. And nevertheless it appears that he should recline, for according to the Mechaber's ruling one must specifically recline and has not fulfilled without heseiba; and he should change somewhat. But after twelve months in a leap year, it is obvious that mourning has already ceased, and he should recline without change and wear the kittel; and see siman 660, Taz 2; and there at first glance it is implied that within twelve months or a mourner on the festival for the thirty-day relatives reclines; and in Magen Avraham letter 5, God willing, it will be explained.
