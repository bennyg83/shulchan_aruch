And there is no distinction between a left-handed person, etc. See Mishnah Berurah. And where he is missing his right hand, or has a defect in his right hand such that he cannot eat with it, this requires further study how he should act: for if he reclines on his right, there is concern lest the windpipe precede the esophagus; and if on his left like other people, with what will he eat? And thus far they required a left-handed person to lean on his left only because in any case he still has another hand, and even though he is not accustomed to eat with it always, nevertheless he should eat with it this time, as Rashbatz wrote in Yavin Shemuah; but in our case he can eat only with his left, upon which he is leaning, and this is impossible, as is also clarified in the Gemara; see Rashbam s.v. "heseibas yamin," etc. And possibly in such a case the Sages did not require heseiba of him at all; and this requires further study. And see Shibbolei HaLeket siman 218, and his explanation of "lest the windpipe precede the esophagus" differs from the explanation of all the commentators, and according to this all the laws clarified here would change for him; and no posek explained like his words.
