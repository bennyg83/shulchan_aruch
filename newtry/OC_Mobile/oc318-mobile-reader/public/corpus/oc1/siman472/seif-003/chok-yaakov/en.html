On his back. This is what the Gemara in Erev Pesachim 108 says, that lying prakdan is not called reclining, meaning lying on his back, and so Rashi and Tosafot explain there. Rashi likewise explains in Berachos regarding the statement that one lying prakdan should not read Shema. See Rashi at the beginning of Perek Nigmar HaDin, on the Mishnah, “If he turned over onto his heart, they turn him onto his loins,” where Rashi explains that lying prakdan is disgraceful. This somewhat contradicts the explanation of prakdan that Rashi gave in the places mentioned above. I have already answered this difficulty in several ways to my relative, Rabbi Moshe of Hamburg, at length, and I have abbreviated here. For practical halacha, we hold that it is called prakdan whether one lies on his back or on his face, as explained above in siman 63 regarding Shema; see there.
