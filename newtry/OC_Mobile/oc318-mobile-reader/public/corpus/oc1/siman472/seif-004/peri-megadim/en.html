Even. See Taz; and see Chok Yaakov, that it is forbidden to recline unless his teacher gives him permission; and the students of Rabbah did not know that it is forbidden, and Rabbah forgave them; see there. And the Bach ruled that a son with his father, it is proper that his father give him permission; and also for a teacher who is not primary he should ask him to give permission, for it is a doubt; and if his father does not want the son to recline, this requires further study; and see Chok Yaakov letter 5. And it appears to me that it is forbidden to recline; see Yoreh Deah siman 240, se'if 15; and in se'if 7 the Rema ruled like Ravyah where there is a hint of prohibition; if so, honor of father and mother is a Torah doubt and to stringency, and heseiba is a rabbinic doubt and to leniency; and it is obvious that it is proper that he ask permission from his father. And see Peri Chadash on what the Rema wrote: if he eats at another table he reclines: from where does he have this? And in Darkei Moshe he brought this in the name of Mahariv; and nevertheless it is proper that he ask permission also in such a case. And a slave before the master: in Darkei Moshe he wrote that he does not recline, and Peri Chadash wrote that he reclines, with a proof from the Yerushalmi; and it is proper to be concerned for this; see there.
