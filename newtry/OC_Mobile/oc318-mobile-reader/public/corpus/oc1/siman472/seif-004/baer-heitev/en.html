Reclining. The Bach writes that a mourner during the entire twelve months should not recline, and he also does not wear the kittel, since his heart is already subdued. Magen Avraham writes that the same applies to a mourner within thirty days for other relatives when he did not observe shivah before the festival; see there. Knesses HaGedolah, in his work Pesach Me'ubin, siman 176, writes that even during shivah, since the festival terminates his mourning, he is obligated to recline, and this is the custom; see there. Chok Yaakov agrees and writes: From where do we derive grounds to be stringent regarding mourning, which is lenient, and thereby nullify the mitzvah of reclining? Nevertheless, if he can alter his manner of reclining somewhat, he should do so, and in any event he should wear the kittel, which is a garment of the dead. The Taz writes likewise. This is unlike Magen Avraham.
