He needs heseiba even, etc. For even though the son is obligated in his honor and awe, and all the more so if he is his teacher, nevertheless he needs heseiba, for presumably a father forgives his sons.
He does not need heseiba. Because in the opening [clause] it said "he needs," it used here "he does not need"; but in truth there is also a prohibition to recline before him, for the awe of one's teacher is like the awe of Heaven.
Unless, etc. And then he is obligated to recline.
His teacher should give him permission. That is, that he permit him explicitly [Chok Yaakov]; and then it is effective even with his primary teacher.
At a table by himself, etc. Even though he is before his teacher; and his reason is that since he is at a separate table, his heseiba is not a degradation of the teacher's honor; and Peri Chadash disagrees with this, and according to him, whenever his teacher sees him he cannot recline because of his awe; and Peri Megadim wrote that in such a case it is good that he take permission from him.
