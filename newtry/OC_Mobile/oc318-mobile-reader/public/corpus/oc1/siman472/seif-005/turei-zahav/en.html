Even if he is not his primary teacher. The Rosh proves this from what is stated in a beraisa: a person reclines with anyone, even a student with his teacher; and they raise a difficulty from this against what Rav Yosef said, that a student need not recline. And he rejected that [beraisa] as dealing with an apprentice of a craftsman, meaning a student of a trade; and he did not reject it as dealing with one who is not primary; rather, because even with one who is not primary one needs [heseiba]. And it is difficult: nevertheless, if his teacher gave him permission he needs heseiba; why did he not reject it as dealing with one who gives him permission? One may say that the wording of the beraisa indicates an obligation of heseiba, and when permission is taken there is no obligation at all on the student; and from this it is proven that there is no obligation on the teacher to give him permission, for if not so, why not establish it thus that there is an obligation on the teacher to give him permission? Rather, learn from this that this permission is permission and not an obligation, as it appears to me in my humble opinion.
