1) [Seif 1] The custom is to recite the chapter Bameh Madlikin, because it contains the law of kindling and the three matters a person must say in his home shortly before nightfall on Friday. Tur writes this. Beis Yosef writes: It appears from Tur's words that the custom was to recite it after Maariv. This is the custom of Ashkenazic and Musta'arabi communities. It is more proper to recite it beforehand, as is the custom of Sephardic communities, because reading this chapter will remind him of the three matters that he must say in his home and caution his household about. What benefit is there in remembering them after Maariv, when he can no longer correct anything? These are his words. Bach, however, writes that the reason for reciting Bameh Madlikin is not to remind him of the three matters so that he can caution his household. Rather, since Maariv is prayed early from plag haminchah, it is still daytime when the congregation leaves the synagogue, and one cannot sit down to eat and recite Kiddush until nightfall. The custom was therefore to study during this free interval. They chose not to study the chapter Yetziyos HaShabbos or another chapter, but Bameh Madlikin, because it discusses the laws of kindling and the three matters relevant to Shabbos night. Through his study he will remember incidentally that if lamps were kindled with oils and wicks that may not be used, one may not use their light; if tithes were not separated, one may not eat that produce; if an eruv chatzeiros was not made, one may not carry in the courtyard; and if an eruv techumin was not made, one may not travel beyond the techum. These are his words. Bach further writes that it must be recited after Kiddush in the synagogue, because we hasten the entry of the day. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1, cites this. Maharshal, however, writes in Responsa, siman 85, that it is proper to recite it before Kiddush; see there. Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef, cites this. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1, agrees with Bach and also writes that the custom does not follow Maharshal; see there. Nevertheless, since this is only a custom and has no esoteric reason, each community may follow its established practice.
