The principal reason for reciting Kiddush in shul is the original enactment, and an enactment does not leave its place, just as the repetition of the Amidah remains even though nowadays everyone is proficient. The same applies to the abridged seven blessing prayer in siman 268. Regarding the curative practice connected with Kiddush, namely that one drinks and places some of the wine upon his eyes, see Bach and siman 328, seif 21. See also Berachos 43 and Shabbos 113b, with Rashi and Rosh there.
On the night of Pesach there was no enactment to recite Kiddush in shul for guests, because every person requires four cups, even a poor person supported from charity. Therefore, Kiddush is not recited in shul. Rosh writes in Pesachim 101 that Rabbeinu Yonah says Kiddush in shul was instituted to discharge the Torah obligation of one who is not proficient. Rosh writes that the statement, “Kiddush is valid only in the place of a meal,” implies that otherwise he has not fulfilled his obligation at all. Since Rosh cites this, it appears that whenever one did not recite Kiddush in the place of a meal, over wine or bread, the Sages ordained that he has not fulfilled even the positive Torah commandment of remembering Shabbos. See Tosafos, Sukkah 3 and Berachos 11a, s.v. Tanna Rabbi Yechezkel. Mentioning Shabbos in prayer certainly does not suffice. See Magen Avraham and Rama, seif katan 1, where, with Hashem’s help, this will be explained. According to Rabbeinu Yonah, then, Kiddush on Shabbos in shul discharges the Torah obligation of one who is not proficient. On Yom Tov, however, Kiddush is D’Rabbanan and applies only in the place of a meal, so the difficulty is valid. Regarding Rosh’s question, why did the Gemara not say that Kiddush is recited in shul for one who is not proficient, one may answer that if this were the reason, why would wine be required? He could recite Kiddush without wine. This should not be prolonged here. With Hashem’s help, more will be explained in the glosses of Rabbi Akiva Eiger. See simanim 619 and 621, and what I wrote on Magen Avraham concerning this. Rama writes that everyone customarily stands. See Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 2.
