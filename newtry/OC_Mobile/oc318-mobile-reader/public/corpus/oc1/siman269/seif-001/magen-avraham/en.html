"He gives it to a minor to taste." Although it is forbidden to feed a minor a prohibition directly, as stated in siman 343, that applies to something involving a negative prohibition. Kiddush itself is only a positive commandment. [Hagahot Maimoniyot in the name of Maharam] According to what is written in siman 343, however, that one may not directly feed a minor even a rabbinic prohibition, one must say either that since some authorities permit even an adult to drink, holding that he fulfills Kiddush through this recitation, although we do not rule that way, it is permitted for minors; or that since there is no other practical way, it is permitted. [Beit Yosef] Bach writes that it should be given specifically to children who have not reached the age of education. Ran, however, in responsum 82 in the name of Rashba, writes that giving it to such a child would cause the blessing to be in vain, because he has not reached the age of education in blessings. Even in places where they give wine to the infant at a brit milah, that is because the blessing is recited for his sake. Kiddush is different. Therefore it should be given to a child who has reached the age of education. According to what Beit Yosef writes in siman 343 in the name of Rashba and Ran, anything that is for a child's own need, such as eating and drinking, may be fed to him directly, because the Sages did not decree there. Even on Yom Kippur there is a mitzvah to feed him when necessary, and one does not educate him even for short periods where food is needed, as explained in siman 616. Furthermore, there is a distinction between feeding an intrinsically prohibited food and feeding something permitted whose prohibition is caused only by the time, as Tosafos write in Pesachim 106b. All the more so may one feed children on Shabbos morning before Kiddush, for there are authorities who permit even an adult, as Raavad writes. It appears to me that one should not impose a stringency upon children where the law permits feeding them. Nor are we concerned that the child may become accustomed to it, because where the matter is genuinely for the child's own benefit the Sages did not impose that concern. The case of giving a cup to a child on Yom Kippur so that an adult can avoid a blessing without tasting is different, because that is for the adult's benefit rather than the child's. The reason we give a child to taste the Kiddush wine and do not fear that he will become accustomed is also that this is not a fixed practice. Sometimes guests are present and there is no need to give the cup to a child. [Rashba, siman 303] Hagahot Maimoniyot writes that if there is no child in the synagogue, the wine of Kiddush and Havdalah may be given to an adult to drink. In Kiddush, it appears that he should drink the requisite measure, as explained in siman 273, seif 5, and Bach writes likewise. It also appears that the person reciting Kiddush should intend to fulfill his own obligation through this Kiddush, because some authorities hold that it is effective. Nevertheless, he may recite Kiddush again at home for his household, as stated in siman 273, seif 4. "They eat and drink." Meaning in a house adjoining the synagogue, as explained in siman 273, seif 6, because eating in the synagogue itself is forbidden, as stated in siman 151. "It is better to establish the practice." Rashba writes in responsum 37 that once this enactment of Kiddush in the synagogue was established, one should seek out wine for it, because it is a fixed enactment and must be maintained. In our countries as well, the mitzvah privilege of supplying wine to the synagogue is purchased for money. The custom should not be abolished, for many Geonim established it, as Rashba writes, and this also appears from Taanis 24.
