(Seif katan 1) He gives him to taste, etc. But the positive commandment is the Kiddush without wine, while not eating before Kiddush is rabbinic.
Since some say even an adult, etc. "That he fulfills with this Kiddush," etc.; this refers to the Sar of Coucy cited by Mordechai. However, these three words, "that he fulfills with this Kiddush," are not in Mordechai nor in Beit Yosef, who copied him. The essence of Mordechai's words in his name is as follows: Rav said, those people who recited Kiddush in the synagogue fulfilled the obligation of Kiddush, but did not fulfill the obligation of wine; that is, when they come home and drink at home, they must bless borei peri hagafen again, for change of place is an interruption. Shmuel said, they did not even fulfill the obligation of Kiddush, for Kiddush applies only in the place of the meal. Since Rav said they did not fulfill the obligation of wine, and Shmuel said even the obligation of Kiddush they did not fulfill, implying certainly not the obligation of wine, if you would think they did not drink wine in the synagogue, this is obvious: how could one have thought they fulfilled the obligation of wine, since there would be an interruption between blessing and tasting? Rather, it must be that they drank from the cup in the synagogue. According to Shmuel, how is it permitted to drink in the synagogue, since Kiddush applies only in the place of the meal? Necessarily, drinking is permitted. This is the substance of his words. Therefore the intent is not that they fulfilled Kiddush through what they recited in the synagogue, for Shmuel explicitly said they did not fulfill Kiddush; rather, it means that this Kiddush permits drinking, for drinking is lighter than eating. So too I found in Eliyah Rabbah, who cited Rashba as permitting one to drink before Kiddush, for they prohibited before Kiddush only eating and not drinking. Thus he holds that drinking is lighter than eating; the same may be said according to the Sar of Coucy in his view. Bach wrote that they should give it specifically to minors who have not reached chinuch. One must say according to Bach that before reaching chinuch it is permitted to feed him a prohibited item even directly, but this is not the view of Rama below in siman 343.
Even in a place where they are accustomed to give it to the circumcised infant, etc.; see Magen Avraham at the end of siman 621.
And according to what I wrote, etc. Meaning, according to them, Beit Yosef's question is in any case resolved: is it not forbidden to feed them neveilot directly?
For wherever it is his growth - meaning his development, and all the more here where it is only a rabbinic prohibition, that he eats before Kiddush. "And there is a distinction," etc. Meaning: if with food it is permitted because it is the child's development, why did they say it is forbidden to feed a minor neveilot directly, since that too is food? To this he writes that there is a distinction: specifically neveilah, whose prohibition is inherent, even though it is the child's development, is nevertheless forbidden to feed him; but to give him to taste from a Kiddush cup through which he does not fulfill the obligation, so the child tastes before Kiddush, is lighter, for the wine itself is not inherently forbidden, only the time causes the prohibition because he has not yet made Kiddush. In such a light prohibition they were lenient since it is the child's development.
And as Tosafot wrote in Pesachim, etc. There we say that Rabbi Yirmiyah bar Abba came to Rav Asi's house, forgot and tasted before Havdalah, and nevertheless recited Havdalah afterward, for he holds that one who tasted may recite Havdalah; and so we rule below in siman 299 seif 5. Tosafot asked: we say that even the animals of the righteous are not brought by the Holy One, blessed be He, to stumble by eating a prohibited item; all the more the righteous themselves. Why did God not save him from tasting before Havdalah? They answered by distinguishing that since the food is permitted and only the time is prohibited, in such a case we do not say that the Holy One, blessed be He, does not bring a stumbling block, etc. This is explicit like Magen Avraham's reasoning: where time causes the prohibition, the prohibition is lighter than when the item is inherently forbidden. All the more, etc., in the morning; for in the morning there is another reason to be lenient, relying on Raavad, as he writes afterward.
As Raavad wrote in chapter 29 of Hilchot Shabbat, they prohibited tasting specifically before the night Kiddush, for the main mitzvah is as we expound, "remember it at its entrance." But in the morning, since he already remembered it at its entrance, that suffices; what they expounded from Scripture regarding daytime Kiddush is only an asmachta. If so, only night Kiddush, which is primary, did they prohibit tasting before Kiddush, unlike daytime Kiddush. He wrote another reason as well; see there.
And it appears to me that it is forbidden, etc. Meaning, since by law it is permitted, it is forbidden to be stringent upon minors.
And we are not concerned lest he come to continue this habit, for we say in Eruvin 40b that on Yom Kippur one says Shehecheyanu without a cup. What should be done? If he blesses over it and drinks it, once he has said Shehecheyanu he has accepted the fast and is forbidden to drink. Not to drink? We hold that one who blesses must taste. Give it to a child? We are concerned lest he come to continue the habit, and when he grows up he will drink from the Kiddush cup. If so, here too regarding eating before Kiddush for a minor, let us be concerned lest he come to continue the habit when he grows up. In this case the reasoning that this is the child's development would not help, for then on Yom Kippur too one should give it to a child to taste. Rather, because of the concern that he may come to continue the habit, it is forbidden even where it is the child's development.
For with something that is for his benefit, etc. Specifically what is his development: the cup of Yom Kippur, although it is his development in the sense that it is food and drink, is nevertheless not for the child's benefit, but for the adult's benefit, so that he not recite an improper blessing. In such a case we decree lest he come to continue the habit. But what is for the child's benefit, such as feeding him before Kiddush, and likewise feeding him on Yom Kippur, see what Taz wrote in siman 609, where the main intention is for the child, in this they did not decree. "And that which he gives him to taste," etc., and we do not decree, etc.: meaning, although above he wrote that it is because it is the child's development, even though it is for our need so there not be a blessing in vain, nevertheless we are not concerned that this is like feeding him neveilot directly. But regarding the decree lest he continue the habit, the fact that it is his development does not help unless it is for the child's need. If so, because of lest he continue the habit, it should be forbidden to give him to taste from the Kiddush cup.
That is because it is not, etc. Wherever it is not a fixed matter, we do not decree. Similarly Tosafot wrote in Shabbat 139a, s.v. veliten, see there. Nevertheless, what Magen Avraham wrote above requires explanation: for this reason, what we give a minor to taste from Kiddush is not like feeding him neveilot, since it is his development, even though the intention is for our need; and he brought proof that even on Yom Kippur it is a mitzvah to feed him. What proof is this? Yom Kippur is different, for it is for the child's benefit, which is stronger as above. One must say that Magen Avraham held by reasoning that although regarding the concern lest he continue the habit one can distinguish between what is the child's development but for our need, and what is for the child's benefit, as proven above, nevertheless regarding whether it is like feeding him neveilot, he did not hold by reasoning that one should distinguish between them. Hagahot Maimoniyot wrote, etc., that they give it to an adult, etc.; this is not precise, for it is preferable that the one reciting Kiddush himself drink, and so wrote Eliyah Rabbah.
He should drink the required measure, as written in siman 273 seif 5 in the name of the Geonim, that if he drinks a revi'it it is called Kiddush in the place of the meal. According to Rama and Magen Avraham there, one revi'it suffices; according to Bach and Taz there, one needs a revi'it besides the majority of a revi'it that he must drink from the Kiddush cup.
And it appears to me that the one reciting Kiddush should have in mind, etc. Meaning, since Rama wrote there in siman 273 that according to the Geonim it would be permitted for the mohel or sandak to drink from the circumcision cup on Shabbat morning if they drink the required measure, but the practice is to give it to the infant, end quote. Thus the practice is not to rely on the view of the Geonim. Beit Yosef too, in his long work there, was uncertain that perhaps Tosafot disagree with the Geonim; nevertheless, the essential law follows the Geonim. Still, as an added counsel, Magen Avraham wrote that he should have in mind, etc., for some say he fulfills, etc. Tosafot on 115b wrote that the Yerushalmi says if one makes Kiddush in one place with intent to eat elsewhere, this is called Kiddush in the place of the meal. Tosafot wrote that although our Talmud proves that a condition does not help, one must say the Yerushalmi refers from room to room, where he makes Kiddush in one room with intent to eat in another; but two rooms in one house, a condition helps. Our Talmud speaks from house to house, where such a condition does not help. Ran, however, wrote that the wording of the Yerushalmi implies that even from house to house a condition helps; if so, necessarily the Yerushalmi disagrees with our Talmud, and we hold like our Talmud, see there. In any case, according to Ran, the Yerushalmi holds that even from house to house a condition helps. Although we do not rule like it, as above, nevertheless one should make it a supporting factor together with the fact that he drank a revi'it.
And even so he can, etc. Do not say that since he has already fulfilled his obligation and is now exempt, he cannot discharge the members of his household; for this he writes that this is not so, since in Kiddush one can discharge others in such a case, as below in siman 273.
