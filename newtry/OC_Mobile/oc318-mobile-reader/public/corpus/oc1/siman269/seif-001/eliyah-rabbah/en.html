The practice is to make kiddush, etc. See the Tur's question on Rav Natronai. Taz explains at length that Rav Natronai's statement that the congregation tastes the wine refers to their homes for medicinal benefit, while in the synagogue they did not drink but merely placed some on their eyes as a remedy. But he did not examine Sefer HaManhig, which quotes Rav Natronai explicitly that the reason the congregation tastes it is medicinal, namely that they place some on their eyes. Thus he clearly refers to the congregation that tastes and places it on the eyes. Responsa Rashba siman 423 also makes this clear. I am also surprised at Beit Yosef and Bach, who labor to explain Rav Natronai without examining Rashbam's responsum there: kiddush in the place of a meal is not d'oraisa; d'oraisa one must sanctify Shabbos at its entrance, while Chazal attached kiddush to the meal, and because of guests and for medicinal reasons they instituted public kiddush. See below, siman 271 seif katan 21. Rashba there gives further reasons for synagogue kiddush even nowadays. Abudarham likewise supports it. Therefore, in a place where the custom exists one should not treat it lightly. Responsa Rashba siman 37 goes further, ruling that if the chazzan forgot and no wine was brought, one should send to bring wine. Maharil would personally purchase wine for kiddush and havdalah.
