In what case, etc. The words of Rabbi Yosef Karo require analysis. This is the language of the Ran: "If one roofed over an alleyway that has a side-post, it is valid" refers to a closed alleyway, and the sukkah is toward the outside, distant from the middle wall, etc. It is proven in the Gemara that one who makes his sukkah in such a case, since it is not completely open through like one who makes his sukkah in an alleyway in the middle of a courtyard, distant from the courtyard walls, is permitted with a broad handbreadth within less than three near the wall, just as a sukkah that has two adjacent walls is permitted with it. They required a board of four only in a sukkah that is open through-and-through and has no middle wall opposite it. These are his words. His intent is as Tosafos wrote; see there. Rabbi Yosef Karo understood what he wrote, "like one who makes his sukkah, etc.," to mean that this too is valid with a handbreadth. This is difficult, for if so he makes something taught dependent on something not taught. Rather, his intent is that one who makes his sukkah in such a manner - meaning that there are three walls - although it is open because he placed s'chach distant from the third wall, nevertheless it is not completely open like one who makes his sukkah like an alleyway in the middle of a courtyard, etc.; for there, there are not two adjacent walls. But here, where there are two adjacent walls, a broad handbreadth suffices. This is clear in the words of the Ran. For was the Gemara speaking of a sukkah in an alleyway specifically where there is no partition opposite it? Rather, perforce it does not help unless there are two adjacent walls. See Tosafos and Rashi, page 18b.
