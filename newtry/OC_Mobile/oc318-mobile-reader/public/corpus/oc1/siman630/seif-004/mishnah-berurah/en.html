(20) In the middle of the courtyard, etc. This ruling originates in Ran. See Magen Avraham, who disagrees and maintains that Ran permits only where the two opposite sukkah walls are themselves the courtyard walls. In that case the courtyard’s middle wall is effective, even though it is not a sukkah wall, such as where the half of the sukkah toward that middle wall has no sechach at all, so that the sukkah is not treated as one made like an alleyway. This does not apply when the sukkah stands in the middle of the courtyard.
(21) The repair that permits it is sufficient. Meaning, he places the spacious tefach within three tefachim of the wall, specifically on the side facing the courtyard entrance, not on the side facing the courtyard wall.
