(Se'if katan 4) In what case, etc. The words of the Beit Yosef require examination, for this is the wording of the Ran, from whose words the Beit Yosef learned the law written in the Shulchan Aruch.
If he placed s'chach over an alleyway that has a lechi, this is the law mentioned in se'if 7.
And he placed s'chach toward the outside. This means over the half of the alleyway that is on the side of the lechi. If so, the s'chach is half an alleyway distant from the middle wall.
And it is proven in the Gemara, etc. For it says there on page 7 that this case, where one placed s'chach over an alleyway that has a lechi, is valid on Shabbos.
Even though in an ordinary sukkah we require a generous tefach. Here it suffices without that, because of the principle of miggo, since it helps regarding Shabbos, as written in se'if 7. But he should have said that in an ordinary sukkah a strip of four tefachim is required, for according to what the Ran wrote it speaks where he placed s'chach toward the lechi, and there are not two joined walls.
Or that one who makes a sukkah in such a manner intended, etc. His intent is as Tosafot wrote. This is not directly connected to the Beit Yosef's law; rather, incidentally all of the Ran's words are explained. The Ran was troubled by Tosafot's question: either way, what kind of alleyway is being discussed? If it has three partitions and a lechi, why is it necessary to permit it for sukkah because of miggo, since it helps for Shabbos? It already has three complete partitions. And if it has only two partitions, then even regarding Shabbos the lechi does not help to permit carrying rabbinically; see there in Maharsha. Therefore he answered that it speaks of an alleyway that is not open through, meaning that it has three partitions, but he placed the s'chach toward the side of the lechi. The Beit Yosef understood, etc., that this too, etc., meaning that even when one makes his sukkah like an alleyway in the middle of a courtyard, far from the courtyard wall, a tefach is sufficient, as written in the Shulchan Aruch. According to him the Ran's intent is that where one placed s'chach over an alleyway that has a lechi, and he placed the s'chach toward the lechi, a generous tefach is sufficient, for it has the law of one who makes a sukkah like an alleyway in the middle of a courtyard. On this the Magen Avraham asked: if so, he is making a taught case depend on an untaught case, for the law of placing s'chach over an alleyway, etc., where a tefach is sufficient, is taught and compelled by the Gemara as above, while the case of one who makes a sukkah like an alleyway in the middle of a courtyard, where a tefach would suffice, is not taught; according to the Beit Yosef the Ran said it only from reasoning. How then could the Ran bring proof for placing s'chach over an alleyway from a sukkah in the middle of a courtyard?
But his intent, etc. On the contrary, the Ran's intent is the reverse: placing s'chach over an alleyway is better than a sukkah made like an alleyway in the middle of a courtyard. In a sukkah made like an alleyway in the middle of a courtyard, the Ran agrees that a strip of four tefachim is required, since the side walls do not extend until the middle wall.
And what the Ran wrote: since it is not open through, etc. Like one who makes, etc., in the middle of a courtyard; this means that placing s'chach over an alleyway is not entirely open through, and is not like one who makes the sukkah, etc., in the middle of a courtyard.
For is it so in the Gemara, etc. This is another proof that the Beit Yosef's words cannot be correct. If so, the case in which we require a strip of four tefachim would be found only where one makes his sukkah like an alleyway at the edge of a field.
For there is no middle wall opposite it. But in a city, wherever he makes it, there is a wall opposite it, even if it is far away; what of it? It is forced to establish the case specifically in that manner.
For there, there are not two joined walls. But here, a generous tefach is sufficient; so it should read.
See Tosafot and Rashi on page 18. It says there: if one placed s'chach over an exedra that has no posts, whose explanation is stated in se'if 8, Abaye says it is valid because we say that the edge of the roof descends and closes. Rava says it is invalid because we do not say that the edge of the roof descends and closes.
And Rava said to Abaye: If so, if the middle wall was breached, would this also be so? Rashi explained that if one made his sukkah in the middle of the courtyard, placed s'chach with thick boards, and the middle wall was breached, should it be valid because we say the edge of the boards descends and closes? Abaye said to him: in that case I agree with you, for it is like an alleyway open through. Rashi explained that in such a case, where many people pass and walk between the two remaining walls and use them as a shortcut, they nullify the principle of pi tikrah, unlike one who placed s'chach over an exedra, where its use is not so convenient for many people to pass through and use it as a shortcut, so we say pi tikrah yored vesotem. Tosafot wrote that this reasoning is unusual. If so, according to Rashi, just as we say that when one makes his sukkah in the middle of the courtyard, the passing of many people as a shortcut nullifies pi tikrah, so too one can say that the passing of many people who make a shortcut nullifies the middle wall of the courtyard that is opposite the sukkah, preventing it from joining the sukkah. And although Tosafot disagree and hold that one should not distinguish between placing s'chach over an exedra and making a sukkah in the middle of the courtyard where the wall was breached, nevertheless regarding not joining the middle wall of the courtyard, which is weaker than pi tikrah, one can say that Tosafot agree that the many people nullify it and it does not join.
