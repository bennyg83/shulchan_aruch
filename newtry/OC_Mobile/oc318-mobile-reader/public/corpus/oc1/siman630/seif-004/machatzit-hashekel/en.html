(Seif katan 4) In what case, etc. The words of Rabbi Yosef Karo require study, for this is the language of the Ran; Rabbi Yosef Karo learned the law written in Shulchan Aruch from the Ran's words.
Roofing over an alley that has a lechi is the law mentioned in seif 7.
And he roofed toward the outside. Meaning the half of the alley on the side of the lechi; if so, the sechach is distant from the middle wall by half the alley.
And it is proven in the Gemara, etc. From what is stated there on daf 7, that the law of roofing over an alley that has a lechi is valid on Shabbat.
Even though with an ordinary sukkah we require a wide tefach. Here it suffices without that. The reason is the principle of migo: since it helps for Shabbat, as written in seif 7, it also helps here. He should have said that an ordinary sukkah requires a strip of four tefachim, for according to what the Ran wrote, he is speaking where one roofed toward the lechi and there are not two adjoining walls.
Or that one who makes a sukkah in this manner, since, etc. His intent is as Tosafot wrote. This is not directly part of Rabbi Yosef Karo's law, but incidentally all the words of the Ran are explained, for the Ran had the difficulty of Tosafot: in any case, what kind of alley is being discussed? If it has three partitions and a lechi, why does one need to permit it for sukkah because of migo that it helps for Shabbat? It already has three complete partitions. And if it has only two partitions, then even for Shabbat the lechi does not help to permit carrying rabbinically; see Maharsha there. He therefore answered that it is speaking of an alley that is not open through, meaning it has three partitions, but he roofed on the side of the lechi. Rabbi Yosef Karo understood, etc., that even this, meaning even one who makes his sukkah like an alley in the middle of a courtyard, far from the courtyard wall, needs only a tefach, as written in Shulchan Aruch. The Ran's intent was that one who roofed over an alley that has a lechi and roofed toward the lechi needs only a wide tefach, for it has the law of one who makes a sukkah like an alley in the middle of a courtyard. On this the Magen Avraham asks: if so, he has made the taught case depend on an untaught case, for the law of roofing over an alley, etc., that a tefach suffices is taught and is forced by the Gemara, as above, whereas the law of one who makes a sukkah like an alley in the middle of a courtyard, that a tefach suffices, is not taught but only said by the Ran from reasoning according to Rabbi Yosef Karo. How then can he bring a proof to roofing over an alley from a sukkah in the middle of a courtyard?
But his intent, etc. On the contrary, the Ran's intent is the reverse: roofing over an alley is better than a sukkah like an alley in the middle of a courtyard. In a sukkah like an alley in the middle of a courtyard, the Ran agrees that a strip of four tefachim is required, since the side walls do not extend until the middle wall.
And what the Ran wrote, since it is not open through, etc. Like one who makes, etc., in the middle of a courtyard. Meaning, roofing over an alley is not completely open through, and it is not like one who makes a sukkah, etc., in the middle of a courtyard.
For is it so that in the Gemara, etc. This is another proof that Rabbi Yosef Karo's words cannot stand. If so, the case where we require a strip of four tefachim would only be found where one makes his sukkah like an alley at the edge of a field.
Where there is no middle wall opposite it. But in a city, wherever he makes it there is a wall opposite it, even if distant; what of it? It is strained to establish the case specifically in such a manner.
There there are not two adjoining walls, but here a wide tefach suffices; so it should read.
See Tosafot and Rashi on daf 18, where they say: if one roofed over an exedra that has no posts, the meaning of which is explained in seif 8, Abaye says it is valid because we say pi tikrah yored vesotem; Rava says it is invalid because we do not say pi tikrah yored vesotem.
And Rava said to Abaye: if so, where the middle wall was breached, would it also be so? Rashi explains: one made his sukkah in the middle of a courtyard and roofed it with thick boards, and the middle wall was breached; should it be valid because we say that the edge of the roof of the boards descends and closes? Abaye said to him: in that case I concede to you, for it is like an alley open through. Rashi explains that in such a case, where the public pass and go between the two remaining walls and use them as a shortcut, they nullify pi tikrah. This differs from roofing over an exedra, whose use is not so convenient for the public to pass through and use as a shortcut, and there we say pi tikrah yored vesotem. Tosafot wrote that this reasoning is unusual. If so, according to Rashi, just as we say that when one makes his sukkah in the middle of a courtyard the passage of the public as a shortcut nullifies pi tikrah, likewise one may say that the passage of the public as a shortcut nullifies the middle wall of the courtyard opposite the sukkah from joining the sukkah. Although Tosafot disagree and hold that one should not distinguish between roofing over an exedra and making a sukkah in the middle of a courtyard whose wall was breached, nevertheless regarding not joining the middle wall of the courtyard, which is weaker than pi tikrah, one may say that Tosafot agree that the public nullifies it and it does not join.
