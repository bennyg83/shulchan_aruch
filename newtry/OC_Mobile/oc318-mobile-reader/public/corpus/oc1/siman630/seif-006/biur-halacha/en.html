If he inserted four poles, etc., it is invalid - See the Mishnah Berurah. Now in the Gemara it implies that this is a Torah-level invalidity. At first glance, according to the Rosh's ruling on page 19 that even regarding sukkah one fulfills by Torah law with a tzurat hapesach, then even if he holds that we do not say gud asik, why does he not fulfill by virtue of tzurat hapesach? One cannot answer that the Rosh holds that only for the third wall one fulfills by Torah law with a tzurat hapesach, but not when it is made this way for all of its walls; this cannot be said, for we proved above in siman 362 in Biur Halacha, s.v. keshekol, etc., that the Rosh holds that in every case it is a private domain by Torah law. Perhaps the Rosh holds that since, when he placed the s'chach, he did not intend it for the sake of a tzurat hapesach, it is not a tzurat hapesach, and he does not accept the Rema's ruling above in se'if 2 in the gloss, but rather follows the Yerushalmi cited there by the Magen Avraham to be stringent.
Because we say gud asik mechitzata - See the Gemara, that although regarding sukkah we specifically require partitions, and therefore a pillar ten tefachim high and seven by seven wide is not a sukkah, here he holds that it is better, because the house has actual partitions, as Rashi explains there.
