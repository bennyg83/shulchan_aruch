He roofed over a portico. Meaning: this portico has two walls opposite one another, part of it is roofed, and near the roofing until the other end he placed the s'chach. It emerges that this sukkah has only two walls. If there are posts, meaning projecting pillars on the third side, then although in another sukkah that has only two walls and is made like an alleyway we say in se'if 3 that it requires a board of four and a bit, here posts suffice. Since there are posts, we say that the edge of the roof descends and seals, meaning that the edge of the portico roof, the roofing that the portico has, descends and seals; it therefore gives the sukkah a third wall. But without posts, we do not say that it descends and seals. So the Maggid Mishneh explained these words of the Rambam. These posts, although they are not visible inside the sukkah but only to one standing outside, nevertheless help, for the posts are not the sukkah's partition; rather, through them we say that the edge of the portico roof descends and seals. About this the Rema wrote that others disagree, namely Rashi, Tosafos, and the Rosh. They explain that the portico has no walls on the side of the sukkah; rather, one wants to join the walls of the house to the sukkah. See the Tur for their approach. According to them, there are many posts for the portico, with less than three between each one, and the posts become partitions for the sukkah. Then one need not say that the edge of the roof descends and seals. About this the Rema wrote that one should not make the sukkah in such a manner and say that the edge of the roof descends and seals is effective in place of the third wall. The Levush wrote that after the fact it is valid in this case. And I did not want to elaborate here in explaining the words of the Tur according to the approach of Tosafos, but it appears to me to learn one law from their explanation. This is regarding one who wants to make a sukkah in his house and break open the roof above, as people do, and the walls of the house will be the walls of the sukkah. That is, he wants to make the sukkah in the corner of the east wall and the south wall, and there is one beam lying all year above opposite it along the length of the house near those corners, and the sukkah will be in that corner section until the beam. It emerges that the sukkah has only two walls of the house, and for the third it has only the beam above, regarding which we say that it descends and seals downward, so that it will have three walls, which is enough. He need only place two reeds beneath the beam, one near the wall of the house and one at the place where the third wall ends, meaning at the place where the sukkah's s'chach ends. The reason for these reeds is so that there will be a form of a doorway in the third wall because of the ceiling over the two reeds, for here there is no actual third partition, only through the edge of the roof descending and sealing; therefore a form of a doorway is required. The Tur's approach required posts only in a sukkah outside a portico, because we do not say that the portico roof descends and seals in place of the third partition, since the portico partitions are made to serve inside it and not outside them, as the Gemara says in this matter. This is unlike here, with the walls of the house and the sukkah inside the house, where we certainly say that the edge of the roof descends and seals in place of the third partition. As for why we do not say regarding an ordinary sukkah that the edge of the roof descends and seals, and why two walls alone are not enough: there, the roof belongs only to the sukkah and lacks permanence, so one cannot say that it descends and seals, as the Ran wrote about this. But here the beam is permanent, since it is made for the needs of the house and lies there; therefore one can properly say that the edge of the roof descends and seals in place of the third partition, since there is also a form of a doorway there, as I wrote. Examine the source of the law in the Tur's approach and you will find that there is no uncertainty as to what I wrote.
