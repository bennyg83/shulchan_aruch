Se'if 8, because it is, etc. Meaning: the portico consists of two walls opposite one another, and part of it is roofed. Near the roofing, at one edge, he placed s'chach. "Posts" does not mean within less than three, as Rashi explained, but that there are two posts on the third side, one here and one there. By those posts we say that the edge of the roof descends and seals. With this the difficulty of Tosafos there, s.v. achvi lei, is answered, and one need not press as the Rosh wrote there, that he also made a form of a doorway, and that Rav Kahana follows his own reasoning that he also requires a form of a doorway. By Torah law this suffices, for a form of a doorway is considered a partition everywhere, and a broad handbreadth is needed only rabbinically; and since it is visible from outside, it helps. See there. Likewise, what it says there, "He said to him: does the master not hold, etc.," and the statement that if it has posts it is valid, do not pertain to his question, for having posts is because of lavud, while not having posts is because of the edge of the roof.
But others, etc. According to Tosafos' explanation there, a portico has two attached partitions, but if they are opposite one another we do not say that the edge of the roof descends and seals, as written there: he said to him, I concede to you, etc. On the third side he relies in such a way that the s'chach touches the portico; and the posts, according to Rashi's explanation, are adjacent within less than three of one another, because of lavud. Without posts, although in general this helps, here it does not help, as written in the Gemara, and Rava said to him: perforce, etc. When it is visible from the outside, etc., a form of a doorway is also needed, like the first answer in the Rosh there. See the Tur. It appears that the Rambam explains like the Rif at the end of the ninth chapter of Eruvin, that a portico has three partitions, as Tosafos wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Chananel and Rabbeinu Tam. Here, even though he did not place the s'chach adjacent to the middle wall and the sukkah has only two walls, the edge of the roof helps because it has posts; and according to Abaye in the first version, even without posts. To this Rava challenged him: according to you, who say, etc., even though it has only two partitions. If so, even if the middle wall was breached, he said to him: I concede to you in that case, since it is open from side to side. This is unlike here, where the wall helps, for we say through the edge of the roof; but according to Rava it does not help and it is like an open alleyway. This is what he wrote: "because it is, etc., for behold, etc., and the middle, etc."
