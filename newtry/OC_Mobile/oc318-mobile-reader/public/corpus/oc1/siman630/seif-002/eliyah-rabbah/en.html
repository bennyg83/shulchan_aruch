[2] A reed upon it, etc. If the wall reed reaches the s'chach, one does not need a reed on top of it [Rema]. The Magen Avraham wrote: this requires analysis, for what is the wall doing here? The reed on top rests on the handbreadth and the reed opposite it. So too the Mordechai wrote explicitly, "and if the two reeds reach, etc." Until here is his language [Magen Avraham]. I am astonished, for it is obvious that that handbreadth is called a wall, as the Shulchan Aruch wrote: he makes a wall that is more than a handbreadth wide. There is no proof at all from the Mordechai, for he holds that one does not need a handbreadth, only a reed here and a reed there; examine carefully. Later I saw in old Shulchan Aruch editions this language: "and if the handbreadth and the wall, etc." According to this, perforce "the wall" does not refer to the handbreadth, and if so the Magen Avraham's question is on the old text. See the end of siman 4. As for the Magen Avraham's question from the Yerushalmi, which holds that it is forbidden when it was not made for this purpose, in my humble opinion here is different: since it was made to permit the s'chach, it was also made for this permission of a form of a doorway. Furthermore, in the Yerushalmi in Eruvin, page 19c, it is stated regarding where it was not made for this.
