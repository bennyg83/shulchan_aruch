(6) The walls of the sukkah, etc. To understand this law properly, I shall first introduce several principles. First, the minimum dimensions of a sukkah are seven by seven tefachim, as stated below in siman 634. Second, the Gemara derives from Scripture that sechach alone does not create a sukkah unless it has three walls, while a halachah transmitted to Moshe at Sinai teaches that one of those walls may be only one tefach wide. Third, it must nevertheless have the appearance of a wall. Thus, where there are two adjacent walls forming a corner, one takes a board slightly wider than one tefach, meaning a tefach plus a small amount, and places it within three tefachim of one of the two walls. Any gap of less than three tefachim is treated as closed by lavud, so that there are four tefachim here, which constitute the majority of the seven-tefach minimum wall. To complete the full wall, he must also make a tzuras hapesach. He places a vertical pole opposite that tefach, at the end of the opposite wall, seven tefachim away, and places a horizontal pole from the tefach to the opposite pole above them. It may even be suspended above them without touching, provided it is directly aligned with them, as explained above in the laws of tzuras hapesach in siman 362. A complete seven-tefach wall is thereby formed. We shall now, with Hashem’s help, explain each detail separately.
(7) More than one tefach. Magen Avraham explains that one may construct the sukkah walls from poles spaced less than three tefachim apart, provided that he does so on all four sides. If he wishes to make only three walls, however, he requires two complete adjacent walls and a third wall of one tefach.
(8) He places it within three tefachim, etc. It is then joined by lavud, so that there are four tefachim, which are the majority of the minimum valid sukkah wall. Although an ordinary tefach together with a gap of less than three tefachim would also exceed three and one-half tefachim and thus constitute a majority, an area smaller than four tefachim is nowhere regarded as significant. They therefore required a spacious tefach. Ran.
(9) Opposite the wall. Meaning, opposite the far end of the second, opposing wall, which ordinarily extends seven tefachim. Some say that he should distance it one additional tefach, so that the tzuras hapesach extends over four tefachim.
(10) He must make a tzuras hapesach, etc. This completes the required measure of the wall. Bach writes that this requirement is D’Rabbanan, whereas the spacious tefach mentioned initially is required according to the Torah.
(11) It is valid. One may then carry within it and transfer from the house through a window opening into the sukkah on a Shabbos during Sukkos. Although on other Shabbosos carrying is forbidden unless the fourth side is repaired, on a Shabbos during Sukkos we apply the principle that since it is deemed a wall for the laws of sukkah, it is also deemed a wall for the laws of Shabbos. Nevertheless, except in pressing circumstances, it is proper to be stringent.
(12) If the tefach and the pole, etc. This is the correct reading.
(13) No horizontal pole is required above them. The sechach itself is regarded as the horizontal pole. Magen Avraham challenges this from the Yerushalmi and maintains that it is proper to be stringent because the sechach was not placed for that purpose. The later authorities, however, resolve his difficulty. One who relies upon the Rama has therefore not lost.
