(Se'if katan 3) And it is valid, for it resembles two joined walls. Meaning, it is explicit in se'if 5 that the openings may not be at the corners, for the partitions must be connected. Therefore he wrote that here it resembles joined and connected walls, since it is by means of lavud, etc. But in a large sukkah, etc., if it is more than three tefachim, it is invalid.
For certainly one cannot say that it speaks of less than three. If so, according to the first opinion, why would a tzurat hapesach be needed? We would say lavud, just as for that reason no tzurat hapesach is needed on the other side. Rather, it must be speaking even where it is three tefachim wide, where lavud cannot be applied.
Therefore the first opinion requires a tzurat hapesach. But if it is more than three tefachim, even by means of a tzurat hapesach it is invalid.
So it is according to the text before us. This is difficult, for it would be speaking specifically of an exact measure, namely exactly three tefachim. With less than three tefachim it would not require a tzurat hapesach, and with more than three tefachim a tzurat hapesach would not help. I do not know the basic reasoning for distinguishing why at exactly three tefachim a tzurat hapesach should help, while with more than that it should not. Since there are three tefachim and lavud does not apply, it is the same as a width of four or five or more. Therefore it appears to me that there is a scribal error and the text should read: and more than ten is invalid. Meaning, up to ten, including ten, it has the status of an entrance and is therefore valid; but with more than that, since it no longer has the status of an entrance, it is invalid. Whether a tzurat hapesach helps for more than ten depends on the dispute written by the Shulchan Aruch in se'if 5; see there.
