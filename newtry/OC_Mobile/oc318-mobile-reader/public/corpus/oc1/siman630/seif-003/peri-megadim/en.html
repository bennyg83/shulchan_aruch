And he should make. See Turei Zahav; and rabbinically, for by Torah law a broad handbreadth suffices. See Bach, of blessed memory, and letter 2. If he forgot on Yom Tov, he has fulfilled his obligation if he has no other sukkah; see siman 629 in Magen Avraham, letter 22. To place a form of a doorway through a non-Jew on Shabbos and Yom Tov, one can say it is permitted, as a rabbinic prohibition upon a rabbinic prohibition in the place of a mitzvah, as in siman 306:5; see there. If there is no non-Jew, it requires analysis. One can say that even if a tent less than a handbreadth does not have the law of a tent, nevertheless any partition that permits is rabbinically forbidden; see siman 315. One can say that on Yom Tov, for the sake of food preparation, a rabbinic prohibition in such a case is permitted; see siman 514. All this requires analysis, and with Hashem's help it will be explained further in Magen Avraham.
