(14) He makes a wall, etc. Above, one tefach suffices because the two existing walls are adjacent, and an ordinary one-tefach third wall is therefore sufficient. Here, however, the two walls are opposite one another and separated. He must therefore make a wall four tefachim plus a small amount wide and place it within three tefachim of one wall. Through lavud it is considered a complete seven-tefach wall adjacent to that wall, so that here too there are two adjacent walls.
(15) Here too he must make a tzuras hapesach, etc. See Magen Avraham, who is uncertain whether this is valid only for a small sukkah, whereas in a large sukkah, where the third wall is very distant, even a tzuras hapesach may not suffice. Many later authorities agree, however, that no distinction should be made.
(16) Some say that this does not require a tzuras hapesach. This differs from seif 2, where the repair consists of a strip only one tefach wide and is therefore weak, requiring a tzuras hapesach to complete the measure. Here there is a sound repair through a four-tefach strip, and according to this opinion no tzuras hapesach is needed. The later authorities write that one should be stringent in accordance with the first opinion.
(17) But if there is, etc. Meaning, in this case all opinions agree that no tzuras hapesach is required, even according to the first opinion.
(18) Since there is a seven-tefach wall, etc. Although the third wall facing it is distant, there are nevertheless three walls in the sukkah. It stands to reason that this applies only where the gap is no greater than ten amos, so that it is regarded as an entrance. See below, seif 5.
(19) All the more so they do not require a tzuras hapesach, etc. Meaning, for the fourth wall.
