All the breached area. Meaning: we calculate the breached area of the two sides that are completely breached together with the breach in the open space of the openings, and it is in total greater than the standing material that exists in the two partitions and the handbreadth; then it is permitted. This is explicit in Rashi, and this is his language: "For when you combine them with the two breached sides, it is breached more than standing, and it is permitted." These are his words. This implies that in any event, in the partitions themselves, the standing material must be greater than the breached area. If so, this is difficult, for Rashi wrote before this that a sukkah wall is valid if there is a vertical partition without horizontal pieces, even though there is open air of less than three between them, and the reeds of the partition are only the width of a finger; nevertheless it is valid because of lavud. In that case, the partition itself certainly has more breached than standing, without combining the other partitions. One must say that there is a distinction. When there is no large breach, but all the breaches are each individually less than three, then it is permitted even where the breached area is greater than the standing material in that partition, since we say lavud and all the standing material is as though closed. But if there is one breach larger than three handbreadths, and you must permit it because it is like an opening, then there must in any event be more standing than breached in that partition; otherwise the breached area does not have the name of an opening. The leniency here is only that even though, in the total breach, the breached area is greater. This is clear and simple to one who examines Rashi, and I am astonished that this distinction was not explained in the poskim.
