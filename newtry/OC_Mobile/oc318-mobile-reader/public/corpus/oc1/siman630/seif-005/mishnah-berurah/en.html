(22) The breached area may exceed, etc. Meaning, when the breaches in the two walls are combined with the two wholly open sides, the open area may exceed the standing wall. If, however, within those two complete walls themselves the breached area exceeds the standing area, it is forbidden. This applies specifically where the breaches are greater than three tefachim. A breach smaller than three tefachim is closed by lavud and is not regarded as a breach. See Magen Avraham at the beginning of the siman, who maintains that this is effective only when the sukkah has four walls; if it has only three walls, relying on lavud is insufficient D’Rabbanan.
(23) The standing area. The standing portion must at least constitute a significant area, namely a strip four tefachim wide.
(24) Provided that the entrances are not, etc. This applies even if they are formed as a tzuras hapesach. Although for Shabbos a tzuras hapesach is treated as completely closed, the laws of sukkah are more stringent and require its two walls to be actually joined at a corner, rather than joined only through a tzuras hapesach.
(25) At the corners. It should seemingly say ‘at the corner,’ because the Mechaber is discussing a sukkah with only two complete walls, which has only one corner. The Rama teaches that even where one constructs four complete walls, he may not place entrances at all four corners, because then no two walls remain joined. He may, however, place entrances at the other corners while leaving two walls actually joined.
(26) Provided that, etc., it does not exceed ten amos. If it exceeds ten amos, it is forbidden even when the standing portion exceeds the breached portion, because it is regarded as a breach rather than an entrance.
(27) A tzuras hapesach, even when it exceeds ten amos. It is then regarded as an entrance and not as a breach.
(28) To make complete walls. Meaning, not by employing the devices of lavud and tzuras hapesach. I also saw in the Halachos of Rabbi Yitzchak Gi’as that, for the choicest fulfillment of the mitzvah, the sukkah should have three walls closed on all sides, with no opening except for the entrance itself.
