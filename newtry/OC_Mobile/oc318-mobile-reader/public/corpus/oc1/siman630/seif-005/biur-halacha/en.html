That are made like a Greek gamma, with a tefach and a tzurat hapesach, etc. - At first glance, the Mechaber here copied the words of the baraita taught in the Gemara: Shabbos is stricter than sukkah, for Shabbos is permitted only where the standing part exceeds the breached part, unlike sukkah. Now according to the way the Taz and Magen Avraham explained the Shulchan Aruch, when it wrote that when all the breached area is combined, etc., this means specifically together with the breach in the two walls, and that in the two complete walls the standing portion exceeds the breached portion, as Rashi explains. This is difficult, for regarding Shabbos as well, since we rule that a tzurat hapesach is considered closed for Shabbos, and certainly lavud is considered closed everywhere, and we also rule that three partitions certainly make a private domain, then in such a case it should also be a private domain by Torah law regarding Shabbos. I have no difficulty with Rashi himself, for Rashi explained, unlike sukkah, where two walls according to their law and a third even of a tefach are sufficient, etc.; that is, he refers to the Torah law, where a tefach is sufficient and no tzurat hapesach is needed, as the Bach wrote. In that case, for Shabbos it is certainly not a private domain if additional breaches in the two complete walls combine with the large opening in the third wall so that the standing part does not exceed the breached part. But according to what the Shulchan Aruch wrote, that it is made like a Greek gamma with a tefach and a tzurat hapesach, this is difficult. And even if you say that regarding Shabbos, although three partitions are effective by Torah law, nevertheless the open fourth wall combines with the breaches found in the three walls, it is still difficult, for overall there is more standing than breached. In place of the open fourth wall there is the third wall made by lavud and tzurat hapesach, and there are also two walls whose standing portions exceed their breaches. One can answer that for sukkah it is enough if the third wall has a widened tefach and a tzurat hapesach until seven tefachim are completed, even though the wall is large and most of it is open; and especially if we add the small breaches in the two walls, the breached area will certainly exceed the standing area, yet it is valid. For Shabbos, in such a case it does not have the status of a private domain.
For the partitions must be connected - If the breach there was less than three tefachim, it is permitted, for they are called connected by means of lavud, as the Peri Megadim writes.
Provided, etc., more than ten amot - I am uncertain whether through this the entire wall is nullified. Perhaps it only helps to define it as an important breach and not as an entrance, so that it is forbidden to sit in that place and fulfill the mitzvah, even though it is covered above; but in the remaining part of the wall, where a sukkah measure or more remains, perhaps it is permitted. It is no worse than if the area from the breach onward were entirely missing, in which case the sukkah would certainly be valid. See the Bach, whose wording implies that through this the entire partition is ruined. His reasoning is certainly true as practical halacha regarding Shabbos, for that is indeed the law; but in our matter, where we rule that the measure of a sukkah is only seven tefachim, this requires examination.
