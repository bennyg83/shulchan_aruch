The breached area. Meaning: we calculate the breached area of the two completely breached sides together with the breach in the open space of the openings, and it is included in "greater than the standing material" that exists in the two partitions and the handbreadth; then it is permitted. But in any event, in the partitions themselves the standing material must be greater than the breached area. The Turei Zahav wrote: it appears to me that there is a distinction in the matter. When there is no large breach, but all the breaches are each less than three handbreadths on their own, then it is permitted even if the breached area is greater than the standing material in that partition, since we say lavud and all the standing material is as though closed. This is unlike when there is one breach larger than three handbreadths; then there must in any event be more standing material than breached area in that partition. The leniency is that even though in the total of that breach the breached area is greater. See there.
At the corners. Meaning, at all the corners. But if two partitions are connected, it is permitted. Magen Avraham.
Complete. The Mateh Moshe wrote: the custom was to make a circle inside the opening. But those sukkos that are built from year to year with a full doorway do not need that circle, and there is not even a custom for it. The Maharil wrote: Mahari Segal would mark the sukkah walls in order to see the order of their placement, so that he would not change from year to year. So it is in the Yerushalmi: "And you shall erect the Mishkan according to its law." Is there a law for the boards? Rather, a board that merited being placed in the north should always be placed in the north.
