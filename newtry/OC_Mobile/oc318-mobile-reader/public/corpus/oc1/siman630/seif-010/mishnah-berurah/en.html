(46) One who makes his sukkah, etc. He did not support it upon the tree itself, for otherwise one may not ascend to it on Yom Tov, as stated in siman 628, seif 3.
(47) Strong. If the majority of the wall consists of strong material, Tosafos somewhat implies that it is valid, because it then no longer sways back and forth in the wind.
(48) So that an ordinary wind does not move them. Meaning, if the wind moves them, even if it cannot knock them down completely but merely causes the wall to sway back and forth, it is no longer regarded as a wall. Even if it stands inside a house where there is no wind at all, it is not regarded as a wall.
(49) He filled the spaces, etc. This is required only if the ten-tefach minimum height of the sukkah includes the branches. Otherwise it is unnecessary.
(50) All the walls, etc. Taz maintains that one of the three required sukkah walls may be made from sheets. Several later authorities, however, imply that not even one wall should be made from sheets unless poles are interwoven within less than three tefachim of one another. All agree that the fourth wall may be made from sheets, because the law requires only three walls.
(51) With walls of poles, etc. Even if the wind moves the sheets, the pole walls remain.
