(32) He placed sechach over an alleyway that has a lechi. The Mechaber’s unqualified ruling implies that he follows those authorities who validate even an alleyway open at both ends that has a lechi at one end, provided that the valid measure of sechach is placed alongside the lechi. The reason is that, for Shabbos, even a minimally wide lechi is regarded as a wall on that side. It therefore has three walls and is a private domain according to the Torah. We then apply the principle that since it is a wall for Shabbos, it is also a wall for sukkah on a Shabbos occurring during Sukkos. Although carrying with only three walls is forbidden D’Rabbanan, it was permitted on that Shabbos for the mitzvah of sukkah.
(33) Around the well, etc. These are four corner boards fixed on the four sides, opposite one another, each extending one amah in each direction. They were permitted for pilgrims so that they could draw water for their animals from a well that is a private domain situated in the public domain. Through these boards the area around the well becomes a private domain. Although this arrangement was permitted only for pilgrims, it was likewise permitted here for the mitzvah of sukkah on a Shabbos during Sukkos.
(34) One should not permit, etc. Since the Mechaber ruled without qualification, he appears to permit in every case. The Rama therefore teaches that in practice one should be stringent in accordance with those authorities who permit only where the lechi actually permits carrying on Shabbos, namely where the alleyway is enclosed on three sides and has a lechi on the fourth. There carrying is permitted even D’Rabbanan, and the lechi may consequently be treated as a sukkah wall on Shabbos, although for sukkah purposes only two walls can otherwise be counted because the sechach lies only alongside the lechi.
(35) And the corner boards permit. This applies when pilgrims are present. Nowadays, when there are no pilgrims, it applies where water is not readily available and the need is for a mitzvah. It applies specifically in the public domain, not in a courtyard.
(36) For then the principle applies. Initially it is proper to be stringent in accordance with the Rama. In pressing circumstances one may rely upon the first opinion, which Eliyah Rabbah writes is the principal ruling.
