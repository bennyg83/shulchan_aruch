(1) They are valid for the walls. The implication of the verse, ‘You shall make the festival of Sukkos when you gather from your threshing floor and your winepress,’ from which we learn that the sukkah is to be made from the refuse of the threshing floor and winepress, namely material that does not contract tumah and grows from the ground, applies specifically to the sechach. The walls are not called the sukkah.
(2) They need not be positioned, etc. Meaning, we do not compare this case to the lulav, which must specifically be taken in the direction of its growth, as stated below in siman 651, seif 2.
(3) Even if its sunlight exceeds its shade, etc. Meaning, there were large breaches in the walls, because of which the sunlight exceeded the shade. Nevertheless, it is valid, because we are particular about this only regarding the sechach.
(4) From something with a foul odor. We are concerned that the odor may cause him to leave the sukkah. After the fact it is valid, even when the sechach was made from such material, as stated in siman 629, seif 14. If, however, the odor is so foul that a person cannot tolerate it, it is possible that the sukkah is invalid according to the Torah, because dwelling in the sukkah must resemble dwelling in one’s home. Peri Megadim further writes that those who, for lack of space, construct a sukkah beside a privy or refuse heap, so that a foul odor reaches them, fulfill the mitzvah according to the Torah, but may not recite Birkas HaMazon there, as stated above in siman 79, seif 2; see there, according to the first opinion, which the later authorities follow. If another location is available, one certainly should not construct it there. Even if no foul odor reaches him, but he sees the privy, it is also forbidden. See below in siman 637, Biur Halachah, at the end of the entry beginning ‘Likewise, on ground belonging to the public.’
(5) Or something that dries out, etc. Meaning, we regard it as though it had already dried out. See above, siman 629, seif 12 in the gloss, and the Mishnah Berurah there.
