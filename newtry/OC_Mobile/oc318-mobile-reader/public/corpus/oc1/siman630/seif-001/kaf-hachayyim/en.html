1) [Seif 1] All materials are valid for the walls. For whenever Scripture writes sukkah, it implies the s'chach, since a wall is not called a sukkah. Although we derive the walls from the words basukkos, basukkos, basukkos, in Sukkah 6b, we learn this from the superfluity of the verses and not from their plain implication. Therefore, the verse, "You shall make the festival of Sukkos for yourself when you gather in from your threshing floor," refers to the s'chach. Rashi, Sukkah 12a; Beit Yosef; Taz, s.k. 1.
2) Ibid. All materials are valid for the walls. A conscientious person should take into account the Hagahos Ashri, that the walls as well should be made from materials that are not susceptible to tumah and that grow from the ground. Bach. All the later authorities, however, did not take this into account. Eliyah Rabbah, os 1. So too wrote Birkei Yosef, os 1, and in his work Machazik Berachah, os 1, whose words were cited by Shaarei Teshuvah. This is also implied by the Gemara cited below, seif 11; see there.
3) The walls of a sukkah made from items from which benefit is forbidden, whether they are valid. See Birkei Yosef and Machazik Berachah, os 2, who cite the opinions that permit them. See above, siman 629, os 5, what we wrote concerning this; see there. Regarding stolen wood, whether it is valid for the walls, see Pitchei Teshuvah, who discusses the matter and leaves it requiring further study; see there.
4) Ibid., Rama's gloss: They need not be positioned in the direction of their growth. The Tur writes: Some are meticulous, when making a wall from reeds, to position them in the direction of their growth, as we say concerning the lulav, siman 651, seif 2, that one fulfills the mitzvah with it only in the direction of its growth. This is unnecessary, however, since walls are valid from any material, end quote. Nevertheless, Beit HaShoevah, os 10, writes that it is good and proper to be meticulous concerning this; see there.
5) Maharil said that he saw his teacher, Maharam Shalom, mark the boards for the walls of the sukkah with alef, beis, gimel, in order to know the order in which they stood and not change them from year to year. Hagahos Derashos Maharil, Hilchos Sukkah; Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahos Beit Yosef, os 3. So too is it stated in the Yerushalmi concerning the boards of the Mishkan: R' Ami said, "You shall erect the Mishkan according to its rule." Is there a rule for pieces of wood? Rather, a board that merited being placed in the north shall always be placed in the north, and similarly, one placed in the south shall be placed in the south. Shelah, 245a; Eliyah Rabbah, os 17. Certainly, however, this does not invalidate the sukkah and is only an act of piety. See above, siman 8, os 17.
6) It is permitted to hang curtains of shatnez, provided they are suspended above the level at which a person uses them. If they protect above from the sun and the wind, there is no concern. Derashos Maharil, ibid.; Darkei Moshe, os 5; Mateh Moshe, siman 894; Knesset HaGedolah, ibid., os 4; Ateres Zekeinim; Eliyah Rabbah, ibid. See below, siman 638, os 52.
7) Ibid., Rama's gloss: Even if its sunlight is greater, etc. This means that the open space between one reed and the next is wider than a reed, thereby making its sunlight greater than its shade. Even so, it is valid, for we are not particular about this with regard to walls.
8) Ibid., Rama's gloss: From something that has a foul odor. This is forbidden lest he leave the sukkah and depart. After the fact, however, it is valid, even for s'chach, unless the odor is so foul that a person cannot tolerate it. In that case, it is possible that it is invalid according to the Torah, since we require, "You shall dwell," in the manner in which you ordinarily reside, as written above, siman 629, seif 14, and in our words there, with G-d's help.
9) Those who make a sukkah near a privy or a refuse heap because they lack sufficient space, and a foul odor reaches them, fulfill their obligation according to the Torah, but they should not recite Birkas HaMazon there, since the requirement to recite the blessing in its place is rabbinic. If one has another place, he certainly should not make it in this location. If the odor does not reach him but he can see the privy, it is forbidden. Mishbetzos Zahav, os 1; Mishna Berurah, os 4. See above, siman 79, seif 2, and in our words there, with G-d's help.
10) Ibid., Rama's gloss: Or something that dries out within seven days, etc. In such a case, we immediately regard it as though it had already dried out, as written above, siman 629, seif 12, in the Rama's gloss, and in our words there, os 73.
11) Ibid., Rama's gloss: Or something that dries out, etc. But plants whose leaves fall are invalid only for s'chach, because they fall upon the table. Rashba; Biur HaGra.
