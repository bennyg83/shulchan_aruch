Valid. See Turei Zahav, and Sukkah 12a, Rashi there, and Tosafos 7b, s.v. mechitzah. See Bach: a scrupulous person should be concerned to bring walls from something that does not receive impurity and grows from the ground. The Or Zarua brought this, but the Eliyah Rabbah wrote that the world is not concerned for this. In the Mishnah, 24b, a tree is a valid wall, as in se'if 10. If so, what difference is there between something attached, which is invalid by Torah law, and other invalid materials? This is what Rashi wrote: all of those invalid things that we learned regarding s'chach, so you should not say that it refers to a bundle; see there. Something whose smell is bad should not be used initially, but after the fact it is fine, even for s'chach, as in siman 629:14. Those who make a sukkah near a privy or a garbage heap because they have little space, and the bad smell reaches them, see siman 79:12. By Torah law he has fulfilled his obligation, but he should not recite Birkas HaMazon there, for the requirement that a blessing be recited in its place is rabbinic. If he has another place, he certainly should not make it in this place. If the smell does not reach him but he sees the privy, it is forbidden; see there. And if it is a bad smell that a person cannot tolerate, one can say that by Torah law it is invalid, because "you shall dwell" requires dwelling in the manner one lives.
