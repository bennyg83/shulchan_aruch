All materials are valid for walls - See the Bach, who wrote that a person of spiritual sensitivity should be concerned for what is brought in the Darchei Moshe in the name of the Hagahot Ashri in the name of the Or Zarua, who is stringent regarding walls as he is regarding the s'chach, where the material is invalid by Torah law. See the Eliyah Rabbah and also the Birkei Yosef, who say that no one is concerned for this, and the proof he brought from the Yerushalmi is also not valid according to our text. The Gra also rejected his proof from the Yerushalmi. See Sukkah 2, where it is obvious to the Gemara that one may make partitions of iron, even though one may not use them as s'chach. However, one who studies the Biur HaGra will see that for another reason a person of spiritual sensitivity should be concerned initially not to make the walls from something that is invalid for s'chach by Torah law and then rest the s'chach upon them. This follows what several later authorities agreed above in siman 629:7, that initially one should be concerned for the view of the poskim, the Rashba and the Ran according to the Rif, that one may not support the s'chach with something invalid for s'chach, as a decree lest one come to use it for s'chach. This does not apply to things with which it is uncommon to make s'chach, or to things that are invalid for s'chach only rabbinically, for with those they did not decree against using them as supports. Alternatively, one should set posts at the corners of the sukkah beneath the s'chach, so that they are what support the s'chach; this is permitted according to everyone, as stated in the Gemara on 21b.
