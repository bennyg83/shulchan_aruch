He roofed. See Turei Zahav, for the view of the Mechaber is like the Rif and the Rambam, of blessed memory, chapter 4 of Sukkah, according to the explanation of the sugya in Sukkah 18; see there. This means that there are two partitions and a roof from partition to partition up to half of it. For example, each partition is four amos long, and the roof is over them; if there remains two amos of air and he placed s'chach over those two amos of air, if there are posts near the beam, in such a case we say that the edge of the roof descends and seals, and the sukkah has three partitions. Likewise, if the roofing is in the half of the portico toward one partition and the s'chach is toward the second partition in the air, and there are not four amos from the partition to the end of the roof, we say bent wall, and if there are two posts on the third side near the roof, this law applies. See the Ran regarding whether the roofing must be lower than the s'chach. Regarding s'chach itself, we certainly do not say that the edge of the roof descends and seals, for it is temporary; see there. The second diagram that I wrote requires some analysis. What the Turei Zahav wrote as law, about one who wants to make a sukkah in a house in the southeast corner [or the like], where a permanent beam lies, etc.: even according to the approach of Rashi and Tosafos it is valid, although they disagree with the Rambam, of blessed memory, as the Rav wrote in the gloss here; one can say they agree. Nevertheless, two reeds are required for a form of a doorway, since there is no complete partition, only the edge of the roof; and although above it is explained that wherever there is a full wall, a form of a doorway is not needed, nevertheless here, where it is only the edge of the roof, a form of a doorway is also required. See siman 361 in Magen Avraham, letter 5, that according to those who say the edge of the roof applies specifically with four handbreadths, Tosafos in Eruvin 86a, s.v. korah, requires analysis, for the house beam does not have four handbreadths. According to Pnei Yehoshua in his novellae to Sukkah, the posts are rabbinic, while by Torah law the edge of the roof helps according to everyone. See Bach, se'if 8: in sukkah, the edge of the roof helps even when the beam is one handbreadth wide, whereas on Shabbos it requires four handbreadths; see there.
