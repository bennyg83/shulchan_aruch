12) [Seif 2] If the walls of the sukkah were two, etc. For we learned in the Mishnah, Sukkah 6b: Two walls according to their full halachic requirements, and a third wall of even one tefach, are sufficient, and the form of a doorway is required. Therefore, if it has two walls adjacent to one another, he makes a slightly wider than one-tefach strip and positions it within three tefachim of one of the walls, meaning one of the existing walls. He then positions a pole opposite the end of the wall, meaning the end of the opposite existing wall, and places a pole across it and the one-tefach strip, and it is valid. Tur. This means that he should position a pole at the end of the side containing the one-tefach strip, opposite the end of the wall facing it, and place another pole over this pole at the end of that side and over the one-tefach strip, thereby forming the shape of a doorway. The requirement of three walls, with the third being even one tefach, is derived there in the Gemara from the verses that write basukkas, basukkas, basukkos. Two are written defectively and one is written fully with a vav, which counts as two, totaling four. One is needed for the subject itself, leaving three for the walls. The received law, a halachah transmitted to Moshe at Sinai, then reduces the third wall and establishes it at one tefach; see there.
