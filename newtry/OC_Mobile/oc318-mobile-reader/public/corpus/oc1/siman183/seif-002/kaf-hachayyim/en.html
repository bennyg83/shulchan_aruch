6) [Seif 2] He then dilutes it, etc. Beis Yosef writes that this is the practice in our country even though the wines are not strong, because it contains a secret according to Kabbalah and also has other reasons; see there. Magen Avraham, paragraph 2; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2; Zechor LeAvraham, under kaf. Shaar HaMitzvos, parashas Eikev, similarly writes: During the second blessing, when reaching the word es in the verse, 'You shall eat, be satisfied, and bless es,' then dilute the cup with water, etc., as is known. The word es is Malchus, which is called a cup. Look at that water when placing it into the cup and intend that the numerical value of 'water' is ninety, corresponding to the nine letters yud in the four spellings of Havayah: seventy-two, sixty-three, forty-five, and fifty-two. Maavar Yabok, maamar Sefas Emes, writes that one must place three drops into the cup, one after another; see there. Petach HaDevir, paragraph 1; Ruach Chaim, paragraph 1; Yafeh LaLev, paragraph 2; Ben Ish Chai, parashas Shelach Lecha, paragraph 22. It appears to me that the reason is that wine corresponds to Binah, from which judgments are aroused, and it must therefore be sweetened with the waters of kindness. Since Binah is the Name whose numerical value is sixty-three and contains three letters yud, one must place three drops corresponding to them in order to sweeten it.
7) There: To make known the praise of the Land. This is to show everyone present the praise of Eretz Yisrael, whose wine is strong and requires water to be added. Although the person reciting the blessing is outside Eretz Yisrael, since he does not dilute the wine until Birkas HaAretz, it is apparent that he comes to demonstrate the praise of Eretz Yisrael. Beis Yosef; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 3.
8) There, gloss: Some say that if the wine is not strong, it need not be diluted, etc. We have already written above, however, that according to Kabbalah it must be diluted. Therefore, even if it is not strong, one should dilute it with a small amount, namely, only three drops, as stated above in paragraph 6; see there.
9) There, gloss: He should draw it from the barrel for the sake of the blessing. This is because undiluted wine is called fresh in the vernacular. Rabbeinu Yerucham writes that one should draw it from the barrel immediately before the blessing. Even for us, he should send to the merchant to purchase it close to the blessing so that it is fresh. Magen Avraham, paragraph 3. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 3, however, writes that it is burdensome to wait while it is purchased close to the blessing. Therefore, it appears to me that one should purchase it beforehand in a pitcher, but not pour it from the pitcher until the time of the blessing, when he should pour it into the cup for the sake of the blessing. I later saw that Eshel Avraham, paragraph 3, writes the same.
10) There, gloss: He should fill the cup of blessing, etc. This is contrary to one explanation of adornment, which says that it should not be filled so high that a crown forms around its upper edge. Magen Avraham, paragraph 4. See Taz, paragraph 4. The measure of a cup of blessing is a reviis of a log, corresponding to the final letter heh of the Name Havayah. Zohar, parashas Pinchas, 246a.
11) There, gloss: Full to all its edges. This does not appear literal, since one must add water during Birkas HaAretz. Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 3, writes that if the wine is strong and requires much water, while the cup must be full, one should dilute it somewhat before Birkas HaMazon and add more during Birkas HaAretz, intending to sweeten the judgments. Ben Ish Chai, parashas Shelach Lecha, paragraph 21, however, writes that this is questionable, because some hold that at the beginning of the blessing the wine must be undiluted. This opinion appears primary, since Raya Mehemna, parashas Eikev, 273b, states it without qualification, whereas it presents the other opinions in the name of 'some say.' If one dilutes it before the blessing, the quality of being undiluted is absent according to that opinion. Therefore, even if it requires much water and the cup must necessarily have a visibly missing amount at the beginning of the blessing, this does not concern us, since during Birkas HaAretz he dilutes it and it becomes full. Nevertheless, it appears to me that at the beginning of the blessing it must in any event contain a reviis. These are his words.
12) There, gloss: Full to all its edges. Whoever recites the blessing over a full cup is given a boundless inheritance, as it is stated, 'Full with the blessing of Hashem, possess the sea and the south.' Some say he merits and inherits two worlds, this world and the World to Come. Berachos 51a.
