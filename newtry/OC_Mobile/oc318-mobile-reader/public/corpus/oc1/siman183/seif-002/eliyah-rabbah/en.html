In these lands, etc. The Beit Yosef writes that in his countries this dilution is practiced even though the wines are not strong, because there is a kabbalistic secret and other reasons involved. I also found that the gaon Maharal writes in Netivot Olam, folio 40, that even in these lands one should put a little water into the cup of wine, because wine brought wailing into the world. When water is joined to the wine, the wine is no longer prepared for wailing but for blessing. This is the practice one should follow.
For the sake of the blessing, etc. The word chai, 'alive,' means fresh in the vernacular. Rabbenu Yerucham writes that the wine should be drawn from the barrel immediately before the blessing. Magen Avraham says that one should therefore likewise send to the shopkeeper to purchase it immediately before the blessing so that it will be 'alive.' This is burdensome.
Full to its brim, etc. Even though some wine will spill when he takes the cup in his hand. Some do not fill it quite so much for this reason, despite the verse saying 'and full, etc.' The Gemara further learns from that verse that one merits two worlds. Lechem Chamudot, folio 45, explains that 'sea' represents evening and 'south' represents day, as the verse says 'going toward the south,' and Rashi explains it as always by day. This world is compared to night and the World to Come to day. Kol Bo answers that no verse is needed to teach reward in the World to Come; the verse was needed to teach his reward in this world.
