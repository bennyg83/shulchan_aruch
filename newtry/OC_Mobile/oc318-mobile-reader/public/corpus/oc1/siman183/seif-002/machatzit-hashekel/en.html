Seif katan 2: He dilutes it, etc. In his country, etc. This is intended to demonstrate the praise of Eretz Yisrael, whose wine is so strong that it requires dilution with water. Although the person reciting the blessing is outside Eretz Yisrael, since he waits to dilute it until Bircas HaAretz, it is evident that he does so to demonstrate the praise of Eretz Yisrael. So writes Beit Yosef. There is also support for that custom in the wording of Rambam; see Beit Yosef. He further writes that one must dilute it immediately upon beginning Bircas HaAretz, unlike those who err and do not dilute it until the end of that blessing. Accordingly, at the beginning of Bircas HaMazon one should not fill the cup completely to its brim. See there. Seif katan 3: For the sake of the blessing, etc. For “alive” is one of the ten matters stated regarding a cup of blessing, as taught in Berachos 51a, and means undiluted wine, called fresko. The Mechaber’s statement in seif 3 that the cup must be whole follows those who explain “alive” as referring to the vessel, meaning that it must be whole, since, as we say elsewhere regarding vessels, their breaking is their death. Thus, a whole vessel is called alive.
