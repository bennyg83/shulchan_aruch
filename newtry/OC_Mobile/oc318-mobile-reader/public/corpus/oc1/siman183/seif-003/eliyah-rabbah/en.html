Whole, etc. Kol Bo, folio 24, says the cup should be neither defective nor broken. This implies that even a slight break is a problem. Maharil likewise forbids a vessel whose rim is chipped or slightly broken. Perhaps the defect must involve a small actual loss measurable by a fingernail, since at the beginning of Bechorot concerning blemishes we say there is no defect without loss of material. A crack, however, is explicitly stated there to invalidate even without loss. Do not object that sacrificial blemishes cannot be compared to this law, because Torat Chaim, folio 72, writes that the requirement of a whole vessel is specifically modeled on the altar service. Maharil writes that one should be particular even if the base is broken. Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah explains this as applying even if the cup can stand without support, unlike Olat Tamid. All this applies where another cup is available. If not, it seems to me that Birkat HaMazon may be recited even over a broken cup, because it should be no worse than a pagum cup. Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah remains uncertain. It also seems to me that when necessary one may bless over a cup cover or jug cover. This is not comparable to netilat yadayim in siman 159, where a vessel must have been originally made to receive liquid. Nevertheless, where a normal cup is readily available one should be particular.
[6] [Levush] Without a yud, etc. I am astonished at Levush, for in truth he wrote yadeichem without a yud in the laws of tefillin, siman 134. It appears to me that the Gemara derives the matter from the vocalization: the yud has a sheva and the dalet a tzeirei. If the word meant one hand, the yud would have a segol and the dalet a sheva. The reason for initially taking the cup with both hands is to demonstrate affection for receiving it, after which he holds it with one hand so that it should not appear burdensome to him.
