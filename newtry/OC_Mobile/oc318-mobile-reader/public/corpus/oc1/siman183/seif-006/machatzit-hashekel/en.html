Seif katan 6: Beneath, etc. Berachos 51a states: Rabbi Chanan bar Abba said in the name of Rabbi Yochanan: The early authorities asked whether the left hand may assist the right. Rabbi Asi said: Since the early authorities did not resolve it, we should act stringently. Bach writes that Shibolei HaLeket implies that they prohibited only holding the cup with the left hand from the opposite side, just as the right hand holds it from one side. But if one does not hold the cup at mid-height with the left hand, and merely places the left beneath the cup, so that the left is beneath the right and assists it, this assistance has no substance and is permitted. The Rama, however, understood that it is permitted only to place the left hand beneath the right hand, but placing it beneath the cup is forbidden because one is then holding the cup with the left. Bach rejects this interpretation.
It appears to me that there is proof for Shibolei HaLeket, etc. Meaning, however its words are understood, whether as the Rama understands them or as Bach intends, Shibolei HaLeket’s reason is that assistance has no halachic substance. Rama and Bach dispute only what is considered assistance.
He received it with the right hand. All sacrificial service had to be performed with the right hand, and receiving the blood is one of the four sacrificial services.
The Tur wrote, etc. “To support it,” etc. Since the Tur wrote lesomchah, in the feminine, and not lesomcho, in the masculine, which would have referred to the cup, it must refer to the right hand. The Tur therefore disagrees with Shibolei HaLeket.
It would then be considered that he holds it with both hands. According to Bach’s explanation this is certainly so, because he holds the cup on one side with the left hand just as he holds it on the other side with the right. Even according to Rama, who understands Shibolei HaLeket to permit placing the left hand beneath the right but not beneath the cup, one could have explained that the question concerned a case in which the right hand alone holds the side of the cup while the left is beneath the cup, and they ruled stringently. Yet placing the left beneath the right might still be permitted. The Tur must therefore hold that even placing the left beneath the cup is considered holding it with both hands and was never in doubt, for it is certainly forbidden. The question could only have concerned placing the left beneath the right hand rather than beneath the cup, and even there Rabbi Asi ruled stringently.
“Therefore one should be stringent” is the correct reading. Meaning, since the Tur disagrees with Shibolei HaLeket, one should be stringent in accordance with the Tur. According to the Tur, Magen Avraham’s proof from Shabbos 93a must be understood as Taz explains in seif katan 3. See Responsa Chacham Tzvi, siman 82, who proves from the Gemara’s question whether the left hand may assist that assistance has halachic substance at least D’Rabbanan. They were merely unwilling, for this reason alone, to invalidate a korban whose blood was received with the right hand while the left assisted it. See there.
As Tosafos wrote regarding a large cup. Tosafos write regarding the question whether the left hand may assist: “The case concerns large cups, or Rosh Chodesh Teves that falls on Shabbos, meaning when Bircas HaMazon is lengthy because one must say Al HaNissim, Retzei, and Yaaleh VeYavo, or at a wedding, when one must prolong Bircas HaMazon.”
Magen Avraham mentioned one example. Tosafos imply that absent such a need there would have been no uncertainty and it would certainly be forbidden. Magen Avraham therefore ruled stringently.
