The one reciting the blessing should not speak. This implies that the diners may speak until he begins the blessing, as stated by Beit Yosef and Bach. Nevertheless, it appears proper to be stringent, since Rabbeinu Yonah and Rabbeinu Yerucham are stringent.
They fulfilled their obligation. This resembles one who heard nine shofar blasts over nine hours and fulfilled his obligation, as Tosafos and Rosh write. According to what I wrote at the beginning of siman 65, if the interruption resulted from circumstances beyond his control, he must return to the beginning. Perhaps Bircas HaMazon is more lenient, as I wrote at the end of siman 185 concerning excrement. See also what I wrote in simanim 422 and 488. It appears to me that if he spoke intentionally, he returns to the beginning, as stated in siman 114, seif 7. Tosafos, 46b, at the end of the passage beginning “To where does he return,” write, “for he did not speak in the interim,” implying that if he did speak he returns to the beginning. It appears, however, that this is a scribal error and should read, “for he did not eat in the interim.” See there. See also what I wrote at the end of siman 194 in the name of Ramban and at the end of siman 200.
