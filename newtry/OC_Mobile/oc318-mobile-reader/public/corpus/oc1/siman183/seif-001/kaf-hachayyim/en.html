1) [Seif 1] The cup of Birkas HaMazon requires rinsing inside, etc. Berachos 51a states: Ten things were said regarding a cup of blessing. It requires rinsing inside and washing outside; it must be undiluted and full; it requires adornment and wrapping; one takes it with both hands and places it in the right hand; one raises it a tefach from the ground; and one fixes his eyes upon it. Some say he also sends it as a gift to the members of his household. R' Yochanan said: We have only four, namely, rinsing inside, washing outside, undiluted, and full. The Tur cites and explains this Gemara. He writes that the Rambam, of blessed memory, mentions only the four of R' Yochanan, but Tosafos write that R' Yochanan's count comes only to exclude adornment and wrapping. He agrees that it must be placed in the right hand, for R' Yochanan said that the early Sages asked whether the left hand may assist the right. Presumably he also agrees that it is received with both hands, for they derive this from the verse, 'Raise your hands in holiness.' Likewise, raising it a tefach is derived from, 'I shall raise the cup of salvations.' One also fixes his eyes upon it so as not to divert his attention from the blessing. Rav Nachman similarly used to send it to his wife. Nowadays we observe all these practices, including spreading a cloth so that one not recite the blessing with an uncovered head; only adornment is absent. These are the Tur's words. Tosafos in our texts similarly write that since the Amoraim practiced adornment and wrapping, this implies that they acted stringently and observed them all. Therefore, one should be stringent regarding a cup of blessing. Beis Yosef cites this, and the Shulchan Aruch here rules in accordance with Tosafos and the Tur. Machazik Berachah, paragraph 1, writes in the name of Rema MiPano in Alfasi Zuta, Rabbeinu Yonah, and Rashba that specifically rinsing inside is indispensable, while the remaining practices are for the preferred fulfillment of the mitzvah. He writes that Tiferes Shmuel agrees in his glosses on Rosh; see there. Regarding the secret of these ten matters, see the Zohar, parashas Pinchas, 245; parashas Eikev, 273b; and Tikkunei Zohar, tikkun 47. Tikkunei Zohar writes that 'the wine' has the numerical value of 'secret,' and the ten matters are the yud of Yesod; see there. See below, paragraph 18.
2) Bach writes: As a practical halachah, since Tosafos and Rosh, of blessed memory, testify that in their times people practiced even spreading a cloth, and the Tur and Rabbeinu Yonah write likewise, every G-d-fearing person should take care during the blessing to wrap himself in his outer garment and also place his hat upon his head. He should not recite the blessing wearing only a small cap. He should follow this practice even when reciting the blessing alone. Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef, paragraph 5; Olas Tamid, paragraph 9; Magen Avraham, paragraph 5; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 16. The later authorities write the same.
3) It is proper to adorn the cup of Birkas HaMazon, Kiddush, and Havdalah with six cups surrounding it. Maaseh Rav, Hilchos Netilas Yadayim, paragraph 27; Pischei Olam, paragraph 1. See, however, the Tur, who gives three explanations of adornment. Some say one adorns the cup with students, meaning that students are seated around the leader during the blessing. Some say one adds to it during Birkas HaAretz, according to some wine and according to others water; see Perishah, paragraph 3. Others say one places beautiful cups around it to beautify it; see there. The Zohar there writes that it is adorned with students; see there. Therefore, the present practice is not to adorn it with cups, for we rely upon the Zohar that adornment means students. This is not always available, and when students are present, in any event they sit around the person leading the blessing.
4) There: If it is clean. For example, if people did not drink from it during the meal and now, at the time of the blessing, it is found clean, rinsing inside and washing outside are unnecessary. Beis Yosef; Perishah, paragraph 2. The words of the Zohar and Tikkunei Zohar cited in the preceding paragraph, however, which give a mystical reason for rinsing and washing, imply that rinsing inside and washing outside specifically with water are required even if the cup is clean; see there.
5) There: It contains no remnants from cups, etc. This means remnants of wine in which bread soaked. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1, in the name of Rashi; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1. Wiping it with a cloth is likewise effective. Tur; Magen Avraham there; Eliyah Rabbah there. Eliyah Rabbah writes that it is proper to rinse the cup even when it contains no remnants of bread, unless it is clean and bright, because of the verse, 'Present it now to your governor.' We have already written in the preceding paragraph, however, that according to the Zohar it appears that rinsing is specifically required and must be done with water; see there. In pressing circumstances, when he has no water, one may say that wiping the inside and outside with a cloth suffices. Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 2; Yafeh LaLev, paragraph 1, writes the same.
