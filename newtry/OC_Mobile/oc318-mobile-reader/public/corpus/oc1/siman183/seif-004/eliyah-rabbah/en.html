[7] The left hand beneath, etc. Bach challenges Rama here, because Beit Yosef implies that the same rule applies if one holds the cup with his right hand around its middle and places the left hand beneath the cup. Until here are his words. In my humble opinion, Rama's view accords with Shelah, who writes: “I received a tradition to place the cup upon the palm of the right hand with the fingers upright, unlike those who hold the cup by wrapping their fingers around it.” According to this, Beit Yosef must mean that the left hand is placed beneath the right hand, not beneath the cup itself, for the right hand is already beneath the cup. Magen Avraham comments on the cited words of Shelah: “This is also implied by the Zohar, Bereishis. Nevertheless, it is not conclusive, for perhaps he surrounds it with his fingers in the ordinary manner of holding it.” Responsa Beis Yaakov, siman 174, however, writes that the common practice of grasping the sides of the cup is prohibited, because one's hand interposes between the wine and his hand. Rather, the cup should stand upon the five fingers, as stated in the writings of the Arizal, corresponding to the five leaves that surround it, or he should grasp the rim of the vessel. He brings proof from Tosafos, Sukkah 37b, regarding lulav. In truth, this is no proof, for one may say that when the Sages initially instituted blessing over a cup, they instituted that the cup itself should contain wine, and the law of interposition does not apply in this manner. Furthermore, Bach and Taz in siman 651 explain Tosafos to mean that one may hold it tightly, in which case this proof has no substance, contrary to Olas Tamid, who writes that Tosafos disagrees with Rama. Taz nevertheless questions Rama for ruling leniently like Shibolei HaLeket against the Tur. In my humble opinion, since the underlying law that one should not support the right hand with the left is an unresolved question in the Gemara, and I found it written that according to Shibolei HaLeket the issue arises only when the left hand touches the cup, this creates a double doubt, as is readily understood. Nevertheless, one should not be lenient except where necessary, as Tosafos writes concerning a large cup. The Shulchan Aruch of Rabbi Yitzchak Luria states that Bircas HaMazon should be recited with great joy. One should first say “Lamnatzeach bineginos, Elokim yechonenu,” and so forth; then “I will bless Hashem,” the verse “The end of the matter, all having been heard,” and so forth; then “The praises of Hashem,” and so forth, and “We will bless,” and so forth. He takes the cup in both hands and transfers it to the right in such a way that the cup rests upon his five fingers, raised upright, and he gazes at the cup throughout Bircas HaMazon. If he has no cup, he places his right hand over his left opposite his chest and closes his eyes until he completes all the blessings. He further writes that before placing the cup in his right hand, he should say, “And He spoke to me: This is the table that is before Hashem.”
