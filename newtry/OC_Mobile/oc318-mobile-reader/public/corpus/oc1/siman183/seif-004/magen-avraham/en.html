Beneath the right hand. Shibolei HaLeket, cited by Beit Yosef, writes: “It is permitted for the left hand to assist beneath the right hand, because we rule that assistance has no halachic substance.” When only the right hand touches the cup, placing the left beneath it to assist is permitted. This accords with Rama, unlike Bach, who understands Shibolei HaLeket to permit placing the left hand beneath the cup. It appears to me that Shibolei HaLeket is supported by Shabbos 93a, which says that if one received sacrificial blood with the right hand while the left assisted, it is valid because assistance has no substance. The Tur writes: “What is the law if the left assists the right, meaning supports it with the left?” It appears that the difficulty was: why was this even a question? If the left assists the right, he holds the cup with both hands, and we already said that he receives it with both hands but holds it with his right. The Tur therefore explains that the question concerned supporting the right hand with the left. Even this is forbidden, and one should therefore be stringent unless there is need, as Tosafos write regarding a large cup. Shelah writes in the name of the kabbalists that one should place the cup upon the palm of his right hand, with the fingers upright around it. This also appears from the Zohar to Bereishis, though it is not conclusive, for it may mean that the fingers surround it in the normal manner of holding. One should not hold the cup while wearing gloves, but should remove them first. Hagahos Maharam on Berachos.
Therefore one should not take, etc. Darchei Moshe gives another reason in the name of Rabbi Avraham of Prague: the Gemara says that Rava would drink the cup of blessing in large swallows, and if its mouth is narrow he cannot drink a large amount at once. See siman 472, seif 15. Maharsha explains the passage in Sukkah differently; see there. Rama’s reason is difficult: what proves that one must look at the wine? Perhaps it suffices to look at the cup so as not to divert attention from it. This is also implied by the Gemara’s wording.
