Seif katan 9: A left-handed person, etc. This matter is D’Rabbanan. It differs from tefillin, where the hand-tefillin must be placed on the left hand, and a left-handed person must place it on his own left, because tefillin is D’Oraisa. Taking the lulav in the right hand and the esrog in the left is D’Rabbanan. Since the lulav bundle contains three mitzvos, lulav, hadas, and aravah, it is held in the more important hand, and the ordinary right hand is sufficient. “Nevertheless, it appears to me that one should practice this way” means that although Magen Avraham challenged Beit Yosef’s rulings as inconsistent, and according to Beit Yosef’s ruling regarding lulav one should likewise take the cup in the ordinary right hand, in practice we follow Beit Yosef that a left-handed person takes it in his own right hand. Rama likewise rules regarding lulav that a left-handed person takes the lulav in his own right hand.
