(א) Close to his bed — see Mishna Berurah what we wrote in the name of Knesset HaGedolah, and see in Chayei Adam who tends to say that even if afterward he could not sleep at all, nevertheless the blessing is not void, for one blesses per the custom of the world; and so it appears in one answer in Eliyah Rabbah. And in my humble opinion this requires further study after this blessing blesses for himself "Who causes the bonds of sleep to fall upon my eyes" etc., and as the Sha'agat Aryeh wrote in siman 46 regarding the blessing of the One Who removes sleep when one does not sleep at night — see there. In any case it appears to me that if he is concerned that perhaps afterward he will not be able to sleep, certainly l'chatchila one should not bless. I was in doubt whether he is still before dawn at the time of the blessing of the One Who causes sleep, but he estimates that by the time he sleeps dawn will rise — do we follow the time of the blessing, when it is still night, or the sleep, which will be by day; and for daytime sleep this blessing was not enacted, as the poskim wrote. But after dawn has risen I have no doubt at all, for although there are people who sleep at that hour until sunrise and it is still time for lying down by constraint, as is stated in Berakhot 9, nevertheless regarding the blessing of the One Who causes sleep it appears that the time has passed, for one cannot say Hashkivenu after dawn, as is stated there, in the Gemara; and from this it follows in our eyes as well that one cannot say "that You lay me down in peace," for there is no longer the time of beginning lying down but only the end of lying down, as Rashi explains there.

(ב) And one does not bless etc. — for although they said it is a mitzvah to read it upon one's bed, nevertheless this is not in the category of a rabbinic mitzvah upon which one may bless; but regarding Keriat Shema in the morning and evening, those blessings count as if one blessed "who sanctified us with His commandments and commanded us to read Shema" (Rashba ben Rashbatz siman 74, and the Birkei Yosef brought it).
