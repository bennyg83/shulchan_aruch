Seif 1: He should recite, etc.; and one should not, etc.; and if he recited Shema, etc. The Jerusalem Talmud, first chapter, relates that when Rabbi Shmuel bar Nachman would descend to the ibbur, he would lodge near Rabbi Yaakov the blind. Rabbi Zeira would hide among the baskets in order to hear how he recited Shema. He heard him recite and repeat it until he sank into sleep. What is the reason? Rabbi Acha and Rabbi Tachlifa, his father in law, said in the name of Rabbi Shmuel bar Nachman: “Tremble, etc., and be still, Selah.” Although the Jerusalem Talmud states that Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi disagreed, because Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi recited Tehillim afterward, and we rule like Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi, we nevertheless learn from there that Shema must be close to sleep. The Tehillim are regarded as an extension of Shema.
One does not recite a blessing, etc. See Tosafos at the beginning of Berachos, at the end of the passage beginning “From when,” and Tosafos, Chullin 105a, at the end of the passage beginning “water, etc.”
