(א) First parasha. Rabbeinu Yonah wrote per the midrash that one should say any Keriat Shema that is 248 words to preserve his 248 limbs; and see above siman 235.

(ב) And he blesses the One Who causes sleep etc. It implies that the blessing of the One Who causes sleep one says after Shema so that the blessing should be adjacent to sleep, and so is the essential law as above; and the siddur of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch implies that after Shema one says the One Who causes sleep and afterward says "He who dwells in secret" etc. And it should be said that their reason is because Shema is not only for protection but because we derive from the verse "speak in your hearts" as is stated in the Gemara that words of Torah or a request must be adjacent to sleep; therefore the blessing of the One Who causes sleep must be before, so there not be an interruption between blessing and sleep; but "He who dwells in secret" and the others, which are for protection, are not an interruption. But the Rambam chapter 7 and Maggid Mishneh hold that one also says Shema at the end, and so too the Radak in the name of Rabbeinu Hai who holds that Shema is also for protection, and so too Tosafot at the beginning of Berakhot prove thus in the Gemara, and so too in the Yerushalmi. And in the Yerushalmi chapter 1 there is a dispute of R' Zeira, who would read and repeat and read — he holds one says Shema at the end; and R' Yehuda ben Levi holds one says psalms afterward. It turns out the Rambam and Maggid Mishneh hold like R' Zeira, and the Tur and Beit Yosef ruled like R' Yehuda ben Levi. If so, it is astonishing on the Rama who wrote "one reads and returns and reads" etc., for he is against the view of R' Yehuda ben Levi; and for practical halacha it appears to me the halacha is like R' Yehuda ben Levi as the Rif wrote in the chapter Bnei Ha'ir. And in truth the psalms are not an obligation at all, only Shema and the One Who causes sleep; only they practiced saying them since it appears R' Yehuda ben Levi said them — the custom to say them; and even R' Yehuda ben Levi said only the two psalms. And there is a practical difference regarding a sick person or one under duress; and regarding that women did not practice saying them — perhaps he holds that since it is practiced only at night they are exempt, for it is the community of Israel, as they are exempt from Keriat Shema.

(ג) Close to his bed. And it is written in Sedei Chemed that one should not read Keriat Shema when entering to sleep but when one sees that sleep has come upon him; and so he wrote that one should read immediately lest sleep seize him afterward and he not read; and one need not be concerned about interruption except when doing something else in between.

(ד) Sleep immediately. And if he needs to use his bed he should wash his hands and afterward read, and at minimum say the blessing of the One Who causes sleep and Shema after the use (Shelah and likewise in Knesset HaGedolah).

(ה) When he is lying. And this is specifically regarding obligatory Keriat Shema; but if he already read after tefillah and now reads upon his bed for protection, it is permitted to read while lying, for R' Zeira would read and repeat and read until he sank into his sleep.

(ו) Return and read. And it appears to me that the same applies if he is thinking in words of Torah (Maharshal), for we say in the Gemara a Torah scholar need not read Keriat Shema because he is accustomed always to repeat his learning — see there.

(ז) He who dwells in secret. And even though it is forbidden to heal oneself with words of Torah to protect so that no evil thing, Heaven forbid, come upon him — nevertheless this case is different. And b'dieved siman 179 seif 10: one who sleeps alone in a house — a demon seizes him at night (Shabbat 151b), and "house" means a room (Maharil). These require guarding from harmful forces: a sick person, a woman in childbirth, a groom, a bride, a mourner, and a Torah scholar at night (Berakhot 4a). There are those who practice saying the confession "before I lie down in bed" before sleep, and it appears to me this is not correct, for we say in Berakhot 60 that one should not say "if I die let my death be an atonement" etc. lest one open one's mouth to Satan; and so too 19 that one should not say "I have many sins before You and You have not collected from me one in a thousand" — implying as if he asks that He collect from him, and one would open one's mouth to Satan. And I say that there, since one bathes in a place of danger — night and sleep are also a place of danger, as R' Yosef wrote in siman 231 — therefore they told him only to search through his deeds that he did all day, and if he finds he committed a transgression he should confess upon it and accept upon himself not to do it again; and especially in common transgressions such as flattery, lies, mockery, lashon hara — these require extra scrutiny.
