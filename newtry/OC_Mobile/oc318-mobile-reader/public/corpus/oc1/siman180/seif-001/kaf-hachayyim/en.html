1) [Seif 1] One should not remove, etc. A blessing rests upon something only when an object is present, as in the account of Elisha and the Shunammite woman. Zohar, parashas Lech Lecha, 88; parashas Yisro, 86b; parashas Terumah, 157b; Magen Avraham, paragraph 1. Levush writes that the reason is so that it will be apparent to everyone that we bless Hashem, may He be exalted, for His great kindness and goodness in preparing food for all His creatures. See another reason below in paragraph 4.
2) There: One should not remove, etc. Some practice taking all the bread and the tablecloth and placing them before the person leading the blessing, so that it is visibly apparent that he is reciting the blessing over the bread. Tur and Bach. It appears that this applies specifically if everyone eats at one table, or if the small tables were removed from before each person and only the leader's table remains for everyone. If each person's table still remains before him, remnants of bread must be present upon every table so that a blessing can rest upon it, as stated above in the name of the Zohar.
3) There: One should not remove, etc. Some Kabbalists write that nothing should be removed from the table, even bones and peels, until after Birkas HaMazon, because the sparks of holiness attached to the bones and peels emerge through Birkas HaMazon. Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 1; Ben Ish Chai, parashas Shelach Lecha, paragraph 2. The salt likewise should not be removed from the table until after Birkas HaMazon, so that the table resembles the altar that atones, regarding which it is written, 'Upon every one of your offerings,' etc. Salt also protects from calamity. Kitzur Shelah; Beis Menuchah, paragraph 1. We have received a warning, Heaven forbid, not to remove anything from the table before Birkas HaMazon in order to feed an impure domesticated animal, wild animal, or bird. Chesed LaAlafim there; Ben Ish Chai there. See above, siman 167, paragraph 141.
