[2] A whole loaf, etc. Be’er Sheva, page 104, writes that the universal custom in Ashkenaz is to remove a whole loaf from the table during Bircas HaMazon, and Mahari Weil writes likewise. I am astonished, for the prohibition applies specifically when one brings a whole loaf after finishing the meal, because of those who “set a table for Gad.” If it was already on the table during the meal, however, it need not be removed, since he is not now setting it out. This is also stated in the Zohar, Parashas Terumah. Moreover, the plain language of the Gemara, “one who does not leave bread,” implies even a whole loaf. Thus far his words. Shach, at the end of Yoreh Deah 178, and all the later authorities followed him. In my humble opinion, Mahari Weil’s reasoning may be defended: it should be removed because people entering may think that it was placed there after the meal. One may also say that the term pas ordinarily means a piece, as in, “I will take a piece of bread,” and in Berachos 39b, “the blessing of Hamotzi should conclude together with the bread,” meaning when one slices the piece. See there. Rashi’s wording in the Gemara also says, “one who does not leave bread,” etc. Moreover, Mishmeres HaBayis and Shelah write that the language of the Gemara implies Mahari Weil’s ruling without qualification, and it is proper to be concerned for his view. As for the Zohar in Parashas Terumah, he apparently had in mind the statement: “The table should not be empty, for blessing is not found upon an empty table, as it is written, ‘Tell me what you have in the house,’ for supernal blessings rest only in a complete place.” He understood “a complete place” as a whole loaf. In my humble opinion, there is no proof, for it means only that the table should not be empty; a whole loaf is not required. This is why Shelah did not cite the Zohar.
[3] [Levush:] And it appears as though he is preparing, etc. Mahari Weil gives an additional reason: blessing is not found in a whole object, because it resembles something measured. Be’er Sheva writes that this is unconvincing, for if so, why do we recite Hamotzi over a whole loaf? In my humble opinion, there is no difficulty. For Hamotzi, one blesses Hashem over it, and a whole loaf is required because of “present it now to your governor,” as stated above in siman 177. When one leaves bread for Bircas HaMazon, however, the purpose is that the blessings and prayers we recite should rest upon it, namely that we should never lack food and that Hashem should sustain us honorably. As stated in Midrash HaNe’elam on Rus, the power of Bircas HaMazon is great, for it adds blessing to the work of one’s hands. The Zohar there therefore properly teaches that blessing does not rest upon something whole.
