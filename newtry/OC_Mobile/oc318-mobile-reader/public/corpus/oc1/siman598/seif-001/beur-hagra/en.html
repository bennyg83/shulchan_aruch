Seif 1, Rosh Hashanah that falls. For on days when Hallel is not said, it is not said, since it is an acceptance of the justice of the judgment over Moshe Rabbeinu, peace be upon him.
And some say. Since it is called a moed, as above.
And so we. For we do not say it on a day when Tachanun would not be said if it fell on a weekday, as the Tur wrote in siman 292 in the name of the Ashkenazic custom. Although the Shulchan Aruch also ruled so there, here he holds like the reason of the Tur here in the name of Rav Amram, that they say it because it is a day of judgment and it is fitting to say Tzidkascha. To me another reason appears: because three books are opened on Rosh Hashanah, and we say in Rosh Hashanah 17a, what does He do? Rabbi Eliezer says: He suppresses it, etc.; Rabbi Yehoshua ben Chananyah, etc. And we say in Arachin 8b: the plain meaning of the verse, etc.; regarding what do they disagree? Regarding Rabbi Eliezer and Rabbi Yehoshua ben Chananyah, as was stated, etc. But our custom is as above.
