One may not launder. So that one will launder before Yom Tov and not enter the moed while unkempt.
And hand towels. For even if one laundered them before the festival, they become dirty again (Tur). This implies that even if he has many, it is permitted; for otherwise it would be [the same law as] a shirt. And the reason is that they become dirty immediately, and one needs [a clean one] every single day. And if so, according to the practice of not changing them except from Shabbos to Shabbos, it is forbidden to launder them on the moed, for the laundering from before Yom Tov suffices for them.
One shirt. Since he stated it without qualification, we learn that he holds that even if it does not have its belt [proving he has only one], it is permitted; and even for us.
And in public, by the river. For they are not laundered as well at home as by the river. And it is difficult, for in the Tosefta it teaches explicitly that it is forbidden in public. And one can say that they explain that the Tosefta refers to a mourner (Beis Yosef; and see there). And this requires further study, for the wording of the Tosefta regarding haircutting and regarding laundry is the same; see there. And why do they explain regarding haircutting as referring to Chol HaMoed, as is written in siman 531, se'if 5, and regarding laundry they explain it as referring to a mourner? And in Darkei Moshe he wrote that the Tosefta deals with one who is not laundering by the river, as is written here in the gloss. And nevertheless this requires further study: from where did the Mordechai know to distinguish thus? For in the Gemara from which he brought a proof, it deals with linen garments, which are permitted for every person; but those which the Sages permitted, it is forbidden to launder in public, for not everyone knows that he was in an overseas land or in captivity. Therefore it appears to me that the Mordechai also refers to linen garments, and so it appears explicitly to one who looks there; and the same law applies to hand towels. But those which are taught in the Mishnah, one launders in private. And this is the wording of the Tosefta: All these regarding which they said one may launder, etc., one launders in private; this implies that it refers to those taught in the Mishnah. And that which is permitted: it implies that it refers to garments of small children. And it is difficult: how is this different from haircutting, where it is permitted to cut a child's hair in public, as is written in siman 538, se'if 6? And all the more so here, where there is an additional reason that he becomes dirty at every moment; and even for an adult it would be permitted in such a case, like hand towels. And it is possible that haircutting is different, for his hairs prove about him that they have grown long; but regarding laundry the matter is not proven, for one can say that they have more [garments] at home.
