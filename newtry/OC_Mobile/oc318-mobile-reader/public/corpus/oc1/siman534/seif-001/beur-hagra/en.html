Se'if 1: that they permitted, etc., and one who was released, etc. As I wrote above in siman 531, se'if 4.
And garments of small children. Yerushalmi: they asked before Rabbi Yosei, What is [the law of] garments of small children? He said to them: Like one who has only one shirt.
And a woman who has a stain. The Mishnah: and all who ascend from impurity to purity; meaning, in such a case.
And one who has only, etc. The Rif, Rambam, and Rosh omitted that [law of] "his belt proves about him"; and see Beis Yosef. But the Tur wrote: specifically [when] his belt, etc.; and so wrote the poskim. And the Semak forbids even in such a case, for among us there is no proving belt; and see Taz. And so wrote Tosafos in Taanis 29b, s.v. Mi, etc.
And all of them, etc. So it appears in the Yerushalmi there: it teaches, Cloths of books: one does not launder them in urine water, but one launders them in natron and soap, because of honor. And it appears [permitted] even on Chol HaMoed. And so it appears in the Gemara 17b, and in the Tosefta: All these regarding which they said one may launder on the moed are permitted, etc. And one must also say "when they press him," etc., as the Ramban and Tur Shulchan Aruch, Yoreh Deah, siman 389, wrote.
And in public, etc. From what is written there, 18b: "To me it was seen," etc.
However, not, etc. From what is written there: when he has two and they become dirty, [meaning] that both of them are filthy.
However, etc. So it appears in the aforementioned Yerushalmi; and the reason is that all of them are like one shirt.
And that which is permitted, etc. In the Tosefta at the beginning of chapter 2: All these regarding which they said one may cut hair on the moed are permitted to take a haircut within thirty days of mourning; even though they permitted him to take a haircut, he should not take a haircut in the public domain, but he takes a haircut in private inside his house. All these regarding which they said one may launder on the moed are permitted to launder within thirty [days] of mourning; even though they permitted him, he should not bring his vessels to a launderer, but he launders, and it is in private inside his house. And this contradicts what was written, that even in public it is permitted. And Beis Yosef answered that the Tosefta refers to a mourner; and so it is in the Mordechai and Haggahos Maimoniyos, who wrote after the Tosefta: and for the moed one does not require privacy, etc. And so wrote the Tur in Yoreh Deah, siman 389, regarding a mourner; and so wrote the Shulchan Aruch there. And regarding haircutting in siman 390, neither the Tur nor the Shulchan Aruch wrote [this]; and it is possible that they hold that it refers to Chol HaMoed, as I wrote above in siman 531. But regarding laundry it is impossible that it refers to Chol HaMoed, as above. But in the gloss he wrote there, and he follows his own method, that it refers, whether regarding haircutting or regarding laundry, to both of them; and he answered that the Tosefta is where one does not wash by the river, as I will write below: for in the Mordechai and Haggahos Maimoniyos they wrote the reason that it is permitted, that they are not laundered as well [elsewhere] as by the river; see there.
