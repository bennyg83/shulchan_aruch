(2) His wages. Likewise even if he has what to eat it is permitted to give him his wages — what prohibition is here? Rather the meaning is: even though he has nothing to eat, he must give his wages; one might have thought permitted to take them so he not claim again — it teaches us he gives wages and leaves them with him; if he does not trust him he leaves them, etc.; but if he has what to eat he should not give wages and may not turn them to the adjacent house. It appears difficult: if he has nothing to eat why not bring them publicly, for he may do melacha as siman 542 seif 18? One may say nevertheless forbidden, for an observer will not know he has nothing to eat; therefore all the more forbidden to do melacha publicly — Magen Avraham; see responsum Sha'ar Efrayim siman 36 forbidding playing instruments for a Jew on chol hamoed:
