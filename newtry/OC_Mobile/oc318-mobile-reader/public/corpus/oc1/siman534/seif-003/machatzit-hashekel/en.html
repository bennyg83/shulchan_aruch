(sk 3) One garment, etc. — even with its belt it is permitted, even for us — meaning in Moed Katan daf 14 R' Yehudah permits one who has only one garment to launder it on the moed; and it challenges: is the matter not evident, and there is concern for marit ayin? And it answers: Mar bar Rav Ashi — his belt proves about him; and so in Chullin. And Rashi in Chullin that the belt is always fixed to his garment; and when he removes the garment and wears another garment he takes the belt from this one and fixes it on that one; and this is because he has no other garment — he launders it with its belt, and the viewer when he launders it and its belt is with him knows he has no other garment — end quote. And accordingly, in our garments where the belt is not fixed in them it would be forbidden; except we rule like Rashi's explanation in the first chapter on this that "his belt proves about him" — one who has only one garment, when he removes it to launder he wraps himself in his garment and girds the belt on the garment; and through this everyone knows when he launders it that he has only one garment, for they see the belt girded on his garment; and accordingly for us too it is permitted. Except Rabbeinu Yehudah wrote that according to this explanation it would have been forbidden for another person to launder the garment, even his wife — for if another launders it and they do not see him girded with a belt on his garment there is marit ayin since the matter is not evident; also if someone lends him a garment only until after the laundering — then he does not gird his belt on his garment — it would also have been fitting to forbid; except since both are uncommon — presumably one who has only one garment has no one to use, and he must launder it himself; and likewise it is uncommon that someone lends him a garment specifically only until after laundering and not more — therefore it was permitted in every case, even if another lends him a garment only after laundering or another launders it and not he himself, since it is uncommon the Sages were not stringent. Also Rabbeinu Yehudah wrote initially that perhaps in those two cases where another launders or someone lends a garment — when the belt is not seen on his garment — in truth it is forbidden; however, since Shulchan Aruch is silent it implies even for us and even when the garment is not seen on him in his garment — meaning in one of the two manners above — nevertheless it is permitted because it is uncommon — as above.
