(12) It is permitted to launder them. For they readily become dirty; and even if one had laundered them before the moed; and also there is not so much toil in laundering them.
(13) And these are things that are permitted, etc. See Magen Avraham.
(14) You are not, etc. And nevertheless, since according to the primary law it is permitted, the custom spread to be lenient to give to a non-Jew to launder one tunic, and outside the house; for even though everything that is forbidden is also forbidden through a non-Jew, nevertheless in this they are lenient [Eliyah Rabbah in the name of Prishah, and it is brought in Chayei Adam]. And to give to the non-Jew that he should launder after the moed is in every case permitted, provided that one not count [the garments] in the manner that he does on a weekday, as is written at the end of siman 543.
