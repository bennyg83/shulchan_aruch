(1) If one drank wine—it appears that the same applies to other intoxicating beverages, as the Taz wrote below in siman 128 seif kaf lamed heh regarding lifting of hands; and even per Magen Avraham there, who is lenient regarding lifting of hands, it is reasonable that prayer is stricter and forbidden as with wine—so too in Magen Giborim, see there. And per the Taz below cited, we do not require a revi'it measure for other beverages, see there; but to me it appears that nevertheless we require that there be enough in it to intoxicate in a revi'it of wine.

(2) As much as a revi'it—specifically if they drank in one gulp; but in two times, or if they mixed in a little water, it is permitted. And if one drank more than a revi'it in any manner, it is forbidden until the wine passes from him, as below in siman 128 seif 38. And all this is when he can speak before the king; for otherwise it is not relevant to our topic, for we deal with prayer—there is no distinction in this. The Magen Avraham wrote in the name of Semak that wine within a meal does not intoxicate—see further there; and the Peri Megadim wrote that all this is according to what one understands in his mind.

(3) He may not pray—even if by this the time for prayer will pass, Peri Chadash. And see at the end of the siman in the name of Chokhmas Shlomo that nowadays, when in any event we are not so careful in intent during prayer, one need not be so concerned to be strict if by waiting the time for prayer will pass.

(4) Until his wine passes—even if he is accustomed to drinking a lot and is not drunk at all from a revi'it, nevertheless he may not pray ab initio if he drank a revi'it of wine [Birkhei Yosef].

(5) An abomination—and it is as if he worshipped idolatry; and if he does not pray at the time of intoxication he is saved from every trouble. Shas.

(6) Like the law of one who acts unintentionally—even if he began drinking after its time arrived when he thought he would have time to pray after the little wine he began drinking would pass, with settled intent, and afterward his heart was drawn to the feast and he forgot about prayer and became so drunk that no time remained for him to weaken his wine before the time for prayer passed—he is also called one who acts unintentionally. However, if he is close to Minchah and the time will certainly pass, and if he will certainly become drunk such that he cannot weaken his wine in such a short time, he is an intentional transgressor according to all views and has no compensation [prayer to make up].

(7) And the law of Keriat Shema—and the blessings of Keriat Shema, their law is also like Keriat Shema. So wrote the Rama.

(8) Like the law of prayer—meaning that even one who has drunk moderately is forbidden ab initio in Keriat Shema. And the Levush wrote that there are those who are lenient in Keriat Shema even if he is drunk, for prayer is different in that it is a request for one's needs—and similarly in Likutei HaRamban at its end. However, in Biur HaGra he expanded to explain the words of the Yerushalmi that exist on this matter; and according to his words the conclusion of the Yerushalmi is to forbid a drunk person in Keriat Shema; therefore one should be careful ab initio not to come to this. And after the fact, if he became drunk, he may not exempt himself from Keriat Shema on account of this—and as the Magen Avraham wrote below in siman 185 regarding Birkas HaMazon. And if his wine will pass before the time for Keriat Shema passes, he should return and recite the paragraphs.

(9) But other blessings—the rule here applies even to Birkas HaMazon, which is from the Torah; and see below in siman 185 and what I wrote there.

(10) He may recite a blessing—but they do not count him toward a minyan of ten; and regarding joining three for zimmun, it is possible that it is permitted.

(11) That he is drunk—see Biur HaGra; and according to his words one should be strict ab initio regarding a drunk person who cannot speak before the king, even for other blessings—and similarly the Peri Megadim, Yoreh De'ah siman 1 seif kaf lamed heh in Mishbetzos Zahav. And all this is when he has not reached the drunkenness of Lot; for otherwise he is considered completely like a fool according to all views and is then exempt from all mitzvos; therefore even after the fact, if he recited then and blessed, he is not exempt thereby, for he was exempt at that hour, and he is obligated afterward to return and recite and bless. Peri Chadash and Peri Megadim.
