In the author, seif 2: But riding. NB: See Magen Avraham, Taz, Peri Chadash, and Chemed Moshe; and in my view it is also difficult—what is the reason Rambam omitted in chapter 1 of Laws of Entering the Sanctuary this matter of Italian wine? Yet Rambam brings all the laws; therefore it seems both answers of Bavli are difficult. On the first answer it is difficult: if one holds Italian wine intoxicates more, then what is the reason he said we learned from him that a revi'it of Italian wine intoxicates—for even ordinary wine also intoxicates, and he should have said it intoxicates more. On the second answer it is difficult: if one holds Italian wine equals other wine, only riding is different for it reduces, as written in Chemed Moshe—then what is the reason Rabban Gamliel said, did we drink nothing but a revi'it of Italian wine? And why did he specify Italian specifically—what difference to me whether it is this or another wine? Therefore it seems in my view, per the introduction I say: from reasoning that what we hold in Kerisos—that if he paused in it and did not drink at one time he is not intoxicated, as Magen Avraham wrote seif kaf-alef—that is for the matter of a revi'it; but if he already drank a precise revi'it at one time, what he drinks further combines to be judged as more than a revi'it; and see below siman 128 seif 38. But if he did not drink at one time at all, then even if he drank several half-revi'iot they do not combine to judge him as more than a revi'it; and see Kesef Mishneh chapter 1 of Laws of Entering the Sanctuary halacha 5, who distinguished likewise regarding dilution, end matter. And now it seems to me that Rabban Gamliel, who was precise to say Italian wine, can be explained in two ways: either because it intoxicates more and he wished to teach students that for this reason it requires more than a mil; and according to this it will be strained in the wording of the braita "we learned from him that a revi'it of Italian wine intoxicates"—he should have said it intoxicates more; or we may say Rabban Gamliel drank other wine besides, in which there was no doubt that certainly there was not a revi'it, but in the cup of Italian wine there was doubt whether there was a revi'it in it—and he drank at one time, and the others are found to combine to be judged as one who drinks more than a revi'it, for a mil does not suffice for him; and they spoke well, "we learned from him that a revi'it of Italian wine intoxicates" in any case—for if not so, the others would not combine for more than a revi'it. But if it does not intoxicate more, possibly it also reduces, since he needed to teach this from him. And now when the questioner challenged from this braita, he could answer that Rabban Gamliel drank more than a revi'it—except then the matter of the road disturbing him would be difficult; therefore to guard from this he entered a strained path to say Italian wine intoxicates more and he did not drink more than a revi'it, and he would hold that for this matter it is not stringent that the road should disturb him, since in any case he did not drink more than a revi'it. And again when he challenged that then in more than a revi'it too the road would disturb him, he had to say a rider is like one sitting in his house—each waits until his wine is dispelled according to his measure; and he concludes that now one need not distinguish between Italian wine and other wines, but Rabban Gamliel drank more than a revi'it and rode and it does not disturb him. And for this reason Rif and Rambam omitted the stringency of Italian; also that riding is like one sitting in his house—both to be lenient that it does not disturb him for more than a revi'it, and also to be stringent that walking a mil does not help when he drank a revi'it. And from Rabban Gamliel's precise wording, who said "until his wine is dispelled" and did not state any measure—it implies each estimates for himself. And Rosh's and Rambam's ruling fits well; however the author of Halachot Gedolot, Tur, and Shulchan Aruch must strain themselves to say "but riding"—certainly the road dispels wine, and he did not wish to say it does not disturb him like walking, and automatically it dispels over the length of time, as Magen Avraham wrote.
