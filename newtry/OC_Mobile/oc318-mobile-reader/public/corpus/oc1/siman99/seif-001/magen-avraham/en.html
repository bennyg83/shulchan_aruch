The measure of a revi'is. See siman 128, seif 38, that this applies specifically when he drank it at one time. Semak, siman 133, writes that wine drunk during a meal does not intoxicate. See siman 473, seif 3, where some say that even wine drunk before food does not intoxicate. Hagahos Semak writes that one who ate a festive meal, such as a Yom Tov meal, may not issue a halachic ruling even if he drank only during the meal.
His prayer is an abomination. It is as though he worshipped idolatry. Gemara, Berachos, beginning of chapter 5. If he refrains from praying while intoxicated, he is saved from every trouble. Gemara.
As in the case of an inadvertent omission. Beit Yosef discusses several distinctions at length. It is difficult why he recorded none of them here in Shulchan Aruch. It therefore appears that he retracted them. His view is that Rabban Gamliel's sons were not intoxicated at all, but were drawn after the feast and remained there. They were therefore not compelled. An intoxicated person, however, is considered compelled because he did not know to take care and recite, all the more so according to the view that an intoxicated person may not recite Krias Shema. Regarding prayer, all agree that he may not pray. He is therefore compelled. Even if he began drinking after the time arrived, because he thought there would still be time afterward, he is considered compelled, as stated in siman 108, seif 8. See Bach and Beit Yosef, and see siman 89, seif 5.
