As much as a revi'it. See siman 128 seif 38—that specifically when they drank in one gulp; Semak siman 133 wrote that wine within a meal does not intoxicate; and see siman 173 seif 3, that even before the meal it does not intoxicate; and in the gloss on Semak: if he ate a joyful meal such as a Yom Tov meal, even though he drank only at the meal, one should not rule thus.

His prayer is an abomination—and it is as if he worshipped idolatry [gemara Berachos 65a], and if he does not pray at the time of intoxication he is saved from every trouble [gemara].

Like the law of one who acts unintentionally. Rabbenu Yonah elaborated to distinguish, and there is a question why he did not write this here in the Shulchan Aruch; therefore it seems he retracted. And his view is that the sons of Rabban Gamliel were not drunk at all, but rather their hearts were drawn after the feast where they were sitting; and therefore they are not under duress. But one who is drunk is considered under duress in that he did not know to be careful and recite—and all the more so per the view that forbids reciting. And regarding prayer, all agree it is forbidden and it is duress. And even if he began to drink after its time arrived when he thought he would have time afterward, it is called duress, as written in siman 108 seif 8, Bach, and Beit Yosef on siman 89 seif 5.
