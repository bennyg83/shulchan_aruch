See Magen Avraham, seif katan 2, who answers that Rabban Gamliel's sons were not intoxicated. Darchei Moshe gives the same answer. Bach and Magen Avraham also write this, apparently without having seen Darchei Moshe, which was not always available to them. In my humble view, several observations arise here, which I will record.
What Beit Yosef wrote. Beit Yosef asks from Rabban Gamliel's sons: if dawn had not risen they were obligated, implying that after dawn they were not, although we rule that in pressing circumstances the opinion of Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel may be relied upon even after dawn. If one says that Krias Shema may be made up without its blessings, as written in siman 58, see Magen Avraham there, seif katan 8, then apparently they would necessarily still be obligated in the blessings. Moreover, Rabban Gamliel's sons presumably wished to recite Krias Shema in its normal form, with its blessings, not merely to recite the verses without them. I discussed this at length in my novellae. Since reciting an unnecessary blessing may violate 'You shall not take' on a Torah level, as Magen Avraham writes in siman 216, seif katan 6, Rabban Gamliel's sons may have assumed that the law follows the majority against an individual. In a Torah matter, Yoreh Deah 242 teaches that even pressing circumstances do not permit following the individual against the majority, except where the dispute is between a minor and a greater authority or concerns a Rabbinic matter. This is clear from Shach's rules there, especially s.v. od nireh and s.v. vadai be'issur Torah, and from his abbreviated rules, rule 2. According to their understanding, they properly asked, and he answered that if dawn had not risen they were obligated; if it had risen they were not obligated to recite with the blessings, because it would be an individual view against the majority. This cannot be correct, however, because siman 235, seif 4, rules concerning the other blessings, apart from Hashkiveinu, in accordance with the individual view against the majority. There is also another difficulty. According to the view in siman 58 that Krias Shema has compensation like prayer, what does the phrase 'if dawn had not risen, you are obligated to recite' mean? When would they be exempt? If dawn had risen, they would still be obligated by way of compensation, and according to that opinion compensation is certainly obligatory. Perhaps they were two individuals who wished to sleep and recite later. They thought the law followed the Sages and that the time had passed. Rabban Gamliel told them that if dawn had not risen they were obligated to recite immediately. According to this, there were no pressing circumstances, for why should they wait until after dawn? Beit Yosef's question would thus be resolved, but he apparently did not accept this. Alternatively, 'obligated' means that after dawn they were exempt because they were considered deliberate, and compensation applies only to inadvertence, not to deliberate omission, as in siman 108. If there are three categories, inadvertent, compelled, and deliberate, Rabban Gamliel's sons were not willful sinners, Heaven forbid, but perhaps were inadvertent in a manner close to deliberateness, as Taz writes in siman 235, seif katan 4. The ruling that Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel may be relied upon in pressing circumstances applies only to genuine compulsion, not to inadvertence close to deliberateness. For compensation, however, every inadvertent omission may be made up; only deliberate omission may not. The implication that if dawn rose they were exempt because they were not compelled would still be difficult, since compensation should obligate them. One might answer that after dawn they must wait until they can recognize an acquaintance from four amos away and must first pray Shacharis; see Rambam, Hilchos Tefillah 3:9, Kesef Mishneh there, and siman 233. Understand this, and there is no need to elaborate. With Hashem's help, this will be discussed further in Magen Avraham, seif katan 2.
