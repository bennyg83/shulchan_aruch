Taz, Beit Yosef—he elaborated between the pair of scholars (Berachos 9a) and the sons of Rabban Gamliel in several distinctions; and Magen Avraham (2)—and this is his explanation that the sons of Rabban Gamliel were not drunk, etc.; and similarly in Darkei Moshe this explanation; and Bach on siman 235; and similarly Bach and Magen Avraham—and they did not see Darkei Moshe because the edition was not always complete. And for my poor [understanding] there are here some thoughts; I will write them here.

What the Beit Yosef brought from the sons of Rabban Gamliel: "If dawn has not risen you are obligated" etc.—if dawn has risen, no; for we rule like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel in a time of constraint after dawn. Now, if we say that Keriat Shema has completion [make-up] and without blessings, as in siman 58 (Magen Avraham letter ches and what he wrote there), it would seem they must be obligated in the blessing. And furthermore, presumably the sons of Rabban Gamliel wanted to recite and not exempt themselves and recite thus; rather they spoke with a blessing (in our novellae we elaborated on this). And we say "you shall not bear" is a Torah prohibition, as in siman 296 in Magen Avraham letter vav—if so, the sons of Rabban Gamliel held: individual against many—the halachah follows the many; and regarding Torah law we rule in Yoreh De'ah 242 that even a time of constraint does not help except small against great, or individual against many of rabbinic law—not regarding Torah law, as the viewer will see in the rules of Orach Chayyim there in Shach s.v. od nireh mevuar and s.v. vadai be'issur Torah; and in brief the rules of Orach Chayyim letter bet, see there—if so, per their view it is fine that they asked and he answered them: if dawn has not risen, if dawn has risen they are not obligated in a blessing—individual against many. But this is what Rabbenu Levi ben Chaviv's view explains clearly: other blessings besides HaMapil—individual against many. One may also see per the view in siman 58 that Keriat Shema has completion like prayer—if so, what is the language "if dawn has not risen you are obligated to recite," and when are they exempt? For if dawn has risen they are obligated from completion (for certainly per the view that has completion he is obligated)—rather they were drunk and wanted to sleep and afterward recite, for they held the halachah follows the Sages; the time passed and he answered: if dawn has not risen you are obligated now to recite immediately. And therefore there is no time of constraint here, and why should they wait until after dawn? And the Beit Yosef's difficulty is resolved—but he is not pleased with this, and they are obligated; but after dawn they are exempt, for they are intentional; and completion is specifically for one who acts unintentionally, not for one who acts intentionally, as in siman 108:107. And if we say the three distinctions—unintentional, duress, intentional—and the sons of Rabban Gamliel were not, heaven forbid, negligent but unintentional somewhat close to intentional, as the Taz wrote on siman 235 letter dalet; and the halachah follows Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel in a time of constraint only for one fully under duress—whereas one who acts unintentionally close to intentional, no. And regarding completion: every unintentional actor completes, and only the intentional actor does not—if so, the matter that if dawn has not risen they are obligated, if not they are exempt because they are not under duress—nevertheless there is a difficulty from the reason of completion that they are obligated. And to say: after dawn one must wait until he recognizes his fellow from afar, etc., and to pray Shacharis early—see Rambam chapter 3 of Hilchos Tefillah halachah tet; one prays Minchah twice, and in many places there and in siman 233—and understand this; and it cannot be extended; and with God's help in Magen Avraham letter bet it will be explained further.
