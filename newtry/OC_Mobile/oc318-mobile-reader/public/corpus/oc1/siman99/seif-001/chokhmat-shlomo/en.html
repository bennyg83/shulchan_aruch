Seif 1: If he drank wine etc. NB: We have the reading in chapter 6 of Eruvin (page 65a): Rabbi Chanina said, anyone who dispels [drunkenness] from a shield at the time of pride etc., until the end—what is between them? If so, Rabbi Shimon—Rabbi Shimon entrusted his sleep to Shemaya; the master has Rabbi Shimon and the master does not have Rabbi Shimon, end matter. And Rashi explained there that for the view that says he needs to pass it through, he does not have Rabbi Shimon and it is forbidden to pray until he sobers on his own, end quote. And this lacks understanding—for why for the view that says he needs to pass it through is it forbidden to awaken him, and for the view that says anyone who does not reveal himself is permitted to awaken him? And the intent seems to be: this is known—that positive commandment is preferable to negative commandment, and therefore a positive commandment overrides a negative commandment; and if so, likewise for the view that holds the wording is "to pass through," it is proven that what he does not pray at the time of drunkenness is considered for him as performing a mitzvah in actuality; and accordingly they said: one who sits and does not transgress—they give him reward as one who performs a mitzvah. And if so, likewise for the view that holds the wording is "to pass through," it is proven he is considered as performing an act as in actuality; if so, in this sleep that comes to pass the drunkenness through, he is considered as performing a mitzvah—and what difference to me this or what he prays; and therefore this does not override that, therefore he need not be awakened. But for the view that holds the wording is "to reveal"—meaning he does not reveal his prayer—it is proven he is not considered except for sitting and not transgressing that he is not committing a sin, but he is not considered for rising and doing; therefore it is better to awaken him to pray, for it is rising and doing and overrides sitting and not transgressing—and this fits well. However it is difficult to me why the poskim did not mention this law—whether they hold like Rashi or like Rabbeinu Tam's explanation of the difference what is better if he sleeps or goes on the road; in any case they should have explained this law according to whom they hold, and it requires examination—examine well.
