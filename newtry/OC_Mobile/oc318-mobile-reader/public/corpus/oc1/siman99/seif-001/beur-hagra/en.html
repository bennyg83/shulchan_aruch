Even if. The proof that this is treated as a genuine unavoidable circumstance is from the incident there on 9a concerning that pair of scholars. One who acted deliberately may not pray after dawn, as Rif and the other poskim write there.
But. See the Yerushalmi at the beginning of Terumos, cited by Tosafos there, s.v. shikor, and by Rosh in Berachos, chapter 5, siman 9: 'One who has drunk should not pray. Who is considered one who has drunk? One who drank a revi'is. Who is considered intoxicated? One who drank more.' There they said: anyone unable to speak before a king. Rabbi Zeira asked Rabbi Abahu whether an intoxicated person may recite a blessing. He replied: 'You shall eat, be satisfied, and bless,' even when befuddled. The remaining question was whether he may recite Krias Shema. This is the basis for the statement that the law of Krias Shema follows prayer stringently. It appears, however, that the Yerushalmi contains a scribal error. It later relates that a pious man asked Eliyahu whether one who is naked may recite Krias Shema, and he answered from 'He shall not see in you a naked thing,' interpreting it as naked speech. Chizkiyah taught that this applies both to reciting Krias Shema and to blessings. This is incomprehensible, since even terumah may not be separated in such a state because of the blessing, and likewise one may not recite opposite another person's nakedness. The phrase 'naked speech' is also obscure. The text should therefore read 'whether an intoxicated person may recite,' and so Rash in the first chapter of Terumos cites it without the word 'naked.' It refers throughout to an intoxicated person. Chizkiyah disputes the prior ruling and maintains that both Krias Shema and blessings are forbidden, also disagreeing with Rabbi Asi's derivation from 'You shall eat.'
