[2] As in the case of an inadvertent omission. This is when they began drinking before the time for prayer. If he began after the time arrived, or even shortly beforehand, it is deliberate and has no compensatory prayer. Levush. Nachalas Tzvi writes that this contradicts Levush's ruling below, siman 108, seif 8, that even if he began after the time arrived he is deemed compelled. I found the same difficulty in Lechem Chamudos, page 40, and Beis Yaakov responsum 70 overlooked their words. In my humble opinion, the case below is where he began before the time arrived, but after the time arrived he could easily have stopped and prayed and failed to do so, even though he saw that the activity might continue beyond the time. Nevertheless, he is deemed compelled because he began before the time arrived. This is also implied by the wording there, 'because he is occupied with the matter until he finishes that in which he is engaged,' which indicates that he was already engaged in it. Although Beit Yosef writes that the words of Nimukei Yosef, which are the basis of Shulchan Aruch and Levush in siman 108, prove that he is compelled, his proof is from the law that one should not sit before a barber close to Minchah, implying that even one who began close to the time is included. Shulchan Aruch, however, may hold that only one who had already begun is compelled, not one who began after the time arrived. Nimukei Yosef can also be interpreted this way. Examine this carefully.
