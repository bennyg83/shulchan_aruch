(2) As the law for one unwitting etc. And this is when they began drinking before the time for prayer; but if he began after the time for prayer, or even close to it, he is intentional and has no compensatory prayer (Levush). And Nachalat Tzvi wrote that his words contradict what is below in siman 108 seif 8, where he ruled that even if he began after the time arrived he is under compulsion—until here; and likewise I saw this wonder in Lechem Chamudot page 40, and in the responsum of Beit Yaakov siman 70 their words were omitted. And in my humble opinion, below deals with one who began before the time arrived, but after the time arrived it was possible for him to stop and pray easily and he did not pray—even though he saw reason to fear the matter would drag until after the time, even so he is called under compulsion since he began before the time arrived; and likewise the wording there implies: because he is occupied in the matter etc. after he finishes it, which he is engaged in—implies he was already engaged. And even though Beit Yosef wrote that the words of Nimukey Yosef (which are the words of Shulchan Aruch and Levush in siman 108) prove he is under compulsion—from what Beit Yosef brought in siman 108, that he brought proof that a person should not sit before a book close to Minchah—implies it deals even when he began close to the time; but in Shulchan Aruch it is possible his view is that if he already began, that is when he is under compulsion, not when he began after the time arrived; also the words of Nimukey Yosef can be explained in this manner, and examine well.
