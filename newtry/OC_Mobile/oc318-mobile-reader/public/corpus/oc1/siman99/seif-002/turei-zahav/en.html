But one who is riding. Tur writes this in the name of Halachos Gedolos. It may be challenged from the discussion in Eruvin. Rami bar Abba said that walking a mil and a little sleep dissipate wine. Rav Nachman said this applies only when one drank a revi'is, but if he drank more than a revi'is, travel disturbs him and sleep further intoxicates him. The Gemara asks whether walking a mil dissipates wine from the incident of Rabban Gamliel, who was riding and, upon reaching Keziv, was approached regarding a vow. He asked his companion whether they had drunk a revi'is of Italian wine. When told that they had, he said that the questioner should walk after them until their wine dissipated, and they traveled three mil. The Gemara first answers that Italian wine is more intoxicating. It then asks from Rav Nachman's distinction and answers that riding is different, for riding entails no exertion. It concludes that this also resolves the challenge to Rami bar Abba: riding is different and therefore dissipates wine less. We thus learn that riding entails both a stringency and a leniency. After a revi'is, riding is more stringent because one mil does not suffice and more travel is needed. After more than a revi'is, riding is more lenient, because walking does not help at all, while riding does. Hagahos Ashri states this explicitly. Tur is therefore difficult in two respects. First, he should have written that after a revi'is, one mil does not suffice when riding. Second, after more than a revi'is he should have written that three mil are required when riding, rather than stating generally that travel dissipates the wine. Rif and Rosh are even more difficult, for they omit the entire distinction between riding and walking. They record only the difference between a revi'is and more than a revi'is, ruling that one mil suffices for a revi'is but not for more, and say nothing of riding. It appears that Halachos Gedolos holds that although the Gemara's final approach no longer needs the answer that Italian wine is more intoxicating, Rabban Gamliel's own words still prove a distinction, because he specified Italian wine rather than wine generally. Italian wine is therefore in fact more severe, although the Gemara says only that this distinction is unnecessary to resolve Rami bar Abba. Since Italian wine may intoxicate more than even more than a revi'is of ordinary wine, and since riding is more effective than walking, we may say that Rabban Gamliel required three mil only for Italian wine. For ordinary wine, even more than a revi'is, reason suggests that one mil while riding suffices. Although this is not fully established, the matter is Rabbinic, since after the fact one who prayed after merely drinking has discharged his obligation, and we therefore rule leniently that one mil of riding suffices even after more than a revi'is. This is the view of Halachos Gedolos. Rif and Rosh, however, rule stringently in this uncertainty. Since the status of Italian wine is unclear, they apply a uniform rule that after more than a revi'is riding does not help, nor does travel, just as with walking. They did not state that three mil of riding suffice after more than a revi'is, because that is not fixed: if one drank far more than a revi'is, the condition may be worse than after Italian wine, and three mil may not suffice. Italian wine, by contrast, involved the fixed amount of a revi'is and was seen to dissipate after three mil. The poskim omit the law of Italian wine entirely because it is not common among us. This appears correct.
