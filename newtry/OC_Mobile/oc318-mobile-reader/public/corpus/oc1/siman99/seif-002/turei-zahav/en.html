But riding, etc.—so too in the Tur in the name of Halakhot Gedolot; and one may say on this from the passage in chapter HaDar, for thus it says: Rami bar Abba said—traveling a mil and sleep all the more weaken the wine. R' Nachman said: it is not so, except that he drank as much as a revi'it; but if he drank more than a revi'it, all the more so that traveling agitates him and sleep increases his intoxication. And they challenged: traveling a mil weakens the wine. And it was taught: an incident with Rabban Gamliel who was riding, etc.—when he reached Keziv, one came to ask about his vow; he said to the one with him: "Did we drink any revi'it of Italian wine?" He said to him: "Yes." He said to him: "If so, walk behind us until our wine passes." And he walked behind them three mil, etc. And they answered: Italian wine is different, for it intoxicates more. And R' Nachmani said: it is not so, except that he drank, etc. And they answered: riding is different—Rashi explained that he has no exertion of the road. Now that we have come to this—for Rami bar Abba too there is no difficulty: riding is different—Rashi explained for Rami bar Abba, who said traveling a mil; and here it taught three mil—there is no difficulty, for riding is different in that he has no exertion; therefore it does not weaken so much. Therefore we learn that in riding there is leniency and stringency—namely, if he drank a revi'it there is stringency in riding, for the measure of a mil is not sufficient but more is required; and this is what is said in the gemara in the conclusion: riding is different. And if he drank more than a revi'it there is leniency in riding, for while walking the way does not help at all, but while riding it helps—and this is what is said in the gemara first: riding is different; and similarly explicitly in Hagahos Maimonios there. And if so, there is a difficulty on the Tur—two difficulties: one, that accordingly he should have written that for a revi'it the measure of a mil does not help while riding; second, that for more than a revi'it he should have written that riding requires three mil—and why did he write simply in this that traveling weakens? And on the Rif and Rosh one should wonder more, for they did not write this distinction between riding and walking on foot, but only wrote a distinction between a revi'it and more—for a revi'it a mil suffices by traveling, and for more than a revi'it it does not suffice; and they did not mention anything of riding. And it appears to my mind that Halakhot Gedolot holds that even though per what we now hold, etc., we need not say Italian wine is different because it intoxicates more—nevertheless it is proven from the words of Rabban Gamliel in the baraita that there is a distinction here, for Rabban Gamliel said: "Did we drink any revi'it of Italian wine?" and did not say simply wine; but certainly Italian wine is stricter in truth—only the Talmud said that for the difficulty on Rami bar Abba we do not need this. Nevertheless, since in truth Italian wine is stricter, it is possible that it intoxicates more than more than a revi'it of plain wine—and so is the language of the Talmud, which said that it intoxicates more, and this includes even more than a revi'it. And if so, we may say: since there is superiority in riding more than walking, one may say that specifically for Italian wine Rabban Gamliel required three mil while riding; but for plain wine, even with more than a revi'it and riding, reason dictates that a mil suffices since he is riding. And even though the matter is not so clear, since it is a rabbinic doubt—for after the fact one fulfills with sleep—we therefore go to be lenient, and a mil while riding suffices even with more than a revi'it; this is the view of Halakhot Gedolot. But the Rif and Rosh hold that in this doubt we go to be strict, since the matter of Italian wine was not clarified for us; we go to be strict and grasp because of "do not distinguish"—in all cases of more than a revi'it, neither riding nor traveling helps as with walking. And that which they did not write that more than a revi'it helps while riding with three mil as with Italian wine—one may say that more than a revi'it was not a settled matter, for certainly if he drank a great deal, much more than a revi'it, it is stricter than Italian wine; therefore it is not relevant to minimize here that three mil help, unlike Italian wine, which is a fixed matter in a revi'it and it is seen in it that three mil help in it. And the decisors did not mention at all the law of Italian wine, which is not found at all among us, as above.
