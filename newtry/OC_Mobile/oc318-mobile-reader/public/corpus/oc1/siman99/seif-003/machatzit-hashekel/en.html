(Seif katan 3) As in the case of an inadvertent omission. Beit Yosef discusses at length how an intoxicated person can be deemed inadvertent. The Mishnah at the beginning of Berachos relates that Rabban Gamliel's sons returned from a feast after midnight and said that they had not recited Krias Shema. He told them that if dawn had not risen they were still obligated to recite it. If intoxication is considered inadvertence and compulsion, then even after dawn they could recite until sunrise, as above, siman 58, seif 5. He answers that Rabban Gamliel's sons had attended an optional feast, which is not considered compulsion because they should not have remained there, whereas our case concerns a mitzvah feast, which is considered compulsion. He further answers that specifically regarding Krias Shema it is not considered compulsion, because they could have recited before sunrise even while intoxicated, according to the view that an intoxicated person may recite Krias Shema. Even according to the view that he may not, one who has merely drunk may recite, and they should have done so before becoming fully intoxicated. This does not accord with Rama's gloss, which appears to equate Krias Shema and prayer in every respect. Prayer, by contrast, is a genuine unavoidable circumstance because even one who has merely drunk may not pray initially. Beit Yosef also distinguishes within prayer itself: if he began drinking after the time for prayer arrived, he is not deemed compelled. See there. Shulchan Aruch mentions none of these distinctions, so one must conclude that Beit Yosef retracted them.
