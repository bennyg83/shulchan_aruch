(s.k. 17) waits etc. even if he stipulates etc. explained in Sefer Teshuvot his words — meaning: granted if he hires it even if he stipulates it shall rest on Shabbat the gentile isn't believed in this, for he won't fear to do melacha also on Shabbat; and if the Israelite sues him in their courts that he violated his condition that he stipulated it shall rest on Shabbat, the gentile will say he will add wages for Shabbat's work — and again he has no obligation in their law; what do they care what is forbidden for Israel to leave doing melacha with his animal on Shabbat. Not so when he handed to a shepherd who has no permission at all to use it even for payment — if stipulating helps: the gentile fears doing melacha for the Israelite will sue him in their courts and be obligated also in their laws, since he didn't hand it to him to do melacha with it.
