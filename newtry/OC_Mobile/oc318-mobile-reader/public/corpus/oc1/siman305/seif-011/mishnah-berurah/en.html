(א) With a fox tail — in all these the reason is because there is no need in them for guarding its body, and it is a burden for it; apart from a sandal that they fasten on the feet of the animal, for the reason is lest it fall and they come to carry; and likewise with the bag on the udders of goats it appears from the Gra that the reason is also that it is not tightened well and we are concerned lest the bag fall and their owners come to carry:

(ב) And not goats etc. — see above in s.k. 24 what he wrote there:

(ג) With a sandal etc. — and it appears that the iron that they fix under the feet of horses from below is not included in this, for since it is fixed with nails we are not concerned lest they fall:

(ד) And plugged etc. — that is, that he stops up the bell inside it with wool or cotton, for otherwise it is forbidden because it makes sound:

(ה) It may not go out with it — because it appears as though he is leading it in the market to sell [Gemara]; and it implies that with those to whom this does not apply, such as cats and birds and the like, for whom it is not customary to lead them to sell in pairs, it is permitted when plugged; and in Semag it appears that it is forbidden even with these to go out because of burden; and see Magen Avraham and R' Akiva Eiger; and know that all these that we say regarding an animal that it is forbidden to go out in reshut ha'rabbim because of burden — in a courtyard it is permitted, even if it is not a mixed alley; and regarding a karmelit there are opinions among the Acharonim — some permit and some forbid; and regarding our reshut ha'rabbim one should certainly be careful, for there is in this a possible d'oraisa prohibition, as I explained in Be'ur Halacha; and with a thing whose concern was lest it fall and its owners come to carry — even those who permit above agree that it is forbidden in a karmelit; and with a thing whose reason is because it appears as though he is leading it to sell — even in a mixed alley it is forbidden, since many people are frequently found there, so Magen Avraham; and Peri Megadim and likewise Beit Meir question this law:
