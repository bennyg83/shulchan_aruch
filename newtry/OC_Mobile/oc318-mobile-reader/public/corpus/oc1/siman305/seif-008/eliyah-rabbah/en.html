[Levush] Because when he ties, etc. So wrote the Rosh in the name of the Yerushalmi. According to this, if one transgressed and tied it, or a non-Jew did it, the animal may go out. But in Tosafot, Shabbos 53, it is written that Rashi explained that if he did not tie it before Shabbos, he did not reveal his intent that it serve as clothing, and it is a load. Additionally, it appears as though he intends to take out the saddlecloth. And R. Porat explained that it appears as though he intends to lead the animal to a distant place. These are their words. If so, according to all the reasons it is forbidden even if one transgressed and tied it on Shabbos. This is also reasonable from the Mishnah’s wording, ‘A donkey may not go out,’ etc.; according to the Yerushalmi it should have taught, ‘One may not tie.’ This appears to me to be the reason of Rabbeinu Yerucham, who does not hold like that Yerushalmi, and the Beit Yosef wondered about him. In my humble opinion, there is no difficulty, for he follows the view of the great authorities mentioned above, and holds that since the reason of the Yerushalmi is not mentioned in our Talmud, we do not rule that way, as stated, for one can be careful to tie without using the animal. Furthermore, in seif 9 they permit untying. It is forced to distinguish that untying does not require as much use, for if so, what proof did the Gemara bring, ‘Now, if you said one may not remove it, is it necessary to say one may not place it?’ Removing requires use, while placing refers specifically without tying. Presumably, concern for using a living creature applies more to untying than to placing without tying. Rather, even by tying we are not concerned. It appears to me that the Shulchan Aruch as well, which wrote the Yerushalmi’s reason in seif 8 and not here, holds that the Yerushalmi’s reason is said only as a stringency, but not as a leniency, such as here, where if they transgressed and tied it, it is forbidden for the animal to go out because of the other reasons. So it also appears from the Rambam in his commentary to the Mishnah and from the Bartenura.
But on a horse, etc., and a saddle is forbidden, etc. So wrote the Shulchan Aruch, following his reasoning, for in the Beit Yosef he wondered at the Tur, who permits placing a saddle on a horse and donkey, which is against the sugya on 53; see there at length. All the later authorities followed him in practice, except that they strained to explain how the Tur understood the Gemara, and their words are very forced, as one who studies it will see. The Taz especially bends the words of the Gemara here, and there is much difficulty with him, but this is not the place to elaborate. In my humble opinion, the Tur understood the Gemara correctly. I will first mention the point of wonder that I saw in Sefer HaTerumos, siman 236, and the Mordechai in Perek Bameh Behemah, that they were uncertain whether it is permitted to place a saddle on a horse. If so, the Beit Yosef should have wondered at those earlier great authorities and mentioned them. Now I will explain everything clearly. They hold that when the Gemara asks, ‘What difference is there between a saddle and a saddlecloth?’ and he was silent, this is because he ruled in practice that even a saddlecloth is permitted, and not as explained that he was silent because he held that a saddle too is permitted, such that one must say it implies that according to the conclusion a saddle is forbidden. That is astonishing, for the matter remains simple even according to the conclusion. When it concludes, ‘What is different from a saddle? There it is different, since it may fall off by itself,’ this means that to remove it is permitted, and likewise according to the answer ‘to cool it,’ there is no suffering, meaning also to remove it, but to warm it and place it on is permitted. If so, it emerges from the sugya that for a donkey even a saddle is permitted. Therefore Sefer HaTerumos and Mordechai were uncertain only regarding a horse. The words of the Semag can also be understood as forbidding only by a horse. The Tur’s view is that it is permitted even by a horse, for since we find a distinction between going out to the public domain, where a saddle is forbidden in the previous seif even by a donkey, for cooling it does not help, and here, in a courtyard, it is permitted because we say that nevertheless it helps somewhat, the same applies to a horse: although above it is forbidden, here it is permitted. For Sefer HaTerumos and Mordechai this remains uncertain, since the Talmud found an allowance only for a donkey, which is cold and for which it helps somewhat, but for a horse, which is not cold, it is forbidden. The Semag’s view is to resolve this. So it appears to me in practical halacha: a saddle for a donkey is permitted, and for a horse is forbidden.
[Levush] For it is needless exertion, etc. The Taz explained that the reason is that we are concerned he may come to lean on the animal and use a living creature. This is incorrect, for the Rosh explicitly wrote that even by a saddlecloth, which covers the whole body, without tying he does not need to come close to the donkey and does not lean on it. Only by a rein on the head, which is a more serious matter, did he write in seif 1 that one should be careful not to lean on it. It is also difficult: how did the Gemara derive that a saddlecloth is permitted by a kal vachomer from a feeding basket, when a saddlecloth is worse because it is on the entire body, as he himself explained in seif katan 6? I later saw in Rabbeinu Yerucham that hanging a feeding basket is done by tying. According to this, for calves and foals it is permitted even by tying, and it appears that he follows his own view in seif katan 6. But according to the Rosh it is forbidden, and so it appears from all the poskim, that one hangs it without tying. Perhaps one can distinguish between tying a saddlecloth and other forms of tying, as with tying an animal’s rope, where one is exempt, and by a rein too, in seif 2, it appears that it is by tying. This requires study.
[Levush] Because it appears, etc. But that which does not appear this way is nullified to the halter, as Tosafot 54 wrote. According to this, it appears that with chickens, dogs, cats, and birds, where it is not their manner to lead them this way to sell them, it is permitted, for these things are nullified to the strap on their necks. The Semag wrote that the reason is because it is a load, and according to him all of them are forbidden. There is room to say that each would agree to the other in practice, as the Shiltei Giborim, folio 123, wrote. It appears that his meaning is that one may say the Semag discusses when it is not tied to a halter, as the Magen Avraham wrote, and in that case Tosafot agree. In my humble opinion, Sefer HaTerumos, siman 237, and the Mordechai imply that even by chickens the reason of going to the market applies; see there. However, everything depends on the place. One should not ask against the Semag that the Gemara gives the reason that it looks like one going to the market, for Sefer HaTerumos there answers that the reason of going to the market was stated for additional support, or because, since it is small, it is not considered a load. This requires study. Tosafot there wrote that there is no concern lest it fall and he come to carry it, for it is speaking of something woven.
[Levush] For what purpose, etc. This requires study: from where did he know this? From Tosafot Shabbos 58, s.v. lo, etc., it implies that it is not a load, but the reason is lest it break and he come to carry it. Even if it is woven into its garment, we are concerned for this, as Tosafot wrote there, s.v. hacha b’mai askinan, etc. Although they wrote this regarding a slave, it appears to me that the same applies to an animal, for a bell is normally woven into a garment, but not a seal. Consider this carefully. What Chidushei Maharam wrote on this does not appear correct at all. From Rabbeinu Yerucham, folio 80, it implies that the reason is that it looks like one going to the market, as by a bell, and the words of Tosafot there can also be aligned this way. When he wrote ‘lest it break,’ similar to a seal, he means the seal of a slave, which the Gemara mentioned just above. Consider this carefully, for with this the question of Maharsha there is resolved.
[Levush] Since, etc. Rashi explained this on 54. It is difficult, for if so, why did the Gemara on 52a leave unresolved the case of a goat with its beard inserted, where he tied the hair below? There too it causes it pain. The Kenesses HaGedolah explained the Gemara as referring to where he recognizes it by a knot in its garment and also in its tail. The Kenesses HaGedolah expressed wonder at the Rif, Rosh, and Tur for omitting the above law of a goat with its beard, namely, that a goat for which he carved a place between its horns may go out with a halter. In my humble opinion, it appears that they hold that a halter for a goat is excessive guarding. The Gemara there stated this according to Chananya, who holds that excessive guarding is not a load, but according to our ruling like Rav it is forbidden. A support for this is that the Gemara states these laws after saying there that the halacha follows Chananya, etc.
