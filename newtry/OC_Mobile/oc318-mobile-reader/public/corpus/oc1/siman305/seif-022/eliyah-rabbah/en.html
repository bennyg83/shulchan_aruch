His garments, etc. If the animal went outside on its own with a load upon it, not through a person, its owner need not prevent it; see there and in Ralbach. The Magen Avraham wondered, for throughout the chapter Bameh Behemah Yotze’ah it implies that even if it is on its own, it is forbidden. Now, in Eliyah Zuta as well I relied on Olas Shabbos, who copied the words of the Ralbach this way. But now I examined the Ralbach himself, seif 28, and he did not write there that it had a load upon it; rather, he discusses where it goes outside the techum without a load. It appears to me that in such a case he certainly need not prevent it. On the contrary, the Gemara on 53 says even more than this: he may call it and it comes. Rashi explains that he was not warned about the techum of his animal, provided that he does not actually bring it by hand. These are his words. See siman 306, seif 2. But when it has a load upon it, he certainly must prevent it when it goes out to the public domain, as is explicit from the Rambam that I cited at the beginning of siman 304, ‘and not anything,’ etc. However, with a saddle there is no concern if a non-Jew placed it on it, as the Perishah and Rashal wrote. See siman 266, seif katan 9.
