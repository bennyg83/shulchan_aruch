And there is one who forbids, etc. The Beit Yosef learned this from the implication of the Tur. This requires study, for the Tur is a lone opinion against the Rambam, Ran, and Rabbeinu Yerucham cited by the Beit Yosef. Furthermore, I saw that the Semag, Semak, Sefer HaTerumos, Mordechai, and Tanya also permit. Moreover, this is implied by the language of the Mishnah, 54: ‘but he may insert the ropes into his hand.’ These are its words, and ‘ropes’ is plural. The Beit Yosef wrote that this was said regarding kilayim and on a weekday. From Rashi’s explanation it is clear that this is not so; for if so, why did he explain the statement of Rav Ashi, ‘They taught this only regarding kilayim,’ as referring to ‘he should not wrap,’ etc., and not explain it on the case that permits the ropes, since the Gemara marks it with ‘but he may insert’? Rather, necessarily this discusses Shabbos, and the citation-marker relied on the latter clause, as is the manner of the Gemara. So it appears to me; consider it carefully. Furthermore, if so, how could the Gemara have thought that it referred to human kilayim? The difficulty would still remain: why does it teach ‘ropes,’ since with one rope from an animal and a person there is kilayim? It also appears to me that the Tur permits as well. When he says, ‘but one alone he may take out,’ this is because of the latter clause, provided that the rope not extend a tefach from under his hand, for even with one it is forbidden. Also, with two it is not absolute, since with two species of animals it is forbidden because of animal kilayim, or when one is wool and one is linen because of shatnez, as Sefer HaTerumos and the Rav’s commentary write, Yoreh Deah, end of siman 300.
