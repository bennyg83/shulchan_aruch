Or it is extra guarding (should read: not so) — and the principle: anything that is not guarded thereby, or guarding in excess, is a burden; not so moderate guarding — even though it would be guarded with less, one cannot limit so precisely — Beit Yosef in the name of R' Yerucham; and see there in letter 2. Behold resting one's animal He made "so that your ox and donkey may rest" (Exodus 23:12) and "you shall not do melacha — you and your animal" (Exodus 24:10) for the lav of driving; Tosafot 51b s.v. beha — but the Levush wrote "you shall not do melacha — your ox and donkey" (Deuteronomy 5:14); and in Rambam 63 it is "you shall not do melacha and your animal"; or alternatively "so that it may rest" (both Exodus 1 and 22); and in Rambam ch. 8 halacha 1 Laws of Shabbat it is forbidden to take out a load on an animal, as it says "so that your ox and donkey may rest" and "all your animal" — I did not find the verse thus, neither in Exodus nor Deuteronomy — see there; and in Shach 14:131 s.k. 10 resting an animal is not the lav "you shall not do melacha and your animal," and it is in the latter Tablets, for in the former Tablets it is for the lav of driving and plowing for which a person is liable stoning; and in Beit Yosef ch. 8 halacha 3 in the name of the Ran it appears resting an animal is a lav; and in Ramban (Exodus 23:12) "give it rest" is superfluous — so much there; and resting an animal is either included in the former or latter Tablets; and if one transgresses resting an animal he receives rabbinical lashes though he did no act at all, as stated several places; and he is disqualified from testimony rabbinically; and if women are warned on this per the view that it is a lav — it is obvious; and even per the view that it is a positive command and sanctification of the day — guarding and remembering are equated — nevertheless we learn from it that for all Shabbat matters men and women are equal — Magen Avraham siman 251 and Taz; nevertheless some say 6 — see there. And from what Rambam wrote there regarding plowing and the like — Peri Chadash siman 495: the plowman with the plow in his hand is like an axe in the hand of the woodcutter — meaning he holds one of them; not so in other melachot like grinding with animals — whatever is not melacha with the hands has only the lav of driving.
