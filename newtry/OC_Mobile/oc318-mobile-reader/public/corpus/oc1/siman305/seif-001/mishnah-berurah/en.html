(א) An animal goes out etc. — for it is written "that your ox and your donkey may rest" etc. — behold the Torah warned that even an Israelite animal shall rest on Shabbat, and it is not specifically an animal — likewise birds and all living creatures; therefore specifically when it goes out with something that is for guarding it is an ornament and custom, and behold it is like a garment for a person and is not a burden; but with something in which it is not guarded it is a burden and forbidden; and likewise when it goes out in what is excessive guarding for its matter, since this too is not its custom, it is also included in burden and forbidden:

(ב) Excessively — specifically in excessive guarding greatly; but if only slightly more than what they need, it is not a burden, for it is possible to minimize:

(ג) That it is for ornament — see below in s.k. 12:

(ד) Therefore etc. — he begins with the strong animals that need guarding greatly and are not satisfied with light guarding, such as naakah, which is a white female camel that needs guarding specifically with an iron muzzle on its nose, and an iron peg in the nose is not enough for it, and all the more so with an apsar, and it is forbidden to go out with them; and a Libyan donkey [chamor luba — a donkey from the land of Libya] in an iron peg in the nose, but not in an iron muzzle, for that is excessive guarding for it:

(ה) In an iron peg in the nose — that is, in the promvia written in s.k. 3, and specifically a Libyan donkey; but another donkey not, for for it this is excessive guarding, as is written below in seif 3; and it is in the Gemara that a Libyan donkey goes out even in an apsar, for with that too it is guarded:

(ו) And the camel in an apsar — but not in an iron muzzle, which is excessive guarding for it; and in an iron peg in the nose Magen Avraham and Taz rule to forbid:

(ז) And mule and donkey — it should read: and likewise mule and donkey — that is, that they too go out in an apsar; but in a resen, which is a promvia, they are forbidden to go out, as he wrote in s.k. 3 regarding a donkey, and likewise a mule:

(ח) And a horse goes out etc. — as it should read; for a donkey is forbidden in a resen:

(ט) Which is prino — that is, the bridle mentioned above, which is a promvia; and in our language they call it tsuim, and an apsar is what they call abreits:

(י) But not in both — for one suffices for it and the second is a burden; and if its business is bad so that it is not guarded in one, it is permitted to go out in both together, and everything is per the circumstance. [Mordechai]:

(כ) And it is permitted etc. — specifically with these that are permitted to go out in an apsar it is also permitted to wrap around its neck the rope coming out from it if it is long; but a cow is forbidden to go out in an apsar, and likewise to wrap a rope around its neck, because it is guarded in any case, as below in siman 17, see there:

(ל) Around its neck — from Rashi and Ran it appears that even though it is wrapped tightly around its neck so that a person cannot insert his hands between the apsar and the neck so that it should be comfortable to pull when he wishes, nevertheless it is permitted because it is an ornament; and even though every ornament is forbidden, as at the beginning of the seif and as below at the end of siman 17 — that is, specifically an ornament that is not its custom on a weekday; to exclude a thing that every person is accustomed to do thus on a weekday for ornament for the animal — it is not a burden; but Tosafot and Rabbeinu Yerucham hold that ornament is forbidden in every case, and here it deals with loose ropes and is called guarding, for it can hold the rope when it wishes to flee; and all the more if a bit of the rope went out hanging around its neck it is permitted; and Bach ruled like Tosafot, stringently:

(מ) The apsar — and likewise the resen, for it is prepared for the animal and is not muktzeh:

(נ) That he not lean on it — meaning that when he puts it on the animal's head he should be careful not to lean on its head, for that is using a living creature:
