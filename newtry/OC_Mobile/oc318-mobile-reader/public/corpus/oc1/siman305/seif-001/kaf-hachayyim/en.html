1) [Seif 1] An animal may go out, etc. A person is commanded by Torah law to ensure that his animal rests, as it is stated, 'So that your ox and donkey shall rest,' etc. The command to ensure rest applies equally to an ox, donkey, every other domesticated animal, wild animal, and bird. We maintain that equipment which restrains an animal is considered an ornament and its ordinary apparel, rather than a burden, while equipment that does not restrain it is a burden. Beis Yosef in the name of Rashi; Olas Shabbos, paragraph 1.
2) There: But if it is not restrained by it. This means that the item placed upon it does not restrain it, because it is restrained without that item. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 2.
3) There: It then constitutes excessive restraint, etc. This excludes a case involving equipment that provides moderate restraint. Even if the animal could be restrained with something slightly lighter, it is permitted, because one cannot calibrate the restraint precisely enough to avoid adding even a little. Beis Yosef; Taz, paragraph 1; Olas Shabbos, paragraph 3; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 2.
4) There: Likewise, anything intended as an adornment, etc. This refers to an item the animal does not ordinarily wear. An adornment routinely worn during the week is permitted. Beis Yosef in the name of Rashi and Ran. Bach, however, rules like Tosafos that every adornment is forbidden. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1, cites this.
5) There: Therefore, a female camel, etc. A ne'akah is a female camel. Aruch. A Chamra Luvah is a donkey originating from the country of Libya. Rashi. Tosafos explains that Luv means Egypt; see there.
6) There: And a horse may go out with a bridle, etc. This is the correct text, and it is also Tur's wording. A donkey, however, may not go out with a bridle, which is a perumbia, as stated in seif 3. Bach; Magen Avraham, paragraph 2. The explanation is as follows: a camel may go out with a halter, and a mule and donkey likewise with a halter, but not with a bridle or perumbia, as stated in seif 3. A horse may go out with either a bridle or perumbia, or with a halter. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 2. See below, paragraph 12. Bach's ruling permitting a camel to go out with a perumbia, however, is not compelling. Magen Avraham there. Taz, paragraph 2, likewise holds that a camel may not go out with a perumbia. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 3; Erech HaShulchan, paragraph 1. See there for his challenge to Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 3, who sought to prove Bach's view; see there. This is also implied by the Shulchan Aruch: a camel may go out specifically with a halter, but not a perumbia.
7) There: But not with both. If it is difficult to control, it may go out with both together. Everything depends upon the circumstances. Beis Yosef in the name of Mordechai. See below, seif 4.
8) There: It is permitted to move the halter, etc. Moving it is not forbidden, because it is prepared for the animal's use. Tur.
9) There: Provided that he does not lean upon it. The reason is that one may not use a living creature. Beis Yosef.
