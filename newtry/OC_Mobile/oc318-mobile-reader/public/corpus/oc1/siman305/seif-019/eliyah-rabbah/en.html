That he nullifies, etc. For they are not fit for use while the water is upon them, as the Maggid wrote in chapter 25. It appears to me that this is specifically pillows and cushions, but garments are fit for use, as the Magen Avraham wrote. It appears that he compares it to siman 301, seif 45, that one whose clothes became soaked in water may walk in them, etc. But it is not comparable, for there it is when he has no others, and because of human dignity, as Tosafot Yom Tov wrote in Perek Chaviyos. Here too, since he intentionally places them in water, perhaps it is worse.
Suffering of living creatures, etc. Therefore, if it is impossible by means of pillows and cushions, it is permitted to lift it by hand, for this too is only a D'Rabbanan prohibition, as the Shiltei Giborim, Riaz, and my teacher and grandfather wrote. So too the Bach wrote at the end of siman 308. According to this, it appears to me that what the Rambam wrote, that it is forbidden to lift it by hand, refers to when it is possible by means of pillows and cushions, unlike the Magen Avraham, who did not understand it this way and from whom all the words of the great authorities mentioned above escaped notice. It appears that telling non-Jews to lift it is more permitted than placing pillows, as written in Hagahos Maimoniyos, chapter 8, and in Teshuvos Maharam of Rothenburg, siman 49.
