(Subparagraph 3) In addition to, etc. And however, etc., until he loathes, etc. The Magen Avraham's intent is not to explain the Rema's words, for certainly according to the Rema, even if he loathes his eating, nevertheless with fragrant foods he is liable. The Rema is referring to the Mechaber's words, who wrote that until he loathes his food he is exempt, and about this the Rema wrote that if he ate fragrant foods he is liable. This implies that even if he had already eaten until he loathed his food, he is nevertheless liable. Rather, the Magen Avraham brought the words of the Bach, who disagrees with the Rema and holds that one must distinguish between not desiring to eat and loathing his food, in order to reconcile the view of Rashi and those with him with the view of Rabbenu Tam and the Rambam. Rashi and those who follow his approach hold that if one does not desire eating, even if he does not loathe his food, if he afterward ate, he is exempt. Rabbenu Tam and the Rambam hold that we require specifically that he loathe his food. About this the Bach wrote that Rashi is speaking when he eats foods that are not fragrant; in that case, if he had already eaten until he no longer desires food, that suffices to exempt him. Rabbenu Tam and the Rambam speak when he eats fragrant foods, and therefore he is not exempt until he loathes his food.
