That which - See Taz in letter 1; see there. A half-measure is forbidden from the Torah, as the Magen Avraham wrote in letter 1. In Mishneh la-Melech, chapter 1 of Chametz u-Matzah, law 7, regarding one who eats on the eve of Pesach literally until dark, it implies that a half-measure there is permitted from the Torah, for one can say that "you shall afflict yourselves" makes even a half-measure forbidden from the Torah, and the halachah le-Moshe mi-Sinai only exempts it from punishment. According to this, at first glance there is a new law on Yom Kippur: with other prohibitions a half-measure does not have a positive commandment, only a general Torah prohibition, and an oath can take effect on it. This is not so on Yom Kippur, where there is a positive commandment with a half-measure, so an oath does not take effect on it. The practical difference is for invalidity as a witness; see Tosafos, Shevuos 23b, comment beginning "which establishes," and Yoreh De'ah 239, and what I wrote in my fruit for Yoreh De'ah in the introduction to the laws of shechitah.
