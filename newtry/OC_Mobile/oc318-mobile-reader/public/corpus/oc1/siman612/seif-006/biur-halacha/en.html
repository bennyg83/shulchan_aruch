If one ate foods that are not fit for eating - See the Mishnah Berurah. Know that it is obvious that if one eats foods fit only for animals, he is also exempt. So is evident in the Rambam, chapter 2 of the Laws of Resting on the Tenth, where he wrote regarding one who eats a date's bulk of foods fit for a person; and so too he wrote regarding foods that are not fit; see there. What he wrote about bitter herbs comes to exclude good herbs that are fit for human food. Regarding raw meat, at first glance it is obvious that he is liable, since there are people who eat it. So too it is explained in our sugya, which says that if he ate raw meat with salt it joins, and raw meat means uncooked meat. I found that Rabbenu Manoach cited Rashi's explanation on this, that raw meat is uncooked meat, though in our text of Rashi there it is not found. However, it is somewhat surprising that the Rambam wrote: if one ate roasted meat with salt. At first glance this implies that raw meat is not considered food. In truth, it is impossible to say this from the sugya in Shabbos 128, which the Rambam cites in chapter 26 of the Laws of Shabbos and the Shulchan Aruch in siman 308; see there in se'if 31 in the Mishnah Berurah and Biur Halacha. See the Pri Chadash in Shabbos there, comment beginning "raw meat." This requires study.
