(12) That are not fit for eating - Meaning bitter or spoiled things, to the point that because of this they are not fit for eating at all. But things fit for eating, only they are prohibited, such as forbidden fat, neveilah, tereifah, and the like, certainly one is liable for them also because of Yom Kippur.
(13) That he ate - For otherwise, how would you find a case of gluttonous eating? In any event he would be liable for what he ate first in prohibition before reaching gluttonous eating.
(14) Until he loathes his food - The food he is eating now. This excludes a case where he does not loathe it, but was merely full and had no desire to eat, yet when he eats he still senses taste; in that case he is liable.
(15) He is exempt - For gluttonous eating is not called eating. Nevertheless, at the outset there is a prohibition in this. So too with foods that are not fit for eating, there is a rabbinic prohibition at the outset, and even with less than half the measure of them one must also be careful at the outset (Pri Megadim).
(16) In addition to his eating, he is liable, etc. - The Bach wrote that if he ate until he loathed his food, even in this case he is exempt. In truth, however, everything depends on what he senses within himself. So wrote the Magen Avraham. That is to say, ordinarily we assume that with seasoned food, when he eats it, even if he was initially extremely full, he does not loathe it, and this is the case of which the Rema speaks. Even so, if he senses within himself that the eating pains him and he loathes it, he is exempt.
(17) To taste something and spit it out - Even less than the measure, and even if he knows that he can restrain himself from swallowing anything.
(18) Even aromatic wood - Although it is mere wood, nevertheless when he chews it he senses taste. But it is permitted to smell in order to complete the count of one hundred blessings. As long as he has not diverted his mind from smelling, it is forbidden to recite the blessing again, for that would be an unnecessary blessing. There are people who smell fragrant waters, called spiritus, though when one does not smell, one need not recite a blessing over it at all, and they do not recite any blessing over it; they are doing a prohibition (Chayei Adam).
