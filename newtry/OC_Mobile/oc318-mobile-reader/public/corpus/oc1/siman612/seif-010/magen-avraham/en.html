It is permitted to touch - This is not comparable to chametz on Pesach, as written in siman 446, se'if 3, for there, since he eats other things, we are concerned lest he eat this too. Here it is different (Darkei Moshe, Terumas ha-Deshen, Bach at the end of siman 611). It implies there that according to the Ran and the Maggid Mishneh it is forbidden to touch foods unless he needs to give them to a child who cannot take them by himself. This is forced. Furthermore, in Yoma 14 they say that according to Rabbi Yehudah the Kohen Gadol may not offer as an onen, by decree lest he eat. The Gemara asks: how is this different from Yom Kippur, when he offers as an onen and we do not decree? It answers: on Yom Kippur, since everyone does not eat, he too will not come to eat; here, where everyone eats, he too will come to eat. Thus we see that we do not decree. There is another difficulty in the Gemara: why did it not ask simply, why is it that on every Yom Kippur he offers and we are not concerned lest he eat? Rather, certainly on Yom Kippur we do not decree. The explanation is that we are concerned lest, from the fact that he sees he offers as an onen, he will think it is permitted for him to eat as an onen. Therefore it asks from Yom Kippur, when he offers as an onen, and answers that there he does not eat anyway, and we do not decree lest he eat as an onen on another day, for he will see that it is forbidden to offer; examine there. The Ran and Maggid Mishneh, who do decree, do so specifically with cutting vegetables, because since he is occupied with them and preparing them for eating, we are concerned lest he eat. But with mere touching, we are not concerned. This is the main view. See the Mishnah at the end of chapter 11 of Menachos. In Maharil he forbids touching, and so it is in the Yerushalmi, chapter 4 of Pesachim, that with something fit to be eaten as is, we decree lest he eat. Therefore the loaves are distributed in the evening.
