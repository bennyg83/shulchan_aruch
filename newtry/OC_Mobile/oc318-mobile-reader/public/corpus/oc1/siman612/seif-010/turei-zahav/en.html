To touch, etc. The Terumas ha-Deshen, siman 147, wrote that it is good to be careful, as with chametz on Pesach, but that the custom was not to be careful about this. See there, for there is no correct distinction there. It seems to me that Yom Kippur is different, for the awe of the Day of Judgment is upon him all day, as we find in chapter 3 of Demai: on Shabbos one may ask him and eat based on his word, because the awe of Shabbos is upon him. All the more so on Yom Kippur, when the whole day he is occupied with prayers and the holiness of the day does not leave him.
