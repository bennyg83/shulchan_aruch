Seif 1. One who eats on Yom Kippur the volume of a large date is liable, etc. NB: In the Talmud, Yoma chapter 8, they raised the question whether "a large date" means it together with its pit, or it without its pit, etc. This is difficult to me: why did they not resolve it from the Mishnah in Kelim chapter 17, which lists items measured by a large measure and says, "like a date on Yom Kippur, like it and like its pit"? The difficulty is this: if the intention is like it together with its pit, all is well, for it teaches there "and like its pit" as part of the novelty of the large measure, that we require both the date and its pit. But if the intention is like it or like its pit, it has no place there; on the contrary, that mishnah teaches what is lenient by requiring a large measure, while "like its pit" alone would be the stringency. What relevance would that have there? Its place would be only in the chapter of Yom Kippur. It must therefore mean like it together with its pit, and this requires great study. Examine carefully. Now, the Talmud says that if one ate and drank, he is liable only once, because drinking is included in eating. It appears to my humble opinion to derive a new law from the Yerushalmi, Yoma chapter 8 halachah 3. On the Mishnah "if one ate and drank, he is liable only once," the Yerushalmi says: why? Because drinking is included in eating, but eating is not included in drinking. End quote. The ending, "eating is not included in drinking," requires precision, for it seems unnecessary; it has no relevance here, since the issue is only satiation. Therefore it appears that the Yerushalmi found the Mishnah difficult: why did it teach "ate and drank, he is liable only once"? It should have taught the reverse, "drank and ate," which would be a greater novelty. Therefore it explains as follows: in truth, if he drank first and afterwards ate, eating is not included in drinking, for it is not the normal way to drink and afterwards eat, and therefore he is liable twice. Only when he ate first and afterwards drank is this the normal way of eating, as the Torah says, "you shall eat and be satisfied," and the Sages said that "be satisfied" refers to drinking; therefore in this case he is liable once. This is what it says here: why? Because drinking is included in eating, and therefore it is drawn after the eating and is one act; but eating is not included in drinking, and if he drank and afterwards ate he is liable twice. This is a novel law, and in my view it is reasonable and compelling, with the help of Hashem. Examine carefully.
