1) [Seif 1] One who eats on Yom Kippur the volume of a large date is liable. Although with regard to the prohibition, even the slightest amount is forbidden, as stated in seif 5, and it is not the practice of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch to record the measure of liability rather than the prohibition, this is relevant to the matter mentioned in siman 618. When feeding a sick person, he is fed in a manner that will not combine to form the requisite measure. We therefore need to know the measure that incurs liability. Taz, s.k. 1. The Beit Yosef likewise raised this difficulty regarding the Tur and resolved it in this manner, see there.
2) Ibid. One who eats on Yom Kippur the volume of a large date is liable. The Mishnah, Yoma 73b, concludes concerning this, "it and its pit." The Rambam, Tur, and Shulchan Aruch omitted this. It appears that the reason is that since they stated the definitive measure, that it is slightly less than the volume of an egg, as stated in the Gemara, ibid. 79b, they no longer needed to explain further, "it and its pit," because they had already provided a fixed measure. The Bach wrote that when they said, "the volume of a large date," it implies that they meant together with its pit, as Rav Ashi stated in the Gemara, ibid. 79a, see there. Maamar Mordechai, sec. 1. A large date means a large tamar, as the Rambam wrote in his Commentary on the Mishnah, chapter 8 of Yoma.
3) And the Maamar Mordechai further wrote in sec. 2 that even if one ate the volume of a large date together with its pit, he is exempt from kares, because the requisite measure is lacking, and the pit does not combine with the food, see there.
4) Ibid. The volume of a large date incurs liability. It is a received halacha that three measures were stated, the volume of an olive, the volume of an egg, and the volume of a date; we do not assess by more or less than these. Erech HaShulchan, sec. 1. See below, sec. 8.
5) Ibid. And it is slightly less than the volume of an egg. This means an egg without its shell. Dagul MeRevavah. So too he wrote in his work Noda B'Yehuda, Orach Chaim, siman 38, see there. So too wrote Mateh Ephraim, sec. 1. However, Responsa Binyan Tzion HaChadashos, siman 30, disputes the aforementioned words of the Dagul MeRevavah, and in the novellae of Rav Zalman Henna he strongly questions those words of the Dagul MeRevavah, see there. The Mishna Berurah, sec. 2, and Shaar HaTziyun, sec. 3, cite his words; Pischei Olam, sec. 2. Regarding the measure of an egg without its shell, see above siman 158, sec. 8. Regarding how many olive-volumes there are in an egg, see above in our words to siman 486, see there.
6) Ibid. And it is less than the volume of an egg. An average-sized egg, as stated in tractate Keilim, chapter 17, "the egg-volume of which they spoke is an average one," see there. So too wrote the Mishna Berurah, sec. 2, in the name of the later authorities.
7) Ibid. And it is slightly less than the volume of an egg. But if it is much less, he is exempt. Olas Shabbos, sec. 1.
8) Ibid. And it is slightly less than the volume of an egg. Although with regard to all Torah prohibitions we assess the measure as the volume of an olive, that applies where eating is written. This is not so regarding Yom Kippur, where eating is not written; rather, Scripture prohibited eating through the language of affliction, as it is written, "the soul that will not be afflicted." The Sages knew that with less than the volume of a date, his mind is not settled at all, and he remains hungry; thus, he remains hungry and afflicted as he was initially. Mishna Berurah, sec. 1. This is from the Gemara, Yoma 80a. So too wrote the Rambam in his Commentary on the Mishnah, Yoma, chapter 8.
9) Ibid. Whether for a dwarf or for Og, king of Bashan. The Sages knew that with this amount his mind is settled, while with less than this amount his mind is not settled. However, for everyone else it settles the mind considerably, whereas for Og, king of Bashan, only slightly. Gemara, ibid. 80b.
10) Ibid. Whether for a dwarf, etc. There is no difference whether he eats permitted foods or forbidden foods, such as piggul, nosar, impure food, a neveilah, or a tereifah, because through all of them his mind is settled. Scripture used the inclusive expressions "you shall afflict" and "you shall afflict yourselves" to include that the prohibition of affliction takes effect upon every item, even upon an item to which a prohibition already applies. If he did not afflict himself, even by refraining from forbidden food, he is liable to kares, and with regard to these foods he incurs two liabilities, one because of the food's own prohibition and one because of Yom Kippur. Levush; Pri Chadash; Bigdei Yesha; Mishna Berurah, sec. 12. This is from the Gemara, Yoma 74b and Kerisos 13b. See Chullin 100b and 101, and examine carefully.
