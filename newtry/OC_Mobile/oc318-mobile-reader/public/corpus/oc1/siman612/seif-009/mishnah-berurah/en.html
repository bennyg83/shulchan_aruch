(23) A cheekful - For the Sages knew that with this measure a person's mind is settled and the affliction leaves him.
(24) According to what he is - Meaning that here the measure is not the same for every person, as it is for eating in se'if 1, because regarding drinking the Sages knew that a person's mind is settled only by his own cheekful.
(25) And not literally a cheekful - Meaning that we do not require both cheeks to be full of liquid; it is enough that one is full and protruding, though then it will naturally appear as though both are full.
(26) And it is less, etc. - It implies that it is close to a revi'is. However, in siman 271, se'if 13, he wrote that it is the majority of a revi'is. See the Biur Halacha there.
(27) In an average person - Sometimes his cheekful is a revi'is or more than a revi'is, such as with a very large person. Conversely, with a small person, whose cheekful is less than most of a revi'is, he is also liable, since his mind is settled by this.
(28) Such as brine - This is what drips from fish when it is soaked in salt. Muryas is the fat that comes out of fish.
(29) And undiluted vinegar - This is specifically when it bubbles when thrown on the ground, for then it is not fit for drinking and he is exempt even if he drank much more than the measure, because since it is strong vinegar, it harms him more. Some say that when he drinks a lot he is liable, because then it breaks his hunger and the Torah said, "which shall not be afflicted." But according to all views it is forbidden even in a small amount.
(30) He is liable - For it is fit for drinking.
