And undiluted vinegar, he is exempt. This implies even a large amount, and so the Rambam wrote explicitly. The Beis Yosef asked: did Rav Gidel not say specifically a little, but not a lot? Because of this he wrote that the Rambam did not have this reading in the Gemara, and he brought two views, namely Rabbenu Yerucham and the Kol Bo, who indeed hold that with a large amount he is liable even with things not fit. It seems to me that all is resolved. The Rambam explains the Gemara as follows: Rav Gidel made three distinctions. One, between after the fact and at the outset. Two, between a large amount and a little. Three, between fit and unfit. Therefore he first said that the exemption we said about this is after the fact, meaning exempt but forbidden. About this he said: even if you say that exempt means permitted at the outset, nevertheless that is only with a little, but a large amount is forbidden at the outset, and this is what the Mishnah meant by exempt but forbidden. And if you say that there is no distinction between a little and a lot, and in every case it is exempt and permitted, nevertheless one must distinguish between fit and unfit: diluted vinegar is called fit, and if so its law is like all fit things, and one is liable for it. The Beis Yosef asked that just as with diluted vinegar there is liability, so too with a large amount that he mentioned first. But this is not correct according to what I wrote in explaining Rav Gidel's words: first he distinguishes between what is forbidden at the outset and what is forbidden after the fact; then, when he distinguishes between fit and unfit, fit things carry even liability like all fit things. So I explained the Rambam's words. But Rabbenu Yerucham and the Kol Bo explain that what Rav Gidel said to distinguish between a little and a lot refers to after the fact, which he mentioned first, and not as an "even if you say." This is what he says: say that I said after the fact, but not at the outset; and furthermore, even after the fact, he is exempt only with a little, but with a lot there is even liability. And if you ask: but we say that foods that are not fit are exempt, implying even a large amount. That is not difficult, for exempt with unfit things means relative to the liability measure of fit things, namely foods in their measure and liquids in their measure. With that measure he is exempt when they are unfit. But if he greatly increased the amount, he is liable even with unfit things because of the advantage of the large amount, just as we find in Yoreh De'ah regarding something that gives a bad taste: if there is an advantage because of the great amount, it is not considered detrimental. This appears to me to be the reason for their dispute.
