On the 14th day. Meaning: by candlelight; and so all bedikot even after Pesach — so as not to distinguish between one bedikah and another. So rule the poskim.

Within the festival. And even if he already nullified, and even on Yom Tov itself he checks — though he cannot burn it on Yom Tov, nevertheless he can cover it with a vessel until motza'ei Yom Tov (Taz, Magen Avraham). And even on the last day of Pesach he must check (Magen Avraham). One who did not check chametz before its prohibition time and died — the heirs do not bless on bedikah and biur, since they are not obligated to check and destroy chametz that is not theirs (Kaf HaChayim).

He does not bless. But on bedikah within the festival he must bless even though he nullified (Kaf HaChayim). And Taz was uncertain: if he nullified, must he still destroy? From Magen Avraham's words it appears he holds one blesses even if he nullified; but where he checked and nullified and found chametz within the festival — he left it in doubt whether he blesses, since he nullified before Pesach and fulfilled the Sages' enactment in bedikah; in my humble opinion the main point is that one blesses at the time of biur — and Magen Avraham himself in siman 446 wrote plainly that one must bless at the time of biur whether chametz became leavened during Pesach or motza'ei chametz within Pesach; here he left the matter in doubt and requires study. All this is when definite chametz is found; but chametz in a slight amount found within Pesach — one does not bless on it (Magen Avraham siman 446 s.k. 1).
