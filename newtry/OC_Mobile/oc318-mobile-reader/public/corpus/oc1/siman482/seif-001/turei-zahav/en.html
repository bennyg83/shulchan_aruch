(א) And when he finishes his meal, etc. This is the view of the Rif that the Tur brings, and he brings also the view of the Rosh that he eat immediately a guarded kezayit so that he eat matzah with appetite, and bless on it HaMotzi and al achilas matzah; and he is not concerned if he eats at the end unguarded matzah, end of his words. Many questioned him, for Rav Huna said: in the dough of gentiles a person fills his belly from them provided he eat a kezayit of matzah at the end — specifically we require; and the Rosh brought it in chapter Kol Sha'ah, and from R' Yitzchak of Prague a text that that statement of Rav Huna refers to one who has two kezayit, and he himself rejected this explanation — for if so Rav Huna should have said only that he eat a kezayit of matzah at the first; and Maharshal z"l had a text that this "at the end" that Rav Huna specified is not precise. And to my humble opinion there is a distinction between the dough of gentiles and matzah that is not guarded: for the Rosh bases himself on the words of the Rif, and matzah that is not guarded per the Rif means that it is not guarded as required by law from harvesting; since it is guarded at least from kneading, he eats it even at the end, for in any case the name of matzah is on it — not so in the dough of gentiles, for even from kneading there was no guarding at all and the name of matzah is not on it at all; it is worse, and therefore they said, "provided he eat a kezayit of matzah at the end." And this appears to me very precise in the language of Rav Huna, who said that he eat a kezayit of matzah at the end — for it is difficult: before this too he eats matzah, only it was not guarded; if so, he should have said that he eat a kezayit of guarded matzah at the end. Rather, certainly as stated: here, in gentile dough, the name of matzah is not on it at all, since even from kneading it had no guarding, as stated — to settle the mind of the Rosh correctly and it is fit to establish the halacha thus.
