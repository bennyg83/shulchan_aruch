He recites the blessing over eating maror. That is, he first makes Kiddush and recites hamotzi over matzah that is not guarded, and afterward over the maror.
And when he finishes his meal. And [so] wrote the Magen Avraham: a sick person who cannot eat more than a kezayis of matzah, or one who has only a kezayis of matzah, should eat his cooked food without hamotzi, and after his meal recite hamotzi and al achilas matzah and eat a kezayis of matzah; but if he also has no wine, then he must recite Kiddush over the matzah first, and afterward eat his cooked food; end quote.
From matzah that is not guarded. And as it is stated in the Gemara, chapter Arvei Pesachim, folio 120: sponge-cakes and honey-cakes; a person may fill his stomach with them, provided that he eats a kezayis of matzah at the end; and as Rav Huna said in chapter Kol Sha'ah regarding their soft doughs: a person may fill his stomach with them. This is the view of the Rambam and those who follow him. However, the view of the Rif and the Rosh and those who follow them is that it is better to eat guarded matzah earlier, so as to eat with appetite. And the Rosh wrote that this beraisa of the sponge-cakes and honey-cakes, which teaches that he should eat a kezayis of matzah at the end, refers specifically to the time of the Temple; therefore one must eat a kezayis of guarded matzah at the end, which is not so nowadays; even though that teaching of Rav Huna in chapter Kol Sha'ah refers to the present time, and even so it teaches that he should eat a kezayis of matzah at the end. See the Taz and the Bach, who resolved this at length. But there remains a great and standing difficulty against the Rosh and those who follow him: for if so, regarding those two versions in the Gemara (according to the first version, one may not conclude after matzah with afikoman, and according to the latter version, one does conclude after matzah with afikoman), and the Gemara there brings support for the first version and a difficulty against the latter version from that beraisa of the sponge-cakes etc., provided that he eats a kezayis of matzah at the end (at the end, yes; at the beginning, no): if it is true that this beraisa refers specifically to when the Beis HaMikdash stood, then what difficulty or support is there for the law of concluding after matzah with afikoman, for that subject necessarily refers to the present time, as the Rambam explained there (and see what I wrote above in siman 475, s.k. 3), and as the Rosh distinguishes. And furthermore it is somewhat difficult regarding the main difficulty that compelled the Rosh to distinguish between the time of the Temple and the present time: why does he not answer simply, as the Gemara answers, that when that beraisa teaches "at the end," it is saying "it need not be said": it need not be said that if [he eats] at the beginning he does well, for he eats matzah with appetite; rather, even at the end as well he fulfills after the fact; and it refers to a case where he has only a kezayis of matzah, for then it is better to eat at the beginning according to the view of the Rosh there; and the ruling that one may not conclude after matzah with afikoman refers to when he has enough matzah for all his needs. And this requires further study.
Everything from the broken piece. And the Levush and the Taz wrote: even though the second night is only because of a doubt as to the day, nevertheless we do so, for if not, lechem mishneh would not remain for the second night; end of his wording. And their words are not correct in my humble opinion, for nevertheless even on the second night one must break one matzah before he reaches the blessing of hamotzi and al achilas matzah, and so is the preferred form of the mitzvah, as it is stated in the Gemara: all agree regarding Pesach that one places a broken piece within the whole one and breaks; and the view of the great early authorities is to do so l'chatchila, to break only over one and a half, as explained in the Tur and Beis Yosef at the beginning of siman 475; see there. If so, it is difficult: why should he not fulfill the preferred form of the mitzvah also on the first night, making hamotzi over one and eating-matzah over the second, and for the second night likewise leave one and a half? Therefore it seems to me that the reason is simple: since he must feed from that matzah which he sets aside a kezayis for eating matzah and hamotzi, and a kezayis from it for afikoman, he would then be forced to do everything from the half that he left over from the first night, and that will not suffice for him and for his household; therefore he sets aside a whole matzah on the second night so that from the broken matzah there will be enough for all his needs for himself and for his household. But where it is known to him that it will suffice for all the needs of his mitzvos, certainly it is proper to do as he wrote. And he wrote: If he can leave over a small broken piece from the first night, he should leave it over and place it between the whole ones. And if one does not suffice for him on the first night, he should take a broken piece from the second and break on the second night over one and a half; end of his wording. And his words are correct: even if it is a case where he cannot eat for afikoman from the broken piece, nevertheless in pressing circumstances he may eat a kezayis from another guarded matzah, as above in siman 477; see there.
And the two whole ones for the second night. In the Abudraham he concluded: and on the second night he should do according to this order; and so wrote the Beis Yosef in his name at the end of siman 475. And it seems to me that his simple intent and meaning is that also on the second night he should perform the entire Haggadah order as on the first night; but certainly, since he has two matzos on the second night, he recites the blessing over both of them together, as above at the beginning of siman 475, which is not so on the first night, when he blesses only over one. And all this is simple and clear, unlike Pesach Me'uvin, siman 294, who was not precise about all this; and see there.
