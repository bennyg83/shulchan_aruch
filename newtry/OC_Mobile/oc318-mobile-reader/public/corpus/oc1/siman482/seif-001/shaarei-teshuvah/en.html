And Rabbeinu Yonah wrote in the name of Mahari z"l, who wrote in the name of Tzror HaChayyim in a manuscript, who wrote: I heard in the name of my teacher the Rashba — one who came on a ship and did not reach dry land until the fifteenth shall not perform the seder in the day; and it is not like kiddush, that one recites kiddush in the day, for regarding matzah they said it is eaten only at night, end of his words. And it is obvious that the same applies to all who had an oness on Pesach night — he has no makeup by day; and so Magen Avraham siman 485, and they brought it in Be'er Heitev siman 473 see there. (1) Maror — in Ba'er Heitev; and see responsa Beit Yehudah siman 58: two people who were in prison or in the desert and found for them only a kezayit of matzah in the extreme — who precedes? And he wrote that one should derive from the Mishnah in Yoma, when they are numerous, etc., and he concluded that everything depends on whoever is the stronger man, see there. It is not clear, for in such a case it would be stolen matzah; and even if there is no perutah in it, nevertheless that which requires "for you" in matzah is not because of mitzvah haba'ah b'aveirah, but because we derive lechem from lechem from challah, as it is written "your doughs," to exclude stolen matzah; and if so, anything stolen that is not his, even if there is no perutah in it, does not work. And there is somewhat to reject: perhaps since there is no perutah in it that was given for forgiveness, there is no theft on it at all. (And somewhat it is implied thus from Tosafos in Sukkah, chapter Lulav haGazul, that they questioned: why is the reason of "for you" not needed by matzah — mitzvah haba'ah b'aveirah teaches it; and if it is so, you find it in such a case that one stole a kezayit of matzah and there is no perutah in it, and even though there is no mitzvah haba'ah b'aveirah in it, nevertheless it disqualifies him from the verse that proves we require "for you"; and so it appears to infer from Rashi in Sukkah daf 27b, that we teach that all Israel are fit to sit in one sukkah, and Rashi explained: and we find that a perutah did not reach each one — it implies that all that have no perutah in it, it is not applicable to call it his sukkah with which he fulfills, unless a portion of his fellows is joined to him; and compelled from this is that one fulfills with a borrowed sukkah; and if so, the same applies regarding that one cannot call on it stolen matzah from others; and one could say it is not called "for you," since there is no perutah — if he stole it, it automatically becomes yours. But per this reasoning, on the contrary requires study: for it is implied from Rashi on sukkah that regarding its being called "for you," a perutah is required even though it is actually his; and if so, by matzah we would say thus, and we do not find anywhere that a kezayit of matzah requires a perutah; therefore it appears that also by sukkah Rashi's intent is not to require a perutah, but only his intent is that all who do not have a perutah on his portion — if so, the place on which he sits, which is worth more, is not his alone, but many partners have a portion in this place, and they lend this portion to one another; and Tosafos who wrote that in vain they strained — when one increases partners he increases borrowing, etc. — their intent is to say that even if you say each one has a fixed place in the sukkah because of his money, and he need not borrow the place from his fellow in the manner of borrowing but only in the manner of partnership, nevertheless all are partners together in the whole sukkah, and it is proven that one fulfills in partnership, and the same in borrowing; and in any case there is no compulsion from Rashi to say that enjoyment less than a perutah is as nothing and we do not call it "for you," but rather even in less than a perutah there is room to argue between a slice that is not his and a slice that is his, and requires study. And further, that also regarding less than a perutah, theft applies — half a perutah is forbidden by Torah law, as explained in Choshen Mishpat siman 398; and one could also say that since it is visible to him for a mitzvah, automatically there is a perutah with him. But one could say that since for the victim there is no perutah, see Bava Kamma daf 105; and one could say he has no status of perutah because of mitzvot, since mitzvot were not given for enjoyment; but one could say that although it is not called enjoyment, nevertheless he has the status of perutah — see Yeshuos in HaKones regarding a guardian of a lost object, that we say he is a partner because of a perutah of a poor person, see there — and in Nedarim I shortened.) Nevertheless, it appears more that Beit Yehudah refers to a case where the kezayit of matzah was hefker and no one of them has rights in it; and in such a case it appears simple that whoever is able to acquire should acquire; and a fortiori in a monetary matter, the acquirer in hefker acquires for himself, as we say regarding leket, shikchah, and peah: leave before them and they will take; and so in the Mishnah chapter 2 of Peah, even ninety-nine say to divide, etc.; and so we rule at the end of Bava Kamma that one can say, "From hefker I acquire," or "For myself I save," even in a place where at first it belonged to his fellow and became hefker — the acquirer precedes one who was at first the owner of the chametz; and so we say at the end of the chapter Elu Metziot, etc.: yours precedes every person; and even in a matter that has life-and-death implications, his life precedes, as R' Akiva expounded in that [verse] of the canteen of water: "Support your brother with you" — his life precedes, see there; and if in bodily matters it is so, in matters of the soul all the more so (and so in Iggeres Shmuel on Rus, in what Boaz said to the redeemer on the day you acquire from Rus the Moabite — because he foresaw in ruach hakodesh that from her descendants would come the dynasty of the house of David, therefore he said "the Moabite," so that he refuse and he take her and merit that great chain; and although it is forbidden to deceive one's fellow to take what is his, this is in monetary matters; but in mitzvot, even though it is impossible to take from the hand of the acquirer, such as covering and milah and the like, nevertheless all who have not yet acquired are like a matter of hefker to all, and every person is commanded to complete his soul and acquire mitzvot — and this is the matter of Yaakov our father in Bechoros and Berachos, for he saw and knew that Esav surrendered himself to do evil and he needed this for the completion of his soul; and he wrote that thus Maharam Eizov z"l explained the verse, "From the violence of your brother Yaakov, cover you shame" — the intent is to rebuke Esav to his face on the matters of Yaakov that did not come to him easily what was needed for completion until he needed to strategize and act with schemes; perhaps it will seem like violence — he entered himself into this per the need of the hour to acquire them to revive himself eternal life; therefore cover shame for Esav, who in his hand is good and despised good and chose evil, see there — and the words are adapted from sweet, sweet). But if the matzah is theirs in partnership: if one can overpower and his hand prevails over his fellow's hand to snatch from him — if he does so it is invalid, for stolen matzah is called [invalid]. Still, there is room to question per what is written siman 475 in the name of Machatzit HaShekel, that in any case there is somewhat of a mitzvah in half a measure, see there, that he brought from Ritva in Yoma regarding lechem hapanim that its pay is doubled — it seems from his words that he is uncertain in the law of half a perutah in fulfillment of mitzvot; per this, each one should stand in his place to fulfill somewhat of a mitzvah in any case, and the other is not permitted to waive to his fellow that he fulfill all the mitzvot and he himself not fulfill at all. But it appears that even per this view it is preferable that they cast lots; and if one wins to fulfill the mitzvah in its completeness, his portion will be with him in the reward of the mitzvah that through him was completed — and were it not for him the mitzvah would not have been done in its completeness; and compare Yissachar and Zevulun and many such cases, that the mitzvah in its completeness is very much greater than the reward of a partial mitzvah that he fulfills himself; and when it is fulfilled in its completeness through the other, behold he benefits and benefits his brother with him, and this is not like one who waives a mitzvah he has to perform, since he acts by lot; and also when he gives his fellow his portion through appeasement and reconciliation, it appears we have no issue with it, since this causes fulfillment of the mitzvah in its completeness; and all the more so per what is written, the view of Shabbos 38 and Bnei Chayei, that half a perutah has no fulfillment of a mitzvah at all; and see A.Azar in responsa 478 regarding honoring one's fellow in mitzvot of covering or milah, that he brings from what Chazal said in Yoma that one says to his fellow, "Come, let us measure spice and be perfumed — I and you," see there; and all the more so here, where he is not at all able to fulfill the mitzvot properly and he grants acquittal to his fellow — behold his reward is with him and he elevates him as if he did them himself; and all the more so where there is here a kezayit and half a matzah — the mitzvah is not to divide into halves, but only to divide a kezayit alone and half a kezayit alone; and they cast lots, or one appeases his fellow to leave him the whole kezayit, and it is fine, as appears to me.
