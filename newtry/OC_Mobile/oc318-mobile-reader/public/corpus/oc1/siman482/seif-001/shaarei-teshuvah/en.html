In Responsa Beit Ephraim siman 47 I wrote a proper resolution of the Rosh and also some resolution for the Taz's words about the Rosh. Birkei Yosef writes in the name of his grandfather, citing the manuscript Tzror HaChayim: I heard in the name of my teacher the Rashba that one who came by ship and did not reach land until the fifteenth should not make the seder by day; it is not like Kiddush, which can be recited by day, for regarding matzah they said it is eaten only at night. It is obvious the same applies to anyone prevented by force on Pesach night: he has no make-up by day. So writes Magen Avraham siman 485, cited in Ba'er Heitev siman 473.
(1) Maror. See Ba'er Heitev. See Responsa Beit Yehudah siman 58, concerning two people in prison or the desert who have only exactly one kezayit of matzah, who takes precedence. He wishes to resolve it from the Mishnah in Yoma, "when they are many," and concludes that everything depends on whoever is stronger. This is not clear, for in such a case it becomes stolen matzah. Even if it is not worth a perutah, the requirement of "yours" for matzah is not because of mitzvah haba'ah ba'aveirah, but because we derive bread from bread from challah, where "your doughs" excludes stolen; if it is stolen and not his, even below a perutah, he does not fulfill. There is some room to reject this, for perhaps because it is less than a perutah and is presumed forgiven, it is not called theft at all.
There is some implication from Tosafot at the beginning of Lulav HaGazul: they ask why the verse "yours" is needed for matzah; let it be invalid because of mitzvah haba'ah ba'aveirah. If a stolen kezayit worth less than a perutah were invalid because "yours" is required, though not a mitzvah haba'ah ba'aveirah, this would answer their question. This implies that below a perutah it is not called stolen for this purpose. Rashi on Sukkah 27 also says that all Israel can sit in one sukkah because each share is not worth a perutah, implying that below a perutah is not treated as a separate ownership defect unless all shares combine. Still, this proof is not decisive. It is possible Rashi means only that since each person's share in the place is less than a perutah, the place he sits in is not his alone but owned by many partners who lend their shares to each other, proving borrowed sukkah is valid. It does not prove that benefit below a perutah is as nothing. Moreover, even less than a perutah involves theft, since half a measure is forbidden by Torah law, as explained in Choshen Mishpat siman 398. One could also say that since it is fit for a mitzvah it is worth a perutah to him, though perhaps mitzvot were not given for benefit. This requires study.
It appears more likely that Beit Yehudah speaks where the kezayit of matzah was ownerless and neither had a right in it. In that case it seems clear that whoever can acquire it may acquire it, all the more than monetary ownerless property where the first to acquire takes it, as with leket, shikhechah, and pe'ah, and as in Bava Kamma where one can say, "I acquired from ownerless property" or "I am saving for myself." Even where the property originally belonged to another and became ownerless, the first acquirer precedes the prior owner. If this is true in bodily life, all the more in spiritual life. However, if the matzah belongs to them jointly, and one overpowers the other to seize it, his act does not help and it is called stolen matzah. Still, there is room to doubt whether each should keep his own part to fulfill at least a partial mitzvah, according to the view that half a measure has some mitzvah value. It seems better that they cast lots, so that one fulfills the mitzvah completely, and the other's share in enabling the complete mitzvah is credited to him, like Yissachar and Zevulun and similar cases. This is not like relinquishing a mitzvah he could perform, since it is by lottery; and if one appeases the other and willingly gives his share so the mitzvah be fulfilled completely, that too is proper. All the more according to the views that half a measure has no mitzvah fulfillment at all. If there is a kezayit and a half of matzah, it is best not to divide equally into halves, but to give one whole kezayit and one half-kezayit, by lottery or willing agreement.
