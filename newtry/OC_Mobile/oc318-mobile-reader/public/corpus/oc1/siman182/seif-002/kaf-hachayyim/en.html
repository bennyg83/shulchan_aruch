6) [Seif 2] If wine is not commonly available, etc. The Tur in siman 272 writes that some explain that a beverage is not considered the wine of the province unless wine does not grow within a day's journey surrounding the entire city. Bach cites this in the present siman and writes that accordingly, even in a city where much wine is available from merchants who bring it from places where wine grows, the city is considered a place where wine is not commonly available, since it does not grow within a day's journey surrounding the city. One may therefore recite Birkas HaMazon over a cup of barley beer if he has no wine at home. Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah there, paragraph 4, however, writes that the cited opinion said this only to teach that if wine is found within one day's journey of the city, even though wine is not commonly available in that place, beer is not called the wine of the province. If much wine is available in the city, however, even if it is brought from more than a day's journey away, it is obvious that one does not recite the blessing over beer, since wine is available in that place. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 4, appears to agree, and Beis Menuchah, paragraph 3, rules likewise.
7) There: If wine is not commonly available, etc. Bach writes that one authority ruled that Birkas HaMazon should be recited over a cup of barley beer because it is the wine of the province and also belongs to one of the seven species, and the seven species takes precedence. Bach rejects this. Although the halachah follows R' Yehudah that a species of the seven takes precedence, as stated below in siman 211, this applies when he wishes to eat both foods and their blessings are identical. He then recites the blessing over the seven species and covers the favored food. If he does not wish to eat the species of the seven, however, it is obvious that even according to R' Yehudah he recites the blessing over what he presently wishes to eat. Terumas HaDeshen, siman 32, likewise writes that since they established their drinking upon mead and do not wish to drink barley beer, which they do not favor, it is obvious that Birkas HaMazon should be recited over mead. Moreover, mead is also the wine of the province, just like barley beer. Knesses HaGedolah there, paragraph 2, and Olas Tamid, paragraph 2, cite this, the latter writing that the Shelah rules likewise; see there. Taz, paragraph 1; Magen Avraham, paragraph 2; Beis Menuchah, paragraph 6. Magen Avraham writes that these words apply only in countries where people regularly drink mead, making it the wine of the province. In a place where it is drunk only occasionally, it is not the wine of the province, just like other beverages such as apple wine and pomegranate wine. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 5, Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 1, and Beis Menuchah, paragraph 7, write the same.
