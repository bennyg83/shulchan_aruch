[Levush] And if wine is commonly available, etc. In Krakow, although wine is commonly available, since it is brought from far away it is treated as not commonly available. Magen Avraham in the name of the Bach. He did not see Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah, who disagrees and holds that since wine is commonly found there, it is considered available. Mordechai writes that even in a place where wine is generally available, if the wine crop was ruined that year it is treated as unavailable.
Except water, etc. This is so even if water is the beverage most commonly drunk. The same applies to kvass and borscht even in Russia, as the Taz writes, and likewise licorice beverage drunk on Pesach. The later authorities write that where people drink mead and beer and wine is unavailable, one should use mead for Birkat HaMazon if it is more beloved than beer. This is included in the principle that one's table should not be full of inferior items. This applies only in a place where mead qualifies as chamar medinah. Where it is drunk only occasionally it is not chamar medinah, like other beverages such as apple or pomegranate wine.
Expensive, etc. Nevertheless, if he has wine in his house he must use it for Birkat HaMazon unless he needs to reserve it for Kiddush. Magen Avraham. The same applies to Havdalah.
