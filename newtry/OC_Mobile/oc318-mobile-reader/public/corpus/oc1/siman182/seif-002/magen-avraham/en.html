Wine is available. In Tur siman 272 it is written that if wine does not grow within a day's journey around the entire city, it is called a place where wine is not available; and even if much wine is available at the shopkeeper, that is of no consequence, as Bach writes. It implies that if a little wine grows near the city, that is of no consequence. Bach wrote that at large meals, where they drink beer during the meal and after the meal establish themselves to drink mead, he should bless over mead, for it too is chamar medinah, and furthermore it is preferred. Even according to the one who says that one of the seven species has precedence, that is where he wants to eat from both, as written in siman 211 seif 5, end quote. It appears to me that beer is not included among the seven species, for its blessing is shehakol; see siman 211, and so wrote Shelah, and see siman 483. It appears to me that these words apply only in countries where people are accustomed to drink mead and it is chamar medinah, but in a place where they drink it only occasionally, it is not chamar medinah, just like other beverages such as apple wine and pomegranate wine. Rashal wrote: the halachah is that Birkat Hamazon requires a cup even for an individual; and in pressing circumstances, where he has no beer that is chamar medinah, he may bless over any beverage, such as kvass or borscht, since most common people drink from them. It is forbidden to rule this way before the masses, so that they not come to be lenient even when it is not pressing circumstances. This applies specifically in the countries of Russia, where they are accustomed to drink these kinds. It appears to me that beverages people are accustomed to drink on Pesach, such as apple water and licorice water, are not chamar medinah, since they are not accustomed to drink them all year; and the same applies to anything similar. See siman 483.
Except for water. Although most of their drinking is water, one does not bless over it.
And they establish the meal. Meaning, there are those who say that if one established his meal over other beverages, he blesses over them even in a place where there is wine.
And wine cannot be purchased. But if he has wine in his home, he must bless over it, for this is not called beautification, as Bach writes. It appears to me that if he has a little wine in his house for Kiddush, he does not need to bless over it, for according to everyone Kiddush requires a cup and is more stringent than Birkat Hamazon.
Not to hold it. I do not know why. Although Midrash HaNe'elam holds that an individual does not need a cup, nevertheless it is preferable to bless over a cup; therefore it appears to me that he should hold it in his hand. Furthermore, it appears that even those who are meticulous said this only regarding wine, but regarding other beverages that are chamar medinah they agree that he should hold it in his hand. So too it appears from the language of Beit Yosef and Rama, as Bach writes. Mahariv, siman 193, wrote to raise it a tefach, and perhaps he speaks where one takes it and afterward sets it down, as Knesset HaGedolah says. However, in the writings they wrote not to take it in his hand, but to place his right hand over his left opposite the chest and close his eyes.
