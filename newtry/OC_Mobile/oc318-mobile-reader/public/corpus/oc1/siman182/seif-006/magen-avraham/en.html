They can repair it. The principal defect is that someone drank a little from it. When one adds more wine, he thereby repairs it, analogous to a blemish in the altar, which is repaired when one fills it in again. This also appears from Bach in the name of Maharam Mintz. Bach writes that according to this, if one returns the wine from the cup to the pitcher, all the wine becomes pagum. It appears to me, however, that there the pagum wine is nullified in the wine in the pitcher by the majority, whereas here the added wine repairs the entire cup. This must be the correct reading in the Tur. Furthermore, when permitted liquid falls into prohibited liquid, we do not say that each first portion becomes nullified as it enters, as Beit Yosef writes in the name of the Ran regarding wine that falls into water.
Water repairs it. Perhaps specifically wine is repaired by water, since wine is commonly diluted with water, but other beverages are not. Alternatively, any liquid may be repaired if the added liquid does not spoil it, as explained in siman 296, seif 3.
