And some say that this is unnecessary. This is the view of the Ra'ah cited by Beit Yosef, who holds that only the one reciting the blessing requires a cup that is not pagum. It appears that he has proof from the incident at the end of the third chapter of Berachos. Ulla recited Bircas HaMazon in the presence of Rav Nachman. Rav Nachman said to Ulla, ‘Let the master send the cup of blessing to Yalta.’ He answered, ‘Rabbi Yochanan said that the fruit of a woman's womb is blessed only from the fruit of a man's body.’ Meanwhile Yalta heard, arose in anger, and broke four hundred barrels of wine. Rav Nachman said, ‘Let the master send her another cup.’ Ulla sent her the message, ‘All the wine in this vessel is wine of blessing.’ Rashi explains that all the wine in the barrel is considered like the cup of blessing, so she should drink from it. This implies that when one recites the blessing over a cup that is not pagum, the blessing extends to all the wine, even wine that is pagum, for no blessing was recited over the wine in the barrel. Accordingly, being free of pagum has no independent advantage; rather, one cup alone requires the blessing, and all the other wine follows it regardless of its condition. Beit Yosef likewise cites Hagahos Maimoniyos in siman 206: ‘They further said that all the vessels are vessels of blessing, which shows that the blessing takes effect upon everything placed before him.’ This implies even if it is pagum. So it appears to me.
