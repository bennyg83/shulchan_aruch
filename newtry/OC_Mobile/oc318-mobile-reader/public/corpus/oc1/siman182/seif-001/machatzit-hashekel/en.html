(Seif katan 1) ‘And he should not eat,’ etc. As we find regarding Ameimar in Pesachim 107a.
This requires study, for granted, etc. But regarding Bircas HaMazon, etc. Eliyah Rabbah writes: ‘It appears to me that Pesachim 105 states that one may eat before Havdalah in order to recite the blessing over the cup, as Rashba writes in responsum 342 and Tosafos on 102b.’ His intent is as follows. Tosafos on Pesachim 102b, s.v. manicho, and more explicitly the Rosh, write regarding Ameimar, who remained fasting rather than eat before Havdalah: The Gemara earlier, on 102b, states that one who enters his house on Motzaei Shabbos and has only one cup should set it aside until after the meal, recite Bircas HaMazon over it, and afterward recite Havdalah. This implies that one may eat before Havdalah. Rabbeinu Yitzchak answers that if one has no cup but expects to find one the next day, he should not eat that night, but should wait until the next day and recite Havdalah over the cup. This was the case involving Ameimar. But if he has one cup and does not expect to find more wine the next day, it is preferable that he eat before Havdalah and recite Bircas HaMazon and Havdalah over that cup, rather than recite Havdalah over the cup and Bircas HaMazon without a cup. So writes the Rosh in siman 17, and so write Tosafos and the Rosh in Berachos; this is also the ruling below in siman 296, seif 3. The meaning is that even regarding Havdalah, one is required to postpone eating only for the duration of one meal. Thus, if he expects to have a cup the next day, he may not eat that night and must wait until the next day. If he does not expect to have a cup the next day, however, and would therefore need to forgo two meals, the Sages did not burden him. Here too, although he has one cup, he does not expect to have another cup the next day. If we say that he should first recite Havdalah, because eating before Havdalah is prohibited, then after Havdalah he would be permitted to eat even though he lacks a cup for Bircas HaMazon. For even according to the Tur, who requires one to postpone a meal when he lacks a cup for Bircas HaMazon, that applies only when he expects to have a cup for a later meal. If he does not expect to have a cup for the following meal, he may eat immediately without a cup, because the Sages did not burden him to forgo two meals, just as regarding Havdalah. Consequently, since here he does not expect to have a cup the next day, he would be permitted to eat after Havdalah and recite Bircas HaMazon without a cup. It is therefore preferable that he eat before Havdalah and recite both Bircas HaMazon and Havdalah over the one cup. We thus see that eating without a cup for Bircas HaMazon is treated more stringently than eating before Havdalah. Since regarding Havdalah he must delay one meal, all the more so regarding Bircas HaMazon, if he lacks a cup but expects to obtain one. Eliyah Rabbah spoke very well on this matter.
If he has only one cup, Kiddush of the day takes precedence. Meaning, on Friday night, if he has only one cup and no wine to drink during the meal, whether by night or by day, although drinking wine during the meal is part of honoring Shabbos, Kiddush nevertheless takes precedence. He should therefore recite Kiddush at night even though he will have no wine to drink during either the nighttime or daytime meal.
Another difficulty, etc. Nevertheless, a cup is required, etc. Shmuel and Rav both said that Shehecheyanu is recited only on the three pilgrimage festivals, but not on Rosh Hashanah or Yom Kippur. A Baraisa challenges this: ‘Give a portion to seven, and also to eight.’ Rabbi Yehoshua says: Seven refers to the seven days of Pesach; eight refers to the eight days of Sukkos. When it says ‘and also,’ this includes Shavuos, Rosh Hashanah, and Yom Kippur. What is the meaning of ‘Shehecheyanu’? The Gemara answers that it refers not to Shehecheyanu but to the blessing, meaning the middle blessing established for Yom Tov, Rosh Hashanah, and Yom Kippur. This must be so, for if it referred to Shehecheyanu, is Shehecheyanu recited on all seven days? The Gemara rejects this: perhaps it indeed refers to Shehecheyanu, and if one did not recite it on the first day, he may make it up during all seven or eight days. It then asks: nevertheless, a cup should be required for Shehecheyanu. This supports Rav Nachman, who holds that Shehecheyanu does not require a cup.
Even when Motzaei Shabbos coincides with Yom Tov. Magen Avraham likewise writes below in siman 296, seif katan 8, in the name of the Rif.
Regarding Havdalah, some are lenient, because they hold that Ameimar's fasting was merely an act of stringency and piety.
