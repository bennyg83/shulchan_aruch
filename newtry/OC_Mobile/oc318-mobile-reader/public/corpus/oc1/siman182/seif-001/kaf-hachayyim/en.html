1) [Seif 1] Some say Birkas HaMazon requires a cup even for an individual; some say it requires a cup only with three; and some say it does not require a cup at all, etc. Since Maran, of blessed memory, places the third opinion last, this implies that he rules accordingly in practice, as stated above in siman 13, paragraph 7; see there. Furthermore, this is the opinion of Rif and Rambam, as stated in Beis Yosef, and it is known that Maran, of blessed memory, rules according to the majority among the three pillars of halachic ruling: Rif, Rambam, and Rosh. The Zohar and the Arizal, however, follow the second opinion, that specifically with three does Birkas HaMazon require a cup, but not for an individual. They give a mystical reason, as the Zohar, parashas Terumah, 157b, states: A cup of blessing applies only with three, because it is blessed from the mystery of the three Patriarchs, and therefore a cup is required only with three. Parashas Pinchas, 246a, likewise states: Three require a cup. Why? Binah is the third of the ten Sefiros, meaning that the order of the ten Sefiros from above to below is Keser, Chochmah, Binah, etc., so Binah is third. Therefore, fewer than three do not require a cup; see there. Shaar HaMitzvos, parashas Eikev, similarly writes that an individual should not recite Birkas HaMazon over a cup, as stated in parashas Terumah. If three are present and there is wine, they should recite Birkas HaMazon over the wine; see there. R' Meir Poppers similarly writes in Or Tzaddikim, siman 23, paragraph 33, that with three one recites Birkas HaMazon over a cup, but without three there is no cup. He likewise writes in Derech Seudah that according to Kabbalah, when one recites Birkas HaMazon without the Zimun of three, it is forbidden to hold a cup of Birkas HaMazon; see there. We find that the Zohar and the Arizal hold that specifically three recite Birkas HaMazon over a cup, whereas an individual may not do so. We have already written above in siman 25, paragraph 75, that regarding a matter not explicitly mentioned in the Gemara and disputed by the halachic authorities, the Kabbalists' opinion is decisive; see there. The cited words of the Zohar also imply no distinction between Shabbos and a weekday, contrary to one who writes that even an individual should recite the blessing over a cup on Shabbos, cited by Zechor LeAvraham, under kaf. Shaar HaKavanos likewise does not state in its discourses on the Shabbos meals that Birkas HaMazon should be recited over a cup. It apparently relies upon the statement in Shaar HaMitzvos, parashas Eikev, that specifically three recite it over a cup. Understand this. If three people ate together and no wine is present, they nevertheless recite the Zimun even without wine. Shaar HaMitzvos there.
2) There: It requires a cup, etc. This is because using a cup is a manner of honor and a beautiful praise, arranging one's praise and blessing of Hashem, may He be exalted, over a cup, as the verse states, 'I shall raise the cup of salvations,' etc. According to Kabbalah, it also contains a great secret, for it alludes to the tenth attribute, called the cup of salvations, which receives from them all and bestows blessings and sustenance upon the entire world. Understand this. Levush. By 'the tenth attribute,' he means the aspect of Malchus, as is known. The Zohar, parashas Eikev, 273b, likewise writes that the cup of Birkas HaMazon corresponds to Malchus; see there. This is also implied by the Zohar in parashas Terumah cited in the preceding paragraph. When parashas Pinchas says Binah, it means the Binah of Malchus, or that Malchus receives illumination from Binah. Both are true, as is known. See above, siman 157, paragraph 24, and understand this well. According to the cited reasons of the Zohar in parashas Terumah and parashas Eikev, and according to Levush, it appears that women do not recite the blessing over a cup even when they make a Zimun for themselves, as is known to those acquainted with the hidden wisdom.
3) There: He should not eat if he has no cup, etc. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1, writes that this statement is not found among the halachic authorities other than in the Tur. He raises many difficulties and concludes that one who is lenient loses nothing; see there. See, however, Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2, who resolves Magen Avraham's difficulty. Nevertheless, he concludes that it may be permitted because some say Birkas HaMazon does not require a cup; see there. See the following paragraph.
4) There, gloss: Nevertheless, it is the preferred fulfillment of the mitzvah, etc. This implies that even for an individual it is the preferred fulfillment to recite the blessing over a cup. Levush and Beis Menuchah, paragraph 1, write the same. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1, however, holds that this preferred fulfillment applies specifically with three, and writes that this is the practice; see there. Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, siman 45, paragraph 1, and Ben Ish Chai, parashas Shelach Lecha, paragraph 16, write the same. This is also implied by the Zohar and Shaar HaMitzvos cited above in paragraph 1: even with three it is only the preferred fulfillment, while an individual should not recite the blessing over a cup; see there.
5) The custom of pious people when reciting Birkas HaMazon over a cup is to place the bread on the table to the left of the person leading the blessing, so that the cup of blessing is in his right hand and the bread at his left. There is a mystical reason for this. Ben Ish Chai there, paragraph 17.
