(s.k. 3) It became, etc. — it became a wall. So in the gemara daf 125b regarding a stone in a gourd, and from it Magen Avraham also inferred regarding a basket — for in the gemara it asks on a basket and it should be a basket as a base for another object, namely for the stone; and R' Yochanan answers: in a basket full of fruits we deal; and it asks — let us throw them for the fruits and for the stone, and afterward gather the fruits from the ground; and it answers: with fruits that spoil, namely moist and wet; and it asks — let us shake the fruits to one side inside the basket and the stone will remain on the other side, and then shake the stone alone; rather R' Chiya bar Abba said: here we deal with a small basket, for the stone became a wall for the basket; and according to this version the first establishment is rejected and there is no permission except in a small basket; but Rambam and those who follow him did not have the gemara's question "let us shake the fruits to one side," etc.; and also they do not read except "R' Chiya bar Abba said: here we deal with a small basket," etc.; and there are two establishments — R' Yochanan establishes it in a basket full of spoiling fruits, and Rav Chiya bar Abba establishes it without fruits but the basket is small; and both hold both are true in law; and Rif and Rosh omitted all this back-and-forth; and Rabbeinu Yehonatan wrote their reason — they had our version where the first establishment is rejected and there is permission only in a small basket, and they did not need to copy this since they relied on what they wrote regarding a stone inside a gourd — see there; if so, necessarily the law of a stone inside a basket and inside a gourd is the same; therefore Magen Avraham's comparison of a stone inside a basket that must be packed tight to a stone inside a gourd — also logically they should be equated:
