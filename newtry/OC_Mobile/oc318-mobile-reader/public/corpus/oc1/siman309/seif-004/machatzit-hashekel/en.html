(s.k. 4) He takes, etc. and they do not burden, etc. — this is from Tosafos daf 142a — we learned: impure terumah is moved with pure terumah; and R' Chiya said: they taught only when pure is below and impure above; but pure above, etc. — he takes the pure and leaves the impure; and the gemara asks: even if pure is below, nevertheless he should shake it; and it answers: with moist fruits that spoil; and Tosafos challenged: for according to this, even with spoiling fruits, if pure is above — forbidden to move impure with pure; rather he must take the pure in his hand alone from inside the basket; and if so, the same applies to a basket with a stone inside — even though full of spoiling fruits, nevertheless he should take the fruits in his hand from inside the basket; and they answered: regarding terumah it deals when the fruits are placed in small baskets inside a large basket — therefore if pure is above he must take the pure in small baskets inside the large basket; but if the pure fruits are placed one on another without small baskets, then he need not gather them one by one from inside the large basket — end of their words; and according to plain understanding Magen Avraham understood the distinction between placed in small baskets or not — when placed inside baskets there is not so much trouble, therefore he must take the pure; unlike when placed without small baskets, where he must take each fruit alone — there is much trouble, therefore the Rabbis did not burden him; and with this Magen Avraham's words are resolved; but in Sefer Teshuvah he challenged Magen Avraham — Maharshal explained in the name of Teshuvah Yerushalayim the reason: why when not placed in baskets they did not require gathering in hand — lest they fall from his hand to the ground and become soiled, since they are moist fruits; and presumably our Tosafos too have this intent — not because of trouble; and accordingly he also challenged what Magen Avraham wrote siman 310 s.k. 10 to resolve Rabbeinu Yehonatan's question — he should gather the dough alone in hand since there is much trouble they did not burden him; and in truth Tosafos' intent is not because of trouble, as stated — see there; and in my humble opinion Magen Avraham necessarily understood Tosafos' intent to distinguish between placed in small baskets or not, as Teshuvah Yerushalayim wrote — when not placed inside baskets — lest they fall from his hand; for otherwise it would be difficult for Magen Avraham who holds gathering fruits one by one is much trouble and the Rabbis did not burden him — if so, why with dry fruits that do not spoil must he shake the stone with the fruits? For certainly he will not leave them on the ground — rather after shaking to the ground he gathers one by one and places them back in the basket so there will be no stone, since he shook it — behold that is much trouble; on the contrary, to gather from the ground scattered by the shaking is much more trouble than gathering from the basket where they rest in one place — if so, this is not called much trouble; and the reason they did not require gathering from the basket is lest they go to the ground, as Teshuvah Yerushalayim wrote; but it was difficult for Magen Avraham according to Teshuvah Yerushalayim — the reason lest they fall to the ground and become soiled — behold a reasonable person can be careful that no fruit falls to the ground; therefore Magen Avraham holds Teshuvah Yerushalayim's intent is to make excellent guarding and extra caution that no fruit falls to the ground — this is called extra trouble and they did not burden him so much; and Magen Avraham holds below siman 310 that gathering ash which is a very fine thing — even without "lest it fall to the ground" — nevertheless is much trouble and similar to the trouble of fruits when one must be careful that none fall to the ground; and if so, Magen Avraham's words there too are resolved:
