(13) He forgot a stone, etc. — for when placing intentionally on the mouth of the barrel — behold the barrel itself is muktzeh and forbidden to tilt, for it became a base for the stone, as below:

(14) On its side — and it deals when he needs to take from the wine inside; and so with a pillow when he needs to lie on it; therefore they permitted moving muktzeh in this manner that he does not move it directly — only via shaking; but if he does not need them at all, only fears loss of the muktzeh itself lest it be stolen — even shaking is forbidden, so Magen Avraham in the name of Rabbeinu Yerucham; and if he tilts the barrel and pillow with his body and not with his hand — permitted even in this manner:

(15) And the coins fall — and he can move the pillow afterward since the muktzeh was removed from it. And nevertheless permitted to study on Shabbat on the table even while coins or other muktzeh is on it from another side — for even though it is customary to sway with study — nevertheless it is not psik reisha [Maharil]:

(16) In a manner that he cannot, etc. — that he fears lest it fall on the barrels and break them:

(17) He can lift it, etc. — for behold it is only moving from the side, since he does not move the muktzeh directly — only with the permitted; and his intent is also for the permitted, as stated; and this is permitted as below siman 311 seif 8; and nevertheless at first when it was possible via shaking — shaking specifically is required:

(18) With intent that they remain, etc. — but if he placed them intentionally on a weekday and his intent was not explicitly that they remain there on Shabbat and afterward he forgot them there — it is called forgetting [writings of Mahariv]; and Beit Yosef's view — even if he placed them on erev Shabbat, as long as his intent was not explicitly that they remain there at entry of Shabbat, and at bein hashemashos he forgot to remove — this too is in the category of forgetting. And behold regarding the essence of the law of base, Magen Avraham concludes it is not called base unless he placed a muktzeh object on it in order that it settle well upon it; but what people place incidentally, as is customary to place objects in a box one on another because he has no room to clear a place for each object at the edges of the box — such a case is not called placing but forgetting, and permitted to move the permitted object after shaking the muktzeh off it [and nevertheless the box itself according to all is a base for a forbidden object if the muktzeh is more important and forbidden to move it — for in any case his intent was that muktzeh rest in the box. Chayei Adam]; and accordingly what people do on Shabbat — remove the upper tablecloth from the table after removing the forbidden object, the menorah, from upon it — as is proper to do, for the menorah need not be specifically on the tablecloth but on the table, and he does not stand it on the tablecloth except because he cannot clear space for the menorah on the table itself, or because he is not concerned to clear space for it; therefore the tablecloth did not become a base for the menorah upon it that it be forbidden to move [and with the lower tablecloth all this is unnecessary — for behold in our time the way is to place lechem on it on erev Shabbat and it becomes a base for this, which is more important]; and some are strict in all this; and in a place of need it appears one may be lenient, for many Acharonim hold like Magen Avraham. A chicken coop and there is an egg with a chick in it — that egg is forbidden to move even if he did not know from beforehand that the egg would be there — nevertheless since it is customary thus it is like placing and not like forgetting, and the coop became a base for the egg; and so in every similar place with something whose way is that muktzeh is certainly upon it — it is like placing:

(19) At entry of Shabbat — meaning all the time of bein hashemashos:

(20) Forbidden to tilt and shake — for the barrel and pillow became a base [meaning: yes, and place of rest — Aramaic translation of "kanu" to "basisah"] for the muktzeh upon them, and they themselves are muktzeh:

(21) And some say that even, etc. — they hold it does not become a base at all since he thought to remove the muktzeh afterward; and in a place of loss one may rely on this view [Acharonim]. And know: even according to this view it is reasonable that specifically when he thought explicitly to remove afterward the muktzeh on Shabbat [via shaking or via another object], or something whose way is to remove muktzeh afterward; but if he thought stam that muktzeh would rest upon it on Shabbat — according to all it becomes a base even if he did not think explicitly it would rest all Shabbat:

(22) At entry of Shabbat — and the same even if he thought it would rest all day until some time before sunset and afterward remove them too — according to them it is not called base; and if he reconsidered afterward to remove the muktzeh earlier — permitted to shake, since it is not for all Shabbat day — base did not apply at all; but if he thought to remove at bein hashemashos — it is like one who thought for all day, for at bein hashemashos there is doubt of night [Peri Megadim]:

(23) And then even if removed, etc. — this applies also to the first view — that even if he placed only for entry of Shabbat and it was removed afterward — nevertheless the utensil is forbidden all Shabbat:

(24) And likewise with all muktzeh — such as dried figs and raisins that dried on Shabbat and became fit for eating — nevertheless they are muktzeh all Shabbat since they were muktzeh at bein hashemashos:

(25) A muktzeh object, etc. — and even if the muktzeh object is also his fellow's:

(26) We do not say, etc. — meaning permitted to move the utensil when he needs it after shaking the muktzeh from it:

(27) Without his knowledge — but if he did so for his fellow's benefit and presumably he is pleased — such as Reuven took a utensil and stood it intentionally under a candle in Shimon's house so the candle fall into it and there not be a fire, and the candle fell into it before bein hashemashos and remained until after bein hashemashos — the utensil became a base for the candle and forbidden to move all Shabbat even after the candle was removed — so many Acharonim; and in Biur Halacha one can lean: if his intent was to remove afterward the muktzeh on Shabbat — we can combine the view of the "some say" above that in this it is not called base at all:
