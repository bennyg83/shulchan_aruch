1) [Seif 1] A person may pick up his son, etc. This means that a person may carry his son in his arms in a courtyard although it is as though he himself holds the stone in his hand. Rava said there that one who carries a living child into the public domain with a wallet hanging from the child's neck is liable because of the wallet. Here, however, no carrying between domains occurs; the concern is only muktzeh because he moves a stone in a courtyard or house. Since the child yearns for his father and might become ill if he is not picked up, the Sages did not uphold their decree where a risk of illness exists, although there is no danger to life. Bach. If you ask why he does not throw the stone from the child's hand, as stated in seif 3, one can answer that the child would shout and cry. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1, in the name of Tosafos. This implies that if the child would not cry, he should be told to throw down the stone before being picked up.
2) There: But if he does not yearn for him, etc. Carrying the child together with the muktzeh is considered as though the person himself moves the muktzeh. Since no illness is involved, it is forbidden. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 3.
3) There: Lest it fall, etc. Since one is ordinarily particular about the dinar, we properly fear that it might fall from the child's hand and that the adult might carry it, thereby directly moving muktzeh. This follows Rashi's explanation. Levush writes that he might carry it four amos in the public domain. His words are difficult, because in the public domain this is certainly forbidden in every case; the permission applies only in a courtyard, where the sole concern is muktzeh. It is possible that this is a scribal error and the words 'four amos in the public domain' should be deleted. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 4. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1, and the later authorities likewise question Levush's words. See, however, Eshel Avraham, paragraph 2, who reconciles them.
4) There: Some say they forbade only, etc. This is Ramban's view. He maintains that we certainly do not fear that the dinar might fall and that the adult might carry it. If we did, it should be forbidden even to stand beside a child holding a dinar. Rather, the Sages forbade only carrying a child holding a dinar, lest people mistakenly say that just as one may carry a child holding a dinar, one may carry the dinar itself in his hand. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 5.
5) There: Some say they did not forbid, etc. Bach rules stringently like the first opinion. Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef, paragraph 1, and Olas Shabbos, paragraph 1, cite this. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 2, also rules this way. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2, however, writes that one may rely upon this opinion because of the risk of illness, and also because Ramban is a later authority and Ran cites his view. These are his words. We already wrote above in siman 13, paragraph 7, that when the Shulchan Aruch first gives an unattributed ruling and then cites an opinion as 'some say,' it intends to rule like the unattributed view. Only in pressing circumstances may one rely upon the opinion introduced as 'some say'; see there.
