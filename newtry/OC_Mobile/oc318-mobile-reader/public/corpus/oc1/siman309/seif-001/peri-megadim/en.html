At entry of Shabbat — behold, the upper tablecloth: if there are no permitted utensils on it and the like — one can say the place where the pillow rests is a base and the rest is permitted to fold, for the rest is not needed for the base; but after removing the candle via a gentile to lift the whole tablecloth — one can say the place of the candle is a base; and the lower tablecloth on which there is permitted — properly it is considered a base for prohibition and permission; and see siman 279 and 276 — even with an oil candle the candle is not muktzeh only because of base; and one can say regarding covering a stone — it was muktzeh at bein hashemashos, for if he wants he can shake at bein hashemashos; unlike an oil candle — for it needs a candle at night, Shabbat candle is obligatory, and properly it was muktzeh all day; and see siman 279. And a stone on the mouth of a barrel — when placing intentionally the barrel becomes a base even though there is wine inside, as Rashi explained Shabbat 142:2 — to take wine from the barrel; nevertheless above, the barrel does not serve the wine except on the sides, and above it serves only the stone — it is a base; and this is what Levush wrote on an empty utensil; and Orach Mishor letter 9 at its end; and Tosefes Shabbos; and see siman 311 in Taz letter 10 and Magen Avraham 23 — there is no distinction between foods and utensils, only if it is a base and serves muktzeh then forbidden even after removing the muktzeh; and if muktzeh serves the permitted — it is not a base even if resting on it; and if not so, it will be explained there further. And there Taz wrote: at minimum to move the menorah moving from the side; and in truth even directly it is permitted when no candle or oil of the menorah remains — a utensil whose function is for prohibition is permitted for its place; he only said "at minimum moving from the side" — plainly permitted.
