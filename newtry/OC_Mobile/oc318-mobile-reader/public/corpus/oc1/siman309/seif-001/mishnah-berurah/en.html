(1) And the stone in his hand — and it deals when he is in a courtyard, for there is only prohibition of moving; therefore they did not forbid this because of the child's distress, as below; but in reshut ha-rabbim — certainly forbidden because of the prohibition of hotzaah — for when he carries the child it is as if he carries the stone in his hand [gemara]:
(2) Longing, etc. — and therefore they did not forbid moving not with the hands in a place of danger of illness; and if you ask — let him throw the stone from his hand, as is written in seif 3 that he must shake the stone from the basket — one can say the child will cry out and weep. And if the stone fell from the child's hand — forbidden to lift and give to him, for this is moving with the hands; rather he lowers the child from his shoulder and the child lifts the stone and returns and carries him:
(3) But if he does not have, etc. — for what he moves the child with muktzeh is as if he himself moves the muktzeh — and forbidden:
(4) And his father comes to bring — meaning he ends up moving muktzeh with his hands; and they did not permit this even in a place touching concern of the son's illness, since it is not concern of danger to life:
(5) With a dinar in his hand — meaning then they can err and say: just as they permitted for the child to carry him when the dinar is in his hand, even though the dinar is moved incidentally — so too permitted to carry the dinar itself in hand after it fell and give it to the child's hand:
