[1] [Levush] To carry it four cubits, etc. His words are surprising, because the case concerns a courtyard, not a public domain, as Rashi explains at the beginning of chapter Notel, and Responsa Rosh, rule 22, likewise implies. The concern is moving muktzeh at all; even merely lifting it from one place to another is prohibited. See siman 308, seif 5, where even causing it to move slightly is prohibited. Therefore Tur and Shulchan Aruch write only "he may come to carry it" and do not mention four cubits. I did, however, find in Rashi printed with Rif the wording "he may come to carry it in the public domain." This too is surprising. The phrase "if he has no, etc." refers to an ordinary child, as Sefer HaTerumah writes. If the child holds it in his hand, however, it is permitted.
[2] And some say, etc. Bach rules stringently like the first view. In my humble opinion Rambam is sufficiently authoritative to rely upon where illness poses danger, and Ramban is also a later authority, and Ran cites him.
[3] [Levush] For it is like moving, etc. Meaning that one may then come to move the coin by itself after seeing that carrying it together with the child was permitted. This concern does not exist when the child holds it in his own hand. Malbushei Yom Tov's objection is therefore unnecessary.
