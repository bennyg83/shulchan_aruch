(s.k. 8) On Yom Tov etc. Even though they are two sanctities. And if so, Beis Yosef, who ruled like Rashi to be lenient on the second Yom Tov by reason of "whichever way you turn," if so also on Yom Tov adjacent to Shabbos it should have been fit to permit, even though "whichever way you turn" does not apply; nevertheless, since they are two sanctities, it is enough if he waits at the departure of Yom Tov toward Shabbos, or the reverse, the time it takes for them to be done. And even though nevertheless on Shabbos after Yom Tov or the reverse, even if he waited the time it takes for them to be done, he still benefits from melacha of Yom Tov, for on Shabbos after Yom Tov or the reverse it would not have been possible to do it himself: if so, also on the two Diaspora Yom Tov days, which Rashi permits on the night of the second day after waiting the time it takes for them to be done by reason of "whichever way you turn," the same difficulty applies, for of what benefit is it that he waited the time it takes for them to be done when on the second Yom Tov he cannot do it himself? And even so Rashi permits; and likewise regarding adjacent Yom Tov and Shabbos, since we do not say they are two sanctities it would have been fit to permit; and so is clarified in the Gemara in Beitza, folio 4a, regarding an egg born on Yom Tov adjacent to Shabbos; see there.
Because of preparation: since on its day it is forbidden, if one permits on the second day, behold it was prepared from Yom Tov to Shabbos or the reverse, since he did not prepare it for himself on the day it was brought; and this preparation is rabbinic, for the reason Magen Avraham wrote in siman 513, s.k. 15, that according to the Torah even on the first day it is permitted, and if so he prepared it for himself; see there.
Siman 507, seif 2, s.k. 4.
But fish etc. As written at the end of siman 513: Maharshal wrote that a chick born on Shabbos that is forbidden on the Yom Tov after it, by reason of preparation, is not complete preparation like an egg, because it was already in the world; and the same law applies to fish; and see there what is written. But detached fruit is like wood that fell, which Maharshal wrote is called preparation according to the Torah, as Magen Avraham wrote in his name in siman 507. And what Magen Avraham wrote, "detached fruits that are not eaten raw": if they were detached on Yom Tov, Maharshal agrees that it is only rabbinic preparation, for according to the Torah Yom Tov prepared for itself, since according to the Torah they are permitted on Yom Tov; which is not so on Shabbos, where according to the Torah they are forbidden on Shabbos, for it is impossible to cook them; and if so according to the Torah he did not prepare Shabbos for itself; and for this reason we also require that they are not eaten raw, for if they are eaten raw, Shabbos still prepared for itself according to the Torah, since it is possible to eat them raw according to the Torah.
