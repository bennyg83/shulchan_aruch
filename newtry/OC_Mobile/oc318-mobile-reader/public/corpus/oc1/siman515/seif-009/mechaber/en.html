If a Jew sent a gift to his fellow via a non-Jew from before Yom Tov, and the non-Jew was delayed and brought it on Yom Tov from outside the boundary — it' is permitted even to the one for whom it was brought to eat it. However, it is forbidden for any Jew to handle it beyond four cubits, or outside a walled city, or outside a fortified settlement known to have been enclosed for dwelling purposes.This applies specifically when the sender had it in mind the day before, so there is no prohibition of muktzeh. But if he did not have it in mind the day before, even though there is no violation of the boundary laws, it is still forbidden due to muktzeh, since anything belonging to a Jew requires designation before the holiday — and even for one for whom it was not brought it is forbidden.
