(ט) And specifically that his intent was etc. — I set before you the source of the matters, and from it the law will be clarified; thus Beit Yosef in the name of Rashba: that which is permitted — an item brought from outside the techum — specifically in produce of a non-Jew, but in produce of an Israelite forbidden to all, for they are muktzeh, since they are outside the stipulated techum the owners had in mind that an Israelite's requires preparation, but a non-Jew's does not require preparation — end quote; and at the end of the siman he wrote in his name this law: an Israelite who sent a gift etc.; because of this Rama wrote to distinguish here that his intent was upon it; and I have a difficulty: behold the question was a general Israelite who sent a gift etc. — if so Rashba should have answered specifically that his intent was upon it; and it requires further study: if it were muktzeh because it was outside the techum, would his intent upon it not help, as written siman 498 seif 3? And further, in seif 510 we say that fruit that left outside the techum are permitted to city residents who were brought there to eat them, as Rashi wrote, and that teaching in Yom Tov deals where muktzeh is forbidden, as Ran and Maggid Mishneh wrote, and nevertheless permitted; and one can say: since they were at twilight within the techum of the owners, they were not set aside; and all Israelites are permitted to eat with him, for it is obvious that if one person brought an item with him outside the techum, his friend may eat with him; and so Ra'avan; and the reason: a non-Jew's does not need preparation, for the non-Jew sets nothing aside from his intent, and since it is prepared for the owner it is prepared for all; but here the owner already set it aside from his intent, and the giver too did not know at all; therefore forbidden; and so in Levush; nevertheless this requires further study, for it should be only ownerless fruit that came from outside the techum that are permitted to eat even though his intent was not upon it, as stated in Eruvin daf 45b; and so Ran end of Beitza regarding a fattened ox; and Maggid Mishneh chapter 2 wrote: brought from outside the techum — there is no muktzeh concern — see there Gra; behold even though Israelites sent, there is no muktzeh concern; and his reason: specifically regarding animals that are outside the techum we say the owners set them aside from their intent, since they did not bring them into the techum from erev Yom Tov; but here the owners did not set aside from their intent, and it is like one who grants them portions through another, as written siman 397; and even though one cannot grant through a non-Jew, nevertheless there is no muktzeh, for it is no worse than finding ownerless fruit as explained; and so siman 586 seif 22; and per what is written siman 498, for R' Shimon it is permitted — no need for all this; it deals with siman 497 seif 4:
