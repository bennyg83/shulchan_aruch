Permitted even for the one for whom it was brought etc. The reason is that the non-Jew is not bringing from his own [stock] regarding which they decreed lest he say "bring me"; thus in the Rashba. And Maharshal wrote on this that this is specifically according to Rabbeinu Tam who holds the reason is only lest he say [to him], and one can distinguish in this; but according to Rashi who holds [the reason is] so that he not benefit from a prohibition of Yom Tov, here too it was brought from outside the techum for him, and because it is rabbinic they were lenient for another; if so there is no distinction between whether the non-Jew brought or the agent was delayed: nevertheless he benefits from a techum prohibition done for him; and nevertheless for another it is permitted, for it is no more severe than the fruits of a non-Jew; end quote. And I wonder at a great man like him that his heart filled him to disagree with the Rashba in this; and not only that, but the Rashba would be contradicted by his own words, for Maharshal wrote that these words of the Rashba are only for Rabbeinu Tam; and behold the Rashba holds like Rashi, as Beis Yosef wrote in his name in the name of the Maggid Mishneh that the Rashba holds like the Rambam and like Rashi; and I wonder how Maharshal did not notice this, for Beis Yosef's book was before him. And in truth there is nothing difficult here, for certainly even according to Rashi it is permitted, for Rashi wrote that the reason for forbidding the one for whom [food] was brought from outside the techum is that there is no prohibition in it other than a mere fine, as written before this; and a fine applies only where a non-Jew brings for him on Yom Tov and there is concern lest he command him ab initio to bring; which is not so here, where it is not the non-Jew's but a Jew sends to him in a lawful way so that he can arrive before Yom Tov, except that the agent erred and was delayed on the way: why should we fine the Jew to whom it was sent here? Therefore certainly here it is permitted according to everyone, as appears clear to me in my humble opinion. In every place we mentioned in this siman that techumim are rabbinic, Terumat HaDeshen wrote in siman 83 that even if a non-Jew brought a gift on Yom Tov beyond twelve mil, although most Geonim wrote that this is according to the Torah, nevertheless one should not be more stringent in it than regarding things that come from outside the techum, for there is no decree here lest he tell him to bring, since it is uncommon that he would tell him to bring from so far. At first glance it appears to me that regarding a matter whose muktzeh permission depends specifically on designation on erev Yom Tov or Shabbos, such as those laws mentioned in siman 28, it is permitted for a person to make that designation on the first Diaspora Yom Tov day for the next day by force of "whichever way you turn," and it is permitted for everyone on the second Yom Tov, for Rabbeinu Tam's stringency of lest he tell him to bring etc. does not apply here; and if so it would be well permitted after waiting the time it takes for them to be done on Motzei the first Yom Tov; but it appears there is a prohibition to make preparation on the first Yom Tov lest the second be weekday; and although Rabba's prohibition of preparation is only from Yom Tov to Shabbos or the reverse, nevertheless there is a rabbinic prohibition, and so is implied in the first chapter, folio 2: weekday prepares for Shabbos and weekday prepares for Yom Tov, and Yom Tov does not prepare for Shabbos and Shabbos does not prepare for Yom Tov; and Rashi explained that the Merciful One considered the Shabbos and Yom Tov meal significant that he designate it while it is still day, on a weekday, but a weekday meal is not significant and designation does not apply to it; therefore on an ordinary Sunday one need not forbid an egg born on it (and completed yesterday), since it was prepared by Heaven, for a weekday meal the Merciful One did not require designation while it is still day, for muktzeh does not apply to it; end quote. It implies specifically that since it was prepared by Heaven it is permitted from Shabbos to weekday, but it is forbidden to make preparation by hands or by speech from Shabbos to weekday; and if so here there is a prohibition to make preparation from Yom Tov to weekday. Again I saw in Maharshal's words, chapter "Ein Tzadin," siman 90, and this is his language: it is written in the name of the Kol Bo that this question was asked regarding muktzeh of a storehouse and the like: if he reconsidered regarding it on the first Yom Tov and said, "behold I prepare it for tomorrow," is it permitted for him by "whichever way you turn," since they are two sanctities, as we permit regarding a non-Jew who brought a gift to a Jew on the first Yom Tov that we permit for him on the second Yom Tov; and they answered that regarding muktzeh where one can decree lest he handle and abandon, such as a hen standing to raise eggs, in this it is certainly forbidden, as we see regarding dovecote pigeons that "from here I will take tomorrow" does not suffice for them until he says "this and this I will take"; but regarding other muktzeh where this decree does not apply, such as wood and fruits of a storehouse, it certainly appears that "whichever way you turn" is effective for them; end quote. Beis Yosef also brought it at the end of siman 518. It appears to me that this deals only with the muktzeh prohibition, that if he prepared on day one it helps for tomorrow; but nonetheless he commits a prohibition in that he stipulates, for it is like a weekday act, as appears to me in my humble opinion.

And in the Levush he challenged: what difference is there from one who invites his fellow when he did not know; and he did not intend Rama's words properly, for the prohibition here is since they were outside the techum and they belong to a Jew, and this is muktzeh.
