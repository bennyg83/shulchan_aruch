(א) Permitted even for one for whom it was brought etc. The reason: for behold the non-Jew does not bring of his own, for it was decreed lest he say "bring me" — thus Rashba. And Rashal wrote on this that this is specifically according to Rabbenu Tam who holds the reason is lest he speak alone — one can distinguish in this; but for Rashi who holds so as not to benefit from a Yom Tov prohibition — here too it was brought from outside the techum for him, and because it is d'rabbanan they were lenient for another; if so there is no distinction between a Samaritan bringing or the agent being delayed — nevertheless he benefits from a techum prohibition done for him; and nevertheless for another it is permitted, for they were not stringent regarding non-Jew's fruits — end quote; and I wonder at a great man like him who filled his heart to disagree with Rashba here; and not only that — Rashba would be contradicted in his own words, for Rashal wrote that these words of Rashba are only for Rabbenu Tam; and behold Rashba holds like Rashi, as Beit Yosef wrote in his name in the name of the Rav HaMaggid that Rashba holds like Rambam and like Rashi; and I wonder how the rabbi Rashal did not notice this, for behold Beit Yosef's book was before him; and in truth it is difficult for me, for certainly even for Rashi it is permitted, for Rashi wrote that the reason for prohibiting one for whom it was brought from outside the techum is not due to a prohibition but only due to a mere penalty, as I wrote before; and penalty applies only where a non-Jew brings for him on Yom Tov and there is concern lest he command him l'chatchila to bring — not so here where it is not the non-Jew's but an Israelite sends to him as law so he can come before Yom Tov, except the agent erred and was delayed on the way — why penalize the Israelite who was sent to him here? Therefore certainly permitted here — in my humble opinion clearly. In all places we mentioned in this siman that techumim are d'rabbanan, Taz wrote siman 83 that even if a non-Jew brought a gift on Yom Tov beyond twelve mil — although most Geonim wrote this is d'oraisa — nevertheless one need not be more stringent in it than things that come from outside the techum, for there is no decree lest he tell him to bring, for it is not common that he would tell him to bring from afar — thus explicitly. It appears to me that regarding something where the permission of muktzeh alone depends on Yom Tov or Shabbat — therefore such as those laws mentioned in siman 28, it is permitted for a person to make that designation alone on the first diaspora Yom Tov day for tomorrow by force of "either way," and permitted for everyone on the second Yom Tov day, for Rabbenu Tam's stringency of lest he tell to bring etc. does not apply here; and if so it would be well permitted after bichdei sheya'asu on the first Yom Tov day; but it appears there is prohibition to make preparation on the first Yom Tov day lest the second be weekday; and although there is no preparation prohibition of Rabba except from Yom Tov to Shabbat or the reverse — nevertheless there are d'rabbanan prohibitions, as explicit in the first chapter chapter 2: weekday prepares for Shabbat and weekday prepares for Yom Tov, and Yom Tov does not prepare for Shabbat and Shabbat does not prepare for Yom Tov; and Rashi explained the Merciful One considered significant a Shabbat and Yom Tov meal that he designated from his own initiative on weekday, but a weekday meal is not considered significant and designation does not apply to it; therefore on an ordinary Shabbat one need not forbid an egg born on it (and completed yesterday), since it is possible in Heaven's hands that a weekday meal does not require the Merciful One's designation from his own initiative, for muktzeh does not apply to it — end quote. It implies specifically since it is possible in Heaven's hands it is permitted from Shabbat to weekday; but forbidden to make preparation by hand or by speech from Shabbat to weekday; and if so here there is prohibition to make preparation from Yom Tov to weekday. Again I saw in Rashal's words chapter "one may not trap" siman 90, and this is his language: Maharshal in the name of Kol Bo was asked this question on muktzeh of a storehouse and similar: if he reconsidered on the first Yom Tov day and said "behold I prepare it for tomorrow" — is it permitted for him in any case, since there are two sanctities, as we permit with a non-Jew who brought a gift to an Israelite on the first Yom Tov day that we permit for him on the second Yom Tov day; and they answered that regarding muktzeh where one can decree on it lest he carry and abandon, such as a chicken standing to raise eggs — in this certainly forbidden as seen in the month of Sivan in the dovecote that they cannot suffice with "from here I will take for tomorrow" until he said "this and this I will take"; but regarding other muktzeh where this decree does not apply, such as wood and fruits of a storehouse — certainly it appears effective in any case — end quote; also Beit Yosef brought siman 518; it appears to me this deals only regarding muktzeh prohibition — that if he prepared on day one it helps for tomorrow; but nevertheless he commits a prohibition in what he stipulated, which is like weekday work — in my humble opinion:
