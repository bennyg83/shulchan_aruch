{33} From things that have of their species among what is attached: and the same law applies from things that lack trapping.
{34} On the first Yom Tov of Rosh Hashana: and he did not mention Diaspora Yom Tov, for he follows his approach in seif 1 that it was permitted even on the second day.
{35} There are those who permit eating them on Shabbos: for we do not say preparation from two days beforehand, only from one day.
{36} Waiting the time it takes for them to be done: although on Shabbos doing does not apply, nevertheless it is no better than if a weekday had fallen after Rosh Hashana, where one would need to wait on Motzei Rosh Hashana the time it takes for them to be done [Beis Yosef].
{37} And there are those who forbid: their reason is that since we hold in general that the two days of Rosh Hashana are like one long day, it is like one day before Shabbos, and automatically one must wait on Motzei Shabbos the time it takes for them to be done; and according to this view even if they fell or were trapped of themselves it is also forbidden on Shabbos day, and for Motzei Shabbos it is permitted in this immediately.
{38} The law is thus: that is to say, there are also in this the two aforementioned views; only there is in this a practical difference: on the two days of Rosh Hashana it is forbidden on Shabbos according to those who forbid even if they fell of themselves because of the prohibition of preparation; which is not so on the two Diaspora Yom Tov days, where if they fell of themselves on day one or a non-Jew picked them for himself, it is permitted at the departure of the first Yom Tov immediately as above in seif 2.
{39} One should be stringent on Shabbos: that is to say, like the view of those who forbid above; and see Taz who wrote in the name of Maharshal that on Motzei Shabbos it is permitted immediately and waiting the time it takes for them to be done is not needed [unless it was brought on Friday]; but in the novellae of R' Akiva Eiger he challenged him that Maharshal does not deal at all with the two days of Rosh Hashana, and his view is that on the two days of Rosh Hashana one must wait on Motzei Shabbos the time it takes for them to be done.
{40} One may be lenient on the Shabbos that is after them: for we do not decree lest he tell a non-Jew on the first Yom Tov, which is two days before Shabbos, to bring them on Shabbos; and therefore it is permitted on the night of Shabbos after waiting the time it takes for them to be done; and the same law applies if the two Diaspora Yom Tov days fall on Sunday and Monday and the non-Jew brought for him on Shabbos, that it is permitted to eat on the first Yom Tov in the evening after waiting the time it takes for them to be done.
{41} That needs preparation etc.: meaning such as fish that need cooking, and on Shabbos it is forbidden to cook them, and on the second Yom Tov likewise one cannot cook them, for they are muktzeh and forbidden even for another Jew unless guests happened upon him as above. And even though for the need of guests we are lenient, nevertheless the status of muktzeh rests upon them as long as guests have not happened upon him.
{42} Forbidden: nevertheless it is permitted to cook through a non-Jew (in a manner that has no issue of bishul akum, and in the way explained in Yoreh Deah siman 113; see there) on erev Shabbos for the need of Shabbos eating after he waits on the night of Shabbos the time it takes for them to be done, since the handling prohibition on day two is only a mere stringency. And all this is when he has of this species in his house for the honor of Shabbos; but if he does not have of this species in his house and he needs it for the honor of Shabbos, it is permitted to handle it and cook it himself for the honor of Shabbos, only that he be careful not to taste of it until he waits on the night of Shabbos the time it takes for them to be done.
