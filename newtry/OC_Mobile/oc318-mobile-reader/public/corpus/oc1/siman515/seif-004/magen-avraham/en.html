And some prohibit. Even if they fell on their own, because of preparation.
The law is so. This applies when the non-Jew brought a gift to a Jew. If he gathered them for himself, they are permitted, as stated in seif 3, and certainly if they fell on their own.
One may be lenient. Two days beforehand, we do not decree lest he tell the non-Jew to bring them.
And even to move them. It requires study whether one may permit cooking them through a non-Jew. At first glance, the incident involving Rav Adda's host implies that it is forbidden to cook them even through a non-Jew. Here, however, one may argue that since some authorities permit eating them on the second day of Yom Tov, it is at least permitted to have a non-Jew cook them.
