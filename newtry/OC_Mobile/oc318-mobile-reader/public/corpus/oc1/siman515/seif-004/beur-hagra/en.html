(ד) Non-Jew etc. And there are those who forbid — because of preparation as in Beitza, and as written in seif 1; and the reasoning of the first view holds that the second Yom Tov of Rosh Hashanah is one sanctity only because of a decree, for we are expert in fixing the month and we established and decreed preparation of Rabba for Shabbat; but Rashba wrote like the latter reasoning: since the Rabbis equated them as one long day, for all opinions the Rabbis equated them. And all the more so per Tosafos' explanation that Rabba's preparation is d'oraisa, as written 2:2 s.v. "and it was" on erev Shabbat:

(ב) And for us etc. There are those who forbid lest he say to bring for Shabbat — as explained; and there are those who permit, for two days prior it does not apply that perhaps he will say, as Tosafos wrote s.v. "and for night" etc. — Rabbeinu Yerucham wrote etc.:

(ג) And regarding etc. One should be stringent etc.; and so he ruled in Shulchan Aruch above siman 507 seif 2 that preparation is d'oraisa, and more severe on Shabbat after Yom Tov than from Yom Tov itself; and so in the gloss in siman 513 seif 6:

(ד) And even to handle etc. And certainly because of muktzeh — attached is permitted on the second Yom Tov like lacking trapping in that Tavya, and likewise an egg; and regarding handling there is no distinction between on its own or for an Israelite, only he learned from Rif's words there; and that which we say "if there is" etc. — even to handle it is forbidden because it is not fit for eating — it is muktzeh; and so Rosh wrote there; and he wrote: but what comes from outside the techum etc. is permitted to handle since it is permitted to another Israelite, unlike here where we practice like the gloss of Semak unless for the need of guests; and this is what is said "since we practice" etc.; and this distinction is explained in the Mishnah of clearing terumah tahorah — even though it is fit only for a kohen, but not tevel etc. forbidden to all, even if only a rabbinic prohibition as written in the Gemara in tevel — tevel rabbinically; and even though what did they permit except because if he wishes he can declare ownerless etc.:
