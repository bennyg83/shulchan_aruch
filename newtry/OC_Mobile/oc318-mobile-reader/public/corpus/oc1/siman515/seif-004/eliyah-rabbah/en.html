[4] [Levush] So that he not benefit etc. This gloss requires study, for it implies he agrees with Tosafos and Rabbeinu Yeruham above that even according to Rashi's explanation it is permitted when he does it for himself, while before this in the main text it is clarified that according to Rashi it is forbidden as above; also the proof from water drawn of itself requires study per his explanation above. And it appears that the author of the gloss relied on what he ruled in Shulchan Aruch seif 2 as the anonymous ruling that it is permitted, implying he relies on those who permit; therefore he wrote these reasons. And in truth I wonder at Beis Yosef regarding Rabbeinu Yeruham, folio 34, who wrote that according to Rashi's explanation it is forbidden, while Beis Yosef wrote the opposite in his name; and thus it appears to me the main view for the practical law, for Beis Yosef wrote in the name of R"i of Paris that it is forbidden until the departure of the second Yom Tov after waiting the time it takes for them to be done; even though Bach challenged Beis Yosef that R"i of Paris forbids only until the departure of the second Yom Tov and not with waiting the time it takes for them to be done, nevertheless he agrees that it is forbidden on the first with that waiting, for within two hundred is one hundred. Also one can wonder that Bach did not see in the Kol Bo, folio 61, who wrote explicitly in the name of R"i of Paris like Beis Yosef, and concluded that R"i and R' Yechiel practiced thus; and thus it appears to me in any case for practical law to forbid when a non-Jew did it for himself until the night of the second Yom Tov after waiting the time it takes for them to be done; and so is implied in Avodas HaKodesh and the Ran, chapter Ein Tzadin. However, regarding fruits that fall or are trapped of themselves and were brought for a Jew, it is forbidden to be lenient immediately on the second night.
