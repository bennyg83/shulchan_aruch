Magen Avraham, s.k. 11 Even according to those who are stringent above. And we deal with within the techum. And that which needed to be taught that it is permitted even though no prohibition at all was done for a Jew, namely: nevertheless a prohibition of handling muktzeh was done for a Jew, for after the non-Jew picked them for himself they are forbidden in handling because of muktzeh of what is attached. And in truth one should examine: since from outside the techum it is forbidden for the one for whom it was brought, even though it is only a rabbinic melacha, why then when the non-Jew brings something forbidden to handle is the time it takes for them to be done not required?
Seif 4 On the two Diaspora Yom Tov days as well the law is thus. The wording is not precise, for according to those who forbid it is forbidden even for another Jew, and even for the need of guests, since it is forbidden because of preparation; whereas on the two Diaspora Yom Tov days, where there is no preparation [prohibition], only the reason lest he say [to him to bring], and it is permitted for the need of guests: if so the Rama's language "as well the law is thus" is not so precise.
