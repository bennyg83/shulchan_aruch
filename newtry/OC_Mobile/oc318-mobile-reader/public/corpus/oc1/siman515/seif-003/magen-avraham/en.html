They are forbidden. If the non-Jew brought them to a Jew, there is a dispute whether one must wait the time needed for them to be done; see siman 325, seif 7. Semak asks that the Gemara states that if there is a species like it attached to the ground one must wait that period, which implies that even in a doubtful case one must wait. Yam Shel Shlomo answers that here the uncertainty is whether there is any such species attached to the ground, creating something like a double doubt, and therefore no waiting is required. This is difficult, for Semag's wording does not imply that distinction; see Beit Yosef. It is also difficult why Tur and Beit Yosef cite two opinions without qualification in siman 325, seif 7, and do not state that the case is one of uncertainty whether such a species is attached to the ground. It therefore appears to me that when a non-Jew brings a gift, we assume that he intends to bring the finest produce and therefore brings items gathered or trapped that day. The same applies when he brings them to sell, as Ran writes. Above, however, the case is that the non-Jew told the Jew before Yom Tov that he would give him fruit, and the Jew then went to the non-Jew's house and received it. There is therefore doubt whether he gathered it for the Jew, intending from the outset to give him those fruits, or whether he did not gather it for him and initially intended to give him other fruit. We therefore permit these fruits because of the doubt and do not require waiting. For this reason, it appears to me that even according to the view permitting doubtful preparation on the second day of Yom Tov, as stated in siman 497, seif 4, that applies only where nets were spread before Yom Tov and there is doubt when the animal was trapped, as Ran writes. When a non-Jew brings a gift, however, it is forbidden because we treat it as certain. This is all the more so according to Rabbeinu Tam, whose reason for the prohibition is lest one tell the non-Jew to bring it. According to that reason there is no distinction between the first and second days, contrary to Beit Yosef's ruling here.
