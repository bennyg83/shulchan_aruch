Magen Avraham, s.k. 8 Forbidden on the second because of preparation. At first glance it seemed puzzling to me: if here the prohibition is because of preparation, why does one need, when Yom Tov and Shabbos [meet], also to wait the time it takes for them to be done? And after examining, it appears to me that Magen Avraham's intent was that his difficulty was: since they are two sanctities, it should suffice to wait the time it takes for them to be done on the first night, for even though it is not fit for doing, it counts toward the measure of the time it takes for them to be done (see Machatzit HaShekel, that Magen Avraham's proof is from the two Diaspora Yom Tov days, where it is permitted in the evening of the night of the second Yom Tov after waiting the time it takes for them to be done: behold, even if before Heaven it is clear that the second Yom Tov day is weekday, it is not fit for doing, for we who are in doubt are forbidden to do; see there; and in my humble opinion there is a stronger proof from seif 4, where there are those who permit eating them on Shabbos after waiting the time it takes for them to be done: behold, it counts toward the time it takes for them to be done even though it is not fit for doing). Nevertheless, all this is only if the waiting of the time it takes for them to be done is only because of the melacha done for him: in that case there is recognition and it counts for this. Therefore Magen Avraham answered that even if [the fish] were trapped of themselves, they are forbidden all of Shabbos because of preparation; if so, all of Shabbos does not count toward the time it takes for them to be done, since without the melacha for a Jew it would have been forbidden to eat them because of preparation; and for this one must wait at Motzei Shabbos the time it takes for them to be done. Examine carefully.
