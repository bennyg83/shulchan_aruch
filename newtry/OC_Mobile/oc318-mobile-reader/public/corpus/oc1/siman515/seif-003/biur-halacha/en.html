Even if it is a doubt — see Mishna Berurah what he wrote that regarding a non-Jew who brought a gift, this is not in the category of doubt — so Magen Avraham, and so in Beit Yosef who brought in the name of Ran in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah to resolve Rashi's view who holds doubt is for leniency — that incident of a gift is as certainly gathered today, for their way is not to gather from erev (so they will not wither); however all this is for Rashi and his supporters; but per the Geonim who forbid in doubt outside the techum and similar other doubts, there is no need to say a gift is certain and only doubtful — nevertheless forbidden; and so is implied in Milchamot in Shabbat chapter Sho'el and in Ran there who challenged from the gift incident against Rashi's explanation that a gift is a doubt whether gathered for an Israelite and nevertheless forbidden; and so in Semak it counts as a doubt whether detached on Shabbat; and see in Gra in this siman and in siman 497 seif 4 that his view too is that it is in the category of doubt [see in Mishnah Beitza daf 24 and incident of a non-Jew who brought fish to R' Gamliel and R' Gamliel said I do not wish to receive from him — it implies it was a gift and nevertheless in the Gemara it counts only as doubt; and already Chemdas Moshe stood on this, and see Machatzit HaShekel in siman 497 who reconciles with strain]; but for practical law there is no difference in a gift, for all agree it is forbidden with "so that they be made" as explained in the Gemara explicitly, whether from certainty or from doubt; and as explained. However this is somewhat difficult: if we count that gift incident as doubt, apparently there are two doubts whether gathered on Shabbat and Yom Tov and whether gathered for an Israelite; and since we forbid with "so that they be made," they are also forbidden in double doubt; and this is explained in later authorities that double doubt permits; and it must be said one of them is certain. For Ramban in Milchamot one can say he holds certainly gathered today as Rabbeinu Yonah wrote, and only doubts for whom he gathered — see there; and for Semak and likewise for those who disagree with Rashi brought in Ran one can say the opposite: since he brought as a gift we suspend that certainly he gathered for himself as Magen Avraham wrote, and only doubt whether gathered today, and nevertheless forbidden from doubt; and for Rashi who holds even one doubt we go to leniency — perforce regarding a gift we consider both as certain as Magen Avraham wrote. Nevertheless there is practical difference in a gift itself: since for Milchamot and its supporters it counts only as doubt, if there were another doubt, e.g. doubt whether there is of its species attached — then it is double doubt and permitted at night even for those stringent in one doubt; and so is implied in Yesh Seder, and so ruled the Gra; however for practical law all agree a gift is forbidden with "so that they be made" as explained in the Gemara explicitly:
