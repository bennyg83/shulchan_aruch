Even if it is a doubt See Mishnah Berurah what he wrote, that regarding a non-Jew who brought a gift this is not included in "doubt"; so wrote Magen Avraham; and so it is in Beis Yosef who brought in the name of the Ran in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah to answer the view of Rashi who holds that a doubt is toward leniency, that that case of a gift is like a certainty that they were picked today, for their way is not to pick from the evening beforehand (so that they not wither). However all this is according to Rashi and those who support him; but according to the Geonim who forbid in a doubt of outside the techum and the like of other doubts, there is no necessity to say that a gift is a certainty, only a doubt, and even so it is forbidden; and so is implied in Milchamot on Shabbos, chapter "One who borrows," and in the Ran there, who raised a difficulty from the gift case on Rashi's explanation that a gift is a doubt whether it was picked for a Jew and even so it is forbidden; and likewise in Shittah Mekubetzes he counts it as a doubt whether he detached them on Shabbos; and see in the Gr"a in this siman and in siman 497, seif 4, that it is likewise implied that his view is that it is included in "doubt" [and see in the Mishnah, Beitza folio 24, and an incident with a non-Jew who brought fish to Rabban Gamliel, and Rabban Gamliel said "I do not wish to receive from him," implying it was a gift, and even so in the Gemara he counted it only as a doubt; and Chemdas Moshe already raised this; and see Machatzit HaShekel in siman 497 who resolves it with difficulty]. But for the practical law there is no difference regarding a gift, for according to everyone a gift is forbidden until waiting the time it takes for them to be done, as clarified explicitly in the Gemara, either for a reason of certainty or for a reason of doubt, as above. However this is somewhat difficult: if we count that gift case as a doubt, then at first glance there are here two doubts: whether they picked them on Shabbos and Yom Tov, and whether they picked them for a Jew; and since we forbid until waiting the time it takes for them to be done, if so even in a double doubt they are forbidden; and this is clarified in the later authorities that a double doubt is permitted; and one must say that one of them is a certainty. According to the Ramban in Milchamot one can say that he holds they certainly picked them today as Rabbeinu Yonah wrote, and only a doubt for whom they picked them; see there. And according to Shittah Mekubetzes and likewise according to the reasoning of those who disagree with Rashi brought in the Ran, one can say the reverse: that since he brought as a gift we assume they certainly picked them for him as Magen Avraham wrote, and only a doubt whether they were picked today, and even so it is forbidden for a reason of doubt. And according to Rashi who holds that even one doubt we go toward leniency, necessarily regarding a gift we reckon both as certainty as Magen Avraham wrote. Nevertheless there is a practical difference regarding a gift itself: since according to Milchamot and those who support him it counts only as a doubt, if there were a further doubt for this, such as a doubt whether there is of its species among what is attached, then it becomes a double doubt and it is permitted in the evening even according to those who are stringent in a single doubt; and so is implied in Yam Shel Shlomo, and so the Alter Rebbe ruled.
