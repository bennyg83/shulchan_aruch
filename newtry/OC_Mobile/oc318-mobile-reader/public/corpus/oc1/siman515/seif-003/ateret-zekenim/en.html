It is permitted for one for whom they were not brought — that is to say, even guests at a baal habayit for whom they were brought; nevertheless it is permitted for them, since they are not accustomed to be frequent at this baal habayit; it is permitted for them to eat, for the mind of the bringer was not on them at all; therefore it is permitted for them to eat (Beit Yosef Taz). And something done on its own: if a non-Jew brought them and not for an Israelite, there are three laws in it. (1) If there is concern of attached on the first day, it is forbidden for everyone, for muktzeh is forbidden for that day; but on the second Yom Tov day it is permitted immediately even without bichdei sheya'asu — and this means diaspora Yom Tov; but on Rosh Hashanah Yom Tov, and likewise from Yom Tov to Shabbat and from Shabbat to Yom Tov, it is forbidden also on the second day; but after Yom Tov it is permitted even without bichdei sheya'asu, since it was done on its own or for a non-Jew. (3) But if there is no concern of trapping deficiency or attached, then it is permitted even on the first Yom Tov day immediately when they were brought, even from outside the techum, since it was not done for an Israelite. But if the non-Jew brought for an Israelite, there are two laws — one can say: (1) if there is concern of a d'oraisa prohibition such as attached and trapping deficiency, it is forbidden for two days until bichdei sheya'asu of the night after Yom Tov ends, even on diaspora second Yom Tov day, as Rashi explained; and even for an Israelite for whom they were not brought they are forbidden; and Yom Tov that falls on Thursday or Friday and was picked on the first day is permitted on Shabbat — and he said one need not be so stringent to forbid on the third day. (2) And in a matter where there is no concern except outside the techum and for this Israelite, it is permitted for another Israelite even on that day; but for the Israelite for whom they were brought — forbidden until after the second Yom Tov day bichdei sheya'asu; and perhaps one may permit even for the second Yom Tov day bichdei sheya'asu, since even regarding attached Rashi permits on the second Yom Tov day; even regarding attached and trapping deficiency brought for an Israelite one must be stringent until the second Yom Tov day bichdei sheya'asu, as I explained; and I found in Tosafot in the chapter "one who brought him out" s.v. "all of four amot" means — and this is his language: and one must be careful regarding a thing brought from outside the techum for this Israelite that is permitted for another Israelite, or that came for a non-Jew, not to carry it beyond four amot in a city that has no partitions enclosed for residence; and Mahara'i wrote that even where there is concern of trapping deficiency and attached brought for an Israelite on the first Yom Tov day, one may not permit on Yom Tov even for another; and even according to R' Yehuda who explained the reason that he should not tell a non-Jew — and for another there is no such concern, as the Rosh wrote and in Tur Orach Chayim — nevertheless it is fitting to be stringent like Semag who forbids even for another; nevertheless for a matter of need such as for honored guests he invited, it is permitted for another on the first Yom Tov day; and one may rely on the Rosh, since according to Rashi it is permitted even for himself that was brought on the second Yom Tov day; and he further wrote and this is his language: and according to what I wrote that one must be stringent like Semag — if so, fish brought on the first Yom Tov day that fell on Thursday are forbidden to benefit from even on the following Shabbat; for although the Rosh wrote in the name of R' Yehuda of Paris that even according to R' Yehuda it is permitted for the Shabbat after — that is specifically regarding fruits that were picked that are fit to eat raw on Shabbat; but not fish that require cooking, for on Shabbat it is forbidden to cook, and on Yom Tov too they are muktzeh and forbidden even for another Israelite; and it is forbidden to carry them, for in Or Zarua it is written that a thing brought from outside the techum l'chatchila is permitted to carry, for it is fit for an Israelite etc.; if not so it is forbidden — therefore all the more so where diaspora second Yom Tov occurred and they were brought on the Shabbat before Yom Tov, for it is a simple matter that they are permitted for the second Yom Tov day in any case, and these too are fit for cooking, for we are not concerned all this much for the Rosh in the name of R' Yehuda of Paris — see in Sh'eilot u'Teshuvot Maharshal siman 39:
