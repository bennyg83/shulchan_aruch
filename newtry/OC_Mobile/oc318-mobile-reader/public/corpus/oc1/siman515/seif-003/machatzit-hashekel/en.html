(Seif katan 3) All the more so he should not benefit etc. — for it is stated Beitza 24b: R' Pappa taught: the law is — a non-Jew who brought a gift to an Israelite on Yom Tov is forbidden, and at night also forbidden with "so that they be made"; and Rashi wrote the reason: so he not benefit from melacha of Yom Tov; and Rabbeinu Tam rejected this reason and wrote the reason is a decree lest an Israelite tell a non-Jew to bring from attached for him; and from both reasons there is stringency and leniency. Per Rashi, if he waited Motzei the first Yom Tov, all the more so from then on on the second Yom Tov of the Diaspora it is permitted, for perforce one of them is weekday and he does not benefit from melacha of Yom Tov; and for Rabbeinu Tam, also on the second Yom Tov it is forbidden, for otherwise there is still concern he will tell a non-Jew on the first Yom Tov to bring so he eats on the second; and Rabbeinu Tam has a leniency that for another person for whom it was not brought it is permitted even Motzei the first Yom Tov and does not need "so that they be made," for there is no concern he will tell a non-Jew to bring from attached so his friend eats — a person does not sin and not for himself — so is Rabbeinu Tam. But for Rashi, even for one for whom it was not brought it is forbidden until after Motzei the first Yom Tov with "so that they be made" — so is the view of most poskim for Rabbeinu Tam; but Semak wrote that even for Rabbeinu Tam it is forbidden also for others that were not brought for them; and Bach wrote the reason: perhaps since this prohibition is light in people's eyes, the Sages were stringent that if brought for one Israelite, forbid it for all, even for one for whom it was not brought; and now the words of Shulchan Aruch will be explained: Rabbeinu Yehuda ruled like Rashi's view, but Rama brought Rabbeinu Tam's view and the ruling of Semak who is more stringent, as explained:

(ו) As he wrote in the name of Mordechai etc. on this that we learned daf 29b and likewise below siman 517: a person comes to a shopkeeper accustomed to him and says give me eggs etc. kid or lamb etc.; and Ba'al Halachot Gedolot wrote specifically Israelite shopkeeper, but gentile shopkeeper regarding a matter that has concern of attached or trapping deficiency or muktzeh — forbidden lest he increase for him, even in a city mostly gentiles. And Mordechai wrote that from his words it implies regarding a matter without the above concerns — even though he told him perhaps he will increase from outside the techum — we do not need it; and he wrote one must bring proof from the incident of turnip in Eruvin daf 40: there is that turnip that comes to the district on Yom Tov — Rava saw shriveled ones and said this was certainly uprooted yesterday and there is no attached concern; and what they told him perhaps from outside techum it was brought — behold we establish that something from outside techum brought for this Israelite is permitted for another Israelite, all the more so here that with gentile's knowledge it was brought, for in the district most buyers are gentiles and Rava permitted to buy from them, and behold it is a gentile shopkeeper; although also with gentile shopkeeper it is permitted in such a case — he further brought proof from Eruvin daf 47b those sheep (that is, lambs) brought on Yom Tov to Mametzri (that is the city name) and came from outside their techum; and we establish also gentiles' objects acquire residence — nevertheless behold we establish something that goes outside techum has at least four amot; and Mametzri was surrounded by a wall and all of it considered as four amot; and Rava permitted Mametzri's residents to buy from them — and why, behold they were gentile shopkeepers? Therefore there is no concern even for a shopkeeper that he will increase for an Israelite. And on this Mordechai wrote one must reject these two proofs — one can say it deals with an Israelite shopkeeper said etc. and Rava permitted buying from the Israelite shopkeeper; and if so it is difficult how Rava permitted and said with his knowledge etc. — meaning there are two difficulties: one, how did he say with gentile's knowledge when behold with Israelite shopkeeper's knowledge they were brought; and further how did Rava permit them at all for the shopkeeper, for behold with his knowledge they were brought:

Or this is what it means — non-Jew etc. — what is written "all the more so here" that with the non-Jews' intent they come — the intent is not with the intent of the non-Jews buying who were there, the majority non-Jews, but he means with the intent of the non-Jews who bring; and he means that per the view that they brought with their own intent to receive their wages since he stipulated, and Magen Avraham's intent is not to prove from this only regarding techum prohibition, but where there is concern of attached and trapping, in truth there is no proof from this:
