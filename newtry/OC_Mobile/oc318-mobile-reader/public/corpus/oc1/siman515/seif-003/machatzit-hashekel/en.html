(s.k. 3) Waiting the time it takes for them to be done so that he not benefit etc. For it is stated on folio 24b: Rav Papa said the halacha is that a non-Jew who brought a gift to a Jew on Yom Tov is forbidden, and in the evening they are forbidden until waiting the time it takes for them to be done. And Rashi wrote the reason: so that he not benefit from melacha of Yom Tov; and Rabbeinu Tam rejected this reason and wrote the reason is a decree lest a Jew tell a non-Jew to bring for him from what is attached. And for each of the reasons there is a stringency and a leniency. According to Rashi, if he waited at the departure of the first Yom Tov the time it takes for them to be done, from then on on the second Diaspora Yom Tov it is permitted, for whichever way you turn one of them is weekday and whichever way you turn he did not benefit from melacha of Yom Tov. And according to Rabbeinu Tam it is forbidden also on the second Yom Tov, for if not so there is still concern that he will tell a non-Jew to bring for him on the first Yom Tov so that he eat on the second. And according to Rabbeinu Tam there is a leniency: for another person for whom it was not brought it is permitted even at the departure of the first Yom Tov, and waiting the time it takes for them to be done is not required, for why would he tell a non-Jew to bring from what is attached so that his fellow eat, since a person does not sin and not for himself. So it is according to Rabbeinu Tam. But according to Rashi even for one for whom it was not brought it is forbidden until after the departure of the first Yom Tov after waiting the time it takes for them to be done. Such is the view of most poskim per Rabbeinu Tam; but the Semak wrote that even according to Rabbeinu Tam it is forbidden also for others for whom it was not brought; and Bach wrote the reason: perhaps since this prohibition is light in people's eyes, the Sages were stringent that if it was brought for one Jew they forbid it for everyone, even for one for whom it was not brought. And now Shulchan Aruch's words will be clarified: Beis Yosef ruled like Rashi's view, but the Rama brought Rabbeinu Tam's view and like the Semak's decision which is more stringent, as above.
Permitted in the evening immediately etc. That is to say: granted that on that very day it is forbidden because of muktzeh as written in s.k. 1, nevertheless in the evening it is permitted immediately and one need not wait the time it takes for them to be done. And if it is etc., it is permitted even on Yom Tov, for in such a case there is no issue of muktzeh.
All the more so when it comes from outside the techum, since techumim are rabbinic.
And even according to those who are stringent there in siman 252 regarding a garment and shoes.
And the same law applies regarding a complete melacha: it is permitted at the departure of the first Yom Tov after waiting the time it takes for them to be done; that is to say, even according to those who are stringent like Rabbeinu Tam that also on the second Yom Tov it is forbidden, nevertheless in such a case he agrees that it is at least permitted on the second Yom Tov, for with a fixed price why would he tell a non-Jew to bring; and this is specifically regarding one who brought a gift, where the melacha was done for a Jew, that there is concern that if we permit him he will err that he is also allowed to tell a non-Jew to bring for him; which is not so with a fixed price, where the non-Jew acts with his own intent to receive his wages and not with the Jew's intent, so he will not err to tell a non-Jew to bring for him. Rather the reason of those who are stringent to forbid is as Magen Avraham wrote in the name of Bach in siman 252, s.k. 12, that he not benefit from melacha of Shabbos; and if so even according to Rabbeinu Tam it suffices if he waits at the departure of the first Yom Tov the time it takes for them to be done.
Who wrote in the name of the Mordechai etc. On that which we learned on folio 29b, and likewise below in siman 517: a person may go to a shopkeeper who is accustomed with him and say to him, "Give me eggs etc., a kid or a lamb etc."; and Halachos Gedolos wrote: specifically a Jewish shopkeeper, but a non-Jewish shopkeeper, regarding a matter in which there is concern of what is attached or trapping or muktzeh, is forbidden lest he increase for him, and even in a city whose majority are non-Jews. And the Mordechai wrote that it is implied from his words that regarding a matter without the aforementioned concerns, even if you say lest he increase bringing from outside the techum, we are not concerned. And he wrote that one may bring proof from the incident of the turnips in Eruvin folio 40: that turnip that came to Mechoza on Yom Tov; Rava saw that they were withered and said this was certainly uprooted yesterday, and there is no concern of what is attached. And what of saying perhaps it was brought from outside the techum: behold we hold that regarding what was brought from outside the techum, what was brought for this Jew is permitted for another Jew; all the more so here that it was brought with the non-Jew's intent, for in Mechoza most buyers are non-Jews, and Rava permitted buying from them; and behold it was a non-Jewish shopkeeper, and even regarding a non-Jewish shopkeeper it is permitted in such a case. He further brought proof from what is stated in Eruvin folio 47b: those rams that were brought on Yom Tov to Mavrachta (that is a city name) and came from outside their techum; and we hold that also a non-Jew's objects acquire a place of rest; nevertheless we hold that if it went outside the techum it still has at least four amos; and Mavrachta was enclosed by a wall and is entirely reckoned like four amos; and Rava permitted the people of Mavrachta to buy from them; and why, behold they were non-Jewish shopkeepers? One must say that even regarding a shopkeeper we are not concerned that he will increase for a Jew. And on this the Mordechai wrote that one may reject these two proofs, for one can say that we deal with where a Jewish shopkeeper said etc., and Rava permitted buying from the Jewish shopkeeper. And if so it is difficult how Rava permitted and said "with their intent etc.": that is to say, there are two difficulties: one, how did he say "with the non-Jew's intent" when with the Jewish shopkeeper's intent they brought them; and further, how did Rava permit them in any case for the shopkeeper, for they were brought with his intent.
Rather this is what it means: a non-Jew etc. What is written "all the more so here that they came with non-Jews' intent": the intent is not the non-Jewish buyers of whom there were a majority there; rather it means with the intent of the non-Jews who bring; that is to say, they brought with their own intent to receive their wages, since he fixed a price. And Magen Avraham's intent is not to prove from this anything except regarding the techum prohibition; but where there is concern of what is attached and trapping, in truth there is no proof from this.
Who lit a lamp etc. Even if he fixed a price, as written in Darkhei Moshe siman 276: to use it on Shabbos; and even if he fixed a price, he still acts with the Jew's intent; which is not so regarding a garment or shoes, where the non-Jew's intent is not that a Jew wear it today on Shabbos and Yom Tov specifically; then the non-Jew acts with his own intent to complete his work and receive his wages.
And if so it is possible that foodstuffs etc. That is to say: one can also say that presumably the non-Jew's intent is that a Jew eat it on Shabbos or Yom Tov day; and if so there is no proof to permit from siman 252; and those incidents in tractate Eruvin and the Mordechai's words above must be explained that in truth Rava permitted only for all the Jewish buyers, but for the shopkeeper himself it is forbidden.
