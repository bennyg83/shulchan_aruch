{27} Even if it is a doubt: this too refers to a non-Jew who picked for himself, where there is only a muktzeh prohibition; nevertheless even in a doubt it is forbidden, for a doubtful prepared status is forbidden as clarified in siman 497, seif 3; and therefore the Mechaber did not mention here regarding waiting the time it takes for them to be done, for when he picked them for himself that waiting is not needed as above in the previous seif. However if he certainly picked or trapped them today and only a doubt whether he picked and trapped them for a Jew, whether one must wait the time it takes for them to be done depends on the dispute clarified in siman 325, seif 7, and it is clarified in the words of the later authorities there that one may be lenient for the need of a mitzvah. It is further clarified there that if both doubts were together, whether they were picked today and whether they were picked for a Jew, on their day they are forbidden, for even if certainly not for a Jew he picked them it is forbidden because of a doubtful prepared status as above; but in the evening they are permitted immediately because of a double doubt, and even if it was Yom Tov in the evening such as the two Diaspora Yom Tov days. However regarding a non-Jew who brought a gift to a Jew [or brought to sell in a city whose majority are Jews], it is forbidden until waiting the time it takes for them to be done as clarified in seif 1, for we reckon them as certainly picked today and for a Jew.
{28} Forbidden: see Taz and Beer HaGolah that even on the second Yom Tov one should forbid; and Maamar Mordechai questions this; and Peri Chadash in siman 497 decides that regarding picking one should forbid because there are poskim who hold that wherever there is of those species among what is attached, people's way is to pick them on their day that they bring; but regarding trapping it is permitted on the second Yom Tov, for the aforementioned reason does not apply, and it is a rabbinic doubt.
{29} If it is recognizable in them: that the fruits were withered; and the same law applies regarding fish that are recognizable as trapped on erev Yom Tov, for example that their moisture dried and the redness under their gills by which one checks them there.
{30} For one for whom they were not brought: for we hold below in seif 5 that what comes from outside the techum for this Jew is permitted for another Jew even on that very day.
{31} A non-Jew speaks casually etc.: the reason is that he does not improve by his words; on the contrary he worsens, for those trapped and picked today are better; and we deal with where the non-Jew does not know that what was picked and trapped today is forbidden to a Jew; for if not so we say he intends to improve his merchandise.
{32} Believed: and even if the non-Jew brought on Yom Tov after Shabbos and spoke casually that it was picked on erev Shabbos [or brought on Rosh Hashana and spoke casually that it was picked on erev Rosh Hashana], he is also believed. However if they were fruits that are not eaten raw and he brought them on Yom Tov after Shabbos, or he brought them on Shabbos and speaks casually that they were picked on erev Shabbos, it is forbidden to eat them on Yom Tov, for perhaps they were picked on Shabbos and according to some poskim there is a Torah-level prohibition in them, and he is not believed through casual speech regarding a Torah-level prohibition [later authorities].
