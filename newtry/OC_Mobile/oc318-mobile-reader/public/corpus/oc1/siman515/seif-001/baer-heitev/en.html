That it lacks. If the Jew bought fish from the non-Jew on erev Yom Tov and left them with the non-Jew without a seal, and on Yom Tov he sees that it is as good and fine as the first, it is permitted, for we are not concerned that the non-Jew switched them if he does not benefit by the switch, as written in Yoreh Deah siman 118. And in Yam Shel Shlomo he wrote that from the second night onward it is permitted in handling even though for us it is forbidden in eating; see there; and Magen Avraham disagrees with him and wrote that regarding a matter that has no permission to eat it is forbidden in handling, as written at the end of seif 4 in the gloss.
That they be done. So that he not benefit from melacha of Yom Tov. And this requires further study: if the non-Jew does not bring for free, only that a Jew fixed a price with him beforehand that he bring fish from erev Yom Tov, and the non-Jew delayed and brought them on Yom Tov, are they permitted immediately in the evening? For behold even if a non-Jew made a garment on Yom Tov it is permitted to wear it if he fixed a price, as written in siman 252, seif 7; see Magen Avraham.
Immediately. Even for the one for whom it was brought, for whichever way you turn, one day is weekday.
To be lenient. And Taz in the name of Maharshal ruled to permit for another on the second Yom Tov in every manner, and for himself for a great need; see there and in Magen Avraham. And when he invited guests from another city, even he himself is permitted to eat with them, for it is not their honor that he not eat with them; see Magen Avraham; and in the responsa of the Bach, siman 53.
That he go. And everything depends on the circumstance: if he was with a caravan and delayed one day, one must wait one day; and if he was riding a horse and delayed one hour, one must wait until one hour; and if the place is very far, one need not wait more than one day, namely the Shabbos day on which it was done in prohibition; and see above, siman 325, seif 8, in the later authorities.
