(ב) To carry them is forbidden. For they are not fit for eating and are like muktzeh, since because they are forbidden in eating due to the day's prohibition they are forbidden in carrying (Rif and Rosh); and Yeshuot Shalom disagrees on this and elaborated and wrote that the reason for prohibiting carrying is because it is muktzeh; therefore from the second night onward carrying is permitted even though eating is forbidden — end quote; and all his proofs are not compelling, for what he wrote from Rashbag contradicts himself in his gloss siman 5; and what he wrote from fruits that went outside their place that carrying is permitted as written in siman 405 — that case is different, for they have permission to eat if they returned to their place; but regarding a thing that has no permission to eat, carrying is forbidden as the anonymous tanna wrote in the gloss and as explicit in Beit Yosef siman 486 in the name of Hagahot Maimoniyot and the poskim, and as explicit in Rashba siman 487; also what Yeshuot Shalom wrote and added to Rif and Rosh's words — since it is forbidden in eating due to the day's prohibition it is forbidden in carrying — his intent is to answer that behold carrion is forbidden in eating and permitted in carrying, and the reason is because its prohibition is not due to the day's prohibition; and it appears to me the reason is because carrion is fit for dogs; but this one's intent is not to throw to dogs and it is forbidden, similar to tevel, as written siman 308 s.k. 31-32; and likewise Tosafot in Beitzah daf 6b requires prepared and designated; and see siman 446 and siman 513:

(ג) With "so that they be made." So he should not benefit from melacha of Yom Tov; and one should analyze: since the Gemara and poskim cited "a non-Jew who brought a gift" etc., it implies that if he does not bring for free but the Israelite stipulated payment to bring fish from erev Yom Tov and the non-Jew waited and brought them on Yom Tov — they are permitted at night immediately; and if it is a matter that has no species attached and lacks trapping, it is permitted even on Yom Tov even from outside the techum, for even if a non-Jew made a garment on Yom Tov it is permitted to wear if he stipulated — the non-Jew acts with his own intent, as written siman 502 seif 7; a fortiori when it comes from outside the techum; and even for those stringent there it is regarding full melacha, but regarding techum they agree; and likewise full melacha is permitted at Motzei the first Yom Tov with "so that they be made"; and so in Beit Yosef siman 518 who wrote in the name of Mordechai that incident of turnip of Mechoza and remember that it is in chapter 3 of Eruvin — Israelite shopkeepers said before Yom Tov to bring them and they assessed their price, and the non-Jews delayed and brought them on Yom Tov etc.; and so Ra'avan; if so, how did Rava permit and say it comes with the non-Jew's intent? Rather this is what it means: the non-Jew acts with his own intent, since he stipulated with him before Yom Tov; and it deals with siman 247; however one can say it is similar to a non-Jew who kindled a lamp on Shabbat that it is forbidden to use even with stipulation, because the non-Jew did it with intent that the Israelite use it on Shabbat, unlike shoes that were not made specifically to wear on Shabbat; if so, perhaps food types are similar to a lamp — and this requires further study; perhaps specifically a lamp must be used now, for tomorrow it is gone, unlike food types:
