The law of items brought on Yom Tov from outside the techum. It contains 9 seifim: If a non-Jew brings a gift to a Jew on Yom Tov, and items of that species are still attached to the ground or require trapping, it is forbidden even for a person for whom they were not brought to eat them that day. Even if he transgressed and put one in his mouth and chewed it, he may not swallow it. [R' Shimshon in his commentary to the Mishnah, chapter 2 of Terumot] It is even forbidden to move them. In the evening they become permitted after the amount of time required to perform the act has elapsed. On the 2 Yom Tov days observed in the Diaspora, if they were brought on the first day, they are permitted on the night of the second Yom Tov after the required amount of time has elapsed. {Rama: Some are stringent and forbid them until after the second Yom Tov. [Tur in the name of Rabbeinu Tam, Rashi, and Semag] The custom is to be stringent when they are not needed for Yom Tov itself. For guests and similar needs, however, the custom is to be lenient for people for whom they were not brought. [Terumat HaDeshen, siman 78]} Likewise, if they were brought on the second Yom Tov, one must wait after Yom Tov for the required amount of time. But on the 2 days of Rosh Hashana, or when Yom Tov adjoins Shabbos either before it or after it, if they were brought on the first day, one must wait until after Yom Tov and Shabbos and then the required amount of time. The required amount of time is enough time for the non-Jew to go to the place where he gathered them, complete the melacha, and return here. If there is uncertainty as to where they were brought from, the required time is the amount needed for them to have been brought from outside the techum.
