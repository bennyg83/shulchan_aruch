Laws of items brought on Yom Tov from outside the Shabbat boundary. Containing 9 paragraphs: If a non-Jew brought a gift to a Jew on Yom Tov, if there is produce of that type still attached to the ground, or if it requires trapping, it is forbidden even to someone for whom it was not brought, to eat it that same day. Even to handle it is forbidden. After the holiday it is permitted after waiting the time it would have taken to perform the action. For the two days of Yom Tov observed in the Diaspora, if it was brought on the first day it is permitted immediately on the night of the second Yom Tov after waiting that time. Similarly, if it was brought on the second Yom Tov, one must wait after Yom Tov ends for the required time. But for the two days of Rosh Hashanah, or for Yom Tov adjacent to Shabbat whether before or after — if it was brought on the first day one must wait until after both Yom Tov and Shabbat end, plus the required time. The measure of "time it would have taken" means the time for the non-Jew to go to the place where he gathered it, complete the work, and return. If it is uncertain from where he brought it, the measure is the time it would take to come from outside the boundary. {Rama: [And even if one transgressed and put it in his mouth and chewed it, he may not swallow it.] [R. Shimshon in his commentary to Mishnah, Terumot ch. 2] Rema: Some are stringent and forbid it until after the second Yom Tov ends [Tur in the name of Rabbeinu Tam, Rashi, and Semag], and the custom is to be stringent if it is not needed for Yom Tov itself — but for guests and similar circumstances the custom is to be lenient for others for whom it was not brought [Terumat HaDeshen §78].}
