(s.k. 1) Forbidden etc. Because of muktzeh; even though even at the departure of Yom Tov it is forbidden until waiting the time it takes for them to be done, whether because he should not benefit from melacha of Yom Tov according to Rashi, or according to Rabbeinu Tam lest he tell a non-Jew to bring them for him: all the more so on Yom Tov itself it is forbidden even without a reason of muktzeh. Certainly according to Yam Shel Shlomo in the following s.k. there is a practical difference in that it is forbidden because of muktzeh, for thereby even handling is forbidden. But according to the Rif and the Rosh who hold that whatever is forbidden in eating is also forbidden in handling, the difficulty is: why do we need a reason of muktzeh? And likewise according to most poskim per Rabbeinu Tam who hold that for one for whom it was not brought it is permitted, for why would he say to a non-Jew etc., since a person does not sin for another: then it is fine that the reason of muktzeh is needed. But these words that it is forbidden because of muktzeh are Rashi's words there on that of Rav Papa; for him the difficulty remains why the reason of muktzeh is needed. And one must say that the practical difference is if a non-Jew detached or trapped them for his own need, that they are permitted for a Jew as written in seif 2; nevertheless on day one they are forbidden because of muktzeh as clarified there; and so wrote the Ran there in chapter Ein Tzadin.
For such muktzeh even R' Shimon agrees; and if so, according to those who permit muktzeh on Yom Tov in siman 495 who ruled on Yom Tov like R' Shimon, nevertheless such muktzeh is forbidden even according to R' Shimon. And the reason Rashi wrote there, folio 24b, and this is his language: and even according to Rashi there is muktzeh in dried figs and raisins (that he pushed them away by hand, as clarified above in siman 310, seif 2), and what is attached is like dried figs and raisins, for since he did not pick them yesterday he set them aside intentionally; and you should not distinguish in what is attached between his own and a non-Jew's; end quote. And Beis Yosef explained his words in siman 310, and this is his language: since through their picking they become permitted, and it is forbidden to benefit from them while attached; and since he did not pick them, it is as if he pushed them away by hand and showed that he does not wish to benefit from them; therefore even if a non-Jew picked them they are forbidden etc.; and for this reason they decreed regarding all that is attached even where it was not in his hand to pick them, for the Sages did not distinguish etc.; and it appears that for this reason there is muktzeh in a matter that lacks trapping even according to R' Shimon; end quote of Beis Yosef.
