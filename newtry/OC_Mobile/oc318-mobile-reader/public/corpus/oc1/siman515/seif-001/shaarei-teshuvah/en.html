(In Shulchan Aruch seif 1) If there is of its species attached etc. — Mishna Berurah wrote in the name of Zera Emet: a non-Jew who brought roses as a gift on the first Yom Tov, and the hostess mixed them with other roses in vessels bought on Yom Tov, and those the non-Jew brought are the minority — he tended to permit to smell on the second Yom Tov by removing some of them — see there; and see in responsa Pri Tevuah siman 69: fish that a non-Jew brought as a gift to an Israelite — permitted on the second Yom Tov for another Israelite for Yom Tov need, even not for guests' need; and that Israelite for whom it was brought — if he invited guests incidentally, they are permitted to eat in the host's house; but for guests who regularly dine with him at the beginning of Yom Tov one should be stringent; and also what Magen Avraham was in doubt regarding inviting guests whether the host is permitted to eat with them — one should be stringent; and on the second Yom Tov adjacent to Shabbat, permitted to handle what was brought on the first Yom Tov to cook for Shabbat need — see there; and see in Radbaz part 2 siman 562: regarding a host whose guests regularly dine with him that Shabbat or Yom Tov, and he brought for the host — forbidden also for guests; but if they are not fixed, only came incidentally, they should not eat in the host's house, but in their house permitted — see there; and see in responsa Ginat Veradim part 1 letter 4: a non-Jew who brought a gift from an Israelite living in another city via ships on rivers that flow — he ruled leniently, and one of the reasons: rivers in Egypt are not clear whether they are congealed, and one can say the halachah is above ten handbreadths and one may be lenient combining further reasons; and a certain sage disagreed and forbade, and wrote thus his teacher ruled; see there in siman 184:2,3; and see in Pnei Meirut part 2 siman 115 regarding a non-Jew who brought fish to sell in a city whose majority are non-Jews — he wrote there several distinctions in law:
