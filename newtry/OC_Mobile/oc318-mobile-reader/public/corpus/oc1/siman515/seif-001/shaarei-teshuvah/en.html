In Shulchan Aruch, seif 1, that there is of its species among what is attached. And Machatzit HaShekel wrote in the name of Zera Emes: a non-Jew who brought roses as a gift on the first Yom Tov, and the woman mixed them with other roses in vessels that had been bought on erev Yom Tov, and those that the non-Jew brought are the minority; and he leaned toward permitting smelling them on the second Yom Tov by removing some of them; see there. And see in the responsa Pri Tevuah, siman 69: fish that a non-Jew brought as a gift to a Jew are permitted on the second Yom Tov for another Jew for the need of Yom Tov, even not for the need of guests; and that Jew to whom they were brought: if he invited guests only by chance, they are permitted to eat in the householder's house; but for guests who depend on him from the beginning of Yom Tov, one should be stringent; and also that which Magen Avraham left in doubt regarding inviting guests, whether the householder is permitted to eat with them, one should be stringent; and on the second Yom Tov adjacent to Shabbos it is permitted to handle what was brought on the first Yom Tov in order to cook for the need of Shabbos; see there. And see in Radbaz, part 2, siman 562, regarding a householder whose guests depend on him that Shabbos or Yom Tov, and [the gift] was brought for the householder: it is forbidden for the guests as well; but if they are not fixed with him, only came by chance, they should not eat in the householder's house, but in their own house it is permitted; see there. And see in the responsa Ginas Veradim, Orach Chaim, klal 4, siman 1: a non-Jew who brought a gift from a Jew living in another city by way of ships on flowing rivers, and he ruled to be lenient, and one of the reasons is that the rivers in Egypt are not clearly ones people ford by foot, and one can say the law is [travel] above ten handbreadths, and one may be lenient by combining further reasons; and a certain sage disagreed with him and forbade, and wrote that so his teacher ruled thus; and see there in simanim 1, 2, and 3. And see in Penei Meiros, part 2, siman 115, regarding a non-Jew who brought fish to sell in a city whose majority are non-Jews, where he wrote several distinctions of law.
