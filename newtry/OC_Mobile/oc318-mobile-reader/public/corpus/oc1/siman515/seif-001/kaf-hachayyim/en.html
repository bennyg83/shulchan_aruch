1) [Se'if 1] A non-Jew who brought a gift to a Jew on Yom Tov, etc. The same law applies if the non-Jew brought produce to sell in a city whose residents are mostly Jewish, because it is presumed that he brought it for the majority. This is evident from Eruvin 40a concerning a certain turnip, etc. It is also stated explicitly in Machshirin, chapter 2, mishnah 6: 'If he found vegetables being sold there, if the majority are non-Jews, etc.' See there. This is likewise evident from what is written below, se'if 6; siman 517, se'if 1; and the later authorities there. Chayei Adam, rule 100, subsection 9, rules leniently regarding a non-Jewish seller, treating the produce as though he trapped it for himself. He means that one need not wait after Yom Tov for the time required to perform the act. Mishnah Berurah, in Biur Halachah at the beginning of this siman, and Responsa Maharam of Rothenburg, Acharonim, siman 14, already dispute his ruling. See there. Pischei Teshuvah cites them. See there. It is possible that Chayei Adam means a city whose residents are mostly non-Jews. Although he wrote without qualification, he relied upon the reader's understanding.
2) There. A non-Jew who brought a gift to a Jew on Yom Tov, etc. This implies that if the non-Jew did not bring it without payment, but the Jew fixed a price with him before Yom Tov to bring fish, and the non-Jew delayed and brought them on Yom Tov, they are permitted immediately that evening. If the item is of a type that does not grow attached to the ground and does not require trapping, it is permitted even on Yom Tov, even if brought from beyond the techum, because the non-Jew acts for his own benefit. Magen Avraham, subsection 50. Magen Avraham nevertheless writes that this might resemble a non-Jew who lit a lamp on Shabbos, where fixing a price does not help because he acts for the Jew, as written above at the beginning of siman 276. See there for his discussion. Magen Avraham there rules leniently that one need not wait the time required to perform the act. Beis Meir, however, disputes this ruling of Magen Avraham. See there. Mishnah Berurah in Biur Halachah likewise disputes Magen Avraham's ruling, but concludes that if another doubt can be combined with this one, then where a price was fixed one may permit even an item involving a full melachah immediately after the first day of Yom Tov. Regarding techumin, when a price was fixed, one may permit it immediately after the first day of Yom Tov without waiting the time required to perform the act, even if no additional doubt can be combined. See there.
3) There. If some of that species remains attached to the ground, etc. We are concerned that it may have been detached that day. Anything detached from the ground on Yom Tov is forbidden, because the Sages decreed lest one detach it. If it is known that it was not detached that day, it is permitted even if it appears as though it was detached that day. Riaz. Shiltei HaGibborim on Rif, chapter 3 of Beitzah, cites him. Rashba writes likewise in Avodas HaKodesh, gate 1, subsection 10. See below, subsection 61.
4) There. If some of that species remains attached to the ground, etc. Regarding why we do not presume that it was detached the preceding day, Rabbeinu Yonah writes: 'Whenever some of that species remains attached, people ordinarily gather it on the day it is needed and do not gather it the preceding day. Since the majority act this way, we do not presume leniently that it was gathered earlier.' These are his words. Ran in the chapter Shoel of Shabbos and Rashba in his novellae to Beitzah cite him. Only when it is recognizable that the produce was not gathered or trapped that day do we presume leniently, as written below, se'if 3. See there.
5) There. If some of its species remains attached to the ground, etc. Truffles and mushrooms brought by a non-Jew to a Jew are forbidden. Rokeach, siman 300. This teaching is necessary lest one say that they are not considered attached to the ground, since they sometimes also grow upon wood. Since the place of their growth is treated as attached to the ground, they have that status. Thus the chapter Shemonah Sheratzim states in Shabbos 107b that one who uproots a mushroom from the handle of a pitcher is liable for uprooting something from its place of growth. Beis Yosef; Olas Shabbos, subsection 6. See above, siman 336, subsection 40.
6) There. If some of its species remains attached to the ground, etc. A non-Jew brought roses as a gift on the first day of Yom Tov. The woman of the house, not knowing the law, mixed them with other roses in vessels that had been purchased before Yom Tov, and the roses brought by the non-Jew were the minority. Zera Emes, Orach Chaim, siman 72, inclines to permit moving the vessels and smelling the roses on the second day of Yom Tov after removing some of the roses. Machazik Berachah, subsection 1; Zechor LeAvraham, subsection 10; Shaarei Teshuvah, cite him. It is possible that he means that even according to the stringent opinion cited by Rama, it is permitted in such a case. See there. See below, subsection 14.
7) There. Or if it requires trapping. An item that requires trapping has the same law as something attached to the ground. This follows the incident in Eruvin 39b involving a young deer that was trapped on the first day and slaughtered on the second, etc. Beis Yosef.
8) There. It is forbidden even to one, etc. Even Rabbi Shimon agrees regarding this form of muktzeh. Rashi, Beitzah 24b. Beis Yosef cites him. Levush; Olas Shabbos, subsection 1; Magen Avraham, subsection 1, write likewise. Accordingly, even the alternative opinion cited by Rama above, siman 495, se'if 4, in the gloss, which does not accept muktzeh even on Yom Tov, agrees that this is forbidden as muktzeh, because it resembles dried figs and raisins. The statement above, siman 497, se'if 15, that an item belonging to a non-Jew does not require designation applies only to muktzeh involving living creatures and similar cases. Muktzeh resulting from attachment to the ground or the need for trapping is forbidden even when the item belongs to a non-Jew. Rashi there writes this explicitly. Olas Shabbos there; Levush.
9) There. It is forbidden even to one, etc. This follows the rule for every forbidden melachah performed by a non-Jew for a Jew: it is forbidden to every Jew until evening and until the time required to perform the act has passed. This is explained in Shabbos 122a regarding a lamp and a ramp, and in Shulchan Aruch above, siman 266, se'if 1, and siman 325. See there. There is no distinction between produce belonging to the non-Jew and produce belonging to the Jew, because the prohibition results from the melachah performed for a Jew. Even if the non-Jew harvested it for the Jew's animal, the Jew may not feed it to the animal. If the non-Jew feeds it to the animal, however, the Jew need not object, as explained in the Talmud there and in siman 325, se'ifim 11, 12, and 13, and our words there, with Heaven's help.
