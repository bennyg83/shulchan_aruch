Behold, before we enter the explanation of this siman I will copy the wording of the Gemara and its explanation briefly. It is stated [Beitza 24b]: Rav Papa said the halacha is that a non-Jew who brought a gift to a Jew on Yom Tov, if there is of that species among what is attached, is forbidden, and in the evening too they are forbidden until waiting the time it takes for them to be done. And Rashi explained that this "forbidden" is because of muktzeh: since he did not pick them yesterday he set them aside intentionally; and even according to R' Shimon who generally does not hold of muktzeh, he agrees in this; see there. And that which it says "and in the evening they are forbidden until waiting the time it takes for them to be done," Rashi explained: so that he not benefit from melacha of Yom Tov; and that which it says they are forbidden for the time it takes for them to be done, implying that afterward they are permitted, means even on the evening of the first as well: even though it is the night of the second Yom Tov, he waits only the time it takes for them to be done and afterward it is permitted, whichever way you turn: if the night is weekday, behold he waited the time it takes for them to be done; and if it is holy, it turns out they were picked on a weekday. And with this approach most Rishonim agreed. And there are among the Rishonim who explained that from its saying "the time it takes for them to be done" it implies we require a time fit for doing, namely the evening of the second Yom Tov after waiting the time it takes for them to be done, for then it is weekday time and fit for doing; and their reason is that therefore the Sages were stringent to forbid the entire second day and also afterward the time of doing, for if you say they are permitted at the departure of the first Yom Tov we are concerned lest he tell a non-Jew on the first Yom Tov, "go and pick for me," so that he can eat of them on the second. And there is a further practical difference between these approaches: according to Rashi and those who support him who explained that the reason they were stringent with waiting the time it takes for them to be done is so that he not benefit from melacha of Yom Tov, this law applies both to the one for whom it was brought and to others; but according to the later view that the reason they were stringent with waiting the time it takes for them to be done is so that he not come to tell a non-Jew ab initio, "go and pick for me," there are among the poskim who hold that this reason applies only to forbid waiting the time it takes for them to be done for the one for whom it was brought, but not for others, for certainly he will not tell a non-Jew "go and pick for me" in order to benefit others, since a person does not sin and not for himself. And automatically according to this approach there is a leniency regarding others: it is forbidden for others only the first day alone because of muktzeh, and afterward it is permitted immediately and one need not wait even the time it takes for them to be done. And there are among the poskim who hold that even according to this approach the prohibition of waiting the time it takes for them to be done includes both for him and for others. Further it is stated there in the Gemara: if there is not of that species among what is attached within the techum it is permitted; outside the techum it is forbidden. And this "forbidden," the poskim agreed, is only to eat and to benefit from this, but in handling it is permitted [and behold from the Gemara's not mentioning that afterward one must wait the time it takes for them to be done, it is implied that regarding techumim they were lenient that one need not wait in the evening the time it takes for them to be done; but most Rishonim agreed that also in this one must wait in the evening the time it takes for them to be done; however in this, even those who practice to be stringent regarding what is attached like the second view, regarding techumim we practice to be lenient like the first view, that it suffices to wait the time it takes for them to be done on the evening of the first Yom Tov alone]. There is a further leniency regarding techumim: what was brought for this Jew is permitted for another Jew to eat that thing even on that very day [nevertheless one may not handle outside four amos from the place where the non-Jew set the thing down, except if he set it in a house or in a city prepared with eruvin, which is considered entirely like four amos]. And now we come to explain this siman with Hashem's help.
{1} A gift to a Jew: and the same law applies when he brought to sell in a city where most Jews live, for as a matter of course he brought to sell to Jews; and likewise if he fixed a price with the non-Jew beforehand to bring him fruit or fish from erev Yom Tov and he brought them on Yom Tov: in all of these it is forbidden, and we do not say regarding a non-Jew that he acts with his own intent in order to profit or to cast off his work, since in any case he does his melacha so that the Jew benefit on Yom Tov; and see Biur Halacha.
{2} If there is of its species among what is attached etc.: for we are stringent from doubt, perhaps he detached or trapped them on Yom Tov with intent to bring them to a Jew; and there are those who hold that for this reason we do not assume they were detached and trapped from the evening beforehand, for most people's way is not to detach from the evening beforehand for the need of tomorrow, but on their day, unless it is recognizable in them that they are withered as in seif 3.
{3} Forbidden etc.: like the law of every forbidden melacha that a non-Jew did for a Jew, which is forbidden for every Jew until the evening after waiting the time it takes for them to be done; and on that very day aside from this it is forbidden in this, for regarding what is attached and what lacks trapping there is muktzeh according to everyone: since he did not pick them yesterday, presumably he set them aside intentionally, like dried figs and raisins that were not fully dried [and also regarding fruits that are easy to detach and a person desires them, there is because of a decree lest he ascend and detach, like fruits that fall]; and even if the non-Jew picked and trapped them for himself this reason applies; but for this reason it is forbidden only on its day, while in the evening it would be permitted immediately as clarified in seif 2.
{4} To eat it: and the same law applies to benefit from it, for example myrtle that was cut is forbidden to smell, and likewise other benefits.
{5} And even if he transgressed and put etc.: and the same law applies if unwittingly he put it into his mouth; and it is implied from the silent assumption of the poskim that even if he already recited a blessing over it it is forbidden to swallow, for it is muktzeh like other eating of a prohibition.
{6} And even to handle them: for it is muktzeh as above; and also since it is forbidden in eating, automatically it is also forbidden in handling, similar to tevel [and it is not comparable to neveila which is forbidden in eating and permitted in handling: there it is fit for dogs, but here he does not intend to throw it to the dogs; and also neveila's prohibition is not because of the day's prohibition]. And for this reason even in the evening which is the beginning of another day, since it is forbidden in eating until waiting the time it takes for them to be done, automatically it is also forbidden in handling; and see at the end of seif 4 in the gloss and in what is written there in Mishnah Berurah.
{7} Waiting the time it takes for them to be done: so that he not benefit from melacha that was done for a Jew on Yom Tov; and since he waits a measure such that he would benefit on a weekday, he no longer benefits from melacha of Yom Tov.
{8} And on the two Diaspora Yom Tov days: for originally it deals with the Land of Israel which is only one day, or outside the Land where the melacha was done on the second Yom Tov, that he waits until evening which is weekday; and here he added even on the two Diaspora Yom Tov days, that for the evening of the first day it is Yom Tov, even so one need not wait for the evening of the first Yom Tov even for the one for whom it was brought, only the time it takes for them to be done then: for whichever way you turn one day is weekday; for if the first day is holy, behold now it is weekday and waiting the time it takes for them to be done suffices; and if the first is weekday, behold the melacha was done in permission and one need not delay at all.
{9} And there are those who are stringent: who hold that the main prohibition is that if you permit him, perhaps he will tell the non-Jew to act; and therefore even on the second Yom Tov it is forbidden until evening at the hour fit for doing, for if not so there is still room to decree that he will tell a non-Jew to act on the first day in order to prepare for the second Yom Tov [because he knows that if he tells the non-Jew on the second Yom Tov itself it will be forbidden to benefit on that day until evening after waiting the time it takes for them to be done]. However some say there is also a leniency according to this view: according to them the entire second day is forbidden only for that Jew for whom it was brought, but for other Jews it is permitted immediately on the evening of the first day, and one need not wait even the time it takes for them to be done.
{10} Until the departure of the second Yom Tov: and that is also with waiting the time it takes for them to be done.
{11} Tur in the name of Rabbeinu Tam and Rashi: a scribe's error, for Rashi is the main first view, and it should say "and the Rosh" [so wrote Darkhei Moshe the long version].
{12} For the need of guests etc. that was not brought etc.: meaning, the reason is since it was not brought for them; namely, for the need of guests which is a mitzvah we rely on those who are lenient according to this view for others on the second Yom Tov after waiting the time it takes for them to be done; but aside from this one should not be lenient even for others, for the poskim do not distinguish in this. And know that regarding our reckoning guests as others, some explain that this is specifically when he invited them after they brought the gift, for then it was not brought with their intent; for guests whom he invited beforehand, one can say they are considered like members of his household and it is forbidden. And the later authorities further wrote to be lenient for guests from another city even if he invited them beforehand, and even he himself eats with them because of their honor, for this is included in the mitzvah of hospitality to guests; and we rely in this on the first view that permits on the second Diaspora Yom Tov after waiting the time it takes for them to be done; and before waiting the time it takes for them to be done one should not be lenient in any manner even for others.
{13} For them: and if he fixed a price with the non-Jew on erev Yom Tov that they bring him at a permitted time and they brought him on the first Yom Tov, one may be lenient on the second Yom Tov after waiting the time it takes for them to be done even for that Jew himself for whom it was brought. Taz wrote that when he has great pressure and need in eating, one may rely entirely on the first view to permit even for that Jew himself for whom it was brought on day one after the measure of waiting the time it takes for them to be done at the beginning of the second night.
{14} And likewise if it was brought on the second Yom Tov etc.: and in this according to everyone there is no distinction between him and others, since it was brought on this day; however if he brought at the beginning of the night when there is no opportunity to trap today, and necessarily they were trapped on the first day, it has the law of one who brought on the first Yom Tov; and likewise if the non-Jew brought at the beginning of the first night it has the law of trapped on erev Yom Tov; and if he brought from within the techum it is permitted.
{15} But on the two Yom Tov days of Rosh Hashana: for "whichever way you turn" does not apply to them, for it is one sanctity and it is like one long day.
{16} Or on Yom Tov adjacent to Shabbos etc.: for example where Yom Tov was on Friday and he brought them on Yom Tov, or where it was on Sunday and he brought them on the Shabbos before Yom Tov.
{17} Until Motzei Yom Tov and Shabbos: this measure one must wait even if they were picked or trapped of themselves; for even though they are two sanctities, nevertheless it is forbidden from one to the other because of preparation, for Yom Tov does not prepare for Shabbos and Shabbos does not prepare for Yom Tov [namely, since on this day it is forbidden to eat it, it appears as if he prepares only for its fellow]; and as above in siman 513, seif 5, regarding an egg; however in this, on Motzei Shabbos it is permitted immediately, and they would not need to wait after Motzei Shabbos and Yom Tov the time it takes for them to be done.
{18} Waiting the time it takes for them to be done: for if he does not wait and eats immediately, behold he benefits from melacha of Yom Tov, for had he not brought it for him on Yom Tov he could not have benefited from them in permission unless he brought it for him on Motzei Shabbos; and likewise regarding Yom Tov that falls after Shabbos, so that he not benefit from melacha of Shabbos.
{19} And the measure of waiting the time it takes for them to be done etc.: the Mechaber refers to the entire seif.
{20} Enough time that he go: and if a non-Jew brought them by riding on a horse, one need wait only enough time for riding there [later authorities].
{21} To the place from which he picked: that is, if he knows from which place he picked, whether far or near, we measure by that.
{22} Their measure etc.: that is to say, both for stringency, namely if there is a doubt perhaps within the techum or outside the techum, one must wait enough time that they bring from outside the techum; and likewise for leniency, for example if he knows with certainty from outside the techum but it is not known if near the techum or very far, one should not be stringent to wait except enough time of a measure from just outside the techum.
