Before entering the explanation of this siman, I will copy the language of the Gemara and explain it briefly. It is stated in Beitzah 24b: Rav Pappa said: The halakhah is that if a non-Jew brought a gift to a Jew on Yom Tov, if that species exists attached to the ground, it is forbidden, and in the evening it is also forbidden for the time needed for it to be done. Rashi explained that "forbidden" is because of muktzeh: since he did not pick them yesterday, he removed them from his mind. Even according to R. Shimon, who generally does not accept muktzeh, he agrees here; see there. What it says that in the evening they are forbidden for the time needed for it to be done, Rashi explained, is so that one not benefit from labor done on Yom Tov. The statement that they are forbidden for the time needed for them to be done, implying that afterward they are permitted, means even on the first evening; although it is the night of the second Yom Tov, he waits only the time needed for it to be done and it is afterward permitted whichever way you look at it: if the night is weekday, he waited the time needed for it to be done, and if it is holy, then they were picked on a weekday. Most early authorities agreed with this approach. Some early authorities explained that since it says "for the time needed for it to be done," it implies that we require a time fit for doing it, namely the evening after the second Yom Tov, for that is weekday and fit for doing. Their reason is that the Sages were stringent to prohibit the entire second day and afterward the time needed for doing, for if you say they are permitted after the first Yom Tov, we are concerned that he may tell the non-Jew on the first Yom Tov: go pick for me, so that he can eat from them on the second. There is another practical difference between these approaches: according to Rashi and those with him, who explained that the reason for stringency for the time needed for it to be done is so one not benefit from Yom Tov labor, this law applies both to the person for whom it was brought and to others. But according to the latter view, that the reason for the stringency is so that one not come to tell a non-Jew initially, "go pick for me," some poskim hold that this reason applies only to prohibit for the time needed for it to be done to the person for whom it was brought, but not to others, for he certainly will not tell a non-Jew to go pick for him in order to benefit others, since a person does not sin for no benefit to himself. Consequently, according to this approach there is a leniency regarding others: it is forbidden to others only on the first day because of muktzeh, but afterward it is immediately permitted and they need not even wait the time needed for it to be done. Some poskim hold that even according to this approach, the prohibition of the time needed for it to be done includes both him and others. It is further stated there in the Gemara: if that species does not exist attached to the ground, if it is from within the techum it is permitted; if from outside the techum it is forbidden. The poskim agreed that this prohibition is only to eat and benefit from it, but moving it is permitted. Since the Gemara did not mention that one must afterward wait the time needed for it to be done, it implies that regarding techumin they were lenient and one need not wait in the evening for that time. But most early authorities agreed that even in this one must wait until evening the time needed for it to be done. Yet in this matter, even those who customarily are stringent regarding attached produce like the second opinion, regarding techumin our custom is to be lenient like the first opinion, that it suffices to wait the time needed for it to be done only on the evening after the first Yom Tov. There is another leniency regarding techumin: something brought for this Jew may be eaten by another Jew even that same day. Nevertheless, it may not be moved outside four amot from the place where the non-Jew placed it, unless he placed it in a house or in a city arranged with an eruv, where all of it is considered like four amot. Now we will explain this siman with God's help.
{1} A gift to a Jew: The same applies if he brought it to sell in a city where most residents are Jews, for presumably he brought it to sell to Jews. Likewise, if one fixed a payment for the non-Jew to bring him fruit or fish before Yom Tov and he brought them on Yom Tov, in all these cases it is forbidden. We do not say that the non-Jew acted on his own behalf in order to profit or complete his work, since in any case he performs his labor so that the Jew will benefit on Yom Tov. See Biur Halakhah.
{2} If that species exists attached, etc.: We are stringent because of doubt, perhaps he detached or trapped them on Yom Tov with intent to bring them to the Jew. Some hold that therefore we do not assume they were detached or trapped before evening, because most people do not usually detach the day before for the next day, but rather on the day itself, unless it is recognizable that they are withered, as in seif 3.
{3} Forbidden, etc.: Like any prohibited labor performed by a non-Jew for a Jew, it is forbidden to all Jews until evening after the time needed for it to be done. On that same day, in any case, it is forbidden here because attached produce and something lacking trapping have muktzeh according to all views; since he did not pick them yesterday, presumably he removed them from his mind, like figs and raisins not fully dried. Also with fruit that is easy to detach and that a person desires, there is a decree lest he climb and detach, like fallen fruit. Even if the non-Jew picked or trapped them for himself, this reason applies; but because of this reason it is forbidden only that day, while in the evening it would be permitted immediately, as explained in seif 2.
{4} To eat it: The same applies to benefiting from it, such as a hadas that was cut; it is forbidden to smell it, and so too other benefits.
{5} And even if he transgressed and put it, etc.: The same applies if he unwittingly put it into his mouth. It is implied from the plain words of the poskim that even if he already recited a blessing over it, he may not swallow it, for it is muktzeh like other forbidden foods.
{6} And even to move them: For they are muktzeh, as above. Also, since they are forbidden to eat, they are automatically forbidden to move, similar to tevel. This is not like neveilah, which is forbidden to eat but permitted to move, because there it is fit for dogs; here his intention is not to throw it to dogs, and also neveilah is not forbidden because of the day's prohibition. For this reason, even in the evening, which is the beginning of another day, since it is forbidden to eat until the time needed for it to be done, it is automatically forbidden to move as well. See the end of seif 4 in the gloss and what is written there in Mishnah Berurah.
{7} For the time needed for it to be done: So that one not benefit from labor done for a Jew on Yom Tov. Once he waits the amount of time needed to benefit during the weekday, he is no longer benefiting from Yom Tov labor.
{8} And on the two Yomim Tovim of the Diaspora: At first he was speaking of Eretz Yisrael, where there is only one day, or of the Diaspora where the labor was done on the second Yom Tov, so he waits until evening, which is weekday. Here he adds that even on the two Yomim Tovim of the Diaspora, where the evening after the first day is Yom Tov, nevertheless even the person for whom it was brought need wait only the time needed for it to be done on the evening of the first Yom Tov, for whichever way you look at it, one day is weekday: if the first day is holy, now is weekday and the time needed for it to be done suffices; and if the first day was weekday, the labor was done permissibly and no waiting is needed at all.
{9} And some are stringent: They hold that the main prohibition is that if you permit it, perhaps he will tell the non-Jew to do it. Therefore even on the second Yom Tov it is forbidden until evening at a time fit for doing it, for otherwise there is still reason to decree that he will tell the non-Jew to do it on the first day in order to prepare for the second Yom Tov, since he knows that if he tells the non-Jew on the second Yom Tov itself, it will be forbidden to benefit from it that day until evening after the time needed for it to be done. However, some say that according to this opinion there is also a leniency: according to them it is forbidden all of the second day only to the Jew for whom it was brought, but to other Jews it is permitted immediately on the evening of the first day, and they need not wait even the time needed for it to be done.
{10} Until after the second Yom Tov: This too means with the time needed for it to be done.
{11} Tur in the name of Rabbeinu Tam and Rashi: This is a scribal error, for Rashi is the principal first opinion; it should say "and the Rosh" (so wrote Darkei Moshe HaArukh).
{12} For guests, etc., for whom it was not brought, etc.: The explanation is that the reason is because it was not brought for them. That is, for guests, which is a mitzvah, we rely on those who are lenient according to this opinion for others on the second Yom Tov after the time needed for it to be done. Otherwise one should not be lenient even for others, for the poskim do not distinguish in this. Know that what we consider guests as "others" is explained by some as only where he invited them after the gift was brought, for then it was not brought with them in mind; but guests whom he invited earlier may be considered like members of his household and forbidden. The later authorities further wrote to be lenient for guests from another city even if he invited them earlier, and even if he himself eats with them because of their honor, since this is included in the mitzvah of welcoming guests. In this we rely on the first opinion, which permits on the second Yom Tov of the Diaspora after the time needed for it to be done. Before the time needed for it to be done, one should not be lenient in any manner, even for others.
{13} For them: If he fixed a payment with the non-Jew before Yom Tov for him to bring it at a permitted time, and they brought it on the first Yom Tov, one may be lenient on the second Yom Tov after the time needed for it to be done, even for that Jew himself for whom it was brought. The Taz wrote that when he has great pressure and need for eating, one may rely entirely on the first opinion to permit even that Jew himself for whom it was brought on the first day after the time needed for it to be done at the beginning of the second night.
{14} Likewise if it was brought on the second Yom Tov, etc.: In this everyone agrees there is no distinction between him and others, since it was brought that day. However, if it was brought at the beginning of the night, when there is no time to trap that day and it must have been trapped on the first day, it has the law of something brought on the first Yom Tov. Likewise, if the non-Jew brought it at the beginning of the first night, it has the law of something trapped before Yom Tov; and if he brought it from within the techum, it is permitted.
{15} But on the two Yomim Tovim of Rosh Hashanah: The whichever-way reasoning does not apply to them, for they are one sanctity and are like one long day.
{16} Or on Yom Tov adjacent to Shabbat, etc.: For example, Yom Tov was on Friday and he brought them on Yom Tov, or it was on Sunday and he brought them on the Shabbat before Yom Tov.
{17} Until after Yom Tov and Shabbat: This amount of time must be waited even if they were picked or trapped by themselves. Although they are two sanctities, nevertheless it is forbidden from one to the other because of preparation, for Yom Tov does not prepare for Shabbat and Shabbat does not prepare for Yom Tov. Since on this day it is forbidden to eat it, it appears as though it is preparing only for the other day, as above in siman 513 seif 5 regarding an egg. However, in this case on Motzaei Shabbat it is permitted immediately, and they did not require waiting after Motzaei Shabbat and Yom Tov for the time needed for it to be done.
{18} For the time needed for it to be done: For if he did not wait and ate immediately, he would benefit from Yom Tov labor, since if the non-Jew had not brought it to him on Yom Tov, he could not have benefited from it permissibly unless it were brought to him on Motzaei Shabbat. So too where Yom Tov falls after Shabbat, this is so that one not benefit from Shabbat labor.
{19} And the measure of the time needed for it to be done, etc.: The Mechaber refers to the whole seif.
{20} The time for him to go: If a non-Jew brought them by riding a horse, he need wait only the time needed to ride there (later authorities).
{21} To the place where he picked: This means that if he knows the place from which he picked, whether far or near, we measure according to it.
{22} Their measure, etc.: Meaning both stringently, namely where there is doubt whether it is from within the techum or outside the techum, he must wait until the time needed to bring it from outside the techum; and leniently, such as where he knows for certain that it is from outside the techum but does not know whether near the techum or very far, one need not be stringent to wait except the measure from outside the techum.
