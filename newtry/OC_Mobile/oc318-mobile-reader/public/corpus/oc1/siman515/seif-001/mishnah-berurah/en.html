Behold before entering this explanation of the siman I will copy the Gemara's wording and its explanation briefly. It is stated [Beitza 24b]: Rav Pappa said: the law is — a non-Jew who brought a gift to an Israelite on Yom Tov — if there is of that species attached, it is forbidden; and at night also forbidden with "so that they be made"; and Rashi explained: these "forbidden" are because of muktzeh, since they did not gather from yesterday he set them aside from his intent; and even for R' Shimon who has no muktzeh in general, he agrees here — see there. And what it says "and at night forbidden with so that they be made" — Rashi explained: so he not benefit from melacha of Yom Tov; and what it says "with so that they be made they are forbidden" and it implies afterward they are permitted — meaning even for the first night, even though the night of the second Yom Tov is weekday, he waits only with "so that they be made" and afterward permitted; from "perforce": if the night is weekday, behold he waited with "so that they be made"; if it is holy, it turns out they were gathered on weekday; and for this approach most early authorities agreed; and there are among the early authorities who explained from what it says "so that they be made" it implies we require time fit for doing — namely until the night of the second Yom Tov with "so that they be made," for then it is weekday time fit for doing; and their reason: therefore the Sages were stringent to forbid the entire second day and also afterward with time of doing, for if you say they are permitted at Motzei the first Yom Tov, we are concerned lest he tell a non-Jew on the first Yom Tov go and gather for me so he can eat from them on the second; and there is further distinction between these approaches: for Rashi and his supporters who explained the reason they were stringent with "so that they be made" is so he not benefit from melacha of Yom Tov — this law applies both for one for whom it was brought and for others; but per the other view whose reason for "so that they be made" is so he not come to tell a non-Jew initially go and gather for me — some poskim hold this reason applies only to forbid with "so that they be made" for one for whom it was brought, but not for others, for certainly he will not tell a non-Jew go and gather for me in order to benefit others — a person does not sin and not for himself; and automatically for this approach there is leniency for others: it is not forbidden for others except the first day alone because of muktzeh, and afterward permitted immediately without waiting even with "so that they be made"; and there are poskim who hold that also for this approach the "so that they be made" prohibition includes both for him and for others.
