(א) A non-Jew who brought a gift etc. I will set out the beginning of the sugya briefly with Rashi's and Rabbenu Tam's explanations and the laws branching from them in the chapter "one may not trap," in brief: the halacha is — a non-Jew who brought a gift to an Israelite on Yom Tov: if there is of its type attached, it is forbidden; and for evening it is forbidden with bichdei sheya'asu; and Rashi explained due to muktzeh, for even R' Shimon concedes regarding dried figs and raisins and attached is similar to this since they were not picked yesterday — he designated them from his mind; and do not distinguish regarding attached between his and a non-Jew's; and for evening with bichdei sheya'asu so as not to benefit from Yom Tov melacha; and that which it said for evening with bichdei sheya'asu and afterward permitted — that is even for the first evening also permitted in any case: if the night is weekday behold he waited with bichdei sheya'asu, and if it is holy it turns out they were picked on weekday; and Tosafot wrote in the chapter "in all we may mix" that bichdei sheya'asu for Rashi does not require waiting until he goes and traps and comes and detaches and brings, but only the measure of detaching for a small moment — then he does not benefit from Yom Tov melacha; and likewise Tur for Rashi; but Beit Yosef brought in the name of Ran and Rashba for Rashi that the bringing too is included. They further wrote there that also for Rashi when the non-Jew did for a non-Jew he need not wait for evening with bichdei sheya'asu but it is permitted for evening immediately — and this is not as the Rosh and Tur imply for Rashi, that there is no distinction; and Rabbenu Tam challenged Rashi who explained due to not benefiting from Yom Tov melacha — from that which we learned one who cooks on Shabbat b'shogeg may eat; and why, behold he benefits from Shabbat melacha and nevertheless permitted — all the more so when the non-Jew does the melacha for himself from himself that it is permitted; and therefore Rabbenu Tam explained the reason that bichdei sheya'asu is required is due to a decree lest he tell a non-Jew to bring for him on Yom Tov so he eat at evening immediately; and if so he must wait also for the second evening lest he tell him to bring on the first day for the second Yom Tov day; and if he does for a non-Jew it is permitted for evening immediately; and likewise regarding something trapped on its own where "lest he tell him to bring" does not apply; and the Rosh wrote two distinctions between Rashi and Rabbenu Tam regarding bichdei sheya'asu: if the non-Jew does for this Israelite on Shabbat — if there is prohibition for another Israelite, bichdei sheya'asu is required; for Rashi it is forbidden for he benefits from Shabbat melacha, and likewise Semag; and for Rabbenu Tam it is permitted for a person does not sin and it is not for his benefit to say he should bring on Yom Tov for another. There is further a distinction regarding one who cooks for an Israelite b'shogeg — for Rashi bichdei sheya'asu is required, and for Rabbenu Tam not required, for we are not concerned he will tell a non-Jew to cook on Shabbat. It turns out regarding bichdei sheya'asu there are two leniencies for Rabbenu Tam; and regarding the second Yom Tov day itself there is stringency for Rabbenu Tam, for what the non-Jew brought on the first Yom Tov day for an Israelite is forbidden until the evening of the second Yom Tov day with bichdei sheya'asu; and for Rashi it is permitted for the first evening with bichdei sheya'asu; and I am not fit to decide — nevertheless I said what was difficult for me on Rabbenu Tam's explanation, that we do not find a decree except due to a d'oraisa prohibition, but not a decree on a decree unless in places where they said it is all one decree — that is when they decreed the first decree they included with it also a second decree; and if so here where the prohibition of telling a non-Jew to bring on Yom Tov is shevut, for every telling a non-Jew is shevut — why forbid on the first day for evening with bichdei sheya'asu, and bichdei sheya'asu all of the second day, since by Torah there is no prohibition at all? But for Rashi it is satisfactory that bichdei sheya'asu is required due to not benefiting etc.; and to answer Rabbenu Tam's challenge on Rashi, it appears to me in combination with Ran's answer on Rashi's explanation who explained due to muktzeh — forbidden on Yom Tov and after Yom Tov for evening with bichdei sheya'asu due to not benefiting etc. — why does the reason of not benefiting not suffice also on Yom Tov itself, and why is the muktzeh reason needed? And he said that for another Israelite it would be permitted, as with a non-Jew who lit a candle for an Israelite or made a lamb; but regarding attached here it is forbidden even for another due to muktzeh — this is not so, for according to Rashi also regarding a candle it is forbidden for every Israelite if he lit for one; and therefore it must be said that the muktzeh reason is needed, for even if the non-Jew picked for his own need on Yom Tov it is forbidden for an Israelite on his day — thus Ran's words. And Rashal answered that the muktzeh reason is needed to forbid even carrying — for according to Rashi who wrote not benefiting from Yom Tov melacha, that itself relates to the muktzeh reason; for certainly every melacha the non-Jew does for himself or an Israelite b'shogeg does not relate thereby to Yom Tov melacha, since there is no prohibition at the time of doing the melacha, but due to muktzeh they touched it on Yom Tov; and every muktzeh on the day requires the Israelite to wait somewhat in the evening and prepare for himself so he can eat that thing; and here where the non-Jew prepared for an Israelite — when the Israelite eats it immediately in the evening without any preparation and detaching the attached, he profits thereby through the non-Jew who prepared for him on Yom Tov; and this is Rashi's language: not benefiting from Yom Tov melacha does not mean from the melacha itself, but that through the non-Jew's melacha he benefits in what is prepared before him from what was muktzeh for him during the day — and this is forbidden; also regarding a candle the non-Jew lit for an Israelite, even after Shabbat until he can light the candle himself, since the non-Jew intends for Israelite's benefit that it be ready before him — one may not benefit from that preparation at all, for the non-Jew intends there should be permission for an Israelite through him in a place forbidden to him; and likewise a non-Jew who cooks on Shabbat for an Israelite's benefit is forbidden for everyone with bichdei sheya'asu — and this does not apply to an Israelite who cooks b'shogeg, for he does not intend to permit any prohibition thereby; if so Rabbenu Tam's challenge and Ran's challenge on Rashi are answered in my humble opinion. And there is another dispute according to Rabbenu Tam that we mentioned: the Rosh and Tur wrote the leniency I mentioned regarding another Israelite that it is permitted for Rabbenu Tam; and Semak wrote that even for Rabbenu Tam it is forbidden for every Israelite even on the second Yom Tov day if they were brought on the first Yom Tov day, as Beit Yosef wrote. And for practical halacha Beit Yosef ruled like Rashi, for thus hold Rambam, Rashba, Rif, and Ra'avad; and Rama brought Rabbenu Tam's view in the name of "some are stringent"; and what he wrote until motza'ei second Yom Tov day — that is with bichdei sheya'asu, likewise Tur in explanation. And Rama decided in the name of Taz to be lenient where there is need — for guests specifically; but not for guests' need it is forbidden for others, to be concerned for Semak's view according to Rabbenu Tam. It turns out he ruled like Rabbenu Tam except it appears to be lenient — that is in what he is stringent on the second Yom Tov day; but the two leniencies Rabbenu Tam is lenient regarding bichdei sheya'asu on Shabbat that we mentioned — one should practice like Rashi who is stringent even for others with bichdei sheya'asu as written; and likewise a non-Jew who cooks on Shabbat for Israelite's need requires bichdei sheya'asu after Shabbat; and likewise Rashal's conclusion is to be stringent like the stringency of two explanations — that is, bichdei sheya'asu is required in every case where it is forbidden on the day even for others, and on the second Yom Tov day it is forbidden for him too, but for others permitted on the second Yom Tov day even not in a place of guests — not like Semak, and thus practical halacha:

(ב) Not needed for Yom Tov for guests' need etc. Thus Terumat HaDeshen siman 78: one of the great ones ruled like Semak's words; however if there is need in the matter, such as he invited important guests and established those gifts for them, it appears one may rely on the view of the Rishonim who wrote: even per Rabbeinu Tam's view permitted for another Israelite, for there is no room to decree in such a case; therefore it appears to permit, since for Rashi it is permitted even for one for whom it was brought; and Beit Yosef challenged this: it implies guests have the law of another Israelite, and this is the opposite of what is written in chapter Bekhol Ma'aravin: whoever comes to the house of the Exilarch comes with the intent of all the rabbis; and Beit Yosef answered: guests are different, for they do not regularly dine at this table like rabbis who regularly are at the Exilarch's house; and it is difficult: if so Terumat HaDeshen should have distinguished in this; and Maharshal went far against Beit Yosef to say he erred in this challenge, for Terumat HaDeshen did not deal with one for whom it was brought who invited others — that is obvious like him; rather with one who invited guests to his house — although forbidden to him from Semak's stringency, nevertheless because of guests' honor it is fit to permit him; and I say: one who raises the difficulty raises well — for if you say that other person is concerned for Semak's stringency per Rabbeinu Tam — why should that guest be concerned for this, that because he is honored it should be permitted to him what is not permitted to one who is not honored? And further, if so it should have said: and if the other Israelite is honored, it is permitted to him; rather this explanation has no legs; the matter is as its simple meaning: that which the Rabbis forbade at the house of the Exilarch is if the prohibition on the Exilarch himself is clear without doubt, like that which was brought to him outside the techum; unlike the concern of attached brought on the first Yom Tov that on the second Yom Tov is permitted for Rashi even for himself, only for Rabbeinu Tam forbidden for himself; and for Semak even for others; for Rabbeinu Tam we can rely to permit this host to give to others even regular at his table, since the prohibition is not clear even for him; and so Terumat HaDeshen's law that said therefore it appears to permit, since for Rashi permitted even for that Israelite etc. — implies regarding guests he decided unlike Rabbeinu Tam per Semak, and we say two lenient voices: one that for another it is permitted, unlike Semak; second that a guest is called another even though in his house, since even for himself the prohibition is not clear; and with this Terumat HaDeshen's wording is resolved that wrote: one may rely on the view of the Rishonim etc., therefore it appears to permit etc. — this is doubled language; rather certainly as I said: first he spoke against Semak per Rabbeinu Tam, and afterward said it appears to permit even for regular guests at his table, on which Semak forbids even for others; and likewise Rama's wording seems thus, who wrote for guests' need and in such a case we practice to be lenient for others — his wording contradicts itself: from "and in such a case" it implies even not for guests' need, only for himself it is also permitted where there is great need; and afterward he said we practice to be lenient for others' need; rather his intent is two types of guests: namely both outside his house and in his house, as he wrote; but more plausible: even for him permitted when there is great need and pressure in eating; and for this he said "and in such a case"; and what he concluded "then we practice to be lenient for others" refers to the other distinction that we are lenient with them because they are considered others; and this is since for Rashi it is fully permitted, and most great ones hold accordingly, and so Rama's siddur came to decide between the first view that is Rashi and "there are those stringent" that is Rabbeinu Tam; one may be lenient like Rashi where there is great need; and if he did not come to decide, only Rabbeinu Tam's view against Semak, he should have brought first that some forbid even for others, only the practice is to be lenient for guests; rather certainly he refers to both Rashi and Rabbeinu Tam; and it appears in any case that also for Rabbeinu Tam it is permitted to handle on the second Yom Tov even for himself, for there is no muktzeh prohibition, only forbidden from the decree lest he say; and automatically he will beware from telling, and even if you say Rabbeinu Tam is stringent even in handling on the second Yom Tov — nevertheless for practical law it is fine, and fine to rely on this;
