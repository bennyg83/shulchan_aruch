A gift to an Israelite — see Mishna Berurah what he wrote that likewise for selling etc.; so is implied from the sugya regarding turnip, since it is seen that they improve and he brings — they forbade them; and there regarding sale it is evident we do not say in this that it is with his own intent for the sake of money, since in any case he brought them so Israelites would eat on Yom Tov; and there it deals only regarding outside the techum; and all the more so one who does full melacha for an Israelite; and so is explained in Beit Yosef siman 517 in the name of Halakhot Gedolot and Rosh that a gift is not precise, for likewise one who acts in order to sell — see there regarding flour; and so is explained in Ran there and the Mishnah is explicit in Machshirin and Ran brought it in Shabbat chapter 23 — vegetables sold in a city whose majority is Israelites are forbidden to buy on erev until "so that they be made"; and so is explained in seif 7 here and in Shulchan Aruch and later authorities in siman 518 regarding flour; and all this is unlike Chayei Adam who is lenient in sale from reasoning that he acted with his own intent; and he derived this from Magen Avraham seif katan 3 from the law of stipulation; and one cannot say thus, for since he acts so an Israelite will benefit on Yom Tov it is forbidden; and that which is explained in siman 502 that if he makes shoes for payment it is permitted to buy from him on Shabbat — perforce it must be as the later authorities wrote in the name of the later authorities: namely in a city whose majority is non-Jews, for he does not act for an Israelite; and Chayei Adam inferred thus from Magen Avraham's wording that it implies specifically a gift, but if he does not act for free but the Israelite stipulated payment etc. — he inferred from this that specifically for free; and so is the reasoning apparently: what difference if he acts to sell and they take his payment, or acts to complete the work he stipulated with the Israelite; but perforce one must distinguish, for Magen Avraham is lenient specifically with stipulation; and that is because with stipulation there is no relevance to the non-Jew when it reaches the Israelite's hand, for in every case he will receive payment for his stipulation; but in sale, although the non-Jew's main intent is to sell his merchandise, for this reason does he not want it to reach an Israelite's hand as soon as possible so the buyer will owe him payment — if so his intent is that it reach an Israelite now that it is Yom Tov, and it is similar to any melacha a non-Jew does for an Israelite that is forbidden even if he takes payment afterward. I found in Peri Megadim in the name of responsa of Maharam that he too agrees to prohibition, unlike Chayei Adam. However regarding the essence of the law, even in stipulation itself, from which Magen Avraham tends to permit, proof is from Or Zarua to the contrary, and from this matter itself that Magen Avraham brought supports his words — from Mordechai and Ra'avan who establish that incident of turnip that deals with stipulation that Israelite shopkeepers stipulated with the non-Jew to bring before Yom Tov so there would not be loss of blood on Yom Tov; and Magen Avraham inferred: if so, why does the Gemara say "all the more so here" that it is with the non-Jew's intent, since they stipulated with Israelites — behold with the Israelite's intent he brought; and he explained the intent is with their own intent that they are non-Jews, and because it is through stipulation; and he strained in his explanation — see there in Machatzit HaShekel; but now that we merited the light of the great Or Zarua from whom is the source of Mordechai's words, it is explained there that just as they stipulated with Israelite shopkeepers, so they also stipulated with non-Jewish shopkeepers; and this is what it says "with the non-Jew's intent" — since they delayed bringing until Yom Tov, certainly not with Israelite shopkeepers' intent did they bring so they would not buy from them on Yom Tov, only with non-Jewish shopkeepers' intent; if so it is explained to the contrary that if they had not stipulated with non-Jewish shopkeepers and brought only to Israelites, even with stipulation it would be forbidden, unlike Magen Avraham; and perforce, as the Mechaber wants to distinguish from what is explained in siman 502 that they permit with stipulation — it is specifically when he did not act so an Israelite would benefit on Yom Tov; but if he acts so he would benefit on Yom Tov as here, it is forbidden, and as explained in siman 276 regarding a lamp and the like. And so I saw in Beit Me'ir who disagrees with Magen Avraham here, and he did not have Or Zarua that explains explicitly to forbid this [however per what Shulchan Aruch ruled to be lenient, unlike Ra'avan, and he explained the Gemara's sugya literally, we have no proof here for prohibition — end gloss]. And so it implies from Rava himself that he did not copy Magen Avraham's permission only regarding the second Yom Tov, for then one may rely to permit with "so that they be made"; and there are later authorities who added on Magen Avraham to make this a definite permission and did not analyze all this. Nevertheless it appears that if there is further doubt one may permit with stipulation even regarding full melacha for the night of Motzei the first Yom Tov immediately; and regarding techum one may permit with stipulation that he need not wait "so that they be made" for Motzei the first Yom Tov even without further doubt combination — in my humble opinion:

(ז) On Yom Tov adjacent to Shabbat — see Magen Avraham, and this is his language: although they are two sanctities as written in siman 416 seif 1, nevertheless even if they fell or were trapped on their own it is forbidden on the second day due to preparation etc.; and see in Machatzit HaShekel who explained Magen Avraham's words that he refers to Shulchan Aruch's law that forbids on Shabbat — and on this Magen Avraham questions, for behold there are two sanctities and it suffices on Shabbat with bichdei sheya'asu; and on this he answered that the prohibition here is due to preparation — and it is astounding, for if so why did Shulchan Aruch afterward require bichdei sheya'asu, for due to preparation one forbids only on Shabbat itself [and I saw that R' Akiva Eiger also addressed this and they resolved it with difficulty see there]; and also according to his explanation Magen Avraham's question has no explanation whatsoever — for what is it if there are two sanctities? He compares them to two diaspora Yom Tov days see there — and in truth it is not comparable at all, for there on the second day bichdei sheya'asu is permitted per the Mechaber, because one day is truly weekday; therefore it suffices to wait bichdei sheya'asu, and as Rashi explained that afterward the act of the non-Jew done on Yom Tov does not help him; not so here where both are sanctities and he could not have done anything all Shabbat day — if so, if you permit it on Shabbat behold he benefits from Yom Tov melacha; therefore it is forbidden all Shabbat and also afterward until bichdei sheya'asu, as I explained in Mishna Berurah; and likewise explicit in Beit Yosef that the reason here is because on Yom Tov adjacent to Shabbat there is no "either way"; and likewise explicit in Or Sameach this reason; and likewise Gra also copied this reason (and see in R' Akiva Eiger's notes who brings proof that bichdei sheya'asu on Shabbat suffices from that which one would think regarding two Rosh Hashanah Yom Tov days adjacent to Shabbat that bichdei sheya'asu on Shabbat suffices even though Shabbat is not fit for doing — and in my humble opinion it is not comparable at all, for there the essence of the reason is because we do not say preparation from two days prior [and also according to Rabbenu Tam who holds due to lest he tell a non-Jew to do for this on Yom Tov — it does not apply from two days prior as the commentators wrote]); and it would have been fitting for us to permit on Shabbat night immediately before bichdei sheya'asu — except there is no reason to be more lenient on motza'ei Rosh Hashanah for Shabbat more than if two Rosh Hashanah days occurred on weekdays that require waiting afterward on motza'ei Rosh Hashanah bichdei sheya'asu, as Beit Yosef wrote this reasoning explicitly regarding if diaspora second Yom Tov falls on Thursday and Friday see there; not so in our matter — behold bichdei sheya'asu is required from the law that melacha was done on Yom Tov that was erev Shabbat; and if so, since all Shabbat time is not fit for doing for an Israelite himself — did the non-Jew's melacha done for him on erev Shabbat which is Yom Tov help him? And he must wait until bichdei sheya'asu at motza'ei Shabbat so he not benefit from the non-Jew's melacha) — but in my humble opinion it appears simply that Magen Avraham did not at all intend to question Shulchan Aruch dealing with a non-Jew bringing for him, but rather came to add a matter in its own right that even if they fell on their own it is forbidden; and although they are two sanctities that if he wished he could prepare for each one separately and it is not like one long day as written in siman 416 seif 1 — nevertheless it is forbidden from one to the other due to preparation; and his intent was to copy here only the law of Tosafot; and because in Shulchan Aruch he copied this law only regarding if a non-Jew brought this thing for an Israelite — Magen Avraham informs us that regarding this day itself, even if they fell on their own, it is forbidden due to preparation; and Shulchan Aruch who copied regarding bringing informs us that when brought for an Israelite it is forbidden also after Shabbat until bichdei sheya'asu:
