A non-Jew who brought etc. See in Shaarei Teshuvah on "a non-Jew who brought fish" etc. what he wrote in the name of Har HaCarmel; and this book is not before me to examine. And at first glance this requires further study, for behold Magen Avraham brought in the name of the Ran that also regarding sale we say that certainly he detached them today and there is no double doubt here; and if it deals with a city whose majority are non-Jews, even aside from this we say that he picked for a non-Jew; and it is possible that because he brought to a Jew it is more severe; and see below in seif 6; and this requires further study in the body of the book.
A gift to a Jew See Mishnah Berurah what he wrote that the same law applies to selling etc.; so is proven from the sugya regarding turnips, since it is seen that they increase and he brings, they forbade them; and there it is a sale, so evidently we do not say in this that it was with his own intent for the sake of the money, since in any case he brought them so that Jews would eat on Yom Tov; and there it deals only regarding outside the techum; and all the more so one who does a complete melacha for a Jew. And so is clarified in Beis Yosef in siman 117 in the name of Halachos Gedolos and the Rosh that "gift" is not precise, for the same law applies to one who acts in order to sell; see there regarding flour; and so is clarified in the Ran there; and there is an explicit Mishnah in Machshirin, brought by the Ran in Shabbos chapter 23, that vegetables sold in a city whose majority are Jews are forbidden to buy in the evening until waiting the time it takes for them to be done. And so is explained in seif 7 here, and likewise in Shulchan Aruch and the later authorities in siman 517 regarding flour. And all this is to exclude the view of Chayei Adam who is lenient in sale from the reasoning that he acted with his own intent, and he derived this from Magen Avraham s.k. 3 from the law of a fixed price; and one cannot say so as above, for since he acts so that a Jew will benefit on Yom Tov it is forbidden. And that which is clarified in siman 252, that if he makes shoes for the market it is permitted to buy from him on Shabbos, one must say as written there in the name of the later authorities that this is specifically in a city whose majority are non-Jews, for he is not acting for a Jew. And Chayei Adam inferred thus from Magen Avraham's wording that it implies specifically a gift, but if he does not act for free, only that the Jew fixed a price with him etc., he inferred from this that specifically for free; and so is the reasoning at first glance, for what difference is there if he acts in order to sell and take their money, or acts in order to complete the work for which he fixed a price with the Jew; but one must distinguish, for Magen Avraham is lenient specifically with a fixed price, because with a fixed price it makes no difference at all to the non-Jew when it reaches the Jew's hand, for in every case he will receive the money of his fixed price; but in a sale, granted that the non-Jew's main intent is to sell his merchandise, nevertheless for this he wants it to reach a Jew's hand as soon as possible so that the buyer will owe him money; and if so his intent is that it reach a Jew now while it is Yom Tov; and it is similar to every melacha that a non-Jew does for a Jew, which is forbidden even if he takes money afterward. Afterward I found in Pischei Teshuvah in the name of a responsum of Maharam that he too agrees to forbid, unlike Chayei Adam. However, regarding the essence of the law, even regarding a fixed price itself that Magen Avraham tends to permit, proof is from Or Zarua to the contrary, and from this very matter that Magen Avraham brought as support for his words, namely from the Mordechai and Raavan who establish that incident of the turnips as dealing with a fixed price that Jewish shopkeepers fixed with the non-Jew that he bring before Yom Tov so that there not be an aspect of buying and selling on Yom Tov; and Magen Avraham inferred: if so, what is this that the Gemara says "all the more so here" that it is with the non-Jew's intent, since they fixed a price with Jews, behold with Jews' intent he brought; and he explained that the intent is with their own intent as non-Jews, and because it is through a fixed price; and he strained in his explanation on this; see there in Machatzit HaShekel. But now that we have merited the light of the great Or Zarua, from whom is the source of the Mordechai's words, it is clarified there as follows: just as they fixed a price with Jewish shopkeepers, so they also fixed a price with non-Jewish shopkeepers; and this is what it says "with the non-Jew's intent," for since they delayed bringing until Yom Tov, certainly they did not bring with the intent of the Jewish shopkeepers who would not buy from them on Yom Tov, but only with the intent of the non-Jewish shopkeepers; and if so it is clarified to the contrary that had they not fixed a price with non-Jewish shopkeepers and had brought only to Jews, even with a fixed price it would be forbidden, unlike Magen Avraham; and one must distinguish, as he himself wants to distinguish from what is clarified in siman 252 according to the Mechaber that they permit with a fixed price: that is specifically when he did not act so that a Jew benefit on Yom Tov; but if he acts so that he benefit on Yom Tov as here, it is forbidden, as clarified in siman 276 regarding a lamp and the like. And so I saw in Beis Meir who disagrees with Magen Avraham on this, and he did not have Or Zarua before him which clarifies explicitly like him to forbid this [however, according to what Shulchan Aruch ruled to be lenient, unlike Raavan, and they explained the sugya of the Gemara according to its simple meaning, we have no proof on this matter for prohibition; thus far is a gloss]. And so it is implied from Eliyah Rabbah that he did not copy Magen Avraham's permission except regarding the second Yom Tov, for then one may rely on this to permit after waiting the time it takes for them to be done; and there are later authorities who added upon Magen Avraham to make this a definite permission and did not analyze all this carefully. Nevertheless it appears that if there is a further doubt for this, one may permit with a fixed price even regarding a complete melacha immediately in the evening of the departure of the first Yom Tov; and regarding techumim one may permit with a fixed price that he need not wait the time it takes for them to be done at the departure of the first Yom Tov even if there is no further combining doubt; as appears to me in my humble opinion.
If there is of its species among what is attached And where the majority are not of a species attached, and the minority still have of their species attached, we do not permit by force of "whatever separates, separates from the majority," because there is a majority opposing this, for most who bring fruit to sell bring those detached today more than those detached earlier; and as the Ran wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah [Maharshal and Olas Shabbos]. The Rokeach wrote: truffles and mushrooms that a non-Jew brought to a Jew on Yom Tov are also forbidden, lest he detached them on Yom Tov; and it teaches us that one should not say they are not considered attached since they also grow on trees [Beis Yosef].
There are those who are stringent This is the view of several Rishonim, namely Halachos Gedolos [as Rashi brought in his name, and in the Halachos Gedolos before us he ruled explicitly like Rashi's view] and Rabbeinu Yitzchak HaLevi, teacher of Rashi; and Tosafos [the Rosh brought this in the name of Rabbeinu Tam, and in Or Zarua and Rashba they brought this in the name of R"i]; and Or Zarua, and he brought thus in the name of R"i of Bonn and the Semag. But nevertheless most Rishonim poskim stand in the Mechaber's approach that the anonymous ruling follows Rashi's view; for besides Rabbeinu Gershom Meor HaGolah and Rabbeinu Kalonymus brought in Rashi, so is also the view of the Meor and the Ramban in Milchamot, and Riaz, and Rashba in his novellae to Eruvin, and Ritva; and so is the view of Maggid Mishneh, and he explains likewise the view of the Rambam in chapter 1, who wrote explicitly that what was trapped or picked from what is attached on the first is permitted on the second [and Lechem Mishneh wanted to steer the Rambam's words to the view of Halachos Gedolos, as implied from Hagahos Maimoniyos in chapter 6 of the Laws of Shabbos where they inferred from his words that the reason for waiting the time it takes for them to be done is so that he not tell a non-Jew to act in order to prepare, evidently he also holds the reason lest he say, which is like Halachos Gedolos and Rabbeinu Tam; and Beis Yosef rejected Hagahos Maimoniyos from the Rambam's words in chapter 1 of the Laws of Yom Tov, and Lechem Mishneh strained to reconcile; see there; but the simple meaning is like Maggid Mishneh and as Beis Yosef wrote; and from the Rambam's mentioning the reason lest he say there is no proof at all for one who examines Rashba, Ritva, and Meiri who wrote that also according to Rashi the reason that he not benefit means also that if he benefits we decree lest he say; and with this they answer Rabbeinu Tam's difficulty on Rashi; if so the Rambam's words too can be explained thus, and he stands in Rashi's approach as clarified explicitly in chapter 1]. And so is the view of Meiri; and Rabbeinu Yeruham also wrote that Rashi's view was taken as primary; and so Rashba and Rabbeinu Yeruham brought in the name of the Raavad; and also Ritzva in Tosafos Eruvin (folio 40) brought support for Rashi from the Yerushalmi; and Rabbeinu Yeruham wrote that the Rif's view is also thus, as brought in Beis Yosef. Therefore where there are further sides to permit, it appears at first glance that one may rely to be lenient like Rashi's view.
The need of Yom Tov etc. That is, when he does not have of that species; for if he has, it is not called a need [Magen Avraham].
And likewise if it was brought on the second Yom Tov The Mechaber used the wording "that it was brought," but in truth the same law applies if the non-Jew picked or trapped on the second Yom Tov and brought immediately at the departure of Yom Tov, that one must wait the time it takes for them to be done, since his picking was on Yom Tov for a Jew; and so is clarified explicitly in Shittah Mekubetzes, and he brought proof from a bathhouse heated on Shabbos in a city whose majority are Jews, that one must wait until evening the time it takes for them to heat; and likewise from Machshirin where he found vegetables sold, that also at night it is forbidden to buy until waiting the time it takes for them to be done. And see in the Raah who is lenient where there was only a techum prohibition, that one should not be stringent to require waiting the time it takes for them to be done except if he brought them on Yom Tov itself and not at the departure of Yom Tov (and that is even if the Jew sent to the non-Jew and it was by his command; see there).
On Yom Tov adjacent to Shabbos See Magen Avraham, and this is his language: even though they are two sanctities as written in siman 416, seif 1, nevertheless even if they fell or were trapped of themselves it is forbidden on the second because of preparation etc. And see in Machatzit HaShekel who explained Magen Avraham's words as referring to the law of Shulchan Aruch that forbids on Shabbos; and on this Magen Avraham raises a difficulty, for behold they are two sanctities and it suffices on Shabbos to wait the time it takes for them to be done; and on this he answered that the prohibition here is because of preparation. And it is astonishing: if so, why afterward did Shulchan Aruch require waiting the time it takes for them to be done, for because of preparation one need forbid only on Shabbos itself [and I saw that R' Akiva Eiger also raised this and they resolved it with difficulty; see there]. And also according to his explanation Magen Avraham's difficulty has no clarification at all: for what is it if they are two sanctities? He compares them to the two Diaspora Yom Tov days; see there. And in truth it is not comparable at all, for there it is permitted on the second after waiting the time it takes for them to be done according to the Mechaber, because whichever way you turn one day is truly weekday; therefore it suffices that he wait the time it takes for them to be done; and as Rashi explained that afterward the act of the non-Jew done on Yom Tov does not help him; which is not so here, where both are definite sanctities and he could not have done [it] all of Shabbos day; if so, if you permit it on Shabbos, behold he benefits from melacha of Yom Tov; therefore it is forbidden all of Shabbos and also afterward until waiting the time it takes for them to be done, as I explained in Mishnah Berurah. And so is clarified explicitly in Beis Yosef that the reason here is that on Yom Tov adjacent to Shabbos there is no "whichever way you turn"; and so Olas Shabbos wrote this reason explicitly; and likewise the Alter Rebbe also copied this reason (and see in the novellae of R' Akiva Eiger who brings proof that waiting the time it takes for them to be done on Shabbos suffices from that which one might have thought regarding the two Yom Tov days of Rosh Hashana adjacent to Shabbos, that waiting the time it takes for them to be done on Shabbos suffices even though Shabbos is not fit for doing; and in my humble opinion it is not comparable at all, for there the main reason is that we do not say preparation from two days beforehand [and also according to Rabbeinu Tam who holds because lest he tell a non-Jew to act for this on Yom Tov, it does not apply from two days beforehand as the commentators wrote]; and it would have been fit for us to permit on the night of Shabbos immediately before waiting the time it takes for them to be done, except that there is no reason to be more lenient on Motzei Rosh Hashana toward Shabbos than if the two days of Rosh Hashana had fallen on weekdays, where afterward on Motzei Rosh Hashana one must wait the time it takes for them to be done, as Beis Yosef wrote this reasoning explicitly regarding if the second Diaspora Yom Tov falls on Thursday and Friday; see there; which is not so in our matter, for behold waiting the time it takes for them to be done is required by law because the melacha was done on Yom Tov that was erev Shabbos; and if so, since the entire time of Shabbos is essentially not fit for doing by the Jew himself, behold the non-Jew's melacha that he did for him on erev Shabbos which is Yom Tov helped him, and one must wait until the time it takes for them to be done on Motzei Shabbos so that he not benefit from the non-Jew's melacha). But in my humble opinion it appears simply that Magen Avraham did not at all intend to raise a difficulty on Shulchan Aruch dealing with a non-Jew who brought for him; rather he came to add a matter of its own, that even if they fell of themselves it is forbidden; and even though they are two sanctities, such that if he wished he could make an eruv for each one separately and it is not like one long day as written in siman 416, seif 1, even so it is forbidden from one to the other because of preparation; and his intent was only to copy here the law of Tosafos; and because in Shulchan Aruch he copied this law only regarding if a non-Jew brought this thing for a Jew, Magen Avraham informs us that regarding this very day, even if they fell of themselves, it is forbidden because of preparation; and Shulchan Aruch who copied regarding bringing informs us that when brought for a Jew it is forbidden also after Shabbos until waiting the time it takes for them to be done.
And the measure of waiting the time it takes for them to be done etc. And it is implied from the silent assumption of the poskim that for us who practice like Rabbeinu Tam to be stringent to wait until the departure of the second Yom Tov the time it takes for them to be done, its measure is also as the Mechaber wrote, and as clarified explicitly in Tosafos according to Rabbeinu Tam's view. And know that the Semak's view [brought in Beis Yosef] is that for others the measure is only the time of picking alone; however Olas Shabbos holds not to distinguish in this, and this requires further study; and the view of the Rosh and Tur is enough time that they can bring of this species from the nearby place found next to the city; and the Mechaber copied for practical law the view of Rashba and the Ran as clarified in Beis Yosef. Nevertheless at the departure of the second Yom Tov [if he brought on the first], perhaps one need not be stringent for others with this measure, and the Rosh's measure suffices.
And if he was in doubt etc. See Mishnah Berurah where we wrote both for leniency and for stringency; and because they hold like the view of the Geonim that in a doubt we assume toward stringency. And behold according to what is clarified in siman 325, seif 7, the view of "some say" there is that we assume toward leniency; and see there in Mishnah Berurah that for the need of a mitzvah one may rely on this; if so also in our matter, if he has a doubt whether he brought from a nearby place that is within the techum or from outside the techum, he need wait only the measure of bringing that is within the techum if it is for the need of a mitzvah.
