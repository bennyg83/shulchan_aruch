[7] [Levush] And Maharai wrote etc. I did not see it in his glosses; rather he wrote: and there are those who are stringent, and it is forbidden until the departure of the second Yom Tov, and the practice is to be stringent if it is not needed for Yom Tov; and for the need of guests and in such a case they practice to be lenient for others for whom they were not brought; end quote. And it appears that Maharai is Moreinu HaRav Yisrael, author of Terumat HaDeshen (Malbushei Yom Tov). And behold in his Levush I saw that Semag's words to forbid etc. until "and so is the practice" were not written; and for our version the words are as their simple meaning; and it appears to me one must say that the practice is like the Semag to forbid etc. Olas Shabbos wrote that one may wonder, for it implies that if guests are not needed it is forbidden on the second day even for others; yet on the second day it is permitted according to everyone for others, for if according to Rashi it is permitted for everyone, and if according to Rabbeinu Tam he permits even on the first day as well what was not brought for him; end quote. And one may wonder at his wonder, for it is clarified in Beis Yosef that the Semag holds like Rabbeinu Tam that it is forbidden even on the second for others; and so I saw in the Mordechai, in the Agudah, and in the Kol Bo; and so Bach concludes; and Taz wrote that even for the one for whom it was brought one may be lenient when there is great need and pressure in eating.
