And behold in his Levush it was not written Semag to forbid etc. until "and so is the practice," and for our version the words are as their simple meaning; and it appears to me one must say: the practice is like Semag to forbid etc. Olat Shabbat wondered: it implies that if guests are not needed, it is forbidden on the second day even for others — behold on the second day it is permitted for all; if per Rashi permitted for all, and if per Rabbeinu Tam he permits even on the first day for what was not brought for him — until here; and you may wonder at his wonder, for it is explained in Beit Yosef that Semag holds like Rabbeinu Tam that it is forbidden even on the second for others; and so I saw in Mordechai in Agudah and in Kolbo; and so Bach concludes; and Taz wrote that even for one for whom it was brought one may be lenient when there is great need and pressure in eating:
