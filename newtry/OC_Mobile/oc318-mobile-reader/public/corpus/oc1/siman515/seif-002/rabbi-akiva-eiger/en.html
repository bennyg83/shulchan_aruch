There Enough time for the non-Jew to go to the place. And in my humble opinion, if there is a measure of his walking, his detaching, and his return of a journey of two days, there is a distinction in this: for if the non-Jew left his place on Shabbos, detached, and returned on Sunday toward evening, then Sunday, which is weekday, does not count toward the measure of the time it takes for them to be done against Shabbos day, and they are forbidden until Monday toward evening; for even though the non-Jew's return on Sunday was on a weekday, nevertheless had he not gone on Shabbos and had gone from his place only after Shabbos, he would not have returned until Monday toward evening; if so, if one benefits before that, he benefits from melacha of Shabbos; and according to Tosafos there applies the decree lest he say [to him to bring] in order to benefit from this on Monday. But in the reverse case, if the non-Jew advanced his departure on erev Shabbos and returned on Shabbos, then we do not reckon the non-Jew's walking on erev Shabbos, and we judge his acts from the beginning of Shabbos, as if the non-Jew brought produce from there to here on Shabbos day, and as if he was already in that place at the beginning of Shabbos and dwelled there. See Eliyah Rabbah, siman 325, s.k. 14, and Kenesses HaGedolah there, that according to his proof this applies to the second scenario above, but there is no proof for the first scenario above.
