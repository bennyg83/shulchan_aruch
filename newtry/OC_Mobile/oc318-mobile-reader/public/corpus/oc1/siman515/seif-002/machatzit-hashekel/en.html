(s.k. 2) To handle them etc. Therefore from the second night onward it is permitted etc.; that is to say, to permit handling one need not wait on the second night the time it takes for them to be done. And all the more so one should not forbid them in handling on the second day even according to Rabbeinu Tam who forbids even in eating also on the second day. Nevertheless handling is permitted, for since the muktzeh prohibition of pushing them away by hand departs from it immediately at the beginning of the second night; and from the side of the eating prohibition there is no muktzeh.
From what is written from Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel. That is to say: we learned on folio 24 that a doubtful prepared status is forbidden, and Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel permits; and an incident with a non-Jew who brought a gift to Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, and he said they are permitted, except that I do not wish to receive from him (Rashi explained: because I hate him); and there on 24b Rav said: what did Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel permit, meaning to receive, that is, handling is permitted but in eating they are forbidden; and Levi said that Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel permits even in eating; and if so it is difficult for the Rif and the Rosh against Rav who said they are forbidden in eating: how is handling permitted, for once they are forbidden in eating, automatically it is muktzeh. (And see what Taz wrote on this at the end of siman 405.) So wrote Yam Shel Shlomo, chapter Ein Tzadin, siman 9.
In the gloss of siman 5, also he is in chapter Ein Tzadin, and this is his language: that is because by law it is permitted even in eating, only they were stringent in a doubt and were not so stringent as to forbid even in handling; and so Rashi explained; end quote of the gloss.
From fruits that went outside the techum. For the Tur wrote in siman 405, and this is his language: fruits that one took outside their place (that is, outside their techum from the place of rest in Beis Hillel) etc.; and as long as they have not been returned and they are outside their place, if he took them out unwittingly they are permitted; if deliberately, it is forbidden to handle them outside four amos, and it is forbidden to eat them even for another who did not take them out; end quote of the Tur. And from his writing "forbidden to handle them outside four amos" it implies that within four amos handling is permitted. And why? Since they are forbidden in eating, automatically they are muktzeh and forbidden in handling.
This case is different etc. If they returned to their place; that is, unwittingly; or deliberately and he has other fruits. And as Magen Avraham wrote there in siman 405, s.k. 14.
And as written at the end of seif 4 in the gloss, who wrote "and they are forbidden to handle on the second Yom Tov etc." For from Beis Yosef's words here who wrote "and even to handle them is forbidden, and in the evening they are permitted etc.," one could say that since he wrote the handling prohibition before writing that in the evening they are forbidden until waiting the time it takes for them to be done, on the contrary it implies that handling is forbidden only on its day, because he pushed them away. But from the Rama's words at the end of seif 4 it is clarified explicitly.
And so is implied in Beis Yosef, siman 586, who wrote: a non-Jew who brought a shofar from outside the techum, even the one for whom it was brought is permitted to blow a mitzvah blast on it and fulfills his obligation with it; and so we hold in siman 586. And Hagahos Ashri gave a reason for this because we hold that a thing brought from outside the techum is permitted for one for whom it was not brought, as written here in seif 5; and since it is permitted in benefit for one person, it is permitted in handling for every person, even the one for whom it was brought; and if so even the one for whom it was brought is permitted to blow on it to fulfill his obligation, for this is not benefit, since mitzvos were not given for benefit. It implies that if the law were that a thing coming from outside the techum is forbidden for the whole world, even for one for whom it was not brought, then handling too would be forbidden, for it would be muktzeh according to the view of the Rif and the Rosh.
Also what Yam Shel Shlomo wrote and added etc. From the prohibition of the day etc.; that is to say, these three words "because of the prohibition of the day" are not written in the Rif and the Rosh; rather Yam Shel Shlomo added them of his own accord.
For behold neveila etc. That is, that became neveila on erev Shabbos or erev Yom Tov.
He does not intend to throw it etc. Since it is fit for him himself at the departure of Yom Tov, he does not throw it to the dogs.
Similar to tevel. Meaning that we hold that tevel is muktzeh, as stated in Shabbos at the beginning of the chapter "They clear out"; and even though it is permitted to feed tevel to an animal, nevertheless it is muktzeh, for he does not intend to feed it to an animal, since it is fit for himself after he tithes. But clarification is needed regarding which tevel we deal with: if regarding a matter whose work is completed, behold Shabbos is one of the six things that fix for maaser, as the Rambam wrote in chapter 3 of the Laws of Maaser, laws 3 and 4, and so it is in the Gemara at the end of the chapter "One who brings" in Beitza; and if so it is forbidden to feed it to his animal. And if he means a matter whose work is not completed, such that Shabbos does not fix and it is permitted to feed it to his animal: if so, granted that its fitness for an animal does not help and nevertheless it is muktzeh because he does not intend to give it to an animal, still why is it muktzeh? For in such a case even a person eats a casual eating. And one must say that since a person is not allowed to eat a fixed eating, it is muktzeh.
For it does not stand for a casual eating.
And as written in siman 318; see there in Magen Avraham s.k. 56: that which raw meat is permitted to handle because it is fit to eat raw applies specifically to soft meat; but hard meat that is impossible to eat raw, even though it is fit for an animal, nevertheless is muktzeh, since it is fit for him at Motzei Shabbos and he does not intend to give it to an animal.
And so wrote Tosafos in Beitza etc. For Rav said there that a chick born on Yom Tov is forbidden, for it is muktzeh; and so we hold at the end of siman 513. And it is stated there: even though a calf born on Yom Tov is permitted, because it is prepared through its mother; and even a calf born from a terefah on Yom Tov is likewise permitted, as written in siman 498, seif 5, for it is prepared through its mother for the dogs, and what is prepared for dogs is prepared for a person. And Tosafos wrote: if so a chick born on Yom Tov too should have been permitted, for even while still in the shell, granted it is forbidden for a person, nevertheless it is prepared for dogs. And they answered that we require prepared and standing ready, and a chick is not prepared and standing ready for dogs, since it is fit for himself when it emerges from its shell, and he does not intend to give it to the dogs.
