(In Magen Avraham at the beginning) And one may ask from Pesachim etc. Gloss: according to my humble opinion, R' Akiva perforce does not hold this exposition on Shabbat day, for the Rav Maggid perforce that this exposition says even per one who distinguishes "it went out" — for Rambam ruled explicitly in Laws of Shabbat that one is liable to stoning for kindling, and for extinguishing he said the Gemara proves well we do not say "from the fact he holds" for R' Akiva. And what Tosafos wrote in Beitza daf 23 that those who disagree, if not for "it went out" Tosafos would have another version in Yerushalmi — if the version were on Shabbat day and not Yom Tov, there is no place for their words to say one who permits holds "it went out," for then on Shabbat day it would not be melacha. And the second difficulty he raised — how can Beit Shammai and Beit Hillel disagree in the dispute of R' Yehuda and R' Nasan — this is not so, for both hold "it went out"; rather they disagree in this exposition on Shabbat day: Beit Hillel expound this exposition and nevertheless the Torah permitted, for we say "from the fact" even though it is a matter not for need; and Beit Shammai do not hold this exposition like R' Akiva; and now Rambam is settled to the view of Beit Hillel on kindling, and all the difficulties Rosh raised can be resolved: from that of baking from Yom Tov to weekday that he is lashed — the reason is we do not say "from the fact" except at the time of eating fit for human food; and likewise Scripture tied it three times to human food; but from Yom Tov to weekday, which is after eating, we do not say "from the fact" in it; and see from this in Maginei Shlomo; and what the Peri Chadash challenged from what is not said "from the fact" in cooking for Kutim or for dogs — there Scripture forbade explicitly, as written "for you"; and what he challenged from burning consecrated items — this difficulty is only for Rav Ashi, unlike the other view who all derive from the verse one does not burn consecrated items on Yom Tov, and it is a Scriptural decree; and Rav Ashi Tosafos themselves answered this difficulty later daf 27 — the Merciful considered it, as written "in fire they shall be burned" — see there; and from roasting too there is no difficulty, for roasting is an irregular matter and inferior to a regular matter and not for need at all; and from this I say Rambam's approach is sound and established; Rambam holds in all matters we say "from the fact," and kindling for example he cited; and with this several difficulties in the sugya on him are resolved; and what the Peri Chadash challenged from what is written in the Magban that it is forbidden only rabbinically — he wrote that if he curdled from erev Yom Tov there is no loss of taste, it implies that from the Torah it is permitted; if so the builder should not be liable to lashes, for we would say "from the fact"; and Tosafos challenged this in Shabbat daf 95; and it appears according to my humble opinion: since cheese made from yesterday is better than cheese of today, as stated in Shabbat daf 134, even for human food he is lashed, for he should have made it from erev Yom Tov; and so I found explicitly in Megillah daf 7 in Tosafos s.v. "here" — see there; and what the Peri Chadash proved from chapter Ein Tzadin in Machshirin distinguishing between possible from erev Yom Tov and impossible — it implies that in human food there is no distinction; that is in a matter that is not improved and not degraded; but in a matter better if made from erev Yom Tov than if made on Yom Tov, even for human food it is forbidden and he is lashed; and this is the clear approach in Rambam; examine:
