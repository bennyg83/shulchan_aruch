{57} In a city whose majority are non-Jews: until now he wrote the laws of a gift, and now he deals with a non-Jew who brought to sell his merchandise in the market.
{58} As a matter of course etc.: but if we know that he brought for a Jew, or even that he intended also for a Jew, it is forbidden even in a majority of non-Jews according to what he explains below in the gloss.
{59} For the need of the majority etc.: and if so, if it is a thing that has not of its species among what is attached, it is permitted immediately to eat, provided that he not mention a sum of money to him, only take from him on credit, and not negotiate with him in the manner of a sale, and not handle them outside four amos since they were brought from outside the techum as above in seif 5. And if it is a thing that has of its species among what is attached or that lacks trapping, it is forbidden on that day because of muktzeh, and in the evening it is permitted in eating and one need not wait the time it takes for them to be done. And the poskim wrote that if we see that through our permitting to buy from them they increase bringing, we forbid them to buy from them; but as a matter of course we are not concerned lest he increase bringing also for a Jew, and it is permitted to take from him in a city whose majority are non-Jews. Nevertheless a non-Jew who brings fish to sell in a city whose majority are non-Jews, even as a matter of course there is concern that he will increase bringing for a Jew if we permit buying from him, since Jews' way is to buy fish for the honor of Yom Tov; and this depends on the circumstance according to the eyes of the posek: for if the city is entirely non-Jews and only individual Jews are found there, one need not be concerned for this.
{60} Brings: and in a city whose majority are Jews, as long as we do not know that he brought for a non-Jew, as a matter of course we assume he brought for Jews who are the majority; and if it is a thing that has of its species among what is attached or that lacks trapping, we say that he picked and trapped for Jews to sell to them, and one must wait until the time it takes for them to be done as above in seif 1. And the later authorities leaned that all this is when the non-Jew is a city resident who went outside the city to trap and pick; but regarding a village non-Jew who brought his merchandise to the city, it appears that it does not apply at all to measure by the city into which he brought whether it is a majority of non-Jews or Jews; rather even if they are Jews we follow the majority of the world, and most are non-Jews, and with their intent he picked and trapped; and even though he brought them to sell to the city we say that afterward he reconsidered and brought them here [and accordingly they do not have the law of trapping for a Jew, and they are forbidden only on the first Yom Tov because of muktzeh, and on the second night they are permitted immediately; and if brought from outside the techum, even though the bringing was certainly for a Jew, in any case it suffices if he waits on the night of the departure of the first Yom Tov the time it takes for them to be done]. And in the book Mor UKetziah he questions this, that the final act follows the initial thought, and certainly with intent for this city he picked and trapped; however if the non-Jew was known always to bring his merchandise to this city to sell, it is obvious that according to everyone we say that with intent for this city he picked and trapped.
{61} Half and half: for presumably it was done for the need of both; and therefore in this too one must wait until the time it takes for them to be done; and some say it is a doubt whether for a Jew or for a non-Jew.
{62} Or that it is known etc.: that is to say, in this even if a majority are non-Jews, since he explicitly intends also for a Jew it is forbidden; and see above in siman 276 in Biur Halacha what we wrote on this.
{63} That he picked: and the same law applies when he brought from outside the techum for the need of both.
