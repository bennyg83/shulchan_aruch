The majority. And if there is concern that many will be brought for an Israelite, it is forbidden — and specifically when they see that many are being brought; but from the default case one is not concerned, as written in siman 517. However, regarding a non-Jew who sells fish — if we permit buying from him, one must be concerned that he will increase bringing for an Israelite, and one must settle the matter. And a non-Jew who lives in a village and brought fish to a city that is mostly Israelite — one must be uncertain: do we say that from the default they trapped for Israelite need, or do we follow the majority of the world and they trapped for non-Jew need and afterward reconsidered and brought here — and if so they do not have the law of lacking trapping deficiency; and it appears to me to be lenient.
