In a city that is mostly gentiles etc. — and I am uncertain: if most buyers of this type are Israelites, do we follow them or not? For behold in tractate Machshirin chapter 2 mishna 9 it states that if one found in it cooked meat, we follow the majority of eaters of cooked meat — and this means if they are Israelites, even though the majority of the city is non-Jew we permit for them and we explain it fell from them; and likewise in our matter; and similar to this Bava Metzia daf 24b: majority non-Jew and majority Israelite butchers [and one must distinguish here for that of mishna 7 that if one found an abandoned child where the first tanna and R' Yehuda disagree there we follow majority of throwers, for the first tanna does not consider it a majority — see there Shach 8 and on Yom Tov there]. Afterward I found in siman 517 in Taz that he wrote thus explicitly, and likewise the Achronim there; and therefore regarding fish where it is common for most buyers on Shabbat and Yom Tov to be Israelites who dwell there — and as Machatzit HaShekel wrote — even if they are a minority against the non-Jews, it is forbidden by law to buy fish there on Yom Tov from a non-Jew even if he knows they were not trapped today, because certainly he brought with their knowledge from outside the techum, which is considered for this matter like a city mostly Israelite; but if so, why does Magen Avraham in s.k. 22 be uncertain to be stringent not to buy fish from them lest he increase for Israel — it should be sufficient that regarding fish it is like a city mostly Israelite; and therefore since it is not concluded for him that most buyers are Israelites — nevertheless in my humble opinion I have room to examine the opposite, that even if most of the city are Israelites there is apparently reason to permit buying from them on Yom Tov if he knows they were not trapped today; for behold an Israelite who buys from them on Yom Tov causes them to sell on credit in general without money and without fixing the price that is forbidden to carry and give with him regarding the purchase, and without weight as proper on weekdays as the poskim wrote — if so his mind is certainly close to selling to a non-Jew and he brought from outside the techum with their knowledge. And behold if he brings on the second Yom Tov day it is possible he brought with Israelite knowledge that they would buy from him today in the evening for motza'ei Yom Tov which is weekday; and therefore he must wait until evening bichdei sheya'asu so as not to benefit from what the gentile did for him on Yom Tov [and similar to this manner is the mishna in Machshirin from which this seif is sourced: if one found in it vegetables — if majority non-Jew he takes immediately, and Rambam explained there that "immediately" means for motza'ei Shabbat; and if majority Israelite he waits bichdei sheya'asu, and explained there because otherwise he would tell a non-Jew to do on Shabbat so he would have cash ready immediately at motza'ei Shabbat see there]; but if he brought on the first Yom Tov day in the diaspora where the evening is also Yom Tov, and he will not sell to an Israelite except on credit and without fixing the price — as above it is more reasonable that with non-Jew's knowledge he brought so he could take money immediately [unless the non-Jew has in the city several Israelites among his customers he relies on — one can say he brought with their knowledge]; but since the poskim ruled unanimously that majority Israelite is forbidden and did not distinguish between first and second Yom Tov day — it implies they do not hold the reasoning we wrote and my opinion is rejected against theirs; and even in majority gentile one should l'chatchila be careful to buy from them because of Magen Avraham's reasoning that if Israelites buy on Yom Tov perhaps he will increase bringing for them:
