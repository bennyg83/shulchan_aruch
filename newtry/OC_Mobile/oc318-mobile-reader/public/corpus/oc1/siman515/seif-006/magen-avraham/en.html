He brings for the needs of the majority. If there is concern that he is bringing extra for Jews, it is forbidden, as stated concerning the incident of the turnips, by Yam Shel Shlomo, Beit Yosef, and the Geonim. This applies only where we see that he is bringing extra. Ordinarily, we are not concerned, as stated in siman 517. Regarding a non-Jew who sells fish, however, if we permit purchasing from him there is concern that he will bring extra for Jews, and the matter requires careful consideration. If a non-Jew who lives in a village brings fish to a city whose population is mostly Jewish, there is room to doubt whether we presume that he trapped them for Jews, or whether we follow the majority population of the world and assume that he trapped them for non-Jews and only afterward decided to bring them here. If so, they would not be considered items in need of trapping. It appears to me that one may be lenient; see what I wrote at the end of seif 2.
Half and half. It is considered to have been brought for both groups, as Semag writes. Beit Yosef, siman 276, writes in the name of Rashi that the matter is doubtful. The practical difference is whether one must wait the time needed for it to be done.
