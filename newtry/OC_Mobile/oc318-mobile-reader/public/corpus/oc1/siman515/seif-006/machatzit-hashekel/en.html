(Seif katan 6) To be lenient etc. — meaning when that other person etc. — Rama's words to be lenient for honor of guests are the words of Maharai in Terumat HaDeshen; and Rabbeinu Yehuda understood he permits even for one for whom it was brought if he brought in guests; on this Rabbeinu Yehuda challenged: it turns out he relies to be lenient for honor of guests on most poskim who disagree with Peri Megadim and hold that for Rabbeinu Tam it is permitted for others for whom it was not brought; nevertheless guests who dine at his table have the law of members of his household; and as forbidden for one for whom it was brought, so for members of his household and guests who dine at his table; and as written seif 5; and Rabbeinu Yehuda answered: specifically members of his household who regularly dine at his table, for also for them it was brought; but if he invited guests incidentally, since they do not regularly eat at his table they are like others — presumably it was not brought for them; and one can distinguish in another matter and say guests are also considered members of his household, and whatever is forbidden to the host is forbidden to them; however this applies when he invited them before the gift was brought to him — then it is with their intent he comes; but if he invited them after the gift was brought to him, there is no need for their intent when Maharai permitted — when he invited them after the gift was brought to him etc.; and the first explanation seems primary — until Rabbeinu Yehuda's wording; but Maharshal and Bach and Magen Avraham were not pleased to explain Maharai's words as Rabbeinu Yehuda wrote; and in order to resolve Rabbeinu Yehuda's difficulty they explained: when that other person invited others — meaning one for whom it was not brought; we are stringent like Semak and it is forbidden for him; nevertheless if one for whom it was not brought invited guests, we are lenient for honor of guests and rely on poskim who disagreed with Semak and hold that for Rabbeinu Tam it is permitted for others for whom it was not brought:

(ב) Nevertheless it is possible etc. — meaning perhaps Mahara'i's intent appears to Magen Avraham as Maharshal and Bach wrote, not like Rabbeinu Yerucham; nevertheless one can say Rabbeinu Yerucham's law is true in reason, that if he invited guests after a gift was brought for him it is permitted; and Rabbeinu Yerucham's second answer (although Rabbeinu Yerucham wrote the first seems the essence — that is to resolve Mahara'i's words; it appears to Darkhei Moshe more explanation one, that two must be that Mahara'i deals specifically when he invited after a gift was brought for him; and behold Mahara'i stated his words plainly; but Magen Avraham agreed to Maharshal and Bach in Mahara'i's intents but from reasoning agrees to Rabbeinu Yerucham's words — it appears to Magen Avraham more the second explanation) in Maharil he was uncertain etc. — meaning if one says Rabbeinu Yerucham's first explanation above, since they do not rely on his table regularly they are not considered as members of his household:

And these are Radbaz's words etc. — he invited guests incidentally etc., and from the stam of his words it implies even if he invited them after the gift was brought to him, nevertheless forbidden:

And in my humble opinion etc. — they rely on the first reasoning; and accordingly, even if the gift was brought before he invited them, nevertheless guests are permitted because of honor of guests; for per the first view, even for himself it is permitted after he waited "so that they be made," all the more so; and for himself and actual members of his household we are stringent like Rabbeinu Tam to forbid also on the second day; nevertheless guests who are not actual members of his household, and also great is honor of creatures — they rely on the first view to permit from the first Yom Tov with "so that they be made"; and it deals with siman 533 that every shevut permitted for the need of a mitzvah is also permitted for honor of guests; and Rama wrote there specifically guests who are not from this city — see there; and likewise here:

Siman 568 — we are lenient in such a case regarding bread of a non-Jew: if the host is careful and the guest is not careful — see there; and perhaps even Radbaz etc. — meaning Radbaz deals with the first Yom Tov that for all opinions it is forbidden to the host for whom it was brought; if so, why was Radbaz lenient when he invited them incidentally to eat in their house — behold on the first day it is forbidden for others per Shulchan Aruch and certainly, and also for Rabbeinu Tam per Semak's view; on this Magen Avraham wrote Radbaz deals only where there was no concern of attached and trapping, only that it was brought from outside the techum; and in such a case for all it is permitted for others for whom it was not brought, as written seif 5; but for members of his household it is forbidden like for himself; therefore he distinguished regarding guests between incidental and regular — see there; between in their house and in the host's house:

Taz was more lenient, for even if he did not invite guests but the host has great need in the matter, they permit at Motzei the first Yom Tov for another — all the more so; and they rely on Rashi's view; nevertheless per Magen Avraham's view he permitted for guests because they rely on the first view; if so, even if the gift was brought after he invited them it is permitted; and above he wrote: nevertheless it is possible that also Rabbeinu Yehuda's view is true etc., and he was not lenient except when he invited them after the gift was brought; this is not a contradiction, for above he wrote that Maharai did not wish to rely on Rashi's view alone, and here Magen Avraham wrote his own reasoning to be more lenient than Maharai was lenient:
