(s.k. 6) To be lenient etc. Meaning when that other etc.: the Rama's words who was lenient for the honor of guests are the words of Maharai in Terumat HaDeshen. And Beis Yosef understood that he permits even for the one for whom it was brought if he brought in guests. On this Beis Yosef raised a difficulty: granted that he relied to be lenient for the honor of guests upon most poskim who disagree with the Semak and hold that according to Rabbeinu Tam it is permitted for others for whom it was not brought; nevertheless guests who depend on his table have the status of members of his household; and just as they are forbidden for the one for whom they were brought, the same law applies that it is forbidden for members of his household and for guests who depend on his table, as written in seif 5. And Beis Yosef answered, and this is his language: specifically members of his household who regularly depend on his table, for it was brought for them as well; but if he invited guests only by chance, since they are not regular to eat at his table, they are like others, for presumably it was not brought for them. And it is possible to distinguish in another way and say that guests too are considered like members of his household, and whatever is forbidden for the householder is forbidden for them as well; however this applies when he invited them before they brought him a gift, for then one can say they came with their intent; but if he invited them after they brought him a gift, why would they have come with their intent; when Maharai permitted, that is when he invited them after they brought him a gift etc. And the first appears primary; until here is Beis Yosef's language. But Maharshal and Bach and Magen Avraham are not satisfied to explain Maharai's words as Beis Yosef wrote; and in order to resolve Beis Yosef's difficulty they explained "when that other invited others," meaning one for whom it was not brought: granted that we are stringent like the Semak and it is forbidden for him, nevertheless if one for whom it was not brought invited guests we are lenient for the honor of guests and rely on the poskim who disagreed with the Semak and hold that according to Rabbeinu Tam it is permitted for others for whom it was not brought.
Nevertheless it is possible etc. That is to say: granted that Maharai's intent appears to Magen Avraham as Maharshal and Bach wrote, unlike Beis Yosef; nevertheless one can say that Beis Yosef's law is true by reasoning, that if he invited guests after the gift was brought to him it is permitted, like Beis Yosef's second answer (even though Beis Yosef wrote that the first appears primary, that is to resolve Maharai's words: Beis Yosef preferred answer 1 more, for for answer 2 one must say that Maharai deals specifically where he invited after a gift was brought to him, while Maharai spoke anonymously; but Magen Avraham who agreed with Maharshal and Bach on Maharai's intent, while by reasoning agrees with Beis Yosef's words, finds the second answer more reasonable for Magen Avraham). In Maharil he was in doubt etc.: namely whether one can say like Beis Yosef's first answer above, since since they do not regularly depend on his table they are not considered like members of the household.
And this is the language of Radbaz etc. He invited guests by chance etc.; and from his speaking anonymously it implies even if he invited them after the gift was brought to him, nevertheless it is forbidden.
And it appears to me etc. That we rely on the earlier reasoning. And accordingly, even if the gift was brought before he invited them, nevertheless it is permitted for guests because of the honor of guests; for according to the earlier view even for him himself it is permitted after he waited the time it takes for them to be done. And granted that for him and likewise for actual members of his household we are stringent like Rabbeinu Tam to forbid also on the second day; nevertheless guests who are not actual members of his household, and also the honor of people is great, we rely on the earlier view to permit from the first Yom Tov after waiting the time it takes for them to be done. And see siman 333: that which Magen Avraham wrote specifically that he invited guests who are not from this city; the reason is that the Rama wrote in siman 333 that every shevus that was permitted for the need of a mitzvah was also permitted for the honor of guests; and the Rama wrote there specifically guests who are not from this city; see there. And the same law applies here.
See siman 168, where we are lenient in such a case regarding bread of a non-Jew if the householder is careful and the guest is not careful; see there. And it is possible that even Radbaz etc.: that is to say, Radbaz deals with the first Yom Tov, where according to everyone it is forbidden for the householder for whom it was brought; and if so why was Radbaz lenient when he invited them by chance to eat in their house, for on day one it is forbidden for others according to Rashi as a certainty, and also according to Rabbeinu Tam per the Semak's view; on this Magen Avraham wrote that Radbaz deals where there was no concern of what is attached and trapping, only that it was brought from outside the techum; and in such a case according to everyone it is permitted for others for whom it was not brought, as written in seif 5; but for members of his household they are forbidden like him; therefore he distinguished among guests between by chance and those who depend; see there; and between in their house and in the householder's house.
Taz was more lenient: that even if he did not invite guests, only that the householder has a great need in the matter, they permit at the departure of the first Yom Tov after waiting the time it takes for them to be done, and rely on Rashi's view. Nevertheless according to Magen Avraham he permitted for guests, for we rely on the earlier view; and if so even if the gift was brought after he invited them it is permitted. And above he wrote, and this is his language: nevertheless it is possible that Beis Yosef's view too is true etc., and he was lenient only when he invited them after the gift was brought. This is not a contradiction, for above he wrote according to Maharai's view who did not wish to rely on Rashi's view alone; and here Magen Avraham wrote a reasoning of his own to be more lenient than Maharai was lenient.
