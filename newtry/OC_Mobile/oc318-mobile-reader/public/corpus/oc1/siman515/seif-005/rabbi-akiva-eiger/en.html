Magen Avraham, s.k. 13 Nevertheless it is akin to Torah-level. The intent of Magen Avraham, of blessed memory, is from what we rule above in siman 507, seif 2, regarding wood that fell from the palm on Shabbos: it is forbidden on the Yom Tov after it to add other wood upon it, for regarding a Torah-level [matter] it is forbidden to add and to handle it; if so it is proven either that we reckon this falling from the tree as Torah-level preparation and it is not fit for that very day, or that it is akin to Torah-level; if so the same applies regarding speech uttered casually.
Seif 5 But it is permitted to handle them within four amos, since they are fit for others: the Rosh. And at first glance, what the Rashba wrote in his novellae on Shabbos at the beginning of the chapter "They clear out," that regarding demai, were it not for the reason that if he wished he could declare his property ownerless and it would become fit for him, it would have been forbidden to handle, for even though it is fit for the poor, it is worse than terumah, since he does not intend to give it to the poor but to keep it for himself until after Shabbos to separate upon it, and for himself it is not fit today, so it is muktzeh; see there: if so here too, even though it is permitted for others, his desire is to keep it for himself to eat tomorrow. And afterward I saw that Shaar HaMelech, Laws of Lulav, end of chapter 7, raised this point.
