{43} Something whose type, etc. - Or, for example, where the gentile brought dried fruits, as above in seif 3.
{44} And it lacks no trapping - Such as geese, chickens, and the like.
{45} From outside the techum - Meaning that at the beginning of Yom Tov they were still outside the techum.
{46} For the one for whom it was brought - And with a gentile who brought fruit to a city whose majority is Jewish, even without specification it is forbidden to everyone, because he brought it with all of them in mind, as below in seif 6.
{47} And for all members of his household - For presumably he brought it with all of them in mind, since he knows that the householder does not eat alone. Regarding guests, if they are constantly dependent on his table, according to everyone they are like members of his household; and even if he invited them incidentally, some are stringent and forbid. However, if he invited them after the gentile brought the items to his house, one may be lenient.
{48} But it is permitted to move them - Since they are permitted to others, as just below, the name muktzeh is not upon them.
{49} Within four amot - From the place where the gentile placed them, and no more, for in any event they are outside the techum and acquired shevitah there. Even if the gentile brought them for himself, the law is also so, as explicitly stated above in siman 325 seif 8.
{50} Or within the city, etc. - For presumably it was enclosed for dwelling. The same applies within a city that has an eruv repair: it is permitted, for it is considered like four amot.
{51} Or a fortress that is known, etc. - For a fortress is presumably not enclosed for dwelling.
{52} And others are permitted - For regarding the prohibition of techumin, which is rabbinic, the Rabbis were not stringent to forbid it even to others as in seif 1, where the Sages made their measures equal. Also, even without this, the prohibition of techumin is not equal for every person; for this one it is outside the techum, and for that one it is inside the techum. Even where it was brought from beyond three parsah, where according to several poskim there is also a Torah prohibition, they were stringent only for the one for whom it was brought [Acharonim]. They further wrote that if it was an animal and another person eats a little of it, it is permitted to slaughter it immediately; nevertheless, even after slaughter it remains forbidden to the one for whom it was brought, for even something that does not require slaughter is forbidden to the one for whom it was brought.
{53} Even to eat them - And all the more so to move them. Nevertheless, outside four amot it is forbidden for others too to move them, because they acquired shevitah in their place. Therefore, even if it is impossible to prepare or cook them unless he moves them outside four amot, it is forbidden, unless it is for the need of Yom Tov, in which case one may permit taking them out through a gentile, for it is only a rabbinic prohibition upon a rabbinic prohibition in a place of mitzvah. See what I wrote on this above in siman 325 in Mishnah Berurah seif katan 37, which also applies here.
{54} The first day of Yom Tov - This does not mean specifically if he brought it on the first day, for the same applies if he brought it on the second: one must also wait until evening the time it would take to do it. Rather, he comes to teach that if he brought it on the first day, he needs to wait only at night for the time it would take to do it. Even for us, who practice like the stringent view above in seif 1 in the gloss regarding trapped items and attached produce, that one must wait until after the second day of Yom Tov the time it would take to do it, here regarding techumin it suffices to wait the time it would take at the beginning of the second night, and afterward it is permitted, as Rama himself concludes at the end.
{55} The time it would take to do it - According to the amount of time that the one who brought it delayed. If he does not know, we estimate only the measure of bringing from outside two thousand amot. See above siman 325 regarding Shabbat, where some are stringent to wait this amount during the following day and not at the entrance of night, because it is not usual to bring at night from outside the techum, and he still benefits from the fact that the gentile brought it to him by day. The Acharonim wrote that since Rama omitted bringing this view also in this siman, presumably he holds leniently on Yom Tov, and the entrance of the second night suffices. Nevertheless, perhaps specifically regarding techumin he did not wish to be stringent; but if it also involved a Torah prohibition, where it was picked from attached produce outside the techum, one should be stringent. For a mitzvah matter or in pressing circumstances, one may be lenient in every case.
{56} With other things - Meaning trapped items and attached produce. The reason is that regarding techumin we rely on the first view in seif 1.
