{43} A thing that has not of its species etc.: or such as when the non-Jew brought dried fruits, as above in seif 3.
{44} And it does not lack trapping: such as geese and chickens and the like.
{45} From outside the techum: that is, that at the beginning of the entrance of Yom Tov they were still outside the techum.
{46} For the one for whom it was brought: and regarding a non-Jew who brought fruit to a city whose majority are Jews, even as a matter of course it is forbidden for all of them, for he brought with intent for all of them, as below in seif 6.
{47} And for all members of his household: for presumably he brought with intent for all of them, knowing that the householder does not eat alone. And regarding guests: if they regularly depend on his table, according to everyone they are like members of the household; and even if he invited them by chance there are also those who are stringent and forbid; however if he invited them after the non-Jew brought the things to his house, one may be lenient.
{48} But it is permitted to handle them: since for others they are permitted as below, they do not have upon them the status of muktzeh.
{49} Within four amos: from the place where the non-Jew set them down, and not more, for in any case they are outside the techum and acquired a place of rest there; and even if the non-Jew brought them for himself the law is thus, as explicit above in siman 325, seif 8.
{50} Or within the city etc.: for presumably it was enclosed for dwelling; and the same law applies within a city that has an eruv repair, that it is permitted, for it is like four amos.
{51} Or a fortress that is known etc.: for a fortress is presumably not enclosed for dwelling.
{52} And others are permitted: for regarding the techum prohibition which is rabbinic the Sages were not so stringent as to forbid it even for others, as in seif 1 where the Sages equalized their measures there; and also aside from this the techum prohibition is not equal for every person, for for this one it is outside the techum and for that one it is within the techum; and even when brought from beyond three parasangs, regarding which according to several poskim there is a Torah-level prohibition, they were also stringent only for the one for whom it was brought [later authorities]. They further wrote that if it was an animal and the other eats a little of it, it is permitted to slaughter it immediately; nevertheless even after the shechitah it is forbidden for the one for whom it was brought, for even a thing that does not need shechitah is forbidden for the one for whom it was brought.
{53} Even to eat them: and all the more so to handle them; nevertheless outside four amos it is forbidden to handle even for others because they acquired a place of rest in their place; and therefore even if it is impossible for them to prepare or cook them unless one handles them outside four amos, it is forbidden, unless it is for the need of Yom Tov, for then one may permit to take them out through a non-Jew, for it is only a shevus of a shevus in a place of mitzvah. And see what I wrote on this matter above in siman 325 in Mishnah Berurah s.k. 37, which applies here as well.
{54} First Yom Tov: it does not mean specifically if he brought on the first, for the same law applies if he brought on the second that he also must wait until evening the time it takes for them to be done; rather he comes to inform us that if he brought on the first one need wait only at night the time it takes for them to be done; and even for us who practice like the view of those who are stringent above in seif 1 in the gloss regarding trapping and what is attached, that one must wait until the departure of the second Yom Tov the time it takes for them to be done, here regarding techumim waiting the time it takes for them to be done at the beginning of the second night suffices, and afterward it is permitted, as the Rama himself concludes at the end.
{55} Waiting the time it takes for them to be done: according to the measure of time that the bringer delayed. And if he does not know, we measure only by the measure of bringing from beyond two thousand amos; and see above in siman 325 regarding Shabbos that there are those who are stringent to wait this measure on the following day and not at the entrance of night, for it is not the way to bring at night from outside the techum, and he still benefits properly from this when the non-Jew brought for him by day; and the later authorities wrote that since the Rama omitted bringing this reasoning also in this siman, presumably he holds to be lenient on Yom Tov and the entrance of the second night suffices. Nevertheless it is possible that specifically regarding techumim he did not wish to be stringent, but if there was also a Torah-level prohibition that he picked from what is attached outside the techum, one should be stringent. And for a matter of mitzvah or at a time of pressing need one may be lenient in every manner.
{56} In other matters: that is to say, in trapping and what is attached; and the reason is that regarding techumim we rely on the earlier view in seif 1.
