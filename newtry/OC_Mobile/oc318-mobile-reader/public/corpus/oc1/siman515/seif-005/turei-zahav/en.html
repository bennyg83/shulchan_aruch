(א) For those who were brought for him. I did not hear any acceptable taste of Daei because of my mother’s end, it is permissible for him, and not because he was forbidden to Israel after his death, nor did he mention that he had lighted a candle for Israel, they did not divide it between him and his father, and that he would not destroy all the island, nor if he had not been banned

(ה) But permitted to handle them. Rosh wrote the reason: since permitted for another Israelite in eating, it does not have the law of muktzeh to forbid in handling:

(ג) Within four cubits. Non-Jew's objects acquire residence, and since they left outside the techum they have no four cubits; and a city enclosed by a wall — the whole city is like four cubits; and this carrying prohibition outside four cubits applies even if brought for a non-Jew, as Rosh wrote chapter Mi SheHotzi'uhu; if so, those not brought for them still have at least the prohibition of carrying outside four cubits from where they were brought to there; and the world is not careful about this from lack of knowledge — namely where there is no eruv:

(ד) And for the evening of the first Yom Tov day. In this, even "some are stringent" of seif 1 — that is Rabbenu Tam — agrees it is permitted for the first evening, for within techumim they were lenient; and nevertheless Rama wrote afterward: even for us who are stringent in other matters etc. — explanation: in a matter where there is of its type attached:
