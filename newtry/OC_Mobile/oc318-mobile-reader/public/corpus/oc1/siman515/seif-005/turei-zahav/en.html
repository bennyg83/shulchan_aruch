For the one for whom they were brought. Rashi explained: I have not heard an accepted reason for this matter; for if because of muktzeh, why is it permitted for another; and if because a prohibition was done for him, then for another Jew it should also be forbidden, for [regarding] a non-Jew who lit a lamp for a Jew they did not distinguish between this one and his fellow; and one can say that regarding the techum prohibition, which is rabbinic, they were not so stringent, and it is enough that they forbade him for this; end quote.
But it is permitted to handle them. The Rosh wrote the reason: since it is permitted for another Jew in eating, it does not have the status of muktzeh to be forbidden in handling.
Within four amos. For objects of a non-Jew acquire a place of rest; and since they left outside the techum they have no "where" of four amos, and a city enclosed for dwelling is entirely like four amos; and this prohibition of carrying outside four amos applies even if they brought them for a non-Jew, as the Rosh wrote in the chapter Mi SheHotziuhu; and if so, those for whom they were not brought still have at least the prohibition of carrying outside four amos from the place to which they were brought; and people are not careful about this from lack of knowledge, namely where there is no eruv.
And in the evening of the first Yom Tov. In this, even those who are stringent of seif 1, that is Rabbeinu Tam, agree that it is permitted in the evening of the first [day], for regarding techumim they were lenient; and therefore the Rama wrote after this, "even for us who are stringent in other matters etc.," meaning regarding a matter that has of its species among what is attached.
