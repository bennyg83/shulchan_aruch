From outside the techum. Even from beyond twelve mil, and even according to the view that this prohibition is according to the Torah, since it is not explicit in Scripture the Sages did not impose the same stringency upon it, as Terumas HaDeshen and Yam Shel Shlomo write.
Within four amos. It appears to me that even if they cannot be cooked unless they are moved beyond four amos, it is forbidden to have a non-Jew move them, since the Gemara explicitly states the prohibition. See siman 307, seif 5, where one may be lenient for a Yom Tov need.
And others are permitted. If it is an animal and one person will eat a small amount from it, it may be slaughtered immediately, as Maharil writes. See siman 503.
For the time needed for it to be done. See siman 325, where some authorities rule that the night is not included in the calculation. Since Rama writes nothing here, it is possible that his view is that on Yom Tov one may be lenient and count the night. Moreover, many poskim maintain that no such waiting period is required for a techum violation. One may therefore be lenient. Yam Shel Shlomo is also lenient.
