Seif 2: One does not change, and so on, and one is not exacting, and so on. In the Gemara there it says: Purim collections are for Purim, and a city collection is for that city, and one is not exacting in the matter; rather, they buy calves, slaughter them, and eat them, and the remainder falls into the charity fund. Rabbi Eliezer says: Purim collections are for Purim, and a poor person may not take from them even a strap for his sandal unless he stipulated in the presence of the townspeople. These are the words of Rabbi Yaakov, who said it in the name of Rabbi Meir, while Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel is lenient. Rashi explains “one is not exacting” to mean that we do not say that less is enough for them, but rather they buy, and so on. So too it is written in Hagahos Ashri in the first chapter of Megillah. But the Rambam explained “one is not exacting in the matter” to mean that anyone who extends his hand is given, and so on. He omitted what is written there, “but they buy,” and so on. This follows the text of the Rif and the Rosh there: Purim collections are for Purim, and one is not exacting in the matter, and a poor person may not take, and so on.
And specifically, and so on, but, and so on. See Tosafos, s.v. Rabbi Eliezer, and so on. He ruled like the first Tanna. The Rif and the Rosh wrote there that the halachah does not follow him, but rather follows Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, who is lenient, for Rabbi Meir follows his own reasoning, as written there; and we rule like the Mishnah, whose anonymous latter clause says that if the item became slippery he is exempt, and so on. This is all the more so according to those Amoraim who do not establish the Mishnah as Rabbi Meir at all. Furthermore, in the Mishnah's case of “to dye it red,” we rule like Rabbi Yehudah, as written there in the first chapter there and in the first chapter of Avodah Zarah. Our text requires analysis, for it begins with the words of Rabbi Eliezer and concludes with the words of Rabbi Yaakov, who said it in the name of Rabbi Meir. The text of the Rif is correct, and this is what he means by saying that Rabbi Meir follows his own reasoning. This is the language of the Yerushalmi, first chapter of Megillah, halachah 4: One may not change Purim money. Does this imply that all other money may be changed? Rather, all money, before it is given to the treasurers, you are permitted to change, and so on. See Yoreh De'ah, siman 259.
