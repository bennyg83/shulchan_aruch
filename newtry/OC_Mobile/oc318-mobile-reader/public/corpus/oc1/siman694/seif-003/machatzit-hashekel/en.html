(s.k. 3) From age twenty — this is per Raavad etc. in tractate Shekalim chapter 1 mishnah 3, and Tiferet Yisrael wrote Raavad's reason: two verses are written in parashat Ki Tisa — in one it is written "this they shall give, all who pass among the counted, half a shekel"; and the second verse is written "all who pass among the counted from twenty years and above shall give the terumah"; and Chazal expounded there were two types of half-shekel, one from which the sockets for the Mishkan were made and one from which communal offerings were taken all year, and that which was for offerings applied also for generations; and Raavad holds the second verse "from twenty years" refers to half-shekel for offerings, therefore we require specifically age twenty. But Rambam holds the first verse that writes plainly "all who pass among the counted" etc. refers to offerings, and if so since he is thirteen years old when he is called gadol he is obligated in half-shekel for offerings; and if so since the half-shekel we give on Purim is remembrance of those half-shekalim given for offerings, and they established on Purim as we learn in mishnah on 1 Adar they announce about shekalim that each bring his half-shekel — therefore our custom depends on the dispute of Raavad and Rambam above. And it is in mishnah every minor etc. in that mishnah — and Rambam specifically: his father is obligated but if the father died the minor is exempt, for the father cannot obligate his minor son. Tiferet Yisrael wrote to explain Raavad's words that he wrote also a minor after his father's death is obligated — it refers where the father left property to the minor and then the obligation falls on the father's property — see there.

In a place etc. but they do not fulfill etc. even the chazan is poor — nevertheless since he is included in the chazan's hire he pays his debt with matanot laevyonim.
