Obligated — Taz and Eliyah Rabbah letter 1, and he added that in mishloach manot too he is obligated and it is simple, and so somewhat in gemara; matanot laevyonim one might have thought is included in charity and exempt — it teaches us. Peri Chadash in 695 wrote he is obligated in mishloach manot and here wrote exempt from matanot laevyonim — requires study; perhaps all were in the miracle category as woman case 695 s.k. 4, and he may give food or money — I did not find now how much is measure of gifts. It appears to me l'chatchila he must give a gift to a poor person something fit to enjoy on Purim, food or money he can spend on Purim; see Tur who ruled the poor may not change the money like R' Meir per baraita of artisans ch. 8 of gemara collection Purim to Purim, and therefore the poskim and Mechaber ruled as them in s.k. 2 except the poor may change but the homeowner must give what he can enjoy on Purim. Though one could say the opposite that Tur ruled in gemara collection Purim to Purim that they gave with intent it be consumed on Purim (Peri Chadash here for R' Meir: reason one who changes without owner's knowledge is a robber, and though we do not hold so on Purim certainly they gave with this intent) — not so when the homeowner gives him a garment and clothes one could say permitted for all; nevertheless it appears l'chatchila he should give something he can enjoy on Purim — requires study; see Yoreh Deah 248 s.k. 1 and Shach 1 and 693 in Shach note 11 and Rosh note 11; the same here a poor person supported from charity gives two gifts to two poor people and they return and give him. I was in doubt regarding gift to poor whether it helps on condition to return, and mishloach manot does not help because mitzvah is food and drink they rejoice on Purim — case 695 portions language of preparation min and Imrei Esh there explained — requires study. And three half-shekel if they distribute to poor people do they fulfill matanot laevyonim — see Magen Avraham 3 and it will be explained there; and what Rama wrote in s.k. 1 three half-groats that half-shekel is upon them — Darkei Moshe wrote half-groat is nine small pashutim and per se ten since ten gerah is half-shekel, and per se meaning including the half-groat itself, and study is needed because coins changed to know the measure.
