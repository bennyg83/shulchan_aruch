Regarding gifts to the poor, one might have thought that it is included in ordinary tzedakah and he is exempt; therefore it teaches us otherwise. See the Pri Chadash in siman 695, who wrote that he is obligated in mishloach manos, while here he wrote that he is exempt from gifts to the poor. This requires analysis, for all were included in the miracle, like a woman; see siman 695, seif 4. He may give food or money. I have not presently found what the measure of the gifts is. It appears to me that initially one must give a poor person something fit for him to benefit from on Purim, either food or money that he can spend on Purim. See the Tur, who ruled that the poor person may not change the money, like Rabbi Meir in the baraisa of craftsmen, page 78, that Purim collections are for Purim. Therefore the authorities disagree, and the Mechaber ruled like them in seif 2, only regarding whether the poor person may change it. But the householder must give something from which the poor person can benefit on Purim. Although one could say the reverse: necessarily the Tur ruled that Purim collections are for Purim because they gave with this intention, that they should eat on Purim. See the Pri Chadash here, that according to Rabbi Meir the reason is that one who changes from the intent of the owner is a thief; and although we do not rule this way generally, on Purim they certainly gave with this intent. This is unlike when the householder gives him a cloak or garments, where one could say that it is permitted according to everyone. Nevertheless, at first glance it appears that initially one should give something from which he can benefit on Purim. This requires analysis. See Yoreh De'ah 248:1 with the Shach there, letter 1; siman 293 in the Shach, letter 11; and siman 251:11. The same applies here: a poor person who is supported by tzedakah should give two gifts to two poor people, and they should then return and give to him. I was uncertain whether a gift to a poor person is effective if given on condition that it be returned. For mishloach manos, such a condition is not effective, because the mitzvah is food and drink through which they will rejoice on Purim; see siman 695. The word manos implies prepared portions, and with Hashem's help it will be explained there; this requires analysis. Regarding the three half-shekels, if they are distributed to the poor, one fulfills gifts to the poor; see Magen Avraham, seif katan 3, and it will be explained there with Hashem's help. What the Rema wrote in seif 1, “three large halves that have the name half upon them,” follows the Darkei Moshe, who wrote that a large half is nine small peshutim, and with itself it is ten, corresponding to the ten gerah of the half-shekel. “With itself” means with inclusion of the large half itself. This requires analysis, because the coins have changed and one must know the measure.
