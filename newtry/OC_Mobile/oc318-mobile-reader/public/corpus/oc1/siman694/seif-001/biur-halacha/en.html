(ד) One should give three half-groats in these countries — behold the common way is to interpret "half-groats" as half of three groats, namely what we call in our country (half-groats), and it does not seem so in Darkei Moshe — see there; perhaps it is another coin name called thus in the country he wrote about, where he wrote that a half-groat is nine small pashutim. In our country now we do not have this coin. Per our country it appears a medina issues a small coin called half-groat, but it is not an important coin at all even if one gives three halves unless he also gives for his small children. I saw some collectors who first put three half-reichstaler in the charity bowl, and when one comes to give the half-shekel the collector gives him in place of his money the three half-reichstaler and he puts them in the bowl — but this is not clear, for if the collector gives them only as a loan for a few moments and the giver does not intend to acquire them for charity, behold this is not giving at all and he only deceives himself. If the collector's intent is truly to acquire for him and he gives them to charity, this is advice only for the first one and not for those after, since they already became charity the collector has no permission to exchange them for three half-groats; perhaps since they already practiced thus it is as if all givers to charity stipulated explicitly that the collector may do so. Behold in the time of the Gra a Polish half-zahuv was found and it is written in Maaseh Rav that he would give only one Polish half-gold, but now this coin is not found at all unless one gives half-groats or half-reichstaler. One whom the Holy One blessed be He helps that he can give one reichstaler in memory of half-shekel and three half-groats for what is written in the parashah three times terumah — so wrote the latter Rama: fortunate and good for him.
