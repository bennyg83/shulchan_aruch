"To give at least," etc. I am uncertain whether a gift to a poor person is effective if given on condition that it be returned. Regarding mishloach manot, such a condition is not effective, because the mitzvah is specifically to give food and drink with which the recipient will rejoice on Purim. If the half-shekel money is distributed to the poor, one fulfills the mitzvah of gifts to the poor through it. [Pri Megadim] "To 2 poor people." One should not give them the money before Purim, lest they consume it before Purim. [Magen Avraham in the name of HaMaor] The common custom is nevertheless to give money before Purim. Although the mitzvah of gifts to the poor is specifically on Purim day, we must say that people also give 2 gifts to poor people on Purim itself. In addition, there are many poor people who go from door to door on Purim. [Machatzit HaShekel] "Some say that one should give," etc. It requires study whether a poor person who himself is supported by charity is obligated to give the half-shekel. [Pri Megadim] "And one should give 3 large halves in these countries." The common interpretation is that "large halves" means 3 half-groschen coins, namely what in our country are called half-groschen. Darkei Moshe does not appear to mean this; see there. Perhaps in his country there was another coin called by that name, because he writes that 1 "large half" equals 9 small peshutim. In our country today that coin does not exist. According to the currency used in our country, it appears that by strict law one fulfills the practice with the small coin called a half-groschen. Nevertheless, this is not an important coin at all, even if one gives 3 such halves, unless he is also giving on behalf of his minor sons. I have seen some charity collectors who initially place 3 half-ruble coins into the charity bowl. When a person comes to give the half-shekel, the collector gives him those 3 half-ruble coins in exchange for his money, and the donor then places them into the bowl. This procedure is not satisfactory. If the collector gives them merely as a loan for a few moments and the donor has no intention to transfer ownership of them to charity, then this is not a genuine act of giving at all, and he is merely deceiving himself. If the collector genuinely intends to transfer ownership to the donor, and the donor then gives them to charity, this is a solution only for the first person, not for those who come afterward. Once the coins have become charity funds, the collector has no authority to exchange them for 3 half-groschen coins. Perhaps, however, because this practice has already become established, it is as though all donors explicitly stipulated that they give the charity under this condition, namely that the collector may use the coins in this manner. In the time of the Gra, a Polish half-zahuv coin was common. Maaseh Rav records that he would give only 1 Polish half-zahuv. Today that coin is no longer available. One must therefore use either half-groschen coins or a half-ruble coin. One whom Hashem has blessed with the means to give 1 ruble as a remembrance of the half-shekel, together with 3 half-groschen coins corresponding to the 3 times the word terumah appears in the parashah, in accordance with the latter opinion cited by Rama, is fortunate and it is good for him.
