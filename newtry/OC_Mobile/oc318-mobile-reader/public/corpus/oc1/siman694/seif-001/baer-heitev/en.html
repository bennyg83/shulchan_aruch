Two gifts. See Peri Chadash and Yad Aharon. If he gave one person the measure of two gifts, whether he fulfilled the obligation, see Halachot Ketanot II siman 39, which leans this way and that; see there. He should not give them before Purim, lest they eat them before Purim. The Bach and Taz wrote that even a poor person supported by charity is obligated, as with the four cups, unlike other charity, where he is obligated only once a year in order to fulfill the mitzvah of charity, as written in Yoreh De'ah simanim 248 and 249.
And there is no. Meaning, there is no obligation, but there is a custom. The Levush wrote: the practice is to give Purim money for each and every person, even for the children, and pregnant women also give for the fetus. It is stated in the Mishnah: any minor on whose behalf his father began to give the shekel, he no longer stops. In a place where they practice giving the machatzit ha-shekel money to the cantor, there is no prohibition in the matter, for anyone who gives does so according to the custom; Mishnah Berurah siman 54. But they do not thereby fulfill matanot la-evyonim unless they distribute from it to the poor; Magen Avraham. The Shelah wrote that machatzit ha-shekel, and all the more so matanot la-evyonim and mishloach manot, do not come from ma'aser money; but if he comes to add, he may add from ma'aser. Maharil in Responsa siman 56; see Kenesset HaGedolah and Yad Aharon. Money that he resolved in his heart to distribute on Purim may not be changed. Beit Yosef in the name of Hagahot Ashiri; and see Yoreh De'ah at the end of siman 258.
