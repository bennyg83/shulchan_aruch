1) [Seif 1] Gloss: Vihi Noam is recited. This is because it is a psalm of blessing with which Moshe blessed the Jewish people when they completed the work of the Mishkan. The custom is to repeat the verse Orech Yamim Asbi'eihu, thereby completing the Divine Name derived from the psalm. Tur. See Magen Avraham. Tashbetz writes that the psalm contains 130 words, equal to the numerical value of hakohanim. The Chashmonean Kohanim would recite it seven times in battle and prevail. For this reason the letter zayin is hidden in Vihi Noam, because anyone who recites it seven times is saved from weapons. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2. Shaar HaKavanos, folio 60, writes that the custom of reciting Vihi Noam after every Shabbos serves to draw the additional sanctity of Shabbos into all the days of the coming week until the next Shabbos. The entire psalm of Vihi Noam should be recited while standing. At the least, one should recite this verse while standing and then sit. The reason is that the present intention is to receive the returning light from Binah, called 'the pleasantness of Hashem,' from the mystical source of the additional Shabbos sanctity, and one must therefore stand. See there, where the intentions of this verse are discussed at length. See further below, siman 300, paragraph 2.
