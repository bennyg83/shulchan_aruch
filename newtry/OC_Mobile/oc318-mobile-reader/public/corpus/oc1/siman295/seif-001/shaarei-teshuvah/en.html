Vihi Noam. See Baer Heitev. Shaarei Tzion writes in the name of Rabbi Chaim Vital that the entire passage of Vihi Noam should be recited while standing. At the very least, the verse Vihi Noam itself should be said while standing, because at that moment the intention concerns the spiritual aspect called the pleasantness of Hashem, drawn from the additional sanctity of Shabbos toward the coming week. He also writes that the Arizal recited the verses of Veyiten Lecha at home while making Havdalah. During the week. See Baer Heitev. Birkei Yosef writes that he saw some great authorities quietly recite Vihi Noam to themselves even during a week in which Yom Tov occurs, because according to the mystical teaching, Vihi Noam must be recited every Motzaei Shabbos. See siman 491, where he writes that this applies even when Motzaei Shabbos falls during Chol HaMoed. See the Sheiltos, parashas Bereishis. Sheilas Yaavetz, siman 19, writes that if Pesach begins on Shabbos, Vihi Noam is not recited on the preceding Motzaei Shabbos. If, however, another Yom Tov follows Shabbos, even Yom Kippur, Vihi Noam should be recited on the preceding Motzaei Shabbos. Birkei Yosef cites this. Shiyurei Leket writes that the reason is the prohibition of work on Erev Pesach. He adds that, to the best of his recollection, in these communities it is recited even when Pesach begins on Shabbos. See there. See Responsa Knesses HaGedolah concerning one who omitted Vihi Noam on the Motzaei Shabbos before parashas Zachor because Purim followed during that week. He writes that this was an error.
