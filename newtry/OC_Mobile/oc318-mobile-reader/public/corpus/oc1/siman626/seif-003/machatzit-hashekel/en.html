(S.k. 3) And nullify them, etc. And the Ran wrote, etc. His intent is that the Ran too holds like the some say view in the Shulchan Aruch, as is explained there. The Magen Avraham's intent is that you should not say that according to the some say view, where the Rema wrote, however if he lowered them, etc., and mixed them, etc., the sukkah is valid, there is also the difficulty that one may not nullify, etc.; and you should not say that according to the some say view too we must use one of the answers written by the Bach. On this the Magen Avraham wrote that the Ran wrote, etc., and from this, according to his approach, there is another answer. The Ran explains that since the Mishnah teaches, one who makes his sukkah beneath a tree, etc., and calls it a sukkah, it implies that the valid s'chach alone has the necessary measure, which is the view of the some say opinion. Regarding this Rava says that when the tree's sun is greater, it is valid. The difficulty is: why do we need the sukkah to have a majority of valid s'chach? We rule like Rav Pappa, that even half and half is valid. On this he answered: aside from that, there is another difficulty: what does beating down and mixing help, since we rule that where one can remove the prohibition, it is not nullified? Even if it speaks where the forbidden item is not recognizable because of the mixture, nevertheless he can sort the invalid from the valid. For this reason we rule in Yoreh De'ah 98:4 that if fat fell into a cooked dish, although it was nullified, he must place cold water there so the fat congeals and hardens, and remove it. This is all the more difficult according to what the Magen Avraham wrote in s.k. 4, that according to the Ran we do not require that it be unrecognizable. On this the Ran answered: nevertheless, here this is what we were taught, etc., that since they are mixed, a majority is needed, as above. Meaning, our intent is not to nullify the prohibition. Rather, since the valid s'chach alone has the measure of a valid sukkah even with half and half, only because it became mixed its importance was diminished; although the importance of the prohibition was also diminished, what of it? In any event the valid s'chach no longer has a valid measure. Through its being a majority, even though its importance was diminished by the mixture, this majority is at least considered like half and half and is valid. We do not want to nullify the prohibition, for we do not need the invalid material to validate the sukkah, only to diminish its importance through the mixture so that it should not have the power to invalidate the valid s'chach. Therefore even though one can remove the invalid material, he need not remove it, for he does not need the invalid material at all. According to this, there is likewise no difficulty from the rule that one may not nullify, etc., for we do not intend to nullify it, as above. You will automatically understand that all this works well according to the Ran, who holds like the some say opinion and speaks where the valid s'chach alone has the measure of a valid sukkah, and as the Magen Avraham wrote in s.k. 4, this is not nullifying a prohibition, for the invalid material is not combined. But according to the first view, where even if the valid alone does not have the measure, but only through joining the invalid, nevertheless it is valid through mixture, it is called nullifying a prohibition, and one must use the Mordechai's answer cited above. As for the Ran's difficulty that one can remove the prohibition, this indeed requires explanation according to the first view.
