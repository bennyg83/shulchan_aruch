It is valid. Even to sit beneath the beams themselves, as written in siman 631:9, since he performed an act and removed the roof tiles. But if it had previously been built without a roof and he did not now perform any act, then those beams or rafters have the law of invalid s'chach, and their law is as written in se'if 1 regarding a tree. See simanim 632 and 629:8. The Bach wrote that one should place s'chach on top of the beams, provided that there is not less than three tefachim between them, for then they are lavud and all become like one board, and the valid s'chach is not greater than it and cannot nullify it, since they are recognizable and each stands by itself, etc. See there; all his words are astonishing, for it is obvious that we do not say lavud here since valid s'chach lies between them. There is proof from what is written in siman 631:8, for an ordinary spit is not three tefachim wide, as written there in the gloss. If there is space between them equal to them, it is valid, even though the spits are within three tefachim of one another and are recognizable. They are valid, as written in se'if 1. So too it is in the Gemara on folio 14b, that boards do not combine. See the end of siman 632. So too the Beis Yosef wrote in siman 629 in the name of the Ramban, and it is in Responsa Rashba, siman 213: Regarding a sukkah made of boards whose width is not four, which are fixed with nails and interlocked like the rungs of a ladder, and there are not three tefachim between them, and one covers them with valid s'chach, is the s'chach invalid because of the nails? For one sage forbade it. He answered: perhaps he forbade it because, since the boards are fixed with nails, they all became like one wide board and have the decree of a ceiling; but others disagreed with him, and leave them to their custom. End quote. This implies that it is not forbidden because of lavud; see Rashi on folio 9b. One should not say that here too the beams are fixed to the roof, for it is obvious that we say they combine only when he attached them as one before placing them there, unlike where he fixed them in the wall. So too it appears from siman 631:9, that when he removes one from between them, although the other boards are fixed, they do not combine even according to the Rambam; see there. Were it not for the issue of make it, and not from what was already made, he would not need to remove the nails at all. If so, we learn that they do not combine. So wrote Mahari Weil; see what I wrote at the beginning of siman 632.
And likewise it is permitted to make, etc. The Bach wrote that one should be stringent and remove the roof first and then place the s'chach, and that it is not comparable to one who makes a sukkah beneath a roof, etc.; see there, where he was forced. The Levush too was forced. In truth, it appears to me that the stringent view like Mahari Weil holds as follows: granted, with a sukkah beneath a roof, when he removes the roof afterwards, that is an act and the roof itself becomes valid, as the Beis Yosef wrote in the name of Orchot Chaim. But here, when he opens it, this is not called an act. By contrast, when he placed the s'chach first, it was made validly, and he then closes the roof because of rain and opens it again; this is valid according to everyone, for it is like one who spread a sheet over it and then removed it, where it is valid since it was made validly, as stated in the Gemara. This appears clear to me. Therefore one should be stringent. The Shiltei Gibborim in the first chapter of Sukkah is stringent; see there. If he placed the s'chach on the ceiling and afterwards removed the ceiling, it is invalid.
Hinges. For it is like an opening.
That one should not sit. One might have thought that since he raised them and then put them back down, it is like loosening boards, and they themselves should become valid. Therefore he teaches that this is not called an act, since he did not intend to place them for the sake of shade. See siman 631.
