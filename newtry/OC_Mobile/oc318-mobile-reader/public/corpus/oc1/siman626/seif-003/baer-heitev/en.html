And to close. This applies specifically when he places the s'chach while it is open; see the Magen Avraham. If he placed the s'chach on the ceiling and afterwards removed the ceiling, it is invalid. Regarding those sukkahs made beneath a roof, where sometimes the sides are sloped, one should be careful not to sit under the slope, in any event. See Responsa Panim Me'iros, siman 61, where he discusses a sukkah on a roof. A sukkah made beneath the duwar, meaning that they open a space in the middle of the roof, which is called duwar in Arabic and bageh in the vernacular, and sometimes they make over the open area of the duwar a lattice or netting, and sometimes this netting is made from copper wires or linen threads, is valid. Even if one places the valid s'chach directly on top of the netting, it is valid. Ginas Veradim, Orach Chaim, klal 4, siman 8; see there.
