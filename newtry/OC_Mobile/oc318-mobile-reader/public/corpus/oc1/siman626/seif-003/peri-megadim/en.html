Or See the Taz; his words require explanation. His intent is that the Rav, by adding: or where the s'chach is abundant, implies that there are only two views. The first reasoning here is Rashi's view. The second reasoning is the view of the Avi Ezri, who is stringent if the tree has more sun than shade and stands opposite valid s'chach, so that if the valid s'chach opposite it were removed, more shade than sun would not remain; in such a case, without lowering and mixing them, it is invalid. It is valid only where there is much valid s'chach, so that even if the shade of the valid s'chach opposite the tree's shade were removed, because the valid shade does not help where the tree's shade exists, the valid s'chach would still have more shade than sun, or where the tree's shade is opposite the airspace, etc. But from the Tur it implies that there is a third view, namely that even if abundant shade remains in the valid s'chach, whenever the tree's shade, though its sun is greater, is opposite valid shade, it is forbidden to sit in that place because of two coverings, as the Taz concludes below nearby. He proved this from the Tur's wording: after the view of the Avi Ezri, he wrote, and for this reason some are stringent beneath the latis; and Rabbi Yechiel would repair this by mixing everything into one s'chach; and it appears to me that it is permitted, for even according to the one who invalidates, this is where, if the valid s'chach were removed, the valid material would have more sun than shade, unlike where more shade remains, etc. This implies that those who are stringent are stringent even when more shade remains in the valid material after what is opposite the tree is removed. Why are they stringent? The Avi Ezri validates when the tree is opposite airspace; the same should apply where it is opposite valid material, if valid s'chach remains, for what difference is there? And if you say that those who are stringent decreed where the tree is opposite valid material because of a case where, if removed, the valid s'chach would not retain more shade, and this is what is meant by some are stringent, not by law but as a mere stringency, see the Beis Yosef, then how could the Tur write, and it appears to me that it is permitted? Those who are stringent also admit that by law it is permitted, and are stringent only as an added distancing decree. We learn that they are stringent by law. This is difficult: why? According to the Raavyah it should be permitted when valid s'chach remains, just as where the tree is opposite the airspace. Rather, necessarily those stringent authorities hold that whenever the tree is opposite valid s'chach, it is invalid because of two coverings, not because we view the valid s'chach opposite the tree as though it were absent. The Tur, however, holds that the Raavyah's reason is because we view it as though absent, and therefore where there is abundant valid s'chach it is valid. This is what the Taz concludes afterwards when he writes, and incidentally, etc. If so, the Mechaber may hold like the second view, like those who are stringent regarding latis, that whenever the invalid material is opposite valid s'chach it is invalid even if the s'chach is abundant, and it is valid only where it is opposite airspace. The Rav follows his own view in siman 624, Taz letter 1, where he added beneath the open air of the heavens, for in invalid s'chach there is no invalidity of sukkah beneath a sukkah. The Mechaber here in se'if 2, who validates with latis, can be explained based on Baal HaItur or where they are four tefachim apart, etc. Now with two valid coverings, conditions are required as in siman 628:1. With invalid s'chach above or below valid s'chach, he holds that conditions are not required; even where the invalid material has more sun than shade and there are not ten tefachim between them, it is invalid from the verse basukkot, deficient, and not in a sukkah beneath a tree. Such a case is discussed in Sukkah 9; see Pnei Yehoshua there and you will understand. Rabbi Yechiel would set up reeds so that it should be one s'chach and not two coverings, not because of nullification, for wherever it is recognizable, nullification does not apply. Nevertheless, this requires examination, for one can say that wherever there are not three tefachim between them, lavud applies and there are not two coverings, and it is not comparable to setting reeds upright until the latis. What the Taz wrote as proof from Rashi in Sukkah 9a, s.v. what is the novelty, and what the Taz wrote in explaining Rashi, can be discussed. What Rashi wrote about the latis, the Bach challenged that beneath the latis should also be permitted. According to what the Taz wrote, it works well: specifically on the latis, because of two coverings. According to this, Rashi too would invalidate even where the tree's sun is greater, at least because of two coverings. But according to Rashi, one need not lower and mix where it is not recognizable. What the Taz wrote that we do not say lavud to be stringent, see Magen Avraham letter 6 and siman 502 letter 9, where one can say that we do say lavud to be stringent; see there. What he wrote in the name of Baal HaItur, see the Bach about this. With God's help, it will be explained further in the Magen Avraham. See the next letter; this is not the place to elaborate.
