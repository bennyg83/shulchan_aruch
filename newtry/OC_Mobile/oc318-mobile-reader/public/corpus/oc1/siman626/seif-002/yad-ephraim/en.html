There: And perhaps when it is placed above, it is worse, etc. This is not understood, for there itself the difficulty remains: why is it in fact valid when its sun is greater? Also the distinction that when it is placed above it is worse is not understood. In my humble opinion, his intent follows what appears to me in explaining the Rosh's words on page 102. He wrote that Tosafot challenged Rashi, for a breached area equal to the standing area is permitted, and it appears to me that this does not apply here. Specifically regarding invalid and valid s'chach placed next to one another, one can mention breached area equal to standing area, as they bring on page 16 regarding the Mishnah of one who roofs his sukkah, etc. But regarding s'chach and empty air, the halacha given is that the shade must be greater, etc.; see there. This distinction is not so understandable. It appears to me based on what my grandfather, the Maharsha, wrote on folio 9b, on Rashi s.v. what is the novelty, etc.: one can say that when one first covered with invalid s'chach, he covered only the minority, for no more was visible below; when he afterwards covered with valid s'chach, all that airspace that was visible below was covered, so he covered the majority. This is the Mishnah of one who roofs his sukkah, etc.; see there. It appears that there is no distinction between whether he first covers with invalid s'chach or with valid s'chach, for we do not follow the earlier moment when he did not want to fulfill his obligation with this sukkah. For this reason too the poskim hold that there is no difference whether the tree preceded the sukkah, etc. If so, where we now have before us a sukkah whose s'chach is valid and invalid, there is room to say that it is as though the invalid s'chach was placed first and covered only the minority, and through the valid s'chach all the rest of the airspace was covered; thus through the valid s'chach the shade became greater than the sun, which is what the Torah insisted upon. There is also room to say the reverse, that we consider the valid s'chach as if it was placed first, and consequently it did not help to make the shade greater than the sun, and that benefit came through the other half, the invalid s'chach. Therefore the Talmud properly compares it to a breached area equal to the standing area, for according to Rav Pappa the Torah's insistence is that the forbidding side not be greater than the permitting side; in such a case, where at least the forbidding side is not greater than the permitting side, since there are aspects in both directions, we follow the lenient side. But if there is no invalid s'chach here, only half s'chach and half air, since it is before us with more sun than shade, and the halacha was given that the shade must be greater, there is no room to be lenient by Rav Pappa's reason that he is lenient with half and half. In this matter half and half does not help at all, for the Torah required that the shade below be greater. What does it help that the s'chach and air are equal above, since below the sun is greater, according to Rashi's view that we are now discussing? So it appears in understanding the Rosh's words. According to this, this is what troubled the Magen Avraham: since there, if you remove the invalid s'chach, the shade would be greater, except that we do not judge by removing it but judge which creates the greater shade, the valid or the invalid; and since both are like breached area equal to standing area regarding Shabbos, which is permitted, then also regarding a tree, if the sukkah without the tree is half valid s'chach, so that below its sun is greater, what the tree covers over the second half should help in such a case, and we should say it is half and half and permitted because breached equals standing. On this the Magen Avraham properly wrote that perhaps when it is placed above, it is worse. Meaning, specifically where valid and invalid s'chach lie next to one another and both are equal in covering the sukkah, in such a case where it is half and half it is permitted according to Rav Pappa. But if the sukkah is covered halfway with valid s'chach and the tree covers above the other half, it is not at all reasonable to judge it as though the tree was covered first and then the valid s'chach covered most of the airspace, since the main s'chach lying directly on top of the sukkah is the valid s'chach, and below its sun is greater except through the tree. In such a case the invalid s'chach indeed joins, etc. On this the Magen Avraham wrote that nevertheless, from the Rosh's wording regarding one who roofs, etc., that if you remove it the valid s'chach is sufficient, this is not correct, for there is no place for this validation if one does not remove it, since then there is room to judge it as half and half; but if we remove the invalid entirely, automatically only half of the valid remains, and in such a case the Rosh invalidates because below the sun is greater. Therefore he wrote that the Rosh's proof is from the case established as where he adds extra. Regarding that, he properly wrote that if you remove it, the valid s'chach is sufficient. Indeed, this is implied by Tosafot's statement that majority does not help since it is recognizable, etc.; it implies that the case speaks of a valid majority, namely according to the conclusion, where it speaks of adding extra, since the valid s'chach alone has more shade. One should not ask that from the initial assumption it proves that even when its sun is greater, such as half and half, he validates because breached equals standing; if so, we should learn from it that where the sukkah itself has more shade than sun without the tree, there is no distinction whether it is placed above or not. I saw in the Ran in chapter HeYashen that he also wrote like the Rosh to explain Rashi's words, that there is a distinction regarding breached equals standing, which is brought regarding invalid s'chach because the Merciful One said not to cover with a majority of invalid material; since there is not a majority of invalid material, it is valid, unlike actual air. See there. If this is also the Rosh's view, there is no room to distinguish whether it is placed above, since in any event there is not a majority of invalid material. I discussed this at length in my novellae.
