Se'if 2: But it is permitted, etc. There on 10a, if he spread a sheet over it, etc., this applies only, etc. He wrote that specifically for decoration, they are subordinate and nullified to it, but not for the need of the garment. He wrote that the case of Menimin there was certainly forbidden even while the garment was wet; only Rav Ashi permitted it when it was not the time of eating or sleeping, and said that he should take it down when it dried, so that people would not say he left it there even while using the sukkah for eating and sleeping. But while it is wet, there is no such concern, for it is not the way to use the sukkah then because the garment drips. This shows that removal helps. Although the Rashba wrote there that some disagree with them and hold that anything not for the need of the sukkah is valid, and that the phrase for decorating it is a novelty, that even though it is for the need of the sukkah it is valid, and the Yerushalmi supports them, saying: but not because of falling leaves, it is valid; nevertheless the Rashba there agreed in practice with the first reasoning. So too it appears from the Gemara, which says: obviously, because of, etc.; what might you have said, etc. One should not answer that because of falling leaves excludes for the garment's need, which is valid, for although he rejected that there, saying that if so, it is forced as an exclusion, it is a forced answer. Even according to the final reasoning, there is some proof from what is written, when it dries, for he should have had to remove it before then; afterwards, even if he removes it, people will still say, and what he removed does not help. Likewise, according to Menimin's reasoning, that even while it is wet people will say so, how does its removal help?
