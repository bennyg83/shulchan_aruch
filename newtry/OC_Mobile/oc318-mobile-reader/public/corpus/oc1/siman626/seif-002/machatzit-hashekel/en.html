(S.k. 2) It is valid, etc. Tosafot and the Rosh, etc., siman 631, etc. This refers to what we learned on folio 15: one who roofs his sukkah with spits or with the long side-rails of a bed, which are things invalid for s'chach, if there is space between them equal to them and he placed valid s'chach there in the space, it is valid.
And in truth Tosafot follow their own approach, etc. On folio 22b the Gemara sets Mishnayos against one another. In the first chapter we learned that where its shade is greater than its sun, it is valid; this implies that if its sun and shade are equal, it is invalid. In the second chapter we learned that if its sun is greater than its shade, it is invalid; this implies that if they are equal, it is valid. The Gemara answers: here above, here below. Rashi explained that if above, the s'chach and airspace are equal, it is invalid, but below, if the shade and sun are equal, it is valid. Rabbeinu Tam, because of his question, explained that if one stands above and sees that the s'chach and airspace are equal, it is valid. But if one stands below and looks upward, and it appears to him that above the s'chach and airspace are equal, it is invalid, because air, which has no substance, is not grasped so strongly by eyesight when looking from below upward, and the air appears smaller than it is. By contrast, s'chach, which has substance, is not reduced so much by looking from below upward. Therefore when one looks from below upward, although it appears to him that the air and s'chach are equal, it may be that the air is more than the s'chach, and it is therefore invalid. From this came the dispute: according to Rabbeinu Tam, if one stands above and sees that the air and s'chach are equal, it is valid; according to Rashi, in such a case it is invalid. The Rosh agreed with Rashi's explanation; see there. On this the Magen Avraham wrote: and in truth Tosafot follow their own approach. Meaning, they hold that where the air and s'chach are equal, it is valid. Therefore they properly proved from the case of one who roofs his sukkah with spits, where if there is space between them equal to them it is valid, and we do not say that invalid s'chach joins, because the s'chach alone is the valid measure of the sukkah, since there is space between them equal to them. If so, even if you remove the spits, the remaining valid sukkah measure is present, for half the sukkah is covered with valid s'chach. But the Rosh holds like Rashi, that if the air and s'chach are equal it is invalid. If so, if you remove the spits, the air and s'chach will be equal, which is invalid according to Rashi, for through this, according to Rashi, it is called having more sun than shade. Nevertheless, they validate by means of the spits, even though invalid s'chach joins. If so, the same law should apply to a tree.
But we rule the opposite. For the Rosh learned it from there. Perhaps so it should read.
And perhaps when it is placed above, it is worse. Meaning, worse than where the valid and invalid s'chach are placed next to one another. When it is placed above, one can somewhat use the reasoning of the some say opinion brought in the Shulchan Aruch, that the upper invalid s'chach nullifies the lower. The Rosh learns by degrees: since we find regarding spits, where valid and invalid s'chach are placed next to one another, that it is valid even when the valid s'chach alone does not have the full measure, then regarding invalid s'chach above, which is worse, we go down a level, and it is at least proper to validate where the valid s'chach alone has the measure of a valid sukkah.
Nevertheless, it requires examination to reconcile the Rosh's wording, etc. That the valid s'chach is sufficient. But according to the Rosh it is not sufficient with the valid s'chach alone, for he holds like Rashi, and here the air and s'chach would be equal, as above. However, one must understand what the Magen Avraham wrote: and in truth Tosafot follow their own approach, which implies that Tosafot's words sit well with him, since they hold that the valid measure of a sukkah exists when the air and s'chach are equal. Nevertheless, Tosafot's own question there on folio 8b, s.v. ha kamitztaref, remains difficult. They challenged what is taught there: one who makes his sukkah under a tree is as though he made it inside a house, and it is invalid. Rava said: they taught this only regarding a tree whose shade is greater than its sun, but if its sun is greater than its shade, it is valid. The Gemara asks: if its sun is greater than its shade, what of it? Invalid s'chach joins with valid s'chach. Rav Pappa answers: when he beat them down and mixed them. On this Tosafot wrote that it must be where the sukkah's shade is greater than its sun apart from the tree, and we do not say that it joins, even if he did not beat them down. They bring proof from spits, where if there is space, etc., as above. According to this, Tosafot have a difficulty: what did the Gemara ask, but it joins, etc.? Perhaps it speaks where the sukkah has more shade without the tree. These are their words. Now according to their approach, that where the air and s'chach are equal this is a valid sukkah measure, it is proven from the case of spits that wherever there is a valid measure in the valid s'chach, even if the invalid s'chach alone also has a valid measure, nevertheless it is valid, and we do not say it joins. For there, regarding spits, the invalid s'chach has a valid sukkah measure, since half the sukkah is covered with spits, as it teaches: if there is space between them equal to them. If so, if you remove the valid s'chach, the spits and airspace would be equal, and nevertheless the Mishnah teaches that it is valid, because the valid s'chach alone also has a valid sukkah measure. If so, what is difficult to Tosafot? The Gemara properly asks, but it joins, etc., for it necessarily speaks where the valid s'chach does not have a valid sukkah measure. If it did have a valid sukkah measure, then even if the tree's shade were greater, it would still be valid, as is proven from the case of spits. Maharsha asks similarly on folio 10 on Tosafot s.v. poreis. What Maharsha answered there is that Tosafot hold that if the valid s'chach came first, then even if the invalid s'chach has a valid sukkah measure, nevertheless since the valid s'chach alone also has a valid sukkah measure, it is valid. But the Mishnah of one who makes his sukkah beneath a tree implies that the tree came first, and in such a case Tosafot hold that if the tree alone has a valid sukkah measure, then even if the valid s'chach alone also has a valid sukkah measure, it is nevertheless invalid. Therefore their question is proper: what did the Gemara ask, but it joins, etc.; perhaps it speaks where the valid s'chach alone has a valid sukkah measure? See there. This answer suffices to resolve his question there. But from the case of spits it is proven that if the valid s'chach has a valid measure, even if the invalid also has a valid measure, and even if the invalid came first, nevertheless it is valid. For we learned: one who roofs his sukkah with spits, etc., if there is space, etc. This wording implies that he first began covering with spits and left spaces between them, and afterwards filled the airspace with valid s'chach; so Rashi explains there. If so, the question returns: what was difficult to Tosafot in the Gemara's question, but it joins, etc., as above? It is forced to say that the Magen Avraham holds like Maharsha's other answer, that if the tree's shade is greater, it is invalid because of sukkah beneath a sukkah; however, Maharsha himself already rejected this answer there. More plausibly, in my humble opinion, for Tosafot the Magen Avraham is comfortable with the reasoning he wrote regarding the Rosh, that perhaps when it is placed above, it is worse. Tosafot too hold that we go down one level: if the law when they lie next to one another is that where the valid s'chach has a valid measure it is valid even if the invalid also has a valid measure, then when the invalid s'chach is above the valid s'chach, it is enough to validate only when the valid s'chach has the measure and the invalid does not itself have a valid sukkah measure. But if the invalid too has a valid sukkah measure, it does not help that the valid s'chach also has the measure. If so, their question is proper. But regarding the Rosh, the Magen Avraham was not satisfied with this reasoning, because the Rosh's wording still remains difficult, as the Magen Avraham concludes. But according to Tosafot, the wording works well, because according to their approach, if the air and s'chach are equal, it is a valid sukkah measure. Therefore what Tosafot wrote, since if you remove it the valid s'chach is sufficient, is understandable; see there.
For it establishes it as where he adds extra. There, on the Mishnah of spits, the Gemara concludes: let this be a refutation of Rav Huna son of Rav Yehoshua, for it was stated regarding breached equal to standing: Rav Pappa says it is permitted, and Rav Huna son of Rav Yehoshua says it is forbidden. Invalid s'chach has the law of airspace and breach regarding this. It answers: Rav Huna son of Rav Yehoshua said to him, it is where it enters and exits. Rashi explains that the valid s'chach enters and exits in the space, so necessarily the airspace is greater than the spits, and the valid s'chach is therefore the majority. The Gemara asks: but it is possible to be exact. This is Rashi's text, and he explains that the initial thought was that the answer, where it enters and exits, meant that this is necessarily so because it is impossible to be exact that the airspace and spits should be equal. On this the Gemara asks: but it is possible to be exact. It answers: where he adds extra, meaning not because it is impossible to be exact, but because it speaks where he adds and places more valid s'chach than the spits. Tosafot challenged Rashi's explanation. One of the questions is that if so, the entire discussion must be specifically according to Rav Huna son of Rav Yehoshua, whereas we rule like Rav Pappa, that breached equal to standing is permitted. If so, it is forced that the discussion would not be according to the halacha. Therefore they read: but it is impossible to be exact, and the question applies both according to Rav Pappa and according to Rav Huna son of Rav Yehoshua. Since it is impossible to be exact and fill the whole space with valid s'chach, some airspace remains, and that airspace joins with the spits. If so, according to Rav Huna son of Rav Yehoshua, who establishes it as where it enters and exits so that the valid s'chach is the majority, now they will be equal. And according to Rav Pappa, who does not establish it as where it enters and exits because he holds that breached equal to standing is permitted, now the breach will be greater through the joining of the airspace. On this the Gemara answers according to everyone that it is where he adds extra, so that he covers all the airspace and also has some of the valid s'chach over the invalid. These are their words.
And the Rosh agreed with Tosafot's explanation. If so, according to the conclusion, the valid s'chach is more than half, for some of it lies on top of the invalid s'chach. Therefore it is understandable that if you remove the invalid, its shade is still greater than its sun through the valid material. But it remains difficult regarding the questioner, who had not concluded that it speaks of adding extra. Instead of asking, but it is impossible to be exact and some airspace remains, as above, he should have asked a stronger question: even if it is possible to be exact and he fills the whole airspace with valid s'chach, nevertheless it is half and half. If so, if you remove the invalid, the valid s'chach alone will not have the measure of a valid sukkah according to the Rosh, who holds like Rashi, as above. Thus he should have asked: but invalid s'chach joins with valid s'chach. On this the Magen Avraham wrote that the reader should examine the Maggid Mishneh, chapter 5 of Hilchos Sukkah. He wondered about the Rambam according to his text, which was like Rashi's text, but it is possible to be exact, where the question is only against Rav Huna son of Rav Yehoshua. If so, according to Rav Pappa, whom we rule like, there should be no need for the establishment of adding extra. Therefore he wondered about the Rambam for copying the establishment of adding extra. He answered that even according to Rav Pappa one must say it speaks of adding extra, for otherwise, since if you remove the invalid, the airspace and s'chach will be equal, in such a case it is invalid.
That is, he holds in this like Rashi's approach, that if they are equal above, below its sun is greater than its shade. He answered that the Gemara could have said: according to your reasoning, even if it were impossible to be exact, according to Rav Pappa too it must speak where he adds extra; see there. If so, the same can be said for the Rosh, that the Gemara could have said according to your reasoning.
