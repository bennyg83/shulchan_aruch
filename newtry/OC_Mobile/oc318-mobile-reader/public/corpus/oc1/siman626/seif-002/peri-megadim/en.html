He must See the Taz. In the Levush it implies that initially one may do this, and in the Gemara 9b this is explicit even initially. In Olas Shabbos 3 he asked this as well, and this is also the Taz's intent here. As for what he wrote from boards, one can say that there the decree is lest people err and think that even a house, etc., is permitted, unlike here where it is deliberate, etc. What he wrote in the name of the Bach, that one nullifies before Sukkos and that mitzvos were not given for benefit, is not identical to the Levush's answer. See Yoreh De'ah 99 in the Shach, where some say one may not nullify according to the Torah; if so, even for a mitzvah one can say so, unless one uses the Taz's answer that sitting in an invalid sukkah is not a prohibition, only that one does not fulfill his obligation, and in such a case the rule of not nullifying does not apply. What he wrote regarding liquid mixed with liquid before Pesach is as in siman 447, letter 5 in the Taz. But dry mixed with dry is not so; see there. And liquid with liquid, to mix it initially for Pesach is forbidden; only adding to it is permitted, as written there. Regarding an esrog that is forbidden, to nullify it among others, one can say that since in the end it is forbidden for eating, it is no longer called yours, even if the prohibition is rabbinic. Regarding tzitzis spun by a non-Jew and similar invalid items, whether one may take them and mix them with permitted tzitzis, see what I wrote in the laws of tzitzis. Now according to the reason that there is no prohibition to sit in it, with tzitzis he nullifies a positive commandment, and according to the one who says one may not nullify according to the Torah, this requires examination regarding tzitzis. See Eliyah Rabbah 4.
