(13) If he cut, etc. - Meaning, although initially he trained the tree over the sukkah to serve as s'chach, and it was invalid s'chach because it was attached, this is not called make it, and not from what was already made. Since in the end he cut it for the sake of s'chach, that is a new act of making and it is valid, even if there is no other s'chach there. Nevertheless, cutting alone is not called a complete act, since he did not cut it before training it there; therefore he must also shake it after cutting, so that it is as though at that moment he placed it in its location.
(14) And places it - For the sake of shade. It need not be placed for the sake of the mitzvah of sukkah. If one inserted a branch of a tree into the ground, with many branches and leaves, for the sake of shade over the sukkah, it is valid. If it was not for the sake of shade, and when the time of sukkah arrived he intended it to be for shade, it is invalid because of make it, and not from what was already made. If it took root, even if he inserted it for the sake of shade, it is invalid because it is attached, as the Peri Megadim writes.
(15) And it is not called make, etc. - Meaning, in this case one need not shake the s'chach anew. Know that everyone agrees to this law, meaning even the stringent authorities who disagree with what is written in se'if 3 in the gloss, as explained there, agree here to be lenient, as explained in the Bach and Magen Avraham; see there.
