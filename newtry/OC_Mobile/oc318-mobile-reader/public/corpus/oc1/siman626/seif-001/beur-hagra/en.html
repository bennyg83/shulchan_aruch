Se'if 1: Some say that if, etc. Tosafot there proved it from what is stated there on 4a: if it was higher than twenty, etc. This shows that s'chach higher than twenty does not invalidate it, since the hanging tips have more shade than sun. Likewise there on 15a, one who roofs his sukkah with spits, etc., if there is, etc. And although there beating down does not help, since each one is recognizable by itself. What is written, that if the tree, etc., in every case, etc., also appears in Tosafot there, for they said there: and why did it not answer, for example, etc. So too it appears from the Gemara there, which says it is as though he made it inside a house, and from what it says there: not in a sukkah beneath a sukkah, and not, etc. Regarding a sukkah beneath a sukkah, all the more so it is invalid if the upper and lower ones have more shade than sun, as written there. Furthermore, the Gemara answers that it is where he beat them down and the valid s'chach is greater. If it were so, even where the tree has more shade than sun it should be valid, since the valid s'chach is greater; necessarily its shade is greater than its sun.
In a manner, etc. Tosafot there, and there on 15b, s.v. veha.
And some say, etc. For everything beneath the tree is considered as though it is absent. The proof that Tosafot brought from the hanging tips is not a proof, as Rabbeinu Tam wrote in the Tosafot cited above, for s'chach higher than twenty is not invalid s'chach. His proof is from what is stated at the beginning of Eruvin, regarding part of the s'chach being above twenty, etc., even though if you removed it, its sun would be greater than its shade. And the proof they brought from what is written, one who roofs, etc., is not needed according to Tosafot's own explanation there, that it is impossible to be exact, and he answered that it speaks where one adds extra, through which the airspace is filled so that without the invalid s'chach there is the required measure. This is what is written here: but if the branches are opposite, etc. So too the Gemara there answered: if they were placed lengthwise, etc. This is what is written here: or if the s'chach is much, etc., which is like there, where the s'chach is over the whole sukkah and is much, etc. Rather, even according to Rashi's explanation there, the Mordechai already wrote that if one placed valid s'chach on top of the invalid, it is called a mixture. This is what is written here: and likewise if he placed, etc. Tosafot's words require examination, for there, s.v. veha, perhaps they challenged Rashi and wrote, however it is difficult, etc., and explained another interpretation according to the text of Rabbeinu Chananel and Rabbeinu Tam, while here they bring proof from Rashi's explanation there. According to the explanation here, there is no difficulty on Rashi there. One must say that there it is after they wrote here Rabbeinu Tam's explanation, that higher than twenty is not called invalid s'chach; if so, here one can explain like the Raavyah.
Whether the tree, etc. That is, unlike the words of the Razah, who wrote that this applies specifically where the tree came first. Since he placed some s'chach while its sun was still greater than its shade, and the tree then made its shade greater than its sun, it became invalid; and although he later added more, this does not help, because the invalid material made the shade, and what he added is considered as though absent. The Gemara speaks in such a case. But if the sukkah came first, he agrees with Tosafot. The Rashba wrote that his words do not appear correct, for the Gemara teaches plainly. There is proof from the fact that the Gemara asks, but it joins, and establishes the case where he beat them down, and does not answer in that way. Although Tosafot there noticed this and answered that if so, what novelty would there be, this is not an obvious reasoning to those poskim. All the more according to the Razah, the Gemara could have answered that we decree lest the tree precede the s'chach, just as it now answers that we decree, etc. All the more where the tree's shade is greater than its sun, it is invalid in every case, even if the sukkah preceded it, unlike Rabbeinu Tam, who validates even in such a case. Therefore he explained there on 10a, if he spread a sheet over it because of the sun, etc., that the sun dries the s'chach, etc.; see Tosafot there. He also inferred from what is said there on 11a: he trained it over it, etc., and placed s'chach on top of it, etc.; this implies specifically where he first trained it, for otherwise it should teach the reverse. The Rashba challenged him: there it speaks where the valid s'chach alone does not have more shade than sun, and the valid s'chach is not greater than the invalid, as taught in the latter clause: and if the s'chach was much, etc. I do not understand his question. But there is no proof according to Rabbeinu Tam's explanation, for necessarily the Mishnah there speaks where the attached material does not have more shade than sun, for otherwise how could it say, and if the s'chach was much, since that would not help in such a case, as Rava says: they taught this only, etc., and we establish it there as where he beat them down. But according to Tosafot's explanation above, and as the Razah wrote, there would have been a proof from there, except that Rabbeinu Tam did not enter into distinguishing this way between where the tree's shade is greater or where its sun is greater. He holds, in the basic law, like the Raavyah, that whenever invalid s'chach joins, it is like a tree whose shade is greater. Since there it teaches specifically that he trained it first, the same applies to a tree whose shade is greater. And there, regarding Rava's law, he holds like the Raavyah's explanation, that there the tree came first, as it says: one who makes his sukkah, etc. But the Razah disagreed with him about this, since the tree's shade is greater. The Rashba explains the Mishnah of he trained it, etc., as follows: he trained it means over the s'chach, and placed s'chach over it means, or he placed s'chach over it, so it teaches two cases. See the Ran, page 27. This is what is written here: in all this, we do not distinguish between, etc. The case where he spread a sheet over it, he explains like Rashi there. What is written in Responsa Rashba, siman 106, where he brought proof from he trained it, etc., has already been rejected as above. Likewise he rejected the case of Menimin, as written below.
However, if, etc. The Gemara there; and the words of the Magen Avraham here, s.k. 4, require great examination.
