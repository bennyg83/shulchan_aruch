Beneath a house. A house is invalid, for the Merciful One said sukkah, and not one's house of all the days of the year, as Rashi writes on folio 14. See the end of siman 636. A tree is invalid because it is attached.
It is valid. Even if he did not lower it. Tosafot and the Rosh proved this from what we say in siman 631:8, that if the invalid and valid s'chach are equal, it is valid, even though beating them down does not help to nullify by majority, since they are recognizable and not mixed, for each stands by itself. If so, it should be invalid because the invalid s'chach joins. Rather, the reason is that when you remove the invalid s'chach, the shade is still greater through the valid s'chach. End quote. In truth, Tosafot follow their own approach; see the beginning of siman 631. But the Rosh, who agreed with Rashi's explanation, holds that when you remove the invalid s'chach, the sun will be greater, and nevertheless it is valid. If so, here too regarding a tree, he should validate even where its sun is greater without the tree, for the Rosh learned it from there. Perhaps when it is placed above, it is worse. Nevertheless, it requires examination to reconcile the Rosh's wording, for he wrote: since when you remove it, the valid s'chach is sufficient. Perhaps he brings proof from the conclusion, which establishes the case as where he adds extra, and if so its shade is greater. See there, and Maggid Mishneh, chapter 5.
And nullify them. Although one may not intentionally nullify a prohibition, one can say that before Yom Tov, when it was not yet forbidden, they were already nullified; according to this, it is forbidden on Chol HaMoed. One can also say that this rule applies where one derives benefit, but mitzvos were not given for benefit, as the Bach and Mordechai write. The Ran wrote: although a prohibition that one can remove is not nullified, nevertheless this is how the Merciful One taught Moshe: half is sufficient, for a breached area equal to the standing area is permitted; only here, since they are mixed, we require a majority. End quote. If so, there is no nullification of a prohibition here.
However, if he lowered. This is specifically when the sukkah's shade is greater without the tree and the tree does not join with it. For this is not complete nullification, since he can remove it; therefore it does not invalidate and does not join, as the Ran and Maggid Mishneh write, and so wrote Rabbeinu Yerucham. All his words are the words of the Rosh, unlike the Bach, who wrote that when he mixed them it joins even according to this reasoning; that is not so. Rather, it must speak here where its shade is greater. What the Rema wrote, that they are not recognizable, is not precise, for even if they are recognizable it is permitted, since its shade is greater. So it is explicit in the Rosh, Ran, and Tosafot. Rather, he used the wording of the first reasoning. However, when they are not mixed at all, but the invalid and valid s'chach each lies separately by itself, no majority is required, as written in siman 638:8.
