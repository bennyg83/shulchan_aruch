One who makes a sukkah under a tree or under a roof. It contains 3 seifim: One should not make a sukkah beneath a house or a tree. One who makes his sukkah beneath a tree: some say that if the tree's shade exceeds its sunlight, the sukkah is invalid in every case, even if the sukkah itself has more shade than sunlight. But if the tree has more sunlight than shade, and the sukkah has more shade than sunlight without the tree, it is valid even if he did not lower the branches to mix them with the sukkah's schach. If, however, the sukkah has more shade than sunlight only because of the tree, he must lower the branches and mix them with the schach so that they are not distinguishable, with the valid schach exceeding and nullifying them. Others say that even when the sukkah has more shade than sunlight without the tree, and the tree has more sunlight than shade, if the tree branches are directly aligned above the valid schach the sukkah is invalid, whether the tree preceded the sukkah or the sukkah preceded the tree. {Rama: Nevertheless, if he lowers the branches and mixes them with the schach so that they are not distinguishable, they are nullified and the sukkah is valid. [Rosh and Ran] Likewise, if he placed valid schach upon the invalid schach, this is considered mixing and it is valid. [Mordechai, chapter 1 of Sukkah]} But if the branches are above the empty spaces between the valid schach, or there is so much valid schach that even if the amount corresponding to the tree were removed a valid measure would remain, the sukkah is valid, since the shade from valid schach still exceeds its sunlight. In all of this there is no distinction whether the tree preceded the schach or the schach preceded the tree; their law is the same.
