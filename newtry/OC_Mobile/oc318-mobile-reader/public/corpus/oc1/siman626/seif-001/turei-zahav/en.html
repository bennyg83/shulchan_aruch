Beneath a house, etc. For we require the sukkah to be beneath the open air of the heavens, as it is written basukkot teshevu without a vav, meaning in one, and not in a sukkah beneath a sukkah, or beneath a house or tree.
He must lower, etc. This implies that even initially one may do so, and so it is stated in the Gemara. This is difficult, for we rule everywhere that one may not intentionally nullify a prohibition. The Levush answered that the reason in general is that we decree lest one come to permit the entire prohibition, but here, with a mitzvah, this does not apply, since he intends for a mitzvah and will not perform the mitzvah through a transgression. These are his words. This is difficult, for we find decrees even in this mitzvah, such as boards that may not be used as s'chach because of the decree of a ceiling, as will be stated below. But there is no question from the outset, for here there is no name of prohibition on this. It is not necessary to say so only where one makes the sukkah before the festival, where it is obvious that no prohibition applies yet because the obligation of sukkah has not yet begun. Rather, even if he makes the sukkah during the festival, there is no name of prohibition here, for there is no intrinsic prohibition in sitting in that sukkah; it is only that one who sits in it does not fulfill the mitzvah. Therefore no prohibition applies to making it, even if he makes it in a fully invalid manner. Afterwards I saw that my father-in-law wrote in the name of the long Mordechai and Agudah to answer this, in this wording: one can say that before Yom Tov, when it was not yet a prohibition, it was repaired; furthermore, the rule that one may not nullify initially is rabbinic, and that applies where one benefits, but mitzvos were not given for benefit. End quote. From this we learn regarding a mixture of chametz before Pesach, liquid in liquid, where there is not sixty times as much, that one may add more to it, even initially, since it is before the prohibition.
Or where the s'chach is abundant enough that even, etc. This implies that this is equal to the case where the invalid material is opposite the airspace. In the Tur, however, it slightly implies that some views distinguish between them. After citing the view of the Avi Ezri, who is stringent like the second reasoning here, he writes that for this reason some are stringent not to make a sukkah below, in a house under the roof, etc.; and Rabbi Yechiel would place reeds upright from the s'chach to the roof, etc.; and it appears to me that it is permitted, for even according to the words of the one who invalidates, etc. End quote. This implies that the reason of those who are stringent is a matter of law according to the one who invalidates, not merely some stringency. If it were only a stringency not required by law, why would he conclude: it appears to me that it is permitted, etc., since even those who are stringent would agree that it is permitted by law? Rather, certainly they hold it is forbidden by law. This is difficult, for they too certainly hold that we regard what is opposite the invalid material as though it is absent, since they permit where the invalid material is opposite the airspace for this reason, as he wrote explicitly. Incidentally, let us explain the Tur's words. He wrote that Rabbi Yechiel was careful, when making a sukkah in a house beneath a roof whose tiles had been removed, to set reeds upright, etc. So too the Beis Yosef wrote in the name of the Mordechai, explaining that the minister of Coucy said that the Raaf would command them to place the willow s'chach on the latis or to set upright, etc. The Beis Yosef challenged this repair, for Rashi validated where they were beaten down only when the invalid s'chach is mixed with the valid and not visible to the eye; here this repair does not help, since the latis are recognizable. My father-in-law answered that Rabbi Yechiel speaks where they are mixed in such a way that the latis are not recognizable. None of this appears correct to me in explaining the Tur's words. They understood Rabbi Yechiel's repair of standing reeds upright as a substitute for the repair of beating down mentioned in the Gemara, namely what the Tur wrote that one must lower the branches, etc. This certainly is not so. If so, it speaks where it is invalid without this repair, and how could the Tur conclude: it appears to me that it is permitted because the valid s'chach opposite the invalid material is considered as though absent, since there is otherwise a valid sukkah measure? Above, he did not speak of a case requiring repair, for even those who are stringent hold at least like the Avi Ezri's explanation, that it is valid when the invalid material is located opposite the airspace below for this reason, and it is invalid only where there is not enough remaining without it. Rather, it appears to me that everything is correct. We begin with Rashi's words in the Gemara on folio 9, where he wrote: why did the Mishnah need to say that it is as though he made it inside the house, etc.? This is not beneath two coverings, and since its sun is greater because its branches are few and the valid s'chach is greater than it, from where would you think to invalidate? It is not because of two coverings, for it is one covering, and not because of attached material, for it is nullified by majority. We learn from here that one may place s'chach on the roof slats called latis, even though they are within less than three tefachim of one another, since their sun is greater than their shade, etc. End quote. We thus have before us that although the branches have more sun than shade, nevertheless if they were not one s'chach with the valid s'chach below, it would be invalid because of two coverings, namely that the upper one is invalid s'chach. So too it is stated in the second chapter of Sukkah in the Rif and Rosh regarding one who sleeps under a bed, that it is invalid because of sukkah beneath a sukkah, and the bed is invalid s'chach. The same applies where the upper covering is invalid, as here. Therefore the reasoning of those stringent authorities in the Tur works well: they hold according to the Avi Ezri that although the valid s'chach has abundant shade, the upper invalid covering whose sun is greater harms everything opposite it. Therefore what remains of the valid s'chach beyond that place does not help, for there is still an invalidity of two coverings. And although here one does not need the repair of beating down mentioned in the Gemara, namely lowering the branches, since the tree's sun is greater and it does not harm the entire sukkah where there is otherwise more shade, nevertheless I have not found permission to sit in that place, for he sits beneath two coverings. Therefore the Avi Ezri validates where the lower s'chach has more shade only when the upper covering, whose sun is greater, is opposite the airspace of the lower covering, for then there is no invalidity of two coverings. But where it is opposite the valid s'chach below, there is an invalidity of two coverings. Thus, although one need not lower it because the invalid material's shade is not greater, and it itself does not harm the whole sukkah as invalid s'chach, nevertheless because of two coverings there remains a concern for that place. Therefore Rabbi Yechiel's repair helps well, for he mixes the coverings by means of reeds and makes them into one s'chach. On this the Tur concludes that it is permitted even according to the Avi Ezri, for the main invalidity is because the invalid material is in a non-empty place and joins with the airspace already in the valid covering; here, where the valid covering has more shade regardless, we do not count that place at all and it is entirely as though absent, so there are not two coverings. He holds that invalid s'chach has no relevance to sukkah beneath a sukkah, as written in the next siman. But in my humble opinion the main view is that of the stringent authorities. For the Avi Ezri, cited in the Ashri, wrote in this wording: the tree's shade does not invalidate, since they are not aligned and the valid material has the required measure if the invalid material is removed, etc. This shows that he validates only where the tree is opposite the airspace and not aligned opposite the valid material. Otherwise he should have written briefly: since the valid material has the required measure. The Beis Yosef wrote that perhaps according to Rabbi Yechiel, if the valid material has enough shade without it, no repair is needed and no reeds need be stood upright; however, perhaps even this is forbidden without repair because of a decree from one case to another. We do not enter this discussion; rather, Rabbi Yechiel himself speaks of this and requires repair because of two coverings, and as above. So it appears primary as halacha: there is no permission except where the tree is opposite an airspace and there is abundant shade in the sukkah's valid s'chach. According to this, there is no permission to make the s'chach beneath the latis, and Rashi permitted only making the s'chach on top of the latis; but beneath the latis is forbidden because of two coverings, as I wrote. Still, one can say that according to us it is permitted even beneath the latis, since the latis are distant from one another the measure of a significant place, four tefachim, as is stated regarding boards in the first chapter of Sukkah, folio 14, and even three tefachim is enough. These latis therefore do not have the name of s'chach at all. Those who forbade beneath the latis were speaking where there is less than three tefachim between them, as with tile roofs, which are made according to the size of a brick, as in Poland and Germany. There the latis are close to one another so the brick can rest on two latis. Although we do not say lavud to be stringent, nevertheless since the latis are close by a small measure, they have the name of s'chach and there are two coverings. This is unlike places where they make roofs from boards and there are at least three or four tefachim between the latis; there it is not called s'chach at all, and there is no concern if the valid s'chach has the measure of a sukkah apart from the latis. In practice, one should be lenient only where there are four tefachim between them; this appears primary to me. And in the Tur he wrote in the name of Baal HaItur to validate with latis because one removes every other one. The Beis Yosef wrote that there is no proof from what we learned, that one removes every other one, because there the remaining one is invalid, etc. I did not understand his words, for certainly the remaining one too is valid when he places s'chach between them, as written below in se'if 4. Therefore here too, placing valid s'chach opposite the airspace between the latis should help. But it appears that the cases are not comparable. Below is different, for he removes every other one, and in the place where he removes one, there he places the valid s'chach; it turns out that even the reed remaining beside it becomes valid. But here, he does not place valid s'chach between the latis, only below them on the sukkah, so the latis themselves do not have valid s'chach on them at all. From this angle the Beis Yosef's question is proper, but what he wrote, that below there is invalidity on the remaining one, is not exact. And what the Beis Yosef wrote, that there is proof for Baal HaItur from what we say below, that it is valid by loosening, I did not understand, for loosening is different: he must loosen each one individually, and whatever he does not loosen remains invalid. If so, with what would the latis here, which are above the valid s'chach, become valid? So it appears to me.
