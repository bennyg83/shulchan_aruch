(1) Beneath a house, etc. - For we require that the sukkah be beneath the open air of the heavens, as it is written basukkot teshevu without a vav, meaning in one sukkah, that it should not be covered by two coverings, and not in a sukkah beneath another sukkah, or beneath a house or a tree. A house itself, even if one wants to sit in it during the festival for the sake of sukkah, is invalid, for the Merciful One said sukkah, and not one's house of all the days of the year. A tree is invalid to serve as the covering over the sukkah walls because it is attached.
(2) In every case - Meaning, even if he lowered the branches down and mixed them with the sukkah's s'chach, and the sukkah's s'chach is greater than them and it is not recognizable, nevertheless it is invalid. For although the sukkah has more shade than sun, since the tree too has more shade than sun, the sukkah's s'chach contributes nothing, as the Bach writes.
(3) But if the tree's sun is greater than its shade, etc. - For then the shade is considered as though it is not present, and it is not called a sukkah beneath a tree.
(4) But if there is not, etc., except through the tree - Meaning, since the tree's s'chach is invalid because it is attached, one cannot combine it with valid s'chach to validate the sukkah.
(5) He must lower, etc. - This is permitted even initially, and even if he made the sukkah on Chol HaMoed. The rule that one may not intentionally nullify a prohibition does not apply here.
(6) If they are aligned opposite the valid s'chach, it is invalid - This view holds that whatever is beneath the tree is considered as though it is absent and contributes nothing, since the upper covering shades it. If so, the remaining s'chach has more sun than shade. It cannot combine with the tree's s'chach, even if together this would make its shade greater than its sun, for the tree is invalid s'chach.
(7) However, if he lowered the branches, etc. - In this case it helps even if the sukkah does not have more shade than sun without the tree, as with the first opinion, according to the Bach and several later authorities. Therefore the Rema concluded that they are not recognizable, for if the sukkah's own s'chach had more shade than sun, then even if they were recognizable it would be permitted according to what we wrote below. Some say that according to this final opinion, even where he lowered the branches it does not help unless the sukkah's own s'chach has more shade than sun without the tree. In practice, it is correct to be stringent like them.
(8) That they are not recognizable - If it had more shade than sun, then even if they were recognizable or lay directly one on top of the other, it is permitted, since they are more numerous than the tree's s'chach. Some disagree with this.
(9) It is called a mixture and is valid - This is when the valid s'chach was greater than the invalid. If they were placed on the sukkah each by itself and were not mixed at all, but one next to the other, the valid s'chach need not be a majority over the invalid. Rather, even if they are half and half the sukkah is also valid, for this is a halacha transmitted to Moshe at Sinai. However, because it is impossible to be exact, one should add a little extra, as will be explained in siman 631:8; see there.
(10) But if the branches, etc. - This speaks where the branches do not make much shade, and their sun is greater than their shade.
(11) Such that even if what is opposite the tree were removed, etc. - It implies from the poskim that we regard the s'chach opposite the tree as invalid s'chach. Therefore one should not validate unless it is less than four tefachim in a large sukkah, or less than three tefachim in a small one.
(12) Such that even if the tree were removed, etc. - Meaning, one should not ask: what difference does it make if the branches are opposite the empty air, since in any case the tree and the s'chach cover the sukkah? For this he says that even if it were removed, etc., meaning that the tree contributes nothing; without its shade, the s'chach itself has more shade than sun, and by contrast the tree branches themselves have only more sun than shade, as above in s.k. 10.
