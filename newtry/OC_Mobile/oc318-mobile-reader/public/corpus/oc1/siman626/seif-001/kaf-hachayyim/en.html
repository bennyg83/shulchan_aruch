1) [Seif 1] Rama's gloss: One may not make a sukkah beneath a house, etc. For we require that it be made beneath the open sky. It is written, You shall dwell in sukkos, with the word sukkos written defectively, without a vav, and we expound: not in a sukkah beneath another sukkah, not in a sukkah beneath a tree, and not in a sukkah beneath a house. Gemara, Sukkah 9b; Tur; Levush; Taz, s.k. 1. A house itself is invalid, for the Merciful One said sukkah, and not one's house of the entire year. Rashi, Sukkah 14a. A tree is invalid because it is attached to the ground. Magen Avraham, s.k. 1, and Levushei Serad. Something attached is invalid because it is written, when you gather from your threshing floor and your winepress, and we expound: make your sukkah from that which you gather from your threshing floor and your winepress, and not from something attached to the ground. Beit Yosef; Levush. See below, at the beginning of siman 629.
2) In all of these cases, it makes no difference whether the sukkah preceded them or the invalid items preceded the sukkah. The sukkah is always invalid because of them, on account of the rule of a sukkah beneath another sukkah, even if he intermingled them, and even if the sukkah independently has more shade than sunlight in accordance with the law. Nevertheless, it is invalid. Levush.
3) A person fulfills his obligation only with a sukkah that was made solely for shade, similar to the Clouds of Glory. If, however, it was also made as a dwelling, meaning to reside in it permanently and not merely to shelter in its shade, or if it was also made for a private use, such as a storehouse or another use that must be performed in a private place, or if it was also made as protection and shelter from storm and rain, then whenever it was not made solely for shade, it is not a sukkah but a house. A house is designated for all these purposes, and the Torah said sukkah, not a house. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, sec. 1.
