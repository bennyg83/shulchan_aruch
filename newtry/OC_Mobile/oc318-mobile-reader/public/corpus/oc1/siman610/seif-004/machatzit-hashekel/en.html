(Subparagraph 4) And if they were extinguished, etc. See what I wrote in siman 276, se'if 3, that it is permitted to tell a non-Jew to take a lit candle and walk with him, since there is only the prohibition of moving muktzeh and the non-Jew also needs it, for he walks with him. But it is forbidden to tell him to kindle a candle, even if the non-Jew walks with him. If so, regarding moving the dripping wax, in a place where the custom is that that non-Jew also has a share in the dripping wax, it would have been possible to permit telling the non-Jew to catch the dripping wax, since the non-Jew also acts for his own benefit, as above where the non-Jew walks with him. However, the Magen Avraham wrote there in the name of Radbaz that this should be permitted only for Torah scholars, lest people come to draw improper conclusions. In Eliyah Rabbah he wrote as follows: In the old Levushim he wrote, see siman 276, se'if 2, and siman 307, se'if 5. The Peri Megadim explained that he means that according to one opinion there, one can learn a reason for the custom to be lenient and tell a non-Jew to repair the candles, for he holds that telling a non-Jew is permitted even for full labor in a mitzvah situation. The Eliyah Rabbah concluded: and I wrote there that all the Acharonim agreed to forbid, end quote. The Magen Avraham there also wrote that one should not protest against those who permit it, for it is better that they be inadvertent sinners. The Magen Avraham also wrote there: specifically a mitzvah from which his body benefits, such as a candle for eating; see there, and see what the Eliyah Rabbah wrote about him.
