A man - But not a woman, because the reason for the candles is that the Torah was completed on Yom Kippur, and Torah is called a candle, and a woman is not obligated in Torah study (Mahari Weil). In Maharil: there are those who say they correspond to a person's limbs, and a woman has additional limbs, or that they correspond to the positive commandments, and a woman was not obligated in them. Nowadays they make a candle only for one who is married. See Yoreh De'ah at the end of siman 256 in the Shach regarding settlements that bring candles, and Responsa Maharam, siman 153.
And if they were extinguished, etc. One should not tell a non-Jew to catch the dripping wax (Minhagim). See what I wrote in siman 276, se'if 2. The custom became to hire a non-Jew to guard the candles so that, God forbid, they not lead to a fire, and because of this people became accustomed to tell him to extinguish and kindle. One should protest against them, for this has no permission at all. See the Shelah, especially on the Day of Judgment.
Like angels - According to this, women do not wear white garments, for they cannot be like angels, since it is written "the city of men," as the Mateh Moshe wrote regarding immersion. Nevertheless, they may wear the kittel, so that their hearts be subdued. Even one who wears sackcloth because of repentance may not wear it on Yom Kippur (Sefer Chasidim 615; R. Shlomo Halevi, siman 6). It is stated in Yalkut Shimoni, Shoftim, page 9a, to make thick wicks in the synagogue in order to increase their light.
