1) [Seif 1] In a place where the custom is to kindle a lamp, etc. This means that in a place where the custom is to kindle a lamp in one's bedroom, he must kindle it, and in a place where the custom is not to kindle one, he must not kindle it. Bach. Both places intend the same purpose, namely, to prevent marital relations. One who kindles intends that marital relations are forbidden in the light of the lamp, while one who does not kindle intends that he not see his wife and desire her. Pesachim 53b and Rashi's explanation there; Beit Yosef; Taz, s.k. 1; Magen Avraham, s.k. 1. However, regarding what the Taz concluded there, "And although it is forbidden, etc.," see Maamar Mordechai, os 1, who questioned his words, for the reason of one who does not kindle is not because of improper thoughts, but so that he not come to marital relations, see there.
2) Even when his wife is a niddah, in a place where lamps are not kindled, he should not kindle one, so that he not see her, for he has still not escaped improper thoughts. Mishbetzos Zahav, os 1.
3) Ibid.: In a place where the custom is to kindle a lamp, etc. In the Yerushalmi, the version reads: A place where the custom is to kindle is praiseworthy in comparison with a place where the custom is not to kindle. Beit Yosef. This teaches regarding a newly established city that it is proper to adopt this practice. Pri Chadash; Mishna Berurah, os 2.
4) Ibid.: In a place where the custom is not to kindle, one may not kindle. They cannot obtain permission through annulment of the custom. Rashba, responsa, siman 98; Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur; Eliyah Rabbah, os 1. See Yoreh De'ah, siman 214, seif 2.
