(Subparagraph 2) Since, etc. It implies that some say on the table and not in the room, for he wrote that they kindle candles on their tables, and some also kindle in the rooms. This implies that some add to kindle also in the rooms, and some do not kindle in the rooms, but on their tables everyone kindles.
This is unlike what the Rema wrote in the Shulchan Aruch, and the difficulty is as the Magen Avraham wrote at the end of this subparagraph: if so, when he sees her in the house, he will desire her, etc.
So it also appears there at the end of Yoma, where he wrote, meaning that the Mordechai wrote at the end of Yoma.
And one should not say that this is, etc. This refers to what is written there, that one does not recite a blessing over lighting the Yom Kippur candle, unlike what is written here in the Shulchan Aruch, se'if 2. Specifically on Shabbos, where lighting the Shabbos candle is an obligation, one recites a blessing. But on Yom Kippur, where lighting a candle depends on custom, even in a place where they customarily kindle, one should not recite a blessing over it, for one does not recite a blessing over something that is only because of custom. About this he wrote: and one should not say that specifically in the room, etc., it depends on custom, but in the house it is an obligation to kindle, and if so one may properly recite a blessing over the candle one kindles in the house. He rejected this, for necessarily also in the house it depends on custom, since we learned: they kindle in synagogues, etc.
And it is difficult, for if so, etc. That is, regarding what the Mordechai wrote at the beginning of Yoma, that some kindle on the table and not in the room, one should ask, according to the truth: if so, when he sees her he will desire her, etc. And regarding what the Mordechai wrote at the end of Yoma, it is difficult: why did he need to reject it by saying that necessarily also in the house it depends on custom, from the Mishnah, "they kindle in synagogues, etc."? Let him say instead: how could one say that in the house one is obligated to kindle everywhere, while in the room it depends on custom? If so, in a place where the custom is not to kindle in the room but in the house it is obligatory to kindle, then when he sees her he will desire her, etc.
All this is to justify the custom. Meaning that the Magen Avraham saw in his days that the custom was not like the Rema, but that they kindled in the house and not in the room. Therefore the Magen Avraham found merit for them, that they rely on the Mordechai mentioned above.
