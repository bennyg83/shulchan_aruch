(Subparagraph 3) A man, etc. It is completed on Yom Kippur. Meaning, Moshe Rabbeinu descended on Yom Kippur with the second tablets. So it is written in Eliyah Rabbah.
And a woman is not obligated in Torah study. Although she recites the blessing over Torah, as written at the end of siman 47, that is because in any event she is obligated to learn the laws that apply to her, as written in Yoreh De'ah. With this the Magen Avraham in siman 187 resolved why women say "Torah" in Birkas ha-Mazon; see there. One must say, nevertheless, that the essence of Torah was given specifically to men.
Corresponding to a person's limbs. Meaning that a man has 248 limbs, and in addition to them spirit and soul, giving 250, the numerical value of ner. So wrote the Eliyah Rabbah. But a woman has additional limbs, as stated in Bechoros, chapter 7, 45a, a dispute of tanna'im: according to one view she has 252 limbs, and according to one view she has 251. See the Rambam, chapter 2 of the Laws of Impurity from the Dead, and the Kesef Mishneh there.
Or corresponding to positive commandments. For they are 248, and two prohibitions repaired by a positive commandment, making 250. So wrote the Eliyah Rabbah. Although there are many prohibitions repaired by a positive commandment, one can say he means those in which lashes are possible even though they are repaired by a positive commandment, namely where he fulfilled it or did not fulfill it, or nullified it or did not nullify it. See Makkos 17. They are more similar to positive commandments, and there are only two; see there.
And a woman was not obligated. Meaning in time-bound positive commandments, and if so she is short of the count of 250.
Only one who is married. Regarding what is written in the Shulchan Aruch, also a soul candle for his father and mother, the Eliyah Rabbah wrote that from this language it implies that one candle suffices for his father and mother. However, he questions this somewhat from what is written in Sefer Amarkal.
