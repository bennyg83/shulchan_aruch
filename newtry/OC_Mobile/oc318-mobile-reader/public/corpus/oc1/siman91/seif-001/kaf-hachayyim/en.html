1) [Seif 1] If a tallis was wrapped around his waist, etc., until he covers his heart. Bach writes that one must cover his entire body, as though he were standing in awe before a king. The teaching, "For prayer, until he covers his heart," does not mean specifically his heart; rather, he must cover his entire body. It mentions the heart only because the first clause states that for Krias Shema he need not cover his heart, and the latter clause therefore states that for prayer he must cover his heart. It is indeed true that he must likewise cover his entire body, etc.; see there. Yafeh LaLev, paragraph 1, cites his ruling. We likewise wrote this above in siman 74, paragraph 24; see there.
2) If his hair is long and covers the opening of his ear, he must remove it during prayer so that the opening of his ear is exposed while he prays. Tikkunei Zohar, tikkun 70, states: "For this reason, one must remove the hair from over the ears so that the gates through which prayer is heard will not be closed. Through those gates, the One of Whom it is said, 'You hear prayer; to You all flesh shall come,' hears the prayers." End quote. It further states in that tikkun: "The ears contain chambers and porticoes, etc. If the ears below are sealed, the corresponding gates of hearing above are likewise sealed to them. These are the gates concerning which it is said, 'You hear prayer' and 'Hear, O Israel.' From there one must direct one's intention through hearing." End quote. See there further, where it writes that hearing is in Binah. Totzaos Chaim writes that one must also remove the hair from over his lips during prayer so that it will not obstruct his mouth; see there. Petach Einayim, paragraph 1, cites his ruling. Rabbi Yisrael Sarug, a disciple of the Arizal, likewise writes in paragraph 55 that during prayer one must remove the hair from over his ears and from around his mouth. Yafeh LaLev cites his ruling in the kuntres Yosher Levav, siman 92, paragraph 3.
3) There: If he did not cover his heart, or circumstances beyond his control left him with nothing with which to cover himself, then since he covered his ervah and prayed, he fulfilled his obligation. Rambam writes this in chapter 4 of Hilchos Tefillah, halachah 7, and concludes there: "Initially, one should not act this way." End quote. Tur cites his ruling. Rambam's wording implies that he lists two alternatives: either one already prayed in this manner, or one has nothing with which to cover himself and may therefore pray this way initially, since circumstances beyond his control render his case equivalent to an after-the-fact situation. The words "Initially, one should not act this way" refer to someone who can readily find a covering. Levush likewise writes: "After the fact, or if circumstances beyond his control have left him without," etc. End quote. This explicitly shows that he understood Rambam as listing two alternatives. Nehar Shalom, paragraph 1, writes likewise, contrary to Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 1, who explains the matter in the reverse manner; see there.
