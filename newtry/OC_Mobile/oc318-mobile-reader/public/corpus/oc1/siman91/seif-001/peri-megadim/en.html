Written in the Zohar, etc. — that is with a tallit of mitzvah; and so is the custom to cover the head until the eyes with a tallit of mitzvah at the prayer of the Eighteen Benedictions; and see seif 6.

There is in Keriyas Shema what is not in prayer, and in prayer what is not in Keriyas Shema: in Keriyas Shema, if in doubt whether he read or not, he returns and reads as in siman 67; and in prayer he does not return if he prayed, section 1 in Maadanei Yom Tov, even for Rambam as in siman 106 section 2. And Keriyas Shema section 11 — one cannot discharge another, for it is written "and speak of them" — speech specifically; not so in prayer where the prayer leader discharges, siman 62 Eliyah Rabbah note 2, and Magen Avraham siman 59 note 5 in the name of R' Moshe Alshkar — see there; and what is written in Pri Megadim and siman 124 section 1. And regarding urine dripping on the feet in Keriyas Shema one must remove his garments, and in prayer not, siman 98 (nevertheless this is because of the stringency of prayer that one may not interrupt). And Keriyas Shema first verse with Baruch Shem — if he did not have intent he returns and reads as in siman 63 section 4; in prayer if he did not have intent he does not return as in siman 101. And at first glance there is a difficulty with what is written in siman 64, Netsiv in the name of Bach and Levush and Perishah, that the first verse if in doubt whether they said it — obviously he returns, for certainly he said it without intent, or establish it in presumption — if so, go out and earn a living; doubt whether he prayed — he does not return; but from the law of the Gemara intent is required in all of prayer, and if he did not have intent he returns — so how can doubt whether he prayed be conceived? And regarding if the time of Keriyas Shema passed — in the fourth hour he reads with blessings, and all day he reads without blessings, and it is implied there is obligation all day; for Rambam he reads all day with blessings, siman 58 section 17; and prayer chapter 8 after noon — not. And in Levush siman 58 he can read it, and at first glance the law is with him for completion — section 11 that one completes in the evening prayer; and if we say completion is not the reward of prayer in its time (see Perishah 89), then what difference is there between all day and completion? And this can be resolved.

And there is in prayer what is not in Keriyas Shema: all those eight matters that Rambam counted in chapter 5 of prayer — five of them are required lechatchilah in prayer but not in Keriyas Shema, as explained. And those five matters he wrote in chapter 4 of Hilchos Keriyas Shema that impede — namely: purity of hands, covering nakedness, purity of the place of prayer, distracting things, and intent of the heart — here purity of place and covering nakedness also apply in Keriyas Shema and impede; intent of the heart — for us in prayer lechatchilah requires intent in all of prayer as in siman 101; and Keriyas Shema in siman 63 — if dozing he need not wake him from the first [section] onward. Distracting things — implied in the Gemara chapter 23 and in siman 92 specifically for prayer the law is thus; but in Keriyas Shema even if unable to stand on his feet he has fulfilled; and Levush 92 because of "prepare" and not in Keriyas Shema; nevertheless it is also a mitzvah in Keriyas Shema to relieve himself beforehand. Purity of hands — implied from Rambam there that in retrospect if he did not clean with a pebble his prayer is not prayer — and this will be explained in siman 92. And regarding Keriyas Shema it is implied that even lechatchilah he need not go to urine — and this will be explained there in 92; and ordinary [unwashed] hands are invalid for prayer and Keriyas Shema — it is implied they are valid. And in 92 it will be explained.
