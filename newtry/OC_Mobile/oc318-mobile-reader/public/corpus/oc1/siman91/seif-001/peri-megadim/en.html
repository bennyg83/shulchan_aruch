There are laws that apply to Krias Shema but not to prayer, and laws that apply to prayer but not to Krias Shema. Regarding Krias Shema, one who is uncertain whether he recited it must recite it again, as stated in siman 67. Regarding prayer, however, one does not repeat it if he prayed once within the twenty-four-hour period, even according to Rambam, as stated in siman 106, seif 2. Some maintain that one person cannot discharge another’s obligation in Krias Shema, because the verse says, “You shall speak of them,” requiring actual speech, unlike prayer, where the chazzan may discharge others. See siman 62, Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2, and Magen Avraham, siman 59, paragraph 5, in the name of Rabbi Moshe Alashkar; see there, and what I wrote in Peri Megadim. See also siman 124, seif 1. If urine is flowing from him during Krias Shema, he must remove his garments, whereas during prayer he does not; see siman 78. This distinction, however, results from the greater stringency of prayer, during which interruption is forbidden. Regarding the first verse of Krias Shema together with “Blessed is the Name of His glorious kingdom forever and ever,” if he lacked intent he repeats it, as stated in siman 63, seif 4. Regarding prayer, if he lacked intent he does not repeat it, as stated in siman 101. At first glance, this conflicts with what is written in siman 64 in Netz HaChaim, in the name of the Bach, Levush, and Perishah, that if one is uncertain whether he said the first verse, he certainly repeats it, because he presumably recited it without intent, or because we retain the prior presumption. If so, explain how a case of uncertainty whether one prayed can exist without requiring repetition, since by the law of the Gemara one requires intent throughout the entire prayer, and if he lacked intent he must repeat it. How, then, can uncertainty whether he prayed ever arise? Regarding one who missed the time for Krias Shema at the fourth hour, he recites it with its blessings, and throughout the day he recites it without its blessings. This implies that an obligation remains throughout the day, for according to Rambam he recites it with its blessings throughout the day; see siman 58, paragraph 17. The Amidah, however, may not be recited after midday. Levush, siman 58, writes that he may recite Krias Shema throughout the day, and at first glance his ruling is correct, for some maintain that one may make it up at Maariv. If making it up does not carry the reward of prayer in its proper time, as discussed in Perishah, siman 89, then what difference is there between reciting it throughout the day and making it up? This can be resolved.
There are laws that apply to prayer but not to Krias Shema. All eight matters enumerated by Rambam in chapter 5 of Hilchos Tefillah, halachah 1, are initially required for prayer but not for Krias Shema, as explained. Of the five matters listed in chapter 4 of Hilchos Tefillah as indispensable, namely clean hands, covering the nakedness, cleanliness of the place of prayer, matters that press upon him, and intent of the heart, cleanliness of the place and covering the nakedness apply likewise to Krias Shema and are indispensable. Regarding intent of the heart, according to our ruling prayer initially requires intent throughout the entire prayer, as stated in siman 101, whereas regarding Krias Shema, siman 63 states that if one dozes there is no need to awaken him from the second paragraph onward. Matters that press upon him appear from Berachos 23a and siman 92 to apply specifically to prayer. Regarding Krias Shema, even if he cannot restrain himself, he has fulfilled his obligation. Levush, siman 92, explains that this is because of “Prepare,” which does not apply to Krias Shema. Nevertheless, it is also a mitzvah before Krias Shema to relieve himself first. Regarding clean hands, Rambam there implies that after the fact, if one did not clean them with a stone, his prayer is not prayer. With Hashem’s help this will be explained in siman 92. Regarding Krias Shema, it appears that even initially one need not seek water, as will be explained there. Ordinarily unexamined hands are invalid for prayer, but appear valid for Krias Shema. With Hashem’s help this will be explained in siman 92.
