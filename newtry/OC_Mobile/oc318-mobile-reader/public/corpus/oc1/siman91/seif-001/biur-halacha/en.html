Since he covered his nakedness - It appears that this refers to the case discussed at the beginning of the seif, where the tallis was tied around his waist, so that his heart likewise did not see the nakedness. If, however, his heart sees the nakedness, such as where he wore a robe without trousers, then although this is only D’Rabbanan, as the Peri Megadim writes in siman 74, he must nevertheless repeat the prayer. This is also evident from Derech HaChaim, in the sixth of the matters requiring care during prayer and Krias Shema. See Chayei Adam, rule 3, halachah 33. The practical ruling requires study.
He has fulfilled his obligation - See Rambam, who concludes, “But initially he should not do so.” The commentators struggled to explain this. It appears to me that Rambam teaches that one who was compelled and has nothing with which to cover himself should initially refrain from praying altogether. I later found this explanation in the Maggid Mishneh. It appears that only when one was compelled do we permit him to recite Krias Shema while naked from the waist upward, as stated at the end of siman 75, but otherwise not. I likewise found this in Tevuos Shor, siman 1, seif katan 71; see there.
