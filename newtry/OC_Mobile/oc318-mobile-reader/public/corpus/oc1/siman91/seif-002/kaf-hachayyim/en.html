4) [Seif 2] One must gird himself with a belt during prayer, etc. One who goes without a belt throughout the day need not gird himself during prayer. Beis Yosef in the name of Rabbeinu Yerucham; Olas Tamid, paragraph 1; Magen Avraham, seif katan 1; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2; Solet Belulah, paragraph 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 2. Nevertheless, it is a pious practice to wear one even in such a case. Magen Gibborim; Mishnah Berurah; Petach Einayim, paragraph 3.
5) There: One must gird himself with a belt during prayer, etc. Bach writes: "There is a prohibition against praying without a belt. Nevertheless, after the fact, Rashi and Rabbeinu Shemayah are sufficiently authoritative to rely upon, and he need not pray again." End quote. Knesses HaGedolah, in Hagahos Beis Yosef, paragraph 1, and Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2, cite his ruling. Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 4, however, writes that according to all opinions one who prayed without a belt fulfilled his obligation; see there. Nehar Shalom, paragraph 2, writes likewise and adduces proof from seif 1, which states that one who prayed without covering his heart fulfilled his obligation after the fact; see there. Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1.
6) There: One must gird himself with a belt, etc. Sefer Chasidim, siman 773, likewise writes that when a person prays, the belt must be fitted closely around his waist; see there. Levush explains the reason: when a person girds a belt around his waist, he appears to stand with greater readiness than when he is without a belt. See what the commentators write at the beginning of II Kings, chapter 9, concerning the verses "Elisha the prophet called," etc., and "He said to him, gird your waist," etc.; and in Jeremiah, chapter 1, concerning the verse "You shall gird your waist," etc. Analyze this carefully. Yafeh LaLev, paragraph 2.
