(11) With his money-pouch — this is a hollow belt in which money is placed, and it is not honorable to stand thus before the Almighty. And likewise one should not pray in a caftan and undergarment, or in a sleeping robe [Chayei Adam].

(12) Uncovered — and in our times one must put on a hat on one's head at prayer time as one goes in the street, and not the small hat under the hat, for there is no way to stand thus before important people, and all the more so before the King of kings. One may not pray in linen sock-robes alone, for only wool sock-robes is it proper to stand thus before great ones, and all the more when wearing a sandal with exposed heel. Also one may not wear at prayer time house slippers called "entshes" as wayfarers do; and I call upon them: "Let not the foot of arrogance come upon me, and let not the hand of the wicked move me" [Bach]. And from Chayei Adam above it is implied that even with wool sock-robes alone it is also not proper, and all depends according to local custom. Maharil Mordechai enacted not to enter the synagogue in long shoes — that is, boots — and if the custom of that place is to stand thus before great ones it is permitted [Peri Megadim].

(13) Exposed — nevertheless if the garment is long and covers the legs, or in very hot countries where there too they stand barefoot before great ones, there is no concern even if the garments are short and the legs are visible.
