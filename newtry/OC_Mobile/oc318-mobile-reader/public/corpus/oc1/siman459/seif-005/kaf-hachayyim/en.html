5) There. On a cloudy day it is forbidden, etc. This applies specifically when the sky is completely overcast. If clouds come and go, with the sun alternately revealed and covered, there is no concern. Peri Chadash in the name of Ramban. He nevertheless writes that according to Terumas HaDeshen, cited in the gloss, the custom is at least to avoid kneading beneath the open sky. Although Darkei Moshe writes that Terumas HaDeshen may agree here, as stated in the preceding subsection, he presents this only as a possibility. The custom is therefore to be careful, because the opposite may be true: this case may be no different from kneading beneath a window, since those occupied with kneading and smoothing might not notice when the sky becomes overcast. Another reason for the custom not to knead beneath the open sky even when there are no clouds is that if clouds appear, the dough must be moved elsewhere. While people search for another place and move the dough and utensils, the dough might remain unattended for some time. Therefore, the custom is not to knead beneath the open sky. Before kneading begins, people designate a suitable location where the dough will not need to be moved if clouds appear.
