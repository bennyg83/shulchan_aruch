One who is shaping [kituf]. Meaning she smoothes the face of the cake with water; and in these lands they are not accustomed to trim the matzos with water.
In which the hands are cooled The Bach agrees that she must cool her hands while working with the dough even if she is not busy with the oven, because the hands warm from working with the dough; and such is the view of most poskim; and so wrote Shiltei Giborim, see there. However, the view of the Ran and Maggid Mishneh is not so: so long as she is not warming from the heat of the oven and afterward touching the dough, she need not cool at all. And likewise the custom is not to cool the hands when working at kneading the dough; and so it appears to me to be somewhat precise from the implication of the Talmud's language, which says a woman needs two vessels, one with which she trims and one with which she cools; and if it were that cooling is because of working with the dough, it should have said the reverse, for that comes before the trimming; and this is sufficient.
