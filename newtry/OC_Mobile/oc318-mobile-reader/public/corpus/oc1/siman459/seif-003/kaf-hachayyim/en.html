3) There. One may not knead in sunlight. If a woman transgressed and kneaded in sunlight, the bread is permitted. Rambam, chapter 5. The Shulchan Aruch likewise rules below in se'if 5. Peri Chadash writes there in se'if 5 that this is certainly so if one kneaded beneath the open sky on a cloudy day. It may also be assumed that according to Rambam, if one kneaded near the mouth of an oven or in a similar place, the dough is permitted after the fact. Mishbetzos Zahav, subsection 1, likewise writes that according to the Shulchan Aruch, dough kneaded on a cloudy day is permitted after the fact. See there. Chemed Moshe, subsection 1, writes likewise. Rosh at the end of the chapter Kol Sha'ah, however, prohibits dough that was kneaded in sunlight, as cited by Beis Yosef. Bach rules likewise and writes that it is prohibited even after the fact and even when done inadvertently. The same applies on a cloudy day, whose law is like kneading in sunlight. It is certainly prohibited after the fact if kneaded near the mouth of our ovens, whose openings are on the side, because the dough warms immediately near the oven opening. See there. Magen Avraham, subsection 15, cites this and writes, 'It appears to me that this applies specifically when the dough became warm.' Chok Yaakov, subsection 26, cites his words. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 6; Chemed Moshe, there; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 8; Chayei Adam, rule 128, subsection 17; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 21, write likewise. Magen HaElef, subsection 6, writes that when one acted deliberately, one should be stringent even if the dough did not become warm, since the entire kneading was performed in sunlight. If one began in sunlight but finished elsewhere, however, it is possible that one need not be stringent. These are his words. The authorities cited above, however, imply that there is no distinction: whenever the dough did not become warm, it is permitted after the fact. Shulchan Aruch HaRav there explicitly writes that if it did not become warm, it is permitted even if one kneaded deliberately. He also implies that even when it did become warm, it is prohibited only for consumption but permitted for benefit. See there. It appears that everything depends upon the circumstances. If we see that it became very warm, it should be prohibited even for benefit if this occurred during Pesach or on the eve of Pesach after chametz became prohibited.
