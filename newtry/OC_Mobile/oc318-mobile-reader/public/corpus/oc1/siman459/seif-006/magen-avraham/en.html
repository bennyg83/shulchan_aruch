He should not add flour to it. For that flour is not kneaded well, and after baking it is still [distinctly] present, and perhaps that flour was not baked well, and there is concern that it may fall into soup in a bowl and become chametz. And if it were possible without leaving the dough idle, he should make a stiff kneading, etc., end quote of Terumat HaDeshen. And it appears to me that this is specifically when there is another person who can knead a stiff dough; but one person is forbidden to knead both together, for it is impossible to knead two doughs together and tend them properly. And it is implied from his words that if it is impossible to make another stiff dough, there is no prohibition to add flour; rather one should guard the matzos not to put them into something liquid. And if [the concern is] lest one chew them, they do not become chametz immediately; and so it is implied at the beginning of siman 466. And furthermore, the flour is roasted by the fire, as is written in siman 463, se'if 3. And also Maharil in the name of Maharash permits l'chatchilah. But Mahariv in siman 430 wrote, and this is his language: And one should not add flour, because it is not kneaded with the first [dough], and it turns out that it separates between [parts of] the dough; from the mouth of Mahari, of blessed memory, end quote. It is implied that he holds that the dough does not bake well because of the interruption of the flour; see the beginning of siman 460. After every dough, one should wash his hands and again wash the basin and dry them with a cloth, then knead immediately without interruption. And if it is impossible to wash the basin, he should fill it with cold water to its brim (Maharil). And the proper custom is to have two basins to switch between one dough and the next (Shelah). And likewise the one who put the flour, and those who shaped the matzos, should wash their hands and clear the crumbs from the table after every dough; and after finishing the kneading they should sweep the place.
