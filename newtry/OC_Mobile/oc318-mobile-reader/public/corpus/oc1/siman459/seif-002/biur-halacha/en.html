And if he left it, etc., it is chametz - And even if there is a doubt whether perhaps it sat idle for the measure of a mil, it is also forbidden, for it is a doubt according to the Torah [Peri Megadim].
It is a quarter of an hour, etc. - See Mishnah Berurah, that there are poskim who dispute this measure, and according to them the measure of a mil is a third of an hour and a fifteenth of an hour (see Magen Avraham, Peri Chadash, Chok Yaakov, and Beur HaGra). And some of this school of thought (see Beur HaGra, and according to our reading in the Gemara, and see Sefer Seder Zemanim) reckon the measure of a mil as twenty-two and a half minutes. And l'chatchilah, where there is no major loss, from when it sat idle eighteen minutes it is chametz and forbidden in benefit, like the view of the Shulchan Aruch; and so wrote the Alter Rebbe. However, where there is major loss, perhaps one may rely on those poskim who disagree, and so long as it did not sit idle at least twenty-two and a half minutes one should not forbid, unless we saw in it signs of si'ur and cracking. And see Sefer Seder Zemanim, who wrote that also regarding salting one should be stringent even b'dieved and forbid so long as it has not sat idle for this measure.
And after they worked with it, etc., it will immediately become chametz - The source of this law is from the responsa of the Rosh; and similarly wrote R' Eliezer of Metz and the Mordechai regarding kneading in a warm house or beside the mouth of the oven; and likewise Agudah brought in the name of the Geonim regarding a house in which there is an oven; see Beis Yosef and Taz. And according to this reason the Rosh also wrote regarding dough after they worked with it, that it too is warm because of the abundant work on it, that it likewise becomes chametz immediately; and he holds that in this [case] the Sages did not set a measure of a mil. And the measure of a mil stated in the Gemara is regarding dough after its kneading, before they rolled it out; for the kneading itself does not warm the dough much according to all opinions [see Pesachim folio 7a, and Rabbeinu Manoach, chapter 3 of Hilchos Chametz, law 10; see there carefully]. Now although regarding the essential reasoning of R' Eliezer of Metz and the Mordechai we do not find anyone who disagrees with them, nevertheless regarding the law of dough after they worked with it, which the Rosh adopted, we find many of the poskim who disagree with him. See Rambam, chapter 5 of Hilchos Chametz U'Matzah, who wrote, and this is his language: So long as a person is working with the dough, even all day, etc.; and if he lifted his hand, etc., if it sat idle for the walking of a mil, etc., see there: thus even after he worked with it all day and then left it, it also does not become chametz except after the measure of a mil. And so it is clearly proven in the responsa of the Rashba, part 1, siman 124, what he wrote in the name of the Ramban [and it is brought in the later authorities], that he would bring the matzos to an oven that was several houses distant; and although in the end he retracted, that was because he feared that [the dough] might be delayed up to the measure of a mil, as is explicit there. And I found that Darkei Moshe already remarked on this; and he further wondered at the owner of Terumat HaDeshen regarding idle periods combining, whereas according to the Rosh's reasoning even without combining, from the first idle period it was already forbidden [and all the more so one should not distinguish, according to his reasoning, between much work on the dough and little work; on the contrary, the more one works with the dough, the closer it is afterward to become chametz with a short idle period before he begins to work with it again, according to the Rosh]. And he left it as requiring further study. And it is astonishing that the later authorities did not bring the words of Darkei Moshe on this; and also on Darkei Moshe himself it is astonishing that he copied the Terumat HaDeshen in the gloss, and it is a contradiction from itself to the Rosh's [ruling] that the Mechaber brought immediately after this, unless we say that Darkei Moshe retracted and held that the Rosh is dealing where we see that the dough is warm, and in such a manner perhaps all agree. And according to this, the Mechaber's law does not apply to ordinary doughs, but only where we see explicitly that it became warm; and nevertheless this requires further study.
