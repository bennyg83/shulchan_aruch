And the measure of a mil, etc. So wrote Terumat HaDeshen according to the reckoning of ten parasangs in a day. But it is very astonishing, and a very great error: for we say in the chapter "One who was [impure]" that ten parasangs are from dawn until nightfall; and if so, twelve hours in an average day according to him are from dawn until nightfall. And it is a great error, for all the astronomers have written that at the tekufah of Nisan [and] the tekufah of Tishrei, which are average days, there are twelve hours from sunrise to sunset, and from dawn until nightfall is fifteen hours. And it is also known that the heat of the summer days equals the coolness of the rainy season, and vice versa, as is written in the Midrash Rabbah, "they borrow from one another," etc.; and that is from sunrise until sunset. But from dawn until nightfall it is more than six hours beyond the nights; and all the more so in latitudes that incline far north, and likewise on the equator, where all days and nights are equal only from sunrise until sunset, but from dawn until nightfall the day is much longer than the night. And there are still several powerful difficulties, and I wearied of writing them. And the essential [view] is like those who say that the reckoning is from sunrise until sunset, and one should not budge from it; and see Magen Avraham. And so wrote the Rambam in his commentary on chapter 3 of Pesachim, Mishnah 2, that the measure of a mil is two-fifths of an hour; and so wrote Bartenura there. And the Rambam follows his view that he ruled in chapter 5 of Hilchos Kiddush HaChodesh like Ulla; but that is astonishing, for Ulla was refuted, and if so the measure of a mil is three-eighths. And perhaps he had a different reading there, that he was not refuted. But in his commentary on Mishnah 1 of chapter 1 of Berachos he wrote that from dawn until sunrise is an hour and a fifth, which is like R' Yehuda; and there is another contradiction, that there he holds that twelve hours are from dawn until nightfall. And you have nothing but as I wrote: the reckoning from sunrise until sunset is twelve hours, and a mil is three-eighths, and from dawn until sunrise is an hour and a half. But this measure from dawn until sunrise is not equal in every latitude and also not in every season; and also the reckoning of seasonal hours in every place is only from sunrise until sunset, and those are called hours; for the hours of which they spoke were certainly measured by an average day, which are called twenty-four hours for day and night. And I already said that twelve hours in an average day is from sunrise until sunset. And although they said that we divide every day into twelve hours and likewise the night, nevertheless they did not speak of those hours, but rather we divide thus; rather as I wrote. And even regarding the Shema it is so; and not like Magen Avraham at the beginning of siman 58, who wrote: it appears to me that here according to all we count from dawn, and he brought proof from Tosafos 3a, s.v. "leman," etc. And his proof regarding the Shema is worthless from there; but the proof from there is that the reckoning of three night watches is until dawn. And in truth, Tosafos seem to hold that the reckoning is from dawn, as they wrote in Pesachim 11b, s.v. "echad," etc., and this comes out to an hour and a half, etc. But I have already refuted [this]; and besides, Tosafos there require further study, for they did not state the truth that it is only four milin. Rather the essential [view] appears that Tosafos too hold that the reckoning of twelve hours is from sunrise until sunset; rather there is a scribal error in Tosafos there, "five milin," and one must say "four milin." An erring student corrected Tosafos to "five milin" because he thought Tosafos's view is like the Rambam's view that four milin are an hour and a fifth according to the reckoning of a person's walking, etc., and Tosafos wrote "an hour and a half," and therefore he corrected Tosafos to "five milin," which is an eighth of ten parasangs, and an eighth of an average day is an hour and a half. And this continued afterward in Tosafos at the beginning of Berachos, s.v. "bi'as oro," etc. But it is an error, as above, for it is against the conclusion of the Gemara mentioned above; rather one must say "four milin," and so it is in Tosafos of Shabbos 35a, s.v. "telata": four milin, etc.; and so it is in Tosafos of Pesachim 94a, s.v. "R' Yehuda": four milin, etc. And Tosafos hold that what is written there, "a person walks ten," etc., means from dawn until nightfall, as is written there. And those four milin from dawn until sunrise and from sunset until nightfall are not in the reckoning of the twelve hours according to their view, for we reckon from sunrise until sunset, as above. And according to this, from sunrise until sunset there remains a walk of eight parasangs for the twelve hours mentioned above; and if so, the measure of a parasang comes out to an hour and a half. And according to this, what Tosafos wrote is fine: that from dawn until sunrise is four milin and comes out to an hour and a half, as above. And the Rambam's view also appears that he certainly holds that the reckoning of twelve hours is from sunrise until sunset, as above. Rather, what he wrote that from dawn until sunrise is an hour and a fifth is because he interprets the sugya of the chapter "One who was" not like Tosafos, but rather that R' Yehuda, who holds that a person walks ten parasangs, means from sunrise until sunset; and likewise the reckoning of the twelve hours of walking, etc., is also from sunrise until sunset; only the four milin, both of sunrise and of sunset, are besides the ten parasangs that a person walks, etc. And the Gemara there raises two difficulties: one, that Rava and Ulla there said that from dawn until sunrise and from sunset until nightfall are five milin, and R' Yehuda said four milin; and the second, that they said it is one-sixth of the day and they also reckon what a person walks before sunrise and after sunset among the forty milin, while R' Yehuda does not reckon [thus], since he said one-tenth of the day. And what R' Yehuda said to him, "I [reckon] by day," etc., means: what [is said], that a person walks, etc., means by day, which is from sunrise until sunset; and the Rabbis, etc., for they erred and also reckoned what a person walks before sunrise and after sunset in the reckoning of the ten parasangs. And according to this the language of the Gemara there is precise: R' Yehuda said to him, "I [reckon] by day," etc., and the Rabbis who reckon the early and the dark, etc.; and likewise the language of the Beraisa of R' Yehuda, "one-tenth of the day," is well parallel to what is written there, "it is found that the thickness," etc., "one-sixth of the day," which refers to from sunrise until sunset, as above; whereas according to Tosafos's view the Beraisa of R' Yehuda should have said "one-eighth of the day," and likewise according to their view the Gemara's language "R' Yehuda said to him... I... for they reckon" is not precise. And all this compelled the Rambam to interpret as above. And proof for the Rambam's view is from the Yerushalmi at the beginning of Berachos, and this is its language there: as it was taught in the name of R' Yehuda: the thickness of the firmament is a walk of fifty years; an average person walks four milin; the sun walks five hundred years; until the sun cuts through the firmament is a walk of fifty years; a person walks four milin; you find that the thickness of the firmament is one-tenth of the day, etc., see there: thus it is explicit that the four milin are besides the ten parasangs, as above. And according to this everything is fine. And also the proof that Magen Avraham brought from what is written there, "two of the night and two of the day," is according to what Tosafos wrote, that the watches end at dawn; but according to what I wrote, that it is a famous falsehood that the six hours end on an average day at sunrise, as above, the opposite is proven: that we reckon from sunrise. And also Tosafos's difficulty mentioned above, s.v. "leman," is thereby answered: that R' Eliezer follows his own reason, that he said one completes it with sunrise; for that indication is not for the diligent but for the lazy, as is written, "the door turns," etc., this is one who lies in the last watch. And also for R' Yehuda there is a practical difference for the meticulous and for the rest of the people, etc.; and even though its time is from dawn, nevertheless the measure of the hours the Gemara reckons in the manner of all hours. And likewise everywhere, even though its time is until nightfall, nevertheless the reckoning of the hours is until sunset, like "plag haminchah" and the like. And likewise what is written, "the first hour is food," etc., is also from sunrise, not like Magen Avraham. And what is written in Taanis, s.v. "for so we find regarding Achav," etc., even though Rashi explained that we reckon from when they rise, that is not so; rather that is the way of kings, as is written in Pesachim 107b, "even Agrippas," etc. And Tosafos already refuted Rashi's explanation there, s.v. "that it was," etc.; and it is proven that the nine hours of the kings are not as Rashi explained, that they are not like six hours for us, as Tosafos wrote there.
