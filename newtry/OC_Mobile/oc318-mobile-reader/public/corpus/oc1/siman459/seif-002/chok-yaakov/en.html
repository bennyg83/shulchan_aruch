And the measure of a mil is a quarter of an hour and a twentieth of an hour. So wrote Terumat HaDeshen, siman 223, and Mahariv, siman 193: that is, according to what we say in the chapter "One who was impure," an average person walks ten parasangs in a day; and his intent, since in every parasang there are four milin, ten parasangs are forty milin; and when you divide ten hours into forty parts, each mil gets a quarter of an hour; and afterward divide the two hours, each hour into twenty parts, so each mil gets a further twentieth of an hour. Or go by this path: divide forty mil into eight parts, and afterward divide the twelve hours of the day into eight parts, so that for each hour and a half there are five milin; and in an hour and a half there are six quarters of an hour, so each mil gets a quarter of an hour and a fifth of the remaining quarter-hour, which is a twentieth. And regarding salting it is clarified in Yoreh De'ah, siman 69, and it too is from Terumat HaDeshen, siman 160, that the measure of salting is like the measure of a mil, namely a third of an hour minus a thirtieth of an hour; and certainly both are one measure: for five milin to an hour and a half, and in an hour and a half there are four thirds of an hour and half a third. And if there were five full thirds, each mil would get a third of an hour; and now that half a third is lacking, which is a sixth of an hour, and if you divide that part among five milin, a thirtieth of an hour is lacking when you divide a sixth of an hour into five parts; and this is sufficient. Or go by this path: if you divide twelve hours among forty parts, each part gets a third of an hour; but still four thirds are lacking, for in twelve hours there are only thirty-six thirds; and when you lack and divide four thirds of an hour among forty parts, you lack from every ten mil one third of an hour; and when you divide a third of an hour into ten parts, you lack from each part a thirtieth of an hour; so the reckoning is exact and precise here and there. And it appears to me that for this reason he used a different language there: regarding leaving in salt he used "a third of an hour minus a thirtieth," so as not to be lenient regarding salting if one does not know how to be so exact and leave it a third of an hour by estimate to be stringent; and here he used "a quarter," so as to be stringent if one does not know how to be exact, that if he left without work a quarter of an hour it became chametz: all this is correct and clear according to Terumat HaDeshen and his company, and after him the later authorities followed. However, I saw that the Rambam and Bartenura wrote in the Mishnah of "These pass" that the measure of a mil is two-fifths of an hour of equal hours, end quote (meaning all hours are equal, each a twenty-fourth of day and night, not seasonal hours); and that is a third of an hour and a fifteenth of an hour, for when you divide the hour into fifteen parts, two-fifths of them are six parts, so it is a third of an hour and a fifteenth of an hour. And it appears that according to their view Maharil meant what he wrote, that it is a third of an hour and a bit more, for that is like the Rambam's view. But the matter is astonishing regarding such great ones, that they did not aim the reckoning according to the sugya of the Talmud that an average person walks ten parasangs in a day, as I wrote; at any rate the reckoning I wrote is a little less than a third of an hour. And the later authorities already noticed this astonishment: Maggid Mishneh, first chapter of Berachos, folio 7a; and Tiferes Yisrael, chapter 13 of Pesachim, Mishnah 2; and Chut HaShani at the end of the sefer in the accounting pamphlet. And Maggid Mishneh there answered that the Rambam is of the school who hold that the day is reckoned from sunrise to sunset, like the view of the Levush in siman 233 and siman 266; and when you deduct ten milin that Ulla said there in the Gemara an average person walks, namely five milin from dawn until sunrise and five milin from sunset until nightfall, thirty milin remain from sunrise until sunset, the twelve hours being reckoned thus according to his view; so the reckoning comes out to two-fifths, for there are six fifths in twelve hours, each hour into five parts, so each mil gets two-fifths. And one must say that the Rambam ruled like Ulla; for according to R' Yehuda, who holds that before sunrise and after sunset they walk only eight mil, thirty-two milin remain from sunrise until sunset, and then each mil does not get two-fifths, end quote of the content of his words there, though he wrote at length, see there. Now what he wrote, that the Rambam ruled like Ulla, his words are correct; and even though in the Talmud they left his words as a refutation, as he explained the matter there at length, and so it appears from Tosafos of the chapter "They would examine" (Sanhedrin folio 41) and Pesachim 11, and from Ra'am in Sefer Yere'im, and from Rashi at the beginning of Berachos. But what he wrote, that the Rambam's view is like the school who hold that the day is reckoned from sunrise to sunset: that is a great stretch, as Chut HaShani at the end of the responsa argued against this at length; and furthermore, on the contrary the Rambam's view is the opposite in several places in the commentary on the Mishnah and in his sefer Yad HaChazakah, as Menachat Kohen discussed at length in Chelek Mavo HaShemesh, maamar 1, chapter 2, see there. And in truth I have not fathomed the view of Maggid Mishneh and Responsa Chut HaShani and Magen Avraham in s.k. 3 who followed him and wrote that this depends on the dispute whether the day is reckoned from sunrise or from dawn. And anyone who examines and is precise in the sugya of the Talmud will see explicitly that the reckoning of the Rambam and Bartenura is a true reckoning according to the sugya, and the later authorities erred in this according to my humble opinion. And I will present the language of the Talmud and Rashi's explanation there on folio 93: we learn, Ulla said: from Modi'in to Jerusalem is fifteen milin (Rashi: since the Mishnah said regarding the Pesach offering, what is a distant journey: from Modi'in outward, that one cannot reach there from the time of slaughtering the Pesach, which is midday, until sunset). He holds like the statement of Rabbah bar bar Chanah who said in the name of R' Yochanan: how much does an average person walk in a day? Ten parasangs; from dawn until sunrise five milin; from sunset until nightfall five milin; thirty remain: fifteen from morning to midday and fifteen from midday to evening (Rashi: an average person walks on an average day of the tekufah of Nisan and Tishrei when day and night are equal). Thus it is clear explicitly that on the days of Nisan and Tishrei when days and nights are equal, an average person walks from sunrise until sunset thirty milin; and this is a peg that will not budge: for what we say that on the days of Nisan and Tishrei day and night are equal means specifically from sunrise until sunset, for from dawn until nightfall the day will be longer than the night by some hours, as the matter is visible to the eye by proof and by sense; and so it is in the Yerushalmi at the beginning of Berachos; and so Menachat Kohen discussed at length in Chelek Mavo HaShemesh, maamar 1, chapter 4, and that such is according to astronomy and nature, see there in maamar 1, chapter 11 at the end; and it is simple. And accordingly the Rambam's view aligns with the sugya of the Talmud and is precise according to the reckoning: for it is clear from this sugya that on the days of Nisan the day from sunrise until sunset, which is twelve equal hours, one can walk thirty mil, so each mil gets two-fifths of an hour. But if there is a difficulty upon the Rambam, it is this: since he ruled like Ulla, it turns out that from dawn until sunrise one can walk five milin, which is two hours according to the Rambam's reckoning; yet at the beginning of Berachos in the commentary on the Mishnah he wrote that it is only an hour and a fifth of an hour; and Responsa Chut HaShani similarly wonders at him there, see there. And one may say that the Rambam holds that the dispute of R' Yehuda and Ulla at first glance is difficult: how do they dispute with opposite reasonings against reality, that R' Yehuda holds that from sunrise until sunset they walk thirty-two milin, which proves he holds they walk faster than Ulla who holds they walk only thirty milin; and regarding before sunrise and after sunset R' Yehuda would hold they walk only four milin while Ulla holds five milin. Therefore the Rambam interprets that Ulla too holds one cannot walk five milin, and that he said five milin is according to those who rise early and stay late; and that he said "dawn" is only a fixed time, but in truth they rise early and stay late as R' Yehuda said there; and there are further ways to distinguish, see Menachat Kohen in Mavo HaShemesh. And he disagrees with R' Yehuda only regarding the walk from sunrise until sunset: according to R' Yehuda one can walk thirty-two milin and according to Ulla thirty milin. So the reckoning of the Rambam and Bartenura who ruled like Ulla is precise according to the sugya: two-fifths of an hour, namely a third of an hour and a fifteenth of an hour; and this is Maharil's view who wrote "a third of an hour and a bit more." And that he did not use exactly the Rambam's language "two-fifths of an hour" is perhaps because he is concerned for the view of poskim like R' Yehuda that an average person walks in twelve hours thirty-two milin; and see Menachat Kohen, who also wrote that many poskim hold like R' Yehuda. And if so, when you divide thirty-two milin among twelve hours of the day, for an hour and a half there are four milin; and in an hour and a half there are four thirds of an hour and half a third, so each mil gets a third of an hour; and afterward divide half a third, which is a sixth of an hour, into four parts, so it is a twenty-fourth of an hour; so each mil gets a third of an hour and a twenty-fourth of an hour. Therefore Maharil wrote simply that it is a bit more than a third of an hour and did not wish to write exactly in the Rambam's language. And however it may be, at any rate it is proven explicitly from what I wrote that the measure of a mil, both according to Ulla and according to R' Yehuda, is more than a third of an hour, both to be lenient as here regarding chametz and to be stringent regarding salting; and the view of the author of Terumat HaDeshen and Maharil, after whom the later authorities followed, cannot be settled at all according to the sugya (and it is even more astonishing regarding Menachat Kohen, that even though he saw and knew that on the days of Nisan and Tishrei when day and night are equal means from sunrise until sunset, as I wrote above in his name, even so he followed Terumat HaDeshen in this in maamar 8, chapter 1, see there; and this requires further study), unless we wish to say that Terumat HaDeshen stated his measure regarding seasonal hours that exist on the days of Nisan, namely from dawn until nightfall that time divided into twelve hours, and if so the reckoning also aligns with the Rambam's view in equal hours; but the plain language of Terumat HaDeshen here, and all the more so regarding salting, and all the other later authorities, does not so imply, and their words do not work at all according to the sugya. And regarding the Rambam we say that Moshe spoke well; and blessed is Hashem who guided me on the true path, and they left me room to elaborate on this, and I know a mere reckoning. And if one says, what need is there for all this length: let us see regarding an average person in what time he walks a mil: Tosafos already noticed such a difficulty in Shabbos folio 35a and answered that they were uncertain what is an average person; see there in Tosafos, and examine carefully. And a twentieth of an hour, etc. And this measure was stated only in a house that has no fire; but if there is fire in it, or any excess of warmth, it hastens to become chametz, and one must give heed according to the matter (Ateres Zekenim).
To hurry in the matter of making the matzos. And Shiltei Giborim wrote in siman 59: and one should not leave idle from the beginning of its kneading until he places it in the oven for the measure of four milin (and that is according to the reckoning I wrote in s.k. 10: an hour and a half according to R' Yehuda; and according to the Rambam it is an hour and a half and a tenth of an hour, for here there are eight fifths, thus an hour and two-fifths, and half is half an hour and half a fifth is a tenth of an hour); and so it is in the Yerushalmi, end quote. And so wrote the Bach in the name of Riaz, who further concluded: and it appears that even though the woman did not knead more than the measure and did not lift her hand from the dough at all, but rather was making very thin wafers and was lazy with them for the walking of four milin, this dough is forbidden even though no sign of chimutz is recognizable in them, end quote. And the Bach wrote, and this is his language: and this is not like the view of the Rambam, Rashba, our master, and other great ones, that so long as they are working with it, even all day, it does not become chametz; and at any rate he is dealing with kneading the measure of a dough and not more; and therefore it is not proper to rule prohibition for others b'dieved, but only to warn l'chatchilah to be concerned for the words of the greatest among the later authorities; and one who is stringent for himself even b'dieved, may blessing come upon him: it is proper to be concerned for his words, especially since Riaz is not alone in this law; and all the more so now that they are accustomed to knead doughs larger than the measure, as I wrote above at the end of siman 456. But b'dieved one should not rule prohibition against all those great ones, even if he kneaded more than the measure, for we do not find that they distinguished thus; and see above, siman 456.
That the idle periods combine. And Terumat HaDeshen wrote that working the hands and rolling with which they roll cancels the chimutz; but regarding piercing with which they pierce the matzos, even though it is work so that it not become chametz, nevertheless it does not cancel the idle periods like real work, end quote. And so is the consensus of the later authorities; and they further wrote in the name of Mahariv that one should not pass his hands over the matzos and rub them (and Kenesses HaGedolah wrote: and all the more so they do not do well when the multitude sticks a knife or a stick into the dough). And they further wrote in the name of Sefer Zichronos: and one should not begin to shape and roll the matzah until they sweep the oven, end quote. And it appears that this is not specifically all the oven's scrapings: for if he sweeps the whole oven and afterward begins to shape and roll the matzos and also pierce, through this the oven will sometimes cool greatly; rather one should not begin to shape until he begins to sweep, so that he can bake immediately; and so I have seen practiced in the house of the Gaon, my teacher's teacher. Also at first one should not begin to knead until some time after the oven was heated, so that immediately after kneading he can sweep the oven and begin to shape the matzos and bake immediately; and see what I wrote above, s.k. 5 and s.k. 6. And it is proper to warn the multitude about all such; and we do not say here "better that they be unintentional," since one can come to a prohibition according to the Torah, as the later authorities wrote. Nevertheless b'dieved there is no prohibition so long as it did not sit idle for the measure of chametz; and l'chatchilah one should be careful.

Meaning of "whitened" is like "the upper whitened and not the lower" in the chapter "With what may one light"; and see Aruch, entry "kesef"; and it is a middle line between blackness and whiteness, which is the beginning of blackness, like green that they call in Ashkenaz "bleichlich"; and this is like a person whose hair stood from fear and trembling and whose appearance changed; and this is forbidden, but we are not expert in this measure (and see above in Hilchos Shabbos, siman 261); therefore one should not forbid afterward unless it sat idle for the measure of a mil without work.
