(11) They should not leave the dough - That is to say, after its kneading, before he began to shape it, even though it has not yet been warmed by much handling with the hands.
(12) Without work - And "work" is called kneading it or rolling it with a stick and the like; but not like those who are accustomed to press and poke the dough with a stick in one place, for that does not help to prevent chimutz throughout the entire dough in the place where the stick is not pressing [later authorities].
(13) And even one moment - And even if he is not busy with something else, only with the needs of the oven, it is also forbidden l'chatchilah; and one must heat the oven and sweep it beforehand so that he not need to leave the matzos without work, as is clarified below.
(14) It is chametz - Even though no signs of chimutz clarified below are recognizable in it.
(15) It is a quarter of an hour, etc. - And that is eighteen minutes altogether. And see Biur Halacha regarding salting, whose measure is also the walking of a mil: b'dieved one should not be lenient unless it sat idle at least twenty-four or twenty-three minutes; and l'chatchilah its measure is about an hour's measure, and one should not change.
(16) For there is concern that the idle periods, etc. - And Terumat HaDeshen wrote that when one works with the dough real work, namely working the hands and rolling, even if he sat idle a little in between and again sat idle a little, it does not combine to the measure of a mil, for the work he does afterward cancels the beginning that started to awaken the force of chimutz while it lay without work. But when he works with it little work, such as when piercing the matzos, even though that too is called work in that it does not allow it to become chametz at that time, nevertheless it does not cancel the first idle period; and if he sits idle further it will combine to the measure of a mil and be forbidden. And Maharil's view is to be stringent in every case.
(17) That hastens to become chametz - For the measure of a mil was stated only regarding ordinary houses that have no great heat; but where there is extra warmth it hastens to become chametz in less than this measure.
(18) It will immediately become chametz - And according to this one must be very careful after they shaped and rolled the matzah and leave it before the piercer, that he pierce it immediately; and also after piercing they should see to take it at once to the oven, since after they worked with it it becomes chametz immediately when they remove their hands from it. And the world is not so careful about this. And perhaps "immediately" that the Mechaber said is not literal, but rather means a short measure; and nevertheless l'chatchilah one should certainly be careful not to leave it so even for one moment if possible, as above at the beginning of the se'if. And see what I wrote in Biur Halacha.
(19) Even if one does not see, etc. - And even if it did not sit idle for the measure of a mil, even so we say that presumably there was some warmth there that caused it to become chametz quickly.
(20) Until there are cracks in it - And even if there are cracks in part of the dough, the entire dough is chametz; and likewise below regarding "its face whitened," the same applies.
(21) One who eats it is exempt - But it is forbidden for eating and benefit. And whether there is a prohibition according to the Torah in this, or only a rabbinic prohibition, is clarified above in siman 442, se'if 1. And the later authorities wrote that one must be precise about this measure, for it is common that its face whitened and no one takes it to heart to notice.
(22) It is permitted to break it, etc. - But it does not help to pass his hands over the matzos and rub, for that is not called work; and on the contrary it is worse, for he warms it with his hands.
(23) It is good to be careful l'chatchilah - Lest when he fixes it a second time he not shape it very well, and it not be one lump stuck together as at first, but there be cracks and folds in it; and a doubled matzah we are accustomed to forbid because the fire does not take hold there, as will be clarified in siman 461. And therefore one should not begin to shape and roll the matzos until he first sweeps the oven, so that the matzos not lie without work until he prepares the oven.
