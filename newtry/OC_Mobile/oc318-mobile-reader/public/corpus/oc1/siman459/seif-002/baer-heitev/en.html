And so wrote the Bach in the name of Riaz, and he concluded: and it appears that even though the woman did not knead more than the measure and did not lift her hand from the dough at all, but rather was making very thin wafers and was lazy with them for the walking of four milin, this dough is forbidden even though no sign of chimutz is recognizable; and this is not like the view of the Rambam, the Rashba, and our master, that so long as they are working with it, even all day, it does not become chametz. And therefore it is not proper to rule prohibition for others b'dieved, but only to warn l'chatchilah; and one who is stringent for himself even b'dieved, may blessing come upon him, end quote. A quarter. That is, according to what we say, an average person walks ten parasangs in a day, and each parasang is four milin, so ten parasangs are forty milin; when you divide ten hours into forty parts, each mil gets a quarter of an hour; and afterward divide the two hours, each hour into twenty parts, so each mil gets a further twentieth of an hour. And for those who reckon from sunrise to sunset, it is two-fifths of an hour; and so wrote Maharil. And see Chok Yaakov, who discussed this matter at length.
They combine. And Terumat HaDeshen wrote that working the hands [into the dough] and rolling with which they roll cancels the chimutz; but regarding piercing with which they pierce the matzos, even though it is work so that it not become chametz, nevertheless it does not cancel the idle periods like real work; see the later authorities. And one should not pass his hands over the matzos and rub them. And Kenesses HaGedolah wrote: and all the more so they do not do well when the multitude sticks a knife or a stick into the dough and leaves it without work. Also those who press their hands hard on the matzos are not called [doing] work, and it is worse. And Sefer Zichronos wrote: and one should not begin to shape and roll the matzos until they first sweep the oven, so that he can bake immediately and not leave even one moment idle. And see Chok Yaakov (who wrote explicitly that everything is according to the abundance of the oven's heat). And because of our many sins, most of Israel, even the learners, are not careful about this; rather they leave several doughs on the table before beginning to sweep the oven, and it is a criminal iniquity.
