4) There. On a cloudy day it is forbidden, etc. On a day that is not cloudy, one may knead and smooth the dough outdoors in the shade, provided that the location is not so near the sunlight that the dough will become warm. Beis Yosef in the name of Rabbeinu Yerucham. Darkei Moshe, subsection 1, comments that even Terumas HaDeshen, who writes in siman 122 that the custom is not to knead beneath a window, may agree here. Since one kneads in the shade, there is no concern about a cloudy day; the shade itself provides a recognizable indication that there is no sun. See there. Magen Avraham, subsection 1; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 4; Chok Yaakov, subsection 1; Chok Yosef, subsection 1; Chemed Moshe, subsection 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1, write likewise. Chok Yaakov writes there that the Talmud and authorities explain that there is no concern unless the dough is exposed to actual sunlight; merely being near sunlight is not a concern, contrary to Rosh Yosef's uncertainty. See there. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 2, and Ma'amar Mordechai, subsection 1, however, challenge him from the words of Rabbeinu Yerucham cited above. Yad Aharon in Hagahos HaTur likewise writes that Rosh Yosef's ruling is clear. Mishbetzos Zahav, subsection 1, writes that whenever the shade is near sunlight, the shade itself is warm and will warm the dough; only a distant shaded place is permitted. These are his words.
