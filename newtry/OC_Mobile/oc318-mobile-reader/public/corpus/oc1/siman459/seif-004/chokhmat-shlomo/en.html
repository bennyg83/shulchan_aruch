Seif 4 in the gloss: and one must be careful in the kneading trough that there be no dent or crack that one cannot scrape out after kneading, lest it chametz. Marginal note: behold, if b'dieved one kneaded in it, whether it is forbidden or not, is not explained in the Acharonim. From the words of Kaf HaChayim in siman 451 s.k. 52 in what he wrote that if one transgressed and worked on the sheet without turning it over, if it is during Pesach, even b'dieved one must forbid — it is proven there that likewise if there was a crack or dent in the kneading trough, the law is likewise that during Pesach it is forbidden even b'dieved, and before Pesach one does not forbid b'dieved. However, it is possible to distinguish: Kaf HaChayim deals perhaps with one who did not interrupt the measure of a mil without dealing with this, even if a little chametz was on it, since they spread on it each time and engage with it — if so, it was without staying a mil without engagement, therefore one need not be concerned b'dieved before Pesach; but in a basin and kneading trough, as is customary to change the basin from time to time and rinse it and it stands a while empty until it dries from the water — in this, since it stands a long time empty, it becomes actual chametz, and even before Pesach one must forbid. Furthermore, one should not compare to a sheet whose holes are not so clear, but with clear holes one must forbid even before Pesach. Furthermore, it appears Kaf HaChayim deals with a matter where chametz is not clearly visible on it; but if chametz is visible on the sheet or in the kneading trough, actual chametz is found in its holes — here, since there is certainly chametz, in doubt whether a crumb mixed in, and in such doubt one may say even before Pesach it is forbidden, as Rama implies in siman 447 that even for doubtful mixtures of the smallest amount we are concerned, and even before Pesach we are concerned for mixtures of the smallest amount in doubt; and as implied also in siman 446 regarding flour on which water fell that if it dried, shaking does not help, for we are concerned perhaps something mixed in, as implied in the poskim that it is only doubt whether something mixed there, and nevertheless forbidden even before Pesach; therefore it would seem if actual chametz is found in a hole, even before Pesach one must forbid. However, one may distinguish that one may say in a small hole that one can scrape with a nail, even through kneading the dough would not come out of it — this is not a common matter that dough comes out of the hole, and for an uncommon matter one need not be concerned from doubt. Nevertheless, from Rama's language who wrote and afterward when one returns and kneads in it it mixes into the dough — it implies that presumptively it is so and it is common to mix — if so, it is very similar to those laws above that we are concerned for doubt of the smallest amount; therefore it appears that not in a pressing situation one should forbid the matzot, and in a pressing situation or great loss one may permit before Pesach, and one may rely on She'eilot when chametz is nullified in sixty, and even though Kaf HaChayim wrote in siman 447 we do not rely on She'eilot except with many sides to be lenient — one may say here, since one may say dry in dry is nullified on Pesach, we do not hold thus, nevertheless there are two sides to be lenient: perhaps dry in dry is nullified, for here it is dry in dry before Pesach at kneading time, behold nullified in sixty, and on Pesach it is already dry in dry — if so, perhaps nullified in majority and perhaps nothing mixed at all; and since there are two sides to be lenient, we rely on She'eilot that it is in sixty. Therefore it appears this is only to eat them dry; but to throw them in water and sauce or make doughnuts from them, since it becomes dry in wet, all agree it returns and requires shaking, and flour ground after baking certainly does not have the law of wet in wet, as Chak Yosef wrote — if so, there is only one side to be lenient that perhaps nothing mixed in, and on one side we do not rely on She'eilot, and especially since this is not a pressing situation, for it is possible without loss — so it appears to me correct and clear for practical halachah; from this we learn all similar cases — study well.
