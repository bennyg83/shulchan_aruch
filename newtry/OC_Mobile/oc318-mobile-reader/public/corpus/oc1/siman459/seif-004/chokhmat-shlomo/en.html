Se'if 4 in the gloss: And one should be careful with the basin in which they knead that there not be any depression or crack from which he cannot scrape after the kneading, and it will become chametz. Note: Now, if b'dieved he kneaded in this, whether it is forbidden or not was not clarified in the later authorities. And from the words of Chok Yaakov in siman 451, s.k. 52, in what he wrote that if he transgressed and made [matzos] on the sheet without turning it over, if it is during Pesach even b'dieved one should forbid: it is proven there that the same law applies if there was a crack or depression in the basin: during Pesach it is forbidden even b'dieved, and before Pesach one should not forbid b'dieved. However, one may distinguish: that Chok Yaakov is dealing with a case where perhaps he did not interrupt for the measure of a mil without work on this, even if there was a little chametz on it, since they shape upon it each time and work with it; if so, it was without sitting idle for the measure of a mil without work, and therefore there is no concern b'dieved before Pesach. But regarding a bowl and basin, as people are accustomed to switch the bowl from time to time and wash it, and it stands empty for some time until it dries from the water: in this, since it stands empty a long time, it becomes real chametz, and even before Pesach one should forbid. And furthermore one should not compare a sheet, whose holes are not so clear in it; but with clear holes one should forbid even before Pesach. And furthermore it appears that Chok Yaakov is dealing with a matter in which chametz is not clearly visible to the eye upon it; but if chametz is visible to the eye on the sheet, or in a basin real chametz is found in its holes: in this, since there is definite chametz here, and a doubt whether one crumb mixed into it, and regarding a doubt like this one may say that even before Pesach it is forbidden, as is implied in the Rama, siman 447, that even for a doubt of a mixture of a bit we are concerned, and even before Pesach we are concerned for a mixture of a bit and for a doubt; and as is also implied in siman 446 regarding flour upon which water fell, that if it dried, sifting does not help, for we are concerned lest a bit mixed in; and as is implied there in the poskim that it is only a doubt whether a bit mixed there, and even so it is forbidden even before Pesach. And therefore it would appear that if real chametz is found in a hole, even before Pesach one should forbid. However, one may distinguish and say that with a small hole from which one can easily scrape, even through the kneading the dough will not come out of it, and it is not an uncommon matter that dough come out of the hole; and for an uncommon matter we are not concerned from doubt. However, from the language of the Rama who wrote "and afterward when he returns and kneads with it, it mixes into the dough," it is implied that ordinarily it is so and it is common to mix; if so, it is exactly comparable to those laws above, that we are concerned for a doubt of a bit. And therefore it appears that not in a time of pressure one should forbid the matzos; and in a time of pressure or major loss one may permit before Pesach, and one may rely on the She'iltos that chametz is nullified in sixty. And even though Chok Yaakov wrote in siman 447 that we rely on the She'iltos only where there are many sides to be lenient, one may say here that since some say that dry [mixed] with dry is nullified on Pesach (though we do not so hold), nevertheless there are two sides to be lenient: perhaps dry with dry is nullified, for here it is dry with dry, since before Pesach at the time of kneading it was already nullified in sixty, and on Pesach it is already dry with dry; and if so, perhaps it is nullified in a majority, and perhaps it did not mix into it at all; and since there are two sides to be lenient, we rely on the She'iltos that it is [nullified] in sixty. And therefore it appears that this is specifically to eat them dry; but to cast them into water and gravy or to make soaked fritters from them: in this, since it will be dry in moist, according to all opinions it revives and is stirred [to reunite], and flour ground after baking certainly does not have the law of moist in moist, as Chok Yosef wrote; if so, there is here only one side to be lenient, that perhaps a bit did not mix into it, and with one side we do not rely on the She'iltos; and especially since in this it is not a time of pressure, for one can manage without this. So it appears to my humble opinion correct and clear for the law; and from this we learn for every such case; and examine carefully.
