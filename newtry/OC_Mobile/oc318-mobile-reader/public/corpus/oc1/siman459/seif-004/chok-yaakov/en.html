After the time of the chametz prohibition. And Beis Yosef wrote: but before the time of the chametz prohibition one pours even in a place of standing water; and the Taz disputed him because he follows his view in siman 445, where he finds difficulty between the words of the Shulchan Aruch and siman 433 against each other; but I already clarified above that there is no difficulty. And see Magen Avraham, who wrote, and this is his language: and on a stone floor even a place of a slope is forbidden.
He should scrape them continually at the time of production. And Magen Avraham wrote, and this is his language: not like the practice of the world, who do not scrape them until after production; for even though they do not sit idle for the measure of a mil, nevertheless l'chatchilah it is forbidden; therefore another should stand over them who will scrape every utensil at the time it is set down, end quote. And so is the implication of the Ra'ah brought in Beis Yosef, siman 460. Also in the Shelah he wrote to have two basins so that he can rinse between every dough and dough. And in truth I have not seen people careful about all this, even the meticulous ones, within one kneading, namely what is baked from one heating. And the reason appears to me: for if so, even with one matzah one should be concerned and scrape between piercing and piercing and the like; and the Torah was not given to the ministering angels, but only to urge and hasten the matter of its making; and on the contrary such great precision will lead to idle periods. And furthermore, through scraping one cannot clean them well, so he would also need rinsing, and that is impossible to do at the time of kneading. Also there is concern that if we are so stringent, his heart will strike him and he will refrain from eating matzos; and its ways are ways of pleasantness. And one should not be more stringent than the early authorities were stringent; and so wrote the Rokeach, siman 273, and this is his language: after he kneads all his dough he should scrape the dough from the vessel, end quote. And likewise regarding the basin and the bowl, as Mahariv wrote, siman 193, and he should rinse the bowl unless he kneaded immediately without interruption; and so wrote Maharil in Hilchos Tikkun HaMatzos, that one should not be stringent whenever he kneads immediately within the bowl. And that Maharil is stringent regarding scraping utensils refers to when he kneads many doughs, where there is concern that the dough on the utensils sit idle without work for the measure of a mil; but otherwise there is no concern; go out and see what the people do. However, those who are accustomed to leave one cloth on the table and work with them and then heat the oven again and reuse all the utensils they used at first without any scraping and rinsing, and also on the first cloth, or that they turn the cloth over: this certainly one should warn and protest not to do; and it is proper to do as Maharil wrote, that it is not proper to begin kneading a second time until after the oven has been heated a second time, and in the meantime rinse the utensils and take another cloth; and see above, siman 451, s.k. 3.
And afterward rinse them. And the peel need not be rinsed; Derashos Maharil. And see above, siman 451, s.k. 54.
He should cast them into cold water or put them in the oven. In every Shulchan Aruch this gloss is printed as Maharil; and see Magen Avraham what he wrote in the name of Maharil, that it is forbidden to eat this dough even though it was baked, because on the other side in the cracks it was not baked well; and it is difficult: if so, how is he permitted to bake? And he forced a resolution. And in truth I see in the sefer Maharil before us that he did not write at all this permit to bake; and on the contrary he wrote, and this is his language: And I do not know how they purify the vessels in which they knead on Yom Tov of Pesach, for it is forbidden to rinse, being unnecessary toil; and if you say to bake what is rolled in the oven, behold that within in the cracks it was not baked, and furthermore it is unnecessary. To cast into cold water is impossible, for they float above and the water does not touch every side (see Mahariv, siman 193, who wrote that one should weigh them down below); and furthermore perhaps the wood interrupts between the water; and if to burn them there is loss. And were it not that my teachers murmured about the matter, I would have said that it is permitted to rinse them like bowls for a need, for it is considered somewhat a need lest it become chametz and ruin the vessel, and it is considered somewhat a need of the day because of chimutz; but my heart did not fill me. Therefore it is good to rinse them by means of a non-Jew, or to make stiff doughs that do not stick; and the bowl in which he kneaded he rinses by means of a non-Jew; and one who has no non-Jew should rinse himself, since he can put something in it; and likewise the knife he should rinse for the need of Yom Tov, and pierce the matzos with small sticks and burn them for a need before they become chametz, end of Maharil's language. Thus he explicitly forbids baking and putting into cold water; and this requires further study. And see Magen Avraham, who wrote that he should bitul before it becomes chametz, or give the utensils with the dough as a gift to a non-Jew; and if he needs to bake a second time on Yom Tov, obviously it is permitted to rinse them, end of his language.
Or put them in the oven that it bake. That is, in that same oven that was heated for baking, and not to heat anew for these utensils, which is like kindling not for a need, as I wrote in s.k. 2 in the name of Maharil, since it is forbidden to eat the dough, and it is also a small amount and people do not care about eating it.
That there not be in it any depression. And this is the language of Magen Avraham: Some plate the kneading-board with a metal sheet, and some make it of smooth stone so that it cool (Zichronos, folios 22, 25). And a vessel that cracked at its rim: Maharil ordered to widen the crack and scrape it well and hagalah, and to seal the cracks and smooth them to make them even, or widen it to the width of a finger; but one should not stick a copper piece onto it (meaning with nails) but rather by melting, as I wrote above in siman 451, s.k. 41. And also in the peel there should not be a crack or groove (Mahariv). The sheet must be changed between one kneading and the next; and if one works on a board without a sheet and there are holes in the board, one must seal them so that dough not enter them, end quote of Magen Avraham. And my brother-in-law the Rav wrote in Sefer Ateres Zekenim that it is better to make on a board than on a sheet, end quote. And most of the world is accustomed to make on a sheet, because most tables are not smooth. And see above, siman 451, s.k. 52, regarding the law of the sheet.
