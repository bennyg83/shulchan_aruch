And Magen Avraham wrote: not like the practice of the world, who do not scrape them until after production; for even though they do not sit idle for the measure of a mil, nevertheless l'chatchilah it is forbidden; therefore one should stand over them who will scrape every utensil continually at the time it is set down, end quote. And see Chok Yaakov.
He should rinse them. And the baker's peel need not be rinsed; Derashos Maharil.
Into cold water. And he should weigh them down so that they be below the water and not float on top; Mahariv. And see Chok Yaakov.
That it bake. That is, in that same oven that was heated for baking, and one should not heat anew for these utensils; Chok Yaakov. And nevertheless it is forbidden to eat that dough, for on the other side and in the cracks it was not baked well; Maharil. And Magen Avraham wrote: and it appears to me that one should bitul the chametz before it becomes chametz, or give the utensils with the dough as a gift to a non-Jew. And if he needs to bake a second time on Yom Tov, obviously it is permitted to rinse them, but it is forbidden to scrape them, as is written at the end of siman 323.
A crack. Some plate the table and the kneading-board with a metal sheet, and some make [it] of smooth stone so that it cool; Sefer Zichronos. And after every dough one should wash his hands and the basin well and dry them with a cloth, unless he kneads immediately without interruption. And in the Shelah he wrote that it is proper to prepare two vessels, and immediately after the first kneading one should be ready to wash the vessel thoroughly, and the second dough should be measured in the second vessel, and so he alternates, end quote. (And Chok Yaakov wrote that I have not seen people practicing thus, for if so, between matzah and matzah and piercing and piercing one should also be concerned, see there, until [he says] the Torah was not given to the ministering angels.) And it appears to me that this applies specifically with a copper vessel, which one can wipe dry; but with wood, where one cannot wipe so that there not still be rinse-water in it, this does not apply. Maharil wrote not to begin kneading a second [dough] until the oven has been heated a second time, and in the meantime wash the basin and all the utensils. And Mahariv wrote: the one who puts the flour should wash his hands, and likewise the one who shaped the matzos. The sheet must be changed between one kneading and the next; and if one works on a board without a sheet and there are holes in the board, one must seal them so that dough not enter them. And also in the peel there should not be a crack or groove; and see above, siman 451, se'if 18. And Or Zarua wrote that it is better to make the matzos on a board than on a sheet; and so wrote the Taz in siman 453, s.k. 5, see there. And the world is accustomed to make them on a sheet, because most tables are not smooth.
Wool. But a woolen garment is permitted; Magen Avraham. And b'dieved in every case there is no prohibition; Chok Yaakov.
