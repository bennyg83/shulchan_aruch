(28) After the time, etc. - And before the time of its prohibition one pours in his courtyard even not in a place of a slope; and the view of the Bach and Taz is that even before the time of the prohibition one should not pour them in his courtyard not in a place of a slope.
(29) These waters - That is to say, the waters with which one trims or cools his hands.
(30) So that they not gather, etc. - For the chametz is his, since it is in his domain. However, to pour them in the public domain is permitted in every case: what does it matter to us if they become chametz afterward, since he already declared them hefker? And some say that even in the public domain it is not proper l'chatchilah not in a place of a slope, for since it is at the time of the chametz prohibition he should be precise about crumbs of his dough that they not come to chimutz.
(31) In one place - Rather they should be absorbed into the ground. And Magen Avraham wrote that one should not pour them onto a stone floor, for they are also not absorbed into the ground. And know that one must hurry and pour them after one trimmed with them or cooled his hands in them, or after rinsing the basin, because they hasten to become chametz.
(32) And the utensils - Namely the basins and the round sticks with which they shape the matzos and the piercers with which they pierce the matzos. And see Peri Megadim, who wrote that it is proper sometimes also to check and see the peel with which they put the matzos into the oven, whether there is no dough on it [for sometimes when the dough is soft, dough sticks to it].
(33) At the time of production - Namely between every dough and dough to clean them; for even though they do not sit idle for the measure of a mil between every dough and dough, and it turns out that the dough stuck to the utensils does not sit idle without work, even so l'chatchilah one must be careful about this, as above in se'if 2 [and what he wrote "after production" means after what was baked from one heating]. And likewise one must wash his hands and also wash the basin in which they knead and dry it with a cloth between every dough and dough. And our custom today is for a woman who kneads to wash her hands and likewise the utensils in which they knead after they made two or three doughs with it, so that from the beginning of the kneading there not be more than the measure of a mil; and likewise those who roll should watch if dough is stuck to their hands and wash their hands also from measure of a mil to measure of a mil; and likewise they should watch the rolling sticks and piercers that they be clean, and switch them or scrape the chametz from them at the mentioned time; and likewise the sheets with which they cover the tables they also switch at the mentioned time; and where they shape on the tables they scrape them with glass at the mentioned time. And the later authorities wrote that the proper custom is to have two basins to knead in them, and while kneading in the second, one person should be ready to wash the first and dry it well, and so he alternates. And the proper custom is to appoint a supervisor in the matzah bakery to watch over everything, for the stumbling-block is very great.
(34) And afterward rinse them - And the peel need not be rinsed.
(35) By means of a non-Jew - For rinsing the utensils is a shevus, and by means of a non-Jew it is a shevus of shevus, and it is permitted in a place of a mitzvah, as is written in siman 307, se'if 5.
(36) He should cast them into cold water - For cold delays becoming chametz, as above in siman 457, se'if 2; and he should weigh them down so that they go down below in the water and not float on top. And all this is when he does not intend to bake again that day; but if he intends to bake, he may rinse them himself; and to scrape them is forbidden in every case.
(37) Or put them in the oven, etc. - And nevertheless it is not proper to eat the baked dough, lest the other side within the cracks not have been baked well.
(38) That the dough bake - And the later authorities are doubtful about this advice, for since it is not proper to eat what was baked, as above, and also he does not intend to eat, then it is baking not for a need, which is forbidden. And there is another advice: that he bitul the chametz before it becomes chametz, or give the utensils with the dough as a gift to a non-Jew.
(39) That there not be in it any depression, etc. - And even on the rim of the vessel in which he kneaded there should not be a crack. And a vessel that cracked at its rim: the poskim wrote that one must widen the crack and scrape it well and hagalah, and seal the cracks and smooth them to make them even, or widen it to the width of a finger so that the hand can enter to clean; but one should not stick a copper piece onto it with nails, for chametz enters under the nail, unless he sticks the copper by melting. Also in the peel with which they take out the matzos there should not be a crack or groove; and likewise on the board on which they shape and roll the matzos it must also be smooth without cracks and grooves; and if there are holes in them one must seal them, for dough enters them. And some shape for this reason on a sheet; however, on the sheet too it is not good, for dough enters the weave-holes [unless they switch them for new ones every measure of a mil], and turning them to the other side also does not help. And therefore it is better to shape on a smooth board. And meticulous people are accustomed to plate the tables with a smooth metal sheet or on smooth stones; and some also roll with wooden rollers that are plated with a metal sheet or with glass.
(40) A wool cushion - And it is good to be careful not to set [dough] even on a woolen garment; but b'dieved in every case there is no prohibition.
