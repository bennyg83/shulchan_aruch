Under. See Taz: that is, from what it says in Yoma 28b, "A cloudy day is entirely sun," regarding Rava's ruling; it is implied that when it is not cloudy, under shade it is permitted, provided one does not draw near the sun. It is implied that even in shade, whenever one is near the sun the shade too is warm and will warm the dough, and only far away is it permitted. And see Chok Yaakov and Eliyah Rabbah (1). And one may say that kneading opposite a window requires care, against a cloudy day, for the Mechaber in se'if 5 holds that if she [nevertheless] kneaded [under the sun], it is permitted; and presumably the same applies on a cloudy day. Even though it says in Yoma that the glare of the sun is harsher than the sun itself, etc., and the Rav, since he was silent, conceded to him: if so, we do not make such a case l'chatchilah, as is written in Yoreh De'ah (1), that whatever is permitted b'dieved we do not decree l'chatchilah. And if Hashem wills, in Magen Avraham (1) this will be further clarified. And what he wrote in the name of the Bach, he brought there many poskim that b'dieved it is forbidden if one kneaded under the sun or on a cloudy day, and there is no [fixed] measure for the matter. And what he wrote in the name of Agudah, chapter Ein Omdin, siman 44, and the Bach: it is implied that he interprets [the case of] an oven used for baking where they put wood at the mouth of the oven as forbidden throughout the entire house; and the Taz seems to interpret it as a winter-house oven. And one may further say that regarding a baking oven, even not in front of the mouth of the oven it is still warm, and even less than a mil it becomes chametz; therefore it is good to bake in the oven and make the matzos in another room. And see Eliyah Rabbah (3): regarding benefit, at any rate it is permitted, see there. And the Mechaber in se'if 5 permitted even for eating. And see Magen Avraham 15, and see Levush on this.
