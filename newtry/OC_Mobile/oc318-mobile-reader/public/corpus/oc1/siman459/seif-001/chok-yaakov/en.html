In a place of the sun. But not in a place of the sun it is permitted to knead, and we are not concerned lest it become cloudy, since he is in an uncovered place under the open sky and will certainly notice when it becomes cloudy. And it is clear in the Talmud and poskim that so long as one is not drawn right up to the sun itself, but is only near the sun, there is no concern; for it is not comparable to near the oven, whose heat is abundant and takes effect nearby, which is not so of the sun's heat, which takes effect only where it shines. And all this is simple and clear, not like Rosh Yosef who is doubtful about this.
And on a cloudy day it is forbidden. See Bach, that if one kneaded in the sun or on a cloudy day, even b'dieved it is forbidden; and see above, se'if 5, s.k. 26.
Opposite the window. Which is not so from the side of a window, or far from the window, in such a way that the sun cannot shine onto the place of kneading, even if the sun were over the whole expanse of the sky like "a cloudy day is entirely sun," for example when the window stands in a narrow place between the walls of other houses and the like: it is permitted to knead. And this is the intent of the Rokeach, siman 273, see there; and so it is somewhat implied in Maharil; and see Bach, who so explained the words of the Tur.
The open window. But if the window is not open, even if it is of glass as ordinary windows are, even so it is permitted to knead opposite it. And it is not comparable to what I wrote above in siman 455, s.k. 4, regarding water, that whenever the sun shines through the glass it burns and warms more: that is said specifically regarding water, and as the language of the Talmud in Beitza folio 33, "one does not take fire out of the water"; and so it is implied in Kol Bo; and so I have seen practiced to knead opposite a closed glass window.
Lest it be cloudy. But at bein hashemashos there is no concern at all (Beis Yosef in the name of Sefer HaTerumah).
From carrying the matzos. And in the responsa of the Rashba, siman 124, he wrote that the Rambam sent the matzos to the bakery much farther than fifteen houses, and he concluded: and in the end he refrained, not because of the distance of the bakery, but that he feared that perhaps the bakery was full and his bread would be delayed outside for the measure of a mil or more; but to be concerned for removing the hand, we have not heard, end quote. And see what the Mechaber and the Rav wrote in se'if 2 in the gloss.
Uncovered. And Magen Avraham wrote: rather he should cover it with a cloth; and one should not leave one cloth many times on the matzos, lest dough stuck to it and became chametz.
Or the flour. Therefore one should be careful when carrying flour from the mill that the sacks not be open above or full of holes; and b'dieved there is no concern for all this, since it is only mere transit; only l'chatchilah one should be careful, and as the language of the Rav; and so ruled the Bach. And regarding the law of the water, see above, siman 455.
Beside the oven. In the Tur he wrote "beside the mouth of the oven," which implies that so long as one is not beside the mouth of the oven there is no concern; however, the Mechaber copied the language of the Rosh, who wrote simply that one should not knead near the oven. Nevertheless it appears to me that it is one and the same: so long as one is not near the mouth of the oven but behind it, there is no concern with our ovens in which they bake, which are made of thick bricks so that the heat does not take effect outside, as with a winter-house oven; only by way of the mouth of the oven is there concern, which is not so if one is not near the mouth of the oven. And so I found in Tosafos, chapter Kol She'asek, folio 36a, s.v. "bemakom," and this is their language: bread of the oven, they would knead in the Azarah near the oven of consecrated [use] so that it not become chametz, end quote. And in Bach he wrote, and this is his language: where they put burning wood and glowing coals before the mouth of the oven, it is forbidden to knead in that entire room, for everything is beside the fire and the whole room is warm; and so wrote Agudah in the chapter "These pass," in the name of the Geonim: the measure of a mil is specifically in a house that has no oven in it, but in a house that has the oven in it it is excessively warm, and even less than a mil it becomes chametz, end quote of Bach. And I have not seen people careful about this; and the reason appears to me essential: that Agudah said this only regarding winter-house ovens that through heating hold their warmth, and similarly a place where one feels the warmth of the fire near the mouth of the oven or fire; and so it is clearly implied from the words of the Rokeach in siman 273 and Maharil (and this is also the view of Hagahos Maimoniyos, chapter "These pass," brought in Beis Yosef); which is not so when one bakes in a place where one does not feel the warmth and is not near a winter-house oven that holds warmth, or when the house is not sealed and one is not near the mouth of the oven beside the fire: even if one bakes near the oven in which they bake, there is no objection, and as I wrote explicitly in the name of Tosafos to the contrary; and go out and see what the people do.
