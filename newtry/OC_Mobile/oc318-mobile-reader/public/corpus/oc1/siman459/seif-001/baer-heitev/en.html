The sun. But not in a place of sun it is permitted to knead, and we are not concerned lest it become cloudy, since he is in an uncovered place under the open sky and will certainly notice when it becomes cloudy. And it is clear in the Talmud and the poskim that so long as one is not drawn right up to the sun itself, but is only near the sun, there is no concern; for it is not comparable to being near the oven, whose heat is abundant. And it is obvious that a winter-house that was heated with an oven is worse than a cloudy day. And one should not heat it that day, or should open the door and windows until the heat leaves.
Forbidden. See Bach, that if one kneaded in the sun or on a cloudy day, even b'dieved it is forbidden; and see se'if 5.
The window. And from the side of a window, or far from the window, in such a way that the sun cannot shine onto the place of kneading, it is permitted; Chok Yaakov.
The open [window]. But if the window is sealed, even if it is of glass as ordinary windows are, even so it is permitted to knead opposite it; Chok Yaakov, see there.
Cloudy. But at bein hashemashos there is no concern at all; Beis Yosef.
From carrying. And in the responsa of the Rashba, siman 124, he wrote that the Ramban sent the matzos to the bakery much farther than fifteen houses; and see Chok Yaakov.
Uncovered. Rather he should cover it with a cloth; and one should not leave one cloth many times on the matzos, lest dough stuck to it and became chametz; Magen Avraham.
