1) [Se'if 1] One may not knead in sunlight. This is because the sun's heat warms the dough. Rashi, Pesachim 42a; Beis Yosef; Levush.
2) There. One may not knead, etc. One who lacks a spacious place inside the house may knead matzos in the middle of a courtyard exposed to sunlight by spreading tents or sheets overhead. Responsa Beis Yehudah, siman 46; Birkei Yosef, subsection 1; Kisei Eliyahu, subsection 1.
3) There. One may not knead in sunlight. If a woman transgressed and kneaded in sunlight, the bread is permitted. Rambam, chapter 5. The Shulchan Aruch likewise rules below in se'if 5. Peri Chadash writes there in se'if 5 that this is certainly so if one kneaded beneath the open sky on a cloudy day. It may also be assumed that according to Rambam, if one kneaded near the mouth of an oven or in a similar place, the dough is permitted after the fact. Mishbetzos Zahav, subsection 1, likewise writes that according to the Shulchan Aruch, dough kneaded on a cloudy day is permitted after the fact. See there. Chemed Moshe, subsection 1, writes likewise. Rosh at the end of the chapter Kol Sha'ah, however, prohibits dough that was kneaded in sunlight, as cited by Beis Yosef. Bach rules likewise and writes that it is prohibited even after the fact and even when done inadvertently. The same applies on a cloudy day, whose law is like kneading in sunlight. It is certainly prohibited after the fact if kneaded near the mouth of our ovens, whose openings are on the side, because the dough warms immediately near the oven opening. See there. Magen Avraham, subsection 15, cites this and writes, 'It appears to me that this applies specifically when the dough became warm.' Chok Yaakov, subsection 26, cites his words. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 6; Chemed Moshe, there; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 8; Chayei Adam, rule 128, subsection 17; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 21, write likewise. Magen HaElef, subsection 6, writes that when one acted deliberately, one should be stringent even if the dough did not become warm, since the entire kneading was performed in sunlight. If one began in sunlight but finished elsewhere, however, it is possible that one need not be stringent. These are his words. The authorities cited above, however, imply that there is no distinction: whenever the dough did not become warm, it is permitted after the fact. Shulchan Aruch HaRav there explicitly writes that if it did not become warm, it is permitted even if one kneaded deliberately. He also implies that even when it did become warm, it is prohibited only for consumption but permitted for benefit. See there. It appears that everything depends upon the circumstances. If we see that it became very warm, it should be prohibited even for benefit if this occurred during Pesach or on the eve of Pesach after chametz became prohibited. See above, siman 455, subsection 71.
4) There. On a cloudy day it is forbidden, etc. On a day that is not cloudy, one may knead and smooth the dough outdoors in the shade, provided that the location is not so near the sunlight that the dough will become warm. Beis Yosef in the name of Rabbeinu Yerucham. Darkei Moshe, subsection 1, comments that even Terumas HaDeshen, who writes in siman 122 that the custom is not to knead beneath a window, may agree here. Since one kneads in the shade, there is no concern about a cloudy day; the shade itself provides a recognizable indication that there is no sun. See there. Magen Avraham, subsection 1; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 4; Chok Yaakov, subsection 1; Chok Yosef, subsection 1; Chemed Moshe, subsection 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1, write likewise. Chok Yaakov writes there that the Talmud and authorities explain that there is no concern unless the dough is exposed to actual sunlight; merely being near sunlight is not a concern, contrary to Rosh Yosef's uncertainty. See there. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 2, and Ma'amar Mordechai, subsection 1, however, challenge him from the words of Rabbeinu Yerucham cited above. Yad Aharon in Hagahos HaTur likewise writes that Rosh Yosef's ruling is clear. Mishbetzos Zahav, subsection 1, writes that whenever the shade is near sunlight, the shade itself is warm and will warm the dough; only a distant shaded place is permitted. These are his words. See the following subsection.
5) There. On a cloudy day it is forbidden, etc. This applies specifically when the sky is completely overcast. If clouds come and go, with the sun alternately revealed and covered, there is no concern. Peri Chadash in the name of Ramban. He nevertheless writes that according to Terumas HaDeshen, cited in the gloss, the custom is at least to avoid kneading beneath the open sky. Although Darkei Moshe writes that Terumas HaDeshen may agree here, as stated in the preceding subsection, he presents this only as a possibility. The custom is therefore to be careful, because the opposite may be true: this case may be no different from kneading beneath a window, since those occupied with kneading and smoothing might not notice when the sky becomes overcast. Another reason for the custom not to knead beneath the open sky even when there are no clouds is that if clouds appear, the dough must be moved elsewhere. While people search for another place and move the dough and utensils, the dough might remain unattended for some time. Therefore, the custom is not to knead beneath the open sky. Before kneading begins, people designate a suitable location where the dough will not need to be moved if clouds appear.
6) There. On a cloudy day it is forbidden, etc. It appears obvious that a winter room whose oven was lit is worse than a cloudy day. The oven should therefore not be lit that day, or the door and windows should be opened until the heat has escaped. Taz, subsection 1; Chok Yosef, subsection 2; Chemed Moshe, subsection 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 7. After the fact, the dough should not be prohibited unless it became warm, as stated above in subsection 3. See there. See below, subsection 19.
