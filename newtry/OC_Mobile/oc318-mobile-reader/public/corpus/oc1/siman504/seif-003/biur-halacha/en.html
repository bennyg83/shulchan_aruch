One does not pound the groats. In the Shulchan Aruch it is mentioned only regarding the groats; but in the Talmud it is stated, "one does not make tisni," and Rashi explained that tisni is that one pounds the wheat until they are split into four, and that is great trouble; and porridge and chilka do not have so much trouble; see there in the sugya. And the implication is apparently that according to Rava, who said that one does not make tisni and one does not pound in a mortar are one matter, and what reason does he state: what is the reason one does not make tisni? Because one does not pound in a mortar; and as the Talmud establishes it as applying to Eretz Yisrael, where it is forbidden both with a large and with a small [mortar]. And if so, for us who permit at least with a small one, tisni too is permitted to make with a small one, even though it is great trouble; and so is implied from the Maggid Mishneh; see there. And so is implied from the Tur, who wrote "groats and the like," implying that he includes all things that are pounded; and so wrote Maharam Schiff; see there. However, the Meiri wrote explicitly that porridge and chilka for us are permitted to make with a small [mortar], but tisni is forbidden even for us; see there. And see in Kitzur Piskei HaRosh, whose words are somewhat unclear; and see in the Itur and in the commentator there; and this requires further study.
And since we do not, etc. See Peri Chadash, who disagrees with the Tur and wrote that from the words of all the poskim it is proven that even for us this law applies, and we are quite expert in this: that whatever they are not accustomed to pound with during the rest of the year because of its smallness is called small; and he concludes: and so it appears proper to be lenient in a rabbinic matter; end quote. [And so Bach challenged this law: if he pounds the wheat in a spice mortar, it is certainly a change; see there.] And likewise the Yam Shel Shlomo in his composition did not copy this of the Tur. And according to their words, in our country, where there is for this a designated vessel that they call shtuppe, in which they pound barley, and they never pound in another vessel: if so, when he pounds on Yom Tov not in a mortar but in another vessel, there is certainly no greater change than this. And one might have said that in a time of need, when he did not have leisure from before Yom Tov for this, one may be lenient to pound not in a mortar, as above. However, one should be stringent about this from another side, because afterward he is forced to come to another melacha that is forbidden as a matter of law: for it is known that after pounding the barley, the custom of our country when they pound barley that has much waste upon it is that they put them in a trough and winnow the waste from it in the very manner of winnowing; and this is forbidden, as is proven below in siman 510, se'if 1; see there.
Because there is no grinding in foods. See Mishnah Berurah regarding selecting matzah crumbs, and it is from Magen Avraham in the name of Maharil; and so Taz brought these words of Maharil in siman 495. And behold Darkei Moshe rejected this, for we hold below in siman 510 that on Yom Tov one selects in his usual way. And behold the aforementioned commentators each answered according to his way: Taz answered that in truth even in siman 510 they permitted selecting only for a meal that one needs, and in a manner that is permitted even on Shabbos; see there in siman 510. However, most of the later authorities there rejected his words. And Magen Avraham wrote to make our matter depend on that of siman 506, se'if 2 in the gloss, that there are those who are stringent there to select by hand a stone and a chip. And in truth this is not settled at all, for even upon that of siman 506 the question from that of siman 510 above is difficult; and several later authorities already noted this; see Maamar Mordechai, Nahar Shalom, and Beit Meir. And we are forced to answer that specifically there regarding legumes they permitted, because it is not the practice to select for many days; which is not so regarding other grain and flours, where the practice is to prepare them for many days. And so Shulchan Aruch HaRav brought in the name of the Shitah Mekubetzet to distinguish. However, this answer is not applicable to our case, for selecting matzah crumbs seemingly are prepared for many days even less than legumes; and if so, why did Maharil forbid them? And this requires further study. Indeed, in Chayei Adam he wrote a reason for those who are stringent when a stone or chip fell in, to take it by hand, because that is the way to take it by hand, and if so that is the way of its selecting; and he further wrote there that a matzah crumb in flour likewise has the law of a stone and a chip; see there; and his reason is that even in this the way is to take it even by hand. And according to this everything is settled; and nevertheless this requires further study.
