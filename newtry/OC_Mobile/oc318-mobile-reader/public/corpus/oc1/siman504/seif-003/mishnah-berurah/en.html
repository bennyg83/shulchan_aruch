{14} One does not pound the groats, etc. For this is like full grinding, which is forbidden on Yom Tov, and like peppers in their mill. And if he pounded, the implication of the Gemara is that it is forbidden to eat from this.
{15} For this is its change. For on a weekday it is not the practice to pound in a small [mortar], because it is not pounded fine well.
{16} And in Eretz Yisrael, etc. There are several explanations of this. Some explain that they forbade it in Eretz Yisrael because they were accustomed to keep servants and they were lax about this: they do it in their weekday way and say, we did it in a small [mortar]; therefore the Sages forbade it entirely. And if so, for practical law in our time it depends on this: wherever they keep Canaanite servants it is forbidden in every respect. And some wrote that in Eretz Yisrael the wheat was superior, not spoiled, and its appearance did not change if one pounded it from before Yom Tov; therefore the Sages did not permit them to pound on Yom Tov. And if so, the same law applies in every place where the wheat is good and is not damaged if pounded before Yom Tov: it is forbidden. And some wrote that in Babylon they were accustomed to eat porridge constantly, and since their need for this was great they would not pound in small mortars; and when one pounds on Yom Tov in a small one it is a change; which is not so in Eretz Yisrael, where they were not so accustomed to this, and even on weekdays it is common that they pound in a small mortar, and there is no change on Yom Tov, and therefore it is forbidden. And the same law applies in our time as well: it depends on this: if it is not the practice to pound in a small [mortar] on a weekday, it is permitted. And one should be stringent like all the explanations.
{17} And since we do not, etc. See Biur Halacha.
{18} And it is permitted to grate, etc. And it is not similar to peppers and mustard in a mill, for in a mill the weekday labor is greater.
{19} However, one needs a change. For for the most part it does not dissipate flavor; and however, if it does dissipate flavor it is permitted even without a change as a matter of law, except that according to those who customarily [are stringent] above, in every case they change. And horseradish that they call chrein: even though often they grind the horseradish in a large measure for two or three days, even so one may argue that the Sages did not decree about this to forbid even one who grinds for that day alone, for they decreed only regarding a mill, which is a complete weekday act and like all grinding. And nevertheless, in any case one should change a little. And when one grinds the horseradish not upon a dish but upon a cloth or upon the table, it is called a change. It is permitted to cut vegetables fine on Yom Tov without a change; however, all these are permitted only in the measure that one needs for that day.
{20} And the same law applies to matzos without a change. That is to say: the same law applies to matzos, that it is permitted to crush them in the vessel designated for that as they customarily do, and to grind them on a threshing-board; however, in this it is more lenient, for it is permitted even without any change at all, because there is no grinding after grinding. And after the grinding it is proper to be careful not to select matzah crumbs that were not yet crushed well from within the flour, even by hand, for it is like waste from food, which according to one opinion is forbidden even on Yom Tov, as will be explained below in siman 506, se'if 2 in the gloss [Magen Avraham in the name of Maharil]. And if he wants to take them, he should take them with a little flour, so that there is no concern of selecting, as was explained in the laws of Shabbos, siman 319; and see Biur Halacha.
