One does not pound. The implication of the Gemara, folio 14, is that if he pounded in a large [mortar], he should not eat from it; and it is possible that specifically an important person must be stringent; see the beginning of siman 318.
And in Eretz Yisrael, etc. And the same law applies wherever there is laxity of servants, that we decree lest they do it in a large [mortar] (Yam Shel Shlomo). And in Beis Yosef he wrote the reason that in Eretz Yisrael porridge is uncommon.
And it is permitted to grate. And it is unlike mustard, which is forbidden to grind in a mill, for there they are accustomed to grind a large amount together, and it resembles weekday labor; but here there is no practice to grate except what is needed for the moment (Rivash). And according to this, horseradish that they call kren is forbidden to grate on Yom Tov (without a change, as regarding salt), for the practice is to grate a large amount for two or three days; and so is implied in siman 510, se'if 3; see there what is written.
One needs a change. And if it dissipates flavor, one does not need a change, for it is like spices (Rivash; Beis Yosef; Yam Shel Shlomo). However, there are those who are stringent if he knew from yesterday, as is written above.
And the same law applies to matzos. Maharil wrote that it is forbidden to select large matzah crumbs from among the ground ones. And in Darkei Moshe it is written that it is permitted, for even waste from food is permitted, as is written in siman 510; and so wrote the Yam Shel Shlomo. And it appears to me that Maharil holds that it is forbidden to take a chip by hand from flour, as is written in siman 506, se'if 2 in the gloss; and therefore it is likewise forbidden to take the matzos. And even though in siman 319, se'if 3, he wrote that it is permitted to select large pieces from small ones of one kind, that is specifically regarding pieces; but here what has been ground is like flour, and therefore one should be stringent as Maharil wrote.
