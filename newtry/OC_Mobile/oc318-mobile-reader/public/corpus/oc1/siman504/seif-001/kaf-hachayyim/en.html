1) [Se'if 1] Spices may be pounded in their usual manner, etc. This is the view of Rif and Rambam in chapter 3. According to Tosafos, however, even peppers and spices must be pounded with the mortar tilted if one knew before Yom Tov how many pots he wished to cook. If he did not know, it is permitted without this alteration. Garlic, mustard, and garden cress are permitted even if he knew before Yom Tov, because they deteriorate more severely when ground before Yom Tov. Tur. See below, in the gloss at the end of this se'if, and our words there, with Heaven's help. Although in any event it is forbidden to eat peeled garlic, a peeled onion, etc., over which the night passed, as stated in Niddah 17a, one may say that if they are cooked or mixed with another substance, allowing the night to pass over them causes no harm. Zivchei Tzedek on Yoreh Deah, siman 116, subsection 61, writes this. See there.
2) There. Spices may be pounded in their usual manner, etc. This means that although all authorities agree that grinding is forbidden on Yom Tov, as written above, siman 495, se'if 2, pounding spices is nevertheless permitted because pounding them before Yom Tov would diminish their flavor. Certainly, garlic, onion, garden cress, and similar items may be pounded according to all authorities, as written in the preceding subsection.
3) There. Spices may be pounded in their usual manner. This means with a pestle and similar implements used for pounding on a weekday, without any alteration. This follows the ruling of Shulchan Aruch. Nevertheless, see below in the gloss, which states that the custom is to make a slight alteration.
4) There. Their flavor will diminish. This means that their flavor changes. The phrase 'its scent has not changed' is translated into Aramaic as 'its scent has not diminished.' Rashi, Beitzah 11a.
