(א) [Levush] It is permitted to grind, etc. — that is, per the Rif, whom Beit Yosef brought: the halacha follows Rav Yehuda, for Shmuel said (daf 14, Yam Shel Shlomo siman 39) — he challenged: but we maintain that wherever Shmuel and Rabbi Yochanan disagree, the halacha follows Rabbi Yochanan; and this is no difficulty, since the amoraim Rav Sheshet and Rav Acha hold like Shmuel, and so is the halacha. See in Ra'ash in Kiddushin chapter HaOmer; and most poskim in all of them ruled like Shmuel against Tosafot's explanation — and not like the later authorities who forbade according to Tosafot's explanation. Bach wrote: it appears to me that the Rif holds that all kinds of spices do not require salting (hazlalah) even if he knew what pot he needs to cook in — even turmeric; and his proof is from what we challenge 'perhaps it was spices' — what is the difficulty? Perhaps even if you were to say it was spices, nevertheless it would be forbidden with turmeric or where he knew. Until here. And one could say that in any case the questioner challenges well: from where does he know that perhaps it was other spices and he did not know? In my humble opinion the Rif infers from that he challenges anonymously 'perhaps spices' — that only salt requires a change; for if it also referred to turmeric he should have challenged 'perhaps it was other spices and he did not know.' And behold Taz wondered: if the Rif holds that with turmeric one does not need a change, if so what does he answer 'spices give off more scent when the kernels break' — perhaps it was turmeric, for its scent is not greater than salt's; and so he challenged Tosafot there. Until here. In my humble opinion to resolve: in Avodat HaKodesh daf 38 he ruled like the Rif, and even turmeric that does not nullify taste does not require a change; and he wrote the reason salt requires a change — because salt is soft and is ground well with a change. Until here; and so is explained at the end of Ran's words that Beit Yosef brought. And if so it appears to me that he explained 'spices give off scent' etc. because they are hard — that is, more than salt which is soft — and not as Rashi explained because the kernels broke. And in truth one could wonder on Rashba in responsum siman 484, who wrote the reason salt requires a change is because it does not nullify taste — if so his words contradict. I further wonder on Ran and Beit Yosef and the great ones mentioned above who explained per the Rif that even with turmeric one does not need a change — but the Rif concluded that so ruled Baal HaHalachot Gedolot. And I examined Baal HaHalachot Gedolot daf 36, who wrote thus: everything that one examines does not need salting except salt and turmeric, for Rav said to Rav Acha: when you examine for me. Until here — behold explicitly that change is required with turmeric; and it is plain to the examiner that all of Ran's words refer to answer his challenge on the Rif; and so is clear from what he concluded 'and further because it is ground' etc., as I wrote in the name of Avodat HaKodesh — to exclude Taz; see there that I abbreviated. Also what he answered Ran's challenge — since the Rif ruled like the tanna of the braita, he permits in every manner with spices and not with salt only — perforce it is plausible the reason of Rav Huna and Rav Chisda.
