[1] [Levush] It is permitted to pound, etc. That is, according to the view of the Rif whom Beis Yosef brought, that the halacha follows Rav Yehudah said Shmuel on folio 14. In Yam Shel Shlomo, siman 39, he challenged: but we hold that [where] Shmuel and Rabbi Yochanan [disagree], the halacha follows Rabbi Yochanan; and this is no difficulty, since the Amoraim Rav Sheshet and Rav Acha hold like Shmuel, and so is the halacha. And see in the Rosh in Kiddushin, chapter HaOmer; and likewise most poskim in all of them ruled like Shmuel against the explanation of Tosafos; and not like the later authorities who forbade according to the explanation of Tosafos. Bach wrote: it appears to me that the Rif holds that all kinds of spices do not require tilting, even if he knew what he needs to cook, even morika; and his proof is from what we challenge, "and perhaps it was spices": what is the difficulty? Perhaps even if you were to say that it was spices, nevertheless it is forbidden with morika or where he knew; end quote. And it is surprising: in any case the questioner challenges well, from where does he know? Perhaps it was other spices and he did not know. And in my humble opinion the Rif infers from that he challenges simply "and perhaps spices," that he required a change only regarding salt alone; for if it also referred to morika, he should have challenged "and perhaps it was other spices and he did not know." And behold the Taz wondered: if the Rif holds that morika does not require a change, if so what does he answer, "spices make a louder cracking sound when the kernels break": perhaps it was morika, whose sound does not crack more than salt; and so he challenged Tosafos there; end quote. And in my humble opinion, to resolve: in Avodat HaKodesh, folio 38, he ruled like the Rif, and even morika that does not dissipate flavor does not require a change; and he wrote the reason that salt requires a change because salt is soft and is pounded well with a change; end quote. And so is explained at the end of the words of the Ran that Beis Yosef brought. And if so it appears to me that he explained "spices make a louder sound, etc." because they are hard, that is, more than salt which is soft, and not as Rashi explained on account of the kernels having broken. And in truth it is surprising to me regarding the Rashba in a responsum, siman 684, who wrote the reason that salt requires a change because it does not dissipate flavor; if so his words contradict. I further wonder at the Ran and Beis Yosef and the great ones mentioned above who explained the words of the Rif that even with morika one does not need a change: but the Rif concluded that so ruled Baal Halachos Gedolos. And I examined Baal Halachos Gedolos, folio 36, who wrote this language: whatever one pounds does not need tilting, except for salt and morika, for Rav said to Rav Acha, when you pound, tilt for me; end quote. Behold explicitly that he requires a change with morika; and it is plain to one who looks that all the words of the Ran are going to answer his challenge on the Rif; and so is clear from what he concluded, "and further because it is pounded, etc.," as I wrote in the name of Avodat HaKodesh, to exclude the Taz; see there that I abbreviated. Also what he answered to the challenge of the Ran: since the Rif ruled like the tanna of the beraisa, he permits in every manner with spices and not with salt alone; necessarily he holds the reason of Rav Huna and Rav Chisda.
