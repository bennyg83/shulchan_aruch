{1} One pounds the spices, etc. That is to say: even though grinding is forbidden according to everyone on Yom Tov, as above in siman 495, se'if 2, and according to several poskim there is in this a Torah prohibition even when his intent is for the need of ochel nefesh that same day, nevertheless pounding spices is permitted; and all the more so garlic, onion, cress, and the like.
{2} In their usual way. With the pestle and mortar with which one pounds on a weekday, without any change.
{3} For if he will pound, etc. That is to say: even if he knew from yesterday that he would need it for tomorrow, we still do not say that he should have prepared from yesterday, since if he will pound, etc.
{4} It will dissipate their flavor. And peppers and mustard are included among spices, and they too dissipate their flavor when they remain crushed for a long time.
{5} But salt, etc. For example coarse salt that needs crushing; and it was explained above in siman 321, se'if 8, that the prohibition of crushing salt applies only to sea salt, which is coarse from the outset; which is not so regarding salt that they call sleynish, which is made fine from the outset and afterward is cooked and they make large flakes from it: there is no prohibition of crushing as a matter of law, and only because of appearances there are those who are stringent about this.
{6} It is not pounded, etc. And if the salt is mixed with the spices, he pounds in the usual way and need not be concerned, since he is not troubling himself with it by itself.
{7} And the like. That is, that he pound with a wooden pestle.
{8} For if he ground the salt, etc. And there are poskim who wrote that the same law applies to kinds of spices that do not dissipate flavor, for example saffron that they call zafran, for it is known that it does not dissipate flavor; and especially where he knew from yesterday that he would need saffron tomorrow for his pot, that it is forbidden for him to crush without a change; and one should be stringent and be concerned for this view.
{9} Because it is like weekday labors. More so; and some wrote that through this it is full grinding, which is forbidden on Yom Tov according to everyone, as above in siman 495. And even if he wants to make a change, for example to grind one by one and the like, it is likewise forbidden in a mill. And the same law applies to grinding coffee in their mill: it is likewise forbidden; rather he should crush it in a mortar; and see Biur Halacha.
{10} Rather pound them, etc. However, one must be careful not to pound them except what is needed for that day; and even to prepare for the second day of Yom Tov is likewise forbidden.
{11} However, they customarily, etc. The reason is that there are stringent poskim who say that even regarding spices that dissipate flavor it is not permitted to pound them on Yom Tov in their usual way unless he did not know from yesterday that he would need them on Yom Tov; and we are concerned for their words ab initio. However, even they said this only regarding spices; but regarding garlic, onion, and cress, according to everyone he should not pound them from before Yom Tov even if he knew he would need them tomorrow, for they dissipate their flavor entirely and become spoiled. And some wrote that according to the custom there is no difference between whether he knew from yesterday or did not know, and in every case they are not accustomed to pound on Yom Tov unless by means of some change, so that through this people will remember that it is forbidden to pound except what is needed for that day.
