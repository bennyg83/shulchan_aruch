And one does not grind the peppers, etc., in their mill. See Mishnah Berurah. And behold, the reason of the Mechaber was written by the Rashba in his novellae on Beitzah 14; but in his novellae on Shabbos, folio 141, he wrote that it is grinding, and his words there derive from the words of the Ramban in his novellae; and so wrote the Ritva there; and in Avodat HaKodesh he wrote even more, that this is full grinding; and so wrote the Taz in the name of Rabbeinu Tam that it is like grinding wheat; and this was already mentioned in Eliyah Rabbah. And if so, it is possible that according to this, for the Rashba who tends to say that all melachos before kneading are according to the Torah, this too is according to the Torah. And I found in the Rashba in his novellae on chapter HaMatznia (Shabbos, folio 95) that he wrote regarding that Yerushalmi, "one does not select and one does not grind, etc.," and they challenge upon it: but it was taught that the household of Rabban Gamliel would grind peppers in their mill, etc.; Rabbi Yosi son of Rabbi Bun said: grinding in its usual way was not permitted; and the Rashba explained that grinding peppers in their mill according to Rabban Gamliel is not grinding in its usual way; end quote. And the implication of this is that according to the Rabbis who disagree about this at the end of chapter 2 of Beitzah, for them it is full grinding. And so I found that Chayei Adam wrote that according to the Rashba it is according to the Torah. And if so, the same law applies to one who grinds coffee in their mill: it is a complete prohibition; and even if the flavor will dissipate somewhat when he crushes it in a mortar, that too does not help, for in any case before kneading a melacha was not permitted; except that one should distinguish in this and say that necessarily, even according to the Rashba for whom it is a Torah prohibition, nevertheless the matter was delivered into the hands of the Sages, and they forbade only a melacha that is ordinarily done in a large amount at one time for many days; for if not so, what difference is there between a mortar and a mill? Behold even one who crushes in a mortar is liable on Shabbos because of grinding, as is explained in Rambam, chapter 8, law 15; see there. And it is only because in a mortar the practice is to crush only for that day; and so wrote the Rashba explicitly in Avodat HaKodesh, that the Torah forbade these because they are done for many days. And if so it is possible to say that also regarding a mill, which they forbade, that is specifically in such a case as peppers or mustard, whose bitterness means that grinding once in a mill suffices for many days; and so wrote the Rivash, siman 184, that peppers and mustard are forbidden in a mill because the practice is to grind for many days. But regarding grinding coffee, if the mill is small as is customary among homeowners in their houses, it is known that coffee enters the grinder only in a measure for one time or for two times; it is possible that about this the Sages did not decree. And so in fact Shaarei Teshuvah brought; and so wrote in Bigdei Yesha. And in Chayei Adam he wrote that in any case by means of a non-Jew it is permitted even in a mill, only that the non-Jew should grind with some change; and he wrote that if one removes the small box that receives the coffee from the grinding, it is called a change.
