(א) (s.k. 1) The spices, etc. — and the same applies to saffron, etc. Know that on daf 14a we learned in a braita: spices are ground in their usual manner without a change, and salt requires a change — to grind it in a wooden pestle. And the gemara concludes: what is different about salt from spices? Rav Huna and Rav Chisda disagree: one said because spices nullify their taste if ground on Yom Tov, therefore they were lenient and permitted grinding them on Yom Tov without a change; but salt does not nullify and it would be for Yom Tov needs, therefore they were stringent and it requires a change. And one said: because all pots need salt and he knew yesterday he would need salt and it would be for Yom Tov needs. But not all pots need spices and he did not know yesterday he would cook a dish that would need spices. And they said: what is the difference between them? And Abaye said: where he knew yesterday what pot he needs to cook and knew he would need spices for that dish — for taste not all pots need spices in such a case spices also need a change. And per the reason that spices nullify taste, even in such a case no change is needed. Or turmeric — which is a spice that does not nullify taste and he did not know yesterday he would need turmeric — for taste all pots do not need spices, no change is needed; and per the reason that spices nullify taste, turmeric needs a change. And Rabbi Yochanan said: even salt does not need a change, for he found a braita that argues on the mishnah; and after that Rav Acha bar Adda said: if one grinds salt it changes at least somewhat — meaning the line ground on its side. And Rif and Rambam ruled like Rabbi Yochanan and like Rav Acha bar Adda; and Rambam did not distinguish between knowing what pot he needs to cook and between saffron and other spices; and likewise Rif only copied the words of Rabbi Yochanan and Rav Acha bar Adda and did not bring the dispute of Rav Huna and Rav Chisda on the reason that salt is stricter than spices. For us too there is relevance with turmeric or knowing what pot he needs to cook — both require at least some change, meaning tilting. And Beit Yosef's text holds the main view is as those who say the reason is spices nullify their taste, therefore spices in every case do not need a change even if he knew what pot he needs to cook. And if it were necessary to copy this reason so we would know turmeric that does not nullify taste needs tilting — Beit Yosef's text holds that turmeric nevertheless nullifies somewhat; and even though the gemara says there is a difference regarding turmeric — that is for the tanna of the mishnah who holds salt needs a great change in a wooden pestle; and since they were very stringent, therefore turmeric — even though it nullifies taste somewhat — nevertheless since it does not nullify much like spices, it needs a change. Not so for us who hold like Rabbi Yochanan that salt suffices with a light change by tilting — likewise we are lenient with turmeric and it suffices with nullifying taste somewhat, and turmeric does not need a change.
