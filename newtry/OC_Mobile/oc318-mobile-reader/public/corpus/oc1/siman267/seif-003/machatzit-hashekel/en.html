(Se'if Katan 3) Since, etc. This implies that one should not say, 'For God is our Guardian.' For the same reason we do not say, 'Who guards His people Israel': by the merit of observing Shabbos we require no protection, as stated in Midrash. The same should apply to, 'For God is our Guardian.'
Recanati writes, etc. This means that he gives a different reason why we do not say, 'Who guards His people Israel': on Shabbos, harmful forces truly have no permission to exercise dominion. I do not understand this, for on the contrary harmful forces are more common on Shabbos. Perhaps they are nevertheless not permitted to cause harm.
