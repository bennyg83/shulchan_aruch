(Se'if Katan 2) And to eat, etc. This means before Krias Shema. Although we rely upon Rabbi Yehudah to pray after plag haminchah, that applies to prayer. It is irrelevant to Krias Shema, concerning which even Rabbi Yehudah agrees.
He did not juxtapose Geulah to Tefillah. Rosh, at the beginning of Berachos, gives two possible reasons. Either Rav holds like Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi that Maariv does not require juxtaposing Geulah to Tefillah because the prayers were instituted between the two daily recitations of Krias Shema: the three prayers required each day were placed in the middle, with the morning Krias Shema before Shacharis and the evening Krias Shema after Maariv. Alternatively, Rav holds that because of the mitzvah of adding to Shabbos, Geulah need not be juxtaposed to Tefillah. Rosh gives this latter reason at the beginning of the chapter Tefillas HaShachar; see Lechem Chamudos there. In any event, this proves that Rav did not recite Krias Shema during the day, for otherwise he would also have juxtaposed Geulah to Tefillah. Rosh nevertheless writes that Rav ate during the day. This disproves Beis Yosef's resolution, so Magen Avraham supplies a different one: 'What he ate before,' etc.
In any event, those who customarily, etc., on Erev Shabbos while it is still day, etc. During the week, however, they pray at tzeis hakochavim. 'When one-half hour arrives,' etc.: Since Magen Avraham rejects Beis Yosef's answer and explains Rav's conduct differently, the independent reasoning remains that even if one already recited Krias Shema, he may not eat once its proper time arrives. Those who always follow Rabbi Yehudah, however, recite Krias Shema during the day and rely upon Rashi and Rabbeinu Tam. Although the principal fulfillment of their obligation is through Krias Shema at bedtime, Magen Avraham writes above in siman 235:2 that they may eat because they have at least already recited Krias Shema; see there.
