And he has the collateral in his possession. Otherwise there is no basis for permission, because ordinarily a creditor acquires collateral only from the time of collection onward. This means that we do not view it retroactively as though it had belonged to the non-Jew during Pesach. The Jew must tell him, 'from now,' for otherwise the non-Jew does not acquire it, it remains in the Jew's ownership, and it is forbidden. Likewise, if the non-Jew does not have possession of the collateral, even if the Jew said, 'from now,' the case is no better than chametz belonging to a non-Jew that is in a Jew's possession and for which the Jew bears responsibility. So writes the Rosh.
It is permitted even if the time did not arrive, etc. If the payment date arrived before Pesach, however, it is permitted even without saying, 'from now.' This is the view of the Ra'avad and the Rosh, unlike Rabbeinu Ephraim. Regarding the initial permissibility of borrowing money from a non-Jew against chametz, it appears to me that if, at the time of the loan, the Jew intends to redeem it after Pesach, it is certainly forbidden to make the loan. This is comparable to hiding chametz in the possession of a non-Jew, concerning which the Rambam writes at the beginning of chapter 4: The Torah teaches that chametz may not be found in your houses, even if one hid it or deposited it with a non-Jew. If, however, he intended to redeem it before Pesach and circumstances later continued so that he did not redeem it before Pesach, it appears that this is not included in the ruling at the end of siman 440 that the depositor must destroy it, as we explained there according to the Rambam and Rabbeinu Yonah. There, it is merely a deposit, and whenever the Jew wishes, he can retrieve it. Here, by contrast, the chametz secures a loan. There is no reason to require him to redeem it because he may perhaps wish to redeem it after Pesach. Why should we compel him to redeem it? If he does not redeem it before Pesach, he can no longer redeem it after Pesach, for it will then be forbidden because ownership is determined retroactively. It is therefore regarded as though it had been conclusively transferred to the non-Jew from today in payment of his money. This appears correct to me.
