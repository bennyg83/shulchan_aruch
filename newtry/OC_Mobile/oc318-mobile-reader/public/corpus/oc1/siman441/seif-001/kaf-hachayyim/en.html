1) [Se'if 1] A non-Jew lent money to a Jew against the Jew's chametz, etc. Initially, it is forbidden to borrow from a non-Jew against one's chametz with the intention of redeeming it after Pesach. This is regarded as hiding it in the non-Jew's possession, and one violates the prohibition against chametz being seen. If, however, one intended to redeem it before Pesach but circumstances continued until one failed to redeem it before Pesach, one is not obligated to eliminate it. Once one does not redeem it before Pesach, one can no longer redeem it after Pesach, because it would then be prohibited. Taz, subsection 2; Chok Yosef, subsection 1. Their words imply that if one intends not to redeem it at all, even after Pesach, the arrangement is initially permitted, provided that a due date was established and the transfer was effective retroactively from the present. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 2, writes likewise. Peri Chadash also rules that it is permitted even initially, on condition that when the eve of Pesach arrives one firmly resolves never to redeem it. See there. Chok Yaakov, subsection 1, likewise writes that if, when the time for elimination arrives, one declares that one will not redeem it, one no longer violates any prohibition and there is no prohibition in the matter at all. See there. Eliyah Rabbah, there, and Mekor Chaim, subsection 1, cite his ruling. Chayei Adam, rule 120, at the end of subsection 7, nevertheless writes that it is proper to be stringent and redeem it before Pesach.
2) There. And gave it into the non-Jew's possession as a pledge. If the Jew did not place it into the non-Jew's possession, but the non-Jew merely lent money against it while the chametz remained in the Jew's domain, then even if the Jew said that the transfer should be effective from the present, it is no better than chametz belonging to a non-Jew that is in a Jew's domain while the Jew bears liability for it, which is prohibited. Rosh; Taz, subsection 1; Magen Avraham, subsection 1. If the Jew said that the transfer should be effective from the present and conveyed it to the non-Jew through an act of acquisition or together with land, it is permitted even if it was not delivered to the non-Jew until after Pesach, provided that the Jew bore no liability for it throughout Pesach. Ri'az, cited by Shiltei HaGibborim in the chapter Kol Sha'ah; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur; Magen Avraham, there. Magen Avraham writes there that the symbolic acquisition must be performed with an item belonging to the non-Jew, as stated in Choshen Mishpat, siman 123, se'if 4. Shach there, subsection 30, however, writes that barter and symbolic acquisition are ineffective with a non-Jew; only money or physical transfer is effective, according to each authority's respective position. See there. Nehar Shalom, subsection 1 in this siman, agrees with him. See below, subsection 9.
