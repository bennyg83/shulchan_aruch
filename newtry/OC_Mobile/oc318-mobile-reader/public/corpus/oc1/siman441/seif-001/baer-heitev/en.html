(א) In his hand. And if they transferred it to the non-Jew through kinyan or via land, even though they did not deliver it to him until after Pesach, if it is not on the Jew's responsibility it is permitted. Shach.

(ב) From now. And if he did not lend him money but only said to him: if I do not do a certain matter, the chametz shall be yours from now — it is forbidden, for it is asmakhta (and according to Raavad there is no law of asmakhta with a non-Jew). And know that if one of these matters is lacking it is forbidden — meaning it is forbidden for any Jew to derive benefit from that chametz even though it is in a non-Jew's hand; and it requires study whether if he did not say to him from now he is obligated to redeem it from the non-Jew and destroy it, for otherwise he pays his debt from prohibited benefit. Magen Avraham. And Chayei Adam wrote one should be stringent to redeem it — see there. And if it is still in the Jew's hand only that he pledged it to the non-Jew, it is obvious it is forbidden to give it to him.

(ג) Permitted. And if at the time of the loan he intends to redeem it after Pesach, it is certainly forbidden, for it is like hiding it in the non-Jew's hand. Taz — see there.
