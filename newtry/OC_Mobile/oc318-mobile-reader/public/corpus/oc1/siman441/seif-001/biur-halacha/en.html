And he pledged it in his hand — see Mishna Berurah regarding if they transferred through kinyan: we wrote to be lenient in the name of several acharonim, and they wrote some are stringent, and this is in Peri Megadim; and there is concern for Rama's view in Choshen Mishpat siman 207 seif 14 that from now does not remove asmakhta without an important court unless it was tevoi yadai and from now, as Semag wrote s.k. 18 — see there. But in truth it is not proven there at all that tevoi yadai is required; only that it must be in the manner of a loan and that he did him a favor, as explained in Tosafot 66 and Rosh, and likewise in Piskei Tosafot; if so, the same applies here, for it was in the manner of a loan and he pledged his pledge to him even though it still rests in his possession — unless one holds that here it is worse because it rests in the borrower's possession; requires study.
