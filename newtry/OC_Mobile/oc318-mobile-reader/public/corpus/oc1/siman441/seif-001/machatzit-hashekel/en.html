(א) (s.k. 1) And he pledged it etc. — it is no worse etc. in the Jew's possession. Meaning the liability is on the Jew, and the Jew transgresses as written siman 440.

(ב) And in Shach he wrote etc. through kinyan — meaning sudar-kinyan. And know Shach does not disagree with Rosh; both intended the same thing. Only Rosh left his words unspecified, and Shach explained the matter more, as known from the dispute of Rashi and Rabbeinu Tam: Rashi ruled like R' Yochanan that with a Jew pulling acquires from the Torah; if so, with a gentile money acquires and not pulling. Rabbeinu Tam ruled like R' Yehudah that with a Jew money acquires from the Torah; therefore with a gentile specifically pulling is required. Rosh holds like Rabbeinu Tam that with a gentile specifically pulling is required. If so, that which Rosh wrote: but if he did not pledge it etc. — it is no worse than a gentile's chametz in a Jew's possession that is forbidden — implies the prohibition is only because liability is on the Jew; meaning he said to him from now, and if so if he did not pay after Pesach the matter is revealed retroactively that from the time of the loan it belonged to the gentile, and it is like selling it to the gentile at the time of the loan — nevertheless with what did he acquire, since he did not pledge it with the gentile and did not pull? There is only giving money, which does not acquire from a gentile per Rosh; therefore it deals when he acquired through sudar-kinyan or via land, only Rosh left it unspecified and did not speak of kinyan; and even if he acquired, he wrote nevertheless it is forbidden because liability is on the Jew; therefore Shach wrote: if the gentile acquired through sudar-kinyan or via land and liability is on the gentile it is permitted; but if the gentile did not acquire, even though liability is on the gentile, nevertheless it is forbidden, as written end of siman 440.

(ג) And in Choshen Mishpat etc. in the gentile's sudar — for we hold like Rav that the kinyan shall be in the sudar of the acquirer; however the custom is that since the majority there are witnesses at the time of kinyan who will not deny one of them in the kinyan, therefore they acquire in the witnesses' sudar; and for this reason the witnesses write in the document and kinyan from so-and-so etc., meaning they acquired from so-and-so — and that is through the acquirer's agency, for one may benefit a person not in his presence, for presumably it pleases him that he be his agent and acquire on his behalf. Not so with a gentile, since we hold there is no agency for a gentile and he cannot appoint an agent; therefore one cannot acquire through the witnesses' sudar, only in the gentile's sudar himself. However Shach there doubted the essence of the law — namely Rabbeinu Tam holds a gentile also acquires through chalipin, but Shach was compelled that there is no law of chalipin with a gentile; and he wrote thus in responsum of Maharam Mintz and responsum of Maharashdam — see there.
