(א) And he pledged it in his hand. But if he did not pledge it in the non-Jew's hand, only the non-Jew lent him on it and the chametz still rests in the Jew's possession — although he said to him from now, it is no worse than a non-Jew's chametz in the Jew's possession with liability on the Jew, which is forbidden (Rosh). And in Shach he wrote: if they transferred it to the non-Jew through kinyan or via land, even though they did not deliver it to the non-Jew until after Pesach, it is permitted provided it is not on the Jew's responsibility — end quote. And in Choshen Mishpat siman 123 seif 4 one must acquire through the non-Jew's sudar.

(ב) And said to him from now. And if the time arrived before Pesach, even if he did not say to him from now he acquired it, for there is no law of asmakhta with a non-Jew (Raavad); and Magen Avraham wrote that most acharonim hold everything depends on from now, and so appears from the halachot and Rabbeinu Yehudah and R' Yehudah ibn Gayat, and such is primary — end quote; and such is Rav Yosef Karo's view in Shulchan Aruch. And if he did not lend him on it, only said to him: if I do not do for you a certain matter before Rosh Chodesh Nisan the chametz shall be yours from now — it is forbidden, for it is asmakhta, as written in Choshen Mishpat siman 207 in Semag that specifically in a pledge from now is effective; and see what we wrote siman 448 seif 3 whether we follow their law.

(ג) And he did not pay, it is permitted. And it is difficult: let us say since it is in his hand to redeem on Pesach he transgresses, as we say regarding challah — since if he wishes he can remove the liability; and one can say: here, since he lacks money we do not say since (Ran); and Bach was in doubt whether it is permitted l'chatchila to lend a non-Jew in this manner — this explanation applies only b'dieved. Difficulty: even if he left it in the non-Jew's hand, nevertheless if the pledge is compelled the Jew would have to pay, as stated in Choshen Mishpat siman 72 — if so, it is in the Jew's possession; and one can say: it is like one who accepts liability for a non-Jew's chametz in the non-Jew's house, which is permitted as written siman 440; or we judge him by their law as we wrote. And know: if one of these matters is lacking that we said is forbidden — meaning it is forbidden for any Jew to derive benefit from that chametz even though it is in the non-Jew's hand; and requires study whether if he did not say to him from now he is obligated to redeem it from the non-Jew and destroy it, for otherwise he pays his debt from prohibited benefit, and it is like one whom the king compelled regarding his threshing floor in payment of a debt that he must tithe, as stated in Chullin chapter 11. And if it is still in the Jew's hand only that he pledged it to the non-Jew, it is obvious it is forbidden to give it to him. But if he did not lend him on it, only gave it to the non-Jew as a gift, such as if he said to him: if I do not do this thing for you on a certain day the chametz shall be yours from now as we wrote — he need not give its value to the non-Jew and destroy it, for even if you say the non-Jew did not acquire, nevertheless the Jew does not remove anything from it; but if it is in the Jew's hand it is forbidden to give it to him, for it is forbidden in benefit and forbidden to give to others.
