Seif 1. "A non-Jew who lent money to a Jew against his chametz, etc." NB: See what I wrote on this in a responsum to the holy community of Nadvorna, regarding what he asked about the Rambam's view that the time must have arrived before Pesach. It is placed, incidentally, in the omissions to Hilchot Nedarim, siman 221; see there in my work, which is still in manuscript, and examine carefully. See Magen Avraham s.k. 2, who brought the view of the Raavad that there is no asmachta with a non-Jew, and see my work on Choshen Mishpat, mahadura 4, siman 47, where I wrote a proof to this from Gittin 43b; see there and examine carefully. See Magen Avraham s.k. 3, who wrote that he is obligated to tithe, for it is comparable to a royal seizure, etc.; see my work on Choshen Mishpat, siman 443, and in the numbering of the Shulchan Aruch it is siman 386, where I wrote that this is not at all comparable to that. In my work on Hilchot Pesach I elaborated on this at greater length; see there, with God's help, and examine carefully. Now we read in the Yerushalmi, Pesachim chapter 2 halacha 2: There we learned, a slave whom his master made an apotiki, etc. R. Chaggai sat before R. Yosei: the baraita conflicts with Rav: a Jew who lent money to a non-Jew against his chametz, after Pesach it is permitted in benefit, etc. See the Korban HaEdah in Shirei Korban, who asked: instead of challenging from a baraita, he should have supported it from the Mishnah, where we learned that a Jew who lent money to a non-Jew against his chametz, after Pesach it is forbidden in benefit. See there, where he was pressed to say that it speaks where he stipulated "from now," but that is forced, for in any such case it should have explained. Rather, it appears more correct to say that the Mishnah speaks where after Pesach his intention is to leave it in the creditor's hand; therefore, stringently, we say breirah, and it is clarified that during Pesach too it stood in the Jew's domain, and therefore it is forbidden. Conversely, where a non-Jew lent to a Jew and his intention afterward was to leave it with him, it is permitted in benefit. But the baraita speaks where his intention is not to leave it with him, and therefore it is permitted in benefit. The baraita of a slave whom his master made an apotiki, however, explicitly speaks where his intention is not to leave it with him, since it says that both can free him, proving that his intention is not to leave it with him. Therefore it properly asks from that baraita, which likewise speaks where his intention is not to leave it with him. This resolves it, with God's help. Examine carefully.
