(א) And he pledged it. There regarding that he left it as pledge with him etc. — the objection that he said to him etc. implies it refers to leaving as pledge; and likewise in the baraita: a Jew who left as pledge etc. and if etc. — see Magen Avraham.

(ב) Even if the time did not arrive etc. — for otherwise Rava would not have challenged from the Mishnah, and from now would not be needed. And not like Rambam and R' Efraim who wrote specifically when the time arrived before Pesach, and they inferred from the Tosefta; and see Rosh. Nevertheless, that which is written even etc. implies that also when the time arrived before Pesach one must say from now, and the reason is asmakhta as written in chapter Eizehu Neshech 66:1–2. And for Nemyonim the Mishnah is difficult; but in truth that he said to him etc., and Magen Avraham wrote: although Rambam's view is there is no asmakhta in a pledge, here a Jew from a non-Jew does not acquire etc. And the explanation is Rambam wrote in chapter 11 of Hilchot Mechirah and in Shulchan Aruch Choshen Mishpat siman 207 seif 11 that in a deposit when he forgives you there is no asmakhta; and see Ran in Nedarim 27b s.v. she'ani heinah that said etc. nevertheless here a Jew from a non-Jew etc. And at first glance his words require study, for a Jew from a non-Jew does not acquire before collection — but why not acquire at its time? And one can say they challenged him from that in Bava Metzia cited above that specifically when he said from now; and one can explain this case is different — a movable pledge that he acquired for her; and see Tosafot there 67b s.v. shevi'it etc., and this is what he said here a Jew etc. But he wrote there in chapter 11 one who transfers whether land etc., and likewise Shulchan Aruch there seif 11 — if so that answer is void; and see there Magen Avraham, and several answers are possible. But Raavad's view here is before Pesach one need not from now, for there is no asmakhta with a non-Jew, and only after Pesach one needs from now; and his proof from what the Tosefta says before Pesach — therefore one must say as Ran wrote that the Tosefta deals when he did not say from now; and see Magen Avraham there, for it appears from his words they did not agree with him on the first law. And he holds even with a non-Jew one needs from now; and it appears the reason is they explain what the Tosefta says before Pesach refers to when he said to him, as Rosh wrote. But Raavad's words are primary, and there is no necessity in the geonim's words to contradict him, for they only wrote the Gemara's law after Pesach. And see what we wrote in Choshen Mishpat siman 207 seif 2 that Rambam's view is not as in the case of one who acquired with one [statement] etc., that what is in his hand is not asmakhta whether land or movable property, as Magen Avraham wrote — rather another reason in a matter that depends whether he acquires for him immediately, which is like saying to him from now, which is not so when he says if; and as Rav Hai Gaon wrote. And that which is written therefore one who gives a deposit refers only to the latter clause if the seller retracted; and this is what is written there, and likewise one who paid etc., and likewise all conditions etc., that whoever says if it will be etc. — all implies asmakhta that he did not acquire; therefore Rambam's words and Shulchan Aruch here are correct.

(ג) If he pledged it etc. and said to him. There, only the common case such as etc.; but a Jew who lent etc. But if he did not pledge it, even though he said to him from now the Jew does not acquire since they did not acquire from him through kinyan; and see Magen Avraham s.k. 5: even if there is not etc. and there is not etc. Tosafot s.v. shekoneh etc., and the whole sugya etc.
