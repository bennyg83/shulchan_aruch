(א) From now it shall be yours — meaning he said to him: acquire for yourself from now if [I do not repay] etc., and not merely as a general lien on the pledge.
(ב) And the time arrived and he did not pay him, etc. — and the reason is: since when the time arrived and he did not pay, it is acquired by him retroactively and it is the non-Jew's chametz over which Pesach passed, and it is permitted even for eating; therefore we require that he say to him from now, and also that the chametz be tevoi yadai — for if he did not say to him from now, behold on Pesach the chametz was the Jew's, only that afterward from when the time arrived and onward it was acquired by the non-Jew and is forbidden even in benefit to every Jew; and likewise if the chametz was not tevoi yadai, the chametz was not acquired for him at all even through from now, for with what did he acquire — behold he did not pull it to himself. However, if he transferred the chametz to the non-Jew through kinyan and from now if he will not pay by a set time, several acharonim hold that in this case even if the pledge remained tevoi yadai of the Jew it is permitted if the Jew conditioned that he does not accept liability for the pledge; and some are stringent on this — see Biur Halachah.
(ג) Permitted — meaning the chametz is permitted to derive sustenance from it; nevertheless l'chatchila the poskim were in doubt whether it is permitted for a Jew to lend in this manner even in his heart not to redeem it, since it is in his hand to redeem on Pesach — therefore perhaps he nevertheless transgresses; therefore one should be stringent and redeem before Pesach; and all the more so when at the time of the loan he intends to redeem after Pesach — certainly forbidden, for it is like hiding in his hand [Taz]; and Chayei Adam wrote: if at bi'ur time he said to the non-Jew he will not redeem again, there is no prohibition.
(ד) Even if the time did not arrive etc. — meaning: even though during Pesach the matter was in doubt lest he redeem it, nevertheless we do not forbid because of this after Pesach, as he concludes when the time arrived etc. And it appears from the Mechaber's view who wrote even that likewise if the time arrived before Pesach and he did not redeem, it is also not permitted unless he said to him at the time he pledged the chametz in his hand that it shall be his from now if he does not redeem it; and the reason is: without from now the gentile did not acquire his pledge even when the time they set arrived and he did not redeem, because it is mere asmakhta, and automatically the chametz is the Jew's and forbidden even after Pesach because of chametz over which Pesach passed; and likewise several acharonim. And some are lenient on this, and one may rely on them in a time of need; and all this is only regarding forbidding the chametz if Pesach already passed; but if it is still before Pesach, which is a d'oraisa concern, certainly one should be concerned for the stringent view that it is only asmakhta, and therefore he should appear to declare it to the non-Jew at the time bi'ur arrives.
(ה) For when the time arrived and he did not pay him etc. — and the acharonim wrote that from now being effective is specifically like in our case where he lent him money and the chametz was as a pledge, and in this we say from now is effective [for he certainly agreed to transfer to him in truth, since he did him a favor and lent him]; but without a loan, only a general condition, such as if he gave him his chametz and said to him: if I do not do for you a certain matter before Pesach the chametz shall be yours — and he did not fulfill the condition — even if he said to him from now, it is also asmakhta for many poskim [unless he transferred in an important court, as explained in Choshen Mishpat siman 207 seif 14 in Hagahah], and the chametz is still the Jew's and he must give the chametz to the non-Jew as a complete gift before Pesach; however b'dieved if Pesach passed, it appears we need not be stringent to forbid the chametz.
(ו) Retroactively — before Pesach, because he said from now, as above; and Magen Avraham was in doubt where he did not say to him from now whether the chametz is forbidden forever if he is obligated to redeem it from the non-Jew after Pesach and destroy it, for otherwise behold he benefits by paying his debt to the non-Jew from prohibited benefit; and in Beit Meir and Sefer Mekor Chayim they agreed to permit, for behold he does not pay his debt with his hands — only it remained in the non-Jew's hand by itself, and what the non-Jew does not claim him for this benefit is automatically not our concern; however with chametz in the Jew's hand that he pledged to the gentile and Pesach passed, it is forbidden in benefit, and likewise in this it is obvious it is forbidden to give it to the non-Jew, for this benefit itself.
