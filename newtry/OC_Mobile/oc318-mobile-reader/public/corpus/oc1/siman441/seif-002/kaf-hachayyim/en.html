If the Jew did not place it into the non-Jew's possession, but the non-Jew merely lent money against it while the chametz remained in the Jew's domain, then even if the Jew said that the transfer should be effective from the present, it is no better than chametz belonging to a non-Jew that is in a Jew's domain while the Jew bears liability for it, which is prohibited. Rosh; Taz, subsection 1; Magen Avraham, subsection 1. If the Jew said that the transfer should be effective from the present and conveyed it to the non-Jew through an act of acquisition or together with land, it is permitted even if it was not delivered to the non-Jew until after Pesach, provided that the Jew bore no liability for it throughout Pesach. Ri'az, cited by Shiltei HaGibborim in the chapter Kol Sha'ah; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur; Magen Avraham, there. Magen Avraham writes there that the symbolic acquisition must be performed with an item belonging to the non-Jew, as stated in Choshen Mishpat, siman 123, se'if 4. Shach there, subsection 30, however, writes that barter and symbolic acquisition are ineffective with a non-Jew; only money or physical transfer is effective, according to each authority's respective position. See there. Nehar Shalom, subsection 1 in this siman, agrees with him. See below, subsection 9.
