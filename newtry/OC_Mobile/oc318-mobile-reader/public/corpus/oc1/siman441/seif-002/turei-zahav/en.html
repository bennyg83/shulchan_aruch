Even if he is not liable for it etc. In the Gemara they say: a non-Jew who left bread as pledge with a Jew does not transgress, and Rashi in Pesachim explained: he holds a Jew also does not acquire a pledge from a non-Jew; and if he said to him I have reached you, he transgresses — and we explain it that he said from now I have reached you; and Rashi explained: from now he says — if I do not repay you by a certain day I transgress; and even though the time did not arrive, perhaps when it arrives he will not pay and he ends up transgressing retroactively bal yeira'eh — end quote. And per this there is a difficulty on Tur who mentioned the law after Pesach and did not mention the law applicable on Pesach that he must destroy it if the non-Jew said from now, even though there is no liability on him; and it appears Tur included this in what he wrote that if liability is on the Jew, even in a mere deposit he must destroy — and that is during Pesach days; if so, it is a fortiori to what he wrote where the non-Jew said from now and even though there is no liability, he must destroy on Pesach, for there the doubt is in a matter that may become entirely his, and it is stricter than Jew's liability where there is no side that the chametz itself belongs to the Jew, as in my humble opinion.
