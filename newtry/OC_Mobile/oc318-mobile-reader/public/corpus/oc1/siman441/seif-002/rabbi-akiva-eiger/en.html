(Magen Avraham s.k. 3) The Jew would have been obligated to pay — for although the gentile acquired the chametz retroactively, that is when he wishes to transfer it; the borrower penalized himself that if he does not pay the chametz will be acquired retroactively; but nevertheless the lender can say: I do not wish to acquire it, and can compel him to pay my debt, for the condition is not to the borrower's detriment but to the lender's detriment. And what Magen Avraham did not challenge further: if he is compelled, automatically the borrower will not wish to acquire it, and retroactively it becomes the Jew's chametz — and this too is not resolved in Magen Avraham's answer that it is gentile's chametz in a gentile's house; for behold it is Jew's chametz — meaning in truth on Pesach the Jew transgresses in doubt lest the borrower compel him to pay; but nevertheless now after Pesach the gentile agrees to receive the chametz for his debt, and the matter is revealed retroactively that the chametz was gentiles' and permitted in benefit; therefore Magen Avraham challenged only that although it was revealed it was gentile's chametz, nevertheless it was on the Jew's responsibility and forbidden after Pesach like chametz of a gentile deposited in a Jew's hand who accepted liability, which is forbidden after Pesach as Magen Avraham holds siman 440, and as Rambam wrote explicitly and as proven also from Rosh and Shulchan Aruch seif 1 that if he did not leave as pledge it is forbidden after Pesach because of liability [this is not like Chayei Adam]; and on this Magen Avraham properly answered that it is gentile's chametz in a gentile's house; for behold now that he did not compel him to pay, the matter is revealed that it is gentile's chametz; and per this reasoning what Magen Avraham wrote initially and Bach was in doubt whether permitted l'chatchila to lend a non-Jew etc. — at first glance there is no room for doubt, for certainly forbidden lest the gentile compel him to pay and it becomes Jew's chametz and he transgresses; examine. But per this reasoning there is a difficulty on seif 2: a Jew who lent etc. who acquires retroactively — behold the lender Jew can save his chametz and renounce its forbidden status by saying I do not wish to acquire it, and return the chametz to the gentile and compel him to pay; requires study.
