(א) Forbidden — see Mishna Berurah in what we wrote that he is obligated to destroy it before Pesach, and this is the view of all the acharonim, not like Magen Avraham; and see Mekor Chayim and Bigdei Yesha and Yad Ephraim, who wrote to uphold Magen Avraham's words. And the content of their words is nearly one: there is no need to destroy and lose the pledge with one's hands and lose his debt; it is better for him to wait until the time the non-Jew set, and perhaps he will pay his debt and redeem his pledge; and if he sees that soon the time will expire and he does not redeem, he may then forgive him the debt and say: I am as though I received payment, and its value as though the non-Jew paid — and the matter is revealed that the pledge belonged to the non-Jew and he did not transgress bal yeira'eh. But perhaps one should be concerned lest he forget to make this arrangement before the time arrives and come to bal yeira'eh; but in truth one should make this arrangement orally [meaning he forgives the debt before a court or before witnesses not in the non-Jew's presence, for that too is mechilah and automatically the pledge is not his; if so, even when the time arrives and he did not redeem, he did not transgress bal yeira'eh and the pledge remains tevoi yadai as Yad Ephraim intended] — and in this manner the chametz will also be permitted to him afterward; but perhaps it is like an evasion, like that in siman 448 seif 5 — see there.

(ב) But if he did not say to him — see what Mishna Berurah wrote: per the Mechaber's view, the same applies before Pesach — we need from now; and we inferred this from the Mechaber's language that wrote even, as Magen Avraham and Gra inferred in the seif before this. And it is further proven thus according to Rambam per Rambam that the Mishnah in chapter Kol Shehu and the whole sugya deal before Pesach; if so, when we establish the Mishnah where he said to him from now, the latter clause also refers to a non-Jew who lent a Jew on his chametz. And likewise there specifically when he said from now; otherwise the Jew does not acquire; and likewise proven further from that baraita brought in the Gemara regarding a non-Jew who left as pledge festival bread with a Jew that he does not transgress, and if he said to him I have reached you he transgresses — and they establish it at the outset that he did not say to him from now. And for Rambam it certainly deals before Pesach per Rambam's words; therefore also here if the non-Jew did not say to him from now the Jew does not acquire, because it is asmakhta, and he does not hold like Raavad that there is no asmakhta with a non-Jew. And I saw one of the acharonim who wrote the opposite of this, and it is proven in his words that certainly the other poskim do not disagree with Raavad's reasoning except in the first clause where the Jew left the pledge in the non-Jew's hand — and the reason is that although asmakhta does not apply in non-Jewish law, nevertheless one cannot obligate the Jew and declare his pledge in a gentile's hand forbidden, since in our law it is asmakhta; as we say in general: a Jew and a non-Jew who come to judgment — if you can vindicate him by Jewish law, vindicate him and say such is our law — unlike in the latter clause where the non-Jew left the pledge in the Jew's hand and by their law it is declared when the time arrives and he did not redeem; there the reasoning is reversed, and certainly we follow their law to leave the pledge in the Jew's hand, as we say: if you cannot vindicate by our law, etc.; if so, according to all, even if he did not say to him from now, if the time arrived before Pesach it is declared in the Jew's hand and he transgresses. In my humble opinion it appears as we wrote that it depends on the dispute cited, as proven from Magen Avraham and Gra in the Mechaber's view; and because one can say those who disagree with Raavad — their reason is there is no reason to distinguish here between non-Jew and Jew, for the reason asmakhta does not acquire is because he relies that it will not happen so and therefore does not finalize and acquire [as Gra challenged on this reasoning against Raavad's view]; if so, there is no difference between a Jew borrowing on his pledge and a non-Jew borrowing on his pledge — in both we do not put it in their minds that they will not redeem and they did not finalize and acquire. And what was proven from that we say if you cannot etc. vindicate him — one can say this reasoning does not help to establish it in his possession except from when they came to court and the court declares the pledge in his possession; and likewise if they came to court and established the pledge in his hand, none disagree it is his and he transgresses. However, our case deals in an unspecified manner when the time expired some days before Pesach and until they ceased borrowing and lending in this and finished the matter between them, or in court, Pesach days arrived — certainly by itself the pledge is not acquired by him because of his acquisition, for from the outset since it was asmakhta and now he certainly does not want this right to vindicate him by their law and it becomes forbidden in benefit and he loses his debt. And likewise I found in Peri Megadim who wrote explicitly as our words that this law also depends on the dispute of Raavad and Shulchan Aruch.
