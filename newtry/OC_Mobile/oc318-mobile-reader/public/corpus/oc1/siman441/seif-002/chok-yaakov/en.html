If he placed the collateral in his possession. Rosh Yosef asks according to Rosh and Rabbeinu Yonah, cited above in siman 440, seif katan 14, who hold that chametz belonging to a Jew and deposited with a non-Jew remains forbidden even if the non-Jew accepted liability. Here too, even though the chametz is in the non-Jew’s possession, it should be forbidden, because the Jew retroactively acquires it and it is then like a Jew’s chametz deposited with a non-Jew. In my opinion this is no difficulty. Delivery of the collateral is necessary because otherwise, even in an outright sale, money alone does not acquire from a non-Jew. See Choshen Mishpat, siman 134, and Shach there, seif katan 1. Furthermore, even according to the opinion that money ordinarily acquires, here the transaction is merely in the form of an asmachta and the Jew does not fully rely upon the non-Jew. Even saying “from now” is ineffective unless the collateral is delivered. See Choshen Mishpat, siman 73, seif 17, and what I wrote above in seif katan 3. Because he acquires it retroactively. It follows that the Jew violated the prohibitions of seeing and finding chametz. He must therefore nullify and remove it as his own chametz. This is implied by the poskim, and Bach writes likewise. This is unlike Magen Avraham, who writes that during Pesach the Jew is not obligated to remove it, and unlike Olas Shabbos, who writes that nullification alone suffices. I do not know the source of these leniencies. And he bears no liability for it. Ordinarily, a Jew bears no liability for collateral belonging to a non-Jew, as explained at the end of siman 440. See Tosafos, Kiddushin 8b, and Gittin 37, under the heading “Collateral is different.” These laws do not distinguish between collateral given at the time of the loan and collateral taken afterward. It is permitted. This is because a Jew does not acquire a pledge from a non-Jew. See below, siman 443, concerning collateral belonging to a Jew.
