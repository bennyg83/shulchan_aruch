(א) In his hand — for otherwise the pledge is not acquired for him, for he did not pull; unless he transferred it to the Jew through a valid kinyan and from now, as above in Mishna Berurah s.k. 1.

(ב) Forbidden — for it is chametz of a Jew over which Pesach passed, forbidden in benefit because he transgressed bal yeira'eh. And know the Mechaber stated its law regarding prohibition of the chametz after Pesach, and the same applies before Pesach regarding bal yeira'eh that he is obligated to destroy it or sell this chametz at the time of selling like his other chametz, lest the non-Jew not redeem when the time arrives as they set, and it turns out retroactively the chametz was his and he transgresses bal yeira'eh; and see Biur Halachah. And if he transgressed and did not destroy it and the non-Jew came during Pesach to redeem it, he may accept money from him and let him take his chametz [acharonim].

(ג) But if he did not say to him etc. — for we hold a Jew from a gentile and a gentile from a Jew do not acquire a pledge; it turns out it was not in his possession until after Pesach. And it appears from the Mechaber's view that likewise for its entire time before Pesach, if he did not pay, the chametz is also permitted after Pesach if he did not say to him from now, for asmakhta does not acquire; and we already wrote above some disagree that this is not asmakhta, and per that view it becomes chametz of a Jew and he must sell it before Pesach or destroy it; and one should be careful in this, for many stand on this view. However if Pesach passed, perhaps one may rely on the Mechaber's view here and it is permitted, as above in seif 1.

(ד) And he is not liable for it — for if he were liable for it, it would in any case be forbidden, for it is no worse than if the non-Jew's chametz were deposited with him with liability, in which case he transgresses, as above siman 440.

(ה) Permitted — and even if it appears from the non-Jew's intent that he intends it to remain in his (the Jew's) possession, nevertheless he does not transgress as long as he did not sell it to him explicitly; and this is when the pledge is between a Jew and a non-Jew. But a Jew who takes a pledge from his fellow Jew who lent him — even without from now, the lender acquires the pledge and he transgresses, for a Jew acquires a pledge from his fellow Jew; and see above at the end of siman 440 that the borrower also transgresses since the chametz itself is his.
