(א) And he said to him from now etc. — there is no law of asmakhta with a gentile. Meaning: in chapter 11 of Hilchot Mechirah Rambam and Raavad disagree. Rambam's view: in a matter the acquirer is holding, there is no asmakhta; therefore he wrote: the acquirer acquires a thing from his fellow and the acquirer gave the seller a deposit and said to him: if I retract, my deposit is forgiven you; and the seller says: if I retract I will double your deposit for you (meaning I will pay you double the deposit) — if the seller retracts he need not double his deposit, for it is asmakhta; but if the acquirer retracts, the seller acquired the deposit since the seller holds the deposit. Raavad disagrees and holds even what is in the acquirer's hand has asmakhta; if so, here where he said if I do not redeem by a set time it shall be yours, it is asmakhta; namely with from now asmakhta is removed, as written in Choshen Mishpat siman 207, since he said he should acquire from now — we see he transfers to him wholeheartedly and his intent is only to rely on complete acquisition; nevertheless where he did not say from now, per Raavad even in a pledge in a gentile's hand there is asmakhta, and therefore he said there is no asmakhta. And a gentile acquires in every matter.

(ב) Everything depends on from now. Meaning: even if the time arrived before Pesach, nevertheless from now is required, for otherwise it is asmakhta; even per Rambam who holds where it is in his hand we do not say asmakhta — nevertheless with a gentile in every matter his acquisition is weak and it is asmakhta even if it is in the gentile's hand; therefore from now is required to remove asmakhta. And such is Rav Yosef Karo's view in Shulchan Aruch that wrote even if the time he set did not arrive until after Pesach — implying not only when the time arrived before Pesach is the law so, but even when the time arrived after Pesach the law is likewise, for such is the nature of the language everywhere; and likewise Rav Yosef Karo. Behold also when the time arrived before Pesach he holds from now is required, for specifically in a pledge from now is effective. For in siman 297 seif 9 he wrote: he lent him on a field worth a hundred gold and it is worth more, and said to him: if you do not pay me within three years it shall be mine — it is asmakhta; but if he said: if you do not pay me within three years it shall be mine from now, it is not asmakhta — it is clear from now asmakhta is removed; and in seif 14 Rama wrote: and some say from now alone is insufficient to remove asmakhta unless he acquires from him in an important court and from now; and in seif 9 Rama did not emend — implying he agrees. Therefore Semag wrote s.k. 18, and such is his language: this case is different (in a pledge), for they gave it to his hand immediately as a pledge and lien on his money, and it is like a sale; and also he did him a favor by lending him; therefore we say his intent is it should be his from then, a complete acquisition, only he conditioned it should be in his hand to redeem within three years — end quote Semag; if so, the same here without lending him money, only he said to him: if I do not do for you etc., even if the time arrived before Pesach it is forbidden, for it is asmakhta per the view Rama brought in Choshen Mishpat unless he acquired in an important court.

(ג) And see what is written siman 440 seif 1: we do not follow their law. For he wrote there in Shulchan Aruch: even if he is not liable for it by law, only the gentile was compelled etc., he is obligated to destroy it; and some disagree, and their dispute depends on the Gemara sugya where Rava said to the people of Mechoza: in your city chametz of soldiers is forbidden, since these steal or lose and you will need to pay etc.; and Rambam holds there was no obligation from the law, only from compulsion, and nevertheless Rava said they must destroy. Raavad holds where the obligation is only from compulsion, destruction is not required, only where it was from the law and royal decree — see there. And what is written siman 448 seif 1 that he brought Mishna Berurah's view that we follow their law regarding acquisition, and Magen Avraham holds we do not follow their law — see there. But in truth one can distinguish between the law of asmakhta explained in this siman and the law of acquisition explained in siman 448, as Magen Avraham wrote in siman 448.
