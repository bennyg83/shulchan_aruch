Permitted. Taz: behold the dispute of Rosh and Raavad with Rabbeinu Efraim and Rambam is explained well in Magen Avraham chapter 4 of Choshen Mishpat, and I will explain briefly: behold Rambam and Rabbeinu Efraim — all three conditions: left as pledge, did not redeem, and from now we require specifically before Pesach; after Pesach from now is not effective, since for stringency and it is in his hand to redeem (and of Heaven's property one who does not transgress must distinguish — see Tosafot Pesachim 46b s.v. he'eil 2). And before Pesach three conditions: first, without from now the non-Jew does not acquire; and although Rabbeinu Efraim from Mechirah holds asmakhta does not apply to a pledge, a non-Jew is weaker still; and likewise without leaving as pledge — with what will he acquire? Or because of liability; and likewise if he transferred through kinyan to a minor and without liability, one need not leave as pledge; and see Magen Avraham 2, explained for us not to rush without leaving as pledge. And Rosh and Raavad's view: those three conditions apply when the time arrived after Pesach and it is effective; before Pesach two suffice: he did not redeem and left as pledge, even if he did not say from now, for there is no asmakhta with a non-Jew; and leaving as pledge is required — with what will he acquire? Or because of liability; and where he transferred through kinyan and conditioned there is no liability on him, it is permitted when the time arrived before Pesach without leaving as pledge and without from now. And from Taz's conclusion it appears the Mechaber also holds that before Pesach without from now it is permitted; and the word even explains not entirely equating them; and likewise above; and see Levush and Magen Avraham note 2 did not explain so. And Taz ruled: if he intends thus it is forbidden; and if he lent him and bi'ur time arrived and he did not redeem, we do not compel him to redeem — implying l'chatchila in any case it is forbidden to borrow from the non-Jew on such a condition lest he redeem; and even if he finalizes in his mind not to redeem, nevertheless l'chatchila it is forbidden because of since it is in his hand to redeem; and although b'dieved it is permitted because he lacks money it is not considered since, as Ran wrote, or how could he redeem since it is forbidden in benefit — but l'chatchila forbidden and inexpensive. And in R' Akiva Eiger note 2: even l'chatchila one may lend in this manner if his intent is not to redeem after Pesach — see there.
