(s.k. 7) They do not wish. For it does not depend on willingness — whenever he can bury he is obligated to attend burial and aninut applies; therefore Shulchan Aruch wrote "and he does not wish" — meaning he has no ability; Shulchan Aruch afterward "and he wishes to bury" — meaning he has ability; question on Shulchan Aruch below also on second Yom Tov this language "and he wishes to bury" is redundant since permitted to bury, aninut applies even if some coercion prevents burial that day; so Mechaber Yoreh De'ah 341:1: on first Yom Tov if he wishes to bury through gentiles he practices aninut; on second Yom Tov it does not say "if he wishes" but since he himself may bury, aninut applies; see above 426:4; strained to say Shulchan Aruch here on second Yom Tov "if he wishes" comes to exclude total coercion such as corpse in captivity — as Yoreh De'ah 341; why teach these laws on Yom Tov since on weekday too the law is so — requires some study; know all Shulchan Aruch seif 7 is from Rosh Moed Katan as is, and there he did not write "if they wish to bury" only on first Yom Tov; on second Yom Tov he wrote anonymously aninut applies — see there:
