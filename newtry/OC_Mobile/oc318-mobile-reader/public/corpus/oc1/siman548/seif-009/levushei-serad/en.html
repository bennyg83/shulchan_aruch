Seif katan 7. They do not want. The matter does not depend on his will, for whenever he can bury him he is obligated to involve himself in the burial, and aninus takes effect upon him. Therefore what the Shulchan Aruch wrote, 'and he does not want,' means that he does not have the ability. What the Shulchan Aruch later wrote, 'and he wants to bury him,' means that he has the ability. However, there is a slight difficulty in that the Shulchan Aruch nearby also uses this phrase regarding the second day of Yom Tov, 'and he wants to bury him,' which seems unnecessary, since because it is permitted to bury him aninus takes effect even if he has some compulsion preventing him from burying him that day. This is also implied by the Mechaber in Yoreh Deah, siman 341, seif 1: regarding the first day of Yom Tov he wrote, if he wants to bury him through non-Jews he practices aninus, while regarding the second day he did not write 'if he wants,' but wrote that since he himself can, meaning is permitted to, bury him, aninus takes effect. See above, siman 526, seif 4. It is forced to say that the Shulchan Aruch's phrase here regarding the second day, 'if he wants to bury him,' comes to exclude a complete compulsion, such as where the deceased is in custody and the like, as explained in Yoreh Deah, siman 341, for why would he need to teach these laws on Yom Tov when the same law applies on a weekday? This requires some study. Know that all the words of the Shulchan Aruch in this seif are from the Rosh in Moed Katan 23, and there he wrote 'if they want to bury' only regarding the first day of Yom Tov, but regarding the second day he wrote simply that aninus takes effect.
