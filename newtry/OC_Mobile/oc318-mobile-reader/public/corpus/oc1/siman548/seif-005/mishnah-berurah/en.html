(17) Aninus applies to him: he is forbidden in meat and wine, along with all laws that pertain to aninus. See Yoreh Deah, siman 341.
(18) And he does not want to bury him: meaning that the non-Jews do not want to bury him.
(19) From the time it becomes dark: but not before. This applies specifically if it is becoming dark; if not, even though it is close to evening he remains obligated in all mitzvos. The Magen Avraham wrote that this applies specifically at the time it becomes dark, but on the night of the second day of Yom Tov he is obligated in all mitzvos, for only on the daytime of the second day of Yom Tov did the Sages treat it like a weekday, not on the night before, since people do not usually bury at night. According to this, if one's dead relative died on Shemini Atzeres, he may recite kiddush on the night of Simchas Torah and recite HaMotzi and Birkas HaMazon.
(20) And he wants to bury him that day: this is not precise; it means whenever he has the ability to bury him. This excludes a case where he cannot be buried because of a non-Jewish holiday or another compulsion, in which case the law of aninus does not take effect upon him. See above, siman 71, Mishnah Berurah seif katan 11.
(21) Aninus takes effect upon him: an onen on the festival is forbidden in Torah study, for he is forbidden in joy, and the precepts of Hashem are upright and gladden the heart. All the more so he may not receive an aliyah to the Torah, as stated by the Magen Avraham. From his words it seems that even when he does not want to bury him, on the first day of Yom Tov, or at night when burial is not customary, he is still forbidden in Torah study. See Dagul Merevavah, who challenges this: since at that time the law of aninus is not upon him, why should he be forbidden in Torah study? The work Bigdei Yesha also questions this; see there.
