Private matters. See Baer Heitev. It appears from the words of the Magen Avraham that even Torah study is permitted only in sorrowful subjects. But the Chacham Tzvi, siman 100, wrote that he ruled for a sage who was accustomed to reviewing Mishnayos by heart that he may review them throughout the entire festival. Although private mourning matters apply, nevertheless it is permitted. First, there is no greater loss than this, for if he does not review them he will certainly forget them, even though we generally find permission for loss only in matters that do not themselves gladden. Second, during the rest of the year he can study sorrowful matters in Yirmiyahu and the like, but on the festival it is not proper to engage in those matters, and it is not proper to sit entirely idle from Torah. Also, in such a case the Rambam, who holds that on the festival even private mourning matters do not apply, is worthy to rely upon. See Yoreh Deah, siman 399, in the Tur and Beit Yosef, that the Rambam is not alone. Therefore, even where there is no loss, regarding Mishnayos fluent in one's mouth one may rely on the Rambam and those who support him to study all matters of Torah. If so, it is obvious that if he wants to review the festival parsha, it is permitted, even though there is no obligation, for it is no worse than other study that is permitted. See there.
