Even though mourning is not observed on the festival, aninus is observed, etc. Note: behold I was asked about one who died on the second Yom Tov, and they would not allow him to be buried until close to evening according to the king's religion, may his glory be exalted, whether the law of aninus applies in this or not. And I answered that it appears obvious that aninus applies in this; for even though Magen Avraham wrote that where it is impossible to bury him because of fear of a gentile, aninus does not take effect, that is [only] if the entire day it is impossible to bury him; but if today it is possible to bury him, only after some time, we do not split the body of the day itself, and whenever he will be buried that day, aninus applies to him. And it is not comparable to what we hold that if he died on Yom Tov, then at night he may recite Kiddush and aninus does not take effect upon him: that too is because night and day are a recognizable change and are separate entities, so aninus does not take effect upon him in between; but within the day itself, where the change is not recognizable, we do not split it into two. And behold some wished to bring proof from what Magen Avraham wrote above in siman 526, that it is possible that he holds that since his intent is not to bury him until after the meal, the law of aninus does not take effect upon them until after the meal; see there; and if so the same would apply here too. But in my opinion the very words of Magen Avraham are puzzling: for if there is reason to distinguish so, what proof does the Rosh in chapter 3 of Berachos bring from the Rambam that there is no aninus on Yom Tov, from the fact that he would command to bring a shofar before him to discharge his obligation? And what proof is that: perhaps there his intent was to bury them after the meal, so aninus did not take effect upon him, but where his intent is to bury them beforehand there is a law of aninus on Yom Tov; and perforce that reasoning is not [accepted]. However, even according to Magen Avraham's words it is not comparable: first, behold we see that Magen Avraham was precise specifically if his intent is to bury him after the meal, but if his intent were to bury him after the prayer they would be exempt from the prayer; and perforce we require something else that interrupts in between, between the mitzvah and the burial: there must be something else that interrupts and makes the distinction recognizable, like day and night, so that at night aninus does not take effect upon him; so too here. But if there is no interrupting change in between, he is exempt from the prayer; and if so here too, since between the hour they do not allow him to bury him and the hour they will allow him to bury him there is no recognizable change. For granted there, since the matter depends on the meal and it is something depending on an act and is recognizable, therefore it is possible to distinguish within the day itself; but between hours, where there is no recognizable change, it is impossible to split the day itself into two. And further: granted [regarding] after the meal, since this is a fixed time for all the dead and it is known that today they do not bury except after the meal, therefore there is no disgrace to the dead in this, for everyone knows that his burial time was not until after the meal; but here, by the king's religion, since the hours are not all equal and there is a dead person whom they allow to bury in the morning when his time is then finished, if so who would know that this one they are not burying because his time has not finished? And therefore there is disgrace to the deceased if they occupy themselves with prayer; and for this reason it is forbidden for an onen to be stringent on account of the honor of the dead; so too here disgrace of the dead applies, and therefore it is forbidden to pray. And however [granted] that if they do not allow him to be buried the entire day, aninus does not take effect upon him: there, when the world will see that they did not bury him the entire day, they will know that this is because it was impossible to bury him, and there is no disgrace to the deceased in this, since his burial is literally on another day; but on that very day itself, if his burial will be today, one may say that from negligence they are slack regarding him, and then it is possible to split the day itself into two where there is no fixed time equal for all the dead; therefore they certainly have the law of aninus. And likewise if there is a delay and they do not wish to allow him to be buried until tomorrow, yet the onenim are able to exert themselves to bury him today close to evening, aninus also takes effect upon him in the morning; and it is not comparable to one who is imprisoned and they have not despaired of burying him, upon whom aninus does not take effect but only mourning: for there at present he is not buried at all, so even though they can exert themselves to bury him, since it is doubtful he has no law of aninus; but here it is known that he will be buried tomorrow, and since it is certain he will be buried tomorrow and also it is possible through exertion that he will be buried today, aninus takes effect upon them from today. And especially there the matter depends on much money and it is doubtful whether it will help, so aninus does not take effect upon them; but here with small sums it will help and it is nearly certain that it will help, so aninus takes effect upon them. And on the contrary it appears that for this reason aninus takes effect upon them from now, even if they will not bury him until after the meal or after a full day and night finishes: nevertheless from now this exertion takes effect upon them, and therefore it is like one who darkens upon the techum, upon whom aninus takes effect from when he begins to darken upon the techum; so too here; and this is clear and correct for the practical law, that they are exempt from all the mitzvos as the law of other onenim on Yom Tov; and see a clear proof for this from the words of Magen Avraham himself, siman 696, s.k. 6, from which it is proven there that even though by law it is forbidden to bury until after the Megillah, nevertheless aninus takes effect upon him and he is exempt, and all the more so in this; see there and examine. And even according to Magen Avraham's conclusion there, that is only regarding the Megillah, for Megillah precedes burial of the dead as Magen Avraham wrote; and also in Sefer Eliyah Rabbah he disagrees with Magen Avraham also there; but in our case he certainly may not pray; and examine carefully. (There:) And if he died on Yom Tov and does not wish to bury him that day, he has no law of aninus, etc. Note: behold from this it is clarified that even though his intent is to bury him at night, nevertheless there is no law of aninus on Yom Tov, since his intent is not to bury him today; and all the more so on Shabbos and Yom Kippur, when it is forbidden to bury him that day, that there is no law of aninus upon him. And I wonder greatly from the Gemara of Yoma, folio 13, which asks: and did not R' Yehudah say they prepare another wife for him lest his wife die, and if his wife dies in the middle of the service he becomes an onen, etc.? And what is the question, and how does it compare Yom Kippur to a weekday? Behold one may say that on Yom Kippur, since it is impossible to bury her today, aninus does not take effect upon him; and one should not say that regarding an onen with sacred offerings we do not follow whether he can bury or not, for from where [would we know] to ask thus? And perhaps also for aninus of sacred offerings aninus takes effect upon him only if he wishes to bury today, for this is learned from the verse of Aharon, 'behold today they offered,' etc., and there it was possible to bury that very day; but where burial that day is not available, perhaps aninus does not take effect upon him at all; so what is the question? And this requires great further study. And even though there is one Tanna in Moed Katan that Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur are not like the festivals, nevertheless what is the question? Let us say that R' Yehudah holds like the view that Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur are like the festivals, and therefore aninus is not observed on it if he does not bury him today. In short, this requires further study; and examine. And see in my composition on Orach Chaim of the year 5596, folio 132, that a certain scholar named Moreinu HaRav Ichel of the holy community of Buczacz wished to refute the aforementioned difficulty, and I refuted all his words, and the difficulty remains in its place; see there carefully. And behold, while speaking of the aforementioned sugya of Yoma, it appears to decide from this regarding that in which the Taz and the Shach disagree in Yoreh De'ah, siman 341, and Nekudas HaKesef brings on this a dispute of the Rishonim: if mourning occurred to a groom after the chuppah and he has not yet had relations, whether mourning takes effect upon him first or the seven days of feasting; see there. And in my humble opinion one may prove from this: for seemingly it is difficult what the Gemara asks, that R' Yehudah said perhaps his wife will die, and if she dies he performs the service and behold he is an onen, etc.; and what is the question? Perhaps wherever it is possible to arrange we arrange, and what is impossible we do not arrange; and especially since the Gemara concludes in Sukkah that R' Yehudah is not concerned for death, only for a mere additional stringency he is concerned for death: if so, that is fine regarding what touches the atonement of all Israel, but regarding what touches the prohibition of aninus and lest he eat thereby, we are not concerned for death; so what is the question? And one must say that the Gemara understood from R' Yehudah's language, that he said they prepare another wife for him, that perforce he marries her before Yom Kippur, for if on Yom Kippur he would not need prior arrangement, not like the Yerushalmi; and therefore it is difficult for it: why in truth does he not marry her on Yom Kippur? And perforce [it is] because of a shevus, that one may not effect kiddushin on Shabbos and Yom Tov; and wherever it is possible to arrange so that he not come to such [a problem], we arrange; and therefore it asks: since the concern is because of a shevus that one may not effect kiddushin, all the more should we say that he not marry now, for if he marries her now perhaps his wife will die and he will be an onen; it is better to wait to marry her on Yom Kippur, and then when he sees that she is about to die he would marry her then, like the Gemara asks there in a similar fashion; see there. So one may say here: since he sees that she is about to die, he would marry her on condition if the other dies, and there would not be two houses; and then if she dies the days of feasting take effect upon him retroactively and he would not further become an onen; and if so it would be more proper not to be concerned for a shevus prohibition, which is not so now that he married beforehand, for we require a chuppah fit for relations; and granted on Yom Kippur the chuppah fit for relations would be in the evening, and that is considered fit for relations as I wrote in our novellae previously; see there; but if he marries beforehand, behold for all seven days they separate him from his house, and perforce since he marries beforehand the seven days of feasting finish before Yom Kippur, so he would have the law of an onen; thus goes the Gemara's difficulty that he should have been more concerned for the prohibition of aninus than for the shevus prohibition that one may not effect kiddushin; and examine, for I abbreviated. And from this it is proven that even without relations the days of feasting take effect upon him first, and he does not even have the law of an onen, for on Yom Kippur he will not have relations, and it asks thus; and understand, for I abbreviated. And behold I returned and saw that this is a mistake for several reasons: for even though the seven days of feasting override mourning, nevertheless they do not override aninus if they wish to bury him today, for they are not superior to Yom Tov, on which aninus is observed if they bury him today; and to say that behold his intent is to bury him today is already difficult without this, as above. And further, kiddushin without chuppah is not his house, and perforce he brings her in, and if so again it is impossible to say [it is] on condition, for there is no condition in chuppah. And further, either way: if he betroths her from now, then if the first dies, retrospectively he performed the service with two houses; and if he betroths her only on condition if she dies, then if she dies aninus takes effect upon him from the beginning and afterwards the joy. And further, behold the Gemara concludes that its difficulty is not because of aninus but perhaps he will be distracted, and behold this is not fixed even if he betroths her and brings her in and the days of feasting take effect upon him: nevertheless, will distraction not distract? And behold in the end it is impossible to fix the matter; and therefore those words that we wrote are not [correct]. Nevertheless a teaching does not move from its place for mere pilpul; and examine carefully.
