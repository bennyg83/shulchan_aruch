Aninus. That he is forbidden in meat and wine; see Yoreh De'ah, siman 341.
From when it grows dark. And Magen Avraham wrote: but on the night of the second Yom Tov he is obligated in all the mitzvos, for specifically regarding the second day the Rosh wrote that the Sages made it like a weekday; but regarding the night before it, no; and all the more so for us, who practice burial needs through a gentile, that he is obligated at night; and so wrote Maharil: where a death occurred to him on Shemini Atzeres, it is permitted to recite Kiddush on the night of Simchas Torah, but by day everyone agrees he is exempt; however he should not say the Haggadah on Pesach, since it contains midrash of verses (Maharam of Lublin); and so wrote the Derishah in Yoreh De'ah, siman 341; and so ruled the Taz, not like the Shach there; see there. And it is implied that by day, even if he does not wish to bury him now, the law of aninus takes effect upon him, for the Sages made it like a weekday. However it appears to me that if it is impossible to bury him because of fear of the gentile or because of another forced circumstance, the law of aninus does not take effect upon him. And it is implied from a responsum of Maharam that it is forbidden to be called up to the Torah; end of his words; see there.
