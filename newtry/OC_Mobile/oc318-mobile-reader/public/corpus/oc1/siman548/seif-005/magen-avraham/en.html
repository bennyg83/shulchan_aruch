Aninus applies to him. That he is forbidden in meat and wine; see Yoreh De'ah, siman 341.
And he does not wish to bury him. That the gentiles do not wish to bury him.
From when it grows dark. But at a time when he is not occupied with burial needs, the law of aninus does not apply to him (the Rosh). But on the night of the second Yom Tov he is obligated in all the mitzvos, for specifically regarding the second day the Rosh wrote that the Sages made it like a weekday; but regarding the night before it, no; and all the more so for us, who practice burial needs through a gentile, that he is obligated at night; and so wrote Maharil: where a death occurred to him on Shemini Atzeres, it is permitted to recite Kiddush on the night of Simchas Torah, but by day everyone agrees he is exempt; however he should not say the Haggadah on Pesach, since it contains midrash of verses (Maharam of Lublin there). And so wrote the Derishah in Yoreh De'ah, siman 341; and so ruled the Taz, not like the Shach there who disagrees: his view is nullified against their view; and so he ruled below at the end of siman 696 regarding Purim, and all the more so on Yom Tov. And it is implied that by day, even if he does not wish to bury him now, the law of aninus takes effect upon him, for the Sages made it like a weekday; and so it is implied in siman 71; and so it is in Taz Yoreh De'ah and in Beis Yosef there, siman 393; and so it is implied in the Rosh. However it appears to me that if it is impossible to bury him because of fear of the gentile or because of another forced circumstance, the law of aninus does not take effect upon him, for it is similar to one who is imprisoned, as written in Yoreh De'ah, siman 348; and see what I wrote at the end of siman 526. And it is implied from a responsum of Maharam above that an onen on the festival is forbidden in words of Torah; and so it is implied in Yoreh De'ah 341, se'if 5 in the gloss, where he wrote that he is forbidden in rejoicing, and 'the precepts of Hashem are upright, rejoicing the heart,' and all the more so that it is forbidden to be called up to the Torah.
