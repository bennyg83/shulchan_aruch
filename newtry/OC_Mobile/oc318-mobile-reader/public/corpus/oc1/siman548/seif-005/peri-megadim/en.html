(א) And it is so — Taz; and see here siman 18, section 14, siman 502 seif 10, and Shach letter 8: a close relative on Shabbat erev the festival — Shabbat counts as day one and Yom Tov cancels the decree of seven; and Eliyah Rabbah here: a close relative — we count from the day of death, not from burial — end of Shach there letter 5; and in Nekudas HaKesef there he left it requiring further study; and Taz there letter 6 ruled close and distant from the day of death. And see letter 9 there regarding year time; however, to be a doubt even so, all agree in Shulchan Aruch that whoever did not know at all about the festival does not count toward seven; and even Mishna Berurah in the name of Maharshal would agree so; and specifically when he died on the festival and knew — they hold that in any case it counts toward thirty — Magen Avraham letter 1. Eliyah Rabbah 533 letter 10: they light a candle all seven days in a room where they do not eat, so as not to cause distress — and the festival does not cancel this; kaddish — one may say the festival does not cancel and he has the law of seven and thirty even if he observed an hour before the festival, see there; and requires further study somewhat — and in Peri [Megadim] there we wrote.

Ateret Zekenim wrote letter 7: if seven days passed from death one does not comfort the mourner afterward; likewise when erev Shabbat falls on last Yom Tov, even if seven days from death were not completed, one does not call "go out against the mourner" for it is public — Taz 427 beginning of siman. Requires study on close report: do they call "go out against the mourner" since seven days from death already passed? Perhaps all who did not know there is no comfort; specifically when he knew he already comforted himself that seven days passed — unlike this case.
