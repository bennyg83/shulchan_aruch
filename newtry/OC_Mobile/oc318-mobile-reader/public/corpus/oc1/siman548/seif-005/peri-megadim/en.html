And therefore - See Taz; and see here se'if 18; see Yoreh De'ah, siman 402, se'if 10, and Shach letter 8: recent tidings on Shabbos that is the eve of the festival: Shabbos counts as day one, and Yom Tov cancels the decree of seven. And see Eliyah Rabbah here: recent and distant tidings we count from the day of death, not from burial; see Shach there, letter 5; and in Nekudas HaKesef there he left it as requiring further study; and in Taz there, [s.k.] 6, he ruled that both recent and distant [are counted] from the day of death. And see letter 9 there regarding yahrzeit. However, to create a double doubt even in such a case, everyone agrees that Shemini Atzeres [counts], wherever he did not know at all on the festival that it does not count as seven; and even Mish'as Binyamin in the name of Maharshal will concede in such a case; and only when he died on the festival and he knew do they hold that at least it counts toward thirty; see Magen Avraham [s.k.] 1. See Eliyah Rabbah 133, letter 10: they light a candle all seven in a room where they do not eat, so as not to cause anguish, and the festival does not cancel this; [regarding] Kaddish one may say the festival does not cancel [it], and he has the law of seven and of thirty even if he observed [mourning] for an hour before the festival; see there; and this requires a bit of further study; and in Pri [Megadim] there we wrote [about it].
Wrote. Eliyah Rabbah, letter 7: if seven [days] from the death of the deceased have passed, one should no longer console the mourner afterwards; and likewise when Friday falls on the last Yom Tov, even though seven from the death of the deceased were not completed, one should not proclaim 'Go out' opposite the mourner, for that is public; see Taz 127 at the beginning of the siman. And this requires further study regarding one who heard recent tidings, whether they proclaim 'Go out' opposite the mourner, since there are already seven from the death of the deceased, or [whether] one should say that as long as he did not know there is no consolation, and [the restriction applies] specifically when he already knew and had comforted himself that seven days had passed, which is not so in such a case. Regarding commerce: wherever seven from the death of the deceased were completed, it is permitted in private through his servants or through others in their homes, as Rabbeinu Yeruham wrote; and a partner in a shop, one may say public [commerce] is forbidden. And see Eliyah Rabbah, letter 6: I did not understand [him regarding] recent tidings, [that] even though seven from the death of the deceased were completed, commerce is forbidden as with other mourning: [that is] specifically on the festival, when he refrains from work for all seven, as we said. And the remaining matters will be clarified, God willing, in Magen Avraham.
