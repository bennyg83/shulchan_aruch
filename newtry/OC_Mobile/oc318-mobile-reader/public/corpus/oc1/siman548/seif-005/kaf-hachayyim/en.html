Maharshal, Responsa siman 5; Mishnah Berurah, siman 71; Derishah on Yoreh Deah, siman 399, subsection 2. This also appears to be the view of Shach there, subsection 7. Hagahos Maimoniyos, chapter 10, however, writes that Shemini Atzeres counts in place of seven specifically when the burial occurred before Sukkos, as written below, se'if 16. In the present case, where burial occurred during the festival, it counts only as one day. See there. Beis Yosef cites this in Yoreh Deah, siman 399. Shulchan Aruch rules accordingly there, se'if 2, as do Levush there, Bach there, and Taz there, subsection 4, and in this siman, subsection 4. Taz writes that this is unlike Maharshal and Mishnah Berurah, which follows him. See there. This is also the view of Magen Avraham, subsection 1; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 13; Yad Aharon, Hagahos HaTur; Beis David, siman 314, who writes that this is the proper practice; Nehar Shalom, subsection 1; and Birkei Yosef, subsection 8, who writes that this is the custom and that one should not apply here the rule that the halachah follows the lenient opinion in mourning. See there. He writes likewise in Yosef Ometz, siman 93, subsection 2. Bigdei Yesha also agrees with the aforementioned ruling of Taz. Chochmas Adam, rule 169, subsection 2; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 4, rule likewise. Chochmas Adam nevertheless writes that since some say Shemini Atzeres counts in place of seven, one may be lenient for a distressed person and similar cases regarding bathing and regarding women. See there. This follows Rama's statement in Yoreh Deah, siman 381, se'if 1, in the gloss, that nowadays the custom is to forbid all bathing throughout the thirty days. Under the strict law, however, bathing is permitted after seven days. See there. In a place that does not follow Rama's custom, bathing is permitted after seven days. See below, subsection 37.
