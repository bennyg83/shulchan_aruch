In the Shulchan Aruch, seif 8: and needless to say. If his eighth day falls on Erev Shabbos, etc. See the new responsa of the Bach, siman 12, who permits him to return to his place immediately at the entrance of that Shabbos. He may also wear Shabbos clothing, but he may not enter a feast on that Shabbos. See there. This is also the view of the Beis Hillel in Yoreh Deah, siman 399. However, Shevus Yaakov, part 1, siman 90, responded to the Bach and holds that returning to his place and wearing Shabbos clothing is forbidden. He wrote that no proof can be brought from a case where his thirtieth day falls on Shabbos, where the Rama is lenient because one can say that the twenty-ninth day is like the thirtieth, as appears from the Darkei Moshe, siman 400. Nevertheless it appears that since the Bach permits in this matter, which is only a custom, one who rules leniently has not issued an erroneous ruling, as the Shevus Yaakov wrote, for he has upon whom to rely. What he wrote from the Darkei Moshe is no proof, for perhaps since it cannot be done on Shabbos he needed to combine the idea that the twenty-ninth is like the thirtieth regarding washing, which is forbidden by law, and one might have thought it forbidden. But changing place and wearing Shabbos clothes, which are only a custom, are permitted. Perhaps if the Shevus Yaakov had seen the words of the Bach, he would have retracted and ruled like the Beis Hillel. Nevertheless, regarding wearing Shabbos clothes it appears proper to be stringent if they are new and their pressing is still very noticeable. See Yoreh Deah, siman 389, seif 5, and below in the Magen Avraham, siman 551, seif katan 20. If he has no others, perhaps one may be lenient even in this.
