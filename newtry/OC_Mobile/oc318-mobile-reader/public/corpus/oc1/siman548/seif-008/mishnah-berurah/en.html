(29) Even if the seventh day falls on Erev Yom Tov, etc.: we hold that part of the day is like the whole day regarding mourning. Therefore, the seventh day counts both ways: the beginning of the seventh day completes the seven days, and afterward the eighth day begins, which is considered the start of the thirty days. Since one day of the thirty has begun, he need not observe anything further from the thirty after the festival, for the festival comes and interrupts it. More than this, even on that day itself he may shave and launder, even with pressing, in honor of the festival.
(30) And the same applies to washing according to our practice, etc.: meaning that even for us, who generally practice that washing is forbidden all thirty days, nevertheless in our case it is no stricter than hair cutting, and even washing in hot water is permitted.
(31) Close to evening: so that it should be recognizable that it is for the honor of the festival. This law applies even if the eighth or ninth day falls on Erev Yom Tov. This is specifically on every other Erev Yom Tov, but on Erev Pesach it is permitted anytime after midday, meaning from the time of slaughtering the Pesach offering onward, for then it is like Yom Tov and labor is forbidden. Therefore washing is permitted immediately after midday, and so is laundering. But shaving is permitted even before midday, since afterward he may not shave after midday, as stated in the laws of Pesach.
(32) And he need not wait until night: this refers to washing, for hair cutting and laundering are forbidden at night and certainly must be done close to evening, namely after Minchah, as follows.
(33) He should not shave, etc.: it is obvious that the same applies to laundering.
