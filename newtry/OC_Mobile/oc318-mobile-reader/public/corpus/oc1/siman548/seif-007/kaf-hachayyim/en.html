7) [Se'if 2] He counts the seven days from the final second day of Yom Tov, etc. The same applies to the two days of Rosh Hashanah. For example, if a relative died on the first day of Rosh Hashanah, he begins counting the seven from the second day, which is also ordained by the Sages. Bach, at the end of Yoreh Deah, siman 399; Shach there, subsection 8. Taz there, subsection 7, however, distinguishes: the second day counts specifically when burial occurred on the second day of Rosh Hashanah. If burial occurred on the first day, the second day does not count. See there. Taz writes likewise in this siman, subsection 1, as do Mabit, volume 2, siman 89, and Perach Mateh Aharon, volume 1, siman 98. Magen Avraham, subsection 2, however, writes that one may be lenient in accordance with Bach and those who agree with him. See there. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 2, writes likewise. Toras Chessed, siman 4, and Ginas Veradim, Yoreh Deah, rule 5, siman 7, agree with Bach as practical halachah. See there. Beis David, siman 313, likewise agrees, as do Shulchan Gavoah, subsection 3; Yeshuos Yaakov, subsection 3; Mateh Yehudah in Shevet Yehudah on Yoreh Deah, siman 399, who writes that this is their custom. Maharam Amarillo likewise testifies that this was the practical ruling in the beis din of Rabbi Perach Mateh Aharon. Toras Chaim, volume 3, in Olelos HaKerem, siman 11, cites this. Yad Aharon, Hagahos Beis Yosef; Erech HaShulchan, subsection 1; Nehar Shalom, subsection 2; Chochmas Adam, rule 169, subsection 2; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 8, write likewise.
