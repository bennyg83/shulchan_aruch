And the days of the festival count for him toward the thirty. In Yoreh De'ah, siman 399, the Shulchan Aruch wrote: behold, the law of thirty, namely pressing [garments] and haircutting, applies on the festival, and it is forbidden not merely as festival law alone, but even as mourning law; for as festival law one may wear newly pressed garments and white ones, etc., yet by the law of mourning he is forbidden in all of them; and this is the view of the Ramban. But the Tur wrote there in the name of the Rosh that even by the law of mourning, on the festival that which was permitted on the festival is permitted; and I wrote in Yoreh De'ah there that one should rule like the Ramban and not like Sefer Mish'as Binyamin who ruled like the Rosh. I further wrote there regarding a groom who married a wife on the eve of the festival and during the festival a death occurred to him, that that sefer ruled that even though an ordinary groom to whom mourning occurred during the seven days of feasting does not count thirty except after the days of feasting, etc., nevertheless for this groom the days of feasting count toward the thirty; and I wrote there that this ruling is not correct, but rather here too they do not count for him; see there.
