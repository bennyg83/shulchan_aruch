But for us, etc. This is difficult, for in Yoreh Deah, siman 399, seif 5, the Rama wrote regarding the opinion that permits close to night, 'and so is the custom.' The Levush, Bach, Taz, Shach, and Magen Avraham answered that here he speaks of the law, while there he speaks of the custom, for in honor of Yom Tov the practice is to be lenient. In my eyes this is forced. It is even more difficult because I saw in Darkei Moshe there that Rabbi Yisrael Isserlein wrote: since our practice is that when a festival interrupts the seven days he may wash in hot water, if someone died on Erev Shabbos and that Shabbos is Erev Yom Tov, he may wash on Erev Shabbos. The Darkei Moshe then writes: for us, who practice that washing is forbidden for all thirty days, its law is like hair cutting, and so is the practice. Thus the Rama explicitly wrote that the custom is to be stringent. This is also clear from the fact that in the glosses to the Shulchan Aruch he did not write that marital relations are permitted when the death occurred on Erev Shabbos. Perforce, he holds that the custom is always to be stringent. It is also difficult that below, in siman 606, the Rama writes that for immersion for a mitzvah it is permitted on Erev Yom Kippur, which shows that on every other Erev Yom Tov it is forbidden. Therefore it appears to me simple and clear that words are missing from the Rama in Yoreh Deah, namely: 'and for us, who practice, etc., it is forbidden,' and on this he wrote, 'and so is the custom.' This is exactly as he wrote in the Darkei Moshe cited above, though to many this would seem too great an emendation. To broaden the matter, one can also say that when the Rama wrote 'and so is the custom,' he referred to one who buried his dead seven days before the festival, where it is permitted close to night, whereas according to the first view it is forbidden until night, as the Beit Yosef wrote there, and as I found in the Perishah. One may also say that even within seven days he refers to cold washing, which is permitted after seven days, as the Shach wrote in siman 381, or to laundering with water alone, which is also permitted after seven days, as the Shach wrote in siman 389, seif katan 2. He teaches only that the custom is to permit close to night, but hot water and laundering with cleansing agents remain forbidden. The general rule is that in practice one should conduct himself stringently.
