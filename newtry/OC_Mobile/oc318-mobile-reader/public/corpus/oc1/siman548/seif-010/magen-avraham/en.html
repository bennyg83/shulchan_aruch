One of the days of mourning. For example Thursday or Friday, for then the festival cancels the decree of the seven and does not cancel the decree of the thirty.
Until night. Since he can wash at night in cold water; see siman 511, se'if 2.
After the Minchah prayer. This implies that he must pray Minchah first, for then the sanctity of the festival takes effect upon him; and so it is implied in the Bach, siman 606; and see what I wrote in siman 292. And specifically these [acts] are permitted that come for the honor of the festival, for when he washes now he benefits from it on the festival; but other laws of mourning, such as sitting on the ground and removing the shoe, are observed until it grows dark, as written in Taz Yoreh De'ah, siman 400; and so wrote the Nimukei Yosef explicitly regarding overturning the bed; and see what I wrote in siman 696.
And for us, etc. That is, by [strict] law; but the custom is to be lenient, as Rama wrote in Yoreh De'ah, siman 399, se'if 5 [Shach and Taz and Bach]. And in Terumas HaDeshen, siman 287, he wrote that one to whom a death occurred on Friday, and that Shabbos is the eve of the festival, is permitted to wash in hot water on Friday; see there. And this requires further study, for at the end of siman 606 it is implied that he permitted it only because of a mitzvah immersion, implying that on another festival it is forbidden. Therefore it appears to me that what Rama wrote in Yoreh De'ah, 'and so they practice,' refers to those who do not practice a washing prohibition for the entire thirty; but for us, Rama holds that one should practice a prohibition on another festival, and only on Yom Kippur it is permitted. Nevertheless it appears to me to be lenient, since all the later authorities are lenient.
