For the festival does not cancel, etc. — see Mishna Berurah that the custom is to be lenient; and therefore one whose relative died on erev Shabbat when that Shabbat is erev the festival is permitted to bathe on erev Shabbat close to dark in hot water for honor of the festival [Terumat HaDeshen siman 287, and Shach brought it in Yoreh De'ah siman 399, and Magen Avraham in our matter]; and see Avnei Nezer who challenged them, for Darkei Moshe wrote there the custom in this is to be stringent; and see Peri Megadim who is also doubtful: even though we are lenient on the eve of the festival itself for honor of the festival, there is no proof from this to be lenient also on erev Shabbat for this reason [and there is no proof from that which we are lenient when the eighth day falls on Shabbat to bathe on erev Shabbat, for there the seven days of mourning already ended]; and one may side to be lenient only in cold water on erev Shabbat, and likewise Chayei Adam leans to be stringent in this [and it is obvious that therefore even if his seventh day falls on Shabbat erev the festival, it is likewise forbidden to bathe on erev Shabbat, and so in Chacham Adam there]; however in Darkei Halacha and likewise in Noda B'Yehuda Moed Katan part Yoreh De'ah siman 99 they brought Terumat HaDeshen's words as halakha [however one may question, for it contradicts what he wrote in Bedek HaBayis to Yoreh De'ah where he answered Rama's words that he permits only in cold water, per Avnei Nezer]; the upshot: one who wishes to rely to be lenient certainly they do not protest, after Shach and Magen Avraham and Darkei Halacha brought Terumat HaDeshen as halakha; and Avnei Nezer's challenge is only from custom, for Darkei Moshe wrote there they practice to be stringent:
