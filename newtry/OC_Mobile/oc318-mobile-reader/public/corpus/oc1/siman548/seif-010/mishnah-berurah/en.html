(35) One of the days of mourning: even the first day.
(36) It is permitted to launder: since at night he cannot launder because of Yom Tov, they permitted it during the day for the sake of the festival. But hair cutting is forbidden until thirty days.
(37) Until after midday: according to the Rama above in seif 8, he should not launder until close to evening.
(38) To wash is forbidden until night: this means even in cold water, for he can wash at night in cold water. On Chol HaMoed, however, even hot water is permitted, since the decree of the seven days has been cancelled from him.
(39) And some permit washing, etc.: specifically washing. But the other laws of mourning, such as sitting on the ground and removing shoes, apply until nightfall, as stated by the Magen Avraham.
(40) Minchah: this implies that he must first pray Minchah, for then the sanctity of the festival takes effect upon him, as stated by the Magen Avraham. See Yeshuos Yaakov, who wrote that while the day is still early he practices the laws of mourning, but close to nightfall, which is bein hashmashos, and perhaps even during the period of adding to Yom Tov, he does not practice them. See the Bach, siman 261, who wrote that the time of accepting Shabbos takes effect two hours close to evening.
(41) It is forbidden to wash, etc.: meaning close to nightfall. Likewise on Chol HaMoed it is forbidden to wash until the thirty days are completed, for its law is like hair cutting.
(42) For the festival did not cancel, etc.: nevertheless the custom is to be lenient in this, as the Rama wrote in Yoreh Deah, siman 399, seif 5. Some later authorities hold that one may be lenient only to wash in cold water, not in hot water. In Chol HaMoed, however, one may certainly rely on the lenient view. Even according to those who are lenient with hot water, this is specifically on Erev Yom Tov and on Chol HaMoed because of the honor of the festival. After the festival, however, washing and laundering remain forbidden until thirty days, since the festival did not cancel the decree of the thirty days. See Hagahos Imrei Baruch in Yoreh Deah, siman 399.
