15) [Se'if 4] Mourning does not take effect upon him. He should not change his clothing or his place, as written above, subsection 3, and in siman 547, subsection 35. See there.
16) There. Mourning does not take effect upon him. It appears that he puts on tefillin on the first day. Magen Avraham, subsection 5. Shevus Yaakov, volume 2, siman 25, however, disputes this ruling of Magen Avraham and holds that he does not put on tefillin on the first day. On the second day as well, he should not put them on until after sunrise, as during the rest of the year. See there. See Yoreh Deah, siman 388, where Shach writes that some halachic authorities permit putting them on during the second day before sunrise, as on other days. Accordingly, it appears that one may permit it here. Regarding whether he may be counted in a minyan before the deceased is buried, Shevus Yaakov there writes that he may not be included. See there. Shaarei Teshuvah, subsection 4. This follows the Ashkenazic custom of putting on tefillin during Chol HaMoed, as written above, siman 31, in the gloss. See there and our words there, with Heaven's help. See also above, siman 38, se'if 5, and our words there, with Heaven's help. Regarding removing tefillin during Chol HaMoed, see above, siman 423, subsection 28, and siman 490, subsection 9.
17) There. But he observes private mourning practices. Some say, however, that even private mourning practices are not observed during the festival. Beis Yosef in the name of Rambam. Rashdam, Yoreh Deah, siman 202, writes likewise, and Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef, cites him. Shulchan Aruch, however, rules according to the majority of halachic authorities, who hold that private mourning practices are observed even during the festival, as explained in Beis Yosef. See there. Private practices mean marital relations, bathing, and Torah study, as written in Yoreh Deah, siman 400. See there also for which practices are considered public. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 3, however, permits Torah study during the festival. One opinion cited by Tur, Yoreh Deah, siman 400, considers study a public practice. Although the halachah does not follow that view, during the festival one may rely upon the authorities who hold that even private mourning practices are not observed. See there. Responsa Chacham Tzvi, siman 100, likewise permits study during the festival. See there. Shaarei Teshuvah, subsection 5, cites this and writes that it is therefore obvious that one may review the festival Torah portion even though there is no obligation, as written at the end of siman 285. This is no worse than other permitted Torah study. See there. Nevertheless, regarding receiving an aliyah during the festival as one of the required number of readers, it appears preferable initially not to do so. Mishnah Berurah, subsection 16.
18) One who was hired by others for work involving wine during Chol HaMoed and became a mourner should see above, siman 542, subsection 15, regarding whether he may continue that work during Chol HaMoed.
