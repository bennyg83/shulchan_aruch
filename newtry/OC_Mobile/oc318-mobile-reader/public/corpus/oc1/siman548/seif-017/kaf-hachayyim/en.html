Rashdam, Yoreh Deah, siman 202, writes likewise, and Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef, cites him. Shulchan Aruch, however, rules according to the majority of halachic authorities, who hold that private mourning practices are observed even during the festival, as explained in Beis Yosef. See there. Private practices mean marital relations, bathing, and Torah study, as written in Yoreh Deah, siman 400. See there also for which practices are considered public. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 3, however, permits Torah study during the festival. One opinion cited by Tur, Yoreh Deah, siman 400, considers study a public practice. Although the halachah does not follow that view, during the festival one may rely upon the authorities who hold that even private mourning practices are not observed. See there. Responsa Chacham Tzvi, siman 100, likewise permits study during the festival. See there. Shaarei Teshuvah, subsection 5, cites this and writes that it is therefore obvious that one may review the festival Torah portion even though there is no obligation, as written at the end of siman 285. This is no worse than other permitted Torah study. See there. Nevertheless, regarding receiving an aliyah during the festival as one of the required number of readers, it appears preferable initially not to do so. Mishnah Berurah, subsection 16.
