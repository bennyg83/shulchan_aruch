1) [Se'if 1] One who buries his deceased relative during the festival, etc. This applies whether the burial takes place on Yom Tov or Chol HaMoed. Tur and Beis Yosef; Levush.
2) There. Mourning does not take effect upon him during the festival. The festival days are days of joy. The positive commandment, 'You shall rejoice in your festival,' which is a communal positive commandment, supersedes the individual's positive commandment of mourning, even on the first day, when mourning is a Torah positive commandment. Levush. This certainly follows the opinion that no aspect of mourning is under Torah law, even on the first day, apart from aninus, as Beis Yosef writes in the name of Rosh. See there. See Yoreh Deah, siman 399, se'if 13, which states that common practice follows those who say that no aspect of mourning is under Torah law. See there. See below, se'if 2.
3) There. Mourning does not take effect upon him, etc. He does not change his place. Although another mourner changes his place during the festival, one whose mourning has not yet begun should not do so during the festival. Maharam of Lublin, siman 107. We wrote likewise above, siman 547, subsection 35, in the name of Mahari of Bruna. See there. This follows Rama's statement in the gloss at the end of Yoreh Deah, siman 393, that even on Shabbos a mourner changes his place. According to Shulchan Aruch there, however, those who customarily do not change their synagogue place on Shabbos act properly. See there. According to that view, in any event he does not change his place. See what Birkei Yosef writes regarding this there, subsection 2.
4) There. Mourning does not take effect upon him, etc. The festival does not cancel lighting a lamp in honor of the deceased. Even when the person died during the festival, the lamp is lit. Nevertheless, in the room where everyone eats, even if the person died there, the lamp should not be lit on Yom Tov, lest this lead to a eulogy. It should be lit in the synagogue or in a room of the house where people do not eat. Responsa Nechalas Shivah, siman 73; Eliyah Rabbah in siman 133, at the end of subsection 7; Shaarei Teshuvah, subsection 1; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 3. When it is not lit in the synagogue or in the place where people eat on Yom Tov, it should be lit by a non-Jew. Mishbetzos Zahav, at the end of siman 132. See above, siman 514, subsection 79, and below, at the end of subsection 32.
5) There. He counts the thirty days from the day of burial, etc. Shemini Atzeres counts in place of seven days and completes the thirty, as written in se'if 16. Maharshal, Responsa siman 5; Mishnah Berurah, siman 71; Derishah on Yoreh Deah, siman 399, subsection 2. This also appears to be the view of Shach there, subsection 7. Hagahos Maimoniyos, chapter 10, however, writes that Shemini Atzeres counts in place of seven specifically when the burial occurred before Sukkos, as written below, se'if 16. In the present case, where burial occurred during the festival, it counts only as one day. See there. Beis Yosef cites this in Yoreh Deah, siman 399. Shulchan Aruch rules accordingly there, se'if 2, as do Levush there, Bach there, and Taz there, subsection 4, and in this siman, subsection 4. Taz writes that this is unlike Maharshal and Mishnah Berurah, which follows him. See there. This is also the view of Magen Avraham, subsection 1; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 13; Yad Aharon, Hagahos HaTur; Beis David, siman 314, who writes that this is the proper practice; Nehar Shalom, subsection 1; and Birkei Yosef, subsection 8, who writes that this is the custom and that one should not apply here the rule that the halachah follows the lenient opinion in mourning. See there. He writes likewise in Yosef Ometz, siman 93, subsection 2. Bigdei Yesha also agrees with the aforementioned ruling of Taz. Chochmas Adam, rule 169, subsection 2; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 4, rule likewise. Chochmas Adam nevertheless writes that since some say Shemini Atzeres counts in place of seven, one may be lenient for a distressed person and similar cases regarding bathing and regarding women. See there. This follows Rama's statement in Yoreh Deah, siman 381, se'if 1, in the gloss, that nowadays the custom is to forbid all bathing throughout the thirty days. Under the strict law, however, bathing is permitted after seven days. See there. In a place that does not follow Rama's custom, bathing is permitted after seven days. See below, subsection 37.
6) There. All restrictions of the thirty days. This includes shaving, cutting hair, bathing in hot water, and the other matters explained in Yoreh Deah, Hilchos Aveilus. See there.
