(1) During the festival: whether on Yom Tov or on Chol HaMoed.
(2) It does not take effect, etc.: for the positive commandment of rejoicing on the festival, which is a public positive commandment, comes and overrides mourning, which is only the mitzvah of an individual.
(3) On the festival: nevertheless, the lamp that is customarily lit throughout the seven days should be lit immediately. This does not depend on whether the festival cancels the mourning or on whether it does not take effect until after the festival. However, he should not light it in the place where people eat, and certainly not in the room where the person died, lest this lead him to eulogize. Rather, he should light it in a place where people do not eat. Since he derives no benefit from this light at all, he should not light it himself on Yom Tov, but through a non-Jew, as stated by the Peri Megadim in siman 132 and Rabbi Akiva Eiger in siman 514.
(4) And he counts thirty days from the burial: Shemini Atzeres is counted in this only as one day.
(5) Like all decrees of the thirty days: namely shaving, hair cutting, washing in hot water, and other matters explained in Yoreh Deah in the laws of mourning.
