Se'if 1. One who buries his dead during the festival: mourning does not take effect upon him on the festival, etc. Note: a case came to my hand of one who became a mourner on Sukkos, may his rest be in Eden, and I instructed him to permit being called up to the Torah on Simchas Torah; and the reason is this: since the entire world has the practice of being called up to the Torah, if the mourner is not called up to the Torah that is public [mourning display]. And even though one may distinguish that there Rabbeinu Tam had the practice of being called up in that place and [hence] it was public, but here perhaps he was called up in another place; and similarly wrote Maharshal in a responsum, siman 71, regarding a mourner who circumcised his son who is not called up to the Torah. Nevertheless one may say that here there is a different reason: since it is clarified in the Shulchan Aruch that on the eve of Rosh Hashanah a mourner is permitted to leave his house to say Selichos, even though on other days a mourner is forbidden to leave; nevertheless in this, since the world multiplies Selichos, it would cause him anguish if he sat in his house. If so, here too, since on Simchas Torah all Israel have the practice of being called up to the Torah, there is anguish if he sits and is not called up to the Torah, and it is similar to there, where he is permitted to leave his house. And further it appears that this resembles what the Taz wrote in Hilchos Shabbos, siman 244, regarding attached [field] work: it is not permitted on account of appearance only if there are two sides [grounds] for permission; but if there is only one side for permission we are concerned that perhaps someone will know that it is not contracted work, etc., and will assume he is a day-hired worker; see there; so too here. Granted, regarding one who circumcised his son there are two sides for permission: one, perhaps they did not know at all that today is the day of his son's circumcision, and if he has no son's circumcision he would in any case have no [reason] today to be called up to the Torah; and even if they knew that today he circumcised his son, still perhaps he was already called up in another synagogue; and therefore there are two sides for permission, and so we are not concerned for public display. But on Simchas Torah, since everyone is called up to the Torah today and there is no side for permission except perhaps he was called up elsewhere, it is proper to be concerned that perhaps many will know he was not called up elsewhere and will recognize that because of mourning he refrained from being called up, and that is public. It is true that one may distinguish between the Taz's case and our case, for one may say that at any rate it is not [true] public [display]; nevertheless, since it is a merely rabbinic matter, especially on the festival when mourning has not taken effect upon him, and also because of the aforementioned reason that it is anguish for him, one should be lenient in such a case; and this is clear and correct for the practical law; and examine carefully.
