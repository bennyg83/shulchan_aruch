Seif 1 — one who buries his dead during the festival, mourning does not apply on the festival, etc. NB: a case came before me where one became a mourner on Sukkot, may he read Torah on Simchat Torah? I permitted based on this: since everyone practices reading Torah, if the mourner does not read it is public; although one may distinguish — there Rabbenu Tam practiced reading in that place and it is public; but here perhaps he read elsewhere; R' Shlomo responsum 71 regarding a mourner who circumcised his son — he does not read Torah; nevertheless here another reason: since Shulchan Aruch permits a mourner on erev Yom Tov to leave for slichot, though other days forbidden — here since people increase slichot, pain if he sits home; likewise on Simchat Torah since all Israel read, pain if he sits and does not read; compare Taz Hilchot Shabbat 244 regarding attached melacha — marit ayin only when two sides to permit; if only one side to permit we fear perhaps this one knows it is not in the pot and will think he is a day laborer, etc.; so here. For one who circumcised his son there are two sides: perhaps they do not know today is milah day; if he has no milah he has no reason to read today; even if they know milah day perhaps he already read in another synagogue — therefore two sides to permit, no marit ayin concern. But on Simchat Torah since all read today and no other permit side except perhaps he read elsewhere — one may fear many know he did not read elsewhere and will recognize he refrains because of mourning — public. True one may distinguish Taz's case from ours; nevertheless at least no public concern, since rabbinic matter, especially on festival mourning does not apply; also from pain reason above one may be lenient — clear and correct for law; examine well:
