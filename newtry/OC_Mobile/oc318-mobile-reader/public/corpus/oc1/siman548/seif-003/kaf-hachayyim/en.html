10) [Se'if 3] One who buries his deceased relative on the second day of Yom Tov, etc. This applies specifically to the seven relatives explicitly stated in the Torah, for whom a kohen may become impure. Mourning for the additional relatives listed in Yoreh Deah, siman 374, se'if 4, is only rabbinic, and mourning is not observed for them on any Yom Tov, as written in Yoreh Deah, siman 399, se'if 13. See there. See below, subsection 13.
11) There. Which is the final day of Yom Tov, etc. Unlike the second day observed in the Diaspora at the beginning of Pesach or Sukkos, this final day is no less stringent than Chol HaMoed. Levushei Serad; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 11.
12) There. The day of death and burial. If the person died the preceding day and was buried on the final day, mourning on the day of burial is ordained by the Sages and does not override the second day of Yom Tov, although that day too is only ordained by the Sages. Shiltei HaGibborim; Pischei Olam, subsection 6; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 12.
13) There, in the gloss. Some disagree, etc. Shulchan Aruch likewise writes in Yoreh Deah, siman 399, se'if 13, that universal practice is not to observe mourning on the second day of Yom Tov in the Diaspora for any deceased relative, even when it is both the day of death and burial. This follows the opinion that no aspect of mourning is under Torah law. See there. See above, subsection 2.
14) There, in the gloss. This is the widespread custom and should not be changed. If a person seeks to act in accordance with the Geonim, he should be stopped because of the prohibition, 'You shall not form separate groups.' We have already accepted the practice according to Rabbeinu Tam and Rosh. Bach.
