(10) His dead relative: specifically the seven relatives for whom mourning is a mitzvah and who are explicit in the Torah. But those added to them, as stated in Yoreh Deah at the end of siman 374, have mourning only D'Rabbanan.
(11) The last: unlike the second day of Yom Tov in the diaspora at the beginning of Pesach or Sukkos, which is no worse than Chol HaMoed.
(12) The day of death and burial: but if he died yesterday and was buried on the last day, the day of burial is from the words of the Sages and does not override Yom Tov, even though Yom Tov itself is only from the words of the Sages.
(13) According to the Torah: since the Torah warned that a kohen should become tamei for his relatives, certainly ordinary Jews are obligated to become tamei for their relatives. All this is so that they should not refrain from mourning for them, which shows that on the day of death one is obligated to mourn for them.
(14) That he does not practice, etc.: their reason is that they hold that even mourning on the first day is only D'Rabbanan, and therefore the joy of the festival is preferable, for it is a mitzvah of the public.
