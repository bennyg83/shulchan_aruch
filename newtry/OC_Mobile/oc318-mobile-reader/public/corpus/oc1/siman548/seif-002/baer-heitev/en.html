This implies even if he was buried on the first day by a gentile, and not like Maharshal; but on Rosh Hashanah specifically if he was buried on the second day he counts seven from the second day, but if he was buried on the first day he does not count even from the second day (Taz); and so wrote the Mabit and Peri Chadash [and] Magen Avraham. But most poskim ruled that even on Rosh Hashanah, if he was buried on the first day he counts seven from the second day; and see Yoreh De'ah, siman 399, s.k. 13.
