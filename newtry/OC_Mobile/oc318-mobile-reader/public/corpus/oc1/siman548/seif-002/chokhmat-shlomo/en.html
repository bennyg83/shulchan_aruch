(There seif 5) Although mourning does not apply during the festival, onen status applies, etc., etc. — note: I was asked about a deceased on the second day of Yom Tov whom they would not allow to be buried until near evening per the king's law, may it go well with us — whether onen status applies here or not. I answered it seems obvious onen status applies here, for even though Magen Avraham wrote where it is impossible to bury because of non-Jewish dominion onen status does not apply — that is if impossible all day; but if possible to bury him today only after a time, what difference does the day make — we do not split it, and whoever will be buried today, onen status applies to him. And it is not comparable to what is established regarding a deceased on Yom Tov — that then at night he becomes impure and onen status does not apply to him — namely, also night and day the change is evident and they are separate entities, therefore onen status did not apply to him in the interim; but on the day itself, where the change is not evident, we do not split it into two. And behold some wished to bring proof from what Magen Avraham wrote above siman 526 that perhaps he holds since his intent is not to bury until after the meal, onen status did not apply to them until after the meal, see there — if so, here too. But in my opinion behold Magen Avraham's words themselves are astonishing — if there is reason to distinguish thus, what proof does the Rosh bring in chapter 3 of Berachot from the Rambam that there is no onen status on Yom Tov from that they had to bring a shofar before him to take him out on the 18th — what proof is it? Perhaps there his intent was to bury them after the meal, therefore onen status did not apply to him; but where his intent is to bury them earlier, there is onen status on Yom Tov — and perforce this view does not exist. However, even according to Magen Avraham's words it is not comparable: one, behold we see Magen Avraham was precise only if his intent is to bury after the meal — but if his intent is to bury after prayer they would be exempt from prayer, and perforce we require something else interrupting in the interim between the mitzvah and the burial — something else interrupting and the distinction evident in this, similar to day and night where at night onen status did not apply to him — so too here; but if there is no interrupting change in the interim, exempt from prayer — if so, here too, since between the hour they do not allow him to be buried and the hour they will allow him to be buried there is no evident change. For granted there, since the matter is contingent on the meal and it is a matter contingent on action and evident — therefore one may split the day itself; but between hours where the change is not evident, it is impossible to split the day itself into two. And further, granted after the meal, since this is a fixed time for all deceased and it is known that today they do not bury except after the meal — therefore there is no disgrace to the deceased, for all know it is not his burial time until after the meal; but here, per the king's law, since hours are not all equal and there is a deceased they leave to bury in the morning when his time has ended — who will know this one is not buried because his time has not ended? Therefore it is disgrace to the deceased if they engage in prayer; and behold for this reason an onen is forbidden to be stringent based on honor of the deceased — so too here disgrace of the deceased applies, therefore forbidden to pray. And it is as if they do not allow him all day to be buried — onen status does not apply there, for when the public sees they did not bury him all day, all will know it is because burial was impossible and there is no disgrace to the deceased since burial is literally another day; but on that day itself, if burial will be that day, one may say from laxity they delay him — then one may split the day itself into two where there is no fixed time for all deceased equally; therefore they certainly have onen status. And likewise if there is delay — they do not wish to allow burial until tomorrow but the onenim have ability to strive to bury him today near evening — onen status also applies in the morning. And it is not comparable to one in captivity who did not despair of burial — onen status does not apply to him, only mourning — for there he is not yet buried at all; therefore even though they can strive to bury him, since it is in doubt he has no onen status; but here it is known he will be buried tomorrow, and since he will certainly be buried tomorrow and it is also possible through striving he will be buried today, onen status applies from today. And especially there it deals with something hinging on much money and it is doubtful whether it will help — therefore onen status did not apply to them; but here with a small sum it helps and it is close to certain it will help — therefore onen status applies to them; and on the contrary it appears that because of this onen status applies from now even if they will not bury him until after the meal or after the festival ends — nevertheless from now this striving applies to them, and therefore it is like one who darkens before the boundary — onen status applies from when he begins to darken before the boundary — so too here. And this is correct for practical law that they are exempt from all mitzvot like other onenim on Yom Tov; and see clear proof to this from Magen Avraham's own words siman 496 s.k. 6 — it is proven there that even though by law burial is forbidden until after the megillah, nevertheless onen status applies and he is exempt — all the more so here, see there and examine; and even for Magen Avraham's conclusion there, it was only regarding megillah, for megillah precedes burial of the deceased as Magen Avraham wrote; and also in the book Avnei Nezer disagrees with Magen Avraham — also there; but in our case certainly he is not allowed to pray — examine well. (There) And if he died on Yom Tov and does not wish to bury him that day, onen status does not apply to him, etc. — note: from this it is clear that even if his intent is to bury at night, nevertheless there is no onen status on Yom Tov since he has no intent to bury that day — all the more so on Shabbat and Yom Kippur when burial is forbidden that day, there is no onen status. And I was very astonished from the Gemara Yoma daf 13 that asks: and R' Yehuda — even another wife they prepare for him lest his wife die — and if his wife dies in the middle of the service is he an onen, etc. — what does it ask? And what compares Yom Kippur to weekday? Behold one may say on Yom Kippur, since if possible to bury her today onen status does not apply to him — and one should not say that for onen in kodshim we do not follow whether he can bury or not — from where to challenge thus? And perhaps also for onen status in kodshim onen status applies only if he wishes to bury that day — for this is learned from the verse of Aharon 'on that very day they offered,' etc., and there burial was given that day; but without burial being given, perhaps onen status does not apply at all — why does it ask? And requires further study. And even though there is one tanna in Moed Katan that Rosh Hashana and Yom Kippur are not like festivals — nevertheless what does it ask? And let us say R' Yehuda holds like the one who says Rosh Hashana and Yom Kippur are like festivals and therefore onen status does not apply if he does not bury that day — end of the matter that it requires further study — examine. And see in my work on Orach Chayim from the year 5596 section 132 that a certain scholar named our teacher R' Eikel from the community of Botoshatsh wished to refute the above difficulty and I refuted all his words and the difficulty stands in its place — see there well. And behold when I was speaking in the sugya of Yoma there, it seems to decide from this what Taz and Shach dispute in Yoreh De'ah siman 391, and Nekudas HaKesef brought the dispute of early authorities whether mourning befell a groom after chuppah who has not yet consummated — whether mourning applies first or the seven days of festivity, see there. In my humble opinion one may prove from this: at first glance it is difficult — why does the Gemara ask that R' Yehuda said lest his wife die and if she dies he performs service — behold he is an onen, etc. — why does it ask? And perhaps where possible to fix they fix, and what is impossible they do not fix; and especially since the Gemara concludes in Sukkah that R' Yehuda was not concerned for death except based on a general virtue he was concerned for death — if so, it works for what relates to atonement for all Israel; but for what relates to prohibition of onen status and lest he eat in this we are not concerned for death — why does it ask? And one must say the Gemara understood from R' Yehuda's language that he said 'they prepare another wife for him' — perforce he married before Yom Kippur, for if on Yom Kippur there would be no need for preliminary invitation, not like the Yerushalmi — therefore it is difficult to him why he does not marry on Yom Kippur; and perforce based on rabbinic enactment one does not betroth on Shabbat and Yom Tov, and where possible to fix so he does not come to this they fix — therefore it asks: since the concern is from rabbinic enactment not to betroth, better he should not marry now — for if he marries her now perhaps his wife will die and he will be an onen; better to wait to marry her on Yom Kippur, and then since he will see he needs to die first and marry her for that — like the kind the Gemara asks there — so one may say here: since he sees he needs to die first and marry, and conditionally if the other dies and he will not have two homes — then if she dies mourning days of festivity apply retroactively and he will not become an onen again; if so, better not to be concerned for rabbinic prohibition of betrothal than now that he married beforehand and behold we require a chuppah fit for consummation. And granted on Yom Kippur the chuppah fit for consummation will be in the evening and this is considered fit for consummation as I wrote in my novellae already, see there; but if he marries beforehand, behold all seven days they separate him from his house — perforce he marries beforehand — if so, the seven days of festivity already ended before Yom Kippur, therefore he will have onen status — so goes the Gemara's difficulty, that better to be concerned for prohibition of onen status than rabbinic prohibition of betrothal — examine, for I abbreviated. And it is proven from this that even without consummation mourning days of festivity apply first and he has no onen status — for behold on Yom Kippur he will not consummate, and it asks thus — understand, for I abbreviated. And behold I reconsidered and saw this is an error for several reasons: for even though seven days of festivity override mourning, nevertheless they do not override onen status if they wish to bury that day — it is no better than Yom Tov where onen status applies if they bury that day; and one should not say behold his intent is to bury that day — this is difficult without this, as above. And further, behold betrothal without chuppah is not his home — perforce he brought her in — if so, again impossible to say conditionally, for there is no condition in chuppah; and further from either way: if he betroths her now, again if the first dies he would have service in two homes retroactively; and if he betroths her only conditionally if she dies — then if she dies onen status applies from the start and afterward festivity; and further behold the Gemara concludes his difficulty is not from onen status, only lest he be troubled — and behold this will not be fixed even if he betroths and brings her in and mourning days of festivity apply — nevertheless will he not be troubled? Who is not troubled? And behold in the end it is impossible to fix the matter — therefore let these words I wrote be omitted; and in any case the mishnah did not move from its place — mere pilpul — examine well.
