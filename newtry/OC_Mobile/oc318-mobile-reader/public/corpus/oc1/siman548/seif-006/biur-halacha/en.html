Within his house - And see what I wrote in the name of the Chacham Tzvi. And behold, even though in Or Zarua it appears that his practical conclusion is like his grandfather the Gaon Maharash, and not like his [own] view [and as we will quote his language below], the Chacham Tzvi adopted like Eliyah Rabbah because that colleague came later, and there he mentioned only his view, as we wrote in the Mishnah Berurah in his name. However, that in which the Chacham Tzvi was still more lenient than him is not clear at all; and I will copy the language of the Chacham Tzvi, and these are his words: And a partner of a mourner to whom a death occurred on Chol HaMoed is permitted to engage in the shop immediately after Yom Tov [Eliyah Rabbah in siman 548] (and what Eliyah Rabbah concluded, 'as with other mourning,' means: just as there on the seventh day it is permitted to open his shop, so here if he died on the first day of Chol HaMoed it is permitted immediately after Yom Tov, which is the seventh day); and it appears to me that for us, who practice to forbid only three days, the same applies if he died on the eve of the last Yom Tov, it is permitted; end quote. And in truth it is not comparable at all: for there the mourner at least sat three days in mourning, whereas here he has not yet sat at all; and it is enough for us to be lenient in our matter if he died on Chol HaMoed on the first day, according to the view of Eliyah Rabbah, for at least it is now the seventh day from the death of the deceased, and not to seize two leniencies, namely three days and without observing mourning at all. [And especially since Peri Megadim also questions even the leniency of Eliyah Rabbah and his view is to be stringent in this like his grandfather; and it is enough if we are lenient like Eliyah Rabbah and not more.] And I will copy the language of Or Zarua, and it will be obvious to every eye that the truth is as our words; and these are his words: It appears to me that a partner of a mourner to whom a death occurred on the first Chol HaMoed day is permitted to engage in the shop immediately after Yom Tov, since we are lenient regarding other mourning after three days; but I heard in the name of my grandfather the Gaon, of blessed memory, that he ruled to forbid as with other mourning; end quote. Thus you have that Eliyah Rabbah's view as well regarding an ordinary mourner's partner is only three days, like the view of Maharshal in this; and even so, in our matter he was stringent specifically if he died on Chol HaMoed on the first day so that overall the opening of the shop would be at least on the seventh day from the death of the deceased, as above. And therefore the reason is as we wrote above; so it appears clear in my humble opinion. And know that regarding recent tidings it is obvious that even though seven days from the death of the deceased have already been completed, commerce is forbidden for the entire mourning as with other mourning, and as is written in Yoreh De'ah, siman 402, that recent tidings have the law of the day of burial; and so wrote Peri Megadim at the end of the siman.
