(22) His work is done by others, etc.: even for something that is not a loss. For something involving loss, it is permitted through others even in his house and even during the first days after the festival, as stated in Yoreh Deah, siman 380, seif 5. See the Levush, who wrote that if it involves loss he may do it himself, but the Maamar Mordechai questions this; see there for the reason. However, if he cannot do it through others and it involves loss, perhaps in our case one may rely on the opinion brought there in the gloss.
(23) In their homes: although for every mourner as well, his work may be done by others in their homes, as stated in Yoreh Deah there, seif 18, there it is only by contract work and when they accepted the work before he became a mourner. Here it is permitted even during the days of mourning, as appears from the Gra.
(24) His house: regarding the partner of a mourner whose relative died on Yom Tov, the Gaon Rabbi Shimon of Prague ruled to forbid opening his shop, as in other mourning, meaning that he must wait three days after Yom Tov, as the practice is in other mourning regarding a partner, as stated there in Yoreh Deah, seif 21, in the later authorities. So the Baer Heitev wrote in the name of Eliyah Zuta. However, in Chochmas Adam, laws of mourning, klal 169, law 2, he cited the Eliyah Rabbah as lenient, namely that if the death occurred on the first day of Chol HaMoed, the partner may open his shop immediately after Yom Tov. See there in Chochmas Adam, where his view is that even if the death occurred on Erev the last Yom Tov, the partner may open his shop immediately after Yom Tov. See Biur Halacha, where we clarified that he went too far; it is enough if we are lenient like the Eliyah Rabbah.
(25) And there is no need, etc.: similarly, one does not console a mourner on the Erev Shabbos after the festival, before Barchu in the synagogue as is customary, if seven days have already passed since the death, as stated by the later authorities.
