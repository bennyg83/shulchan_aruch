That the meal. See the Taz. At first glance, this is after the time of minchah, namely six and a half hours, as in siman 291. See Magen Avraham here, s.k. 1, and siman 291, in Eshel Avraham, s.k. 4, that he should also divide the morning meal into two, for some say he fulfills the obligation that way; that is, he recites birkas hamazon.
If. If he forgot to separate challah from Friday, see Magen Avraham siman 506, letter 8; see there, and see Baer Heitev. Declaring it ownerless is also impossible. According to the one who says that a half-measure has no prohibition, keeping the challah of fire has no minimum measure; but we rule that a half-measure is forbidden; see siman 447 in Magen Avraham. If he separated challah on Friday and forgot to burn it, and Shabbos arrived at the fourth hour, it is forbidden to give it to a non-Jew, only to a minor kohen; but giving it to an adult, or nullifying it in a majority, is not allowed, for one may not nullify on Shabbos. See Magen Avraham siman 323, s.k. 14, and Yoreh Deah siman 99, and my Peri Megadim there and in siman 324. However, one may say that he should make it ownerless; or perhaps challah that is not his, he does not transgress over it. See Pesachim regarding how one separates challah in impurity. To give it to his dog, one may say it is permitted; and see Beitzah folio 27b, where one can say that we do not destroy kodshim on Yom Tov and Shabbos. Challah is something from which one keeps away; see the Rambam, chapter 11 of Terumos, that a Yisrael is forbidden to benefit from it by way of destruction. The same applies to tevel, which is forbidden for benefit by way of destruction; see Tosafos, Pesachim folio 9a. This requires examination: if so, one must throw everything into the sea and not give it to a non-Jew. Perhaps challah of the Diaspora is not so stringent and is permitted for benefit. If he separated challah intentionally, he may not eat; if unintentionally, he may eat. See the Rambam, of blessed memory, Hilchos Shabbos, and what I wrote in siman 323 on Magen Avraham s.k. 11. In the Taz, s.k. 5, he concluded there that a rabbinic matter is permitted. If so, challah of Eretz Yisrael is different, for it is Torah law, unlike challah of fire, which is rabbinic; one may say this is not so much an act of fixing, although it is impossible to eat it as is, as the Maggid Mishneh wrote. As practical law, where he has no other bread except this from which he forgot to take challah, perhaps one may incline to what the Taz wrote there; with Hashem's help it will be explained further in siman 506.
See. See Responsa Maharil, siman 99, regarding the third meal, that there is no positive commandment to override, etc. One may say it is a positive commandment that arose through negligence; see Tosafos, Eruvin folio 100a, s.v. matan. See what I wrote about this in siman 291, and see siman 446, Magen Avraham s.k. 2. In my Sefer HaMaggid I wrote the laws of Pesach when the eve of Pesach falls on Shabbos, and I marked them in the Magen Avraham. I will also mention here that charoses and other things must be prepared on Friday, for on Yom Tov pounding in the normal way is somewhat forbidden; after the fact, see siman 504. Regarding roasting meat and an egg as a remembrance of the chagigah and the Pesach offering, which are left for the second night: when the eve of Pesach is Shabbos, he should roast them on Friday, for on the first night of Yom Tov it is forbidden to prepare from one Yom Tov day for the next. Some say that we do not apply the principle of since regarding cooking. Filling a pot to cook many eggs is not relevant; that applies specifically to meat, which improves thereby. See Taz siman 473, s.k. 4; see there. Whether it is permitted to place the maror in water on Shabbos so that it not wither, see Eliyah Rabbah s.k. 3 and siman 473, s.k. 14, that it is permitted. This requires examination, for moving and watering, since they are not for the need of Shabbos, should be forbidden; see siman 321 in Magen Avraham s.k. 13 and siman 654. This requires examination if he inserted the maror into the ground so that it not wither and it did not take root; see Mahariv, Hilchos Pesach. With Hashem's help it will be explained in siman 473. See Magen Avraham there, letter 8, regarding what one should do on the night of Yom Tov if he forgot to roast the egg and meat; see there.
Water. Our drawn water should be drawn on Thursday night when Pesach falls on Sunday. See siman 455 for the law, and with Hashem's help it will be explained there. If he forgot to filter wine from raisins on Friday, it is good to leave it until the night of Yom Tov, for on Shabbos there are several concerns of selecting and the like; see siman 319 and siman 510. See siman 467 in Magen Avraham s.k. 8, that not eating raisins on Pesach and filtering beforehand is a mere stringency; but here, where this is impossible, it is good to leave it to filter on the night of Yom Tov according to the methods explained in siman 510. With Hashem's help it will be explained there.
