5) There. And one retains food, etc. If one forgot on Friday to separate challah from chametz bread baked for this Shabbos, it is forbidden to eat from it and there is no remedy. Magen Avraham, siman 506, subsection 8; Chok Yaakov, siman 457, subsection 9; Eliyah Rabbah in this siman, subsection 1; Mekor Chaim, subsection 1; Chemed Moshe, subsection 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 12; Chayei Adam, rule 129, subsection 11. Nesiv Chaim in siman 506, however, challenges this ruling of Magen Avraham. He writes that the lenient authorities, Rabbeinu Chananel, Rosh, Baal HaTerumos, Raavad, Ran, and Rabbeinu Avigdor, are sufficiently authoritative to permit separating challah and giving it to a minor kohen or to an adult kohen who immersed after a seminal emission. It may even be given to a ritually impure kohen, who can nullify it in a majority and eat it, in accordance with the established ruling in Yoreh De'ah, siman 323. If no kohen is available, one may continue eating and leave less than an olive-sized quantity, covering it with a utensil until after Yom Tov, like other chametz remaining when no non-Jew is available, as stated in siman 444, se'if 5. See there. Responsa She'eilas Yaavetz, siman 133, and Responsa Panim Meiros, volume 2, siman 96, write likewise, as cited by Sha'arei Teshuvah in this siman, subsection 1. Bigdei Yesha, siman 506, and the novellae of Rabbi Akiva Eiger write likewise. Therefore, when no other bread can be found, it appears that one may rely upon the lenient authorities so that the Shabbos meals will not be lost.
