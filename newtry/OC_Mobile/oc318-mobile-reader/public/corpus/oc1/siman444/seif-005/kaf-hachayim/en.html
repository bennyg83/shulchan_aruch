Magen Avraham siman 486 seif kaf 8. Chayei Yisrael siman 457 seif 9; Eliyah Rabbah in this siman seif 1; Makor Chaim seif 1; Maggid Mishneh seif 2; Razah seif 12; Chayei Adam general rule 129 seif 11. However, the Rav Netiv Chayim there in siman 486 challenged the words of Magen Avraham mentioned; and he wrote that it is fitting — these are the words of R' Chaim and the Rosh and Ba'al HaTerumot and Ra'avad and Ran and Ra'avatz, who ruled leniently to separate challah and give to a minor kohen; or to an adult who immersed for his keri; and even to an impure kohen, that he may nullify it in a majority and may eat, as is explained in Yoreh De'ah siman 323. And if there is no kohen, he eats and continues and leaves less than a k'zayit and covers it with a vessel until Motzei Yom Tov, like other chametz that remains where he has no non-Jew — siman 464 seif 5; see there. And likewise in She'eilat Ya'avetz siman 133; and in responsa Pnei Me'orot part 2 siman 96; and Beit Din that is on the table in this siman seif 1; and likewise in Sefer Bigdei Yesha siman 486; and in Chiddushei R' Akiva Eiger. And therefore it appears that where it is impossible to find other bread, one may rely on the lenient ones, so that Shabbat meals not be nullified; and see below siman 457 in the gloss and in our words there seif 16.
