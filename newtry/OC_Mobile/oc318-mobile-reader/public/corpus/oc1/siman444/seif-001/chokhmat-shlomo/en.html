Seif 1 — "The 14th when it falls on Shabbat" etc. NB: Behold, by law if one forgot to separate challah on Erev Pesach that fell on Shabbat — see in Ba'er Heitev, who wrote that one gives the remainder to a minor kohen; and he wondered about this in Sefer Chak Yosef — how did he separate challah initially on Shabbat? And he wondered well. However, according to what is stated in his novellae to the laws of Shabbat siman 563, still in manuscript, one may say that in such a case it helps to take challah in thought — it fits well, for certainly taking the body of the challah is possible in thought; but if one fulfills challah in thought, what shall he do with what he leaves over, since the law of challah applies to it? And in this he wrote well that one gives the remainder to a minor kohen in the manner explained below siman 457. And even though Ba'er Heitev did not intend this, nevertheless for the law he spoke correctly, in my humble opinion; and understand well. I again saw in Shulchan Aruch Shaarei Teshuvah that he brought in the name of Sefer She'eilat Ya'avetz that he ruled thus — and my intent is his opinion; and I do not have this book to examine his reason; and understand well. And behold, see in Magen Avraham here seif kaf 1, who wrote that the morning meal too one should divide into two, for some say that one fulfills thus etc. Behold, according to this one may resolve the explanation of Rashi that is wondrous in chapter 1 of Pesachim [daf 13] s.v. "mazon shtei se'udot velo shelosh" — that all three meals are for Shabbat: evening, morning, and minchah; and on Erev Pesach it is forbidden to eat from minchah and above; end of his words. And it is wondrous, for he did not state this reason — for in any event it is forbidden to eat chametz until after the fourth hour; and I already asked about this from the sharp son R' Chaim Yehuda, may he rest in Eden, and from the rabbi of the holy community of Lita — but it appears that it fits well: Rashi holds like the opinion of those who say that one fulfills in the morning in place of the third meal, like the opinion of the one authority that Magen Avraham brought. But Rashi holds that this is not as an obligation — for certainly the main obligation of three meals is at minchah; nevertheless it helps as completion, like in prayer where completion applies, and in the sukkah meal according to R' Eliezer. But Rashi holds that completion applies only if he is obligated at its time and at its season — then completion applies also afterward or before; but if at the time of his main obligation there is no obligation at all, how does completion apply before or after it, since at the time of its main obligation there is no obligation? And like an onen in havdalah, where the poskim wrote this distinction — see in the Rosh and Taz, laws of aninus. And if so, here too this is Rashi's intent: certainly in general, since the main obligation applies at minchah, it also helps beforehand as completion, as if he ate at its time; but on Erev Pesach that falls on Shabbat, since at its main time there is no obligation of the third meal at all — if so, since at his main obligation he is exempt automatically, completion beforehand does not apply. And this is what Rashi said: the Shabbat meal — its main parts are evening, morning, and minchah; and on Erev Pesach it is forbidden to eat from minchah and above — if so, the obligation of the third meal does not apply at all, and completion does not apply here; therefore he leaves only two meals and not three; and to eat it in the morning in place of the third meal — for on this day completion does not apply — and it fits well. And this appears very correct as Rashi's intent, and there is no place to deviate here except as stated; and understand well.
