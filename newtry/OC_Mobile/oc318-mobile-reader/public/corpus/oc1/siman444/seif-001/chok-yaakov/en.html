As below, siman 462. Although this has no connection to siman 462, for perhaps specifically there, during Pesach, the custom is to forbid it lest one come to eat it on the first night because of the obligation of matzah, as explained in the Tur and Beis Yosef below in siman 462. The Beis Yosef wrote there: there is no reason for that custom to decree and be concerned about what the Sages of the Talmud and the later Sages did not concern themselves with. If so, let us not add to it on the eve of Pesach, where there is a good distinction, since at that time it is forbidden to eat complete matzah. Perhaps the Rav's intent is that since the practice in these countries on Pesach is not to use egg matzah, they did not trouble him so much for the third meal on the eve of Pesach to knead egg matzah, and one fulfills it with kinds of fruit, etc. But where one has egg matzah, it is permitted to eat it at the third meal, and this does not involve nullifying the custom. However, Maharil in Hilchos Shabbos HaGadol and the glosses to Semak, siman 219, also wrote that even on the eve of Pesach one should not eat egg matzah because we are concerned that perhaps a little water became mixed into it and it became chametz. Although there is a remedy through guarding it according to what is written below in siman 462, nevertheless we are concerned that he will not perform guarding, since it is fruit juice and it will not occur to him to be concerned that a little water became mixed in. The Rav followed this.
With kinds of fruit, or meat and fish. Although above in siman 291 it is explained that it is better to make the meal with meat and fish than with fruit, and here the Rav wrote fruit first, it appears to me that because of eating matzah with appetite, it is better to fulfill it with kinds of fruit, which are not so filling. Furthermore, since it is common with meat and fish to eat bread with them, we are concerned that perhaps one will err and eat matzah with them. In Magen Avraham he wrote that on the eve of Pesach as well it is better to fulfill it with meat and fish, but that is not the implication of the Rav's language here; rather, it is as I wrote. He further wrote in the name of the Zohar that one should engage in Torah in place of the third meal. Maharil wrote: it is proper to make it with cooked matzah, but I have not seen people practice so. End quote. The later authorities wrote that nevertheless it is good to make another meal in the morning before the time of destruction, since one fulfills the obligation with it; see the Bach.
