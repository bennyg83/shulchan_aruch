(א) And we leave over food for two meals. Beit Yosef brought the words of the Rif and what was asked on him and what was answered on him; and in my humble opinion the Rif interprets in chapter 1 of Erev Pesach that what R' Elazar bar Tzadok stated "chulin in their time" — he did not mean all chulin, but only what one will need to burn from the chulin — that is, after food for two meals that he leaves on Erev Shabbat with ample leeway, for thus is implied the reason of his words; and that is there are three disputes: the Tanna Kamma holds we burn everything before Shabbat, and certainly one must leave two meals — only he estimates thus in narrow measure in a manner that one will not need any burning on Shabbat, because he holds there is a prohibition to do burning on Shabbat; and the Sages hold "in their time" — that is, since the main time of burning is on the 14th one may do so even on Shabbat, and on Erev Shabbat one does not burn at all; and R' Elazar bar Tzadok holds that now too there is time for burning even on Erev Shabbat — only there is a distinction: matters that are not needed at all on Shabbat, such as terumah whose time of eating is not fixed on Shabbat — one burns on Erev Shabbat; but chulin — that is, in what is needed for Shabbat — one leaves with leeway in a manner that it will certainly come to burning on this Shabbat, that one does on Shabbat; for if you wonder — let all chulin be burned on Shabbat? — what differs from terumah? And this is precise from the words of the baraita that R' Elazar bar Tzadok stated chapter 1 etc. "the time has come to burn the chametz" — why did he say "the chametz" with the definite article? It should have said "the time of burning chametz" has come! Rather certainly he speaks of the chametz that remains; but the main chametz was already burned from Erev Shabbat. And therefore the Rif stated well, who holds like R' Elazar of Bartuta — R' Elazar of Bartuta stated explicitly thus, that we leave two meals in order to eat until four hours; the question is: how does this depend on the measure of eating, for the measure of eating is a law that applies without Shabbat? And further — why did he not state at all "we leave over food"? For certainly the Sages also hold thus; rather necessarily this is what he said: and we leave food for two meals with leeway in order to eat until four hours — and that is even a measure that is not restricted — and the remainder one burns on Shabbat. And if so the words of the Rif are like Rambam and like the words of Tur, and everything is resolved amply according to my humble opinion; this burning of Shabbat regarding what remains must be that one burns it from the house before its time of prohibition — but not the rest of removal from the world, as is proven in siman 446; see there.

(ב) For the third meal its time is after minchah — so it must read.

(ג) Before the tenth hour. For from then onward it is forbidden to eat bread, as is stated siman 481; and matzah ashirah — meaning that it is kneaded in fruit juice.
