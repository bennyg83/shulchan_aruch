(א) He shall shake out the tablecloth — and those fine crumbs and wiping the bowls — one need not remove them from his domain, for they are trampled underfoot and burned by themselves; and nevertheless it is good that the house be cleaned afterward through a gentile, and if he has no gentile the Jew may clean himself by changing his garment [Acharonim].
(ב) He may give it etc. — as a complete gift; and we permit this on Shabbat for the sake of mitzvah so that the chametz not be in his possession, as was explained in siman 306.
(ג) On condition that he not go out with it — not specifically; rather it means that not on condition to go out with it; and even in an unspecified case it is permitted; unlike where an Israelite tells him explicitly that it is forbidden because of shevut of telling a gentile; and nevertheless a scrupulous person should give it with him on condition not to take out [as is implied in Hagahot Maimoniot there].
(ד) To the public domain — but to a karmelit, which is only shevut, it is permitted even to tell him explicitly to take it out, for shevut in a place of mitzvah is permitted. And see in siman 345 that there are views among poskim that the public domain is specifically with 600,000 — and according to them all our towns are karmelit.
(ה) As an evasion — meaning even though his intent was that he take it outside, nevertheless it is permitted since he does not tell him explicitly.
(ו) And a small amount — but much is forbidden, for it is evident that one gives him to take them out; however a small amount is permitted to give him even several times, for in any event it is only evasion and permitted. And a small amount is enough for one meal; and in a karmelit it is permitted in every manner, as above. If he has much chametz in a special room and forgot to sell it on Erev Shabbat, he must give it as a gift to a gentile; and he may give it to a gentile acquaintance who knows to return it to him after Pesach — only he must transfer it to him with a valid kinyan, that is, through hagbahah or meshichah that the gentile draw it to his domain; and if the chametz is much that he cannot draw it, he transfers through giving the key; and even though it is forbidden to give a gift on Shabbat, for the sake of mitzvah it is permitted. And if he fears to give as a gift lest he not return it to him — some Acharonim side that he may also sell to a gentile, and it is not called buying and selling on Shabbat since he does only to escape the prohibition of chametz; but many Acharonim disagree on this, and their opinion is that sale is forbidden in every manner, even if he does not take money from him at all but only fixes with him the price of the sale, or sells to him at market price without fixing the price and transfers the chametz through one of the ways of kinyan — even though this sale is for the sake of the mitzvah of burning chametz, the Sages did not permit complete buying and selling on Shabbat even for the sake of mitzvah, as was explained in siman 306 seif 6; see there in Mishna Berurah.
