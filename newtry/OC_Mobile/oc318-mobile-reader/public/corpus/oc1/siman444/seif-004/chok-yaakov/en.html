On condition that he not take it out. This implies that without specification it is forbidden with chametz, for it resembles other objects that were explained above in siman 321, since it is not the way to eat chametz in a Jew's house. So too it is implied in the Mordechai, first chapter of Shabbos, according to the view of some; and so wrote the Gedulas Mordechai there. This appears to me to be the simple intent of the Mechaber's language, unlike the Magen Avraham. Although above in siman 325 se'if 2 in the gloss he wrote that it is permitted to send even through a non-Jew for the sake of a mitzvah, such as removing chametz from the house on Pesach, nevertheless here he wrote without qualification like the Mechaber, to forbid it, because here he speaks where he has another remedy for the chametz, such as placing it before his dog or animal whose sustenance is upon him, as the Rokeach wrote in siman 266; see above, siman 324. Or, according to some halachic decisors, scattering it to the wind, as explained below in siman 446; see there in s.k. 9. If so, there is no mitzvah need, since it is possible in another way. This is why the Mechaber was precise and wrote, he may give it to a non-Jew, a language of permission; if it were a mitzvah obligation, he should have written language indicating that he is obligated to give it to a non-Jew. This is unlike above, where he speaks of a case where it is impossible in another way; then it is a mitzvah need. This appears simple to me, unlike some later authorities who press very much to distinguish in several ways in order to answer this difficulty, but their words do not fit the language of the Mechaber and the gloss. And a small amount. If he forgot to sell his chametz that he has in a designated room on Friday, he must give the room with delivery of the key to a non-Jew as a gift on Shabbos. It is possible that even a sale is also permitted, and it is not like buying and selling, which is forbidden on Shabbos, since he acts only to save himself from the prohibition of chametz. Tosafos wrote similarly in Eruvin folio 66, s.v. well, that it is permitted to rent domain from a non-Jew on Shabbos, and it is not like buying and selling because they do it only to permit carrying, as explained above in siman 382 and 383; see there. Nevertheless, where it is possible to do it as a gift, we do so, if he knows that the non-Jew will return it to him after Pesach, as explained below in siman 448.
