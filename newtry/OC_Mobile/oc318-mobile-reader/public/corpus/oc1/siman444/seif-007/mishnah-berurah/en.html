(א) To circumcise his son — and for a circumcision feast it implies that it is not called a mitzvah for this matter to override the mitzvah of burning.

(ב) Betrothal meal — and the same applies to the meal they make when the bride receives gifts; and the Acharonim wrote that the same applies in our time when they make a meal at the time of writing the conditions — it too is called a meal of mitzvah.

(ג) At his father-in-law's house — it implies that specifically the groom himself is discussed; and some are lenient also regarding another if it is called a betrothal meal.

(ד) And he remembered that he has chametz — and the same if he is uncertain that perhaps he has chametz [gloss of Maharam Chalavah]; and likewise the Acharonim.

(ה) He returns and burns — since the mitzvah will not be cancelled through the burning he fulfills both; and it implies that the mitzvah of burning takes precedence.

(ו) And if not etc. — meaning that if he returns and strives regarding the burning he will not remain time in the day to do the circumcision or the meal.

(ז) He nullifies it in his heart — in a place where after nullification he need not burn it but rather from doubt; and in a place of mitzvah the Sages established it on d'oraisa. And it deals when he is still in the fifth hour that he can nullify; but if he already stands in the sixth hour — necessarily he returns to burn, for then it is not in his power to nullify; and even though through this the mitzvah of circumcision will be cancelled — the positive commandment of "tashbitu" is more stringent, for every moment one transgresses upon it, which is not so with circumcision and the like. But if he goes to deal with a dead body of mitzvah [who has no one to bury him and lies exposed in the field in disgrace, for whoever encounters him is obligated to bury him] and remembers he has chametz in his house — even within Pesach when every hour he transgresses bal yera'eh — and even if there is time in the day he could return to his house and burn the chametz and return and go and bury him the same day — nevertheless it is forbidden for him to move from him until he buries him, for such great honor of creatures overrides all the mitzvot of the Torah in sit and do not act.

(ח) Even if there is time — to burn chametz and return and rescue; and the reason is because of saving lives they were lenient to rely on nullification l'chatchila, even where he can fulfill both. And Magen Avraham wrote that after the time of burning when nullification does not help he returns to burn, since there is time to save afterward — and specifically when it is clear to him that afterward he can also save; but in doubt he does not return; and many Acharonim wrote that when there is certainly time afterward to save too — he also does not return to burn, lest meanwhile he be preoccupied and not be able to save afterward.

(ט) For his own need — see among Acharonim regarding which poskim hold that even if he goes to the evening in order to learn at his rabbi it is called a matter of permission relative to the mitzvah of burning; and therefore he must return and burn in every case; and the reason is that the act of mitzvot is greater than study, even a d'rabbanan mitzvah when it cannot be fulfilled through others.

(י) He returns immediately — to burn as the Sages' mitzvah, even if he already set out on the way, for we do not push aside the Sages' mitzvot because of a matter of permission.

(כ) How far does he return — this applies to everything mentioned above; even if he went for a matter of permission, since he already set out on the way the Sages did not burden him to return when the chametz is less than an egg's volume — rather nullification suffices for him; and if the sixth hour arrived when he can no longer nullify he must return immediately even if there is only an olive's volume of chametz, for as long as he does not burn it from the world completely he transgresses a Torah positive commandment; but if there is only less than a k'zayit the Sages did not burden him to return but he goes on his way and when he returns home he burns; and if it is Yom Tov or Shabbat he covers a vessel over it until evening, as will be explained in siman 446; see there.
