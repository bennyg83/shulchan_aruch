(א) Betrothal. That is, if he betrothed on Erev Shabbat then it is also permitted to make a meal; not so if he betrothed beforehand — it is forbidden to make a meal on Erev Shabbat, as is stated siman 249 seif 2, Magen Avraham. And the same applies to the meal of gifts or the purchase meal that they make now at the time of the match — it too is called a meal of mitzvah. Magen Avraham and Chok Yaakov. And see in Chavot Yair siman 9, who expanded to explain what thing is called a meal of mitzvah; and at the end of the responsum he wrote, and what they do — a meal after writing the conditions that they call knas from the groom — certainly it is not called a meal of mitzvah. End of his words.

(ב) His father-in-law. No — rather the groom himself; but the same law applies after one goes to a meal of mitzvah. Acharonim (and Chok Yaakov raised specifically the groom and not another — for even when he goes to his rabbi he returns).

(ג) He shall nullify it. And if it is after the time of burning, when he then cannot nullify it — he returns and burns. And Magen Avraham wrote: if he goes to deal with a dead body of mitzvah he does not return and burn. And in Sefer Even HaOzer he ruled that the same applies to any matter of mitzvah — he does not return to burn; and the reason is that Tosafos wrote: one who delays his chametz and intends to burn it has not transgressed in Beit Yosef, since it is not a disconnected positive commandment. End of his words. (And see in Sefer Eliyah Rabbah what he wondered on Magen Avraham in this; and nevertheless in a place of mitzvah they established it d'oraisa. Acharonim.)

(ד) Time. And even after the time of burning when he cannot nullify and there is still time to rescue — nevertheless he does not return — so Kesef Mishneh chapter 3 of Hilchot Chametz uMatzah, not like Magen Avraham who wrote the opposite. End of his words.

(ה) Himself. Even when he goes to his rabbi to learn — it is called a matter of permission relative to the mitzvah of burning. Magen Avraham and Chok Yaakov.

(ו) Less. And if it is less than a k'zayit — even if it is after the time of prohibition when he cannot nullify — nevertheless he need not return. Magen Avraham and Chok Yaakov.
