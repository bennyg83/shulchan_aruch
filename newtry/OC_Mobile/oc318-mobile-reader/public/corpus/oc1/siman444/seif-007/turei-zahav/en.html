(א) Betrothal meal. In the Gemara R' Yehuda and R' Yosi disagree regarding the second meal of betrothal — according to R' Yosi it is optional and according to R' Yehuda it is a mitzvah; and Rashi explained: second meal — such is the way of grooms after the betrothal meal to again send gifts to his betrothed and feast there; and we rule like R' Yosi. And Rambam did not mention the second, which was called gifts, but simply betrothal — and that is both of them that apply one after the other at the time of betrothal. And the Tur was careful and mentioned in explanation the first of betrothal or of gifts — meaning the second that follows; and Beit Yosef understood that gifts is a different term and is not called second of betrothal; and nevertheless he wrote that the Tur rules like R' Yosi, and Rashi's words escaped him.

(ב) And if he went out for his own need. The Rosh asked — behold, one does not make an eruv at all per the words of permission; and he explained in the name of the Yerushalmi — that is, one who goes to his rabbi to learn; here the year R' Hamma'aseh is greater than Talmud, which calls it permission regarding betrothal meal and slaughtering his Pesach and circumcising his son; and I do not know why he did not copy this in Shulchan Aruch; and it implies simply that one who goes not for a mitzvah matter at all — his law is equal to one who goes to learn, that he does not return except up to an egg's volume. And in Levush he wants to infer that for a matter of permission he returns even for half an egg's volume, and he has no proof; and he himself wrote that the Mishnah does not imply thus, but rather he wants to divide three divisions — that is, between mitzvah and study regarding even an egg's volume, and he further divided regarding less than an egg's volume between study and permission — and it is not so, for here there are only two divisions — mitzvah and study and no more; and necessarily he wrote "for his own need" regarding one who goes to his rabbi.
