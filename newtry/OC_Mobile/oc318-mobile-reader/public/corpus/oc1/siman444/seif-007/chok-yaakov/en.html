To eat from a betrothal feast. The same applies to a sivlonos meal or a kinyan meal that is made nowadays at the time of the shidduch; it is also called a mitzvah meal. This is so although it is forbidden to make a betrothal feast on Friday. See further what the later authorities wrote to answer this difficulty. See the Rambam, chapter 5 of Hilchos De'os, who wrote that it is not called a mitzvah meal from which a Torah scholar may benefit except when it is the daughter of a Torah scholar marrying a Torah scholar. The Kesef Mishneh there wrote that this is only by way of proper conduct; they said in Perek Eilu Ovrin that it is not called a mitzvah meal only with the daughter of a Torah scholar marrying an am ha'aretz, and the daughter of a kohen marrying a Yisrael. But where both are equal, even if they are amei ha'aretz, it is called a mitzvah meal; see there. See the Mordechai in Perek Makom Shenahagu regarding the law of a mitzvah meal.
In his father-in-law's house. This does not mean specifically the groom himself; the same applies to another person who goes to a mitzvah meal. So it implies in the Rambam, chapter 3 of Hilchos Pesach, and so wrote the Magen Avraham. See above, siman 551.
He nullifies it in his heart. If it is after the time of destruction, when he can no longer nullify it, as explained above in siman 434 and nearby in se'if 1, he returns and destroys it. The Magen Avraham wrote that if he is going to occupy himself with a meis mitzvah, he does not return to destroy it.
Even if there is time. Even after the time of destruction, when he cannot nullify it and there is still time to save the person, nevertheless he should not return, for we are concerned that perhaps he will become occupied with destroying it and be prevented from saving lives. So wrote the Kesef Mishneh, chapter 3 of Hilchos Chametz U'Matzah, unlike the Magen Avraham, who wrote the opposite.
And if he went out for his own need. He followed the language of the Rambam, for the Beis Yosef wrote that from the plainness of his language it is clear that he disagrees with the Tur in the name of the Rosh and the Yerushalmi, who ruled that even one going to make an eruv in order to learn Torah returns, because action is greater than study. Nevertheless, it appears that the main ruling follows the view of the Tur and the Rosh, and so appears to be the view of the later authorities. Although the Rema did not gloss anything here, perhaps he relied on what he wrote at the end of siman 431, that even if one began learning, he must interrupt because of checking for chametz, which is the purpose of destruction; all the more so here. As for the Magen Avraham's difficulty, why here do we say that he returns because of the rabbinic mitzvah of destruction, from the law that a kohen may become tamei in a rabbinic beis haperas in order to go learn Torah, see what he answered about this. In my humble opinion, from the outset there is no difficulty, for we say the reason that action is greater than study, which implies specifically an action that is a positive mitzvah, similar to the concept that a positive commandment overrides a negative commandment, even rabbinically, such as checking for and destroying chametz. This is unlike tumah in a beis haperas, which is only a rabbinic negative commandment; this is simple. Aside from this, one cannot compare one decree of the Sages to another, for sometimes they upheld their words in the face of kares, and sometimes they did not uphold their words in the face of kares. They said and they said, as explained in Responsa Rambam, siman 127; see there.
Less than this, he nullifies it. After the time of destruction, when he cannot nullify it, he returns for up to a kezayis; less than this he does not return. So is the implication of Tosafos at the end of Perek Eilu Ovrin in the name of the Yerushalmi, and so wrote the Magen Avraham.
