(א) "To circumcise his son" — it is simple that the same applies if he goes to circumcise his friend's son, if the father cannot himself — for behold the obligation is then upon all Israel; and likewise Rambam chapter 3 did not mention at all "goes to circumcise," but rather "to do a mitzvah"; see there.

(ב) "Betrothal meal" — the later authorities wondered — behold, above in siman 249 it is explained that it is forbidden to make a betrothal meal on Erev Shabbat, and the same on Yom Tov; and many forced answers to this. And in truth there is no difficulty; and this case is different on Erev Pesach — and needless to say if the meal was close to noon, as it appears from what he said "if he can burn and return to his mitzvah," and the time of burning is before noon — if so, necessarily the entire meal is without bread, for chametz is forbidden and matzah is also forbidden then; and it deals only with a general feast that is not forbidden; and on the contrary, much wine is consumed, as is stated in the Gemara. And even if we say the meal was in the morning and because of his meal he would be forced to burn chametz before the time — likewise there is no difficulty; for since in any event he would be forced to stop his meal at the end of the fourth hour because of the prohibition of chametz — this is not in the category of a large meal that they forbade on Erev Shabbat and Yom Tov, and because of "lest he enter Shabbat when he is not desirous to eat" — for certainly he will be desirous to eat in the evening, since he is forced to stop at the fourth hour. And this case is different on every Erev Shabbat and Yom Tov, for we are concerned the meal will extend a long time; and akin to our words is explained in Sefer Chak Yosef and Magen Elef. And know further that from the Tur it implies that the second meal of betrothal is not in the category of betrothal meal — only the meal of gifts is like a betrothal meal; and so he explained his words in Beit Yosef; and all the later authorities wondered on him — behold, second meal and meal of gifts are one, as is explained in Rashi in Pesachim 49. And I found in Ittur daf 120 side 2 explicitly like the Beit Yosef's words; and it appears that the Tur's words flow from it.

(ג) "To rescue from the river" — see Mishna Berurah. And it appears that the same applies if it does not touch saving a life at all — only monetary damage — nullification suffices; if he fears that meanwhile while he is occupied with burning the river will wash his property or another Jew's property. And it is further possible that even if he can return and rescue, the Sages were lenient regarding it to rely on nullification in such a case; and this requires further study for the law.
