(א) Betrothal meal. This law is in a complete Mishnah chapter 3 of Pesachim; and if so it implies that it is permitted to make a betrothal meal on Erev Pesach, and all the more so on Erev Shabbat — for it is proven at the beginning of chapter 7 of Pesach that Erev Pesach is more stringent than Erev Shabbat. And if so it is difficult on what is stated in siman 249 seif 2 that it is forbidden to make a betrothal meal on Erev Shabbat — and that is from the Yerushalmi, and it disagrees with our Talmud; for in chapter 1 of Ketubot there is in the Yerushalmi that it is forbidden to marry on Erev Shabbat because of honor of Shabbat, and in our Gemara we are not concerned for this; and nevertheless this requires further study — how can the Yerushalmi disagree with the Mishnah that permits making a betrothal meal? Therefore it appears to me that here it means: it is forbidden to betroth on Erev Shabbat — that which is stated "betrothal meal" (meaning: if he betrothed beforehand it is forbidden to make the meal on Erev Shabbat); but to betroth is permitted (meaning: it is permitted to betroth on Erev Shabbat lest another precede him) — and then it is also permitted to make a meal; and as Rama wrote there, that a meal whose time is fixed on Erev Shabbat, and the meal of gifts which is the second meal after the betrothal meal, is also a meal of mitzvah (Bach); and so is implied in the Gemara and Rosh and Tur; and likewise Rivash siman 200, not like Beit Yosef; and Rambam siman 109 wrote regarding the meal of gifts that they make in Ashkenaz on the night before the wedding, when the groom sends gifts before eating — even though he has not yet betrothed it is called a meal of mitzvah; end of his words. And in these lands where they send gifts long before the wedding and make a meal that they call purchase meal — this requires further study whether it is called a meal of mitzvah; and it appears to me that it is called a meal of mitzvah.

(ב) At his father-in-law's house. And so the Mishnah implies that specifically the groom is not obligated to return, for it is impossible to fulfill through others; and therefore it says in the Yerushalmi that Torah study on Erev Shabbat is preferable; and as we say in chapter 1 of Moed Katan that a mitzvah that cannot be fulfilled through others is preferable to Torah study. But another person who goes to a meal of mitzvah must return — for even one who goes to his rabbi must return, in Beit Yosef; and Rambam wrote: one who went out to do a mitzvah or to eat a meal of mitzvah, such as betrothal meal and wedding meal etc. — it implies that he does not hold to distinguish between a mitzvah that can be done through others or cannot be done through others.

(ג) He shall nullify it in his heart. For in a place of mitzvah they established it d'oraisa; and it implies that if it is from the beginning of the sixth hour and above, when it is not in his power to nullify, he returns immediately; and so is implied in Rambam, because this positive commandment and this positive commandment — the positive commandment of "tashbitu" is more stringent, for every hour one transgresses upon it, which is not so with circumcision and the like; and nevertheless it appears to me that a dead body of mitzvah is preferable to "tashbitu" of chametz — for behold it overrides even Pesach which has karet, and siman 687 what is called nevertheless; and it appears to me that even if he has chametz in his house on Pesach that he transgresses a negative commandment — nevertheless a dead body of mitzvah is preferable, as is stated in chapter 3 of Berachot that sit and do not act is preferable.

(ד) Even if there is time. And the reason is that d'oraisa mere nullification suffices (nevertheless Rashi and Ran); and if so it implies that at the sixth hour he returns, for nullification does not help; and it appears to me specifically when it is clear to him that he can save afterward — but in doubt he does not return, for doubt of lives overrides even severe Shabbat.

(ה) For his own need. And Tur wrote that even if he goes to the evening in order to learn at his rabbi it is called a matter of permission relative to the mitzvah of burning; and he wrote in Shulchan Gavoah the reason — that likewise he can learn in his house and fulfill the mitzvah too; end of his words. And there is a difficulty — behold it is permitted for a kohen to become impure in Beit HaPeras, which is d'rabbanan, in order to go learn before his rabbi, even though he has a rabbi in the city, for not everyone succeeds in learning, as is stated in chapter 1 of Avodah Zarah; and if so here too it is d'rabbanan; and one may say that in any event here there is a d'oraisa prohibition in this chametz if he did not nullify it; alternatively this case is different, for he can return to his house and afterward go to his rabbi to learn — and if so the words of Shulchan Gavoah do not fit well; and see what is stated siman 415 that sometimes one makes an eruv for a matter of permission — and to this Tur intended, such as that he makes an eruv with his legs; and so explicitly in Rashi's explanation; and it appears to me the Rosh had a different version in Rashi; see there.

(ו) Less than this. But if it is less than a k'zayit — even if it is after the time of its prohibition when he cannot nullify — nevertheless he need not return (as is implied in Tosafos); and see what is stated siman 442 seif 7.
