(3) [Levush] Between dry, etc. — this reason, since through preserving, etc., applies to dry; but wet is fit for eating even before it is preserved. Olas Tamid wrote: it is astonishing — what is different from a soft walnut in siman 202 seif 14 that one blesses shehakol? And it is possible — since on moist ginger one blesses borei peri haAdamah, therefore when dry and preserved one blesses borei peri haAdamah as well. And in this manner Bach wrote in siman 204 note 5; and see in Levush there seif 11. And see in Bach, who extended and concluded that if figs were preserved dry — it is forbidden on account of bishul akum, since it is not eaten when raw; but when preserved moist — it is permitted according to all. And in the book Or Chadash he did not write thus in the name of Bach, and he wondered at Bach from the Talmud Berakhot — see there — and he was not precise in Bach's words. And Magen Avraham holds that even when preserved dry it is permitted, since it is eaten when raw with bread and salt; and since bishul akum is d'rabbanan, one may be lenient. Taz wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 113: large nuts that they preserve with bitter peel and their pits are forbidden on account of bishul akum, for they are not eaten raw.
