Birchos Maharam writes the same, and this is the practice. Bach. Agudah, however, writes that over blackberries and strawberries one should recite Borei Pri HaEtz, because the branch endures for many days and after its fruit is removed, fruit returns upon that same branch. This is primary. Piskei Tosafos on the sixth chapter of Berachos writes the same. The same applies to fruits called black Polish berries. The red ones, however, grow among grasses, and one recites Borei Pri HaAdamah. Regarding the red berries found in Tammuz, and another kind found in winter, which contain only liquid gathered inside their seeds, so that people suck them and discard them, it is proper to recite Shehakol, as stated in siman 202, seif 8 and seif 15. Although some swallow them with the peel and seed, our eyes see that the peel and seed are entirely unfit for consumption. One therefore recites a blessing only over the liquid within. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1; Yad Aharon, Hagahos Beis Yosef. Regarding Magen Avraham's statement that Shehakol is recited over fruits that are sucked, Nishmas Adam, rule 51, paragraph 6, writes that perhaps he refers to a type of berry whose flesh is wholly unfit and only sucked. Over berries called brusznice, zaribin, and similar fruits, however, it is obvious that one recites Borei Pri HaAdamah, and this is the universal practice; see there. Regarding Magen Avraham's statement that Borei Pri HaEtz is recited over black berries, Chayei Adam there, paragraph 9, writes that the universal practice is to recite Borei Pri HaAdamah because they are not sufficiently significant as fruit; see there.
