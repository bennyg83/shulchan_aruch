[Levush] Like a tree. Perishah writes that one should not ask from small nuts, over which Borei Pri HaEtz is recited, because where the same species commonly grows in gardens one does not treat its wild-tree form as ownerless fruit. Magen Avraham appears to rule otherwise, but the matter requires further examination. Regarding certain spices, I found that they are crushed after drying and mixed with other spices. Therefore, when preserved in sugar, it appears to me that one should recite Shehakol and rely on the general rule. See below, siman 204 seif katan 5.
[Levush] Whether dried, etc. The reason that preservation changes it applies to dried ginger, whereas fresh ginger is fit to eat even before being preserved. Olat Tamid wonders why this differs from a soft nut in siman 202 seif 14, where one recites Shehakol. Perhaps since fresh ginger receives Borei Pri HaAdamah, one likewise recites Borei Pri HaAdamah when dried ginger is preserved, similar to what Bach writes in siman 204 seif katan 5; see Levush there, seif 11. Bach concludes at length that if dried ginger was preserved by non-Jews, it is prohibited because of bishulei akum, since it is not eaten raw, whereas fresh ginger that was preserved is permitted according to all opinions. Or Chadash cites Bach differently and challenges him from the Gemara in Berachot, but he did not read Bach carefully. Magen Avraham's view is that even dried ginger preserved by non-Jews is permitted because it can be eaten raw with bread and salt, and since bishulei akum is d'rabbanan one may be lenient. Taz in Yoreh Deah siman 113 writes that large nuts preserved together with their bitter shells and kernels are prohibited because of bishulei akum, since they are not eaten raw.
