4) [Seif 2] Over berries that grow on a shrub, one recites Borei Pri HaAdamah. This is the view of R' Yosef cited by the Tur. In the name of R' Yitzchak, however, the Tur writes that one recites Borei Pri HaEtz, and writes that his father Rosh, of blessed memory, practiced accordingly; see there. Bach writes that the present practice follows the Shulchan Aruch to recite Borei Pri HaAdamah, and that this is proper and correct in order to avoid a doubtful blessing; see there. Levush likewise writes that this is the practice, as do the later authorities. See below, paragraph 7.
5) There: Over berries, etc. This refers to the fruits called mulberries and raspberries. In some countries, however, certain kinds of mulberries grow on a tree, and one then recites Borei Pri HaEtz over them. Orach Chaim; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1.
6) There, gloss: It is not called a tree, etc. See Be'er HaGolah, who writes that Rema's gloss belongs after seif 3. Maamar Mordechai follows him, but they did not examine the Tur, which explicitly writes this concerning the berries themselves; see there. Meorei Or, section Be'er Sheva, folio 41, similarly writes that the gloss is correctly placed in seif 2; see there.
7) There, gloss: It is not called a tree, etc. The sign for determining which produce is tree fruit and which is ground produce is as follows: Any tree whose fruit falls while its trunk and roots remain in the ground, and which returns from year to year and produces branches, leaves, and fruit, or leaves and fruit without branches, is called tree fruit. Anything whose roots do not remain in the ground and which must be sown every year is called ground produce. There are species whose fruit falls while the trunk and roots remain in the ground, but during winter the trunk dries and is entirely consumed, leaving only the roots. The following year, the root again produces a new trunk upon which fruit grows again, such as berries that grow on a shrub. Some say one recites Borei Pri HaEtz because the trunk remains for many days after the fruit falls, the roots endure, and it need not be sown every year. Others say that since the trunk is consumed every year and only the roots remain, although it grows again by itself and need not be sown every year, it is ground produce, and one recites Borei Pri HaAdamah. Tur, Beis Yosef, and Levush. We have already written above in paragraph 4 that Maran, of blessed memory, and the later authorities follow the second opinion, to recite Borei Pri HaAdamah. Only after the fact, if one recited Borei Pri HaEtz, did he fulfill his obligation, because some hold that view and in cases of doubt concerning blessings we rule leniently.
8) There, gloss: Since its leaves are consumed, etc. If so, the same applies to the fruit called blackberries and the red fruit called strawberries: one recites Borei Pri HaAdamah. Birchos Maharam writes the same, and this is the practice. Bach. Agudah, however, writes that over blackberries and strawberries one should recite Borei Pri HaEtz, because the branch endures for many days and after its fruit is removed, fruit returns upon that same branch. This is primary. Piskei Tosafos on the sixth chapter of Berachos writes the same. The same applies to fruits called black Polish berries. The red ones, however, grow among grasses, and one recites Borei Pri HaAdamah. Regarding the red berries found in Tammuz, and another kind found in winter, which contain only liquid gathered inside their seeds, so that people suck them and discard them, it is proper to recite Shehakol, as stated in siman 202, seif 8 and seif 15. Although some swallow them with the peel and seed, our eyes see that the peel and seed are entirely unfit for consumption. One therefore recites a blessing only over the liquid within. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1; Yad Aharon, Hagahos Beis Yosef. Regarding Magen Avraham's statement that Shehakol is recited over fruits that are sucked, Nishmas Adam, rule 51, paragraph 6, writes that perhaps he refers to a type of berry whose flesh is wholly unfit and only sucked. Over berries called brusznice, zaribin, and similar fruits, however, it is obvious that one recites Borei Pri HaAdamah, and this is the universal practice; see there. Regarding Magen Avraham's statement that Borei Pri HaEtz is recited over black berries, Chayei Adam there, paragraph 9, writes that the universal practice is to recite Borei Pri HaAdamah because they are not sufficiently significant as fruit; see there.
