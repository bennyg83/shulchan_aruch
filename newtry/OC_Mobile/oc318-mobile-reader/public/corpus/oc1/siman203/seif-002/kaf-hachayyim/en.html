2) There: Borei Pri HaAdamah. Over eggplant, one recites Borei Pri HaAdamah. Rabbeinu Avraham, son of the Rambam, in the novellae at the beginning of Maaseh Rokeach. Maran, of blessed memory, likewise writes this in Beis Yosef in the name of a responsum of the Geonim. It consequently follows that one need not treat eggplant stringently because of orlah. Although this conclusion could be rejected, we have already written in Yoreh Deah, siman 294, paragraph 4, that the holy and exalted Arizal, of blessed memory, and R' Chaim Vital ate them; see there. Birkei Yosef, paragraph 1. Although Radbaz, siman 296, writes that one recites Borei Pri HaEtz, cited by Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur, paragraph 1, we rule like the view that one recites Borei Pri HaAdamah because in cases of doubt concerning blessings we rule leniently. Furthermore, since those exalted holy men were not concerned about orlah, they too evidently agreed that it is not a tree and its blessing is Borei Pri HaAdamah. Therefore, this is our practical ruling and the common practice. This applies specifically when cooked. If one eats it raw, he recites only Shehakol, as stated below at the beginning of siman 205; see there. See further below, paragraph 13.
