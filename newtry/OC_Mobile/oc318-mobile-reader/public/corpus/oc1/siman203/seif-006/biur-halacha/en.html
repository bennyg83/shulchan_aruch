On ginger that they preserve — see in Sha'arei Teshuvah in the name of Emek Berachah that specifically when they preserve pieces of it; but if it is crushed and ground until it is not recognizable what it is — even though one knows it is ginger — one blesses shehakol. And I did not understand this, for is it not explained below in seif 7 regarding spices that are ground, etc. — and that is even when they are ground completely. And even according to those who are stringent regarding dates that one made them like a dough so that its previous blessing is lost — as explained in siman 202 seif 7 in the gloss — nevertheless regarding spices they agree that even when it is completely dissolved, nevertheless one blesses its previous blessing, since their custom is thus to grind completely; and as explained in Terumat HaDeshen siman 29, who brought a source for this from Rashi's words, who explained regarding hemp in spices — ground, that they spice in honey — see there; and behold hemp applies also to ginger, as is evident in the Gemara. If so, it is proven that even when it is ground and crushed, nevertheless one blesses its appropriate blessing, because its custom is thus. And perhaps one may say that even though hemp includes ginger as well, nevertheless regarding grinding it is possible to distinguish between ginger and other spices: ginger, when they preserve it in honey, is not customary to grind it so much — and therefore its previous blessing is lost. But from the words of Ateret Zekenim, and likewise from Taz, it appears as we wrote — and this requires further study.
