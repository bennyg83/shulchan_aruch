7) There, gloss: It is not called a tree, etc. The sign for determining which produce is tree fruit and which is ground produce is as follows: Any tree whose fruit falls while its trunk and roots remain in the ground, and which returns from year to year and produces branches, leaves, and fruit, or leaves and fruit without branches, is called tree fruit. Anything whose roots do not remain in the ground and which must be sown every year is called ground produce. There are species whose fruit falls while the trunk and roots remain in the ground, but during winter the trunk dries and is entirely consumed, leaving only the roots. The following year, the root again produces a new trunk upon which fruit grows again, such as berries that grow on a shrub. Some say one recites Borei Pri HaEtz because the trunk remains for many days after the fruit falls, the roots endure, and it need not be sown every year. Others say that since the trunk is consumed every year and only the roots remain, although it grows again by itself and need not be sown every year, it is ground produce, and one recites Borei Pri HaAdamah. Tur, Beis Yosef, and Levush. We have already written above in paragraph 4 that Maran, of blessed memory, and the later authorities follow the second opinion, to recite Borei Pri HaAdamah. Only after the fact, if one recited Borei Pri HaEtz, did he fulfill his obligation, because some hold that view and in cases of doubt concerning blessings we rule leniently.
