As though. See Taz, who rules stringently where the Jew sets a deadline, because the non-Jew then works for the Jew’s purpose in order to finish within the allotted time. Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 12, permits such a case, reasoning that if the non-Jew does not perform the work on Shabbos he will do it during the week. See there. Taz’s reasoning appears correct: when the Jew sets a deadline and needs the garment within that time, and it is known that it cannot be completed unless work is also done on Shabbos, it is as though he instructed the non-Jew to work on Shabbos. Even if the non-Jew could force himself to sit through the night and complete it during the week, he is still working for the Jew’s purpose. See Levush regarding his phrase “each day,” and Eliyah Rabbah there. It may be that he explains the dispute between Rambam and Raavad in the manner described by Taz and as I have written. A non-Jew hired by the year to extinguish fires may be permitted. See Beit Yosef here and Magen Avraham, siman 334, seif katan 28, for they permitted this regarding a fire. With Hashem’s help it will be explained there.
