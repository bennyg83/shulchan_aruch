As though he had fixed a price with him, etc. This is Rambam’s view. The opinion that forbids it is Raavad, who writes that although the Jew does not object when the gentile is idle, the work performed on Shabbos nevertheless benefits the Jew, and the arrangement is not comparable to contracted work. This implies that even Rambam permits hiring for a fixed period only where the Jew does not object if the gentile remains idle on a particular day. If he insists that the gentile not be idle on any day, it is forbidden according to all opinions. Likewise, even without an explicit stipulation, if he hired him to complete a book or garment within that period, it is as though he insists on uninterrupted labor. Rambam permits only where the gentile is not obligated to complete a particular task and the Jew does not object to his being idle. So it appears to me.
