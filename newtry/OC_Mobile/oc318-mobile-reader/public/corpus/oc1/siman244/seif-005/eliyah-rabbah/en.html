Here, however, they may still suspect that he hired the non-Jew by the day and merely claims that he hired him as a contractor. These are the words of Magen Avraham. According to what I wrote in the name of Ran, that it appears to people as though he is a day laborer, perhaps the intent is that even if they know he is a contractor, it nevertheless appears to ordinary people like day labor, because they do not know how to distinguish. Magen Avraham writes that in some places the custom is to hire a contractor to remove refuse from the street, and the non-Jew works on Shabbos, because with communal property there is no suspicion, as stated in Yoreh Deah, siman 141. Nevertheless, the great authorities did not wish to permit construction of a communal synagogue because of chillul Hashem. Even repairing a street in a place where the permissive custom does not exist should not be treated leniently. The reason of chillul Hashem appears forced here. It is more plausible that they did not permit it because perhaps the rule that a public body is not suspected applies specifically to idolatry, but not to a Rabbinic prohibition of this kind. Shach in Yoreh Deah there writes that where two people are involved there is no suspicion. See siman 276, seif katan 14.
