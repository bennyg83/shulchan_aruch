That he write for him. It appears to me that Rambam refers to the practice of noblemen who retain a designated scribe or tailor. Whenever the nobleman needs writing or sewing, the worker must do it, and when there is no need he sits idle. Therefore, the worker may write or weave on Shabbos, because he works whenever he wishes. The Jew did not instruct him to work on Shabbos. If he wishes, he may do it tomorrow, and the Jew gains nothing from its being done on Shabbos.
And some forbid. They hold that the Jew benefits from the work done on Shabbos, because he may also need writing tomorrow and the worker would be unable to do both jobs at once.
According to all opinions it is forbidden. It is highly likely that the Jew will need a different task tomorrow, and thus he benefits from the non-Jew's work on Shabbos. The same applies if he hired him to weave constantly or write constantly. It is plainly forbidden for him to write on Shabbos.
