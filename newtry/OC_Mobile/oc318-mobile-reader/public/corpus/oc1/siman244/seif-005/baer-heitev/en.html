It appears to me that Rambam refers to the manner of government officials, who have a designated scribe obligated to write whenever the official requires it, while at other times he remains idle. The gentile may therefore write on Shabbos, since he performs the work whenever he chooses. The Jew did not instruct him to do it on Shabbos, and if he wishes he may do it tomorrow. The Jew gains nothing from its being done on Shabbos. The opinion that forbids it is Raavad, who holds that the Jew does gain from the work performed on Shabbos, since perhaps he will need the gentile to write something else tomorrow and the gentile will not be able to perform both tasks at once.
According to all opinions it is forbidden. It is nearly certain that the Jew will require the gentile to perform another task tomorrow, so the Jew profits from the gentile’s work on Shabbos. The same applies if he hired him to write for him continually; it is obvious that the gentile may not write for him on Shabbos. Magen Avraham.
