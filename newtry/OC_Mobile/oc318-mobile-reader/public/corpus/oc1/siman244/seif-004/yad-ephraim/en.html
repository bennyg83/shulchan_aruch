Seif katan 19: “It is permissible to give silver to a gentile.” Meaning, a Jew appointed by the king over the mint may give the gentile silver to prepare the coins even on Shabbos. There is no concern of prohibited sound production, which Hagahos Maimoniyos forbids, as explained in the gloss to siman 252. That concern applies only when the sound is produced by a Jew’s equipment, such as a mill or similar machinery. Here, however, both the striking hammer, the silver being prepared, and the minting equipment belong not to the Jew but to the king or the king’s craftsmen. The Jew is merely appointed by the king over the work. The king makes an arrangement with him and fixes a sum for which he will hire workers and produce a specified number of coins. The silver and all the tools used in the work nevertheless remain the king’s property. In such a case there is no concern regarding the sound, because it is not Jewish property. Nor is there concern regarding his hiring the workers, because they come for their wages, perform the work in the gentile’s premises, and he does not insist that they work specifically on Shabbos. This is comparable to giving hides to a tanner under a fixed-price arrangement, which is permitted. Some interpret Magen Avraham’s citation of Hagahos Maimoniyos, that there is no sound production involving Jewish property, to mean merely that it is not publicly known that the enterprise belongs to a Jew. This is incorrect. His straightforward intent is as stated here, and the gloss itself uses the same wording: although coin minting produces sound, it is permitted because the equipment does not belong to a Jew. This is clear.
