The principal difficulty of Bach in siman 543 concerns Beit Yosef’s statement that the Tur did not write that only an important person is different because he ruled like the second version. On Chol HaMoed, work outside the techum is prohibited by law because of maris ayin. Certainly, even according to the first version, every person is prohibited, for the questioner accepted that construction within the techum is prohibited under contract even though the concern is only maris ayin. His difficulty was that outside the techum it is completely permitted. The Gemara answers according to the first version that an important person should be stringent even outside the techum. This refers to Shabbos or Yom Tov, but on Chol HaMoed, even according to the first version, every person is prohibited. Taz answers that Beit Yosef’s intent is indeed inferred from the fact that the Tur did not rule in Hilchos Shabbos that an important person is prohibited outside the techum. This shows that he follows the second version, relying on the ruling here that there is no distinction. Whenever the building was constructed through a maris ayin prohibition, all agree that it is merely proper to be stringent, not prohibited by law; where it was built permissibly, even an important person need not be stringent. The sugya may be explained as follows. According to the first version there are two stringencies: first, an important person should be stringent even where the act is entirely permitted; second, where the building was constructed in a manner prohibited because of maris ayin, everyone is prohibited by law. This follows from the Gemara’s question, “But it was outside the techum,” implying that construction within the techum would indeed be prohibited. According to the second version there are two leniencies: where the act is permitted, even an important person need not be stringent; and where there was a prohibition, it is only proper to be stringent, not legally prohibited, as indicated by the fact that the Sages did not enter the building because they were stringent. The Tur rules leniently in this Rabbinic matter according to the second version. This means that the second version is actually more stringent regarding an ordinary person where there was a prohibition, for according to the first version an ordinary person might be permitted and only an important person would need to be stringent. The Tur nevertheless follows the second version as primary. One may further explain that according to the second version, only assistance with straw, which is an actual prohibition, warrants stringency, whereas contracted work involving only maris ayin would require no stringency at all. According to this, the Tur’s statement, “and even if it was built in a prohibited manner, it is proper to be stringent,” means: not only where it was built under contract, where there is only maris ayin and the matter is merely proper rather than prohibited by law, but even where it was built through an actual prohibition, by day laborers or by assisting with straw, it is only proper and not legally prohibited. This is strained. One could also say that according to the second version there is no stringency at all where the concern is only maris ayin, and therefore Beit Yosef explains the Tur to mean that even where it was built in an actually prohibited manner, it is merely proper to be stringent after the fact. Bach writes here that Rabbeinu Tam is sufficiently authoritative to rely upon after the fact, and disagrees with Taz on two points. First, where work was contracted it is at least proper to be stringent, though not legally prohibited; this is why the Tur in siman 543 writes “proper” and not “prohibited,” since he discusses contracted work. If it was built through an actual prohibition, by day laborers, it is legally prohibited to occupy it. According to Taz, even where it was built through actual prohibited labor it is legally permitted to enter, because those Sages merely acted stringently, and the Gemara did not formulate an actual prohibition. It is nevertheless proper for every person to be stringent in such a case. Regarding maris ayin in contracted work, there is no need even for initial stringency, because assisting with straw was an actual prohibition and not merely maris ayin.
