(14) To cut the stones, etc. This applies specifically where the stones and beams belong to the Jew and he gave them to a non-Jewish craftsman to shape and prepare. If the non-Jew shapes his own stones and prepares his own beams under a contract for the entire project, and he still has the option to use other stones and keep these for himself, then even if he does so for the Jew, it is permitted when not done on the Jew's premises. The work is not yet considered the Jew's property, even if it is publicly known to be intended for him. Dagul Merevavah writes this, and Radbaz preceded him. Radbaz concludes that if the non-Jew cuts the stones on Shabbos next to the construction site, it is improper because of maris ayin, lest people say he is the Jew's employee.
(15) Because it is needed for attached work. Since the stones are intended to be set into the building, they are treated like attached property. If the work is within the techum, the Jew must protest even when it is performed far from the building.
(16) If they did so. See below for the ruling.
(17) Some say that if it is not, etc. This opinion applies to all the preceding cases. Even fully attached work was forbidden under contract only in an ordinary case, because the Jew's name is ordinarily associated with it. If it is known that the building is not publicly identified as his, they did not forbid contracting. Rama cites this opinion specifically regarding cutting stones because it is uncommon for an actual building not to be identified with its owner.
(18) It is permitted. The Jew need not protest if the non-Jew works on Shabbos, provided he did not tell him to work on Shabbos. This applies only if the non-Jew works at his own premises, or at least far enough from the construction site that it is not recognizable as the Jew's work. Several later authorities accept this opinion regarding cutting stones, and perhaps the Mechaber also agrees. Regarding the attached building itself, however, one should not be lenient even when it stands in a place where ownership is not publicly known, because neighbors or household members who know it belongs to him may suspect him.
