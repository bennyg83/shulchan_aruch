If it is not publicly known, etc. Rama writes this in the name of Kol Bo. It requires study, because I examined Kol Bo and it can be understood as referring only to movable items, whereas regarding cutting stones he may agree with the stricter view. The Levush implies that even the lenient opinion permits only placing already-cut stones into the building, but forbids allowing the non-Jew to cut them even when ownership is publicized. Rama implies that even cutting itself is permitted. Magen Avraham asks that Olat Shabbat contradicts himself, saying that with attached property it is forbidden even when ownership is not publicized. In my humble opinion, one may answer that his statement of prohibition follows the first opinion, while the implication of permission follows the "some say" opinion.
