In a case of loss, etc. Magen Avraham asks that Shabbos wages are not permitted even in a case of loss, as is evident in siman 243 concerning an oven and bathhouse, where the loss may sometimes exceed that of customs revenue. He answers that regarding customs there is a loss on that very day, since each day is assessed separately, unlike a bathhouse where it is merely lost profit. See there for his further answer, copied in Mishnah Berurah. His conclusion implies that Magen Avraham was not certain whether actual Shabbos wages are ever permitted because of loss. Taz in siman 245, seif katan 6, explicitly holds that for customs, which is a major enterprise, and likewise for coinage, Shabbos wages are not prohibited. A person is anxious over his money, and if it is not permitted he may come to a greater prohibition. Tosefes Shabbos likewise holds that a major loss permits Shabbos wages. Biur HaGra implies that Rama's phrase “in a case of loss such as this” also explains why Shabbos wages were not a concern, indicating agreement with Taz and Tosefes Shabbos that Shabbos wages exist here but were permitted because of loss. Magen Avraham compares it to a case where one knows that non-Jews will bring merchandise on Shabbos and he sells it before Shabbos to another non-Jew who will receive it then. Tosefes Shabbos objects that here the Jew cannot transfer ownership because the subject matter does not yet exist. This appears also to have been the Gra's reasoning. Peri Megadim's resolution is somewhat forced. Thus Taz, Tosefes Shabbos, and the Gra follow one approach, and Levush likewise appears to agree. Eliyah Rabbah notes that Responsa Maharit Tzahalon also implies this. One may therefore be lenient, but all agree that no leniency exists for an actual day laborer.
