Under a contracted arrangement. In that case it is permitted as a matter of law. Although there is a distinction between ordinary contracted work, where the Jew does not specifically assign the gentile’s work to Shabbos, as stated at the beginning of siman 252, and collection of customs duties, where he assigns him specifically for Shabbos, it is nevertheless permitted because of financial loss. For this reason it is permitted here even though the work is public. One should not ask why the bathhouse in siman 243 was not likewise permitted because of financial loss. The Sages permitted this only where a major loss is involved. See Taz. A Jew who leased the salt concession may hire gentile workers under a contracted arrangement, stipulating that for producing a given quantity of salt he will pay them a given sum. Maharshal, siman 100. See Yad Aharon, who cites Sefer Nachalas Yehoshua and Sefer Das VaDin, siman 6.

This is forbidden not only where the customs concession belongs to the Jew, but even where he leased it to a gentile for the Shabbos days and has no involvement in it, because people may say that the gentile is acting on his behalf. Taz writes that the prohibition against sitting there applies when the Jew sits in order to observe the gentile’s activity so he can receive an accounting the following day. If he sits there only to prevent the gentile from stealing, it is permitted. This explains the practice of those who lease mills from the ruler and station a Jew there even on Shabbos, provided that he is careful not to discuss the business at all. Yad Aharon disagrees and holds that it is forbidden to sit there in every circumstance; see there. A Jew may not accept food received as customs duty, even if it was not brought from outside the techum and there is no concern of trapping or produce attached to the ground. Magen Avraham; see there.
