It is proper to be stringent. Bach writes that regarding contracted work one may rely on Rabbeinu Tam after the fact, and it is permitted. The same applies where stones were prepared on Shabbos: after the fact, they may be set into the building.
They should not enter it. Even others may never enter, as implied by the Gemara. Although siman 325:14 states that only the Jew for whom the work was done is forbidden, there the non-Jew acted on his own for the Jew's benefit. Here the Jew hired the non-Jew. See the end of siman 664. Nevertheless, here the work is permitted by strict law because the price was fixed and the non-Jew works for himself. Therefore, the Shulchan Aruch writes only that it is proper to be stringent. See what I wrote there.
However, if he stipulated, etc. This requires study. The stipulation appears effective only regarding movable work, not attached work, since Mordechai and Rabbeinu Yerucham discuss movable items, as stated at the end of siman 543. Moreover, when Mar Zutra hired non-Jews, he certainly told them not to work on Shabbos, yet when they did so without his knowledge he still refused to enter. This implies that even such a case is forbidden. It may be answered that those non-Jews acted on their own for Mar Zutra's benefit, whereas here they acted for their own benefit to finish sooner. Thus, whenever they act for the Jew's benefit, even on their own, it is forbidden, as stated in siman 325:14. Where the price was fixed by contract, however, it is permitted after the fact, as explained above.
