It is proper to be stringent, etc. In siman 544 the Tur writes regarding Chol HaMoed that anything forbidden on Shabbos within the techum is forbidden there even beyond the techum, since one can travel there and suspicion remains. He further writes that even if the house was already built unlawfully by a gentile, it is proper to be stringent and not enter it. Beit Yosef explains the reason from the Gemara in the chapter Mi SheHafach: gentile contractors built a mansion for Mar Zutra beyond the techum. Rav Safra and Rav Huna bar Chinana came there and did not enter. Some say Mar Zutra himself also did not enter. The Gemara asks that Shmuel permits gentile contractors beyond the techum, and answers that a distinguished person is different. Another version states that Mar Zutra had assisted them by supplying straw for the work, and this made it forbidden. This certainly concerns Shabbos, since the Gemara challenges from Shmuel’s ruling concerning Shabbos. The two versions yield different results. According to the first, the case involves an entirely permitted arrangement beyond the techum, yet a distinguished person must still refrain. Where there is an element of prohibition, namely within the techum on Shabbos or even beyond the techum on Chol HaMoed, perhaps every person is forbidden, or perhaps only a distinguished person is forbidden and the novelty is that even beyond the techum such a person must refrain. According to the second version, which involves his having assisted with straw, the case is one of actual prohibition, and all people must refrain from entering. The same would then apply where he did not assist with straw but the construction was nevertheless forbidden, namely within the techum on Shabbos or even beyond the techum on Chol HaMoed. Where the work was entirely permitted, such as beyond the techum on Shabbos without his assistance, even a distinguished person may enter. The prohibition for ordinary people mentioned above is not an absolute prohibition like other prohibitions. We find only that those Amoraim refused to enter, indicating a proper stringency rather than a strict prohibition. The Tur rules in accordance with the second version and therefore writes that it is proper to be stringent not to enter a house built unlawfully. This implies that it is not strictly prohibited. He did not repeat this law here because he relied on what he wrote in siman 544 regarding Chol HaMoed, where the Gemara discusses it, and all the more so it applies on Shabbos. Beit Yosef’s explanation there is concise but includes the entire analysis stated here. It is sound, unlike my father-in-law Bach, who writes that Beit Yosef’s learning failed him. His learning is complete and clear. The Tur’s statement in Hilchos Chol HaMoed that even a structure already built unlawfully by a gentile should not be entered does not refer only to a case beyond the techum on Chol HaMoed; it applies equally within the techum on Shabbos. The rulings are therefore consistent. It appears to me, however, that this stringency applies only where the gentile built it under an arrangement forbidden according to all opinions. If it was built under a contracted arrangement, which Rabbeinu Tam permits, then although he himself did not rely on this permission when building his house, we do not find that he prohibited entering a house already built in that manner. Bach likewise concludes that Rabbeinu Tam is worthy of reliance in a pressing circumstance. This appears primary.
