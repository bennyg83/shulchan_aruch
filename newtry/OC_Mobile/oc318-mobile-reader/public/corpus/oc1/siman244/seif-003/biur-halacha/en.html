And the non-Jew did it against his will. See Mishnah Berurah at the end of seif katan 22, where we cited Eliyah Rabbah and explained that the Jew should have stipulated this initially. There is apparent proof from Avodah Zarah 22a. The Gemara implies that only according to Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar can one instruct the non-Jew and expect compliance, whereas according to Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel the non-Jew will not comply. Therefore, it is forbidden initially to rent him a bathhouse, because he certainly will not listen later when told not to heat it on Shabbos. Likewise regarding contracting, the Jew was negligent by failing to stipulate initially, because the non-Jew will not comply afterward. This also appears from Rashi to Moed Katan 12a, who explains that contracted work within the techum is forbidden, meaning that one may not give it to him by contract when he will perform it on Shabbos. We do not rely upon protesting afterward unless a stipulation was made initially. This explains Eliyah Rabbah's reasoning. Nevertheless, the practical law requires study. Rambam, and the Shulchan Aruch that copies him in seif 1, imply that contracted work may be given initially, but when Shabbos arrives one must protest and not allow the work. Rambam also rules that a bathhouse may not be rented, following the Gemara's language. We must therefore distinguish between a bathhouse, which is entirely rented to the non-Jew and concerning which Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel holds that he will not listen to a demand to cease on Shabbos, and ordinary contracted work.
