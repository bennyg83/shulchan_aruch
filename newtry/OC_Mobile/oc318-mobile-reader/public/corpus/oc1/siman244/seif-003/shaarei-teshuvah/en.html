Shevus Yaakov, part 3, writes that if a house is in danger of collapse, one may hire a non-Jew on Friday as a contractor to support the collapsing structure with beams. He should not hire him explicitly, however, but should say, “Whoever repairs it will not lose.” The same may be done with a cracked barrel; see there. Responsa Noda BiYehudah, first edition, siman 12, discusses one who was pressed to construct a roof during Chol HaMoed Sukkos because afterward the workers would be taken to the fortress and he would be unable to obtain laborers. He would then have to remain without shelter from rain during the winter. He writes that there is room to permit contracting even on Shabbos and Yom Tov, because in Prague most Jewish construction is performed by contract, especially roofing. There is therefore no suspicion among residents of the city, only among visitors, and they do not know at all whether the work belongs to a Jew. Nevertheless, he permitted it only on Chol HaMoed, together with the fact that it was a pressing circumstance. He also required that no Jewish supervisor stand over the workers. If this was impossible, they should employ a poor Jew who lacked food for the festival. See there.
