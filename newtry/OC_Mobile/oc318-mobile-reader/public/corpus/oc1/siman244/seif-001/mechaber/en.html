Which forms of melacha a non-Jew may perform for a Jew. It contains 6 seifim: A person may contract with a non-Jew for a melacha and fix a price, and the non-Jew performs it for his own benefit. Even though he performs it on Shabbos, it is permitted. When does this apply? When it is done privately, so that it is not generally recognized that the melacha being performed on Shabbos belongs to a Jew. But if it is known and public, it is forbidden, because one who sees the non-Jew engaged in it does not know that a fixed price was agreed upon, and will say that so-and-so hired the non-Jew to perform melacha for him on Shabbos. Therefore, one who contracts with a non-Jew to build his courtyard or wall, or to harvest his field, if the melacha is within the city or within the techum, he may not allow the non-Jew to perform the melacha for him on Shabbos because of observers, who do not know that a fixed price was agreed upon. {Rama: Even if he lives among non-Jews, there is concern for guests who come there, or for members of his household, who may suspect him. [Beit Yosef in the name of the responsa of the Ashkenazim]} If the melacha is outside the techum, and there is also no other city within the techum of the place where the melacha is being performed, it is permitted. Regarding a non-Jew who brought a Jew's sheep into a pen in his field, see above, siman 537, seif 14.
