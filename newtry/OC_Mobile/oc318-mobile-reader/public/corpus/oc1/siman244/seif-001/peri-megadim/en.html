To build. His courtyard for him. See Taz and Avodah Zarah 21b, Tosafos. It requires examination whether the prohibition because of onlookers applies specifically to labor prohibited by the Torah. Although instructing a non-Jew is itself only Rabbinic, the prohibition was supported by the verse, “It shall not be done among them,” with a tzeirei, and some hold that an asmachta has the force of Torah law, as I wrote in the general introduction. By contrast, regarding a Rabbinic shvus, although instructing a non-Jew is prohibited even for a shvus when no mitzvah is involved, it is not as severe, and perhaps the Sages did not prohibit contracted work because of onlookers. This may explain what Rabbi Yitzchak bar Yosef wrote in the name of Rav Natronai, that one may give a watermill to a non-Jew under a contract. This follows Magen Avraham, siman 252, seif katan 20, that operating a watermill involves no Torah prohibition, only a shvus; in such a case perhaps they did not decree because of onlookers. Beit Yosef, however, does not agree, for he writes that this is only according to those who permit it. See Taz, seif katan 6, where, with Hashem’s help, this will be explained further. Regarding the reason, etc., Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 11, challenges what Taz wrote in seif katan 3. See siman 247, seif katan 2, where it is explained that it is prohibited.
