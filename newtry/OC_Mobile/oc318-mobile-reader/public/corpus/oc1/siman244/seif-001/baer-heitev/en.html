For himself. Meaning, the Jew has no objection in this matter, since if the gentile does not perform it today, he can perform it tomorrow.
The gentile. Meaning, he hired him by the day, and this is prohibited as a matter of law.
His courtyard. Tosafos at the end of the first chapter of Avodah Zarah writes in the name of Rabbeinu Tam that Rabbeinu Tam permitted constructing a house through a gentile contractor, since the Jew gains no advantage from its being built on Shabbos. Just as regarding a field we say that the sharecropper is performing his own sharecropping arrangement, so too here the gentile is performing his own contracted work. Rabbeinu Yitzchak, however, forbids it for the reason stated in this seif, and Rabbeinu Tam himself did not rely on his own ruling when he built his house. Hagahos (Maimoniyos) [Mordechai] writes that maidservants may not perform work in a Jew’s house on Shabbos, because onlookers may say that she is performing the Jew’s work.
To harvest. Although fields are ordinarily given under sharecropping arrangements, and onlookers might therefore assume that he received it as a sharecropper, as stated in siman 243, this case is different. If people investigate there, they will discover the truth, namely that he received it as a sharecropper. Here, however, when they see at harvest time that the gentile receives no share of the produce, they will know that he is not a sharecropper and will suspect that he is a day laborer.
To a resident of his household. See siman 243, seif katan 3, which appears to contradict this. Taz rules that one should be stringent in accordance with the ruling here. Magen Avraham reconciles the sources; see there. In our city the custom is to permit hiring a gentile contractor to remove refuse from the street even though the gentile performs the work on Shabbos. It appears to me that regarding communal property there is no concern for suspicion. A day laborer, however, is certainly forbidden. Accordingly, it would appear permissible to build a synagogue on Shabbos through a contractor. Nevertheless, I have seen that the great authorities were unwilling to permit this, because nowadays the gentiles do not allow anyone to perform public work on their festival days. If we allow it, there is a desecration of Hashem’s name. Street cleaning, however, is not so closely associated with the Jewish community. In a place where the street-cleaning leniency is not customary, one should not be lenient. Magen Avraham. It appears to me that if there is concern that, Heaven forbid, the synagogue’s construction will otherwise be entirely abandoned, it is permitted through a contractor. See siman 544, seif 1, and examine carefully.
