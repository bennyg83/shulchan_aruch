And it was sealed on Shabbat — but if it was sealed while still day, even if it reopened on Shabbat they are forbidden, for they never acquired an eruv at all — acquisition of eruv is at bein hashemashot, and here there was no eruv at bein hashemashot since the opening was sealed; and even if they already joined for the Shabbat that passed for one Shabbat and that eruv still remains, they cannot rely on it this Shabbat after the opening opened, since it was already nullified while still day when it was sealed and does not revive at nightfall; and one who joins for a year — I join from the start for all Shabbatot and acquire the eruv on the first Shabbat when the opening was open — therefore even if afterward the opening was sealed and opened on another Shabbat, even at year's end the eruv returns and revives.

And even if he joined for a year, etc. — see Magen Avraham siman 383 where this law remains in doubt; however many later authorities resolve his difficulties there; nevertheless this law is not clear — behold the source of this law flows from Rosh beginning of Eruvin who brought proof from the law of ships that were severed and reconnected [Shabbat 101] that they return to their original permission, and Rosh proved it deals when they were severed on a weekday and nevertheless it helps when they reconnected afterward on Shabbat — for if severed on Shabbat, even if not reconnected they would be permitted because once permitted, permitted — and likewise in our matter; however per Tosafos [Shabbat 101] and likewise per many rishonim [Rambam see there, and in Rambam chapter 3, and Rashba and Ritva] who explained that baraita — even if severed on Shabbat it is forbidden because without the connection between them they drift from their place and it is like karmelis interrupting between them, and once permitted, permitted does not apply — thus there is no source for this law, for there it says they return to original permission because they were tied at the start of Shabbat and the eruv took effect — unlike our case where it was sealed at bein hashemashot and opened on Shabbat, and already Avkat Rochel raised this and likewise Magen Avraham in siman 1; however Tosafos' own view is not clear here, for in Eruvin 17 they wrote this law in the name of R' Yehuda and also proof from the ship — implying that for Tosafos' authors there were differing views, and Rosh ruled like Tosafos beginning of Eruvin [and what Magen Avraham wanted to reconcile Tosafos' views is unclear, for Rosh always follows Tosafos — and in Rosh it is explicit that his main proof is: if the baraita of ships deals with severance on Shabbat it would be permitted even without reconnecting, which is not Tosafos on that Shabbat — already raised in Nehar Shalom] — in any event this law is not clear per the rishonim above, and reasonably we should not say that what took effect on one Shabbat helps another Shabbat since at bein hashemashot of that Shabbat it was void; see siman 383 Beit Meir who also agrees with Magen Avraham that this law should be settled. Nevertheless after Rosh, Tur, Mechaber, and later authorities after Shulchan Aruch ruled this law, certainly we cannot reject it from practice; however regarding sealing intentionally that Magen Avraham brought from Maharil who permitted only when it opened before Shabbat, and likewise Avkat Rochel and Tosafos Yeshanim agreed — certainly we cannot be lenient here since from the root of the law even opening on Shabbat unintentionally is not clear to be lenient as above; and though some later authorities strain to reconcile Rama's view permitting in all cases, for practice it appears we should not be lenient as above; and in Netiv Chayim by Knesset Yisrael his view is that even Rama himself did not be lenient except when it opened afterward on a weekday.

And the opening was sealed on a weekday, etc. — and it appears the same when eruv chatzerot was nullified by another cause, e.g. the post in the alley was ruined and thereby the eruv of that alley was nullified — as explained end of siman 365: if the post was repaired on Shabbat by a gentile, etc., even though at bein hashemashot it was breached, nevertheless when repaired on Shabbat the eruv returns — since the eruv was made initially for the whole year; though one can distinguish somewhat that with breached partitions it is worse and joining for the year may not help — nevertheless in pressing circumstances one may be lenient here too, and I saw in one authority general 72 lenient here; however what he argues to be lenient even when joining for one Shabbat and it became known that on a weekday the post was ruined and repaired on Shabbat by a gentile — that courtyard eruv never took effect at all at bein hashemashot — because in that place they regularly make a post always, therefore it stands to be repaired, and when repaired later on Shabbat the courtyard eruv takes effect retroactively — his words are not clear at all; proof: for eruv via opening sealed on Shabbat Rashi explained e.g. a collapse fell against it — that certainly stands to be cleared, yet it is permitted only when sealed on Shabbat itself, because at bein hashemashot the eruv took effect and once permitted, permitted — not sealed while still day; therefore Shulchan Aruch was not lenient even when it stands to open except when joining for a year because the eruv already took effect on the first Shabbat, therefore it helps even if sealed afterward — not when joining for one Shabbat where the eruv never took effect at all at bein hashemashot; especially since the root law is not clear as above — therefore do not add to it; and what one authority wrote that some disagree in Tosafos reading "for both" — Or Zarua also wrote "for a year," and so in Rosh.
