(א) And even if he sealed intentionally. For when it opens before Shabbat they are like partners in the bread, and at bein hashemashot which acquires eruv there is an opening between them fit for joining (responsum Maharil siman 192); if so when it opens on Shabbat it is forbidden, for since he sealed intentionally he revealed he does not want an eruv, and thus bein hashemashot did not acquire eruv; therefore Agur cited Maharil; and see what I wrote siman 355 seif 1 and siman 383 — Rosh's proof from ships sealed on a weekday is not proof; see there; nevertheless Tosafos 17 also wrote permitted even if opened on Shabbat and brought proof from the ship; and on 101 on Shabbat they wrote even if opened on Shabbat forbidden; therefore we must say their proof is as there automatically returns to original permission — so here; however regarding intentional sealing in the default case opening on Shabbat does not help — doubt; Bach wrote if he broke its pegs the eruv was nullified — not required; see there:
