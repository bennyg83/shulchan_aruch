(א) And it was sealed on Shabbat — e.g. a collapse fell against it [Rashi]:
(ב) They are permitted to use — from one to the other on this Shabbat; and all the more so each for itself is permitted, even the one where the eruv is not placed:
(ג) Via the height of the wall and its holes — meaning: even though now that it is sealed they are not fit to join since the holes lack the measure of a window, nevertheless the eruv was not nullified for this Shabbat, for since it was permitted at the start of Shabbat it remains permitted for the whole Shabbat:
(ד) And it opened on Shabbat — meaning: not only if it opened before Shabbat is it certainly an eruv, for at entry of Shabbat they were already open to one another; rather even if it opened on Shabbat itself, since at the time of placing the eruv that he placed for many Shabbatot or a year there was an opening from one to the other and the eruv took effect — therefore even on another Shabbat when from the start of Shabbat it was sealed and then the eruv was not effective for all, nevertheless it was not nullified and as soon as it opens the eruv returns to its original permission:
(ה) And even if he sealed it intentionally — meaning intentionally. And know that Magen Avraham and many later authorities sided to forbid here when it reopened on Shabbat, for since he sealed the opening between the two courtyards intentionally it is like nullifying the eruv by hand — he revealed he does not want an eruv; if so, at bein hashemashot when it was still sealed it does not help when it opens later on Shabbat itself; we are not lenient when sealed intentionally except when it opened before Shabbat — then we say even if nullified initially, when it reopened they are still partners in the eruv bread and at bein hashemashot there is an opening fit for joining; see Biur Halacha where we explained practice should follow this. However if when sealing he intended only temporarily and afterward to reopen, it is reasonable to permit even when it reopened on Shabbat:
(ו) And for Rambam, etc. — refers to the beginning of the seif where the opening was sealed on Shabbat: per the first view permitted to use from one to the other via holes; per him not permitted except each courtyard for itself; and the stringency is that even the courtyard where the eruv is not placed is permitted; nevertheless if it reopened, all agree permitted to carry from one to the other as initially.
