(א) And even — in siman 372 Bach implies he holds: even though sealed on a weekday intentionally and opened on Shabbat it is permitted; and on this he wrote: if he broke its pegs on a weekday and opened on Shabbat — forbidden; and so Rabbenu Yerucham in gloss — even if sealed intentionally by a gentile and opened on Shabbat by gentile or Jew the eruv returns to its place; and Magen Avraham letter 1; and Taz holds: even if he broke pegs, whenever it opens on Shabbat the eruv returns to original permission; proof: Rosh and Tosafos Eruvin 17 s.v. "eruv" wrote: sealed on a weekday and opened on Shabbat returns to permission; or it fell on its own and was sealed — nevertheless he diverted attention not to open on Shabbat, yet permitted because it opened on Shabbat; and proof from ships Tosafos and Rosh brought — tied and permitted and reconnected return to permission — meaning permitted at twilight; for if on Shabbat once permitted, permitted; if so certainly he diverted attention and it is permitted — likewise intentional and breaking pegs, whenever it opens on Shabbat returns to first permission; Bach could say: with direct action it does not:

And know what is written "joined for a year" — it is an inference; or they joined at twilight and it was fit that the eruv acquire — there was an opening and it was sealed afterward at twilight and opened on Shabbat; even though at Shabbat start in eruv matters it was not permitted, the eruv took effect when it opened; strained to say specifically for a year since perhaps he minimized the eruv — doubt; and what Taz wrote "and they were permitted on Shabbat" — perhaps they were permitted — severed at twilight; and the word "on Shabbat" refers afterward: he diverted attention not to reconnect on Shabbat. The rule here: when Shabbat enters it was permitted even if sealed afterward it was permitted; conversely also forbidden at Shabbat start and repaired on Shabbat — since at making the eruv it was fit and sealed and opened afterward on Shabbat — permitted.
