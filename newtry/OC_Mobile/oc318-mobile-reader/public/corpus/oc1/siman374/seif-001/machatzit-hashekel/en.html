(Siman 1) And even, etc. — likewise as partners in the [eruv] bread. Meaning: even though through sealing the eruv was nullified, when it opens — even if they did not join anew — they remain partners in that bread placed initially for eruv and are partners in that bread, and need not join as above siman 366 seif 1 and below siman 386 seif 3:

(Siman 1) If so when it opens on Shabbat, etc. — bein hashemashot did not acquire eruv, for at bein hashemashot it was still not open and the original eruv was already nullified by sealing; therefore Agur cited in Beit Yosef wrote: Maharil was asked if he sealed the opening (intentionally) and later reconsidered to open — does the eruv return to original permission for the coming Shabbat? Maharil answered: I cannot distinguish unintentional from intentional — meaning even intentional returns to original permission; and from the questioner asking if it returns for the coming Shabbat it implies that for that Shabbat — if it opened on its own on Shabbat — it is obvious to him it does not return to original permission, as Magen Avraham — since he sealed intentionally the eruv was nullified and at bein hashemashot it was still sealed, bein hashemashot did not acquire eruv; therefore Rama is difficult who permits even sealed intentionally and opened on Shabbat; certainly if sealed unintentionally it is fit to permit even if opened on Shabbat, for the eruv was never nullified; and likewise if sealed intentionally and nullified, if it opened before Shabbat bein hashemashot again acquires eruv as above — but sealed intentionally and opened this Shabbat we have not heard:

(Siman 1) From ships is not proof, etc. — Rosh beginning of Eruvin siman 23: it appears that if one joined for a year from courtyard to courtyard, etc., even if sealed mid-week and opened on Shabbat the eruv returns to its place; proof from Shabbat 101 chapter HaZorek: tied ships may join and carry from one to the other; severed — forbidden; reconnected — return to original permission — for all partitions made on Shabbat, etc.; implies severed even on a weekday; for if on Shabbat, even without reconnecting we say Shabbat: once permitted, permitted — end of his words; Magen Avraham siman 355: per Tosafos there on Shabbat it is not proof — one could say ships deal with severance on Shabbat and nevertheless if not reconnected they are forbidden, and we need not say once permitted, permitted since the sea interrupts as karmelis; and such we do not say once permitted, permitted as here seif 2; and even though ships are not yet separated, since they stand to be separated they are considered separated — so Maggid Mishneh in Raavad there; see what Magen Avraham wrote to explain Rosh that he holds like Rambam:

(Siman 1) Nevertheless Tosafos, etc. — meaning Rosh must disagree with Tosafos; for Tosafos there is no proof for Rosh's law; nevertheless Tosafos' own words are difficult:

(Siman 1) Tosafos Eruvin 17:

(Siman 1) And they brought proof from the ship — however Tosafos did not conclude as Rosh — implying severed on a weekday; for if on Shabbat we should say once permitted, permitted; rather they left their proof open: "even if sealed mid-week and afterward opened on Shabbat it is permitted, etc.; proof from chapter HaZorek: tied ships," etc., "severed — forbidden; reconnected — return to original permission" — end. And as plainly understood, their intent is as Rosh — severed on a weekday; otherwise we should say even without reconnecting, say once permitted, etc.; but then their words in Eruvin and Shabbat contradict each other:

(Siman 1) Therefore we must say their proof, etc. — meaning Tosafos do not hold as Rosh; rather Tosafos hold the ship case can be explained as severed on Shabbat as in Shabbat — in any event we learn that even though forbidden, for some reason if reconnected — even if reconnected on its own without human intent — they return to original permission; likewise if severed on a weekday, if reconnected on its own on Shabbat they return to original permission; likewise opening sealed and opened even if opened on its own returns to permission:
