And they open to the public domain, etc. The same applies when they open to a mavoi, as stated by the Tur.
The Levush writes: And they have no right of passage, etc. For if they were arranged one inside the other, the inner courtyard would prohibit the outer one, because even by itself each would be prohibited from use, as Rashi explains on 45. This is difficult, because the case concerns where they all made an eruv, so it is a foot permitted in its own place. Furthermore, why should it be prohibited by itself if they made an eruv? One must say that on 75 there is a dispute in the Mishnah, and Rabbi Akiva holds that even a foot permitted in its own place prohibits. Rashi holds that the statement here does not enter into that dispute. This requires study.
In one house. For example, where one vessel was full and the remainder was placed in another, as stated by the Beit Yosef citing the Maggid Mishneh, chapter 7. This is astonishing. I examined the source and found that an error fell into the Beit Yosef. The Maggid Mishneh actually writes: In one house and one vessel, or in two vessels in one house, such as where one vessel was full and there was a remainder, for this is not called dividing the eruv. End quote. Avodat HaKodesh, p. 22, likewise writes this.
