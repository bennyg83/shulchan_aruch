Seif 2 in Shulchan Aruch: both are forbidden because forbidden foot in its place forbids outside its place. See responsa Chinuch Beit Yehudah siman 10: in walled towns or by congregation permit they may carry; outside live some Jews without eruv who must come on Shabbat to the permitted area — forbidden foot does not apply, for specifically an inner courtyard with no exit except through the outer is unlike here where they can turn anywhere; even a synagogue share does not matter; outsiders in the city may carry as guests (end of his words: one who wishes to carry should take part in the city eruv as withdrawing from outsiders — see siman 386; per guest reasoning perhaps no eruv needed and eruv may detract). Netiv Beit Yehudah 40: this permit is widespread for Jews outside the wall and villages in techum coming to synagogue, some with acquired seats — no objection since they have other routes; even if they come Shabbat foot-traffic does not negate as Magen Avraham on alley — congregation helps withdrawal; even forbidden foot may be lenient per Rama siman 392 hagahah seif 6 though not clear; outsiders cannot carry street to street or leave tallit/siddur in a house to carry to synagogue — therefore another permit: only if they could join and did not they forbid; if they could not join they do not forbid — Ritva Eruvin 59. Not only unwalled cities with lechi and tzurat ha-petach — even walled with non-Jews between who forbid; streets without non-Jews made tzurat ha-petach; outsiders who could not join do not forbid. See Magen Avraham 363:27, Yad Efraim, Even HaEzer, Bach responsa 2, Gevurat Anashim. Study.
