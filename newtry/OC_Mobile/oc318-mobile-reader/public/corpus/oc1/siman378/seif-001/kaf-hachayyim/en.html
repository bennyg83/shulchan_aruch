1) [Se'if 1] Three courtyards open into one another. Otherwise, they could not establish an eruv together, as stated above in siman 372, se'if 4. See there.
2) There. And open to a public domain. The same applies if they open to an alleyway. Tur; Olas Shabbos, subsection 1; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1; Mishbetzos Zahav, subsection 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1.
3) There. And open to a public domain. Each courtyard is a domain unto itself, and the residents of none have a right of passage through another. If they were arranged one inside another, the inner courtyard would prohibit carrying for the outer courtyard, since even independently each would be forbidden to carry. Rashi. This means the outer and middle courtyards, as Rabbeinu Yehonasan on the Rif likewise writes. The later authorities, however, question this. The case concerns courtyards whose residents each established their own eruv and then established an eruv with the neighboring courtyard. Why, then, would the inner courtyard prohibit carrying for the outer and middle courtyards? It is a permitted passageway in its own place, which does not prohibit carrying elsewhere, as stated in se'if 2. They therefore permit carrying in this case even if the courtyards are arranged one inside another and only the outer courtyard opens to a public domain or alleyway. See Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 2, and Ma'amar Mordechai, and examine the matter carefully. Mishbetzos Zahav, subsection 1, rules likewise.
4) There. The outer courtyards are forbidden to one another. It is forbidden to take utensils that began Shabbos in the houses from one outer courtyard to the other through the middle courtyard. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, there. Utensils that began Shabbos in a courtyard, however, are permitted, as stated at the beginning of siman 372.
5) There. When does this apply? When the middle courtyard placed its eruv in this one, etc. The middle courtyard then resembles one whose residents live in both outer courtyards, while the residents of the outer courtyards do not live in the middle courtyard, so they cannot be considered equal. Rashi; Beis Yosef. If the outer courtyards placed their eruvei chatzeiros in the middle courtyard, however, it is as though all of them live in that one house, and they all become the courtyard of that house. Taz, subsection 1.
