(א) He nullifies — now even if chametz remains in his house that he did not find he does not transgress (Tur), for if he did not nullify he transgresses even though he did not know; likewise siman 446: as if one ate milk b'shogeg — Rambam chapter 3: if he found chametz he had in mind and in his heart and forgot at the time of removal and did not remove it, he transgresses lo yera'eh, for he neither removed nor nullified, etc. — see there; it implies if he did not know from the outset he does not transgress at all; likewise from the Rosh's words regarding a fine roll — see Tosafot beginning of Kol Sha'ah. Know that crumbs are automatically nullified; nullification is lest he find a fine roll — see Tosafot siman 433.

(ב) If they said — in some versions it is written: if they said in Hebrew he fulfilled; it appears specifically when he knows at least the matter of nullification — that he knows he declares his chametz hefker; but those who do not understand at all and think they say some prayer — clearly they did not fulfill, for nullification is from the law of hefker and requires mouth and heart aligned; likewise Maharil and Mahariv that the checker for a woman should nullify in a language she understands. In Siddur He'arot Yosef he wrote the reason we practice saying it in the language of Targum.

(ג) It is good to nullify again — because he mentions bread he ate and perhaps something remains (Tur); but one should not rely on nullification by day and not nullify at night, for since it is not time of prohibition nor time of removal we are concerned he will forget to nullify; even for us who burn before the sixth hour and nullify at that time, we are concerned he will forget to burn until after the sixth hour when nullification does not help.

(ד) Except after he burned — therefore he must burn before the sixth hour.
