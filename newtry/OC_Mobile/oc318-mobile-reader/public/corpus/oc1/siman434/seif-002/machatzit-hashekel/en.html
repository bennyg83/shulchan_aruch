(א) (s.k. 2) If a mouse takes it, etc. — the Ran wrote he must nullify once; meaning if he checked again and did not find what the mouse dragged.

(ב) End of Rabbeinu Yosef; it should read end of Darchei Moshe. In Chok Yaakov he wrote he did not find this in Rabbeinu Yosef, and in truth it is so — it is not found in Beit Yosef but in Darchei Moshe. It appears to me the Ran did not intend this; Darchei Moshe understood the Ran's intent when he wrote that this chametz the mouse dragged from the house was not yet nullified because the Ran's formula in nullification was to say all chamira that is in this house (and in truth according to what I saw in the Ran it seems his formula was also like the Tur's formula: on what the Rif wrote, R' Yehuda said the checker must nullify — how does he nullify? He said thus: all chamira in my possession that I did not see, etc.; on this the Ran wrote this language is not in the Gemara but thus is accepted from the Geonim, z"l — see Beit Yosef). If so, since the weasel dragged it outside the house it was not in the scope of nullification. Therefore according to what the Tur wrote not to say all chamira in this house but in my possession, to include chametz he has in other places — if so, also chametz the weasel dragged outside the house is in his nullification. On this Magen Avraham wrote this is not the Ran's intent; rather even the Tur's formula that says in my possession — nevertheless chametz the weasel dragged is not in the scope of nullification.

(ג) For he left it, etc. — and therefore they say at night "that I did not see," for chametz he sees and leaves for tomorrow he does not nullify.

(ד) However we practice in any case to nullify, etc. — although as Magen Avraham wrote in s.k. 7 that even for us who burn before six we do not rely on nullification by day — that is fixed practice and to change the custom of the Talmud who nullified at night; but in this case where he already nullified at night per enactment of the Talmud, only because of an incident he must nullify again — one may rely on nullification by day, and we are not concerned he will forget to burn until after six, for that is a distant concern.
