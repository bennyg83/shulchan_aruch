(א) Immediately at night he nullifies — the reason is that although d'oraisa bedikat chametz alone suffices, for from the Torah one of them suffices — either removal or nullification — for even if chametz remains in the house he does not transgress as long as it is unknown to him, since he checked well and did all that is incumbent upon him; nevertheless the Sages were concerned lest he find a fine roll that does not nullify itself like crumbs, and he delay a bit before burning it and transgress bal yera'eh. But if he nullifies again he does not transgress bal yera'eh in any case, for it is hefker and not his.

(ב) That I have not seen and have not removed — but what he saw and removed he does not nullify now, since he wishes to eat more of what he left and also to fulfill the mitzva of burning his chametz tomorrow; therefore he does not nullify everything until tomorrow after eating and after burning the chametz.

(ג) And it shall be like the dust of the earth — it is proper that he say "and it shall be hefker like the dust of the earth." According to all opinions this language of hefker helps even if he said this language between himself and himself and not before people [Acharonim].

(4) In the language that one understands - after all, the void is from the doctrine of the poor, and if he does not know what the void is. And so it is good to learn for the rabbis who do not understand the blaq or a woman who cancels to say in a language that one understands, that is (Allen hamtz or eksir teig waas am ez in mein izwat zaal zein hafakar und zaal nit zain girekhint nar soi vea arad in gas)

(ה) In the holy tongue — not specifically; rather it means in the language of the Targum as we say.

(ו) It is good to nullify again — because people are accustomed to buy bread on the 14th for food, and also with bread he left at night for his food he did not intend to nullify it [and even if he intended to nullify it, behold he reacquires it], and there is concern lest a k'zayit remain from it and he transgress; therefore he nullifies again. Nevertheless one should not rely on nullification by day alone and not nullify at night, for we are concerned he may forget to nullify until the sixth hour when nullification no longer helps.

(ז) Except after burning the chametz — therefore he must burn before the sixth hour, and afterward nullify again immediately before the sixth hour begins.
