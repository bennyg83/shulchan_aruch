(א) At night he nullifies — Tur wrote that now even if chametz remains in the house that he did not find he does not transgress, for it is hefker and not his. Do not explain he transgresses on what he did not know if he does not nullify but finds it afterward — who can understand mistakes? Likewise from the Rosh's words on crumbs: if there were no permission in them because they are not important and nullified I would transgress on them, for since it is established to us they remained from bedika it is as known; if so, on a fine roll the prohibition applies specifically when he finds them — on this the Gemara asks: do you find them to nullify? Rather explain chametz remained that he did not find in his bedika and he finds it during Pesach — thus in the Gemara daf 6, R' Chiya said the checker must nullify. Why? If you say because of crumbs they are not important — Rava said: lest he find a fine roll and his mind is on it. Rashi: it is important in his eyes and he spares it from burning and delays even a moment and transgresses bal yera'eh. Then he asks: do you find it to nullify? and answers: perhaps you find it after prohibition. Tosafot asked: according to Rashi why does the questioner ask — he already said he spares it from nullifying? Rather the explanation of "and his mind is on it" is he did not nullify it by itself. It appears per the Rambam he first said if he does not nullify but relies that he will take it out to burn if he finds it, perhaps he will spare burning a bit and transgress; on this he asks: if you find it, nullify! In nullification we are not concerned he will spare nullifying since he does not remove it from the world.

(ב) That is in my possession — Tur wrote he should not specify saying all chamira in this house, to include chametz he has in other places — end; requires study: why should he say so, a superfluous matter? It appears he should say so to include chametz his wife and sons have in their portions — he informs us it is better to say in my possession, for in truth all is included; requires study: he should have enacted only all chametz I have and no more. It appears if so I would explain "for my need" — he nullifies only what he needs and not the permitted; and if he did not say whether I saw or did not see, I would say what he said "I have" means known to him but not what is doubtful; therefore they enacted thus.

(ג) It is good to nullify again, etc. — see Tur who wrote the reason: because with bread he left for his food he did not intend to nullify, and there is concern he may leave some in the house and transgress. One may emend: because the Rosh gave another reason — bread he nullifies at night he reacquires; therefore here too emend this reason. You may ask: how did the Rosh write in the first reason "because he reacquires" — it implies at the time of nullification what he left was also nullified but he reacquires; yet he said at night "that I did not see" — implying specifically chametz unknown to him, and we do not say that in truth if he does not nullify by day he also says at night whether I saw and did not see — this is not so, for Tur wrote as it should be whether I saw and did not see per Rabbeinu Peretz; but to the Rosh it seems he does not say that even by day. One may say: for the first reason he explains in what he says "that I did not see" includes also chametz he will leave for eating — meaning he did not see it to burn; and his intent was also for that known chametz that what remains he does not intend to burn; if so one may say also chametz he leaves is in the scope of nullification; therefore he needs the reason "because he reacquires," unlike the second reason he holds he did not intend at all to nullify that chametz — so the Rosh's explanation. Do not say for Rama who wrote in the gloss s.k. 7 it is good to burn before nullifying to fulfill burning his chametz — if so he burns before prohibition and nullifies by day near it, as we practice; if so, why for us nullification at night — nullify at burning and remember through burning? For what the questioner asked, they nullify at six, etc., and answers at that time it is not in his possession — see Gemara; but according to our custom we need not all this, since burning is before prohibition. One may say in the Gemara he could ask properly, since this is the set time for all, namely beginning of the sixth hour; but for our custom where each burns when he wishes, it is not a set time — as above.
