(14) His agent. This means that he instructed the agent to nullify his chametz. If, however, he instructed him only to search, all agree that the agent cannot nullify it. Magen Avraham and other later authorities.
(15) May nullify. Although nullification operates through hefker, and one who tells another, “Go and declare my property ownerless,” accomplishes nothing until he himself declares it ownerless, one may be lenient regarding chametz. When a person would transgress bal yera’eh and bal yimatzei, the chametz is not truly his, since benefit from it is forbidden; the Torah nevertheless placed it in his domain so that he would transgress regarding it. Therefore, a mere disclosure that he does not want any right in it is sufficient. Some are stringent, but in pressing circumstances one may be lenient, since most poskim agree with the Mechaber. This applies only when he appointed the other person as his agent for this purpose. Without agency, one may not nullify another person’s chametz even where doing so benefits him, such as when the owner is traveling and forgot to nullify it. Peri Megadim, introduction.
(16) The chametz of so and so. He should not say only, “that is in this house,” for the owner may have chametz elsewhere. The later authorities write that during the day he should say, “Any chametz in the possession of so and so, whether he knows of it or does not know of it,” etc. At night he says only regarding what the owner does not know of. A guardian of orphans must search, destroy, and nullify their chametz. Where payment is customary for the search, he pays it. If he failed to destroy or nullify it and Pesach passed, he is not liable unless he acted negligently. Regarding a minor orphan who has no guardian and whose chametz remained through Pesach, it is possible that the chametz is not prohibited, for whom would the Sages penalize? Peri Megadim; see there further.
(17) If the man is not at home. This applies when he did not appoint an agent to nullify. In view of those who hold that agency does not help for nullification, it is proper that he also nullify the chametz himself wherever he is, even if he appointed an agent to search and nullify.
(18) If he does not do so. At first glance, how would the wife know that her husband is not nullifying the chametz where he is? Rather, the meaning is that he is not accustomed to do so, and we are therefore concerned that he may forget. It is consequently proper for his wife to nullify the chametz even if he did not instruct her, since it is presumed that he grants her permission. She should be warned about this. Nevertheless, ideally one should not rely completely on his wife’s nullification, since the chametz is not hers; he should nullify it where he is.
(19) His wife should nullify. She should say, “Any chametz in my husband’s possession, whether he knows of it,” etc. If she does not understand the Holy Tongue, she should say it in a language she understands. Even if she cannot search herself and appointed an agent to search her rooms, it is preferable that she personally perform the nullification in a language she understands. Some authorities question even a husband’s appointment of an agent for nullification, and all the more so a wife’s appointment, since the chametz is not inherently hers. A widow, whose chametz belongs to her, may nullify it herself or appoint an agent, as stated above regarding a man.
