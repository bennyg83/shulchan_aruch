(א) His agent — that is, when he commanded the agent to nullify his chametz; but if he commanded him only to check, according to all opinions he cannot nullify [Magen Avraham and Sha'arei Teshuvah].

(ב) May nullify — for although nullification is from the law of hefker, and one who says to his fellow "go and declare my property hefker" — it is nothing until he himself declares it hefker; here regarding chametz one may be lenient, for chametz at the time he transgresses bal yera'eh and bal yimatzei is not his, since it is forbidden in benefit, but the Torah established it in his possession so he transgress on it; therefore with mere revelation of intent in his heart that he reveals he does not wish to have any rights in it at all suffices. Some are stringent in this; in a pressing situation one may be lenient, for most poskim agreed with the Mechaber. Nevertheless this is specifically when he made an agent for this; without agency he cannot nullify his fellow's chametz even where it is to his benefit, such as if he is on the road and forgot to nullify [Peri Megadim in the introduction].

(ג) So-and-so's chametz — that is, he should not say "there is in this house," lest he have chametz elsewhere. Acharonim wrote that by day he should say: all chamira that is in so-and-so's possession that he knows and does not know, etc.; at night he says that he does not know. A guardian of orphans must check, burn, and nullify; where they give wages for bedika he gives wages. If he was negligent and did not burn or nullify and Passover passed, one does not obligate him, since he is liable only for negligence. A minor orphan without a guardian who passed Passover — perhaps his chametz is not forbidden, for whom would we penalize [Peri Megadim; see there what he wrote on this].

(ד) If the man is not at home — and he did not appoint an agent to nullify. According to what we wrote earlier that some are stringent that an agent does not help for nullification, it is proper that he nullify there where he is himself, even if he made an agent for bedika and nullification.

(ה) If he does not do so — it seems: whence does the woman know the husband does not nullify in his place? Rather the intent is the husband is not accustomed to do so; if so we are concerned he may forget; therefore it is good that his wife nullify even though he did not command her, for presumably just as he gave her permission for damim so too it is fit to warn her on this. Nevertheless it is not worthwhile l'chatchila to rely entirely on his wife's nullification, since in any case it is not hers, and he should nullify where he is.

(ו) That his wife nullify — and she says: all chamira that is in my husband's possession that he knows, etc.; if she does not understand Hebrew she says in a language she understands, as above. Even if she does not know how to check herself and appointed an agent to check her rooms from chametz, nevertheless nullification is better that she do herself in a language she understands, for even for the husband himself some doubt the appointment of agency for nullification, all the more so for his wife since the chametz is essentially not hers. A widow, since the chametz is hers, may nullify herself or make an agent, as above regarding a man.
