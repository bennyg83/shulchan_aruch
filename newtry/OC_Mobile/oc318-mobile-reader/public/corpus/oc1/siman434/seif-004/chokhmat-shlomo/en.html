There, seif 4. "His agent can nullify it, etc." NB: See Taz s.k. 6, who wrote that presumably he gives it to him as a gift. The Mekor Chayim wondered by what act he acquired it, and I wrote in my novellae that this is like makirei kehunah. Now I see a proof to this from the Mishnah in Parah 7:1: if one said to another, "sanctify these waters for yourself," only the first is valid; see there. This is difficult: by what did the second acquire it? True, the Rav of Bartenura there wrote, "since he said, sanctify for yourself, and gave it to him, they became his," implying that handing it from hand to hand is required and not mere speech. But if so, why does the Mishnah distinguish between saying "sanctify for yourself" and saying "sanctify for him"? It should have distinguished within "sanctify for yourself" itself, that if he did not hand it to him but merely said "sanctify for yourself" and the other did not lift it, all are valid. Perforce it is proven that even by speech alone without an act it becomes his. By what did he acquire it? Perforce, since it is a mitzvah and a person is pleased to fulfill a mitzvah with his property, we are witnesses that he transferred it to him so that he acquire it by speech alone. If so, the same applies in this law of the Taz. This is a clear proof in my humble opinion. Examine carefully. It also appears to me that there is proof from what Tosafot wrote in Bekhorot 8, s.v. aknuyei akni lei makom bechatzeiro; they wrote explicitly that even without a kinyan he fully resolves and obligates himself. See there. This proves explicitly as we said: where one is pleased to perform a mitzvah with his property, he fully transfers ownership even without any kinyan. The word of God is true in the mouth of our master the Taz, may the memory of the righteous be a blessing, according to the reason we have explained with God's help. Examine carefully.
