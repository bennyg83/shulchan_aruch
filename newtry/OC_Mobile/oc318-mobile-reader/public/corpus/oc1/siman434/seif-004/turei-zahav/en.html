(א) His agent may nullify, etc. — this is the view of Tosafot, Magen Avraham, Ran, and Bach, who hold everywhere a person's agent is like him except annulment of vows and asking the owner where verses are written; but the Ran brings others who hold since nullification is from hefker they touched it and an agent cannot declare hefker — see Beit Yosef. Therefore also what is said about his wife — according to this view she cannot nullify through his wife, for certainly his wife cannot declare hefker without his knowledge. It appears to me to compare this to what the Rosh wrote in Kiddushin chapter 1: if a woman was betrothed with stolen goods of hers she is betrothed, because presumably a woman wants the money to be a gift so kiddushin take effect — see at length; if so here too we say since he wishes to remove himself from chametz prohibition, presumably he gave his chametz as a gift to his agent so he can nullify; therefore the agent need not say so-and-so's chametz, for presumably he gave it as a gift. It appears further: since the homeowner wishes to make an agent for such a mitzva — are we dealing with fools that he would not do such a mitzva himself? Rather presumably he had this necessarily — he travels in a caravan and fears he will come during Pesach and forget to nullify before Pesach; therefore he made an agent; therefore one may rely on the lenient view.

(ב) If he does not do so, etc. — the language seems forced; it appears he wanted to say: if there is concern that for some reason he will not do so, as above.
