Shiyurei Leket, klal 7, siman 7, writes that when an agent nullifies it, he should say: “All leaven in the possession of so-and-so that he does not know of,” etc. May nullify it. Ran writes that others disagree. Since nullification operates through ownerlessness, an agent cannot nullify it, just as one person cannot say to another, “Declare my property ownerless,” and thereby accomplish anything. Beit Yosef rejects this reasoning and therefore rules here that an agent may nullify it. Kol Bo also holds this way. Bach challenges Beit Yosef and rules that nullification should not be performed through an agent, but his arguments are not compelling. The primary ruling follows Beit Yosef. Ran’s assumption that an agent cannot declare another’s property ownerless is based upon the fact that the owner did not transfer the property to the agent. Here, however, we presume that the owner wishes the nullification and transfers sufficient authority over the chametz to the agent so that he may declare it ownerless and nullify it.
