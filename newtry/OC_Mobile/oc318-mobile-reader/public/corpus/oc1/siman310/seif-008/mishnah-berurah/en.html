(32) That there is upon it from erev Shabbat, etc. — to exclude if it was kindled on Shabbat — forbidden in this according to all because of nolad, for a new act was made: at first wood and now ash; and if permitted ash became mixed with forbidden ash and the prohibition was not recognizable before it became mixed — it is batel in the majority; for if it were recognizable we rule that something that has those who permit is not batel even in a thousand; and muktzeh has those who permit on erev. Chayei Adam wrote: it appears to me that sap dripping from trees in Nisan days is also forbidden because of nolad; and even if there is already a utensil with water from erev Shabbat into which it drips — nevertheless forbidden, and we do not say here that it is batel, for the drop that drips was recognizable before it mixed with the water, and we rule that something that has those who permit is not batel even in a thousand:
(33) Important from the forbidden object — and if both are equal — forbidden. The poskim wrote: if for the whole world the permitted is more important and for him it is not important — we follow his view; and the same applies in the reverse:
(34) And the reason for permitting moving, etc. — meaning why in siman 309 seif 3 we say with fruits placed with a stone in a basket — even though the basket is a base for prohibition and permission, nevertheless where it is possible he should not move the fruits and the basket together when the stone rests there — rather he shakes everything onto the ground first and afterward gathers the fruits into the basket and moves them; and here too with ash one might say he should shake the soot with the wood chips onto the ground and afterward put the soot alone into the utensil and move it wherever he wants; and for this he wrote that here it is not possible to put the soot in afterward alone because wood chips are small chips or small coals that it is impossible to take the ash without them:
(35) To the place of the shovel — for then it is impossible to shake, for it will fall to the place he needs it; therefore he moves it all together:
(36) He shakes the ash, etc. — and if there is loss in shaking — he need not shake, as is written in siman 309 seif 3 [Acharonim]:
(37) The prohibition is upon it alone — meaning at bein hashemashos therefore placing the permitted next to it does not help; even if the prohibition were removed entirely from there — nevertheless forbidden, since it was designated at bein hashemashos, as above in seif 7; but if on Shabbat a forbidden object was placed upon it — since at bein hashemashos it was not designated, it is muktzeh for half Shabbat, and we rule there is no muktzeh for half Shabbat — rather as long as the forbidden object rests upon it, moving is forbidden; therefore permitted to place upon it a more important permitted object and move everything together if he cannot shake the forbidden object off:
