(23) And it was upon it at bein hashemashos — and it deals when he placed, for if through forgetting — behold even while the coins are still upon it it is permitted to shake them off, and all the more so the bed did not become a base for the entire Shabbat, as above in siman 309 seif 4:
(24) And even for the need of his body — for the law of a base is like that muktzeh that is upon it; and since coins are muktzeh machmat gufo that is forbidden even for the need of his body and its place — the law of the bed is likewise:
(25) And some forbid when designated for this — the reason is it is inferior to other utensils whose melacha is for prohibition, since he designated it explicitly for this — presumably he designates a place for it and is particular not to use it for any other use except this [and nevertheless if he did not yet place coins in it at all, only designation alone — there is no muktzeh upon it, for designation is not a matter]; and therefore its law is like muktzeh machmat cheisron kis — so too the poskim; and Magen Avraham wrote: and accordingly even for the need of his body and its place it is forbidden; and he remained astonished at Rama who wrote according to this view that for the need of his body or its place it is permitted — it is not so; and he brought proofs for this; and so Perishah and Biur of the GRA agree with his words; and see what we wrote below:
(26) Unless he performed, etc. — for this act nullifies the thought of designation, and the act he performed with it at first when he placed coins in it [GRA]:
(27) However for the need of his body — see in s.k. 25 what we wrote in the name of Magen Avraham and other Acharonim who were astonished at this; and they hold that according to this view it is forbidden in every manner; and nevertheless for practical law the book Acharonim agrees: even though the pocket is designated for coins, as long as it is not his way to be particular to place other things in it — it is not in the category of muktzeh machmat cheisron kis but only like an ordinary utensil whose melacha is for prohibition, and permitted for the need of his body or its place; and so agreed Sefer Beit Me'ir — see there:
(28) For the pocket is batel to it — meaning and therefore permitted to go out with it even on Rosh Hashana, and we do not say the pocket is a burden [Magen Avraham]; he further wrote that even if one holds above according to Rabbeinu Tam that even for the need of his body and its place it is forbidden, as we wrote in s.k. 25 — nevertheless here it is permitted to place inside the pocket and take from it, for we see he was not particular about it on a weekday to place also other things in it:
(29) But if he forgot — his main intent is to clarify for us that since there are still coins in the pocket — forbidden to wear it lest he forget and go out, as he concludes at the end; and only to move is permitted. And know further: it is not precise that it is only forgetting — for even if he placed coins intentionally from erev Shabbat and his intent was that they remain there all Shabbat — even though the pocket became a base for the coins, nevertheless permitted to move the garment even while the coins rest in the pocket, for the garment did not become a base since they are not on the main part of the garment [and nevertheless forbidden to put his hand in the pocket, for in any case the pocket became a base for the coins, and even after the coins were removed from inside, for behold at bein hashemashos the pocket was designated for coins]; and nevertheless if shaking is possible, one should shake off first, for there are those who hold that even though the coins are not on the main part of the garment, one must also shake off before moving the garment, whether with placing or with forgetting — as will be written below:
(30) Since, etc. — meaning the pocket is not sewn along its length to the garment but only its mouth is sewn and it entirely hangs [Beit Yosef]; if the pocket were sewn entirely to the garment we would say the entire garment became a base; and even though here we deal with forgetting — nevertheless he needed to shake off the muktzeh at first, as above in siman 309 seif 4; unlike when the pocket hangs — he need not shake even at first; and some are strict and hold that in this too he needs shaking at first:
(31) And the coins are not, etc. — Magen Avraham wrote: specifically when the mouth of the pocket is sewn to the garment do we say it is batel to the garment; and so when there are coins inside a box in a table [called Tishkestil] made in a manner that it is impossible to remove it entirely from the table — therefore it is batel to the main table; but when a pocket full of coins is tied to a garment — it is an independent utensil and is not batel to the garment; and so the box if they can remove it entirely from the table — if so each one is an independent utensil and did not become batel to the table, and consequently forbidden to move the garment and the table, for they become a base for the coins when he placed the coins in them from erev Shabbat with intent that they be there on Shabbat [unless a permitted object rests on the table that is more important and the table becomes a base for it, as below in seif 8]; and if he forgot to take them from erev Shabbat — in any case he needs shaking off by law at first. However if there are only a few perutot inside that are batel relative to the garment and table, for a person does not nullify his garment and table for a few perutot [Chayei Adam]. He further wrote: it appears to me that specifically when the box in the table is always designated for coins or other muktzeh; but when the box hanging on the table is always made to place bread and knives in it and it happened that he placed coins in it — even if there is no bread and knives in it, even though the Tishkestil is certainly forbidden to move — nevertheless permitted to move the table, for the Tishkestil too is essentially a utensil whose melacha is for permission — if so the table became a base for prohibition and permission — requires further study; and see there what he further wrote on this matter:
