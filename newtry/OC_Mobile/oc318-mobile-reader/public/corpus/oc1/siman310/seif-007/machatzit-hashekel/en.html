(s.k. 7) Since, etc. — but, etc. — he need not shake; but Rambam, etc. — this permission that is written in Shulchan Aruch are Terumat HaDeshen's words, and he derived it from the law of a drawer-board that the gemara daf 74 challenges Rav who said: even if he did not designate it for coins — if there were coins upon it in this case forbidden — from that which we learned chapter 8 of tractate Kelim: a drawer-board of a chest (and it is a board or rim on which the chest rests, as Magen Avraham wrote afterward, and that is Rabbenu Tam's explanation there) — when it is removable (meaning it can be removed from the chest — if so the drawer-board is an independent utensil and is not attached for this matter to the chest and is not batel to the chest) — therefore if there are coins on the drawer-board it is forbidden to move it; and the gemara infers: if there are no coins upon it — permitted to move it, even though there were upon it at bein hashemashos; and this is difficult for Rav. And Tosafos there inferred: if it is not removable — it is attached to the chest, batel to the chest, and permitted to move the chest even though there are coins on the drawer-board, since the coins are not on the main part of the utensil, namely the chest. And from this Terumat HaDeshen derived to permit also with a pocket — even if there are coins inside — to permit moving the garment since the coins are not on the main part of the garment and the pocket is batel to the garment. And accordingly Magen Avraham wrote: according to what Rambam wrote in explaining the mishnah in this case of a drawer-board — why when it is not removable is it permitted to move it with the coins and he need not shake, since shaking in such a case is difficult, for he must turn the entire chest — it does not suffice with turning the drawer-board, since it is attached to the chest and the Rabbis did not burden him so much; therefore Magen Avraham wrote: since he permitted a pocket derived from a drawer-board — he did not permit a pocket more than a drawer-board; therefore if it is possible to shake in a pocket without great effort he must shake:

And know, etc. — that even if he placed, etc. — Tosafos challenged the gemara's difficulty on Rav from a drawer-board: it implies when there are no coins upon it, even though there were at bein hashemashos — nevertheless permitted to move it; they challenged Tosafos: perhaps a drawer-board deals with forgetting coins upon it — therefore when there are no coins upon it permitted to move it; and it will be seen: necessarily it deals with placing, for it teaches and states: if there are coins upon it — forbidden to move it — it is implied even if he needs the place of the chest and the drawer-board — nevertheless forbidden to move it; and with forgetting we rule above siman 309 seif 5 that when he needs its place it is permitted to move it with the muktzeh and he need not shake; if so, necessarily it deals with placing; and the gemara's difficulty is not resolved. And since "removable" deals with placing — from this we infer "not removable" that it is permitted, namely even with placing:
