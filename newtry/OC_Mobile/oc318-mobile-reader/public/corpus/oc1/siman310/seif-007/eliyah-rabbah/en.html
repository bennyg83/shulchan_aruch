[5] [Levush] Since it was set aside, etc. This applies specifically to items whose completion occurs through Heaven, such as dried figs that become ready only through the sun. But something completed by human action, such as dates over which water was poured before Shabbos and which become fit on Friday night after absorbing the taste, is permitted. See Beit Yosef.
[6] [Levush] And furthermore it is possible, etc. Terumat HaDeshen, siman 67, writes so. This requires study from touching a corpse in siman 311, seif 7, where the touching is for the corpse's benefit. See Bartenura, chapter Shoel, Mishnah 5. As a practical matter, siman 308, seif 5, implies that touching muktzeh is permitted even for the muktzeh item's benefit. Magen Avraham at the beginning of siman 513 writes likewise. See the end of siman 265.
[7] [Levush] Whether for use of its body, etc. This parallels an extinguished lamp discussed in siman 279, where all authorities hold that it is prohibited even for use of its body, contrary to Hagahot Ashiri, who permits it (Beit Yosef). This requires study, because earlier Beit Yosef cited Hagahot Ashiri as prohibiting an extinguished lamp. See what I wrote there, seif katan 5. Perishah's quotation of Beit Yosef, saying "unlike Hagahot Ashiri who permits an extinguished lamp for use of its body," is therefore even more surprising. The case here is where coins are on the item even though they were not there at bein hashmashot, and a non-Jew or child placed them there at the Jew's direction. If they were forgotten there, one simply shakes them off. See siman 309, seif 4.
[8] Nevertheless, for use, etc. Beit Yosef writes this. Magen Avraham disagrees and prohibits even for use of its body, and Perishah similarly compares it to muktzeh because of monetary loss, where the owner is particular. See the beginning of siman 308. This is difficult in light of Beit Yosef himself at the end of siman 309, commenting on Tashbetz's statement that if a garment has a money pouch attached it may not be worn on Shabbos. Beit Yosef suggests that this may apply even when the pouch contains no money, based on Rabbenu Tam's rule concerning a bed designated for money. Wearing it is plainly use of its body or place, as Levush writes. The issue is especially difficult regarding a sewn-on pocket. It is also difficult in light of Magen Avraham, seif katan 6, who permits placing something into such a pocket and removing it because use of its place is permitted even according to Rosh when the owner is not particular about the pocket. See Bach on siman 266, seif 8, who explains that Tashbetz viewed the garment as subordinate to the money. It seems Beit Yosef at the end of siman 309 escaped Magen Avraham's notice. As practical law, the general principle appears to me to be that whether a bed or a pouch was designated for money, so long as the owner is not particular and also uses it for other things, it may be moved for use of its body or place.
[9] [Levush] Or he may move, etc. Levush added this himself, because Beit Yosef and Rama mention only the concern that he may go out wearing it. Accordingly, for us who have no biblical public domain, there may be room for leniency when needed. It is obvious that if the coins can be shaken out, they must be shaken out, because the case concerns Shabbos itself.
