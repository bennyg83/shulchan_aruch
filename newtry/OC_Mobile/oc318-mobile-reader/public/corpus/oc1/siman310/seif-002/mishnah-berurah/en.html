(2) There is no food detached, etc. — for with something that is not food, even according to R' Shimon there is sometimes muktzeh, such as muktzeh machmat cheisron kis, as is written in siman 308 seif 1; and so with something that is not a utensil, such as coins, bundles, and stones and the like:

(3) Detached — for with something that was attached from erev Shabbat, likewise according to all it is muktzeh since his mind was not on it from yesterday; and see in Beit Yosef:

(4) For commerce — for even though they stand for commerce, his mind is on them to eat from them when he wants:

(5) And even wheat that he planted, etc., and eggs, etc. — meaning that even though he rejected them with his hands from using them — nevertheless since they are fit to gather and eat they are not muktzeh, for when they would be muktzeh we require two factors — he rejected them with his hands and they are not fit — as written at the end of the seif:

(6) In the ground — and see in Me'iri who wrote that specifically when the seeds were not yet covered in earth; and see below in siman 311 seif 8 and in Mishna Berurah there s.k. 27:

(7) They had not sprouted — for if they sprouted he would be liable for uprooting something from its growth:

(8) And eggs under the hen — meaning he seated the hen on the eggs from erev Shabbat in order to raise chicks:

(9) Before their cooking — for even when he placed them in baskets they are still eaten thus, unlike dried figs and raisins: when he placed them to dry and they spoiled somewhat they are again unfit for eating until they dry, and certainly he removed his mind from them. It is in the gemara that specifically dried figs and raisins, but other fruits that he brought to the roof to dry — there is no muktzeh, for they are fit for eating even before they dry:

(10) In the storage area — a yard behind the houses is called moktzeh. And even if he was eating from the grapes until it grew dark and left over and placed them in the storage area or brought them to the roof to dry to make them raisins — nevertheless there the name moktzeh applies to them [gemara], for now since he placed them to dry he removed his mind from them:

(11) It is muktzeh — meaning even for moving they are forbidden, for behold they are like stones and earth:

(12) There is no designation, etc. — meaning something that is forbidden to an Israelite because it became designated at bein hashemashos and was not prepared from erev Shabbat by an Israelite — this does not apply, for a gentile does not designate anything with intent; and since it is prepared for one person it is prepared for all; but something forbidden at bein hashemashos because of attachment, or because it lacked trapping and the gentile detached or trapped on Shabbat — even though the thing belongs to a gentile, according to all it is forbidden; and so with the prohibition of nolad likewise:

(13) And even dried figs, etc. — meaning that even though the gentile brought them up from erev Shabbat to dry and they are rejected with hands — nevertheless the name muktzeh did not apply to them; and see in Sefer Beit Me'ir who is very uncertain about this, since there is rejection with hands and they are not fit for eating — is it not because of the gentile that he gives more attention to this? See there where he expands on this; and the GRA also holds like Beit Me'ir; and therefore explain that Shulchan Aruch deals specifically with dried figs and raisins that are fit and not fit, as below in seif 5 where Shulchan Aruch rules that designation helps in this — Kol Bo ruled that with a gentile, even if he did not designate it helps, for he does not designate with intent; but if they are not fit at all — we rule that even designation does not help with an Israelite in this — there is no difference between Israelite and gentile:
