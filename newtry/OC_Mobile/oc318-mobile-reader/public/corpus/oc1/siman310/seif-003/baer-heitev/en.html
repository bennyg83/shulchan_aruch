"Returned." Taz writes that the same applies to a scalpel used for a brit milah on Shabbos. At bein hashmashot it was muktzeh machmat chisaron kis, like a scribe's knife, because a person is very particular not to use that scalpel for any other purpose lest it become damaged, and certainly he does not use it for eating, which is contrary to ordinary human behavior. Therefore, although it is designated for the mitzvah of brit milah on the following day, once its needed use has ended it returns to the prohibition of moving that applied at bein hashmashot. It should therefore be put away in the same room in which the brit milah was performed. Maharil writes likewise; see Yoreh De'ah, siman 266. Bach, siman 308, permits moving it, and Maharshal writes likewise. Magen Avraham, siman 331, seif katan 5, discusses this at length and concludes that one should be stringent not to put it down until he has brought it home, because any muktzeh object already in one's hand may be carried to any place one wishes. See there, and see Beit Yaakov, siman 102.
