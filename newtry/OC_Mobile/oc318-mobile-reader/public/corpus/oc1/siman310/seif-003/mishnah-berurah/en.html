(14) "Whether regarding a prohibition of eating," etc. The phrase "whether ... whether" teaches a greater novelty in the first case, regarding eating. It means that not only with respect to ordinary moving do we certainly say that there is no muktzeh for half of Shabbos and therefore permit moving it once its muktzeh status has departed, but it is even permitted to eat it. (15) "If something happened to it," etc. For example, rain fell on raisins and they swelled until they were unfit to eat, and afterward the sun shone upon them and they dried until they again became fit to eat. They return to their permitted status. The reason is that the concept of muktzeh for half of Shabbos does not apply when the item was fit at bein hashmashot. Taz writes that a scalpel used for a brit milah on Shabbos may not be moved after the brit milah, because it is muktzeh machmat chisaron kis: a person is particular not to use it for any other purpose. This is unlike those who permit moving it on the ground that there is no muktzeh for half of Shabbos, because at bein hashmashot it too was muktzeh machmat chisaron kis and could not then be moved for any purpose. Therefore, even though it was designated for the mitzvah of brit milah the following day, once its needed use has ended it returns to the prohibition of moving that applied at bein hashmashot. It should therefore be put away in the same room in which the brit milah was performed. Magen Avraham in siman 331, seif katan 5, agrees with the basic law, but holds that one should be stringent in another respect: while the scalpel is still in his hand, he should not put it down until he places it in a guarded location or carries it home, because any muktzeh object already in one's hand may be moved to whatever place one wishes, as stated above in siman 308, seif 3. If the mohel also performs priyah and therefore must put down the scalpel, Eliyah Rabbah writes that another person should receive it directly from his hand and carry it to a guarded place. Nevertheless, after the fact, if he put it down and there is concern that it may be stolen, Eliyah Rabbah inclines to rely upon Rama and Shach, who hold that it may be moved. Chayei Adam cites this in the laws of brit milah. (16) "It is forbidden for the entire day." The principal designation must be made before the day begins, as it is written, "And it shall be on the sixth day that they shall prepare that which they bring." This item, however, was set aside from use at the very beginning of the day.
