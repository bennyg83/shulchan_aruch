(14) Whether with prohibition of eating, etc. — this "whether" at the beginning is an additional stringency: first regarding prohibition of eating — and meaning that not only regarding moving in general do we certainly say there is no muktzeh for half Shabbat and it is permitted to move it after the name moktzeh left it — but even to eat it, it is permitted:
(15) If something happened to it, etc. — such as rain fell on the raisins and they swelled until they were unfit for eating, and afterward the sun shone and they dried until they became fit — it returned to its permission; and the reason is that muktzeh for half Shabbat does not apply, since at bein hashemashos it was fit. Taz wrote: a knife with which he circumcised on Shabbat — one may not move it after the circumcision, for it is muktzeh machmat cheisron kis, for a person is particular not to use it for anything else; and not like those who are lenient to move it from the reason that there is no muktzeh for half Shabbat, for behold at bein hashemashos too it was muktzeh machmat cheisron kis and one may not move it then for any matter; and therefore even though it stands for tomorrow for the melacha of circumcision — nevertheless after his need is finished it returns to the prohibition of moving from bein hashemashos; therefore they hide it in that room where he circumcises there; and Magen Avraham in siman 331 s.k. 5 agrees with him also on the essence of the law; but his view is that one should be strict that while the knife is in his hand he not remove it from his hand until he places it in a guarded place or brings it to his house, for any muktzeh that is in his hand he may move it to any place he wants, as above in siman 308 seif 3. And if he is also uncircumcised and must remove the knife from his hand — the Acharonim wrote that another should receive it from his hand and he brings it to the guarded place. And nevertheless b'dieved if he removed it from his hand and there is concern it will be stolen — the Acharonim wrote one may rely on Rama and Shach who hold it is permitted to move it; and Chachmat Adam brought this in the laws of circumcision:
