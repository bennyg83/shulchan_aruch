For we rule that in muktzeh due to repulsiveness we follow R' Shimon who permits. And Tur wrote: but in muktzeh due to prohibition, such as a candle lit on that Shabbat — we do not rule like R' Shimon, for muktzeh applies only to dried figs etc.; and Beit Yosef wrote the halacha is difficult against the halacha that we rule like R' Shimon that muktzeh applies only to dried figs etc.; and we rule that many things are forbidden due to prohibition muktzeh as stated siman 308; rather R' Shimon must concede in all muktzeh due to prohibition, whether because it is not fit for permitted use on its own or due to something on it, whether because there is no utensil status on it and one does not eat from it — R' Shimon concedes it is muktzeh; and similarly to move a utensil whose function is for prohibition for shade and not for warmth — R' Shimon is not lenient except in a thing that was forbidden at bein hashemashos and the forbidding factor departed from it on Shabbat, for R' Shimon permits; but we do not rule like him; rather since it was muktzeh at bein hashemashos it was muktzeh for all Shabbat — end of his words Beit Yosef; and requires study, for in siman 318 seif 2 Shulchan Aruch ruled regarding slaughtering for a sick person who became ill on Shabbat — permitted for a healthy person and R' Shimon's muktzeh does not apply as stated there — see there:
