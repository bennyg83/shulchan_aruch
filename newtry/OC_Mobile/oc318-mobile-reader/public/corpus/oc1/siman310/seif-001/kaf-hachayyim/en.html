1) [Seif 1] A stick upon which fish are hung, etc. This refers to meat that is not so repulsive. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 1.
2) There: A stick upon which fish are hung, etc. This stick is a utensil because it was designated for hanging fish. It is considered muktzeh only because it is repulsive, and we rule like Rabbi Shimon regarding muktzeh because of repulsiveness. Levush Serad. This implies that if it was not designated as a utensil and fish were merely hung upon it temporarily, it may not be moved. See above, siman 308, seif 16, and our words there in paragraphs 107 and 118.
3) There: It may be moved. This applies even from the sun to the shade. The reason is that no category of muktzeh applies to it, so it is like a utensil whose designated use is permitted. Derishah, paragraph 2. Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 1, however, writes that it may be moved only when needed for its own use or its place, like a utensil whose designated use is forbidden. He cites the preceding words of Derishah and leaves the matter requiring examination; see there. It appears that if salted fish are hung upon it, everyone agrees that it may be moved even from the sun to the shade, since they are fit to be eaten raw, as stated above in siman 308, seif 32. It is therefore like a utensil whose designated use is permitted, which may be moved even from the sun to the shade, as stated above in siman 308, seif 4; see there.
4) There: Like Rabbi Shimon, who permits it. Regarding muktzeh because of a prohibition, however, such as a lamp that was lit that Shabbos, we do not rule like him. It is forbidden even after it is extinguished. See above, siman 279, and our words there, with the help of Heaven.
5) There: Like Rabbi Shimon, who permits it. Regarding muktzeh because of potential financial loss, even Rabbi Shimon agrees that it is forbidden. Shabbos 157a. Rabbi Shimon also agrees that clods, stones, money, and anything that is neither a utensil nor food may not be moved, since it is fit for no use. Beis Yosef. Hagahas HaLevush writes similarly: Although in several matters we rule like Rabbi Shimon, who maintains that the prohibition of muktzeh applies only to dried figs and raisins, we nevertheless rule that many other things are forbidden as muktzeh because Rabbi Shimon himself agrees that they are forbidden. These are his words.
