(s.k. 1) Wheat that he planted, and it is not comparable to a candle, etc. — end of Bach's words; meaning that Bach brought in the name of Maharshal Magen Avraham's difficulty from a candle, and raised this difficulty in Bach: on that which they asked on daf 48 — wheat that he planted in the ground (for he rejected them with his hands) according to R' Shimon what — and it answers: there is no muktzeh according to R' Shimon except with dried figs and raisins, for there are two adverse factors — he rejected them with his hands and also they are not fit; unlike wheat that he planted — although he rejected them with his hands, nevertheless they are fit at bein hashemashos [and so ruled in Shulchan Aruch; therefore at the beginning from dates and almonds, etc., until "and so dates," etc. — all of them that he rejected with his hands are nevertheless permitted since they are fit; and it will be seen: and so dates, etc., that are not fit — nevertheless he did not reject them with his hands; on the contrary he performs an act to fix them that they be fit — therefore they too are permitted] — and Tosafos challenged: what is he uncertain about? Let him resolve from that which R' Shimon holds a candle that went out is permitted to move — behold even though he rejected with his hands, nevertheless permitted according to R' Shimon; and it will be seen: Tosafos — a candle that went out is different, for as soon as it went out the rejection passed, unlike wheat that he planted that are still in rejection — therefore he was uncertain, for perhaps in such a case R' Shimon agrees it is forbidden at present; behold it is clear from Tosafos' words that with a candle that went out there is more room to permit than with wheat that he planted; and since we rule regarding a candle that went out like R' Yehuda that it is forbidden — all the more so we should forbid with wheat that he planted; and why did Rabbeinu Yehudah rule with wheat that he planted that it is permitted? Therefore for practical law he disagrees with Maharshal and holds that with wheat that he planted too it is forbidden for us — see there; and Magen Avraham settles this difficulty: Tosafos wrote their words for R' Shimon who does not have the argument of since it became designated at bein hashemashos — for according to him the gemara should have asked; therefore they wrote that a candle that went out is lenient compared to wheat that he planted; but for us who rule in this like R' Yehuda who has the argument of since it became designated at bein hashemashos, etc. — on the contrary, a candle that went out is stricter:
