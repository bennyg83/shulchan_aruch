(32) Even though he cannot, etc. — and one might have thought he is considered as dead and it would be a residence without owners; it teaches us that as long as he is alive he is considered alive for all his matters.
(33) And they shall make an eruv for him — Magen Avraham wrote that if he died on Shabbat he restricts, since the eruv is not fit to remain valid the entire Shabbat, for most of those in a dying state die, end of his words. And behold if one was living with him in that house it is obvious the eruv is not nullified; and if one of the marketplace inherited and does not come to live there on Shabbat and the house remained without owners for that Shabbat — it is also obvious it is permitted, as above at the beginning of seif 4. But Magen Avraham deals with one of the marketplace inheriting and coming to live there on Shabbat, or one of the courtyard residents inheriting who lives in another house; and the reason in all this is that the eruv placed on behalf of the one in a dying state was not fit to remain valid the entire Shabbat, for we see he died on Shabbat — and it does not apply here to say "once permitted, permitted." Therefore it appears that even if one made an eruv that erev Shabbat on behalf of the entire courtyard and automatically the one in a dying state was included, and afterward the one in a dying state died that Shabbat — the portion of the eruv given on behalf of this one in a dying state is also nullified. However if the eruv was placed on the previous Shabbat and thus the eruv took effect from the outset — even though this Shabbat when he became in a dying state it was destined to be nullified — nevertheless it applies here to say "once permitted, permitted," as will be explained in siman 374 seif 1, see there; and see Biur Halacha what we wrote in the name of Atzei Almogim.
(34) And likewise a minor restricts — until they make an eruv for him [Rambam].
