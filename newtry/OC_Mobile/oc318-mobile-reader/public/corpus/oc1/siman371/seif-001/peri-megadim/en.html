And some say — Taz — what he wrote from the outset I did not merit to understand; and Tosafos Shabbos already stood on this, sign 7, that there too it was not permitted after it became ruined. And what is the reason that here too after the non-Jew came he restricts and it was not permitted? Behold before he came — why also what he wrote further here? And in sign 2 I, the poor one, did not merit to understand them. For it appears there are three measures here: (1) accustomed to return; (2) balanced doubt; (3) clearly evident from his actions that he diverted from coming today — regarding a non-Jew. And on this Taz wrote to Rosh and Tur who explained Mishnah Eruvin ch. 6 (1): in doubt, a Jew in his house presumably diverted, not at his son's; and a non-Jew did not divert; and the rabbi ruled in his gloss like Rosh and Tur on this; therefore he pointed to 383 — meaning specifically in doubt he restricts when the non-Jew returned on Shabbat; but if he clearly diverted, even if the non-Jew returned he does not restrict — "once permitted, permitted"; and from this, without return, in doubt regarding a non-Jew too it is permitted — we say he diverted his attention, etc. Therefore this of ch. 6 (1) — they disagree in doubt regarding a gentile; and that of 65b there "the innkeeper on Shabbat who returned" — this too was in doubt and he did not clearly divert his heart. However according to one view, Maggid Mishneh's view, that in doubt even a Jew restricts even if he did not come and clearly diverted his attention — it deals with his daughter; manner of speech; Magen Avraham (1) on this. If so, necessarily that of 65b "the master of the house came" — a non-Jew returned restricts — it deals with clearly diverted; nevertheless he restricts regarding a non-Jew; therefore the author in seif 2 brought Maggid Mishneh's ruling ch. 4 halacha 13 of Laws of Eruvin who wrote specifically "Jew," as said — and to say Taz holds like Olat Shabbat in 383 that the author rules like view 2 here — this is far.
