1) [Se'if 1] One of the courtyard's residents who left his house, etc. A residence without its occupants does not prohibit carrying. Tur. It is permitted to carry from that house into a courtyard containing other residents who established an eruv among themselves, and this is not considered carrying from one domain to another. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1.
2) There. Who left his house and went, etc. This means together with all members of his household. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, there.
3) There. Who left his house and went, etc. This means before the onset of Shabbos. Tur. It appears to me that this detail was stated to teach that even if he returns on Shabbos, he does not prohibit carrying. Alternatively, it states that he left before the onset of Shabbos because of the law concerning a non-Jew, who does prohibit carrying if he returns on Shabbos, as stated in siman 383. In truth, however, even if the resident was present and did not establish an eruv but left on Shabbos, he does not prohibit carrying, as stated in se'if 4 regarding one who died on Shabbos without having established an eruv. Magen Avraham, subsection 1. In my humble opinion, it appears that the Tur discusses an unspecified case, which requires that he leave before the onset of Shabbos. If, however, he is asked and states that he does not intend to return, then even if he leaves on Shabbos he does not prohibit carrying. The Shulchan Aruch and Levush discuss this latter case. Avodas HaKodesh, folio 23, writes similarly. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1. However, regarding the statement of Magen Avraham and Eliyah Rabbah that even if he leaves on Shabbos he does not prohibit carrying, as in se'if 4, see Beis Meir, who distinguishes between the two cases. Gaon Yaakov on tractate Eruvin likewise rules that the Tur specifically means that he left before the onset of Shabbos, and also distinguishes between the two cases. See there. Rashi on 47a likewise writes that the discussion concerns one who left before the onset of Shabbos. Rashba and Ritva also write that it applies specifically before the onset of Shabbos. See there. One should therefore rule stringently.
4) There. If he put it out of his mind, etc. This is from Rambam, chapter 4. Although the discussion there states that one who goes to spend Shabbos with his daughter does not prohibit carrying, but one who goes to his son does, and the Tur writes likewise, Maggid Mishneh explains that Rambam maintains this was stated only as a common example. People more commonly spend Shabbos with a daughter than with a son. In every case, if he put returning out of his mind, even if he went to his son he does not prohibit carrying; if he did not put it out of his mind, even if he went to his daughter he does prohibit carrying. See there. Beis Yosef cites his words. The halachic authorities likewise rule in accordance with the Shulchan Aruch.
5) There. He does not prohibit carrying for them. If they established an eruv among themselves, even though he did not join their eruv, they may also carry from his house into the courtyard. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1.
6) There. When does this apply? To a Jewish person, etc. Since he put returning out of his mind, we presume that he will not reconsider. A non-Jew, however, continues to prohibit carrying even if he went to another city, because as long as he can return, he is regarded as though he had already returned. Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 3.
