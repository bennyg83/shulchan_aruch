(1) Who left his house and went, etc. — see Mishna Berurah that it deals with one who went while it was still day; thus Tur wrote. And although Magen Avraham wrote that Tur does not speak precisely — that the same applies if he went on Shabbat when he diverted his attention not to return that day — and he brought proof from what we ruled in seif 4 regarding one who died on Shabbat and did not make an eruv, where the inheritor did not come on Shabbat to live there; we may adopt there Taz's view to be lenient also — there is no proof for our matter. See in Sefer Beit Meir who distinguished plainly that here, since he is alive and at all times can return, R' Yosi's reason that he already diverted his attention does not apply at all — this applies only to one who goes to spend Shabbat before the day was sanctified; and likewise Rashi on daf 47 explicitly that it deals with "while it was still day"; and likewise in Sefer Gaon Yaakov his view is that Tur speaks specifically of "while it was still day." And he wrote another correct reason to distinguish — it is not comparable to seif 4 that deals with death on Shabbat, for here while he is alive and acquired residence in it after the day was sanctified, his residence was not uprooted from here by going part of the remaining day to spend Shabbat elsewhere; and likewise Rashba and Ritva wrote explicitly in our matter that it deals specifically with "while it was still day." And although one may reject that from them there is no proof, for they are stringent also in that of seif 4 — nevertheless, since we find three of the early authorities [Rashi, Rashba, and Ritva] who wrote explicitly that it deals specifically with "while it was still day," it is reasonable not to be lenient when he went on Shabbat. And behold I saw in Eliyah Rabbah who wrote to distinguish that Tur deals with an unspecified case and therefore wrote "while it was still day"; but if he explicitly diverted his attention not to return on Shabbat it helps even on Shabbat. And behold in truth at his daughter's, unspecified is for him as though at another's he explicitly diverted his attention, as explained in the poskim; and nevertheless Tur wrote "while it was still day." Also what he wrote that in this manner it is in Avodat HaKodesh — there is no proof for this; for what he wrote in gate 4 sign 3 that if he explicitly diverted his attention from his heart it helps even at his son's, and "other" deals with one who went while it was still day — know that he rules afterward in sign 7 regarding one who died on Shabbat and did not make an eruv: even if the inheritor did not come to live there on Shabbat, it is forbidden for the entire Shabbat, since once it was forbidden for part of Shabbat it is forbidden for all of it; if so it is plain that in our matter in all respects we require specifically "while it was still day," otherwise it is forbidden; and likewise Rashba wrote explicitly in his novellae that we require specifically "while it was still day," otherwise once it was forbidden for part of Shabbat it is forbidden for the entire Shabbat. According to this reason it is clear there is no distinction between unspecified and explicitly diverting his attention. Therefore it appears plain not to be lenient when he went on Shabbat, unless in a time of need; perhaps one may rely on Magen Avraham's view when he explicitly diverted his attention.

(2) If he diverted, etc. — although in the sugya there it states specifically at his daughter's but not at his son's; and all the more so "other" — the sugya deals with unspecified: we say when he went to spend Shabbat at his daughter's he diverts his attention from his residence, and at his son's he does not divert lest his daughter-in-law quarrel with him. But if he explicitly diverted his attention from his residence for that Shabbat — such as they asked him and he said he does not intend to return to his residence for this Shabbat — he does not restrict even if he went to spend Shabbat at his son's; and the same applies to "other." And if he did not divert his attention — such as they heard from him that he intends to return on Shabbat — even if he went to his daughter's he restricts, for we consider him as though he is in his house. All this is explained from Rashba's words in Avkat Rochel; and it is reasonable that Rambam too concedes that at his daughter's even unspecified we say he diverted his attention — for otherwise in what does the Gemara distinguish between his daughter and his son? Rather he wrote a definitive expression "if he explicitly diverted," for this helps for every person; and thus Beur HaGra implies. And behold in Tosafos Shabbos it implies that for Rambam there is no distinction between his daughter and others; and such is Shulchan Aruch's view according to him — and it is not clear, for all the early authorities brought the Gemara's statement that there is a distinction between his daughter and his son: R' Chananel, Rif, Semag, Rosh, Ri az, and Rashba; and it is reasonable Rambam too would not disagree on them, but he wrote a definitive expression as Beur HaGra wrote — this helps even at his son's and at others; and likewise above in the name of Avkat Rochel.

(3) In another courtyard — see Bach who brought that Tosafos disagree on this, and thus it appears from Tur that specifically if he went to another city we are lenient also regarding a non-Jew, and not in another courtyard; and in psakim of Rosh he wrote thus explicitly; afterward I found in Avkat Rochel that it is also proven thus, and also in Sefer Korban Netanel he wondered on this ruling, for it suffices for us to be lenient per R' Yehuda [as Rosh wrote] and he speaks specifically of another city and not another courtyard. However in Ri az brought in Shelati Giborim his view is thus that even in another courtyard it is permitted, and it appears his reasoning is that we are lenient with two leniencies that we hold like R' Yehuda that Jew and non-Jew are equal in the matter, and we also hold another city and another courtyard are equal; but in any case it is a solitary view, for Tosafos and Rashba and Tur disagree, and especially for the first view regarding a non-Jew in all respects it is forbidden. Afterward I found in Beit Meir who brought further proof from Rosh that regarding a non-Jew we do not say he diverted his attention and automatically in another courtyard it is as if he is in his house, and his view is an error in Rosh.
