Who left his house, etc. Before Shabbos, as the Tur writes. It appears to me that this was stated to teach that even if he returns, he does not prohibit. Alternatively, it specifies before Shabbos because of a non-Jew, but in truth even if he left on Shabbos he does not prohibit, as Magen Avraham writes. In my humble opinion, the Tur discusses the ordinary case, where we require that he leave before Shabbos. But if one asks him and he states that he does not intend to return, then even if he leaves on Shabbos he does not prohibit. This is the case discussed by the Shulchan Aruch and Levush. Avodat HaKodesh, p. 23, writes similarly.
In another courtyard, etc. In the Remezim to Perek Keitzad Mishtatfin it states: A non-Jew, as long as he is in the city, prohibits; once he leaves, he does not prohibit.
But with a non-Jew, etc. The Taz explains at length that this applies only where there is uncertainty, but if it is known that he would not return on Shabbos and later changed his mind and returned, his law is like that of a Jew. He leaves the words of the Tur requiring study. According to my explanation in seif katan 1, however, the Tur's words are correct, because he discusses the ordinary case. There is also a proof from the Remezim, where the Tur writes that a non-Jew who returned does not prohibit, which otherwise would contradict his words elsewhere.
