(s"k 1) Who left, etc. It appears to me he wrote thus — meaning while it was still day, for even if he went on Shabbat they are permitted, as Magen Avraham wrote in this s"k — that even if he returned he does not restrict, as written in seif 2. And this is specifically when he went while it was still day, for then even if he returned we say "a Shabbat permitted at twilight will not become forbidden again," as Magen Avraham wrote in s"k 3. Not so one who goes on Shabbat — in all cases where he does not return they are permitted; nevertheless when he returns we do not say "once permitted, permitted," etc., since at twilight they were forbidden. Alternatively he wrote "while it was still day" because of the non-Jew — meaning to include that regarding a non-Jew, even if he went while it was still day, he restricts in the case of death in seif 4.
