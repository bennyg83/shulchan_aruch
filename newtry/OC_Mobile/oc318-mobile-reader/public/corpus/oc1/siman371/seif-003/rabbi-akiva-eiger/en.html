Magen Avraham s"k 5 — specifically when he was. And this is strained, for he was compelled to divert his attention since his benefactor was alive, and he would not cease diverting his attention merely because he went away. But in truth Magen Avraham's difficulty from the outset is not understood — for what of it that he diverted? Now that he came he should restrict; rather, diverting his attention helps by the rule "once permitted, permitted," as Magen Avraham wrote in s"k 3. And here at the beginning of Shabbat in any case it was forbidden due to the courtyard resident who did not make an eruv; therefore now that the inheritor came to live he restricts. However at the end of the seif, where he died while it was still day and there was no prohibition at the beginning of Shabbat in this case, it must be as Magen Avraham's words, and there it works out well since he had already inherited he did not divert his attention.
