(3) There — who left his house and went, etc., while it was still day (Tur). It appears to me he wrote thus to inform us that even if he returned on Shabbat he does not restrict; alternatively he wrote "while it was still day" because of the non-Jew — if he returned on Shabbat he restricts, as written in siman 383. But in truth even if he was here and did not make an eruv and went on Shabbat he does not restrict, as written in seif 4 regarding one who died on Shabbat and did not make an eruv (Magen Avraham s"k 1). In my humble opinion it appears Tur deals with an unspecified case, and then we require "while it was still day"; but when they ask him and he says he does not intend to return, even if he went on Shabbat he does not restrict — and thus Shulchan Aruch and Levush deal with this case; and in this manner Avodat HaKodesh wrote on daf 23 (Eliyah Rabbah sign 1). However what Eliyah Rabbah and Avodat HaKodesh wrote — that even if he went on Shabbat he does not restrict like seif 4 — see in Sefer Beit Meir who wrote to distinguish between there and here; and likewise in Sefer Gaon Yaakov on tractate Eruvin that Tur speaks specifically of "while it was still day," and he also wrote to distinguish between there and here — see there. And likewise Rashi (on daf 47a) that it deals with one who went while it was still day; and likewise Rashba and Ritva that it deals specifically with "while it was still day" — see there. Therefore one should be stringent.
