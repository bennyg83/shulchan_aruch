(26) A Jew and a convert who were living, etc. — it deals with when they made an eruv together; therefore he does not restrict except when the convert died while it was still day, for his eruv was nullified.

(27) And another Jew took possession of his property — for if that Jew lived there in all respects it is permitted, for he remains alone in his courtyard; and the same when no person at all took possession in this courtyard this Shabbat — it is permitted, for it has no owners.

(28) Even if he did not take possession, etc. — meaning: although at the entry of Shabbat all this time this Jew did not enter the convert's house, it was a residence without owners and did not restrict; nevertheless we do not say regarding this Shabbat "once permitted, permitted," since it was fit to take possession in it while it was still day and the permitted status was suspended and standing — it was not clear it would be permitted the entire Shabbat.

(29) Until nightfall — meaning he also entered to live in it.

(30) Like any other inheritor — meaning we consider the one who acquires the convert's property like an inheritor of his father's property, that it helps when he renounces his domain, as stated in siman 381 seif 6 — see there.

(31) After nightfall the one who took possession does not restrict, etc. — meaning: since at the entry of Shabbat it was permitted due to the convert's eruv, even though when he died his eruv was nullified, we say "once permitted, permitted."
