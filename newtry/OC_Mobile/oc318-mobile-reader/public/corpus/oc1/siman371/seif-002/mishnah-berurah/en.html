(8) He does not restrict — for since at the time he went he did not intend to return on Shabbat, he already diverted his residence from his heart and it is a Shabbat that was permitted and will not become forbidden again. But regarding a non-Jew we rule that if he came on Shabbat he restricts, for it does not apply regarding a non-Jew to say he already diverted his attention — this applies only to a Jew, for in the place where he spends Shabbat at Shabbat's entry, which is his day of rest, presumably his intent then was not to uproot; and what came afterward was a different spirit for him. Not so regarding a non-Jew: when he comes his end proves his beginning, and we say that already at twilight his intent was to come; and even if he says he diverted his attention from the outset, we do not live by his mouth.
