(1) Who died on Shabbat, etc. — know that according to what Magen Avraham wrote in s"k 8, therefore it deals here that at the entry of Shabbat he was not yet in a dying state — for otherwise the eruv would not take effect at all since it is not fit to remain valid the entire Shabbat. However in Sefer Atzei Almogim he disagrees on Magen Avraham's words there, as I will quote there; therefore Shulchan Aruch here deals in all respects.

(2) As long as he does not come, etc., he does not restrict — see Mishna Berurah. And behold the author ruled without comment like Rosh and Tosafos; but this law is not clear, for Rashba in Avkat Rochel and likewise in his novellae disagrees on this and holds that since it was forbidden for part of Shabbat it is forbidden for the entire Shabbat; and Rabbeinu Yerucham brought it; and likewise Ritva ruled in the sugya of "what is the law of an inheritor who nullifies," see there [and see in their novellae that they brought regarding the matter of "went to spend Shabbat at his daughter's" mentioned at the beginning of the seif — it deals specifically when he went while it was still day; but if he went on Shabbat, even if he does not intend to return on Shabbat we say "once forbidden, forbidden"; and it is implied from their words that this depends on that, like Magen Avraham's view in s"k 1; and if so Rashi too who explained (on daf 47) that it deals with one who went to spend Shabbat while it was still day — he holds also in our matter like Rashba and Ritva, not like Tosafos and Rosh. However according to what we wrote at the beginning of the seif in the name of Beit Meir and Gaon Yaakov to distinguish here — there is no necessity that Rashi holds like Rashba and Ritva]. See Beur HaGra who also raised this, and it appears his view inclines to Rashba's view; and for practice it requires study.

(3) And provided the inheritor comes, etc. — the later authorities questioned: behold since at the beginning of Shabbat the residence was without owners and did not restrict the courtyard residents on the residence, why do we not say "once permitted, permitted" like that of seif 2? And one may say: specifically in seif 2, where he was here and went away while it was still day, we say he diverted his attention to return that day; therefore we say regarding him that even if it happened he returned and came, "once permitted, permitted." Not so here — we do not say at all that the inheritor diverted his attention at the time of the beginning of Shabbat's entry not to come at all on Shabbat to live in the house [Maggid Mishneh and novellae of Ra'ah — see there].
