(1) Seif 4 — one from, etc. Here are five cases. In the first case, (5) is the law of the baraisa there "one of the courtyard," etc. In the second case "and if not," etc., is the question there "what is the law of an inheritor who nullifies" — and thus it deals with such a case that he died on Shabbat, for otherwise he should have made an eruv from yesterday; and it is also difficult from the baraisa mentioned. See Rashi there s.v. "inheritor," etc., and what is written "if an outsider inherits," etc. — all this, etc., meaning R' Shimon who left his house, and as explained even if he went on Shabbat, as above. In the third case "and if one inherited," etc., "and was living," etc., is the mishnah of five groups who spent Shabbat in one inn, etc.; see Rosh there. In the fourth case "and if he was not living," etc. — meaning: even though he did not come to live in the benefactor's house, since he lives in that courtyard it is the law of the baraisa there; and "one from the marketplace who died, etc., after nightfall restricts" — Rashi and Tosafos wrote there that the first part of the baraisa "one of the courtyard," etc., goes according to the first tanna of R' Yehuda there ch. 5; but here nothing is written; and likewise in Shulchan Aruch seif 3 it is written "and if he died after nightfall," etc., and it is not written "that he came to live."

(2) As long as he does not, etc. — thus Rosh. But Maggid Mishneh in the name of Rashba disagrees on this and Beit Yosef brings him; and as written above "whatever was forbidden for part of Shabbat," etc.; and Rosh's reason is because we rule like R' Shimon even if he left his house, etc., as written in seif 1, and he explains even if he went on Shabbat. But Rashba explains specifically "while it was still day"; and likewise Tur there; and Magen Avraham s"k 1 strained himself in Tur's words — see there.

(3) And provided he comes. As above.
