(15) One of the courtyard, etc. — and the same applies if the deceased was one from the marketplace; it also depends on whether he made an eruv or not; but the usual manner of speech is that when one lives in the marketplace he does not make an eruv on the house he has in the courtyard — therefore the author wrote "one of the courtyard who died," etc.
(16) Who died on Shabbat — meaning he did not leave members of his household in the house; if so, since he died his eruv was nullified and one of the marketplace inherited and came to live there on Shabbat — and behold he did not leave an eruv while it was still day — nevertheless he does not restrict the courtyard residents, since it was permitted at twilight through the deceased's eruv, it is permitted for the entire Shabbat.
(17) And if he did not make an eruv — this too deals with one who has no members of his household there.
(18) He does not restrict — although at the beginning of Shabbat it was forbidden due to the deceased who did not make an eruv with the courtyard residents; nevertheless when he died and the house remained without residents it is not considered to restrict.
(19) He restricts — since there are now owners for the residence, he restricts the courtyard residents until he renounces his domain, as will be explained in siman 380.
(20) Even though the deceased did not make an eruv — the members of a person's household make an eruv for him without his knowledge.
(21) And if, etc., with the deceased — although even now he did not come to live in the house he inherited; nevertheless, since he has a house in that courtyard in which he lives and it is fitting for him to use that inherited house too, we consider it as though he entered to live in it, and it is a residence with owners.
(22) Even though the inheritor made an eruv — for that house in which he lives.
(23) And if he died while it was still day — here he did not distinguish between made an eruv and did not make an eruv, for even if he made an eruv, since he died while it was still day his eruv was nullified and it is as though he did not make an eruv.
(24) And one of the marketplace inherited — for if one of the courtyard residents inherited, the eruv he places for the house in which he lives applies also to this house.
(25) And provided he comes, etc., to live in it — and it is a residence with owners, and behold he did not make an eruv.
