1) [Se'if 1] It is a mitzvah to increase, etc. Rosh Chodesh is compared to an appointed festival, as it is written, 'On the day of your rejoicing, on your appointed festivals, and on your Rosh Chodesh days.' It is also stated in the Pesikta that all of a person's food expenses are fixed from one Rosh Hashanah to the next, except what the person spends for Shabbos, Yom Tov, Rosh Chodesh, Chol HaMoed, and what children bring to their teacher. If the person increases these expenditures, the allocation is increased; if the person reduces them, it is reduced. Tur. Regarding the phrase, 'what children bring to their teacher,' Beis Yosef explains that it means the teacher's wages. Bach explains that it refers to the customary Rosh Chodesh money given to their teacher, and writes that this custom should not be abolished. Whether one does much, etc.
