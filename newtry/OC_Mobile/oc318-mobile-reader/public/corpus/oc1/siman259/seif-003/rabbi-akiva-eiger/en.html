Ibid.: “Even when cold, it has the same law as when hot.” Magen Avraham’s view is evidently that whenever there is no concern of cooking, namely when the food has not cooled completely or when it is dry, it may be placed on the oven before it is fired, or after it is fired if a brick intervenes. In truth, however, this requires further study in practical halacha. The principal basis for permitting the oven’s plaster covering, or an intervening brick, is that it is considered like grufa u’ketumah. Yet above, in siman 253, Magen Avraham s.k. 36 explains that once the food has cooled completely, the first act has ceased and the placement is considered an initial placement; grufa u’ketumah helps only for returning. For this reason, one can argue that even placing it beside the oven is forbidden, because Rama at the end of siman 253 permits placing nearby only under the conditions of returning. The sole additional leniency is that nearby placement is permitted even when the stove is not grufa. Thus, when the food has cooled and is no longer considered returned, even nearby placement should be forbidden. See there. This requires further study.
