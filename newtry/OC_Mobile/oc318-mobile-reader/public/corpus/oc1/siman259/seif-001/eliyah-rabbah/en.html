[Levush] They are important, etc. Shulchan Aruch writes this based on Beit Yosef's understanding that Rashba follows Tosafot, the Rosh, and Tur that wool stuffing is more important than shearings; Rabbenu Yerucham and Shiltei Giborim also write so. I am surprised, because Tosafot on Shabbos 48 clearly imply the opposite, that wool shearings are more important than stuffing, and Piskei Tosafot states so. Tosafot's own conclusion appears to make no distinction after Abaye's answer: whether stuffing or shearings, if not placed into storage they may be moved, but if placed into storage they require designation. There it is clear that Rashba agrees, and Maggid Mishneh, chapter 6, also implies this. The Rosh and Ran ultimately write similarly. Proof may be brought from Baal Halachot Gedolot at the beginning of Bameh Tomnin, who says that stuffing in which food was insulated may be moved. At first glance this contradicts the Gemara, but it can be explained as ordinary stuffing not placed into storage, as I have explained. This is the primary ruling.
It is forbidden to move them, etc. The Taz asks why this differs from siman 309, regarding a stone upon the mouth of a barrel, where, if one needs the place occupied by the barrel, one may move the barrel; see his lengthy discussion there. In my humble opinion, that case is different because he moves the permitted object, namely the barrel, and the stone is merely carried along with it. Here, however, he touches and moves the forbidden object itself. His difficulty from Tosafos at the end of Bameh Tomnin is also not difficult, because we rule like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, as is implied in Shabbos 142b and by all the poskim.
Some of them are exposed, etc. If none of them are exposed, it appears to me that if one can insert a spindle and shake them off, it is permitted. Magen Avraham. He overlooked the words of the Mordechai in the chapter Bameh Madlikin: “But one may not touch it and remove it, since it is possible to do so with a spindle,” etc. This implies that when it is impossible, one may even touch it. This requires further study.
He designated them for this purpose, etc. This means that he designated them permanently. See what I wrote in siman 308, se’if 22. Magen Avraham. The Levush in se’if 2, however, indicates that designation for one Shabbos is sufficient. Nachalas Tzvi rules likewise, and this is also implied by the Tur and by the Shiltei Gibborim in the chapter Bameh Tomnin. The Bach is even more lenient: if one insulated with them for another Shabbos as well, they are permitted even without explicit designation. Rashi on Shabbos 50a, however, explains as stated above.
It is permitted to move them, etc. Once one has insulated with them even a single time, they may be moved forever. According to my conclusion, the same applies to stuffing.
For commerce, etc. This applies specifically when he is particular about them, as stated in siman 308, at the end of se’if 1 and in se’if 25. Magen Avraham. It is possible to distinguish that only utensils require such particularity. This requires further study.
Levush: And even, etc. According to the view of Sefer HaTerumos in siman 309, se’if 4, there is no difficulty, since he placed it there with the intention of removing it on Shabbos, as the Beit Yosef writes there. Rather, this statement follows the first opinion there. One should not ask why it is permitted in the case of the oven, since the seal is not made to remain. It appears to me that there all opinions agree that this is not considered demolition. The Beit Yosef at the end of this siman refers to the law of the coals; see there. This is clear, contrary to the Taz, who was not precise in this matter.
