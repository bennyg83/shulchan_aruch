1) [Seif 1] Soft material in which one insulated food on an incidental basis may not be moved, etc. This is because it was set aside for labor and may not be moved. Beis Yosef; Magen Avraham, paragraph 1. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1, however, writes that there is no distinction between soft material and wool shearings. Whenever one did not place them in storage, if he insulated food in them, they may be moved; see there. Erech HaShulchan, paragraph 1, similarly writes in the name of several halachic authorities that there is no distinction between soft material and wool shearings, and once one has insulated food in them, they may be moved; see there. Bach writes that soft material used incidentally for insulation need not be expressly designated for that purpose permanently. Only on the first Shabbos that he incidentally insulates food in it may it not be moved unless he designated it before Shabbos. If he uses it again to insulate food on another Shabbos, it may be moved, because his repeated use on another Shabbos is regarded as though he expressly designated it for insulation; see there. See below, paragraph 5.
2) There: Rather, he shakes off the cover, etc. This is because the cover has the status of a utensil. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 3.
3) There: Provided that part of it is exposed, etc. He grasps the cover at its exposed portion and removes it, causing the insulating material to fall away on its own. This is only indirect movement, which was permitted even for muktzeh. Levush.
3) There: Provided that part of it is exposed, etc. If no part is exposed, but he can insert a knife and shake it off, it is permitted, as stated below in siman 311, seif 9. Magen Avraham, paragraph 5; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 3; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 7. Similarly, if he can shake the cover so that the insulating material falls away, it is permitted even if the cover is entirely covered. Tur and the Shulchan Aruch discuss a case in which it cannot be shaken unless part of it is exposed. Nehar Shalom, paragraph 1.
4) There: If he designated it for this purpose, etc. Rashi explains that once he designates it for insulation, it acquires the status of a utensil and may be moved on Shabbos. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 1. The phrase 'designated it' implies that he must expressly say so and that thought alone is insufficient. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 2. Mishnah Berurah, paragraph 5, however, writes that thought is sufficient; see there. See below, siman 308, seif 20.
5) There: If he designated it for this purpose, etc. Magen Avraham, paragraph 2, writes that this means he designated it permanently. Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 2, however, writes that since the word 'permanently' is not stated here as it is in seif 2, it implies that permanent designation is unnecessary here, because this material is not inherently muktzeh like stones. This is also demonstrated by Maran Beis Yosef's precise wording and implied by Levush, contrary to Magen Avraham; see there. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 2, similarly writes that Levush, seif 2, and Nezer Yisrael, paragraph 2, make clear that for soft material, designation before Shabbos for this Shabbos alone suffices; see there. Accordingly, since the matter is disputed and several halachic authorities maintain that using it once for insulation suffices and permits moving it just like wool shearings, as stated above in paragraph 1, it appears that one may be lenient as a matter of practical halachah. Whenever he designates it for insulation, even if not permanently but only for one Shabbos, it may be moved.
6) There: But if he insulated food in wool shearings, etc. This applies specifically if he insulated food in them. If he did not, they may not be moved because they are muktzeh. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 1.
7) There: But if he insulated food in wool shearings, etc. This is because they are not as valuable as soft material. Beis Yosef and Magen Avraham, paragraph 3. This follows Maran and those who agree with him. According to several halachic authorities, however, they are equivalent, as stated above in paragraph 1; see there.
