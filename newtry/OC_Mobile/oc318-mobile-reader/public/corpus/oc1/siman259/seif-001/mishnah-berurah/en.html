(1) Wadding. Meaning dry wadding, as stated in siman 257, seif 3. (2) On an occasional basis. This excludes one who regularly uses it for insulation, in which case it loses its status of muktzeh even without an explicit designation. Bach holds that even doing so twice is sufficient. (3) It is forbidden to move them. Ordinarily they are valuable and a person sets them aside because they are intended for making felt. They are therefore muktzeh machmat chisaron kis, since he is particular not to use them for other purposes. Consequently, even if he insulated with them only on an occasional basis, they remain set aside and do not become subordinate to the insulation. (4) Some of them are exposed. This leaves a free place by which to grasp the cover, so that he will not have to move the wadding directly. (5) If he designated them for this. Meaning, he designated them before Shabbos permanently for insulation. Even designation by thought alone, without verbalizing it, is sufficient. (6) But if he insulated, etc. Although ordinary wool shearings are muktzeh because they are intended for spinning and weaving, they are not as important as wadding. Once he insulated with them, it is as though he designated them for this purpose. (7) For merchandise. Since he holds them as merchandise, he presumably intends to return them afterward to storage. He therefore does not subordinate them to the insulation, and they remain muktzeh unless he designated them for insulation before Shabbos. (8) That he takes the cover, etc. This refers to a case where some of it is exposed, as explained above regarding wadding. If none of it is exposed, he may insert a spindle and shake off the cover, as stated below in siman 311, seif 9. (9) For it did not become a base. The cover does not serve the wool. On the contrary, the wool serves the pot by warming it.
