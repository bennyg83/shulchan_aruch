It is permitted through a non-Jew. Through a Jew, however, it is forbidden because it is a psik reisha. This is stated by the Agur and in the responsa of Maharil, siman 62. Terumas HaDeshen implies that it is likewise forbidden to open the oven when it is known that glowing coals are inside, because the wind fans the coals, as stated in siman 277, se'if 2. Terumas HaDeshen writes that some forbid sealing the oven because the wind blows through a small opening, resembling bellows, and causes the oven to burn more strongly than if it were fully open. He inclines to permit it, and the Rama likewise cites him permissively in his responsa, siman 102. The Agur and Maharil write that if the Jew does not seal it completely, perhaps it is permitted. Their meaning is that he leaves a substantial opening, so that it is as though the oven had remained open from the outset. If he leaves only a small opening, however, it is obvious that the wind fans the fire, and according to their view it is forbidden. This is how we rule. It appears to me that when a fire is burning inside, even Terumas HaDeshen agrees that it is forbidden to seal the oven completely, because this causes extinguishing by making the fire die more quickly. This is not comparable to siman 277, se'if 2, because there is ample air in the house, whereas there is not ample air inside an oven.
Because it causes cooking. Even after the fact, the food is forbidden, as stated in the responsa of the Rama, siman 102. See siman 257, se'if 1, in the gloss. Where a non-Jew did it, there is room to question whether the food may be permitted after the fact, since it would in any event have cooked there. The Shelah writes that even if night has not yet fallen, although strictly speaking one may instruct a non-Jew, one should nevertheless be careful because the Sages did not distinguish among cases. See there, and see what I wrote in siman 261, at the end of se'if 1. The laws of shehiyah and hatmanah in brief are as follows. In our times we generally have no true hatmanah, since the pot remains uncovered above. According to the Beit Yosef, however, the pot must not touch the coals. The case is therefore governed by the laws of shehiyah. Meat must either be placed there raw immediately before nightfall or have reached ma'achal ben Derusai before nightfall. For roast meat, cake, and pie, placing them there raw does not suffice, and they must reach ma'achal ben Derusai before Shabbos. In an oven sealed with clay, everything is permitted. If glowing coals are inside, the oven may be opened only by a non-Jew. If the coals surround the pot, only a non-Jew should remove the pot. If the pot stands directly on the coals, it may be removed by a Jew when there is no other way. The same applies on Friday night. It is permitted to place hot food next to the oven even after the oven has been fired. The common practice is to permit placing it on top of the oven if the oven has not yet been fired. If a brick or another object was placed on the oven, it is permitted to place the pot upon it even after the oven has been fired. Cold food, however, may not even be placed next to the oven before it is fired, except through a non-Jew. After it has been fired, it is forbidden even through a non-Jew, unless needed for a sick person or for a child who has nothing to eat. A food without liquid has the same status when cold as when hot. If a food containing liquid has not cooled completely, the common practice is to be lenient. If it is placed where it cannot become heated to yad soledes bo, it may be placed next to the oven, but not on top of it unless there is an intervening object. The same applies inside a tiled stove, where an intervening object is required. On the ledge surrounding the oven, however, it is considered merely placing it alongside. The same laws apply to beverages and fruit, and likewise to placing something upon a pot fixed in the oven. It is plainly forbidden to place a pitcher of liquid inside such a pot. A Jew may not pour water into that pot even before the oven is fired. If a non-Jew placed the water there before the oven was fired, one may benefit from it. If a Jew placed it there on erev Shabbos close to nightfall, it is likewise forbidden, as stated at the end of siman 254. On Shabbos one may not wrap a pot of hot food in pillows and cushions. If, however, he transferred the food to another pot, he may wrap that second pot.
