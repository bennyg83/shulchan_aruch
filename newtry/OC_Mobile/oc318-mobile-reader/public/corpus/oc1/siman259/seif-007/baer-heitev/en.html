Cooking. Even after the fact, the cooked food is forbidden. If this was done by a non-Jew, there is room to question whether it may be permitted after the fact; see Responsa Rama, siman 102, and above, siman 253, s.k. 11. The Shelah writes that if night has not yet fallen, although strictly speaking it is permitted to instruct a non-Jew, one should nevertheless be careful because the Sages did not distinguish between cases. See siman 261, se’if 1. A concise summary of the laws of shehiyah and hatmanah. The general rule is that nowadays we do not have actual hatmanah, since the pot is uncovered on top. According to the Beit Yosef, however, the pot must not touch the coals, and the case then has the law of shehiyah. Meat must be placed raw immediately before nightfall, or else it must have reached ma’achal ben Derusai before dark. With roasted food, cake, and pastry, placing them raw does not help; they must reach ma’achal ben Derusai before Shabbos. In an oven sealed with clay, everything is permitted. If there are glowing coals, the oven may be opened only by a non-Jew. If the coals surround the pot, a Jew should not remove the pot; a non-Jew should do so. If the pot stands on the coals, a Jew may remove it when no other method is possible. The same applies on Friday night. One may place hot cooked food near the oven even if it has already been fired. The custom is to permit placing it on top of the oven if the oven has not yet been fired. If one places a brick or another object on the oven and then places the pot upon it, this is permitted even after the oven has been fired. A completely cold cooked dish is forbidden even before the oven is fired, even merely to place it nearby, unless a non-Jew does so before it is fired. After it has been fired, even a non-Jew may not do so, except for a sick person or a child who has nothing to eat. Food without liquid has the same law when cold as when hot. If a food containing liquid has not cooled completely, the custom is to be lenient. If it is placed where it cannot become heated to yad soledes bo, it may be placed near the oven, but not on top of it unless there is an intervening object. The same applies inside the tiled warming compartment: an intervening object is required. On the ledge surrounding the oven, however, it is considered merely placed nearby. The same laws apply to beverages and fruit, and to the top of a pot fixed in the oven. It is obviously forbidden to place a jug of beverage inside that fixed pot. A Jew may not place water into that pot even before the oven is fired. If a non-Jew puts it in before the oven is fired, one may benefit from it. If a Jew put it there on erev Shabbos close to nightfall, it is also forbidden, as stated at the end of siman 254. It is forbidden on Shabbos to wrap a pot containing hot food in pillows and blankets. If the food was transferred to another pot, however, that second pot may be wrapped.
