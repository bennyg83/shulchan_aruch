The Talmud speaks where the gentile himself, the owner of the field, cuts it, and since it was his from the outset and he intends to steal it, the despair properly takes effect in their hand, and change of domain in yours. In that case, since he himself is the seller, he acts for his own intent and not for the Jew, and therefore the despair is in their hand. But if the Jew hires another gentile to cut, this gentile does not intend to steal it and does not do it for his own intent but only for the Jew. If so, the despair does not take effect in the gentile's hand, but only when it reaches the hand of the Jewish buyer who intends to acquire it; if so, it is still as though the Jew stole it. Regarding the law whether it is permitted to make a sukkah in the public domain, I wrote to permit in Sefer HaChayim. Now I found a clear proof to this from the words of the Yerushalmi and the Rashba in his novellae to Bava Kamma at the end of chapter HaKones. This is his language there: R. Yochanan says that regarding a Chanukah lamp he is exempt because it is with permission, meaning with permission of mitzvah, as we say in chapter HaManiach Et HaKad regarding one who puts out his flax. However, not everyone who puts something out with permission of mitzvah is exempt, for if he made a mitzvah sukkah at the entrance of his house outside and people were damaged by it, he is liable, as stated in the Yerushalmi, such as those who make a sukkah at the entrance of their shops on the festival. But with Chanukah he is exempt, because there is a mitzvah to place it at the entrance of his house because of publicizing the miracle. End quote. His intent is clear: specifically with Chanukah, where it is specifically a mitzvah to place it outside, he is exempt. But with sukkah, since it is not specifically a mitzvah that it be outside, he is liable, even though in any event it is a valid sukkah and a mitzvah, for otherwise it is obvious he is liable. Perforce it is a valid sukkah. And what obligates him must be where he made it in the public domain, for if it were in his own domain he would be exempt for all damages. The main law of damages is where he did something in the public domain, as is proven in Bava Kamma in several places. Perforce it speaks where he made the sukkah in the public domain, and it is proven that it is a mitzvah and the sukkah is valid. This is clear and correct.
