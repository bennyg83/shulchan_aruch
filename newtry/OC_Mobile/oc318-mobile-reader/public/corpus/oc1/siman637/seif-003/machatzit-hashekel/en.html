Specifically from a Jew. This refers to what Rav Huna said there to the lulav merchants, and to all the items needed for it: when you buy myrtles from gentiles while attached, have the gentile cut them, for ordinary gentiles are robbers of land. Perhaps that land is stolen, and land is not stolen; the myrtles are acquired only at the time of cutting, when they become detached and subject to theft. Therefore the gentile should cut them, so that there is owner-despair in the gentile's hand and afterward a change of domain in your hand. Rashi explained: ordinary gentiles are robbers of land, meaning they rob land from Jews, and perhaps that land belonged to a Jew. End quote. From this language it seems at first glance that if they stole from a gentile it is permitted. If so, the custom of making a sukkah in the public domain would be resolved, for Jews forgive, and regarding gentiles we do not care even if they do not forgive.
And although we rule, etc. With this he comes to reject that answer. In truth, the language of the Magen Avraham is somewhat unclear here. The intent is that there is no proof from Rashi for the custom, because we rule that theft from a gentile is forbidden. Rather, Rashi's intent is that with a gentile there is no issue of a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. The reason may be that Rashi follows his own view in chapter 4 of Sanhedrin, that theft from a gentile is forbidden only rabbinically, as the Shach wrote in Choshen Mishpat siman 359, small paragraph 2, because of desecration of the Name. See Even HaEzer siman 28, Beit Shmuel small paragraph 5. Its prohibition is weak and is not called a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. According to this, since with a gentile there is no mitzvah that comes through a transgression, Rashi needed to say that they stole from a Jew in order to resolve Rav Huna's words according to both explanations Rashi wrote there regarding Rav Huna's statement that there should be owner-despair in the gentile's hand and change of domain in your hand. Rashi wrote: He holds that despair alone does not acquire, but despair and change of domain acquire. Alternatively, even if it does acquire, it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. End quote. Rashi wanted to resolve the matter even if we say Rav Huna holds despair alone acquires and the prohibition is only because of a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. Since with a gentile there is no mitzvah that comes through a transgression, as above, he needed to say that they stole from a Jew. This is enough to resolve Rashi's words, but the common custom is not resolved by this. For us, besides the fact that the Rambam's view is that theft from a gentile is Torah-forbidden, so according to him with a gentile too there is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, even if you say they relied on Rashi's view above and there is no mitzvah that comes through a transgression, there is nevertheless a prohibition of theft. We also rule that despair alone does not acquire. Even aside from this, although land is not stolen, it is nevertheless forbidden at the outset, as the Rema wrote in the gloss, since it is not acquired; this is because land is not stolen. Therefore it is not a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, since the transgression was not completed.
As written at the beginning of siman 649: if one bought a stolen lulav or the other three species without the assistance of the mitzvah, it is not called a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. Nevertheless, one should not bless over it, for this is not blessing but blaspheming. That law derives from the words of Tosafot at the beginning of chapter 3 of Sukkah and in other places that the Magen Avraham brought here, who wrote here that where one acquired an item without the assistance of the mitzvah, it is not considered a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. If so, there is a difficulty from what we say in tractate Bava Kamma: one who stole a se'ah of wheat, ground it, and baked it, although change acquires and he acquired it through a physical change before separating challah, nevertheless if he blesses over separating its challah, this is not blessing but blaspheming. Why? In such a case it is not a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. Tosafot answered in one answer: blessing is different, because it also contains mention of the Name of Heaven. End quote.
