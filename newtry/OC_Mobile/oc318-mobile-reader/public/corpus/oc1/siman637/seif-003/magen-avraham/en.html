And likewise on land, etc. This requires examination, for some are accustomed to make a sukkah in the public domain. If so, it is a stolen sukkah. One cannot say that the people of the city forgive them, for everyone in the world has a share in the public domain (see Choshen Mishpat siman 162, seif 2). And even if you say that all Jews forgive, nevertheless gentiles also have a share in it. From Rashi's language in Sukkah 30b it implies that it is forbidden specifically from a Jew, but from a gentile it is permitted. Although we rule that theft from a gentile is forbidden, nevertheless it is not called a mitzvah that comes through a transgression; so wrote Shiltei HaGibborim and Rabbenu Yerucham. But in Sefer Yere'im he wrote that even according to the one who says theft from a gentile is permitted, nevertheless it is not called "yours." In Responsa Rashba, siman 852, he wrote that Rashi's wording "from a Jew" is not specific, for theft by a gentile from a gentile is forbidden, and a Jew who steals from a gentile is also invalid. So too wrote the Beit Yosef at the end of siman 664. If so, it is forbidden to make a sukkah in the public domain, for gentiles do not forgive this. See what I wrote in siman 640, seif 2. Although after the fact it is valid, nevertheless it seems to me that one should not recite a blessing over it. Even though it does not involve a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, since it is not acquired at all, as the Ran and Rabbenu Yerucham wrote, nevertheless one should not bless over it, as written at the beginning of siman 649, and so it appears from Tosafot at the beginning of chapter 3 of Sukkah. If so, it would be a blessing in vain.

He has not fulfilled with it. For it does not have the status of a borrowed sukkah, since it does not stand in Reuven's domain. This is not like land, which remains in the possession of its owners, and therefore land is not stolen. End quote of Tosafot. In Hagahot Ashiri it says "without his knowledge"; the Bach wrote that this is how it should read in Tosafot, and so wrote R. Shmuel Eidels, and so wrote the Darchei Moshe. But if Shimon lent him the land for the seven days of the festival and afterward removed him from his sukkah, the wood stands in Reuven's domain and is not stolen, and it is like a borrowed sukkah in Shimon's hand (Bach). From the language of the Rema, who omitted the words "without his knowledge," it implies that he holds the same applies even if he made it with Shimon's knowledge, as is found in the old texts of Tosafot, for in any case the land does not stand in Reuven's domain; it is only borrowed for seven days and does not stand in Reuven's possession. This is difficult, for although land is not stolen, the wood is stolen; if so, when his fellow removes him from his sukkah it is invalid, since the enactment for repentant thieves does not apply because he did not toil in it. One must say that what is attached to the ground is like the ground. This is difficult according to the view in Choshen Mishpat siman 95 that a house is not like something attached to the ground; see there in the gloss, and see the Shach there, who answered him at length. All his questions can be rejected, for one can say that since land is acquired by money, document, and possession, the house is acquired with it, for movable items are acquired incidentally to land; and the same applies to the other matters; see there, for this is not the place. But from here it is difficult. One can say that since he did not acquire the land, he also did not acquire the wood, for he did not pull it. So it is explicit in Bava Kamma at the end of 117, that according to the rabbis who hold land is not stolen, even movable items lying within it are not stolen; and according to R. Eliezer, the land is stolen and the movable items are acquired together with the land. Now one can say that this is why Hagahot Ashiri specified "without his knowledge": if he built with his knowledge, the land is Reuven's, and land is not stolen, so he also did not acquire the wood. But since he built without his knowledge, Shimon stole only the wood, which is movable property; and what can be dismantled is considered as if already dismantled. See Choshen Mishpat 95 regarding anything standing to be cut down. If so, it is theft, and the enactment for repentant thieves does not apply, since he did not toil in it. However, in Choshen Mishpat siman 313, when land is lent to others, it acquires the movable items upon it. See Yoreh De'ah siman 221, seif 7.
