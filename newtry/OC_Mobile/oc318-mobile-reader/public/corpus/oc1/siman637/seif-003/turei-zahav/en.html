And likewise Jews should not cut, etc. It seems to me that in a place where the people of the place have permission to take wood from the forest by the law of the kingdom, there is no prohibition if the Jew takes from there for roofing. It resembles what is stated in the final chapter of HaGozel, that they cut palm trees, made bridges from them, and crossed over them. See what I wrote in siman 664 about this.
But if he stole a sukkah, etc. For then it itself is stolen, since it is not attached to the ground. He is not obligated to return money, but to return it as it is built, for the enactment for repentant thieves does not apply here, since he did not toil to build it and did not spend expenses on it; therefore it is stolen (Rashi). It seems obvious to me that it is permitted for a person to enter his fellow's sukkah without his knowledge, for a person is pleased that a mitzvah be done with his property. This is a fortiori from what is stated in siman 649, that it is permitted for a person to take his fellow's lulav without his knowledge, for a person is certainly more particular about his etrog and lulav than about someone entering his sukkah. However, it seems to me that this applies only as long as the owner of the sukkah is not there. But when he is there, perhaps he objects, lest his fellow see his affairs and eating without his knowledge. This is agreed by reasoning, as appears to me.
