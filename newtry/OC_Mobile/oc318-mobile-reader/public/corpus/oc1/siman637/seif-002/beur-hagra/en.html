Seif 2: And likewise one fulfills, etc. This follows a fortiori from a borrowed sukkah, and all the more so here, where he has a share in it and the remainder is borrowed to him, for they became partners with that understanding. A lender can prohibit use, as written regarding one who rents in Arachin 21, but a partner cannot prohibit, for we rule like R. Eliezer ben Yaakov in Nedarim 47a. See Rashi, s.v. "all the native-born," etc.: meaning that it is impossible that all of them would be partners, and nevertheless the verse includes both partners and a borrowed sukkah. Therefore they said "yours" to exclude a stolen one; and it is not difficult that it should exclude partnership. With tzitzit, although borrowed items are excluded, nevertheless partnership is obligated. See Rivash, siman 344.
