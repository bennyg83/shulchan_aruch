In Magen Avraham, small paragraph 3: "And although we rule, etc., nevertheless it is not called, etc." Meaning: this is proven, for otherwise what forced Rashi to explain "robbers of land" as from Jews? Perhaps it follows the view that it is forbidden, and so Tosafot in Bava Kamma indeed hold. About this he wrote that Sefer Yere'im states that even if it is permitted, it is not called "yours." If so, it is impossible to explain that Rashi meant this specifically, since even if they stole from gentiles he would not fulfill the obligation, for it is not called "yours," even if theft from a gentile is permitted, and all the more so if we say it is forbidden. Perforce, Rashi's wording "from Jews" is not specific, as written in Responsa Rashba, siman 852. If so, automatically one may say that even where we do not require "yours," such as here with sukkah, where a borrowed sukkah is valid, nevertheless since theft from a gentile is forbidden, it is properly considered a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. If so, it is forbidden to make a sukkah in the public domain. See my Responsa Beit Ephraim, etc.
