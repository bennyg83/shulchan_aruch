One fulfills with a borrowed sukkah. Although with an etrog it is invalid, since it is written "and you shall take for yourselves," meaning from your own, and not stolen or borrowed, and here too it is written "you shall make for yourself," nevertheless we exclude only a stolen one. Here is different, because it is written, "all the native-born in Israel shall dwell in sukkot," teaching that all Israel are fit to sit in one sukkah. Rashi explained: this is impossible for it to belong to all of them, for not even the value of a perutah would reach each one, except by way of borrowing. See what I wrote, a major difficulty, in the laws of tzitzit in siman 14, against the Rosh, who permits a borrowed garment for tzitzit.
