One makes a sukkah on Chol HaMoed - I saw a novel matter in the book Ma'amar Mordechai, and this is his language: It is clear that it speaks where he does not need to do something forbidden to do on Chol HaMoed because of building and dismantling, but only makes the roofing, placing reeds and willow branches on poles that were already fixed from before Yom Tov, or the like. Although this too is forbidden to do on Yom Tov, for it is forbidden to dismantle or make a temporary tent on Yom Tov, and so it appears below in siman 666 that it is forbidden to diminish the sukkah on the eighth day because it is Yom Tov, and this is because of building and dismantling, nevertheless it is clear that on Chol HaMoed there is no prohibition at all in making a temporary tent, etc. End quote. Although the author wrote "it is clear," in my humble opinion this is not clear at all. The plain sense of the sugya implies that it is permitted to build it anew on Chol HaMoed. So too it is proven from the Rambam's language, for he wrote: even at the end of the seventh day one makes a sukkah, for its mitzvah is all seven days. Not only if the prohibition of labor on Chol HaMoed is rabbinic is it certainly permitted to build it anew; even if the general prohibition is Torah law, here too it is permitted to build it anew, for the mitzvah is all seven days. This is similar to what they said: a person may write tefillin and mezuzot for himself, especially when he needs to put them on, as above in siman 545. So too the language of the Gemara indicates, for it said that they agree that if it fell, he may build it; this implies that the rabbis permit building it even if it did not fall. So too it appears from what they said that according to the rabbis, what is written "the festival of Sukkot you shall make for yourself seven days" means: make a sukkah during the festival. This implies according to its plain meaning that complete making is permitted, as appears clear in my humble opinion.
