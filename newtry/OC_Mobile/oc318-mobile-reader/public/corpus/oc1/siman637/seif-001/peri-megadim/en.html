One who - See Taz. What the Mechaber wrote, "even at the end of the seventh day," and during twilight through a gentile one may do so, as in siman 261: a rabbinic prohibition during twilight, and instructing a gentile, is permitted wherever there is pressing need and an element of mitzvah. Rabbinically there is a mitzvah to sit in the sukkah on the eighth day outside the Land. See Levush, who distinguishes between "seven days" with a tav, septaine in French, according to Rashi's explanation in Chumash, like "three days"; see there, meaning in the general span of the seven days. According to the Taz it is according to its plain meaning, for wherever it can be explained plainly, we explain it plainly, unlike with sukkah, etc. The halachah follows the sages. See Sukkah 23 in the dispute of R. Meir and R. Yehudah regarding a sukkah fit for seven days; it implies that all agree so, as written in the Ran's novellae. One can say that indeed this derashah is literal, that one must make a sukkah fit for seven days, but the sages did not expound from it that one may not make one on Chol HaMoed.
