One who did not make a sukkah. In the Gemara, R. Eliezer and the rabbis disagree. R. Eliezer holds that "the festival of Sukkot you shall make for yourself seven days" means: make a sukkah fit for seven days. Automatically we learn that one may not make a sukkah on Chol HaMoed, for it is not for seven days, and we also learn that one may not leave one sukkah for another, for then it is not for seven days. The rabbis hold that this is what the Merciful One said: make a sukkah during the festival, meaning on whichever day of the seven days you wish, even on the last day, if you did not make one earlier. We rule like the rabbis. Therefore we say that one may make a sukkah on Chol HaMoed, as written here. The Levush objected from what is written, "seven days leaven shall not be found in your houses," which implies that all seven days are included in the prohibition "shall not be found," and he answered what he answered. There is no difficulty at all, for certainly wherever we can explain the verse according to its plain meaning, we do so, as in that case of "shall not be found." This is unlike "the festival of Sukkot," which cannot be explained according to its plain meaning, for we are certainly not commanded to make sukkot all seven days. Likewise, "seven days you shall eat matzot," which stands in place of "you shall not eat chametz" all seven days, is also according to its plain meaning, for there is no obligation except on the first night. Therefore R. Eliezer and the rabbis disagree: R. Eliezer holds that one must make it at one time for seven days specifically, and the rabbis hold that at any time during the seven days you may make a sukkah. This is simple, and he labored there for no reason to answer otherwise.
