Seif 1 One who did not make a sukkah, whether inadvertently or intentionally, etc. Note: Regarding the law if the sukkah became invalid on Yom Tov or on Shabbat, whether it is permitted to repair it through a gentile, in my humble opinion it is certainly forbidden to do so through a gentile because of making a tent. Although afterward I saw in two places in the Peri Megadim that he wrote that one can say that where he has no sukkah in which to eat, it is like preparation for food needs and is permitted, and he noted to see siman 514, meaning that there it is explained that one may sweep the house when he has no place to eat; see there. His words are astonishing. It is understandable there, for if he has no house, he truly will have no place to eat, and therefore this is preparation for food needs. But with sukkah, if he has no place to eat in a sukkah, he is permitted to eat this way without a sukkah. If so, it is not preparation for food needs, since he can eat in any case. Therefore it appears that it is forbidden to build the sukkah on Yom Tov through a gentile. True, according to the Ba'al HaMaor, who holds that dismantling a sukkah is only rabbinic since it is a temporary tent, see there in the sugya of not taking wood from a sukkah, then its building is also only rabbinic. One could again say that through a gentile it is a rabbinic prohibition upon a rabbinic prohibition, which is permitted in a place of mitzvah. However, since this is not agreed upon, one should not desecrate Yom Tov with this, and one should not do it through a gentile, as appears correct in my humble opinion. Examine carefully. Regarding what else requires examination in several matters in this siman, see my work on Orach Chayim from the year 607, in the laws of Eruvin, siman 362, in the sugya of Eruvin 44, what I wrote there. Also regarding the principle that since it is a wall for Shabbat it is a wall for sukkah, explained in siman 632, see there and examine carefully.
