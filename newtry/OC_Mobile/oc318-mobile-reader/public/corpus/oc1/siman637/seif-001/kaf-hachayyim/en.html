1) [Seif 1] One who did not make a sukkah, etc. In the Gemara, Sukkah 27, R' Eliezer and the Sages disagree. R' Eliezer holds that the verse, 'You shall make for yourself the festival of Sukkos for seven days,' means: Make a sukkah fit for seven days. It follows that one may not make a sukkah on Chol HaMoed, since it will not be for seven days; and it follows that one may not go from one sukkah to another, for then it is not for seven days. The Sages hold that this is what the Torah means: Make a sukkah during the festival, meaning on whichever of the seven days you wish, even on the final day if you did not make one beforehand. We rule like the Sages; therefore, we say that one may make a sukkah on Chol HaMoed, as stated here. Taz, s.k. 1. That is to say, the Shulchan Aruch needed to write this in order to state that the halacha follows the Sages. This is also the consensus of the poskim, as explained in the Beit Yosef; see there.
2) Ibid. Whether unintentionally or intentionally. This teaches us that they did not penalize him for this. All the more so, if his sukkah fell on Yom Tov, he may rebuild it on Chol HaMoed, as stated in the Gemara there.
3) Ibid. He makes a sukkah on Chol HaMoed. A convert who converted on Chol HaMoed, and likewise a minor who matured and became bar mitzvah on Chol HaMoed, may make a sukkah on Chol HaMoed, even according to R' Eliezer, as stated in the Gemara there.
