(s.k. 4) Already, etc. For judgment is appointed, and perhaps they examine this man's case and do not find him worthy, and his tekiah is rejected Above, as is written in siman 591 regarding prayer.
There is no concern, as is written in siman 591, since the congregation is also blowing then. And since the Holy One, blessed be He, does not despise the congregation's tekiah, his tekiah too rises with the congregation's tekiah and is not rejected.
See se'if 6, where it is explained that in such a case, where he does not intend to fulfill, there is no prohibition because of causing an unnecessary blessing, as is written in siman 215.
As he wrote in siman 8 regarding tzitzis, that walking to the synagogue is an interruption; [whereas] as is written in siman 592; that even if he conversed between the seated tekios and the tekios in the order of the blessings, even in matters that are not needed for prayer and tekiah, even so it is not an interruption.
And it is unlike tzitzis, which are two mitzvos, etc. And he wrote in Sefer Chokhmat Manoach, and this is his language: this requires further study; how is this different from what the Magen Avraham wrote in siman 639, s.k. 13, regarding sukkah, that walking to the synagogue is an interruption? For is sukkah not also one mitzvah? End quote. And I do not know what difficulty there is: what difference is there between tzitzis and sukkah? For regarding tzitzis as well, that which he said they are two mitzvos means that the second wearing has no connection to the first wearing; and so after one wearing the mitzvah is completed, which is not so here regarding the tekios, for all the tekios, even the standing ones, have a connection to the seated tekios; for according to the Sages' enactment one must blow both seated and standing, and so the mitzvah is not completed until he has heard all the tekios. And so sukkah is exactly like tzitzis: when he went outside the sukkah, the first mitzvah of sitting in the sukkah was already completed.
Know that what is written in the Shulchan Aruch, se'if 7, "one who vowed," etc. is permitted, etc., because mitzvos were not given for the sake of benefit; nevertheless if he said to him, etc., it is forbidden, for this is called benefit; that he performs his agency. And if he said "konam" his blowing, etc., it is forbidden, because vows take effect upon a matter of mitzvah.
