1) [Seif 1] Anyone who is not obligated in the matter, etc. This means one who is not inherently obligated in the mitzvah, such as those specified below in the Shulchan Aruch. However, one who is inherently obligated in the mitzvah but has already fulfilled his obligation may discharge the obligation of others, as stated above, siman 585, seif 2, in the Rama's gloss, see there.
2) Ibid. He cannot discharge the obligation of others. Since Hashem does not desire him personally in this mitzvah, how can he discharge the obligation of others? Levush.
3) A man of evil deeds who exalts himself in his heart, saying, "I will sound the shofar on Rosh Hashanah," should be opposed by the holy congregation out of concern for the honor of their Creator, and they should prevent him from sounding it until he repents from his evil ways. Ikkarei HaDat, siman 30, os 32. See above, siman 581, os 44.
4) The Eliyah Rabbah wrote that it is possible that a mourner should not be appointed to sound the shofar, as written in siman 582 regarding prayer. In responsa Olas Shmuel, siman 54, he agrees with him. This applies specifically where the shofar blower willingly relinquishes the role and there is someone preferable to him. However, if there is no one preferable, or he does not wish to relinquish it and has an established right to sound the shofar, one should not introduce a dispute because of this. There, in siman 86, he relates that he himself sounded the shofar while in mourning, because he was told that there was no one preferable, and all the great scholars of the city agreed with him, see there. Pitchei Teshuvah; Pischei Olam, os 1. See above, siman 581, os 33, and siman 585, os 23.
5) Men who are obligated according to the Torah in hearing the shofar should not hold young children with them at all during the blasts, lest the children's antics distract them from hearing the sound. If a woman cannot leave her child at home, it is preferable that she keep the child with her in the women's section of the synagogue, since women are not as fully obligated as men. Derashos Maharil, Hilchos Shofar; Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahos Tur; Magen Avraham, beginning of the siman; Eliyah Rabbah, os 1; Eshel Avraham, beginning of the siman; Mateh Ephraim, os 4. Nevertheless, it is a mitzvah to bring minors who have reached the age of chinuch and to warn them to incline their ears to the sound of the shofar, in order to train them in mitzvos. Mateh Ephraim, ibid.; Mishna Berurah, siman 587, os 16.
