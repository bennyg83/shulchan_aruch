6) [Seif 2] A deaf-mute, a mentally incompetent person, and a minor are exempt. A blind person is obligated in all the mitzvos of the Torah and in sounding the shofar according to the Torah, and he may discharge the obligation of others. Rabbeinu Yerucham, Nesiv 13, part 1, disagrees. The Radbaz wrote in vol. 1, siman 59, that one need not be concerned for his view. Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahos Tur. So too wrote the Pri Chadash in seif 4, that a blind person is obligated according to the Torah and may discharge the obligation of others, and one need not be concerned for the view of Rabbeinu Yerucham, who wrote that he is obligated only rabbinically, see there. See the following os.
7) If he had an established right to the mitzvah of sounding the shofar and they removed him because of Rabbeinu Yerucham's view, then if they cannot find a proficient shofar blower equal to him, they should restore him to his appointment. If they find someone equally proficient, the second person retains his established position, and the first, once displaced, remains displaced, since the view was issued by Rabbeinu Yerucham. Radbaz, ibid.; Knesset HaGedolah, ibid.; Magen Avraham, s.k. 2. We have already written further concerning this above, siman 53, os 65; take it from there.
8) Ibid. A deaf-mute, a mentally incompetent person, and a minor are exempt. A minor who cannot discharge an adult's obligation does not discharge it even after becoming a full 13 years old, until it is known that he developed two pubic hairs after age 13. For an obligation according to the Torah, we do not rely on the presumption that once he has reached the requisite age he has developed the signs of maturity. He cannot discharge even the obligation of one like himself, who is already 13 but whose development of two pubic hairs is uncertain. One should be stringent in this even on the second day of Rosh Hashanah, where another person is available. All this concerns discharging the obligation of an individual. However, for appointment as the shofar blower for the congregation, to discharge their obligation, one should seek a shofar blower who is 30 years old and possesses the other qualities that we wrote in siman 581 regarding prayer. At the very least, his beard must have filled out, in accordance with the law of a shaliach tzibbur explained in siman 53. Mateh Ephraim, os 7. See above, siman 55, seif 9 and seif 5, and siman 53, seif 8, and our words there, with G-d's help. Also see above, siman 271, os 9, and Choshen Mishpat, siman 35, seif 1, and our sefer Kaf HaChaim, Yoreh De'ah, siman 39, os 2 and os 3, and examine carefully.
9) On the first day of Rosh Hashanah, if the shofar blower lacked the strength to sound the blasts arranged during the blessings, and a minor who had reached 13 years sounded them, but it was not known whether he had developed the signs of maturity, the congregation fulfills its obligation through him. This is because they had already fulfilled the Torah obligation through the blasts sounded while seated, and for a rabbinic obligation we rely on the presumption that he developed two pubic hairs. Responsa Shoel U'Meishiv, vol. 3, siman 177, see there; Pitchei Teshuvah; Pischei Olam, os 2. See above, siman 55, seif 5, in the Rama's gloss, and our words there, with G-d's help.
