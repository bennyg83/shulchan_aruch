One who is half a slave (see the Taz; which is not so regarding an androginos, as I wrote in letter 1. And see the Levush: there is a difficulty) why is this different from sukkah (see there. And one may say the difficulty is: why does a half-slave recite the blessing "to sit in the sukkah" for himself) "Who has sanctified us with His commandments and commanded us" when the slave half is not obligated? And seemingly, regarding time-bound positive mitzvos women are permitted to recite the blessing, for a woman and a man are each a complete body, which is not so regarding slaves. And even if we say that a slave too may recite the blessing for a time-bound positive mitzvah, nevertheless how can the slave side, which is not obligated, discharge the free side, which is obligated, even though the blessing is rabbinic, and speech comes from the whole body, like tekiah; and the same law applies similarly regarding all the mitzvah blessings and Birkas HaMazon: why should he not hear from another? And this requires further study.
Behold, the hearing is the essence, not the blowing at all; and even so, one who hears from a deaf-mute, a shoteh, or a minor has not fulfilled, for one who is not himself obligated does not discharge others. And it is unlike Hallel, where a minor reads and he [the adult] says after him. And see the Levush; and in Sefer Kapos Temarim: even if he heard from a deaf-mute, a shoteh, or a minor and afterward blew himself, and put the shofar into a pit and blew (he has not fulfilled, as stated; and see Kapos Temarim, who wrote this) see there.
