Se'if 2. Even one who speaks, etc. For behold they recite the blessing over the hearing, and he is not a person subject to the obligation; and as the Rosh wrote, that it depends on the hearing.
Since he is not. See the Rosh there, s.y.
But one who hears, etc. Chagigah 2b; Gittin 41a. And even though we say a mute person may not separate terumah, the Yerushalmi explains that this is because of the blessing, and another cannot recite the blessing for them; and as is written in Hagahot Asheri at the beginning of Chullin, s.v. "And in the Or Zarua he wrote: a mute person, etc., and another may not, etc., but, etc." And even though he wrote there: however, I do not know if the same law applies, etc. nevertheless all the poskim have already written that the same law applies regarding a mute person; and specifically where another is also obligated. And therefore here he may blow l'chatchila to discharge the many of their obligation, in such a case where another will recite the blessing. And see Yoreh De'ah, siman 1, se'if 7, and the Shach there, s.k. 12. And for this reason the Mishnah taught that he may not separate terumah, because another who is obligated is not commonly available.
