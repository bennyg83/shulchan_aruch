(Same section 8) One engaged in blowing shofar to learn does not fulfill his obligation, etc. Ran wrote at end of Rosh Hashanah in mishnah that one who hears from one engaged does not fulfill — for even per the view that mitzvot do not require intent, here it is deficient since he intends for those children, it is min be-mino and invalidates it — see there. I have many difficulties from gemara there which asks: one who hears from one engaged does not fulfill, but one who hears from one who sounds for himself fulfills — let it be a refutation of R' Zeira who told his attendant "intend and blow for me" and answers "since," etc. It is difficult for me why it asks per Ran's view above — one may say on the contrary it teaches a greater matter that mitzvot never require intent, and if one heard from a blower not at all for mitzva purpose he certainly fulfills and does not need the blower to intend to fulfill him; only R' Zeira deals when Shemaya intended to fulfill for himself, therefore since it is min be-mino it invalidates, therefore must also intend to fulfill R' Zeira, therefore mishnah teaches greater matter that not from one who sounds for himself who is truly for mitzva purpose — obvious it is considered truly min be-mino and invalidates and does not fulfill; only also if like one engaged, then there is a presumption since he only intends to educate and the listener intends truly for mitzva, this is not called min be-mino and does not invalidate — therefore teaches also this is min be-mino and invalidates and requires intent — this is a great difficulty per Ran's view in my humble opinion, requires study. Also difficult what it asks — one may say as we hold one who seizes for a debtor where he owes others is not troubled and elevates found object for another — he acquires through migo that he could acquire for himself, etc. — say here too where the blower has not yet fulfilled, he does not need intent to fulfill the listener through migo he could acquire for himself; but if the blower already fulfilled, then we require intent; and R' Zeira deals when the blower already fulfilled — even if strained to say Shemaya would fulfill before R' Zeira, still say R' Zeira holds l'chatchila in every case intent is required and mishnah deals b'dieved, requires study. And what is clear in Shulchan Aruch — one engaged in shofar to learn does not fulfill: at first glance Shulchan Aruch wording is not precise, for it states here out of order per Ran in Rosh Hashanah in two places that even per view one who blows for song fulfills — mitzvot do not require intent — nevertheless admits one engaged does not fulfill, for this is deficient in that he intends for mitzva to educate children as Zevachim says min be-mino invalidates, not min be-mino does not — therefore Shulchan Aruch fits: first wrote one who blows to learn does not help, then wrote one who blows for song does not fulfill — this is "not only this but also this" — for one view who intended to learn is min be-mino and does not fulfill; only also one who blows for song who is not min be-mino does not fulfill. But what it wrote afterward when listener intended and blower did not intend to fulfill listener — does not fulfill — this is "if so all the more" — for if he intended only to educate children which is min be-mino and invalidates, all the more if intended to fulfill himself for mitzva purpose which is min be-mino and invalidates since he did not intend to fulfill listener — kal va-chomer he does not fulfill; from one engaged who does not fulfill, all the more here; and kal va-chomer from blower for song — if blowing for song which is not min be-mino and invalidates, all the more one who blows for mitzva purpose without intent to fulfill listener does not fulfill — "if so" and "all the more." But truly I am troubled on Ran from gemara Rosh Hashanah daf 33 which asks there one hearing from engaged does not fulfill — but one hearing from listener for himself fulfills — and it teaches, etc. Troubled per Ran's words why it asks — say on the contrary greater matter that even one hearing from engaged who did not intend full mitzva only to educate children and it is only rabbinic — still called min be-mino and helps that he does not fulfill from Torah even though from Torah there is no mitzva at all and not his type from Torah — nevertheless does not fulfill from Torah and must return and blow from Torah — difference for many matters whether obligated from Torah or rabbinically — all the more when intended for himself and did not intend to fulfill listener — in this case truly his type and Torah mitzva, min be-mino and invalidates and requires intent for all; if did not intend to fulfill listener it does not help — "if so" and "all the more" — why ask? Here there is no difficulty to Shulchan Aruch wording — from that gemara proved engaged is worse than intending for oneself, therefore wrote properly "not only this but also this." But on Ran it is still properly difficult from where gemara must ask — there is reasoning to say intending for oneself is worse than engaged per Ran's reasoning above — why ask, requires study. I examined Rashi who wrote s.v. engaged "he did not," etc., but one who blows for song who intends blowing in general fulfills — end of his words. This is astonishing — gemara wants to prove only if blows for mitzva purpose but does not intend to fulfill listener he fulfills, and on this asks from R' Zeira — why mention blowing for song? It should be answered Rashi explains what gemara said "but one hearing from one who sounds for himself" means blowing for song who sounds for himself — meaning for his pleasure, not from obligation of the day upon him, only his pleasure — is not so; Rashi wrote afterward s.v. tanna taught end too engaged: and in any case one hearing from one who sounds for himself who did not intend to fulfill the listeners does not fulfill — end of his words; if so he also explains sounding for himself means only he did not intend to fulfill listeners but for himself intended to fulfill — as proven from R' Zeira's wording; why first explain intent for song? Requires study: per his way proven from here that for song too he fulfills — he should ask from this on view that mitzvot require intent; why ask R' Zeira specifically — should teach mitzvot do not require intent as Berakhot and many places — should teach here opposite that mitzvot do not require intent — why mention R' Zeira's words? Perforce proven like Ran that blowing for song is worse than engaged, therefore asks only R' Zeira, requires study.
