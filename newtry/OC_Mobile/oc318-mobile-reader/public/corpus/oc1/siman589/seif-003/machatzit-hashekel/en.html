(s.k. 3) They may blow, and not, etc. For the Bach inferred from the Tur's specifying specifically women and minors, that he wrote: and even though women and minors are exempt, they may blow and recite the blessing, and we do not protest against them, etc. Therefore the Bach wrote, and this is his language: and it appears specifically women, since they have understanding, may take the obligation upon themselves; for we hold "greater is one who is commanded and does than one who is not commanded and does" hence one who is not commanded and does also has reward. And likewise a minor, even if he has reached the age of training, since he will come to obligation when he grows up; we do not protest against them. End quote.
And see siman 266, se'if 5: that we hold that one for whom it became dark on the way, who has money in his hand and has no gentile with him; the Sages permitted him to give it to a deaf-mute, a shoteh, or a minor. And if he has with him a deaf person and a minor, he may give it to whichever he wishes; implying that regarding prohibition a deaf person and a minor are alike. And see siman 343, the law of a minor who eats neveilos.
See siman 597: that even l'chatchila it is permitted to tell a minor who has reached the age of training to blow, in order to train him.
