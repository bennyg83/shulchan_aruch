One who is occupied [without intent]. See the Taz; see siman 60; and there we wrote that one may say [to be] stringent, not lenient, and there is nothing clear; and this requires further study regarding the blessing afterward. And see Magen Avraham there, letter 3: regarding rabbinic [mitzvos], mitzvos do not require intent. However, the second day of Yom Tov is like the first day of Yom Tov, so as not to treat it lightly; and likewise matzah and lulav are similar; and see siman 475 regarding matzah when gentiles forced him (see there); and in Beis Yosef there.
That which the Mechaber wrote regarding one who vowed not to benefit from his fellow (in [siman] 586 it refers to a shofar) see there. And see Peri Chadash: if he said to his fellow, "Blow and discharge me," he has fulfilled after the fact even though he transgressed "do not profane [a vow]" (see there. And even according to the view that whatever the Torah said not to do, if one did it, it is of no effect) one may say that where the case cannot be so conceived, in such a case he fabricated the prohibition himself, as is found in Choshen Mishpat regarding Rosh Chodesh and the like. And further, behold it is subject to [vow] release; and like one who shechts on Shabbos, whose shechitah is valid; somewhat similar to this; and one should not elaborate.
