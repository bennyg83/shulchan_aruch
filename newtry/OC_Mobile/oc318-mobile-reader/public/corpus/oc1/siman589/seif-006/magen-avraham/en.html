They may blow. And we do not say that there is in it a [concern of] shevus; and likewise a minor; we do not protest against him. But for a deaf person and a shoteh we protest against blowing, for they never come to obligation in any way (Bach). And his words require further study, for behold the court is not commanded to restrain him even if he eats neveilos, as is found in Yevamos, chapter 14 (see there); and see siman 266, se'if 5; and see siman 343. Therefore it appears to me that he mentioned minors and women for an additional point: that even though they are people of understanding, they are permitted to blow (see siman 596).
They have already fulfilled, etc. Therefore he should blow for them before he hears the tekios in the synagogue (Levush). But in Maaglei Tzedek he wrote in the name of the Maharil that one should not blow during the first three [hours], because judgment is then appointed, as is written in siman 591; therefore he should blow for them after the synagogue tekiah (end quote). He will not be able to recite the blessing for them; therefore it appears to me that if he has another shofar he should blow at the time of the synagogue tekiah, for then even if it is within the first three there is no concern, as is written there; and if not, he should intend in his heart not to fulfill his obligation with the synagogue tekiah, and then he can recite the blessing for them (see se'if 6). But in this one must carefully consider whether walking from his house to the synagogue is considered an interruption, so that he would need to recite the blessing a second time over the tekios he hears in the synagogue in the order of the blessings, as is written in siman 8, se'if 13. Nevertheless it appears to me that he need not recite the blessing, as is written in siman 592, se'if 3; and it is unlike tzitzis, which are two mitzvos (which is not so here, for it is all one mitzvah, as the Rif wrote at the end of Rosh Hashanah) see there. One who comes to the synagogue to fulfill his obligation with the congregation (even though at the time he heard he did not direct his heart, but only in a general way) has fulfilled; but one who comes to the synagogue in a general way has not fulfilled (Radbaz, part 1, [siman] 160).
