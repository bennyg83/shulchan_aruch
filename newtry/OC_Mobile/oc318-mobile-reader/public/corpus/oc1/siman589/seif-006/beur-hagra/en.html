And likewise another, etc. The Rosh in the name of the Ravyah, not like the Baal HaItur. And even though it is a shevus, nevertheless in such a case it is permitted, just as they permitted leaning [on a korban] for them, even though they rested their hands [upon it]; nevertheless it is similar to the Temple service, all the more so here, where it is not melachah. And see what I wrote in siman 254, se'if 5; and in Chullin 84b it is implied that there is a prohibition to blow for women, for it is a shevus. And see Tosafos there at the end of s.v. "Tekios," etc., only that according to Tosafos' first answer the prohibition is in carrying out; but the Ravyah holds that all this is according to R' Yehudah, whereas according to R' Yosi everything is permitted, as is written there: "And R' Yosi follows his reasoning," etc. And this is what is written in the Rosh of Rosh Hashanah there; and the Ravyah wrote: since, etc. And it is also permitted to carry out, etc. But that which he wrote "and so it appears to me that it is no worse," etc. requires further study. And see Tosafos of Chullin 85a, s.v. "Women," etc.
But they do not, etc. Rambam, chapter 3 of Hilchos Tzitzis: for "Who has commanded us" does not apply; for even regarding a Chanukah candle it raises a difficulty in chapter 2 of Shabbos.
And the custom. This is the view of Rabbeinu Tam, Rosh Hashanah 33: that even though she is exempt, nevertheless she may recite the blessing; for if not so, what did Rav Yosef say ("I would make a festive day for the rabbis") would he then forfeit the blessings of all the mitzvos and the other blessings? And in chapter Arvei Pesachim: "Who said the aggadah," etc. And it is difficult only from his discharging others, and it is not difficult how he [himself] recites the blessing. And the first reasoning holds that there is no proof from a blind person, for nevertheless he is obligated by rabbinic law and therefore he recites the blessing, as is written in chapter 2 of Shabbos: "And where did He command us," etc. And this is what is written in chapter Arvei Pesachim: they hold that matzah nowadays is rabbinic, etc. And in chapter 3 of Megillah, R' Yehudah concedes that a blind person may recite the Shema blessings aloud and discharge others.
