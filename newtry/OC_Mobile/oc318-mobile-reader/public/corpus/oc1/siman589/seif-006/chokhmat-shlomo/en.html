There, seif 6. "Although women are exempt, they may blow." NB: See the Magen Avraham, who wondered at the Bach, since we hold that when a minor eats neveilot the court is not commanded to separate him. In my humble opinion this is not difficult at all. There is a distinction whether the neveilah belongs to the minor or to the adult. The rule that when a minor eats neveilot the court is not commanded to separate him applies where the neveilah belongs to the minor, or to others who are not before us; then those who see him need not separate him, for this is not like placing it in his hands. But if the neveilah belongs to the adult, and the adult sees him and does not protest, that is like placing it in his hands. So too here, it speaks where the shofar belongs to the adult; therefore with a woman and a minor he is not obligated to protest, while with a deaf-mute, shoteh, or minor he is obligated to protest. This appears correct and clear to me. Examine carefully. See the source of this law in the Gemara and the early authorities; from there it is clear that they ruled like Rabbi Yosei, that women may perform semichah voluntarily. This is also proven from the Rambam's language in Hilchot Ma'aseh HaKorbanot 3:5: "Olot of non-Jews are not brought with libations, etc., and they do not require semichah, for semichah applies only among Israel, to men and not women." From the phrase "they do not require semichah," it implies that if he wishes, he may perform semichah, proving that the Rambam rules like Rabbi Yosei, that it is voluntary. With this one may explain the Rambam in Hilchot Chagigah 3:3: "A tumtum and an androginos are exempt because they are possibly women." The Lechem Mishneh already wondered that this is against Chagigah 4, where the Gemara derives it from "your males," asks that a tumtum is a doubt and whether a verse is needed to exclude a doubt, and answers that his testicles are visible from outside; the Rambam omitted this. He also wrote that an androginos is exempt because of possible womanhood, while the Gemara says it is a distinct creature. What appears to me is this. At first the Rambam was difficult: if it is a possible woman, why is he not obligated from doubt like every Torah doubt? Even according to the Rambam that every Torah doubt is rabbinic, he should at least be obligated rabbinically. The answer is that either way there is a problem: if he brings korbanot, perhaps they are chullin in the Temple courtyard; if he brings them conditionally as voluntary offerings, vows and voluntary offerings are not brought on Yom Tov. To offer after Yom Tov also does not help, since anything not fit on the first day is not fit for the compensatory days, for all are tashlumin of the first. Thus if he offers there is an issur, and without a korban there is the opposite issur of "he shall not appear before Me empty"; therefore shev v'al ta'aseh is preferable. I later saw Tosafot in that sugya write this. Yet it remains difficult from Eruvin 97, where Yonah's wife would go up for regel and the Sages did not protest, and the Gemara establishes it according to the view that women may perform semichah voluntarily. Tosafot wrote there that according to Rabbi Yehudah they would have protested because of the korban, which would be chullin in the Temple courtyard. This implies that according to Rabbi Yosei she did bring a korban, so with a doubtful male/female we should be stringent and bring, since even if it is a woman, women may offer. It appears, however, that besides the Gemara in Eruvin saying the anonymous Sifra follows Rabbi Yehudah, this baraita of "your males" excluding tumtum and androginos is also Sifra and Rabbi Yehudah, who holds women may not perform semichah. It is also proven internally: if it followed Rabbi Yosei, why derive tumtum and androginos from "your males"? Perhaps it comes to exclude women, as the simple wording implies; and if "male" already implies that, perhaps "your males" excludes women even from voluntary ascent. Perforce it follows Rabbi Yehudah, and since women are otherwise forbidden to ascend, "your males" comes to exclude tumtum, etc. The Chavot Da'at and others wrote that the Torah speaks only of certainty, and this is explicit in Sotah 25: "every pure person may eat meat" means certainly pure, not doubtfully pure. Thus the Gemara's question is: why need "your males"? If it had written only "male," we would exclude a doubt, for male means certainly male. Granted, for an androginos one may say the baraita follows the view that it is a distinct creature and needs "your males"; but a tumtum is merely a doubt, so why need a verse? Even without a verse he would be exempt because a Torah doubt has an opposite concern, perhaps he is a woman and bringing is impossible according to Rabbi Yehudah, so shev v'al ta'aseh is preferable. Therefore it answers that his testicles are visible from outside. This is all according to Rabbi Yehudah. For us, however, since we hold like Rabbi Yosei that women may perform semichah voluntarily, yet Tosafot brought the Pesikta that the Sages did protest against Yonah's wife, one may say the Rambam rules like Rabbi Yosei and like the Pesikta: even according to Rabbi Yosei, a woman is forbidden to perform semichah, because "your males" excludes women even from voluntary participation. If so, "your males" excludes women, while tumtum and androginos need no verse because they are possibly women and one cannot be stringent because of the opposite issur. If his testicles are visible, so that he is known to be male, he would indeed be obligated. The Rambam did not explain that women are forbidden to ascend because it is implied: since he exempts one who is possibly a woman, and a Torah doubt should be stringent, perforce no remedy is possible because of the opposite issur. That proves he rules like the Pesikta; and it is also the Rambam's way not to write what is not explicit in the Gemara. This appears correct to me, with God's help. Examine carefully.
There, seif 6, in the gloss. "And the custom is that women recite berachot over positive time-bound mitzvot; therefore here too they should recite for themselves, etc." NB: I saw in Shemot BaAretz on Rosh Hashanah 33 that he asked that the Tur contradicts himself: here he writes that women may recite the beracha if they wish, while above, in siman 17 regarding tzitzit, he writes that it is better that they not recite it. In my humble opinion one may say as follows. If women do not intend to add, there is no issur in their reciting, for bal tosif requires intent. Yet we hold that in its proper time one violates bal tosif even without intent, and therefore it is proper that they not recite, for the absence of a beracha is a recognizable sign that they do not act for the sake of obligation, similar to what is brought in the name of the Mordechai and in the Shulchan Aruch of the Vilna Gaon, siman 667. But for women perhaps the day is not its time, and outside its time bal tosif requires intent; since they do not intend to add, why should they not recite? This depends on whether women are considered a separate people, as discussed in Shabbat chapter 6, fol. 62. If they are a separate people, then for them it is outside its time and requires intent; if not, since it is the time for men, women too would violate without intent. Thus regarding a positive time-bound mitzvah where men are obligated and cannot exempt themselves, women, who are exempt while men are truly obligated, are considered a separate people, and for them it is outside its time. But where even men have a way to exempt themselves, women are not considered a separate people. This resembles the rule in Menachot regarding like-kind mixtures, that something able to become like the other is considered its kind. Therefore with shofar, where men are obligated and cannot exempt themselves, and it is a true personal obligation, women are a separate people; for them it is outside its time and bal tosif does not apply, so they may recite. But tzitzit is an obligation of the garment, not the person, and even men can exempt themselves by not wearing a four-cornered garment; therefore women are not a separate people, and since it is the time for men it is considered its time for women too, so intent is not needed to violate. Therefore the Tur wrote that it is better that they not recite. This appears correct to me in reasoning and in practical ruling.
