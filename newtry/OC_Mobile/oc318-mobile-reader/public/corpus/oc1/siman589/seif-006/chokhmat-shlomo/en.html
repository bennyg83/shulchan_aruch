(There, se'if 6) Even though women are exempt, they may blow. Gloss: See the Magen Avraham, who was astonished at the Bach (for behold we hold that [regarding] a minor who eats neveilos, the court is not commanded to restrain him) see there. And in my humble opinion there is no difficulty: for there is a distinction whether the nevelah belongs to the minor or to the adult. That [ruling that] a minor who eats neveilos (the court is not commanded to restrain him) is when the nevelah is the minor's, or belongs to others who are not before us; in that case those who see him need not restrain him, for it is not like giving it into his hands. But if the nevelah belongs to the adult, and the adult sees him and does not protest against him, then it is like giving it into his hands. And so here too the case is that the shofar belongs to the adult; therefore regarding a woman and a minor one is not obligated to protest, but regarding a deaf person and a shoteh one is obligated to protest, so it appears to my humble opinion, correct and clear; and examine carefully. And behold, see the source of this se'if's law in the Gemara and the Rishonim: and from this it is clear that they ruled like R' Yosi, that women's leaning [on a korban] is optional. And behold, this is evident from the language of the Rambam in chapter 3 of Maaseh HaKorbanos, halachah 5: "Olot of gentiles; they do not bring drink-offerings with them, etc., and they do not require leaning, for leaning applies only to Yisrael, to men and not to women" (end quote). Behold, from this language "they do not require leaning," it is implied that if he wishes he may lean; and it is evident that he rules like R' Yosi that it is optional. And behold, thereby one may resolve the words of the Rambam in chapter 3 of Hilchos Chagigah, halachah 3, and this is his language: a tumtum and an androginos are exempt because they are a doubtful woman. And the Lechem Mishneh there already wondered that this contradicts the Gemara of Chagigah, folio 4, which says that this is derived from the verse "your males," and also objects there that a tumtum is a doubt (does a verse come to exclude a doubt?) and answers: when his testicles are outside; and the Rambam omitted this. He also wrote that an androginos is [exempt] because of a doubtful woman, whereas the Gemara says he is a distinct creature (see there, where he left it as requiring further study). And what appeared to me to resolve it is as follows. From the outset I found difficult against the Rambam: what of it that he is a doubtful woman; nevertheless he is obligated to fulfill the mitzvah out of doubt, as is the law of every Torah doubt; and even according to the Rambam that every Torah doubt is [treated as] rabbinic, nevertheless by rabbinic law he would be obligated to fulfill. And it is resolved for me: since it is heads-or-tails, if he brings korbanos they are chullin in the [Temple] courtyard; and if he brings and stipulates on condition that if not [he is obligated] it should be a freewill offering (freewill vows and donations are not offered on Yom Tov; and to offer after Yom Tov) since it was not fit on the first day it is not fit on the second, since we hold that all are make-ups for the first. And so heads-or-tails: if he offers there is a prohibition as above, and without a korban there is a prohibition the other way, for "and they shall not appear before Me empty" is written; therefore Shev ve'al taaseh is preferable. And afterward I saw in Tosafos on that sugya that they wrote likewise. But it is still difficult for me: for we say in Eruvin, folio 97, that Yonah's wife would go up for the pilgrimage festival and the Sages did not protest against her, and the Gemara there establishes it according to the view that women's leaning is optional; and Tosafos wrote there that according to R' Yehudah they needed to protest against her because of the korban, which would be chullin in the courtyard (see there); if so it is implied that according to R' Yosi she would also bring a korban. And so, since it is optional, all the more so we should say that one might have thought to be stringent: what should he do with the korban; even were he a woman, she too is permitted to offer, and there is no opposite concern of prohibition. But it appears that, aside from what is written in Eruvin, folio 97, where it objects: but is an anonymous Sifra not [according to] R' Yehudah, etc., and so here too this beraisa of "your males" to exclude a tumtum and an androginos is likewise Sifra and is R' Yehudah's; and R' Yehudah holds that women are forbidden to lean. Aside from this as well, it is evident that it accords with R' Yehudah: for if it were according to R' Yosi, from where would we exclude a tumtum and an androginos from "your males" more than saying it comes to exclude women, as the language implies? And if from the language "male" that too would be implied (then why [say] "your males"? Perhaps it comes to exclude women [even] from the optional [ascent], that they are forbidden to go up) and from where that it comes to exclude a tumtum and an androginos from obligation? Of necessity it accords with R' Yehudah. And so, since throughout the Torah they are forbidden to go up, "your males" is not needed to exclude [women], and of necessity it comes to exclude a tumtum, etc. And according to this it is well. For behold the Chavvos Daas wrote, and so other authors, that the Torah spoke only of a certainty; and this is an explicit Gemara in Sotah, folio 25, regarding Rabban Gamliel who posed: it is written "every pure person shall eat meat" a certain pure person shall eat, but a doubtful pure person shall not eat, etc. And so the Gemara's objection is thus: why do we need "your males" if only "male" were written we would also exclude a doubt, for "male" implies a certain male and not a doubt; and so why "your males"? And to this it objects: granted regarding an androginos there is no difficulty, for one may say that this beraisa accords with the view that he is a distinct creature and needs "your males"; but a tumtum is a doubt (and would a verse come to exclude a doubt?) in astonishment; for if only "male" were written, that too would imply "and not a doubt" from the language "male"; and even without any verse at all we would exempt him, for automatically, since a Torah doubt [is treated] stringently, there is the opposite concern: perhaps he is a woman, and one cannot offer, since it accords with R' Yehudah that women are forbidden to lean; certainly Shev ve'al taaseh is preferable. Therefore it is necessary to answer "when his testicles are outside." And behold this is according to R' Yehudah; but for us, since we hold like R' Yosi that women's leaning is optional, and behold although the Gemara brings a beraisa in Eruvin that regarding Yonah's wife the Sages did not protest against her, Tosafos brought the Pesikta that the Sages did protest against her, and according to this one may say that the Rambam rules like R' Yosi and rules according to the Pesikta that the Sages protested against her; that is, that even according to R' Yosi she is forbidden to lean, for "your males" excludes women even from the optional. And so "your males" comes to exclude women; and a tumtum and an androginos in truth need no verse, for they are a doubtful woman, and they cannot be stringent, for there is a prohibition the other way; that women are forbidden to go up. Automatically one may say that if his testicles are outside, so that it is known he is truly male, he is in fact obligated to go up. And that the Rambam did not clarify that women are forbidden to go up is because it is automatically implied: since he exempts a doubtful woman, [and] a Torah doubt [is] for stringency, of necessity it is impossible to "fix" [the matter], for there is a prohibition the other way. And what of it that she is a woman; [if] a woman is optional, of necessity he holds that a woman is forbidden to lean; and it is evident that he rules like the Pesikta. Also it is not his way to write what is not explicit in the Gemara, as is known of his way. So it appears to my humble opinion, correct, with the help of Hashem; and examine carefully.
(There, se'if 6 in the gloss) And the custom is that women recite the blessing over time-bound positive mitzvos; therefore here too they should recite the blessing for themselves, etc. Gloss: Behold I saw in Sefer Shemos BaAretz on Rosh Hashanah, folio 33, that he raised a difficulty that the Tur's words contradict one another: for here he wrote that they may recite the blessing if they wish, and above in siman 17 regarding tzitzis he wrote that it is better that they not recite the blessing (see there, where he was pressed in his resolution). And in my humble opinion one may say thus: for if women do not intend to "add," there is no prohibition that they recite the blessing, for to transgress "do not add" requires intent; and if they do not intend to add, there is no prohibition in their blessing, only that we hold that in its proper time one transgresses "do not add" without intent; therefore it is proper that they not recite the blessing, for then there is a distinguishing sign that they are not doing it for the sake of the mitzvah, as he wrote something similar in the name of the Mordechai: that therefore there is no concern of "do not add," since they do not recite the blessing; brought in the Shulchan Aruch of the Gaon, the pious Moreinu HaRav R' Eliyahu of Vilna, of blessed memory, siman 667. But one may say: regarding women today is not "its time," and so relative to them it is counted as not in its time, and then intent is required; and since they do not intend to add, why should they not recite the blessing? But one may say that this depends on whether women are counted as a people of their own or not; and see regarding this a dispute in tractate Shabbos, chapter 6, folio 62 (see there). And if we say women are a people of their own, relative to them it is counted as not in its time and intent is required; but if we say they are not a people of their own, then since it is its time for males, regarding women too one transgresses "do not add" without intent. And according to this one may say thus: regarding a time-bound positive mitzvah from which males are obligated and it is impossible to exempt oneself from it at all (and since women are exempt and men are truly obligated) women are counted as a people of their own, and for them it is not its time, and intent is required. But regarding a time-bound positive mitzvah from which males too have a side by which to exempt themselves, in that case women are not counted as a people of their own. Similar to what we say in Menachos regarding same-species [mixing]: that where it is possible to be like its fellow it is counted as same-species (see there); so one may say here. And therefore regarding shofar, where males are obligated and it is impossible to exempt oneself from it, and it is truly a personal obligation, therefore women are counted as a people of their own, and it is not its time, and "do not add" does not apply; therefore they are permitted to recite the blessing. But regarding tzitzis, which is an obligation of the garment and not a personal obligation, in that case, since even upon males there is no absolute obligation, and it is in his hand to exempt himself if he does not wear a four-cornered garment, therefore they are not counted as a people of their own, and it is counted as its time for women too, since it is its time for men; therefore intent is not required to transgress; therefore the Tur wrote that it is better that she not recite the blessing. So it appears to my humble opinion, correct in reason and as a practical ruling in this.
