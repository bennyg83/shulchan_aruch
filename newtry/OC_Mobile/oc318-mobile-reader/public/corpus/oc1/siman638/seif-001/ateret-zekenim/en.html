Whether the wood of the walls, etc. The gloss comments on this, and so it also appears from the words of Tosafot in chapter HaMevi and in Torat Adam, gate 58, part 1. I saw my ancestors practice prohibition even with the wood of the walls. All these laws apply even to an old sukkah. So too the Ran wrote in Beitzah, chapter HaMevi, page 293, that even the wood of the walls is forbidden. He wrote: However, after he made the required measure of the walls and the sukkah's validity was completed, if he afterward added a wall, it implies that it does not become forbidden; but if he made all four at once, all are forbidden. End quote. See above, siman 518, if he threw reeds onto the roofing after he had roofed it properly, and likewise if he leaned reeds against the walls after he had made them properly, whether this addition is forbidden. The Ran wrote in chapter Lulav HaGazul, page 360, that regarding myrtle used as sukkah roofing, some say it is forbidden to smell it, as with the myrtle of the lulav, and some say it is permitted, since he could use other wood. End quote. It implies there that they disagree only regarding myrtle, which is forbidden regarding lulav; but if something was hung in it that originally was not intended for fragrance, it is certainly permitted to smell it, for it is permitted to smell the etrog of the lulav, as below in siman 653. Hagahot Ashiri in chapter HaMevi wrote: where one made a sukkah and did not sit in it, it is permitted in benefit, for designation is not legally effective. End quote.
