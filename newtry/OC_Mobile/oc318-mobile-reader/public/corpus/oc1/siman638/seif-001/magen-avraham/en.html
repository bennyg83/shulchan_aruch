Rashi explained the reason that a condition does not help with sukkah wood: since it is impossible to remove it during twilight because he would be dismantling a tent, it is set aside for all seven days. If so, if the case is one where there is no dismantling of a tent, as written at the end of siman 518, a condition is effective. The Ran wrote that a condition does not help in any case, because it is forbidden by Torah law. R. Yitzchak's view is that once it fell it is permitted by Torah law; this implies that while it stands, even what is more than the valid measure of the sukkah is forbidden. The Yam Shel Shlomo, chapter 4 of Beitzah, wrote that if he built it on Chol HaMoed, then there is no dismantling of a tent, and a condition is effective so that he may use what is more than the valid measure of the sukkah when it falls, according to all opinions; see there, for he wrote another answer of his own. Wood adjacent to the sukkah is permitted. If he leaned wood against the sukkah in order to thicken it, even after it was completed, it is forbidden, for it is no worse than sukkah decorations, unless he stipulated. Yam Shel Shlomo. It seems to me that the same applies to sacks placed there to thicken it.
And all this, etc. Rabbenu Yerucham wrote: All its law likewise applies to an old sukkah, even if he did not renew anything in it, and it was not made for the sake of sukkah, as written in part 1. End quote. One must say that in any event he sat in it. It seems to me that one who has a sukkah built from year to year, nevertheless it is not forbidden until he sits in it during the festival. Although he sat in it the previous year, when the festival passed its sanctity ceased, and another act is needed for sanctity to take effect.
For designation is not legally effective. This is difficult, for in siman 42, seif 3, he wrote that designation for the body of sanctity is legally effective. One can say that articles of a mitzvah are more lenient, as written in siman 21.
