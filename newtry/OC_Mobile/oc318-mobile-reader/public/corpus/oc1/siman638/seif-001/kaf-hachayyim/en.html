1) [Seif 1] The wood of a sukkah is forbidden, etc. As we say in the Gemara, Sukkah 9a: From where do we know that the wood of a sukkah is forbidden throughout all seven days? The Torah teaches, 'The festival of Sukkos shall be seven days for Hashem.' It was taught: R' Yehudah ben Beseira says, just as the Name of Heaven rests upon the festival offering, meaning the festival peace-offerings, so the Name of Heaven rests upon the sukkah, as it is stated, 'The festival of Sukkos shall be seven days for Hashem.' Just as the festival offering is for Hashem, so too the sukkah is for Hashem. Tosafos there wrote that the Torah prohibition applies while the sukkah is standing, when it is forbidden to take wood from it; but once it has fallen and its mitzvah has ceased, it is forbidden only by rabbinic law. Rabbeinu Tam explains that sanctity rests upon the amount of wood needed for a valid sukkah, and it is forbidden by Torah law, whereas the excess beyond what is needed for validity is forbidden only by rabbinic law; see there. Regarding the walls, the view of the Rosh and the Tur is that there is no prohibition at all, but the Rambam's view is that the wood of the walls is also forbidden. This is also the view of the Ran, as stated in the Tur and Beit Yosef; see there. The Shulchan Aruch ruled according to the Rambam that the wood of the walls is also forbidden, and so ruled the Levush and the later authorities. However, the Bach wrote that according to the Rambam the wood of the walls is forbidden by Torah law, while the Taz, s.k. 1, wrote that it is forbidden by rabbinic law; see there.
2) Ibid. The wood of a sukkah is forbidden, etc. Even if he made another sukkah on Chol HaMoed and no longer needs the first one, nevertheless its wood is forbidden for benefit throughout all seven days. Beit Yosef in the name of Orchos Chaim; Beit HaSho'eivah, sec. 4; Mishna Berurah, sec. 1.
