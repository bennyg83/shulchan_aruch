(1) The wood of a sukkah, etc. - For it is written, "the festival of Sukkot, seven days, for Hashem": just as the Name of Heaven takes effect upon the festival offering, so the Name of Heaven takes effect upon the sukkah. Even if he made another sukkah on Chol HaMoed and no longer needs the first, nevertheless its wood is forbidden in benefit.
(2) Eight - And for us, also on the ninth, for on the eighth he is still obligated to eat in the sukkah, and since it was set aside at twilight it is set aside for the whole day. If Shabbat falls after Yom Tov, the practice is not to make use of it even on Shabbat, for that would be like preparing from Yom Tov for Shabbat.
(3) Whether the wood of the walls, etc. - There is no practical difference between a new sukkah and an old one, whenever they roofed it for the sake of the festival. But one who sits in a shepherds', watchmen's, or field-keepers' sukkah, it is like an ordinary sukkah: because this person entered and ate his bread there, it did not become sanctified.
(4) One may benefit - This means by way of nullifying it, for by this he nullifies its sanctity; but while it remains standing, there is no prohibition to benefit from it, such as by leaning on it or placing something on it, since its sanctity is still upon it.
(5) Even if the sukkah fell, etc. - Meaning that even according to the decisors who hold that by Torah law it is forbidden only while it is standing, nevertheless rabbinically it is forbidden to benefit from it because of muktzeh, since it was set aside at twilight at the beginning of the festival.
(6) A condition does not help with it - Even if he said, "I am not separating myself from it during all of twilight," since it is impossible to remove it during twilight because he would be dismantling a tent, it is set aside for all seven days. This is unlike sukkah decorations in seif 2, where a condition is effective. Know that what is implied from the Rema's plain wording, that a condition does not help in any manner, even if it fell, applies specifically during the seven days; but on its eighth day, when its prohibition is only because of muktzeh, a condition is effective for when it falls, so that he may use it.
(7) But wood adjacent, etc. - See above in siman 518, Mishnah Berurah small paragraph 39, where we explained all the details of this law.
(8) And see above in siman 518, seif 8 - Meaning that there it is explained in the gloss that if he threw bundles onto the roofing, they are not nullified to the roofing. The same applies here with a mitzvah sukkah. See there in Mishnah Berurah.
(9) Only in a sukkah in which he sat once - See Magen Avraham: even if it was not made for the sake of sukkah, meaning it had been made for shade, since by law it is a valid sukkah and he sat in it once, it is again forbidden all the days of the festival. It seems to me that this speaks where he initially designated it for the festival sukkah, or when he sat in it he intended to sit in it all the days of the festival; otherwise it is not sanctified by one sitting so that the name of festival sukkah rests on it, as we wrote above in small paragraph 3. One who has a sukkah built from year to year, nevertheless it is not forbidden until he sits in it during the festival. Although he sat in it the previous year, when the festival passed its sanctity ceased, and another act is needed for its sanctity to take effect. So wrote the Magen Avraham. Some later authorities question this, and it is good to be stringent.
