Whether the wood of the walls or the wood of the roofing - Know that the Rosh's view, copied by the Tur, is that there is no prohibition at all regarding the wood of the walls, for the verse refers only to the roofing. However, the Mechaber ruled plainly in practice like the Rambam, that the wood of the walls is also forbidden, for the walls are necessary to it; without the walls, the sukkah does not stand. They disagreed in explaining him: the Beit Yosef's view is that according to the Rambam the prohibition is only rabbinic, and so wrote the Taz. However, the Bach and several other later authorities hold that according to the Rambam it is a Torah prohibition. So too from the Ran it appears that he holds it is Torah law. See the Itur, who also brought the proof from page 10, where they said there, perhaps from the side; according to Rashi's explanation there, that means from the side of the walls, and the walls are certainly no worse than their decorations; see there. However, he is uncertain, perhaps the meaning there in the Gemara is from the side of the roofing.
Wood adjacent to the sukkah - If he added to the roofing until rain does not fall into it, where some say it is invalid, as in siman 631 in the Magen Avraham, small paragraph 2, although nevertheless all the roofing is forbidden in benefit according to them all seven days, at least rabbinically, and further, he cannot estimate well by approximation what belongs to the roofing, nevertheless if he wants to remove it on Yom Tov through a gentile in order to validate the sukkah so it not be so thick, it is permitted. This is a rabbinic prohibition through a rabbinic act in a place of mitzvah, for it is only rabbinic dismantling of a tent [Peri Megadim].
And if after he made the measure, etc. If he made all four walls plainly, they are not forbidden? See Chiddushei Anshei Shem, who inclines to say that the Ran also holds like Tosafot, that only the measure needed for a valid sukkah is Torah law and what is beyond this is rabbinic; and the Ran's intent regarding all four walls is also that the required measure is Torah law and the excess beyond it is only rabbinic. In my humble opinion this is not clear at all. The Ran's words are similar to what they said in Shabbat 133 regarding circumcision: as long as he is occupied with circumcision, he returns for both shreds that invalidate and shreds that do not invalidate; the reason is that it is all one matter, as Rashi explains. But after he has withdrawn, he does not return for shreds that do not invalidate. Therefore he said: if after he made, etc., he added a wall, meaning that after he completed the measure that validates the sukkah, he then decided that he needs to make another fourth wall, this is an independent matter. But if he made all four, meaning he did not interrupt in between, all four are considered part of the sukkah and all are Torah law. At first glance one might say that this is why he did not say like Rabbeinu Tam, that only the measure needed for a valid sukkah is valid, for who can determine which are included in the sukkah and which are the excess? Say the opposite. It seems more likely to me that he too holds like Rabbeinu Tam, but he explains Rabbeinu Tam's words as meaning that at first he made the valid measure and afterward added. But if he made all the walls one after another in order, they are all equal in law. Therefore the Beit Yosef copied the Ran's words in the Shulchan Aruch, although the view of Rabbeinu Tam was mentioned in Tosafot and the Rosh, because the Ran explains Rabbeinu Tam's words. He differs from Rabbeinu Tam only in this: according to Rabbeinu Tam, even what is more than the valid measure is at least rabbinically forbidden; but he holds that it is not forbidden at all, and in this the Beit Yosef holds like the Ran.
Plainly - It seems to me that this comes to exclude if he specified: these I make for the sake of the sukkah, and these are only an addition.
But if he designated it for a sukkah - Even if he made it for the sake of sukkah, as above in siman 42 in the laws of designation; see there.
It was not forbidden - It seems to me that this was said only regarding the sanctity of the festival that takes effect on the roofing, where we require that he sat in it during the festival. But regarding muktzeh because of dismantling a tent, which is forbidden on every Yom Tov, it does not depend at all on sitting in it; rather, it is forbidden only on Yom Tov, and in such a case it is permitted on Chol HaMoed [Mor U'Ketziah].
For designation is not legally effective - This requires examination: if so, even if he sat in it after the day became sanctified, the whole day should not become forbidden, but only the time that he sat in it, since it had still not been set aside at twilight and had not been set aside for the whole day; when he leaves, it should become permitted [the above Mor U'Ketziah]. According to what he wrote earlier, even if he did not sit in it at all, it is forbidden until Chol HaMoed because it is muktzeh due to dismantling a tent. It appears that his intent is that because of the sanctity of the festival, it is forbidden only for the time that he sat in it.
