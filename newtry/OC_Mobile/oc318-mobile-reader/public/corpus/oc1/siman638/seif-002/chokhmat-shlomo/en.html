Seif 2. "And likewise foods and drinks that are hung in the sukkah to beautify it, etc." NB: The Mechaber wrote simply that sukkah decorations are forbidden and did not distinguish whether they are within four tefachim of the s'chach or not. In my novellae on the sugya of spreading a sheet over it, and in my novellae to siman 629, I wrote proof from the Gemara that the decoration becomes forbidden specifically where it is within four tefachim of the s'chach, in a case where the sukkah remains valid even if the s'chach is a broad object. But if it is four tefachim distant from the s'chach, in a manner where the sukkah would be invalid if the decoration were a broad object, then it is not nullified to the sukkah, is not considered like s'chach, and is not forbidden in benefit. Although wall decorations are also forbidden, that is because they are adjacent to the wall and are wall decoration. But if it is not wall decoration, only hanging from the s'chach and four tefachim distant from the s'chach, then it is permitted in benefit. See there, where I discussed this at length. It requires study why the poskim wrote this without qualification.
Seif 2, in the gloss. "And it appears to me that this is because the mitzvah of sukkah is a man and his household, a man and his wife, etc." NB: See the Taz, who asked that the Rama's words contradict what he wrote in siman 240; see there. In my humble opinion they do not contradict each other at all, as I will explain with God's help. First we must introduce that this matter is compelled from the Gemara: even if one cannot sleep in the sukkah, he is nevertheless obligated in sukkah for eating and drinking. This is from what we read in Sukkah 43: from where do we know sukkah itself, etc., until the end of the sugya, where it derives "teshvu, teshvu" by gezerah shavah. Tosafot there asked why it did not bring the verse of "teshvu" as dwelling, and they later asked that sukkah requires sleeping there too, which does not apply to miluim, for they were not permitted to sleep there. In my humble opinion, one question answers the other. Certainly, where one can sleep there, he is obligated both by day and by night. The Gemara's doubt is where one cannot sleep there at night: is he obligated by day and night, or only by day, for then it is not the time for sleep and he is certainly obligated, but at night, which is the time for sleep, if he cannot sleep there perhaps he is exempt from sukkah altogether? On this the Gemara analyzes whether we derive from miluim that even though one cannot sleep there, he is obligated even at night, or from lulav. The Gemara derives from "teshvu" that even if he cannot sleep there at night he is still obligated at night. If so, on the contrary, from there we derive that even one who cannot sleep is obligated even at night; where he can sleep, we derive from "teshvu" in the manner of dwelling. However, the Mordechai holds that since it is proven in several places that something in one's power, even by way of issur, is called in one's power because in any event he can do it, so too here: regarding miluim, they were at least able to sleep there, only the Torah's mitzvah prevented them from sleeping; this is not considered coercion, for if he wished he could do it. So too with sukkah, where one can sleep there but does not want to because he wishes to fulfill the mitzvah of onah, and because of the mitzvah of onah he refrains from sleeping there, though if he wished he could sleep there, he remains obligated in sukkah for eating even at night. But where he is distressed or the like, so that he cannot sleep there because it is an unavoidable circumstance, there is no proof from "teshvu" of miluim, for there it was in his power, whereas here it is not in his power even to sleep there. This is then learned again from "teshvu" in the manner of dwelling, and if he cannot sleep there, he has not fulfilled even through eating. Therefore the Rama's words do not contradict one another. On the contrary, this is proof why the Rama gave this reason and rejected the first reason based on cold, for the Taz's question would be difficult: if so, he would not fulfill even through eating. Therefore he gives the reason that he can sleep there and it is like miluim. This resolves it. Examine carefully. This appears correct to me, with God's help, in resolving the matter. See the Magen Avraham's question about the Gemara contradicting itself, and see what I wrote in my novellae to siman 631, in my novellae to that sugya, resolving that question properly. What I wrote there depends on whether, when one cannot sleep, he fulfills through eating or not. Examine carefully.
