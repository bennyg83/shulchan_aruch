And on Yom Tov and Shabbat it is forbidden, etc. In the Tur, in the name of Ba'al Halachot Gedolot, it is written that on Yom Tov it is permitted to move them, and on Shabbat it is forbidden to move them. Our teacher the Tur wrote about this: I do not understand what distinction there is regarding moving them in their place between Yom Tov and Shabbat. The Beit Yosef further objected that Ba'al Halachot Gedolot ruled like R. Shimon, who has no muktzeh on Yom Tov and Shabbat, as he wrote in the laws of Yom Tov in his name; and he found no resolution to this difficulty. Therefore the Rema ruled here that on Yom Tov too it is forbidden. In my humble opinion, this can be compared to what is stated in Yevamot, chapter Cheresh, page 114: Abba Shaul says, we practiced nursing from a kosher animal on Yom Tov. The Gemara asks: if there is danger, then even on Shabbat too; if there is no danger, even on Yom Tov it should be forbidden. It answers: it is a case of distress, and he holds that extracting in an unusual manner applies. Meaning: specifically milking by hand is extracting something from its place of growth and is a Torah liability, but nursing is a change, meaning an unusual manner. Regarding Shabbat, where the ordinary extraction prohibition is stoning, the rabbis decreed regarding nursing; regarding Yom Tov, where the prohibition is a negative commandment, the rabbis did not decree. We see that in a matter dependent on a decree, Shabbat is stricter than Yom Tov. So too Ba'al Halachot Gedolot holds regarding decorations that fell, with respect to moving: there is no disgrace of the mitzvah here, and nevertheless it is forbidden, as the Beit Yosef wrote nearby in the name of the Ran: since he made them subordinate to the sukkah, he made them like it, etc.; and if he benefits from them even after falling, there is disgrace of the mitzvah; therefore a condition is nothing, for just as the sukkah is forbidden, so too its decorations. End quote. Ba'al Halachot Gedolot holds that with sukkah wood, where there is a connection to a Torah prohibition because of "festival," etc., it is obvious one must be stringent even after it fell, even regarding moving. But decorations, where the prohibition is only rabbinic because of disgrace of a mitzvah even while they are standing, and they were stringent also after they fell regarding eating, nevertheless it is not fitting to prohibit moving them after falling, except that the sages made their measures uniform with sukkah wood and decreed against moving the decorations because of moving sukkah wood, where there is a connection to Torah prohibition. Therefore it is understandable that there is a distinction between Shabbat and Yom Tov, for Shabbat is stricter regarding decrees. But regarding something that depends only on muktzeh, with no connection to a Torah prohibition, it is permitted both on Shabbat and on Yom Tov according to Ba'al Halachot Gedolot, who rules like R. Shimon, as the Beit Yosef wrote in his question. This seems correct and clear to resolve the difficulty of our teacher the Tur and the Beit Yosef's difficulty against Ba'al Halachot Gedolot.

Before the first twilight. For otherwise, since the prohibition takes effect at the first twilight, it automatically takes effect during all of them, for regarding this it is like one long day, and a condition no longer helps.
The practice is not to stipulate. Because people are not properly expert in conditions.
That there are those who say it is not forbidden even with the walls themselves, etc. These words are not clear, for no decisor holds that even wall decorations are permitted, all the more so the walls themselves, which is an explicit Talmud in the first chapter of Sukkah, as I wrote at the beginning of this siman. Rather, the Beit Yosef wrote so in explaining the words of the Rosh, but not precisely, as I wrote there. However, it seems to me to be lenient with painted sheets where there is concern because of thieves: we are witnesses that he did not hang them there from the outset to remain there permanently, and therefore they were not set aside at all, even if he did not stipulate. We find many similar cases. Even regarding the prohibition of interest, the Rema wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 169, seif 8: it is possible that even without specification it is as though they stipulated so, since that is the custom.
