(10) That are hung in the sukkah, etc. - Whether they are hung from the roofing or from the walls.
(11) In order to beautify it - If he knows that the children will eat from them, it is better not to hang them, lest they detach the strings and come to desecrate Shabbat, and also lest they come to eat them. However, if he can hang them high enough that the children cannot reach them, or if he has no children, it is a mitzvah to hang sukkah decorations [Eliyah Rabbah in the name of Sefer Chasidim 263]. The Shelah also wrote that it is proper to beautify the sukkah with hangings and sheets and to hang important fruit in it.
(12) It is forbidden to make use of them - For it is a disgrace to the mitzvah when one makes use of them.
(13) Even if they fell - Since they were set aside at twilight, they are set aside for the entire Yom Tov, and even if they fell on Chol HaMoed they are forbidden.
(14) And on Yom Tov and Shabbat, etc. - But on Chol HaMoed there is no prohibition of moving them. Therefore, if they fell, it is permitted to move them and return them to their place [Tur].
(15) It is forbidden to move them, etc. - This means even according to those who permit muktzeh on Yom Tov above in siman 495 as on Shabbat; with this type of muktzeh, all agree it is forbidden, because they were set aside for their mitzvah.
(16) At the time he hung them - This comes to exclude the thought that it is specifically near twilight.
(17) So during twilight - For there is a beginning of twilight and an end of twilight, and we require that he not separate himself from them throughout the entire twilight period.
(18) Of eight days - Meaning, to exclude when he says, "I am not separating myself from them during all of twilight of the first eve of Yom Tov," for then sanctity takes effect in any event during the twilight of the other days, and it is forbidden from the second day of Yom Tov until the end of Yom Tov. If he said plainly, "I am not separating myself from them during all twilight periods," it is possible that all eight days are included in this.
(19) Whenever he wishes - Meaning even while they are hanging; and all the more so the condition helps to benefit from them when they fall.
(20) And if he said, etc. - This also speaks where he said it at the time he hung them, or at least before twilight.
(21) The condition before, etc. - For once twilight enters, it is set aside. According to what we concluded above in siman 261, that twilight begins immediately after sunset, he must make the condition before sunset.
(22) The first - For otherwise, since the prohibition takes effect at the first twilight, it automatically takes effect during all of them, for regarding this it is like one long day, and a condition no longer helps.
(23) The practice is not to stipulate - Because people are not properly expert in conditions. Some later authorities question this reason, for the wording of the condition is explicit in the Gemara, and expertise is not relevant.
(24) All the more so regarding decorations - Many later authorities objected to this, and their view is that sukkah decorations are certainly forbidden without a condition by law, for decorations are like the roofing. See the Taz, who agreed to be lenient with painted sheets when there is concern because of thieves: we are witnesses that he did not hang them there from the outset to remain there permanently, and therefore they were not set aside at all, even without a condition. Nevertheless, at the outset it is good to stipulate as the Rema wrote, namely that before twilight he should say: "I am not separating myself from them during all of twilight," as above. One who intends to remove the sukkah decorations on Yom Tov because of rain or thieves should be careful from the outset not to tie the decorations in the sukkah with a full knot, for it is forbidden to untie them on Yom Tov; rather, he should tie them with a bow. One should not make the sukkah walls from shatnez curtains, and likewise should not hang shatnez as decoration unless they are higher than human use. It is forbidden to engrave the verse "in sukkot you shall dwell" on a gourd or the like and hang it in the sukkah, for it will lead to disgrace; also, it is forbidden to write verses from the Torah unless they contain an entire book, as explained in Yoreh De'ah siman 283. Although people are accustomed to permit this for learning from them, that is specifically for study, but without need it is forbidden [later authorities]. They further wrote that even after Sukkot one should not step on sukkah wood, for it is an article used for a mitzvah, like tzitzit and lulav. See above, siman 21, Mishnah Berurah small paragraph 6. Therefore one should protest against people who, after Sukkot, throw the roofing wood outside in a place where many people commonly step on it, even if it is not a garbage place.
(25) If he roofed with myrtle - Although ordinary myrtle is intended for smelling, here, since he roofed with it as with other wood, we say that by treating it as wood he set it aside from use and burning, but not from its smell. Indeed, if he had hung it in the sukkah as decoration, he certainly set it aside from smelling [Peri Megadim]. With an etrog, even if he hung it as decoration, it is permitted to smell it, for its primary purpose is eating and not smelling; therefore we say he set it aside from eating, but not from smell.
(26) And some prohibit with myrtle - But with an etrog, according to all opinions it is permitted, for its primary purpose is eating and he set it aside from eating. See Taz, small paragraph 3, who ruled to permit like the first view; so too the Derech HaChayim copied.
