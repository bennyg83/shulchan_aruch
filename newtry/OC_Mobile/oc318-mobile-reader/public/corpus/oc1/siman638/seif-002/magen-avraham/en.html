Of eight days. The Yam Shel Shlomo wrote that this is an excessive stringency, for since he said plainly, "I am not separating myself from them during all twilight," all eight days are implied.
The practice is not to stipulate. The Yam Shel Shlomo wrote that this follows the custom of Austria, but in other places the practice is to stipulate. It seems to me that Maharil follows his own approach, that one must stand at twilight and stipulate; but for us, who may stipulate while it is still day, expertise is not relevant, for the language of the condition is explicit in the Gemara.
All the more so regarding decorations. This requires examination, for it implies that regarding decorations the Rosh agrees that they are decorations of the roofing, and so it appears in the Gemara, page 10b, which answers: perhaps from the side; this explicitly implies that decorations from the side are forbidden. So too it appears in the Tur, who wrote a distinction between walls and roofing specifically regarding wood, but regarding decorations he wrote no distinction. So too it is explicit in Responsa Rosh, rule 22, section 8, in the name of the Yerushalmi; see there, section 24. The Darchei Moshe wrote: one should not tie the decorations, because it is forbidden to untie them on Yom Tov; rather, he should make a bow. See siman 317. One should not hang shatnez in the sukkah unless it is higher than human use [Darchei Moshe, Maharil, Hagahot Minhagim]. See Yoreh De'ah siman 301, seif 1 and seif 11. Those who engrave the verse "in sukkot you shall dwell" on a gourd do not act properly, as written in Yoreh De'ah siman 283 in the Beit Yosef and Taz. It seems to me that even after Sukkot one should not step on sukkah wood, for it is an article used for a mitzvah, like tzitzit and lulav, as stated in chapter 4 of Megillah.
