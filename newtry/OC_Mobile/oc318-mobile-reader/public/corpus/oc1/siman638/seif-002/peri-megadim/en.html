And see siman 518; see Taz. It implies that he concludes it is forbidden here even beyond the valid measure of the sukkah with respect to walls, and all the more so with roofing, which is Torah law. See Levush here and in 518, where it also implies that it is forbidden. With a bundle in the roofing, the Taz permits there. One can say that this is on the roofing, but with a wall, whenever he intended to strengthen and thicken it, a bundle is obviously forbidden, for it was set aside for it. What the Yam Shel Shlomo wrote in the Magen Avraham here, letter 1, see there: to thicken and strengthen it, even after it was completed, is forbidden like a sukkah decoration. A condition is effective at least regarding a sukkah decoration; but when it was not to make it, it is permitted in a mitzvah sukkah regarding what is more than the measure that he made afterward by leaning wood against it. But walls beyond the measure may be forbidden. What is written in 518, letter 11, refers to an ordinary sukkah, not a mitzvah sukkah. With God's help this will be explained in the Magen Avraham.
And know that the Rav who glossed "wood adjacent to the sukkah" seems at first to mean a wall; but this is all the more so from what the Mechaber wrote about more than the valid measure of the sukkah. To say that it refers to the roofing, although that is Torah law and he speaks on Chol HaMoed without a bundle, is somewhat difficult. Know that if it was roofed properly and he added more and then removes it, there is no Torah prohibition but only rabbinic, because it appears like dismantling; and with a bundle it is permitted. With a wall, whenever it is not seen with it and a wall will remain, it is also rabbinic. See Magen Avraham 518, letters 11 and 12; with God's help, this will be further explained here in the Magen Avraham. If so, if he added to the roofing until rain does not fall into it, where some say it is invalid as in siman 631, Magen Avraham 2, although all the roofing is nevertheless forbidden in benefit for other purposes all seven days, at least rabbinically, and further, he cannot estimate well by approximation what belongs to the roofing, nevertheless if he wants to remove it on Yom Tov through a gentile to validate the sukkah so that it not be so thick, it is permitted: it is a rabbinic prohibition through a rabbinic act in a place of mitzvah, since it is not dismantling a tent but only like dismantling a tent rabbinically, as stated.
