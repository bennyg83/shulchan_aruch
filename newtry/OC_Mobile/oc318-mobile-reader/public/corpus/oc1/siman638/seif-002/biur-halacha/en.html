It is forbidden to make use of them, etc. - This is if he hung them before the first twilight. If he hung them on the first day or on Chol HaMoed, its prohibition begins from the first twilight after that until the end of Yom Tov; but from the time of hanging until evening it is not forbidden [so is proven below].
All eight - See Responsa Chatam Sofer, Orach Chayim siman 184, regarding one who had an extra etrog and hung it in the sukkah as decoration, and afterward on Yom Tov someone came from a village who had not found an etrog to buy. He ruled that it is permitted to take the etrog hanging in the sukkah as decoration and fulfill the mitzvah with it. Although on Yom Tov it is forbidden to move because of muktzeh, nevertheless on Chol HaMoed, when it is permitted to move it, it is also permitted to fulfill the mitzvah with it, for mitzvot were not given for benefit. Further, the prohibition of benefit is because of disgrace to the mitzvah, and here there is no disgrace, for at first it was only a mitzvah decoration and now it is the mitzvah itself, and one recites a blessing over it. See there further, where he discusses this at length and concludes that the owner of the etrog may also take money for the price of the etrog; see there for his reason.
And on Yom Tov and Shabbat it is forbidden - If the decorations fell onto the table and it is impossible to eat, some say that because of food preparation on Yom Tov it is permitted to move the muktzeh and remove them. The Peri Megadim concludes that if it is possible by moving indirectly, such as shaking the board or the like, he should do so and not move them by hand; see there. In my humble opinion, if many splinters of the roofing fall onto the table, it is possible that it is permitted even on Shabbat to move them in order to remove them if one is disgusted by it, just like a chamber pot.
For they are muktzeh - This means whether they are hanging on the wall or whether they fell.
