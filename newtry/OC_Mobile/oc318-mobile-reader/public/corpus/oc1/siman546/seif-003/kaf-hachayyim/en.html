3) There. Marriages are not held during the festival, etc. The Talmud discusses the reason for the prohibition at length and derives from a verse that one joy may not be mixed with another. Hagahos Sefer Mitzvos Katan writes: 'From here, some take care not to hold two chuppahs on one day. They also bring a proof from the Yerushalmi, which derives from a verse that one joy may not be mixed with another, based on Lavan's statement to Yaakov, 'Complete the week of this one, etc.' This proof is incorrect. There the two joys belonged to one person, for Yaakov married two women. Likewise, regarding the festival, the joy of the woman he marries and the joy of the festival both belong to him. Mixing joys is therefore applicable. Regarding two chuppahs held for two women and two men, however, mixing one joy with another is not applicable. My teacher, Rabbi Yechiel, therefore had the custom to hold the chuppah of an orphaned young man or woman together with the chuppah of one of his children, all on one day. Regarding two sisters, however, Sefer Chassidim writes that their chuppahs should not be held on one day. Some editions state that they should not be held even during the same week, and the custom should not be changed.' These are the words cited by Bach and Taz, subsection 1. Tzavaas Rabbi Yehudah HaChassid, subsection 36, however, writes that even two chuppahs for two men and two women should not be held in the same week, because some unfavorable occurrence may befall them. See there. Sefer Chassidim, siman 477, writes likewise. Yad Aharon, Hagahos Beis Yosef, cites this. See Even HaEzer, siman 62, se'if 2.
