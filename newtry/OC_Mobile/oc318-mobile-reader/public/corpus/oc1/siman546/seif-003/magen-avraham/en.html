And to make a feast on the festival — for the main joy is only the beginning of the marriage; and even though they rejoice at the wedding feast all seven days it is permitted, end quote Rashba; and in another responsum of Rashba it is found to forbid making the wedding feast on the festival even if he married on the eve of the festival — and so in Rambam; and since these are rabbinic matters we rule leniently, end quote Beit Yosef — and his words are astonishing, for behold Tosafos in Moed Katan proved this is a complete d'oraisa derivation, and also in Kesubos daf 47 they wrote it is d'oraisa; and further in Sukkah daf 25 it is explicit: if they married on the eve of the festival it is permitted to make a feast on the festival (see siman 496 seif 6); and likewise in Moed Katan in the Gemara; therefore it appears to me the first night is forbidden to make a feast, for it is stated in the Gemara that the main joy is only one day, and the first night is drawn after the day — joy begins by day and ends at night; and likewise per the one who says because of trouble at the first feast there is extra trouble — but on the other days of the festival it is permitted to make a feast, for the main joy of marriage has already passed. And Tosafos wrote in Kesubos daf 47: even one hour before Yom Tov, which is the addition of Yom Tov and forbidden in melacha, it is permitted to marry; and it appears to me this is specifically by Torah law, but rabbinically it is forbidden to marry at that hour, for it is like acquisition of acquisition, as written siman 339; however if he did not accept Yom Tov upon himself at that hour it is permitted.
