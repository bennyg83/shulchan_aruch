(s.k. 4) And to make, etc. For it is a genuine derashah; and he brought proof from what we say in the first chapter of Chagigah: that even though each and every one of Israel fulfills the mitzvah of the simcha offering that he is obligated in on the festival even if he brings vows and freewill offerings that he vowed and pledged before Yom Tov from animal maaser, etc., nevertheless he does not fulfill simcha with menachos and a bird offering, for it is written "and you shall rejoice on your festival," that the joy should be on your festival with a species through which one fulfills the Chagigah offering that each person is obligated in on Yom Tov; with menachos and birds one does not fulfill Chagigah. Rav Ashi said: we do not need reliance [on juxtaposition]; and "and you shall rejoice on your festival" itself: rather from the word "and you shall rejoice" itself we learn that there is no joy except with the meat of an animal. And [the Gemara] asks: if so, according to Rav Ashi, why do I need the verse "and you shall rejoice on your festival"? And it answers: it is needed [to teach] that one does not marry women on the moed, for we expound "and you shall rejoice on your festival, and not with your wife." Behold, then, it is not an asmachta but a genuine derashah; for it answers that this [verse] is needed [to teach] that one does not marry women, etc. And this proof Tosafos also wrote in Kesubos folio 47.
And further in Sukkah folio 25 it is explicitly implied, etc. For we say there that a chasan and his shoshvinin are exempt from the sukkah, for there is no joy except under the chuppah, and it is impossible to make a chuppah in the sukkah; and this is clear below in siman 640, se'if 6, see there. And how will you find [this case]? Necessarily it deals with one who married on erev ha-regel, and the days of the chuppah continue into the festival; and so wrote Rashi there. And even if you say it deals with one who married some days before the festival and not on erev ha-regel, nevertheless this and that are one and the same: since it is permitted if he married two days before the festival even though the seven days of feasting continue into the festival, the same law applies to one who marries on erev ha-regel. And further, it teaches explicitly there that a chasan and all the shoshvinin are exempt from the sukkah all seven; this implies that all seven days of the chuppah are within the festival; and necessarily it deals with one who married on erev ha-regel. In another responsum [regarding] a wedding meal on the festival: if so, Magen Avraham's first difficulty is on the Beis Yosef, who wrote that since these are rabbinic matters we take the lenient side; for it is proven that it is according to the Torah, and it is proper to be stringent if there were a doubt in the matter. And afterward he asked that it is proven explicitly in the Gemara of Sukkah that it is permitted and there is no doubt; and this is difficult also for the responsum of the Rashba. For it is stated in the Gemara that the main joy is only one day; and that is what Magen Avraham wrote at the beginning of this subparagraph, that the main joy is only the beginning of the nissuin; and those are the words of the Gemara. And possibly his intent is also to resolve that responsum of the Rashba who forbids a wedding meal on the festival, that is to say, specifically the first night; but the wording of the Rashba responsum that the Beis Yosef cites is forced according to this. And Tosafos wrote in Kesubos folio 47 "even one hour," etc.: those are the words of Tosafos that I brought above in s.k. 1, in what he wrote to resolve that [case of] delivering her on Shabbosos and Yomim Tovim.
And this is specifically according to Torah law. That is to say: Tosafos come to resolve the words of the Gemara that establish the verse "he acquires for the father [the right] to deliver her to the chuppah" as speaking of delivering her on Shabbosos and Yomim Tovim: properly, one wishes to say that the verse deals with one hour before Yom Tov, when according to Torah law it is then permitted to marry, for then there is no concern of "and you shall rejoice on your festival, and not with your wife"; but nevertheless by rabbinic law it is forbidden, for it is like acquiring a kinyan, which is forbidden also in the added [time of] Yom Tov.
And it appears to me that this is for the reason I wrote: that it is forbidden to make nissuin on the first night, and that means on Yom Tov; therefore they practice a prohibition close to nightfall on erev Yom Tov; which is not so on Shabbos, for there is no prohibition of mixing joy with joy, and also he has not yet accepted Shabbos, so there is no prohibition of acquiring a kinyan; therefore they were accustomed to marry on erev Shabbos close to nightfall; and see what the Rama wrote in siman 339, se'if 4.
