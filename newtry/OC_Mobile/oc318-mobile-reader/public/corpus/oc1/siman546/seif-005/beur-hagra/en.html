Se'if 5. And that is provided she can, etc. See Be'er HaGolah; and so wrote the Maggid Mishneh. And this requires further study: what does "admits" mean? And it appears that he relied on the Yerushalmi, as is his way in many places where the sugya is clearer there. And see the Raavad in the hasagos, chapter 3 of Kerias Shema; and in the Maggid Mishneh, chapter 18 of Shabbos, halacha 2; and see the Rashba at the beginning of chapter 4 of Nedarim; and there: some say she should not plaster, etc. Two Amoraim, R' Chananya and R' Mana: one said they disputed about lime that she can remove within the moed, but regarding lime that she removes only after the moed, all agree it is forbidden; and the other said they disputed about lime that she removes after the moed, but regarding lime that she can remove within the moed, all agree it is permitted; and we do not know who said this and who said that. From what R' Chanina [said,] R' Yosa in the name of R' Yochanan: R' Yehuda is consistent with his view: just as R' Yehuda said there that distress of an hour is distress, so he says here that disfigurement of an hour is disfigurement; that [Amora] said they disputed about lime that she can remove within the moed, but regarding lime that she removes only after the moed, all agree it is forbidden. And with this the conclusion there rises: that R' Chanina and R' Yehuda hold so; and not like the distinctions they made in the Gemara regarding repayment, where Rav Nachman bar Yitzchak said, etc.
And she removes hair from the place, etc. The Rif's version: and she applies rouge to her face; and another version: and she passes a knife over her lower body; and in the Yerushalmi there: she removes her hair and her nails and passes an implement over her face. Said R' Yudan, father of R' Matanya: the Mishnah is in clean language; that is to say, her lower body. And this "removes hair" is not a [head] haircut, since it did not say "and she cuts" or "and she shaves," but rather it speaks of the body; and as Tosafos wrote in Moed Katan 18a; and so explained the Prisha regarding what is written in mourning laws, that a woman is permitted in removing hair, etc. Even though the Rambam there permits, that is because of the sugya of "HaCholetz," where they said "what [is true] in a place where it is permitted," etc.; and see in Tur Yoreh De'ah siman 390. But here [it is] so that they not enter the festival while disfigured; and this decree applies to a woman as well. And it is impossible that it refers to the place of ervah, for afterward it says "and she passes," etc.; rather it refers to the armpit. And since it said "she passes a knife," and it said "and she removes her hair and her nails," it is permitted whether by hand or with an implement, as is written above in siman 532; and that is what [the Mechaber] said "whether," etc.
And she passes a knife. Tur; and like the Rosh's version: and she passes a knife over her forehead; and another version, etc.
