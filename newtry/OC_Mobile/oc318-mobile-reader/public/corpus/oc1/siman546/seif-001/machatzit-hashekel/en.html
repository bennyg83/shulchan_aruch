(ד) Since it challenges Rav's words against each other — meaning that which the Talmud rejected "perhaps he gave her on Shabbatot and festivals" — this is to maintain the first question, for Rav holds the act of the maiden daughter belongs to the father — the verse "when a man sells" etc. deals with a minor, and from that the Merciful One granted her to be given for huppah there is no proof, for perhaps it deals when he gave her on Shabbatot and festivals. On this Tosafot challenged: according to Rav, from Torah law one may not marry women on the festival, for Rav derives in tractate Moed Katan what the mishnah teaches that one may not marry women on the festival from the verse "and you shall rejoice in your festival" — and this is a full derashah. If so, from Torah law it is forbidden to marry on Yom Tov — how did he reject according to Rav that he gave her on Yom Tov? And what Tosafot conclude at the end — because one does not mix joy with joy, and this is Shmuel's reason — see in Biur HaGra in tractate Ketubot; but we hold like Shmuel etc. And it must be said that Magen Avraham holds that which the tanna of the house of Shmuel taught — they betroth but do not consummate, and also the betrothal feast from which the question on Tosafot as explained — this tanna of the house of Shmuel and Shmuel follow his view that the reason is logical that one does not mix etc. If so, all rejoicings are forbidden, even minor rejoicing, meaning marriage without a feast. And it must be said according to Magen Avraham's words that for Rav who derives from the verse "and you shall rejoice in your festival" — one only forbids marriage with a feast, for thus the verse implies: the joy of the feast that you make for festival needs shall be in your festival and not in your wife. If so what is Tosafot's question on this answer? And he wrote as follows: even though we say in HaCholets that marriage without a feast there is joy — as explained — nevertheless according to Magen Avraham it is that marriage without a feast has joy — what of it? Nevertheless it is a Torah decree that the Torah only forbade marriage with a feast. And it must be said that Tosafot too have a question, for the verse can be explained as Magen Avraham said — the joy of the feast that you make for festival needs etc. — and accordingly only marriage with a feast is forbidden. Nevertheless from where do we know that this is not a Torah decree? For we say there daf 8: Rabbah on Rosh Hashana said the reason one may not marry etc. because one leaves the joy of the festival and occupies himself with the joy of his wife, and Abbaye said to R' Yitzchak: that which Rabbah on Rosh Hashana is Rav — for he said it is written "and you shall rejoice in your festival" etc. — behold for Rav the reason is one leaves the joy of the festival etc., and if so it is impossible that marriage without a feast is full joy — for Rav too would forbid from this verse "and you shall rejoice in your festival," and therefore the explanation of the verse is "and you shall rejoice in your festival and not in your wife" — that the joy of his wife on the festival should not be any joy, even marriage without a feast. Granted if marriage without a feast there is no joy — fine; but since it is proven in Yevamot chapter HaCholets that marriage without a feast also has joy — if so the joy of marriage without a feast equals the joy of marriage with a feast according to Tosafot's view — and on this Tosafot answered that marriage without a feast has no joy as follows etc., and if so we establish the verse "and you shall rejoice in your festival" more reasonably — namely marriage with a feast where there is extra joy — specifically forbidden, and we explain the verse as Magen Avraham said: the joy of the feast that you make for festival needs etc.
