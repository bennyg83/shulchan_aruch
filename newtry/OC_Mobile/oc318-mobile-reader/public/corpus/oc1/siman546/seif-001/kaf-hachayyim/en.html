1) [Se'if 1] Marriages are not held during the festival, etc. The reasons are that one joy may not be mixed with another, that holding a marriage entails exertion during the festival, and that otherwise everyone might wait to marry until the festival, when a meal is already being made, thereby neglecting the mitzvah to be fruitful and multiply. All this is stated in Moed Katan 8b. It is clear there that marriage is forbidden even without a meal. Magen Avraham, subsection 1. Ritva likewise writes in his novellae to the Talmud there, page 18b, that marriage is a joy even without a meal, while the Torah states, 'You shall rejoice in your festival,' and not with your wife. See there. Birkei Yosef, subsection 1, cites this and writes that the Talmud there likewise proves that marriage is forbidden even without a meal. See there. Tosafos, Kesubos 47a, s.v. demasar, according to one resolution, write that marriage without a meal is permitted because it is not considered so great a joy. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 2, explains that Tosafos speaks under Torah law, but concedes that marriage alone is forbidden rabbinically, as Maharsha writes there. See there. Birkei Yosef there, Bigdei Yesha, and Chemed Moshe, subsection 1, write likewise. Maamar Mordechai, subsection 1, also proves from the Talmud and halachic authorities that marriage without a meal is forbidden. See there. Chayei Adam, rule 117, subsection 1; Mikraei Kodesh, siman 59, subsection 1; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 1, write likewise.
