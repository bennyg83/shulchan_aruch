And it is permitted to betroth. See the Taz, Moed Katan 18b. And at first glance this is according to the initial assumption; but according to the conclusion, erusin without a meal has no joy, and without this erusin would be permitted anyway; rather, the practical difference is for writing the tena'im. And as to what he wrote to forbid a first meal after the shidduchin, one can say according to his view in letter 3 that a first meal of nissuin on the festival is forbidden, and the same law applies on Chol HaMoed, and the same law applies to a first meal of tena'im, for it is joy for him; and the same law applies to a meal of erusin: one can say that a first meal after erusin is forbidden; and that which the Shulchan Aruch permits to feast not at the time of erusin means a second meal; and see Pesachim, "Makom SheNahagu." However, in the house of the chasan without the kallah, one can say that he admits it is permitted; and see Eliyah Rabbah, who permits. And in the Shulchan Aruch it implies without qualification that whenever there was erusin or nissuin before the moed, a first meal on the moed is permitted; all the more so a first meal after tena'im that took place before the moed. And one can say that even if it was on the moed, so long as it is not at the time of the writing it is permitted. And erusin with a meal, that is, to effect kiddushin at a meal, is forbidden; see Magen Avraham 2. And a meal of tena'im one can say is permitted even at the time of the writing. And one can say regarding this that since the Taz is stringent in every respect [it remains difficult].
