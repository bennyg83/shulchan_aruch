And it is permitted to betroth — lest another precede him with mercy; and according to this reason all the more so it is permitted to write conditions for matchmaking on chol hamoed as is our custom; however it requires further study regarding the feast that they are accustomed to make after writing the conditions — whether this is in place of an engagement feast and forbidden as written shortly, or let us say this feast is not for the engagement feast of one who already betrothed, for then specifically the matter of not mixing joy with joy applies; and it appears to me it is forbidden, for perforce it is not specifically an engagement feast, for a circumcision feast would also be forbidden if not for the reason that its time is fixed; and likewise pidyon haben — automatically this feast is forbidden. And further proof from what we say in chapter Eilu Metzi'os daf 18: that which they say these write kiddushin of women on the festival — and they sought to establish it there as documents of stipulation, per R' Gidal who was stringent: how much do you give your son so-and-so and how much your daughter so-and-so — they stood and betrothed — these are matters acquired by speech; evidently documents of stipulation are included in kiddushin generally; and writing our conditions are also like documents of stipulation; therefore it appears to me this feast is forbidden on chol hamoed, and all the more on the festival when he feasts with the bride the first time after writing our conditions:
