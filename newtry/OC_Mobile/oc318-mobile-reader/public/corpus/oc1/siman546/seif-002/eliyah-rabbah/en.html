[2] But it is permitted to betroth, provided that, etc. But nissuin even without a meal is forbidden; see se'if 3; and so concludes Ra'avach. And Magen Avraham and Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah wondered, for in Kesubos folio 47 Tosafos wrote that nissuin without a meal is permitted; and in Chokhmat Shlomo he strained over this, that [Tosafos] derive from what is written "and you shall rejoice on your festivals, and not with your wife," and if so the first Tanna [speaks of] the joy of the meal, etc.; and Magen Avraham answered that [Tosafos] deal according to Rav, but according to Shmuel it is forbidden. And that is not so, for in Moed Katan folio 19 it is explicit in a beraisa that according to everyone nissuin without a meal is forbidden, as R' Shimon wrote, and he does not hold of [Magen Avraham's] answer; also what Tosafos wrote there from chapter "HaCholetz" poses no difficulty according to his words; also in Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah he wrote a reason to the opposite effect, see there. And what appears to my humble opinion regarding the main difficulty of Tosafos is that they speak according to the Torah, but by rabbinic law they admit that it is forbidden through nissuin alone; and so it is implied in Maharsha there.
