Ibid., seif 4: One who departs with a caravan into the wilderness, etc., and one who ascends to Eretz Yisrael, etc. Note: See the Ran in the chapter Rabbi Eliezer DeMilah, where he cites the dispute between the Baal HaMaor and Ramban concerning a case in which the hot water intended for after circumcision was spilled. The question is whether one may perform the circumcision because at present it can be done permissibly and afterward danger to life will override Shabbos, or whether it is forbidden because he will subsequently have to desecrate Shabbos. According to those who permit circumcision there, one cannot challenge them from the present ruling, which forbids departure within three days before Shabbos rather than saying that he now departs permissibly and danger to life will afterward override Shabbos. One may answer that a mitzvah is different: only circumcision was permitted in this fashion because it is a mitzvah whose appointed time is today, unlike a discretionary journey. Conversely, according to those who forbid circumcision there, one cannot ask why the present seif permits departure for a mitzvah even on Friday. First, Friday differs from Shabbos itself. Second, there it is certain that he will afterward have to desecrate Shabbos, whereas here it is only doubtful, for he stipulates with the caravan that they rest, and even apart from this there is no clearly established danger requiring him to desecrate Shabbos. It is therefore unlike the case of circumcision. Examine this carefully. As for the implication here that ascending to Eretz Yisrael is unquestionably a mitzvah even in the present era, see Beis Shmuel, Even HaEzer, siman 75, who cites differing views on this point. Tosafot maintain that there is no mitzvah to ascend to Eretz Yisrael in the present era. According to their view, the same should apply here: the journey would not be considered a mitzvah, and it would be forbidden to depart within three days before Shabbos. Examine this carefully.
