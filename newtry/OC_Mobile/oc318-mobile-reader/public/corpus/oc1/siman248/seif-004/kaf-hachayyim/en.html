Tur and those who follow him, however, rule like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, who says that no stipulation is required, as explained in Tur and Beis Yosef; see there. Levush rules like the Shulchan Aruch that a stipulation is required. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1; Chayei Adam, klal 4, paragraph 1. Nevertheless, when it is impossible to stipulate, such as on a steamship that has a fixed departure time because of the cargo aboard and does not wait for individuals, or on a ship whose passengers are mostly non-Jews and they will certainly not heed his request to rest, and waiting for another ship might prevent him from performing the mitzvah, one may be lenient. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1, and Shulchan Aruch HaRav there write the same. This applies only where he will not subsequently need to commit a prohibition on Shabbos. If he will subsequently need to commit a prohibition on Shabbos, he must specifically stipulate, as stated below in seif 4, even if this will prevent the mitzvah. Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 3, writes the same.
4) Bach writes that Rambam makes clear that the dispute between Rebbi and Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel applies only when one departs on Friday for the purpose of a mitzvah. When one sets sail three days before Shabbos, even Rebbi agrees that he need not stipulate that the shipmaster rest, since he commits no prohibition. Other commentators, however, imply that they also disagree regarding departure three days before Shabbos: according to Rebbi, a stipulation is required, while according to Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel it is not. Tur's words also imply this; see there. Rav Yaakov Castro in Hagahos HaTur similarly writes that according to Rambam and Sefer Mitzvos Gadol, only one who departs on Friday for the purpose of a mitzvah must stipulate, but one who departs three days earlier need not stipulate. According to Rosh and Tur, however, it appears that even one who departs three days earlier must stipulate. He adds that the Shulchan Aruch appears to rule like Rambam and Sefer Mitzvos Gadol, that only one who departs on Friday for the purpose of a mitzvah must stipulate, whereas one who departs three days earlier need not; see there. This matter is therefore disputed. Consequently, ab initio, even when departing three days before Shabbos, one should stipulate with the non-Jew that he rest on Shabbos if possible. If it is not possible, one may be lenient.
