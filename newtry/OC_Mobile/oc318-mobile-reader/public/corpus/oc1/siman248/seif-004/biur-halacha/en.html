And on Sunday, Monday, and Tuesday it is permitted to depart - This follows the position of the Baal HaMaor as explained in the Beit Yosef; see there. According to this view, the night belonging to Wednesday is considered within the three days preceding Shabbos.
And he stipulates with them that they rest - See the Mishnah Berurah, where it is stated that this stipulation is indispensable. This is certainly so according to the Magen Avraham in seif katan 1, who maintains that even when embarking on a ship the stipulation is indispensable, for the rule in seif 4 is derived from that case. Even according to the other Acharonim, who hold that the stipulation there is not indispensable, it appears that no leniency is possible here. Quite apart from this, many authorities question the ruling in this seif, which the Mechaber and Rama stated without qualification above in seif 1 in the gloss. It derives from the position of the Baal HaMaor, and is difficult to understand: how could they permit a person, for the sake of a mitzvah, to place himself in a situation in which he will actively desecrate Shabbos? Even at the beginning of the week, when he knows that he will have to perform labor on Shabbos, how could this have been permitted? One who examines the Rif will see that he questions even the matter of techumin when embarking on a ship, which is rabbinic: how could this have been permitted even from Sunday? See the Beit Yosef, who explains that the Rif indeed holds stringently unless the ship at sea is more than ten handbreadths above the seabed. This applies even for a mitzvah. Although, regarding embarking on a ship at sea, which involves only a rabbinic prohibition, we rule leniently during the first three days of the week, as the Rama wrote, or within the final three days for the sake of a mitzvah, how could this have been permitted where actual labor will be performed? The Baal HaMaor derived his ruling from the baraita stating that one may not embark fewer than three days before Shabbos. Yet according to the interpretation given to that baraita by many other Rishonim, namely the Rif, Rambam, Ramban, Tosafot in the name of Ri, and Rashbam, there is no source whatever for permitting a person at the beginning of the week to enter a situation that will ultimately lead to desecration of Shabbos. The Radbaz already discussed this at length in opposition to the Rivash, who cited the Baal HaMaor as practical law. He therefore concludes that when it is known with certainty that desecration of Shabbos will ultimately result, it is forbidden even at the beginning of the week. The Mahari ben Lev likewise agrees, and the Magen Avraham cites both of them in seif katan 14, writing there that even for the sake of a mitzvah there is no basis for permitting a case in which one knows with certainty that desecration of Shabbos will ultimately result. See Eliyah Rabbah; its remarks do not resolve the Radbaz's fundamental final objection: can the fact that this is a mitzvah justify such a result? See there. It therefore appears clear that the stipulation here is indispensable even for the sake of a mitzvah, because without stipulating with him, desecration of Shabbos will certainly result. This is unlike embarking on a ship, regarding which we wrote in the Mishnah Berurah that the stipulation is not indispensable, because there the ship is more than ten handbreadths above the seabed. Even were we to be lenient when it is below ten handbreadths, that would apply specifically aboard a ship, where according to all opinions the prohibition is only rabbinic, even over a distance of twelve mil, as the Rambam wrote. This case, however, takes place on land. I later found that the Peri Megadim in Eshel Avraham, seif katan 6, likewise inclines to this conclusion in the name of the Levush, as I have written.
