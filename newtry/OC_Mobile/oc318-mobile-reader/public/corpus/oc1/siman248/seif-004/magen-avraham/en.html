To desecrate Shabbos. This applies even to a rabbinic prohibition. Rivash, siman 18.
And on Sunday, Monday, or Tuesday. Mahari ben Lev, part 2, siman 53, writes that since he knows with certainty that he will desecrate Shabbos, it is forbidden. Radbaz, part 1, siman 10, likewise rules so. On this basis, people now rely on endangering themselves rather than desecrating Shabbos. Knesses HaGedolah.
And one who ascends to Eretz Yisrael. This applies specifically when he intends to settle there. Some say it applies even when he intends to return, since even walking four cubits in Eretz Yisrael is a mitzvah. Knesses HaGedolah.
He may continue walking, etc. If he fears remaining in the wilderness lest bandits encounter him and steal his animal, leaving him unable to continue on foot; or lest they take his money, leaving him unable to sustain himself; or lest they take his clothing during cold weather, so that he may soon die from exposure, each of these is considered danger to life, and he may continue with them. Maharam Galanti, siman 109.
And if he entered, etc. It appears to me that this applies specifically when he traveled there for a mitzvah and not for commerce.
For he departed for the sake of a mitzvah. If he departed more than three days beforehand, even for a discretionary purpose, since he now had to continue because of danger to life, he has two thousand cubits in every direction, as stated in siman 407. It appears to me that if he departed within three days because he thought it was permitted to do so, he is considered one who acted inadvertently and has two thousand cubits in every direction. As Taz writes in Yoreh Deah, siman 99, one who says, “I thought it was permitted,” is considered inadvertent.
For commerce. This applies even if he has provisions and travels commercially merely to increase his profit. See siman 531, seif 4. Traveling to collect money from a non-Jew who is ill is not considered a certain loss so long as the debtor is not moribund. Mishpetei Tzedek and Rabbi Yosef HaLevi. The practice has already spread to embark even on Shabbos. Ralbach, siman 28. Mishpetei Tzedek, however, writes that one should not be lenient against the majority of authorities, especially when the ship travels for his sake, which all authorities forbid. Rabbi Aharon Sasson likewise rules so in simanim 31 and 32.
And traveling with a caravan. It appears to me that one should not be lenient regarding a caravan, as I wrote above. Olas Shabbos likewise writes this in the name of Knesses HaGedolah.
