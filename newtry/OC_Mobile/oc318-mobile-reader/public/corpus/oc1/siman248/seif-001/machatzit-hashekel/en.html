(Seif katan 1) For the sake of, etc. “For he cannot stipulate, etc.” This is because he enters it on Shabbos with the intention of departing immediately.
But even for the sake of a mitzvah, he must stipulate with him, etc. “And see seif 3,” meaning rivers fed by springs. Although they too are more than ten handbreadths deep, it is nevertheless forbidden to embark on Shabbos unless he established his Shabbos residence aboard the ship before Shabbos. This is because of the prohibition against swimming, and the same is stated in simanim 619 and 339. As the Magen Avraham wrote in seif katan 10, although for the sake of a mitzvah we are not concerned for all the other potential prohibitions, and consequently there would likewise be no concern for the prohibition against swimming, he must nevertheless stipulate with him that he will rest. Since he enters on Shabbos in order to depart, and did not establish his Shabbos residence beforehand, it is impossible to make such a stipulation. Therefore, even for the sake of a mitzvah, it is forbidden.
