For a mitzvah. On Shabbos itself, however, it is forbidden, since he cannot stipulate with him that he rest. See seif 3. Eliyah Rabbah disagrees with the Magen Avraham on this point and proves that for the sake of a mitzvah it is permitted even on Shabbos. The Tur says specifically that initially one should stipulate with him that he rest; when that is impossible, it is permitted without a stipulation. See there.
Days. Meaning that it is forbidden on Wednesday. See seif 4. The same applies on the second day of Yom Tov: one may not embark. See Yad Aharon, who discusses this at length, and Chavos Yair, siman 112.
Three days. Meaning on Tuesday.
Ten. One might have thought that the prohibition of techumin applies; it therefore teaches that it does not. The reason is that he began in a permitted manner.
There. Meaning that when a favorable wind is blowing, the voyage can be completed in one day. Although there is presently no favorable wind, it is permitted to depart on Friday. Beit Yosef; Darchei Moshe.
