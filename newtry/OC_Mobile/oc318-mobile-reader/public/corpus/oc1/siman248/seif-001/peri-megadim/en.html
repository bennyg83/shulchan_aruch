And he stipulates. See Taz. Rambam, Hilchos Shabbos 24:6 and 30:13, rules like the first tanna, Rabbi, rather than Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel. It certainly appears that according to Rabbi, throughout the three days before Shabbos he must stipulate that the ship rest, for the baraita mentions departure only within three days. For a discretionary voyage more than three days beforehand, however, which is permitted, no stipulation is necessary. The Tur likewise refers to departure within three days. Bach writes that the Tur implies that even more than three days beforehand one must stipulate. Taz therefore writes that this inference is not compelled. Taz distinguishes between hides placed with a non-Jew and the present case, because here the Jew himself remains there. See Bach. Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 5, cites Raavan, siman 60, who holds that even for a discretionary voyage more than three days beforehand one must stipulate. He also cites Shach, Yoreh Deah, siman 266, seif katan 18, concerning a circumcision not performed at its proper time that falls on Thursday, where Shach forbids it because stipulation is inapplicable. Shach holds that when stipulation is impossible and the mitzvah would otherwise be canceled, one may proceed without stipulating. See Tosefes Shabbos.
