Ibid., seif 2: Provided that it is not known to us that the water is less than ten handbreadths deep, etc. Note: This is difficult, for there is a compound uncertainty: perhaps the water is not ten handbreadths deep; and even assuming that it is ten handbreadths deep, perhaps the prohibition of techumin applies above ten handbreadths. See my work on Even HaEzer, sixth edition, siman 35, second section, in my responsum there, where I incidentally resolved this and derived several new laws from it, with God's help. See there. I later saw this very question in Shaar HaMelech, Hilchos Mikvaos, in his discussion whether a compound uncertainty in a rabbinic matter is ruled stringently. See there. According to what I wrote in the responsum cited above, some of his proofs are rebutted. See there and examine it carefully.
