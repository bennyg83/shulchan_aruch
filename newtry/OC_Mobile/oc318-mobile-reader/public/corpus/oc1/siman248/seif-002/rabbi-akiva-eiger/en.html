Seif 2. “Provided that it is not known.” This proves that although there is a double uncertainty tending toward stringency, we nevertheless rule leniently in a rabbinic matter. Here there is a double uncertainty tending toward stringency: perhaps the water is ten handbreadths deep, and even assuming that it is ten handbreadths deep, perhaps the prohibition of techumin applies above ten handbreadths. See below, siman 404.
