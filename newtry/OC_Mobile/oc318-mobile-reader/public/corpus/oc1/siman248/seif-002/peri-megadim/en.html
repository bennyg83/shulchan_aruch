And even. See Taz, who explains that the first three days of the week relate to the preceding Shabbos rather than the coming Shabbos. Therefore, if he began the journey permissibly and an unavoidable danger to life later arises, danger to life overrides Shabbos. See Responsa Sama DeChayei, in the section on Orach Chaim, who questions Rambam's ruling like Rabbi against his father, Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel. After all, wherever Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel taught a ruling, even in a baraita, the halachah follows him. He cites Hilchos Tumas Meis, chapter 9, and Hilchos Parah, chapter 7. Moreover, the rule that the halachah follows Rabbi against an individual colleague should not apply against his father and teacher, as discussed in Gittin, chapter HaNizakin, and Bava Metzia, chapter HaMekabel. See there. The Rosh, however, in Moed Katan, chapter Elu Megalchin, page 37, rules like Rabbi even against his father. See there. Tosefes Shabbos likewise discusses this at length. Taz's statement that departure within three days is forbidden rests on the reason he states afterward in seif 2, namely that it deprives him of Shabbos enjoyment; this is forbidden unless the journey is for a mitzvah. With God's help, see what I write concerning this in seif katan 3 and 4.
