They experience discomfort. For the sake of a mitzvah it is permitted, because one occupied with a mitzvah is exempt from another mitzvah. See siman 38.
From the bottom of the ship. Meaning that although the water is ten handbreadths deep, if the ship is submerged in the water and he sits lower than ten handbreadths, he is traveling below ten handbreadths. Even if he sits above that height, it is forbidden, because the ship is four handbreadths wide, as stated in siman 404. See there. Bach likewise writes this in the name of Sefer Yereim. Nevertheless, Sefer Yereim itself implies that it is permitted when he sits above ten handbreadths. Bach writes that the words of the Rif, Tosafot, and Rambam do not imply this, but I do not know where this is indicated. They subsequently write: “Nevertheless, one must say that even the vessel with which he draws water, when submerged in the water, is above ten handbreadths; otherwise, how could he draw water?” Their words are difficult: how does this follow here after their answer? Rather, they themselves found it difficult why they did not answer as Sefer Yereim does, namely that the water is deep but the ship is submerged and travels below ten handbreadths. They therefore wrote, “Nevertheless, one must say,” meaning that here, when the Gemara says that it is established that a ship does not travel in water less than ten handbreadths deep, it refers to the depth apart from the ship's submersion, so that the drawing vessel will still be above ten handbreadths. If so, one must say that the passage in Eruvin concerns a small ship. Rambam's responsum likewise concerns one traveling on a raft of beams that float and are not submerged in the water. One should therefore not depart from the Shulchan Aruch's ruling.
It is forbidden to depart. This requires study: what did Rama add? One may answer that he teaches that departure within the techum is permitted, and we do not decree lest he go beyond the techum.
Traveling in a wagon. That is forbidden lest he cut a branch, but here the animals travel at a distance from him. Olas Shabbos overlooked this gloss and wrote in the name of Tosafot and the Rosh that travel by ship is forbidden. This is inaccurate, for they wrote only about traveling in a wagon. According to what is stated in the fourth chapter of Eruvin, since the animal travels at a distance from the ship, this is indirect use of an indirect support, as stated in siman 305, seif 18.
