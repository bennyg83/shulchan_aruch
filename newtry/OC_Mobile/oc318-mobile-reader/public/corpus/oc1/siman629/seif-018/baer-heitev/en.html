With boards. See siman 631, se'if 9.
And the custom is. The reason is given in the Semak: perhaps one will come to use them as s'chach in a fixed manner, in such a way that rain will not be able to descend there. Therefore the custom is to cover with willow branches or with reeds. In pressing circumstances, when they have nothing with which to cover, they use boards as s'chach even if they are four tefachim wide, as the Shiltei Gibborim writes. The same applies to anything forbidden because of a decree. The Bach wrote that laths may not be used as s'chach, for one must be concerned that he will place so much s'chach that rain will not fall into it, as the Semak wrote, and certainly shingles are included. The Taz wrote that stalks of grain after the grain has been beaten out from them are plainly permitted, and this is the principal mitzvah explicitly stated as the refuse of the threshing floor. It appears that the custom was also not to cover with straw because of the Semak's concern, since with straw one can easily make a very thick covering. It is obvious that where there is no other s'chach, it is permitted, provided he is careful to make it so that rain can descend there.
