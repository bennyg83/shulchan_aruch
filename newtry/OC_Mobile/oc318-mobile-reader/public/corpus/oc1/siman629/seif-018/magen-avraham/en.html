Planed. They are not susceptible to midras tumah, since he did not designate them for sitting, as the Levush writes. One must say that he did not designate them for any use at all; see what I wrote at the end of se'if 7.
And the custom is. In the Hagahot Maimoniyot it is written that it is invalid, since nowadays people roof their houses with boards that are less than four tefachim wide, so the decree of a ceiling applies, as the Darchei Moshe writes. In pressing circumstances, when they have nothing with which to make s'chach, they use boards as s'chach even if they are four tefachim wide, as the Shiltei Gibborim writes, and so it appears from the Gemara. The same applies to anything forbidden because of a decree. The Bach wrote that laths, although they are not subject to the decree of a ceiling because people do not roof their houses with them, nevertheless one must be concerned that he will place so much s'chach that rain will not descend inside, as the Semak wrote. Therefore one may not use them as s'chach, and certainly shingles.
