1) [Seif 1] An item used for s'chach must grow from the ground, etc. For it is written, You shall make for yourself the festival of Sukkos, etc., when you gather from your threshing floor and your winepress. Therefore, one may use for s'chach only something that grows from the ground, is presently detached, and is not susceptible to tumah, similar to the waste of the threshing floor and winepress, namely straw and empty clusters, which possess all these characteristics. Levush. See above, at the beginning of siman 626. The custom is to use tree branches for s'chach, because the numerical value of the word ilan, tree, equals that of sukkah. Therefore, tree branches are the preferred manner of performing the mitzvah. Ben Ish Chai, beginning of Parashas Haazinu. Also, the word sukkah contains the letters that allude to 26 and 65. Twenty-six is the numerical value of the Name Havayah, and 65 is the numerical value of the Name Adnus. Therefore, when one uses tree branches for s'chach, he should have in mind that ilan has the same numerical value as sukkah and also as Havayah combined with Adnus. He should also have in mind that the sukkah is an aspect of surrounding light, as written above, siman 625, sec. 1. See further there, sec. 11.
2) Ibid. It must grow, etc. All these requirements are derived from verses. Therefore, they are Torah laws and indispensable. Thus, something that does not grow from the ground, something attached to the ground, or something fit to contract tumah according to the Torah, is invalid according to the Torah. However, something fit to contract tumah only rabbinically is invalid only rabbinically. Therefore, if one has no other material for s'chach, it appears that he should use this material but should not recite a blessing. Mishbetzos Zahav, sec. 1; Mishna Berurah in Biur Halacha.
3) Ibid. And it is not susceptible to tumah. Meaning, it is not fit to contract tumah. Taz, s.k. 2; Mishna Berurah, sec. 2.
4) Ibid. And it is not susceptible to tumah. Meaning, according to the Torah. However, something susceptible to tumah only rabbinically may be used for s'chach. So too wrote the Mordechai in the name of Maharam. Nevertheless, since the Mechaber stated the ruling without qualification, it appears that he holds that no distinction should be made. Olas Shabbos, sec. 1. See below, sec. 12.
5) Regarding s'chach or walls of a sukkah made from items from which benefit is forbidden, whether we say in this case that mitzvos were not given for personal benefit, or whether we do not say so because the body derives benefit from them, and likewise regarding wood from an asheirah tree, see Beit HaShoevah, secs. 5 and 6, where he discussed this at length and concluded that the sukkah is invalid even after the fact, see there. However, the Erech HaShulchan, sec. 1, wrote that if permitted s'chach is unavailable, one may rely on the lenient opinions throughout all seven days of the festival. On the eighth day, however, one should be stringent and not sit in it, since there is bodily benefit and there is an opinion that, regarding a rabbinic mitzvah, we do not say that mitzvos were not given for personal benefit, see there. See below, siman 630, sec. 3. See also above, siman 586, seif 3, and our words there. Nevertheless, it appears that one should not recite a blessing, because of the principle that in a case of uncertainty regarding blessings we are lenient.
6) The author of Sefer Bikkurei Yaakov, at the beginning of this siman, was uncertain whether it is permitted to use paper for s'chach, even paper made from vegetation. He wrote that paper made from woolen garments is invalid for s'chach according to the Torah, whereas paper made from vegetation or linen garments is invalid only rabbinically. There is a practical difference in a pressing circumstance when one has only paper for s'chach, see there. Ruach Chaim, sec. 2, cited his words and wrote that this requires great consideration. First, if rain falls on the paper, it will destroy it and it will fall. If it is exceedingly thick, the stars will not be visible through it at all. If one makes holes in it, it is possible that it would be permitted in a pressing circumstance, see there. We already wrote above, sec. 2, that whenever the prohibition is rabbinic, in a pressing circumstance one should not recite a blessing.
7) Ibid. Although it grows from the ground. Meaning, that it develops upon the ground. The word from is not precise. Mishna Berurah, sec. 3. Regarding whether one may use for s'chach the feathers of birds that grow on a tree, such as where one detached them while the birds were still attached to the tree, see Pischei HaDevir, vol. 2, siman 204, folio 39c, who wrote that it is permitted. Nevertheless, because of a decree, it appears proper to forbid it, lest people come to use for s'chach the feathers of other birds that are created from eggs, for those certainly are invalid because they do not grow from the ground, see there. See Yoreh Deah, siman 84, seif 15.
8) Ibid. Or types of metal, etc. For example, pieces of metal from which no utensils were made, which are not yet susceptible to tumah. Even though their source is also from the ground, and the same applies to actual earth, nevertheless one may not use them for s'chach, because we specifically require something that grows from the ground, not the ground itself. Mishna Berurah, sec. 4.
9) Ibid. Rama's gloss: Likewise, one may not use earth for s'chach. This means hard clods of earth and anything that is of the earth category, such as bricks and the like. Beit HaShoevah, sec. 2.
