10) [Seif 2] Such as spits. It appears that this refers to wooden spits, for if they were metal, they would already be excluded because they do not grow from the ground. Beit Yosef; Taz, s.k. 4. So too the Levush wrote: wooden spits. However, the Magen Avraham, s.k. 2, wrote that it refers to iron spits, whereas wooden spits are not susceptible to tumah even rabbinically, like male arrows, since they are flat wooden utensils. He left the words of the Beit Yosef requiring further study, see there. The Bach wrote that it refers even to metal spits whose handles are wooden. Although the handles have no receptacle and are like male arrows, one may not use even their wooden portion as s'chach because of the spit, which was susceptible to tumah, see there. However, the Shiltei Gibborim, sec. 4, wrote that the words of the Beit Yosef are primary, since the Mishnah teaches spits together with bedposts, all of which are wooden. So too the Eliyah Rabbah, sec. 1, wrote that although flat wooden utensils are not susceptible to tumah, they are nevertheless forbidden rabbinically, and they are inferior to the male arrows of seif 3. So too wrote Eshel Avraham, sec. 1, and Mishbetzos Zahav, sec. 4, that wooden spits are susceptible to tumah rabbinically because they serve a person and serve in the manner of a table, see there. So too Sefer Bigdei Yesha wrote that the case concerns spits designated for roasting and other uses, which serve a person. Utensils that serve a person's needs are susceptible to tumah even though they are flat wooden utensils, as explained at length in Tosafos, Sukkah 5a, and in Toras Kohanim, see there. See below, sec. 14.
11) Ibid. And the bedposts. Even if they are wooden, and even though they are not an entire bed, they are invalid for s'chach because they came from a complete bed. Ran. It is implied there that they are invalid only rabbinically, and this is also evident from the Gemara. Mishna Berurah, sec. 6, and Shaar HaTziyun, sec. 3.
12) Ibid. And all utensils, etc. Even an item that is susceptible to tumah only rabbinically. Taz, s.k. 5; Beit HaShoevah, sec. 5; Mishna Berurah, sec. 7. See above, sec. 4.
13) Ibid. Even if they broke and nothing remained in them, etc. Although they are presently not susceptible to tumah, since they came from a utensil, the Sages decreed concerning them. Rashi, Sukkah 15b. So too wrote the Rambam, Hilchos Sukkah 5:2. If one transgressed and used them as s'chach, his sukkah is invalid, like one who used s'chach that is invalid according to the Torah. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, sec. 2. However, regarding a table and similar flat utensils, which according to all opinions are susceptible to tumah only rabbinically, if they broke, one can say that using them would involve a decree upon a decree, and therefore one may use them for s'chach. Mishbetzos Zahav, sec. 5; Bikkurei Yaakov; Mishna Berurah, sec. 8. See below, sec. 17.
