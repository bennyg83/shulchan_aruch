(Se'if katan 11) That are susceptible, etc. The Bach asked, etc., on top of an attached wall. This means a wooden wall. Regarding a stone wall, the Bach in truth wrote according to his view that by law, if it is supported by an invalid item, it is invalid. As the Magen Avraham wrote in his name in se'if katan 9, one must place reeds beneath the s'chach, not merely as an act of piety as the Ran wrote and the Magen Avraham brought in se'if katan 9. Rather, the Bach's question concerns a wooden wall, where the custom is not to place reeds, since there is no invalidity in them, for they grow from the ground. Nevertheless, there is another invalidity in it, namely that it is attached.
And know, etc. Because it resembles a receptacle and receives tumah rabbinically.
If so, one may not place a spade or rake, even though they have no receptacle, since they are nevertheless fit to place something upon them, and are designated for that; see se'if katan 21. For this reason even if it broke, as above in se'if 2, since it comes from something susceptible to tumah, the same applies here. Although when they are broken they are not fit to place anything upon them, nevertheless at one time they were fit to place something upon them and were then susceptible to tumah. I do not know what the Netiv Chayim challenged against him.
