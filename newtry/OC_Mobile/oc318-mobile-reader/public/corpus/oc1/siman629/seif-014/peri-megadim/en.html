However, with something whose way is to dry within the seven days of the festival, and then its sun will be greater than its shade, one can still say that as long as it has not dried, it is valid. This differs from where the leaves dry and fall, leaving no substance; that is like leaves that have fallen now. The Levush requires examination, for he implies that the reason is lest within the seven days he will not be able to make another sukkah; see there. See the Bach, who implies that if according to the strength of the heat they would dry within seven days, nevertheless, since that is not their usual way, as long as they have not dried they are valid. Know that things whose way is to dry may have both aspects: invalid s'chach, for if the sukkah is otherwise proper and these are on top of it, as in siman 626, we view everything opposite the invalid material as though it is absent, and we do not say it is merely stringency and the sukkah is valid. This is what he wrote: although they are invalid because they receive tumah, meaning that he teaches that they are more severe and are called invalid s'chach; they are also considered like empty space. See the Levush. See the Maggid Mishneh, chapter 5, halacha 3, that even though they do not receive tumah they are also considered like empty space.
