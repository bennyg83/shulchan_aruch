(51) If one spread a sheet over it because of the sun, or beneath it because of falling debris. Until this point is the language of the Mishnah. According to the first opinion cited by the Mechaber, 'because of the sun' is understood literally: the sukkah already had more shade than sunlight, and he spread the sheet above it so that no sunlight would enter at all. 'Because of falling debris' means so that leaves and similar material should not fall upon him.
(52) It is invalid. Because he is thereby covering and protecting himself with an object that contracts tumah.
(53) If it was spread for decoration, it is valid. Since it serves the needs of the sukkah, it is subordinate to it. This applies specifically where the sukkah already had more shade than sunlight, as stated above; otherwise it is invalid.
(54) Within four tefachim of the sechach. If it is more distant, it is not subordinate to the sechach under any circumstances, as stated above in siman 627, seif 4.
(55) Some say that a sukkah, etc. This opinion disputes the first. It maintains that the Mishnah's case of spreading a sheet because of the sun applies only in the specific circumstance described below. If, however, one spreads it merely for protection from the sun or falling leaves, the sukkah is valid and one may even sit beneath the sheet, because it is subordinate to the sukkah.
(56) But if he did not intend, etc. Meaning, if the sukkah was well covered so that there was no concern that falling leaves would cause its sunlight to exceed its shade, and he spread the sheet only to protect himself from the sun or from falling leaves, it is valid. This is the ruling of Beit Yosef.
(57) Nevertheless, initially one should not do so, etc. Onlookers may say that his intention is to use the sheet as sechach. It is forbidden even outside mealtime, because they may say that he leaves it there during the meal as well.
(58) When his intention is to protect. This leniency applies only according to the second opinion mentioned above. According to the first opinion, it is forbidden even in this case. See Biur Halachah, which rules that in practice one should be stringent, and so write Bach and other authorities. Nevertheless, in pressing circumstances, when one cannot eat in the sukkah because leaves fall into the food, rain drips through, or wind extinguishes the candles, it is preferable to spread a sheet beneath the sechach within four tefachim rather than eat outside the sukkah. He should not, however, recite the blessing leisheiv basukkah over such a sukkah.
(59) Soaked in water. See Biur Halachah. Since the later authorities agree that one should be stringent in accordance with the first opinion, one should avoid spreading a sheet even to dry it except outside mealtime.
