(Se'if katan 19) Shaking and also untying. Although with a ceiling below in siman 631:15, the Beit Yosef wrote that regarding a house roofed with boards it is enough to remove the nails for the purpose of making a sukkah, for then there is no decree of a ceiling, since by going back to it to nullify it he knows that it is forbidden to sit under a ceiling. If so, the same should apply here: since he had to shake it, there should no longer be a decree for this reason. And indeed the Ran already wrote this. The Bach, however, distinguished between them. This is his wording: perhaps there it is different, for with loosening, namely removing the nails, there is a nullification of the ceiling, so there is no reason to decree. This is unlike a bundle, where shaking does not nullify the drying purpose, for it dries in the other place just as it dries in the first place; the decree therefore remains in place. Accordingly, the only permission is through shaking together with first untying, and the shaking is to move it from its place. These are the Bach's words. Untying alone does not help, because he did not perform an act in the body of the s'chach; so the Bach wrote. In the book Netiv Chayim he disagreed with the Bach from the case of a ceiling, so it should read. It escaped him that the Bach himself distinguishes between them, as above.
