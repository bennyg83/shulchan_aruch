19) [Seif 4] If he covered it with flax that was not crushed and not combed, etc. The Gemara is uncertain regarding flax that was soaked in water, though it was neither crushed nor combed, whether one may use it for s'chach. The poskim rule leniently, and the Ran wrote that the reason is that the prohibition is merely rabbinic. Beit Yosef. However, the Riaz wrote stringently regarding this uncertainty, and the Shiltei Gibborim cited his words in the first chapter of Sukkah. So too the Ritva wrote, according to Rashi and those who follow his explanation that tufts of flax are invalid according to the Torah because they are susceptible to the tumah of tzaraas, that even if they were merely soaked and not crushed, we rule stringently. So too wrote Orchos Chaim, folio 110c, in the name of Mahariag, that even if it was soaked and not crushed, it is invalid. So too wrote the Baal HaIttur and Shibolei HaLeket. However, from the words of the Rambam, Tur, and Shulchan Aruch, who wrote, that was not crushed and not combed, etc., it is implied that if it was soaked, it is permitted. This is also implied by the words of Rabbeinu Yerucham, path 8, part 1. So too the Ritva wrote in his novellae to Sukkah that it is a rabbinic doubt and we rule leniently. Beit HaShoevah, sec. 21, cited his words and wrote that, as a matter of law, this is how we rule, since the majority of the early and later poskim, namely the Rif, Rambam, Rosh, Tur, Ran, and Ritva, ruled that flax which was soaked but neither crushed nor combed is valid. Maran also ruled so in the Shulchan Aruch. In practice, however, since according to the Riaz, Orchos Chaim in the name of Mahariag, and the Ritva according to Rashi, even mere soaking renders it invalid, one should at least be stringent initially not to use flax stalks that came from the soaking vat as s'chach, even if they were not crushed, see there. So too ruled the Mishna Berurah, sec. 11.
