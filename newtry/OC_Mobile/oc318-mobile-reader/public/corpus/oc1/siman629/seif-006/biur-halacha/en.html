Is intended for lying upon - See the Magen Avraham, that in these places an unspecified mat, whether large or small, is for lying upon. According to this, even if he made them explicitly for s'chach, nevertheless they are rabbinically invalid for the reason ruled by the Rosh: who knows whether they were made for s'chach? They are forbidden because of appearances. Recently a new practice has come, that in our countries people have begun to use willow mats as s'chach, which our forefathers never imagined. Those mats are presumably intended for lying and sitting on wagons; if so, they have an invalidity because of appearances even if he made them for s'chach. Therefore anyone who guards his soul should keep far from this. This is especially so if those mats had already been used in a wagon, even not for lying or sitting but to receive things, for then they are invalid by Torah law, etc. These are the words of the Bikkurei Yaakov. However, in the book Beit Meir he wrote that in his city too he found this practice, that they use as s'chach branches of willows, which they call bleid kerib. Those kerib are indeed mostly prepared and grown to be placed inside wagons, and if even those used as s'chach had that form, in such a way that one could say about them that their ordinary purpose was to place them inside wagons, etc., then according to the Rosh it would not help even if they were made explicitly for s'chach. However, he writes: I see that these kerib, which have no receptacle at all but are flat, are not made at all in the manner of those placed inside wagons, for those made for wagons have an inside and are made with bottoms and backs. These, which are only one flat side, even if they were made to stand on the side inside wagons, are only a partition to prevent things from falling from the side of the wagon, and they receive no tumah, etc. This is especially true since they are large, and it is not at all the manner to place them inside wagons, and all the more so where they were explicitly prepared by the craftsman for s'chach; they are valid without any hesitation. These are his words. See the Peri Megadim at the end of Hilchot Sukkah in siman 643, who hesitates about this because of the decree of a ceiling, but I do not know what connection the decree of a ceiling has in a place where the custom is not to roof with this. In any event, one who wishes to rely on the view of the Gaon Beit Meir certainly should not be protested, especially since, where they were made for s'chach, it is only a rabbinic matter according to everyone, as written in Bikkurei Yaakov. However, in a place where the practice all year is to roof roofs with them, one must certainly be careful with them because of the decree of a ceiling, as the Rema wrote.
