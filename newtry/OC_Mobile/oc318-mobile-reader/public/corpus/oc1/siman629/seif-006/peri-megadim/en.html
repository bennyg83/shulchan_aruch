Beaten See the Taz. According to all the reasons, the invalidity is rabbinic, since it has not yet been placed into a cushion or pillow; see Magen Avraham 3. If it was beaten but not combed, it requires examination for leniency; see Lechem Mishneh, chapter 5, halacha 4. In the Shulchan Aruch one can say that not beaten and not combed means one may initially use it as s'chach; beaten and combed is invalid even after the fact, rabbinically; and beaten but not combed, one should initially not use it as s'chach, but where he has only this, one can say it is valid.
And know Female arrows are invalid. If they are made for permanent filling, they are not susceptible to tumah, and one can say they are rabbinically invalid where the iron has not yet been inserted, because of a decree due to filling that is not permanent. But if they are made so that the filling is sometimes removed, they are invalid by Torah law; see Rambam Hilchot Sukkah 5:5, like any receptacle. The Lechem Mishneh brought Rashi's explanation in Sukkah 12 of permanent filling, and the Mishneh LaMelech in Kelim 2:3 alluded to it in his fine way; see there. See Tosafot Sukkah 12, s.v. mahu deteima. To say that female arrows, since if they are filled they become a handle and therefore either way they receive tumah: from where do we know that? In any event, for now there is neither handle nor receptacle. Know further that regarding something susceptible to rabbinic tumah, some say it is permitted to use it as s'chach; see Terumat HaDeshen 90, and the Bach brought this in the name of the Mordechai in the name of Rabbi Eliezer of Metz. We do not rule so; one may not use them as s'chach. Nevertheless, if he has only this, one can say that because it is a disputed doubt, he may use it as s'chach but not bless; see letter 1 and Magen Avraham 22. In such a case one can say that where the vessel broke, we do not decree according to everyone, because it is a decree upon a decree. See what I will write in letter 9, with God's help, about this.
