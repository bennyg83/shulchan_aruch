(32) If one covered the sukkah with vegetables, etc. Meaning, with vegetables that in certain places dry quickly during the seven days of Sukkos, causing their leaves and stems to fall and leave open air.
(33) Although they are invalid as sechach, etc. This seif concerns vegetables fit for human consumption, which are invalid as sechach. One might have thought that no additional form of invalidity can apply to them. The ruling teaches that this is not so.
(34) To invalidate with four tefachim. As explained in siman 632, invalid sechach in the middle invalidates the sukkah when it is four tefachim wide, while open air invalidates it, whether in the middle or at the side, when it is three tefachim wide.
(35) Rather, they are considered like open air, etc. This applies even before they have dried. Since they crumble and fall when dry, and the sukkah is therefore unfit to last for seven days, the Sages treated them as open air from the outset. The poskim write that the same applies when one uses vegetables unfit for human consumption, which do not themselves contract tumah and would otherwise be valid as sechach, if they dry quickly within seven days; they too are invalid.
