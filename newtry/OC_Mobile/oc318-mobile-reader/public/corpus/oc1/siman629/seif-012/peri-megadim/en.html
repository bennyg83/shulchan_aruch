All See the Taz. The Mechaber too is speaking of human food, for pinono is not fit; one can say as the Rosh wrote, that it is long. See letter 13. However, we rule like the Rav, that animal food and items used by people medicinally may be used as s'chach even if they are themselves food. It certainly implies that even if they were not made susceptible by water after being detached, they are invalid by Torah law. This is unlike a planed board: so long as he did not designate it for midras, one may use it as s'chach if it is less than four tefachim wide. See Tosafot Sukkah 13b, s.v. yerakot, regarding vegetables that were not made susceptible, and nevertheless they count as empty space and not invalid s'chach, as Rashi explains s.v. uposlan and behold, etc.; see there. We see that this is so. Rabbi Yehudah, who says that sukkah applies only with the four species, excludes even etrog, even if it was not made susceptible, as stated.
And know All kinds of foods is a general rule. Even garlic, onions, and vegetables with which a person fulfills his obligation on Pesach, although he does not eat them plain but only by dipping or with something else, nevertheless they become impure by Torah law, as Tosafot wrote in Sukkah 13b, s.v. yerakot. This is unlike what is written in Uktzin 3:5 and the Rambam in Tum'at Ochalin 1 regarding kost, chamas, and the heads of spices, that foods not used for bodily enjoyment are different. Those are not purchased with second-tithe money, and whatever is not purchased does not become impure, while whatever is purchased becomes impure. Palm-heart is different, as the Rosh wrote there, since it is fit through boiling. See Hilchot Ma'aser Sheni 7:8-9, and see Eruvin 28b and our great teacher in Pnei Yehoshua on Sukkah there. If so, one may not use any food as s'chach, including garlic and onion. But palm-heart before it is completed may be used as s'chach. Cinnamon and any spice that is not eaten for bodily enjoyment but for smell, appearance, and the like may be used as s'chach. Regarding fresh pepper and fresh ginger, see halacha 2. Even after they later dry, once tumah descended upon them they are no longer valid for s'chach afterwards. However, this latter invalidity is rabbinic, like worn-out vessels, whereas the first invalidity is Torah-level, as stated. See what I wrote in letter 1 about this.
