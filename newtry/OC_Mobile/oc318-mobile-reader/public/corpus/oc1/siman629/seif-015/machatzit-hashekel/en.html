(Se'if katan 15) That shed; and specifically, etc. It is valid, for he can eat in it when there is no wind. It does not become invalid because it was not fit to eat in at the time of wind. We find similarly at the end of this siman according to the Ran's view, that at the time of eating it is forbidden to place a garment soaked in water on top of the s'chach, but before eating it is permitted; see there. We do not say that since it was invalid while the garment was placed upon it, it remains invalid. The same applies here. Or one can say that when the Magen Avraham wrote, as written at the end of this siman, Bach, he meant that the Bach wrote this law at the end of this siman.
