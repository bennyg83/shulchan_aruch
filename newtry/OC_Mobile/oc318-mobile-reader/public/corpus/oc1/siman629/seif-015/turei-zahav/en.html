Any bundle that is not tied, etc. The Gemara states that willow huts or baskets become valid for schach once their upper binding is released. Rashi explains that they are round like a cap, woven from willows, wide below, and tied above with a single cord. The “heads of their bindings” refers to the upper knot. The Gemara asks that they remain bound below, where the opening is braided. Rav Pappa answers that the ends of the thread used for braiding must also be untied, though the braid itself need not be dismantled. Rav Huna son of Rav Yehoshua says that even if those threads are not untied, any binding not made for carrying is not considered a binding. Once the upper knot is released, the object would come apart if carried; the lower braid merely holds the reeds together. Rif and Rosh cite this discussion without explicitly ruling between the views, while Tur omits it. Beis Yosef expresses surprise. It appears that an earlier teaching resolves the matter: Marimar taught that the bundles of Sura may be used for schach. Tur and Shulchan Aruch cite this as the case of a bundle tied only for sale. Rav Pappa appears to disagree, since in his case the item cannot even be carried and nevertheless remains a disqualified bundle until further untied. Certainly a bundle tied so it can be sold and transported should be disqualified. Rif and Rosh therefore had no need to rule explicitly like Rav Huna son of Rav Yehoshua, because they had already ruled like Marimar. For the same reason Tur omitted this later discussion. If a bundle tied for carrying is nevertheless permitted when it is intended to be untied, then all the more so an arrangement that cannot be carried is permitted. This explanation is correct. See siman 46.
