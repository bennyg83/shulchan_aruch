(Se'if katan 3) It is invalid, because it changed, etc. According to this, even, etc.; and the same applies according to the Ran's reason that he writes afterwards. He comes only to exclude the view of Tosafot, who gave the reason that it is close to being spun and will receive tumah of nega'im, as the Taz brought. If so, cotton and hemp, whose spun form does not become impure with nega'im, would be permitted. This is unlike the reasons of the Rambam and the Ran.
The Maggid Mishneh challenged the Raavad, who also wrote the Ran's reason: why is this different from male arrows, which are also fit to be placed into female arrows and thereby receive tumah, and nevertheless as long as they have not been placed there they are valid?
And if so, dakdekta. Rashi explained in Chullin 51b that this is the tow, the broken pieces of stalk shaken from the flax when it is combed; the finest of the fine is called dakdekta. According to the Rambam it may perhaps be permitted, for this has not changed and still has the form of wood upon it. But according to the Ran's reason it is forbidden, because it too is fit for filling a cushion or pillow. One must say that he attached it with water and kneaded it until it became one mass, so that it would be fit for s'chach.
