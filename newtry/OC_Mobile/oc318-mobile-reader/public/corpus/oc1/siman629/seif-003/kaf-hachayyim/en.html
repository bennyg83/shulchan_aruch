14) [Seif 3] If he covered it with arrows, etc. Although they are utensils, flat wooden utensils are not susceptible to tumah. Although there are flat wooden utensils that are susceptible to tumah rabbinically, these are not susceptible to tumah even rabbinically. Beit Yosef in the name of the Ran. So too wrote Tosafos, Sukkah 12b.
15) Ibid. If he covered it with arrows, etc. This refers to the handle portions of arrows. Rashi, Sukkah, ibid. The case is where the iron heads had not yet been attached. If the iron heads had been attached, even the male arrows are invalid, because they became susceptible to tumah through the iron, which is a utensil. So ruled the Riaz, cited by the Shiltei Gibborim in the first chapter of Sukkah; Bach; Knesset HaGedolah in Hagahos Beit Yosef; Magen Avraham, s.k. 2; Eliyah Rabbah, sec. 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, sec. 3; Mishna Berurah, sec. 10.
16) Reeds, although they have a hollow receptacle, are not susceptible to tumah, because their hollow was not made for the purpose of receiving. Therefore, they may be used for s'chach. Responsa of the Rosh, rule 24, siman 12; Beit Yosef; Ateres Zahav; Knesset HaGedolah in Hagahos Beit Yosef; Magen Avraham, ibid.; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, sec. 4; Chayei Adam, rule 146, sec. 11; Mishna Berurah, ibid. The same applies to a gutter or pipe, which may be used for s'chach because it was not made to receive. Magen Avraham, ibid.; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ibid.; Chayei Adam, ibid.; Mishna Berurah, ibid. See Yoreh Deah, siman 201, seif 36, which states that if one carved a hollow in a gutter to catch pebbles, it is susceptible to tumah. Regarding the required size of the hollow, there is a distinction between wood and earthenware, see there. Machatzis HaShekel.
17) Know that every wooden utensil that is somewhat broad and fit for placing something upon it is susceptible to tumah, because it resembles a receptacle. So wrote Tosafos, Sukkah 5a. Accordingly, one should not place a shovel or rake upon the sukkah, even if it broke. Magen Avraham, s.k. 11; Eliyah Rabbah, sec. 11; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, sec. 14. Nevertheless, see Bikkurei Yaakov, whose view inclines toward leniency in a pressing circumstance, permitting use of it for s'chach after it broke. Since even while intact it was susceptible to tumah only rabbinically, there is no reason to decree once it is broken, see there. The Mishna Berurah, sec. 10, cited his words. This is also the view of Beit HaShoevah, sec. 11, see there. If they are not fit for placing anything upon them, they are permitted even initially. Beit HaShoevah, ibid. See above, sec. 13, and below, sec. 98.
18) Ibid. And those that have a receptacle are invalid. See Tosafos, Sukkah 12b, s.v. According to the one who said. It appears from their words that they are invalid according to the Torah. The Mabit, however, wrote in Kiryas Sefer that female arrows are invalid only rabbinically. Kovetz on the Rambam; Pischei Olam, sec. 10.
