[4] That was not beaten and not combed, etc. This implies that if it was beaten, even if it was not combed, it is invalid. This is difficult, for in the latter clause it implies the opposite. Afterwards I saw in Lechem Chamudot that he wondered this about the Rambam in chapter 5. As practical law I found in Sefer Tanya that he ruled that beating alone invalidates. This is unlike the Perishah, who wrote that the matter depends on combing. Since it is impossible to comb without beating, it says beaten and combed. This requires examination.
