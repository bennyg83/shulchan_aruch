The Levush appears to have omitted the glosses of his teacher and wrote as the Taz wrote, that the Mishnah on 19b speaks where there is no clear custom, neither among the craftsmen regarding why they make them nor among the buyers regarding why they buy them. Therefore a large mat, when unspecified, is for s'chach and one may use it as s'chach unless the craftsman made it or the buyer bought it explicitly for lying. A small mat, when unspecified, is intended for lying; therefore if the craftsman made it explicitly or the buyer bought it for s'chach, one may use it as s'chach, like the first Tanna. It must be that there are places where the matter is not known and clear; otherwise why does the Gemara challenge the inferences as contradicting one another, from for lying, implying that unspecified is for s'chach, and then from for s'chach, implying that unspecified is for lying? Perhaps there is no unspecified case at all, only local custom, with some places for lying and some for s'chach. We learn from this that there are places where it is unspecified. The dispute of the Rosh with Rabbeinu Yeshayah of Trani is that Rabbeinu Yeshayah of Trani holds that we do not follow the custom at all; even in a place where the custom is to tell the craftsman to make them for lying, if he bought one and instructed the craftsman to make it for s'chach, one may use it as s'chach, and if the way is to lie on them but the craftsman made it without specification and he bought it for s'chach, one may use it as s'chach. On this the Rosh disagrees, holding that in such a case we follow the custom, apparently whether large or small. Even though one person deviated from the custom, who knows? People will think, etc. In any event it is rabbinically forbidden. See the Rosh and the Tur; they do not contradict one another. This is what the Beit Yosef wrote in the Rosh's wording; understand it. With God's help, this will be explained further in the Magen Avraham.
