(1) There is room to doubt etc. — they doubted whether it is a receptacle since it is made to be filled, as written siman 12 seif 2 that it receives impurity and is invalid; even though in Torat Kohanim parashat Shemini it says a ladder is pure, and so in Tosefta Kelim there — the rungs on its legs, unlike its thighs which are pierced to receive the heads of the rungs; but in chapter 17 of Kelim they taught: the bottom of smiths is impure and of coppersmiths pure, and Rambam explained that of coppersmiths is a receptacle made to be filled — see there chapter 2 of Kelim law 3 and Raavad there. Rambam's words require study, for in chapter 1 of Sukkah parashat Dachitzin pierced is invalid even though made to be filled it receives impurity etc.; but Rosh there — the mishnah means the bottom of vessels placed under smiths' and coppersmiths' feet — see there; and so Rashi Sanhedrin 68a explains the dispute of R. Eliezer and the Sages on a receptacle made to be filled: for the Sages pure — Tosafot there and Sukkah challenged him — see there. But what Shulchan Aruch wrote whether permitted to place etc. is not so in Ramban's responsa there nor in Rashba responsa siman 195, only whether permitted to roof with ladders themselves; but in Tosafot Har Hashalom siman 90 there the question is whether permitted to place the ladder in order to roof on the ladder, and he answered that even to roof with ladders themselves is permitted because it is plain wooden vessels, and he brought proof from Torat Kohanim as above and as Rashba wrote in chapter 1 of Sukkah; the correct view is as R. Yitzchak wrote that all plain wooden vessels fit for treading and designated for treading receive all impurity in the world, even to be a father of impurity as in Bava Kamma, Bekhorot, Niddah, Sukkah, Shabbat and elsewhere; but otherwise if they serve a person and are human utensils they are pure from the Torah but receive impurity rabbinically, and if not pure even rabbinically — this is the ladder that Torat Kohanim excludes since it serves the person and is not a human utensil — see there parashat 6; also they are not designated for treading but to ascend and descend, and so for Tosafot's other answer that since they are somewhat wide and fit to place something on them they decreed impurity rabbinically as similar to a receptacle — as in Gemara Bekhorot and several places with that reasoning; nevertheless for a ladder all agree it is pure as above from Torat Kohanim, and the Tosefta there speaks rabbinically even though derived from a verse it is merely an asmakhta since it says table and table are pure from the Torah as in chapter 1 of Menachot — see Tosafot there 96b s.v. velidivrei etc. In Sukkah 5a s.v. misgarto, Bava Batra 66a s.v. veshe'ani, Eruvin 31a s.v. befashutei etc., and in Yoreh Deah siman 198 and 161, and Beit Yosef holds since Tosafot Har Hashalom does not validate roofing on the ladder but because he holds a ladder is pure — but for Rashba who doubts, Tosafot Har Hashalom agrees forbidden; but this is imprecise, for Tosafot Har Hashalom wrote only for clarity that even on the ladder itself one may roof, but even for those who forbid on it, certainly permitted on it — as written siman 91 and Shulchan Aruch later seif 8; and he wrote even though we hold like R. Yehuda in Sukkah 2b, since the amoraim there debate on his view, nevertheless Rosh wrote there the reason is fixed like the principal as concluded in Yerushalmi — and Tosafot there s.v. she'ein etc., and so ruled Shulchan Aruch later siman 634 seif 13; and even though one could answer that the case in seif 8 is not difficult since even Tosafot Har Hashalom wrote specifically for Rosh's ruling who ruled because of fixedness — he did not see Rashba's words who rules like the one who says because he supports it on something that receives impurity, and challenged there from what we say one who makes his sukkah on a tree is valid though a tree is invalid sekhakh, and further challenged Rashi who explained there but if he roofed on the bed it is valid — he planted stakes outside and did not rest sekhakh on them — what is there to say; rather the correct view is as Ramban wrote that he supports his sukkah on the bed's legs — he overturned his bed and the legs are above and he roofed on them — he placed sekhakh on the legs, one reason because it has no fixedness since they say stand and our work is done — it is not standing for that since its use is inverted; another reason because etc., and Abaye said it was taught only etc. — meaning he placed the bed normally and planted stakes on it and rested sekhakh on the stakes even though it moves with the bed — valid for the one who says it has no fixedness, behold it stands normally; and for the one who says because etc. only when he rested sekhakh itself on something that receives impurity; but the supporters are valid, else all sukkah walls would need support on the ground — and this is what one who makes his sukkah at the top of a tree means — sukkah ground is sukkah ground as Rashi wrote — see Tosafot's two challenges on Rashi there and so Ran; accordingly the case in seif 8 is not difficult, but siman 634 is difficult — Magen Avraham answered there is b'dieved and here forbids l'chatchila — strained; and Magen Avraham sec. 9.
