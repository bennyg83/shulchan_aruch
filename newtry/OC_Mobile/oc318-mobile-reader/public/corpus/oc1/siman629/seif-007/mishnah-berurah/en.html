(22) It is uncertain whether it is permitted, etc. Some later authorities explain that the uncertainty concerns whether one may use ladders as sechach. This refers to a ladder four tefachim wide, because invalid sechach in the middle does not invalidate the sukkah when it is less than four tefachim wide, as stated below in siman 632. The doubt arises because a ladder is a flat wooden utensil that does not contract tumah even D'Rabbanan, yet there is reason to prohibit it because the holes in its sidepieces into which the rungs are inserted resemble receptacles. Other later authorities explain that the case concerns a ladder that is not four tefachim wide, for if it were that wide we would certainly be stringent because it resembles a receptacle and may not be used as sechach. Rather, the uncertainty is that it at least serves as a support for the sechach, and perhaps one should be stringent not to support sechach with something that contracts tumah, lest one come to use it as sechach. Although sechach may be supported upon a stone wall, that is because people do not ordinarily use stones as sechach. Several later authorities therefore rule that initially one should avoid supporting the sechach with an object that contracts tumah. After the fact, however, or where no other materials are available, the accepted halachah is that one may support sechach with an object that contracts tumah, as is evident at the end of siman 630.
(23) Therefore, one may not place sechach upon it. See Darkei Moshe, whose words the Gra quotes in his commentary. He considered it obvious that one may not use the ladder itself as sechach, because it is a receptacle and contracts tumah. Consequently, one should certainly be stringent not to place sechach upon it. This is all the more true of wagon ladders, or the ladder of a feeding trough that rests upon two pegs and holds fodder for an animal, which is unquestionably a complete receptacle and contracts tumah according to the Torah. If, however, the ladder has no holes and the rungs are fastened to the sidepieces with nails, it is included among flat wooden utensils, which do not contract tumah even D'Rabbanan. Some authorities are nevertheless stringent even in this case, applying the prohibition without distinction.
(24) And even, etc., it is forbidden to use it to hold the sechach in place. This too is considered supporting the sechach. In addition, the ladder itself is at least invalid sechach.
(25) Which are susceptible to tumas midras. Meaning, they are fit to become susceptible, even when new and certainly not yet tamei. Know that some authorities maintain that any wooden utensil of some width upon which an object can be placed contracts tumah D'Rabbanan because it resembles a receptacle. Therefore one should not place a shovel or rake upon the sechach to hold it down, or beneath the sechach to support it, even if the utensil has broken.
