One should be uncertain, etc. The basis of the uncertainty is that there is a side to permit, since a ladder is not susceptible to tumah, as Tosafot wrote in the first chapter, page 5, in the name of Torat Kohanim, for it has no aspect of receiving anything. There is also a side to forbid, since there are holes in the sidepieces of the ladder into which the rungs are inserted, so it resembles a receptacle. In the Responsa of the Rashba it implies that it is permitted with a ladder where there are no holes, but the rungs are attached to the sides of the ladder's sidepieces. Nevertheless, one should not be lenient in this, because the Sages did not distinguish.
In order to place s'chach on top of it. This is difficult: the valid s'chach is greater than it. So too it is stated in Responsa Rashba, siman 213, that there is no prohibition when one fixes items that do not have, with iron nails in the s'chach, on account of susceptibility to tumah, since the valid s'chach is greater than it, etc. If the reason here is that supporting the s'chach is worse, according to the view that will be mentioned, with God's help, at the end of siman 630, then it is difficult why he permits nearby to connect the poles of the sukkah with iron nails, where the reason is that we are not concerned about the supporting item, as written in se'if 8. The Rema wrote nothing there, implying that he agrees. Likewise at the end of siman 630 it is proven there that we are not particular about the supporting item. One must say that here it speaks where the ladder is four tefachim wide, so it is invalid s'chach, which invalidates in the middle with four tefachim, as below in siman 630. If so, what the Rema wrote afterwards, such as a bench or chair, etc., must likewise speak of similar items, where they are four tefachim wide. But this is difficult, for invalid s'chach does not invalidate in siman 630 unless no valid measure of sukkah remains, as stated in siman 630:2.
