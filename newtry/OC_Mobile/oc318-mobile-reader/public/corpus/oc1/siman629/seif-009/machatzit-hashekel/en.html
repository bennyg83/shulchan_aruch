He placed them on the spits. Meaning, it is stated there that if he roofed with spits, which are invalid for s'chach, and left space between them, namely between each spit and the next, equal to a spit, and placed valid s'chach in that space, it is nevertheless invalid unless the valid s'chach exceeds the spits. Or, if the spits are laid lengthwise, he should place the valid s'chach crosswise, or the reverse. Thus, when the spits are laid lengthwise and he places the s'chach crosswise, or the reverse, the spits support the s'chach so that it does not fall. Since the prohibition of a supporting item is because of a decree, this is understandable, for there there is no reason to decree, since they permitted it only, etc.

For at the end of siman 630, etc. From there the law of a supporting item emerges. The Mishnah on 21b teaches: one who supports his sukkah on the legs of a bed, meaning that the bed is the floor of the sukkah and also its walls, it is valid. Rabbi Yehudah says: if it cannot stand on its own it is invalid. The Amoraim disagree about Rabbi Yehudah's reason. One says it is because it lacks fixedness, and Tosafot explain that from the bed floor to the s'chach there are not ten tefachim. The other says it is because he supports it with something susceptible to tumah, namely the bed. According to this reason, even if there are ten tefachim from the bed floor to the s'chach, it is invalid. The poskim disagree whether we rule like this answer, because he supports it, etc. The Beit Yosef at the end of siman 630 wrote the reason that supporting one's sukkah on the legs of the bed is invalid because it is not ten tefachim high. Where it is ten tefachim high, he wrote there that it is valid. This implies that he does not hold like the one who says it is because it is supported by something susceptible to tumah.
