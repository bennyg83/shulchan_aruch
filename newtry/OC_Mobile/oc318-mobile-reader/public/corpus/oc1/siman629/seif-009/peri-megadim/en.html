There is See the Taz. There are four kinds of ladders. First, where its sidepieces are perforated but not from side to side: this is considered a receptacle and depends on the dispute of the Rambam in Kelim 12:3 and the Rashba in responsum 195, cited by the Magen Avraham letter 8, whether a receptacle made to be filled forever is called a receptacle. In any event it is rabbinically forbidden, like female arrows, unless one says that there, either way, if the iron is fixed, as written in letter 6. Second, where its sidepieces are perforated from side to side: there is no receptacle. Or where the rungs are fixed at the sides of the sidepieces with nails; a groove in the sidepieces requires examination. It is not midras, as written in Terumat HaDeshen 90 and 91, because it is not made for the pleasure of treading, but only for walking on it to climb. The Taz wrote not to use it as s'chach because of the principle that the Sages did not distinguish. One can say that after the fact he fulfilled his obligation; see Magen Avraham. Third, a ladder of a manger, placed on two pegs, with fodder and grass there for animal food. Fourth, a wagon ladder. The Bach wrote that these are impure, because they are receptacles and are impure by Torah law; see there. It implies that even if removed from the wagon or wall, it is impure by Torah law, like a bed that becomes impure limb by limb; see Sukkah 20. It is not comparable to broken vessels, which are only rabbinic.
