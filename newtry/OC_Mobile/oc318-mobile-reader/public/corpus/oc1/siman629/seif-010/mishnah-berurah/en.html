(30) If the waste exceeds the food, etc. This ruling first states a general principle and then its particulars. If the produce was harvested for food, the stems and handles combine with the food. If it was harvested for sechach, they combine with the waste, as explained below.
(31) The status of food. Strictly speaking, a handle is regarded as part of the food only up to three tefachim. Since, however, there are handles that combine with food even when much longer, Magen Avraham writes that one should be stringent and disqualify all handles, even very long ones. Accordingly, one should not cover a sukkah with grain stalks before the grain has been threshed from them. Peri Megadim expresses some doubt even after threshing, but Bikkurei Yaakov disputes him and agrees with the Taz below in seif 18 that it is permitted. Nevertheless, one must be careful, because some grain commonly remains in the ears even after threshing, and the straw then retains the status of a handle. Therefore, one who wishes to use grain straw as sechach should remove the ears. It appears that with stalks of species harvested by sickle, regarding which the handle is legally considered food only up to three tefachim, there is no need to be stringent after threshing when more than three tefachim remain.
