10) [Seif 2] Such as spits. It appears that this refers to wooden spits, for if they were metal, they would already be excluded because they do not grow from the ground. Beit Yosef; Taz, s.k. 4. So too the Levush wrote: wooden spits. However, the Magen Avraham, s.k. 2, wrote that it refers to iron spits, whereas wooden spits are not susceptible to tumah even rabbinically, like male arrows, since they are flat wooden utensils. He left the words of the Beit Yosef requiring further study, see there. The Bach wrote that it refers even to metal spits whose handles are wooden. Although the handles have no receptacle and are like male arrows, one may not use even their wooden portion as s'chach because of the spit, which was susceptible to tumah, see there. However, the Shiltei Gibborim, sec. 4, wrote that the words of the Beit Yosef are primary, since the Mishnah teaches spits together with bedposts, all of which are wooden. So too the Eliyah Rabbah, sec. 1, wrote that although flat wooden utensils are not susceptible to tumah, they are nevertheless forbidden rabbinically, and they are inferior to the male arrows of seif 3. So too wrote Eshel Avraham, sec. 1, and Mishbetzos Zahav, sec. 4, that wooden spits are susceptible to tumah rabbinically because they serve a person and serve in the manner of a table, see there. So too Sefer Bigdei Yesha wrote that the case concerns spits designated for roasting and other uses, which serve a person. Utensils that serve a person's needs are susceptible to tumah even though they are flat wooden utensils, as explained at length in Tosafos, Sukkah 5a, and in Toras Kohanim, see there.
