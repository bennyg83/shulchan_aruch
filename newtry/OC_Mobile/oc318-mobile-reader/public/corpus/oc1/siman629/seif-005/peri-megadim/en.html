And all vessels; see the Taz and Tosafot Sukkah 5a, s.v. umisgarto, at the end of the passage. See letter 5. One can say that with a table and the like, according to everyone the impurity of flat wooden implements is rabbinic, and if it broke perhaps this is a decree upon a decree and one may use it as s'chach. In the Gemara on 16a, worn-out vessels, cloth pieces less than three by three, implies actual woven flax whose growing from the ground is not recognizable. Even flax beaten and combed in this manner is invalid as in se'if 4. It is forced to say that invalid there means by Torah law. Furthermore, by Torah law one may cover with worn-out garments and broken vessels; only rabbinically are they invalid, and the Rambam wrote invalid even regarding rabbinic invalidity. See what I will write, with God's help, in letter 6. In Sukkah 15, regarding the statement that it is impossible to be exact, see Tosafot and the Rosh. If we say that wooden spits and bed legs, when they are broken vessels, are invalid only rabbinically, and doubt in a rabbinic matter is lenient, one can answer in several ways. Some long bedposts receive tumah by Torah law; see Sukkah 15b.
