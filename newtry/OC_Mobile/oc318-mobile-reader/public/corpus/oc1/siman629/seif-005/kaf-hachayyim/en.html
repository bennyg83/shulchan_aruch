5) Regarding s'chach or walls of a sukkah made from items from which benefit is forbidden, whether we say in this case that mitzvos were not given for personal benefit, or whether we do not say so because the body derives benefit from them, and likewise regarding wood from an asheirah tree, see Beit HaShoevah, secs. 5 and 6, where he discussed this at length and concluded that the sukkah is invalid even after the fact, see there. However, the Erech HaShulchan, sec. 1, wrote that if permitted s'chach is unavailable, one may rely on the lenient opinions throughout all seven days of the festival. On the eighth day, however, one should be stringent and not sit in it, since there is bodily benefit and there is an opinion that, regarding a rabbinic mitzvah, we do not say that mitzvos were not given for personal benefit, see there. See below, siman 630, sec. 3. See also above, siman 586, seif 3, and our words there. Nevertheless, it appears that one should not recite a blessing, because of the principle that in a case of uncertainty regarding blessings we are lenient.
