17) Know that every wooden utensil that is somewhat broad and fit for placing something upon it is susceptible to tumah, because it resembles a receptacle. So wrote Tosafos, Sukkah 5a. Accordingly, one should not place a shovel or rake upon the sukkah, even if it broke. Magen Avraham, s.k. 11; Eliyah Rabbah, sec. 11; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, sec. 14. Nevertheless, see Bikkurei Yaakov, whose view inclines toward leniency in a pressing circumstance, permitting use of it for s'chach after it broke. Since even while intact it was susceptible to tumah only rabbinically, there is no reason to decree once it is broken, see there. The Mishna Berurah, sec. 10, cited his words. This is also the view of Beit HaShoevah, sec. 11, see there. If they are not fit for placing anything upon them, they are permitted even initially. Beit HaShoevah, ibid.
