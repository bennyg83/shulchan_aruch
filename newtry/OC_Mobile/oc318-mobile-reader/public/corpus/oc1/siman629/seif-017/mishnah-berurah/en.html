(44) Since, etc. This is not comparable to siman 626, seif 2, which states that when one cuts a tree standing above the sukkah in order to validate it and use the tree itself as sechach, cutting alone does not suffice until he also shakes and repositions it for the sake of sechach, because cutting is not yet considered a sufficient act. Here, however, untying is sufficient, because the bundle was not intrinsically invalid. It had been placed for the sake of sechach and was prohibited only by rabbinic decree, as explained in seif 15. There, by contrast, its invalidity was according to the Torah because it was attached, and therefore a complete act, namely shaking it after cutting, is required. The final clause teaches that a bundle placed there to dry, whose invalidity is according to the Torah, is exactly like that case and must be moved after being untied. Shaking means moving the pieces from their places.
