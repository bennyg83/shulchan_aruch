Section 2: And if it falls. As written in Taanis 27b.
And this fast, etc. Unlike Rabbeinu Tam, who wrote that it is a fixed fast, and so wrote the Raavad, that this is what is meant by "the words of the fasts, etc." This is not so, for in their days it was one of the festivals written in Megillas Taanis, and in the Yerushalmi mentioned above, "is it itself not, etc., the day before the fourteenth, etc., which is forbidden in eulogizing." This shows that even without this it would have been forbidden in eulogy and fasting. One cannot say it refers to eulogy alone, as the Ran wrote at the end of chapter 2 of Taanis, because the language of the Yerushalmi does not imply this, since it says "to inform you, etc." The same difficulty applies to the Rosh, who wrote in Sefer Torah: and according to what I explained above, etc. Regarding what is written, "the words of the fasts," the Ran wrote that he already explained that it refers to the four fasts, and this is what it means: they accepted upon themselves to make joy, as they had already accepted the words of the fasts. From this the Ran wrote that "their cry" is difficult, for the four fasts are not for crying out and prayer. One cannot say that they accepted to fast and when the Temple was built it was nullified, just as the four fasts were then nullified, for if so, why did they need the day of Nikanor? In any case it would be for joy and gladness like the four fasts, just as the Gemara asks in the first chapter of Rosh Hashanah regarding the third of Tishrei.
Such as, etc. For we do not fast even the first or last fasts, as written at the end of the first chapter of Taanis, and they said this only regarding Tishah BeAv and Yom Kippur, as written in chapter 4 of Pesachim. It is obvious for a sick person who does not, etc.
And even. For even regarding the four fasts they said in Rosh Hashanah 18b: if they wish, they do not, etc.; only because of breaching a fence, as the Ramban wrote.
And they repay afterward. For this fast is not fixed, as written in Maseches Sofrim, that they would fast after Purim because of Nikanor and his companions. See the Ran there.
But other. As written in Taanis 11a.
And what if it falls. For the baal bris is permitted even on other fasts, even when they are not deferred, except for Tishah BeAv, as written there, 12a, and Eruvin 41a, where Rabbi Elazar bar Tzadok said, etc. Tosafos in Rosh Hashanah 19a under "forbidden" wrote that this applies specifically to Tishah BeAv, which is stringent; but on other fasts he need not fast at all, from the fact that it says that before them and after them are permitted, and according to the Sages, without completing the fast. Perforce, on a festival even in such a case it is forbidden, and so too a groom on the day of his wedding. See Magen Avraham, siman 559, seif katan 11, in the name of Rav Shmuel HaLevi; see there.
