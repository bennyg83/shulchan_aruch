2) They fast on the thirteenth of Adar. For in the days of Mordechai and Esther they gathered on the thirteenth of Adar to fight and stand for their lives, and they needed to request mercy and supplications that Hashem help them take revenge from their enemies. We find that when they were on a day of war, they would fast, for our Sages said that Moshe Rabbeinu fasted on the day that he fought with Amalek. If so, certainly in the days of Mordechai they fasted on that day. Therefore all Israel practiced fasting on the thirteenth of Adar, and it is called the Fast of Esther, in order to remember that Hashem, may He be blessed, sees and hears every person at the time of his distress when he fasts and returns to Hashem with all his heart, as He did in those days.
3) To fast on Thursday. But on Friday one does not initially establish a fast, because of the honor of Shabbos, since on a fast they are accustomed to say selichos and supplications, and on Friday it is not fitting to do so, because they will not be able to trouble themselves for the honor of Shabbos. One who was on the road and forgot that they fast on Thursday, and ate, and at night came home and heard that everyone fasted today, should fast the next day on Friday. Maharil would not teach the halachah on the Fast of Esther because they were occupied with buying Purim needs, unless Purim fell on Sunday. Eliyah Rabbah wrote: it appears to me that all this refers to going to yeshivah, but fixed study in the study hall is not included in this, for it does not so much neglect the needs of the meal. We do not say Tzidkascha on that Shabbos when Purim is the next day, just as we do not say supplications on Erev Purim on a weekday, so too we do not say Tziduk HaDin on Shabbos that is Erev Purim.
4) In distress. This refers to those with eye pain; but a pregnant woman, even if she is not in distress, does not fast. So wrote Yeshuos Yaakov. Eliyah Rabbah is stringent about this, but regarding a woman within thirty days after childbirth, he too is lenient.
5) And they repay afterward. According to the Yeshuos Yaakov mentioned above, it is reasonable that this law applies only to those with eye pain, upon whom there is an essential obligation not to separate from the community, and only now, because their eyes hurt, they are exempt; therefore after their pain goes away they repay the fast. But pregnant and nursing women are essentially exempt even if they are not in distress. However, at first glance this is not implied from the words of the Bach.
6) They should not separate. Even one who is traveling on the road and the fast is difficult for him.
7) It is permitted to eat at the circumcision. This means all the people invited to the meal, even more than ten, and even in the morning they may eat. However, all of them must fast the next day, as below. See the Taz and Eliyah Rabbah, who disagree with this gloss. Their opinion is that the fixing of the fast is specifically on Thursday, and therefore all of them are forbidden to eat on Thursday, and they should make the meal at night. However, the baalei bris themselves may eat on Thursday after the circumcision and need not fast the next day, as above in siman 559, section 9, regarding Tishah BeAv. See Peri Megadim, who wrote that one who is lenient to practice according to Rama's opinion has not lost. So too Moed Katan and Shevus Yaakov agreed with the opinion of Shulchan Aruch.
