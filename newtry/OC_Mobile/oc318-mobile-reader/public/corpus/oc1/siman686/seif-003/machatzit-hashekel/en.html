(2) From the Hebrew the Gemara 20 if not 20 and 25 when Purim falls on the fifth and sixth day. Dabioum V. He did not recite the Halachah on Purim and Arash, therefore he preached on the Fast of Esther 20 there. And Amash MA C. RNA Sec VI:

(3) And in Hagahot Maimoniyot: even on Purim etc., as in Megillah 7a — R' Eliezer was sitting before Rav Kahana on Purim; it was evening and the rabbis did not come to the beit midrash, and Rav Kahana said: why did the rabbis not come? They said: perhaps they are busy with the Purim feast. He said: is it not possible for them to eat at night (i.e. the night of the 14th)? He said: the Master does not hold like Rava who said: a Purim feast eaten at night did not fulfill etc. — it implies that in other years their way was to come before their rabbi even on Purim; from his asking why they did not come etc. And in Sefer Avodat Yisrael he wondered: for it is explicit in Maharil the reason is because they are busy, and what he wrote that Esther does not teach is only a hint; therefore he wrote that if one has someone to trouble himself for him he must study before his rabbi — if so there is no proof from there that in other years those who were not busy came on erev Shabbat. One may say the intent of Hagahot Maimoniyot is that when he said perhaps they are busy with the feast — the intent is not eating the feast, for on that the language "busy" does not apply; also how did Rava sit before Rav Kahana, and Rav Kahana himself — also Rav Assi needed to eat a Purim feast. Although one must hold like Rav Kahana regarding the night meal per Mishnah Ahzaz, nevertheless on Rosh Hashanah Kesha there is a teaching darba dela izd — a meal does not prevent regular study before one's rabbi, and an hour or two of the day suffices for eating needs. Rather R' Eliezer said perhaps they are busy preparing feast needs and R' Eliezer has someone to trouble himself for him. Here one may say R' Eliezer said perhaps they are busy from doubt to judge them favorably. R' Eliezer is difficult either way: if you think they came yesterday on Fast of Esther before Rav Kahana — they had someone to trouble for them, and this is not an excuse that perhaps they are busy; if they did not come yesterday either, on Purim why did they not come today more — he should have wondered yesterday on Fast of Esther why they did not come. If because he holds as seems that if he made the feast at night he fulfilled — therefore yesterday he did not wonder knowing they were busy, unlike by day per his view the bother already passed — what is it that Rava said perhaps they are busy? This excuse is insufficient; rather he should have answered that Rava said a Purim feast made at night did not fulfill. Or at least he should understand there are matters within what he asked why the rabbis did not come. Or even if busy and they have no one to trouble for them — nevertheless they came before their rabbi on Fast of Esther; Purim itself they have time to prepare. What he said perhaps they are busy (and one strains that Purim then fell on Sunday; also possible they ate a little on Motzei Shabbat even though they ate third meal of Shabbat, as Magen Avraham in name of Bach siman 136 seif 1 — from all places they feast at night meal; nevertheless better on day when only 11th falls Thursday as above) — per words of Hagahot Maimoniyot on Purim they were accustomed thus when not busy, proven from Fast of Esther that even busy people came before their rabbi as above.
