1) Also before them, etc. See siman 670, seif katan 2, and Kol Bo, page 47, that "before them, etc." was said only regarding a fast of distress. But this fast is a remembrance of Esther's fast, so that one remember that the blessed Creator sees and hears every person in his time of distress when he fasts and returns to Him with all his heart, as He did for our fathers in this time; end quote. Hagahos Maimoniyos wrote: Maharil would not teach halachah on the Fast of Esther because Esther did not tell. It appears to me that this is an error, for "Esther did not tell" is what is written afterward. Furthermore, in Megillah, page 7, regarding the students of Rav Kahana, "they were late, etc.," it plainly implies that one may learn from the teacher even on Purim itself; end quote. I am astonished, for in Maharil itself it is explained that he did not write the reason "Esther did not tell" except as an allusion and mnemonic, as he wrote, "and a support for the matter, etc." The main reason is because they are occupied with Purim needs. Know this, for it is written there that unmarried young men, who burden others to take care of their needs, do teach. Also, there is no difficulty from the Gemara in Megillah at all, for it is possible that they were free, and what it says, "they are occupied with the Purim meal," means with eating the meal itself. It appears to me that all this refers to going to yeshivah, but fixed study in the synagogue is not included in this, for there is not so much neglect of meal needs in that. Maharil wrote that when the Fast of Esther is advanced to Thursday, he taught; and likewise when it occurred on Wednesday, he taught that day, because on Friday one should not teach, for it is Shushan Purim; see there.
