And if he was a craftsman, etc., amateur's work - And see Shiltei Gibborim who wrote in the name of Riaz who brought from the Yerushalmi that the same applies regarding shoes. And it appears to me that regarding an amateur who sews in his usual way, they did not permit [this] for shoes (and the reason is perhaps that there, with an amateur who will need to sew one [stitch] at a time and not a full needle like a craftsman, that itself is a shinui; which is not so with shoes, for even a craftsman needs to sew one at a time, so there is no shinui here). For if not so, it would say in Pesachim 54 that the cobblers are permitted to make shoes on the fourteenth [of Nisan], for so an amateur sews in his usual way, as it said there regarding the tailors.
Rather we require, etc. - See Mishnah Berurah, what he wrote: that when one does [it] for others, even for the needs of the festival, it is forbidden. And behold, I saw in Shaarei Teshuvah who cited in the name of the Noda BiYehuda, Tinyana, siman 104, that he permits even for pay for a craftsman to sew by means of a shinui; and this is astonishing, for it is against the ruling of the Shulchan Aruch in siman 542. And I thought to myself: perhaps he ruled for practical law like the view of the Kol Bo that was brought in Beis Yosef in this siman, who permits for the needs of the festival even for pay (and Riaz brought in Shiltei Gibborim also holds thus), even though Beis Yosef wrote about him that it is a mistake, and he ruled for practical law in siman 542 like the view of many Rishonim who forbid this. However, afterward I looked into his responsum, and he sides that the view of the Shulchan Aruch is also thus in this, like Riaz; and that which he forbids in siman 542 is regarding melacha that is not for the needs of the festival; and that which he calls "permitted crafts" means something that is not a true melacha; but something that is a true melacha, and its heter is because it is for the needs of the festival, in this it is permitted to do even for others for pay. And because of this he permits a hired craftsman to sew for pay with dog-tooth stitching. And this is a great wonder, for the words of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch flow from the words of the Rosh that were brought in Beis Yosef; and there it is mentioned explicitly regarding the incident of Rav Chama that necessarily those employees were doing a melacha permitted on the festival, such as repairing benches, etc.; and it is known that repairing benches was necessarily permitted [only] for the needs of the festival, for otherwise even an amateur's work is forbidden, as is explained in the Tur and Mordechai and many poskim; and even so the Rosh wrote that for pay it is forbidden. And also at the end of this siman, Beis Yosef wrote explicitly that for pay it is forbidden even for the needs of the festival. And the truth is as all the Acharonim wrote (Chelkas Mechokek, Peri Megadim, Maamar Mordechai, and Nahar Shalom): that what is written in the Shulchan Aruch at the beginning of siman 542, "even permitted crafts, etc.," includes both what an amateur sews in his usual way and what a craftsman does as amateur's work, namely dog-tooth stitching and all such things as are explained above in siman 540, that they permitted doing amateur's work for the needs of the festival; in all of them it is forbidden to do for others for pay. However, regarding the substance of the matter, it is known that Nahar Shalom and Eliyah Rabbah sided that the Kol Bo is not a lone opinion in this; for Riaz brought in Shiltei Gibborim also holds thus, and likewise Nimukei Yosef who explained Rav Chama's words with a different explanation, and consequently there is no source for the Rosh's ruling; and also Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah held that the Rif's view is also like the Kol Bo. But in Birkei Yosef he rejected his words: for what he wrote from Nimukei Yosef is no proof that he disagrees with the essence of the Rosh's ruling, for he brought a support for his words from elsewhere, from what one writes tefillin and mezuzos for others as a favor and not for pay; and it implies even where the one for whom he writes the mezuzah needs it for the festival to affix in a house where he wants to dwell, and even so for pay it is forbidden. (And that which Eliyah Rabbah rejected, that there writing is a craftsman's work, is not a rejection; for there, what is permitted for oneself as craftsman's work they forbade for others for pay, and likewise in our matters: what is permitted for oneself as amateur's work is forbidden for others for pay.) And you should know further that Rabbeinu Yerucham, who copied the ruling of Nimukei Yosef that he derived there according to his explanation, even so copied the Rosh's ruling; and likewise Nimukei Yosef himself in chapter 3 in the sugya of "for others as a favor" holds that hire is considered melacha. And regarding what Eliyah Rabbah brought from Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah that the Rif's view is also thus, it is already evident from Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah himself at the end that there is no clear proof from the Rif's words; rather he sides thus. And therefore Birkei Yosef concludes, and likewise Maamar Mordechai, that one should not budge from the ruling of the Shulchan Aruch in siman 542, which is the view of several Rishonim, namely the Rosh and the Mordechai and the Tur and Rabbeinu Yerucham; and I also found likewise in the Eshkol and in the novellae of the Ritva in the name of Rabbeinu Yedidyah. However, I found there a novel point: that the Ritva from his own view divided for practical law in this, that this applies specifically where the householder can do that melacha through others (namely a non-Jew even for pay, or a Jew free of charge); but if he cannot obtain other people except these, and they want specifically pay, it is permitted, whether for a matter of loss or for the needs of the festival. And I did not find this in another posek; and even according to him, for the needs of the festival which he permitted is specifically by means of a shinui, for the worker is not better than oneself, as the aforementioned Noda BiYehuda wrote.
A recognizable shinui - See Chayei Adam who wrote that if one does not find a craftsman who will do in this manner, and he needs [it] for the needs of the festival, it is permitted to give pay to a worker who has nothing but bread, according to all opinions, so that he will have Yom Tov needs in abundance. And behold, in truth he goes according to his view there in Nishmas Adam, like Eliyah Rabbah, that "one who has nothing to eat" means that he does not have Yom Tov needs in abundance; and therefore here, where the householder needs the garment for the festival, he relies on Eliyah Rabbah. And nevertheless it is not clear for practical law; for according to Magen Avraham there, one certainly may not be lenient except through someone who literally has nothing to eat.
