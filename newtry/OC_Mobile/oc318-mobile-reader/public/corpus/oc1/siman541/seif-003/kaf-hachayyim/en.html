3) There, in the gloss. It is permitted to prepare wicks, etc. Piskei Tosafos, chapter VeEilu Megalchin, subsection 79, however, writes: 'A wick may not be twisted during the festival. Perhaps this is forbidden even through a non-Jew, and even for synagogue wicks, since this is not so exceptional a mitzvah as tzitzis.' These are his words. Derashos Mahari Weil, Hilchos Chol HaMoed, likewise writes that Mahari Weil taught that wicks may not be made during the festival and one should take care to make them beforehand for the needs of the festival. These are his words. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1, writes that the halachic authorities imply there is no distinction between cotton wicks and those made of another material. See there. Eshel Avraham, subsection 2, writes likewise. Nimukei Yosef in the aforementioned chapter, however, cites an opinion that permits it because it is unskilled workmanship. Rabbeinu Yerucham writes likewise in the name of Ritva. Beis Yosef cites this and writes that this is the custom. This is why Rama writes here in the gloss that it is permitted to prepare wicks, etc. Birkei Yosef, subsection 1, likewise writes that this is the custom: women prepare wicks on Chol HaMoed, as Maran writes in Beis Yosef at the end of the siman. Levush omits the permission because some disagree, as Eliyah Rabbah writes, but the widespread custom is to permit it, as stated. These are his words. Zechor LeAvraham, subsection 8, cites this. It appears to me that all authorities forbid making tallow candles, and certainly wax candles, because smoothing and similar melachos are also involved, contrary to those who customarily permit it. Chayei Adam there, subsection 9.
