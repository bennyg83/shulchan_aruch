[5] To salt upon it, etc. Meat (Tur); and likewise what he wrote, "and it is permitted to use a pretext to salt it, etc.," the Taz explained in siman 500, s.k. 2: he salts a little meat here and a little meat here until he salts the entire hide; and so I found in Raavan, folio 76, on this. And the Taz elaborated to be particular needlessly; and to salt one piece of meat in many places on the hide is forbidden, for since he salted more than needed, it is evident he is doing it for the sake of the hide; and regarding feathers, etc., by analogy to a hide before those who tread (Mordechai). And this requires further study, for there, as the Gemara states the reason because it is fit to recline upon, where that reason does not apply it is forbidden; and one can say that specifically there, where we decree lest he come to salt, we need this reason, whereas regarding feathers, where "lest he come to salt" does not apply, we permit their end for the sake of their beginning. Further one can say we only learn [to forbid] where he makes a fixing, such as placing [it] on pegs, for we decree lest he come to salt for a fixing; but to store them away is permitted, for it is unlike a fixing. But it is difficult for Tosafos Shabbos folio 49, who challenged regarding what was said "they permitted their end, etc.," that a hide is forbidden in handling; end quote: perhaps handling is always permitted, and the need for the reason "they permitted their end, etc." is because he is doing something like a fixing; and a pretext is permitted for every person, even one who is not a scholar. Magen Avraham wrote: It appears to me that the hide lies on a slope, for if not so it would be like an unperforated vessel; end quote. And this requires further study, for certainly the blood exits through the hide, as is written in Yoreh De'ah, siman 71.
