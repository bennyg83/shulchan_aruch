1) [Se'if 1] One may not flay by regel on Yom Tov, etc. This is the wording of Rambam, chapter 3, law 6. Rashi explains in Bechoros 33a that this means removing the entire hide intact through the legs, to make it into a bellows for smiths or for sellers of honey and oil, who place these products in sheepskins removed intact. These are his words. Beis Yosef writes that Rambam's words appear to mean that it is permitted whenever one does not flay in a manner that removes all the flesh through one leg, even though the entire hide remains intact on its front and back. Rashi's explanation, however, implies that on Yom Tov it is forbidden to remove the hide intact in this manner whenever one begins turning the hide from the legs toward the head. If he begins at the mouth and works toward the tail, it is permitted, because only flaying by regel was forbidden and this is not flaying by regel. See there. Bach writes that the aforementioned explanation of Beis Yosef is incorrect, for even flaying from the legs toward the head is forbidden on Yom Tov, and certainly from the head toward the legs. He concludes that in practice it is proper to be stringent in every manner when removing the hide intact, in accordance with Tur. See there. Sheyarei Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef, subsection 1; Olas Shabbos, subsection 1; Magen Avraham, subsection 1, cite him. This also appears to be the view of Taz, subsection 1. Ateres Zekeinim; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1; Mikraei Kodesh, siman 14, Likutei Rima, subsection 1, write likewise. Pri Chadash, however, writes that one may be lenient in accordance with Rambam. Only flaying by regel was forbidden, but one may tear the hide between the two legs, from one leg to the other, and then cut on each side with a knife or by hand until the entire hide comes off intact around its circumference, unlike Bach. See there. Shulchan Gavoah, at the end of subsection 1, writes regarding this statement of Pri Chadash that as a matter of halachah this ruling is correct for us, who have accepted the rulings of Shulchan Aruch. Certainly, according to Rambam it is permitted to flay through the neck, which is easier than through one leg. See there. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1, likewise writes that Avodas HaKodesh of Rashba and Kol Bo use Rambam's wording, unlike Bach, who is stringent in every manner. See there. This matter is therefore disputed. Since the prohibition is due to exertion, it appears that if this is an ordinarily performed procedure and involves no exertion, one may be lenient in accordance with Rambam, Shulchan Gavoah, and those who agree with them. Only removing the flesh through one leg should be forbidden, because all agree that this involves excessive exertion. If, however, it is not a commonly performed procedure and entails great exertion, removing the hide intact should be forbidden in every manner.
