(s.k. 3) To lift, etc. For the reason it is permitted, etc.: that is to say, even though we hold that one does not spread the fats, as is written in se'if 4, for fats are different: the matter is recognizable that he does so for the need of the fats, as Magen Avraham wrote in s.k. 8; but spreading the hide is not recognizable as being done for the need of the hide, for one can say he does so for his own need, and that is for the need of Yom Tov; namely, because the hide is fit to recline upon (that is, to sit upon it as it is spread out).
And so is implied in the Gemara, etc. And they challenge: how does this differ from a hide before those who tread, etc. This is akin to the first proof, except that the first proof was from the words of the one who answers, and as we hold; now from the words of the questioner: why does he challenge the prohibition of spreading fats from placing a hide before those who tread, even though placing a hide before those who tread was mentioned first in the Mishnah, for we learned: Beis Shammai say one does not place a hide before those who tread, and he shall not lift it, etc., and Beis Hillel permit; nevertheless, since lifting the hide onto pegs, according to the Bach's explanation, is exactly like the prohibition of spreading fats on pegs, he should all the more have challenged from "he shall not lift it, etc." And this "he shall lift it" that Beis Shammai forbids means handling, and upon that Beis Hillel's "they permit" refers, meaning handling; but lifting onto pegs is forbidden even according to Beis Hillel. And here we say they permitted their end for the sake of their beginning, meaning: it is implied that without this reason, handling would be forbidden; and also if it was slaughtered on erev Yom Tov, for which we do not have "they permitted their end for the sake of their beginning." And why: for without that, handling the hide is permitted.
Perhaps here it is permitted to lift, etc. That is to say: the need for the reason "they permitted their end for the sake of their beginning," and also what forbids if it was slaughtered on erev Yom Tov, refers to lifting onto pegs, which appears like processing; but handling, even from erev Yom Tov, is permitted without the reason of their end for the sake of their beginning.
And Yam Shel Shlomo wrote that even from sun to shade, etc. And he will come to withhold himself from the joy of Yom Tov, etc. Behold Yam Shel Shlomo follows his own view, for also at the end of siman 497 Magen Avraham wrote in his name that if the animal is treifah it is forbidden to handle, and he is not concerned that perhaps he will refrain and not slaughter, for treifos are uncommon; and therefore nowadays when treifos are common it is permitted. But according to what Magen Avraham wrote there, the reason we do not say "perhaps he will refrain and not slaughter" from concern that he may find a treifah and then be forbidden to move it from sun to shade and it will spoil is that it is possible he will slaughter immediately in a shaded place, and even if he delays moving it he will not lose, since it lies in shade. And if so, the same should apply here and it would be forbidden to move it from sun to shade. And perhaps one can say: granted regarding a treifah, even if treifos were common, nevertheless since it is not [yet] treifah he will not refrain from fear that he may find a treifah; and if even so he fears from a doubt, still he will not refrain, for it is possible to slaughter in the shade. But this reason alone, that it is possible he will slaughter in the shade, we do not rely upon. And if so, regarding a hide, which is certain, we do not rely on the possibility of slaughtering it in the shade, and we are concerned that he may come to refrain and not slaughter, and they permitted the handling.
