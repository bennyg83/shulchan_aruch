[3] [Levush] And if he wishes, etc. Behold Maharshal, Bach, and all the later authorities wondered that there must necessarily be a scribal error in the Tur, since he wrote this ruling in the name of Bahag, and the Rosh in chapter HaMevi wrote in his name that even to eat it is forbidden, because it is easier to flay; and so wrote Tosafos, Mordechai, Agudah, and Riaz; end quote. And I set my heart to look in Bahag himself, and I saw on folio 46: specifically the head and the legs, but we do not perform milug on an entire kid on Yom Tov, for it is possible to flay its hide; end quote. Behold, even-to-eat is not mentioned there; on the contrary, it implies a permit if he wishes to eat the skin, for the reason "it is possible to flay its hide" applies only when he does not wish to eat the skin, but only does milug in place of flaying; and likewise Tosafos on folio 34, Mordechai, and Riaz did not write "even to eat"; and in truth there is no logic to withhold food preparation because of toil; and it is difficult for the Rosh and Agudah; and one must say they hold this is not food preparation, because only the most pampered do so, as the Rashba wrote; and nevertheless the Rashba concludes like those who permit, and it implies even if he does not wish to eat the skin; and so I found in Shibbolei HaLeket, siman 86, and Sefer Tanya, and Agur in the name of Ba'al HaDibburim and Rav Kohen Tzedek; therefore it appears to me to be lenient at least when he wishes to eat the skin; and regarding fowl, according to all opinions it is permitted in every case. And behold in Yam Shel Shlomo, siman 21, he wrote this language: The Tur wrote, if he wishes to scald it in order to eat its skin, because there is more toil [otherwise], it is permitted; and it is astonishing in my eyes that milug should be lighter than flaying; end quote. And so the Taz wondered. And it appears to me that the Tur means as follows: since he wishes to eat the skin, if so he would scald the skin after flaying, and that is more toil than scalding the skin while it is still on the body, as mentioned above. I also wonder: behold the Bach brings the Tur's reading as: it is forbidden to scald a kid because there is more toil in it than in flaying; and if he wishes to scald it in order to eat its skin, it is permitted; end quote. And if so, one can say the reason it is permitted in order to eat the skin is because of food preparation; and so it is clear that the Levush had this reading in the Tur.
