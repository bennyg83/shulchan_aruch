Seif 3 in the gloss: that it is permitted to move them — Peri Chadash disagrees, for in the sugya they resolve the Roman's words of Beit Hillel because it is fit to pour on them; this does not apply to feathers. In my opinion I do not understand: on the law of Beit Hillel who permits raising the hide (although the hide is muktzeh, as Tosafot Shabbat 49a wrote) there is no difficulty, for in that case of one who may not dig, it is truly fitting to permit slaughter where he has only muktzeh dirt; for they permit moving muktzeh because of a mitzvah, as Tosafot wrote that one may remove ashes of a stove to bake a pashtida. And that they forbade slaughter must be as Magen Avraham siman 109 s.k. 15 wrote, that ashes to cover blood — only moving muktzeh they permit because of a mitzvah, but not using muktzeh. If so, automatically the Gemara's challenge of Beit Hillel against Beit Hillel would not be from this that Beit Hillel permits raising them; for there they permit because of its end for a mitzvah, and there would be nullification of a mitzvah that they permit because of a mitzvah. And therefore the Gemara's challenge is because of before the trampler, for it appears like processing; and on this we answer that it is fit to pour — if so, here with feathers they properly permit, for we are concerned lest he not want to slaughter and there will be prevention of a mitzvah; and because of a mitzvah it is permitted to move muktzeh — study.
