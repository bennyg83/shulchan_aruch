To lift its hide. Meaning: to spread it out on pegs (Bach). And it is difficult, for we say in the Gemara that the reason it is permitted to place it where feet tread is because the matter is not evident, for we say it is fit to recline upon, as Rashi wrote; but on poles the matter is evident that he does it for the need of the hide, and it is forbidden; and so is implied in the Gemara, where we say it is forbidden to spread fats on pegs, and they challenge: how does this differ from a hide before those who tread, and they answer: there the matter is not evident; and they do not challenge: how does this differ from that it is permitted to spread the hide on pegs; rather, necessarily, it is forbidden to lift the hide onto pegs, and this "he shall lift it" means to handle it; and so is implied in Tosafos in Shabbos folio 49, where Tosafos challenged from what is stated in Shabbos that it is permitted to handle a hide, and here we say they permitted their end for the sake of their beginning (see siman 308, se'if 25); and what is the challenge: perhaps here we permit lifting onto pegs; rather, necessarily, lifting [onto pegs] is forbidden; and so is implied in the Rosh, Ran, and Yam Shel Shlomo. And Yam Shel Shlomo wrote that even from sun to shade it is permitted to handle it, for if you do not permit him, he will not slaughter and will come to withhold himself from the joy of Yom Tov; but if he knows that the hide will not be lost, it is forbidden to handle it; see what I wrote in se'if 4.
For roasting. So that the salt give taste in it, for roasting does not require salting (Tosafos); see Yoreh De'ah, siman 76. And it is stated in the Gemara: but for a pot it is forbidden, for it requires more salting and processes the hide; and one must say that the hide lies on a slope, for if not so it would be like an unperforated vessel.
From erev Yom Tov it is forbidden. Even if it was flayed on Yom Tov it is forbidden, for because of this he will not refrain from slaughtering, since he can flay it on erev Yom Tov (Rosh, Tur, Bach, Yam Shel Shlomo, Maggid Mishneh). And possibly, if he slaughtered it near nightfall and there was not enough time to flay it on erev Yom Tov, it is permitted; and this requires further study. And Maharil wrote in a responsum, siman 73: even if he forgot to salt it on erev Yom Tov, it is forbidden to salt it on Yom Tov, for one should not distinguish between intentional and forgetful; and if it was slaughtered on the first day of Yom Tov, this requires further study whether it is considered possible, for the first day relative to the second is like a weekday; and this requires further study; end quote.
