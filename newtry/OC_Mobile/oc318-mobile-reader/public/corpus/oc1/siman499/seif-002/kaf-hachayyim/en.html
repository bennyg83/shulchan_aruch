2) [Se'if 2] It is forbidden to scald a kid, etc. This means placing it in boiling water to remove its hair. See Yoreh Deah, siman 68, se'if 10, which states that kids, lambs, and birds may not be scalded in a kli rishon unless their forbidden fats were removed and they were salted. See there. See also below, siman 500, se'if 4, regarding the law of scalding.
3) There. Unless he scalds it in order to eat the hide. Since its hide is tender, some people eat it together with the meat, and the scalding is needed for food preparation. This resembles the permission to scald the head and feet, as stated below, siman 500, se'if 4. Many authorities, however, disagree with this, as written in the following subsection.
4) There. Unless he scalds it in order to eat the hide. Tur writes this in the name of Halachos Gedolos, and Levush writes likewise. Bach, however, writes in the name of several commentaries, which cite Halachos Gedolos, that it is forbidden to scald the entire kid even if one wishes to eat the hide. He therefore writes that the reading found in other copies of Tur is primary. Those copies state in the name of Halachos Gedolos that it is forbidden to scald a kid in order to eat its hide because this entails greater exertion than flaying it. See there. Taz, subsection 2; Olas Shabbos, subsection 2; Magen Avraham, subsection 2, write likewise. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 2; Chayei Adam, rule 91, subsection 8; Mikraei Kodesh, siman 25, subsection 7, rule accordingly. Maggid Mishneh, chapter 3, law 7, however, writes in the name of Rashba: 'Some Geonim ruled that permission was granted to scald only the head and feet of an animal, even when one intends to eat its hide, because only extremely pampered people do this. They forbade it in the same manner that they forbade perfuming utensils. Furthermore, it appears more like a weekday activity. Other halachic authorities said that the Sages did not mean only the head and feet, but spoke of the ordinary case, and this latter view is more compelling.' These are Rashba's words. Shulchan Gavoah, subsection 2, cites this and writes that Shulchan Aruch rules in accordance with the latter halachic authorities. Since the matter is rabbinic, the lenient view is followed, and this is the ruling. This is also the lenient view of Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 3, who writes that there is no reason to prevent food preparation because of exertion. See there. Pri Chadash writes regarding the aforementioned ruling of Shulchan Aruch that it is the view of some commentators, while many forbid it, and one who is stringent will be blessed. This matter is therefore disputed. It appears that in a case of need and where the joy of Yom Tov would otherwise be diminished, such as when one does not have enough meat and needs to eat the hide, one may rely upon those who permit it.
5) With a bird, however, to which flaying does not apply, one may scald and singe the entire body. Sefer Mitzvos Katan, siman 194; Bach; Taz there; Olas Shabbos there; Magen Avraham there; Eliyah Rabbah there; Chemed Moshe, subsection 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav there; Chayei Adam there; Mikraei Kodesh there. Regarding the law of singeing a bird, see Yoreh Deah, siman 68, se'if 9.
