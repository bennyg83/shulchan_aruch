And nevertheless it is permitted to handle them, for they are not muktzeh, since they are fit for kindling; and furthermore, a person's mind is upon them to handle them and store them away so they not be lost (Hagahot Ashri, Bach, Yam Shel Shlomo). And this requires further study: what differs from pits (at the end of siman 501), which are forbidden to handle even though they are fit for kindling. And upon the second reason it is difficult: behold Tosafos wrote in Shabbos folio 29: And if you say, behold we say that when one slaughters a gosling it is permitted to throw the innards that spoiled to the dogs, even though it is nolad, like a nevelah that became nevelah on Shabbos, and even so it is permitted since from yesterday his intention was to give it to the dogs when it spoils; here too, from yesterday his intention was to remove the pit when he eats the date. One can say: there the gosling was slaughtered on erev Yom Tov, and the innards are not needed for the goose, and since from while it was still day his intention was to give them to a cat if they spoil, it is considered a proper preparation; but here the pits are needed for the food and are considered like the food itself until he eats the dates, therefore they are considered nolad, and what his mind was upon from yesterday does not avail; end quote. If so, it is implied that when it was slaughtered on Yom Tov it is forbidden to handle the innards that spoiled, for it is nolad, since yesterday it stood for a person and now it stands for dogs; if so, fats too are forbidden to handle, like date pits. Therefore it appears to me that Hagahot Ashri follows his own view, for he ruled in chapter 4 of Beitzah that it is permitted to throw innards to the dogs, like R' Shimon that nolad is permitted; but according to what I wrote in siman 495 that nolad is forbidden, the fats too are forbidden to handle when it was slaughtered on Yom Tov; rather, to store them away is permitted, for we permit their end for the sake of their beginning; but when it was slaughtered on erev Yom Tov, it is permitted to handle fats, for they are fit for kindling; and the same law applies to a piece of wax, for one can place a wick upon it, so they are fit for kindling. And a hide that was flayed on erev Yom Tov: if it is moist, it is forbidden to handle it; and if it is dry, it is permitted to handle it, as is written in siman 308, se'if 25. But when it was slaughtered on Yom Tov, it is nolad; and if it is moist, it is permitted to handle it so that it not spoil; but if it is dry, it is forbidden to handle it, for it is nolad; as appears to me, these words are primary; examine and you will find praise.
