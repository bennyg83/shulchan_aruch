Baer Heitev — and he wrote that when it has not aged it is fit to eat raw, etc.; see Taz who wrote one could say its planting began with this intent; requires study in our country, where people never eat garlic and onions even when soft unless as accompaniment to bread — then there is no blessing obligation at all on them. And it is clear to bless shehakol as Magen Avraham wrote in the name of Bach; see Peri Megadim; also it appears the main planting is to spice foods with it — pickled, roasted, cooked; but a place where many eat them when soft alone raw — their law is as other vegetables to bless borei peri haAdamah as Taz wrote; garlic and onions shehakol even when aged and cooked with meat — not as Pri To'ar; see Magen Avraham and Eliyah Rabba; and Beit Yosef brought Rosh's words in his rulings that he explicitly distinguishes between this and a nut on that matter; indeed Magen Avraham's intent is also on these words of Rosh on that matter; and what he wrote regarding etrog candied in honey — likewise in Emek Berachah; and preserve of plum pits in honey or sugar — Peri Megadim wrote that if while still raw they are fit for eating under duress one blesses borei peri haEtz; likewise small oranges and almonds candied borei peri haEtz, or Feijoa candied in honey — bless shehakol; and preserve called tzideke — some say it is cucumber peel — bless shehakol, for it is inferior to orange peel; and preserve kraut one could say shehakol; and preserve of radish borei peri haAdamah on that matter; see above siman 202 and 203; see what Yad Ephraim wrote there.
