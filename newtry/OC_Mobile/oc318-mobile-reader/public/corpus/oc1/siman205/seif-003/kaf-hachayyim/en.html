3) There: Likewise, all fruits and legumes, etc. Over fresh beans, whether raw or cooked, one recites Borei Pri HaAdamah. Over dried beans eaten raw, one recites Shehakol. Riaz, cited by Shiltei Gibborim in the chapter Keitzad Mevarchin; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1, likewise writes that one who eats dried legumes raw recites Shehakol. This implies that over fresh legumes eaten raw one recites Borei Pri HaAdamah. The practice, however, is to recite Shehakol over fresh beans eaten raw, and similarly over beans called lubya in Arabic, as the Rambam writes at the beginning of Hilchos Kilayim, over fasolia beans, and over every type of seed ordinarily cooked. When eaten raw, one recites Shehakol; when cooked, Borei Pri HaAdamah. It appears to me that the reason follows Rif in the chapter Keitzad Mevarchin: over anything that people do not ordinarily eat raw, one recites Shehakol, and when it is cooked, Borei Pri HaAdamah. These likewise are ordinarily eaten only cooked, as is known, and people plant them only with the intent to cook them. Therefore, when raw, one recites only Shehakol.
