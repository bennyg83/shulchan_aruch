1) [Seif 1] Over vegetables, one recites Borei Pri HaAdamah. If one erred and recited, 'Who creates species of grasses,' over vegetables, after the fact he fulfilled his obligation. Rishon LeTziyon in his commentary, folio 14, end of side a. Accordingly, the same applies if one recited, 'Who creates species of seeds,' over legumes: after the fact he fulfilled his obligation. There is nevertheless uncertainty if one recited, 'Who creates species of seeds,' over vegetables. Birkei Yosef, paragraph 4. Shaarei Teshuvah, paragraph 1, however, considers it obvious that he fulfilled his obligation, based upon the verse, 'and like a garden causes what is sown in it to sprout'; see there.
2) There: Even if he cooked them. This refers to vegetables that are good both raw and cooked. If they are more significant when cooked, one recites Shehakol when they are raw and Borei Pri HaAdamah when they are cooked. If the reverse is true and they are more significant raw, one recites Borei Pri HaAdamah when raw and Shehakol when cooked, as stated afterward. All this applies when one eats them alone. If he eats them with bread during a meal, he recites no blessing over them, because the bread covers them, as stated above in siman 177; see there.
3) There: Likewise, all fruits and legumes, etc. Over fresh beans, whether raw or cooked, one recites Borei Pri HaAdamah. Over dried beans eaten raw, one recites Shehakol. Riaz, cited by Shiltei Gibborim in the chapter Keitzad Mevarchin; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1, likewise writes that one who eats dried legumes raw recites Shehakol. This implies that over fresh legumes eaten raw one recites Borei Pri HaAdamah. The practice, however, is to recite Shehakol over fresh beans eaten raw, and similarly over beans called lubya in Arabic, as the Rambam writes at the beginning of Hilchos Kilayim, over fasolia beans, and over every type of seed ordinarily cooked. When eaten raw, one recites Shehakol; when cooked, Borei Pri HaAdamah. It appears to me that the reason follows Rif in the chapter Keitzad Mevarchin: over anything that people do not ordinarily eat raw, one recites Shehakol, and when it is cooked, Borei Pri HaAdamah. These likewise are ordinarily eaten only cooked, as is known, and people plant them only with the intent to cook them. Therefore, when raw, one recites only Shehakol.
4) There: And legumes, etc. Over roasted legumes, such as beans, lentils, corn, and similar foods, one recites Borei Pri HaAdamah. Birkei Yosef, paragraph 1, in the name of R' Yosef Binyamin in a manuscript. The Shelah in Shaar HaOsiyos, folio 94b, rule 5, paragraph 13, writes the same. Likewise, after lupin beans are cooked, one recites Borei Pri HaAdamah. Berachos 38b. It is a legume, as Rashi, of blessed memory, writes in Beitzah 25b. It is similarly listed at the beginning of Kilayim; see Rash there. This is contrary to one who wrote that it is included among tree fruits, which is incorrect because it contradicts the Gemara. See Divrei Menachem, Hagahos HaTur, paragraph 1.
5) There: That are good raw and cooked, etc. This means that the item is as good cooked as raw, and as good raw as cooked. Shelah there, folio 94a. Magen Avraham, paragraph 3, however, writes that if they are good raw, one recites Borei Pri HaAdamah when they are raw even if they are better cooked; see there. This applies when most people eat them raw, as stated above in siman 202, paragraph 88; see there. Eshel Avraham in this siman, paragraph 3, and Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1, write the same.
