That are good cooked. Turnip law is written in this siman; the rule is one who eats turnips raw, such as meyerin and rubin, blesses shehakol, for they are better cooked than raw; and further people do not plant them to eat raw but after cooking; therefore their primary blessing is after cooking; and it is included in cabbage and beet mentioned in the Gemara and poskim; but a vegetable pickled for preserving that they call kimpsht — it appears to bless on it even when raw borei peri haAdamah, for its pickling is like cooking and it has good taste like cooked; many are accustomed to eat them raw, as appears to me.

And garlic and leeks. For the way is to eat them raw; and in Tur it is written in the name of Rif: when raw shehakol and after cooking borei peri haAdamah; and so the Geonim; and it is possible they consider them changed for the better, and so is reasonable, end of his words; and Beit Yosef wrote that in Rif it does not imply so, but both raw and both cooked borei peri haAdamah; and I do not know from where our Rabbi erred to learn from his words that they bless on it raw shehakol — certainly Beit Yosef wondered greatly at Tur; and it appears he came to Tur from Rif's language that wrote "and even garlic and leeks," and afterward wrote "raw gourd," etc.; and this "and even" was not written before Tur, only "garlic and leeks"; and it turns out it is attached to what he said "raw gourd," etc.; and also garlic and leeks are like raw gourd; and here Tosafot and Rosh ruled not like Rif's words. And it appears in my humble opinion to distinguish among garlic: that which has not yet aged is fit to eat raw even without bread — therefore its primary blessing in eating it raw; but if it aged, that it is not fit to eat raw without bread — it is worse than vinegar, for in siman 204 it says that even if it is not very sour, only that people refrain from drinking it, one blesses on it shehakol; a fortiori this garlic that burns greatly; and we find regarding onion without bread one brings proof, and garlic that is large burns more than onion — therefore one should bless on this when raw shehakol and after its cooking borei peri haAdamah, like Rif's view according to Tur; and it appears all agree on this, for it does not speak of garlic; and likewise in onion there is this distinction, for here we say its planting began with intent that when it is still young and soft they eat it raw, and when it ages they eat it cooked only, as appears to me — a clear and correct distinction.

There is no improvement from themselves, etc. It is difficult: regarding a nut candied in honey they said above that its blessing is borei peri haEtz, even though it changed for the worse when cooked without honey — nevertheless we now follow the improvement and bless borei peri haEtz; and one must say specifically regarding types of vegetables whose blessing initially is borei peri haAdamah they said that afterward if they changed for the worse through cooking they lost their primary blessing, even if improved through meat; but something whose beginning is borei peri haEtz did not lose that blessing as long as it was afterward improved through honey; and it appears they learned thus from what they said in the Gemara this law of blanched vegetables specifically regarding vegetables; but it is difficult from what Tosafot wrote in the beginning of chapter 38 plainly regarding blanched eggplants and gourds: when raw their blessing is shehakol and when cooked (borei peri haAdamah) [borei peri haEtz] that they are better than cooked, etc. — it implies that this too is included in blanched vegetables, the law of changed for the worse; and therefore to say that nevertheless if improved through anything else there is a distinction between them; and therefore etrog candied in honey, and likewise everything that grows on the tree and they make from them confection that they call in foreign language ein gemachts — one blesses borei peri haEtz, similar to a nut; but what they make from fruit of the ground — there is a distinction: if it is a type of radish that they somewhat candy in honey, its blessing then is shehakol, like garlic and leeks above, since it changes for the worse in cooking without honey; but if they candy meyerin and rubin and kirbis one blesses borei peri haAdamah, for in all cooking they change for the better; and from this one may learn all laws of ein gemachts, as above — a general principle in this per the proof that proves to distinguish; and still requires study.
