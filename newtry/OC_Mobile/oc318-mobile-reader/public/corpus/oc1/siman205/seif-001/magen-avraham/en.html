(א) And legumes. It appears to me that on dry legumes one who eats them raw — one blesses shehakol, as in siman 205 seif 4 in the gloss:

(ב) And cabbage. And if it is pickled such that it is fit to eat raw — one blesses borei peri haAdamah (Shelah), and so in the Gemara regarding salted — its law is like cooked for this matter, and see also if better when salted:

(ג) More cooked. Taz wrote that when not eaten raw — when raw shehakol, and so siman 202 seif 12; but when they are good raw, even though better when cooked — nevertheless one blesses raw borei peri haAdamah; and so is the custom on Pesach night on petrezil borei peri haAdamah, even though better cooked than raw, and so siman 202 seif 12. Nevertheless it is possible if one cooks petrezil without meat it changes for the worse. And in the Gemara it says on maror one blesses borei peri haAdamah, and it is possible if one cooks it by itself that it is for the worse — as I wrote siman 473:

(ד) And carrots and parsnips. And Bach ruled that carrots and parsnips — even when raw — one blesses shehakol, and it is not compelled [to rule otherwise].

(ה) The improvement is not from themselves. And one could ask: in siman 202 seif 13 he wrote to bless on a nut borei peri haEtz even though the improvement is not from itself but only because of the honey. And one can say it is different there, for the nut is primary and the honey comes to improve the nut — and what difference does it make to me whether he boiled it with water or with honey; unlike here, where the meat is primary and the fats receive taste automatically. And so too Taz and Rosh. And see siman 204 seif 11. If so, when one fries vegetables in fat one blesses on the vegetables borei peri haAdamah.
