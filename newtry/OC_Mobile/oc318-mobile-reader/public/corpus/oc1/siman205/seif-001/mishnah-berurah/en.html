(א) And even when cooked — and it deals with those that are good to eat both raw and cooked, as he concludes afterward regarding fruits and legumes.

(ב) And legumes — specifically when one eats them while still moist; but dry legumes and beans — it is not the way of most people to eat them raw but only cooked; therefore one who eats them raw blesses shehakol. And see siman 204 seif 9 in Mishna Berurah.

(ג) More than raw — from this wording it implies that even when they are also good raw, since they are better when cooked one blesses shehakol; nevertheless, if most people also eat them raw, one blesses borei peri haAdamah.

(ד) And after their cooking, etc. — the same applies if they are pickled or salted and people are accustomed to eat them thus when raw — one blesses borei peri haAdamah. And accordingly, what they call kraut — when pickled and they become sour — one blesses on them borei peri haAdamah even when raw; but if not pickled and one eats them raw — shehakol; cooked — borei peri haAdamah. Salad mixed with oil and vinegar — borei peri haAdamah even when raw. And all this when one does not eat them during the meal [latter authorities]. And if one eats the salad with meat it becomes secondary to the meat [Peri Megadim].

(ה) When raw borei peri haAdamah — the latter authorities wrote: specifically soft garlic, for then it is people's way to eat them raw; but garlic that has aged, which is very sharp and not fit to eat raw [without bread] — if one ate it, one blesses shehakol. And the same law applies to onions: if one eats them soft — borei peri haAdamah; aged — shehakol. And see Shaarei Teshuvah, who tends to say that even soft ones one does not bless borei peri haAdamah except in countries where the way is to eat them soft without bread; but in our countries it is never people's way to eat garlic and onions raw — even when soft, without bread — one does not bless on them except shehakol. And Nishmat Adam likewise tends to bless shehakol. And see there further in Shaarei Teshuvah, who wrote that when cooked — whether garlic and onions are soft or aged — one never blesses except shehakol, even if cooked with meat.

(ו) That they are considered to have changed for the worse — and it appears from the latter authorities that the same applies to cooked onions, and likewise to a vegetable called zarzich or what they call trahan. And it appears specifically when one cooked the onions in a dish with water as is customary; but what is customary to make a food from onions alone, cooking them with much cooking until they shrink and are good and pleasant to eat — one may bless on them borei peri haAdamah, for they change for the better through much cooking.

(ז) From themselves — see the latter authorities who wrote that if one was cooking vegetables with meat [or frying in fat] and his intention is certainly also to eat the vegetables themselves, he certainly must bless on them borei peri haAdamah — even if those vegetables are by nature accustomed to change for the worse when cooked without meat. And this case is different, for the fats were not put in the pot for themselves but only to give taste in the meat; but they automatically also receive taste from the meat and are improved — therefore one blesses on them only with the meat's blessing. And accordingly, if one fried onions in oil or butter in order to improve them, one blesses on them borei peri haAdamah, for the onions changed for the better through frying and they are the main item for him; and the same applies in all such cases.
