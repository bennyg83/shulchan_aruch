On the board. See Mishnah Berurah, where I wrote that the same applies to a table with candles. Although Eliyah Rabbah in the name of Perishah brings that a table is important and never becomes a basis for a lamp, I did not state this plainly, because from Bach, Taz, Magen Avraham, and many other later authorities it appears they do not hold so. It is surprising that Ba'er Heitev copied from Or Zarua that one may move what is called a shtender even though a wax candle is burning on it, for Or Zarua wrote this only according to Perishah's reasoning. Since Ba'er Heitev first copied the Bach, Taz, and Magen Avraham regarding a table, implying that importance does not prevent it from becoming a basis, the same should apply here.
He shakes off, etc. Although the lamp is thereby moved automatically, it is permitted because this is only tiltul min ha-tzad. But he may not lift the board with the lamp, even after the lamp has gone out, unless he needs the place of the board or it is impossible to shake it off because of loss; then he may lift the board with the lamp to another place, even while it is burning, since after all, where he forgot it, it did not become a basis. All this is explained below in siman 309.
Through a non-Jew. See the Gra, who explains that the reason is concern for the Yerushalmi that is stringent here. It appears from the Rama that even with a wax candle one should be stringent except in pressing circumstances, because it is close to extinguishing; see Hagahot Maimoniyot. But once it has already burned out, all agree that where he forgot it he may shake the board and cause the lamp to fall, even not gently.
It is impossible that he will not bring it nearer, etc. The wording is abbreviated; in truth one should add "or distance it, and he is found to extinguish," as the Rosh brings from the Yerushalmi. Although this is a psik reisha that is not desired, because that extinguishing gives him no benefit since the oil is spilled and lost, as Hagahot Mordekhai writes, unlike seif 1 where the lamp is behind the door and the oil remains for him after extinguishing, and we rule that all agree he is exempt from a chatat, as Magen Avraham writes in siman 320 s.k. 21, nevertheless most poskim hold it is forbidden.
Since he does not move the lamp. The language is difficult, for the board is a basis and moving it should also be forbidden. The main permission is that the board too is not moved, since the case is where it is fixed, as the Taz writes. Even if we explain that the Rama permits even a hanging board, because any swaying is tiltul min ha-tzad since he does not intend actual swaying, as implied in Or Zarua and as I copied above in siman 265 in Biur Halacha, the language remains difficult; he should have said, "since he does not move it by hand." See nearby.
And the candles. According to the Taz, who explains that the case is a fixed menorah, it is permitted even with an oil lamp. But if we explain that it is permitted even with a hanging menorah, then it must refer only to a wax candle; otherwise even mere touching would be forbidden, lest the touch cause it to sway and tilt oil toward the wick, making him liable for kindling, as above in siman 265 in the gloss and as Eliyah Rabbah writes in the name of Nachalat Tzvi. I later examined the Mordekhai in Kol Kitvei, and it implies that the case is fixed, like the Taz, for he writes there that one may touch the stand before the aron kodesh; see there.
