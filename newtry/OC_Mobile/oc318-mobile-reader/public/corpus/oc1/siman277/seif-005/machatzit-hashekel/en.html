Seif katan 5: The door strikes, etc. It touches the lamp. This excludes the Taz’s interpretation that the lamp is fixed to the wall containing the doorway, though not directly behind the door, and that forcefully opening or closing the door shakes the wall and may extinguish the lamp. Magen Avraham follows Rivash and explains that the lamp is attached directly to the back of the door. On this reading, closing the door is permitted. Although regarding a bonfire the Shulchan Aruch prohibits opening a door only where an ordinary wind is present, the law of a lamp is stricter according to Rashi. Even an ordinary wind can extinguish a lamp, so opening is prohibited even when no wind is presently blowing, as a safeguard against an ordinary wind. A bonfire, however, is normally extinguished only by an unusual wind. The Sages prohibited an ordinary wind as a safeguard against an unusual one, but did not extend the decree to a place with no wind at all, which would constitute a safeguard upon a safeguard.
