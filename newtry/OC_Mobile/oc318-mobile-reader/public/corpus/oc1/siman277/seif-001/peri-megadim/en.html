(א) Forbidden — the notation in s.v. is a scribal error and should read: afterward I fix behind the door. Per the explanation of Rashi on the authority of Rabbenu Chananel, and in Shabbat 122b — a candle behind the door has three explanations in Rashi and Rashi: explanation 1 here; explanation 2 — Rabbenu Chananel — fixed in the wall; explanation 3 — Rabbay — in the door itself. And Rabbenu Chananel's explanation has, in my view, three explanations: 1 — what Magen Avraham wrote in letter 5, that the door opens inward and the candle is fixed in the wall in such a way that if he opens the door it will knock and reach the candle and thereby extinguish it — opening is permitted, and per Rabbenu Yeshayah, see there, and as written in letter 4, and if not so it will be explained there. And in the Bach it appears according to this explanation. The second explanation of the Taz — that the door opens outward — it is forbidden to open lest it knock the wall from outside and extinguish the candle, and all the more so to close, for it will also sway, etc., and this is the explanation of the Levush, per the explanation of Rabbenu Chananel that the Magen Avraham brought — to open and to close is forbidden. And it is further possible to explain per Rabbenu Chananel that the candle rests on the lintel and in the wall of the doorway next to the opening of the door — now to open is forbidden lest it extinguish through opening when wind comes, and closing will knock there and sway and extinguish — this is possibly the view of the Magen Avraham there, and therefore it is proven specifically as usual, and the same for Rashi and the first explanation of the Magen Avraham, and if not so it will be explained there. And see the Prisha — it appears per Rabbenu Chananel that closing is forbidden and opening is permitted, and it deals with a candle in the wall next to the opening and closing of the door, that when closing it knocks there, see there, and it requires study, and if not so in Magen Avraham it will be explained further, and as written if not so in letter 2.
