1) [Seif 1] A lamp placed behind a door, etc. This means that the lamps are fixed in the wall directly opposite the door, with the door on the west and the lamps opposite it on the east. When the door is opened, there is concern that the wind will extinguish them. The expression 'behind the door' teaches that this is forbidden only in a small room, where the lamps are not far from the door and are considered immediately behind it, so that extinguishing them is an inevitable consequence. In a large room, however, it is merely an unintended consequence and is permitted, as stated below in seif 2 regarding a fire. Bach; Magen Avraham, paragraph 2.
2) There: It is forbidden to open the door. It is forbidden even if there is presently no wind at all, because wind might arise. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 2. Magen Avraham, siman 514, paragraph 10; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1; Chayei Adam, klal 46, paragraph 12, write the same. This implies that it is forbidden to open the door even gently. See the following paragraph.
3) There, gloss: In the ordinary manner. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1, writes that this is a scribal error, because even if one opens the door gently, the wind nevertheless blows and the act is forbidden. This gloss should instead appear at the end of the seif, regarding a lamp fixed in the door itself: it is forbidden to open and close the door in the ordinary manner, but one may do so gently; see there. This appears to be the view of Levush and Shulchan Aruch HaRav, who omit the words 'in the ordinary manner.' It also appears to be the view of Chayei Adam there. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 3; Nesiv Chaim; and Mateh Yehudah, paragraph 1, however, explain the words 'in the ordinary manner' as written. According to them, opening the door gently is permitted. Nevertheless, since the matter is disputed, one should be stringent and rely upon the leniency only in pressing circumstances.
4) One who opens the door of a room in which a lamp is burning, and the movement of opening the door unintentionally extinguishes the lamp, is not considered to have inadvertently desecrated Shabbos. To what is this comparable? To one who locks his house to guard it and thereby finds that a deer is also confined inside. Nevertheless, it is proper for him to contemplate repentance through fasting or charity because he unknowingly caused the extinguishing, since ab initio he was not permitted to open the door until he could see that opening it would not cause the lamp to be extinguished, in accordance with the present law. Petach HaDevir, paragraph 2. The phrase 'through fasting or charity' means that he should fast for one day. If he cannot fast, he may redeem the fast with money by donating the value of his meal to charity, as stated below in siman 568, seif 2; see there.
5) There, gloss: But it is permitted to close the door opposite it. This is permitted even according to those who forbid closing an oven, as stated at the end of siman 259, because that concern does not apply to a lamp, which the wind does not intensify. Magen Avraham, paragraph 3; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 5. This means that there is ordinarily a small opening above the oven's mouth. When the oven's mouth is closed and only this small opening remains, it acts like a bellows and intensifies the fire. Regarding a lamp, however, one cannot say that the wind intensifies its flame. Machatzis HaShekel.
