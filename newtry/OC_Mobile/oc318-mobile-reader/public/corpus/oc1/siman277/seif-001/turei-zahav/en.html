It is forbidden to open or close the door, etc. This is Rabbeinu Chananel's explanation, which the Beit Yosef cites in the name of the Rosh. There, however, closing the door is not mentioned. It says only that when one opens the door it strikes the wall, etc. Why, then, does the text here also mention closing it, and what prohibition would there be in closing? The Beit Yosef later also mentions closing in his presentation of Rabbeinu Chananel's explanation. It appears to me that he understands the case as follows. The lamp is fixed in the wall behind the door. The wall in which the door is hung extends far enough that when the door is opened forcefully, it swings back against the wall behind it and strikes it because of the force of the opening, causing the wall on which the lamp stands to shake. Likewise, if the door is closed forcefully, when it reaches the wall in front of it, it strikes that wall and causes the entire wall to shake, even on its rear side. The Rambam's ruling cited by the Beit Yosef can be explained similarly, so that closing is also forbidden. Although Rabbeinu Chananel mentions only opening, closing is forbidden for the same reason.
It is forbidden to open or close it. This implies that here opening or closing gently does not help. Since the lamp is attached to the door itself, even gentle movement will cause it to shake slightly and will bring the oil nearer to or farther from the wick.
And regarding a wax candle, etc. It is obvious that if there is reason to fear that it will be completely extinguished, even a wax candle is forbidden.
