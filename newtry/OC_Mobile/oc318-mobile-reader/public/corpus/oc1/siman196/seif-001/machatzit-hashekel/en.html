As Magen Avraham wrote, as Rabbeinu Yosef wrote, etc.—that he acquired through change he does not bless, for it is blasphemy.

And so responsum 649 that he wrote regarding a stolen lulav—even if he acquired through change without support of the mitzvah, even though he fulfills with it, nevertheless he does not bless on it.

And so from the words of Rama siman 454 that he wrote: it turns out regarding stolen matzah one does not fulfill; but one who stole wheat and made it matzah and acquired through change—he fulfills. And Rama wrote: and regarding blessing see siman 649—it implies he holds as in siman 649 one does not bless—the same regarding matzah.

And siman 11 regarding tzitzit stolen and acquired through change Rama also wrote and regarding blessing see siman 649. And further in siman 649 he is uncertain, etc.—meaning that which Shulchan Aruch wrote there that even if he acquired without support they do not bless—is explained in Rabbeinu Yosef there that he ruled thus because he is uncertain whether the essence of Tosafot's words that which we say stolen lulav—even on the second day of Yom Tov when we do not need "for you" one does not fulfill with it because it is a mitzvah coming through transgression—and they proved that if he acquired without support of the mitzvah—that is, before performing the mitzvah—there is no issue of mitzvah coming through transgression; and they challenged from that Abbaye said in Bava Kamma that R' Meir holds change does not acquire—from that R' Meir said: behold one who stole wheat and ground and baked and separated challah—this one does not bless but blasphemes; and how can it be change acquires—why is it called blasphemy? And Rava answered that R' Meir indeed holds change acquires, and that which is called blasphemy is because it is a mitzvah coming through transgression—behold even if he acquired through change before the mitzvah nevertheless it is mitzvah coming through transgression. And two answers: first answer—Rava's answer is only a rejection; but the truth is R' Meir holds change does not acquire; but if change acquires in such a case where he acquired through change it is not blasphemy. Second answer—blessing is different—for even though if he acquired through change for fulfilling the mitzvah it is not mitzvah coming through transgression, nevertheless it is not fit to bless on it and it is blasphemy; and if so in the first answer if he acquired through change it is not mitzvah coming through transgression even for blessing and he may bless; and in the second answer one does not bless; and since Rabbeinu Yosef was uncertain which answer is primary he wrote uncertainly that one does not bless; and if so we distinguish in other blessings but for birkat hamazon we hold in siman 484 that if he has doubt in birkat hamazon he must bless since it is d'oraisa.
