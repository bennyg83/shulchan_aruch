At the end—see Baer Heitev; and see in responsum Bach Chadashot who also raised to bless at the end and it is not blasphemy even though there is a mitzvah; and in another fascicle he wrote there, and in the gloss under the name of a great one regarding one who vowed or swore not to eat a certain thing and transgressed and ate—he is obligated to bless at the end; but at the beginning when he wishes to eat—for at the time of blessing he wishes to do a prohibition in any case—he should not bless, end of his words. And what Baer Heitev wrote in the name of responsum Be'er Eshek—see Eshel Avraham from responsa Beit Yehudah, and it is in Yoreh Deah siman 23; and see Halakhot Ketanot part 1 and She'elat Yaavetz siman 45; and see responsum Beit Efrayim part 1 siman 63 inside explaining the aggadah in Shabbat daf 139 from the day Yosef separated from his brothers, etc.—see there. And see at the end of responsum Tashbetz in the third Tur siman 29; and see in Beit Yosef in the name of Maharshal who wrote in his glosses from Sefer Zichron Moshe that amen and zimun blessing are equal, and one does not answer after one who blessed on eating a forbidden item as Tosafot on demai; but when it is something the eater permits himself and the listener forbids—such as gentile bread and similar—the listeners may answer amen after him, for this is not blasphemy at all like zimun where they invite zimun upon one not careful from gentile bread, end of his words.
