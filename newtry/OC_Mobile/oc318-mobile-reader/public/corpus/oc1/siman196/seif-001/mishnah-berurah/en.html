(1) Forbidden item—even if its prohibition is not intrinsic but because he forbade this thing upon himself.
(2) And one does not bless on him—and the same applies one does not answer amen to his blessing.
(3) Not at the beginning, etc.—since it is a forbidden item and there is a transgression in eating it, he blasphemes Heaven in his blessing on this; and as it says: one who breaks a covenant blesses, blasphemes Heaven.
(4) And not at the end—and even if he ate to satiety; and if he ate b'shogeg and remembered after eating, the view of Taz and several Acharonim is that in this he may bless at the end (that is, even if he did not eat to satiety)—for here blasphemy is not applicable; but for zimun even b'shogeg one does not invite zimun on this, for eating forbidden food is not regarded as fixed eating. If he stole or robbed wheat and ground and baked—some say that even though he acquired through change and they are his, only that he owes him money for them, nevertheless it is forbidden to bless on this, both opening blessing and birkat hamazon—for regarding blessing which includes mention of the Name it is stricter and it is always in the category of blasphemy; and some say since he acquired he may bless; and the view of Magen Avraham is that for birkat hamazon if he ate to satiety one should instruct that he bless birkat hamazon, for it is d'oraisa, and one should be stringent and bless.
