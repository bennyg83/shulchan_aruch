In Magen Avraham s.k. 1—to eat with his fellow. It appears to me one should be precise: for in the Mishnah at the beginning of the chapter "three who ate," one who ate demai—they invite zimun upon him—it implies the other two bless upon this one who ate demai; and so by law how is it possible that one who eats demai b'issur blesses? If so, what is the ruling—perhaps he declared his property ownerless? In any case these three; nevertheless these two are careful not to eat demai in its strict sense and cannot eat with this man—similar to those careful from gentile bread; and it turns out they cannot bless, and now too how can they bless? And one could say that if he declared his property ownerless he is forbidden to be stringent upon himself not to eat demai, since Beit Shammai hold one does not feed the poor demai, and it is forbidden to act like Beit Shammai as is known—and now too since he did not declare his property ownerless it is like demai and one of these two blesses. And with this the difficulty of Tosafot there daf 47a s.v. and Rav Huna taught: Beit Shammai say one does not feed the poor demai—and Tosafot challenged Rosh Hashanah: Beit Shammai's reason comes to teach us—and it should read thus—and understand well.
