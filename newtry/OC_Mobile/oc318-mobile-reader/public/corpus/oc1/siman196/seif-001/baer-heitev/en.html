And Raavad challenged him and said thus: He erred in this—for they only said there is no zimun upon them to say they have no significance of fixed eating since they eat a forbidden item; but an opening and closing blessing—why should they not bless, since he benefits, end of his words. And Taz wrote Raavad only disagrees regarding one who eats b'shogeg; but if he ate b'mezid he does not disagree and is exempt from blessing, etc. He wrote in responsum Be'er Eshek siman 109: but one who is not careful about stam yeinam—even Rambam admits he blesses, etc. Beit Yosef wrote the same regarding one who steals wheat and grinds and bakes—even though he acquired through change he does not bless neither at the beginning nor at the end, etc.; and Magen Avraham disagrees and ruled they bless on it, for birkat hamazon—for he acquired through change; and so one should instruct, for birkat hamazon is d'oraisa and we go stringently; and it is possible even one who stole bread blesses birkat hamazon, for when he ate it he already acquired through change—see Magen Avraham. And Baer Heitev before me wrote in the name of Magen Avraham that one who steals wheat and makes bread blesses even at the beginning, and he was not precise.
