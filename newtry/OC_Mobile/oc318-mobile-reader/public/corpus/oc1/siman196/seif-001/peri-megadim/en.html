But Taz brought the words of Ramah and R' Eliezer of Metz chapter 1 of Berachot law 19; and for R' Eliezer of Metz who blesses before—it is difficult from Berachot 43b that on incense of idolatry one does not bless; and one must say regarding d'oraisa prohibition Ramah agrees one does not bless—for his main proof is from chapter 3 "they ate" 45a tevel—for d'rabbanan one does not invite zimun, they bless; and on incense of forbidden relations which is forbidden to smell d'rabbanan—likewise one blesses according to R' Eliezer of Metz; and so wrote Malbim there that what Ramah wrote chapter 9 of Berachot law 8 one does not bless on incense of forbidden relations—for his reason he goes; but for R' Eliezer of Metz on gentile incense which is d'oraisa one does not bless, and of forbidden relations d'rabbanan one blesses. And in the margin it is written see R' Yehuda siman 65; but for what Taz wrote R' Eliezer of Metz disagrees only b'shogeg or in danger—it implies Ramah holds even in danger he does not bless; and for R' Eliezer of Metz in danger he blesses beginning and end. And one could say what Taz wrote for Ramah—it teaches zimun, for zimun is worse than fruits—for Ramah in chapter 8 law 8 of Berachot for three fruits do not combine; and so the Mechaber in siman 197 and Beit Yosef siman 71; and in several places he also explained 71—see there; and I return to Taz's intent: what he wrote Mishnah 3 "they ate," etc.—deals with b'shogeg—for b'mezid he is wicked—one could say what of it, since they did not excommunicate him they invite zimun upon him as in siman 199 seif 1 and siman 55—see there; and his general rule is that for Ramah b'shogeg and danger are one; and proof from responsum Rashba that for Ramah a sick person on Yom Kippur does not bless (and how much more one who eats forbidden food in danger of life—he does not bless for Ramah); and Ramah's proof from Mishnah one ate tevel—for d'rabbanan one does not invite zimun even b'shogeg—and he holds the reason is because it is not regarded as eating at all; the same applies he does not bless; and R' Eliezer of Metz holds specifically zimun no, for there is no fixed eating and it is like fruits (and he holds for three fruits not like Ramah chapter 5 law 8); and Rosh agreed with R' Eliezer b'shogeg—and the same in danger; and one could say what R' Eliezer wrote he blesses at the beginning—in danger it refers; and Rosh agreed with R' Eliezer b'shogeg he blesses at the end, and danger even at the beginning; and one does not invite zimun—for it is worse than eating fruits (as siman 199 for Rosh); and proof that he explained blasphemy—that b'shogeg and danger there is no blasphemy. And the Mechaber here ruled like Ramah that one does not bless; and nevertheless he ruled danger one blesses—he holds to distinguish between b'shogeg who regrets and it is not regarded as significant eating, unlike danger—see Sefer Chemed Moshe. But nevertheless it is puzzling why the Mechaber omitted b'shogeg. And from responsum Rashba one could say he says preferentially that in any case there is permission at the forbidden hour Ramah agrees; and if not, in Magen Avraham it will be explained further here; and here one should not expand.

If he ate doubtful d'oraisa prohibition he does not bless—for if it were certain d'rabbanan he would not bless; and what is doubtful d'oraisa for stringency.
