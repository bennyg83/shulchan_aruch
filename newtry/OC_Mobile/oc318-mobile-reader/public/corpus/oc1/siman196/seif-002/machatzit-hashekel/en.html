(s.k. 2) From each other—and we do not say: since, etc. And their proof: if so the Talmud should also regarding terumah say the non-Kohen can ask about terumah—and they challenged in Avodat HaKodesh: for terumah in a Kohen's hand one does not ask as explained in Nedarim daf 59—see there. And one could say somewhat from that the Talmud teaches anonymously a Kohen eats terumah and did not explain what Israel gave him.

And if this Kohen separated terumah from his crop he can ask about it—it turns out the Gemara stated the inclusive reason even if Israel gave him terumah one cannot ask about it; even so he can invite zimun since the Kohen is permitted to eat with the Israelite. Nevertheless it must be said that he inferred from the Talmud's words: since a non-Kohen cannot eat terumah—it implies from its conclusion that an Israelite is not permitted to eat even terumah of the Kohen himself. And in any case it must be said that he inferred from the Talmud's language—for it told him the inclusive reason even for kodashim that one cannot ask about them when the sacrifice was already slaughtered.
