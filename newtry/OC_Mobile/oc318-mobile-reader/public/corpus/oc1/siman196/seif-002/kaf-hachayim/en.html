(2) There—one who ate a forbidden item, etc. It is implied in Beit Yosef that the same applies to one who steals wheat and grinds and bakes—even though he acquired through change he does not bless neither at beginning nor end; but Bach wrote that even if you say regarding one who eats a forbidden item itself such as tevel, nevelah, and treifah, and even a d'rabbanan prohibition one does not bless at all—nevertheless regarding one who steals or robs a grain of wheat and grinds and bakes and makes bread it is obvious he must bless at the end, for he owes him money and it is not even blasphemy since he owes him; and for the opening blessing too it is possible he should bless; but one who steals bread or baked matzah is forbidden to bless at the beginning since he did not acquire through change, but at the end he may bless—see there. And they brought this in Shulchan Gavoah ot 4; and so is the view of Magen Avraham at the beginning of the siman; and so Rabbi Akiva Eiger ot 1; and it seems since there is a dispute here regarding birkat hamazon which is d'oraisa, he should bless so as not to add to his sin, and he should have intent in the responsum before the blessing to give the money to the owners; but for the opening blessing he should not bless but contemplate the blessing in his heart. And if he acquired it through change and afterward others took it from him, one could say even Beit Yosef agrees they bless. Acharonim. And so if he acquired through despair even for baked bread and afterward others took it—according to law he blesses at beginning and end (R' Zalman ot 4).
