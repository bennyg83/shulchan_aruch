2) There: One ate a prohibited item, etc. Beis Yosef implies that the same applies if one stole wheat, ground and baked it. Although he acquired it through a change, he recites neither an initial nor concluding blessing. Bach, however, writes that even if one assumes that no blessing at all is recited when eating an inherently prohibited item, such as untithed produce, an animal carcass, a tereifah, or even a rabbinically prohibited item, nevertheless, if one robbed or stole wheat, ground and baked it, and made bread, it is obvious that according to all opinions he is obligated to recite the concluding blessing. He does not even blaspheme by doing so, since he merely owes the owner money. It is also possible that he should recite the initial blessing. If one stole bread or baked matzah, however, he may not recite the initial blessing because he did not acquire it through a change, but he may recite the concluding blessing; see there. Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef, paragraph 4, cites this. Magen Avraham at the beginning of the siman holds the same view, as does Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1. It appears that since there is a dispute regarding Birkas HaMazon, which is of Torah origin, he should recite it so that he not add to his sin. Before the blessing he should contemplate repentance and intend to pay the owner. He should not recite the initial blessing aloud, but should contemplate it in his heart. If he acquired the item through a change and others afterward took it from him, one may say that even Beis Yosef agrees that they recite blessings. Eshel Avraham. Likewise, if he acquired it through the owner's despair, even if it was baked bread, and others afterward took it from him, all agree that they recite both the initial and concluding blessings. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 4.
