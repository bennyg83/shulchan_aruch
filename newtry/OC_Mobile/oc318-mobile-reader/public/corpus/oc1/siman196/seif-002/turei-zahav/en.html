And the non-kohen eats bread, etc. Beit Yosef cites Agur that they combine because if ordinary Jewish bread became available during their meal, they could eat together. He dismisses this based on Eruvin, where kohanim eating terumah do not combine with a non-kohen, although the terumah could theoretically be undone. In my view, if ordinary Jewish bread is actually available, even if they have not eaten from it, they can eat it together and therefore combine. Beit Yosef would agree to this. Proof comes from the next siman: Rav and Shmuel were eating when Rav Shimi arrived and hurried to eat with them so he could join their zimun. They told him they had already eaten. Shmuel responded that if young pigeons were brought, would they not eat? Rif concludes that whenever additional food could be brought that they could eat, he combines with them. If this possibility creates an obligation of zimun where no obligation previously existed, all the more so here, where all ate but one could not eat the other’s food, the possibility that ordinary Jewish bread may become available allows them to combine.
