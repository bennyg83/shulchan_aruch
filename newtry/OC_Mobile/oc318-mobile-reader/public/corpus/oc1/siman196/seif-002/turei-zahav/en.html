And the non-Kohen eats bread, etc. Beit Yosef brought Agur's words here that they combine since if it happened at their meal with ordinary bread of Israel they can eat together; and he wrote on this they are idle words from that in Eruvin, end of his words—and this is from that which we say regarding terumah they do not combine, and although it is possible through asking, nevertheless behold it was not yet asked; and it appears to me if bread of Israel is available and ready, even though they did not eat from it, nevertheless it is possible for them to eat together from it and they combine; and with this Beit Yosef agrees; only it is difficult from what is said in the next siman in Shulchan Aruch—that is, what is said in the Gemara: Rav and Shmuel were sitting at a meal; Rav Shimi came hurrying to eat with them in order to combine with them for zimun; he said to him: what is your thinking to combine with us—you already ate. Shmuel said: if these bring appetizers to father would he not eat. And Rif explained: wherever if they bring something he could eat from it he combines with them, end of his words; and if so a fortiori—there where they were not yet obligated in zimun at all, nevertheless since it is possible to say if they bring something they would eat from this reasoning to obligate zimun—a fortiori here where all ate, only one cannot eat with his fellow—it is obvious this reasoning helps that if bread of Israel happens to them that he can eat one with his fellow and they combine for zimun.
