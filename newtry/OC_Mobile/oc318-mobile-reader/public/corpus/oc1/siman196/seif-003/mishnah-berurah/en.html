(7) They combine—even though each eats from his own loaf.

(8) They do not combine—for every combination for zimun is only when they can join together in their eating to eat one bread; and here each is careful from his fellow's bread.

(9) For three who vowed benefit from each other—whether each is under a vow from his fellow, or one is under a vow from the two and the two from him—for in any case that one cannot eat from theirs and they from his, therefore they do not combine; but when two are under a vow from one and he is not under a vow from them they certainly combine, since that one can eat with them. The poskim wrote the same when one eats milk or cheese and the two eat meat—they combine, for the cheese-eater can eat from their bread even though it is soiled with meat if he wipes his mouth and rinses (and the custom is that the cheese-eater is the one who blesses zimun and not the reverse, since he causes the zimun); but if he eats hard cheese they do not combine, for the custom now is not to eat meat after hard cheese even with wiping and rinsing; and similarly from Rosh Chodesh until 9th of Av they do not eat meat except at a mitzvah meal, and some are careful also in this and eat only dairy foods as written in siman 551—those careful from meat and eating dairy do not combine with meat-eaters, for behold they cannot eat with each other; unless they ate a kezayit of bread before the meat-eaters began eating meat in their bread—then they were still able to eat together; or if one of the members of the meal ate a kezayit of bread that is not soiled neither with meat nor milk—this combines everyone, for behold all can eat from his bread.
