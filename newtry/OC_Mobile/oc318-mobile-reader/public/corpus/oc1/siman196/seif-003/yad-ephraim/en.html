In Magen Avraham s.k. 2—and we do not say since if he wishes he releases his vow, etc. And see in Beit Yosef in the name of Mahari Prague in responsum manuscript: Reuven swore not to speak with Shimon and they ate together with another—Reuven is not permitted to invite zimun, for he speaks with both of them and tells them to be ready for the blessing, end of his words—and requires study; and therefore if Reuven wants to be shaliach tzibur to say Barchu when Shimon is in synagogue, especially if there are not ten except by combining Shimon—for all of them he tells to bless and Shimon is included—here too it is forbidden; and one could distinguish since there is a majority who are not offended he can speak to them; and Shimon should combine only to answer alone, for there is no matter of sanctity in fewer than ten; but according to this reasoning here too there is room to permit if the diners are many—and requires study; also requires study on the essence of the law that one swore not to speak with Shimon—one could say the intent is only not to speak with him alone privately, but generally when he speaks to many and he too is included it is not in the vow; and see Yoreh Deah siman 221 that Rambam wrote one may speak with another and he hears. But in responsum of the Geonim that Beit Yosef brought it is explained that if he intended to make him hear even though another is between them—his words are toward that person—Reuven does not act properly; and even according to what the Geonim wrote there initially, nevertheless they required the agent to say to Shimon what Reuven says through the agent and not rely on Reuven because Shimon heard his words from his mouth when he said to the agent speak to Shimon thus and so—meaning even though Shimon heard in Reuven's telling the agent that he should speak to Shimon thus, nevertheless the agent must repeat the words to Shimon so it appears Reuven does not rely on Shimon's hearing from his mouth what he said to the agent; and Rambam's words that came in general one may speak with another and he hears—requires study; and it is possible he holds what the Geonim wrote that the agent speaks, etc., for clarity of the matter, but there is no strictness in this, and whenever he does not speak with him face to face it is not in the vow; and from Shach there who cited from responsum of the Geonim what he wrote that if he intended to make him hear he does not act properly—it implies he explains Rambam's words too that he should not intend to make him hear; and from Rambam's language it does not imply so. But nevertheless even though we say intending to make him hear is forbidden—there is no proof for this law, for there he need not speak with the other, only another mediates between them and the essence of speech is directed to the one he swore about; unlike here where the essence of speech is not directed to him but to many, and even though he too is included in the speech—one could say he did not swear except not to speak with so-and-so alone, but not among other people—and requires study for now. And behold from responsum of the Geonim it is proven explicitly that one who swore not to speak with his fellow is permitted to speak with him through an agent—and we do not say one's agent is like oneself; and it must be these are words and there is no law of agency; and one can give a reason according to what Tosafot R' Yehuda wrote that mitzvot on one's body literally have no law of agency; and I expanded elsewhere; and see what is written in responsum part Choshen Mishpat and in the laws of Kaddish for an orphan I expanded further—see there.
