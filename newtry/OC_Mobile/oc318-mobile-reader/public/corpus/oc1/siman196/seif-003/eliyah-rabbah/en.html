(3) (Levush) Behold these, etc. Malbushei Yom Tov wrote one should not learn from this to three who ate when two or one eat meat and the other or others eat cheese—as I heard people say—for zimun is only on bread, and behold they can eat bread together, each cutting for his eating from the loaf and so the other, until here his words. And one must say it deals with hard cheese—for if it is soft cheese it is permitted to eat meat after it as explained in Yoreh Deah siman 89, and then they combine according to everyone. But one may wonder at Malbushei Yom Tov who ties the essence of this law to hearsay—and I found in Agudah first chapter of Eruvin and chapter "three who ate" and end of Taanit and Semak siman 109, and Rokeach siman 334. Also what he challenges—that behold they can eat bread, etc.—is already written in Hagahot Minhagim in the laws of the Ninth of Av, thus: when two eat, one meat and one milk—the one eating milk blesses and discharges the other because he can eat with his fellow and not the reverse. Small gloss in Mordechai on Shabbat before the Ninth of Av: one eats meat and one eats milk—they do not invite zimun together because they cannot eat with each other—and to the author it is difficult: but behold they can eat bread together, until here his words. And in truth it is not proper even for a great difficulty to reject an explicit law among great poskim; and perhaps they had a broad explanation without strain; also Hagahot Minhagim never meant to reject the law but only raises a difficulty—and there is no doubt that if Malbushei Yom Tov had seen all the words of the great ones mentioned above his heart would not be filled to reject this law. Again Magen Avraham was printed and I saw he too agreed with Hagahot Minhagim because they cannot eat one bread as written in Yoreh Deah siman 89. Also he wrote on what was challenged in Birkat HaZevach beginning of Arakhin on Hagahot Maimoniot: behold they can eat meat after waiting the measure—his words; he was not precise—for if he waits six hours the food will digest; until here. And I wonder at him—for he himself wrote earlier that according to Hagahot Maimoniot if one eats hard cheese they do not combine; and I already wrote in siman 173 one need not wait after cheese except one hour, and it seems even for birkat hamazon one need not bless between them—see there and understand. But in any case it is not proper to reject the essence of the law because of the difficulty in Birkat HaZevach—I avoided all the poskim mentioned above, see there; and further it is reasonable that at that time they must be fit to eat with each other; and so it seems from the case of vows of benefit so no poskim should distinguish between his vow and time. And I saw in responsum Maharam of Rothenburg siman 300 on a question where they vowed benefit for one day there is no distinction at all; and from the Stam it implies even for one hour they do not combine—and for this reason too we do not say they combine since if it happened at their meal with bread of Israel, etc.; and it is not similar to the beginning of the next siman where we say if they bring something they can eat—for there it is as if they did not finish the meal, but here in any case this one eating cannot eat with the other; and Taz expanded on this and what appears to my humble opinion I wrote.
