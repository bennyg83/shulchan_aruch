[Levush] These, etc. Malbushei Yom Tov writes that one should not infer from this that where three people ate together, two or one ate meat while the other or others ate cheese, they cannot make zimun. Zimun depends on bread, and they can all eat the same bread by cutting off their respective portions. He says he has heard people rule otherwise. One must say he refers to hard cheese, because after ordinary cheese one may eat meat, as explained in Yoreh Deah siman 89, and then according to everyone they can combine. I am surprised, however, that Malbushei Yom Tov treats this as merely a report he heard, because I found the law explicitly in Agudah, Eruvin chapter 1, Three Who Ate, the end of Taanit, Semak siman 109, and Rokeach siman 334. His question that they can eat the same bread is already stated in Hagahot Minhagim, Hilchot Tishah BeAv. There it says that if two people eat, one meat and one dairy, the one eating dairy should lead and discharge the other because he can eat with the meat eater, but not the reverse. A gloss in Mordechai Katan says that on the Shabbos before Tishah BeAv, if one eats meat and another dairy, they do not make zimun together because they cannot eat each other's food. The writer asks: can they not eat bread together? In truth, even a strong question should not overturn an explicit law of major poskim. They presumably had an adequate answer. Hagahot Minhagim itself did not intend to reject the law, but merely posed a question. There is no doubt that had Malbushei Yom Tov seen all these authorities he would not have dared reject the ruling. Magen Avraham was later printed and I saw that he also agrees with Hagahot Minhagim, because they cannot eat the same loaf as explained in Yoreh Deah siman 89. He also responds to Birkat HaZevach's question at the beginning of Arachin against Hagahot Maimoniyot: since after waiting the requisite time they could eat meat together, why should they not combine? Magen Avraham answers that if one must wait six hours, the original food will already have been digested. I am surprised, because he himself had just written that Hagahot Maimoniyot implies that if one person ate hard cheese they do not combine, yet I wrote in siman 173 that after cheese one need wait only one hour, and perhaps not even recite Birkat HaMazon in between. See there. Nevertheless Birkat HaZevach's question is not sufficient to reject the principal rule, because he overlooked all the poskim cited above. Moreover, the logic is that at the present time they must be fit to eat each other's food. This is implied by the case of people who prohibited benefit from one another, where no posek distinguishes between a vow for a limited time and a permanent vow. Responsa Maharam of Rothenburg, siman 330, discusses a vow for one day without distinction, and its unqualified wording implies that even a one hour prohibition prevents joining. For the same reason we do not say they can join because perhaps kosher bread acceptable to both will happen to be brought during the meal. This is unlike the beginning of the next siman, where we say that if food were brought they could eat, because there the meal has not yet ended. Here, however, the foods they are presently eating cannot be eaten by one another. The Taz discusses this at length. I have written what appears correct in my humble opinion.
Who are prohibited by vow, etc. If two are prohibited from benefiting from the third, they certainly can combine, because the third is still able to eat with both of them.
