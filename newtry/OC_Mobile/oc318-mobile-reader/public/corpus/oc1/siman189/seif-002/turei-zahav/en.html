He should not say “may You be blessed.” For he has already said, “Blessed are You.” In the printed text of this blessing it says, “the God Who every day,” etc., but this has no intelligible meaning. Some say, “God, every day,” but this too is awkward. Why was it necessary to mention the Divine Name Kel here? He already began, “the King Who is good and does good,” and the phrase “every day,” etc., refers back to that. If he wished to attribute the beneficence to the Name Kel, he should have said, “the King, the God, Who is good and does good to all.” Rambam likewise does not have this Divine Name before “every day.” Even regarding the earlier phrase, “the God, our Father,” etc., Beit Yosef writes in the name of Abudraham that it should not be said, since one has already said “our God,” and Rav Amram likewise writes this regarding the blessing after the Megillah. Nevertheless, in Rambam’s text the word “the God” does appear in the fourth blessing. Thus far Beit Yosef. In siman 692 the Tur writes in the name of Rav Amram that we do not say “the God Who pleads our cause,” since we have already said “our God.” The same applies here. Therefore the word “God” before “every day,” which does not appear even in Rambam, certainly has no place. Rather, the entire passage is one continuous statement: the King Who did good and continues to do good regarding those killed at Beitar is the One Who has also done good for us in all our needs, Who continues to do good, and Who will do good for us, past, present, and future. Blessed is He.
