1) [Seif 1] The fourth blessing, etc. This blessing is not of Torah origin. The Sages instituted it concerning the slain of Beitar, who were permitted burial. It therefore begins with 'Blessed,' since it is not adjacent to another blessing, and it has no concluding formula, since it is a short formulation. Although the Midrash states that one must mention three expressions of goodness and three expressions of bestowal, it nevertheless has no concluding formula, because originally they instituted only HaTov VeHaMeitiv and afterward added these matters. Tur. Rashba gives another reason: the Sages did not consider it proper to give this blessing a conclusion and thereby make it superior to the preceding blessings, which are of Torah origin. Rabbeinu Yonah gives another reason: everything said in this blessing is a single concept, for all are descriptive titles, saying that He is King, does good, and bestows. Beis Yosef; Perishah, paragraph 3. See above at the beginning of siman 187. Know that the blessing of HaTov concludes immediately after one says, 'May He never cause us to lack any good.' The passages beginning Harachaman, meaning, 'May the Merciful One,' are additions and are not part of the fourth blessing. Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 1; Chayei Adam, rule 47, paragraph 1. This is the Ashkenazic text. The Sephardic text concludes, 'relief, deliverance, and every good.' The listeners respond Amen here, as Chayei Adam writes there.
