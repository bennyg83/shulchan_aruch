He has done good to us. And now it is customary to say He has done good, does good, will do good to us all together—for they already said two hatovot, namely haTov and haMetiv; and so Maharshal; Kol Bo wrote everything in it: the "El" in "El Who every day" implies heaven forbid two authorities; and the "El" every day we bless—rather say "El every day," etc. (Shelah, Bach). When there is a gentile in the house they are accustomed to say all of us together sons of the covenant; and Taz in Yoreh De'ah wrote it is not correct—for since he says "all of us together" everyone is included; rather say "us, sons of the covenant, all of us together." And the reason: because it is forbidden to bless gentiles as written "do not grant them favor" (Mateh Moshe, Kol Bo). When there is an apostate to gentile worship at the meal place one must say sons of the covenant—for an apostate is not included in the covenant as Tosafot wrote Sanhedrin daf 72; and it is difficult—for if so why did Rama write in Even haEzer siman 141 that an apostate's law is like one disqualified from testimony d'oraisa—implying if the get was validated by its signatories it is permitted to send it by an apostate to all places—let it emerge he is not made an agent because he is not son of covenant as stated Gittin daf 23b; and one could say Tosafot speak of an apostate out of spite as concluded the reason of moridin velo ma'alin—and that is specifically out of spite as written Yoreh De'ah siman 158; and in Even haEzer perhaps Rama speaks of not out of spite—and requires study; and one who left the community is like out of spite as written siman 159 and there Yoreh De'ah siman 119, Choshen Mishpat siman 263, and Gittin daf 23b: women are included in sons of the covenant and likewise slaves. In birkat hamazon the host concludes and they say amen—for he cannot decree he says "and they said amen," unlike in "Who makes peace," etc., which is praise of the Holy One, blessed be He, and all Israel are commanded—therefore he says "and they said amen." On Shabbat and Rosh Chodesh they say magdol yeshuot, etc., and on weekday magdil—for cholam makes a great king and chirik without yod a small king; and further magdil is written in Tehillim and David had not yet reigned (Abudraham 25); and see Bach of Maharam. One may answer amen after haRachaman when he says "and He will bless us"—each one in his name that our forefathers were blessed, etc.; in "from all" every kaf has dagesh; "from Heaven," etc., He will make peace (Mateh Moshe).
