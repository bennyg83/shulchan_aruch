Three mentions of kingship, etc. Berachot 49 records that Rabbi Yochanan says HaTov VeHaMeitiv requires kingship. The Gemara asks what this teaches, since Rabbi Yochanan already said that any blessing lacking kingship is not called a blessing. Rabbi Zeira answers that HaTov VeHaMeitiv requires two additional mentions of kingship besides its own. Maadanei Melech, folio 61, expresses surprise because earlier Abba Yosi and the Sages dispute whether the blessing requires kingship. One opinion says it does because HaTov VeHaMeitiv is d'rabbanan, while the other says it does not. If so, Rabbi Yochanan could simply be teaching that he holds the blessing is d'rabbanan. This question itself is surprising because he overlooked the Rashba's novellae there, which answer that Rabbi Yochanan, as an Amora, should then have stated explicitly that HaTov VeHaMeitiv is d'rabbanan. It also seems to me that if this were the point, why state the ruling indirectly? He should have said the halachah follows the opinion requiring kingship rather than formulate a separate teaching. See above siman 188, seif 3. Know that the HaTov VeHaMeitiv blessing extends through the Harachaman passages.
[Levush] And since we say, etc. Mateh Moshe gives another reason. The three mentions correspond to the three worlds: the world of angels, the celestial spheres, and the lower world. The Sages thereby teach that the Holy One, blessed be He, rules over all of them and bestows kindness upon all, because all receive from Him kindness, sustenance, and spiritual divine influence. These allusions were already explained at the beginning of siman 187.
