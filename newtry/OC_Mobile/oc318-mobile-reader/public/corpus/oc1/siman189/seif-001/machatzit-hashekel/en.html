(s.k.1) He has done good, etc. And now, etc.—for the opinion of Shulchan Aruch is the opinion of Rosh that haTov and haMetiv are not counted among the three hatovot—for haTov and haMetiv were enacted for the slain of Betar: haTov that they did not putrefy and haMetiv that they were given for burial; and three hatovot must be said besides this—therefore he separates these three hatovot and says in each "He has done good to us," etc., unlike the custom to count also haTov and haMetiv among the three hatovot and consider haTov and haMetiv as two—if so "has done good, does good, will do good" counts as one; therefore he does not say the word "He" and the word "to us" except once for all of them together.

Rather he should say "El every day," etc. And Taz wrote that also the word "El" one should not say but "every day"—for he already said "the King Who is good," etc., there. And Taz wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 342 s.k.14 and his reason and proof from what we say there: a vow partially permitted is wholly permitted—how? He saw from afar people eating his fruits and forbade them; when he approached he saw his father among them and said had I known my father is among you I would have said except my father all are permitted; and specifically when he exchanged his words from what he said initially—how he began saying all of you are forbidden and finally said I would have said so-and-so and so-and-so are forbidden and father permitted; or said initially forbidden to this one and that one and when he recognized them said I would have said all of you forbidden except father; and since he exchanged he uprooted the vow and since permitted regarding father permitted regarding all; and Ran wrote the reason this matter is called exchange (chiluf): each wants that they not suspect him his intent was to forbid also his father; and each chooses language for himself that hurries to reveal his intent he did not intend regarding father; and some choose to say "all of you forbidden except father"—for if he said so-and-so and so-and-so forbidden except father, since they are many people before reaching "except father" they might suspect him he will finally forbid also his father—unlike when he says "all of you forbidden except father" who hurries immediately after saying the word "all of you" to exclude his father; and some choose the opposite and it is comfortable for him to say so-and-so and so-and-so forbidden except father—for if he says "all of you forbidden except father" then when he said "all of you" behold also his father was included—even though afterward he said except father, nevertheless it is not comfortable that his father be a moment in the prohibition—until here Ran.

If so it is the same here when he says "all of us together sons of the covenant"—it comes out that finally he excludes the gentile worshippers from the berachah; nevertheless initially when he said "all of us together" there was an hour the gentile worshippers were included in the berachah; therefore it is better to say the opposite: "us, sons of the covenant, all of us together."

And the reason according to his explanation, etc.—this refers to the essence of the matter that they say sons of the covenant.

As Tosafot wrote, etc.—for we teach there the order of warning for a murderer that witnesses tell him as written "one who sheds man's blood, by man shall his blood be shed" and he is Israel and son of covenant; and Tosafot wrote: therefore one must say two—Israel and son of covenant Israel—to exclude gentile worshippers who are not liable for killing him; and son of covenant to exclude one who is not Israel—an apostate who is in the category of moridin velo ma'alin—for likewise he is not liable for killing him—until here.

And it is difficult, etc.—for an apostate's law is like disqualified testimony d'oraisa implying if validated, etc.—for there it is written disqualified testimony d'rabbanan are fit for agency of the get by them but disqualified testimony from the Torah one does not send a get by them unless the get was validated (according to one opinion the law is so); and therefore Rama wrote an apostate's law is like disqualified testimony from the Torah and accordingly one should not send a get by him unless validated; we learn even if validated it is excluded from agency—for we derive from terumah as written "take from them" and we expound in Gittin and Kiddushin: as you are sons of covenant so your agents sons of covenant. And the reason we require validation for disqualified testimony—see there in Even haEzer.

And in Gittin, etc.—women, etc., slaves, etc.—for they are fit for agency of the get for they are sons of the covenant; and also slaves likewise circumcised. And Rashi wrote there regarding them "from hewer of your wood," etc., regarding cutting of covenant.

And regarding women see what Magen Avraham wrote siman 187.

For it is praise of the Holy One, blessed be He, etc.—even though they conclude "He will make peace upon us," etc., nevertheless the essence is praise. And in any case it is a mitzvah to answer amen because of the praise of the Holy One, blessed be He that is in it.

The cholam makes a great king—meaning a great vowel called king. A small king—meaning a small vowel; unlike chirik with yod which is also a great vowel.

One should answer amen—for one who hears prayer from the mouth of Israel should answer amen.

Each one as they were blessed, etc.—it should read "from Heaven" and not "from the people of Heaven"—for it is better the language of the verse. And one should emphasize the alef of "from" so it not appear heaven forbid "dead."
