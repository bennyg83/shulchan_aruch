Seif katan 1, "well," etc. "And now," etc. The view of the Shulchan Aruch is the view of the Rosh, who does not count HaTov VeHaMeitiv itself among the 3 expressions of beneficence. HaTov VeHaMeitiv was instituted concerning those killed at Beitar: "HaTov," because their bodies did not decompose, and "VeHaMeitiv," because permission was given to bury them. Accordingly, 3 additional expressions of beneficence must be said. He therefore separates these 3 and says concerning each one, "He has done good to us," etc. The prevalent custom, by contrast, counts HaTov VeHaMeitiv itself among the 3 expressions, treating "HaTov" and "HaMeitiv" as 2, so that "He did good, does good, and will do good" is counted as the remaining unit. According to this custom, the words "He" and "to us" are said only once so that the phrases are joined together. "Rather, he should say Kel every day," etc. Taz writes that even the word "Kel" should not be said there, and one should begin "every day," because he already said "the good King," etc.; see there. The exact wording should avoid unnecessary repetition of Divine descriptions and should preserve the sequence established by the earlier authorities. The blessing HaTov VeHaMeitiv is not one of the original 3 Torah blessings of Birkat HaMazon, but was later instituted at Yavneh concerning the dead of Beitar. This explains several distinctions in its wording and why Amen is answered after Boneh Yerushalayim before beginning it. The expressions "He has done good to us, He does good to us, and He will do good to us" correspond to past, present, and future beneficence. According to the Rosh's formulation they are 3 distinct expressions beyond HaTov VeHaMeitiv itself, while according to the prevalent wording the count is arranged differently. One should therefore follow the established nusach of his community and not create combinations of the phrases that would duplicate the same expression or disturb the intended count. The purpose of the discussion is to explain the textual variants and the numerical arrangement of the expressions of goodness, not to alter the essential blessing established by the Sages.
