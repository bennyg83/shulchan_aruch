(1) Do not say titbarach—for he already said Blessed are You, Heaven.
(2) haChai—for in some siddurim it was written "the King the Living, the Good," etc.
(3) Only in a house of mourning—and as below in seif 2.
(4) Three malchuyot, etc.—and the reason of all this is that berachat haTov vehaMetiv is not from the Torah but the Sages enacted it for the slain of Betar when they were given for burial; and they said haTov that they did not putrefy and haMetiv that they were given for burial; and they fixed it in birkat hamazon because all of it is acknowledgment and praise for the good He did with us; and this too is among the good things; and they adjoined it to berachat Boneh Yerushalayim because when Betar was destroyed the horn of Israel was cut off and it will not return until ben David comes; and because they already finished berachot that are from the Torah and this is d'rabbanan—it is not called a berachah adjacent to its companion; and therefore it opens with Baruch [and that we do not conclude therein with Baruch even though it is a long berachah—because initially they enacted it in short coinage where only haTov vehaMetiv for all was mentioned and afterward they added the rest; and they enacted therein three malchuyot because they already enacted to mention malchut of the house of David in Boneh Yerushalayim; and since they mention malchut of the house of David it was fitting to mention also malchut of heaven—but because it is not proper conduct to mention immediately malchut of Heaven next to malchut of the house of David they wished to complete it and mention in berachat haTov vehaMetiv malchut corresponding to it; and incidentally that they enacted to mention malchut corresponding to berachat Racham they enacted to mention malchut again corresponding to berachat haAretz where malchut was also not mentioned (and even though fundamentally one need not malchut therein for it is adjacent to berachat haZan and malchut is mentioned in it); and since they say therein three malchuyot they say therein also three hatovot because the essence of the berachah is on the name of the benefit—namely haTov and haMetiv; and since they say therein three hatovot and it is a berachah of acknowledgment as we wrote—they say also three gemulot—until here Levush.
(5) The King Who is good—one should not say "El Who every day," etc., for it implies heaven forbid there are two authorities; and that "El" every day we bless—rather say "El every day," etc.; and more correct not to say the word "El" at all—for behold he already said "there, our Father." In birkat hamazon the host concludes and they say amen—for he cannot decree he says "and they said amen," unlike in berachat "Who makes peace in His heights" which is praise of the Holy One, blessed be He, and all Israel are commanded—he says "and they said amen." After haRachaman one should answer amen and likewise after every stanza and request even if there is no Name therein (Kol Bo and Shelah). He should say "and may blessing be borne from Heaven" in the language of the verse "may blessing be borne from Heaven." They are accustomed to say on Shabbat and Yom Tov and Rosh Chodesh magdol and on weekday magdil; and further grammatical niceties in the nusach in birkat hamazon—see Avodat Yisrael siman 187 where he expanded on this.
