(Seif katan 5) If they wished, etc. For kiddush is more stringent, etc. Meaning, although regarding kiddush we rule above in siman 271 that in such a case he must recite a blessing, that is because kiddush is more severe, since even one sitting and eating must interrupt. It is therefore a full removal of attention, and in such a case a blessing is required. By contrast, regarding havdalah, if he was sitting and eating he need not interrupt. Therefore, even if he was sitting and drinking and must interrupt from drinking, it is still not a full removal of attention and he need not recite a new blessing. So wrote the Beit Yosef. See there.
But in the Kesef Mishneh. Chapter 4 of Hilchos Berachos.
Even if they said, etc. Meaning, although regarding eating we rule in seif 1 that even once it is certainly dark he need not interrupt, nevertheless, once they said, 'Come and let us recite havdalah,' they must interrupt. Still, they do not need a new blessing.
And the one who disagrees, etc. The first opinion is the Rambam, and the one who disagrees is the Raavad in his gloss.
But when they are sitting and drinking, according to all opinions a blessing is required. This is the correct reading. Meaning, even the first opinion, the Rambam's view, agrees to this according to the Kesef Mishneh, unlike what the Beit Yosef wrote here. And as written in siman 271, etc., meaning that the same law that applies regarding kiddush in siman 271 applies to havdalah according to the Kesef Mishneh.
But if not, etc. For once it is certainly dark he must interrupt even from eating. He should not have said, 'and there is one who explains differently,' which implies an individual opinion, but should have said, 'and some say,' as in seif 1, for this is the view of the Rif and the many who stand in his approach. This accords with the Beit Yosef's usual precision in such matters.
And he also should have explained that it speaks, etc. For during doubtful darkness even the 'some say' in seif 1 agree. Rather, this 'one who explains differently' must be the Raavad, who disagrees in all cases for the reason the Magen Avraham wrote above.
