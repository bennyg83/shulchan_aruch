(Seif katan 3) And some say this applies only, etc. And so it appears in the Gemara, etc., unlike the Olas Shabbos, etc. The wording is awkward, and I do not know how to correct it. But his intent is to challenge the Olas Shabbos, who wrote in the name of Shiltei Gibborim that during doubtful darkness it is permitted to drink, and drinking is forbidden only once it is certainly dark. The Magen Avraham disagrees, for the Gemara proves the opposite. As he wrote in seif katan 1, even according to the first opinion, the Rosh's view, that the Talmud applies even to certain darkness and nevertheless one need not interrupt eating, the Rosh must agree that the Talmud also speaks of doubtful darkness, for it speaks in all cases. Therefore, when it says that for drinking one interrupts, this means even during doubtful darkness, and certainly one may not begin drinking then. This is all the more true according to the 'some say,' the Rif's view, that the entire sugya speaks specifically of doubtful darkness.
