And there are those who say that he must. This is the view of Maharik, shoresh 96. It requires study, for in siman 271 he ruled plainly that regarding kiddush one need not recite another blessing. The Bach wrote that there it is different, since they had not yet recited Birkas HaMazon, and the blessing he initially recited over the wine exempts all the wine. But Maharik speaks where they were reclining only for drinking, and therefore once he must interrupt for kiddush or Havdalah, it is a conclusion to what he drank, and he must bless Borei peri hagafen. This requires study, for if so, why did Maharik write that according to the Rif and SemaG one interrupts at definite darkness? For regarding drinking, according to all opinions one interrupts even at doubtful darkness. Furthermore, he wrote there explicitly that he was in the middle of a meal; see there, for it is explicit that he is speaking of eating. What the Beit Yosef wrote, that they were reclining for drinking, means the drinking after the meal before Birkas HaMazon. As for the halacha, the Bach ruled that even if they were occupied with drinking, one need not bless again. It seems to me according to its plain sense that he holds the cup of Havdalah is worse, because it is not connected to the meal, as written in seif 8 and siman 174, seif 4, and therefore he must bless. According to him, when one recites Havdalah over beer in the middle of the meal, he must also bless, for the blessing of HaMotzi does not exempt it, just as Birkas HaMazon does not exempt it from the after-blessing, as written in seif 8. Therefore one should be careful not to recite Havdalah in the middle of the meal. There is also a dispute whether he afterward blesses HaMotzi again, as written in siman 271, seif 4.
