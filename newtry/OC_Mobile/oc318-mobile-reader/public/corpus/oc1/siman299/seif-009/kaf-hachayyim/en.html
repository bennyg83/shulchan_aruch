9) There: He need not stop. Tosafos in Shabbos 9 implies that if he began at a forbidden time, he must stop. This is also implied by siman 271, seif 5 in the gloss. It must be that this view maintains Havdalah is a Torah obligation. According to the opinion that Havdalah is rabbinic, if one washed his hands, he must recite Havdalah before eating. Since its time extends throughout the night, we are concerned that he might act negligently, as stated in siman 235, seif 2. If he already began eating, however, he does not stop. Magen Avraham there. Erech HaShulchan, paragraph 1, however, writes that we are not concerned about negligence in such a case and that this is also Pri Chadash's view. He further writes that in any event we maintain the opinion that Havdalah is a Torah obligation; see there. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 5, similarly writes that if he began at a forbidden time, he stops. The meal is considered to have begun at a forbidden time only once Havdalah's time arrived: according to Bach, even during twilight, and according to Taz, only at definite nightfall. Because of the nighttime Shema, however, one must stop if he began at a forbidden time, meaning half an hour before the stars emerge. Since Shema is a Torah obligation, one cannot rely upon the synagogue attendant, because many people do not attend the synagogue after Shabbos. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 2; Machatzis HaShekel; Chayei Adam, klal 8, paragraph 18.
