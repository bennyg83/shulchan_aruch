Therefore on Yom Kippur Shehecheyanu is said without a cup: once he sanctified the day, Yom Kippur takes effect and he may not drink. Nor may it be given to a child, lest the child continue this practice when older. Similarly, when Tisha B'Av falls on Sunday, havdalah is delayed until Motzaei Tisha B'Av. But with vows we follow ordinary speech, and the fast does not begin until night, as the Magen Avraham writes afterward. This differs from Yom Kippur and Tisha B'Av, which are public fasts and take effect automatically, not depending on his intent or acceptance.

He has not yet accepted the fast, and as the Rama says, he can accept it afterward, so obviously he may drink. The Olas Shabbos asked this too. Therefore the Magen Avraham says it speaks where he vowed to fast, so the obligation already rests on him. The Rama's words, 'and afterward accept the fast,' are not precise, but mean that afterward the fast takes effect. Alternatively, since the Beit Yosef wrote in siman 562, seif 8, that if one accepted in Minchah three or four nonconsecutive fasts, such as Monday, Thursday, Monday, there is room to doubt whether each requires its own acceptance, the Rama here stated the best practice, even though there he wrote that the people practice to accept them with one acceptance. Since he wrote only that the people practice, and not that this is the established custom, it implies the custom is not clear. Or one may say that since he vowed outside Minchah, it is worse and requires accepting each fast before the day at Minchah.

Yikahaz, and it is a puzzling matter. Meaning, when Yom Tov falls on Motzaei Shabbos we say Yaknehaz, but here the letter that represents the candle is missing, because if one does not recite havdalah on Motzaei Shabbos he blesses neither over the candle nor over spices, as stated in the Shulchan Aruch. Thus there would be two changes: whenever kiddush and havdalah are said on one cup, people say Yaknehaz, while he would say Yikahaz; and everyone else recites kiddush alone, while he recites kiddush and havdalah over one cup.
But it appears to me that if it happened, etc. Meaning, the Shelah implies that initially one should not wait and thereby need to do a puzzling thing, but if he did wait, he should recite kiddush and havdalah on one cup and say Yikahaz. The Magen Avraham, however, holds that it is forbidden to do so. Pesachim 102 teaches that where people were reclining on Friday afternoon and the day became sanctified, Rabbi Yehudah says they must interrupt, recite Birkas HaMazon over one cup, and kiddush over a second. Rabbi Yosei says they continue the meal and afterward recite Birkas HaMazon over one cup and kiddush over a second. The Gemara asks why two cups are needed, and answers that one may not put two sanctities on one cup, because one does not bundle mitzvos together, making them look like a burden. It asks from Yom Tov after Shabbos, where Yaknehaz is said on one cup, and answers that there kiddush and havdalah are one matter.
But weekday havdalah, etc. Meaning, the Shelah's case, where there is a weekday between Shabbos and Yom Tov, has no connection to kiddush, for he says the blessing that distinguishes between sanctity and weekday just as he would on any Motzaei Shabbos.
And greater than this, etc. Tosafot write that the Gemara's question, that Birkas HaMazon and kiddush should be said on one cup, is asked specifically according to Rabbi Yosei, but not according to Rabbi Yehudah. Since Rabbi Yehudah requires interruption, Birkas HaMazon, and then kiddush, he must afterward wash again and eat the Shabbos meal, because kiddush must be in the place of a meal. Thus one meal is a weekday meal and the kiddush belongs to Shabbos, so it is certainly impossible to put Birkas HaMazon for a weekday meal and kiddush for Shabbos on one cup. The question was only according to Rabbi Yosei, who says he finishes his meal. He must hold that he need not eat again after kiddush, and the original meal counts as kiddush in the place of a meal. Since it is called a Shabbos meal, the questioner thought that perhaps Birkas HaMazon and kiddush could be on one cup, and nevertheless the Gemara answers that two sanctities may not be placed on one cup.
Do not say that even now he distinguishes between one sanctity and another sanctity. This is the correct reading.
Therefore it appears to me, etc. And he should drink, as is done at a wedding meal, as written in Even HaEzer siman 62: Birkas HaMazon is recited over one cup, then a second cup is taken for the six blessings, and then the first cup is taken again and Borei Pri HaGafen is recited over it as one of the seven marriage blessings. There too the reason is that two sanctities may not be placed on one cup. So too here: he should recite kiddush over one cup but not Borei Pri HaGafen, for if he blesses on it he may not taste before havdalah, and havdalah would interrupt between Borei Pri HaGafen and drinking. Rather, he should recite only kiddush, then take the havdalah cup, recite Borei Pri HaGafen and havdalah over it, and that Borei Pri HaGafen will also exempt the kiddush cup. Havdalah is then not an interruption, for this is its normal order all year. Alternatively, he may also recite havdalah without Borei Pri HaGafen, and after havdalah recite Borei Pri HaGafen over both cups, making it exactly like the wedding procedure.

And he fulfills kiddush, etc. Nevertheless it is considered kiddush in the place of a meal, since his intent is to eat immediately, as written in siman 273, seif 3.
And see what I wrote in siman 167. This refers to the Magen Avraham's statement that afterward he should bless HaMotzi and eat. Although he already fulfilled the blessing of HaMotzi through the one who recited kiddush over bread, and although he interrupted between hearing HaMotzi and tasting by reciting havdalah, the Rama wrote in siman 167 that if the one who broke bread tasted, then even if the listeners interrupted by speaking between hearing HaMotzi and their own tasting, it is nothing and is not an interruption. However, the Magen Avraham there, and likewise the Taz, disagree, since the listeners fulfill only through hearing as though answering, and the listener cannot be better than the one who recites. If the listener had blessed himself and interrupted with speech, it would be an interruption; so too when he fulfills by hearing, speech should interrupt. Therefore, according to the Magen Avraham's approach above, here too, since the listener interrupted with havdalah, he must repeat HaMotzi.
And what he wrote, siman 271, seif 6 and the like, should read seif 14, where he wrote that tasting by the one who recites kiddush alone is sufficient, and nevertheless the listeners fulfill their obligation.
