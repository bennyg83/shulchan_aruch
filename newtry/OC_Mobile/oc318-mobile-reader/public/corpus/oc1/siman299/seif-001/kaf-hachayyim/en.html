1) [Seif 1] It is forbidden to eat, etc. Regarding the verses and other passages customarily recited during Havdalah, see Tur. They are also printed in the siddurim.
2) There: It is forbidden to eat, etc. Anyone who tastes anything before reciting Havdalah will die of askara. Pesachim 105a, cited by Beis Yosef. Even tasting a small amount is forbidden. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1.
3) There: Or even to drink, etc. It appears to me that drinking was forbidden only after definite nightfall, but during twilight one may drink. Riaz, cited by Shiltei Gibborim in the chapter Af Al Pi; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur. Taz, paragraph 1, similarly writes that according to this view, beginning a meal while it is uncertain whether night has fallen is permitted, although according to the opinion introduced by the Shulchan Aruch as 'some say' it is forbidden. One who begins when it is definitely night should certainly be stopped; see there. Nehar Shalom, paragraph 1, and Zachor LeYitzchak, siman 82, write the same. Bach, however, writes that beginning to eat during twilight is absolutely forbidden; see there. Magen Avraham, paragraphs 1 and 3, writes the same. Machatzis HaShekel writes that this applies even according to the Shulchan Aruch's first opinion, which is Rosh's view; see there. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 1; Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1; Chayei Adam, klal 8, paragraph 16; Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 2, agree. Petach HaDevir, paragraph 2, and Ben Ish Chai, parashas Vayeitzei, paragraph 18, also agree. Twilight means the period from when the sun is no longer visible above the earth until three medium-sized stars emerge, as stated above at the beginning of siman 261; see there.
4) If one prayed Maariv while it was still daytime, he may not eat until he recites Havdalah. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 5; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 5. See above, siman 293, seif 3, and our words there, with the help of Heaven. After reciting Havdalah while it is still daytime, however, he may eat, although Borei Meorei HaEish and the blessing over spices are not recited until night, because the essential Havdalah has been recited. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1.
5) There: Until he recites Havdalah. Yalkut Reuveni on the Torah, Kuntres Acharon, folio 6a, writes in the name of Sefer HaMaggid: It is proper to recite Havdalah immediately when a person leaves the synagogue, separating ourselves from those forces before they come to rule over us. In places where Havdalah is recited in the synagogue, this is even better, because they are given no opportunity to rule over us. For this reason the Sages said that one who tastes food before Havdalah dies of askara, meaning that through that food those forces attach themselves to him and remove his soul through askara. These are his words. This applies specifically when Maariv is delayed until three stars are visible. Where the prayer is recited early for some reason, it is more appropriate to delay Havdalah. Yafeh LaLev, paragraph 1. One should recite the liturgical poems arranged for after Shabbos until the time for Havdalah arrives, in order to drive away the external forces. Ruach Chaim, paragraph 2.
6) There: Except water. According to the Geonim in Shaarei Teshuvah, printed at the end of Neharos Damesek, siman 80, folio 6 at the end of the third column, however, it appears that even water should not be drunk before Havdalah over a cup; see there. Midrash Talpiyos, branch of Havdalah, folio 118d, similarly writes: It is forbidden to drink water before Havdalah, and one who does so will die of askara. This applies also during twilight after Shabbos. If he was eating the third meal at his table and the meal continued until the time fit for Havdalah, however, he may drink. These are his words. We wrote the same above in siman 291, paragraph 17, in the name of the Arizal; see there.
7) There: He need not stop. It is possible that if he prayed Maariv during the meal, the Havdalah obligation took effect upon him and he may no longer eat until he recites Havdalah. Magen Avraham, paragraph 2; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 5; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 5.
8) There: He need not stop. Rabbeinu Bachya writes in Shulchan Shel Arba: If he stopped, he would appear to drive away the King. Mechilta similarly expounds zachor and shamor to mean: guard Shabbos as it departs, like a person who does not want his beloved to leave him as long as he can delay the departure. Hagahas Yafeh Nof writes that Maharsha interrupted a wedding meal, went to the synagogue, and afterward returned. It is possible that regarding a meal he followed the opinion introduced as 'some say.' Magen Avraham there.
9) There: He need not stop. Tosafos in Shabbos 9 implies that if he began at a forbidden time, he must stop. This is also implied by siman 271, seif 5 in the gloss. It must be that this view maintains Havdalah is a Torah obligation. According to the opinion that Havdalah is rabbinic, if one washed his hands, he must recite Havdalah before eating. Since its time extends throughout the night, we are concerned that he might act negligently, as stated in siman 235, seif 2. If he already began eating, however, he does not stop. Magen Avraham there. Erech HaShulchan, paragraph 1, however, writes that we are not concerned about negligence in such a case and that this is also Pri Chadash's view. He further writes that in any event we maintain the opinion that Havdalah is a Torah obligation; see there. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 5, similarly writes that if he began at a forbidden time, he stops. The meal is considered to have begun at a forbidden time only once Havdalah's time arrived: according to Bach, even during twilight, and according to Taz, only at definite nightfall. Because of the nighttime Shema, however, one must stop if he began at a forbidden time, meaning half an hour before the stars emerge. Since Shema is a Torah obligation, one cannot rely upon the synagogue attendant, because many people do not attend the synagogue after Shabbos. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 2; Machatzis HaShekel; Chayei Adam, klal 8, paragraph 18.
9) If he remembers after reciting Hamotzi but before tasting the piece of bread, he should taste it immediately and then recite Havdalah, so that his blessing will not be in vain. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 2.
10) There: If he was sitting and drinking, etc. Drinking is not considered a fixed activity, and he must stop. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 3. He must stop even when it has become only uncertain whether night has fallen. Chok Yaakov, siman 271; Chayei Adam there, paragraph 17.
