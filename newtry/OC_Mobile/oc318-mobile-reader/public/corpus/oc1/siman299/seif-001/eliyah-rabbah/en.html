[1] [Levush] And he begins, etc. All of this was explained in siman 295.
[2] [Levush] Begin, etc. Some explain it as an expression of beginning. Others explain it from the sense of "falls," as in a Yom Tov that falls on Shabbos. This latter explanation is correct (Rabbi David Abudraham).
[3] [Levush] Poteh, the minister, etc. Torat Chaim on Sanhedrin 99a implies that it should be pronounced with a tet, for he writes that the name of the angel of forgetfulness has the numerical value one hundred, while the angel of memory is Michael, whose value is one hundred and one. This explains the teaching that one who reviews his chapter one hundred times cannot be compared to one who reviews it one hundred and one times.
[4] [Levush] Six days, etc. When there is a Yom Tov during that week, one should not mention "six." See siman 295, seif katan 5.
[5] From nightfall, etc. Magen Avraham says this applies even during doubtful nightfall. Taz, however, writes that according to this view it is permitted during bein hashmashot, and Shiltei Gibborim in the name of Riaz, p. 249, explicitly says so. The mention of drinking is merely because that is the case under discussion, and the same applies to eating. Magen Avraham, seif katan 3, therefore criticizes Olat Shabbat without basis on this point. Piskei Tosafot nevertheless writes that a scrupulous person should refrain from eating during doubtful nightfall, though others should not be protested. Magen Avraham writes that if one prayed Maariv while it was still day, he may not eat until reciting Havdalah. Likewise, if he prayed during the meal, the obligation of Havdalah takes effect immediately and he may no longer eat. There is room to doubt the case in which he merely said "Baruch HaMavdil, etc." during the meal. At the end of siman 263 Magen Avraham himself is uncertain whether he may thereafter say Retzei in Birkat HaMazon, suggesting that perhaps only when one actually prays the weekday prayer may he no longer say Retzei, whereas saying HaMavdil alone does not prevent it. The same should apply to eating, and it is reasonable that eating remains permitted, for even after actual prayer the prohibition is not entirely clear. It also appears that Retzei may still be said, for siman 695 cites Mordechai that even after praying one says Al HaNissim and need not interrupt, etc. If he began eating when prohibited, however, he must interrupt. See siman 235, seif katan 6.
[6] [Levush] And afterward he recites Havdalah, etc. Then he prays and drinks. If he has two cups, he recites Birkat HaMazon over one and drinks it, then prays, and afterward recites Havdalah. Magen Avraham writes that one who throughout the year is not careful to recite Birkat HaMazon over a cup may not drink from it now either. This is not compelling. See what I wrote in siman 296, seif 3, seif katan 11.
[7] [Levush] And some disagree, etc. The first view is primary, as is implied by Shulchan Aruch and the later authorities, both regarding eating and drinking.
[8] And there are, etc. The first view is primary, as the later authorities imply. Nevertheless, one should take care not to recite Havdalah in the middle of the meal.
