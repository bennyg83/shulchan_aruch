(Seif katan 1) From nightfall, etc. Even doubtful darkness is included. In Pesachim 105a they say in Rav's name that Shabbos establishes the meal with respect to kiddush, meaning that if one is in the middle of eating and the day became sanctified, he must interrupt. But with respect to havdalah it does not establish the meal. When the Gemara says this applies only to interrupting, meaning that he need not interrupt his meal in order to recite havdalah, but it is forbidden to begin, that applies when he is eating. If he had not established himself for eating, but only for drinking, he must interrupt. The Rif holds that the Gemara's rule that he need not interrupt applies specifically during doubtful darkness, but once it is certainly dark he must interrupt even from eating. This is the view cited here in the Shulchan Aruch as 'some say.' The Rosh objected that these words have no reason, and it is far from the mind to say that the give and take of the Talmud concerns only doubtful darkness. Thus according to the Rosh, even once it is certainly dark he need not interrupt eating, and this is the first opinion here in the Shulchan Aruch. The Taz understands that the Rif and the Rosh disagree in two matters: first, whether he must interrupt once it is certainly dark; second, whether, according to the Rosh, during doubtful darkness it is permitted even to begin. But this is not the Magen Avraham's view. He holds that regarding beginning, the Rosh agrees that even during doubtful darkness it is forbidden. Otherwise, the Beit Yosef should have mentioned their dispute also about beginning, not only about interruption. Rather, the Rosh holds that the Talmud speaks in all cases, whether doubtful or certain darkness: even in certain darkness one need not interrupt eating, but even in doubtful darkness one may not begin. This is also the Bach's view. Therefore, what the Shulchan Aruch writes, 'from nightfall,' is not precise, and the same applies during doubtful darkness.
