When it becomes dark. Even in doubtful darkness; see siman 261. It seems to me that if he prayed Maariv while it was still day, it is forbidden to eat until he recites Havdalah, as written in siman 271, seif 4. See siman 293.
He need not interrupt. It is possible that if he prayed during the meal, the obligation of Havdalah has taken effect upon him and it is forbidden to eat until he recites Havdalah; see siman 271, seif 4. Bachya wrote in Sefer Shulchan Arba that if he were to interrupt, he would appear as one who drives away the King. Similar to this they expounded in the Mechilta on Zachor and Shamor, that one should guard it at its departure like a person who does not want his beloved to leave him as long as he can. In Hagahos Yesh Nochalin it is written that the Maharsha interrupted a wedding meal, went to the synagogue, and afterward returned to the meal. Perhaps he held like the opinion of the “some say.” It implies in Tosafot in Shabbos 9 that if one began in a prohibited manner, he interrupts, and so it appears in siman 271, seif 5, in the Rama. One must say that he holds that Havdalah is according to the Torah. According to the one who says Havdalah is D’Rabbanan, if he washed his hands, he recites Havdalah before eating, since its time is the entire night and we are concerned lest he be negligent, as written in siman 235, seif 2. But if he began eating, he does not interrupt; see there. If he has not yet recited Krias Shema and began in a prohibited manner, according to all opinions he interrupts. One may not rely on the call to synagogue except someone who is accustomed to go on Motzaei Shabbos to the synagogue. Because of Havdalah, the prohibition applies only when its time arrives, namely doubtful darkness.
And some say that this applies only, etc. And so it appears in the Gemara, that Havdalah establishes in doubtful darkness, unlike the Olas Shabbos, who wrote the opposite in the name of Shiltei Giborim. This is not found there in Shiltei Giborim; see there.
