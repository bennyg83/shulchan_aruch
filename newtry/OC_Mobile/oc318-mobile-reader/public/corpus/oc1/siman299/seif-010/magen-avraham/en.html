He says Hamavdil. Nevertheless, it is forbidden to eat until he recites Havdalah over a cup, as stated by Abudraham and the Ran in the chapter Arvei Pesachim. On Yom Tov that falls on Motzaei Shabbos, he says “Who distinguishes between holy and holy,” as stated by Mahariv in the laws of Pesach.
Without a blessing. Since it is impossible to say the Name and kingship there; see the end of siman 296, unlike the Olas Shabbos.
And so women. Women who practice not doing labor on Motzaei Shabbos have a custom only until the congregation finishes its prayer, as stated in Minhagim of the Tur. However, women have practiced not doing labor the entire Motzaei Shabbos, as stated by Abudraham.
Who do not recite Havdalah. Although they are obligated in prayer, as written in siman 107, nevertheless most of them did not practice to pray on Motzaei Shabbos. One can say that since Maariv is optional, except that they accepted it upon themselves as obligatory, women did not accept it upon themselves on Motzaei Shabbos.
Lighting the lamp. This is the language of the Darkei Moshe at the end of siman 294: It is written in Or Zarua that one must be careful not to perform labor until after Seder Kedushah, even though he has already recited Havdalah. However, the synagogue attendant lights the lamps in the synagogue once he has recited Havdalah in prayer, even before Seder Kedushah. In the Zohar, Bereishis, it is written that there is a great punishment for not lighting until after he recites Havdalah over the cup. I have not seen anyone concerned for this; rather, the simple custom is to light after one recited Havdalah in prayer, and so it is in siman 681. These are his words. In truth, in Zohar Bamidbar, page 450, it is also written not to light until after Seder Kedushah. Nevertheless, what the Rama wrote here requires study, that the custom is to be lenient and light after Barchu, for this is unlike what he wrote in Darkei Moshe. One must say that what he wrote here speaks of women, since they do not recite Havdalah in prayer and are also not expert in saying Hamavdil, so they practice lighting after Barchu. But men are forbidden until after they have recited Havdalah in prayer, and so is the simple custom. So it also appears at the end of siman 263. The Beit Yosef disagreed with the words of Rabbeinu Yerucham and wondered at him, from where did he derive this? It is also wondrous in my eyes. Perhaps the words of Rabbeinu Yerucham can be explained as referring to what he wrote above in the name of the Rambam, who holds that although one recited Havdalah in prayer, he is forbidden to perform labor until he recites Havdalah over the cup. On this he says that this applies only to complete labor, etc., but lighting a lamp, etc., is permitted, for it is permitted to light a lamp for Havdalah. So it also appears in Shabbos 150 in Rashi and Ran, who wrote that although he has not recited Havdalah, the question is from the latter clause, which permits cutting from what is attached, etc. This implies that they are not particular about his leaving the techum, or about carrying four cubits in the public domain and taking out from one domain to another. This is because he recited Havdalah in prayer. But when he did not recite Havdalah in prayer, all agree that it is forbidden. Therefore one must teach publicly that they should teach their daughters to say Hamavdil, and all the more so those who kindle wood and fire and heat water, which are complete labors. A spiritually sensitive person should act as I wrote above in the name of Darkei Moshe and the Zohar, not to light until after Seder Kedushah. But moving the lamp is obviously permitted after he recited Havdalah in prayer. See siman 623.
