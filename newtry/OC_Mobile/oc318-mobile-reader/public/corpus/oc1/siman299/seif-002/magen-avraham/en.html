They were drinking. Meaning, without eating.
If they wish, etc. Meaning, even though they are not permitted to do so, as written at the end of seif 1, they need not recite the blessing again, for kiddush is more stringent than Havdalah. These are the words of the Beit Yosef. But in Kesef Mishneh he explained that it speaks where they are sitting and eating; even if they said it, they need not bless again; see there. The one who disagrees holds that although they need not interrupt, once they said, “Come and let us recite Havdalah,” they removed themselves from drinking and require a blessing. But when they are sitting and drinking, according to all opinions they do not require a blessing, as written in siman 271, seif 5. One should not say that the one who disagrees is the “some say” in seif 1, who holds that one must interrupt, as the Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Maharik, for if so, it should not have said “and there is one who disagrees,” and it should also have said that the case is definite darkness.
