(Seif katan 6) And some say that he must, this is the view of Maharik, etc. The Beit Yosef cites Maharik, who wrote that according to the Rif and the Semag, the 'some say' in seif 1, who require interruption for havdalah once it is certainly dark, it is obvious that he must recite a new blessing. Maharik wrote that he himself acted accordingly and recited Borei Pri HaGafen in the middle of the meal before havdalah after he remembered, since once it was certainly dark he had to interrupt, which is a removal of attention. The Beit Yosef wondered why he did not cite the Tur in siman 271, that even one who interrupts for kiddush need not recite Borei Pri HaGafen, and there he wrote that this is how we rule. Therefore the Magen Avraham has a difficulty with the Shulchan Aruch: in siman 271 by kiddush, the Beit Yosef rules plainly like the Tur, without citing a dissent, but here by havdalah he cites Maharik's view that one must bless. According to the Beit Yosef, kiddush and havdalah are equal, and kiddush is even more stringent. If so, according to Maharik one should have to bless by kiddush too, and his view should have been cited there as well.
And the Bach wrote, etc. This was to resolve Maharik's words. See the Bach there. But in the Shulchan Aruch this is insufficient, for it says 'when he interrupts to recite havdalah,' implying in all cases, whether from drinking or from eating. This differs from siman 271 regarding kiddush.
If so, immediately, etc. It is considered ended, meaning that an establishment for drinking is not a complete establishment, and such an establishment is removed by a slight matter.
But regarding drinking, according to all opinions, etc. This is the correct reading. Meaning, why did Maharik make the matter depend on the Rif and the Rosh in seif 1? Even according to the Rosh, who says that one need not interrupt eating even once it is certainly dark, he agrees that for drinking one must interrupt, as the Talmud states explicitly: this applies to eating, but for drinking one interrupts. The Magen Avraham adds that this is so even during doubtful darkness, following his view in seif katan 1 that according to the Rosh the teaching speaks both of certain darkness and doubtful darkness. The Bach himself explains this way. Thus when the Talmud says that for drinking one interrupts, it means even during doubtful darkness.
Regarding what the Beit Yosef wrote, that they were reclining for drinking: the Beit Yosef wrote that one should not ask on Maharik why he did not say that he recited HaMotzi after havdalah when he returned to eat. Since Maharik holds that Borei Pri HaGafen must be repeated, even though by kiddush in siman 271 we do not find such a view, all the more so he should have needed HaMotzi. Perhaps, however, they were reclining only for drinking. These are the Beit Yosef's words. If so, the Beit Yosef too is difficult, for Maharik's words imply that they were reclining for eating.
It refers to drinking after the meal. Meaning, at first they certainly reclined for eating, and in the middle of the meal he interrupted for havdalah. Afterward they stopped eating and established themselves only for drinking, so he did not need to recite HaMotzi. But to me it appears simply, etc. As written in seif 8, if he recited havdalah even after washing his hands, the Borei Pri HaGafen of havdalah exempts wine during the meal. If afterward he has no wine during the meal and no wine for Birkas HaMazon, some say he must recite an after-blessing on the havdalah wine, and Birkas HaMazon does not exempt it. Although Birkas HaMazon exempts wine before the meal, and the kiddush cup, since those relate to the meal, havdalah has no connection to the meal, and therefore Birkas HaMazon does not exempt it. So too in siman 174, seif 4, and here in seif 7, some say that if he recited havdalah before washing, the havdalah does not exempt wine during the meal from Borei Pri HaGafen, whereas kiddush before washing does, because kiddush is connected to the meal and havdalah is not. Accordingly, these authorities also hold here that havdalah is worse than kiddush, requiring Borei Pri HaGafen over havdalah during the meal. At first glance, this does not fully explain Maharik, who based himself on the Rif and Semak, that once it is certainly dark he must interrupt even from eating, rather than on this reasoning. The Magen Avraham's reasoning would apply even according to the Rosh. Therefore the Magen Avraham may only be explaining the Shulchan Aruch, that its 'some say' does not mean Maharik but the opinions in seif 8 and siman 174. Or perhaps he also explains Maharik, but holds that this reasoning alone is insufficient unless joined with the Rif and Semag.
Therefore one should be careful, etc. Meaning, although the Bach ruled that he need not recite a blessing, as with kiddush, according to the Magen Avraham one may say that havdalah is worse than kiddush, and the matter depends on great authorities in seif 8 and siman 174. Therefore one should not enter into a doubtful situation.
