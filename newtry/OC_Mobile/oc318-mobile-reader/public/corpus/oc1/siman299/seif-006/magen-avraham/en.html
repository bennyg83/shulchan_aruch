While it is still day. We do not say that since he recited Havdalah he accepted the fast upon himself, for we say this only regarding an obligatory fast, such as Yom Kippur or Tishah B’Av, and so it is explicit in the Gemara. The Olas Shabbos did not know this and objected to the Levush improperly; these are exactly the words of the Terumas HaDeshen. It speaks of one who vowed to fast every Sunday for three days and three nights, or every Erev Rosh Chodesh, and the like. See what I wrote in siman 556. But here, with vows, we follow the language of people, and the fast begins only at night, as stated there in Terumas HaDeshen. It implies that he prays Maariv before reciting Havdalah, and nevertheless he may eat afterward. The same applies on a weekday: one may eat after Maariv while it is still day. In any case, he prays Maariv before Havdalah, and so it appears from what is written at the end of siman 293, that because of Havdalah he also prays Maariv while it is still day. See siman 293, seif 3, and see the Gemara there, where prayer is obvious and Havdalah is the question. If so, wherever it is permitted to recite Havdalah, all the more so it is permitted to pray. The Shelah, laws of Rosh Hashanah, wrote that one who fasts two days and two nights before Rosh Hashanah recites Havdalah on the night after the second day. If Rosh Hashanah falls on Tuesday, he should not wait until Tuesday night, for then he would need to recite Yaknehaz, and it would be a strange matter. It seems to me that if it happened that he waited, he should not say kiddush and Havdalah over one cup, for we say in Pesachim 102 that one does not recite two sanctifications over one cup, but rather sanctification and Havdalah, because they are one matter. Rashi explained that both are because of the sanctity of Yom Tov, and in Havdalah itself he mentions the sanctity of Yom Tov, for he blesses “Who distinguishes between holy and holy.” So too wrote the Rashbam. But Havdalah from weekday has no connection. Even more than this, Tosafot wrote, s.v. and let us say both over one cup, etc., that even according to the questioner who holds that one may say two sanctifications over one cup, nevertheless if Birkas HaMazon relates to a weekday meal and kiddush relates to Shabbos, one cannot say both over one cup. One cannot say that here too he should say “between holy and holy,” for that would be false. Therefore it seems to me that he should recite kiddush over one cup and afterward recite Havdalah over one cup and drink, as is done at a wedding meal. If he has no wine, he recites kiddush over bread and eats HaMotzi, and afterward recites Havdalah over beer, since there are those who say that it is permitted to eat before Havdalah, as written in siman 296, seif 3. Although Tosafot and the Rosh in Arvei Pesachim wrote that it is astonishing how they can say half of Yaknehaz over bread and half over beer, that refers to Motzaei Shabbos that is Yom Tov, where both are one matter, as written above. But here they agree, for this has no connection to that. However, there is a dispute whether he should bless over beer, as written in seif 3, and there are also those who say that if he tasted, he does not recite Havdalah. Therefore it is better to make the kiddush also over one cup of beer, as written above regarding wine, and as written in siman 296, seif 3. For even the Rosh, who holds that one does not recite kiddush over beer, as written in siman 272, seif 9, nevertheless holds that on Motzaei Shabbos that is Yom Tov it is better to say both over beer. Alternatively, he should hear kiddush from others and not taste HaMotzi until after he recites Havdalah over beer and drinks, because it is forbidden to eat before Havdalah. Afterward he blesses HaMotzi and eats, for he has fulfilled the obligation of kiddush even though he has not yet tasted. See what I wrote in siman 167, seif 16, in the Rama, and what is written in siman 271, seif 6.
