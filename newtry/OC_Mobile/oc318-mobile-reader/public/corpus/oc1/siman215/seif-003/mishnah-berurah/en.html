(13) Who is learning, etc. Meaning, amen cannot appropriately be said to affirm and uphold the words of the one reciting the blessing, since the words do not have the status of a blessing.
(14) In vain. Meaning, the teacher is teaching them the blessings outside their proper times. Nevertheless, it is permitted to pronounce Hashem's name. Even the teacher may pronounce the Name in order to teach the child the blessings, because we must teach children in order to educate them in Torah study and observance of the mitzvos, as it is written, “You shall teach them to your children,” etc. This permission to pronounce Hashem's name applies specifically when teaching a child. An adult studying blessings in the Gemara recites them without pronouncing Hashem's name. When studying verses quoted in the Talmud, however, he may recite them as written, including Hashem's name.
(15) To discharge, etc. Such as when the child recites the blessings at their proper times as part of his education.
(16) For they have reached the age of education. This implies that one does not answer amen to a blessing recited over food or the like by a child who has not yet reached the age of education. Peri Megadim in his novellae to Berachos.
(17) Who recite the Haftarah. Meaning, they then read from the Torah and the Prophets and recite blessings before and afterward, and one must answer amen to those blessings.
