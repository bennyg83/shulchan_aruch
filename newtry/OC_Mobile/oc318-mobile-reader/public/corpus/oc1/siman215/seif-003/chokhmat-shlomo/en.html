There, seif 4 — anyone who blesses a blessing that is unnecessary behold he bears the Name of Heaven in vain and is as one who swore in vain, and it is forbidden to answer amen after him. NB: Chayei Adam reached my hands for a time and I saw there in Neshamat Adam that he raised: why should blessing in vain be from the Torah if it is not a way of mockery and jest — behold if one says a thousand times "You are Heaven King of the world, Creator of..." and similar, it would not be forbidden, and when he says Baruch Atah etc. it would be [in vain] — and with this his heart was filled to dispute all the poskim and permit in doubt of blessing; and I say: let the matter be silenced and let it not be said — for certainly the words of the poskim are correct; certainly the greatness of a blessing's elevation is beyond other song and praise — this causes its loss and prohibition if done gratuitously; for behold the matter of blessing is recompense for the good we received from Him, blessed be He — whether receiving worldly good as in all blessings of enjoyment, or good of the soul in a mitzvah He gave us — all is blessing, recompense for recompense, as Chazal said explicitly in chapter 6 of Berakhot: whoever enjoys this world without blessing is as if he steals from the Holy One, blessed be He, and likewise they said it is as betrayal of holy things of Heaven — from all this it is proven blessing is recompense to Him for the good; and automatically, since if one says "You, Heaven, King of the world, created..." he does not fulfill recompense, only if he said Baruch Atah — that is recompense; and it is proven a blessing has much elevation over the way of song and praise; if so from this reason itself it is forbidden if done in vain, per the parable: if a man says to a dignitary "I hold gratitude to you for the good you did me" — if the dignitary knows he did him no good he will be angry, saying you mocked me, I did you no good, why hold me undeserved gratitude — and a speaker of falsehoods is not proper before his eyes; if so how much more before Him, blessed be He — behold Chazal said therefore they did not say "the Mighty and the Awesome," for since He knows He is truly [mighty] they did not lie to Him, only afterward they returned the crown to its place when they said He is not a liar, for these are His mights; but otherwise they would not say to Him, since He is true; so too the way of blessing that is recompense to Him applies only when one enjoys His good from that thing — whether enjoyment of soul or body — then recompense to Him applies; but if at this time he does not enjoy this thing, behold the recompense is a false matter, and a speaker of falsehoods is not proper before His eyes — therefore it is forbidden to bless a blessing in vain; but if not in the language of Baruch Atah but only "You are" — since the way of blessing does not emerge from it, it is proven it is not recompense but praise and song; therefore from this reason even if he does not enjoy at this hour it is not false — therefore permitted to say; and from this reason also from doubt one does not bless, for doubt of falsehood is also forbidden; and the words of the early poskim are fulfilled, there's help; and this is clear and true in my humble opinion. And regarding what is written in Chayei Adam above — whether one blesses in a doubt of doubt — see in my work on Shulchan Aruch Orach Chayim year 5607 on pages 64 and 105 what is written in responsum to the community of Schwabach on this, there's help, and examine well. And behold if blessing in vain is a negative commandment d'oraisa — see in my work on Shulchan Aruch Yoreh Deah edition 5 siman 14, there I wrote incidentally what belongs here, see there in responsum to the community of Ritin. And behold Magen Avraham here brought the dispute: for Tosafot blessing in vain is only d'rabbanan, and for Rambam from the Torah. And now it appears to me, there's help, clear proof it is only d'rabbanan, from what is written in chapter Af: daf 115 — even for the view that mitzvot do not cancel each other, that is only d'oraisa with d'oraisa, but d'oraisa with d'rabbanan they cancel; and it is difficult why maror should be considered d'rabbanan when its obligation body is only d'rabbanan — nevertheless if he blesses on eating maror, if so when he ate it is not blessing in vain since d'rabbanan also applies and "you shall not turn aside" as written chapter 2 of Shabbat; but if he will not eat maror at all, behold he blessed blessing in vain and violated a Torah prohibition — if so if he blessed on it it already became d'oraisa, and why should matzah be cancelled? Perforce it is proven blessing in vain is only d'rabbanan, and even if he does not eat it is blessing in vain; therefore maror is only d'rabbanan. And it is strained to say there is a distinction between one d'oraisa and two d'oraisa that matzah is also a blessing and there are two d'oraisa — that is, for d'rabbanan one may divide between one d'rabbanan and two; but for d'oraisa one may not divide. But it appears we may reject this, that one may say: certainly if he eats matzah and maror together he does not bless first on eating matzah and afterward on maror separately, for that is an interruption between the blessing on matzah and its eating, since maror is only d'rabbanan — therefore it is not considered need of eating, therefore it is an interruption — perforce he blesses one blessing on matzot and marorim, or on matzah and maror; if so since in any case he ate matzah it is not considered blessing in vain even though he also mentioned maror — and thus for all views it is not blessing in vain; and nafka mina for Rashi's view in tefillin that he did not speak — he blesses one, namely on matzot; if so if he blessed on matzot and had only one piece, nevertheless it is not blessing in vain since in any case he fulfilled through one — examine well. And behold in Shulchan Aruch: anyone who blesses a blessing that is unnecessary bears the Name of Heaven in vain and is as one who swore in vain, and it is forbidden to answer amen after him — see Magen Avraham s.v. amrinan in Yoma it is explicit there that even one who causes an unnecessary blessing violates lo tissa. And from this one may prove like Tosafot in Rosh Hashanah daf 33 that any way of blessing does not violate lo tissa except d'rabbanan, and this is from what is written in Sotah daf 43b etc.: what is between R' Yossi and R' Yishmael the Galilean? Between them is a rabbinic transgression, as whom does this follow that is taught: one who spoke between prayer and prayer — a transgression is in his hand and he returns from the battle ranks — as whom? As R' Yishmael the Galilean; and Rashi explained s.v. transgression is in his hand: if he did not return and bless, for thus we say in Menachot 68: spoke — blesses two; did not speak — blesses one; and it is difficult from where does the Talmud know the intent is transgression is in his hand without return and blessing? And we are forced to say it is not like R' Yishmael the Galilean, since it is only a rabbinic transgression; on the contrary, if the intent is transgression is in his hand with return and blessing — it is a Torah transgression, since he caused blessing an unnecessary blessing and violated lo tissa from the Torah, if we say that the way of blessing also violates lo tissa; perforce what the Sages obligated to bless is because he is obligated to bless d'rabbanan, which they anchored on the negative commandment of lo tasur — lo tissa in vain is not included; but here, since if he had not spoken he would not have been obligated to bless even d'rabbanan except through his act of speaking he brought himself to obligation of blessing — if so he caused unnecessary blessing as proven in Yoma that Magen Avraham brought that even this is included in unnecessary blessing — if so automatically he violates lo tissa from the Torah; if so now it may even follow R' Yishmael the Galilean; and who strained that it deals without blessing a second time, and it follows not like R' Yishmael the Galilean? Perforce it is proven like Tosafot that any way of blessing does not violate lo tissa; and for this reason even in unnecessary blessing — if so in any case whether he blessed or did not bless, all is like a rabbinic transgression; perforce it follows not like R' Yishmael the Galilean; and this is clear proof for Tosafot's view — examine well.
