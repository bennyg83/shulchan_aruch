1) [Seif 1] Only after two blessings, etc. According to this, one should respond Amen after one's own blessings over the haftarah. Some follow this practice. Birkei Yosef, paragraph 1. He writes the same in Ledavid Emes, siman 20, paragraph 19; Shalmei Tzibbur, folio 88a. Zechor LeAvraham, paragraph 1, however, writes that this is not our custom. Indeed, in our time we have not seen anyone follow this practice.
2) There: Only after two blessings, etc. Maharshal writes that according to this, the two morning blessings over Torah study require consideration as to whether one should respond Amen after his own blessing. Bach cites this and writes that the practice is not to respond Amen after the blessings over Torah study. The reason is that the Rambam spoke only of two or three blessings that resemble Boneh Yerushalayim, where each blessing was instituted for a separate matter. In Birkas HaMazon, the first is Birkas HaZan, the second Birkas HaAretz, and the third Boneh Yerushalayim. The two blessings over Torah study, however, are considered one, because both concern Torah. These are his words.
3) There: The practice is to respond Amen after Yehalelucha, etc. This is because it is forbidden to interrupt in the middle of Hallel and Pesukei DeZimrah, so the initial and concluding blessings are considered adjacent to one another. This is unlike blessings over fruit. Regarding the blessings over Torah study too, the early enactment was that the first reader recited the initial blessing and the last reader the concluding blessing, so they were not adjacent. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1.
4) There: The practice is to respond Amen after Yehalelucha, etc. The practice is likewise to respond Amen after the conclusion of Shemoneh Esrei. After Ga'al Yisrael in the morning, however, the practice is not to respond because according to the Zohar this constitutes an interruption. Beis Yosef. See above, siman 66, seif 7. Likewise, one should not respond Amen after concluding his own blessing of Borei Nefashos. Beis Yosef.
