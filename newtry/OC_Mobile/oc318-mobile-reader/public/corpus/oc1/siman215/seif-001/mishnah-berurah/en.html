(1) After his own blessings. Meaning, after any single blessing he recites, or even after two blessings that do not conclude a series of blessings, he does not answer amen after himself. The Gemara states that whoever answers amen after his own blessings is reprehensible. The Gemara's language implies that he has not committed a prohibition but has merely acted reprehensibly. This applies where he has not created an interruption. If, however, he recited a blessing over a mitzvah he is about to perform, or a blessing over enjoyment, and interrupted by answering amen between the blessing and the act, he has committed a prohibition and must repeat the blessing. Peri Megadim, siman 51.
(2) That conclude a series of blessings. This means after Shomer Amo Yisrael La'ad, or after HaPoreis Sukkas, etc., recited on Shabbos, which concludes the blessings of Krias Shema; or after Boneh Yerushalayim, which concludes the first three blessings of Bircas HaMazon, which are D'Oraisa. One does not answer amen after the first blessings of Maariv or Shacharis. Although there are two blessings, they do not conclude the subject. Amen is appropriate only when the matter has reached its conclusion and completion.
(3) After Yehalelucha, etc. Although one answers amen after oneself only following two adjacent blessings, these are considered adjacent. Yehalelucha is adjacent to the blessing before Hallel, and Yishtabach is adjacent to Baruch She'amar, because it is forbidden to interrupt with speech between Baruch She'amar and Yishtabach, or between the blessing before Hallel and Yehalelucha. The Pesukei DeZimrah recited between them are not considered an interruption. By contrast, the after-blessing following a blessing over enjoyment is not considered adjacent to the first blessing. Although there are also two blessings, one before and one afterward, the later blessing has no direct attachment to the first, since one may interrupt and speak between tasting after the first blessing and reciting the after-blessing. The after-blessing is therefore regarded as an independent blessing.
(4) Boneh Yerushalayim. Because it concludes the blessings that are D'Oraisa. HaTov VeHaMeitiv is D'Rabbanan. To demonstrate that there is a distinction between them, one answers amen after Boneh Yerushalayim.
(5) Shomer Amo, etc. One does not answer amen after Yir'u Eineinu when reciting it oneself, because it is unrelated to the blessings of Krias Shema and is regarded as an independent blessing.
