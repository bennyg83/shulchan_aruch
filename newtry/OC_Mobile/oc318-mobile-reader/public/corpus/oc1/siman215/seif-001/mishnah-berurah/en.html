(1) After his blessings — meaning after every individual blessing that he blesses, or even after two blessings — as long as they are not the end of blessings, one does not answer amen to oneself after them; for it is in the Gemara: anyone who answers amen after his own blessings — that is disgraceful. And behold from the Gemara's language it implies he did not make a prohibition here, only it is disgraceful — and this is when he did not make an interruption here; but if he blessed on some mitzvah matter he wishes to perform, or a blessing of enjoyment where he interrupted between the blessing and that matter — he made a prohibition here and returns and blesses [Peri Megadim in siman 151].

(2) That they are the end of blessings — meaning: after the blessing Shomer Amo Yisrael La'ad [or after spreading the shelter of peace that they say on Shabbat], which concludes the blessings of Kri'at Shema; or after the blessing Boneh Yerushalayim which concludes the first three blessings that are from the Torah; and not after the first blessings of evening and morning — for even though they are two blessings, they are not at the end of the matter, and amen applies only at the conclusion and departure from the matter.

(3) After Yehallelucha etc. — and even though one does not answer amen after oneself except specifically after two adjacent blessings — nevertheless these are called adjacent, for Yehallelucha is adjacent to the blessing before Hallel, and Yishtabach adjacent to Baruch she'amar, for it is forbidden to interrupt and speak between Baruch she'amar and Yishtabach and between the blessing before Hallel and Yehallelucha [and the psalms of song said between them are not called an interruption]; not so in the final blessing after blessings of enjoyment — even though there are also two blessings, one before and one after — since they have no connection with the first blessing, for one may interrupt and speak between the tasting after the first blessing and the final blessing, the final blessing is like a separate blessing.

(4) Boneh Yerushalayim — because it is the end of blessings that are d'oraisa, for berachat haTov vehaMetiv is d'rabbanan; and in order to show there is a distinction between them, therefore they answer amen after it.

(5) Shomer Amo etc. — but one does not answer after Yir'u Eineinu that he blesses himself, for it does not belong to the blessing of Kri'at Shema and is like a separate blessing.
