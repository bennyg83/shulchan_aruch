That he did not hear the entire thing — see in Mishna Berurah what we wrote, that we require hearing mention of the Name — this is the view of Rosh and Ri. And the second view is the view of Rashi and Or Zaru'a [and other poskim as I noted in Sha'arei Teshuvah]. And ostensibly, this certainly discusses an ordinary amen on a blessing in which one is not now obligated himself, as stated in the Gemara; and if so, even if he did not hear the end of the blessing either, he must answer amen if he knows which blessing it is. And it is possible Rashi's intent is likewise: by hearing the end of the blessing he knows in which blessing the one blessing stands. But on Rosh and Ri, who wrote that one must also hear mention of the Name from the mouth of the one blessing — it is certainly difficult, for from their own words above in the chapter of Three Who Ate on the matter of amen yetomah it is proven that all agree one may answer amen on a blessing in which he is not obligated even if he did not hear the blessing at all if he knows what he is answering [and this is whether for the first answer or the final answer there, on Ri and Rosh]. And were I not afraid, I would say that likewise for Rosh and Ri it is so: we require only that he know what he is answering, and even if he did not hear the voice of the one blessing himself but only through others, or he heard only the end of the blessing. And that they specified mention of the Name is because he wanted to conclude; but regarding a Samaritan, even if he heard mention of the Name it does not help, lest his intent be to the name of the idol they established for them on Mount Gerizim, as Ri wrote. And one can likewise say thus in the words of Rosh there. One can further say: regarding being permitted to answer amen — meaning that it should not be amen yetomah — if he knows what he is answering, and with this the case of Alexandria of Egypt who waved kerchiefs is resolved. But regarding being obligated to answer amen — he is not obligated until he hears mention of the Name from the mouth of the one blessing, for the obligation of amen flows from what is written "for I will call on the name of the the Master," and our sages expounded that Moshe said to Israel: when I mention the name of the the Master, give greatness to our Heaven with amen; and therefore we require specifically that the one answering hear mention of the Name, and then there is obligation upon him. This requires further study regarding the view of Rosh and Ri. And know that the conclusion of Rambam's language that was copied in Shulchan Aruch implies that even if he did not hear mention of the Name, only the end of the blessing alone — he also answers amen; and likewise wrote Bach; and see what is written below:

(ב) From its beginning until its end — because in the Gemara it concludes that regarding a Samaritan we require until he hears the entire thing [namely before they decreed against them]; therefore the Mechaber also used this language, that regarding a Jew we do not require this. But in truth, even if he did not hear also the end of the blessing — if he knows which blessing the one blessing recited, he may answer amen per all views, as above in siman 124 seif 8 in the gloss there. Since here it discusses where the listener does not want to fulfill with that blessing [for one who wants to fulfill, he already ruled above in siman 213 seif 3 that we require specifically until he hears the entire thing, see there]:

(ג) He is obligated to answer amen after him — see in Taz, where it is proven from him that he holds the Tur too concedes to the essence of the obligation, but he holds that regarding obligation we require specifically that he hear from him the entire blessing; and see in Maamar Mordekhai that he too sides thus, and not as some strained in the words of Tur:

(ד) The one blessing is an apikoros — see above in siman 40 in Biur HaLachah s.v. "shekatavan apikoros." And it is plain that minim who do not believe in the words of chazal are like Samaritans before they decreed against them, that one must answer after them amen when they heard the entire blessing from their mouths — and this is specifically regarding answering amen alone. And regarding joining for other matters of holiness they are no better than Karaites; and see above in siman 55 seif 247 in Mishna Berurah:
