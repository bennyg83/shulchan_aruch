(6) He did not hear it in its entirety. Rather, he heard only the mention of Hashem's name and the conclusion of the blessing, and he must answer amen. Some poskim rule that even if he heard only the concluding formula of the blessing, he must answer amen. Eliyah Rabbah. See Chayei Adam, who demonstrates that even the first opinion agrees that when he knows which blessing he is answering, he must answer amen, as stated above in siman 124, seif 8, in the gloss. See there.
(7) Although he is not obligated. Meaning, all the more so when he himself is obligated in that blessing and wishes to fulfill his obligation through the person reciting it, he must certainly answer amen, thereby indicating that he too affirms and upholds the words of the one reciting the blessing. In that case, however, he must hear the entire blessing from beginning to end, as stated above in siman 213, seif 3.
(8) He is obligated to answer. As it is written, “When I call the Name of Hashem, ascribe greatness,” etc. Chazal expounded in Yoma 37a that Moshe said to Israel: When I recite a blessing and mention the Name of the Holy One, blessed is He, you must ascribe greatness to our God by answering amen.
(9) Amen after him. The Midrash states that when one hears another person praying for something or blessing Israel, even without mentioning Hashem's name, he must answer amen. Therefore, the custom became widespread to answer amen after the HaRachaman passages in Bircas HaMazon. Acharonim.
(10) But if he was, etc. Since the Mechaber groups an apostate and a Samaritan together with a child and an adult who altered the formula, it implies that the same law applies to all of them. Just as one does not answer amen after a child or adult who altered the formula, even if he heard the entire blessing from them, because their blessing is in vain, so too regarding these individuals. Although the Gemara states that one answers amen after a Samaritan if he heard his entire blessing, that applied before an image of a dove was found on Mount Gerizim, which they worshipped. Afterward, one does not answer because their intention is directed to Mount Gerizim. Likewise, an apostate's intention is not toward Heaven but toward idolatry. The Gra, however, rules in his commentary that regarding these latter two, if one heard the entire blessing from them, he answers amen.
(11) And altered, etc. This applies specifically when he altered the formula in a manner that prevents him from fulfilling his obligation through the blessing. His blessing is then like a blessing recited in vain, and one may not answer amen after him.
(12) He heard the entire blessing. Idolaters do not ordinarily intend their idolatry when mentioning Hashem's name. It appears that the same applies if one heard a blessing from someone who abandoned Judaism for idolatry. Nevertheless, Taz holds that one is not obligated to answer amen after an idolater; it is merely permitted.
