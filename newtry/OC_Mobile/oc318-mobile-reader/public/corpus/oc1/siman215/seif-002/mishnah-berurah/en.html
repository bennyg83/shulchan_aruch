(6) That he did not hear all of it — rather he heard only that he mentions the Name and the end of the blessing — he is obligated to answer amen after him; and there are poskim who hold that even if he heard only the conclusion of the blessing he must answer amen (Rif); and see in Chayei Adam who compelled that even the first view agrees where he knows which blessing he answers on, he must answer, as above siman 124 seif 8 in the gloss, see there.

(7) Even though he is not obligated — meaning: and how much more when he himself is obligated in that blessing and wishes to fulfill through the blesser's blessing — he is certainly obligated to answer amen to show that he too strengthens and upholds the blesser's words; but then we specifically require that he hear the entire blessing from beginning to end, as above siman 213 seif 3.

(8) Obligated to answer — for it is written "for the Name of Heaven I proclaim, give etc." and they expounded [Yoma 37]: Moshe said to Israel: when I bless and mention the Name, blessed be He, you shall give greatness to our Heaven with answering amen.

(9) After him amen — it is in the Midrash: when one hears that someone prays a matter or blesses Israel, even without mention of the Name — he is obligated to answer amen; therefore the custom spread that they answer amen after haRachaman in birkat hamazon (acharonim).

(10) But if he was etc. — from that he equated heretic and Samaritan with child and adult who altered — it implies they have one law; and just as regarding child and adult who altered, even if he heard the entire blessing from their mouths he does not answer amen, for the blessing is in vain — so too regarding these; and even though in the Gemara they say that regarding Samaritan too they answer if he heard the entire blessing from his mouth — that was before they found for them the image of a dove on Mount Gerizim that they worshipped; but afterward their intention is for Mount Gerizim; and a heretic too — his intention is not for Heaven but for avodah zarah; and Gra in his Biur ruled that regarding these two, if he heard the entire blessing from his mouth, he answers amen after him.

(11) And he altered etc. — and specifically when he altered in a manner that he does not fulfill through the blessing because of this, and behold he is like one who blesses a blessing in vain — it is forbidden to answer amen after him.

(12) He heard the entire blessing — for the way of idolaters is not to intend for their avodah zarah when mentioning the Name [and it appears the same for one who heard from one who converted his religion to avodah zarah]; nevertheless Taz's view is that there is no obligation to answer regarding idolaters, only permission.
