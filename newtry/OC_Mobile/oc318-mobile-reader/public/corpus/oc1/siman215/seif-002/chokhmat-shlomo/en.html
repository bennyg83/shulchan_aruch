There, seif 2. One who hears a Jew reciting a blessing, etc. Note: This is indeed Rambam's view. The Tur's language in siman 215 is as follows: “It appears to me that when it says that one answers amen, this is not an obligation but optional,” etc. “Rambam, however, writes that everyone who hears is obligated to answer amen,” etc. I am astonished how the Tur could have entertained the possibility that answering amen is merely optional. The Gemara explicitly states in Shevuos 36a that Rabbi Yose bar Chanina said: “Amen contains an oath,” etc.; “it contains affirmation of words, as it is written, ‘Yirmiyahu said to Chananyah: Amen, so may Hashem do,’” etc. Rashi explains: “Amen contains affirmation of words, and it is proper to answer amen to a prayer or supplication, for it means that the words should be confirmed, that it should be Hashem's will that they be true.” This appears to prove that answering amen is obligatory. Perhaps the Tur explains the Gemara unlike Rashi and understands that it merely teaches that one may answer amen and that this is not an orphaned amen. There may be support for this explanation, for why did the Gemara bring proof from Yirmiyahu rather than from an earlier verse in Tehillim 72, “Blessed is His glorious name forever, and may His glory fill the entire earth, amen and amen”; or Tehillim 89, “Blessed is Hashem forever, amen and amen”; or Tehillim 106, “Blessed is Hashem, the God of Israel, from eternity to eternity, and all the people shall say amen”? All these verses already show that amen affirms words. Why, then, was the verse from Yirmiyahu necessary? According to the above, however, it is understandable. The Gemara came to teach that this was not an orphaned amen, for even when Chananyah, who was a false prophet, spoke words of blessing to Israel, answering amen was not considered an orphaned amen. Nevertheless, the Tur remains difficult. How can he say that answering amen is optional, when Scripture says, “And all the people shall say amen,” implying an obligation? One cannot explain that the verse merely teaches that they are permitted to answer amen, for this was already known from Tehillim 72 and 89. It must therefore teach that answering amen is obligatory, supporting Rambam. This requires extensive study.
