(א) Obligated to answer amen. Thus Rambam — to exclude what Tur wrote initially that "they answer" that he said is not an obligation; rather he teaches one does not answer after a Samaritan, and the Mishnah is his source that said they answer after Israel; and on this Tur said afterward: but Rambam wrote etc. — meaning he argues on what he said initially regarding Israel, that there is no obligation to answer after him when he did not hear the entire blessing from his mouth; but Rambam makes it an obligation even in this.

(ב) And they answer amen after an idolater. This is from the Yerushalmi that the Rash brought, and he wrote the reason: because idolaters do not have a way to intend for avodah zarah when mentioning the Name — meaning to exclude Samaritan, that if he mentions the Name his intention is for Mount Gerizim; and so Rabbenu Yitzchak in the name of Yerushalmi; and I say amen after all blessers, as it is said "blessed be you from all the peoples"; and in Rambam that was before Beit Yosef it is written idolater similar to Samaritan; and Kesef Mishneh wrote that in Tur's text in Rambam it is not written prohibition regarding idolater but specifically Samaritan — and that is correct; and therefore Rama ruled like him, and that is correct, since we find no dispute in our Talmud — certainly we follow the Yerushalmi, only that he decided specifically when he heard all the blessings from him — and this is to distinguish in any case between idolater and Israel; and further it appears to distinguish that regarding idolater there is no obligation, only that there is no prohibition to answer after him — as Tur wished to explain initially regarding Israel; and this is proven from that we learned: they answer amen after Israel etc., and he did not specify idolater — in any case there is a distinction between them, and that is in the ways I wrote.
