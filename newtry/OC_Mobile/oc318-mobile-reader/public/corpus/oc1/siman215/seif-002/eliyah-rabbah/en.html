[Levush] Greater is the one who answers, etc. Malbushei Yom Tov disagrees. Since Rav told his son Chiya to seize the opportunity and bless, and Rav Huna likewise, the halachah evidently regards the one who blesses as greater. He should have written this above in siman 197 seif 2. See the end of Nazir and Shelah, page 255.
[Levush] And he heard that he mentioned, etc. So write the Rosh, Tur, Rabbenu Yonah, and Rabbenu Yerucham, page 103. I am surprised by Bartenura at the end of chapter Eilu Devarim, who writes that one answers Amen even if he did not hear the mention of Hashem's Name but only the end of the blessing. Nevertheless, Rashi's and Rambam's commentaries appear at first glance to support him, and none of the later authorities noted this.
But if it was, etc. Beit Yosef, Kesef Mishneh, Taz, and Magen Avraham did not examine Rambam's Commentary to the Mishnah, where it is clear that one answers specifically after a fellow Jew, with no distinction whether he heard the entire blessing or not.
