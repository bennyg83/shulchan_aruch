Or a Samaritan. For even though in the Gemara they say regarding a Samaritan too one answers if he heard the entire blessing — that is before the decree; but now they decreed on them to be complete idolaters (Beit Yosef); and Kemach Minchah wrote that Rambam's view is that even regarding an idolater one does not answer even if he heard the entire blessing — not like Yerushalmi — and Bach also wrote thus; Kemach Minchah further wrote that Rambam deals with when he did not hear the entire blessing; but if he heard it all he answers, whether for an idolater or a Samaritan — and this is the Rav's view in the gloss. It is in the Midrash: when one hears someone praying a matter or blessing for Israel even without mention of the Name — he is obligated to answer amen; therefore they answer amen after haRachaman in birkat hamazon.
