As one who swears in vain. For they say one violates lo tissa etc.; nevertheless it is only d'rabbanan and the verse is merely an asmakhta (Tosafot end of Rosh Hashanah and the Rash beginning of Kiddushin); and Rambam's view is it is d'oraisa, and so in Temurah daf 4 the plain meaning of the Talmud; and Tosafot explain it deals without blessing, and per their words if one blesses from doubt he does not violate lo tissa — and it requires study, siman 17 seif 2; they say in Yoma daf 70: bring another Torah scroll and read it — R' Levi said: because of an unnecessary blessing, for when he brings another Torah scroll he will need to bless again; and it is forbidden to cause another blessing when one could be discharged with one blessing. Rashba wrote in Teshuvot HaRashba end of section 1 that it is forbidden to speak between slaughter and slaughter for this reason; Beit Shmuel siman 58 in the name of many poskim wrote that in all blessings we say thus — not like Maharshal in responsum siman 29 who wrote that specifically in the Torah blessing we say thus, for it was not enacted except for honor of the congregation which does not exist; Shelah wrote that on Shabbat if they brought him fruits during the meal he may set them aside until after the meal in order to bless on them the final blessing — for on Shabbat it is permitted to cause a blessing to complete one hundred blessings; and it is difficult — for in the Gemara on Yom Tov they deal with lacking from the count of one hundred blessings, and in Shelah itself it is strained how he can complete the count of one hundred blessings — and nevertheless it is forbidden to cause a blessing, a fortiori on Shabbat; and further in Tosafot Shabbat daf 118 they wrote it is forbidden to divide the morning meal into two in order to fulfill three meals because he causes an unnecessary blessing; and even though we do not hold thus, that was because of the third meal — but because of one hundred blessings it is forbidden to add; R' Moshe Galanti in responsum siman 38 wrote that on Shabbat one may instruct his household not to bring all the fruits before him at once, end of his words; and likewise he may instruct to bring them after the meal; however even on weekdays if he has no desire to eat except little by little, or he desires to eat after the meal — he is permitted; only on Shabbat it is proper to do so because of the blessing; and Kemach Minchah wrote in the name of Sefer HaZichronot that even as a mitzvah that they not bring them before him — it is forbidden to multiply blessings; and see in Sefer Shetei Yadayim daf 102 folio 2.
