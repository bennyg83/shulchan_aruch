(18) A blessing that is unnecessary — such as when he blesses during the meal on things already discharged through berachat haMotzi — that blessing is entirely unnecessary; and further poskim wrote that even if now it would not be blessing in vain, it is sometimes included in unnecessary blessing — such as if they set a table before him and his intent is to go wash his hands and eat, and he takes before netilat yadayim and blesses on things he intends to eat during the meal — this too is forbidden, for he causes an unnecessary blessing; and how much more one who interrupts between the blessing and the action — he causes the first blessing to be entirely nullified — certainly forbidden; and see above siman 46 in Mishna Berurah s.k. 14 and Magen Avraham in this siman regarding completing one hundred blessings on Shabbat.

(19) In vain — meaning: even though he blesses in the order of the blessing's formula as praise and thanks, since it is unnecessary; and how much more if he mentioned the Name in vain, Heaven forbid — and not specifically the four-letter Name of Heaven, but other Names too are included in this prohibition; and likewise if he uttered the Name in a foreign language in vain — i.e., not in a way of praise and thanks — there is also prohibition (acharonim). The Rif wrote in the name of Sefer Chassidim: if you hear your friend mention the Name — do not enter within his words to say "do such-and-such for me," for thereby he will be silent to hear your words and you cause him to utter the Name of Heaven in vain; but if you hear your friend mentioned the Name to curse his fellow — then stop his words, for he will sin when he curses.

(20) As one who swears in vain — this is Rambam's language, and its source is from what is stated in the Gemara: whoever blesses an unnecessary blessing violates lo tissa the Name etc.; nevertheless many rishonim hold the essence of the prohibition is d'rabbanan, since he mentions it in a blessing as praise and thanks, and the verse is merely asmakhta; but if he mentions it, Heaven forbid, in vain without a blessing — all agree there is Torah prohibition, for he violates the positive commandment "and Heaven your Heaven you shall fear," which is a warning for uttering the Name of Heaven in vain, as stated in Temurah [daf 4], for this is part of fear not to mention His great Name except in praise and thanks as obligated, but not in vain. Nevertheless, whether per Rambam or other poskim, if he was in doubt which blessing — whether he blessed or not — if it is a d'oraisa matter he returns and blesses, and if d'rabbanan he does not return and bless; and it is implied from many acharonim there is prohibition to return and bless. Acharonim wrote that even if there is a doubt of doubt to require blessing — such as doubt whether he ate an olive-volume or not, and even if we say he did not eat — perhaps the halacha is that on a creature even less than olive-volume one blesses — nevertheless his doubt is to be lenient and he does not bless.

(21) And it is forbidden to answer after him — for he is not better than a child who mentions the Name through his learning, who did not make a prohibition in his mention — and nevertheless one does not answer amen after him, for there is no blessing upon it; how much more this one who made a prohibition, for he uttered the Name of Heaven in vain (Gra).
