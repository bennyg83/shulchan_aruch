But not grain. In the Mishnah it says so, and R' Shimon permits grain. And in the Baraita it says R' Shimon permits grain — such as wheat to make ludiyas from it (a food made from wheat), barley to place before his animal. And the Rif cited the Baraita, and the Rosh wrote regarding him that it implies he rules like R' Shimon, and we do not know what the reason is. And the Rambam ruled in chapter 5 like the Tanna Kamma, and so is the main ruling — end of his words. And so the Shulchan Aruch ruled here like the Tanna Kamma. And in my humble opinion it appears to me that the Rif explains that R' Shimon, who permits, disagrees with the Tanna Kamma in that he permits grain even if one does not need it for ludiyas or for his animal, but merely because it is possible to derive benefit from it for ludiyas — and in this the law certainly follows the Tanna Kamma. But in the Baraita that taught 'such as wheat etc.' — that is, that one truly needs this for this — even the Tanna Kamma agrees, since it is for his need or for his animal, and it is not muktzeh; therefore it is also permitted to send it. And therefore the Rif cited this Baraita. And it appears to me that regarding the law we mentioned, the other authorities and the Shulchan Aruch agree that it is permitted when one truly needs it for this; they just did not explain the Baraita this way — and as stated above is correct. And regarding the halacha, there is no practical difference from this, for all those items which one may not send, mentioned here — that is, via the reshut harabbim — but for carrying without sending, it is permitted according to all opinions, for there is no muktzeh here at all, since wheat is fit to chew as written in the siman after this, and likewise what pertains to animal food certainly one may carry, as written in siman 512. Rather, regarding transporting via the reshut harabbim is what is discussed here, and we do not have a reshut harabbim, and we send everything that is not muktzeh.
