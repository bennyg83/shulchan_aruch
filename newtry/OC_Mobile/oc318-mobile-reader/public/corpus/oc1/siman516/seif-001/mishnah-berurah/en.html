To send to his fellow on Yom Tov, etc. That is, as a gift, and even via the public domain, as the Rama concludes at the end, since his fellow can benefit from them; for example, when he sends him types of food, and vessels and garments that are fit to use, as below. And even if he sends him living animals, for he can slaughter them for the needs of Yom Tov; and even if he knows that his fellow will not slaughter them, nevertheless, since if he wanted he could slaughter them and eat them, it is permitted to send to him.
A domestic animal, a wild animal, and a bird. According to the opinion of the Mechaber above in siman 495, who forbids muktzeh on Yom Tov, here we are dealing with an animal that is not designated for breeding offspring, and likewise with an ordinary bird; for an animal that is designated for breeding offspring, and likewise a bird that is designated for producing eggs, is muktzeh, and it is forbidden to slaughter them on Yom Tov.
And legumes. Because it is the practice to cook them whole without grinding, and they are therefore fit even that same day.
But not grain. Whether wheat or other types of grain. And even though wheat is fit to make roasted grains from it, or some type of cooked dish, nevertheless, since ordinarily they stand for grinding, and grinding is forbidden on Yom Tov, it appears in his carrying them that it is for a weekday need, and it looks like he is occupied on Yom Tov for a weekday need, and it is forbidden. And for this reason several later authorities agreed that it is forbidden to send even in a place where there is an eruv.
Because it lacks grinding. And even though barley is fit for an animal without grinding, nevertheless most of it stands for grinding for human food. However, if his fellow needs today to give grain to his animal, it is permitted to send to him in a place where there is an eruv [so that there should not be a prohibition of carrying out; for for an animal it is forbidden to carry out, as above in siman 512, seif 3, in the gloss], and some are stringent in this as well.
