1) [Se'if 1] It is permitted to send to another person, etc. This means as a gift, as Rashi explains. Tur and Levush write likewise. This is permitted even through a public domain, as written in the gloss at the end of the siman. See there. Levush and the later authorities write likewise. See below, at the beginning of siman 518.
2) There. Even alive. This is because slaughtering is permitted on Yom Tov. Rambam, chapter 5, law 7; Sefer Mitzvos Gadol; Kol Bo; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1. Although one knows that the recipient will not slaughter them on Yom Tov, since he could slaughter and eat them if he wished, one may send them to him. Shulchan Aruch HaRav there; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 1.
3) There. Even alive. This means that they are designated for eating. If they are designated for breeding, sending them is forbidden because they are unfit for use on Yom Tov. They resemble grain that still requires grinding, as will be explained shortly. Olas Shabbos, subsection 1. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1, writes that a distinction can be made. Grinding is a melachah forbidden on Yom Tov for all grain, whereas slaughtering is permitted for other animals. An animal designated for breeding therefore is not considered to require a forbidden act. These are his words. Maamar Mordechai, subsection 2, however, expresses surprise at these authorities. If the animals are designated for breeding, even moving them is forbidden because they are muktzeh, and sending them is certainly forbidden. He writes that perhaps these authorities speak according to the opinion that does not accept muktzeh, as stated in siman 495, se'if 4, in the gloss. See there. Accordingly, for the opinion that accepts muktzeh, the same applies to a goat designated for its milk or a hen designated to produce eggs: one may not send it to another person on Yom Tov, since it is muktzeh, as written above at the beginning of siman 505 and in siman 497, subsection 32. See there.
