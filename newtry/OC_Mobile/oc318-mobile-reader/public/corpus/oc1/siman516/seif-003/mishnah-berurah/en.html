Vessels, even though they are not sewn. That is, garments; but not a shoe that is not sewn, for one does not benefit from it on a weekday as it is, without sewing.
If they are hard. In such a manner that it is permitted to lean on it, as is written in Yoreh Deah, siman 301; see there.
And one may send tefillin. That is to say, when they are sewn and prepared for all their needs, so that they are fit to benefit from them on a weekday.
Since they are fit, etc. And even though it is forbidden to take out anything on Yom Tov that he does not need, and he takes it out only for a weekday need, as will be explained in siman 518; nevertheless, since he enjoys and rejoices in that he sends gifts to his fellow, this is a need of the joy of Yom Tov.
On a weekday. To exclude grain, which is explained above as forbidden, because even on a weekday it is not fit to benefit from without grinding.
Via the public domain. As below in siman 518, that carrying out on Yom Tov is permitted when it is [for] some need, according to all opinions.
It is permitted to send them. The Meiri cited in the name of the Yerushalmi that one may not send gold ornaments on Yom Tov, and even regarding silver ones he hesitates there; but in the Shittah Mekubetzes in the name of the Ritva, and in the Or Zarua, they agreed that it is permitted to send ornaments, since they are fit to be worn.
