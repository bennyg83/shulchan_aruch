It is permitted to write with it. Terumas HaDeshen, siman 159, wrote that we are not concerned lest he put his pen into his mouth. Although chametz that became unfit for a dog to eat is still forbidden to eat, since he puts it in unintentionally there is no concern; only intentional eating is forbidden, because by doing so he gives it significance as food. According to this, it appears simple that if ink or other chametz that became unfit for a dog to eat fell into food, it is nullified even by a mere majority, since it is like dust and he does not give it significance by eating it, for his intention is only to eat the food, and this is fundamentally entirely spoiled. See below, siman 447. It appears that the same applies to printing ink made with chametz crumbs, which is very spoiled. Ink cooked by a non-Jew on Pesach is forbidden for writing, since it did not become unfit for a dog to eat before its time. So wrote Terumas HaDeshen there. See Yoreh De'ah, siman 134.
