Because it is impossible to clean them out. At first glance this implies that specifically washing with hot water and picking them clean do not help, but scraping would help. This could also resolve the Taz's difficulty about an apparent contradiction in the Tur. However, from the end of the Tur's wording, where he concludes like the Mechaber that one must give them as a gift to a non-Jew and does not mention a remedy of scraping, it somewhat implies that scraping does not help. So too it is explained below in siman 451 regarding hagalah. See the Taz. The Magen Avraham distinguishes between kneading troughs in which bread is kneaded, which have cracks between board and board, and small kneading troughs made from one piece with no crack at all, which certainly can be cleaned out. Mahari Weil, siman 123, and Maharil in the laws of Pesach wrote that the custom is to sell to a non-Jew, or to smear with clay, boards used for filled foods, rolling pins, and all utensils used with dough. Mahari Weil concludes that one should hide them in a room that is not needed on Pesach. The Magen Avraham wrote that one should not tell the non-Jew this condition, but should give them to him as a complete gift, and after Pesach take them back from him. See siman 478.
Laundering. The Magen Avraham wrote that this means with hot water, ash, and beating. See below, siman 451, regarding the law of sacks used for Pesach flour and what I wrote there.
