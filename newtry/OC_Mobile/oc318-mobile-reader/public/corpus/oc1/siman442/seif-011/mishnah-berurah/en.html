(א) One may not rely, etc. — not dealing with kashering to use on Pesach, for in that even a slight amount is forbidden without combining to kezayit; rather dealing with storing; therefore be stringent in every chametz manner. Acharonim wrote: here we deal with troughs made from large boards in which bread is kneaded, with grooves between boards where chametz is found; but small troughs from one piece with no crack — plainly one can scrape to store; but not to use on Pesach; if he transgressed and kneaded in them on Pesach after kashering — permitted.

(ב) Impossible to scrape — not that it is impossible, rather usually one cannot scrape properly; therefore be stringent. Also Acharonim wrote: here large boards with grooves; small one-piece troughs — plainly can scrape well and store in a hidden place.

(ג) Until after Pesach — not that he tells the non-Jew thus; give as absolute gift, and after Pesach he may take them back, as siman 448.

(ד) And the same for dough, etc. — Maharil wrote: so is custom for molyata boards, rolling pins, and all tools used for dough — plaster with clay or sell to a non-Jew. Per Acharonim above, if made from one piece, when scraped well and stored hidden — sufficient.

(ה) In vessels in which they place flour — lest chametz concern enters the cracks.

(ו) In vessels in which they place bread — wicker baskets with crevices, impossible to scrape; hide in a room one does not regularly enter.

(ז) And it requires laundering — good laundering in hot water, ash, and beating [Acharonim]; see below siman 453 seif 66 and Mishna Berurah there.
