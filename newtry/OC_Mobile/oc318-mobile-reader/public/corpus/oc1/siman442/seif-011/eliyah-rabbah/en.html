[11] If he set, etc. An item that causes setting is not nullified even in a thousand. The same applies if they poured chametz, beer, or liquor into it in order to make it sharp: even if the cheese had already been made and was set with a stomach lining, nevertheless it is for taste and is not nullified. Even if it remained until after Pesach, Responsa Nachalas Shiv'ah, siman 35, forbade it. Chok Yaakov wrote that even though an item that sets, and an item made for taste, are not nullified only rabbinically, nevertheless it is like an intact item and is forbidden if it remained until after Pesach. Still, perhaps it is no worse than a mixture of less than sixty over which Pesach passed, where it is enough to cast the value of the forbidden benefit into the Dead Sea, as written in siman 447 se'if 11. However, the Magen Avraham wrote in the name of the Ramban that the whole thing is forbidden. In a case of great loss, one may nevertheless be lenient by casting the value of the forbidden benefit into the sea; these are his words. This is difficult, because the Ramban did not write that it is considered intact except regarding the prohibition to keep it; but if one transgressed and kept it, we have not found that it is forbidden, since it is rabbinic. The Maharam of Lublin's responsum forbidding dough that became chametz through beer yeast and Pesach passed over it is different, because Pesach passed over actual intact yeast. However, I found in the work Amarkal in the name of the Raavyah that honey that became chametz through beer yeast is forbidden to keep, and if one transgressed and kept it, it is forbidden after Pesach, because eating it involves a Torah prohibition like kutach; these are his words. It is obvious that if there was also permitted honey, it would be zeh v'zeh gorem and permitted; if only one thing caused it to ferment, see Yoreh De'ah at the end of siman 87. Know that an item made for taste is called such specifically when he added enough to give taste, as written in Yoreh De'ah at the end of siman 98. Also, a setting agent must contain enough to cause the setting. Radbaz wrote that cheese set with stomach lining that had been in a chametz vessel is forbidden to eat and permitted to keep, end quote; specifically where the stomach lining had been in the chametz vessel for twenty-four hours, as written in Yoreh De'ah at the end of siman 87, Magen Avraham. This requires analysis, for after twenty-four hours it gives spoiled taste. Perhaps it is sharp, though that is not implied there; see siman 447 se'if 5 regarding fat in a chametz vessel. Magen Avraham wrote in the name of Responsa Ramban siman 8 that honey he cooked now was the fourth or fifth generation from honey that became chametz through beer yeast, and he forbade it. So too wrote the Raavan, folio 72. From this I wondered in Eliyahu Zuta at the end of siman 447 about what Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah permitted, to make a third milk-product from chametz; see there at length. As a matter of law, if one transgressed and kept it, perhaps there is room to be lenient. Olas Shabbos wrote about the above law of Maharam, regarding dough that became chametz through beer yeast, etc.: its law is not clear, for at first glance it is not comparable to something made for taste, end quote. I am astonished, for Maharam siman 121 brings proof from the Mordechai, and so it is ruled in Shulchan Aruch, Yoreh De'ah siman 123 se'if 15, that one who ferments dough with wine yeast makes the entire dough forbidden in benefit, and even in a thousand it is not nullified.
