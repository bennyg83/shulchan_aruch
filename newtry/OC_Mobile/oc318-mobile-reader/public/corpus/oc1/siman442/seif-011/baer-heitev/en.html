(א) For dough. And so is the custom to do for molyata boards and rolling pins and all tools used for dough — Maharil. And Knesset HaGedolah wrote: our women scrape them well and store them; and even a trough in which one kneads — it appears from Bach it is only a stringency, and troughs made from one piece with no crack at all — plainly one can scrape them.

(ב) In vessels. That is, wicker baskets.

(ג) Sack. Levush wrote: but sacks themselves do not suffice with shaking and laundering. Or Zarua wrote: likewise a new sack into which they put Passover flour — if they wish to launder it and return and put Passover flour in it, they must undo the stitches and all the patches; and so the elders of the genius would expound. And it requires further study: if they put flour b'dieved during Pesach without undoing stitches — whether there is permission even for eating; and Bach end of siman 452 was stringent even on an old sack without patches and stitches, and the elders were lenient as the meaning of Shulchan Aruch; and so Knesset HaGedolah wrote they practiced thus — end of his words. And Magen Avraham wrote: mere rinsing does not help — on the contrary the dough sticks to it and requires laundering in hot water, ash, and beating. And it is good practice to soak it in beer and close it in a room or make a partition before it. And so Korkom, Magen Avraham siman 467 s.k. 10. And it appears to me all per the locale.
