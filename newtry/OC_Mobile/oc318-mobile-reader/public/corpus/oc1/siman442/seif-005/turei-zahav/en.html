And likewise if he set cheeses, etc. Since the forbidden matter acts as the setting agent, it is considered as though it is present in its own substance. Therefore if one set mead, honey-water, with beer yeast, all the mead is forbidden on Pesach. This is comparable to Yoreh De'ah siman 123, where a whole dough is forbidden when set by forbidden matter. If it was set also with non-chametz yeast, it may be a case of this and this causing and be permitted, as in Yoreh De'ah siman 87 regarding stomach lining. One might ask that in Yoreh De'ah 87, cheese set with kosher stomach lining is nullified in sixty, so why should it not be nullified before Pesach? The answer is that chametz is more severe, for the power of the setting agent continues into Pesach, and regarding a setting agent we say that the prohibition reawakens. Regarding mead made during the year, it appears to be complete chametz because it is cooked in kettles used for beer and because it is often fermented with beer yeast. If the kettle was not used within twenty-four hours, its taste may be impaired and permitted before Pesach, but there remains concern that the yeast itself came from beer. Therefore one who prepares mead for Pesach must use yeast that is not chametz, and must kasher or properly prepare the vessel. The custom is to be careful about this. Some are lenient on the last day of Yom Tov with year-round mead, based on the Rema's leniency for foods cooked in chametz vessels, but that leniency depends on doubt whether the vessel was ben yomo. Where there is concern that it was fermented with beer yeast, or cooked in vessels used for sharp distilled spirits that improve the taste, one should not be lenient even on the last day, and certainly should not buy mead from a non-Jew then, lest beer was mixed into it.
