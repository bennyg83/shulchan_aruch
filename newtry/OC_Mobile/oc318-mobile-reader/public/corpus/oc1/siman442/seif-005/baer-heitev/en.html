(א) To destroy it. And there is karet and bal yera'eh (if there is a kezayit within the time of eating a meal), and if it was mixed into something else it is forbidden like other chametz mixtures. Maharil siman 97 [93].

(ב) With vinegar. Even if there are sixty against the vinegar it is not nullified, for something that sets is not nullified even in a thousand. The same if one pours vinegar, beer, or burnt wine into it. And so he wrote in responsum Nachalat Sheva siman 35: if one put burnt wine in cheese so the cheese would be sharp and strong and did not sell them before Pesach — forbidden for benefit, because done for taste and not nullified; and so Maharam of Lublin in dough that leavened in beer yeast; and so is the agreement of the Acharonim. And see Shevut Yaakov chelek 2 siman 16 and Pnei Me'irot siman 107. Taz wrote: and for this reason, if one set honey water called mead with beer yeast, all the mead is forbidden on Pesach; and if he set it also with yeast that is not chametz — this and that cause it, and it is permitted as explained in Yoreh De'ah end of siman 87; and it appears to me that here with chametz it is stricter, since there is power of setting during Pesach — see there, where he expanded and raised that some are lenient to drink on the last Yom Tov of Pesach mead of the whole year, as Rama wrote siman 447 seif 5 regarding everything cooked in a chametz vessel that one may be lenient on the last Yom Tov. And it appears this is because it is a doubt whether it was a same-day vessel when cooked in it, and we hold an unspecified vessel is not a same-day vessel — only regarding chametz we do not say an unspecified vessel is not same-day; but on the last Yom Tov we say so even for chametz. But if there is doubt lest it was leavened with beer yeast, there is no room to be lenient; and all the more in places where they cook mead all year in cauldrons where they make burnt wine — a sharp, strong thing that gives good taste to mead, as is known — what is not same-day does not help; if so there is no doubt at all — end of his words. And Baer Heitev before me wrote: if one bought mead before Pesach from the yeast, there is room to be lenient — see there. And in my opinion this requires further study. Cheeses set in a stomach that was in a chametz-absorbing vessel — forbidden to eat and permitted to keep — Radbaz, Knesset HaGedolah. And it appears to me its law is like something cooked in a chametz vessel — see siman 447 seif 5, Magen Avraham; it appears to me the stomach was above all in a chametz vessel, as Beit Yosef wrote end of siman 87; and see there s.k. 1. And Baer Heitev before me wrote — it appears to me he set it on Pesach — examine and decide. And I do not know why.
