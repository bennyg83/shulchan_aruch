5) Olas Shabbos further writes there that one should be stringent not to retain during Pesach even food cooked in a utensil used for chametz that same day. See there. Chok Yaakov, there, disputes this as well and writes that there is no reason to be stringent and that it may be retained. See there. Mekor Chaim, there; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 8; Chayei Adam, rule 121, subsection 1, write likewise. Responsa Chasam Sofer, siman 108, nevertheless concludes that a sharp food may not be retained, while other foods may be retained. See there. Mekor Chaim writes there that this applies only if the food will not remain soaking in a chametz utensil during Pesach, meaning that it was poured into a utensil used for Pesach. It appears that 'a utensil used for Pesach' is not literal, but means a utensil suitable for use on Pesach because it was used with chametz only when cold, as stated below in siman 421, se'if 22. See there. Responsa Har HaCarmel, siman 14, writes that preserves or fat kept in chametz pots may be retained. See there. Sha'arei Teshuvah, subsection 1, cites this ruling.
5) If the food was cooked or soaked during Pesach, even in a utensil that had not been used for chametz that day, it must be eliminated. Chayei Adam, there. This follows the authorities who prohibit during Pesach even a detrimental absorbed flavor, as stated below in the gloss to siman 447, se'if 10. According to the Shulchan Aruch there, however, it is permitted even for consumption, as will be explained there, with the help of Heaven. Regarding the practical ruling, see our words there in subsection 231.
