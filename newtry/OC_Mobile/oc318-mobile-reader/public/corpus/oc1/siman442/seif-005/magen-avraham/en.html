If he set. Even if there is sixty against the whey, it is not nullified, for an item that acts as a setting agent is not nullified even in a thousand. The Issur VeHeter, rule 25, writes that according to the Torah it is nullified and is forbidden only rabbinically. But the Tur wrote that it is considered as if present in its own substance, implying that he holds it is forbidden according to the Torah; otherwise, according to his own view, it would not have to be destroyed, as written in se'if 1. In Yam Shel Shlomo, Chullin chapter 8, siman 106, he disagrees and rules that a setting agent is nullified, and that with chametz it is at least permitted to keep it; see there. His words are void against all these authorities. The Rashba in responsum siman 850 also brings in the name of Maharam that vinegar that was fermented with beer yeast is forbidden to keep. The Ramban wrote in siman 8 of an incident involving someone who cooked honey and fermented it with yeast from honey that he cooked all year; the first honey had been fermented with beer yeast, and this honey was the fourth or fifth generation from it. Rabbeinu Yaakov permitted him to leave it until after Pesach and sell it, because Rabbi Shimon penalized chametz that passed Pesach only where the chametz remains intact. I am astonished at his words, for here, since it is a setting agent, it is considered as though it remains intact. Moreover, Rabbi Shimon permitted only when it was chametz and after Pesach it became mixed, but when one placed it before Pesach it is forbidden to keep, as the Mishnah teaches: 'And these transgress,' etc. See what I wrote in se'if 1. One must say that there was no other setting agent there except the chametz; for if there was also permitted honey yeast there, this would be a case of this and this causing, which is permitted, as written at the end of Yoreh De'ah siman 87. The Radbaz wrote in part 2, siman 487, regarding cheese set with the stomach lining that had been soaked in a chametz vessel, that the cheese is forbidden to eat but permitted to keep, and that its law is like something cooked in a chametz vessel, as written in siman 447, se'if 5. This applies specifically where the stomach lining remained a full day in the chametz vessel, as in Yoreh De'ah siman 87.
