Beer made from wheat and barley, etc., because there is a kezayit within k'dei achilat peras and more (Maharam). There was an incident in Worms: a Jew cooked honey and leavened it with honey lees he had cooked all year; the first honey had soured with beer lees, and what he cooked now was the fourth batch. Rabbenu Yaakov ben R' Yitzchak instructed him to leave it until after Pesach and sell it; the sages of his generation disagreed. His reason: R' Shimon does not penalize where it is not b'eyna. He wrote it is astonishing in his eyes — a davar hama'amid is as if it is b'eyna, and if so the lees that leavened are also as if b'eyna — end of his words. Per my explanation there is nothing astonishing: those beer lees placed in honey water are not for taste; they are not put in for taste but so it will ferment and rise quickly, as Tosafos Avodah Zarah wrote. Also what the Gemara says — this vinegar of beer of gentiles is entirely forbidden — therefore it is not considered as b'eyna and is nullified in sixty. However honey water into which beer lees themselves are placed — it appears one must destroy it, not because of taste but because the lees sink to the bottom of the barrel and do not mix into the honey water; therefore the lees are considered as b'eyna, give taste during Pesach, and are not nullified. And even though they mix below within the lees — nevertheless per what I wrote below on folio 131a that beer is nullified even within Pesach in sixty, it is plainly permitted at least to sell — investigate.
