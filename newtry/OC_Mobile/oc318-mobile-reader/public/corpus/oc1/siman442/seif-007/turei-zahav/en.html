And if not, if it was made to strengthen, etc. This is the correct reading; there is a printing error in the Shulchan Aruch. I find difficulty in all the laws of this siman that say one need not destroy unless there is a kezayis. This fits the reason for bedikah based on the stringency of bal yera'eh, which applies only to a kezayis. But according to Tosafos at the beginning of the chapter, that the concern is lest one eat the chametz, since eating half a measure is forbidden according to the Torah even without lashes, even less than a kezayis should have to be destroyed for that reason. Therefore the obligations mentioned here must refer only to liability for lashes; but then why did the Tur and Shulchan Aruch bring these laws if there is no practical difference? The Rosh's language is also difficult: he says he did not elaborate on the place of kneading because Jews are holy and scrape away every bit of chametz, even what is stuck to the wall. Why make it depend on their holiness, when by law one should destroy it lest he eat it, since even half a measure is Torah-forbidden? One may answer that the cases are ones where eating is not a concern. Still, that makes the bedikah reason difficult. The Hagahos in the name of Riaz, cited by the Beis Yosef, speak of something somewhat fit to eat. One can say that the case is where it lies in a place where it will be trampled by people; nevertheless, if it is a kezayis one must destroy it because of bal yera'eh, even for the moment before it is trampled.
