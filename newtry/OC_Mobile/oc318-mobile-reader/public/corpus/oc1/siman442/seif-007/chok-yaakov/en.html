And if not, he is obligated to destroy it. This means that together there is an olive's bulk, for otherwise he is not obligated to destroy it. This is also implied by the Mechaber in seif 8, and so wrote explicitly Piskei Mahari, siman 164, that less than an olive's bulk does not require destruction. In truth, however, this is a dispute among the poskim, for many poskim hold that even less than an olive's bulk requires destruction. See the Beit Yosef and Bach, who discuss this at length, and see what I wrote above at the end of siman 438, seif katan 10. The Taz also wrote that according to the reason that we are concerned lest he eat it, even less than an olive's bulk requires destruction, even where there is no prohibition of chametz being seen, because of the stringency of the Sages, as Tosafot wrote at the beginning of Pesachim in the name of the Rashba. There is no need to elaborate on these laws, as the Rosh wrote that one need not explain them, for Jews are holy and scrape away all chametz that is found, even that which is stuck to the walls of the house.
