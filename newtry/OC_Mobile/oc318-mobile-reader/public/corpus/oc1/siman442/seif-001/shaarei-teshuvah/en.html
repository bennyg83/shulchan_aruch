See Baer Heitev, and see Magen Avraham's difficulty from the law that si'ur must be burned, and what is written in my Even HaEzer novellae, in the work Mekor Chayim, in Responsa Keter Kehunah, and in Responsa Beis Ephraim siman 30, to establish the Magen Avraham's words correctly. Responsa Pnei Yehoshua, siman 15, discusses one who forgot to destroy, before Pesach, candied sugar coating of almonds, anise, and other seeds, and remembered during Pesach. He writes that when he asked the craftsmen, they said that they cook the sugar in water and coat them with sugar, and flour is needed for this, for otherwise it cannot be baked. If so, it is mei peiros with water and is only chametz nukshah. He agreed with his own view that chametz nukshah may be kept; see there. In my view this requires analysis, for one can say that the flour became chametz before the sugar dissolved, and that the water causes the flour to become chametz without being a mixture of the sugar's moisture. If the sugar had already been cooked in water and afterward the flour is put there, at first glance one can say that it resembles raisin wine explained in siman 462. However, the Chacham Tzvi disagrees there too and holds that it is not mei peiros; see there. See further in siman 462 what is written there regarding mei peiros with water, whether it is nukshah. Also, the view of later authorities is that nukshah must be destroyed rabbinically; if so, when he remembered during Pesach he must destroy it on Pesach, and there is no basis to permit keeping it. If the craftsmen's practice is not to put in the flour until the water is boiling well, there is some room to be lenient because of chalitah; see below, siman 434 s.k. 3. What I wrote in the name of Chok Yaakov, that absorbed taste in vessels does not create a prohibition of having had it on Pesach, see Har HaCarmel siman 14, who permits keeping preserves or fat that stand in chametz pots; see there.
