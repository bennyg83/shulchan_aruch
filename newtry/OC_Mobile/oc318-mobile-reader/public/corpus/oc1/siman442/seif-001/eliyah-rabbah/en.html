[1] [Levush] And some say that one is obligated, etc. The Shulchan Aruch ruled without qualification like this view. In Kesef Mishneh, chapter 4, he agreed that even if there is not a kezayis within kedei achilas peras, since in any event there is a kezayis of chametz there, one transgresses bal yera'eh. The Magen Avraham wrote that if it became mixed with its own kind, such as flour with flour, and it is nullified in the majority, then according to Torah law it is also permitted to keep it. If one transgressed and kept it, and he remembered during Pesach, he must destroy it rabbinically; if he did not destroy it, see the end of siman 447 regarding its law after Pesach. The Olas Shabbos wrote that fat from a dish, where there is no substance but only taste, one transgresses bal yera'eh over it, because taste is like substance according to Torah law. My brother-in-law in Chok Yaakov holds that taste is like substance only regarding eating, not regarding keeping it, though rabbinically it is forbidden to keep; if one transgressed and kept it, it is permitted in benefit after Pesach. The Olas Shabbos further wrote that fat cooked in a ben-yomo pot should initially be forbidden to keep, in my humble opinion, because the name chametz is upon it and it is not a secondary imparting of taste. See siman 447. It appears that he speaks where the ben-yomo utensil had absorbed much chametz, to the point that it is known that there is not sixty in what was cooked in it; in that case Tosafos agrees, as Chok Yaakov wrote in siman 447 s.k. 19, and with this his objection here is resolved. See in Eliyahu Zuta the difficulty I raised against the Beis Yosef on this; I have more on it, but I did not bring it because it is not relevant to the law.
