(א) Ta'aruvot (chametz mixtures). Taz: what he wrote on Beit Yosef — per Beit Yosef's view his words fit one who said that in the Bavli the girsa is Rav Nachman and likewise chametz nokshah, and they emended it — see Maharsha z"l in Tosafos s.v. velu and there: Rav Nachman — why did he not speak like R' Yosi? That we hold possibly etc. — it indicates the old girsa: per Rav Nachman chametz nokshah at any rate is not less severe than ta'aruvot and he brings it in any case; and possibly nokshah is more severe, therefore Rav Nachman is not decided to say it is R' Eliezer; but Rav Yehudah rejects explicitly and says if so etc. — per him ta'aruvot is certainly more severe than nokshah, and it was taught thus — this contradicts Rav Nachman's words who brings it to R' Eliezer in any case from the braita that lists four: paste, beer, vinegar, and olives, and does not list one item and rely on the others in any case — all the more nokshah should have been taught and not rely in any case — learn that for R' Eliezer nokshah has nothing to it; and Rashi s.v. Rav Nachman holds in truth nokshah is stricter and there is no baraita like him, for it is all the more so (see there in Tosafos s.v. velu and Maharsha z"l and understand). And so too per what Rav Nachman wrote likewise — and therefore it is satisfactory that he does not bring from the above braita, for certainly likewise etc. — see Eshel Avraham part 1 Choshen Mishpat 6 and per what was written satisfactory. And one may also say that Rif z"l exempts — ruled like the Rabbis from stam mishnah below 45b: se'or shall be burned and one who eats it — all the more ta'aruvot which is lighter than nokshah and like Rav Nachman and Or Zarua, and Bach z"l answered that therefore in any case the mishnah is R' Meir — for if like Rav Nachman the mishnah does not follow R' Meir for nokshah is stricter — if so why did the braita not list R' Eliezer's nokshah to exclude from karet as Tosafos s.v. velu wrote — learn that nokshah and ta'aruvot are equivalent and the mishnah is like R' Meir and R' Eliezer can be satisfied; and as it appears this is also the Taz's intent, and he added a reason that presumably it is not satisfactory to say three disputes etc., and that which he did not say all these for R' Meir in the lav that he brings the dispute of the Rabbis.

(ב) And what Taz wrote at length in Tur in Beit Yosef. Per Taz it is thus: the Rabbis argue on R' Eliezer in ta'aruvot when there is not a kezayit within k'dei achilat peras, and also there is no practical difference except keviyusa (foam — not considered a taste and not ta'am ke'ikar); per the Rabbis chametz is like other prohibitions and therefore permitted, and d'rabbanan forbidden in eating and retaining is permitted for it is a decree upon a decree per Tur; and likewise a thing that has no keviyusa at all and was nullified in sixty does not revive per Tur and is permitted on Pesach to eat — nevertheless forbidden to mix before Pesach and eat on Pesach for it is like nullifying a prohibition with intent to eat on Pesach; and even though it is still permitted and there is a doubt of four eggs per R' Yehuda 109 — that one increases up to sixty in dry for it was already nullified in majority, but to mix b'eyna not — see Taz siman 80 and what Peri Megadim wrote in Yoreh De'ah 98 in Shach note 22, or chametz is its name upon it and it deals with 447 in Taz note 5 and 426:2 and see there it will be explained; but to mix it to drink on Pesach is permitted per Tur for all say the Rabbis permit retaining — all the more this. But for us all agree what is forbidden d'rabbanan is forbidden to retain, and if he transgressed it is permitted after Pesach for he did not acquire it since he did not transgress bal yera'eh. And what Rosh wrote in a mixture from chadash — likewise d'rabbanan forbidden and requires study. And what he challenged on Mechaber Beit Yosef — see Derishah that Tur wrote explicitly ta'aruvot for R' Meir are only d'rabbanan and similar Bach wrote therefore permitted to retain per Tur — see there; in truth these are strong challenges, and to say what Taz wrote at the end refers to d'rabbanan prohibition applies to nokshah and likewise R' Yosi — and this is what he wrote at the beginning ta'aruvot etc. — all this is not equal to me. And to study Torah I need — Heaven forbid to suspect Mechaber Beit Yosef that he did not see Tur's words at the end and say that certainly what Tur wrote and for the Rabbis it is a mere prohibition, meaning a Torah prohibition, and that is from what he wrote afterward immediately and Rambam ruled like R' Eliezer — therefore as Beit Yosef wrote Tur explains per Rambam z"l that R' Eliezer and the Rabbis argue on paste where there is a kezayit within k'dei achilat peras through lapping and through dipping there is not, and one eats it through lapping — for R' Eliezer we derive from kol machmetz for lashes and the Rabbis exempt from lashes for there is not etc., but there is a Torah prohibition — see Eshel Avraham part 5 foundations of Torah and on this Tur wrote a mere prohibition bal yera'eh, like half a measure that has neither lav nor positive — otherwise it should say forbidden d'rabbanan, and this is required for he wrote and Rambam ruled like R' Eliezer — therefore per Rambam permitted to retain them, and on this Beit Yosef wondered: since there is a Torah prohibition why permitted to retain. And what Tur wrote at the end for the Rabbis d'rabbanan prohibition is per himself likewise he establishes their dispute where there is not a kezayit within k'dei achilat peras at all. And in several places part 5 foundations of Torah the Gaon wrote that per the Rabbis one may say that per the Rabbis it is lighter than per R' Eliezer, and Pesachim 24b concludes all the more so per R' Eliezer — meaning what he placed fat etc. on his wound is all the more so, not like per the Rabbis actual eating is stricter — nevertheless part 8 letter 14 from Magen Avraham and understand.

(ג) Or one may say: even if we hold per R' Eliezer there is no Torah prohibition in paste where there is a kezayit within k'dei achilat peras through lapping — nevertheless its taste and substance are called and considered and not nullified per R' Yosi — see 87:1. And now certainly per Rambam z"l who establishes their dispute where there is a kezayit within k'dei achilat peras through lapping — per the Rabbis there is a Torah prohibition, not like Tur below per Rif who establishes where there is not a kezayit within k'dei achilat peras at all — if so there is Beit Yosef's challenge how Tur wrote for the Rabbis permitted to retain per Rambam z"l — this cannot be justified. And see what I wrote in the introduction at the beginning of the book and in Levush siman 88 for Rambam letter 8 forbidden bal yera'eh — nevertheless all that there is a kezayit within k'dei achilat peras, not as written in the book of ta'aruvot; and that which says leave paste if from a harsh regime etc. — does not refer thus in the conclusion. And what Taz wrote: beer where there is no substance at all — our beer its taste and substance are called — see Eshel Avraham letter 8 only per Rashi and some Poskim call this taste and not substance — see 35 Taz; and we hold liable for karet on our beer on Pesach — see what I wrote in the introduction. And we have thoughts in Temim De'im on Ra'avad z"l in his hasagot on Baal HaMaor siman 245 regarding Rif who ruled like the Rabbis in ta'aruvot for they are many and mishnah at the beginning of the chapter is stam like an individual, and nokshah he rules like stam mishnah at the beginning of the chapter — the content of his intent: whatever many argue in a baraita even though stam in mishnah is like an individual, halachah is not so as we say in general a lone mishnah; not so if in baraita R' Meir and R' Yosi and stam in mishnah like R' Meir — halachah is so, and I did not understand one thing: for it is also that R' Yosi, and if we say as Beit Yosef wrote that in truth halachah is like Rav Nachman for nokshah is stricter — still I did not find that Rif z"l rules explicitly like R' Meir, and behold Tur writes the opposite and mishnah 45b and one who eats it is exempt and stam at the end is preferable — see Magen Avraham note 2 what he wrote there.
