A mixture of chametz. See the Magen Avraham. The central issue is whether a kezayis of chametz must be present within the time of eating a peras, and whether the law of destroying chametz follows the law of eating. In eating, if the chametz is not consumed within that measure, one is not lashed. For keeping it, however, where there is a kezayis of chametz in one's possession, even spread through a mixture, there is room to require destruction. The distinction between like-with-like mixtures and unlike mixtures depends on whether the Torah treats the taste or the substance as present, as explained in Yoreh De'ah siman 98.
The Peri Megadim explains that according to the Tur and those who permit keeping a mixture not forbidden according to the Torah, the permission applies only where the prohibition is not Torah-level eating. Tosafos, however, require destruction even of chametz nukshah rabbinically, because the Sages did not differentiate and because one may come to eat it. Therefore the Shulchan Aruch follows the stringent approach for keeping, even where the eating prohibition is not punishable by kareis or lashes. If it was kept, benefit after Pesach depends on whether bal yera'eh was actually transgressed.
Where actual chametz taste is present in an unlike mixture, taste is like substance according to the Torah. Therefore if the mixture contains chametz taste, it is more severe than a case where chametz was added only to sour or prepare the item. When an ingredient is put in for the very purpose of making the food, it is not treated like an accidental mixture that is nullified in its minority; this is the Rashba's principle regarding drinks made from vinegar and honey, and similar mixtures.
