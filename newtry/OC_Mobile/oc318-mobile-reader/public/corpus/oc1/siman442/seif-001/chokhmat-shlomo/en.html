Se'if 1. A mixture of chametz is transgressed, etc. Note: Regarding the rule that permitted material combines with forbidden material, such as where a minority of permitted substance became mixed with a majority of forbidden substance, whether one is lashed for eating a kezayis made up of the permitted and forbidden together, see what I wrote in my novellae to Niddah 27b, on the question whether two clods of earth could combine to a clod with a large measure. There I proved that even the permitted minority joins the majority to convey impurity to the house, and the same applies to prohibitions. In that sugya the questioner and answerer dispute this; see there carefully. See also the language of the Beis Yosef in this siman, who cites the Maggid Mishneh on the Tur and says that the wording of the book seems to contradict itself from one passage to the other. Before answering, the wording itself is not understandable and the answer as printed is also difficult. It appears that there is a printing error: first it should say, 'Before I answer, I will introduce that the reason the author distinguished,' etc. With this introduction he resolves the first difficulty: although the Tur first brought that keeping is one level lighter than eating, he afterward brings the Rambam's language that one transgresses bal yera'eh on mixtures. At first the Tur is following the commentators who explain 'these transgress' to mean they must be removed from the table, and according to them one may say that keeping is lighter than eating in one respect. But the Rambam explains that 'these transgress' refers to bal yera'eh, and therefore he holds that destruction and eating are exactly parallel; the first wonder is removed. Afterward the text must continue that what may be said to permit the contradiction is the second point: the Rambam ruled like the Sages regarding eating but like Rabbi Eliezer regarding bal yera'eh, so according to him destruction is stricter than eating. This follows the Ramach's explanation: therefore, for bal yera'eh one transgresses even when there is no kezayis within the time of eating a peras, while for eating one is not lashed unless there is such a measure. With this the two contradictions he mentioned are resolved. This appears to be the intent of the Maggid Mishneh; otherwise his words are unintelligible. See Magen Avraham s.k. 1, who wrote that in a mixture of chametz the Kesef Mishneh agreed with the second view. The Beis Yosef explains the matter further: regarding bal yera'eh, the Rambam ruled like Rabbi Eliezer because the anonymous Mishnah follows him, since at least the chametz is in his house; but regarding eating he ruled like the Sages. I was asked: if so, why did the Gemara need to say that the Mishnah follows Rabbi Eliezer? It could be even according to the Sages, for they disagree only regarding eating and concede regarding destruction. I answered that although the beginning of the Mishnah may be explained as referring to bal yera'eh, as Rashi explains, the end which says, 'these are under a warning and there is no kareis for them,' necessarily speaks of eating, as Rashi himself explains. If it referred to bal yera'eh, what novelty is there that there is no kareis, since even actual chametz has no kareis for bal yera'eh? Therefore the end proves that the Mishnah follows Rabbi Eliezer regarding eating as well. Only the beginning can be considered anonymous; the end may be part of Rabbi Eliezer's statement and is not anonymous. Therefore the Rambam could rule like the Sages for eating. Regarding the Magen Avraham's citation of the Rashba that anything made intentionally in that way is not nullified, see what I wrote in my Orach Chayim work from 5601, in the Pesachim sugya 'one may not cook the Pesach offering in water,' where I raised many difficulties with it. I also wrote on it in my later responsum to the community of Stanislav, printed in the omissions to Sefer HaChayim in the pamphlet Minei Mat'amim. See there. The Magen Avraham quotes the Maggid Mishneh that only where there is a kezayis of chametz within the time of eating a peras is one liable, and this is primary; some say that even if one does not transgress by eating it, he must destroy it because there is a kezayis of chametz there, and the Kesef Mishneh agreed. In my opinion the Maggid Mishneh's view can be decided from the Yerushalmi, Beitzah chapter 1, halachah 2, on the Mishnah that Beis Shammai say the measure of se'or is a kezayis. Rabbi Abahu said in the name of Rabbi Yochanan that for both destruction and eating the measure is a kezayis. Rabbi Mana asked Rabbi Chizkiyah whether he heard this from Rav, and he answered that he heard it from Rabbi Abahu; he too said in the name of Rabbi Yochanan that both for destruction and eating the measure is a kezayis, for otherwise the Torah should have taught thirty-seven kareis liabilities. This Yerushalmi is like a sealed book, and Korban HaEidah emended it, but in my view the printed reading should stand. The explanation is that Beis Hillel hold that for se'or the measure is a koseves and for chametz a kezayis. Their reasoning is not a mere analogy from eating to destruction; rather, since all eating measures are a kezayis through halachah leMoshe miSinai, and where the Torah does not write eating one might have thought the measure is even smaller, we learn leniently from eating that destruction too needs a measure, and the principle of 'it is enough for the derived law to be like the source' makes it a kezayis. This is precisely the dispute between the Maggid Mishneh and the Beis Meir: if we learn destruction from eating by a simple 'what we find' analogy, one could say that eating is limited by the time of eating a peras but destruction is not, because the chametz is ultimately in his house. But if it is learned as a kal vachomer, then the derived law cannot be stricter than its source, and just as eating requires a kezayis within the time of eating a peras, so too destruction. The Yerushalmi asks: if we learn destruction from eating, perhaps we should also learn kareis for bal yera'eh. The answer is that the Mishnah itself says these are under a warning but have no kareis. Yet this alone would not prove the point, for one might say that the Mishnah deals where there is no kezayis within the time of eating a peras. Rather, the ending forces us to read the Mishnah as Rabbi Eliezer regarding eating, and then the beginning is anonymous only in part. The matter is clear in my humble opinion. The later discussion of se'or and chametz measures likewise supports that the comparison of destruction to eating is not open-ended; the derived measure is limited to what eating itself would obligate. Therefore, where eating would not be liable because the kezayis is not within the time of eating a peras, destruction according to this analysis is not stricter.
