In Magen Chayim s.k. 8: according to the Tur's view, one must say that the case of si'ur that must be burned is speaking on Yom Tov, etc. The later authorities already found difficulty with this, for on Yom Tov it is forbidden to burn it. They answered that the Tur follows the approach of his brother, Rabbeinu Yechiel, in siman 446, that where he did not nullify it, it is forbidden; and here, since it is si'ur, he cannot nullify it, because it is forbidden in benefit and is not in his domain to nullify. In my humble opinion one can say, based on what Tosafos wrote in Pesachim folio 5, that even though one can benefit from its ashes, etc., nevertheless since he cannot benefit at the beginning of its destruction, it is forbidden. At first glance, even at the beginning of its destruction he can benefit when he places additional permitted wood, so that both this and that cause the result. But the Magen Avraham below in siman 445 wrote that even this-and-that-causes is forbidden in such a case with chametz, and the reason is explained there, because chametz is not nullified even in a thousand. According to this, here, where the discussion is while it is still si'ur and it is nukshah, and nukshah is nullified in sixty as Tosafos wrote in Pesachim folio 105b, nevertheless this applies only when the forbidden cause is a Torah prohibition. Here, where si'ur is only rabbinic, they were not so stringent as to forbid it in benefit. One can also say that perhaps the Mishnah that si'ur must be burned follows the Sages, who permit even with a torch opposite it; even without a permitted cause it is permitted to benefit at the beginning of its destruction regarding nukshah, which is nullified in sixty. Even according to the Shach's reasoning, that where the ashes too are forbidden it would be his improvement in the bread, according to this, for the Sages who hold that he crumbles it, etc., and its ashes are forbidden, it should have been forbidden even according to the Sages who permit with a torch opposite it, since the main reason is that the flame comes from the coal, and according to them the coal is also forbidden. Nevertheless this works well, for the Mishnah teaches that si'ur must be burned, namely like Rabbi Yehudah, who holds that chametz is destroyed by burning, as is explicit there in the Mishnah: 'and one who eats it is exempt; what is si'ur? Like locust horns,' see there, which is according to Rabbi Yehudah. If so, he can properly burn it and benefit from it. Although Tosafos wrote, s.v. 'cooked on coals,' that one must distinguish between ashes and coals, for with coals it is initially forbidden, and if so all the more so a torch opposite it according to the Rabbis would at least initially be forbidden, one can distinguish as I wrote: here, where the subject is si'ur, which itself is not forbidden even in eating except rabbinically, it is properly permitted initially in such a case, and all the more so where there is also a permitted cause.
