[9] For there is, etc., that was nullified, etc. My brother-in-law in Chok Yaakov, siman 447, asked: the Tur rules that a prohibition does not return and become reawakened, and nevertheless he rules like the Rambam's law regarding theriac. Rather, one must say that he agrees by theriac that it returns and becomes reawakened, since there is actual chametz substance inside it and it is not well mixed, as the Rosh Yosef wrote; these are his words. I found in the Perishah that he explicitly explains that the Tur does not hold like the Rambam regarding theriac, and brought it only to prove that there is a distinction between eating and reawakening; these are his words. I further found explicitly like the words of the Rema and Levush in the Maggid Mishneh at the end of chapter 4, that those who permit below permit here as well. So wrote the Kesef Mishneh in the Beis Yosef, siman 447, s.v. 'and what they wrote, that it returns,' etc. So too he himself wrote in the name of the Kol Bo. The Taz also wrote that by theriac it is not nullified because it is a setting agent, but he missed the poskim mentioned above; see siman 447 s.k. 10.
