The Rosh wrote regarding elmodi, used to make muries, into which toasted bread is placed, that some say it is permitted after Pesach because chametz through a mixture that passed over Pesach is permitted, and furthermore it was charred before its time and is permitted for benefit after its time. But some great authorities say that we do not rule this way, for Rabbi Shimon permitted only a mixture that occurred unintentionally, but not to mix it deliberately and keep it. Also, because it was charred one cannot rely on it, for perhaps it was not charred thoroughly, since Pesach was not in his mind when he made it. Furthermore, when they said that if one charred it before its time it is permitted after its time, they meant permitted for benefit, because fully charred bread is not normally eaten. But with muries, where this is the normal way to eat it, it is forbidden. The Magen Avraham wondered at the Tur, whose words imply that he ruled like the stringent view, whereas according to that view it should be forbidden even for benefit. He left it difficult. In my view, the greater difficulty is with his understanding of the Tur, for the Tur rules explicitly in two places against the view that one may not mix and keep it. First, in this siman the Tur writes explicitly that a mixture containing chametz entails no negative commandment, only a D'Rabbanan prohibition, and according to this it is permitted initially to mix it before Pesach in order to keep it, provided there is no chametz taste and no olive's bulk within the time of eating a half-loaf. Second, at the end of siman 447 the Tur explicitly disagrees with the one who forbids mixing initially in order to keep it. Therefore it appears to me that one resolves the other: the Tur's view is to rule like the first opinion, that it is permitted for benefit but forbidden to eat. This is also implied in his abridged rulings on the Rosh at the beginning of the chapter Kol Sha'ah. One might ask that according to the first opinion it should be permitted even for eating, for chametz through a mixture is permitted after Pesach, as the Tur himself rules at the end of siman 447. Perhaps the first opinion itself was uncertain whether this is called chametz through a mixture, or perhaps it has taste that is primary or an olive's bulk within the time of eating a half-loaf. The Shulchan Aruch entirely omitted this law, perhaps because it holds that the Rosh and those with him follow their view that one does not violate chametz being seen with a chametz mixture. But the Mechaber at the beginning of this siman states without qualification the Rambam's language that muries is included in chametz mixtures over which one violates the prohibitions of chametz being seen and found, and this muries is also included. It is therefore like other chametz that passed over Pesach. See what I wrote at the beginning of this siman regarding all these laws.
