For practical halachah one should not rely on the lenient side, because the custom is to be stringent where it is impossible to scrape well, and the Rema's wording also implies that this is considered impossible. The Magen Avraham, in his discussion of a beis se'or, follows his own view: by utensils used constantly the concern is like a beis se'or, and according to the Magen Avraham one should not knead in them. With utensils not used constantly, the custom is to plane or smooth them well, kasher them, and then knead on them for Pesach. If one bakes bread in an oven that was merely swept, libun is required, as in siman 451, Magen Avraham s.k. 28. If, after the fact, one kneaded matzah for Pesach, whether on Pesach or before Pesach, in an unkashered chametz utensil, see Yoreh De'ah 91 and Shach s.k. 3. A wise person showed me the Shach and the Rosh and Tur, from whom it sounds that they did not forbid kneading matzah except because it hastens leavening; this implies that other cold use is permitted after the fact. Still, if one kneaded matzah in an unkashered utensil, although chametz generally needs pressure and sharpness to transfer in this way, perhaps with matzah dough it hastens leavening and is forbidden after the fact. I therefore remain uncertain whether to forbid after the fact. See siman 451, se'if 17, and the sources there. Eliyah Rabbah writes that if one kneaded after hagalah it is permitted, but before hagalah it is not. The Levush leaves the matter requiring investigation; more is explained in siman 451, Magen Avraham s.k. 41 and Taz s.k. 26.
