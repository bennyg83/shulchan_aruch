Although with other prohibitions, anything not fit for human consumption is permitted, chametz is different, for even if it is not fit for human consumption, it is still fit to leaven other doughs with it, as the Ran writes. There is no distinction between chametz and se'or; see Rambam at the end of chapter 1 and Maggid Mishneh, unlike the Raavad. The Rosh wrote: as for almudi from which morais is made, into which they place toasted bread, some say it is permitted after Pesach, because chametz through a mixture over which Pesach passed is permitted in benefit, and furthermore they charred it before its time, and it is permitted in benefit after its time. We do not rule this way, for Rabbi Shimon permitted only where it became mixed unintentionally, but not to mix it and keep it intentionally; see what I wrote in se'if 1. Also, because they charred it we do not say it is permitted, for perhaps they did not char it very thoroughly, since when making it he did not have Pesach in mind. Furthermore, when they said that if one charred it before its time it is permitted after its time, they meant permitted in benefit, not permitted in eating, because one does not normally eat bread that is completely charred. But regarding morais, where it is normal to eat it this way, it is forbidden, end quote. From the end of his language it sounds that it is forbidden in eating but permitted in benefit, and so wrote the Tur. This is difficult, however, for the first view also said only that it is permitted in benefit; if so, what are they disputing? Therefore it seems to me that this is the explanation: the Rosh comes to prove that the permission where one charred it applies only to something unfit for eating. For if you would say that once he charred it, even if it remains somewhat fit for eating, the name chametz is no longer upon it, then it should be permitted in eating after its time. Rather, perforce, he speaks of where he charred it until it is not fit for eating at all. But regarding morais, which is fit for eating, there is no permission on account of charring; it is like other chametz over which Pesach passed, which is forbidden in benefit. This is clear in the explanation of the Rosh, and it is astonishing that the Tur wrote that it is forbidden to eat it, for it should be forbidden even in benefit.
