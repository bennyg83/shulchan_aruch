Permitted. See the Taz. When a dog cannot eat it, it is permitted even for eating; when it is unfit for a person but still fit for a dog, it is rabbinically forbidden to eat. What was written from Yoreh De'ah is that there, if it is not fit for a person, he is exempt, whereas here, when it became unfit for a person, he may still be obligated, as se'or proves, for it can leaven many doughs. If it became unfit even for a dog, it is permitted to eat, because it is no longer fit to leaven or is considered mere earth, and it is also permitted for benefit, as in Pesachim 21b and Rashi there, since its taste and appearance have been nullified. This seems unlike Tosafos there regarding bread that was charred. Bread that molded but a dog can eat it is rabbinically required to be destroyed, because it still looks like chametz and one may confuse it with other chametz. The Mechaber's phrase about keeping it may mean that benefit is permitted. Know that it is possible to have a case where keeping is permitted but benefit is forbidden: chametz nukshah that became completely unfit for a person but is fit for a dog, where there is concern one may eat it, yet benefit remains forbidden. Regarding mummy and similar cases, and the permissibility of keeping, see Magen Avraham s.k. 1 and what will be explained in siman 447, se'if 11.
