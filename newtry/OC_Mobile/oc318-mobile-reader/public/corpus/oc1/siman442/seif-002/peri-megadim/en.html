A doubtful setting agent may be treated as a rabbinic doubt if the whole force of a setting agent is only rabbinic; but one may also say that it is a lack of knowledge rather than a true doubt. Regarding keeping it, there is room to be lenient because the doubt is rabbinic; regarding eating it, there is a double doubt for prohibition: perhaps it is a setting agent, and perhaps the halachah follows those who say that chametz which was nullified before Pesach reawakens on Pesach. In siman 447 this matter is explained further, including whether to combine the doubt that perhaps it reawakens.
And behold. We already discussed in the introduction to the book the law of mixtures and a kezayis within the time of eating a peras. Were I not afraid of the later authorities, I would say a new law regarding rice with chametz. The ruling in Pesachim and the words of the Rema, the Mechaber below in siman 453, the Levush, and Yoreh De'ah siman 324, indicate that the special rule of rice follows the principle of being drawn after the wheat. If chametz wheat flour became mixed with rice flour and dough was made from them, even if there is only a little wheat, the whole dough becomes chametz; one who eats a kezayis of it is lashed and transgresses bal yera'eh, and after Pesach it is forbidden for benefit. The ordinary leniencies do not help. But if rice flour fell onto flour or onto already baked matzah, one may say that it does not cause leavening and there is no rule of being drawn after it. See Magen Avraham in siman 453 and Yoreh De'ah 324. Afterward I saw that the words of the Taz may be explained according to their plain sense, that the Ravyah may hold like Rabbi Eliezer that these mixtures must be destroyed. Still, from the Beis Yosef and Maggid Mishneh it seems that there is no need for the Ravyah's distinction; the first explanation appears more correct.
