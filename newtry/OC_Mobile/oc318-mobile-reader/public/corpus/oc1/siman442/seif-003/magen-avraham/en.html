Garments. It appears to me that nevertheless it is forbidden to spread those garments over the table, for we are concerned that some of them may crumble into the food, since sometimes there is some substance from wheat-starch flour in them. All the more so, it is forbidden on Pesach to put flour into garments that were laundered with wheat starch. Women's scarves that are commonly stiffened with flour should also be removed at the time of eating, lest some of them crumble into the food. A proof is from what is written at the end of siman 447.
And some are stringent, etc. For then the form of the chametz still remains. But if it was made before thirty days, it has already hardened and is permitted, as the Semak wrote regarding a barrel, cited by Terumas HaDeshen. According to this, in light of what the Rema wrote in siman 447 at the end of se'if 5, following the Mordechai, that two months are required, why did he rule here thirty days? One may answer that there the concern is that it may give taste into the wine and he will drink it, whereas here the issue is only the prohibition of keeping it in his house. In winter I saw great scholars being stringent to make women's scarves before thirty days as well; one must say that they held it is considered visible from the outside. It appears to me that if there is less than a kezayis in the window, according to all opinions one need not destroy it, as written at the end of se'if 8 and in siman 447, se'if 5.
