That they laundered them. The Rokeach, siman 246, wrote that garments which one soaks in bran after laundering may be worn.
And likewise papers. The Maggid Mishneh wrote that this is not the scribes' paste mentioned in the Gemara, which is forbidden to keep; that case is before the papers were pasted with it, when it is defective chametz by itself and forbidden to keep. Rather, the Rambam speaks after the papers were pasted with it, when it is like a kneading bowl into which hides were placed. The Tur cited another distinction in the name of the Raavyah: scribes' paste means that the paper itself is improved by it, like tanning, and the chametz is recognizable; but when we use it merely to paste parchment onto a board and it is not recognizable, it is permitted. The Mechaber's words, which follow the Rambam, can be explained according to either of these two approaches, unlike the Bach, who wrote in the name of the Beit Yosef that the Rambam himself disagrees with the Raavyah. The Taz objected to the Raavyah, asking how covering the chametz with paper helps, since the prohibition of chametz being seen applies even if one hides it in a pit. In my view there is no difficulty at all. Scribes' paste is defective chametz, and even when the chametz is recognizable one does not violate chametz being seen, as we concluded above that defective chametz by itself entails no such violation. Nevertheless, it is forbidden to keep it lest one come to eat from it. This concern applies only where the chametz is recognizable. But where parchment is pasted onto a board and the chametz is not recognizable, there is no concern that one will eat from it, since there is no prohibition of chametz being seen. This is clearly the Raavyah's intent. The Taz was troubled only because he understood that according to the Raavyah there is a prohibition of chametz being seen, but in truth this is not so.
It is permitted to keep them. The Magen Avraham wrote that nevertheless it is forbidden to spread such garments, laundered with wheat starch, on the table or to place Pesach flour in them. Also, women's scarves that are commonly finished with flour should be removed during eating, lest some of it separate into the food. The custom, however, is to wear garments laundered with wheat starch during eating without concern, for nothing ordinarily crumbles from them. The Magen Avraham is speaking where they are finished with flour until it is somewhat thick, in a way that commonly crumbles, as is clear from his language.
And some are stringent if it is visible from the outside. This is where it was not done before thirty days. The Toras Chatas concludes there that one who is stringent to smear it with clay on the outside shall be blessed. Those who are meticulous have the practice to remove all dough and repair the windows before Pesach.
