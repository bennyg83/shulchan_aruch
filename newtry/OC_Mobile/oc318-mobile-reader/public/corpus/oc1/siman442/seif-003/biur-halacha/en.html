And there are those who are stringent if visible from outside — from Acharonim' conclusion it appears we adopt l'chatchila the view of those who are stringent except Peri Chadash who wrote the primary view is the first view [and from Taz it also implies for practical halachah the primary view is the first] that there is no concern if visible from outside since it dried and thereby is disqualified from a dog's eating — see there; regarding laundering clothes this is also explained in Tur in the name of Rishonim to permit for this reason; since he included there also the law of glued papers it implies all one reason as Peri Chadash. Bookbinders who glue papers with chametz glue even before Pesach — per the first view certainly permitted for it dries immediately as above; per those who are stringent one may also say permitted for chametz is not recognizable outside; what Terumat HaDeshen wrote per those stringent to make it soft at night — one may say only for windows so it will be absorbed between cracks and not protrude outside; especially per Kaf HaChayim since its essence is chametz nokshah it is forbidden only when seen outside and he did not mention the condition it be soft at night; likewise Chayei Adam did not mention this condition — see there. Apart from all this one may say our matter is unlike windows where ultimately one will scrape chametz from windows; likewise glued papers one may separate — not so bookbinders covered outside and made l'chatchila to remain so — possibly it is in the category of chametz that fell on it from collapse as explained.
