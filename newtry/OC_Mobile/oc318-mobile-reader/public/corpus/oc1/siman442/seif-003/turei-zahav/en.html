And likewise papers, etc. The Rambam's ruling is difficult from the Mishnah's prohibition of scribes' paste. The Ravyah, cited by the Tur, distinguishes between the scribes' paste itself, where the chametz remains recognizable, and our case where parchment or paper is pasted onto a board and the chametz is no longer recognizable. The Maggid Mishneh explains that before the papers are pasted the chametz remains as a hard substance and is forbidden to keep, but once pasted, the form of chametz is gone, similar to hides placed in a vat. The Beis Yosef therefore says that the Ravyah's words are unnecessary. In my humble opinion, the Ravyah's distinction is difficult, for if it is chametz, hiding it beneath paper should not help, since bal yera'eh applies even to hidden chametz. Terumas HaDeshen therefore required that one who pastes windows within thirty days of Pesach should use very soft dough and press it fully between the joints so that nothing protrudes; a stringent person should plaster it with clay. This is the stringency cited by the Rema. But where the dough is made so thin that after drying no substance remains, there is no difference between what is visible outside and what is inside, and this is the correct practice.
