And likewise papers, etc. Rambam ruled thus; challenged: from the voice of all scribes the mishnah forbade — Ravyah answers, whom Tur brings: the voice of all scribes means the paper itself is fixed thus, such as processing where chametz is recognizable; but we who paste parchment on the tablet and it is not recognizable — permitted. Maggid Mishneh explained: the voice of all scribes means before papers were pasted, it was hard chametz in its essence and forbidden to keep; but Rambam deals after papers were pasted, like a vat where hides were placed — he said the form of chametz does not remain; and it is clear that after pasting, permitted to keep — end of his words. Beit Yosef wrote: we need not Ravyah's words. In my humble opinion Ravyah's distinction is wondrous: if there is chametz prohibition, what helps covering with paper — bal yera'eh applies even if hidden in a pit one does not see; Terumat HaDeshen siman 117 wrote: therefore stringent if coming to paste windows within thirty days before Pesach — paste with very soft dough and insert all dough between the adhesives so nothing protrudes — thus Rambam: form of chametz does not remain; stringent to plaster outside with clay — see there; this is those stringent whom Rama brings here, like Ravyah — wondrous for the reason I wrote. And further wonder: in laundered garments with starch water, chametz substance is sometimes visible outside on the garment — rather certainly even if somewhat visible it does not forbid, for nevertheless no chametz is on it at all, as Maggid Mishneh — end of my words. One who wishes to be stringent should remove what the paper covers too — no distinction; and what helps plastering with clay in seif 6 is mere stringency, for without plastering it is permitted there since one cannot dig after it, like chametz on which a collapse fell — not so here where you come to fix a prohibition through plastering, which is unreasonable; nevertheless plastering helps on chametz, for it nullifies its form and taste through the clay; and pasting paper on a window — practice as Terumat HaDeshen wrote first: cover with very thin dough so no substance at all after drying; no distinction between visible outside or inside — correct.
