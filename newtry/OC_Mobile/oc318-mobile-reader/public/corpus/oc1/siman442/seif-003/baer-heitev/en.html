With wheat paste. Magen Avraham writes that garments treated with wheat paste may not be spread upon the table. We are concerned that some material may crumble from them into the food, because they sometimes contain actual particles of flour from the wheat paste. All the more so, one may not place flour during Pesach into garments laundered with wheat paste. Women’s scarves that are customarily stiffened with flour should likewise be removed during eating, lest some of the material crumble into the food. In my youth I saw great authorities who were stringent to prepare women’s scarves more than thirty days before Pesach. Chok Yaakov writes that there is no need for concern, because nothing ordinarily crumbles from them. See there. Visible from the outside. This applies where it was not done more than thirty days before Pesach. Taz writes that the dough should be thin enough that no actual substance remains after it dries. Then, even if it is visible from the outside, there is no concern. If the dough in the window amounts to less than a kezayis, it need not be removed under any circumstances, as stated in seif 6. Magen Avraham. One who is stringent to plaster it externally with clay is praiseworthy.
