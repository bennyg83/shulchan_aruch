(18) That became mixed into it. Meaning, before Pesach. Nevertheless, it is forbidden to eat it, because the chametz becomes significant again on Pesach.
(19) Chametz. This means full chametz that became unfit for eating only because it was mixed into the other substance. If it had been chametz nuksheh before it spoiled, many later authorities permit eating it, even when it became mixed during Pesach.
(20) For any person. It remains fit only for sick people.
(21) Although it may be retained, it is forbidden to eat it. Even though it is not fit for ordinary eating, by eating it he demonstrates that he considers it food.
(22) It is forbidden to eat it. It is nevertheless permitted for benefit when the mixture was made before Pesach, like moldy bread discussed above in seif 3. Regarding medicinal use, see siman 466 and the later authorities there.
(23) Some disagree and permit, etc. They hold that since it was already nullified before Pesach, it does not become significant again. The conclusion, “and this is the ruling,” is astonishing, since the Rama wrote there that the custom to be lenient and regard it as already nullified applies only to a liquid mixed with a liquid, not to a dry substance mixed with another dry substance. I later found that Chemed Moshe had already raised this difficulty.
