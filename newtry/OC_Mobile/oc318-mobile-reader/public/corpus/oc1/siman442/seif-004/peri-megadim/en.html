And so. See the Taz. On the surface, the reason is that the setting agent's power has not ceased during Pesach; or even if its action has now ended, we still say that it reawakens. But one who examines the Shach in Yoreh De'ah 87 will see that in meat and milk, even a permitted setting agent is recognizable and nullification does not apply to something recognizable. Here too, on Pesach, the action of the chametz is recognizable, and before Pesach it was not truly nullified; it was merely permitted then, and now it is forbidden because it functions as a setting agent. Although there are sides to permit, relying on the She'iltos that a setting agent is nullified in sixty for Pesach is not accepted by the Magen Avraham. If the yeast was taken before the sixth hour, the matter requires investigation; if it was taken after midday, the prohibited setting agent acted at the time of prohibition. Regarding great loss, I say neither an absolute prohibition nor an absolute permission; for keeping it there is more room to argue leniently, but even this still requires investigation.
And regarding. Regarding honey-water called mead, if it was cooked in a vessel that had absorbed from beer, one may say it is a secondary transfer of permitted taste; see siman 447, se'if 5, where it is permitted to keep but forbidden to eat. If it was cooked in a vessel used for distilled spirits, one may say that there is no leniency of secondary transfer for sharp foods, for the absorbed taste is sharp; see the Pri To'ar and Kereisi U'Pleisi there, and Magen Avraham siman 447, s.k. 25. With beer yeast, however, it is a setting agent. What the Taz wrote that a Torah doubt is treated stringently should be compared with Magen Avraham s.k. 9, for in practice the law of a setting agent may be rabbinic, as explained in Pri Chadash here. Still, if this is the regular practice, there is no ordinary rabbinic doubt to permit. In the case of third or fourth generation honey, one may say that the beer yeast is mixed with honey yeast and this is a case of this and this causing, perhaps permitted and nullified, especially if it was taken from the yeast before Pesach and certainly before the night of Pesach. See Magen Avraham and Chok Yaakov.
