(s.k. 6) A doctor, etc. If the doctor says, etc., that it will harm him, we listen to the doctor, Radbaz. Although in siman 618, s.k. 3, he wrote in the name of Radbaz as follows: If the sick person says, I need it, even if the doctors say that the food will harm him, we listen to the sick person. That case is different, for there the sick person says that he needs it not as a medicine but as food, because weakness has overcome him, and if he does not eat he will be endangered because of weakness. Even if the doctor says that he will not become endangered from weakness if he does not eat, and on the contrary, if he eats the food will harm him, in this we listen to the sick person. For if the doctor had not said that the food would harm him, but only said that even if he does not eat he will not be endangered because of weakness, we would feed him based on the words of the sick person, for the heart knows the bitterness of its soul, and we believe the sick person more than the doctor. If so, even when the doctor says that the food will harm him, nevertheless we feed him, since we believe the sick person's statement that if he does not eat he will be endangered because of weakness. Why should we be concerned for the future danger caused by eating according to the doctor's words and not be concerned for the danger that is before us because of the weakness? This is unlike here, where the sick person says he needs a certain medicine that will heal him and the doctor says it will harm him. The words of the sick person and the words of the doctor are both about the future, and in this we listen to the doctor. We do not say that the heart knows the bitterness of its soul, because this depends on medical wisdom, and the doctor's words are also in the form of refraining from action, and both matters concern the future.
