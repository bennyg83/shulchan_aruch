The Magen Avraham wonders that according to the first view it should also be prohibited on Yom Tov, as the Milchamot writes that one does not permit a rabbinic prohibition merely because of desire for food. [38] [Levush] That he has no, etc. Meaning, there is no excessive pain, as stated by Shibolei HaLeket.
[39] Mastic, etc. The Ran explains this as resin, in Arabic. The Semag writes that it is the substance called mastik in the language of non-Jews. In siman 216, seif 3, it is described as resin from which pitch is made.
[40] One rubs with it, etc. So write the Tur and Shulchan Aruch. The Bach is astonished, because Tosafot, the Rosh, the Rif at the end of Perek Shemoneh Sheratzim, and the Rambam all say that one may not rub the teeth with medicine. Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah writes that perhaps the Tur's text in the Tosefta read 'one may not rub with it.' This is incorrect, because in the Rosh's remazim the wording is likewise as before. Perhaps the Tur's intention in what he writes at the end of the siman refers to mastic, as I wrote in the name of the Semag. The main point, however, is that there is a scribal error in the Tur and it should read 'with medicine.' Rabbenu Yerucham, page 79, writes that one may not rub his teeth with beer. This requires study. Perhaps there too is a scribal error and it should read 'with medicine.' In the Tosefta, chapter 13, I saw that a person may not rub dry medicine on his teeth. So too writes the Rokeach, siman 106.
[41] [Levush] And if he intends, etc. The same law applies to chewing mastic, as is explicit in the Tosefta there.
