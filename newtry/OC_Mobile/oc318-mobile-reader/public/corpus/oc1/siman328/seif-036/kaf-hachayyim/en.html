36) There: Or with chills. The term derives from the verse, 'the hair of my flesh stood on end.' This does not refer to common fever, which is known not to endanger life and begins with cold and ends with heat. It refers to heat and cold striking simultaneously. Taz, paragraph 2; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 8; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 7; Chayei Adam, klal 68, paragraph 5. All the needs of one with common fever may nevertheless be performed through a non-Jew. Chayei Adam there. Common tertian and quotidian fevers are not dangerous, but quartan fever is dangerous. Mor U'Ketziah states this based on the conditions in his locale. In truth, if a physician says danger exists and the case differs from ordinary common fevers, Shabbos is desecrated. This is not an absolute rule and is obvious. Machazik Berachah, paragraph 2. An expert is required to determine whether a fever is extremely hot before Shabbos may be desecrated. Rabbi Yosef Molcho, responsa manuscript, siman 119; Birkei Yosef, paragraph 3.
