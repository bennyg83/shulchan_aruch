(s.k. 22) Like bathing, etc. — that it is wine — these are Rashi's words on soaking cucumbers, who wrote: a person soaks cucumbers in water, etc., and puts on his eyes, etc.; one who sees them thinks it is merely bathing, for he holds it is wine end of his words; and since he needed observers to think it is wine and did not say even if they know the truth it is water — nevertheless they would say it is merely bathing — perforce it must be said that water's way is not to wash via cucumbers but to wash directly — namely putting water on his hand and washing his eyes with it; unlike wine which is expensive — not the way to wash thus, for much wine would be wasted when poured on the hand and then washing with it; unlike via cucumber — a little wine that soaked suffices, placed on the eyes and washed with it; therefore if observers knew it was water they would not attribute it to bathing and would know it is for healing; nevertheless proved from Rashi's words: even though the truth is water — nevertheless observers would attribute it to wine; per this what Magen Avraham wrote in s.k. 18: nowadays when there is no way to wash with wine — forbidden — nevertheless it would be permitted and we say observers attribute it to water even though the truth is wine; and it must also be said that in placing on the eye this is not water's way of bathing but he pours abundantly; and see Sefer Teshuvah VeHanhagah:
