[14] [Levush] Lest the patient become disgusted, etc. For this reason the Bach rules that if the patient needs warm wine, a Jew should warm it rather than have a non-Jew do so. The Magen Avraham writes in the name of the Darkei Moshe that because the prohibition is very slight, the patient will not be disgusted by it. In a responsum, the Rama even permits a patient who is not dangerously ill to drink ordinary non-Jewish wine. See Yoreh Deah siman 123 and the end of siman 155, where it is possible without libation concern. The Radbaz, siman 130, writes that if the patient is overseeing his own needs, such as where no one else is present, and he knows that he will not be disgusted by non-kosher meat, it is preferable that he eat non-kosher meat or tell a non-Jew to slaughter improperly rather than slaughter with his own hand, because here the prohibition is a negative commandment whereas there the prohibition carries stoning.
[15] Some permit even where there is no, etc. The Taz writes at length in astonishment at the Rosh and Tur for being uncertain about this and at the Rashba for ruling leniently. In Beitzah 22, Ameimar applied eye medicine through a non-Jew on Shabbos, and the Gemara asks that he was clearly assisting by closing and opening his eye. It answers that assistance has no substance. This indicates that through a Jew it would be prohibited. The Taz concludes that he does not know how to resolve this until someone whose understanding is complete instructs him. I am astonished at this, for the Ran in Perek Shemoneh Sheratzim, who represents the second view, in fact brings this Gemara as proof to prohibit where there is no danger to a limb. It therefore appears to me that the Rosh discusses illness of the entire body. In an illness limited to a limb where even the limb is not endangered, he agrees that treatment through a Jew is prohibited. Only regarding instructing a non-Jew is such a case treated like other illness. Examine carefully. It also appears to me, by way of introduction, that the Taz's repeated question in seif katan 11 about the Beit Yosef's citation of a responsum of the Rashba, which seems to contradict the Rashba cited by the Maggid Mishneh who rules like the Ramban, can be resolved. The Ramban's view is the opinion that where there is no danger to a limb, treatment may be performed with a shinui. In my humble opinion, the Rashba's responsum likewise concerns a melachah performed with a shinui, in accordance with the Ramban. The Rosh's uncertainty also appears to me to concern such a case. Everything is thereby resolved, with Heaven's help. It further appears to me that the Ran also holds this way, because otherwise there is a difficulty. In Perek Shemoneh Sheratzim he writes that the Ramban stated this in Torat HaAdam, and I saw there explicitly that treatment with a shinui is permitted even where no limb is endangered. The Ran himself also writes this in Perek Chaviyot in the Ramban's name. He adds there another answer, that when treatment through a non-Jew is impossible, treatment through a Jew is permitted. He understands the Rosh's uncertainty to concern which of these two answers should be followed. Examine carefully. Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah writes at length in astonishment based on a responsum of the Rashba cited by the Radbaz in siman 2. In my humble opinion there is no difficulty for one who examines Ramban responsum 124, which implies that he said only that rabbinic prohibitions cannot be analogized to the prohibition of touching one's wife when she is a niddah, for one has only what the Sages expressly prohibited. It is entirely possible that he would permit the Jew himself to act on Shabbos.
[16] [Levush] And this view is primary, etc. The Levush miscounted, because the Shulchan Aruch writes that the third view appears correct, whereas this is actually the fourth view, as stated by Malbushei Yom Tov. I saw that the Bach also understood as the Levush did. The Magen Avraham and Taz, however, explain as Malbushei Yom Tov. According to what I explained above, it is obvious that the halachah follows the Ramban in making this distinction regarding a shinui. I also saw that Tzeidah LaDerech rules this way, and this also appears to be the Tur's conclusion.
