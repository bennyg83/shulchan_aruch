(א) (s.k. 33) This is permitted, etc. — and impossible to come, etc. — or it is davar she-eino mitkaven; so it appears to me it should read: in Maggid Mishneh it was not mentioned davar she-eino mitkaven; and Magen Avraham explained from his own reasoning:

(ב) (s.k. 33) And those, etc. — or perhaps, etc. — and as written siman 314 seif 3: if a hole in barrels closed by the faucet — permitted to pierce on erev Shabbat:

(ג) (s.k. 33) And the same, etc. — requires study likewise; meaning if the hole closed somewhat; but from his citation of siman 314 it implies even if closed completely, since it was already open; however per some opinions there it was permitted only in earthenware barrels but not wood, for the faucet closes the hole forcefully:

(ד) (s.k. 33) And from Shulchan Aruch's language it implies, etc. — for he wrote "one who pierces," etc. "in order to widen the opening," etc. — and did not write "in order to pierce it" — implying even open and he comes to widen it — nevertheless forbidden:

(ה) (s.k. 33) And a poultice is permitted, etc. — as written siman 327 that poultice on the fontanel is only to guard it:

(ו) (s.k. 33) And in Sefer Ra'avan it implies forbidden, etc. — because it draws discharge through the poultice; and as written afterward in name of Sefer HaZikhronot:

(ז) (s.k. 33) And also he has pain; see siman 317 siman 61 where Rama wrote: in a place of pain permitted to untie even two knots one upon the other, for it is only d'rabbanan prohibition; but Magen Avraham there forbids in name of Sefer HaZikhronot:

(ח) (s.k. 33) Because it is deposited and entrusted — meaning the blood is deposited and enclosed and stands independently and is not absorbed in the flesh; therefore even if blood comes out — there is no chibur concern:

(ט) (s.k. 33) See above seif 48 what is written:

(י) (s.k. 33) And it appears to me here is different, etc. — meaning even though Magen Avraham wrote there that from Rashi's words in Eruvin it implies even placing on a wound something that draws discharge — nevertheless permitted, for he does nothing — here is different since there is already a hole — meaning it is as if the person does an act, for immediately upon placing he draws blood; and so he explained his words in Sefer Teshuvah VeHanhagah; except I did not understand what he wrote afterward "and all the more he should not place zinc oxide," etc.; and also below what is written in seif 48 — why forbid zinc oxide; therefore to my humble opinion Magen Avraham's intents are the reverse of what he wrote on seif 48, etc. "here is different," etc. — meaning at first glance there is proof for Sefer HaZikhronot to forbid from what Maggid Mishneh chapter 1 and Rashi wrote — they permitted only with a compress that by nature does not draw and extract blood — if so here too forbidden; and also if Rashi's view is not so — nevertheless Maggid Mishneh's view to forbid something that draws — here too on this Magen Avraham wrote "here is different" — one may say even for Maggid Mishneh chapter 1 it is permitted since there is already a hole — lighter; but nevertheless Magen Avraham does not disagree with Sefer HaZikhronot and even with a hole forbidden; and so he wrote "and all the more he should not place zinc oxide," etc.:

(כ) (s.k. 33) And it appears forbidden to suck, etc. — and one may say regarding discharge that it is deposited and entrusted; but blood that comes through sucking connects and is absorbed in the flesh and makes chibur in drawing blood:
