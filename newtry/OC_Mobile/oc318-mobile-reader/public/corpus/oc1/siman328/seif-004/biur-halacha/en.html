(א) All that is customary, etc. — this is the language of Ramban in Terumat HaDeshen; and Maggid Mishneh wrote that from this it is implied that even though there is no danger in withholding that thing, etc.; and in my humble opinion requires study; for it appears many poskim disagree with this reasoning — behold Rashi wrote "a sick person who is not in danger — if they do not do this healing for him he will not die," etc., meaning he is endangered regarding the remedy; and Beit Yosef already brought this and so explained further in Yoma chapter 4 82b s.v. "there is no doubt," etc., see there; and so explained further s.v. "we open," see there; and so Rosh in Alfasi in several places as Rashi explained; and further proof from the mishnah: "for a sick person we feed him based on experts, and without assessment we do not feed" — as explained in Tosafos Mefinin 128b s.v. "kama" and in Meiri there and in Tosafos Yeshanim Yoma there; and it is explained in several Rishonim that the mishnah deals with a sick person who has danger — therefore assessment is needed whether fasting endangers him, even though on a weekday we would give him to taste from fine foods for a sick person; nevertheless on Yom Kippur we require assessment; and so Rashi explained in the mishnah there "based on experts — if they say he is not endangered if he does not eat"; and automatically the same applies regarding desecrating Shabbat; and further that R' Yehuda's view is we do not feed him until we are uncertain lest he die; and even for Rabbenu Tam who argues on this he also wrote: even though he does not say he will die, nevertheless we are concerned lest he faint and be endangered, and it does not depend at all on his statement — for many times he does not know if he is endangered; therefore even according to him it is only on account of safek danger, not because it is good and beneficial for him; and so is the view of Tosafos Yeshanim s.v. "choleh," see there; and so Tosafos in chapter Mefinin and so Meiri; and thus: "a woman in childbirth for whom they desecrate — not only for what is done for healing but even for what is done to settle her mind, even though in the last chapter of Yoma they were stringent not to feed except based on an expert — nevertheless here from her fear she can be endangered," see there; and so it appears the view of Rashba in responsum 214 cited above — for he wrote "the endangered person is endangered regarding eating," etc., "and we feed him on Yom Kippur," see there — therefore only because of the proof he brought from chapter Cheresh that every endangered person is endangered regarding eating too; but if not for this it would not be difficult for him to feed him on Yom Kippur; and therefore we desecrate only to remove danger; and also from Ramban's own words there is not so much proof — for what he wrote "they do for him all that is customary to do for him on a weekday from foods and remedies" — it is reasonable his intent is not even for something that does not remove danger, but since it deals where there is no expert and they do not know what to do — therefore he said they should do all things done on a weekday to remove danger according to their understanding; but otherwise even according to their understanding they may not do things which even on a weekday they themselves know are not to remove danger; and what he said "from foods and remedies" means light foods customary to give so a sick person not weaken — for sometimes withholding this too will be a cause to strengthen his illness and he can come to danger; and I will bring proof for our words from Ramban's own words in Terumat HaDeshen: "It is needed only for a blind person — lest you say since he does not see it is forbidden — it teaches us to settle the mind of a mere sick person we desecrate Shabbat in something he is endangered in," see there — behold it is proven that settling the mind of a living person is only because Chazal know he is endangered in it; and so it appears further in Meiri chapter 8 of Sheratzim and in Rosh who wrote "we do not say we will not warm until the doctors say with certainty that if we warm him he will be healed; but if they had doubt whether he would be healed we would not warm him — it teaches us," see there — behold the braita did not limit except safek healing; but where there is no doubt at all the patient will be healed thereby — only that it is good and beneficial for him — certainly we do not desecrate; and so it appears from the plain sugya in chapter Ein Mefinin that it says there "they make a fire for an animal — he thought for an animal yes, for a sick person no," even though it is certainly good for the sick person — nevertheless he is not endangered for this until the gemara concludes that for a sick person too there is concern of danger, see there; and especially according to Rambam that even in the conclusion we do not desecrate for a sick person but only for an animal where cold greatly harms her — except that Maggid Mishneh wrote it implies for a sick person who has danger even something not needed for pikuach nefesh we do; and he established the sugya as dealing with a sick person who has no danger; however this view is difficult — for if so for most poskim who hold the conclusion of the gemara is that for a sick person too we make a fire — a novelty arises that for a sick person who has no danger we make a fire through a Jew, and this is unheard of from any posek; and so in Rambam's chiddushim cited in Sefer Shemen Rokeach that Rambam's view is we do not make a fire for a sick person even if there is danger, and he is Machber HaBatim — see there; and so is explained in Shiltei HaGibborim chapter Mefinin — see there. And behold aside from Maggid Mishneh's view I found several poskim who imply they hold like his words: Tashbetz vol. 1 siman 54 — "and even kindling and burning which is full melacha they do for a sick person who has danger even though the primary healing is not in lighting the lamp," etc., "from this it appears that even for settling the mind of a sick person even if he does not need it for healing but only settling his mind — and the same for other melachot by which the sick person's mind is settled that they do on Shabbat," see there — it appears he holds like Maggid Mishneh; and I found further in Meiri end of Yoma in the words beginning "whether to give him to drink or for healing" who wrote "even though this washing for strengthening his limbs has no need for haste and it would seem we should wait so Shabbat not be desecrated — nevertheless he hastens for strengthening his limbs and does not delay any beneficial matter to complete the sick person's healing," see there — from this too it appears he holds like Maggid Mishneh; indeed requires study in his view — for we already brought him in the view of those who forbid, as is evident in his words in chapter Ein Mefinin; and it is possible strengthening his limbs too sometimes removes him from a state of danger. And behold I found in Or Zarua in laws of a woman who gave birth a dispute on this matter: R' Gershom permitted for a woman who gave birth seven days before Pesach to heat hot water for her on Pesach night, and R' Eliezer forbade and said the reasoning of this halacha is not in bathing — for desecrating Shabbat was permitted only in matters that have pikuach nefesh, see there where he expanded; and it appears from this that R' Gershom's reasoning is like Maggid Mishneh's view — but there is no proof, for it is possible he holds that in bathing too there is pikuach nefesh [and so is the view of several Rishonim — see below siman 316]. We return to our subject — it is proven from these poskim cited above, namely Rashi, Rabbenu Tam, R' Yehuda, Tosafos Yeshanim Yoma, Tosafos chapter Mefinin, Meiri there, Rashba in responsum, and Or Zarua in name of R' Eliezer, that wherever he is not endangered for this thing we do not desecrate; and so is the implication of Rosh who copied as Rashi explained in all places; and also from what he wrote in chapter 8 of Sheratzim s.v. "lest he be healed" as we wrote above; and also Ramban's view as we leaned appears he holds thus; and R' Gershom's words are not explicitly clear that he argues on this, as above; and we have no room to be lenient except the view of Maggid Mishneh and the implication of Tashbetz and the implication of Meiri Yoma [and even though we must divide his words so he not contradict himself, as above] — therefore certainly it would be proper to be stringent in Torah prohibitions, especially when not in a place of danger; and also in Rekanati siman 104 he wrote: "Some say that what we say regarding one who is endangered overrides Shabbat — this is things that if they are not done he will come to great deterioration; but in things that do not have much need we do not desecrate Shabbat; and some permit — and the words of those who forbid appear correct," see there; however in Radbaz vol. 4 siman 130 he wrote he was asked on this and answered briefly that there are views on this and his view is to be lenient like those who permit, and he did not bring proof on this but only plain reasoning, see there; and in my humble opinion the essential view is as we wrote and as Rekanati decided. And know further that even per the view of those who forbid cited above — this is specifically in a matter where it is clear to us his illness will not worsen by withholding this thing; but in a matter where there is concern that by withholding this thing he will weaken and his illness worsen — we desecrate Shabbat for him; and similar to what Rabbenu Tam wrote regarding Yom Kippur, as above; and so is ruled below at beginning of siman 618.
