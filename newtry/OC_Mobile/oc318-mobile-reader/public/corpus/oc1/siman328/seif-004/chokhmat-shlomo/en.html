There seif 46, they place a vessel on the eye to cool, etc. NB: Behold I was asked regarding one struck in his eyes with the illness of cataracts called Schwarz in foreign language, until all doctors despaired of finding a remedy for him, only in one city of gentiles they wish to accept him to the gentiles' hospital to find healing so his eyesight not be ruined entirely, that he still can walk on his own at least, but he must eat there treifot — what is his law, whether permitted to do so or not. And this I answered: at first glance it would seem to permit simply, for it is explicit in gemara and poskim and in this siman seif 47 that eye illness is danger to the whole body, for the moisture of the eye depends on the heart, and permitted to desecrate Shabbat for it — and all the more so other prohibitions that were permitted or pushed aside regarding him. But as we are precise, there is no proof from this, for there it deals with an eye that rebelled — that properly is danger to the whole body; but this illness of cataracts is not danger to the whole body, only judged as danger to one limb alone. And it appears from all poskim that d'oraisa melacha we do not push aside in any case for danger to a limb, and one should not distinguish between danger to another limb and danger to the eye, as proven in gemara that it answers "moisture of the eye depends on the heart" — implying otherwise we would not push aside Shabbat in d'oraisa melacha for it, and the same for other d'oraisa prohibitions. However as we are precise, it seems to distinguish between one eye and two eyes: since for one reason pikuach nefesh overrides Shabbat from the principle it is better to desecrate one Shabbat so he observe many Shabbats — thus according to R' Yehuda a blind person is exempt from all mitzvot, and according to R' Yehudai brought in Beit Yosef laws of Pesach siman 473 we rule like R' Yehuda, and though most poskim disagree, nevertheless we do not follow pikuach nefesh against the majority; thus one must be concerned for Rabbeinu Yerucham that a blind person is exempt, etc.; and thus one could say regarding danger to another limb where there is no nullification of Torah study and mitzvot; and likewise in gemara regarding a rebellious eye it deals with one eye — where even if he has no eye he remains obligated in mitzvot, therefore it is judged only as danger to a limb and requires the reason that it is danger to the whole body. But in both his eyes where he will be completely blind and exempt from all mitzvot — one could say certainly better to desecrate one Shabbat to observe many Shabbats. And even if we say there is none who is concerned for Rabbeinu Yerucham, nevertheless it appears since through blindness of eyes he will be nullified from Torah study and reading the Torah — and Chazal said Torah study is greater than action — thus if for action it is better to desecrate one Shabbat so he observe many Shabbats, all the more so for Torah study it is so. Therefore where through danger to that limb there will be nullification from Torah study — certainly permitted to desecrate one Shabbat so he observe many Shabbats, and all the more so in blindness of eyes where certainly he will be nullified from Torah study and many mitzvot will be nullified thereby — it appears certainly permitted to transgress a Torah prohibition; and Chazal said Torah study is greater than saving lives, and saving lives certainly pushes aside all Torah prohibitions; examine. And though my heart was not filled to rely on this alone, nevertheless it appears where he can be careful to eat less and less than the measure — in this we may rely on the lenient ones in this siman regarding danger to a limb to do through a Jew even a matter apt to come to d'oraisa prohibition; and all the more so permitted to transgress less and less than the measure, for Maharalnach wrote and Shach brought Yoreh De'ah siman 239 that chatzi shiur is lighter than a rabbinic prohibition, for on a rabbinic negative commandment the oath does not apply, and on chatzi shiur the oath applies; and thus if permitted to transgress a rabbinic prohibition through a Jew on account of danger to a limb — all the more so permitted to transgress chatzi shiur on account of danger to a limb. Therefore he should eat d'oraisa prohibitions less and less than the measure and minimize where possible, and rabbinic prohibitions he may eat much and need not minimize them, for rabbinic prohibitions are certainly permitted in danger to a limb; so it appears in my humble opinion correct for the law; may Hashem save us from errors and teach us His Torah's wonders, amen.
