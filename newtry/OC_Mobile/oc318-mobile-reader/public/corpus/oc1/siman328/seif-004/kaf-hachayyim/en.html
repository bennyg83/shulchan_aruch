22) [Seif 4] An internal wound requires no assessment. This means that one need not estimate whether the patient will become endangered if treatment is not performed that day. Rather, treatment is provided as a matter of course. See below, paragraph 37.
23) There: An internal wound, etc. Responsa Chasam Sofer, Yoreh De'ah, siman 76, was asked whether epilepsy is dangerous. He cites Taz at the end of Yoreh De'ah, siman 84, in the name of Maharshal from a responsum of Rabbi Yitzchak, who writes that it is like an internal wound and is dangerous. Although Responsa Rosh, klal 42, appears to prove that it is not considered danger to life, we rule leniently in matters of danger to life. One may rely on Maharshal in the name of Rabbi Yitzchak to feed the patient forbidden food if the treatment is established; see there. Pischei Teshuvah there, paragraph 13, cites him. See Knesses HaGedolah there, Hagahos Beis Yosef, paragraph 113, who cites this responsum of Rabbi Yitzchak in the name of several authorities and states that at times the patient is endangered by falling into fire or water; see there. Zivchei Tzedek, siman 84, paragraph 132, cites him; see there. At first glance, the same should apply to desecrating Shabbos to perform an established treatment. According to the end of this seif, however, when it is known that the illness can wait and does not require desecration, Shabbos may not be desecrated. Epilepsy is known to be an illness that can wait. The practical difference may arise when it first appears and physicians say that if immediate treatment is not given, the illness may worsen and become established in the body; it is possible to be lenient.
24) There: Everything ordinarily done for him is performed, etc. This includes foods and medicines beneficial to the patient. Beis Yosef in the name of Ramban. This implies that Shabbos is desecrated for all his needs even when withholding the particular item would not endanger him. Beis Yosef in the name of Maggid Mishneh; Magen Avraham, paragraph 4. In the name of Rashi, however, Beis Yosef writes that Shabbos may be desecrated for a dangerously ill person only for something whose omission could cause his death. Something whose omission would not endanger his life may not be performed through a Jew; see there. Magen Avraham, paragraph 17, cites this. See Biur Halachah, which discusses the matter at length and cites several authorities who agree with Rashi. It therefore writes that the proper course is to be stringent regarding a Torah prohibition; see there. See also below, paragraph 117.
25) There: But when they know, etc. Certainly, if the patient or physician says it is unnecessary, Shabbos is not desecrated. Beis Yosef in the name of Ramban. See below, paragraph 37.
26) There: And they recognize that illness, etc. Meaning they clearly recognize that the illness will not intensify further if they wait until night, after Shabbos. Mishna Berurah, ot 15.
27) If a dangerously ill person suddenly became endangered and many say that worms caused it, and the traditional remedy called ausgiessen lead, an act incurring liability to bring a sin offering, should be performed, the person who pours the lead is believed if he says he has cured patients two or three times. Even if the treatment has also failed several times, since it has succeeded several times and people ordinarily perform this remedy for the illness during the week, there is no reason to be stringent. Responsa Tzemach Tzedek, volume 1, siman 38; Pis'chei Olam, paragraph 4. See above, siman 301, seif 26.
