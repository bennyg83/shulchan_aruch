Except for medicinal purposes. See Eliyah Rabbah, who writes regarding the hot springs of Tiberias that in places where people bathe in them only for medicinal purposes, one may not remain in them even without intending treatment. According to this, soaking water should certainly be prohibited if one lingers in it, even without medicinal intent. This is difficult, because the Mishnah states that one may drink palm water to quench thirst. As we explained above in seif 37, when a person has no medicinal intent he may consume even substances ordinarily used as medicine. The same should apply here.
The Tosefta in chapter 13 likewise states: "A person may bathe in the waters of Tiberias, but not in the waters of Geder or in soaking water. When does this apply? When he intends treatment. If, however, he enters in order to ascend from impurity to purity, it is permitted." The conclusion of our Gemara is that the prohibition applies specifically where he lingers, yet the Tosefta still says it applies only when he intends treatment. Its concluding permission apparently applies even if he lingers while immersing.
Perhaps the Tosefta discusses a healthy person and therefore requires medicinal intent, meaning intent to strengthen his constitution. Magen Avraham, seif katan 43, appears to imply this. Eliyah Rabbah may discuss a person who has an ailment, and therefore forbids it even without medicinal intent, following his own view in seif katan 42 that when one has any illness, even eating for hunger is forbidden. See what we wrote in Biur Halacha to seif 37.
Know that the Yerushalmi, chapter 14, halachah 3, states regarding this baraisa in the name of Rabbi Shmuel, brother of Rabbi Berachyah, that it is permitted only if he does not linger. This contradicts the conclusion of the Bavli, Shabbos 109b. See there.
Rambam, chapter 21 of the laws of Shabbos, halachah 29, writes that one may not bathe in soaking water and the like because all these cause discomfort, whereas the verse states, "You shall call Shabbos a delight." According to Rambam, therefore, it is certainly forbidden even without medicinal intent, contrary to this Tosefta. All this requires study.
