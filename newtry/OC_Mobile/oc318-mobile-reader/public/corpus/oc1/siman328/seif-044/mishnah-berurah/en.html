(137) They bathe, etc. Meaning, even though his intent is for healing, since healthy people bathe there it is permitted. However, at a time when people do not ordinarily bathe in the hot springs of Tiberias except for healing, it is forbidden on Shabbos if he remains there. Magen Avraham and other later authorities.
(138) Salty. Meaning that for this reason they are somewhat beneficial for healing. Know that even if he has sores on his head it is also permitted. This is the consensus of the poskim.
(139) And in soaking water. Meaning water in which flax is soaked. The same applies to the Sea of Sodom.
(140) Because they are repulsive. Rambam states: One may not bathe in water that causes diarrhea, meaning water that chills the body and brings on diarrhea, nor in mud in which one sinks, etc., because all these cause distress, and Scripture states, "You shall call Shabbos a delight." End quote. Semag writes likewise. Accordingly, it is forbidden to drink a purgative beverage, for there is no greater distress than this. Magen Avraham. This applies even if he does not intend it for healing but drinks it for some other reason. If he intended it for healing, it would in any event be forbidden, unless he has reached the status of a sick person not in mortal danger, in which case it is permitted, as stated above in seif katan 37.
(141) Specifically when he remains. For it is evident that he is doing so for healing, since the water is murky and foul and he would not remain there for pleasure.
(142) As one who cools himself. Even though the water is murky, we say that he did not find better water and that his intent is merely to cool himself. Even if he has sores on his head, it is also permitted. Gemara.
