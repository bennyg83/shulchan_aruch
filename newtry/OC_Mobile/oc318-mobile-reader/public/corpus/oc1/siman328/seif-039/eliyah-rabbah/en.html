[42] [Levush] And he has no illness at all, etc. This implies that if he has any illness, it is prohibited even if he eats it because he is hungry.
[43] [Levush] Because he is not doing it for healing. This implies that if he does it for healing, it is prohibited even though he is healthy. So too writes the Magen Avraham.
[44] [Levush] Who has become bedridden, etc. So writes the Rama. The Taz understood that even through a Jew it is permitted and expressed astonishment. His words are themselves astonishing, for what is his source? The Bach explicitly explains that it is through a non-Jew. As a matter of practical law, the Taz rules like the Bach to prohibit even through a non-Jew, because this is worse than mere assistance. Nachalat Tzvi and the Magen Avraham are lenient, and so too writes the Maggid Mishneh in chapter 21. They even permit through a Jew, as stated in seif 17. Nevertheless, there it was explained that this applies specifically when the melachah is performed with a shinui.
[45] [Levush] For this is not, etc. This is because he has no wound in his throat, as stated by the Mordechai.
