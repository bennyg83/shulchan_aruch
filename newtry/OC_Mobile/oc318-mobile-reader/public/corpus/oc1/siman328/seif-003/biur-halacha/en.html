(א) And specifically when it was damaged, etc. But mere discomforts, etc. — behold, this law is from Rashba in name of R' Yehuda and is cited in Maggid Mishneh there; and it is implied from the conclusion of his language there that even where it hurts him greatly inside in the hollow of the body they are not yet in the category of a wound of the hollow as long as one of the internal organs was not damaged; however in Biur HaGra from what he cited the Mechaber on their words from what they said in the gemara "he is concerned lest..." — it is implied where it hurts him greatly they desecrate; and perhaps one can reject that regarding teeth, which are difficult, and the name "wound" is not so applicable to them; since it hurts him greatly he leaves the category of mere discomfort and his law is like a wound of the hollow — unlike other internal organs, as long as no wound or boil was made there he is still in the category of mere discomfort — requires study. And nevertheless it is possible to say that if it hurts him greatly in one of the internal organs and he is uncertain lest something was damaged there and there is no doctor in the city to ask — it is permitted to desecrate Shabbat and travel after a doctor; for even for a wound that is not of the hollow it is stated below at end of seif 5 that if a safek danger arose for the patient they desecrate:

(ב) And suffering, etc. — see in Mishna Berurah what is written that we require that his entire body become ill thereby — this is from Magen Avraham; and in Shulchan Aruch of the Gra he leans that it is possible it is permitted through a gentile even if his entire body did not become ill thereby — see there; and in my humble opinion one should also see what Magen Avraham wrote that extracting a tooth is melacha d'oraisa for he is choveil for healing — meaning he is liable even per the view that one who damages in chaburah is exempt, for this is fixing: he does not intend only to extract the tooth and the blood comes automatically; and even though it is psik reisha, nevertheless it is at least psik reisha de-lo nicha lei and its prohibition is only d'rabbanan; and behold per the view that one who damages in chaburah is liable — he is liable here too, as Gra wrote in siman 316 seif 10; but per the view that he is exempt — why is he liable here; and even though through a Jew one should certainly be careful in this — nevertheless through a gentile it is possible there is room to be lenient even when his entire body is not ill:
