(s.k. 3) And he suffers, etc. — it should read: his whole body became ill, etc.; and extracting a tooth — from this he gives a reason that perforce it deals when his whole body became ill from it; for if extracting a tooth were d'rabbanan prohibition — it is possible to say it deals even when his whole body did not become ill and it is only partial illness, and nevertheless permitted, for in partial illness they permit shvut of shvut — namely telling a non-Jew to do shvut, as Magen Avraham wrote below s.k. 12 and above siman 327; therefore he wrote it is d'oraisa melacha, and to tell a non-Jew to do d'oraisa melacha was not permitted except when his whole body became ill, as below seif 17; and Taz in s.k. 1 disagreed and wrote: namely Ameimar in Beitzah daf 22 permitted kohling the eye which is shvut through a non-Jew; and R' Eliezer said to him: but you master assist — he squeezes and opens; and they answered: assisting has no substance; nevertheless they said end of Makos: both the barber and the wrapper are liable; and they challenged: but the barber does nothing and it is a prohibition without an act; and Rava answered in assisting — he prepares the hairs; behold assisting has substance; specifically in Ameimar's case where everyone's eyes are initially open and he only assists afterward when they kohle him — such assisting has no substance; but in the barber's case where before the wrapper begins to wrap the barber must prepare the hairs — it is as if the barber begins the melacha — such assisting has substance; and if so, here too with extracting a tooth — a person's way is to keep his mouth closed, and when the non-Jew wants to extract the tooth the Israelite must open his mouth — it is similar to the barber and there is substance; and he also brought Maharshal's words that even to extract due to danger it is forbidden, for extraction itself is also danger end of his words; and Shach in Yoreh De'ah siman 338 rejected Taz's words that there is no distinction whether the Israelite begins or assists — rather in all cases there is no substance except the barber's case which is different, for the Torah expanded to obligate the barber, and as Rashi wrote there: "you shall not round" — to include also the barber; but the Gemara challenged that nevertheless he should not be lashed, for it is a prohibition without an act; and they answer on account of assisting — meaning through this it becomes a prohibition with an act; but in general where the Torah did not expand to obligate the assister — we do not obligate him, and they say that on account of slight assistance he is not fit to be obligated; see there; and see in responsum Hagahot HaHagahot part 38 siman 22 what is written on this:
