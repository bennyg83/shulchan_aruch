So too Beit Yosef in name of Orchot Chayyim; and to me this requires further study, for in any case the Israelite assists in this when the non-Jew extracts the tooth. And what is brought from the barber in the last chapter of Makos — that they said he is liable, for thus they said there: Rava said in assisting, and the passage beginning with — the commentator explained there "assisting" means preparing the hairs for the one who wraps; if so, here too in what prepares his tooth for extraction there is proof to the contrary from chapter 2 of Beitzah daf 22: Ameimar permitted having the eye kohled by a non-Jew on Shabbat, and Rav Ashi challenged him there: but you assist him, for he squeezes and opens. Rashi explained: he closes and opens the eyelids to insert the kohl; and they answer: assisting has no substance — meaning one who does not do actual melacha but only assists a little like this, and otherwise it is done without him — has no substance; and so it is also in chapter Hametzni'a (Shabbos daf 94): assisting has no substance; if so, here too it is permitted through the non-Jew's extraction. But the gemaras contradict one another, for since assisting has no substance the barber is not liable for lashes, for we require an act for lashes, and we did not find in Tosafos and the poskim that there are distinctions in this, and presumably both are halacha; and furthermore Rav Ashi himself said so in chapter Hametzni'a — if so Rav Ashi contradicts Rav Ashi, and Tosafos in chapter Hametzni'a challenged Rav Zevid against himself and did not mention that Rav Ashi contradicts himself from what is written; therefore we must distinguish between them: that case of Ameimar — he does no act at all, for his eyes are already open; he only assists him at the time he does the melacha, and since he needs only assistance in general — has no substance; unlike that case of the barber, where before the wrapper begins to wrap the barber prepares his hair — it is as if he begins the melacha and there is substance; and so too in Tur he wrote that he inclines himself toward him for this; if so, here too one who opens his mouth and does an act before the non-Jew — for a person normally keeps his mouth closed, only opening it to eat or the like — if so this is assisting that has substance, similar to the barber itself, and forbidden. And I saw in my teacher z"l who brought in name of Rashal to forbid this, and thus he wrote Rashal: to extract a tooth on Shabbat through a skilled non-Jew — there is no proof to permit it on account of pikuach nefesh, for specifically through a drug which is definite healing they desecrate even d'oraisa melacha; but extracting a tooth is also pikuach nefesh end of his words; but Beit Yosef wrote in name of Orchot Chayyim that one may tell a non-Jew to extract it, and even though the Israelite assists him — we hold assisting has no substance as stated in chapter Hametzni'a end of his words; and we already proved that one must distinguish between that of Hametzni'a and other places; and for this reason they wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 298: a woman whose immersion occurred on the first day of Yom Tov and she forgot to cut her nails on erev Yom Tov — she should not tell a non-Jew to cut her nails on Yom Tov, because she extends her hand intentionally for this; and this is similar to the barber we mentioned; so it appears correct to me for halacha to forbid; and see in seif 7:
